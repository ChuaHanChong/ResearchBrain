# Learning Structured Robot Policies from Vision-Language Models via Synthetic Neuro-Symbolic Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a neuro-symbolic framework enabling Vision-Language Models to generate structured, interpretable, and reactive Behavior Tree policies for robots. It achieves zero-shot transfer of these policies from entirely synthetic multimodal supervision to physical robotic platforms like the Franka Emika Panda and UR5e.

## Problem

- End-to-end visuomotor policies from foundation models often operate as opaque "black boxes," hindering interpretation, analysis, and verification in safety-critical robotic domains.
- Linear action sequences generated by many VLM outputs lack reactivity, preventing autonomous recovery from unexpected environmental perturbations or task failures.
- Training robust VLM-based robot policies typically requires extensive, costly, and labor-intensive real-world datasets and manual annotation.

## Method

- A ==neuro-symbolic framework== is developed to enable a compact VLM to synthesize executable ==Behavior Trees== (BTs) from multimodal inputs (RGB observations, natural language instructions, and structured system specifications).
- An automated, fully ==synthetic dataset generation pipeline==, powered by a large foundation model (Gemini 3 Flash), creates 10,000 instruction-BT pairs from procedurally generated MuJoCo scenes.
- ==Structural priors and constraints==, such as ==reactive guarding==, ==spatial offsetting==, and a flat hierarchy, are enforced during BT generation to ensure valid, robust, and safe robotic execution.

## Results

- Policies learned from synthetic MuJoCo data successfully transferred to physical Franka Emika Panda and Universal Robots UR5e systems without any real-world fine-tuning.
- The fine-tuned **Pixtral-12B** model achieved **100%** Task Success Rate, JSON Validity Rate, and Schema Compliance in a zero-shot setting, matching or exceeding the performance of significantly larger state-of-the-art foundation models.
- The model demonstrated visual referential grounding by correctly identifying target objects based on relative spatial position but showed limitations in compositional generalization for unseen semantic tasks and out-of-vocabulary primitives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs can learn robust symbolic grounding and spatial reasoning from purely synthetic multimodal supervision, enabling zero-shot logic transfer from simulation to real-world robot systems.
> - Carefully configured LoRA adaptation and sufficient context window sizes are crucial for VLMs to internalize complex robotic grammar and accurately map visual-spatial relationships to symbolic categories.
> - Automated synthetic supervision offers a scalable and cost-effective method for training structured robot policies, significantly reducing the reliance on expensive real-world data collection and manual annotation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Detailed Research Report

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is experiencing a significant shift with the advent of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs), which have demonstrated capabilities in mapping multimodal observations and natural language instructions to robot behaviors. Current approaches frequently rely on end-to-end visuomotor policies. However, these policies often operate as opaque "black boxes," making their interpretation, analysis, and constraint challenging. This characteristic limits their applicability in safety-critical robotic domains where transparency, predictability, and reliability are paramount.

In contrast, classical robotic systems have historically leveraged structured policy representations, such as Behavior Trees (BTs). BTs offer advantages including modularity, interpretability, and reactive execution, which are beneficial for complex and safety-critical tasks. The broader research landscape is therefore grappling with the challenge of integrating the powerful multimodal perception and reasoning capabilities of foundation models with the structured, interpretable nature of classical robotic control paradigms.

Prior research has explored using LLMs for BT generation, initially producing preliminary task information or filling templates, and later directly generating executable BTs from text. Some works have moved towards visually grounded BT generation, often using real-world robotic datasets or relying on simulation-only evaluations. For instance, approaches have used VLMs to generate BT condition nodes for context-aware planning or to synthesize BTs from atomic interaction primitives for active perception. However, these methods often depend on real-world data for supervision or exhibit limited forms of symbolic grounding, where the mapping from visual-spatial relations to symbolic BT parameters remains a challenge.

The Sim-to-Real gap is another central challenge in deploying VLMs in robotics. While synthetic data has shown promise in improving spatial reasoning and enabling transfer to real-world tasks, most efforts focus on visual domain adaptation or low-level motor policies.

This work contributes to this landscape by proposing a neuro-symbolic approach that bridges high-dimensional learning and symbolic control. It differentiates itself by specializing VLMs to generate *structured, interpretable, and reactive* Behavior Tree policies *entirely from synthetic multimodal supervision*. This eliminates the need for real-world robotic datasets for training, addresses the limitations of opaque end-to-end policies, and aims to ensure both geometric grounding and logical transparency in robot decision-making. The approach aligns with the growing interest in structured prompting and data synthesis for improving LLM performance in domain-specific planning tasks, particularly in robotics.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate how foundation models, specifically Vision-Language Models (VLMs), can be specialized to generate structured robot policies grounded in multimodal perception. This aims to bridge the gap between advanced high-dimensional learning capabilities and the interpretability, modularity, and reactivity offered by symbolic control representations like Behavior Trees (BTs).

The motivation for this work arises from several identified bottlenecks and challenges in deploying current VLM-based robotic systems, particularly in safety-critical applications:

- **Computational Opacity (P4):** Existing end-to-end visuomotor policies generated by many VLMs operate as "black boxes." This lack of transparency makes them difficult to interpret, analyze, constrain, or verify, hindering their adoption in scenarios where reliable and predictable behavior is essential. The objective is to replace these opaque outputs with interpretable, executable symbolic policies.
- **Decoupled Perception and Planning (P1):** Traditional robotic systems often separate visual feature extraction from logical reasoning, which can lead to error propagation, increased latency, and a less cohesive understanding of the environment for decision-making. The motivation is to achieve a more integrated approach where multimodal perception directly informs the generation of structured task plans.
- **Heuristic Inconsistency (P2):** General-purpose VLM outputs may struggle to adhere to the strict architectural and safety protocols inherent in robotic operations, such as mandatory z-offsets for collision avoidance or flat hierarchy constraints for efficient execution. The goal is to enforce these robotic constraints directly within the synthesized symbolic policies.
- **Execution Fragility (P3):** Linear action sequences, common in many VLM outputs, lack the inherent reactivity of Behavior Trees. This prevents autonomous recovery from unexpected environmental perturbations or task failures. The motivation is to synthesize policies that incorporate reactive checks and recovery behaviors, enhancing robustness.
- **Scalable Supervision:** Training robust VLM-based robot policies typically requires extensive real-world datasets, which are costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive to collect and annotate. A core motivation is to overcome this bottleneck by developing an automated, scalable pipeline for generating high-quality training data, relying exclusively on synthetic supervision without manual annotation. This also allows for explicit control over scene composition and logical consistency.

In summary, the key motivation is to harness the perception and reasoning capabilities of VLMs while preserving the advantages of classical planning representations (interpretability, modularity, reactivity, verifiability). This involves developing a neuro-symbolic framework that enables VLMs to synthesize executable BTs from visual observations, natural language instructions, and structured system specifications, primarily through a novel synthetic supervision strategy.

#### 1.3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a neuro-symbolic framework designed to enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to synthesize structured Behavior Tree (BT) policies from multimodal inputs. The approach is characterized by a fully synthetic data generation pipeline, supervised fine-tuning of a compact VLM, and the enforcement of explicit structural and logical constraints.

**Overall Framework:**
The framework takes high-dimensional RGB observations (`I`), natural language instructions (`U`), and structured system specifications (`S`) as input. It then generates an executable Behavior Tree (`T`) as the output, representing the robot's policy. The system specifications `S` include the available primitive library (atomic actions and conditions), safety constraints, and dynamic object metadata (e.g., current poses).

**1. Fully Synthetic Dataset Generation:**
The core of the approach is an automated pipeline for generating a synthetic multimodal dataset for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). This bypasses the need for real-world data collection and manual annotation.

- **Visual Scenes Generation:**
 - 10,000 unique tabletop scenes are procedurally generated using the MuJoCo physics engine.
 - Scenes feature various geometric entities (prisms, parallelepipeds, cylinders) with randomized colors and spatial configurations.
 - Domain Randomization techniques are applied to bridge the visual sim-to-real gap.
 - Dynamic object pose labels (`ξ_Oj`) and auxiliary workspace poses (e.g., `temp_pose`) are extracted as symbolic metadata for each scene.

- **Multimodal Prompt Generation:**
 - A large foundation model, Gemini 3 Flash, is used to automatically produce 10,000 instruction-BT pairs from the synthetic scenes and metadata.
 - This is a two-stage iterative process:
 - **Stage 1 (Instruction Generation):** Gemini 3 Flash generates natural language instructions (`U`) by conditioning on the rendered image (`I`) and symbolic metadata. A high sampling temperature (T=1.4) is used to encourage linguistic diversity.
 - **Stage 2 (Behavior Tree Generation):** Gemini 3 Flash generates the corresponding ground-truth JSON-encoded Behavior Tree (`T`). A low sampling temperature (T=0.2) is used to favor structural consistency. This step is conditioned on the previously generated instruction, the scene information, and a structured JSON schema providing a one-shot reference example of the expected BT structure and system specifications `S`.
 - This process ensures semantic alignment between visual input, language instruction, and the target BT, facilitating the learning of symbolic grounding.

**2. Problem Formulation and Symbolic Grounding:**
The synthesis task is defined as a mapping function `f_θ: I × U × S → Π`, where `Π` is the set of all valid Behavior Trees. The generated BT `T` must be structurally sound (adhere to JSON-BT grammar) and behaviorally grounded (parameters align with visual object distribution). Crucially, object poses are treated as dynamic variables referenced by symbolic labels, making BTs robust to environmental perturbations.

**3. Structural Priors and Constraints:**
Three core structural paradigms are enforced during synthetic dataset creation to ensure valid and robust robotic execution:

- **Reactive Guarding:** Interaction nodes (e.e., `CloseGripper`) are nested within `Selector` or `Parallel` composite nodes with corresponding `Condition` checks (e.g., `is_at_pose`) to ensure concurrent motion and perceptual verification.
- **Spatial Offsetting:** The model is trained to compute derived poses (e.g., approach poses) by applying a positive z-offset to base object coordinates using symbolic arithmetic within the JSON schema (`ξ_a = T_off * ξ_O`).
- **Structural Homogeneity (Flat Hierarchy):** To prevent logic depth errors and ensure efficiency, a constraint is enforced that a parent node cannot be of the same type as its children (e.g., no `Sequence` inside a `Sequence`). This promotes shallow, wide trees.

**4. Atomic Primitive Decomposition:**
Complex tasks are decomposed into a finite set of atomic actions (e.g., `MovePose`, `OpenGripper`, `CloseGripper`, `MoveDown`) and condition checks (e.g., `is_at_pose`, `is_grasped`, `is_contact`, `is_at_home`). This enhances explainability, execution robustness, and model efficiency by reducing the output space.

**5. Model Training (Supervised Fine-Tuning):**
- **Model Selection:** Pixtral-12B, an open-source VLM with a 400M-parameter vision encoder and a 12B-parameter multimodal decoder, is chosen as the foundational backbone.
- **Memory-Efficient Adaptation:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is utilized to fine-tune Pixtral-12B. A "heavy" module configuration is employed, injecting trainable low-rank matrices into query, key, value, and output projections, as well as MLP gate, up, and down projection modules. The configuration uses rank `r=16`, scaling factor `α=32`, and dropout rate `0.05`.
- **Hardware and Implementation:** Training is performed on an NVIDIA A40 GPU (48 GiB VRAM) using memory-efficient strategies such as 8-bit Paged AdamW optimizer, Bfloat16 precision, gradient checkpointing, and gradient accumulation (effective batch size of 4). A maximum context window of 4,400 tokens is enforced, discarding longer samples.
- **Training Objective:** Standard causal cross-entropy loss over the generated output tokens (`L = -1/T * Σ log P(y_t | y_<t, I, U, S)`).
- **Preprocessing:** A specialized pipeline performs recursive null-key removal and utilizes a minified JSON format to reduce token count.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** During training, Causal Cross-Entropy Loss (LOSS), Perplexity (PPL), Peak VRAM Usage (PVU), and Training Latency (TL) are monitored. PPL below ~1.85 is found to correlate with consistent JSON validity.

**6. Inference Pipeline and Prompting:**
During inference, the fine-tuned VLM receives a real-world RGB image `I`, user request `U`, and system specification `S`. Greedy decoding (T=0) is used for deterministic output. A generation limit of 4,000 new tokens is set.

#### 1.4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy of learning structured robot policies from synthetic vision-language model supervision.

**1. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer and Visual Robustness:**
- Structured policies learned entirely from synthetic MuJoCo data successfully transferred to physical robot systems using raw real-world egocentric images. This demonstrates "Zero-Shot Logic Transfer," indicating the model's robustness to the visual domain gap (textures, shadows, lens distortions) without real-world fine-tuning.
- The fine-tuned VLM (Pixtral-12B) exhibited visual invariance between synthetic and real images, implying it learned robust mappings between symbolic labels and visual features, internalizing spatial logic rather than memorizing low-level visual patterns.

**2. Visual Referential Grounding and Spatial Reasoning:**
- The model successfully resolved referential ambiguities based on visual cues. For example, when given the instruction "Pick up the left cylinder" with two cylinders of different colors, it correctly identified the target based on its relative spatial position in the RGB image, demonstrating that its vision backbone is essential for grounding instructions in unstructured environments.

**3. Ablation Study on Hyperparameters and Architecture (Table IV):**
- **Module Mapping and Capacity:** A "heavy" LoRA configuration, which adapts Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers in addition to attention projections, was crucial for internalizing the rigid JSON-BT grammar and correctly mapping visual-spatial relationships to symbolic categories. Lighter configurations resulted in low Task Success Rate (TSR) and frequent defaulting to natural language or mislabeling node types.
- **LoRA Rank:** A rank of `r=16` was found to be optimal. Lower ranks (e.g., `r=8`) led to syntactic fragility (e.g., unclosed brackets), while higher ranks (e.g., `r=32`) introduced structural "noise" and hallucinated arguments, indicating potential overfitting.
- **Context Window:** A larger context window (4,400 tokens) was essential for learning complex multi-object and long-horizon tasks. Smaller windows (e.g., 1,200 tokens) severely restricted the diversity of training samples, leading to catastrophic distribution shifts and failure in complex tasks (0% TSR for multi-object tasks).
- **Learning Rate and Regularization:** The learning rate was highly sensitive, with `1e-4` proving optimal for stable convergence and syntactic validity. Extreme rates caused catastrophic forgetting or insufficient adaptation. A dropout rate of `0.05` offered the best generalization.
- **Dataset Scaling:** While 2,000 samples allowed the model to grasp the concept of JSON, 5,000 samples showed a significant jump in handling multi-object logic (TSR 10% to 50%). Scaling to 10,000 samples provided the necessary robustness for 100% success, particularly in resolving referential ambiguity across diverse scenes.

**4. Comparative Analysis with SOTA Models (GPT-5, Gemini 3 Flash):**
- **Zero-Shot Performance:** State-of-the-art (SOTA) large foundation models (GPT-5, Gemini 3 Flash) failed to produce executable JSON-BTs in a zero-shot setting, defaulting to natural language or generating invalid JSON schemas with hallucinated keys.
- **One-Shot Performance:** With one-shot in-context learning (providing a single example of a valid JSON-BT), SOTA models achieved high syntactic compliance, but still sometimes produced unpredictable artifacts.
- **Efficiency:** The fine-tuned Pixtral-12B (12B parameters) achieved 100% TSR, JSON Validity Rate (JVR), and Schema Compliance (SC) in a zero-shot setting, matching or exceeding the performance of significantly larger SOTA models. This indicates that through synthetic supervision, the model internalized the robotic grammar as an intrinsic prior, negating the need for context-window space for examples.

**5. Zero-Shot Generalization to Unseen Semantic Tasks:**
- When presented with an unseen high-level command like "Switch the positions of the two cylinders," the model exhibited "Pattern Backsliding." It identified objects correctly but failed to autonomously decompose the task into the necessary buffer-based sequence (e.g., moving one object to a temporary pose). Instead, it attempted to stack them, indicating a limitation in compositional generalization for novel multi-stage procedural reasoning without explicit demonstrations.

**6. Zero-Shot Generalization to Out-of-Vocabulary Primitives:**
- An experiment involving an out-of-vocabulary end-effector (vacuum gripper) revealed a "Functional Grounding Gap." The model successfully generalized lexical vocabulary, replacing "grasp" with "EngageVacuum," but failed in causal grounding. It attempted to engage the vacuum at the robot's home pose, demonstrating a lack of zero-shot understanding of the physics of interaction (e.g., requiring contact before engagement). This indicates a need for diverse training across end-effector modalities.

**7. Real-World Robotic Validation:**
- The fine-tuned Model A1 was successfully deployed on two different industrial robotic platforms: a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda and a 6-DOF Universal Robots UR5e.
- The synthesized BTs were executed successfully on both robots for pick-and-place and stacking tasks within the semantic scope of the training data.
- This demonstrates hardware-agnostic planning, as the high-level task plans were platform-independent, relying on symbolic actions and reactive constraints. The reactive nature of the BTs effectively managed mechanical variances between the robots. The dual-platform success confirms that the model internalizes a logic representation robust enough to transfer across visual domain gaps and different hardware.

#### 1.5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the integration of multimodal foundation models with structured robot control, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

**1. Enhanced Interpretability and Safety in Robotics:** By synthesizing Behavior Trees instead of opaque end-to-end policies, the framework provides an interpretable and verifiable robot decision-making process. This is critical for safety-critical applications where understanding, analyzing, and debugging robot behavior is essential. The modular and reactive nature of BTs inherently allows for easier monitoring, fault detection, and recovery, which can increase trust and accelerate the deployment of intelligent robotic systems in real-world environments.

**2. Scalable and Cost-Effective Policy Learning:** The introduction of a fully synthetic neuro-symbolic supervision strategy represents a significant paradigm shift. It eliminates the traditional bottleneck of labor-intensive and costly real-world data collection and manual annotation. This automated pipeline enables scalable generation of high-quality, logically consistent training data, making advanced robot policy learning more accessible and efficient. This could democratize the development of complex robotic skills by reducing the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners.

**3. Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated successful transfer of policies learned entirely from synthetic data to diverse physical robotic platforms, without any real-world fine-tuning, is a key achievement. This "Zero-Shot Logic Transfer" capability reduces the time and resources required for deploying VLM-based policies. It suggests that high-fidelity synthetic data, when carefully designed to encode structural and spatial priors, can effectively act as a proxy for real-world robotic experience, making simulation a more powerful tool for real-world deployment.

**4. Hardware-Agnostic Robotic Control:** The framework's ability to generate platform-agnostic plans, through the abstraction of manipulation skills into an atomic primitive library, promotes greater flexibility in robotic deployments. The same learned policy structure can generalize across heterogeneous manipulators (e.g., Franka Emika Panda and UR5e) with different kinematic properties, reducing the need for robot-specific retraining. This facilitates the development of universal robot skills transferable across a range of industrial and service robots.

**5. Advancement in Neuro-Symbolic AI for Robotics:** This work contributes to the growing field of neuro-symbolic AI by demonstrating a practical and effective integration of neural multimodal perception with symbolic reasoning for verifiable and reactive robot decision-making. It indicates that foundation models can internalize complex robotic grammar and logical structures through carefully curated synthetic supervision, opening new avenues for combining the strengths of data-driven learning with knowledge-based reasoning.

**6. Informing Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as "Pattern Backsliding" for unseen semantic tasks and the "Functional Grounding Gap" for out-of-vocabulary primitives, provide clear directions for future research. These findings highlight the need for more diverse training distributions that cover a broader range of semantic task structures and end-effector modalities. This could lead to advancements in compositional generalization and a deeper understanding of tool-specific interaction physics within VLM-generated policies.
