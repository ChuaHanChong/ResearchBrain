# Retro*: Optimizing LLMs for Reasoning-Intensive Document Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> An LLM-based retrieval model, RETRO*, enables effective document retrieval for tasks requiring fine-grained reasoning by integrating a rubric-based scoring system and test-time scaling. This approach achieved an average nDCG@10 of 36.6 on the BRIGHT benchmark with its 7B model and demonstrated improved scalability and interpretable relevance assessments.

## Problem

- Traditional information retrieval models struggle with tasks demanding fine-grained reasoning to find indirect or implicit document relevance, a crucial capability for complex LLM agent applications.
- Existing reasoning-enhanced retrieval methods primarily offer relative document rankings without interpretable absolute relevance scores, making it difficult for downstream applications to set thresholds.
- Current advanced re-ranking approaches often suffer from poor scalability and high latency due to sequential processing of document sets (listwise or setwise evaluations) and limited exploration of multiple reasoning paths.

## Method

- **RETRO*** introduces a pointwise LLM-based retrieval model that uses a predefined, user-customizable relevance rubric to generate explicit, interpretable integer relevance scores (0-100) for each query-document pair.
- The model employs test-time scaling by sampling multiple reasoning trajectories for a single query-document pair and integrating their scores, typically via averaging, to produce a more robust and reliable relevance estimate.
- A two-stage training ==strategy combines supervised fine-tuning== (SFT) to instill initial reasoning abilities with reinforcement learning (RL) using novel composite rewards (intra-document for scoring, inter-document for ranking) to optimize both absolute score accuracy and relative ranking performance.

## Results

- RETRO* achieved state-of-the-art performance on the BRIGHT reasoning-intensive benchmark, with the **7B** model attaining an average nDCG@10 of 36.6, outperforming all other **7B**-scale baselines.
- Test-time scaling, integrating 16 sampling scores, consistently improved nDCG@10; for instance, the Retro* (**7B**) model's nDCG@10 increased from 36.6 to 38.7, demonstrating substantial gains.
- The pointwise architecture of Retro* facilitated efficient parallelism, resulting in significantly lower inference latency compared to sequential listwise and setwise re-ranking methods, making it practical for large document corpora.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rubric-based prompting allows LLMs to perform fine-grained, interpretable reasoning for document relevance, yielding concrete absolute scores rather than just relative rankings.
> - Aggregating relevance scores from multiple reasoning trajectories during inference significantly enhances retrieval accuracy and robustness, enabling smaller models with scaling to outperform larger, unscaled models.
> - A combined SFT and RL training approach with composite rewards effectively optimizes LLMs for the dual objectives of precise absolute relevance scoring and accurate relative document ranking simultaneously.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Retro*: Optimizing LLMs for Reasoning-Intensive Document Retrieval" addresses a critical and evolving challenge at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) and information retrieval (IR). In the contemporary AI landscape, LLM agents are increasingly tasked with complex problem-solving in domains like software engineering, mathematics, and scientific research. These applications heavily rely on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigms, where external knowledge is retrieved to enhance LLM responses.

However, a significant limitation arises when the relevance of a document to a given task is not direct or explicit. Traditional IR models excel at capturing superficial semantic matches (e.g., keyword or direct question-answer pairs) but struggle with tasks requiring *fine-grained reasoning* to uncover subtle, indirect, or implicit connections. For instance, identifying code snippets based on similar design patterns rather than exact matches, or retrieving mathematical proofs based on underlying theorems rather than identical expressions, demands a deeper level of analytical capability.

Recent advancements in reasoning-capable LLMs, particularly techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, self-consistency, and Tree-of-Thought, have demonstrated their potential for complex problem-solving. Inspired by these, a growing body of IR research has begun to integrate LLM reasoning into document retrieval, primarily for re-ranking tasks. These methods typically involve either zero-shot prompting of general-purpose LLMs or fine-tuning them to elicit more structured reasoning.

Retro* builds upon this foundation but directly addresses three key limitations of existing reasoning-enhanced IR approaches:
1. **Lack of direct relevance measurement:** Many methods provide only relative rankings, not interpretable *absolute* relevance scores, which are often required by downstream RAG applications for filtering or thresholding.
2. **Inflexible test-time scalability:** Existing methods primarily focus on generating a single reasoning path, neglecting the potential for exploring and integrating multiple trajectories to improve reliability and accuracy.
3. **Limited parallelism:** Many advanced re-ranking methods (listwise or setwise) process document sets sequentially, leading to high latency and poor scalability for large corpora.

By proposing a novel pointwise approach with a rubric-based scoring mechanism and a tailored reinforcement learning strategy, Retro* positions itself as a significant advancement. It aims to bridge the gap between powerful LLM reasoning capabilities and the practical demands of efficient, interpretable, and scalable reasoning-intensive document retrieval, thereby enhancing the utility of RAG systems for increasingly complex tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Retro* stems from the limitations of current retrieval models, especially in the context of advanced LLM agents and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). As LLMs tackle more complex, reasoning-intensive tasks, the need to retrieve documents whose relevance is indirect or implicit becomes paramount. Traditional Information Retrieval (IR) models, designed for straightforward semantic matching, consistently fail in these scenarios, necessitating a new approach capable of fine-grained reasoning.

The paper identifies three critical shortcomings in existing reasoning-enhanced IR methods:
1. **Absence of interpretable relevance measurement:** Current models often produce only relative rankings or uncalibrated scores, which are insufficient for applications requiring an absolute, interpretable measure of a document's relevance (e.g., to decide if a document is *sufficiently* relevant to be included in a RAG prompt).
2. **Inadequate test-time scalability:** While some methods use LLMs to generate single reasoning paths, they typically do not leverage the power of exploring and integrating *multiple* reasoning trajectories, which could yield more robust and reliable relevance estimates.
3. **Inefficient parallelism:** Many contemporary re-ranking models process candidate documents in a listwise or setwise manner, leading to sequential computation and prohibitive latency when dealing with large numbers of documents.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of Retro* are:

- **To develop a novel LLM-based retrieval model (Retro*)** specifically engineered for reasoning-intensive IR tasks.
- **To introduce a rubric-based relevance scoring mechanism** that enables the LLM to reason about the relationship between a task and a document based on explicitly defined, fine-grained criteria. This mechanism aims to produce concrete, interpretable relevance scores, moving beyond mere relative ranking.
- **To support flexible test-time scaling** by allowing the model to generate and integrate multiple reasoning trajectories for a single query-document pair, thereby producing more reliable and stable relevance estimates.
- **To design an efficient and scalable architecture** that naturally supports high parallelism, mitigating the latency issues faced by listwise and setwise methods, making it practical for real-world applications.
- **To optimize Retro*'s reasoning and scoring capabilities** through a tailored two-stage training strategy, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for initial reasoning proficiency and reinforcement learning (RL) with novel composite rewards to refine both scoring accuracy and ranking performance.
- **To achieve state-of-the-art performance** on challenging reasoning-intensive benchmarks, demonstrating superior effectiveness compared to existing document retrieval methods.

In essence, Retro* aims to deliver a comprehensive, effective, efficient, and interpretable solution for document retrieval in complex, reasoning-demanding contexts, thereby significantly improving the capabilities of LLM agents and RAG systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Retro* introduces a novel, LLM-based approach for reasoning-intensive document retrieval, built upon two core designs: rubric-based relevance scoring and test-time scaling via score integration. These designs are supported by a sophisticated two-stage training strategy involving supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning with tailored composite rewards.

#### 3.1. Rubric-Based Relevance Scoring

Retro* fundamentally shifts from producing abstract relevance logits or relative rankings to generating concrete, interpretable relevance scores. This is achieved through a **relevance rubric (Γ)**, which consists of:
- **Relevance Definition:** A task-specific statement explicitly defining what constitutes relevance for a given retrieval task (e.g., "retrieve proofs grounded in the same underlying theorem"). This can be customized by users via a `[Relevance Placeholder]`.
- **Scoring Criteria:** A 5-level categorical scale (80-100 Highly Relevant, 60-80 Relevant, 40-60 Moderately Relevant, 20-40 Slightly Relevant, 0-20 Irrelevant) that maps to an integer score between 0 and 100. Each interval has a clear, explicit meaning, ensuring interpretability.

During inference, Retro* operates in a **pointwise** manner. For a given query (q) and a candidate document (d), the model is prompted with the relevance rubric (Γ) to perform a three-step reasoning process: Query Analysis, Document Analysis, and Relevance Annotation. This process generates a reasoning trajectory (y) and a corresponding integer relevance score (s): `Retro(Γ, q, d) → (y, s)`. This explicit reasoning and scoring mechanism allows for fine-grained assessment and direct measurement of relevance.

#### 3.2. Test-Time Scaling via Score Integration

To enhance retrieval accuracy and robustness, Retro* leverages test-time scaling. Instead of relying on a single inference pass, the model samples multiple times (K times) for each query-document pair. This results in a set of `K` reasoning trajectories and their associated scores: `{(y_1, s_1)..., (y_K, s_K)}`.

To derive a more reliable final relevance estimate, Retro* employs a **Score Integration** strategy. While majority voting is common for discrete outputs, Retro* uses a weighted average: `¯s ← (Σ w_i * s_i) / (Σ w_i)`. In the experiments, a uniform weighting scheme (`w_i = 1/K`) is primarily used for simplicity, effectively averaging the `K` individual scores. This approach aggregates the insights from multiple reasoning paths, leading to a more stable and accurate relevance assessment.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

Retro* employs a **two-stage training strategy** to optimize its reasoning capabilities:

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
- **Objective:** To equip the model with initial reasoning abilities and guide it to generate concise, well-structured thoughts aligned with the rubric format.
- **Data Curation:**
 - **Data Sourcing:** A powerful teacher model (e.g., Qwen3-235B-A22B) is used to generate reasoning trajectories (y) and relevance scores (s) for a collection of query-document pairs, following the predefined relevance rubric (Γ). An explicit length control instruction (e.g., within 512 tokens) is added to the prompt to encourage conciseness.
 - **Data Filtering:** For each (q, d) pair, the teacher model is sampled K times (e.g., 8 times). The generated scores are integrated to produce a more reliable reference score (¯s). The specific trajectory whose score is closest to this reference ¯s is selected for the SFT training dataset, creating high-quality `(q, d, y, s)` tuples.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
- **Objective:** To further optimize the model for both accurate absolute relevance scoring and effective relative document ranking. The SFT model serves as the initial policy for RL.
- **Composite Reward:** To simultaneously address both objectives, a novel composite reward `R(y, s) = α · R_intra(y, s) + (1 − α) · R_inter(y, s)` is introduced, where `α ∈ (0, 1)` balances the two reward components.
 - **Intra-Document Reward (R_intra):** This reward focuses on improving the consistency and accuracy of scores for individual documents.
 - **Roll Out:** For a given (q, d) pair, the current policy model generates N trajectories `{(y_1, s_1)..., (y_N, s_N)}`.
 - **Reward Assignment:** The integrated score (¯s) over these N trajectories serves as a more reliable target. The trajectory with the score closest to ¯s receives a +1 reward, while the one furthest receives -1, provided a minimum threshold (τ) gap exists to avoid trivial cases. Other trajectories receive 0.
 - **Inter-Document Reward (R_inter):** This reward aims to improve the model's ability to discriminate between relevant and irrelevant documents, thus enhancing re-ranking performance.
 - **Roll Out:** For each training sample, one positive (d+) and one negative (d-) document are sampled. For each, N trajectories are generated.
 - **Reward Assignment:** For a positive sample's trajectory `(y_i+, s_i+)`, the reward is the proportion of negative samples `(y_j−, s_j−)` whose scores are lower (`s_i+ > s_j−`). Conversely, for a negative sample's trajectory `(y_j−, s_j−)`, the reward is the proportion of positive samples `(y_i+, s_i+)` whose scores are higher (`s_j− < s_i+`).
- **Optimization Algorithm:** The composite reward is then optimized using the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm.

This comprehensive methodology, from rubric definition to a tailored two-stage training with composite rewards, enables Retro* to effectively tackle the challenges of reasoning-intensive document retrieval, providing both interpretable absolute relevance scores and superior ranking performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Retro* on the BRIGHT benchmark (a suite of 12 reasoning-intensive datasets spanning science, mathematics, and programming) and the BEIR benchmark (traditional IR tasks) demonstrates its effectiveness, scalability, and efficiency.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on Reasoning-Intensive IR

- Retro* consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on the BRIGHT benchmark. The Retro* (7B) model obtained an average nDCG@10 of 36.6, significantly outperforming all other 7B-scale baselines, including strong reasoning-enhanced methods like ReasonRank (33.5).
- Scaling up to Retro* (32B) further boosts performance to an average nDCG@10 of 38.5, surpassing ReasonRank (32B) by 1.9 points.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Test-Time Scaling

- The proposed test-time scaling mechanism, integrating scores from multiple sampling trajectories, markedly improves performance. Averaging 16 sampling scores (mean-score@16) increased the Retro* (7B) model's nDCG@10 from 36.6 to 38.7. The Retro* (32B) model similarly improved from 38.5 to 40.6.
- Notably, the scaled 7B model (38.7) surpassed the unscaled 32B model (38.5), highlighting the significant potential of this approach.
- Performance gains from test-time scaling were observed consistently across different model sizes and initial retrieval methods (BM25, ReasonIR), and even on traditional IR benchmarks like BEIR, demonstrating its broad applicability.

#### 4.3. Efficiency via Parallelism

- Retro*'s pointwise architecture allows for massive parallelism, where each query-document pair can be evaluated independently. This provides significant efficiency advantages over listwise and setwise re-ranking methods, which suffer from sequential processing.
- Empirical results on the TheoT. dataset showed that Retro*'s inference time increased much slower with a growing number of candidate documents compared to setwise models like Rank-R1 (which exhibited prohibitively expensive scalability) and listwise models like ReasonRank. This confirms Retro*'s practical scalability for real-world applications with large document corpora.

#### 4.4. Reliable and Interpretable Relevance Scoring

- A key finding is Retro*'s ability to provide direct and *interpretable* relevance measurements, a limitation of many existing models. Figure 4 illustrates that Retro* generates cleanly separated score distributions for positive and negative documents, allowing for meaningful thresholds (e.g., scores above 60 typically indicate positive relevance).
- In contrast, models like RankLLaMA produced mixed score distributions, and Rank1, while showing some binary tendency, still assigned high scores to a significant number of negative documents, undermining score reliability. Retro*'s explicit rubric-based scoring leads to more trustworthy absolute relevance assessments.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study on Training Strategy Components

- **Two-Stage Training:**
 - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) alone provided a significant performance boost (+7.2 nDCG@10 over the baseline Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model, reaching 30.1), demonstrating the value of learning from teacher model trajectories.
 - Reinforcement Learning (RL) directly from the base model, without SFT, also showed strong improvements (+12.2 nDCG@10, reaching 35.1), highlighting the effectiveness of the ranking-oriented rewards.
 - However, the combination of SFT followed by RL with the composite reward achieved the highest performance (36.6), indicating that SFT provides a beneficial warm-up for effective RL exploration.
- **Composite Rewards:**
 - Using only the Intra-Document Reward in RL yielded an nDCG@10 of 33.2 (from 30.1 SFT baseline), showing its contribution to scoring accuracy.
 - Using only the Inter-Document Reward resulted in 30.8, indicating its role in relative ranking.
 - The composite reward, combining both, achieved the best performance of 36.6, confirming that both components are crucial and synergistic for jointly optimizing scoring accuracy and ranking effectiveness. The training dynamics showed a steady increase in both reward and nDCG@10, indicating stable learning.

#### 4.6. Generalizability and Backbone Model Compatibility

- Retro* exhibited strong generalizability, achieving SOTA performance on selected traditional IR datasets from the BEIR benchmark (55.8 nDCG@10 for 7B, 56.8 with scaling), demonstrating its applicability beyond reasoning-intensive tasks.
- The approach was shown to be effective across different backbone LLMs (Qwen2.5-Instruct, Llama3.1-Instruct, Qwen3), consistently improving their nDCG@10 performance and further enhancing it with test-time scaling.

In summary, Retro* not only achieves new state-of-the-art results in challenging reasoning-intensive retrieval tasks but also introduces a robust, interpretable, and scalable framework that addresses critical limitations of prior LLM-based IR methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "RETRO*: Optimizing LLMs for Reasoning-Intensive Document Retrieval" carries significant implications for the field of information retrieval and the broader application of large language models, particularly in agentic AI systems and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

#### 5.1. Advancing Reasoning-Intensive Retrieval

Retro* directly tackles the crucial problem of retrieving documents whose relevance is indirect or implicit, a capability essential for LLMs engaged in complex tasks like scientific discovery or advanced problem-solving. By achieving state-of-the-art performance on the BRIGHT benchmark, Retro* establishes a new benchmark for excellence in this challenging domain, pushing the boundaries of what LLM-powered retrieval systems can achieve.

#### 5.2. Enhancing Interpretability and Trust

The introduction of a rubric-based relevance scoring mechanism is a pivotal contribution. Unlike previous methods that often provide opaque or uncalibrated relevance signals, Retro* offers fine-grained, interpretable integer scores (0-100) with clearly defined criteria. This transparency is vital for developers and end-users to understand *why* a document is considered relevant, fostering greater trust in AI systems. This interpretability can be crucial in high-stakes applications where the rationale behind retrieved information is important.

#### 5.3. Improving Reliability through Test-Time Scaling

Retro*'s novel test-time scaling approach, which integrates scores from multiple reasoning trajectories, significantly enhances the reliability and stability of relevance estimates. This mechanism provides a robust way to reduce the inherent variability of LLM outputs, leading to more consistent and dependable retrieval results, particularly beneficial for critical decision-making processes.

#### 5.4. Boosting Efficiency and Scalability

By adopting a pointwise architecture, Retro* enables massive parallelism, allowing for the independent evaluation of query-document pairs. This design dramatically improves inference efficiency and scalability compared to sequential listwise or setwise re-ranking methods, making Retro* a more practical solution for real-world RAG systems that operate over vast document corpora. Its performance consistency across different first-stage retrievers (BM25, ReasonIR) further underscores its robustness.

#### 5.5. Pioneering LLM Training Paradigms

The two-stage training strategy, combining supervised fine-tuning with a novel reinforcement learning framework using composite Intra-Document and Inter-Document Rewards, represents a significant methodological advancement. This tailored approach effectively optimizes LLMs for the dual objectives of precise absolute scoring and accurate relative ranking. The ablation studies clearly demonstrate the synergistic benefits of this composite reward structure and the warm-up provided by SFT, offering valuable insights for future LLM training for specialized tasks.

#### 5.6. Broad Generalizability

Beyond its strength in reasoning-intensive tasks, Retro* has also demonstrated strong performance on traditional IR benchmarks (BEIR) and across various LLM backbones (Qwen, Llama), indicating its general-purpose applicability. This suggests that the principles behind Retro* are robust and can be adapted to a wide range of retrieval scenarios, making it a versatile tool for enhancing diverse RAG applications.

**Potential Impact:**
Retro* has the potential to significantly impact the development of more intelligent and autonomous LLM agents by providing them with a more sophisticated and reliable mechanism for accessing and leveraging external knowledge. It can empower next-generation RAG systems to handle complex queries that demand deeper understanding and contextual reasoning. The open-sourcing of resources associated with Retro* is likely to foster further research and development in rubric-based reasoning, composite reward design, and scalable LLM-based retrieval. This work sets a new standard for building LLM-powered retrieval systems that are not only powerful but also transparent, reliable, and efficient.
