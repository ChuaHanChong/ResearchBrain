# Retro*: Optimizing LLMs for Reasoning-Intensive Document Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> An LLM-based retrieval model, RETRO*, enables effective document retrieval for tasks requiring fine-grained reasoning by integrating a rubric-based scoring system and test-time scaling. This approach achieved an average nDCG@10 of 36.6 on the BRIGHT benchmark with its 7B model and demonstrated improved scalability and interpretable relevance assessments.

## Problem

- Traditional information retrieval models struggle with tasks demanding fine-grained reasoning to find indirect or implicit document relevance, a crucial capability for complex LLM agent applications.
- Existing reasoning-enhanced retrieval methods primarily offer relative document rankings without interpretable absolute relevance scores, making it difficult for downstream applications to set thresholds.
- Current advanced re-ranking approaches often suffer from poor scalability and high latency due to sequential processing of document sets (listwise or setwise evaluations) and limited exploration of multiple reasoning paths.

## Method

- **RETRO*** introduces a pointwise LLM-based retrieval model that uses a predefined, user-customizable relevance rubric to generate explicit, interpretable integer relevance scores (0-100) for each query-document pair.
- The model employs test-time scaling by sampling multiple reasoning trajectories for a single query-document pair and integrating their scores, typically via averaging, to produce a more robust and reliable relevance estimate.
- A two-stage training ==strategy combines supervised fine-tuning== (SFT) to instill initial reasoning abilities with reinforcement learning (RL) using novel composite rewards (intra-document for scoring, inter-document for ranking) to optimize both absolute score accuracy and relative ranking performance.

## Results

- RETRO* achieved state-of-the-art performance on the BRIGHT reasoning-intensive benchmark, with the **7B** model attaining an average nDCG@10 of 36.6, outperforming all other **7B**-scale baselines.
- Test-time scaling, integrating 16 sampling scores, consistently improved nDCG@10; for instance, the Retro* (**7B**) model's nDCG@10 increased from 36.6 to 38.7, demonstrating substantial gains.
- The pointwise architecture of Retro* facilitated efficient parallelism, resulting in significantly lower inference latency compared to sequential listwise and setwise re-ranking methods, making it practical for large document corpora.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rubric-based prompting allows LLMs to perform fine-grained, interpretable reasoning for document relevance, yielding concrete absolute scores rather than just relative rankings.
> - Aggregating relevance scores from multiple reasoning trajectories during inference significantly enhances retrieval accuracy and robustness, enabling smaller models with scaling to outperform larger, unscaled models.
> - A combined SFT and RL training approach with composite rewards effectively optimizes LLMs for the dual objectives of precise absolute relevance scoring and accurate relative document ranking simultaneously.
