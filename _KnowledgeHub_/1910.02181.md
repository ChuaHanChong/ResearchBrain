# To React or not to React: End-to-End Visual Pose Forecasting for Personalized Avatar during Dyadic Conversations

> [!summary] Summary
> DRAM (Dyadic Residual-Attention Model) forecasts a personalized avatar's body pose during dyadic conversation from the avatar's audio plus the interlocutor's audio and pose. It splits prediction into a monadic and a dyadic branch, then blends them with a per-joint, per-timestep attention vector derived from the residual between the two branches. It cuts average position error to 2.8 cm and wins user-study ratings.

## Problem

- Rule-based agent toolkits emit a fixed set of discrete gestures, posing generation as classification, so they cannot generalize to unseen continuous behaviours.
- Prior audio-to-gesture models use bidirectional LSTMs over future speech, making them unusable for real-time, autoregressive avatar animation.
- Intrapersonal cues outnumber interpersonal ones, so a jointly trained model treats the speaker's own audio-pose prior as dominant and ignores the interlocutor.

## Method

- Decomposes forecasting into a ==monadic== function on avatar audio plus pose history and a ==dyadic== function that additionally consumes the interlocutor's audio, pose, and the monadic latent.
- **Dyadic Residual Vector**: tanh of the absolute monadic-dyadic prediction difference, giving per-joint soft-attention weights that need no extra network and stay interpretable.
- Final pose is the convex blend of monadic and dyadic branches under that vector, trained end-to-end with an L2 pose loss; both branches instantiate as ==LSTMs== or dilated residual ==TCNs==.

## Results

- DRAM reaches **2.8 cm** average position error versus **3.0** for the best non-adaptive model and **3.3**-**3.9** for audio-only or single-dynamics baselines; wrist error drops roughly **1 cm**.
- The PCK gap over Early Fusion and DRAM-without-attention widens as the threshold grows, showing lower variance of erroneous predictions.
- User study: DRAM scores **4.6** average on a 7-point Likert scale for naturalness, intrapersonal, and interpersonal dynamics, significantly above monadic and early-fusion TCNs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When one signal source dominates a joint objective, explicit branch decomposition plus learned gating recovers the weaker source that naive early fusion drowns out.
> - An attention weight defined as the residual between two competing predictors is free of extra parameters and directly interpretable as which joints a partner influences.
> - Blend weights hovered near 0.5 rather than saturating, indicating interpersonal and intrapersonal drives overlap continuously instead of switching by conversational role.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: **multimodal machine learning**, **computer graphics (specifically procedural animation and motion processing)**, **human-computer interaction (HCI)**, and **virtual reality (VR)**. Its core contribution is to advance the realism and personalization of virtual avatars, particularly in the context of real-time dyadic (two-person) conversations.

Traditionally, avatar animation has relied on predefined gesture libraries, rule-based systems, or direct motion capture. More recent approaches have leveraged deep learning for audio-driven facial animation and gesture generation. However, much of this work has focused on "monadic" scenarios, where an avatar's behavior is primarily driven by its own speech or internal state, often neglecting the crucial role of an interlocutor in a conversation.

The broader landscape of telepresence aims to create immersive communication experiences that go beyond mere audio-visual links. Non-verbal cues—such as gestures, facial expressions, body posture, and paralinguistic cues—are vital for effective human communication, complementing or clarifying verbal messages. Prior work has highlighted the importance of these cues in fostering a sense of "copresence" and realism in virtual environments. For instance, studies by Bailenson et al. (2006) emphasized the impact of behavioral realism on perceived presence.

The paper differentiates itself from previous pose forecasting work by explicitly addressing the "interpersonal dynamics" of conversation, where a person's behavior is influenced by the interlocutor. While some earlier research explored audio-visual fusion for emotion modeling or speech-gesture correlations, few have integrated both intrapersonal (self-driven) and interpersonal (reaction-driven) dynamics for full-body pose generation in a unified, end-to-end framework. Existing audio-to-gesture generation methods (e.g., Takeuchi et al., 2017) often use bidirectional LSTMs, which, while effective, are not suitable for real-time forecasting due to their dependency on future input. The authors also highlight that many prior methods rely on linguistic features or predefined gesture sets, limiting their generalizability and real-time applicability.

By introducing an adaptive attention mechanism that can dynamically weigh the influence of intrapersonal versus interpersonal cues, this work pushes the boundaries of personalized avatar animation. It addresses a critical gap in creating truly anthropomorphic and socially responsive virtual agents, moving beyond static or purely self-expressive avatars towards those that can naturally react within dynamic social contexts. This is particularly relevant for the future of VR/AR telepresence, where realistic social interaction is a key challenge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptive neural architecture capable of **forecasting realistic and personalized body poses for a virtual avatar in real-time dyadic conversations**. This goes beyond merely animating an avatar based on its own speech to also incorporating its reactions to the interlocutor's speech and body language.

The core motivations driving this objective are:

- **Improving Telepresence and Immersive Communication:** The authors argue that realistic non-verbal behaviors are crucial for evolving telepresence. To foster a sense of immersion and natural communication in 3D virtual spaces, avatars need to represent not only facial expressions but also produce natural full-body cues that reflect both their own speech and their interaction with others.
- **Modeling Complex Conversational Dynamics:** Human conversations involve a rich interplay of "intrapersonal dynamics" (e.g., a speaker's gestures linked to their own speech) and "interpersonal dynamics" (e.g., mirroring gestures, head nods of agreement, posture shifts in response to an interlocutor). Existing avatar generation systems often struggle to capture this complexity, particularly the adaptive nature of human reactions.
- **Addressing the Imbalance of Dynamics:** In typical dyadic conversations, intrapersonal dynamics (e.g., self-generated gestures during speaking) occur more frequently than explicit interpersonal reactions. This natural imbalance can lead models to prioritize the stronger intrapersonal signals, potentially ignoring or downplaying the subtle yet crucial interpersonal cues. The research aims to explicitly address this challenge to ensure that interpersonal dynamics are adequately modeled.
- **Real-time and Personalized Avatar Generation:** The goal is to create personalized avatars whose pose generation is continuous, natural, and responsive in real-time, making them suitable for live telecommunication scenarios. This requires auto-regressive models that only rely on past information.
- **Enhancing Social Realism:** By enabling avatars to dynamically choose "to react or not to react" based on the conversational context, the model aims to generate behaviors that are perceived as more natural, human-like, and socially intelligent by human observers.

In essence, the research seeks to overcome the limitations of monadic gesture generation by introducing an adaptive mechanism that allows avatars to fluidly integrate their own expressive behaviors with appropriate social reactions, thereby making virtual interactions feel more authentic and engaging.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel neural architecture called **Dyadic Residual-Attention Model (DRAM)**, designed to integrate both intrapersonal (monadic) and interpersonal (dyadic) dynamics for end-to-end visual pose forecasting. The key innovation lies in its ability to dynamically weigh the influence of these two types of dynamics using a selective attention mechanism.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is to predict the avatar's next pose ($\hat{y}_{t+1}$) given its own audio features ($X_t$) and pose history ($\hat{Y}_t$), as well as the interlocutor's (human's) audio features ($X^H_t$) and pose history ($Y^H_t$). The model is auto-regressive, meaning it uses previously predicted avatar poses as part of its input history, crucial for real-time applications.

**DRAM Architecture (Figure 2):**
DRAM decomposes the pose generation problem into two core functions:

1. **Monadic Function ($\boldsymbol{f_m}$):** This function models **intrapersonal dynamics**. It predicts the avatar's pose solely based on the avatar's own audio features and pose history. This represents the avatar's self-generated gestures and movements, as if giving a monologue.
 $ \hat{Z}^m_t = f_m(X_{t-1}, Y_{t-1}; \theta_m) $

2. **Dyadic Function ($\boldsymbol{f_d}$):** This function models **interpersonal dynamics**. It takes into account the interlocutor's audio and pose history, *in addition to* the monadic pose prediction from $f_m$. This allows it to capture reactions, mirroring, and other social cues influenced by the interlocutor.
 $ \hat{Z}^d_t = f_d(X^H_{t-1}, Y^H_{t-1}, \hat{Z}^m_{t-1}; \theta_d) $
 *(Note: $\hat{Z}^m_{t-1}$ is used here for computational efficiency, representing the history of monadic predictions)*

**Dyadic Residual Vector ($\boldsymbol{\Delta_t}$):**
The central innovation is the adaptive attention mechanism, implemented as the Dyadic Residual Vector, $\Delta_t$. This vector, with the same dimensions as the pose, dynamically decides how much to focus on monadic versus dyadic predictions for each joint at each time step.
$\Delta_t = \tanh \left| f_d(X^H_{t-1}, Y^H_{t-1}, \hat{Z}^m_{t-1}; \theta_d) - f_m(X_{t-1}, Y_{t-1}; \theta_m) \right|$

The absolute difference between the dyadic and monadic predictions represents the "residual"—the change in pose attributed to interpersonal dynamics. Applying a $\tanh$ non-linearity compresses this residual into values between 0 and 1, effectively creating attention weights for each joint. A high value in $\Delta_t$ for a particular joint indicates a strong influence from interpersonal dynamics for that joint, while a low value suggests that intrapersonal dynamics are dominant.

**Final Pose Prediction:**
The final predicted pose ($\hat{y}_t$) is a weighted sum of the monadic and dyadic predictions, controlled by $\Delta_t$:
$\hat{y}_t = (1 - \Delta_t) \odot \hat{Z}^m_t + \Delta_t \odot \hat{Z}^d_t$
Where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication.

**Temporal Models:**
The functions $f_m$ and $f_d$ can be instantiated with any auto-regressive temporal neural network. The authors explore two popular choices:
- **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs):** Specifically, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, known for handling sequential data.
- **Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs):** These are convolutional networks designed for sequence modeling, often employing residual connections and dilated convolutions to capture long-range dependencies efficiently.

**Loss Function:**
The model is trained using a Mean Squared Error (L2) loss between the predicted pose and the ground truth pose:
$L = \sum_t \| \hat{y}_t - y_t \|^2_2$

**Dataset and Feature Representation:**
- **Dataset:** Dyadic face-to-face conversational data, featuring one consistent participant interacting with 11 others, totaling approximately 11 hours.
- **Audio Features:** GeMAPS (Geneva Minimalistic Acoustic Parameter Set) features extracted using OpenSmile, known for affect recognition. Sampled at 90 Hz to match pose frequency.
- **Pose Features:** 12 upper-body joint positions (Torso, Head, Neck, RArm, LArm, RWrist, LWrist) from Motion-Capture (MoCap) data, converted to local rotation vectors parameterized as quaternions. Translation is ignored, focusing on rotational movements.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
To thoroughly evaluate DRAM, the authors compare it against several baselines, including:
- **Monadic Models ($f_m$):** Avatar Audio Only, Avatar Monadic Only (using avatar's own audio/pose history).
- **Interpersonal-focused Monadic Models ($f_m$):** Human Audio Only, Human Monadic Only (using interlocutor's audio/pose history to predict avatar's pose).
- **Non-adaptive Joint Models:**
 - **Early Fusion ($f$):** A single model directly concatenating all avatar and human inputs (audio and pose) and predicting the pose, representing a non-adaptive approach to combining dynamics.
 - **DRAM w/o Attention ($f_d$):** This effectively uses only the dyadic prediction, corresponding to Equation 5, which still processes both dynamics but without the adaptive weighting.

This comprehensive set of baselines allows for isolating the contribution of the adaptive attention mechanism in DRAM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both objective and subjective evaluations to demonstrate the effectiveness of the Dyadic Residual-Attention Model (DRAM).

**Objective Evaluation Metrics:**

1. **Average Position Error (APE):** Measures the average L2 distance (in cm) between predicted and ground truth keypoint positions.
 - **Overall Performance:** DRAM consistently achieved the lowest APE (2.8 cm for both LSTM and TCN variants), outperforming all baseline models. This confirms its superior accuracy in predicting avatar poses.
 - **Adaptive vs. Non-Adaptive:** DRAM significantly improved upon non-adaptive joint models like Early Fusion (3.0 cm) and DRAM w/o Attention (3.0-3.1 cm). This highlights the importance of the adaptive attention mechanism in dynamically balancing intrapersonal and interpersonal influences.
 - **Intrapersonal vs. Interpersonal:** Monadic models (Avatar Audio Only, Avatar Monadic Only) performed better than purely interpersonal-focused ones (Human Audio Only, Human Monadic Only), indicating the stronger prior of an individual's speech-gesture correlation. However, DRAM, by combining both adaptively, surpassed even the best monadic models.
 - **Joint-Specific Observations:**
 - **Torso and Neck:** Showed low APE values across all models, suggesting these joints are relatively easier to predict due to less frequent and more stable movements.
 - **Wrists:** Had significantly higher APE values across all models (9-21 cm), indicating that hand/wrist gestures are complex and challenging to predict accurately, regardless of the model. However, DRAM still offered the largest reduction in APE for wrists compared to baselines.
 - **Head:** Predictions for the head were notably worse for models relying solely on intrapersonal dynamics, but improved with dyadic information. This supports the hypothesis that head movements often reflect interpersonal cues like mirroring head nods.

2. **Probability of Correct Keypoints (PCK):** Measures the percentage of predicted keypoints that fall within a certain radius ($\sigma$) of the ground truth.
 - **Robustness:** DRAM showed a larger gap in PCK values compared to baselines (Early Fusion, DRAM w/o Attention) as the PCK threshold ($\sigma$) increased. This implies that DRAM's erroneous predictions have lower variance, making it a more robust model with fewer large deviations from the ground truth.

**Subjective Evaluation (User Study):**

A user study involving human annotators was conducted to evaluate the generated avatar poses based on three criteria on a 1-7 Likert scale:
1. **Naturalness (S1):** How natural the motion looked and matched the avatar's audio.
2. **Intrapersonal Dynamics (S2):** How much the avatar behaved like itself (consistent with its own style).
3. **Interpersonal Dynamics (S3):** How realistically the avatar reacted to the interlocutor's behavior and speech.

- **Overall Preference:** Human observers consistently rated DRAM's generated poses as significantly better across all three criteria compared to baseline models (Avatar Monadic Only and Early Fusion). DRAM achieved an average score of 4.6 (indicating "somewhat agree"), while the Avatar Monadic Only model was rated neutrally.
- **Perceived Naturalness:** The higher scores for naturalness confirm that DRAM produces more fluid and believable animations.
- **Adaptive Behavior:** The improved scores for both intrapersonal and interpersonal dynamics validate DRAM's ability to balance self-expression with social reactivity.

**Qualitative Analysis:**

The paper provided examples demonstrating DRAM's ability to:
- **Capture Interpersonal Cues:** Figures 3 and 4 visually illustrate instances where the avatar successfully performs pose switches and head nods in response to the interlocutor's similar actions or exclamations. The mean of the Dyadic Residual Vector ($\Delta_t$) was shown to increase during these reactive moments, confirming that the model dynamically shifts focus to interpersonal dynamics.
- **Model Intrapersonal Gestures:** Figure 7 shows the avatar performing beat gestures (hand raises) and head nods to emphasize its own speech, with $\Delta_t$ remaining low, indicating dominance of monadic dynamics.
- **Handle Hybrid Dynamics:** Figure 3 further exemplifies a scenario where the avatar initially emphasizes its speech with hand gestures (low $\Delta_t$), then shifts to head nodding in agreement when the interlocutor interjects (high $\Delta_t$), showcasing DRAM's ability to adapt to changing conversational flow. The authors also note that $\Delta_t$ values are often closer to 0.5 rather than extremes, implying that both monadic and dyadic contributions frequently overlap.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that DRAM's adaptive attention mechanism enables it to generate more accurate, natural, and socially responsive avatar body poses by effectively integrating and balancing intrapersonal and interpersonal dynamics in dyadic conversations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of virtual avatar animation and human-computer interaction, particularly for applications requiring realistic social presence. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Telepresence and Virtual Reality:** The most direct impact is on telepresence systems and social VR/AR platforms. By enabling avatars to exhibit natural, personalized, and context-aware non-verbal behaviors, this work contributes to creating more immersive, engaging, and less fatiguing remote communication experiences. It makes virtual interactions feel more "human" and less artificial, fostering a stronger sense of connection between participants.
- **More Believable Virtual Agents and NPCs:** The methodology can be extended to develop more sophisticated embodied conversational agents (ECAs) or non-player characters (NPCs) in games and simulations. Avatars capable of naturally reacting to users and other virtual characters will significantly enhance the realism and interactivity of virtual environments, leading to more believable storytelling and training simulations.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Behavioral Modeling:** The Dyadic Residual-Attention Model (DRAM) introduces a novel and interpretable adaptive attention mechanism. This concept of dynamically weighing different sources of influence (intrapersonal vs. interpersonal) is generalizable and could inspire similar adaptive architectures in other areas of multimodal AI, such as emotion recognition, sentiment analysis, or human behavior prediction in complex social scenarios.
- **Real-time Applications:** The auto-regressive nature of DRAM, relying only on past information, makes it suitable for real-time deployment. This is crucial for live communication and interactive virtual environments, addressing a key limitation of some previous approaches that required future input.
- **Personalization and Anthropomorphism:** The model's ability to generate "person-specific" poses that correlate with an individual's unique speaking style and reactions contributes to personalized avatar experiences. This anthropomorphism is vital for users to feel represented and comfortable in virtual spaces.
- **Understanding Human Social Dynamics:** The interpretable Dyadic Residual Vector ($\Delta_t$) offers a unique tool for analyzing the underlying dynamics of human conversations. Researchers can use $\Delta_t$ to quantitatively observe when and how much a person's non-verbal behavior is influenced by their interlocutor versus their own internal state, offering insights into social cues.
- **Data-Driven Approach:** By leveraging large datasets of dyadic interactions, this research underscores the power of data-driven methods for modeling complex human behaviors, moving beyond rule-based or hand-crafted animations.

In conclusion, "To React or not to React" presents a compelling solution to a critical challenge in creating highly realistic and interactive virtual avatars. By thoughtfully integrating and adaptively balancing the multifaceted dynamics of human conversation, the authors pave the way for a future where virtual communication is as nuanced and natural as face-to-face interaction.
