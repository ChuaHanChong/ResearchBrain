# Vision-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Vision-R1 introduces a two-phase training methodology that enhances multimodal large language models' reasoning capabilities through a cold-start initialization using a novel human annotation-free dataset and subsequent reinforcement learning with Progressive Thinking Suppression Training. The 7B parameter model achieves competitive performance on multimodal math reasoning benchmarks, sometimes rivaling models with over 70 billion parameters, while exhibiting emergent human-like reasoning processes.

## Problem

- Extending reinforcement learning-driven self-emergent reasoning, as seen in text-only Large Language Models like DeepSeek-R1, to the multimodal domain is challenging.
- There is a scarcity of substantial, high-quality multimodal reasoning data that captures human-like cognitive processes such as questioning, reflection, and self-correction.
- Multimodal Large Language Models tend to "overthink," generating excessively long and often incorrect reasoning chains after initial training, hindering effective reinforcement learning optimization.

## Method

- A two-phase training methodology is proposed: ==cold-start initialization== via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) training.
- A **200K** sample human annotation-free multimodal CoT dataset, =="Vision-R1-cold"==, is generated using =="Modality Bridging"==, which leverages existing MLLMs and a text-only reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-R1) to extract human-like CoT.
- RL training incorporates ==Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST)==, which progressively relaxes constraints on the CoT length across stages (e.g., from 4K to 8K tokens) to mitigate "overthinking."

## Results

- Vision-R1-7B achieves **73.5%** accuracy on MathVista, comparable to leading closed-source models and surpassing open-source models with significantly more parameters (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B).
- The Vision-R1-cold dataset contains a higher frequency of human-like self-reflective indicators ("Wait," "Hmm," "Mistake") compared to existing multimodal Chain-of-Thought datasets.
- Ablation studies demonstrate Vision-R1's comprehensive approach (cold-start + PTST + GRPO) achieves **55.4%** average accuracy, outperforming RL-only baselines (**50.7%**) and cold-start only models (**44.5%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct Reinforcement Learning is insufficient for activating complex multimodal reasoning; a "cold-start" initialization with high-quality data is necessary.
> - High-quality, human-like multimodal Chain-of-Thought data, free from manual annotation, can be effectively synthesized using a "Modality Bridging" technique by combining MLLMs and text-only reasoning LLMs.
> - "Overthinking" in MLLMs during RL training can be mitigated by progressively increasing the allowed Chain-of-Thought length, enabling the model to learn concise, correct reasoning before expanding to longer, more complex thought processes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of sophisticated reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) is identified as a critical area of research toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Traditional "direct prediction" inference methods often exhibit suboptimal performance on complex reasoning tasks, leading to the exploration of structured intermediate reasoning steps, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT). OpenAI O1 demonstrated the potential of complex CoT for training LLMs with strong reasoning abilities, inspiring various methods to optimize reasoning pathways and generate high-quality CoT.

In the realm of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), research has also begun to explore CoT reasoning. However, existing MLLM CoT approaches, often relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with manually constructed, step-level reasoning datasets, are observed to produce "Pseudo-CoT." This "Pseudo-CoT" is characterized by a lack of natural cognitive processes like questioning, reflection, and inspection, which are considered essential for tackling complex vision-reasoning tasks. DeepSeek-R1 marked a significant advancement in LLMs by demonstrating that large-scale Reinforcement Learning (RL) could induce the self-emergence of complex cognitive reasoning, including human-like CoT.

This paper extends the inquiry into whether RL, specifically R1-like RL, can be utilized to incentivize similar reasoning capabilities in MLLMs. The work addresses the challenges of directly applying RL to MLLMs, particularly the absence of substantial high-quality multimodal reasoning data and the difficulty in activating complex reasoning processes like questioning and reflection. It positions itself as an early exploration into combining cold-start initialization with RL training to overcome these limitations and enhance multimodal reasoning in MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and implement methods for enhancing the reasoning capability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly by leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL) inspired by the success of DeepSeek-R1 in text-only LLMs.

The motivations and specific objectives stem from several identified challenges:

1. **Limitations of Existing MLLM Reasoning Approaches:** Current MLLMs often struggle with complex reasoning tasks due to a lack of structured reasoning processes. Efforts to integrate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into MLLMs, typically through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on manually constructed datasets, often result in "Pseudo-CoT." This "Pseudo-CoT" is deemed insufficient as it lacks human-like cognitive elements such as questioning, reflection, and inspection, which are crucial for intricate multimodal reasoning tasks.
2. **Challenges of Direct RL Training for MLLMs:** While DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated the emergence of complex reasoning in LLMs purely through RL, direct application of this paradigm to MLLMs faces significant obstacles. The absence of large-scale, high-quality multimodal reasoning data and the computational resources required for prolonged training make it difficult for direct RL to effectively guide MLLMs in generating complex CoT reasoning from scratch. Initial experiments confirmed that direct RL training (Vision-R1-Zero) struggled to stimulate reasoning capability and produce sufficiently lengthy or complex CoT.
3. **Need for High-Quality, Human-like Multimodal CoT Data:** To enable MLLMs to tackle complex multimodal reasoning tasks effectively, there is a clear need for training data that exhibits human-like, complex CoT reasoning, incorporating natural cognitive processes. Manually annotating such data at scale is labor-intensive and impractical.
4. **Addressing Optimization Challenges Post-Initialization:** Even after initial training, models can encounter new optimization problems. Specifically, models initialized with a form of CoT reasoning might exhibit an "Overthinking Optimization Problem," where they engage in excessively long and often incorrect reasoning paths, hindering the subsequent RL training process aimed at refining correct reasoning.

To address these issues, the research proposes Vision-R1, with the following key objectives:

- **To develop a method for constructing a high-quality, human annotation-free multimodal CoT dataset.** This dataset is intended to serve as a cold-start initialization to teach MLLMs "human-like" reasoning.
- **To integrate cold-start initialization with RL training to incentivize reasoning capability in MLLMs.** This hybrid approach aims to overcome the limitations of direct RL by providing a foundational understanding of complex reasoning.
- **To propose a novel training strategy, Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST),** to mitigate the "Overthinking Optimization Problem" encountered in post-cold-start MLLMs during RL training. PTST aims to guide the model toward efficient and correct reasoning by progressively adjusting the complexity of reasoning paths.
- **To demonstrate that Vision-R1 can achieve competitive reasoning performance** on multimodal math reasoning tasks, even with fewer parameters compared to state-of-the-art MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Vision-R1 methodology integrates a cold-start initialization phase with subsequent Reinforcement Learning (RL) training to enhance multimodal reasoning in MLLMs.

#### 3.1. Initial Exploration: Direct RL Training

The authors first explored applying the DeepSeek-R1-Zero paradigm directly to MLLMs, naming this model Vision-R1-Zero. This involved training a base MLLM using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on a 10K multimodal math dataset. The training enforced an output format of "<think> reasoning process </think><answer> final answer </answer>" and utilized a hard formatting result reward function, where a reward of 1 was given only if both formatting and the final answer were correct, otherwise 0.
This direct RL approach proved challenging. Vision-R1-Zero struggled to stimulate complex CoT reasoning or achieve significant performance improvements, even with extended training periods that produced longer reasoning processes. This indicated that direct RL alone was insufficient under constraints of data quality, quantity, and computational resources.

#### 3.2. Vision-R1 Overview

To address the limitations of direct RL, Vision-R1 adopts a two-stage approach:
1. **Cold-Start Initialization:** An existing MLLM is fine-tuned on a high-quality multimodal CoT dataset to learn human-like reasoning patterns.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training:** The cold-start initialized MLLM (Vision-R1-CI) undergoes further training with RL using a novel strategy to refine its reasoning and address optimization challenges.

#### 3.3. Cold-Start Initialization

##### 3.3.1. Modality Bridging to Obtain High-Quality Multimodal CoT Data

The core challenge was to create a multimodal CoT dataset that encompasses complex cognitive processes without human annotation. The proposed "Modality Bridging" method leverages existing MLLMs and DeepSeek-R1:
1. **"Pseudo-CoT" Generation:** Multimodal image-question-answer pairs are input into an MLLM along with a prompt to generate "Pseudo-CoT." This "Pseudo-CoT" includes an image description and a structured, step-level reasoning process, explicitly detailing visual information.
2. **Detailed Description Generation:** The original image-question pair is concatenated with the generated "Pseudo-CoT" and a specific prompt. This combined input is fed back into the MLLM to produce a more detailed image description. This step is crucial for converting necessary visual information into a comprehensive textual format, minimizing information loss.
3. **High-Quality CoT Extraction:** The resulting textual descriptions (enriched with visual information) are then passed to the text-only reasoning LLM, DeepSeek-R1, which is proficient in generating human-like complex CoT with natural cognitive processes (questioning, reflection, inspection).
4. **Data Filtering and Integration:** The generated CoT processes are filtered to retain only those where the final answer aligns with the ground truth. Rule-based data filtering is applied to remove logically inconsistent samples and refine semantic coherence.
The filtered and refined CoT data generated by DeepSeek-R1 is then paired with its corresponding images, forming the 200K "Vision-R1-cold" dataset. This dataset serves as the cold-start initialization for the base MLLM.

##### 3.3.2. Overthinking Optimization Problem

After cold-start initialization using the Vision-R1-cold dataset, the MLLM (named Vision-R1-CI) learned to reason in a complex manner. However, it exhibited an "Overthinking Optimization Problem": while capable of prolonged thought processes, correct reasoning was often concentrated in shorter CoT sequences. During subsequent RL training, attempts to extend the allowed thought length (e.g., to 16K tokens) led to the generation of longer, but often incorrect, reasoning paths, complicating performance improvement. This indicated a need to guide the model towards correct reasoning in early stages.

#### 3.4. Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST)

To address the "Overthinking Optimization Problem" and guide Vision-R1 towards correct and progressively complex reasoning, the authors propose Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST) integrated with GRPO.
1. **Progressive Length Constraints:** PTST involves multiple training stages (S=2 in practice), where each stage `s` has a specific sequence length limit `L_s` and a sampling count `G_s`. Initially, during early RL training stages, the reasoning length is suppressed (e.g., 4K tokens). As training progresses, these constraints are gradually relaxed (e.g., 8K tokens), allowing the model to autonomously learn to utilize longer CoT for more complex problems.
2. **GRPO with Hard Formatting Result Reward Function (HFRRF):** The RL training utilizes GRPO with an HFRRF. A reward `r_i = 1` is given only when both the output formatting (e.g., "<think> </think><answer> </answer>") and the final answer's correctness are met simultaneously; otherwise, `r_i = 0`. This reward function encourages adherence to a structured output and accurate problem-solving.
3. **No System Prompt Constraints:** Unlike the initial direct RL exploration, no system prompt constraints are imposed during PTST, as Vision-R1-CI already acquired robust formatting capabilities during cold-start initialization.
The practical implementation involved two stages: Stage 1 with a 4K token limit and 16 samples per input, followed by Stage 2 with an 8K token limit and 8 samples per input. The final Vision-R1 model is the checkpoint from the end of Stage 2. This progressive strategy enables Vision-R1 to balance CoT complexity and accuracy, enhancing its reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of Vision-R1 in enhancing multimodal reasoning capabilities, particularly in mathematical problem-solving.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

Vision-R1 demonstrated competitive performance across multiple multimodal math reasoning benchmarks.
- **Vision-R1-7B:** Achieved an accuracy of 73.5% on the MathVista benchmark, which is only 0.4% lower than OpenAI O1, a leading closed-source reasoning model. On MathVista's complex sub-tasks (geometry reasoning (GEO), algebraic reasoning (ALG), geometry problem solving (GPS)), Vision-R1-7B scored 80.3%, 79.0%, and 83.2% respectively, showing an average improvement of over 10% compared to its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B. It also ranked Top-1 on MathVerse and Top-2 on MM-Math, indicating its effectiveness in complex math problems.
- **Scaled Models:** Vision-R1-32B and Vision-R1-72B, utilizing additional multimodal math data during RL training, achieved 76.4% and 78.2% on MathVista, respectively. These models demonstrated average accuracy improvements of approximately 10% compared to their respective base models across benchmarks. Specifically, Vision-R1-32B showed an 11.0% average improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-32B, and Vision-R1-72B showed an 8.9% average improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-72B.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Cold-start Dataset Quality (Vision-R1-cold):** A qualitative analysis of Vision-R1-cold revealed a significantly higher proportion of human-like cognitive processes (e.g., "Wait," "Hmm," "Mistake," "Alternatively," "Check") compared to existing multimodal CoT datasets like LLaVA-CoT and Mulberry. This complex CoT structure was instrumental in facilitating the base MLLM's initial learning of reasoning mechanisms. Quantitatively, a model trained with Vision-R1-cold (Vision-R1-LlamaV-CI-11B) outperformed LLaVA-CoT-11B and Mulberry-Llama-11B across general and math reasoning benchmarks, confirming the dataset's superior quality.
- **Effect of Main Strategy:**
 - **Vision-R1-Zero (direct RL):** Struggled to generate sufficiently long and complex CoT, leading to an average accuracy of 50.7%.
 - **Vision-R1-CI (cold-start only):** Generated excessively long CoTs but contained numerous incorrect reasoning steps, resulting in a lower average accuracy of 44.5%.
 - **Vision-R1-Long (cold-start + direct long-sequence RL):** Faced optimization difficulties, with an average accuracy of 47.7%, demonstrating that simply extending reasoning length without guidance was ineffective.
 - **Vision-R1 (cold-start + PTST):** Achieved the highest average accuracy of 55.4%, effectively balancing CoT complexity and accuracy.
- **Effect of Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST):** The two-stage PTST setup (4K tokens x 16 samples -> 8K tokens x 8 samples) yielded the best average performance (55.4%). This outperformed fixed short-length training (4K x 16 -> 4K x 16, 54.3%) and significantly outperformed fixed long-length training (16K x 4/16, 47.7%/47.9%). This indicates that early length constraints are critical for mitigating overthinking and that progressively relaxing these constraints allows for performance gains.
- **Effect of Cold Start:** PTST alone on a model without cold-start initialization (Zero+PTST, 51.8%) offered limited benefits compared to Vision-R1-Zero (50.7%). Additionally, SFT without CoT annotations before RL (Zero+SFT+PTST, 39.8%) was detrimental. These results underscore that complex CoT priors acquired during cold-start are essential for RL to learn correct reasoning patterns, with PTST then refining these patterns by suppressing early overthinking and safely extending reasoning length.

#### 4.3. Visualization and Qualitative Observations

Vision-R1-7B was observed to generate complex reasoning processes that exhibit "human-like" questioning and self-reflection, termed "Aha moments." This sophisticated reasoning capability contributes to the model's inference performance on complex reasoning tasks. The comparison between CoT generated with and without "Pseudo-CoT" in the data construction pipeline illustrated that "Modality Bridging" was crucial for DeepSeek-R1 to produce accurate reasoning by providing sufficient visual information in textual form.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an early exploration into applying R1-like Reinforcement Learning (RL) to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), addressing challenges in incentivizing complex reasoning capabilities. The significance and potential impact of Vision-R1 are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement in MLLM Reasoning:** Vision-R1 demonstrates a method for endowing MLLMs with sophisticated, human-like reasoning abilities, including questioning, reflection, and inspection—cognitive processes often lacking in previous "Pseudo-CoT" approaches. This moves MLLMs beyond simple direct prediction or structured step-by-step outputs towards more robust and adaptive reasoning.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Complex CoT:** The proposed "Modality Bridging" method offers a scalable and annotation-free approach to constructing high-quality multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets. This addresses a critical bottleneck in MLLM training, as manual annotation of complex reasoning paths is resource-intensive. The Vision-R1-cold dataset, with its high proportion of self-reflective indicators, represents a valuable resource for the community.
3. **Mitigating RL Optimization Challenges:** The introduction of Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST) is a contribution to RL training methodologies for complex models. It provides a strategy to manage "overthinking" and guide models towards efficient and correct reasoning, which is a common challenge when training large models with long reasoning chains. This technique could be applicable to other domains facing similar optimization difficulties.
4. **Competitive Performance with Efficiency:** Vision-R1-7B achieves performance comparable to, and in some cases surpassing, state-of-the-art MLLMs with significantly larger parameter counts (70B+ parameters). This demonstrates that sophisticated reasoning capabilities can be achieved without relying solely on model scale, offering a path towards more efficient and deployable reasoning MLLMs. This has implications for broader access and application of advanced AI models.
5. **Inspiration for Future Research:** By analyzing the differences between direct RL training and the combined approach of cold-start initialization and RL, this work provides insights into the effective application of R1-like RL paradigms to multimodal contexts. It is expected to inspire further research in multimodal reinforcement learning, particularly concerning how to better bridge modalities for reasoning and refine cognitive processes in MLLMs.
6. **Pathway to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The enhancement of complex reasoning capabilities, as demonstrated by Vision-R1, is considered a critical step towards AGI. By enabling MLLMs to engage in more human-like thought processes, this research contributes to the foundational understanding and development of more generally intelligent AI systems capable of tackling complex, real-world problems.

In summary, Vision-R1 contributes a novel framework for incentivizing complex, human-like reasoning in MLLMs by innovatively combining data generation, cold-start initialization, and a progressive RL training strategy. Its impact is likely to be seen in the development of more capable and efficient MLLMs, as well as in guiding future research on reasoning within multimodal AI.
