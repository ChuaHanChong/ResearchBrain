# Masked Auto-Regressive Variational Acceleration: Fast Inference Makes Practical Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named MARVAL (Masked Auto-Regressive Variational Acceleration) distills multi-step masked auto-regressive (MAR) diffusion models into a single-step generator, achieving up to 32.95x faster inference on ImageNet 256x256 while maintaining or improving sample quality, and subsequently uses Reinforcement Learning for preference alignment to boost scores like CLIP and ImageReward.

## Problem

- Masked Auto-regressive (MAR) models suffer from slow inference speeds due to a nested architecture combining an outer auto-regressive loop and an inner multi-step diffusion denoising chain.
- The inherent computational inefficiency of MAR models makes Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training, which is crucial for aligning generative models with human preferences, impractical and computationally prohibitive.

## Method

- Introduce ==Guided Score Implicit Matching== (**GSIM**) distillation to compress the multi-step guided diffusion sampling process of a teacher MAR model into a single-step generator.
- Utilize an auxiliary network to approximate the single-step student score, optimizing the student generator parameters through a novel gradient equivalent theorem and a score discrepancy objective.
- Apply a post-hoc Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework to fine-tune the distilled single-step generator, treating it as a policy to maximize a reward signal from a pre-trained reward model for preference alignment.

## Results

- MARVAL achieves substantial inference acceleration, with MARVAL-B generating images over **32.95x** faster (**0.61s** vs **20.10s**) than MAR-B, and MARVAL-H achieving a **20x** speedup over MAR-H on ImageNet 256x256.
- Distilled MARVAL models maintain or improve image quality, with MARVAL-H achieving an FID of 2.00 (vs. MAR-H at 2.06) and an IS of 256.3 (vs. MAR-H at 247.7), demonstrating improved perceptual quality without compromising data alignment.
- RL post-training consistently improves preference alignment metrics, increasing CLIP scores (e.g., MARVAL-B from 29.40 to 29.47) and ImageReward scores (e.g., MARVAL-B from -**0.142** to -**0.110**), and shows qualitative enhancements in realism and detail, also generalizing effectively to Text-to-Image generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-step diffusion sampling in MAR models can be effectively distilled into a single-step generation process while preserving generative quality and significantly accelerating inference.
> - A score-based variational objective combined with a gradient equivalent theorem allows for stable and efficient training of a single-step generator from a complex multi-step teacher diffusion model.
> - Decoupling distillation and RL fine-tuning, by applying RL as a post-hoc step to the already high-fidelity distilled generator, prevents unstable policy optimization that would arise from low-quality intermediate outputs during initial training.
