# Masked Auto-Regressive Variational Acceleration: Fast Inference Makes Practical Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named MARVAL (Masked Auto-Regressive Variational Acceleration) distills multi-step masked auto-regressive (MAR) diffusion models into a single-step generator, achieving up to 32.95x faster inference on ImageNet 256x256 while maintaining or improving sample quality, and subsequently uses Reinforcement Learning for preference alignment to boost scores like CLIP and ImageReward.

## Problem

- Masked Auto-regressive (MAR) models suffer from slow inference speeds due to a nested architecture combining an outer auto-regressive loop and an inner multi-step diffusion denoising chain.
- The inherent computational inefficiency of MAR models makes Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training, which is crucial for aligning generative models with human preferences, impractical and computationally prohibitive.

## Method

- Introduce ==Guided Score Implicit Matching== (**GSIM**) distillation to compress the multi-step guided diffusion sampling process of a teacher MAR model into a single-step generator.
- Utilize an auxiliary network to approximate the single-step student score, optimizing the student generator parameters through a novel gradient equivalent theorem and a score discrepancy objective.
- Apply a post-hoc Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework to fine-tune the distilled single-step generator, treating it as a policy to maximize a reward signal from a pre-trained reward model for preference alignment.

## Results

- MARVAL achieves substantial inference acceleration, with MARVAL-B generating images over **32.95x** faster (**0.61s** vs **20.10s**) than MAR-B, and MARVAL-H achieving a **20x** speedup over MAR-H on ImageNet 256x256.
- Distilled MARVAL models maintain or improve image quality, with MARVAL-H achieving an FID of 2.00 (vs. MAR-H at 2.06) and an IS of 256.3 (vs. MAR-H at 247.7), demonstrating improved perceptual quality without compromising data alignment.
- RL post-training consistently improves preference alignment metrics, increasing CLIP scores (e.g., MARVAL-B from 29.40 to 29.47) and ImageReward scores (e.g., MARVAL-B from -**0.142** to -**0.110**), and shows qualitative enhancements in realism and detail, also generalizing effectively to Text-to-Image generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-step diffusion sampling in MAR models can be effectively distilled into a single-step generation process while preserving generative quality and significantly accelerating inference.
> - A score-based variational objective combined with a gradient equivalent theorem allows for stable and efficient training of a single-step generator from a complex multi-step teacher diffusion model.
> - Decoupling distillation and RL fine-tuning, by applying RL as a post-hoc step to the already high-fidelity distilled generator, prevents unstable policy optimization that would arise from low-quality intermediate outputs during initial training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative models has seen advancements in two primary paradigms: auto-regressive (AR) models and diffusion models. Each paradigm possesses distinct characteristics. Diffusion models are recognized for their capacity to generate high-fidelity samples and cover diverse modes of data. However, their inference process typically involves numerous iterative denoising steps, often numbering in the hundreds, which results in slow generation speeds and computationally intensive post-training, particularly with Reinforcement Learning (RL). Conversely, AR models, which generate data sequentially in discrete space, offer inherent support for semantic controllability and are more readily compatible with RL and language-based alignment frameworks. A limitation of AR models, however, is their comparative struggle to achieve the same level of perceptual quality as state-of-the-art diffusion models, especially at higher resolutions.

Recognizing these complementary strengths and weaknesses, a recent direction in generative modeling research has involved combining AR and diffusion paradigms, leading to hybrid AR+Diffusion architectures. These models often leverage AR priors to capture global data structures and employ diffusion decoders to refine fine-grained details. The Masked Auto-regressive (MAR) model is identified as a prominent example of this hybrid design.

A significant challenge within diffusion models has been their slow multi-step sampling. To mitigate this, various distillation techniques have been developed to accelerate sampling while striving to preserve sample quality. These methods often aim to compress the multi-step denoising process into fewer steps, ideally a single step. Additionally, the integration of RL for aligning generative models with human preferences has emerged as a crucial area, requiring efficient model architectures capable of accommodating iterative refinement through reward feedback.

This work addresses the specific challenges presented by MAR models within this landscape, particularly their inference inefficiency and the subsequent difficulty in applying RL for preference alignment. It positions itself by proposing a method to overcome these limitations, thereby aiming to make hybrid AR+Diffusion models more practical for real-time applications and human-centric refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research presented in this paper is motivated by two fundamental limitations identified in existing Masked Auto-regressive (MAR) models:

1. **Slow Inference due to Nested Architecture:** MAR models employ a hierarchical inference mechanism. This involves an outer auto-regressive (AR) unmasking loop, where tokens are progressively unmasked, and an inner diffusion denoising chain, which performs a full diffusion process to generate the unmasked tokens in each AR iteration. This decoupled, nested structure leads to a computationally expensive generation process, making MAR models slow for practical applications.
2. **Impracticality of Reinforcement Learning (RL) Post-Training:** The computational inefficiency of MAR models extends to their post-training capabilities. RL-based fine-tuning is increasingly recognized as essential for aligning generative models with human preferences, enabling scalability and producing outputs that meet specific human criteria. However, the slow inference of MAR models makes incorporating RL post-training computationally prohibitive and impractical.

These issues collectively pose a fundamental research question that the authors aim to answer: "Can we improve the efficiency of MAR-like models while making RL post-training practical and efficient?"

To address this question and overcome the identified limitations, the key objectives of this work are:

- **To significantly accelerate the inference speed of MAR models:** This involves compressing the multi-step diffusion denoising chains into a single generation step while preserving the flexible auto-regressive unmasking order inherent to MAR. The goal is to dramatically reduce the computational cost per generation.
- **To enable practical and efficient RL post-training for MAR models:** By accelerating inference, the authors aim to make the iterative process of RL-based fine-tuning computationally feasible. This would allow MAR models to be aligned with verifiable rewards and human preferences, leading to more scalable and user-preferred generative outputs.
- **To develop a novel distillation principle for MAR models:** Specifically, this involves introducing a score-based variational objective that facilitates the compression of the diffusion chain into a single AR generation step without compromising sample quality.
- **To establish an efficient RL framework tailored for masked auto-regressive models:** This framework, termed MARVAL-RL, aims to effectively incorporate reward feedback to enhance the fidelity and preference alignment of the distilled MAR models.

The overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between the expressive modeling capabilities of MAR diffusion models and the practical requirements of fast inference and human-aligned generation, thereby making MAR models suitable for real-world applications where speed and preference alignment are critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **MARVAL (Masked Auto-Regressive Variational Acceleration)**, aims to transform slow, multi-step auto-regressive generation into a fast, single-step process, subsequently enabling refinement through Reinforcement Learning (RL). The methodology is structured into two main stages: Guided Score Implicit Matching (GSIM) distillation and RL refinement.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Guided Score Implicit Matching (GSIM) Distillation

The primary goal of this stage is to distill the multi-step guided sampling process of the teacher MAR model into a single-step generator, denoted as \( g_\theta(z, c) \). Here, \( z \) is a random noise vector, and \( c \) represents the conditional auto-regressive context derived from unmasked tokens. The student generator \( g_\theta \) is trained to directly map noise and context to the final denoised tokens to be predicted.

- **Objective Formulation:** The distillation aims to achieve distributional alignment between the student's one-step process and the teacher's multi-step process. This is formally expressed as minimizing the KL divergence between the student distribution \( q_\theta(x|c) \) and the teacher distribution \( p(x|c) \), conditioned on the AR context \( c \).
- **Tractability Challenge and Solution:** Directly optimizing the KL divergence is intractable. Inspired by work connecting KL divergence to Fisher divergence along a diffusion process, the authors re-express the objective in continuous time as an integral over the score discrepancy \( D_{score} \). This discrepancy measures the difference between the student's time-dependent score \( \nabla_{x_t} \log q_{\theta,t}(x_t | c) \) and the pretrained diffusion model's score \( s_{p_t}(x_t, c) \).
- **Teacher Score with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Since MAR models employ CFG during inference, the teacher score \( s_{p_t}(x_t, c) \) is defined to correspond to the CFG-guided diffusion process. This involves a weighted combination of the teacher's score network outputs with and without a null condition, parameterized by a guidance scale \( w \). This ensures the student learns from the distribution the teacher actually samples.
- **Student Score Approximation:** The single-step student score \( \nabla_{x_t} \log q_{\theta,t}(x_t | c) \) is approximated by an auxiliary network, \( S_\phi(x_t, t, c) \). This auxiliary network is trained using a standard denoising score matching objective on reconstructions generated by the student generator.
- **Optimization with Gradient Equivalent Theorem:** To optimize the student parameters \( \theta \), the authors leverage a "Gradient Equivalent Theorem" (detailed in supplementary material). This theorem allows for the efficient computation of the gradient of the score discrepancy objective. The optimization problem is formulated as minimizing \( L_{GSIM}(\theta) \), which consists of two terms: \( L_1 \), related to the difference between the student's score approximation and the ground-truth noise prediction, and \( L_2 \), which is the score discrepancy itself. The student score approximation \( s_{q_{sg[\theta],t}}(x_t, c) \) incorporates a stop-gradient operator to ensure stable optimization.
- **Loss Function Robustness:** While theory suggests using the squared \( \ell_2 \) distance for \( D_{score} \), the authors adopt the Pseudo-Huber distance for improved training robustness and faster convergence, using a small constant \( r \) to prevent division by zero.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Post-Hoc Refinement

This stage addresses the potential divergence of the distilled model's output from human perceptual preferences, often due to cumulative errors across the auto-regressive loops.

- **Motivation for Post-Hoc Approach:** The RL process cannot be seamlessly integrated with the GSIM distillation phase. During distillation, the model typically operates with a fraction of tokens unmasked and performs only a single AR iteration, producing low-fidelity intermediate images. If RL were coupled directly, the reward model would evaluate these low-quality outputs, leading to misleading gradients and unstable policy optimization. Therefore, RL fine-tuning is applied as a separate, post-hoc refinement phase to the *distilled* generator \( g_\theta \).
- **RL Formulation:** The single-step generator \( g_\theta \) is treated as a policy. For a given class embedding \( c_{emb} \), the policy generates an output \( x_g = G_\theta(z, c_{emb}, K) \) over multiple AR iterations (where \( G_\theta \) denotes the multi-iteration generation process), which is considered an "action."
- **Reward Model and Objective:** A pre-trained reward model, specifically PickScore, is employed to provide a scalar score \( R(x_g, \text{prompt}_c) \) reflecting the alignment between the generated image and a designed textual prompt related to the class name. The RL objective is to maximize this expected reward, achieved by minimizing the loss function \( L_{RL} = -E_{c_{emb},z} [R(G_\theta(z, c_{emb}, K), \text{prompt}_c)] \).
- **Fine-tuning:** By backpropagating gradients from this reward signal, the parameters \( \theta \) of the generator are fine-tuned. This process aims to produce outputs that not only adhere to the auto-regressive prior but are also explicitly optimized for higher quality and better text-image alignment, reflecting human perceptual preferences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to evaluate MARVAL's performance in accelerating inference, preserving sample quality, and benefiting from RL-based preference alignment. The evaluation primarily focused on ImageNet 256x256 conditional generation and also included a generalization test for text-to-image (T2I) generation.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Diffusion Process:** Cosine noise schedule with 1000 training steps, denoising network predicts noise vector using a lightweight MLP.
- **Model Scales:** Base (208M params), Large (479M params), and Huge (943M params), mirroring MAR model scales.
- **Hardware & Training Time:** 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs. Distillation: 30 epochs (approx. 3 days). RL refinement: 5 epochs (approx. 2 days).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Class-conditional:** FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and IS (Inception Score) on 50K samples across 1,000 ImageNet classes.
 - **Text-to-Image:** ImageReward on 50K prompts from JourneyDB.
 - **Preference Alignment:** CLIP score and ImageReward score.

#### 4.2. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) Scale in Distillation

- An ablation study on the base MARVAL model (MARVAL-B) showed the impact of the CFG scale \( w \) on performance.
- Increasing \( w \) generally improved IS, indicating enhanced image fidelity (e.g., from 182.1 at \( w=1 \) to 317.1 at \( w=3 \)).
- However, overly large \( w \) degraded FID, suggesting a trade-off where diversity and data alignment were sacrificed for fidelity. For instance, FID increased from 3.06 at \( w=1.2 \) to 13.84 at \( w=4 \).
- The optimal setting of \( w=1.2 \) was selected as it achieved a favorable balance between visual sharpness and distribution consistency (FID 3.06, IS 220.2), outperforming MAR-B's FID of 2.60 at its optimal \( w=2.9 \) in terms of speed, while still being competitive in quality.

#### 4.3. Effect of Distillation

- **Performance vs. AR Iterations and Diffusion Steps:** When comparing MARVAL-B (1 diffusion step) against MAR-B with varying numbers of auto-regressive iterations (N_AR) and diffusion steps (N_diff):
 - MARVAL-B achieved performance comparable to MAR-B at its optimal N_diff=100.
 - MARVAL-B even surpassed MAR-B when MAR-B used N_diff=30, indicating effective preservation of generative capability.
- **Overall Comparison (Table 2):**
 - **Speed:** MARVAL models demonstrated substantial speedups. MARVAL-B generated an image in 0.61 seconds, compared to MAR-B's 20.10 seconds, representing approximately a 32.95x acceleration. The diffusion sampling process alone was accelerated by nearly 100x. MARVAL-H achieved a 20x speedup over MAR-H.
 - **Quality (FID/IS):** MARVAL models consistently maintained better or competitive FID scores while achieving significantly higher Inception Scores compared to their MAR counterparts. For example, MARVAL-H achieved an FID of 2.00 (vs. MAR-H at 2.06) with an IS of 256.3 (vs. MAR-H at 247.7). This indicates improved perceptual quality without compromising data alignment.

#### 4.4. The Significance of Reinforcement Learning

- **Reward-Based Metrics (Table 3):** RL fine-tuning consistently improved reward-based metrics across all model scales (Base, Large, Huge).
 - CLIP scores increased: e.g., MARVAL-B (distilled only) 29.40 to MARVAL-RL-B 29.47.
 - ImageReward scores improved: e.g., MARVAL-B (distilled only) -0.142 to MARVAL-RL-B -0.110. Higher scores for both indicate better semantic fidelity and perceptual alignment with human preference.
- **Qualitative Improvements (Figure 4):** Visual comparisons showed clear improvements post-RL.
 - MAR-B outputs often had coarse semantics, unrealistic textures, and structural distortions.
 - MARVAL-B (distilled only) outputs were smoother and less detailed, with blurred or distorted animal faces and loss of fine-grained textures.
 - MARVAL-RL-B outputs demonstrated enhanced perceptual realism: main subjects became more salient, contours and textures were better preserved, and compositions appeared more natural and sharper.

#### 4.5. Generalization to Text-to-Image Generation

- The MARVAL-RL framework was applied to the Text-to-Image (T2I) DC-AR model.
- The 1-step+RL strategy successfully compressed the original 20-step DDIM sampling into a single step without performance degradation.
- RL fine-tuning significantly boosted the ImageReward score from 0.5876 (20-step DDIM DC-AR) to 0.6903 (1-step+RL DC-AR).
- Qualitative results (Figure 5 and Supplementary Material) confirmed that the 1-step+RL DC-AR model maintained excellent visual and semantic fidelity, accurately capturing fine-grained details and adhering strictly to prompts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, MARVAL, that addresses two fundamental limitations of Masked Auto-regressive (MAR) models: their slow inference speed and the resulting impracticality of Reinforcement Learning (RL) based post-training for aligning with human preferences.

The primary significance of MARVAL lies in establishing a practical pathway for the distillation and RL of masked auto-regressive diffusion models. This is achieved through two key contributions:
1. **A novel score-based variational objective (GSIM):** This objective allows for the compression of the costly multi-step diffusion denoising chains into a single generation step. The results indicate that this distillation process can dramatically accelerate inference (e.g., over 30 times faster for MARVAL-B compared to MAR-B) without sacrificing sample quality, often yielding competitive or superior FID and IS scores.
2. **An efficient RL framework (MARVAL-RL):** By making inference fast, MARVAL enables the effective and practical application of RL for post-training. This allows generative models to be fine-tuned with verifiable rewards, leading to outputs that are better aligned with human perceptual quality and semantic preferences. The consistent improvements in CLIP and ImageReward scores, alongside qualitative enhancements, demonstrate the efficacy of this RL refinement.

The potential impact of this work is substantial across several dimensions:
- **Enabling Real-time Generative AI:** The substantial inference acceleration provided by MARVAL makes masked auto-regressive models more suitable for applications requiring fast, near real-time image generation, such as interactive design tools, gaming, and creative content generation workflows.
- **Human-Centric Generative Models:** By making RL fine-tuning practical, MARVAL allows for the creation of generative models that are explicitly optimized to meet human preferences. This is crucial for developing AI systems that produce outputs perceived as high-quality, harmonious, and semantically accurate by users.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** The framework provides a scalable approach to developing efficient generative models, as demonstrated across various model scales (Base, Large, Huge) and its successful generalization to text-to-image tasks. This suggests its applicability beyond class-conditional image generation.
- **Advancement in Generative Model Research:** The methodological contributions, particularly the Guided Score Implicit Matching (GSIM) objective and the principled integration of RL into an AR+Diffusion architecture, offer new avenues for future research in accelerating and aligning complex generative models.

In conclusion, MARVAL offers a solution to overcome critical bottlenecks in masked auto-regressive diffusion models, paving the way for the development of fast, scalable, and human-preferred generative AI systems.
