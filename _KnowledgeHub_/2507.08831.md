# View Invariant Learning for Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces View Invariant Learning (VIL), a post-training framework that efficiently adapts existing Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLNCE) policies to diverse camera viewpoints, encompassing variations in height and angle. VIL improves navigation performance under varied viewpoints by 8-15% on R2R-CE and demonstrates zero-shot transfer capabilities on a physical robot, enhancing success rates from 20-28% to 44-48%.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLNCE) policies are highly sensitive to camera viewpoint changes (height and angle), causing significant performance degradation.
- Real-world robotic platforms exhibit diverse camera configurations, making it challenging to deploy a single navigation policy trained for a specific viewpoint.
- Current solutions for viewpoint invariance often involve computationally expensive retraining or are limited to manipulation tasks, lacking efficient and generalizable adaptation for navigation.

## Method

- Introduces View Invariant Learning (VIL), a post-training framework that adapts existing VLNCE policies (e.g., ETPNav) without requiring full retraining from scratch.
- VIL incorporates view-invariant representation learning using a contrastive loss to ensure consistency between visual features from standard and varied viewpoints.
- It employs teacher-student distillation for waypoint prediction, where a frozen teacher (operating on standard views) guides a student (adapting to varied views) through a lightweight, trainable adapter module.

## Results

- VIL substantially improved VLNCE performance under varied viewpoints, increasing Success Rate (SR) by 8-**15%** on R2R-CE and 11-**15%** on RxR-CE datasets compared to baselines.
- The framework significantly reduced the standard deviation of metrics (e.g., SPL by **65%**) across 81 fixed viewpoints, demonstrating improved stability and generalization to simulated real-robot camera placements and out-of-distribution viewpoints.
- VIL achieved zero-shot transfer on a real TurtleBot v2 platform, increasing success rates from 20-**28%** to 44-**48%** in indoor environments, while converging in approximately **14%** of the baseline's full training time with negligible inference overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic evaluation setting, V²-VLNCE, is essential for rigorously benchmarking and improving the robustness of navigation policies to diverse camera viewpoints.
> - Combining contrastive learning for robust visual features and knowledge distillation for waypoint prediction enables effective post-training adaptation of existing policies to viewpoint variations.
> - Efficient adaptation can be achieved by only training a small, lightweight adapter module within the student's waypoint predictor, preserving computational efficiency and leveraging pre-trained knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Vision-Language Navigation (VLN). Traditional VLN typically assumes navigation along predefined topological graphs. The evolution of this field led to VLN in Continuous Environments (VLNCE), which removes this constraint, allowing agents to move freely in continuous space.

Prior research in VLNCE has primarily concentrated on enhancing navigation capabilities through the development of advanced neural architectures, the integration of richer spatial and semantic features, and the utilization of larger-scale training datasets. However, a persistent challenge in this landscape has been the sensitivity of learned policies to variations in camera viewpoint, such as changes in height and viewing angle. Even minor shifts in these parameters can lead to substantial degradation in navigation performance.

This paper addresses this specific limitation by introducing a more generalized problem setting, V²-VLNCE (VLNCE with Varied Viewpoints), which systematically models and evaluates policy performance under diverse camera viewpoints. While some previous work has explored aspects of viewpoint variation, such as for robotic manipulation (e.g., MV-MVM, RoboUniView, ReViWo) or specific fixed-height camera configurations in VLNCE (e.g., GVNav), these approaches have often been computationally intensive, requiring extensive pre-training or full retraining of models. Furthermore, existing manipulation-focused methods are not directly applicable to navigation tasks, and prior VLNCE work on viewpoint robustness has not addressed continuous variations in both height and angle simultaneously.

This work differentiates itself by proposing a post-training framework, View Invariant Learning (VIL), designed to adapt *existing* VLNCE policies to varied viewpoints efficiently, without the need for retraining from scratch. By focusing on a "plug-and-play" adaptation strategy, this research aims to provide a more practical and generalizable solution for deploying embodied agents in real-world scenarios where camera configurations are inherently diverse and often variable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness of Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLNCE) policies to variations in camera viewpoint, encompassing both camera height and viewing angle. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing Viewpoint Sensitivity:** Current VLNCE policies exhibit sensitivity to viewpoint changes, meaning their performance degrades significantly when the camera's position or orientation deviates from the configuration used during training. This lack of robustness limits their practical applicability.
- **Relevance to Real-World Robotics:** In real-world robotic deployments, different robots (e.g., various models of mobile manipulators, autonomous delivery robots) are equipped with cameras mounted at diverse positions and angles. A navigation policy trained for one specific viewpoint often fails to generalize to another, necessitating a robust solution that can adapt to these varied camera placements.
- **Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - Prior work addressing viewpoint invariance in robotics largely focuses on manipulation tasks, which have different requirements and complexities compared to navigation.
 - Solutions for manipulation often involve computationally expensive two-stage training pipelines (e.g., pre-training a view-invariant encoder then fine-tuning a policy), which are not ideal for VLNCE where policies are typically pre-trained on large VLN datasets. Such a pipeline would discard pre-training benefits and incur high retraining costs.
 - Existing VLNCE-specific efforts, such as adapting to ground-level viewpoints, have been limited to fixed camera configurations and have required retraining the entire model or specific modules (like the waypoint predictor) from scratch, which is inefficient for broad viewpoint generalization.
- **Need for Efficiency and Generalizability:** There is a critical need for a computationally efficient and generalizable method that can adapt *existing*, pre-trained VLNCE policies to operate effectively under diverse and continuously varying viewpoints with minimal additional training effort. The goal is to develop a single, robust policy that can function across a range of viewpoints rather than requiring a separate policy for each configuration.

To achieve this, the researchers establish several specific goals:
1. **Introduce V²-VLNCE:** Define and introduce a new evaluation setting, V²-VLNCE (VLNCE with Varied Viewpoints), to systematically analyze and quantify the robustness of navigation policies to variations in camera height and viewing angle.
2. **Propose VIL Framework:** Develop View Invariant Learning (VIL), a post-training framework that integrates a contrastive learning objective and a teacher-student distillation mechanism to adapt existing VLNCE policies for viewpoint robustness.
3. **Empirical Validation:** Conduct extensive experiments in simulation to demonstrate that VIL significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in the V²-VLNCE setting and under simulated real-robot camera placements.
4. **Real-World Demonstration:** Provide a proof-of-concept evaluation on a physical robot to confirm the practical utility and real-world transferability of VIL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named View Invariant Learning (VIL), is a post-training framework designed to adapt existing VLNCE policies to varied viewpoints without requiring retraining from scratch. VIL builds upon a strong panoramic VLNCE baseline, specifically ETPNav, and consists of two primary components integrated into an end-to-end training paradigm.

#### 3.1. ETPNav Preliminary

The VIL framework is implemented on top of ETPNav, which at each step, processes natural language instructions and panoramic RGB-D observations (12 RGB and 12 depth views at 30° intervals). ETPNav predicts navigable waypoint candidates, incrementally constructs a local topological map, fuses instruction embeddings with graph representations using cross-modal graph attention, and selects the next navigation target.

#### 3.2. View-invariant Representation Learning

This component aims to learn feature representations that are robust to changes in viewpoint.
- **View Generation:** For each observation at time `t`, two versions are generated: a standard viewpoint (O_std_t) and a varied viewpoint (O_var_t). The varied view is created by randomly shifting the camera's height and angle.
- **Shared Visual Encoder:** Both O_std_t and O_var_t are processed by a shared visual encoder, `f_enc(·)`.
- **Projection Head:** Following `f_enc(·)`, a three-layer projection head `g(·)` (inspired by SimCLRv2) is applied.
 - `f_task = g1(f_enc(O_t))` are features used for downstream navigation tasks.
 - `f_contrast = g3(g2(g1(f_enc(O_t))))` are features used for contrastive learning.
- **Initialization:** The first linear layer `g1` is initialized as an identity matrix to preserve the original feature distribution of the pre-trained ETPNav model, enabling gradual adaptation.
- **Contrastive Loss (L_cl):** The InfoNCE formulation is used to enforce consistency between features from the same scene observed from different viewpoints.
 - **Positive Pairs:** Features corresponding to the same panoramic heading (`j`) under both standard (`q` = `f_std_contrast`) and varied (`k+` = `f_var_contrast`) viewpoints.
 - **Negative Pairs:** Generated from two sources:
 - Cross-scene negatives: Samples from different scenes within the mini-batch (by shifting batch indices).
 - Intra-scene hard negatives: Samples from the opposite heading within the same scene (e.g., `(j+6) mod 12`).
 - The contrastive objective encourages the policy to learn sparse, view-invariant features. These learned representations are shared with the navigation policy.

#### 3.3. Teacher-Student Waypoint Prediction Distillation

This component addresses the challenge of viewpoint-sensitive waypoint prediction, which is crucial for VLNCE.
- **Waypoint Predictor Architecture:** Both teacher and student models utilize a transformer-based network for predicting a dense heatmap of navigable waypoints from panoramic RGB-D observations.
- **Model Initialization:** Both teacher and student are initialized from the waypoint predictor of the pre-trained ETPNav model.
- **Teacher Model:** The teacher model is frozen and operates exclusively on standard viewpoint observations (O_std_t), providing a stable target for distillation.
- **Student Model:** The student model processes varied viewpoint observations (O_var_t). To adapt to these varied views efficiently, only a lightweight adapter module within the student model is made trainable. Specifically, the original input linear layer of the waypoint predictor is made trainable, while the rest of its weights are frozen.
- **Distillation Loss (L_wpd):** Knowledge is distilled from the teacher to the student using KL divergence between their softmax outputs (`softmax(S_teacher_t)` and `softmax(S_student_t)`). This allows the student to learn to match the teacher's robust waypoint predictions while adapting to varied inputs.
- **Graph Construction Integration:** During the construction of the local topological map (`G_t`), either the teacher's or student's waypoint predictions are sampled with a probability `p2` or `1-p2`, respectively.

#### 3.4. Training Objective

The VIL framework is trained end-to-end by jointly optimizing three loss components:
- **Standard Navigation Loss (L_nav):** The original navigation loss from the baseline ETPNav model.
- **Contrastive Learning Loss (L_cl):** As described in Section 4.2.
- **Waypoint Predictor Distillation Loss (L_wpd):** As described in Section 4.3.

The overall training objective is given by: `L = L_nav + λ1 * L_cl + λ2 * L_wpd`, where `λ1` and `λ2` are hyperparameters balancing the contribution of each loss.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Initialization:** All models are initialized from official pre-trained checkpoints of their respective backbones (ETPNav and BEVBert).
- **Hyperparameters:** VIL-specific hyperparameters are `p1 = p2 = 0.1`, `λ1 = 0.2`, and `λ2 = 10.0`. Other hyperparameters follow the original baseline implementations.
- **Evaluation Settings:**
 - **V²-VLNCE (Varied Viewpoint):** Viewpoints (height `h`, angle `θ`) sampled from `U([-0.5m, 0.5m]) × U([-30°, 30°])` relative to the standard configuration.
 - **Ground-level Viewpoint:** Adapted from GVNav (fixed camera height of 0.8m).
 - **Standard Viewpoint:** Original VLNCE setting for baseline comparison.
 - **Simulated Real-Robot Placements:** Fixed camera configurations derived from Stretch RE-1, Stretch RE-1 (Factory), and LoCoBot.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Viewpoints:** Evaluation on viewpoints outside the training distribution's range.
 - **Real-Robot Evaluation:** Zero-shot transfer experiments using a TurtleBot v2 platform equipped with a RICOH THETA X 360 RGB camera and an Ouster OS0 Rev6 LiDAR, collecting 360° RGB-D observations in real-time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive empirical results across various settings, demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of the View Invariant Learning (VIL) framework.

#### 4.1. Performance Under Varied Viewpoints (V²-VLNCE)

VIL consistently and substantially improves navigation performance under the Varied Viewpoint setting on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE datasets.
- **R2R-CE:** ETPNav + VIL showed significant gains over the base ETPNav model on both `val-seen` and `val-unseen` splits. For instance, it improved Navigation Error (NE) by 0.68-1.14m, nDTW by 3%-7%, Oracle Success Rate (OSR) by 6%-9%, Success Rate (SR) by 8%-15%, and Success penalized by Path Length (SPL) by 7%-13%. Similar improvements were observed when VIL was applied to the BEVBert baseline.
- **Comparison to GVNav (Ground-level Viewpoint):** Despite GVNav being designed specifically for ground-level viewpoints, ETPNav + VIL, trained for general varied viewpoints, still performed better on R2R-CE `val-unseen` (e.g., +3% OSR, +2% SR, +2% SPL), suggesting broader generalization.
- **RxR-CE:** On the more challenging RxR-CE dataset, ETPNav + VIL outperformed the base ETPNav under varied viewpoints, with nDTW gains of 10%-13%, OSR by 9%-13%, SR by 11%-15%, and SPL by 10%-15%.

#### 4.2. Performance Under Standard Viewpoint

VIL demonstrated that training with varied viewpoints does not compromise performance in the standard VLNCE setting. Instead, it often maintains or slightly improves navigation metrics.
- **R2R-CE `val-unseen`:** ETPNav + VIL increased SR by 1.5 and SPL by 0.8.
- **RxR-CE `val-unseen`:** ETPNav + VIL improved SPL by 2.3.
- Furthermore, on RxR-CE, VIL-augmented models achieved state-of-the-art performance across all metrics compared to other map-free methods, even without specific optimization for the standard viewpoint.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on R2R-CE evaluated the contribution of VIL's components:
- **Retraining on Varied Viewpoint Data Alone:** Retraining the base model on varied viewpoint data without VIL's specific modules yielded only slight improvements in varied viewpoint SPL (+0.7) but could slightly harm standard viewpoint SPL (-1.2). This indicated that simple fine-tuning is insufficient.
- **Waypoint Predictor Distillation (WPD):** Removing WPD substantially degraded performance, with `val-unseen` SPL dropping by 8.3 for Varied Viewpoint and 4.1 for Standard Viewpoint, highlighting its critical role.
- **Contrastive Learning (CL):** Contrastive learning improved `val-unseen` SPL by 2.8 for Varied Viewpoint and enhanced generalization under the standard viewpoint, increasing SR and SPL by 0.7 and 1.0, respectively.
These results confirm that each component of VIL contributes positively to navigation performance, particularly in varied or unseen viewpoint scenarios.

#### 4.4. Viewpoint Robustness Analysis

- **Variance Across Viewpoint Changes:** VIL substantially reduced the standard deviation of metrics across 81 fixed viewpoints on R2R-CE `val-unseen` (e.g., SPL standard deviation dropped by 65%), indicating that it not only improves average performance but also stabilizes behavior under spatial perturbations.
- **Fixed Real-Robot Camera Placements:** VIL consistently improved navigation performance across simulated camera placements derived from real robots (Stretch RE-1, Stretch RE-1 (Factory), and LoCoBot). For example, SPL on `val-unseen` increased by over 20% for Stretch RE-1, demonstrating generalization to practical camera configurations.

#### 4.5. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Viewpoint Generalization

VIL exhibited strong extrapolation capabilities to OOD viewpoints.
- When evaluating on extreme viewpoints (e.g., -0.5m height, 30° angle) outside the training distribution, VIL outperformed the baseline by a large margin.
- Models trained on a smaller viewpoint range showed only a slight performance drop compared to those trained on a larger range, suggesting that VIL maintains robustness even with limited training diversity.

#### 4.6. Real-Robot Evaluation (Proof-of-Concept)

A zero-shot real-robot evaluation using a TurtleBot v2 platform demonstrated VIL's effectiveness in physical environments. The model was trained entirely in simulation (R2R-CE varied-viewpoint setup) and tested in two indoor environments (office and lounge). The real robot's camera height (0.7m) was also out-of-distribution relative to the simulation training range (0.75m to 1.75m).
- VIL significantly improved the success rate (SR) in both environments: from 28% to 44% in the Office, and from 20% to 48% in the Lounge. These results confirm that VIL enhances navigation robustness in real-world deployments.

#### 4.7. Computational Efficiency

VIL proved to be computationally efficient.
- VIL's post-training phase converged in approximately 48 hours, which is roughly 14% of the full training time required for the ETPNav baseline.
- The additional modules introduced by VIL resulted in a marginal increase in total parameters (335.21M vs. 317.31M) and trainable parameters (143.21M vs. 142.93M).
- GPU memory usage increased only marginally (from ~6000 MB to 6200-6300 MB).
- Inference overhead was negligible, with no distinguishable difference in per-step runtime, making it deployment-friendly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a critical gap in embodied AI by tackling the viewpoint sensitivity of Vision-Language Navigation policies in continuous environments. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Advancement in Embodied AI Robustness:** The introduction of V²-VLNCE provides a more realistic and systematic benchmark for evaluating the robustness of VLNCE policies to varied camera viewpoints, pushing the research frontier toward more practical and deployable agents.
- **Efficient and Practical Solution:** VIL offers a computationally efficient post-training framework to adapt existing VLNCE policies. Unlike methods requiring extensive retraining or full model re-engineering, VIL's "plug-and-play" nature allows for the enhancement of pre-trained models with minimal additional effort and cost. This makes it a practical strategy for developers and researchers working with diverse robotic platforms.
- **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The framework demonstrates significant improvements in navigation performance under various simulated viewpoint changes, including in-distribution variations, fixed real-robot camera configurations, and out-of-distribution scenarios. This enhanced generalization is crucial for real-world deployments where environmental conditions and robot configurations are rarely static or perfectly matched to training data.
- **Maintaining Baseline Performance:** A notable impact is that VIL does not degrade, and often slightly improves, performance in standard VLNCE settings, despite being trained for varied viewpoints. This indicates that VIL provides a net benefit without trade-offs in conventional scenarios.
- **Bridging Simulation-to-Real Gap:** The proof-of-concept real-robot experiments are particularly impactful. By demonstrating zero-shot transfer from simulation to physical environments, VIL shows promise in enhancing the robustness of simulated policies for real-world robotic applications, a long-standing challenge in robotics.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across different VLNCE baselines (ETPNav, BEVBert) and datasets (R2R-CE, RxR-CE) suggests broad applicability, potentially benefiting a wider range of navigation systems.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** By formally defining V²-VLNCE and providing a robust solution, this work sets a new standard and encourages future research into view-invariant representation learning and adaptation strategies for various embodied AI tasks.

In summary, this work contributes a practical, efficient, and robust solution to a fundamental challenge in embodied navigation, thereby facilitating the deployment of intelligent agents in complex, real-world environments with diverse and variable sensor configurations.
