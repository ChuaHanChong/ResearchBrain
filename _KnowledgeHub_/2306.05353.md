# Negotiated Reasoning: On Provably Addressing Relative Over-Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Stein Variational Negotiated Reasoning (SVNR) is presented, which is theoretically proven to avoid Relative Over-generalization (RO) in multi-agent reinforcement learning by satisfying a "consistent reasoning" condition. This approach consistently achieves optimal cooperation in challenging environments, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Fully cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) suffers from Relative Over-generalization (RO), where agents overfit their policies to exploratory behaviors of others, leading to sub-optimal joint policies.
- Existing MARL methods lack a theoretical understanding of RO, preventing the development of provably RO-free solutions.
- Current reasoning-endowed methods, while showing empirical mitigation, do not offer theoretical guarantees against RO and often struggle with scalability.

## Method

- A **Negotiated Reasoning** (NR) framework is introduced, enabling agents to explicitly reason and negotiate on action selections to achieve consistent beliefs about others' behaviors.
- This framework is instantiated as Stein Variational ==Negotiated Reasoning== (SVNR), using Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) to learn negotiation policies under a strict nested negotiation structure.
- SVNR is integrated into a maximum entropy policy iteration and made practical for complex environments through an amortized learning scheme with neural networks.

## Results

- SVNR consistently achieved global optimal returns and highest average performance in various RO-challenged differential games, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines that often converged to local optima.
- The method successfully captured multiple optimal modalities in PRO-challenged environments, unlike other baselines which settled on a single modality.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the necessity of inter-agent negotiation and confirmed the effectiveness of the strict nested negotiation structure for achieving robust, optimal cooperation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relative Over-generalization (RO) can be provably avoided in MARL if agents achieve "consistent reasoning" at convergence, accurately modeling others' behaviors.
> - Distinct concepts of Perceived Relative Over-generalization (PRO) and Executed Relative Over-generalization (ERO) clarify the nature of RO and its avoidance.
> - A strict nested negotiation structure among agents is theoretically proven to be essential for the Negotiated Reasoning framework to converge to the optimal joint policy.
