# Negotiated Reasoning: On Provably Addressing Relative Over-Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Stein Variational Negotiated Reasoning (SVNR) is presented, which is theoretically proven to avoid Relative Over-generalization (RO) in multi-agent reinforcement learning by satisfying a "consistent reasoning" condition. This approach consistently achieves optimal cooperation in challenging environments, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Fully cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) suffers from Relative Over-generalization (RO), where agents overfit their policies to exploratory behaviors of others, leading to sub-optimal joint policies.
- Existing MARL methods lack a theoretical understanding of RO, preventing the development of provably RO-free solutions.
- Current reasoning-endowed methods, while showing empirical mitigation, do not offer theoretical guarantees against RO and often struggle with scalability.

## Method

- A **Negotiated Reasoning** (NR) framework is introduced, enabling agents to explicitly reason and negotiate on action selections to achieve consistent beliefs about others' behaviors.
- This framework is instantiated as Stein Variational ==Negotiated Reasoning== (SVNR), using Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) to learn negotiation policies under a strict nested negotiation structure.
- SVNR is integrated into a maximum entropy policy iteration and made practical for complex environments through an amortized learning scheme with neural networks.

## Results

- SVNR consistently achieved global optimal returns and highest average performance in various RO-challenged differential games, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines that often converged to local optima.
- The method successfully captured multiple optimal modalities in PRO-challenged environments, unlike other baselines which settled on a single modality.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the necessity of inter-agent negotiation and confirmed the effectiveness of the strict nested negotiation structure for achieving robust, optimal cooperation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relative Over-generalization (RO) can be provably avoided in MARL if agents achieve "consistent reasoning" at convergence, accurately modeling others' behaviors.
> - Distinct concepts of Perceived Relative Over-generalization (PRO) and Executed Relative Over-generalization (ERO) clarify the nature of RO and its avoidance.
> - A strict nested negotiation structure among agents is theoretically proven to be essential for the Negotiated Reasoning framework to converge to the optimal joint policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is a field that investigates how multiple agents can learn to act cooperatively or competitively in a shared environment to maximize their utility. MARL finds applications in diverse areas such as multiplayer games, robotics, and traffic control. This paper specifically addresses fully cooperative MARL settings, where agents share a common goal of increasing team profitability.

A significant challenge in both cognitive science and MARL is "over-generalization." In cognitive science, this refers to the tendency of individuals to apply overly broad rules to specific situations based on limited experience, potentially leading to excessive caution (e.g., "once bitten, twice shy"). In MARL, this phenomenon manifests as "relative over-generalization (RO)." RO occurs when agents, learning from their limited interactions, overfit their policies to the exploratory behaviors of other agents. This often leads to sub-optimal cooperation, where agents become trapped in local optima, failing to achieve the globally optimal joint policy. For instance, in cooperative tasks like "Particle Gather," agents might learn to avoid areas where they previously experienced losses due to asynchronous actions, even if synchronous action in that area would yield high rewards.

Existing approaches to mitigate RO in MARL generally fall into two categories:
1. **Lenient learning methods:** These approaches aim to reduce RO by introducing leniency toward past experiences, often through a temperature factor that controls the influence of experience on policy updates. While showing some success in simple tabular environments, these methods typically require extensive hyperparameter tuning and may not scale well to complex environments.
2. **Reasoning-endowed methods:** These methods equip agents with the ability to model and reason about the behaviors of other agents. For example, some approaches allow agents to model others' actions as best responses to their own potential actions. These methods have empirically mitigated RO in certain tasks, particularly those involving a small number of agents. However, a significant limitation across these reasoning-endowed methods has been the lack of a theoretical understanding of RO itself and, consequently, an absence of methods that are provably RO-free.

This research builds upon the foundation of reasoning-endowed MARL, also drawing connections to opponent modeling techniques and the probabilistic inference paradigm for reinforcement learning (RL). Traditional opponent modeling often assumes stationary policies or requires Nash equilibrium updates, which are less suitable for dynamic MARL settings. Probabilistic inference reformulations of RL, particularly those leveraging maximum entropy principles, have been successful in single-agent contexts. Extending these to MARL involves challenges related to consistent modeling of opponent policies, an area where existing reasoning methods have not provided theoretical guarantees of consistency. This paper aims to bridge the theoretical gap in understanding RO and develop a provably effective solution within the reasoning-endowed MARL framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the identified lack of a theoretical understanding of Relative Over-generalization (RO) in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) and the consequent absence of provably RO-free methods. While existing reasoning-endowed methods have empirically shown some success in mitigating RO, the underlying mechanisms and conditions for guaranteed RO avoidance remained largely unexplored.

This paper sets out to address two fundamental questions:
1. **Can relative over-generalization be provably avoided, and if so, under what conditions?** This objective aims to establish a theoretical foundation for understanding RO by defining precise conditions under which MARL methods can avoid this pathology. The paper introduces new conceptualizations of RO, namely Perceived Relative Over-generalization (PRO) and Executed Relative Over-generalization (ERO), to facilitate a more rigorous analysis within the context of centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) MARL schemes. The goal is to prove that RO can be circumvented if a "consistent reasoning" requirement is met.
2. **How can a method be designed that provably addresses relative over-generalization?** Building on the theoretical insights, the second objective is to develop a novel MARL framework and an instantiated algorithm that satisfies the conditions for provably avoiding RO. This involves creating a mechanism where agents can reach consistent beliefs about others' behaviors, thereby preventing the pitfalls of over-generalization.

Specifically, the paper's key objectives and contributions can be summarized as follows:
- **Theoretical Justification for RO Avoidance:** To prove that RO can be avoided if a MARL method satisfies a "consistent reasoning" requirement at convergence. This condition mandates that each agent's model of others' behaviors must be consistent with their actual updated or executed behaviors.
- **Introduction of Negotiated Reasoning (NR) Framework:** To propose a novel reasoning framework inspired by human negotiation and graphical model inference. This framework is designed to allow agents to explicitly reason about and negotiate on action selections, aiming to reach an "agreement" that reflects consistent reasoning.
- **Development of Stein Variational Negotiated Reasoning (SVNR):** To instantiate the NR framework with a concrete algorithm, SVNR. This objective involves using Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD) to derive negotiation policies that facilitate convergence to the optimal joint policy, particularly under a "strict nested" negotiation structure.
- **Integration with Maximum Entropy Policy Iteration:** To embed SVNR within a maximum entropy policy iteration framework, demonstrating that it can provably reach consistent reasoning and achieve optimal cooperation at convergence under specified conditions.
- **Practical Implementation with Amortized Learning:** To address the computational challenges of SVNR in complex environments by parameterizing the method with neural networks and introducing an amortized learning scheme. This aims to make the method efficient by distilling multi-round negotiation dynamics into single neural network inferences.
- **Empirical Validation:** To demonstrate the superiority and efficiency of the proposed SVNR method compared to state-of-the-art baselines in several RO-challenged multi-agent environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research addresses Relative Over-generalization (RO) by first establishing a theoretical framework and then proposing a novel algorithmic solution, Stein Variational Negotiated Reasoning (SVNR), which is further optimized for practical implementation.

#### 3.1. Conceptualizing Relative Over-Generalization (RO)

The paper refines the definition of RO by introducing two new concepts within the Centralized Training Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm of MARL:
- **Perceived Relative Over-generalization (PRO):** This occurs during the training phase. An agent suffers PRO if its optimal perceived joint policy could be made closer to the true optimal joint policy by knowing the optimal opponent policies. This signifies a mismatch between an agent's belief about opponents and their actual optimal behavior.
- **Executed Relative Over-generalization (ERO):** This occurs during the execution phase. An agent suffers ERO if the utility of the executed joint policy could be improved by letting agents know others' actions during execution. This highlights issues arising from decentralized execution when agents lack complete information about others' real-time actions.
The paper posits that RO is avoided when ERO is avoided at convergence, which in turn is linked to avoiding PRO.

#### 3.2. Consistent Reasoning

A core theoretical contribution is the proof that RO can be avoided if the MARL method satisfies a "consistent reasoning" condition at convergence. Consistent reasoning is defined as agents modeling others' behaviors consistently with their optimal policies during training (i.e., `ρ_i = π*α(u_{-i})`) and consistently with their executed actions during execution.

#### 3.3. Negotiated Reasoning (NR) Framework

Inspired by human negotiation processes and message-passing in graphical models, the paper proposes the Negotiated Reasoning (NR) framework to enable agents to achieve consistent reasoning.
- **Particle Representation:** Agents represent the initial perceived joint policy distribution using a set of `M` particles `{u_ℓ,0}`.
- **Negotiation Policies:** Each agent `i` possesses a "negotiation (perturb) policy" `f_i(u_i | u_Ci, s)`, which updates its action `u_i` based on the actions of other agents in its "negotiated set" `C_i` for a given state `s`. This process involves `K` rounds of negotiation, iteratively updating particles: `u_ℓ,k_i = f_k_i(u_i | s, u_ℓ,k-1_Ci)`.
- **PRO-free Condition:** NR is proven to be PRO-free if, at convergence (i.e., `k -> K`), the negotiation policies converge to an identity map, and the perceived joint policy converges to the optimal joint policy (`lim_k->K p(u_k | s) = π*(u_k | s)`).
- **ERO-free Condition:** If agents are PRO-free and the entropy factor `α` is annealed to a sufficiently small value, sampling actions from the converged policy `u_0,K_i` ensures ERO-free decentralized execution.

#### 3.4. Stein Variational Negotiated Reasoning (SVNR)

SVNR instantiates the NR framework by leveraging Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD), specifically its multi-particle variant (MPSVGD), to learn negotiation policies.
- **Learning Negotiation Policies via MPSVGD:** The negotiation policy `f_i` is designed as an update rule `u_ℓ_i + εϕ_i(u_C_i)ℓ`, where `ϕ_i` is the optimal transformation direction derived from MPSVGD. This direction minimizes the KL-divergence between the agent's perceived policy and the induced optimal joint policy, given fixed actions of other agents. The formula for `ϕ*_i` involves an expectation over current policy samples, a kernel function, and the gradient of the log-probability of the optimal policy.
- **Strict Nested Negotiation Structure:** A crucial aspect for guaranteeing convergence to the optimal joint policy and satisfying the PRO-free conditions (5.2) is the design of the negotiation sets `{C_i}`. The paper proves that if `{C_i}` satisfies a "strict nested requirement" (e.g., `C_i = {1..., i}` for agent `i`), then the negotiated reasoning converges, and the agreement is identical to the optimal joint policy.

#### 3.5. Maximum Entropy Policy Iteration

SVNR is integrated into a maximum entropy policy iteration framework, allowing agents to learn optimal Q and V functions iteratively.
- **Soft Bellman Operator:** The standard Bellman operator is adapted for maximum entropy settings.
- **Joint Policy Evaluation and Improvement:** The framework alternates between evaluating the current policy's performance (Q-function) and improving the policy based on these evaluations. The policy improvement step uses the SVNR negotiation process (Equation 6.6) to update the perceived joint policy.
- **Theoretical Convergence:** Under the condition of strict nested negotiation, SVNR policy iteration is proven to converge to the optimal joint policy `π*`, which in turn, combined with Theorem 5.2, leads to an ERO-free executed joint policy by annealing `α`.

#### 3.6. Practical Implementation: Amortized SVNR

To address the computational and storage complexities of SVNR (inefficient particle-based policy representation, intractable expectations in the soft Bellman operator), the paper proposes an amortized learning approach with neural networks.
- **Neural Network Parameterization:** Policies `f_ψ_i(u_i | ξ_i, ξ_C_i, s)` are parameterized by neural networks `ψ_i` that map initial noise `ξ` to actions.
- **Amortized MPSVGD:** Instead of iterating SVNR until convergence for each policy update, amortized MPSVGD distills the multi-round negotiation dynamics into a single forward pass of the neural network. The policy parameters `ψ_i` are updated using gradients derived from the MPSVGD direction (Equation 7.2) backpropagated through the mapping network (Equation 7.3). This allows for efficient, gradient-based optimization.
- **Stochastic Optimization for Value Functions:** The intractable expectation in the soft Bellman operator is approximated using importance sampling, and the Q-function is updated by minimizing the Bellman error (Equation 7.4).
- **Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution:** The final algorithm (Algorithm 1) maintains the CTDE structure, with a centralized critic `Q_θ` and decentralized execution policies `f_ψ_i`. Agents share noise signals `ξ` within their negotiation sets during execution for coordinated action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields both theoretical justifications and empirical demonstrations of the effectiveness of Negotiated Reasoning (NR) and its instantiation, Stein Variational Negotiated Reasoning (SVNR), in addressing Relative Over-generalization (RO).

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Provable RO Avoidance:** The paper theoretically confirms that RO can be provably avoided in MARL. This is contingent on the MARL method satisfying a "consistent reasoning" requirement at convergence. Consistent reasoning implies that agents accurately model others' behaviors (optimal policies during training, executed actions during execution).
- **New RO Definitions (PRO and ERO):** The introduction of Perceived Relative Over-generalization (PRO) and Executed Relative Over-generalization (ERO) provides a refined analytical framework for RO. The work establishes that if agents are PRO-free and the entropy regularization parameter `α` is annealed to zero, they achieve ERO-free execution, thus being RO-free.
- **PRO-free Negotiated Reasoning:** The NR framework is shown to be PRO-free if, after `K` negotiation steps, the negotiation policies converge to an identity map, and the perceived joint policy converges to the optimal joint policy.
- **ERO-free Negotiated Reasoning:** For PRO-free agents, setting the decentralized execution policy `π_i = u_0,K_i` and annealing `α -> 0` ensures ERO-free operation.
- **SVNR Convergence Guarantees:** SVNR, when employing a "strict nested negotiation structure" (e.g., `C_i = {1..., i}` for agent `i`) and integrated with maximum entropy policy iteration, is proven to achieve consistent reasoning and converge to the optimal joint policy under mild conditions. This makes SVNR the first MARL method demonstrated to be provably RO-free.

#### 4.2. Empirical Findings

The empirical evaluation of SVNR was conducted across various challenging multi-agent environments designed to expose RO issues, comparing against state-of-the-art baselines including MADDPG, MASQL, PR2, ROMMEO, and Lenient MADRL.

- **ERO-Challenged Scenario (Max of Three Differential Game):**
 - **Consistent Optimal Performance:** SVNR consistently achieved the highest average returns and converged to the global optimum across all tested `s2` values (1.5, 2.0, 3.0), which control the severity of the RO problem. Baselines like MADDPG frequently became stuck in local optima (yielding 0 reward). MASQL, PR2, and ROMMEO could sometimes escape local optima but showed significant variance and often failed in more challenging `s2` settings.
 - **Learning Dynamics:** Visualizations of sampled joint actions showed that SVNR agents, after initial exploration of sub-optimal areas, successfully shifted their visitation probability towards the global optima and maintained consistent performance. Other baselines largely remained concentrated around sub-optimal solutions.
- **PRO-Challenged Scenario (Two Modalities Differential Game):**
 - **Multi-modality Capture:** In an environment with two equally optimal solutions, SVNR successfully captured both modalities in its converged perceived joint policy. In contrast, all baseline methods converged to a single modality, demonstrating their susceptibility to PRO.
- **Particle Gather Environment:**
 - **Global Optimum Achievement:** SVNR steadily converged to the global optimum, a difficult outcome in this scenario where agents tend to avoid the landmark due to initial penalties from asynchronous arrivals. Most baselines converged to sub-optimal solutions, with some like PR2 and MADDPG falling into local optima.
 - **Highest Test Performance:** Across all tested scenarios (Max of Three with varying `s2`, and Particle Gather), SVNR consistently achieved the highest execution returns in test environments (Table 1).

- **Ablation Studies on Negotiation Structure:**
 - **Importance of Inter-Agent Negotiation:** Comparing SVNR (strict nested negotiation), SVNR-F (full negotiation where `C_i = -i`), and SVNR-M (no negotiation, `C_i = {}`), the studies demonstrated that both SVNR and SVNR-F significantly outperformed SVNR-M. This highlights the necessity of considering other agents' actions/noises for effective cooperation and RO avoidance.
 - **Effectiveness of Nested Structure:** SVNR, with its strict nested negotiation structure, performed comparably to or slightly better than SVNR-F, indicating that the computationally simpler nested structure is sufficient and effective for achieving RO-free learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces significant advancements in the field of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), particularly concerning the challenge of Relative Over-generalization (RO).

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Formalizing RO Avoidance:** The paper provides the first theoretical proof that RO can be provably avoided in MARL, establishing a foundational connection between consistent reasoning and RO-free behavior. This moves beyond empirical observations to a deeper theoretical understanding of this game pathology.
- **New Conceptual Framework for RO:** By introducing Perceived Relative Over-generalization (PRO) and Executed Relative Over-generalization (ERO), the research offers a more precise analytical framework for understanding RO in the context of centralized training and decentralized execution. This distinction helps in diagnosing and addressing RO issues at different stages of MARL.
- **Provably RO-Free Method:** The proposed Negotiated Reasoning (NR) framework, instantiated as Stein Variational Negotiated Reasoning (SVNR), represents the first MARL method that is theoretically proven to address RO and converge to optimal cooperation. This theoretical guarantee provides a strong basis for its reliability and robustness.
- **Role of Negotiation Structure:** The theoretical justification for the "strict nested negotiation requirement" as a condition for convergence to the optimal joint policy provides a principled guideline for designing effective multi-agent coordination mechanisms.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact

- **Improved Cooperative Performance:** Empirically, SVNR consistently outperforms state-of-the-art MARL methods in various RO-challenged environments, achieving globally optimal cooperation where other methods often get stuck in sub-optimal equilibria. This directly translates to more effective and reliable multi-agent systems in real-world applications.
- **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** SVNR's ability to consistently find optimal solutions even in highly competitive or deceptive environments (like Max of Three with severe RO) suggests its potential for deployment in complex, dynamic, and potentially adversarial multi-agent settings.
- **Efficient Amortized Learning:** The development of an amortized learning scheme for SVNR, leveraging neural networks, addresses the computational intractability of particle-based reasoning. This makes the provably RO-free method practical for modern deep MARL problems, enabling its application to larger state and action spaces.

#### 5.3. Future Directions and Broader Implications

- **Scalability to Large Agent Populations:** The identified limitation of SVNR regarding scalability with a large number of agents (due to one agent needing to reason about all others) points to crucial future research. Investigating attention mechanisms, sparse negotiation structures, or domain-specific knowledge to reduce the computational burden of negotiation in large teams could unlock SVNR's potential in highly distributed systems.
- **Theoretical-Practical Gap in Sparse Structures:** Exploring the theoretical and practical implications when sparse negotiation structures (which might violate the strict nested requirement) are adopted is an interesting avenue. This could lead to new theoretical understanding of approximate consistency and its impact on RO.
- **Generalizability of Negotiated Reasoning:** The core concept of negotiated reasoning, where agents explicitly model and agree upon joint actions, could be extended to other MARL paradigms (e.g., mixed cooperative-competitive settings) or even to human-agent collaboration, where negotiation is a critical component of successful interaction.

In summary, this paper significantly advances the theoretical understanding of RO in MARL and introduces a robust, provably effective, and practically implementable solution. Its contributions lay groundwork for designing more intelligent and reliable cooperative multi-agent systems in complex environments.
