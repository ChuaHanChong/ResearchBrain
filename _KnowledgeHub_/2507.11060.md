# Personalized Exercise Recommendation with Semantically-Grounded Knowledge Tracing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zürich and LMU Munich developed ExRec, a comprehensive framework for personalized exercise recommendation that integrates semantically-grounded knowledge tracing with reinforcement learning. ExRec achieved substantial gains in student knowledge improvement, particularly through its Model-Based Value Estimation (MVE) which enhanced continuous RL algorithm performance by up to 2.4 percentage points, especially in dynamic learning scenarios and when scaling to larger question corpuses.

## Problem

- Existing Knowledge Tracing (KT) models for exercise recommendation often neglect the semantic content of questions, relying on identifier-based embeddings that limit generalization.
- Computational inefficiency arises from representing a student's state as their entire exercise history, hindering scalability for long learning sequences.
- Reward computation in RL for recommendations is inefficient, often requiring extensive inference over many questions, and prior systems lacked flexibility, supporting only a single RL algorithm or relying on hardcoded rewards and pre-defined question-KC graphs.

## Method

- **ExRec** implements an end-to-end framework: LLM-powered automated ==Knowledge Concept== (KC) annotation mapped to solution steps, then contrastive learning generates semantically rich embeddings for questions, solution steps, and KCs beyond simple ID-based representations.
- A calibrated recurrent Knowledge Tracing (KT) model processes these embeddings to create compact student state representations and directly predict KC mastery.
- An RL framework, using the calibrated KT model as an environment, incorporates Model-Based Value Estimation (MVE) for continuous Q-learning methods, leveraging the KT model structure to stabilize and improve value function estimation and reward computation.

## Results

- MVE consistently boosted continuous value-based RL algorithms (DDPG, SAC, TD3) across all four tasks (up to 2.4 percentage points higher knowledge improvement) and demonstrated robustness to extended question corpuses, where non-MVE models experienced performance drops.
- Ablation studies showed that LLM-based KC annotation yielded higher quality KCs, and contrastive learning increased semantic coherence for KC retrieval (F1 score from **0.2305** to **0.8865**).
- KC calibration significantly reduced the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for direct KC-level knowledge state prediction from **0.08** to **0.028**, validating efficient and accurate mastery estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating semantically-grounded representations of questions and KCs significantly enhances the generalizability of recommendation systems to new content.
> - Efficiently predicting KC mastery directly from the KT model's latent state, rather than inferring from many questions, enables scalable RL reward calculation, while Model-Based Value Estimation further stabilizes continuous Q-learning in dynamic environments.
> - LLMs can effectively automate the creation of high-quality, consistent KC annotations, reducing the reliance on costly and often inconsistent manual processes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid expansion of online learning platforms has increased the demand for personalized educational experiences. A central component of personalized learning systems is the ability to recommend exercises that align with a student's evolving knowledge state. Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a prominent method in this area, focusing on modeling the temporal dynamics of student learning and predicting their performance on future exercises. KT models typically track a student's mastery of fundamental skills, referred to as knowledge concepts (KCs).

Recent research has begun to integrate KT models with Reinforcement Learning (RL) frameworks to simulate student behavior and develop optimal recommendation policies. These integrated approaches aim to identify sequences of exercises that maximize learning gains. However, the existing body of work in KT-guided RL for exercise recommendation exhibits several limitations:
1. **Neglect of semantic content:** Many approaches rely on identifier-based embeddings for questions, which do not capture the underlying semantic meaning of the exercises. This limits their ability to generalize to new questions or understand conceptual relationships.
2. **Computational inefficiency of state representation:** Defining a student's state as their entire exercise history can become computationally impractical, especially for long learning sequences in real-world platforms.
3. **Inefficient reward computation:** Calculating rewards often requires running inference over a large set of questions at each step to assess knowledge improvement, which can hinder real-time application.
4. **Limited algorithm compatibility:** Existing systems often support only a single RL algorithm, restricting the exploration of diverse policy learning strategies.
5. **Reliance on pre-defined structures:** Some methods depend on handcrafted rewards or pre-defined question-KC graphs, which are frequently unavailable or difficult to construct and maintain.

This paper introduces ExRec, a framework designed to address these limitations. ExRec integrates the semantic content of questions and KCs into the knowledge tracing process and subsequent RL-based recommendation. By providing an end-to-end pipeline—from automated KC annotation and semantic representation learning to calibrated KT models and optimized RL methods—ExRec aims to offer a more comprehensive, efficient, and flexible solution for personalized exercise recommendation. Its introduction of a model-based value estimation (MVE) further refines the RL training process by directly leveraging the KT model structure, thereby enhancing the stability and efficiency of continuous RL algorithms in educational contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce ExRec, a general framework for personalized exercise recommendation that integrates semantically-grounded knowledge tracing. The framework aims to overcome the identified limitations of existing approaches by providing an end-to-end pipeline for developing and deploying effective exercise recommenders.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are as follows:

- **Integrating Semantic Content:** The work is motivated by the observation that current KT-based exercise recommendation systems often overlook the semantic content of questions. Traditional methods frequently rely on arbitrary question IDs, which do not inherently capture the conceptual relationships or the nuances of educational material. ExRec aims to address this by learning rich semantic representations of questions and KCs, enabling a deeper understanding of the learning content.
- **Addressing Sequential and Structured Learning:** Acknowledging that student learning follows a sequential and structured progression, the research seeks to embed this understanding directly into the recommendation process. This involves modeling how a student's knowledge evolves across specific KCs, rather than treating learning as an undifferentiated process.
- **Improving Computational Efficiency:** Existing RL-based recommendation systems often define student states as their full exercise history, leading to computational impracticality for long learning sequences. The paper is motivated to design compact student state representations that are more efficient to process, thereby enhancing the scalability of the system.
- **Enabling Real-time Applicability:** Reward computation in prior RL-based systems often necessitates extensive inference over all available questions to determine knowledge improvement, limiting their real-time application. A key objective is to develop efficient knowledge state computation methods that enable faster reward calculation, making real-time personalization feasible.
- **Supporting Diverse RL Algorithms:** Many existing methods are designed for a single RL algorithm, which restricts flexibility and the ability to leverage different policy learning strategies. ExRec aims to create a framework that supports a broad range of RL algorithms, including both discrete and continuous action spaces, allowing for greater adaptability to various educational objectives.
- **Enhancing RL Training Stability and Efficiency:** For continuous RL methods based on Q-learning, the paper is motivated to introduce a novel approach, Model-Based Value Estimation (MVE), that directly utilizes the components of the KT model to estimate cumulative knowledge improvement. This aims to guide and stabilize the Q-learning process, leading to more efficient policy optimization.
- **Facilitating Interpretability and Generalization:** The framework seeks to produce interpretable student learning trajectories, allowing educators and researchers to understand how a student's knowledge evolves. Additionally, it aims to demonstrate robust generalization to new, previously unseen questions, a critical capability for dynamic learning environments.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a comprehensive, efficient, and semantically-aware framework for personalized exercise recommendation that overcomes the limitations of current KT-guided RL approaches, thereby enhancing student learning outcomes in online education.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ExRec framework is structured into four interconnected modules, designed to provide an end-to-end pipeline for personalized exercise recommendation.

#### 3.1. KC Annotation via LLMs (Module 1)

This module automates the annotation of knowledge concepts (KCs) for educational questions, addressing the limitations of manual, expensive, and often inconsistent annotation processes. A Large Language Model (LLM), specifically GPT-4o, is utilized in a three-step process:
1. **Solution Step Generation:** Given a question and its final answer, the LLM generates a step-by-step solution using chain-of-thought prompting. Each step is produced sequentially.
2. **KC Annotation:** The LLM then assigns a set of KCs to the question based on its content and generated solution steps. This process is instructed to align KC annotations with the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics to enhance consistency.
3. **Solution Step–KC Mapping:** The LLM establishes a structured many-to-many mapping between individual solution steps and their relevant KCs, indicating which KCs are applied at each stage of the solution.

#### 3.2. Representation Learning via Contrastive Learning (Module 2)

This module focuses on generating semantically meaningful embeddings for questions, solution steps, and KCs, moving beyond ID-based representations. It employs a contrastive learning (CL) approach:
1. **Embedding Generation:** A learnable LLM encoder (fine-tuned BERT) `Eψ` encodes the text of questions, solution steps, and KCs into vector embeddings (`z_q`, `z_σ_k`, `z_c_j`).
2. **False Negative Elimination:** To prevent semantically equivalent KCs from being treated as negatives in contrastive learning, KCs are pre-clustered using HDBSCAN based on their Sentence-BERT embeddings. KCs within the same cluster are excluded from the negative sample set.
3. **Contrastive Learning Objectives:**
 - **For Questions:** A contrastive loss `L_q` is applied to align a question's embedding (`z_q`) with the embeddings of its associated KCs (`z_c_j`) while pushing it away from unrelated KCs (filtered by clustering).
 - **For Solution Steps:** Similarly, a contrastive loss `L_s` aligns the embedding of each solution step (`z_σ_k`) with the KCs it practices (`z_c_j`).
4. **Final Objective:** The framework jointly optimizes these objectives (`L = L_q + L_s`) to ensure that question and solution step embeddings are semantically aligned with their corresponding KCs. The learned embeddings enrich the input space for downstream KT models.

#### 3.3. KT Training with KC Calibration (Module 3)

This module integrates the learned semantic representations into a Knowledge Tracing (KT) model and calibrates it for direct knowledge state prediction.
1. **Input Preparation:** Instead of randomly initialized question embeddings, the KT model utilizes the learned representations. For each exercise `e_i`, the input `z̃_q_i` is formed by averaging the question embedding (`z_q_i`) with the mean-pooled embeddings of its solution steps (`z̃_σ_i`). This `z̃_q_i` and the student's binary response embedding (`z_y_i`) constitute the input.
2. **State Representation:** A recurrent state encoder `Eθ` (an LSTM) processes the sequence of exercises to compute a compact latent student state `s_t`. This recurrent formulation avoids the need to store the entire exercise history.
3. **KT Training:** The KT model, consisting of `Eθ` and a classifier `Fθ` (a multi-layer perceptron), is trained to predict a student's performance on the next question (`ŷ_q_t+1 = Fθ(s_t, z̃_q_t+1)`) using a binary cross-entropy loss `L_pred`.
4. **KC Calibration:** To enable direct and efficient prediction of a student's knowledge state for any KC, the KT model is calibrated. The knowledge state `y_c_t` for a KC `c` at time `t` is defined as the expected performance on questions associated with `c`. To avoid computationally expensive inference over all questions for a given KC, the model is trained to directly predict the knowledge state from a KC embedding (`ŷ_c_t+1 = Fθ(s_t, z_c_t+1)`) using an additional binary cross-entropy loss `L_KC`. The final calibration loss `L_calib = L_pred + L_KC` maintains predictive accuracy while enabling direct KC-level mastery estimation.

#### 3.4. RL Framework for Exercise Recommendation (Module 4)

The calibrated KT model from Module 3 serves as the reinforcement learning (RL) environment for exercise recommendation, formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
1. **MDP Formulation:**
 - **State Space (S):** The student's state `s_t` is the compact latent state from the KT model.
 - **Action Space (A):** The RL agent selects the next exercise `q_t+1`, represented by its continuous embedding `z̃_q_t+1`. The framework supports both continuous action RL (where the agent outputs an embedding, and a question is retrieved by semantic similarity) and discrete action RL (where the agent outputs a question ID mapped to an embedding).
 - **Transition Dynamics (P):** The environment transitions to a new student state `s_t+1` based on the KT model's predicted probability of a correct response (`ŷ_q_t+1`) to the recommended question.
 - **Reward Function (R):** The reward `r_t` is defined as the immediate improvement in the predicted knowledge state for a target KC (`r_t = ŷ_c_t+1 - ŷ_c_t`).
 - **Discount Factor (γ):** Balances short-term and long-term learning gains.
2. **Model-Based Value Estimation (MVE):** For Q-learning-based continuous RL algorithms (e.g., DDPG, SAC, TD3), ExRec introduces MVE to improve value function estimation. Instead of training a Q-network from scratch, the critic network is initialized with the pre-trained KT model's state encoder `Eθ` (as `Eω`) and classifier `Fθ` (as `Fω`). The Q-value `Q(s_t, z̃_q_t+1)` is computed by simulating the two possible next states (correct or incorrect response) using the KT model, predicting the accumulated knowledge state for the target KC in each scenario, and weighting these outcomes by the KT model's predicted response probability. This provides a structured, model-informed estimate of future knowledge improvement, reducing the need for extensive trial-and-error interactions during RL training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the ExRec framework was evaluated across four distinct educational tasks using the XES3G5M dataset, which contains 7,652 unique math questions and 5.5 million student interactions. The original Chinese questions were translated to English, and new KC annotations were generated using LLMs, which the authors indicate resulted in higher quality and more consistent KCs than the original dataset.

**Performance across Evaluation Tasks (Figure 3):**
The evaluation measured the percentage of maximum achievable knowledge improvement over a 10-step exercise recommendation horizon.
- **Non-RL Baselines:** A random policy and a historical data baseline generally showed marginal or negative gains across all tasks. This observation highlights the necessity for tailored recommendation policies.
- **Discrete-Action RL Methods:** Algorithms such as Discrete SAC, C51, Rainbow, and DQN generally performed well in tasks (1-3) that involved a relatively static or predictable target KC, indicating their ability to optimize for specific learning objectives.
- **Continuous-Action RL Methods:**
 - Value-based continuous RL algorithms (DDPG, SAC, TD3) consistently outperformed policy-based approaches (TRPO, PPO).
 - **Model-Based Value Estimation (MVE):** The integration of MVE consistently boosted the performance of DDPG, SAC, and TD3 across all four tasks. This improvement was particularly pronounced in Task 4 (Knowledge Improvement in Weakest KC), which is characterized by a dynamically shifting target KC. In this challenging scenario, MVE-augmented continuous value-based methods surpassed the performance of discrete methods, suggesting that MVE contributes to improved adaptability and planning capabilities in dynamic learning environments.

**Use Case: Extending the Question Corpus (Figure 4):**
To assess the framework's generalization capabilities, the original question corpus was extended by generating three times more questions using GPT-4o.
- **Default Models:** Continuous-action models without MVE experienced a performance drop when exposed to the extended corpus, suggesting difficulty in adapting to the expanded and potentially novel question space.
- **Models with MVE:** In contrast, models augmented with MVE demonstrated robustness, often improving student knowledge more effectively with the larger question set. This indicates that MVE enhances the ability of RL agents to leverage a broader range of exercises, even when new and unseen.

**Use Case: Visualizing Conceptual Growth (Figure 5 and Appendix H):**
The framework's ability to produce interpretable student learning trajectories was illustrated by visualizing a student's knowledge state evolution for Task 4 (weakest KC).
- **Random Policy:** Showed no meaningful gains, often failing to address the student's weaknesses.
- **Vanilla DDPG:** Achieved moderate improvements, demonstrating the benefit of learned policies.
- **MVE-augmented DDPG:** Led to significantly larger knowledge gains. These models addressed specific KCs more efficiently, adapting quickly and moving on to other weak concepts.
- **Extended Corpus with MVE:** Further improvements were observed when MVE-augmented models were trained on the extended corpus, demonstrating that a broader action space, when effectively leveraged, can lead to enhanced learning outcomes.

**Ablation Studies (Appendix C):**
- **KC Annotation (Module 1):** LLM-based annotation yielded higher quality and more consistent KCs compared to original dataset annotations, resulting in a more coherent KC space (5,139 unique KCs grouped into 1,377 clusters, an improvement over prior work's 8,378 KCs and 2,024 clusters).
- **Representation Learning (Module 2):** Contrastive learning significantly improved the semantic coherence of question embeddings, increasing micro-averaged F1 score for KC retrieval from 0.2305 to 0.8865.
- **KT Training with KC Calibration (Module 3):** Freezing the learned embeddings during KT training resulted in a small AUC drop (82.13% to 81.26%) compared to models that fine-tune embeddings, but still outperformed ID-based baselines (78.33% AUC). Crucially, KC calibration reduced the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for direct KC-level knowledge state prediction from 0.08 to 0.028, while also boosting overall AUC to 81.65%. This validates the framework's ability to efficiently and accurately estimate KC mastery.

Overall, the results indicate that ExRec, particularly with its model-based value estimation, offers a robust, efficient, and semantically-aware approach to personalized exercise recommendation, demonstrating clear advantages over baseline and un-augmented RL methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ExRec framework presents a comprehensive approach to personalized exercise recommendation, addressing several limitations identified in prior research. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

- **Holistic Solution to Personalized Education:** The framework provides an end-to-end pipeline, encompassing automated KC annotation, semantic representation learning, calibrated knowledge tracing, and a flexible RL environment. This integrated approach offers a more complete solution compared to methods that focus on isolated components, thereby advancing the field of adaptive learning systems.
- **Integration of Semantic Content:** By learning semantically-grounded representations of questions and KCs, ExRec moves beyond ID-based approaches. This enables the system to understand the conceptual relationships between exercises and knowledge domains, which is crucial for more intelligent and contextually relevant recommendations. The ability to generalize to new, unseen questions (as demonstrated in the extended corpus experiment) is a direct benefit of this semantic understanding, critical for dynamic learning platforms with evolving content.
- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** The design of compact student state representations and efficient, direct knowledge state estimation for reward computation significantly enhances the computational efficiency of the system. This addresses a major bottleneck in real-time personalized recommendations, making the framework more scalable for large-scale online learning platforms.
- **Enhanced Reinforcement Learning Performance:** The introduction of Model-Based Value Estimation (MVE) for continuous Q-learning-based RL methods represents a methodological contribution. MVE leverages the inherent structure of the KT environment to provide more stable and accurate value function estimates, leading to improved policy learning. Its consistent performance boost, particularly in dynamic and complex tasks like targeting the weakest KC, indicates a more robust and adaptable RL agent. This can lead to more effective long-term learning strategies than purely model-free approaches.
- **Flexibility and Algorithm Compatibility:** ExRec's design supports a wide range of RL algorithms, allowing researchers and practitioners to explore and deploy different policy learning strategies. This flexibility contributes to the adaptability of the framework across diverse educational objectives and platform constraints.
- **Interpretability of Learning Trajectories:** The framework facilitates the visualization of conceptual growth, providing insights into how a student's knowledge evolves over time and across different KCs. This interpretability can be valuable for educators to monitor student progress, identify learning gaps, and tailor interventions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By open-sourcing the pipeline, the authors provide a benchmark and a foundation for subsequent research in personalized education. Future work could build upon ExRec by exploring more advanced KT models, incorporating difficulty-aware question representations, or integrating multi-modal learning content to further enrich personalization capabilities.

In summary, ExRec contributes to the development of more effective, scalable, and adaptable personalized exercise recommendation systems. Its methodology provides advancements in integrating semantic understanding, improving computational efficiency, and enhancing the learning capabilities of RL agents in educational contexts, with potential positive implications for student learning outcomes in online environments.
