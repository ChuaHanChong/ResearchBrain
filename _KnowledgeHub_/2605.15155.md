# Self-Distilled Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Zhejiang University, Meituan, and Tsinghua University developed Self-Distilled Agentic Reinforcement Learning (SDAR), a method that robustly integrates on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) with reinforcement learning for multi-turn Large Language Model agents. This approach utilizes a token-level gating mechanism to selectively apply teacher guidance, achieving up to a 9.4% improvement in success rates on ALFWorld and outperforming other hybrid methods while effectively internalizing skills without needing them at inference.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) provides coarse, trajectory-level feedback, making it inefficient for long-horizon, multi-turn LLM agent tasks with sparse rewards.
- Naive on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) suffers from multi-turn instability due to compounding errors and an "asymmetric trust" issue where privileged teacher guidance can be unreliable (e.g., negative signals from imperfect skill retrieval can be detrimental).
- Existing hybrid RL-distillation methods often rely on rigid schedules or unstable updates, struggling to combine task-level optimization with dense token-level guidance robustly in multi-turn interactive settings.

## Method

- SDAR treats reinforcement learning (specifically GRPO) as the primary optimization backbone and integrates OPSD as a controlled, auxiliary objective, guided by a mixing coefficient.
- A token-level gating mechanism is introduced, which computes a trust weight `g_t` for each student-sampled token by composing the teacher-student log-probability gap `∆_t` (or student entropy `h_t`) with a sigmoid function, effectively filtering unreliable signals.
- The gate `g_t` is detached via a stop-gradient operator to ensure it acts solely as a confidence weight, modulating the OPSD signal without introducing self-referential instabilities in the gradients.

## Results

- SDAR consistently improved success rates across ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA benchmarks for Qwen2.5 (3B, 7B) and Qwen3 (1.7B) models, with up to +9.4% on ALFWorld compared to pure GRPO.
- The method demonstrated effective skill internalization, often surpassing skill-augmented baselines during inference without requiring external skills, indicating robust knowledge transfer.
- SDAR exhibited strong generalization, performing particularly well with smaller models (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B on ALFWorld, 53.9%) and showed robustness to varying skill retrieval qualities, including random retrieval, due to its adaptive gating mechanism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The privileged teacher's guidance in OPSD is often asymmetric; positive endorsements are valuable, but 'negative rejections' from lower-probability tokens can be misleading due to imperfect skill retrieval or multi-turn drift.
> - Dynamically gating token-level distillation based on the teacher-student log-probability gap or student uncertainty allows for adaptive knowledge transfer, prioritizing beneficial signals while suppressing potentially harmful ones.
> - Maintaining RL as the primary optimization objective, with OPSD providing a carefully calibrated, gated auxiliary signal, is crucial for stable and effective training of multi-turn LLM agents.
