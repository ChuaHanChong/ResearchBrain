# Streaming Diffusion Policy: Fast Policy Synthesis with Variable Noise Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Norwegian University of Science and Technology and Harvard University developed the Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP), a method designed to accelerate the inference of diffusion-based robotic policies without relying on distillation. This approach leverages variable noise levels and an action buffer, achieving a 0.85 success rate in a real-world Push-T task with a sampling time of 0.07 seconds per action chunk, surpassing existing diffusion policies.

## Problem

- Diffusion models, despite their capabilities in visuomotor control, are computationally slow due to their iterative denoising process, restricting real-time robotic applications.
- Current diffusion policies often generate a full action trajectory but utilize only the initial actions, leading to inefficient use of computational resources.
- Existing acceleration methods like distillation frequently introduce high training costs, instability, or compromise the quality and desirable properties of generated actions.

## Method

- The **Streaming Diffusion Policy** (SDP) generates partially denoised action trajectories with variable noise levels and maintains a persistent action buffer for future actions.
- It employs a recursive sampling procedure that performs only a fraction of the total denoising steps per control iteration (N/h steps), achieving an h-fold speedup by selectively denoising the immediate action chunk.
- The policy is trained using specific trajectory noise corruption schemes, such as a combination of '80% Chunk-wise 20% Constant' variance, to align with the streaming inference process.

## Results

- SDP achieves comparable or marginally superior performance to Diffusion Policy and generally surpasses Consistency Policy across various simulated robotic tasks (e.g., Push-T, Lift, Can, Square, Transport).
- In real-world Push-T experiments, SDP demonstrates a **0.85** success rate, exceeding Diffusion Policy (**0.50**) and Consistency Policy (**0.40**).
- The method provides substantial inference acceleration, achieving a real-world sampling time of **0.07** seconds per action chunk, which is faster than Diffusion Policy (**0.13** seconds) and exhibits greater robustness with reduced oscillatory movements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A robot requires a noise-free immediate action for execution, but subsequent actions in a planned trajectory can remain partially noisy and be refined as they become immediate.
> - Reusing partially denoised future actions from a persistent buffer and recursively updating them drastically reduces the computational load per control cycle.
> - Optimizing the training noise distribution to match the streaming inference's chunk-wise variable noise levels is crucial for maintaining high policy performance during acceleration.
