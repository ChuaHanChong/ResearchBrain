# Regularizing Action Policies for Smooth Control with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CAPS introduces a two-part regularization on RL action policies — temporal smoothness (consecutive actions stay close) and spatial smoothness (similar states yield similar actions). On a real quadrotor, training with CAPS produced an almost 80% reduction in power consumption and a 96% improvement in policy smoothness compared to a state-of-the-art controller, while still training flight-worthy agents under a simpler reward structure.

## Problem

- Deep RL-trained controllers often produce oscillatory, high-frequency control signals that cause hardware wear, high power consumption, and even component failure.
- Standard RL formulations have no inherent mechanism to enforce smoothness; addressing it through reward engineering alone is tedious, indirect, and offers no guarantee of the desired behavior.
- Sim-to-real transfer accentuates oscillatory failure modes; post-hoc filtering of NN-controller outputs behaves inconsistently across networks, so smoothness must be enforced during training.

## Method

- Proposes **CAPS** (Conditioning for Action Policy Smoothness), an action-policy regularizer added directly to the RL objective rather than encoded in the reward.
- ==Temporal smoothness== loss `L_T = D(π(s_t), π(s_{t+1}))` penalizes consecutive-step action divergence; ==spatial smoothness== loss `L_S = D(π(s), π(s̃))` with `s̃ ∼ N(s, σ)` penalizes action divergence under small state perturbations.
- Training objective `J^CAPS = J_RL − λ_T · L_T − λ_S · L_S`; ==L1 or L2 distance== for `D`, with ==Gaussian state perturbations==. Architecture-agnostic, plugs into any continuous-control RL algorithm (PPO, SAC, DDPG).

## Results

- On a real quadrotor: **80%** reduction in power consumption and **96%** improvement in policy smoothness compared to a state-of-the-art controller, with a simpler reward.
- Reduced training time and consistently produced flight-worthy controllers across runs.
- Marginally worse raw training rewards in some cases (controllers more conservative), but never at the cost of learnability or deployment utility — verified on OpenAI continuous-control benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Smoothness is better enforced as a direct policy regularizer than as a reward shaping term — direct optimization of action consistency is more reliable than indirect reward engineering.
> - Two complementary signals (temporal across time, spatial across nearby states) cover the main sources of oscillation in continuous control.
> - The simplicity and explainability of CAPS make hyperparameter tuning tractable — `λ_T` and `λ_S` have intuitive interpretations and minimal interaction with task reward.
