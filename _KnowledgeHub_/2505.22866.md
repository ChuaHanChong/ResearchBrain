# Scaling Offline RL via Efficient and Expressive Shortcut Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents Scalable Offline Reinforcement Learning (SORL), an algorithm that enables offline RL policies to dynamically adjust their computational budget during inference. The method integrates 'shortcut models' into a unified training procedure, allowing for flexible speed-accuracy trade-offs and competitive performance across various offline RL tasks.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning (RL) struggles to efficiently leverage expressive generative models, such as diffusion models, for policy learning.
- Traditional methods using generative models face challenges with slow training (due to expensive backpropagation through time) and inference bottlenecks (requiring many denoising steps).
- Existing offline RL algorithms lack the flexibility to dynamically adjust computational resources (fixed compute budgets) for policy inference based on task requirements.

## Method

- Introduces Scalable Offline Reinforcement Learning (**SORL**), a single-stage training algorithm that unifies actor-critic learning with '==shortcut models==' for policy generation.
- The policy network is parameterized by shortcut functions that can operate at different step sizes, allowing for variable numbers of inference steps at test time.
- Employs a self-consistency mechanism and a combined objective function (Q-loss, flow matching loss, self-consistency loss) to ensure efficient training and coherent behavior across different compute budgets.

## Results

- SORL demonstrated superior performance on 5 out of 8 OGBench environments, including substantial improvements on challenging tasks like antmaze-large and antsoccer-arena.
- Performance consistently improved with increased inference steps, validating the sequential scaling capability across various environments.
- The method successfully generalized to more inference steps than used during training and could compensate for reduced training-time compute by utilizing more inference-time resources.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shortcut models, by learning to take adaptive step sizes, can generate high-quality actions with variable inference budgets while maintaining consistency across different computational scales.
> - A self-consistency loss ensures the policy's behavior is coherent across different step sizes, enabling generalization to more inference steps than seen during training.
> - The training objective acts as a Wasserstein behavioral regularization, providing stronger guarantees against out-of-distribution actions compared to typical KL-divergence methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a crucial paradigm for training intelligent agents using pre-collected datasets, avoiding the need for costly or dangerous online exploration. However, a fundamental challenge persists: how to effectively leverage expressive generative models like diffusion models while maintaining computational efficiency during both training and inference.

This paper introduces Scalable Offline Reinforcement Learning (SORL), an algorithm that addresses the expressivity-efficiency dilemma by employing "shortcut models" - a novel class of generative models that can adaptively adjust their computational budget at inference time. The key innovation lies in enabling policies to scale their performance with available compute resources, similar to recent advances in large language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Traditional offline RL methods often rely on Gaussian-based policy classes, which struggle to model the complex, multi-modal action distributions present in large, diverse datasets. While generative models like diffusion models can capture such distributions, they face significant challenges:

1. **Training Inefficiency**: Iterative sampling processes require expensive backpropagation through time (BTT)
2. **Inference Bottlenecks**: Multiple denoising steps make real-time decision-making impractical
3. **Fixed Compute Budgets**: Existing methods cannot dynamically adjust computational resources based on task requirements

Current approaches typically fall into two categories:
- **Distillation-based methods** (FQL, IFQL, SRPO): Fast inference but potential performance degradation
- **Direct generative models** (IDQL, CAC, FBRAC): High expressivity but slow inference

SORL bridges this gap by introducing flexibility in compute allocation across training and inference phases.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The SORL Algorithm

SORL operates as a unified, single-stage training procedure that combines actor-critic learning with shortcut model training. The algorithm maintains three key components:

1. **Policy Network**: Parameterized by shortcut functions that can operate at different step sizes
2. **Q-function**: Standard critic for value estimation
3. **Self-consistency mechanism**: Ensures coherent behavior across different inference budgets

The core innovation lies in the policy's ability to generate actions using variable numbers of inference steps while maintaining consistency across different computational budgets.

#### 3.2. Shortcut Models for Policy Learning

Shortcut models extend flow matching by learning to take larger steps along trajectory paths. Unlike traditional flow models that operate with fixed step sizes, shortcut models condition their predictions on both the current state and a step size parameter `h`.

The key mathematical insight is the self-consistency property: one large step of size `2h` should be equivalent to two consecutive steps of size `h`. This property enables the model to generate high-quality samples under any inference budget.

For policy learning, SORL models the policy π_θ(·|x,m) using a shortcut function s_θ that predicts normalized action directions. Actions are sampled via the Euler method, where the number of inference steps M_inf can be varied at test time.

#### 3.3. Training Procedure

The SORL training procedure optimizes a combined objective function:

**Actor Loss**: L_π(θ) = L_QL(θ) + L_FM(θ) + L_SC(θ)

- **Q-Loss (L_QL)**: Maximizes Q-values for policy-generated actions
- **Flow Matching Loss (L_FM)**: Provides behavioral regularization to stay close to offline data
- **Self-Consistency Loss (L_SC)**: Ensures consistent behavior across different step sizes

**Critic Loss**: Standard Bellman error minimization for Q-function training

The algorithm samples actions using up to M_BTT backpropagation steps during training, where M_BTT is kept small (1, 2, 4, 8) to maintain computational efficiency. This contrasts with full diffusion models that may require hundreds of steps.

#### 3.4. Inference Time Scaling

SORL provides two mechanisms for scaling inference performance:

#### 3.5. Sequential Scaling

The policy can generate actions using any number of inference steps M_inf, potentially different from the training budget M_BTT. More steps generally lead to higher-quality actions, enabling dynamic speed-accuracy trade-offs.

#### 3.6. Parallel Scaling (Best-of-N Sampling)

For critical decisions, SORL can sample multiple actions independently and use the learned Q-function as a verifier to select the highest-valued action. This leverages parallel compute resources to improve action selection quality.

#### 3.7. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical guarantees showing that SORL's training objective acts as a Wasserstein behavioral regularization. Specifically, the combination of flow matching and self-consistency losses ensures the learned policy remains close to the behavior policy in 2-Wasserstein distance.

This theoretical result is stronger than typical KL-divergence regularization and provides principled guarantees against out-of-distribution actions - a critical safety concern in offline RL applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SORL was evaluated on 40 tasks across 8 OGBench environments, demonstrating:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- Superior performance on 5 out of 8 environments
- Substantial improvements on challenging tasks like `antmaze-large` and `antsoccer-arena`
- Consistent outperformance of distillation-based baselines

#### 4.2. Sequential Scaling Validation

Performance generally improved with increased inference steps, confirming the sequential scaling capability across multiple environments.

#### 4.3. Compute Flexibility

A key finding is SORL's ability to compensate for reduced training-time compute by utilizing more inference-time resources. This flexibility allows practitioners to optimize for their specific computational constraints.

#### 4.4. Generalization Beyond Training

SORL successfully generalizes to more inference steps than used during training, indicating robust learning of the shortcut model properties across different scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SORL represents a significant advancement in offline RL by successfully bridging the expressivity-efficiency gap. The algorithm's ability to dynamically adjust computational budgets makes it particularly valuable for real-world applications where compute availability varies.

Key contributions include:
- **Unified Framework**: Single-stage training avoiding distillation complexities
- **Flexible Scaling**: Both sequential and parallel inference-time scaling
- **Theoretical Grounding**: Wasserstein regularization guarantees
- **Practical Efficiency**: Competitive runtime with state-of-the-art baselines

The work opens several promising research directions, including adaptive test-time scaling strategies and integration with online fine-tuning procedures. The successful application of LLM-inspired scaling techniques to RL also suggests potential for cross-domain methodological transfer.
