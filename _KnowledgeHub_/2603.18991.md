# CRAFT: Aligning Diffusion Models with Fine-Tuning Is Easier Than You Think

> [!summary] Summary
> CRAFT introduces a framework for aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences, achieving superior generation quality and prompt fidelity using a self-curated dataset of just 100 samples. The method significantly reduces training time, making it up to 220 times faster than existing preference optimization techniques.

## Problem

- Text-to-image diffusion models, pre-trained on vast web datasets, often misalign with nuanced human preferences for aesthetics and complex instructions.
- Existing alignment methods (SFT) are limited by the scarcity and high cost of pristine, high-quality image datasets.
- DPO-style preference optimization methods incur substantial computational overhead due to large data requirements or repeated online sampling.

## Method

- **CRAFT** employs a ==Composite Reward Filtering== (CRF) strategy to automatically construct a high-quality, compact training dataset by leveraging the base model's own generations and evaluating them with multiple reward models (HPS, PickScore, AES).
- It utilizes a weighted Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) objective, where a theoretically derived advantage function from group-based reinforcement learning guides the model toward generating preferred samples.
- The approach involves full-parameter fine-tuning of the UNet part of diffusion models on the small, high-quality filtered dataset, with optimized training schedules for efficiency.

## Results

- CRAFT achieved superior human preference alignment, obtaining the highest HPSv2.1 scores across all test sets for both SD1.5 and SDXL, and an average winning rate over **85.7%** in human evaluations.
- The framework dramatically reduced computational costs, requiring only 4.0 GPU hours for SDXL (**15.8x** faster than SPO) and 1.9 GPU hours for SD1.5 (**11.6x** faster than SPO), using just 100 training samples.
- CRAFT-finetuned models generated images with enhanced qualitative attributes, including improved complex instruction following, accurate text rendering, better stylistic interpretation, and stronger compositional reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing a group-based reinforcement learning objective is theoretically proven to be equivalent to optimizing a lower bound of the expected reward via a weighted SFT loss, providing a principled connection.
> - The quality and precise selection of a small dataset, rather than its quantity, is paramount for effective preference alignment, as demonstrated by the efficacy of filtering down to 100 samples.
> - A composite reward function, combining HPS v2.1, PickScore, and AES, is crucial for robustly identifying and filtering high-quality images that align with diverse human preferences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models have significantly advanced image generation capabilities, producing outputs with high visual fidelity and strong text-image alignment. Models such as DALL-E, Imagen, and Stable Diffusion have become prominent in this field. However, these models are typically pre-trained on vast, web-sourced datasets like LAION-5B, which, despite their scale, can be noisy and may not consistently align with nuanced human preferences for aesthetics or complex instruction-following. This misalignment necessitates post-training techniques to fine-tune diffusion models for human preference alignment.

The current landscape of post-training approaches for diffusion models largely draws inspiration from advancements in large language models (LLMs), particularly Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). SFT methods for diffusion models typically rely on carefully curated, high-quality text-image pairs. While computationally efficient, this approach is constrained by the scarcity and high cost of obtaining such pristine datasets.

Conversely, DPO-style methods, exemplified by Diffusion-DPO, SmPO, and SPO, learn directly from large-scale preference pairs. These methods can achieve robust alignment but often face two significant challenges:
1. **Data Dependency:** They either require extensive, high-quality preference datasets, which can be inconsistent in quality and difficult to scale, or they generate preference pairs dynamically, which leads to computational inefficiency.
2. **Computational Cost:** Online methods, like SPO, involve immense computational overhead due to repeated online sampling and evaluation during the denoising process. Offline DPO-style methods, while avoiding online sampling, still rely on large datasets, contributing to substantial training times.

The research presented in this paper, CRAFT, aims to address this trade-off between data requirements and computational cost. It positions itself as a data-efficient and computationally lightweight fine-tuning paradigm. By proposing a method that requires significantly less training data and converges faster than existing preference optimization techniques, CRAFT seeks to make human preference alignment of diffusion models more accessible and practical, particularly for scenarios with limited data and computational resources. This work contributes to the ongoing efforts to enhance the human-centric alignment of generative AI models, moving towards outputs that better reflect user intentions and aesthetic standards.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective fine-tuning paradigm for text-to-image diffusion models that enhances their alignment with human preferences. This objective is driven by two main challenges identified in current alignment methodologies:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality Inconsistency:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) techniques, while computationally economical, are fundamentally limited by their reliance on exceptionally high-quality image datasets. Such datasets are expensive to acquire and curate, making widespread application challenging. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)-style methods, which emerged from the success in Large Language Models (LLMs), adapt by learning from large-scale preference pairs. However, these preference datasets, although large, often exhibit inconsistencies in quality, which can hinder optimal model alignment. The authors identified a need for a method that could circumvent the dependency on vast, externally curated, or perfectly consistent datasets.
 - **CRAFT's Approach:** To tackle this, CRAFT proposes a self-curated training paradigm. It introduces a **Composite Reward Filtering (CRF)** technique designed to automatically construct a high-quality and internally consistent training dataset. This process avoids reliance on external high-quality datasets or data distilled from stronger models, instead leveraging the base model's own generations and a composite reward function to identify superior samples.

2. **Improving Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Motivation:** Many advanced alignment methods, particularly online Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based approaches like SPO, incur substantial computational overhead. This is due to the necessity of repeated online sampling and evaluation at each denoising step, making the fine-tuning process time-consuming and resource-intensive. Offline DPO-style methods, while avoiding real-time sampling, still process large datasets, contributing to significant GPU hours. The authors recognized that the high computational cost acts as a barrier to wider adoption and faster iteration in model development.
 - **CRAFT's Approach:** CRAFT aims to be computationally lightweight. By reducing the reliance on large-scale preference datasets through its filtering mechanism and by employing an enhanced variant of SFT, it seeks to achieve faster convergence and lower GPU hour consumption compared to prevalent preference optimization methods. The theoretical underpinnings also contribute to efficiency by providing a principled connection between SFT with selected data and reinforcement learning, allowing for a more targeted optimization.

In addition to these practical motivations, the research also aims to provide a theoretical foundation for its approach. A key objective is to **theoretically prove that CRAFT optimizes a lower bound of group-based reinforcement learning**. This establishes a principled connection between a data-selected SFT approach and reinforcement learning, providing a deeper understanding of why fine-tuning with carefully filtered data can be an effective strategy for preference alignment. The overall motivation is to offer a fine-tuning framework that is both data-efficient and computationally economical, while also being theoretically sound.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRAFT (Composite Reward Assisted Fine-Tuning) is a framework designed to align diffusion models efficiently. Its methodology primarily consists of three interconnected stages: data construction, composite reward filtering, and weighted Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

#### 3.1. Formulation and Theoretical Insights

The theoretical foundation of CRAFT begins by framing the objective within a reinforcement learning context. The goal is to optimize the expected reward `J(θ) = E_c~D_c, x_0~p_θ(·|c) [r(c, x_0)]`, where `p_θ(x_0|c)` is the diffusion model, `c` is the prompt, and `x_0` is the clean image. This objective is approximated using a Monte Carlo method and group-based fine-tuning:

`J_hat(θ) = E_c~D_c, {x_0^(i)}_G_i=1~p_θ_old(·|c) [ (1/G) * Σ_i=1^G (p_θ(x_0^(i)|c) / p_θ_old(x_0^(i)|c)) * A_hat_i ]`

Here, `A_hat_i` represents the normalized advantage for each image `x_0^(i)` within a group of `G` images generated for a given prompt `c`. The advantage is calculated as `(r(c, x_0^(i)) - mean_group_rewards) / (std_group_rewards + ε)`. This formulation ensures that the sum of advantages within a group is zero, focusing on relative quality.

**Theorem 3.1** establishes a theoretical connection between this group-based RL objective and SFT. It demonstrates that, for a small learning rate, optimizing `J_hat(θ)` is equivalent to optimizing a lower bound of `J(θ)` through a loss function structurally similar to a weighted SFT loss:

`L(θ) := Σ_i (A_hat_i * ||ε_θ(x_t^(i), t, c) - ε_t^(i)||^2)`

This theorem suggests that the quality of the data (represented by `A_hat_i`) directly influences the magnitude of the gradient in an SFT-like objective, encouraging the model to generate high-quality images while minimizing the generation of low-quality ones. This provides a principled bridge between SFT with selected data and reinforcement learning.

#### 3.2. Composite Reward Filtering (CRF)

The CRF strategy is crucial for constructing a high-quality, compact training dataset.

1. **Data Construction:**
 - **Prompt Curation:** The process starts by randomly sampling `n = 10,000` original prompts from the HPD dataset.
 - **Prompt Refinement:** Each original prompt `c_i^(0)` is refined `N` times (e.g., `N=4`) using a large language model (Qwen-Plus) to generate `N` more descriptive or varied versions (`c_i^(1)..., c_i^(N)`). This creates a set of `n(N+1)` prompts (original + refined).
 - **Image Generation:** The base diffusion model `p_θ(x_0|c)` is then used to generate images for *all* `n(N+1)` prompts. This creates a large pool of candidate images `I`.

2. **Data Filtering:**
 - **Reward Model Application:** Three distinct reward models are employed to evaluate the generated images: HPS v2.1 (human preference), PickScore (human preference), and AES (aesthetic score). For each image `x_0^(i,j)` generated from prompt `c_i^(j)`, its rewards `r_ξ(c_i^(0), x_0^(i,j))` are computed *using the original prompt* `c_i^(0)`. The refined prompts `c_i^(j)` (for `j > 0`) are solely used to enhance the diversity of generated images, but evaluation is anchored to the original prompt.
 - **Filtering Strategies:** Several filtering strategies are defined based on comparing the rewards of images generated from refined prompts against those from the original prompt:
 - `I_ξ`: Retains a batch of generated samples if at least one refined prompt produces an image with a higher reward than the original image for a single reward model `ξ` (e.g., HPS, PickScore, or AES).
 - `I_ha`, `I_pa`: Retains a batch if images from refined prompts simultaneously yield higher rewards for two specific reward models (e.g., HPS and AES).
 - `I_hpa`: This is the strictest rule, retaining a batch only if images from refined prompts simultaneously yield higher rewards than the original for *all three* reward models (HPS, PickScore, and AES). This strategy is used for the final training dataset in the experiments.

#### 3.3. Composite Reward Assisted Fine-Tuning Algorithm

Algorithm 1 outlines the CRAFT fine-tuning process:

1. **Dataset Input:** The pre-trained diffusion model, original and refined prompt sets, the refined image dataset `I`, the three reward functions, and the chosen reward filtering rule (`~I` e.g., `I_hpa`) are provided.
2. **Iterative Training:** The model trains in batches.
3. **Group-based Advantage Calculation:** For each small batch of data (selected from `~I`), the algorithm calculates the group-based advantage `A_hat^(i,j)` for each image `x_0^(i,j)`. This calculation uses a weighted sum `r_total^(i,j)` of HPS, PickScore, and AES scores (with weights 0.4, 0.4, and 0.2 respectively).
4. **Weighted-SFT Loss Computation:** The training loss `L(θ)` is computed as a weighted SFT loss, where `A_hat^(i,j)` acts as a weighting factor for the denoising error `||ε_θ(x_t^(i,j), t, c_i^(0)) - ε_t^(i,j)||^2`. The indicator function `I(x_0^(i,j) ∈ ~I)` ensures that only the filtered, high-quality data contributes to the gradient.
5. **Optimization:** The model parameters are updated using the AdamW optimizer.
6. **Full-parameter fine-tuning** is performed on the UNet part of the diffusion models.
7. **Training Schedules:** SD1.5 models are trained for 120 steps, and SDXL models for 200 steps, with an effective batch size of 128 and a learning rate of 5e-5. All experiments are conducted on 8 H100 GPUs.

By combining theoretical grounding, intelligent data curation via composite reward filtering, and a weighted SFT objective, CRAFT aims to achieve efficient and effective preference alignment using significantly fewer training samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive empirical results demonstrating the efficiency and effectiveness of CRAFT across various metrics and diffusion models (SD1.5 and SDXL).

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (Figures 2, 4, 6, 8) indicate that CRAFT-finetuned models generate images with superior quality and prompt fidelity compared to baseline models (Vanilla-SDXL, Diff-DPO, SPO, MaPO). CRAFT-SDXL demonstrated improvements in:
- **Complex Instruction Following:** Excels at interpreting and executing intricate prompts.
- **Text Rendering:** Accurately renders specified text (e.g., "CRAFT" in Figure 4).
- **Stylistic Interpretation:** Produces images that better capture requested aesthetics (e.g., "Unreal Engine" style).
- **Compositional Reasoning:** Correctly interprets spatial relationships (e.g., "in front of") and adheres to object attributes (e.g., "white bottle and blue sheep") and object counting (e.g., "four broccolis").
- **Control Fidelity:** When integrated with ControlNet for conditions like Canny and Depth maps, CRAFT maintains strong geometric control and generates visually superior images, indicating robustness.

#### 4.2. Computational Costs

CRAFT demonstrated a significant reduction in computational resources required for fine-tuning:
- **Data Efficiency:** CRAFT achieves superior alignment with a training dataset of only 100 samples, whereas baselines typically utilize thousands (SPO: 4,000) to hundreds of thousands (Diff-DPO, MaPO, SmPO, Diff-KTO: 851,293 to 928,068) of preference-paired samples.
- **Training Speed:**
 - For SDXL, CRAFT required approximately 4.0 GPU hours. This is 15.8 times faster than SPO (63.0 GPU hours) and up to 159.5 times faster than Diff-DPO (638.1 GPU hours).
 - For SD1.5, CRAFT required approximately 1.9 GPU hours. This is 11.6 times faster than SPO (22.0 GPU hours) and up to 218.7 times faster than Diff-KTO (415.6 GPU hours).
- **End-to-End Efficiency:** Even when including the costs for candidate generation and reward-based filtering (preprocessing), CRAFT's total H100 hours (e.g., ~24.1 hours for SDXL) remain substantially lower than most baseline methods, which often do not include their data curation costs.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results

CRAFT consistently surpassed baselines across various quantitative benchmarks and metrics:
- **Human Preference Alignment (HPS v2.1):** CRAFT achieved the highest HPSv2.1 scores on all three test sets (HPDv2, Parti-Prompt, Pick-a-Pic) for both SDXL and SD1.5. For instance, on HPDv2 with SDXL, CRAFT scored 32.67, outperforming SPO (32.32) and SmPO (31.40).
- **Generation Quality (ImageReward, MPS):** CRAFT demonstrated leading performance on ImageReward (IR) and MPS scores, which were unseen during training. This indicates robust generalization beyond the training rewards. For example, on HPDv2 with SDXL, CRAFT achieved an IR score of 1.312 and an MPS score of 15.62, the highest among all compared methods.
- **Aesthetic Appeal (AES Score):** CRAFT generally achieved high AES scores, indicating visually attractive outputs.
- **Winning Rate:** In human evaluation on the Parti-Prompt dataset (Figure 5), CRAFT exhibited a dominant average winning rate of over 85.7% against the base SDXL model, confirming its superior alignment with human preferences.
- **Compositional Reasoning (GenEval):** CRAFT achieved the best or second-best performance across most evaluation categories on the GenEval benchmark for SD1.5 (Table 4) and the highest "Overall" score for SDXL (Table 12), particularly excelling in object presence and spatial fidelity. Improvements were also noted in T2I-CompBench for attributes and object relations (Table 11).

#### 4.4. Ablation and Analysis

- **Data Selection Strategy (Table 5):** An ablation study confirmed the efficacy of the Composite Reward Filtering. Training on the "Top 100" highest-scoring samples (less than 5% of the initial filtered pool) achieved the highest HPS, AES, and ImageReward scores, even with significantly fewer training steps (200 steps) compared to strategies using larger datasets (e.g., "All" 2,312 samples trained for 1,000 steps). This underscores the importance of high-quality data over quantity.
- **Composite Reward Models (Table 6):** Analysis of reward model combinations revealed that using a composite reward function with specific weights (HPS: 0.4, PickScore: 0.4, AES: 0.2) yielded the best performance. PickScore was identified as a particularly crucial individual component, outperforming HPS or AES alone, and even the combination of HPS and AES.
- **Advantage Weighting:** A comparison against vanilla SFT on the same "Top-100" filtered dataset demonstrated that the advantage weighting mechanism in CRAFT contributes clear performance gains, especially for HPS and ImageReward, confirming that improvements are not solely due to data filtering.

In summary, the findings indicate that CRAFT significantly advances the state-of-the-art in diffusion model alignment by achieving superior qualitative and quantitative performance with dramatically reduced data and computational requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CRAFT framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of generative AI, particularly in the domain of text-to-image diffusion models.

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Computational Bottlenecks:** The most direct impact of CRAFT is its ability to achieve robust human preference alignment with a minimal dataset (as few as 100 samples) and substantially reduced computational overhead (up to 220x faster training compared to baselines). This directly addresses two long-standing challenges in diffusion model fine-tuning: the high cost and scarcity of high-quality preference data, and the immense computational demands of existing methods. This efficiency can accelerate research and development cycles, allowing for quicker iteration and exploration of new models and applications.

2. **Democratization of Alignment Techniques:** By significantly lowering the data and computational barriers, CRAFT has the potential to democratize access to advanced alignment techniques. Smaller research groups, individual practitioners, and developers with limited computational resources may now fine-tune powerful diffusion models to their specific preferences without requiring access to supercomputing clusters or massive proprietary datasets. This can foster innovation and broaden the adoption of preference-aligned generative AI.

3. **Theoretical Foundation for Data Selection:** The theoretical proof that CRAFT optimizes a lower bound of group-based reinforcement learning provides a principled connection between supervised fine-tuning with selected data and reinforcement learning. This theoretical grounding offers a deeper understanding of why meticulously curated, small datasets can be highly effective, moving beyond purely empirical observations. This insight could inspire new research into data-centric AI and the interplay between data quality, selection, and model learning dynamics.

4. **Enhanced Model Capabilities and Generalization:** Empirically, CRAFT-finetuned models demonstrate superior performance across various metrics (HPSv2.1, ImageReward, MPS, AES) and benchmarks (HPDv2, Parti-Prompt, Pick-a-Pic, GenEval). Crucially, its strong performance on unseen metrics (ImageReward, MPS) indicates robust generalization of learned human preferences rather than overfitting to specific training rewards. The improved compositional reasoning, stylistic interpretation, and text rendering capabilities lead to more controllable and reliable image generation, which is valuable for diverse applications.

5. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines future research avenues that build upon CRAFT's foundation. These include evolving CRAFT into an online learning framework for continuous adaptation, enriching the filtering stage with adaptive and noise-aware selection criteria, and extending its application to other generative modalities like text-to-video (T2V) and text-to-3D (T23D). These proposed directions indicate that CRAFT is not merely a terminal solution but a foundational step that can inspire further advancements in efficient and effective generative model alignment.

In conclusion, CRAFT offers a practical, efficient, and theoretically-backed approach to aligning diffusion models with human preferences. Its ability to achieve high-quality results with significantly fewer resources represents a notable step towards making advanced generative AI more accessible and adaptable for a wider range of users and applications.
