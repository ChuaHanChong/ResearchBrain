# CRAFT: Aligning Diffusion Models with Fine-Tuning Is Easier Than You Think

> [!summary] Summary
> CRAFT introduces a framework for aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences, achieving superior generation quality and prompt fidelity using a self-curated dataset of just 100 samples. The method significantly reduces training time, making it up to 220 times faster than existing preference optimization techniques.

## Problem

- Text-to-image diffusion models, pre-trained on vast web datasets, often misalign with nuanced human preferences for aesthetics and complex instructions.
- Existing alignment methods (SFT) are limited by the scarcity and high cost of pristine, high-quality image datasets.
- DPO-style preference optimization methods incur substantial computational overhead due to large data requirements or repeated online sampling.

## Method

- **CRAFT** employs a ==Composite Reward Filtering== (CRF) strategy to automatically construct a high-quality, compact training dataset by leveraging the base model's own generations and evaluating them with multiple reward models (HPS, PickScore, AES).
- It utilizes a weighted Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) objective, where a theoretically derived advantage function from group-based reinforcement learning guides the model toward generating preferred samples.
- The approach involves full-parameter fine-tuning of the UNet part of diffusion models on the small, high-quality filtered dataset, with optimized training schedules for efficiency.

## Results

- CRAFT achieved superior human preference alignment, obtaining the highest HPSv2.1 scores across all test sets for both SD1.5 and SDXL, and an average winning rate over **85.7%** in human evaluations.
- The framework dramatically reduced computational costs, requiring only 4.0 GPU hours for SDXL (**15.8x** faster than SPO) and 1.9 GPU hours for SD1.5 (**11.6x** faster than SPO), using just 100 training samples.
- CRAFT-finetuned models generated images with enhanced qualitative attributes, including improved complex instruction following, accurate text rendering, better stylistic interpretation, and stronger compositional reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing a group-based reinforcement learning objective is theoretically proven to be equivalent to optimizing a lower bound of the expected reward via a weighted SFT loss, providing a principled connection.
> - The quality and precise selection of a small dataset, rather than its quantity, is paramount for effective preference alignment, as demonstrated by the efficacy of filtering down to 100 samples.
> - A composite reward function, combining HPS v2.1, PickScore, and AES, is crucial for robustly identifying and filtering high-quality images that align with diverse human preferences.
