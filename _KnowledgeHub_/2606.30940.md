# Motion Planning in Compressed Representation Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT LIDS created a generative framework that merges classical planning's flexibility with learning-based methods' realism by enabling objective-guided trajectory generation through search in a highly compressed, hierarchically ordered latent space. The system demonstrates efficient planning for autonomous driving tasks, achieving competitive performance and generating realistic behaviors in single- and multi-agent scenarios.

## Problem

- Classical motion planners offer flexibility with explicit cost functions but struggle with realism in complex, unstructured environments without extensive data priors.
- Learning-based motion planners generate realistic behaviors from data but often lack flexibility for new objectives or constraints at test time without requiring retraining.
- Existing learning-based methods often embed task-specific structures, limiting adaptability to diverse, non-differentiable objectives.

## Method

- A ==conditional autoencoder== learns a highly compressed, discrete, and hierarchically ordered ==latent representation of trajectories==, conditioned on environmental information via a ==transformer== backbone.
- ==Adaptive soft quantization== during training, transitioning to ==hard quantization== at test time, creates a discrete latent space while ==variable-length encoding== with ==nested dropout== and ==causal masking== ensures a coarse-to-fine hierarchy.
- Motion planning is formulated as a ==decode-and-score search== problem (==greedy== or ==beam search== over discrete latent tokens), allowing for optimization of arbitrary, potentially non-differentiable, test-time objectives, with optional ==gradient-based refinement==.

## Results

- The method achieves high expressiveness, with three heavily quantized latent tokens yielding a reconstruction Average Displacement Error (ADE) of **0.386**, which is lower than some state-of-the-art prediction models (e.g., DriveGPT's **0.524** minADE).
- For single-agent maneuver editing, greedy search attained **90.2%** success for left-turn objectives and **99.04%** compliance for non-differentiable lane-change objectives in open-loop WOMD evaluations.
- In closed-loop multi-agent nuPlan scenarios, the planner demonstrates performance comparable to imitation learning baselines for route-following and generates significantly more realistic multi-agent trajectories (meanADE **0.767**) than controllable generation baselines like CTG (**1.947**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extreme compression of trajectories into short sequences of low-dimensional, discrete tokens effectively shifts the burden of realism to the learned decoder, enabling efficient search in the latent space.
> - The learned hierarchical and causally ordered latent space allows greedy search to effectively refine trajectories from coarse to fine detail, outperforming even the learned encoder in reconstruction when guided.
> - Discrete search over latent tokens provides robust test-time control, accommodating non-differentiable objectives and facilitating the transfer of high-level semantic behaviors across environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic motion planning primarily operates within two paradigms: classical model-based approaches and modern learning-based methods. Classical planners, relying on search or optimization, offer flexibility through explicit cost functions and constraints, and can be efficient in controlled environments. However, their performance often degrades in complex, unstructured, or open-ended domains where realistic behavior necessitates rich priors derived from extensive data.

Conversely, learning-based planners leverage large-scale data to acquire these priors, enabling more realistic behavior in complex scenarios. A common limitation of many learning-based planners is that they embed task-specific structures through conditioning, reward shaping during training, or specialized inference procedures. This can restrict their adaptability to new objectives or constraints at test time without requiring retraining.

This work aims to unify these two paradigms by introducing a generative framework that separates the generation of realistic trajectories from test-time control. It builds upon advancements in deep learning for generative models and learned representations.

**Related work discussed in the paper includes:**

- **Test-time guidance for generative models:** Previous research, particularly with diffusion models, often employs gradient-based steering during sampling (e.g., classifier guidance, classifier-free guidance, or general function guidance). This has been applied to trajectory generation in robotics, such as Diffusion Policy and MotionDiffuser. This paper differentiates its approach by using a decode-and-score search mechanism over ordered latent tokens, which inherently supports non-differentiable objectives and direct evaluation of partial trajectories.
- **Tokenization and ordered representations:** Autoencoders for dimensionality reduction are standard in generative modeling, with techniques like VQ-VAE/VQGAN creating discrete latent spaces for images. More recently, nested dropout has enabled learning ordered, variable-length image tokenizations, establishing coarse-to-fine hierarchies. This work extends this concept to trajectory representation, but crucially, it develops an *environment-conditioned* tokenizer, transferring a portion of the conditional modeling burden to the decoder.
- **Training-free generation in tokenizer latent spaces:** Methods involving optimizing latent codes of pretrained generators for specific objectives (e.g., GAN latents with CLIP-based objectives, VQGAN-CLIP) have shown efficacy, particularly for image editing. Some work suggests that highly compressed tokenizers can make latent manipulation effective for generation. The presented research is inspired by the principle that extreme compression can shift generation complexity to the decoder but distinguishes itself by employing tree search over discrete, ordered tokens instead of continuous, gradient-based latent optimization.
- **Planning over latent actions:** Other research uses compact learned latents for control by planning over latent actions with learned dynamics via tree search. While algorithmically related through the use of search over learned discrete representations, these methods focus on latent action planning rather than objective-guided trajectory generation.

This paper positions itself by proposing a novel mechanism for test-time guidance that leverages a highly compressed, environment-conditioned, and hierarchically ordered latent space, which contrasts with iterative gradient-based methods and extends tokenization concepts from image generation to complex robotic motion planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generative framework that unifies the strengths of classical model-based motion planning and modern learning-based approaches, addressing their respective limitations. This framework seeks to achieve a separation between generating realistic, data-driven trajectories and exercising flexible, test-time control over these trajectories.

The specific key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Unifying Planning Paradigms:** The work is motivated by the observation that classical planners offer flexibility through explicit cost functions but struggle with realism in unstructured environments, while learning-based methods provide data-driven realism but often lack flexibility for new objectives or constraints without extensive retraining. The proposed framework aims to integrate the benefits of both by allowing learned models to handle realism and classical-style search to handle objective-driven control.
2. **Achieving High Compression for Decision Space Reduction:** A central objective is to map high-dimensional robot trajectories into short sequences of low-dimensional, discrete latent tokens. This is motivated by the need to drastically reduce the dimensionality of the decision space that a planner must search over, thereby enhancing planning efficiency.
3. **Concentrating Realism in a Decoder:** By compressing trajectories into succinct tokens, the burden of producing realistic and context-appropriate motion is shifted to a learned decoder. This allows the latent space to focus on abstract representations of intent or maneuver classes, while the decoder ensures the physical plausibility and contextual relevance of the reconstructed trajectory.
4. **Enabling Flexible Test-Time Objective Optimization:** A key motivation is to allow users to specify arbitrary objective functions at test time to guide trajectory generation, without requiring task-specific training or architectural modifications. This provides a high degree of flexibility for incorporating domain knowledge, user preferences, or dynamic constraints, which is often difficult with conventional learning-based planners.
5. **Leveraging Hierarchical Coarse-to-Fine Structure:** The framework aims to learn a latent space where tokens are hierarchically ordered. This ordering, combined with variable-length encodings, is intended to enable efficient exploration of a combinatorial space of maneuvers, allowing for coarse-to-fine refinement of trajectories during the planning process.
6. **Supporting Non-Smooth and Non-Differentiable Objectives:** The chosen mechanism of decode-and-score search over discrete tokens is motivated by its ability to accommodate objectives that are not smooth or differentiable, unlike gradient-based guidance methods. This expands the range of practical objectives that can be directly optimized.
7. **Maintaining Efficiency and Generating Realistic Solutions:** The goal is to achieve the above objectives while ensuring that the planning process remains computationally efficient and that the generated trajectories are consistently realistic and safe in complex environments. This is particularly crucial for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire for a motion planning system that is both highly adaptable to diverse, arbitrary objectives at test time and capable of generating realistic behavior informed by large-scale data, without compromising on efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a generative framework built around a conditional autoencoder and a latent token search mechanism.

#### 3.1. Conditional Trajectory Autoencoder

The core of the system is a conditional autoencoder designed to learn a compact and expressive latent representation of trajectories conditioned on environmental information.

- **Inputs:** The autoencoder takes as input the environment (E) and the full trajectory (T) of the agent of interest. In autonomous driving, E comprises static features (road geometry, signs) and dynamic object histories. T is represented as a sequence of position samples over time.
- **Encoder (Enc):** Enc(T, E) produces a sequence of N D-dimensional latent tokens, denoted as z = {z_i ∈ R^D}_(i=1)^N.
- **Decoder (Dec):** Dec(z, E) reconstructs the trajectory, producing T_pred. The decoder predicts mean and variance parameters for a Gaussian distribution at each point along the trajectory.
- **Loss Function:** The autoencoder is trained by minimizing the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of the ground truth reconstruction under the decoder's prediction, specifically using the β-NLL formulation.

#### 3.2. Compression via Adaptive Soft Quantization

To facilitate discrete search at test time while mitigating training challenges of hard quantization, a soft quantization method is employed during training.

- **Soft Quantization:** The bottleneck applies `corrupt(z) = tanh(z) + ϵ_t`, where `ϵ_t ~ N(0, Iσ_t^2)`. A tanh activation limits amplitude, and Gaussian noise is injected.
- **Adaptive Noise Schedule:** The noise level σ_t is adaptively controlled during training. It starts at zero and gradually ramps up based on the reconstruction accuracy (average displacement error, ADE) in the current minibatch, aiming to reach a target ADE. This process is designed to improve convergence.
- **Hard Quantization at Test Time:** During inference, the noise level σ_t is set to zero. Latent tokens are then element-wise rounded to the nearest of N_l uniformly spaced discrete levels.

#### 3.3. Variable-Length Latent Encoding

To allow for flexible reconstruction fidelity and structured exploration of the latent space, the latent tokens z_i are imposed with a causal ordering.

- **Causal Ordering:** This is enforced through masked self-attention mechanisms within both the encoder and decoder networks.
- **Nested Dropout:** During training, a random number of tail tokens are dropped from the encoding (nested dropout). This forces the decoder to reconstruct the trajectory from a truncated code, which encourages earlier tokens to capture coarser information and later tokens to add fine-grained detail. This establishes a coarse-to-fine hierarchy.

#### 3.4. Network Architecture

The conditional autoencoder is implemented using a transformer-based architecture:

- **Environment Encoder:** Processes static world features and dynamic object history, utilizing local neighborhood attention (similar to Motion Transformer, MTR).
- **Trajectory Encoder and Decoder:** These are transformer models that attend to the environment information via cross-attention and process the latent tokens via causal self-attention.
- **Bottleneck:** Between the encoder and decoder, transformer tokens are projected to the desired low dimensionality D, where nested dropout and adaptive soft quantization are applied during training.
- **Multi-agent Modeling:** For multi-agent scenarios, self-attention blocks with a hybrid attention mask are used, allowing each agent's trajectory token to attend to other agents bidirectionally, while latent tokens aggregate information from all agents. Agent-centric coordinate frames and permutation equivariance are maintained.

#### 3.5. Latent Token Search

Leveraging the causal ordering and variable-length encoding, motion planning is formulated as a search problem in the discrete latent space.

- **Greedy Search Strategy:**
 1. Starting with the first token z_1, the search enumerates its possible quantized values (N_l^D candidates).
 2. Each candidate prefix `[z_1]` is decoded by `Dec([z_1], E)`.
 3. The decoded trajectory T_pred is scored using a user-specified test-time objective function f(T_pred).
 4. The prefix `[z_1]` that minimizes the objective is selected.
 5. This process is iteratively repeated for subsequent tokens (z_2, z_3, etc.), always keeping previously selected token assignments fixed and refining the trajectory.
- **Efficiency:** The low dimensionality (D) and heavy quantization (N_l) of tokens are designed to keep enumeration computationally inexpensive.
- **Variance Prediction for Out-of-Distribution Detection:** The decoder's predicted variance for trajectory points is used as an indicator of reconstruction quality. Trajectories with high predicted variance can be penalized or discarded during search.
- **Optional Continuous Refinement:** After discrete token search, an optional post-processing step allows for gradient-based refinement of the selected latent tokens. This can be used with differentiable task objectives or auxiliary criteria like predicted variance minimization. This refinement is intended to improve performance further, but discrete search is presented as a necessary prior step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed framework across reconstruction accuracy, semantic interpretability of tokens, and guided trajectory generation in single-agent and multi-agent autonomous driving scenarios.

#### 4.1. Reconstruction

- **Quantization Impact:** The conditional autoencoder maintains modest reconstruction accuracy loss even with heavy quantization (N_l=2), as measured by Average Displacement Error (ADE) on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD).
- **Greedy Search Efficacy:** Greedy token search, when used with a reconstruction objective (minimizing ADE), demonstrates effective exploration of the latent space, outperforming the learned encoder in reconstruction accuracy (e.g., ADE of 0.386 for 3 tokens with greedy search vs. 0.403 for the autoencoder without quantization). This indicates that the hierarchical structure of the latent space is well-suited for greedy traversal.
- **Beam Search Benefits:** Using beam search offers only marginal improvements over greedy search for reconstruction, suggesting that greedy selection is a sufficiently effective strategy due to the learned latent space's structure.
- **Expressiveness:** The latent space, using just three tokens heavily quantized to three bits each, achieves a reconstruction ADE significantly lower than state-of-the-art motion prediction models (e.g., 0.386 compared to DriveGPT's 0.524 minADE across 6 predictions), indicating high expressiveness and efficient hierarchical representation of a large maneuver library.

#### 4.2. Semantics

- **Token Swapping:** Qualitative experiments involving decoding a trajectory's latent encoding from one environment into a different environment demonstrate that latent tokens carry high-level semantic information.
- **Behavior Transfer:** Swapping tokens between scenarios reliably transfers behaviors such as "straight," "slow left," "fast left," or "harsh deceleration" to novel environments, suggesting that specific token sequences can correspond to interpretable maneuver classes. For instance, decoding a "left turn" encoding in a new intersection consistently produces a left turn, provided the environment permits it.

#### 4.3. Guidance (Single-Agent)

Experiments on WOMD (open-loop) and nuPlan (closed-loop) evaluate the adaptability of token search to various objectives.

- **Maneuver Editing (WOMD):**
 - **Left-Turn Objective:** A non-realistic objective (maximizing cumulative leftward heading change, penalizing curvature) was used. Greedy search achieved a high success rate (90.2% with 3 tokens) in scenarios where a legal left turn was possible. It also showed robustness, producing minimal left turns in scenarios where turns were unavailable (2.38% with 3 tokens), and maintaining low rates of road edge contact.
 - **Lane-Change Objective:** A non-differentiable, lexicographically ordered objective (prioritizing number of lane changes, then minimizing lane centering deviation) was optimized. Full-depth greedy search achieved near-perfect compliance (99.04% with 3 tokens) while maintaining realistic speed profiles.
 - **Search Depth and Breadth:** Increased search depth improved success rates. Greedy search (beam size 1) was generally more computationally efficient and produced more robust, realistic behaviors for underspecified objectives compared to broader beam search (beam size 4), which could exploit objective loopholes and decoder imperfections.
- **Closed-Loop Planning (nuPlan):**
 - **Route-Following Objective:** A test-time objective maximizing progress along a target route and penalizing deviation was used. The planner, trained without route conditioning, achieved performance comparable to recent imitation learning baselines (CLS-NR 86.71, S-CR 95.91, S-PR 87.31).
 - **Ablations:** A search depth of N=3 and a small beam size of two yielded the best performance, reinforcing the value of a large maneuver library and a greedy traversal. Reduced compression (N=4) led to decreased performance. Including an explicit road edge collision loss and a gradient-based post-processing step (minimizing predictive variance) further improved the aggregate score.
- **Computational Efficiency:** With N=3, D=3, N_l=2, greedy search required 24 decoder evaluations, translating to approximately 115 trajectories per second (2760 decoder calls/second) on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPU.

#### 4.4. Guidance for Multi-Agent Trajectory Generation

A multi-agent autoencoder (N=12 tokens, D=3, N_l=2) was trained and evaluated on WOMD.

- **Terminal Position Guidance:** An objective minimizing the final displacement error (FDE) for agents was used. The method achieved significantly better realism (meanADE 0.767) compared to controllable trajectory generation baselines like CTG (1.947) and MotionDiffuser (2.194), particularly for mean metrics, suggesting reliable generation of realistic samples without relying heavily on sample filtering.
- **Optional Gradient-Based Optimization:** Adding a post-search gradient-based optimization step further reduced FDE, improving adherence to the prescribed terminal positions.
- **Consistent Joint Trajectories:** Even when only a single agent's terminal position was guided, the joint trajectory decoder synthesized consistent and valid behaviors for other unguided agents in the scene (e.g., a vehicle yielding to a pedestrian).
- **Search Depth Trade-off:** Increasing search depth improved FDE, but realism (meanADE) for single samples plateaued after N=4 tokens, and minADE could reduce with higher depths for multiple samples, indicating a trade-off between objective satisfaction and maintaining diverse, realistic candidates.

#### 4.5. Representation Learning Ablations

- **Token Dimensionality (D):** Increasing D generally improved reconstruction, but for guided generation with sparse supervision (e.g., terminal goal reaching), D=3 provided the best balance between trajectory realism (ADE) and controllability (FDE). Higher capacity tokens were found more beneficial when dense supervision was available.
- **Architectural and Training Choices:**
 - **Nested Dropout:** Found to be critical for the desired coarse-to-fine hierarchy, as its absence led to substantial degradation in search performance, despite maintaining strong full-token reconstruction.
 - **Causal Masking:** Had a small impact on search performance but worsened reconstruction under quantization when removed.
 - **Adaptive Soft Quantization:** Significantly outperformed Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) across all metrics (reconstruction and generation), and better supported subsequent continuous refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers a method for controllable trajectory generation in robotics, particularly in complex domains like autonomous driving.

**Significance:**

- **Unification of Paradigms:** The work introduces a generative framework that effectively bridges model-based planning (with its explicit objective functions) and learning-based approaches (with their data-driven realism). This is achieved by separating the learned ability to produce realistic motions from the test-time optimization of arbitrary objectives.
- **Enhanced Flexibility and Control:** By allowing direct search over a highly compressed, ordered latent space with a decode-and-score mechanism, the framework enables optimization of a wide variety of user-specified objectives, including those that are non-differentiable, without requiring task-specific retraining. This significantly increases the flexibility of learned planning systems.
- **Efficient and Realistic Generation:** The method demonstrates that planning in such a compressed latent space can be computationally efficient, even with greedy search, while consistently generating realistic and context-appropriate trajectories by leveraging the generative capabilities of the trained decoder.
- **Performance in Safety-Critical Domains:** The evaluations on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset and nuPlan, prominent benchmarks in autonomous driving, show strong performance in tasks such as closed-loop planning and multi-agent scenario synthesis, indicating the method's applicability to safety-critical applications.
- **Interpretable Representations:** The qualitative findings on token semantics and behavior transfer suggest that the learned latent codes can acquire meaningful high-level interpretations, potentially contributing to more interpretable and debuggable control systems.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Development and Deployment:** The ability to adapt to new objectives without retraining could streamline the development and deployment of robotic systems, reducing the engineering effort required for new tasks or modified specifications.
- **Improved Safety and Robustness:** For autonomous systems, the framework allows for direct incorporation of safety-critical objectives and expert knowledge at test time, potentially leading to more robust and verifiable planning behaviors. The robustness against invalid guidance demonstrated in experiments suggests a "learned guard rail" effect from the decoder.
- **Broader Applications in Controllable Generation:** While evaluated in autonomous driving, the underlying principle of guided generation via search in compressed, ordered latent spaces could be extended to other robotics domains (e.g., manipulation) and potentially other areas of controllable generative AI.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a new direction for exploring latent search and optimization algorithms, as well as extending compressed trajectory representations to higher-dimensional state/action spaces and more complex dynamics.
