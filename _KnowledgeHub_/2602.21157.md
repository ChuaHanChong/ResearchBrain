# HALO: A Unified Vision-Language-Action Model for Embodied Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, HALO, from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, implements embodied multimodal chain-of-thought (EM-CoT) reasoning by generating textual subtask plans and visual subgoals prior to action execution. This approach leads to improved performance in long-horizon and out-of-distribution manipulation tasks across both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon tasks and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios such as novel layouts or unfamiliar objects.
- Prior attempts to integrate reasoning into VLA models (textual CoT or visual subgoal prediction) often lack a unified framework that combines both semantic textual reasoning and visual imagination with action prediction.
- Architectures attempting to combine visual and textual reasoning frequently tightly couple image and text generation, potentially constraining the rich understanding capacities of underlying Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

## Method

- HALO employs an Embodied Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (EM-CoT) process where it first generates textual reasoning, then visual subgoals, and finally predicts actions conditioned on these multimodal insights.
- The model utilizes a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that integrates three specialized 'experts' for multimodal understanding, visual generation, and action prediction, allowing for independent parameter sets and cross-expert collaboration.
- An automated three-phase pipeline is introduced to synthesize EM-CoT training data by extracting action primitives, augmenting textual reasoning with a VLM, and extracting visual subgoals from robotic trajectories.

## Results

- HALO achieved an average success rate of 80.5% on Easy and 26.4% on Hard RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline (π0) by 34.1% and 10.1% respectively, indicating enhanced robustness to OOD challenges.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the versatile pre-training (VQA, visual generation, action prediction) and both textual reasoning and visual subgoals within the EM-CoT framework are critical for performance.
- In real-world evaluations on a COBOT Mobile ALOHA platform, HALO consistently outperformed baseline policies in long-horizon manipulation tasks, demonstrating greater robustness to visual distractions, lighting, and novel objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling specialized experts within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture allows for robust integration of heterogeneous capabilities (textual reasoning, visual foresight, action prediction) without compromising individual generative workflows.
> - Embodying a sequential 'thought-foresight-action' process, mirroring human cognition, enables VLA models to better handle complex, long-horizon tasks and generalize effectively to out-of-distribution environments.
> - An automated pipeline for generating intermediate textual reasoning and visual subgoal data provides scalable supervision, which is crucial for training complex multimodal reasoning capabilities in robotic systems.
