# HALO: A Unified Vision-Language-Action Model for Embodied Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, HALO, from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, implements embodied multimodal chain-of-thought (EM-CoT) reasoning by generating textual subtask plans and visual subgoals prior to action execution. This approach leads to improved performance in long-horizon and out-of-distribution manipulation tasks across both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon tasks and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios such as novel layouts or unfamiliar objects.
- Prior attempts to integrate reasoning into VLA models (textual CoT or visual subgoal prediction) often lack a unified framework that combines both semantic textual reasoning and visual imagination with action prediction.
- Architectures attempting to combine visual and textual reasoning frequently tightly couple image and text generation, potentially constraining the rich understanding capacities of underlying Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

## Method

- HALO employs an Embodied Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (EM-CoT) process where it first generates textual reasoning, then visual subgoals, and finally predicts actions conditioned on these multimodal insights.
- The model utilizes a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that integrates three specialized 'experts' for multimodal understanding, visual generation, and action prediction, allowing for independent parameter sets and cross-expert collaboration.
- An automated three-phase pipeline is introduced to synthesize EM-CoT training data by extracting action primitives, augmenting textual reasoning with a VLM, and extracting visual subgoals from robotic trajectories.

## Results

- HALO achieved an average success rate of 80.5% on Easy and 26.4% on Hard RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline (π0) by 34.1% and 10.1% respectively, indicating enhanced robustness to OOD challenges.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the versatile pre-training (VQA, visual generation, action prediction) and both textual reasoning and visual subgoals within the EM-CoT framework are critical for performance.
- In real-world evaluations on a COBOT Mobile ALOHA platform, HALO consistently outperformed baseline policies in long-horizon manipulation tasks, demonstrating greater robustness to visual distractions, lighting, and novel objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling specialized experts within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture allows for robust integration of heterogeneous capabilities (textual reasoning, visual foresight, action prediction) without compromising individual generative workflows.
> - Embodying a sequential 'thought-foresight-action' process, mirroring human cognition, enables VLA models to better handle complex, long-horizon tasks and generalize effectively to out-of-distribution environments.
> - An automated pipeline for generating intermediate textual reasoning and visual subgoal data provides scalable supervision, which is crucial for training complex multimodal reasoning capabilities in robotic systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which translate perceptual inputs into motor commands. However, existing VLA models frequently exhibit limitations when confronted with long-horizon tasks or out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, such as novel layouts, unfamiliar objects, or contact-rich interactions. These limitations stem from a lack of explicit mechanisms for reasoning about task structure or anticipating environmental evolution under action.

Current research efforts have attempted to integrate intermediate reasoning processes into VLA models. Some approaches, inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in large language models, focus on textual reasoning to enable multi-step task decomposition prior to action prediction. Examples include methods that train VLAs for textual reasoning, which facilitates task breakdown but often lacks grounding in visual state changes. Conversely, other works explore visual reasoning by autoregressively generating subgoal images as intermediate visual steps, but these approaches typically do not incorporate semantic textual reasoning capabilities.

A key challenge within this landscape is the development of a unified framework that can simultaneously support human-like textual reasoning, visual imagination, and action prediction. Existing methods that attempt to combine visual and textual reasoning often tightly couple image and text generation within a single autoregressive model or rely on discrete visual token prediction, which may potentially constrain the rich understanding capacities of the underlying Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This coupling can hinder the distinct generative workflows required for effective multimodal reasoning. Therefore, a unified architecture capable of jointly and effectively handling multimodal reasoning, visual generation, and action prediction remains an open problem. This paper aims to address this gap by proposing a unified architecture and methodology to integrate these capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified VLA model, named HALO, that can perform embodied multimodal chain-of-thought (EM-CoT) reasoning. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in existing VLA models and the desire to emulate human-like cognitive processes in robotic systems.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Limitations in Long-Horizon and Out-of-Distribution Scenarios:** Existing VLA models, which typically map perceptual inputs directly to motor commands, struggle in complex, long-horizon tasks or when encountering out-of-distribution environments. Such scenarios demand deliberation and foresight beyond reactive pattern matching. The authors aim to overcome these challenges by embedding explicit reasoning mechanisms.
- **Integrating Joint Textual Reasoning, Visual Foresight, and Action Prediction:** Prior works have separately explored textual CoT for task decomposition or visual subgoal prediction for intermediate guidance. However, a unified framework that integrates both capabilities—semantic textual reasoning and visual imagination—alongside action prediction is largely absent. The paper seeks to provide such a unified human-like reasoning framework.
- **Emulating Human Cognitive Processes:** The design of HALO is specifically engineered to mimic the human cognitive sequence of deliberate reasoning ("thinking"), mental imagination ("foresight"), and subsequent execution ("action"). This process is hypothesized to be critical for handling intricate sequential reasoning and maintaining contextual understanding over extended periods in complex tasks.
- **Overcoming Architectural Coupling Issues:** Existing approaches that combine visual and textual reasoning often couple these generation processes within a single autoregressive model, potentially degrading the distinct reasoning capabilities of the underlying VLM. A motivation for HALO is to develop an architecture that supports strong multimodal generation while preserving reasoning capacity through a decoupled yet collaborative design.
- **Enabling Scalable Learning:** To facilitate the practical application and training of a complex unified VLA model, the development of an automated and scalable method for synthesizing embodied multimodal chain-of-thought training data is a key objective. This data generation pipeline is intended to provide rich supervision for the intermediate reasoning steps.

In essence, the paper aims to advance robotic intelligence by moving beyond purely reactive policies toward systems capable of deliberate, visually grounded, and semantically consistent multimodal reasoning, thereby enhancing performance and generalization in challenging manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HALO (Human-like Adaptive Learning for Orchestration) is proposed as a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed to facilitate embodied multimodal chain-of-thought (EM-CoT) reasoning. The methodology encompasses a specific architectural design, an automated data synthesis pipeline, and a two-stage training recipe.

#### 3.1. HALO's Core Design and EM-CoT Process

HALO orchestrates reasoning in a sequential manner:
1. **Textual Reasoning and Task Planning:** The model first generates a textual chain-of-thought (r) that outlines the subtasks and logical steps required to achieve the overall goal.
2. **Visual Subgoal Prediction:** Based on the textual reasoning and current observations, HALO predicts a visual subgoal image ($\hat{o}_{t+h}$) that provides fine-grained visual guidance for the next step.
3. **EM-CoT-Augmented Action Prediction:** Finally, actions ($a_{t:t+m}$) are predicted, conditioned on the current observations, the instruction, the generated textual reasoning, and the predicted visual subgoal. This makes actions a product of deliberate reasoning and physical world modeling.

#### 3.2. Unified Architecture: Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)

HALO employs a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture, which integrates three specialized "experts" while allowing for cross-expert collaboration:
- **Multimodal Understanding Expert:** Responsible for semantic reasoning and textual generation.
- **Visual Generation Expert:** Specializes in visual foresight and the generation of subgoal images.
- **Action Prediction Expert:** Handles the prediction of continuous robot actions.

These experts maintain independent parameter sets but interact through a shared self-attention mechanism, enabling multimodal interactions. The architecture preserves each expert's natural generative workflow—autoregressive text generation for reasoning and diffusion-based prediction for visual states and actions—to avoid conflicts that might arise from forcing heterogeneous capabilities into a single monolithic model.

- **Initialization:** The MoT architecture is initialized using the Qwen2.5-1.5B LLM, resulting in approximately 4.5 billion parameters in total.
- **Modality-Specific Encoders:**
 - **Text:** Uses the standard Qwen2.5 tokenizer, augmented with special control tokens (e.g., `think start`, `vision start`, `action start`) to delineate the reasoning, visual generation, and acting phases.
 - **Visual:** Decouples encoding for understanding and generation. For understanding, a ViT encoder initialized with SigLIP2 and enhanced with NaViT for varied aspect ratios is used. For generation, a pre-trained VAE from FLUX is employed for high-fidelity image compression and reconstruction; VAE parameters remain frozen. MLPs project visual features into the LLM's embedding space.
 - **Action:** A linear projection layer maps raw continuous actions into the LLM’s hidden dimension, with a symmetric linear decoder for output.
- **Attention Masking Strategy:** A structured masking strategy within the shared self-attention manages information flow: causal masks for autoregressive textual generation, bidirectional attention within each visual frame (causal across frames/modalities), and restrictions to prevent noise tokens from attending to ground-truth targets or other tokens from attending to noise tokens.

#### 3.3. EM-CoT Data Synthesis Pipeline

To generate the necessary intermediate textual reasoning (r) and visual subgoals ($\hat{o}_{t+h}$), an automatic three-phase pipeline is introduced:
1. **Action Primitives Extraction:** Low-level continuous actions from raw robot trajectories are translated into high-level motion primitives using rule-based matching.
2. **VLM Augmentation for Textual Reasoning:** A large-scale VLM (Qwen3-VL) annotates the trajectories with dense textual reasoning, including overall task narratives and subtask decompositions. This process generates explicit reasoning traces (e.g., descriptions of current subtasks, movement explanations).
3. **Visual Subgoal Extraction:** The terminal frame of each identified subtask is automatically designated as its corresponding visual subgoal image. This provides sparse yet effective supervision for the visual foresight component.

This pipeline produces a high-quality dataset ($D_{ft}$) where each trajectory is augmented with aligned textual reasoning and visual goals, supervising the intermediate steps defined in the problem formulation.

#### 3.4. Training Recipe

HALO's training proceeds in two stages:

- **Stage 1: Versatile Pre-training:**
 - **Objective:** Establish a robust generalist foundation in multimodal understanding, physical dynamics prediction, and foundational manipulation skills.
 - **Data Sources:**
 - **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** LLaVA-NeXT-779k dataset for multimodal conversational priors, trained with a cross-entropy loss ($L_{CE}$).
 - **Visual Generation (VG):** Robotic trajectories from OXE and egocentric videos from SSv2. Formulated as future frame prediction ($ (l, I_{t-k:t}) \rightarrow I_{t+h} $), trained with a flow-matching MSE loss ($L_{MSE}$). A dual-path visual pathway (ViT for semantic, VAE for spatial latents) is employed.
 - **Action Prediction (AP):** Repurposed OXE dataset for direct action prediction ($ (l, o_{t-k:t}) \rightarrow a_t $), supervised by an L1 flow-matching loss ($L_{L1}$).
 - **Loss Function:** The total pre-training loss is a weighted sum: $L_{pt} = 0.25L_{CE} + 0.5L_{MSE} + L_{L1}$, with higher weights for manipulation-intensive tasks.

- **Stage 2: EM-CoT-Augmented Fine-tuning:**
 - **Objective:** Transition HALO from a reactive policy to a deliberative one by injecting structured multimodal reasoning capabilities.
 - **Data Source:** The EM-CoT dataset ($D_{ft}$) synthesized by the pipeline.
 - **Training Process:** The model is trained to orchestrate the complete "thought-foresight-action" chain (Eq. 1-3).
 - **Loss Function:** A joint loss is minimized: $L_{ft} = L_r + L_{\hat{o}} + L_a$, covering textual reasoning, visual subgoal generation, and action prediction respectively.
 - **Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation:** General VQA data is incorporated during fine-tuning to preserve broad multimodal capabilities.

This comprehensive approach aims to equip HALO with both foundational general knowledge and specialized multimodal reasoning for complex robotic tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and real-world environments demonstrate the effectiveness of HALO.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results on RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark

HALO was evaluated on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, comprising 50 manipulation tasks, under both "Easy" (clean environment) and "Hard" (domain-randomized environment) settings, and compared against baselines such as $\pi_0$, RDT-1B, and Diffusion Policy.

- **Superior Performance:** HALO consistently outperformed all competitive baselines. It achieved an average success rate of 80.5% on Easy tasks and 26.4% on Hard tasks. This represents a significant improvement over the $\pi_0$ baseline, which achieved 46.4% on Easy and 16.3% on Hard tasks, meaning HALO surpassed $\pi_0$ by 34.1% and 10.1% respectively.
- **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Challenges:** The performance gap, particularly on Hard tasks (where HALO showed a 62.0% relative improvement over $\pi_0$), indicates that HALO can handle out-of-distribution (OOD) challenges more robustly. This is attributed to HALO's foundational capabilities combined with its EM-CoT reasoning.
- **Impact of Pre-training:** A variant, HALO-w/o EM-CoT, which lacks the explicit reasoning chain but benefits from the versatile pre-training, still surpassed the strongest baseline ($\pi_0$) by 28.92 points on average for Easy tasks, suggesting the strength of the foundational pre-training.
- **Fine-grained Task Performance:** On intricate tasks like "Blocks Ranking Size" and "Stamp Seal," HALO achieved a multi-fold increase in success rates compared to baselines, indicating its ability to manage detailed object interactions.
- **Limitations of Reactive Policies:** Traditional reactive policies like Diffusion Policy demonstrated significant struggles in randomized environments, with success rates dropping to 0.6% in Hard settings.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of the versatile pre-training and the EM-CoT components.

- **Effectiveness of Versatile Pre-training:**
 - The full versatile pre-training, utilizing VQA, visual generation (V), and action prediction (A) datasets, yielded the highest performance.
 - Removing visual generation data (w/o V) led to a substantial degradation, especially on hard tasks (over 50% reduction).
 - Further exclusion of textual VQA (w/o V+T) and action prediction data (w/o V+T+A) resulted in progressively worse performance.
 - Without any pre-training (w/o V+T+A), the model’s performance dropped to 0% on hard tasks, emphasizing the foundational role of this stage. These findings confirm the complementary roles of the diverse pre-training datasets.

- **Effectiveness of EM-CoT:**
 - Both visual subgoal images (V) and textual reasoning (T) were identified as crucial components of EM-CoT.
 - Removing either component (w/o T or w/o V) degraded performance.
 - Combining both reasoning modalities in HALO achieved the highest accuracy, particularly on hard tasks, validating the effectiveness of the proposed EM-CoT formulation.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results of EM-CoT

- **Fidelity in Clean Settings:** In clean environments, HALO's EM-CoT generated textual reasoning and visual subgoals that closely aligned with the ground truth, indicating effective mastery of task logic and the provision of a multimodal roadmap for action.
- **Robustness under Randomization:** Under aggressive environmental randomization, EM-CoT maintained robustness. HALO correctly identified targets and planned trajectories despite significant visual shifts in object appearances and backgrounds, suggesting that the agent engaged in semantic reasoning rather than relying on simple pattern matching. This deliberate reasoning process guided low-level control in challenging OOD scenarios.

#### 4.4. Real-World Results

HALO was evaluated on a real-world COBOT Mobile ALOHA platform across four long-horizon manipulation tasks requiring multi-step planning, semantic grounding, and dual-arm control.

- **Superior Real-World Performance:** HALO consistently outperformed baseline policies ($\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$) in both basic and generalization settings.
- **Robustness to Real-World Variations:** While baselines showed noticeable performance degradation due to visual distractions, lighting variations, background changes, and novel objects, HALO maintained robustness and achieved higher success rates across all tasks. This indicates that EM-CoT reasoning contributes to more stable long-horizon execution and stronger generalization in real-world robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces HALO, a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, which represents an advancement in embodied artificial intelligence by integrating embodied multimodal chain-of-thought (EM-CoT) reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Unified VLA Architectures:** HALO presents a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that effectively decouples and integrates three specialized experts for textual reasoning, visual foresight, and action prediction. This design addresses the challenge of unifying heterogeneous capabilities without compromising individual expert performance, offering a blueprint for future multimodal foundation models in robotics.
- **Enhanced Robotic Reasoning and Generalization:** By enabling explicit textual reasoning and visual imagination prior to action execution, HALO moves beyond purely reactive policies. This deliberative approach improves performance in complex, long-horizon tasks and demonstrates strong generalization capabilities in out-of-distribution scenarios, including aggressive environmental randomization and various real-world variations. This suggests a pathway for robots to handle novel and unstructured environments more effectively.
- **Scalable Data Generation for Complex Models:** The introduction of an automated pipeline for synthesizing EM-CoT training data is a practical contribution. This pipeline addresses a key challenge in supervising sophisticated multimodal reasoning processes at scale, making it feasible to train models that require explicit intermediate thought processes.
- **Human-like Cognitive Paradigm for Robotics:** HALO's sequential "thinking-imagination-execution" process aligns with human cognitive approaches to problem-solving. This human-like reasoning paradigm could lead to more intuitive, interpretable, and ultimately more capable robotic systems that can adapt and plan in complex environments.
- **Robustness in Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated superior performance in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, particularly under challenging generalization settings (e.g., visual distractions, novel objects, lighting changes), indicates HALO's potential for practical deployment. This suggests that robots equipped with EM-CoT reasoning could operate more reliably and autonomously in dynamic, less controlled human environments.
- **Foundational Training Recipe:** The two-stage training paradigm, combining versatile pre-training on diverse multimodal datasets with EM-CoT-augmented fine-tuning, provides a robust methodology for developing and specializing VLA models. This recipe helps establish strong foundational skills while injecting advanced reasoning capabilities without severe catastrophic forgetting.

In conclusion, HALO contributes to the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems by offering a principled approach to integrate multimodal reasoning, foresight, and action within a unified framework, paving the way for more general-purpose and robust robotic manipulation.
