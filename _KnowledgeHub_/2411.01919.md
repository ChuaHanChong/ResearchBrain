# Real-Time Polygonal Semantic Mapping for Humanoid Robot Stair Climbing

> [!summary] Summary
> A real-time polygonal semantic mapping algorithm for humanoid robot stair climbing was developed, utilizing GPU-accelerated anisotropic diffusion and RANSAC to extract accurate planar regions from noisy depth data. This approach achieved an average step height error of 2.1 mm and an IOU of 0.93, processing frames within 15 ms and improving normal vector accuracy compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots require highly accurate and real-time traversable semantic maps for safe navigation in complex environments like staircases.
- Existing methods suffer from performance degradation when transitioning from noise-free simulated data to noisy real depth camera data, impacting plane stability and accuracy.
- Computational efficiency and robust handling of odometric drift and dynamic obstacles are critical for real-time and globally consistent map construction.

## Method

- A GPU-accelerated pipeline integrates depth images and robot pose estimates, applying anisotropic diffusion filtering to reduce noise while preserving edges.
- The system uses the Sobel operator for normal vector calculation, Canny edge detection, and RANSAC for fitting optimal plane models to polygonal segments derived from contours.
- A Kalman filter is implemented to estimate and compensate for vertical odometric drift, ensuring global consistency when integrating new polygonal planes into the existing semantic map.

## Results

- The system demonstrated high single-frame accuracy with an average step height error of **2.1** mm and an Intersection over Union (IOU) of **0.93** on staircase models, with plane extraction errors on the order of sensor measurement errors.
- The proposed method outperformed PPRCoRT for single-frame plane extraction, achieving a step height error of **2.1** mm compared to PPRCoRT's **20.2** mm, and processing frames in **7.5** ms versus 24 ms.
- When compared to EM cupy, the method exhibited superior effective planar area extraction, better normal vector accuracy (**4.74**° vs. **8.17**°), and higher IOU (**0.96** vs. **0.61**), while maintaining real-time performance and enhanced interference resistance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anisotropic diffusion filtering is effective at mitigating noise in real depth images while meticulously preserving critical edge details, which is crucial for accurate normal vector calculations and plane fitting.
> - Leveraging GPU acceleration for computationally intensive tasks like anisotropic diffusion and RANSAC enables real-time performance (under 15 ms per frame), allowing for prompt decision-making in dynamic robotic environments.
> - Implementing a Kalman filter for vertical drift compensation effectively maintains the global consistency and spatial accuracy of the semantic map over time, addressing a key challenge in long-term autonomous navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The capability for robots to accurately perceive their environment and construct traversable semantic maps is a fundamental requirement for autonomous navigation, particularly for humanoid robots operating in complex environments. Humanoid robots, with their increased degrees of freedom, are designed for intricate tasks such as stair climbing, which necessitate precise and efficient environmental sensing and reliable semantic map construction to ensure stable and safe movement.

Existing research in computer vision has proposed algorithms for extracting planes from ordered depth images, often prioritizing real-time performance and Intersection over Union (IOU) metrics. Simultaneously, the field of Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) has integrated planar and curved spatial semantic information for robust robot localization and mapping, with some successful projects being open-sourced, such as GPU-accelerated elevation mapping.

However, a recognized limitation in current methodologies is the discrepancy in performance between noise-free simulated data and real depth camera data. For instance, methods achieving over 95% IOU in simulations may drop below 80% with real sensor data. While such performance might be acceptable for some robotic platforms (e.g., quadruped robots), humanoid robots require larger and more stable planar areas for safe operation, making this issue critical. Furthermore, the accuracy of extracted plane parameters, including normal vectors and centroid heights, directly affects robot safety during operations. Real-time performance is another significant factor, demanding that computationally intensive processes operate within the sensor's cycle time. Challenges related to dynamic obstacles and odometric drift also require robust solutions in map construction.

This work builds upon previous research by some of the authors, which utilized supervoxel-based plane segmentation for humanoid robot stair climbing. That prior approach was identified as time-consuming. The current paper addresses these identified gaps by proposing a system designed for real-time extraction of planar polygons and incremental semantic map management. It specifically focuses on mitigating noise in raw depth data, optimizing computational efficiency through GPU acceleration, and managing map consistency in the presence of odometric drift, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and reliable mapping solutions for humanoid robot navigation in challenging terrains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel algorithm for real-time planar semantic mapping specifically tailored for humanoid robots navigating complex terrains, such as staircases. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed objectives and motivations:

- **Enhance Environmental Perception Accuracy:** A core objective is to improve the quality of environmental perception for humanoid robots. This includes accurately extracting planar regions from noisy real-world sensor data, which is crucial for identifying stable footholds.
- **Achieve Real-Time Performance:** The algorithm aims to process single frames at rates exceeding 30 Hz. This real-time capability is considered critical for enabling prompt decision-making in dynamic environments and facilitating responsive gait planning for humanoid robots.
- **Minimize Noise while Preserving Edges:** A significant motivation is to overcome the performance degradation observed when transitioning from simulated to real sensor data. This involves effectively reducing noise from gradient jumps in depth images while meticulously preserving essential edge details to enhance the accuracy and smoothness of normal vector calculations.
- **Generate Globally Consistent Semantic Maps:** The system is designed to create maps that are globally consistent, integrating multiple observations and compensating for odometric drift to maintain spatial accuracy over time.
- **Optimize Computational Efficiency:** To meet real-time requirements, the methodology explicitly leverages GPU acceleration for computationally intensive processes such as anisotropic diffusion filtering and RANSAC-based plane extraction. This optimization is motivated by the need to handle extensive data from complex scenes swiftly and efficiently.
- **Improve Safety and Accuracy for Humanoid Robot Movement:** Accurate extraction of plane results, including precise planar normal vectors and centroid heights, is paramount for the safety and accuracy of humanoid robot movements, particularly during complex maneuvers like stair climbing where stable contact points are vital.
- **Address Odometric Drift and Dynamic Obstacles:** The research aims to develop methods for managing new observations within an existing map, including estimating and compensating for odometric drift in the vertical direction, and addressing issues related to dynamic obstacles that can interfere with map construction.
- **Practical Application in Gait Planning:** The ultimate motivation is to provide a reliable semantic map that can be directly utilized for humanoid robot gait planning, thereby improving the robot's ability to navigate dynamic and structured environments like staircases effectively. This also includes making the developed system accessible to the research community through open-sourcing its code.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system for real-time planar semantic mapping employs a multi-stage, GPU-accelerated pipeline, integrating depth sensor data with robot pose estimates to construct a polygonal planar semantic map.

**System Overview:**
The system inputs depth images from depth sensors and pose estimates obtained from odometry or SLAM. These are processed through a series of steps on the GPU:
1. **Data Transfer and Filtering:** Depth images are transferred to GPU memory and undergo anisotropic diffusion filtering.
2. **Gradient and Edge Detection:** The Sobel operator computes normal vector images, followed by the Canny detector to identify edges and extract contours.
3. **Polygonal Segmentation and Plane Fitting:** Contours are simplified into polygonal segments, and the RANSAC algorithm fits optimal plane models for each polygon using the segmented polygons and depth images.
4. **Map Integration and Drift Compensation:** Newly obtained polygons are merged with the existing semantic map using robot pose estimates, incorporating a vertical drift compensation process.
The resulting polygonal semantic map is then available for applications such as humanoid robot gait planning.

**Sensory Equipment:**
The robot is equipped with a multi-sensory setup:
- **Realsense L515 Sensor:** Oriented towards the ground, this LiDAR-based sensor captures depth data, effective even in low-light conditions, though less effective on light-absorbing surfaces.
- **Realsense D435i Sensor:** Used for odometric pose estimation, utilizing its stereo infrared cameras and IMU. The infrared speckle projector is disabled to avoid interference with visual odometry.
- **VINS Fusion:** Employed for odometry to ensure computational efficiency and stability in pose estimation.

**Anisotropic Diffusion and Normal Vector Calculation:**
- **Anisotropic Diffusion Filtering:** This technique is applied to depth images to reduce noise while preserving significant edges. The diffusion coefficient, \(c(\lVert\nabla I\rVert)\), is designed to decrease with increasing gradient magnitude, thereby smoothing homogeneous regions while maintaining sharp edges. This process, defined by the equation \( \partial I/\partial t = \nabla \cdot (c(\lVert\nabla I\rVert)\nabla I) \), is parallelized on the GPU.
- **Normal Vector Calculation:** Following diffusion, normal vectors for each pixel are computed. The Sobel operator is used to calculate image gradients (\(G_x, G_y\)), which are then projected into 3D space using the camera's intrinsic matrix \(K\) via the formula \(n_p = -K^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} G_x \\ G_y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\). This GPU-accelerated step yields smoother and more accurate normal vector images crucial for subsequent plane extraction.

**Plane Segmentation:**
- **Edge Detection and Contour Extraction:** The Canny edge detection algorithm is applied to the normal vector image to identify edges. These edges are then used to extract contours, which are subsequently simplified into polygonal shapes, delineating potential planar regions.
- **RANSAC for Plane Fitting:** For each simplified polygon, the corresponding 3D points (derived from depth values and camera intrinsics) are used to fit an optimal plane model via the RANSAC (Random Sample Consensus) algorithm. This process is accelerated using CUDA, minimizing the sum of distances of inliers to the fitted plane. Models where the fraction of outliers exceeds 10% are discarded, ensuring the quality of extracted planes.

**Polygons Integration and Drift Compensation:**
- **Polygons Integration:** To construct a global semantic map, newly extracted polygonal planes are transformed into the map coordinate system. A merging strategy is applied: if the vertical distance (\(\Delta z\)) between the centroids of a new polygon and an existing map polygon is within a predefined drift tolerance (e.g., 5 cm) and they overlap, they are considered for merging.
- **Vertical Drift Compensation:** A Kalman filter is implemented to estimate and compensate for vertical drift in the robot's pose. The filter predicts the state estimate of vertical drift (\(\hat{x}_{k|k-1}\)) and its covariance (\(P_{k|k-1}\)), then updates these estimates based on a new measurement of drift (\(z_k\)), which is calculated from the average vertical centroid distances of all detected polygons relative to the map. The Kalman gain (\(K_k\)) determines the weighting between prediction and measurement. Adjustments to noise parameters (\(\sigma_p, \sigma_m\)) can be made to account for sensor uncertainty, such as IMU noise, ensuring the map's accuracy and positional integrity.

**Gait Planning Integration:**
The final polygonal semantic map is utilized to inform humanoid robot gait planning. The research includes demonstrations of using these maps to plan footsteps, generate CoM and angular-momentum trajectories via a walking pattern generator, and integrate them into a whole-body admittance control framework for simulations.

The code for this system has been made publicly available on GitHub.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation comprised three distinct parts: single-frame mapping accuracy, performance of algorithmic components, and comparative analysis with an existing mapping method, concluded by a motion planning simulation. The experiments were conducted using an Intel i9 13900HX processor and an NVIDIA RTX 4060 graphics card.

**A. Single-Frame Mapping Accuracy:**
Tests on a standard-sized staircase model yielded quantitative results:
- The average angle (\(\alpha\)) between the step normal vectors and the direction of gravitational acceleration was approximately 2.2°, with a standard deviation (\(\sigma_\alpha\)) of 0.44°.
- The average error in step height (\(\Delta d\)) was about 2.1 mm, with a standard deviation (\(\sigma_{\Delta d}\)) of 2.1 mm.
- The Intersection over Union (IOU) for the extracted areas was approximately 0.93.
These results indicate high single-frame repeatability, with plane extraction errors being on the order of sensor measurement errors.

A comparison with the PPRCoRT method for single-frame plane extraction (Table II) demonstrated:
- Our method achieved an average step height error (\(\Delta d\)) of 2.1 mm compared to PPRCoRT's 20.2 mm.
- The standard deviation of step height error (\(\sigma_{\Delta d}\)) for our method was 1.75 mm, versus 5.33 mm for PPRCoRT.
- Our method's IOU was 0.93, while PPRCoRT's was 0.88.
- The standard deviation of normal vector angle (\(\sigma_\alpha\)) for our method was 0.44°, slightly higher than PPRCoRT's 0.48°.
- Our method's runtime for single-frame processing was 7.5 ms, significantly faster than PPRCoRT's 24 ms (which was for merged results from three frames).
The comparison indicates that, with the exception of a marginal difference in normal vector standard deviation, the proposed method exhibits superior performance across critical semantic mapping metrics.

**B. Performance Evaluation of Algorithmic Components:**
The runtime efficiency of the GPU-accelerated anisotropic diffusion and RANSAC plane extraction processes was evaluated. With 10 iterations for each component:
- Processing time for parallel operations remained relatively stable as image resolution increased (from 320x240 to 640x480).
- RANSAC processing time showed a linear increase with the number of polygons per frame, but this increase was deemed negligible in practical scenarios due to the typically small number of valid polygons extracted from a single frame.
- The algorithm consistently maintained processing times below 15 ms for both resolutions tested, indicating that it meets the real-time performance requirement of exceeding 30 Hz (equivalent to less than 33 ms per frame).

**C. Comparative Analysis with Elevation Mapping (EM cupy):**
A comparative analysis with the elevation mapping cupy (EM cupy) method was performed on a straight staircase, using VINS Fusion for odometry input.
- **Effective Planar Area:** The proposed method demonstrated a greater effective planar area extraction compared to EM cupy.
- **Accuracy (Table III):** Both methods achieved millimeter-level height errors for stair centroids after vertical drift compensation (Our \(\Delta d\) of 5.3 mm vs. EM cupy's 5.7 mm). However, our method surpassed EM cupy in normal vector accuracy (Our \(\alpha\) of 4.74° and \(\sigma_\alpha\) of 1.02° vs. EM cupy's 8.17° and 4.39°, respectively) and IOU (0.96 vs. 0.61).
- **Resolution and Real-Time:** The proposed method is not limited by resolution constraints, whereas EM cupy at a 1 cm resolution required over ten seconds for plane extraction in the experiments, precluding a real-time performance comparison.
- **Interference Resistance:** Observations indicated that EM cupy struggled with dynamic obstacles, potentially retaining interference information in the map. The proposed method, by preemptively removing non-planar areas during mapping, exhibited better interference resistance.
- These advantages, particularly the larger effective plane extraction area, were identified as beneficial for humanoid robot motion planning.

**D. Motion Planning Simulation:**
The practicality of the developed polygonal semantic map was validated through motion planning simulations with a humanoid robot.
- Simulations of a humanoid robot climbing a straight staircase in Choreonoid, using footsteps planned from the generated map, demonstrated the effectiveness of the mapping approach.
- The map was successfully integrated with a walking pattern generator and the Whole-body Admittance Control framework to develop CoM and angular-momentum trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in environmental perception for humanoid robots, particularly in complex and challenging terrains such as staircases. The proposed real-time polygonal semantic mapping approach offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Safety:** By providing highly accurate and real-time semantic maps, the method directly contributes to improving the autonomy and safety of humanoid robots. The precise identification of planar regions and their properties (normal vectors, heights) is crucial for stable foothold planning and safe navigation, reducing the risk of falls or inaccurate movements.
- **Robustness to Real-World Noise:** The innovative application of anisotropic diffusion filtering to depth images specifically addresses the common challenge of sensor noise in real-world environments. This allows the system to maintain high accuracy and map fidelity, mitigating the performance drop typically observed when moving from simulated to physical deployments.
- **Real-Time Decision Making:** The GPU-accelerated architecture ensures that map generation and updates occur at high frame rates (exceeding 30 Hz). This real-time capability is essential for dynamic environments, enabling humanoid robots to adapt promptly to changing conditions and make immediate decisions regarding gait planning and obstacle avoidance.
- **Globally Consistent Environmental Representation:** The integration of odometry data with a robust vertical drift compensation mechanism allows for the creation of globally consistent and spatially accurate semantic maps. This consistency is vital for long-term navigation and repeated operations within an environment.
- **Direct Applicability to Motion Planning:** The output of the system—a detailed polygonal semantic map—is directly usable by humanoid robot gait planning algorithms. This provides a clear, actionable representation of the traversable environment, facilitating more sophisticated and reliable motion generation for complex tasks like stair climbing.
- **Contribution to the Robotics Community:** By open-sourcing the code, the authors enable further research and development in real-time semantic mapping for legged robots, potentially accelerating advancements in related fields such as SLAM, mobile manipulation, and human-robot interaction in unstructured settings.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The success of this mapping system in simulations paves the way for its adaptation and validation in real-world robot applications, forming a crucial foundation for integrating real-time motion planning with complex environmental understanding to achieve higher levels of humanoid robot capability.
