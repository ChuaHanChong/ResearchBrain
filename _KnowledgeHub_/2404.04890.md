# A Unified Diffusion Framework for Scene-aware Human Motion Estimation from Sparse Signals

> [!summary] Summary
> S2Fusion is a conditional diffusion framework that estimates full-body human motion from sparse head/hand tracking signals by fusing 3D scene geometry, a pretrained motion prior for initialization, and periodic autoencoder features, then applying loss-guided sampling to regularize unobserved lower-body motion; it outperforms prior sparse-tracking methods on CIRCLE and GIMO benchmarks.

## Problem

- Sparse-to-dense mapping from head/hand trackers to full-body pose is inherently one-to-many, and prior regression and generative baselines fail to narrow this ambiguity.
- Existing sparse-tracking methods ignore surrounding 3D scene geometry, discarding contextual cues that could disambiguate plausible lower-body poses.
- Without lower-body observations, prior methods let legs penetrate scene geometry and move incoherently relative to the tracked upper body.

## Method

- A ==VAE-based motion prior== pretrained on AMASS supplies a non-Gaussian initial motion sample for the ==reverse diffusion== process, improving diversity and cutting inference steps.
- A ==periodic autoencoder== extracts frequency-domain amplitude, offset, frequency, and phase-shift parameters from tracking signals to build time-aligned periodic motion feature embeddings.
- A ==conditional denoiser== fuses scene features (via ==PointNet++== on a cropped point cloud), periodic features, and tracking signals, guided at sampling time by ==scene-penetration== and ==phase-matching losses== on the lower body (**S2Fusion**).

## Results

- On CIRCLE and GIMO, S2Fusion beats AvatarPoser, AGRoL, and AvatarJLM, e.g. GIMO MPJPE **57.8mm** vs AvatarJLM's **70.7mm** and CIRCLE MPJPE **19.2mm** vs **24.6mm**.
- Lower-body position error drops sharply versus baselines, from **132.6mm** (AvatarJLM) to **107.9mm** on GIMO and from **69.1mm** to **57.3mm** on CIRCLE.
- Ablations show motion prior, scene modality, and periodic autoencoder are each additive, moving CIRCLE MPJPE from **26.2mm** (none) to **19.2mm** (full) with corresponding jitter and foot-skate reductions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting environment geometry as a conditioning signal is a general way to resolve one-to-many ambiguity in any sparse-observation pose or motion estimation task.
> - Frequency-domain phase features can encode inter-limb temporal correlation, letting observed body parts constrain unobserved ones without direct supervision.
> - Loss-guided diffusion sampling lets domain constraints (e.g. non-penetration) be injected at inference time without retraining the denoiser, generalizing beyond motion tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the field of human motion estimation, particularly relevant for Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR) applications. Traditional AR/VR devices typically provide sparse tracking signals, often from a head-mounted display (HMD) and hand controllers. Estimating full-body human motion from these limited inputs is an inherently ambiguous "one-to-many" mapping problem, as multiple plausible full-body motions can correspond to the same sparse observations.

Existing research in this area largely falls into two categories:
1. **Motion Estimation from Sparse Sensors:** This line of work focuses on reconstructing full-body motion from limited sensor inputs. Early approaches used multiple IMUs (e.g., six on head, wrists, knees, torso), while more recent methods often utilize three 6D trackers (head and hands). These methods include regression-based models (e.g., RNNs, Transformers) and probabilistic generative models (e.g., VAE, normalizing flow, diffusion models). A common limitation is their inability to fully resolve the one-to-many ambiguity and the tendency to generate implausible lower-body motions due to a lack of direct observation.
2. **Diffusion-based Probabilistic Models (DPMs):** DPMs have demonstrated capabilities in various generative tasks, including image, video, and speech synthesis, due to their robust probabilistic modeling and flexible conditioning. Their application to human motion generation, particularly text-to-motion, has shown promise. The concept of "loss-guided diffusion" (LGD) further enhances control over the generation process.
3. **Human Motion Generation with Scene Awareness:** There is a growing trend to incorporate 3D scene information into human motion generation, recognizing that human movement is context-dependent. While some methods generate scene-aware motions or reconstruct human-object interactions, integrating scene information with sparse tracking signals for full-body estimation is an active area.
4. **Motion Frequency Analysis:** Techniques that analyze motion in the frequency domain, such as periodic autoencoders (PAE), have been used for motion synthesis, editing, and stylization, as they can capture holistic and consistent movement patterns over time.

This paper positions itself within the intersection of these fields by explicitly introducing 3D scene information to constrain the estimation process, addressing the lower-body plausibility issue, and leveraging the generative capabilities of diffusion models combined with frequency-domain features from sparse signals. It specifically aims to improve upon methods that rely solely on sparse sensor data by adding crucial contextual cues.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to accurately estimate full-body human motion in 3D scenes using sparse tracking signals, typically from head-mounted displays and hand controllers. This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Resolving One-to-Many Ambiguity:** The core challenge in estimating dense full-body motion from sparse observations is the inherent ambiguity where a single set of sparse signals can correspond to multiple possible full-body poses. Existing data-driven methods, even probabilistic generative models, struggle to sufficiently narrow down this distribution, leading to less precise or physically implausible outputs.
2. **Improving Lower Body Plausibility and Coherence:** Sparse tracking signals primarily originate from the upper body (head, hands), leaving the lower body unobserved. This lack of data often results in unrealistic leg motions, such as limb penetration through scene geometry, or movements that are incoherent with the upper body (e.g., hands and feet moving on the same side without coordination). The authors aim to address this by introducing mechanisms that regularize lower-body motion.
3. **Leveraging Scene Context:** Human motion is inherently influenced by the surrounding environment. Introducing 3D scene geometry provides rich contextual information that can significantly reduce ambiguities, especially for inferring lower-body movements where direct observations are absent. This is a key motivation to integrate scene modality into the estimation framework.
4. **Enhancing Motion Diversity and Realism with Motion Priors:** Training generative models on limited scene-motion datasets can restrict the diversity and realism of generated motions. The authors are motivated to overcome this by incorporating knowledge from large-scale motion capture datasets through a pre-trained motion prior, thereby providing a more robust starting point for motion generation.
5. **Capturing Temporal Movement Patterns:** The authors observe that changes in sparse tracking signals reflect underlying temporal movement patterns. They are motivated to extract these patterns, specifically focusing on periodic motion features, to improve the spatial-temporal alignment and coordination between upper and lower body movements.
6. **Developing a Unified and Controllable Framework:** The paper aims to integrate these solutions (scene information, motion priors, periodic features, lower-body regularization) within a unified, flexible, and controllable generative model framework, specifically a conditional diffusion model, known for its ability to incorporate various conditioning signals and loss functions during sampling.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a robust system that can generate plausible, coherent, and scene-aware full-body human motions from sparse VR/AR inputs, overcoming the limitations of ambiguity and lower-body realism in current state-of-the-art methods by systematically incorporating scene context and sophisticated motion regularization techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors developed S²Fusion, a unified framework for scene-aware human motion estimation from sparse signals, leveraging a conditional diffusion model. The overall process is depicted in Figure 2 of the paper.

**Problem Formulation:**
Given a sequence of sparse tracking signals $p_{1:N} \in R^{N \times c}$ and scene geometry $S \in R^{P \times 3}$, the goal is to predict full-body motion $x_{1:N} \in R^{N \times n}$. The human pose is represented using the SMPL model (first 22 joints), and rotations are represented using 6D representation. $N$ is the sequence length, $c$ is the input dimension, $n$ is the output dimension, and $P$ is the number of points in the scene point cloud.

The S²Fusion framework comprises three main components: motion prior for initialization, condition acquisition, and a conditional denoiser with lower body regularization during sampling.

#### 3.1. Motion Prior for S²Fusion Initialization

To address the limited volume of existing scene-motion datasets and generate diverse motions, S²Fusion initializes the reverse diffusion process from a pre-trained motion prior, rather than pure Gaussian noise.
- **Architecture:** A VAE-based generative model is used, consisting of a conditional encoder ($E$) and decoder ($D$). The encoder takes full-body motion ($x_{1:N}$) and sparse tracking signals ($p_{1:N}$) to output a latent code ($z$). The decoder reconstructs full-body motion ($\hat{x}_{1:N}$) from the latent code ($z$) and sparse tracking signals ($p_{1:N}$).
- **Training:** This VAE is pre-trained on the large-scale AMASS motion dataset. The training objectives include a KL divergence loss ($L_{KL}$) to regularize the latent space, a reconstruction loss ($L_{recon}$) to ensure motion recovery, and a geometric loss ($L_{geometric}$) to enforce generated motions to lie on the motion manifold (using forward kinematics).
- **Inference:** During inference, only the conditional decoder ($D$) is used to sample an initial noisy motion $\tilde{x}_{1:N} = f_{\phi}(z, p_{1:N})$ by conditioning on tracking signals and a sampled latent code $z \sim N(0, I)$. This initial motion, which captures complex motion manifold structures, is then refined by the subsequent conditional diffusion process, leading to improved sample quality and accelerated inference speed compared to starting from Gaussian noise.

#### 3.2. Condition Acquisition for S²Fusion

The overall conditioning input $c = (p_{1:N}, f_{1:N}, E_S)$ for the diffusion model consists of three elements:
- **Scene Conditioning ($E_S$):** Only a local region around the human is considered for scene context. A $2m \times 2m \times 2m$ bounding box is cropped around the human (based on HMD global translation) from the 3D scene point cloud. This cropped point cloud is then fed into a PointNet++-based scene encoder ($h_{\theta}$) to extract a scene feature vector $E_S$.
- **Periodic Motion Feature ($f_{1:N}$):** To capture temporal movement patterns and ensure coordination between upper and lower body motions, periodic motion features are extracted from the sparse tracking signals.
 - A periodic autoencoder (PAE) ($g_{\tau}$) is employed. The encoder part of the PAE uses 1D convolution followed by a Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) layer to compute frequency ($F$), amplitude ($A$), and offset ($B$) parameters from the sparse tracking signals ($p_{1:N}$).
 - Phase shifts ($S$) are derived from a separate fully-connected network applied to the convolved signals.
 - These frequency-domain parameters (F, A, B, S) are then used to reconstruct a smoothed periodic alignment feature $f_t$ in the temporal domain using a multi-resolution sinusoidal function: $f_t = A \cdot sin(2\pi \cdot (F \cdot t - S)) + B$. This feature summarizes the spatial-temporal alignment of full-body motion.
 - The entire PAE is pre-trained using a reconstruction loss $L_{PAE}$ that minimizes the difference between the input sparse signals and the reconstructed signals.
- **Sparse Tracking Signals ($p_{1:N}$):** These are the raw position and rotation measurements from the head and left/right hands, augmented with computed angular and linear velocities.

#### 3.3. Conditional Denoiser of S²Fusion

S²Fusion models the motion generation as a reverse diffusion process.
- **Diffusion Process:** The forward diffusion process progressively adds Gaussian noise to a clean motion $x_0$ over $T$ steps, creating noisy samples $x_t$. The reverse process aims to gradually denoise a corrupted signal $x_T$ (which is $\tilde{x}_{1:N}$ from the motion prior in this case) back to $x_0$.
- **Denoising Network (G):** Instead of predicting the added noise $\epsilon_t$, the network $G$ is trained to directly predict the clean signal $\hat{x}_0$ from a noisy input $x_t$ conditioned on time $t$ and the combined condition $c$.
- **Training Objectives:** The denoising network $G$ is trained with a simple objective $L_{simple}$ (mean squared error between predicted clean motion and ground truth clean motion) and a geometric loss $L_{geometric}$ (similar to the VAE prior, enforcing physically plausible joint positions via forward kinematics). The total training loss is $L_{train} = L_{simple} + L_{geometric}$.

#### 3.4. S²Fusion Sampling with Lower Body Motion Regularization

To address the challenges of unrealistic lower body motions (penetration, incoherence), the sampling procedure of S²Fusion incorporates loss-guided sampling with two specially designed loss functions:
- **Scene-Penetration Loss ($\ell_{penetration}$):** This loss aims to prevent specific body joints from penetrating the 3D scene geometry. It is defined as the sum of maximum penetrations for a set of critical joints (e.g., ankles, knees) into the scene's point cloud. Penetration is calculated based on the distance to nearest neighbors within a defined radius.
- **Phase-Matching Loss ($\ell_{phase}$):** This loss encourages coherent movement between the upper and lower body. It quantifies the difference between phase features of the upper body and the generated lower body. The phase feature ($P$) is computed from amplitude ($A$) and phase shift ($S$) parameters as $P = [sin(2\pi \cdot S), cos(2\pi \cdot S), A]$. The rationale is that upper and lower body movements are often coordinated for balance. For the lower body, anchor joints (pelvis, ankles) are used to compute its phase features from the generated motion.
- **Loss-Guided Sampling:** During the reverse diffusion sampling process, after the denoising network $G$ predicts a clean motion $\hat{x}_{1:N}$, this prediction is refined by injecting the gradient of the combined regularization loss $\ell_{sample} = \alpha \cdot \ell_{penetration} + \beta \cdot \ell_{phase}$. This step guides the motion generation towards scene-aware and coherent movements without requiring additional training of the diffusion model itself.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents both quantitative and qualitative results, along with ablation studies and a human perceptual study, to support its claims.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison with State-of-the-Art

S²Fusion was compared against AvatarPoser, AGRoL, and AvatarJLM on two human-scene interaction datasets: CIRCLE and GIMO.
- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** S²Fusion consistently achieved superior performance across most metrics on both datasets.
 - **Pose Accuracy:** Achieved the lowest Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) (e.g., 57.8 mm on GIMO, 19.2 mm on CIRCLE) and Mean Per Joint Rotation Error (MPJRE) (e.g., 4.65 deg on GIMO, 2.32 deg on CIRCLE), indicating more accurate pose estimation.
 - **Motion Smoothness:** Demonstrated lower Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE) (e.g., 235.7 mm/s on GIMO, 117.6 mm/s on CIRCLE) and Jitter (e.g., 10.1 on GIMO, 5.8 on CIRCLE), suggesting smoother generated motions.
 - **Scene Awareness:** Achieved the lowest Foot Slip (FS) (e.g., 1.39 on GIMO, 1.48 on CIRCLE), indicating reduced accumulated drift of foot joints during contact with the ground, which is crucial for scene awareness.
- **Per-Body Part Evaluation (Table 2):** S²Fusion showed significant improvements, particularly for the lower body.
 - **Lower Body:** Demonstrated a substantial reduction in Lower PE (Position Error) compared to other methods (e.g., 107.9 mm on GIMO vs. 132.6 mm for AvatarJLM; 57.3 mm on CIRCLE vs. 69.1 mm for AvatarJLM), indicating more accurate lower body reconstruction despite the lack of direct tracking signals.
 - **Upper Body and Hands:** Also achieved lower Hand PE and Upper PE, demonstrating overall improved motion estimation across the entire body.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Figure 4, Figure 10)

Visualizations of generated poses in 3D scenes demonstrated that S²Fusion produces accurate and smooth motions. The method exhibited fewer scene penetrations and more correlated leg motions compared to baseline methods, particularly in scenarios where other methods generated implausible limb configurations (highlighted in red boxes).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of S²Fusion Modules (Table 3, Table 4):**
 - **Scene Modality:** Introducing scene information significantly improved motion estimation quality, particularly in MPJPE and FS. This supports the claim that scene context reduces ambiguity.
 - **Pre-trained Motion Prior (MP):** Drawing initial samples from the pre-trained motion prior led to notable improvements in motion smoothness (MPJVE, Jitter) and estimation accuracy (MPJPE, FS), demonstrating its benefit in overcoming limited data volume and providing robust initialization.
 - **Periodic Autoencoder (PAE):** The PAE also contributed to improved motion estimation quality, indicating its effectiveness in extracting spatial-temporal alignment features from sparse signals.
 - Combining all components yielded the best performance.
- **Effectiveness of Loss-Guided Sampling (Table 5, Table 6):**
 - **Scene-Penetration Loss ($\ell_{penetration}$):** This loss specifically helped reduce MPJPE and FS, showing its role in preventing implausible human-scene penetration and improving grounding, though sometimes with a minor increase in jitter.
 - **Phase-Matching Loss ($\ell_{phase}$):** This loss was effective in producing accurate motions (reduced MPJPE, MPJVE, FS), reinforcing the importance of correlating upper and lower body movements.
 - Using both losses together generally resulted in the best overall performance, demonstrating their complementary roles in regularizing lower-body motion. The authors noted that large scaling factors for these losses could introduce jittering.

#### 4.4. Extra Evaluation with Head-Only Input (Table 7)

When the input was limited to head motion only (even sparser signals), S²Fusion still outperformed a recent method (EgoEgo) that also uses a diffusion backbone, in terms of MPJPE and Accel (acceleration error), while maintaining comparable FS. This further highlights the benefit of incorporating scene modality even with severely limited inputs.

#### 4.5. Human Perceptual Study (Figure 7, Figure 8)

A study involving 25 users indicated that motions generated by S²Fusion were preferred over those from other state-of-the-art methods on both CIRCLE and GIMO datasets. The results also suggested that S²Fusion's generated motions were competitive with ground truth motions on the CIRCLE dataset in terms of realism.

#### 4.6. Failure Cases (Figure 9)

The authors identified limitations, specifically that while the method excels at realistic lower body motions, it occasionally struggles with faithfully capturing fine-grained hand-object interactions, such as picking up clothes or wiping a blackboard.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The S²Fusion framework represents an advancement in the field of human motion estimation from sparse signals, offering several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced AR/VR Experience:** By providing a more accurate, plausible, and scene-aware full-body motion estimation from the limited inputs typical of AR/VR devices, S²Fusion can significantly improve the realism and immersion of virtual avatars in applications such as virtual conferencing, gaming, and simulation. This enables more natural human-avatar interaction and reduced visual artifacts.
2. **Addressing Core Ambiguity:** The introduction of scene geometry as a direct conditioning input is a notable step towards resolving the inherent "one-to-many" ambiguity in sparse-to-dense motion mapping. This approach leverages contextual information that human perception implicitly uses, leading to more deterministic and accurate motion predictions.
3. **Improved Lower Body Realism:** The specialized scene-penetration and phase-matching losses effectively regularize lower-body motion, a common challenge due to the absence of direct tracking signals. This leads to physically more plausible leg movements and reduces instances of unrealistic limb penetration through surfaces, thereby enhancing the overall fidelity of the generated motion.
4. **Robustness with Sparse Inputs:** The framework demonstrates effectiveness even with very sparse inputs (e.g., head-only tracking), indicating its potential for broader application in scenarios with varying sensor availability.
5. **Integration of Diverse Modalities and Priors:** The unified diffusion framework effectively integrates diverse inputs—raw sparse signals, frequency-domain motion features, and 3D scene geometry—with a pre-trained motion prior. This multi-modal approach showcases a robust strategy for complex generative tasks, potentially inspiring similar integrations in other domains.
6. **Advancement in Generative Models:** The work extends the application and control capabilities of conditional diffusion models, demonstrating how loss-guided sampling can be effectively employed to inject domain-specific constraints (like physical plausibility and inter-limb coherence) without modifying the core generative network, making it adaptable to new requirements.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified failure cases regarding fine-grained hand-object interactions open avenues for future research, suggesting the need for more sophisticated physical constraints or interaction modeling. This work provides a strong foundation upon which more complex human-scene-object interaction models can be built.

In essence, S²Fusion contributes to making virtual avatars more lifelike and responsive to their environment, which is a critical step for the evolution and adoption of immersive computing technologies.
