# Recursive Self-Aggregation Unlocks Deep Thinking in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Recursive Self-Aggregation (RSA), an inference-time method for Large Language Models that enhances reasoning by iteratively refining and recombining multiple candidate solutions. When combined with aggregation-aware reinforcement learning, RSA consistently improves performance across various complex tasks, enabling smaller models to achieve reasoning capabilities comparable to larger, specialized models.

## Problem

- Existing test-time scaling methods for LLMs often focus solely on sequential refinement, which can propagate errors, or parallel exploration, which lacks iterative recombination of promising fragments.
- There was no universal and effective test-time scaling method that allowed reuse of promising fragments from multiple candidate solutions without relying on external verifiers.
- Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for LLMs did not explicitly train models on the task of aggregating multiple candidate solutions, potentially degrading performance when used with aggregation-based inference strategies.

## Method

- ==Recursive Self-Aggregation (RSA)==: An iterative, evolutionary-inspired algorithm that maintains a population of candidate reasoning chains and repeatedly forms aggregation sets, instructing an LLM to analyze these sets and generate improved, refined solutions.
- ==Aggregation-Aware Reinforcement Learning==: A novel training approach that augments RL datasets with =="aggregation prompts"== alongside standard prompts, explicitly teaching the LLM to effectively combine multiple candidate solutions.
- The method is parameterized by ==population size (N), aggregation set size (K), and number of recursive steps (T)==, allowing for flexible control over the breadth and depth of reasoning during inference.

## Results

- RSA consistently outperformed all evaluated sequential and parallel baselines (e.g., self-refinement, majority voting) across diverse tasks, including math (AIME-25, HMMT-25), general reasoning, and code generation.
- RSA yielded substantial Pass@1 improvements across various LLM architectures (e.g., Qwen3, gpt-oss, Nemotron), enabling a **4B** parameter model to achieve reasoning performance comparable to, or exceeding, much larger, dedicated reasoning models.
- Performance generally improved monotonically with an increasing number of RSA steps (T) and aggregation set size (K), while the proposed aggregation-aware RL consistently boosted RSA performance compared to using the base model or standard RL-tuned models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can implicitly verify and effectively aggregate intermediate reasoning steps from multiple candidate solutions to construct superior final answers, eliminating the need for external verifiers.
> - Iterative, recursive refinement and recombination of candidate reasoning chains lead to monotonic performance improvements, allowing LLMs to achieve deeper, more robust problem-solving capabilities.
> - Aligning LLM training objectives with specific inference strategies, such as using aggregation-aware RL for RSA, is crucial, as a mismatch between training and inference can paradoxically lead to degraded performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of test-time scaling for Large Language Models (LLMs), which aims to enhance model capabilities during inference without modifying their parameters. Existing test-time scaling methods generally fall into two categories: sequential scaling, which iteratively refines solutions, and parallel scaling, which explores multiple independent solution paths. Sequential methods, such as self-refinement, can improve a single candidate solution but do not leverage information from other candidates. Parallel methods, like Best-of-N selection or majority voting, can identify superior candidates or aggregate them, but typically do not facilitate the recombination of promising fragments from diverse reasoning chains to produce improved solutions. Hybrid approaches exist, often integrating LLMs into genetic algorithm loops or employing ensembles of models, but frequently rely on external verifiers or make specific assumptions about the structure of reasoning chains.

The paper identifies a gap: the lack of a universal and effective test-time scaling method that facilitates the reuse of promising fragments from multiple candidate solutions without strong assumptions or external verification. The proposed Recursive Self-Aggregation (RSA) method aims to bridge this gap by combining aspects of both parallel and sequential scaling, drawing inspiration from evolutionary algorithms. It positions itself as an advancement over single-step aggregation methods and other hybrid approaches by introducing a recursive refinement loop and operating without explicit external verification, relying instead on the implicit verification capabilities of the LLM itself. The research also extends into the post-training phase by addressing how reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning can be adapted to be "aggregation-aware," thereby better aligning training objectives with test-time aggregation strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and general test-time scaling framework that enhances the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by allowing them to deeply integrate and refine information across multiple candidate solutions. This framework aims to overcome limitations of existing methods that are either purely sequential or purely parallel.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Scaling Methods:** Current test-time scaling strategies often trade off between breadth (parallel exploration) and depth (sequential refinement). Purely parallel methods (e.g., Best-of-N, majority voting) can explore diverse solution paths but may not effectively recombine promising intermediate steps from different candidates to form a superior solution. They also lack the "deep thinking" required for complex multi-step problems. Conversely, purely sequential methods (e.g., self-refinement) allow for iterative improvement of a single solution but are susceptible to persisting in unproductive reasoning chains due to a lack of branching or consideration of alternative perspectives. The authors seek a method that harnesses the benefits of both.
- **Enabling Reuse of Promising Reasoning Fragments:** A core motivation is to allow LLMs to "cross-reference" and aggregate information from multiple reasoning chains, even if individual chains contain errors. The hypothesis is that an LLM can identify and reuse correct intermediate steps from different candidates to construct a more robust final solution, a capability not universally present in prior methods.
- **Reducing Reliance on External Verifiers:** Many advanced test-time scaling and evolutionary methods require an external mechanism to verify the correctness or quality of generated solutions (e.g., learned reward models, formal verifiers). This can be complex to implement or unavailable for certain tasks. RSA is designed to operate using only the LLM itself for "implicit verification" through its aggregation capability, simplifying deployment.
- **Improving Performance Cost-Effectiveness:** Enhancing LLM performance often involves scaling model size, which significantly increases computational cost. Test-time scaling offers an alternative by increasing inference-time compute. RSA aims to provide substantial performance gains with increased compute budgets, potentially enabling smaller models to achieve performance levels competitive with or superior to much larger, more expensive models, thereby improving cost-efficiency.
- **Optimizing RL Fine-tuning for Aggregation:** The authors observe that standard reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning, which typically trains models to generate correct solutions directly, can inadvertently degrade performance when combined with test-time aggregation strategies. This mismatch between training and inference objectives motivates the development of an aggregation-aware RL approach to explicitly teach the model how to effectively combine solutions during post-training.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to unlock deeper, more reliable reasoning in LLMs, making them more versatile and efficient for complex problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Recursive Self-Aggregation (RSA) as a hybrid test-time scaling method and proposes an aggregation-aware reinforcement learning (RL) approach to complement it.

#### 3.1. Recursive Self-Aggregation (RSA)

RSA is designed to iteratively refine a population of candidate reasoning chains, drawing inspiration from evolutionary algorithms, specifically the concepts of crossover and mutation. It operates in a loop without requiring external verifiers.

- **Population of Trajectories (P_t):** At each step `t`, RSA maintains a population of `N` independent candidate solutions, denoted as `P_t = {τ_1^(t)..., τ_N^(t)}`.
- **Initialization (P_1):** For a given query `x`, the initial population `P_1` is generated by sampling `N` responses directly from the pretrained LLM, `p_θ_ref`, based on the query `x`.
- **Subsampling:** In each subsequent step, `t` from `1` to `T-1`, `N` aggregation sets are formed. Each set, `S_i^(t)`, consists of `K` distinct candidate solutions sampled uniformly without replacement from the current population `P_t`.
- **Aggregation:** For each aggregation set `S_i^(t)`, the LLM (`p_θ_ref`) is prompted with the original query `x` and the `K` candidate solutions in `S_i^(t)`. The model is instructed to generate a new, refined response `τ_i^(t+1)`, effectively combining and improving upon the input candidates. These `N` newly generated solutions form the next population `P_t+1`.
- **Recursion:** This process of subsampling and aggregation is repeated for `T` sequential steps, allowing the population of solutions to iteratively evolve. Errors and inconsistencies are intended to be gradually pruned, while effective reasoning patterns are preserved and propagated.
- **Termination:** After `T` steps, the final population `P_T` is obtained. The ultimate solution is derived either by randomly sampling one from `P_T` or by using majority voting among the solutions. In the experiments, uniform random sampling is employed.

Key aspects of RSA include:
- **Implicit Verification:** The method relies on the LLM's inherent ability to evaluate and improve reasoning chains, rather than an explicit scoring function.
- **Diversity Maintenance:** By maintaining a population size `N` larger than the aggregation set size `K`, RSA aims to preserve diversity within the candidate pool, facilitating the recombination of various reasoning patterns.
- **Generalizability:** RSA is implemented with a simple prompt template adaptable to diverse tasks without extensive task-specific prompt engineering.
- **Combinatorial Power:** It allows for joint consideration of more proposals than single-step aggregation, which is limited by the model's context length.

#### 3.2. Aggregation-Aware Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The research also addresses the challenge that standard RL fine-tuning, which optimizes a model to produce correct answers given a query, may not adequately prepare it for aggregation tasks during test-time scaling. This can lead to performance degradation when an RL-tuned model is used within an aggregation framework like RSA.

- **Problem Formulation:** The task of aggregation is framed as an RL problem, where the model learns to produce a single correct reasoning chain given a problem and a set of candidate solutions.
- **Data Augmentation Strategy:** An aggregation-aware training dataset is created, comprising two types of prompts:
 1. **Standard Prompts:** These contain only the problem (`x`) and are used to train the model to generate initial candidate reasoning chains, similar to conventional RL fine-tuning.
 2. **Aggregation Prompts:** These include the problem (`x`) along with `K` candidate solutions (`S_0`) generated by the reference model, formatted with the same aggregation prompt used in RSA.
- **Objective Optimization:** The LLM is trained to optimize an RL objective that accounts for both prompt types. This involves maximizing the expected reward `r(τ, y)` (for a generated trajectory `τ` and ground truth `y`) while applying an optional KL regularization against a reference policy `p_θ_ref`.
- **Training Algorithm:** RLOO (Reinforcement Learning with Offline Optimization) is used for training, initializing the model parameters with those of the base model.

This aggregation-aware RL approach aims to align the model's training more closely with its inference-time usage within RSA, teaching it not only to generate solutions but also to effectively combine and refine them.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of RSA and the aggregation-aware RL approach across various tasks, model families, and sizes.

- **Performance on ARC-AGI-2:** RSA, when applied with Gemini 3 Flash, improved the Pass@1 score from a base of 37.78% to 59.31% (using N=16, K=4, T=10 steps). This performance level approached that of an average human test-taker and surpassed other advanced models like Gemini 3 Deep Think at a significantly reduced computational cost (approximately 10% of the cost). The results indicated a monotonic improvement in score with increasing sequential depth (T), and larger aggregation sizes (K) generally enhanced performance.

- **Outperformance of Other Test-Time Scaling Methods:** Using Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as the base model, RSA consistently outperformed both purely sequential (T-step self-refinement) and purely parallel (rejection sampling, majority voting, single-step self-aggregation) test-time scaling baselines.
 - On Math tasks (AIME-25, HMMT-25), RSA achieved Pass@1 scores of 73.18% and 47.55% respectively, significantly higher than self-refinement (53.33%, 39.17%) and single-step aggregation (56.51%, 36.15%).
 - For Code generation (LiveCodeBench-v6) and General reasoning (Reasoning Gym Games, Cognition+ARC), RSA also showed superior performance.
 - On SuperGPQA (Knowledge recall), RSA achieved 47.39%, outperforming rejection sampling (46.18%) and self-aggregation (45.91%), though majority voting (48.2%) was slightly more effective, likely due to the multiple-choice format of the task.
 - Recursive aggregation (T=10) consistently outperformed its single-step variant (T=1), demonstrating the benefits of the iterative refinement loop.

- **Consistent Gains Across Diverse Models:** RSA yielded substantial performance improvements across a range of instruction-tuned models, including various Qwen3 models (4B-Instruct, 30B-Instruct, 4B-Thinking), GPT-OSS Medium (20B), and NVIDIA Nemotron Nano-9B-v2. A notable finding was that applying RSA enabled the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 model to achieve performance levels competitive with, and in some cases surpassing, significantly larger reasoning models such as DeepSeek-R1 and o3-mini (high) that were not using RSA.

- **Hyperparameter Analysis:**
 - **Sequential Depth (T):** Performance generally improved monotonically with an increasing number of RSA steps (T), confirming the benefit of iterative refinement.
 - **Aggregation Size (K):** Increasing the aggregation set size K led to improved performance. The most significant gain was observed when moving from K=1 (equivalent to self-refinement) to K=2, highlighting the value of combining multiple reasoning chains. Diminishing returns were observed beyond K=3 on most tasks, potentially due to context length limitations.
 - **Population Size (N):** Increasing the population size N initially enhanced performance. However, scaling N to very large values sometimes resulted in a small performance decrease on certain tasks, suggesting an optimal balance is required.
 - **Pass@N as Predictor:** The Pass@N score (at least one correct solution in N candidates) served as an upper bound for Pass@1 performance. The gap between Pass@N and Pass@1 decreased faster for smaller N, indicating quicker mixing of high-quality chains in smaller populations. Larger N offered higher asymptotic performance potential but required more iterations (T) or larger aggregation sizes (K) for effective mixing.
 - **Tuning under Compute Budgets:** The analysis provided guidance for optimizing RSA under compute constraints: N controls asymptotic performance; K (for K>1) accelerates mixing; T monotonically improves performance. A large N necessitates a proportionally large K or T for optimal results.

- **Impact of Aggregation-Aware RL:**
 - Standard RL fine-tuning generally *degraded* RSA performance when compared to using the base reference model. This supported the hypothesis that a mismatch exists between standard RL training and aggregation-based inference.
 - Aggregation-aware RL training, which jointly optimized for standard and aggregation prompts, *outperformed* standard RL in all five test cases and significantly improved upon the reference model in four out of five tasks (AIME-25 being the exception).
 - Notably, aggregation-aware RL yielded substantial gains on LiveCodeBench (code generation) despite the training dataset not containing coding problems, suggesting the learned aggregation skills exhibit strong out-of-domain transferability.

In summary, the results indicate that RSA is a powerful and general test-time scaling method, capable of significant performance improvements, particularly when combined with aggregation-aware RL training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Recursive Self-Aggregation (RSA) represents a notable advancement in test-time scaling methods for Large Language Models. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** RSA provides a mechanism for LLMs to achieve deeper and more reliable reasoning by iteratively combining and refining multiple candidate solutions. This enables models to overcome individual errors, leverage partial insights from diverse reasoning paths, and effectively self-correct over multiple steps.
- **Cost-Effectiveness and Democratization of Advanced LLMs:** By extracting substantially improved performance from existing LLMs, RSA offers a more cost-effective way to achieve high reasoning abilities. The finding that a smaller model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) with RSA can match or exceed the performance of larger, more resource-intensive models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, o3-mini) without RSA, and that Gemini 3 Flash with RSA can outperform Gemini 3 Deep Think at a fraction of the cost, suggests that advanced reasoning capabilities could become more accessible. This could reduce the reliance on continually scaling up model parameters.
- **Generalizability and Robustness:** RSA operates without requiring external verifiers or making strong assumptions about the structure of reasoning chains, making it broadly applicable across diverse tasks such as math, code generation, general reasoning, and knowledge recall. Its consistent gains across various model families and sizes underscore its robustness as a test-time scaling strategy.
- **Improved RL Alignment:** The development of aggregation-aware reinforcement learning addresses a critical limitation where standard RL fine-tuning can hinder performance in aggregation-based inference. This novel training approach ensures that models are explicitly optimized for the kind of "deep thinking" facilitated by RSA, leading to synergistic performance gains and increased model robustness when aggregation is employed. The observed out-of-domain transferability of aggregation skills suggests a fundamental improvement in how LLMs handle complex information synthesis.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** RSA's evolutionary algorithm-inspired design opens new avenues for research, particularly in integrating more sophisticated evolutionary concepts. Future work could explore combining RSA with explicit self-verification mechanisms to introduce a fitness function, or developing multi-step reinforcement learning algorithms to optimize the entire RSA procedure, moving beyond greedy single-step aggregation.
- **Practical Implementation:** The method is described as simple to implement, making it potentially adoptable by practitioners seeking to enhance their LLM applications without extensive architectural changes or specialized external tools. Its compatibility with existing LLM inference pipelines could facilitate broader integration.

In conclusion, RSA contributes to making LLMs more intelligent, versatile, and efficient at inference time, offering a path towards more sophisticated problem-solving capabilities without necessarily requiring continuous increases in model size.
