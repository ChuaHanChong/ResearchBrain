# Aux-Think: Exploring Reasoning Strategies for Data-Efficient Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China and Horizon Robotics developed Aux-Think, a framework for Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) that integrates explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning during training through auxiliary tasks, while performing direct action prediction at inference. This approach achieves higher navigation success rates on R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks compared to direct CoT application and demonstrates competitive performance with state-of-the-art models using less training data.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents struggled with robust generalization and long-horizon tasks, often requiring vast, diverse datasets for training.
- Applying explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, successful in static tasks, directly to dynamic, action-centric VLN environments led to a "Test-time Reasoning Collapse" (TRC), degrading performance.
- A lack of systematic evaluation for various reasoning strategies in VLN and the absence of large-scale, CoT-annotated VLN datasets hindered research into reasoning-aware navigation agents.

## Method

- ==Constructed R2R-CoT==-320k, the first large-scale Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotated dataset for VLN, by leveraging a powerful VLM to generate reasoning traces for existing R2R-CE trajectories.
- Introduced **Aux-Think**, a co-training strategy that incorporates auxiliary tasks for CoT-based reasoning and instruction reconstruction during training.
- The framework employs receding-horizon action planning as the primary task during training and performs direct action prediction at inference, thereby decoupling explicit reasoning from test-time execution to avoid TRC.

## Results

- Aux-Think achieved a **46.0%** Success Rate (SR) and **40.5%** Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL) on R2R-CE Val-Unseen when trained on only 320K data, outperforming No-Think (**35.1%** SR, **30.2%** SPL) and explicit reasoning methods.
- The framework demonstrated competitive performance on R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks using substantially less training data; for instance, it achieved **54.8%** SR and **46.9%** SPL on R2R-CE Val-Unseen with 1600K data, comparable to or exceeding models using 2770K-5570K data.
- Aux-Think showed strong robustness for long-horizon tasks and improved cross-dataset generalization, achieving the highest reported SR (**29.5%**) and SPL (**23.6%**) on RxR-CE Val-Unseen when trained exclusively on R2R-CoT-320k.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct application of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning at test time in dynamic VLN tasks results in "Test-time Reasoning Collapse" (TRC) due to error accumulation from inaccurate or hallucinated reasoning in off-distribution states.
> - Decoupling reasoning acquisition during training (via auxiliary tasks) from direct action execution at inference time is an effective strategy, allowing models to internalize structured reasoning patterns without the performance overhead or risks of explicit CoT generation during navigation.
> - Combining CoT-based reasoning and instruction reconstruction as auxiliary training tasks with receding-horizon action planning significantly enhances navigation performance, robustness, and data efficiency for VLN agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a foundational task in embodied artificial intelligence, where an agent interprets natural language instructions to navigate through complex, real-world environments. Historically, VLN methods relied on components such as waypoint predictors or topological maps, often struggling with generalization and the sim-to-real gap.

Recent advancements in large pretrained models, specifically Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have shifted the paradigm towards direct action prediction via supervised fine-tuning on paired visual data and instructions. These models have demonstrated improved generalization and instruction grounding in VLN, particularly in continuous environments (VLN-CE) that simulate realistic scenarios without relying on discretized actions or panoramic observations. Examples include models like NaVid, Uni-NaVid, and NaVILA, which leverage extensive datasets to achieve their performance.

Despite these advancements, the application and effectiveness of explicit reasoning strategies, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), in the context of action-centric and long-horizon tasks like VLN have remained underexplored. CoT has shown success in static tasks like visual question answering and video understanding by generating intermediate reasoning steps before producing a final answer. This paper addresses this gap by systematically evaluating different reasoning strategies in VLN and proposing an alternative approach that integrates reasoning during training without explicit inference at test time. The work distinguishes itself by focusing on reasoning methodologies rather than solely on model architecture or data scale, aiming for data-efficient solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Systematic Evaluation of Reasoning Strategies in VLN**: The paper aims to conduct the first systematic study of how different reasoning strategies integrate with VLN. Prior work on CoT predominantly focused on static tasks, leaving its utility in dynamic, embodied navigation largely unexplored. The authors investigate three distinct paradigms:
 - **No-Think**: Direct action prediction without explicit reasoning.
 - **Pre-Think**: Generating an explicit CoT reasoning trace *before* predicting an action.
 - **Post-Think**: Predicting an action *before* generating a retrospective reasoning trace.

2. **Addressing the "Test-time Reasoning Collapse" (TRC) Issue**: A core motivation is the observed performance degradation when explicit CoT reasoning is applied during test time in VLN tasks. The authors term this phenomenon "Test-time Reasoning Collapse." This issue arises because models trained on optimal (oracle) trajectories may generate inaccurate or hallucinated reasoning when encountering off-distribution states during navigation, leading to error accumulation and cascading failures in action prediction. This highlights a fundamental limitation of multi-turn explicit reasoning in dynamic, partially observable environments.

3. **Developing a Data-Efficient and Robust Reasoning Framework (Aux-Think)**: Motivated by the TRC issue, the paper aims to develop a novel reasoning strategy that benefits from CoT supervision without suffering from its test-time vulnerabilities. The objective is to enable models to internalize structured reasoning patterns during training, thereby improving data efficiency and navigation performance, while maintaining direct action prediction at inference time for robustness and speed. This approach is inspired by the dual-process theory of human learning, where explicit understanding during learning transitions to implicit skill-based execution.

4. **Creating a Chain-of-Thought Dataset for VLN**: To support the proposed framework and facilitate future research into reasoning-aware VLN agents, a crucial objective is the creation and release of a large-scale, CoT-annotated dataset specifically for VLN. Existing datasets lacked aligned CoT-style reasoning traces necessary for training models in this paradigm.

In summary, the research is driven by the need to integrate reasoning capabilities into VLN models effectively, overcome the challenges of explicit test-time reasoning in dynamic environments, and develop a more data-efficient training paradigm, all while providing the necessary dataset for such advancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves a multi-faceted approach, encompassing problem setup, dataset construction, systematic evaluation of existing reasoning paradigms, and the development of a novel co-training strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The study focuses on monocular Vision-and-Language Navigation in continuous environments (VLN-CE). An embodied agent is tasked with navigating photo-realistic indoor environments by following natural language instructions. At each timestep, the agent receives:
- A natural language instruction (paragraph).
- An RGB observation from the current viewpoint.
- Historical observations (8 uniformly sampled frames, including the first).
The agent then selects an action from a continuous space (move forward, turn left/right by specific degrees, or stop). The objective is to follow the instruction accurately and efficiently to the target. The VLN model (based on NVILA 8B) is trained via supervised fine-tuning to imitate expert demonstrations, where each trajectory provides sequences of navigation context (observations + instruction) and expert actions.

#### 3.2. R2R-CoT-320k Dataset Construction

To enable reasoning-aware training, the authors constructed R2R-CoT-320k, the first VLN dataset with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations, tailored for the R2R-CE benchmark.
- **Trajectory Reconstruction**: Step-wise navigation trajectories from the Habitat simulator were reconstructed.
- **Annotation Generation**: For each navigation sample (current view, historical visual context, instruction, ground-truth action), the Qwen-2.5-VL-72B VLM was used to generate detailed CoT reasoning.
- **Formatting**: For Pre-Think and Post-Think strategies, reasoning traces were formatted with `<think></think>` and `<answer></answer>` tags.
- **Scale and Diversity**: The dataset comprises over 320,000 reasoning traces, covering a range of scenarios and CoT content. Analysis showed CoT lengths mostly between 200-300 words, with some exceeding 450 words, indicating varied complexity.

#### 3.3. Systematic Investigation on Reasoning Strategies for VLN

The paper systematically compared three reasoning strategies by modifying the training loss for the VLN model (πθ) using the R2R-CoT-320k dataset:

- **No-Think**: The model directly predicts the next action (a\*t) conditioned on observations (Ot, ot) and instruction (Iτ). Loss: log πθ(a\*t | Ot, ot, Iτ). This serves as a baseline for direct action prediction.
- **Pre-Think**: The model generates a CoT trace (c\*t) *before* predicting the action (a\*t). The action is conditioned on the CoT. Loss: log πθ(< c\*t, a\*t > | Ot, ot, Iτ).
- **Post-Think**: The model predicts the action (a\*t) *before* generating a retrospective CoT trace (c\*t) explaining the decision. Loss: log πθ(< a\*t, c\*t > | Ot, ot, Iτ).

Experiments also explored adjusting the loss weight of the CoT part to analyze its impact on performance. This systematic comparison led to the identification of the "Test-time Reasoning Collapse" (TRC) issue.

#### 3.4. Aux-Think: Reasoning-Aware Co-Training Strategy

To address the TRC issue, Aux-Think was proposed, which leverages reasoning solely during training through auxiliary tasks, while maintaining direct action prediction at test time. This framework includes three distinct tasks during training:

1. **CoT-based Reasoning (Auxiliary Task)**: The model is trained to generate CoT traces (c\*t) conditioned on the instruction, current observation, and historical observations. This explicitly encourages the model to acquire structured reasoning patterns. Loss: LCoTτ(θ) = − Σt log πθ(c\*t | Ot, ot, Iτ).
2. **Instruction-based Reasoning (Auxiliary Task)**: The model is trained to reconstruct the original natural language instruction (Iτ) given a sequence of visual observations (Σt ot). This inverse task provides complementary semantic grounding and strengthens the vision-language alignment. Loss: LInsτ(θ) = − log πθ(Iτ | Σt ot).
3. **Receding-Horizon Action Planning (Primary Task)**: The model predicts a sequence of the next 'n' actions (a_t..., a_t+n-1) based on the instruction, current observation, and history. This encourages short-term forecasting and reactivity. Loss: LActτ(θ) = − Σt Σk log πθ(a\*t+k | Ot, ot, Iτ).

**Training and Testing**:
- During training, all three tasks are co-trained using a combined loss function: L = Στ (LActτ(θ) + LCoTτ(θ) + LInsτ(θ)). Task switching is achieved by changing the prompt.
- During testing, only the Receding-Horizon Action Planning task is activated. The model predicts the next 'n' actions but executes only the first predicted action, thereby avoiding explicit CoT generation and its associated risks.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Base Model**: NVILA-lite 8B, which consists of a vision encoder (SigLIP), a projector, and an LLM (Qwen 2).
- **Training**: Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) from stage 2 of NVILA-lite, trained for one epoch (approx. 60 hours) on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs with a learning rate of 1e-5.
- **Action Space**: Discretized into four categories: move forward (25cm, 50cm, 75cm), turn left (15°, 30°, 45°), turn right (15°, 30°, 45°), and stop.
- **Receding Horizon**: The number of predicted steps 'n' was an object of ablation, with 3 steps yielding optimal performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding reasoning strategies in VLN and the performance of the proposed Aux-Think framework.

#### 4.1. Test-time Reasoning Collapse (TRC)

- **Finding 1**: A systematic comparison of reasoning strategies (No-Think, Pre-Think, Post-Think) on the R2R-CoT-320k dataset revealed that Pre-Think and Post-Think consistently performed worse than No-Think (direct action prediction). For example, No-Think achieved a 35.1% Success Rate (SR) and 30.2% Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL), while Pre-Think yielded only 11.4% SR and 8.6% SPL, and Post-Think achieved 29.0% SR and 23.8% SPL (Table 3, Figure 1a).
- **Explanation**: This "Test-time Reasoning Collapse" (TRC) is attributed to a training-testing mismatch. Models are trained on optimal (oracle) trajectories, but at test time, agents frequently encounter off-distribution states. In these unfamiliar states, the explicitly generated CoT by Pre-Think or Post-Think can be inaccurate or hallucinated, leading to incorrect actions and cascading errors along the trajectory (Figure 3).
- **Finding 2**: While carefully balancing the explicit CoT loss weight (e.g., reducing it from 1 to 0.1) provided slight performance gains for Pre-Think and Post-Think (Table 7), their performance still lagged behind No-Think. This indicated that even with optimized CoT supervision, explicit test-time reasoning remained unreliable in dynamic VLN environments.

#### 4.2. Performance of Aux-Think

- **Superiority over other Reasoning Strategies**: Aux-Think consistently outperformed No-Think, Pre-Think, and Post-Think when trained only on R2R-CoT-320k. Aux-Think achieved an SR of 41.3% and SPL of 35.8%, significantly higher than No-Think's 35.1% SR and 30.2% SPL (Table 3, Figure 1a). It also demonstrated faster inference times (1.25s average time) comparable to No-Think, unlike Pre-Think and Post-Think which took significantly longer (30.62s and 28.97s respectively) due to explicit CoT generation.
- **Robustness to Long-Horizon Tasks**: Aux-Think maintained strong performance on longer navigation tasks, where Pre-Think and Post-Think showed sharp performance degradation. Its SR remained relatively stable even for tasks exceeding 70 steps, indicating superior scalability and generalization to complex, multi-step scenarios (Figure 6a).

#### 4.3. Comparison on VLN-CE Benchmarks (R2R-CE and RxR-CE)

- **R2R-CE Val-Unseen**: When trained solely on R2R-CoT-320k (0K extra data), Aux-Think achieved an SR of 46.0% and SPL of 40.5%, outperforming NaVid (24.7% SR, 23.6% SPL) and establishing a strong baseline (Table 1).
 - With additional training data (RxR, DAgger, web data, totaling 1600K extra data), Aux-Think reached an SR of 54.8% and SPL of 46.9% on R2R-CE Val-Unseen. This performance was competitive with, and in some metrics surpassed, state-of-the-art methods like Uni-NaVid (47.0% SR, 42.7% SPL with 5570K data) and NaVILA (54.0% SR, 49.0% SPL with 2770K data), while using significantly less training data (Figure 1b, Table 1). This indicates data efficiency.
- **RxR-CE Val-Unseen**: On the more challenging RxR-CE benchmark, Aux-Think achieved an SR of 52.2% and SPL of 40.2% with 1920K training data. This performance exceeded Uni-NaVid (48.7% SR, 40.9% SPL with 5900K data) and NaVILA (49.3% SR, 44.0% SPL with 3100K data) in terms of SR, again with fewer training examples (Table 2). This suggests strong generalization and data efficiency, although NE and SPL were noted as relatively modest, potentially due to broader exploration encouraged by internalized reasoning.
- **Cross-dataset Generalization**: Aux-Think demonstrated strong cross-dataset generalization. When trained only on R2R-CoT-320k and evaluated on RxR-CE Val-Unseen (without RxR-CE training data), Aux-Think achieved an SR of 29.5% and SPL of 23.6%, which was the highest reported among methods without RxR-CE training (Table 6).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Auxiliary Tasks and Receding-Horizon Action Planning**:
 - Introducing CoT-based Reasoning (A) as an auxiliary task led to a notable improvement across all metrics compared to the No-Think baseline (e.g., SR increased from 35.1% to 41.3%, SPL from 30.2% to 35.8%).
 - Adding Instruction-based Reasoning (B) alongside CoT Reasoning (A+B) further enhanced performance (SR 44.2%, SPL 38.9%), indicating the complementarity of these two forms of reasoning.
 - The full Aux-Think model (A+B+C), incorporating Receding-Horizon Action Planning, achieved the best results (SR 46.0%, SPL 40.5%), highlighting the benefit of integrating both reasoning and planning for robust behavior (Table 4).
- **Impact of Predicted Steps in Receding-Horizon Action Planning**:
 - Ablation studies showed that setting the number of predicted steps to 3 yielded the best performance across metrics (SR 41.4%, SPL 36.1%).
 - Increasing steps beyond 3 led to performance degradation, attributed to the challenges of long-horizon prediction with limited monocular observations and lack of global knowledge (Table 5).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI and Vision-Language Navigation in several ways, with implications for future development:

1. **Fundamental Understanding of Reasoning in VLN**: The work provides the first systematic investigation into various Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning strategies in dynamic, long-horizon VLN tasks. The identification and characterization of the "Test-time Reasoning Collapse" (TRC) issue is a significant finding, demonstrating the inherent difficulties of applying explicit, multi-turn reasoning in partially observable and off-distribution environments. This finding challenges the direct transfer of CoT successes from static tasks to embodied intelligence.

2. **Novel and Robust Reasoning Framework**: Aux-Think offers a new paradigm for integrating reasoning into embodied agents. By decoupling reasoning from test-time execution, it leverages the benefits of CoT supervision (internalizing structured reasoning patterns, improving data efficiency) without incurring the performance degradation and latency associated with explicit reasoning during inference. This approach leads to superior navigation performance and robustness, particularly in complex, multi-step scenarios, as evidenced by its sustained success rates on longer trajectories.

3. **Enhanced Data Efficiency**: Aux-Think demonstrates that reasoning-aware co-training can significantly reduce the amount of training data required to achieve competitive or state-of-the-art performance. This is a crucial impact, as collecting and annotating large-scale datasets for embodied AI is resource-intensive. The ability to achieve high success rates with a fraction of the data used by other large-model-based methods (e.g., Uni-NaVid, NaVILA) suggests a more efficient pathway for training capable navigation agents.

4. **Publicly Available Chain-of-Thought Dataset**: The release of R2R-CoT-320k, the first large-scale CoT-annotated dataset for VLN, is a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset enables further exploration and development of reasoning-aware VLN agents, providing a benchmark for models to learn and evaluate explicit thought processes in navigation.

5. **Implications for Embodied AI**: The principles of Aux-Think—training with auxiliary reasoning while performing direct action prediction—could be extended to other embodied AI tasks beyond navigation, such as manipulation or interaction in dynamic environments. It suggests a promising direction for developing intelligent agents that learn robust skills through explicit reasoning but execute efficiently through internalized knowledge.

**Limitations**: The authors acknowledge that the work evaluates Aux-Think's data efficiency under a controlled, widely adopted setup (SFT on R2R with monocular RGB input). This controlled setting enables fair comparison and isolates the effect of reasoning-aware supervision but also implies future work on scaling to larger navigation datasets and incorporating richer supervision (e.g., depth, panorama, localization). These limitations suggest that while the approach is promising, its generalizability to all possible real-world complexities and alternative sensor modalities warrants further investigation.
