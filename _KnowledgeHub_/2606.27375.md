# Scalable Behavior Cloning with Open Data, Training, and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ABC project from Amazon FAR and UC Berkeley introduces a comprehensive open-source "deep stack" for robot manipulation, encompassing a 3,500-hour teleoperation dataset (ABC-130K), scalable training infrastructure, policies (ABC-Models), and a simulation-to-real evaluation proxy (ABC-Sim). This system supports the training of policies for complex bimanual tasks, demonstrating strong sim-to-real correlation and substantial inference optimizations.

## Problem

- High costs and complexity of robot hardware and data collection limit the scale and diversity of open datasets for robot manipulation research.
- Inconsistent training methodologies and resource-intensive real-world evaluations hinder reproducible comparisons and comprehensive architectural explorations.
- State-of-the-art robot learning systems are often developed in industry with proprietary data and infrastructure, making them difficult for external researchers to replicate or build upon.

## Method

- Develops and releases the **ABC** stack: an open-source, end-to-end system encompassing hardware details, data, training code, models, and evaluation tools for behavior cloning.
- Provides ==ABC-130K==, a large-scale (3,553 hours, 130,000 episodes, 195 tasks) bimanual teleoperation dataset collected on a low-cost robot, with an optimized dataloader for efficient video processing.
- Introduces ABC-Sim, a simulation pipeline with 400 hours of VR teleoperation data, designed to serve as a low-cost, reliable proxy for real-world policy evaluation with demonstrated strong correlation.

## Results

- The ABC-130K dataset, with 3,553 hours of bimanual teleoperation data across 195 tasks, is released as the largest open-source resource for robot manipulation, enabling generalist policy training.
- Trained ABC-DiT and ABC-VLA policies achieve robust performance on complex, long-horizon tasks, including box folding and credit card extraction, with DAgger significantly improving dexterous task capabilities.
- Inference latency for both DiT and VLA models was substantially reduced (e.g., ABC-VLA to **17.5** ms from **47.8** ms) through mixed precision, caching, and `torch.compile` optimizations, and policy conditioning (operator-ID, subtask) improved task execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token-level visual information and pixel-aligned encoders like DINOv3 within cross-attention architectures improve Diffusion Transformer (DiT) policy performance for robot manipulation tasks.
> - A pooled AdaLN conditioning scheme effectively integrates Vision-Language Model (VLM) features into the diffusion action head, leading to superior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model performance compared to other methods.
> - Offline metrics like training loss and validation action error serve as useful, cost-effective proxies for guiding minor policy design and hyperparameter choices, though not for predicting exact real-world success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation, particularly learning complex skills through behavior cloning (BC), faces several challenges that limit widespread progress and reproducibility. State-of-the-art systems are frequently developed within industrial research laboratories, utilizing proprietary datasets and internal infrastructure. This often results in training recipes and design choices that are not fully detailed or are challenging to replicate by external researchers, creating an opaque research frontier. The necessary stack for BC, encompassing hardware setup, data collection, efficient training systems, effective learning recipes, and rigorous real-world evaluation, is extensive and costly.

Existing open datasets have contributed to the field's advancement but possess limitations. DROID and BridgeData-V2 offer high-quality single-arm data but are primarily focused on simpler pick-and-place tasks. Open X-Embodiment aggregates data from multiple robot embodiments and institutions, yet its heterogeneity can lead to wide variations in data quality, noisy metadata, and diverse action spaces, complicating its utility. AgiBot-World provides a substantial volume of bimanual data, but it is collected on an expensive robot platform (approximately $30,000). MolmoAct2 offers 720 hours of bimanual data on the YAM platform, which is closer in spirit to the presented work, but still at a smaller scale. These limitations mean that researchers often lack access to sufficiently large-scale, high-quality, and diverse datasets that are both collected on accessible hardware and accompanied by comprehensive, open-source infrastructure for training and evaluation.

In terms of policy architectures, recent robot learning systems often employ diffusion policies built on pretrained visual representations or Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that adapt pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones for robot control [11–14]. While high-level design principles, such as the efficacy of diffusion objectives and the benefits of reusing pretrained visual features, are recognized, the specific implementations and fine-grained details of these training recipes are not consistently shared. This lack of transparency, coupled with proprietary datasets and varying compute budgets, impedes the community's ability to rigorously compare and evaluate different architectural and training decisions.

The ABC project positions itself to address these bottlenecks by introducing a fully open-source, end-to-end stack. It aims to integrate the benefits of previous work (high-quality data, bimanual manipulation) while overcoming their limitations (scale, cost, opacity). By releasing a large-scale dataset on low-cost hardware, along with transparent training and evaluation infrastructure, ABC seeks to establish a foundational, reproducible toolkit that enables researchers to investigate complex manipulation tasks and architectural choices without the historical barriers of resource disparity and proprietary information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the ABC project is to mitigate significant bottlenecks hindering progress in robot manipulation research, particularly in the domain of behavior cloning. The existing challenges render advancements opaque and difficult to reproduce, thereby slowing down collective community development.

The paper identifies several core problems that define the "deep stack" of behavior cloning:
1. **Hardware and Data Collection Barriers**: Researchers must first establish robot hardware setups and then undertake the resource-intensive process of collecting diverse, high-quality demonstrations. The complexity and cost associated with this initial phase often limit the scale and variety of available datasets.
2. **Lack of Standardized Training Methodologies**: There is no established consensus on efficient training systems capable of handling heterogeneous data streams or on optimal learning recipes for behavior cloning. This forces individual researchers to develop or adapt their own solutions, leading to inconsistencies and difficulties in comparing results across different studies.
3. **Expensive and Logistically Demanding Real-World Evaluation**: Rigorous evaluation of policy design decisions typically necessitates extensive real-world robot trials. These evaluations are costly, time-consuming, and logistically complex, which discourages comprehensive architectural ablations and hyperparameter tuning.
4. **Opaque and Proprietary Systems**: A significant portion of state-of-the-art robot learning systems are developed in industry labs using proprietary datasets and internal infrastructure. While some training recipes may be described, they frequently lack sufficient detail for external reproduction, obscuring the precise design choices that drive performance.

To address these limitations, the ABC project sets forth the following key objectives:
1. **Develop and Release a Large-Scale, Open-Source Teleoperation Dataset (ABC-130K)**: The primary goal is to provide the largest open dataset for robot manipulation, comprising 3,500 hours of real-world interaction across over 130,000 episodes and 195 distinct tasks. This dataset is designed to offer diverse manipulation primitives, including highly dexterous behaviors, collected on an accessible, low-cost bimanual hardware setup. The aim is to overcome the scale and task diversity limitations of existing open datasets.
2. **Open-Source Comprehensive Infrastructure**: To ensure accessibility and reproducibility, the project aims to release not only the dataset but also the entire accompanying infrastructure. This includes the hardware setup details, training and deployment code, and a simulation pipeline. This objective seeks to lower the barrier to entry for researchers by providing a complete, ready-to-use toolkit.
3. **Establish a Reliable Simulation-to-Real Evaluation Proxy (ABC-Sim)**: A crucial objective is to provide a simulation environment coupled with simulation teleoperation data (400 hours). By demonstrating strong correlation between simulation and real-world evaluation performance, the project aims to offer a reliable and low-cost method for researchers to iterate on model designs and training decisions before expensive physical robot deployments. This allows researchers without physical hardware to contribute effectively.
4. **Conduct and Disseminate Architectural and Training Recipe Explorations (ABC-Models)**: The project aims to systematically explore and evaluate various training recipes and architectural design choices for common robot learning models, specifically Diffusion Transformers (DiT) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Findings are grounded in real-world evaluations, with the resulting policies and model weights being publicly released. This objective seeks to provide empirical insights and establish strong baselines.
5. **Foster Community Collaboration and Accelerate Research (ABC-Eval)**: Ultimately, the project's overarching motivation is to accelerate progress in robot learning by providing a comprehensive, extensible, and reproducible manipulation stack. By placing researchers on an equal footing with open data, models, and evaluation tools, ABC aims to facilitate collective exploration and discovery in the field of behavior cloning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ABC project introduces an integrated, open-source stack for robot manipulation based on behavior cloning. This stack comprises four main components: ABC-130K (data), ABC-Models (policies), ABC-Sim (simulation), and ABC-Eval (evaluation).

**ABC-130K Dataset:**
The core of the ABC stack is ABC-130K, a large-scale teleoperation dataset. It consists of 3,553 hours of real-world bimanual manipulation data, spanning 134,806 episodes across 195 distinct tasks. The tasks are categorized into 7 primitives: Pick-and-Place, Fine Pick-and-Place, Folding, Insertion/Ejection, Tool Use, Sorting, and Tying/Untying, covering a spectrum from simple transfers to dexterous behaviors like folding paper boxes and retrieving credit cards. The data is collected on a low-cost, bimanual YAM workstation equipped with three Intel RealSense D405 cameras (one top, two gripper-mounted) streaming at 30Hz. The dataset includes teleoperation metadata such as anonymized teleoperator IDs and collection timestamps. A subset of 1,552 hours also features subgoal annotations, providing contiguous sub-trajectories labeled with subtask descriptions.
To facilitate efficient data loading, a custom dataloader named `abcdl` was developed. Data is stored as pairs of MP4 video files (concatenated camera views) and binary files (states and actions). MP4 encoding uses `+faststart`, disabled B-frames, and constant GOP (Group of Pictures) with GOP=30 to enable analytical reconstruction of the file index, allowing for fast, random frame access and reduced disk I/O. This design supports both local filesystem and cloud object storage streaming.

**ABC-Models (Policy Architectures and Training):**
The project investigates two policy families: ABC-DiT (diffusion-transformer) and ABC-VLA (vision-language-action model with a diffusion action head). Policies take three 224x224 images and a 14D proprioceptive joint state as input, along with a task language instruction. They output a 30-step chunk of absolute joint-position targets, with states and actions normalized using z-score normalization. Training is conducted on NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

- **ABC-DiT**: This model employs a diffusion transformer backbone with 32 layers, 24 attention heads, and a hidden dimension of 1536. Architectural variants were explored for the vision encoder and conditioning scheme:
 - *CLIP-AdaLN*: Uses a CLIP ViT-B image encoder, CLIP text encoder, and standard AdaLN for conditioning.
 - *CLIP-Cross-Attention*: Uses CLIP ViT-B, but the DiT cross-attends to sequences of visual tokens from learned latent queries (12 per image) rather than a single compressed vector, with non-visual conditioning via AdaLN.
 - *DINO-Cross-Attention*: Replaces CLIP ViT-B with a DINOv3 ViT-B encoder within the cross-attention architecture. This variant was selected as the final ABC-DiT model due to performance, totaling 2 billion parameters.
 Training uses AdamW optimizer, a linear learning rate warmup to 1e-4, constant learning rate, weight decay of 0.01, and gradient clipping. A learning rate scale of 0.1 is applied to the vision encoder. Proprioception input is dropped out with 0.1 probability.
- **ABC-VLA**: This model is built upon Gemma 3 (a 4B-parameter VLM with a SigLIP vision encoder and Gemma language decoder) coupled with a smaller DiT action head (45M parameters). The VLM backbone is finetuned end-to-end. Connector mechanisms between the VLM and action head were compared:
 - *Cross Attention*: VLM final-layer token features are projected and serve as keys/values for a cross-attention layer in each DiT block, with diffusion gradients flowing back to finetune the VLM.
 - *Cross Attention + FAST*: Uses FAST-tokenized actions with a next-token-prediction CE objective to finetune the VLM, with detached VLM features for DiT cross-attention to insulate the VLM from diffusion gradients.
 - *Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN)*: VLM features are summarized by an attention-pooling module into 8 tokens, flattened, and projected to produce AdaLN shifts, scales, and residual gates for the DiT. This variant was chosen as the final ABC-VLA model due to performance.
 Training uses AdamW, linear learning rate warmup to 1e-4, constant learning rate, weight decay of 0.01, and gradient clipping. For variance reduction, the VLM pass is amortized by replicating VLM features and clean actions multiple times (e.g., k=8) with independent diffusion noise/timestep samples.

**ABC-Sim (Simulation Pipeline and Data):**
ABC-Sim provides a suite of MuJoCo-based simulation environments that replicate the real robot setup. It generates low-fidelity images and offers a Blender pipeline for higher-fidelity, path-traced renders. Ten tasks, including throwing bottles and turning mugs, are provided. Over 400 hours of VR teleoperation data (using Meta Quest/Apple Vision Pro headsets and GELLO leader arms) are released, along with the VR teleoperation stack itself. Pass-through rendering is used to mitigate motion sickness during data collection.

**ABC-Eval (Evaluation and Optimization):**
- **Real-world Evaluation**: Policies are executed at 30 Hz using asynchronous execution with an action prefix. Tasks are evaluated over 50 trials, measuring strict success and task progress based on defined rubrics.
- **Offline Metrics**: Training loss, validation loss (conditional flow matching loss), and validation action error (L2 distance between predicted and ground-truth action chunks after 10 diffusion steps) are tracked.
- **Sim-to-Real Correlation**: ABC-DiT and ABC-VLA checkpoints are evaluated in both simulation and real-world across three tasks (bottle throwing, dishrack loading, mug flipping) to assess correlation.
- **Finetuning and DAgger**: The effectiveness of pretrained policies as initializations for downstream single-task finetuning (e.g., credit card extraction, LEGO sorting) is evaluated. DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) is employed for complex dexterous tasks like box folding. A state machine facilitates DAgger data collection, allowing operators to intervene and collect recovery behaviors, with a specific data mixing ratio (80% previous data, 10% current interventions, 10% rest of current episodes).
- **Inference Optimization**: Techniques such as bf16 mixed precision, caching visual embeddings/VLM features, and `torch.compile` with `fullgraph=True`, kernel autotuning, and CUDA graph capture are used to reduce inference latency.
- **Policy Conditioning**: The impact of operator-ID conditioning, action prefix length, and subtask conditioning on policy behavior and performance is investigated, particularly on the t-shirt folding task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings derived from the development and evaluation of the ABC stack.

**ABC-130K Dataset Characteristics:**
The ABC-130K dataset establishes itself as the largest open-source teleoperation dataset, comprising 3,553 hours of data across over 130,000 episodes and 195 diverse tasks. It covers a wide range of manipulation primitives on a low-cost, bimanual robot system. The optimized dataloader efficiently processes this scale of video data, achieving higher decode throughput and significantly reducing per-decode read volume compared to naive approaches.

**Architectural Ablations (ABC-Models):**
- **ABC-DiT (Diffusion Transformer)**: Comparisons of DiT variants (Table 1) indicated that preserving token-level visual information through pooled cross-attention improved task progress compared to global AdaLN-based conditioning. Furthermore, replacing the CLIP image encoder with DINOv3 in the cross-attention architecture yielded additional performance gains, suggesting that pixel-aligned representations like DINOv3 are beneficial for manipulation tasks. The DINOv3-Cross-Attention variant was selected as the ABC-DiT model, featuring 2 billion parameters.
- **ABC-VLA (Vision-Language-Action)**: For VLA models based on Gemma 3, the pooled AdaLN conditioning scheme for routing VLM features to the diffusion action head demonstrated superior performance in terms of aggregate task progress (61.4%) compared to vanilla cross-attention (11.7%) and cross-attention with FAST co-training (32.6%). This finding suggests that a proper interface can make diffusion gradients compatible with VLM features, even without directly exposing VLM tokens to the DiT. The Gemma 3 + SigLIP + pooled AdaLN variant (4B VLM + 45M DiT) was chosen as the ABC-VLA model.

**Scaling Compute Across Batch Size and Training Steps:**
Experiments (Figure 5) indicated that increasing training steps and batch size generally improved policy performance for both ABC-DiT and ABC-VLA. While ABC-DiT exhibited greater FLOP-efficiency at smaller batch sizes, ABC-VLA demonstrated a more substantial performance increase as the batch size scaled to 9216, eventually surpassing ABC-DiT in real-world metrics despite ABC-DiT being generally more compute-efficient. The use of multiple diffusion draws (k=8) for VLA training was shown to reduce training loss at a fixed GPU-hour budget (Figure 6), enhancing efficiency.

**Offline Metrics for Policy Evaluation:**
Analysis of correlations between offline diagnostics and real-world performance (Figure 8) revealed that training loss and validation action error were negatively correlated with real-world strict success and task progress. Validation action error showed the strongest negative correlation (r = -0.89, ρ = -0.93 for strict success). In contrast, validation loss did not exhibit a significant correlation with real-world performance. This suggests that training loss and validation action error can serve as cost-effective scalar proxies for guiding minor architectural and hyperparameter decisions, although they do not reliably predict exact task success rates or failure modes.

**ABC-Sim and Sim-to-Real Correlation:**
Evaluations demonstrated a strong correlation between simulation performance and real-world performance for both ABC-DiT and ABC-VLA policies (Figure 9). Pearson correlations were 0.85 for strict success (p = 4.2 × 10⁻⁴) and 0.91 for task progress (p = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵), indicating that simulation provides a meaningful predictor of real-world outcomes. This establishes ABC-Sim as a valuable tool for debugging and iterating on policy designs without requiring physical robots.

**Real-World Capabilities:**
- **Pretraining Capabilities**: Policies trained on ABC-130K demonstrated the ability to execute long-horizon and dexterous tasks, such as inserting 6 AirPods in a row, unlocking a lockbox, folding a cardboard box, and packing a student bag. Quantitative evaluations on tasks like bottle throwing, dishrack loading, and mug flipping showed robust performance for both ABC-DiT and ABC-VLA (Figure 11, Figure 12).
- **Downstream Single-Task Finetuning**: Pretrained policies served as effective initializations for finetuning on high-precision, dexterous tasks like credit card extraction, LEGO sorting, pen cap insertion, and bottle cap unscrewing (Figure 13). Finetuning from larger pretraining corpora consistently yielded stronger single-task policies, highlighting the benefit of diverse and scaled pretraining data.
- **DAgger for Dexterous Tasks**: For complex tasks like box folding, initial finetuning of a pretrained ABC-DiT model only achieved 24% performance. By employing DAgger to collect recovery behaviors (interventions during policy rollout), performance significantly improved (Figure 14, Figure 15). The DAgger strategy involved mixing previous round data with new intervention data (80:10:10 ratio), which was found to be more effective than training on interventions alone or whole episodes alone.

**Policy Conditioning:**
Experiments on the t-shirt folding task (Table 4, Table 5, Figure 25, Figure 26) demonstrated the efficacy of various conditioning mechanisms:
- **Operator-ID Conditioning**: Conditioning on the ID of a more proficient operator (Op-0) at inference time resulted in higher-quality folds and completion rates (4.6 mean score, 8/10 completions) compared to an unconditioned baseline (3.8 mean score, 4/10 completions) or conditioning on a less proficient operator (Op-1). This suggests that operator-ID conditioning can steer policies towards desired stylistic behaviors learned from multimodal data.
- **Action Prefix Conditioning**: While an action prefix improves motion smoothness, a shorter prefix (e.g., length 1) improved performance on t-shirt folding compared to a longer one (length 4) by allowing greater visual responsiveness. Longer prefixes could bias the policy towards continuing the previous trajectory, potentially ignoring crucial visual cues.
- **Subtask Conditioning**: Using a subtask classifier to provide language prompts for the current subtask resolved temporal ambiguity in multi-stage tasks. For instance, without subtask prompting, policies exhibited failure modes such as re-flattening an already folded or placed shirt. Subtask conditioning eliminated this behavior, indicating that it enables the policy to commit to stage-appropriate actions.
Models were stochastically conditioned on action prefix, operator metadata, and subtask annotations during pretraining.

**Inference Optimization:**
Significant reductions in inference latency were achieved through a combination of bf16 precision, caching visual/VLM features, and aggressive `torch.compile` usage. For ABC-DiT, latency decreased from 63.0 ms to 36.3 ms for 10 diffusion steps (Figure 23). For ABC-VLA, latency improved from 47.8 ms to 17.5 ms (Figure 24). The faster inference for ABC-VLA, despite its larger total parameter count, is attributed to the fact that its large VLM backbone is run only once, while its smaller diffusion head is evaluated repeatedly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ABC project represents a substantial contribution to the field of robot manipulation, primarily by establishing an open-source, reproducible ecosystem for behavior cloning research. Its significance and potential impact can be categorized as follows:

1. **Democratization of Robot Learning Research**: By releasing ABC-130K, the largest open-source teleoperation dataset on a low-cost bimanual robot, along with accessible hardware details, the project significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers globally. This enables a broader community, including those without access to expensive proprietary hardware or large-scale data collection facilities, to participate in frontier research in robot manipulation. The provided simulation pipeline further extends this by offering a reliable proxy for real-world evaluation, allowing researchers to iterate on designs and training decisions without physical robots.

2. **Enhancement of Reproducibility and Transparency**: A critical issue in current robot learning is the opacity of state-of-the-art systems, where proprietary datasets and undocumented training recipes hinder reproducibility. ABC directly addresses this by making its entire stack—data, hardware setup, training infrastructure, models, and evaluation results—fully open-source. This transparency allows researchers to scrutinize, reproduce, and build upon the reported findings, fostering more robust and verifiable scientific progress.

3. **Acceleration of Research and Development**: The comprehensive nature of the ABC stack provides a solid foundation for rapid iteration on model architectures and training recipes. The large-scale, high-quality, and diverse dataset enables training of more generalist manipulation policies. The demonstrated strong correlation between simulation and real-world performance offers an efficient method for evaluating design choices, thereby accelerating the development cycle for new policies and algorithms.

4. **Establishment of Strong Baselines and Empirical Insights**: The project's exploration of common architectural choices for Diffusion Transformers and Vision-Language-Action models, grounded in real-world evaluations, yields valuable empirical insights into what design decisions (e.g., choice of visual encoder, conditioning mechanisms, scaling strategies) drive performance. The release of pretrained model weights provides strong baselines for future research, allowing others to focus on specific aspects of improvement rather than building entire systems from scratch.

5. **Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation**: The capabilities demonstrated by the resulting policies, such as box folding and credit card extraction, indicate progress in learning complex, long-horizon, and dexterous tasks. The effective use of DAgger for collecting recovery behaviors on challenging tasks showcases a practical method for improving performance beyond initial data collection.

6. **Guidance for Future Research Directions**: The paper explicitly outlines several open questions for the community, including history conditioning, scaling laws for behavior cloning, and reinforcement learning finetuning. By providing a foundational toolkit, ABC is positioned to be instrumental in answering these questions and stimulating new avenues of inquiry in large-scale real-world robot learning.

In summary, the ABC project's significance lies in its creation of an unparalleled open-source ecosystem that addresses fundamental barriers to progress in robot manipulation. Its impact is expected to accelerate research, foster greater collaboration, and enable more accessible and reproducible scientific inquiry in the field.
