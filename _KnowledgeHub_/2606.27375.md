# Scalable Behavior Cloning with Open Data, Training, and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ABC project from Amazon FAR and UC Berkeley introduces a comprehensive open-source "deep stack" for robot manipulation, encompassing a 3,500-hour teleoperation dataset (ABC-130K), scalable training infrastructure, policies (ABC-Models), and a simulation-to-real evaluation proxy (ABC-Sim). This system supports the training of policies for complex bimanual tasks, demonstrating strong sim-to-real correlation and substantial inference optimizations.

## Problem

- High costs and complexity of robot hardware and data collection limit the scale and diversity of open datasets for robot manipulation research.
- Inconsistent training methodologies and resource-intensive real-world evaluations hinder reproducible comparisons and comprehensive architectural explorations.
- State-of-the-art robot learning systems are often developed in industry with proprietary data and infrastructure, making them difficult for external researchers to replicate or build upon.

## Method

- Develops and releases the **ABC** stack: an open-source, end-to-end system encompassing hardware details, data, training code, models, and evaluation tools for behavior cloning.
- Provides ==ABC-130K==, a large-scale (3,553 hours, 130,000 episodes, 195 tasks) bimanual teleoperation dataset collected on a low-cost robot, with an optimized dataloader for efficient video processing.
- Introduces ABC-Sim, a simulation pipeline with 400 hours of VR teleoperation data, designed to serve as a low-cost, reliable proxy for real-world policy evaluation with demonstrated strong correlation.

## Results

- The ABC-130K dataset, with 3,553 hours of bimanual teleoperation data across 195 tasks, is released as the largest open-source resource for robot manipulation, enabling generalist policy training.
- Trained ABC-DiT and ABC-VLA policies achieve robust performance on complex, long-horizon tasks, including box folding and credit card extraction, with DAgger significantly improving dexterous task capabilities.
- Inference latency for both DiT and VLA models was substantially reduced (e.g., ABC-VLA to **17.5** ms from **47.8** ms) through mixed precision, caching, and `torch.compile` optimizations, and policy conditioning (operator-ID, subtask) improved task execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token-level visual information and pixel-aligned encoders like DINOv3 within cross-attention architectures improve Diffusion Transformer (DiT) policy performance for robot manipulation tasks.
> - A pooled AdaLN conditioning scheme effectively integrates Vision-Language Model (VLM) features into the diffusion action head, leading to superior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model performance compared to other methods.
> - Offline metrics like training loss and validation action error serve as useful, cost-effective proxies for guiding minor policy design and hyperparameter choices, though not for predicting exact real-world success rates.
