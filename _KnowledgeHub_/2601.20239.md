# TouchGuide: Inference-Time Steering of Visuomotor Policies via Touch Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel inference-time steering method, TouchGuide, enhances visuomotor policy performance in fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation by integrating tactile feedback. This approach, paired with the TacUMI data collection system, improved policy success rates (e.g., Diffusion Policy from 16.3% to 36.2%) and demonstrated robust generalization across diverse robots and tasks.

## Problem

- Current visuomotor policies often underutilize tactile feedback for fine-grained, contact-rich tasks, struggling with modality dominance or ineffective cross-modal correlation.
- Collecting high-quality expert demonstrations for complex manipulation is challenging due to limitations in existing teleoperation systems (latency, indirect feedback) and handheld devices (precision vs. cost).
- Robotic policies lack robustness to visual uncertainties such as domain shifts and occlusions, which are common in real-world contact-rich environments.

## Method

- Introduces **TouchGuide**, an inference-time steering paradigm that refines pre-trained visuomotor policies using a task-specific ==Contact Physical Model (CPM)== guided by tactile feedback.
- Developed TacUMI, a cost-effective handheld data collection system that provides high-precision pose estimation via Vive trackers and direct tactile feedback through rigid fingertips.
- The CPM is trained with ==contrastive learning== on noisy actions and uses a frozen ==DINOv2== encoder for robust multimodal embedding and ==feasibility scoring==.

## Results

- TouchGuide enhanced average success rates on contact-rich tasks, improving Diffusion Policy performance from **16.3%** to **36.2%** and "0.5 from **35.9%** to **58.0%**.
- The approach outperformed existing visuo-tactile methods, achieving an average success rate of **36.2%** with Diffusion Policy, surpassing baselines like RDP (**30.3%**) and Policy Consensus (**24.7%**).
- TouchGuide demonstrated generalization across diverse robotic platforms, base policies, and tactile modalities, maintaining robust performance under visual domain shifts and occlusions.
- Policies trained with TacUMI data achieved higher success rates (e.g., Lock Opening for ",0.5 saw **20%** success versus **0%** with SLAM-based UMI), and TacUMI received a higher user satisfaction rating of **9.6**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly steering visuomotor policies at inference time using tactile-derived contact physics significantly improves performance in fine-grained manipulation, compensating for visual uncertainties.
> - Adapting classifier guidance principles for both diffusion and flow-matching models, driven by a contrastively trained Contact Physical Model (CPM), effectively refines actions to satisfy physical contact conditions.
> - Cost-effective, high-precision data collection with direct tactile feedback, exemplified by TacUMI, is essential for acquiring the reliable multimodal datasets needed to train robust robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained and contact-rich interactions, presents ongoing challenges. Current approaches often underutilize tactile feedback, despite its potential to provide crucial localized information for precise control. Existing visuomotor policy models typically employ feature-level or policy-level fusion strategies for integrating visual and tactile data. Feature-level fusion, often involving concatenation of modality features, can suffer from modality dominance issues where one input, typically vision, overshadows the other, especially when tactile signals are sparse. Policy-level methods, which combine multiple single-modality policies, can mitigate this imbalance but may not effectively capture the intricate cross-modal correlations essential for tasks demanding tight multimodal synergy.

Another significant challenge lies in data collection for such complex tasks. Teleoperation systems, while common, frequently introduce latency and provide indirect haptic feedback, hindering the acquisition of high-quality expert demonstrations for contact-rich scenarios. Handheld data collection devices, such as those based on the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) paradigm, aim to offer platform-decoupled solutions. However, existing systems often face trade-offs between precision and cost, with SLAM-based localization sometimes lacking the accuracy for fine-grained tasks, and motion capture (MoCap) systems being expensive and environmentally constrained.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel fusion paradigm and a complementary data collection system. TouchGuide positions itself within the inference-time steering paradigm, a method inspired by classifier guidance in generative models, which aims to direct a policy's actions towards a desired distribution. While some prior works in this area guide visuomotor policies using dynamics models, such approaches can be sensitive to small errors in action, making them less effective for fine-grained manipulation where future observations diverge quickly. TouchGuide deviates by focusing on steering based on current contact physics, offering a solution that can be integrated with various pre-trained visuomotor policies. TacUMI, the accompanying data collection system, seeks to provide a cost-effective, high-precision solution with direct tactile feedback, bridging gaps in existing hardware for collecting reliable multimodal data crucial for training contact-aware robotic policies.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance robot performance in fine-grained and contact-rich manipulation tasks by more effectively integrating visual and tactile information. The authors identify several motivations for this objective:

Firstly, current robotic systems struggle with tasks requiring precise physical contact, such as shoe lacing or handing over fragile objects. These tasks necessitate a synergistic integration of visual and tactile information: visual data provides broad scene context, while tactile feedback offers localized, contact-specific details critical for fine control. The central question motivating TouchGuide is how to fuse these modalities to improve policy planning in such scenarios.

Secondly, existing visuo-tactile fusion strategies exhibit limitations. Feature-level concatenation can lead to modality dominance, where the policy over-relies on visual input, especially when tactile signals are sparse or exhibit limited variation. Policy-level composition methods, while improving on feature-level fusion, typically require training multiple single-modality policies and often do not effectively capture the tight cross-modal correlations essential for complex manipulation. TouchGuide aims to overcome these by introducing a cross-policy fusion paradigm that operates within the low-dimensional action space, decoupling the tactile guidance from the base visuomotor policy.

Thirdly, the quality of expert demonstrations significantly influences policy execution, particularly for fine-grained and contact-rich manipulation. Current data collection methods, such as teleoperation, suffer from high latency and indirect haptic feedback, making intuitive control difficult for human operators. Handheld devices like UMI offer a robot-decoupled solution, but existing implementations often compromise on either accuracy (e.g., SLAM-based localization) or cost/usability (e.g., motion capture, VR headset requirements). The development of TacUMI is motivated by the need for a cost-effective, high-precision data collection system that provides direct tactile feedback, enabling the collection of reliable tactile data crucial for training robust policies for these demanding tasks.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Develop TouchGuide, a novel visuo-tactile fusion paradigm that steers pre-trained visuomotor policies at inference time using tactile feedback, improving adherence to physical contact conditions without requiring base policy retraining.
2. Design and implement TacUMI, a data collection system that achieves a favorable trade-off between precision and affordability, offering direct tactile feedback for acquiring high-quality multimodal manipulation data.
3. Demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of TouchGuide and TacUMI across various challenging contact-rich tasks, robotic platforms, and tactile modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work consists of two main components: the TacUMI data collection system and the TouchGuide inference-time steering paradigm.

**TacUMI Data Collection System:**
TacUMI is designed as a Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI)-like handheld gripper for collecting tactile data without relying on robot proprioception. Its design principles emphasize:
- **Accuracy and Low-cost:** TacUMI utilizes a Vive tracker with two Lighthouse base stations for localization, providing accurate pose estimates. This setup has a base cost of approximately $720, offering a cost-effective alternative to SLAM-based or motion capture systems while maintaining precision for fine-grained tasks.
- **Direct Tactile Feedback:** Unlike teleoperation systems that offer indirect haptic feedback or some handheld grippers with semi-direct feedback via triggers, TacUMI incorporates rigid fingertips. This design allows operators to experience direct tactile feedback, which is critical for perceiving and responding to touch signals promptly in contact-rich tasks.
- **Usability and Synchronization:** The system features a unified hardware-software interface. An onboard RK3576 CPU processes data in real-time. It streams high-resolution visual (640x480x3) and tactile (200x350x3 tactile images or 20x35x3 force fields) signals at 30Hz, compatible with the LeRobot format for effective software-level synchronization. A magnetic encoder is used for precise gripper opening estimation, instead of marker-based methods.
- **Data Processing:** For policy training, the collected end-effector poses are converted into relative end-effector poses ({p^(i)_rel, R^(i)_rel}). However, for policies like π0.5 that condition on a single observation step, absolute end-effector poses ({p^(i)_ee, R^(i)_ee}) are used as the robot state to enable the policy to perceive its pose in the real workspace effectively.

**TouchGuide Method:**
TouchGuide is a two-stage cross-policy multimodal fusion paradigm that operates at inference time to steer pre-trained diffusion-based or flow-matching-based visuomotor policies (e.g., DP, π0.5's action expert). The process is as follows:
1. **Coarse Action Generation:** During the early sampling phase of the denoising or flow-matching process, the base policy generates an initial, coarse action based solely on visual observations. This action is designed to be visually plausible but does not yet incorporate specific tactile constraints.
2. **Tactile Guidance and Refinement:** In the later phase, a task-specific Contact Physical Model (CPM) provides touch guidance. This CPM outputs a feasibility score that assesses how well the proposed action aligns with real-world physical contact conditions implied by current tactile observations. This score is then used to steer and refine the action.

The technical details of TouchGuide are built upon classifier guidance principles:
- **Classifier Guidance for Flow Matching:** The authors extend the concept of classifier guidance, originally formulated for diffusion models, to flow-matching models. They derive a formulation (Proposition 1, Eq. 4) where the velocity prediction is adjusted by a term proportional to the gradient of `log p_phi(y|x_t)` with respect to the action `x_t`, scaled by `t/(1-t)`. This `log p_phi(y|x_t)` corresponds to the feasibility score `s` from the CPM.
- **Contact Physical Model (CPM):**
 - **Architecture:** The CPM takes current tactile feedback (T_t), visual observation (V_t), and the coarse action (A_t) generated by the base policy. Pretrained DINOv2 is used as a frozen encoder for both visual and tactile inputs, producing `T_emb_t` and `V_emb_t`. These embeddings are concatenated and processed by an N-layer Transformer encoder (`TranEnc`) to yield a latent observation `O_t`. The action `A_t` is encoded by a 1D CNN + MLP to produce a latent action `a_t`. Both `O_t` and `a_t` are L2 normalized.
 - **Feasibility Score:** The feasibility score `s` is defined as the cosine similarity between the latent observation `O_t` and latent action `a_t` (s = O_t^T a_t). A probabilistic motivation is provided, where the gradient of `log p(a_real|a)` (representing the likelihood of a real action given a policy action) is proportional to the gradient of `s`.
 - **Training:** The CPM is trained using contrastive learning on a limited set of expert demonstrations. Positive samples consist of `(O_t, a_t)` pairs from the same time step, while negative samples are constructed from `(O_t+Δt, a_t)` and `(O_t, a_t+Δt)` pairs from different time steps. A standard contrastive loss (L_CPM, Eq. 8-10) is used. To ensure the CPM operates effectively in the noisy action space generated by the base policy during inference, ground-truth actions in the training data are augmented with noise (geometric distribution for diffusion, linear interpolation for flow matching).
- **Steering Mechanism:** TouchGuide integrates the CPM's feasibility score into the diffusion or flow-matching sampling process. For diffusion policies, the noise prediction `ϵ_θ` is adjusted by subtracting a term proportional to the guidance scale `η`, `sqrt(1-α_k)`, and `∇_{A_k_t} s`. For flow-matching policies, the velocity prediction `u_θ` is adjusted by `η`, `k/(1-k)`, and `∇_{A_k_t} s`. This gradient-based steering explicitly drives the policy's action distribution toward satisfying contact physics. The guidance is applied only during a specified number of `K_TouchGuide` steps in the later phase of the sampling process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TouchGuide and TacUMI across five challenging contact-rich tasks: Shoe Lacing, Chip Handover, Cucumber Peeling, Vase Wiping, and Lock Opening. Experiments were conducted on both dual-arm (Bi-ARX5) and single-arm (Flexiv Rizon4 with TacUMI) robotic setups.

**Performance of TouchGuide:**
- **Importance of Tactile Feedback (Q1):** TouchGuide consistently improved policy performance over purely visuomotor baselines. For example, for the Diffusion Policy (DP), the average success rate increased from 16.3% to 36.2% with TouchGuide (Tactile Image). For π0.5, the average success rate increased from 35.9% to 58.0% with TouchGuide (Tactile Image). This highlights the critical role of accurate in-hand state information provided by tactile sensing for fine-grained manipulation, which is difficult to infer from vision alone.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Visuo-Tactile Policies (Q2):** TouchGuide demonstrated superior performance compared to existing SOTA visuo-tactile policies. For DP as the base policy, TouchGuide (Tactile Image) achieved an average success rate of 36.2%, outperforming RDP (30.3%), Policy Consensus (24.7%), SafeDiff (22.2%), and Tactile Dynamics (23.6%). Similar improvements were observed for π0.5 as the base policy. This indicates that TouchGuide's approach of explicitly steering the base policy in the action space via contact physics guidance, particularly at the initial contact phase, is more effective than methods that primarily react to tactile changes.
- **Generalization Across Robots, Policies, and Modalities (Q3):** TouchGuide exhibited strong generalization capabilities. It successfully integrated with different robotic platforms (Bi-ARX5 and Flexiv Rizon4), different base visuomotor policies (DP and π0.5), and different tactile modalities (force and tactile images), demonstrating consistent performance gains without retraining the base policy. This decoupling contributes to its adaptability.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Noise Pretraining for CPM:** Training the Contact Physical Model (CPM) with noisy actions, simulating the noisy sampling process of the base policy, was critical. This strategy improved the average success rate from 39.17% to 62.50% in an ablation study across three tasks.
- **CPM Input Modalities:** Both visual and tactile modalities were found to be crucial for the CPM. Removing either vision or touch from the CPM input significantly reduced the average success rate (w/o vision: 43.33%, w/o touch: 43.50%), compared to using both (62.50%). This confirms that each modality provides complementary information that the CPM leverages.
- **Observation Encoder:** Using a pretrained DINOv2 (frozen) as both visual and tactile encoders yielded the best performance with the fewest trainable parameters for the CPM, outperforming co-trained ResNet18 backbones.

**Generalization and Robustness:**
- **Visual Domain Shifts and Occlusions (Q4):** TouchGuide demonstrated robustness to visual domain shifts (unseen objects, unseen scenes, or both) and transient visual occlusions. It consistently improved the base policy's performance under these varied conditions, suggesting that touch guidance compensates for visual uncertainties by providing reliable contact information.
- **Action Distribution Analysis:** t-SNE visualizations of action distributions indicated that the actions generated by TouchGuide (`a_steered`) aligned more closely with expert actions (`a_real`) than those from the unsteered base policy (`a_base`), supporting the effectiveness of the steering mechanism.

**TacUMI System Comparison and User Study:**
- **Data Quality (Q5):** Policies trained on data collected with TacUMI achieved higher success rates on the Lock Opening task compared to those trained on data from SLAM-based UMI or VR teleoperation systems. For instance, π0.5 achieved 20% success with TacUMI data, versus 0% with SLAM-based UMI and 5% with VR Teleop. This indicates TacUMI provides more accurate and reliable data suitable for robot learning.
- **Usability:** A user study involving five volunteers showed that TacUMI demonstrated high usability. It required fewer attempts to collect valid demonstrations (100 attempts for 20 demos) and had a shorter average collection length (13.5 minutes) compared to VR teleoperation (100 attempts, 23.0 min) or VR Motion Tracker (104 attempts, 14.7 min). It also received the highest user satisfaction rating (9.6), attributed to its direct tactile feedback and reliable Vive Tracker localization without the burden of a VR headset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring precise physical contact.

**Advancement in Visuo-Tactile Fusion:** TouchGuide offers a novel and effective cross-policy fusion paradigm that explicitly steers visuomotor policies at inference time using a Contact Physical Model (CPM). This approach is notable for its ability to enhance a policy's adherence to contact physics without requiring re-training of the base policy. This decoupled design simplifies the integration of tactile sensing into diverse existing visuomotor policies (diffusion-based, flow-matching-based, VLAs), which could accelerate the adoption and practical deployment of tactile feedback in complex robotic systems. The demonstration of its generalization across different robots, policies, and tactile modalities suggests a versatile solution for a range of manipulation problems.

**Improved Data Collection for Robotics:** The introduction of TacUMI addresses a critical bottleneck in data-driven robotics: the collection of high-quality, cost-effective multimodal data for contact-rich tasks. By providing direct tactile feedback through rigid fingertips and leveraging precise, affordable localization (Vive trackers), TacUMI offers a practical solution that overcomes limitations of existing teleoperation and handheld systems (e.g., latency, indirect feedback, accuracy/cost trade-offs). Its demonstrated superior data quality and user satisfaction can foster more efficient and reliable data collection, which is fundamental for advancing imitation learning and other data-intensive robot learning paradigms.

**Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The findings indicate that TouchGuide significantly improves the robustness of policies under challenging conditions, including visual domain shifts and occlusions. By leveraging complementary contact information, tactile guidance helps policies maintain performance where visual-only methods might fail, making robotic systems more reliable in varied and unpredictable real-world environments. The ability of the CPM to generalize to out-of-distribution observations further underscores the potential for developing more robust and adaptable manipulation skills.

**Future Research Directions:** While requiring task-specific training for the CPM remains a limitation, the work lays a foundation for future research into learning more generalizable tactile representations and developing non-task-specific CPMs. This could lead to foundational tactile models that further reduce training costs and expand the applicability of tactile guidance across an even broader spectrum of manipulation tasks.

Overall, this work presents a practical and effective framework for integrating tactile intelligence into robotic manipulation, with the potential to significantly impact the development of more dexterous, robust, and autonomous robots capable of performing complex interactions with the physical world.
