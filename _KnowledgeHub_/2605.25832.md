# When Search Becomes Memory: Turning Robot Design Trials into Transferable Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-evolving language agent, AUTO-ROBOTIST, transforms robot design evaluations into a transferable skill library for automated morphology optimization. The system demonstrated a 1.47x average convergence speedup in cold-start searches and an average +1.55 endpoint fitness improvement in cross-scale design transfers, effectively converting costly trials into explicit, reusable design principles.

## Problem

- Traditional automated robot design systems often discard valuable trial data, operating as "memoryless" processes despite costly physical or simulated evaluations.
- This leads to inefficient exploration in vast combinatorial design spaces, as insights into design successes and failures are not systematically accumulated or abstracted.
- Existing LLM-based design approaches primarily act as proposal generators and do not distill explicit, transferable design principles from iterative search processes.

## Method

- Developed **AUTO-ROBOTIST**, a self-evolving language agent that systematically converts robot design trial data into a hierarchical, natural-language skill library.
- The system employs a continuous loop of skill-conditioned proposal generation by an LLM, followed by physical simulation feedback, and then library maintenance operations (ADD, DIAGNOSE, MERGE).
- Skills are represented at three levels: L1 (abstract archetypes), L2 (evidence-grounded positive/negative rules), and L3 (raw evaluation observations) to facilitate explicit knowledge acquisition and transfer.

## Results

- AUTO-ROBOTIST achieved an average **1.47x** convergence speedup and maintained a **70%** average lead fraction compared to a Genetic Algorithm in cold-start 5x5 robot design.
- The system demonstrated superior performance in cross-scale transfer (5x5 to 10x10), yielding an average endpoint fitness improvement of +1.55 over the GA baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of L2 rules, L3 observations, and active library maintenance (DIAGNOSE, MERGE) to the agent's performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language agents can effectively distill costly robot design evaluations into transferable, abstract design principles, moving beyond mere artifact generation.
> - A hierarchical and explicit skill library, grounded in simulator feedback, facilitates the acquisition of relational design knowledge that is both auditable and transferable across different design scales.
> - Continuous maintenance of this skill library, through operations like skill addition, rule diagnosis, and redundancy merging, is essential for sharpening and stabilizing the learned design theory.
