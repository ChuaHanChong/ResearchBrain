# ASI-Evolve: AI Accelerates AI

> [!summary] Summary
> ASI-Evolve is an agentic evolutionary framework for AI-for-AI research, closing a learn-design-experiment-analyze loop. It adds a literature-seeded cognition base that injects human priors into each exploration round and an analyzer that distills verbose experimental logs into reusable insights stored in a node database. It discovered 105 linear-attention architectures beating DeltaNet, data-curation strategies adding 3.96 average points, and RL algorithms beating GRPO by up to 12.5 points.

## Problem

- Scientific question-answering and benchmark-optimization agents operate under cheap execution, bounded search spaces and scalar feedback, so they never face real research loops.
- Evolutionary search frameworks achieve open-ended discovery but each trial edits one short function, keeping feedback immediate and direct rather than multi-dimensional.
- No prior framework demonstrated AI-driven discovery unified across architectures, pretraining data and learning algorithms, where single trials cost hundreds of GPU hours.

## Method

- Four-stage loop: ==Researcher== samples database nodes plus retrieved cognition to generate a candidate program, ==Engineer== runs the task-specific evaluation with timeouts and quick-test early rejection.
- ==Cognition base== stores literature-derived heuristics and pitfalls as embedding-indexed items, retrieved by semantic search over sampled nodes' motivations to steer cold-start hypothesis generation.
- ==Analyzer== consumes full raw logs and metrics, emitting a compact causal report persisted per node; database supports ==UCB1==, greedy, random and ==MAP-Elites== island sampling.

## Results

- Architecture search: **1,773** rounds yielded **105** designs above DeltaNet; best model gained **0.97** overall points, roughly triple Mamba2's **0.34**-point human-designed gain.
- Curated pretraining corpus reached **44.13** average across **18** benchmarks at **3B** parameters and **500B** tokens, **3.96** above raw data, with MMLU up **18.64** points.
- Circle packing hit the **2.63597** state-of-the-art level in **17** rounds versus OpenEvolve's **460**; evolved drug-target architecture gained **6.94** AUROC on unseen drugs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cognition priors mainly accelerate cold start while the sampling policy governs sustained climbing, so the two components address distinct phases of long-horizon search.
> - With strong priors and structured analysis, exploitation-oriented UCB1 beat diversity-preserving MAP-Elites, suggesting good guidance substitutes for enforced diversity.
> - Structured analysis of rich logs, not raw scores, drives sustained gains; agents editing mechanism-level code cannot guarantee hardware-level wall-clock efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of artificial intelligence (AI) is driven by advancements across multiple interconnected components, including data, model architectures, and learning algorithms. Traditionally, progress in these areas relies on iterative cycles of human-driven hypothesis generation, implementation, experimentation, and analysis. This process is often constrained by human limitations in exploring vast hypothesis spaces, managing complex experimental workflows, and systematically accumulating knowledge. The central question addressed by this work is whether AI can accelerate its own development process.

Recent advancements have seen AI applied to scientific discovery, evolving from specialized systems designed for well-defined problems (e.g., AlphaFold for protein structure prediction, GraphCast for weather forecasting, GNoME for materials discovery) to more generalized AI-driven systems. These broader systems include:
- **Scientific Question Answering:** Systems like SciMaster, GPQA, and FrontierScience focus on answering scientific questions, often with low execution cost, limited search space complexity, and simple feedback (low L_task dimensions).
- **Structured Task Execution:** Systems such as ML-Master, MLEvolve, MLE-bench, SWE-bench, and AI Scientist automate specific tasks like optimizing machine learning models under fixed criteria or automating publication pipelines. These involve some execution but still operate within bounded objectives and offer relatively direct feedback.
- **Lightweight Scientific Discovery:** Evolutionary search frameworks like AlphaEvolve, FunSearch, and OpenEvolve have achieved open-ended discovery in areas like matrix multiplication algorithms or combinatorial optimization. While these elevate search space complexity and execution cost, the individual trials remain small-scale, and feedback is often immediate and direct, keeping feedback complexity low.

The research presented here, ASI-EVOLVE, positions itself at the upper end of the "Scientific Task Length (L_task)" spectrum. The L_task framework, introduced in the paper, characterizes scientific research tasks along three dimensions:
1. **Execution Cost (C_exec):** Computational resources and engineering complexity per trial.
2. **Search Space Complexity (S_space):** Openness of the objective and boundaries of candidate solutions.
3. **Feedback Complexity (D_feedback):** Difficulty in extracting actionable insights from multi-dimensional experimental outcomes.

Existing systems have not yet effectively addressed tasks that push all three dimensions to substantially high levels simultaneously, particularly in a unified framework capable of addressing multiple foundational AI development components. ASI-EVOLVE aims to fill this gap by demonstrating AI-driven discovery across neural architecture design, pretraining data curation, and reinforcement learning algorithm design, tasks characterized by high execution cost, vast search spaces, and complex, multi-dimensional feedback. It distinguishes itself by integrating human priors and sophisticated analysis to navigate these challenges, moving beyond simple evolutionary search to an "evolution of cognition" paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether AI can autonomously accelerate and enhance the development of AI itself. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified bottlenecks in traditional human-driven AI research:
- **Limited Hypothesis Space Exploration:** Human researchers are constrained in the number of hypotheses they can explore in parallel.
- **Demanding Experimental Workflows:** Experimental setups and iterations often require substantial manual effort and frequent intervention, leading to high operational costs.
- **Inefficient Knowledge Accumulation and Transfer:** Insights gained across iterations tend to rely on individual experience and intuition, making systematic preservation and transfer of knowledge challenging.

These constraints collectively limit the pace and scale of progress in AI development. To address these limitations, the paper aims to:
1. **Develop a unified agentic framework:** Create a system capable of closing the research loop – from hypothesis generation to experimentation and analysis – across diverse, complex, and long-horizon AI research tasks.
2. **Integrate human prior knowledge:** Design a mechanism to inject accumulated human research literature and domain knowledge into the AI’s exploration process from the outset, enabling it to build on existing knowledge rather than starting from scratch.
3. **Automate insight distillation:** Implement a dedicated component to translate complex, multi-dimensional experimental outcomes into structured, actionable insights that can be reused in future iterations.
4. **Demonstrate broad applicability:** Show that the framework can drive meaningful advancements across the three foundational pillars of AI development: model architectures, pretraining data, and learning algorithms.
5. **Validate generalizability:** Provide initial evidence that this AI-for-AI paradigm can transfer beyond the core AI stack to other scientific domains, such as mathematics and biomedicine.

Ultimately, the motivation is to establish the feasibility of closed-loop AI research, where AI systems can continuously learn, design, experiment, and analyze to improve their own components, thereby accelerating the overall pace of scientific discovery in AI and potentially other fields.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces ASI-EVOLVE, an agentic framework designed for AI-for-AI research, structured around a closed learn–design–experiment–analyze cycle. This framework augments standard evolutionary agents with two key components: a cognition base and a dedicated analyzer. The system iteratively searches over a program space, with each evolution round (t) involving five stages:

**Core Modules and Workflow:**

1. **Database (D):** This serves as the system's persistent memory, storing the outcome of each evolution round. Each entry (node) in the database includes the Researcher's motivation, the generated program, structured results from evaluation, an analysis report, and auxiliary metadata (e.g., runtime, success flag). The database supports various sampling policies (UCB1, random, greedy, MAP-Elites island algorithm) to retrieve past nodes for guiding future exploration. Initially, it contains baseline or seed solutions.

2. **Cognition Store (C):** This module encodes human prior knowledge (heuristics, known pitfalls, design principles) from domain literature and prior runs. Task-relevant textual items are indexed by embeddings. In each round, after context nodes are sampled from the database, the system uses information from these nodes as queries to retrieve a small set of semantically similar cognition entries via embedding-based search. These retrieved entries are injected into the Researcher's context to guide hypothesis generation, accelerating cold-start exploration.

3. **Researcher:** This module is responsible for generating the next candidate program and its natural-language motivation. Conditioned on sampled context nodes (S_t) from the database and retrieved cognition items (R_t) from the cognition store, an LLM (Large Language Model) is used to produce new code artifacts. The Researcher also supports an optional diff-based editing mode for incremental modifications to larger codebases.

4. **Engineer:** This module executes the candidate program in the actual experimental environment. It invokes a user-specified evaluation procedure that runs the experiment end-to-end, producing structured metrics and a primary scalar score (fitness signal). To enhance efficiency for long-horizon tasks, the Engineer incorporates early rejection mechanisms via configurable wall-clock limits and lightweight quick tests. It can also optionally use an LLM-based judge to assess qualitative aspects of candidate quality alongside rule-based metrics.

5. **Analyzer:** This dedicated module addresses the complexity of multi-dimensional experimental feedback. It receives the current program and the full experimental output (raw logs, detailed metrics across benchmarks, training dynamics, efficiency traces). The Analyzer distills this verbose information into a compact, decision-oriented report that contains structured, actionable insights. This report is then stored back into the database, informing subsequent iterations without overwhelming the context length of the Researcher.

**Adaptations for Model Architecture Design (Scenario 1):**
- **Cognition Initialization:** Approximately 150 entries from 100 papers on linear attention, state-space models, and efficient transformers.
- **Database Management:** A periodically refreshed candidate pool retains the top 50 highest-scoring nodes; root architectures are sampled from the top 10, and reference context from the broader top 50.
- **Efficiency and Constraint Handling:**
 - **Static Check Agent:** Verifies complexity bounds, chunk-wise structure, and causal mask correctness before training, preventing costly runs of flawed designs.
 - **Debug Agent:** Inspects error traces for runtime implementation errors and attempts targeted fixes.
 - **Novelty Check:** Filters duplicate proposals based on motivation similarity to encourage genuine exploration.
- **Multi-stage Evaluation:**
 - **Exploration Phase:** Small models (~20M parameters) trained for 2000 steps on 1B tokens, evaluated on 10 core benchmarks. Composite fitness combines quantitative metrics (loss, benchmark scores) with LLM-as-a-Judge qualitative scores (code complexity, efficiency, innovativeness).
 - **Verification Phase:** Promising candidates scaled to ~340M parameters, trained on 1B tokens, with additional causality tests.
 - **Large-scale Validation:** Top architectures scaled to ~1.3B parameters, trained on 100B tokens, evaluated on 16 benchmarks (including 6 held-out OOD test sets).

**Adaptations for Pretraining Data Curation (Scenario 2):**
- **Cognition Initialization:** Quality issues (HTML artifacts, fragments, formatting) identified by examining sampled data from each category.
- **Iteration Process:** Researcher generates curation strategies, Engineer executes on 500 sampled documents, Analyzer evaluates 50 (original, cleaned) pairs (1-10 scale) and provides diagnostic feedback (coverage, executability). New quality issues are added to cognition.

**Adaptations for Reinforcement Learning Algorithm Design (Scenario 3):**
- **Cognition Initialization:** 10 high-quality papers on variance reduction and KL-penalty modifications published post-GRPO.
- **Validation Protocol:**
 - **Exploration Phase:** Candidate algorithms trained on 4B parameter model for 150 steps, evaluated on 6 mathematics benchmarks.
 - **Verification Phase:** Promising candidates scaled to 14B parameter model for 300 steps, evaluation expanded to Abstract Reasoning, STEM, Finance, and Coding domains.
- **Fitness Scoring:** Linear weighted sum of benchmark accuracy and LLM-as-a-Judge qualitative scores for raw performance and algorithmic coherence, without sigmoid normalization.

**Ablation Studies and Comparisons:** The paper further conducts experiments on the circle packing task (a common benchmark for evolutionary frameworks) to compare ASI-EVOLVE against baselines (OpenEvolve, GEPA) and evaluate the impact of individual components (Analyzer, Cognition, sampling algorithms) using controlled ablation studies. The framework's real-world applicability is tested by evolving architectures for drug-target interaction prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ASI-EVOLVE demonstrated AI-driven discovery across three core AI development components and showed transferability to other scientific domains.

#### 4.1. Model Architecture Design (Linear Attention Architectures)

- **Discovery:** Over 1773 exploration rounds, ASI-EVOLVE autonomously generated 1,350 candidate architectures and discovered 105 architectures that surpassed the human-designed DeltaNet baseline.
- **Performance:** The best discovered model achieved a +0.97 point gain over DeltaNet, which is approximately three times the improvement of recent human-designed SOTA advancements (e.g., Mamba2's +0.34 points over DeltaNet).
 - On development benchmarks, evolved architectures achieved up to 57.28% average accuracy (DeltaNet: 55.76%).
 - On generalization benchmarks, they reached up to 45.40% average accuracy (DeltaNet: 44.74%), indicating transferability of gains.
- **Design Principles:** Analysis of top architectures revealed a consistent theme of adaptive, multi-scale routing mechanisms that dynamically adjust computational budgets based on input content, moving beyond fixed allocation schemes (e.g., PathGateFusionNet, ContentSharpRouter, FusionGatedFIRNet, HierGateNet, AdaMultiPathGateNet).
- **Evolutionary Dynamics:** Early stages were driven by benchmark performance, while later stages involved broader subjective refinement, with 78% of high-performing architectures in the second half being improvements on earlier designs.
- **Role of Cognition:** 51.7% of all architectures and 44.8% of SOTA architectures derived from the cognition base or accumulated experience, suggesting that human priors accelerate cold-start search, and the system distills patterns from its own trials.
- **Limitation:** The system operates at the attention mechanism level, not low-level kernel implementation, so hardware-optimized efficiency cannot be directly guaranteed.

#### 4.2. Pretraining Data Curation

- **Strategy Design:** The system successfully designed category-specific curation strategies for Nemotron-CC, a corpus of 672B tokens across STEM fields.
- **Performance Improvement:** Training 3B-parameter models on the AI-curated dataset (Nemotron-CC_ASI+) resulted in a 44.13 average benchmark score, improving over the raw data by +3.96 points. This also surpassed established corpora like DCLM, FineWeb-Edu, and Ultra-FineWeb.
- **Knowledge-intensive Gains:** Significant improvements were observed on tasks like MMLU (+18.64 points), CSQA (+18.80 points), and MedQA (+13.48 points).
- **Design Characteristics:** Evolved strategies consistently focused on cleaning-focused approaches (noise removal, format normalization, domain-aware preservation rules) without explicit guidance. They utilized concrete criteria with measurable thresholds, targeted deletion, and explicit preservation rules.
- **Value of Iterative Refinement:** A 2.93-point gap between optimized and suboptimal strategies underscored the effectiveness of iterative refinement guided by diagnostic feedback.

#### 4.3. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm Design

- **Algorithm Discovery:** Over 300 evolutionary rounds, the system trained and evaluated various policy gradient modifications, yielding 10 algorithms outperforming the GRPO baseline in exploration. Three algorithms showed statistically significant improvements across all tested domains in verification.
- **Performance Gains:** On mathematical reasoning benchmarks, the best evolved variants improved over GRPO:
 - +12.5 points on AMC32 (from 67.5 to 80.0)
 - +11.67 points on AIME24 (from 20.00 to 31.67)
 - +5.04 points on OlympiadBench (from 45.92 to 50.96)
- **Theoretical Innovations:** Two representative algorithms highlighted distinct innovations:
 - **Algorithm A (Pairwise Asymmetric Optimization):** Introduced comparative advantage estimation (averaging tanh-normalized pairwise reward differences), asymmetric PPO clipping, and High-Impact Gradient Dropout to prevent overfitting.
 - **Algorithm B (Budget-Constrained Dynamic Radius):** Adopted percentile-based normalization for advantage calculation and a Global Update Budget (z_cap) to dynamically assign trusted update radii and mathematically guarantee policy update magnitude, stabilizing training.

#### 4.4. Empirical Analysis on Circle Packing

- **Comparison with Baselines:** ASI-EVOLVE reached SOTA-level results (2.63597) in as few as 17 steps, significantly outpacing OpenEvolve (460 rounds) and GEPA (converging around 2.630).
- **Base Model Robustness:** ASI-EVOLVE showed similar convergence and mid-to-late-stage improvement trends using both GPT-5-mini and Qwen3-32B, indicating framework robustness across models.
- **Sampling Algorithm Impact:** UCB1 sampling achieved faster convergence and lower variance than MAP-Elites or Random sampling when combined with a strong cognition base, suggesting that exploitation-oriented sampling can accelerate discovery when guided by prior knowledge.
 - UCB1 with GPT-5-mini achieved 2.63597 in 17 steps, while MAP-Elites with the same model required 79 steps.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of removing Analyzer:** The "No Analyzer" variant showed high initial scores (due to Cognition) but a prolonged plateau with limited sustained improvement, demonstrating that structured analysis is critical for continuous, reliable evolution.
- **Impact of removing Cognition:** The "No Cognition" variant exhibited a more pronounced cold-start cost, with slower and less stable early improvements. However, it eventually reached a productive exploration regime, indicating that ASI-EVOLVE's core learning mechanisms can function without external priors, albeit requiring longer exploration.

#### 4.6. Real-World Applicability: Drug-Target Interaction (DTI) Discovery

- **Performance:** The AI-evolved architecture for DTI prediction achieved consistent improvements over the DrugBAN baseline and competitive or superior performance against six SOTA human-designed baselines.
 - +1.91 AUROC and +2.95 F1 points on BindingDB development set.
 - +1.05 AUROC and +1.39 F1 points on BindingDB-Random test set.
- **Generalization:** Significant improvements were observed in challenging cold-start scenarios:
 - +6.94 AUROC points for unseen drugs.
 - +3.56 AUROC points for unseen proteins.
 - +4.36 AUROC points for unseen drugs and proteins.
- **Architectural Innovations:** The best discovered architecture (ban_sinkhorn_ds_marginal_topk_v6) introduced Sinkhorn Attention (optimal-transport-based doubly-stochastic constraints), Domain-Specific Marginalization, and Top-k Sparse Gating, aligning with domain knowledge in medicinal chemistry.
- **Evolutionary Process:** Early iterations utilized cognition from graph neural networks and molecular representation papers, while later stages integrated cross-paper insights (e.g., Sinkhorn mechanism from optimal transport and bipartite matching).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ASI-EVOLVE represents a step toward enabling AI to accelerate its own development across foundational stages. The framework's ability to operate effectively on tasks characterized by high execution cost, complex search spaces, and multi-dimensional feedback distinguishes it from prior work.

The significance of these findings lies in several areas:
- **Feasibility of Closed-Loop AI Research:** ASI-EVOLVE provides evidence for the feasibility of an autonomous, closed-loop research paradigm where AI systems can learn, design, experiment, and analyze to improve their own components. This challenges the traditional human-centric model of scientific discovery.
- **Breakthroughs in Core AI Components:** The framework's success in discovering superior model architectures, curating higher-quality pretraining data, and designing more effective reinforcement learning algorithms demonstrates its capacity to generate meaningful advances in areas critical for AI progress. The quantitative gains reported (e.g., +0.97 points over DeltaNet, +18 points on MMLU, +12.5 points on AMC32) indicate that these are not merely incremental improvements but represent potentially significant shifts in performance.
- **Integration of Human Prior Knowledge:** The "cognition base" component highlights the value of systematically encoding and leveraging human research literature, allowing AI to build on existing knowledge rather than rediscovering fundamental principles. This approach offers a balance between open-ended exploration and informed search.
- **Sophisticated Feedback Analysis:** The dedicated "analyzer" module addresses a key bottleneck in complex scientific tasks by distilling multi-dimensional experimental data into actionable insights, enabling sustained and directed improvement over many iterations.
- **Generalizability Beyond AI:** The successful application of AI-evolved architectures to drug-target interaction prediction, a biomedical domain, suggests that the "AI-for-AI" paradigm can yield solutions with practical value in broader scientific and engineering fields. This indicates that AI-driven designs can possess transferable scientific utility.
- **Shift in Human Researcher Role:** As agentic systems like ASI-EVOLVE increasingly automate implementation and iterative experimentation, human scientists may transition from executors of solutions to primary definers of problems. This could allow researchers to concentrate on high-level conceptualization and formulating critical questions, while delegating the expansive search through hypothesis spaces to AI.

Overall, ASI-EVOLVE offers a model for future AI research, suggesting a potential paradigm shift where AI systems contribute to their own evolution, accelerating the pace of discovery across the entire AI development stack and potentially beyond.
