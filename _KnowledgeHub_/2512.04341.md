# Long-Horizon Model-Based Offline Reinforcement Learning Without Explicit Conservatism

> [!summary] Summary
> NEUBAY, a model-based offline reinforcement learning algorithm, utilizes a Bayesian principle without explicit conservatism for policy learning. It enables long-horizon planning via adaptive rollout truncation and architectural stability, demonstrating competitive performance across 33 benchmarks and achieving new state-of-the-art results on 7 D4RL and NeoRL datasets.

## Problem

- Most offline reinforcement learning algorithms rely on explicit conservatism (e.g., uncertainty penalties, short planning horizons) to mitigate value overestimation and unsafe extrapolation due to limited data coverage.
- While Bayesian RL offers a framework to optimize average-case performance under epistemic uncertainty, scaling it to complex tasks without reintroducing conservatism has been challenging due to compounding model errors and value overestimation.
- Explicit conservatism can limit a policy's ability to explore potentially high-reward but underrepresented actions, thereby hindering average-case performance and adaptation, especially in low-quality datasets.

## Method

- **NEUBAY** implements a ==Bayesian principle== by optimizing the expected return under a ==posterior distribution over world models==, using a large ensemble to quantify epistemic uncertainty and aiming for ambiguity-neutral average-case performance.
- The algorithm enables long-horizon rollouts by ==adaptively truncating trajectories== based on the model's ==epistemic uncertainty==, utilizing a ==quantile-based threshold== derived from the offline dataset.
- It incorporates design choices like a large ==world model ensemble (N=100)==, ==Layer Normalization== within the world model to stabilize predictions and bound errors, and ==history-dependent recurrent actor-critic networks (LRU)== with a small learning rate for context encoders to support adaptation and prevent overestimation.

## Results

- **NEUBAY** achieved competitive performance across **33** D4RL and NeoRL locomotion, Adroit, and AntMaze datasets, establishing new state-of-the-art mean performance on **7 tasks** (e.g., increasing walker-medium-v2 from **95.5 to 106.4** and pen-cloned-v1 from **74.1 to 91.3**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that adaptive long-horizon rollouts (with **ζ=1.0**) were critical, with shorter horizons leading to performance collapse due to severe value overestimation, and Layer Normalization was essential for stabilizing long rollouts.
- Introducing explicit uncertainty penalties generally degraded NEUBAY's performance, particularly on low-quality datasets, and replacing the history-dependent agent with a Markovian one severely reduced performance in complex domains like AntMaze.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit conservatism is not universally necessary for offline RL, as a non-conservative Bayesian approach can achieve competitive or superior performance through test-time adaptation, especially on low-quality datasets.
> - Long-horizon rollouts, when effectively managed with adaptive truncation and architectural stability (e.g., Layer Normalization), can actively mitigate value overestimation by reducing the reliance on biased bootstrapped terms.
> - Bayesian offline RL framed as an epistemic POMDP necessitates history-dependent recurrent agents to maintain long-term memory, enabling test-time adaptation and Bayes-optimal policies in uncertain environments.
