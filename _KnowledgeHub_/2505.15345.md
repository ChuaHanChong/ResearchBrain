# Hadamax Encoding: Elevating Performance in Model-Free Atari

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hadamax encoder, developed by researchers at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, significantly boosts the performance of pixel-based model-free reinforcement learning agents on the Atari-57 benchmark by approximately 80% over the PQN baseline. This novel architecture, combining max-pooling, Hadamard representations, and GELU activations, allows PQN to surpass Rainbow-DQN's performance much faster while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Neural network architectures in model-free reinforcement learning have remained relatively simple despite advancements in other deep learning domains.
- Scaling pixel-based RL architectures often leads to training instability or only modest performance improvements, especially in high-dimensional Atari environments.
- A more effective and stable encoder design is needed to unlock substantial performance benefits in model-free RL without complex algorithmic changes.

## Method

- The **Hadamax** encoder is introduced, a novel pixel-based convolutional architecture that enhances network capacity through specific design choices.
- It incorporates max-pooling for feature compression, Hadamard representations for rich multiplicative interactions between parallel hidden layers, and GELU activation functions.
- The encoder is integrated into the Parallelised Q-Network (PQN) algorithm and evaluated on Atari, with no changes to the base algorithm or hyperparameters.

## Results

- The Hadamax encoder achieved an approximately **80%** performance increase over the PQN baseline on the Atari-57 benchmark, setting a new state-of-the-art for model-free performance.
- Hadamax-PQN outperformed Rainbow-DQN, reaching comparable performance levels nearly three times faster (at **90M** vs. **260M** frames) with substantially reduced computational resources.
- The encoder's effectiveness generalized to other model-free algorithms, boosting C51 performance by ~**70%** on Atari-10 and DQN/Rainbow by 20-**30%** on Atari-3.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Significant performance gains in model-free RL are achievable through targeted architectural innovation rather than solely relying on algorithmic changes or increased network depth.
> - Hadamard representations effectively increase the network's capacity and effective rank in deeper layers, supporting stable training even with zero-saturating activation functions like GELU.
> - Max-pooling is crucial for efficient feature compression and selection, while the synergistic combination of max-pooling, Hadamard representations, and GELU is key to the encoder's performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has witnessed substantial progress, particularly since algorithms surpassed human performance on the Atari-57 benchmark. Historically, this progress has predominantly stemmed from algorithmic innovations, with the deep learning components of RL agents remaining comparatively simple. Architectures in image-based RL, such as the widely used Nature DQN encoder, typically consist of a few convolutional layers followed by fully connected layers. While modifications like the integration of ResNet encoders, inspired by their success in supervised learning, have been explored, attempts to significantly scale pixel-based RL architectures have often encountered challenges related to training instability or yielded only modest performance gains. Such scaling has found greater success in low-dimensional state-based continuous control or complex model-based architectures.

Concurrently, the development of vectorized RL frameworks leveraging JAX has enabled more efficient and scalable training, facilitating the exploration of new algorithmic and architectural paradigms. This work positions itself within this landscape by revisiting the premise that modifications to baseline network architectures necessarily lead to instabilities in RL. It specifically focuses on model-free RL agents operating in high-dimensional Atari domains, aiming to demonstrate that architectural improvements can yield substantial performance benefits without requiring complex algorithmic changes or hyperparameter tuning. By proposing a novel encoder architecture, the Hadamax encoder, the research seeks to offer an alternative direction for encoder synthesis in RL, distinguishing itself from the prevailing trend of simply adopting deeper ResNet structures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and evaluate a novel encoder architecture, termed Hadamax (Hadamard max-pooling), designed to enhance the performance of pixel-based model-free reinforcement learning agents. The authors aim to demonstrate that architectural innovations can lead to significant performance improvements in RL, challenging the common observation that changes to network architectures often result in minimal gains or training instabilities.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Architectural Simplicity in RL:** Despite advancements in deep learning architectures across other domains like supervised learning, neural network architectures in reinforcement learning have largely remained simple. The authors are motivated to explore whether more sophisticated encoder designs can unlock substantial performance benefits in RL, similar to their impact in other machine learning fields.
- **Leveraging Recent Advances:** The work builds upon two recent developments:
 - The Parallelised Q-Network (PQN) algorithm, which streamlined DQN by removing the replay buffer and target network while improving performance and stability through parallelization and LayerNormalization. This provides a robust algorithmic foundation for testing architectural modifications.
 - Advances in Hadamard representations, which have shown potential for improving effective rank and downstream performance in deep RL by enabling richer high-dimensional interactions within the representation space without increasing hidden layer dimensionality.
- **Seeking Alternatives to Deeper Networks:** Many attempts to improve RL performance through architectural scaling have focused on making networks deeper, often through residual connections (e.g., ResNet-15). The authors are motivated to explore an alternative approach to increasing network capacity and representational power that does not rely solely on increased depth, aiming for an encoder that remains shallow yet highly effective.
- **Improving Efficiency and Performance Baseline:** By significantly boosting the performance of a competitive baseline like PQN without altering its core algorithm or hyperparameters, the research aims to establish a stronger, more efficient baseline for future model-free RL research on Atari, potentially reducing the computational resources and time required to achieve high performance levels.

Specifically, the paper outlines the following contributions:

- Proposal of a novel deep learning architecture for pixel-based convolutional encoders in model-free RL, presenting an alternative to widely used deeper ResNet architectures.
- Demonstration of an approximately 80% performance gain over the PQN baseline in the Atari-57 suite without any algorithmic or hyperparameter modifications.
- Evidence that the Hadamax encoder allows PQN to surpass the performance of Rainbow-DQN significantly faster, setting a more robust baseline for model-free RL in Atari.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research develops a novel encoder architecture, Hadamax, and integrates it into the Parallelised Q-Network (PQN) algorithm. The methodology involves specific design choices for the encoder, an experimental setup comparing Hadamax-PQN with established baselines, and an analysis of the encoder's internal properties and the contribution of its individual components.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Parallelised Q-Network (PQN)

The Hadamax encoder is built upon the PQN algorithm, a simplified deep online Q-learning method. PQN modifies the original Deep Q-Network (DQN) by:
- **λ-return:** Utilizing a λ-return for more stable updates, blending 1-step TD learning with Monte Carlo updates.
- **LayerNorm:** Incorporating LayerNormalization after convolutional and MLP layers, before activation functions, to stabilize training.
- **Vectorized Environments:** Leveraging vectorized environments for efficient, parallel collection of trajectories, which enhances exploration and stabilizes training.
- **Removal of Replay Buffer and Target Network:** Eliminating the experience replay buffer and target network, a departure from traditional DQN, which is enabled by the stability provided by parallelization and LayerNorm, reducing memory usage and accelerating training.

#### 3.2. Hadamax Encoder Design Choices

The Hadamax encoder augments the conventional Nature DQN encoder structure with three principal design choices:

- **Design Choice 1: Down-sampling by Max-pooling:**
 - **Contrast with DQN:** Unlike the conventional DQN encoder which uses convolutional operations with strides for feature compression, Hadamax replaces this with max-pooling operators.
 - **Inspiration from ResNet-15:** This choice is influenced by the Impala ResNet-15 encoder, where max-pooling is a primary mechanism for feature compression. The rationale is that minimizing convolutional strides and using max-pooling allows for a denser representation of convolutional features, highlighting the strongest signals and introducing a degree of translation invariance.
 - **Max-pooling Before Flattening:** The Hadamax encoder also employs max-pooling of the final features before flattening them for the linear layer, contrasting with average-pooling used in some original supervised learning ResNet architectures. This is posited to better preserve feature importance, which is critical for value functions in RL.
 - **Shallow Architecture:** Despite influences from residual networks, the Hadamax encoder remains shallow, consisting of three convolutional layers, thus not requiring residual connections.

- **Design Choice 2: Application of Hadamard Representations:**
 - **Multiplicative Interactions:** The Hadamax encoder incorporates Hadamard products (element-wise multiplication) between parallel hidden layers. This allows for richer, high-dimensional interactions within the representation space without increasing the dimensionality of the hidden layers.
 - **Increased Network Capacity:** This mechanism is hypothesized to enhance network capacity and improve the effective rank of hidden layers, promoting a more expressive representation.
 - **Mathematical Formulation:** Each hidden layer $z_j$ is defined as the Hadamard product of two parallel layers, each activated by a non-linear function and connected to the preceding layer $z_{j-1}$: $z_j = \text{MP}(\text{f}(\text{LN}(z_{j-1}A_{j-1}^1)) \odot \text{f}(\text{LN}(z_{j-1}A_{j-1}^2)))$, where LN and MP denote LayerNormalization and max-pooling, respectively.
 - **Zero-Saturating Activations:** A notable aspect is the successful application of Hadamard products to zero-saturating activation functions (like GELU), which was previously considered problematic for stability. This is attributed to the inherent training stability of PQN, particularly its low fraction of "dead neurons."

- **Design Choice 3: Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU):**
 - **Activation Function:** All ReLU activation functions in the original encoder are replaced with GELU.
 - **Properties:** GELU is characterized by its ability to allow small negative values to pass through in a softened form, which is believed to enable more stable gradient flow for negative inputs compared to ReLU, potentially improving performance in various deep learning tasks.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines:** The Hadamax-PQN agent is compared against several model-free RL baselines: DQN, C51, Rainbow-DQN (a state-of-the-art method), PQN, and PQN with a ResNet-15 encoder (PQN (ResNet-15)).
- **Environments:** Experiments are primarily conducted on the full Atari-57 benchmark for 200 million environment frames, with results averaged over 5 random seeds. Ablation studies are performed on 40 million frames. Generalization capabilities are further tested on Atari-3 and Atari-10 subsets using the C51 algorithm.
- **Metrics:** Performance is evaluated using the median human-normalized score and the Atari-57 score profile. Internal analyses include tracking the effective rank of hidden layers and the fraction of dead neurons to understand the stabilizing effects and representational capacity.
- **Hyperparameters:** No algorithmic or hyperparameter modifications are made to the base PQN algorithm when integrating the Hadamax encoder, ensuring that observed performance changes are attributable solely to the architectural design.
- **Ablation Studies:** To quantify the contribution of each design choice, ablation studies are performed by selectively removing (or adding) max-pooling, Hadamard representations, and GELU activations from the Hadamax encoder and evaluating their impact on performance. Experiments with deeper Hadamax networks (5-layer and 7-layer) are also conducted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the Hadamax encoder's performance, generalization, and underlying mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Model-Free Performance:** Hadamax-PQN achieves state-of-the-art model-free performance in the Atari-57 benchmark.
- **Significant Gain Over PQN:** The Hadamax encoder yields an approximately 80% performance gain over the vanilla PQN baseline in the full Atari-57 suite.
- **Outperforming Rainbow-DQN:** Hadamax-PQN significantly surpasses Rainbow-DQN, a previously state-of-the-art model-free method. Notably, Hadamax-PQN reaches scores equivalent to Rainbow-DQN's 260M frame performance at only around 90M environment frames, while remaining over an order of magnitude faster in terms of GPU hours.
- **Comparison with ResNet-15:** The Hadamax encoder demonstrates clear benefits over the Impala ResNet-15 encoder when integrated with PQN.

#### 4.2. Per-Game Improvements

- Analysis on a per-game basis reveals a substantial performance increase across the Atari-57 suite. More than 17 games showed over 100% improvement with Hadamax-PQN compared to PQN, while only one game exhibited a performance decrease exceeding 50%.

#### 4.3. Generalization Beyond PQN

- **C51 Algorithm:** The Hadamax encoder's effectiveness extends beyond PQN. When applied to the C51 algorithm on the Atari-10 benchmark, it boosts performance by approximately 70% without any changes to C51's algorithmic hyperparameters.
- **DQN and Rainbow:** Similar improvements were observed when applying Hadamax to DQN and Rainbow on the Atari-3 benchmark, with performance boosts of 20% and 30% respectively. This suggests the Hadamax encoder can serve as a robust, generalizable default encoder for various model-free RL algorithms in the Atari domain.

#### 4.4. Effective Rank and Dead Neurons

- **Effective Rank:** Investigations into the effective rank of hidden layers revealed that deeper convolutional layers within the baseline PQN encoder exhibited a more pronounced decay in rank during training and a reduced initial effective rank. In contrast, Hadamax-PQN maintained a higher effective rank in these deeper layers, which is largely attributed to the Hadamard representations. Minimal differences were observed in the first and final hidden layers. This indicates that Hadamard products contribute to a more stable and expressive representation in the deeper parts of the network.
- **Dead Neurons:** The fraction of dead neurons in Hadamax-PQN's penultimate layer showed a minor decrease compared to the baseline PQN. However, both algorithms maintained a very low fraction of dead neurons (less than 8% after 200M frames), which is considerably lower than in standard DQN. This stability is posited to be critical for the successful application of Hadamard products with zero-saturating activation functions like GELU, circumventing previously noted instability issues.

#### 4.5. Contribution of Individual Design Choices (Ablation Study)

- **Max-pooling:** The ablation study showed that the subtraction of max-pooling from the Hadamax architecture led to the largest performance decay. This emphasizes the importance of max-pooling for effective feature compression, selection of prominent features, and maintaining a dense representation in high-dimensional observation spaces.
- **Hadamard Representations:** Hadamard representations were identified as another significant component. Their removal resulted in a notable performance drop, indicating that the increased effective rank and high-order interactions provided by Hadamard products are strongly correlated with downstream performance.
- **GELU Activation:** While contributing positively, the GELU activation function had the comparatively lowest individual impact among the three design choices. However, its contribution was still considered substantial for a relatively minor architectural modification.
- **Combined Effect:** The study highlighted that the synergistic combination of all three components is essential for achieving the full performance benefits, as individual additions to the baseline PQN did not yield the same degree of improvement as the complete Hadamax encoder.
- **Deeper Networks:** Simply increasing the depth of the Hadamax encoder to 5 or 7 convolutional layers, without further architectural modifications, did not result in improved performance, suggesting that a straightforward increase in depth may not be the optimal scaling strategy for this architecture.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the Hadamax encoder represents a significant development in the architectural design for model-free reinforcement learning. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that substantial performance gains in pixel-based RL can be achieved through judicious architectural innovations, rather than solely relying on complex algorithmic modifications or hyperparameter tuning.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

- **New Direction for Encoder Synthesis:** This work proposes an alternative approach to encoder design for RL, moving beyond the common practice of adopting deeper ResNet architectures. By achieving high performance with a shallow (3-layer convolutional) network that integrates Hadamard products, max-pooling, and GELU activations, it opens new avenues for exploring different forms of network capacity and interaction within RL.
- **Enhanced Performance Baseline:** Hadamax-PQN establishes a stronger and more efficient baseline for model-free RL on the Atari-57 benchmark. Its ability to surpass competitive algorithms like Rainbow-DQN while being significantly faster could accelerate future research by providing a more performant and accessible foundation for building new algorithmic improvements.
- **Generalizability Across Algorithms:** The demonstrated effectiveness of the Hadamax encoder when integrated into other model-free agents (C51, DQN, Rainbow) suggests its potential as a default encoder for a broader range of RL methods. This generalizability could simplify the development process for new RL agents, allowing researchers to focus more on algorithmic innovations rather than extensive encoder design from scratch.
- **Understanding Network Dynamics:** The analysis of effective rank and dead neurons provides insights into how the Hadamax architecture influences representation learning. The findings regarding increased effective rank in deeper convolutional layers and stable training with zero-saturating activations contribute to a better understanding of the internal mechanisms contributing to robust performance in deep RL.
- **Encouraging Architectural Innovation:** By showcasing the profound impact of architectural changes, this research may encourage the broader RL community to invest more effort in encoder synthesis, potentially leading to a richer diversity of network designs tailored for the unique challenges of reinforcement learning.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility:** The improved speed and performance of Hadamax-PQN can make high-level RL research more computationally accessible, potentially lowering the barrier for entry and allowing more researchers to conduct experiments with competitive baselines.

**Future work** outlined by the authors includes investigating more sophisticated scaling strategies for the Hadamax encoder (in both width and depth), exploring the integration of mixture-of-experts (MoE) style prediction heads, and augmenting Hadamax-PQN with novel exploration techniques to address challenges in hard-exploration games, thereby aiming to further bridge the performance gap between model-free and complex model-based algorithms. The acknowledged limitations include computational constraints that restricted testing on more complex algorithms (e.g., BBF, Dreamer) and the Hadamax encoder's comparatively higher computational overhead than PQN's conventional Nature CNN. Despite these, the work marks a notable step in advancing architectural understanding and performance in reinforcement learning.
