# Visual Thoughts: A Unified Perspective of Understanding Multimodal Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces "visual thoughts" as a unified concept to explain the effectiveness of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models. The study defines and analyzes four distinct expressions of visual thoughts, demonstrating that their clarity and conciseness are critical for MCoT performance and that they function as a cache, facilitating deeper visual information flow within the models.

## Problem

- The precise mechanisms and underlying reasons for Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) effectiveness in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) remain largely unclear.
- A lack of unified theoretical framework exists to explain why different MCoT paradigms (Textual-MCoT vs. Interleaved-MCoT) enhance LVLMs in distinct ways.
- There is an absence of systematic understanding regarding the optimal MCoT paradigm for various tasks or how to derive generalizable insights across diverse applications.

## Method

- Proposed =="visual thoughts"== as intermediate, logic-driven cross-modal representations that act as a cache for distilled visual information, bridging raw pixels and linguistic rationales.
- Categorized visual thoughts into four distinct strategies based on expression modality: ==Natural Language (N-LANG)==, ==Structured Language (S-LANG)== for Textual-MCoT, and ==Edited Image (E-IMG)==, ==Generative Image (G-IMG)== for Interleaved-MCoT.
- Conducted extensive experiments across four state-of-the-art LVLMs and various multimodal benchmarks, coupled with detailed internal attention and information flow analysis to verify the concept and mechanisms.

## Results

- All four distinct visual thought expressions consistently enhanced LVLM performance, with image-form VTs outperforming text-form VTs in complex tasks, showing up to a **47.83%** improvement on CoMT-Selection.
- Textual-MCoT is more effective for coarse-grained perception tasks, while Interleaved-MCoT excels in scenarios requiring fine-grained visual operations and complex manipulations.
- Internal analysis revealed that models shift attention significantly to visual thoughts, which then convey visual information more deeply into transformer layers and are primarily queried by input questions, optimizing visual data for reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual thoughts are essential for MCoT's effectiveness, functioning as a distilled visual information cache rather than a direct replica of the original image.
> - The clarity and conciseness of visual thought expressions are strongly and positively correlated with reasoning accuracy, enabling more effective information retrieval and utilization.
> - Visual thoughts facilitate deeper and more sustained visual information propagation into transformer layers compared to direct image input, acting as a vital intermediary for sophisticated cross-modal interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and the burgeoning field of multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning. LVLMs have demonstrated remarkable success in various multimodal tasks, largely due to their ability to process and generate information across visual and linguistic modalities. Inspired by the interpretability and enhanced reasoning provided by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in pure language models, researchers have extended this paradigm to multimodal contexts, resulting in MCoT.

The existing MCoT research is primarily segmented into two distinct paradigms:
1. **Textual-MCoT (T-MCoT):** This approach takes multimodal inputs (images and text) but generates purely text-based rationales. Examples include methods that first describe visual elements or integrate structured representations like JSON-format scene graphs before producing an answer.
2. **Interleaved-MCoT (I-MCoT):** A more recent and advanced approach, I-MCoT generates rationales that interleave both image and text. This often involves using external tools, such as code interpreters or specialized visual models, to modify or generate new images that become part of the reasoning process.

Despite the advancements offered by both T-MCoT and I-MCoT, a significant debate persists regarding their comparative advantages and the fundamental mechanisms driving their effectiveness. Some argue that I-MCoT, with its interleaved image-text rationales, better mirrors human cognitive processing, while others contend that text-only rationales might be superior in specific contexts, such as mathematical reasoning. This disagreement highlights a critical gap in the literature: a lack of a unified theoretical framework to explain *why* MCoT works, to identify optimal paradigms for different tasks, or to derive generalizable insights across diverse applications.

This paper directly addresses this fundamental gap. Instead of proposing yet another novel MCoT method, it seeks to provide a *unified perspective* on MCoT's effectiveness by introducing and thoroughly investigating the concept of "visual thoughts." By doing so, it shifts the focus from merely demonstrating performance improvements to deeply understanding the underlying cognitive-computational processes. This work contributes to the broader research landscape by laying a more theoretical and analytical foundation for multimodal reasoning, enabling a more principled development and application of future MCoT techniques. It aims to transcend the paradigm-specific debates by offering a mechanism that explains improvements across different MCoT formats, thereby advancing the interpretability and efficacy of LVLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a fundamental unresolved issue in the field of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT): despite its demonstrated ability to enhance the performance and interpretability of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), the precise mechanisms by which MCoT achieves these improvements remain largely opaque. The existing landscape is characterized by a dichotomy between Textual-MCoT (T-MCoT), which produces text-only rationales from multimodal inputs, and Interleaved-MCoT (I-MCoT), which generates rationales incorporating both images and text. The ongoing debate regarding the superiority of one paradigm over the other underscores a deeper lack of understanding about their respective strengths and the underlying cognitive processes they emulate.

This leads to the core research question the authors aim to address: **"Is there a unified explanation for that different MCoT paradigms enhance LVLMs in distinct ways?"**

To systematically investigate this question, the paper sets out several key objectives:
1. **Unify the Understanding of MCoT's Effectiveness:** The primary objective is to reveal that MCoT consistently enhances LVLMs by incorporating "visual thoughts" into the reasoning process. This concept is proposed as a cross-modal, logic-driven representation that conveys image information, regardless of the MCoT format, relying instead on its clarity and conciseness.
2. **Systematically Explore Visual Thought Expressions:** To comprehensively analyze visual thoughts, the authors aim to define and categorize four distinct forms of visual thought expressions. This categorization allows for a structured investigation into how different modalities (textual vs. visual) and levels of detail impact MCoT performance.
3. **Analyze Performance Differences and Influencing Factors:** The research seeks to demonstrate that these distinct forms of visual thoughts vary in their clarity and conciseness, leading to different levels of MCoT improvement. This involves identifying which scenarios are best suited for each visual thought expression and understanding the core factors that determine their effectiveness, such as expression clarity, conciseness, and susceptibility to external noise.
4. **Investigate the Internal Nature of Visual Thoughts:** A crucial objective is to delve into the internal workings of LVLMs to understand how visual thoughts function at a deeper level. This includes analyzing how visual thoughts serve as intermediaries between the input image and deeper transformer layers, facilitating more advanced visual information transmission and cognitive processing within the model.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond merely developing new MCoT techniques to establishing a foundational, unified understanding of *why* MCoT is effective and *how* it operates internally, thereby providing principled guidance for future research and development in multimodal AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is comprehensive, combining a novel conceptual framework with rigorous empirical validation and internal mechanism analysis. The core of their approach revolves around the introduction and systematic investigation of **"visual thoughts."**

#### 3.1. Definition and Conceptualization of Visual Thoughts

The authors define visual thoughts (VT) as "intermediate, logic-driven cross-modal representations that facilitate and accelerate multimodal reasoning within a unified perspective." Conceptually, they function as a "cache" for distilled visual information, bridging raw pixels and linguistic rationales. This allows LVLMs to access key visual content rapidly and context-awarely without repeatedly reprocessing the entire raw image, thereby improving reasoning efficiency and depth. Formally, a visual thought ($R_{E_{VT}}$) is a reasoning step conveying visual information from the input ($V_I$) and previous steps ($R_{<i}$), driven by the task question ($Q_T$) and explicit instructions ($I_{E_{VT}}$) for the MCoT expression ($E_{VT}$).

#### 3.2. Categorization of Visual Thoughts

Visual thoughts are categorized into four distinct strategies based on their expression modality, covering both Textual-MCoT (T-MCoT) and Interleaved-MCoT (I-MCoT) paradigms:

- **Textual Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (T-MCoT):**
 - **Natural Language (N-LANG):** Expresses visual thoughts as natural language descriptions (e.g., image captions). *Implementation:* LVLMs are prompted to generate image captions based on the query before reasoning.
 - **Structured Language (S-LANG):** Conveys visual thoughts through structured linguistic formats (e.g., JSON-format scene graphs). *Implementation:* LVLMs are prompted to generate scene graphs from input queries.
- **Interleaved Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (I-MCoT):**
 - **Edited Image (E-IMG):** Visual thoughts are represented by edited versions of the original image, produced by external vision tools (e.g., grounding, segmentation, depth estimation). *Implementation:* Edited images (e.g., with highlighted objects) are provided to LVLMs as supplementary input.
 - **Generative Image (G-IMG):** Visual thoughts are expressed as newly generated, logically related images, typically created by diffusion models (e.g., DALL-E 3). *Implementation:* DALL-E 3 is used to generate images based on input queries, which then assist reasoning.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Verification

The experiments were conducted using four state-of-the-art LVLMs: LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2-VL, GPT-4o-mini, and GPT-4o. The evaluation spanned a range of multimodal benchmarks, including:
- **Mathematical Tasks:** IsoBench (chess, math, graph).
- **Commonsense Tasks:** MMVP, V\*Bench, M3CoT-Commonsense, and CoMT (assessing visual grounding, object detection, fine-grained identification, and complex CoT reasoning).

#### 3.3.1. Effectiveness Verification (Section 3)

- **Comparison Conditions:** The authors compared three conditions:
 1. "Image-form visual thoughts" (standard I-MCoT with interleaved image-text rationales).
 2. "w/o visual thoughts" (visual thought cache cleared, forcing re-analysis of the original image, or image replaced by a placeholder).
 3. "Text-form visual thoughts" (cache restored with text descriptions of images from I-MCoT).
- **Baseline:** "Query-Only" (reasoning directly from the query) and "Caption Only" (providing only a generic image caption as context) were used as baselines to contextualize the benefits of dynamic visual thoughts.
- **Complexity Analysis:** Experiments categorized images by "description difficulty" (token count of captions: Easy, Medium, Hard-to-Describe) to evaluate VT effectiveness across varying visual complexities.

#### 3.3.2. Exploration of Different Categories (Section 4)

- **Systematic Evaluation:** Each of the four VT categories (N-LANG, S-LANG, E-IMG, G-IMG) was systematically applied and evaluated across all models and benchmarks, comparing their performance against a "w/o VT" baseline.
- **Reasoning Cost Analysis:** The average number of text and image tokens generated for each VT expression was calculated to quantify the computational burden.
- **Correlation Analysis:** Human annotators scored visual thoughts (on a 0-3 scale) for "Image Relevance," "Expression Clarity," and "Concise Expression." Spearman's (ρ) and Pearson's (r) correlation coefficients were then used to assess the relationship between these quality metrics and model accuracy.
- **Noise Impact Analysis:** For E-IMG and G-IMG, external tools (Grounding DINO and Stable Diffusion v1.5) were used, and their parameters adjusted to introduce varying levels of "noise" (evaluated by MLLM evaluation and CLIPScore). The correlation between noise level and reasoning accuracy was examined.

#### 3.3.3. Internal Rationale Analysis (Section 5)

- **Visual Attention Analysis:** Using LLaVA-1.5-7B, attention weights from the last token to the input image and to the visual thought were extracted across all transformer layers. This allowed observation of attention shifts and how deeply visual information is propagated.
- **Visual Information Flow Analysis:**
 - **Attention Blocking:** Selective blocking of information flow (e.g., between query and visual thought, or image and reasoning) by manipulating attention masks (setting to -inf) was performed to identify critical pathways.
 - **Saliency-Based Information Flow:** Saliency scores were computed using Taylor expansion for elements of the attention matrix to quantify the importance of information flow from visual thoughts to reasoning versus direct flow from the original image to reasoning.

This multi-faceted approach, moving from conceptual definition to empirical validation and deep internal analysis, provides a robust methodology for understanding the unified mechanisms of multimodal Chain-of-Thought.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, substantiating the concept of visual thoughts and elucidating their role in Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning:

#### 4.1. Effectiveness Verification of Visual Thoughts

- **Essential Role of VTs:** The experiments conclusively demonstrated that incorporating visual thoughts is *essential* for MCoT's effectiveness. Removing visual thoughts significantly impaired model accuracy, often performing worse than reasoning solely from the initial query. Conversely, including visual thoughts consistently improved reasoning performance across various tasks and models.
- **Image-form vs. Text-form VTs:** Image-form visual thoughts (I-MCoT) consistently outperformed text-form visual thoughts (T-MCoT), particularly in more complex scenarios (e.g., 47.83% improvement on CoMT-Selection). This suggests that the image modality is a superior channel for conveying detailed visual information, facilitating more efficient "cache-like" visual logic propagation.
- **VTs Beyond Simple Captions:** Visual thoughts were shown to be more than just descriptive captions. While basic captions performed comparably in simple scenes, visual thoughts significantly outperformed caption-only models in scenes of increasing complexity, delivering over 7% improvement when captions omitted essential details. This highlights their dynamic, cache-like function in integrating contextual visual cues.

#### 4.2. Exploration of Different Categories of Visual Thoughts

- **Universal Improvement:** All four distinct expressions of visual thoughts (N-LANG, S-LANG, E-IMG, G-IMG) consistently enhanced the performance of nearly all tested LVLMs across different benchmarks, confirming the general effectiveness of incorporating visual thoughts.
- **Task-Specific Benefits:** Visual thoughts provided the most substantial performance improvements in tasks requiring complex visual operations, such as those involving visual deletion, selection, and update (e.g., CoMT benchmark).
- **Paradigm Suitability:** T-MCoT (N-LANG, S-LANG) demonstrated superior reasoning performance on coarse-grained perception tasks, while I-MCoT (E-IMG, G-IMG) excelled in scenarios requiring fine-grained visual operations and complex visual manipulations. This implies that the choice of MCoT paradigm should be adapted to the specific task features.
- **Reasoning Costs:** Visual expressions (E-IMG and G-IMG) incurred higher reasoning costs due to the generation and inclusion of more image tokens, significantly increasing the computational burden compared to textual expressions.
- **Scenario-Specific Efficacy of Categories:**
 - **N-LANG:** Effective for coarse-grained, perception-oriented tasks, efficiently extracting high-level semantic cues from an entire scene.
 - **S-LANG:** Excelled in reasoning about object relationships by converting images into detailed scene graphs, accurately modeling spatial and semantic connections.
 - **E-IMG:** Achieved strong results in detailed image analysis by refining visual content through operations like magnification and annotation, improving attribute predictions.
 - **G-IMG:** Well-suited for multi-step reasoning requiring iterative image generation, dynamically creating new visuals to refine hypotheses and deepen understanding of complex concepts.

#### 4.3. Core Factors Influencing Visual Thought Effectiveness

- **Cache vs. Replica:** Visual thoughts function as a *distilled visual information cache*, not a faithful replica of the original image. There was no significant correlation between the fidelity (image relevance) of visual thoughts and model accuracy, indicating that VTs prioritize distilled information over exact reconstruction.
- **Clarity and Conciseness:** Both the "Expression Clarity" and "Concise Expression" of visual thoughts correlated strongly and positively with reasoning accuracy (Spearman's ρ and Pearson's r > 0.8, P < 0.01). This indicates that clearer and more compact representations of visual logic enable more effective retrieval and utilization of information from the visual thought cache.
- **Impact of External Noise:** For I-MCoT, noise introduced by external image editing/generation tools (e.g., Grounding DINO, Stable Diffusion v1.5) negatively impacted reasoning performance. Excessive noise could even degrade performance below that of native, direct reasoning, highlighting the criticality of managing external tool quality.

#### 4.4. Internal Rationale Behind Visual Thoughts

- **Attention Shift:** During reasoning, the model's attention shifts significantly from the original input image to the visual thoughts when present. This redistribution of attention facilitates the flow of visual information crucial for the logical framework.
- **Deeper Information Conveyance:** Visual thoughts demonstrated an ability to convey visual information *more deeply* into the transformer layers than the original image itself. While attention to the original image diminished sharply after layer 12 in "No-VIS" settings, attention to visual thoughts persisted and even maintained high levels in deeper layers, enabling more sophisticated cross-modal interaction.
- **Architecture vs. Scale:** The influence of visual thoughts on attention distribution depended more on the model's *architecture* (e.g., number of layers) than on its parameter scale.
- **Querying Visual Thought Cache:** Internal information flow analysis revealed that input questions primarily query visual information from the *visual thought cache*. Blocking the flow from the query to the visual thought cache significantly impacted correct predictions, whereas disrupting the direct flow from the original image had little effect.
- **VT as a Bridge:** The information flow from visual thoughts to reasoning stages was considerably stronger than the direct flow from the original image to reasoning. Crucially, most of the original image's input information was initially passed to visual thoughts, which then fed into the reasoning stage. This establishes visual thoughts as a vital intermediary, optimizing visual data for deeper reasoning in LVLMs.

These findings collectively provide a robust, unified explanation for MCoT's effectiveness, detailing *how* and *why* different visual thought expressions contribute to enhanced LVLM performance, both at a behavioral and an internal mechanistic level.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant contribution to the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and multimodal AI, primarily by offering the first comprehensive exploration into the **underlying unified mechanisms** driving the effectiveness of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unified Theoretical Framework:** By introducing the concept of "visual thoughts" as intermediate, logic-driven cross-modal representations, the paper provides a cohesive framework that bridges the disparate paradigms of Textual-MCoT (T-MCoT) and Interleaved-MCoT (I-MCoT). This unified perspective moves beyond simply proposing new methods to explaining *why* existing MCoT approaches work, thus offering a deeper, more principled understanding of visual cognition in AI.
2. **Enhanced Interpretability of LVLMs:** The detailed analysis of attention distribution and information flow within transformer layers sheds critical light on how visual information is processed and integrated into the reasoning path. Revealing that visual thoughts enable information to penetrate deeper into the model's architecture, unlike direct image input, significantly enhances our understanding of LVLM internal workings and contributes to the broader goal of building more interpretable AI systems.
3. **Practical Guidance for MCoT Design:** The systematic definition and evaluation of four distinct strategies for expressing visual thoughts (N-LANG, S-LANG, E-IMG, G-IMG) provide invaluable practical guidance for researchers and practitioners. By outlining the advantages, disadvantages, reasoning costs, and optimal scenarios for each strategy, the paper enables more informed decisions in selecting and developing MCoT approaches tailored to specific tasks and resource constraints. For instance, knowing that T-MCoT is better for coarse-grained tasks while I-MCoT excels in fine-grained operations allows for more efficient model design.
4. **Inspiration for Future Research Directions:** The findings open several promising avenues for future work. The confirmation of visual thoughts as a critical intermediary, the impact of expression clarity and conciseness, and the influence of external noise are all fertile grounds for further investigation. The authors explicitly suggest exploring multi-round visual thought interactions and ensembling diverse visual thought paths (as demonstrated with "diverse-VT" in the appendix), pushing the boundaries of dynamic and adaptive multimodal reasoning.
5. **Advancing Multimodal AGI Systems:** By offering a more profound understanding of how LVLMs "think" visually and integrate this thinking into complex reasoning, this work contributes directly to the advancement of multimodal Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Better understanding these mechanisms is crucial for developing AI systems that can reason more like humans across diverse sensory inputs.
6. **Addressing Key Debates:** The paper effectively addresses the long-standing debate between T-MCoT and I-MCoT by demonstrating that their differing effectiveness can be explained by the clarity and conciseness of the visual thoughts they generate, rather than an inherent superiority of one format. This reframes the discussion from "which is better" to "how can each best express visual thoughts for a given task."

In conclusion, "Visual Thoughts" provides a foundational piece of research that elevates the understanding of multimodal reasoning in AI. By offering a unified conceptual framework, empirical validation, and deep mechanistic insights, it not only explains *how* MCoT works but also provides clear guidance for its future development, promising to inspire new breakthroughs in the design of more intelligent, interpretable, and effective LVLMs.
