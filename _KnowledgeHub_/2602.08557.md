# Combined Constrained Sampling and Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CSRL trains universal non-prehensile manipulation policies by replacing hand-designed episode resets with a physics-informed constrained state sampler that solves contact, friction-cone and quasi-static equilibrium constraints to draw diverse contact modes as starts and goals, combined with projected interpolation and a reset curriculum. Across four contact-rich domains it lifts final success from 0.04 to 0.96 over random-drop resets.

## Problem

- Model-based contact solvers struggle with stiff non-smooth complementarity dynamics, while black-box RL sidesteps them but ignores rigid-body contact structure and explores rare contact states poorly.
- Existing reset designs, reverse curricula, replay-visited states, Go-Explore archives, expand the visitation distribution only locally, insufficient for distant, rarely reached manipulation configurations.
- Prior manipulation reset samplers use random scene generation or grasp-specific heuristics, offering no general way to specify non-prehensile, whole-body, balancing contact configurations.

## Method

- **CSRL** ==constrained state sampler==: sample binary contact modes, then an ==Augmented Lagrangian== solves a proximal ==nonlinear program== over pose, points of attack, and forces under non-penetration, friction-cone, and quasi-static equilibrium constraints.
- ==Projected Interpolation Resets (PIR)==: interpolate start and goal in a state embedding, then snap to the nearest constrained sample via kd-tree, keeping every reset feasible and collision-free.
- ==Reset curriculum== over off-the-shelf goal-conditioned RL (==TD7==, optional ==HER==): a phase variable schedules interpolation toward the goal and scales episode truncation time during early training.

## Results

- On panda-sphere, full CSRL reached **96.5%** success versus **4.4%** for random-drop starts and goals; the sparse-reward variant reached **95.9%** versus **0.2%**.
- Interpolation and curriculum were decisive on cube domains: sphere-cube rose **0%** to **39.9%** to **70.0%**, panda-cube **15.7%** to **21.1%** to **41.1%**.
- Projected interpolation beat reverse-curriculum resets (**96.5%** versus **54.2%** on panda-sphere), and HER without constrained sampling reached only **54.7%** versus **92.3%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reset and goal distribution design can matter more than the RL algorithm; encoding physics as sampling constraints beats heuristic scene randomization for exploration.
> - A constraint manifold gives a reusable curriculum metric: projecting convex interpolants onto feasible samples yields graded resets without simulator rollouts or task-specific engineering.
> - Gains assume privileged state; sampling cost grows with scene nonlinearity and precision-critical balancing goals still fail, leaving sim-to-real and solver speed open.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Non-prehensile manipulation, which involves controlling objects without grasping them directly, is recognized as a fundamental challenge in robotics. This domain frequently requires complex, contact-rich interactions, making it difficult for traditional control and planning methods.

**Traditional Model-Based Approaches:** Model-based solvers, which explicitly optimize robotic manipulation sequences through contact mode switches, face significant difficulties. These challenges arise from the non-linearities, complementarity structures, and inherent discontinuities in controllability that characterize contact dynamics. While these methods offer a deep understanding of manipulation's structure in rigid body domains, their application to real-world, dynamic scenarios remains constrained by these computational complexities.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Reinforcement Learning has emerged as a promising paradigm for contact-rich tasks, largely due to its ability to implicitly smooth over the underlying discontinuities in contact dynamics. However, RL methods in these settings typically suffer from a severe exploration problem. The optimal manipulation strategies often depend on reaching rare and specific contact states before the agent can receive informative rewards, hindering efficient learning.

**Existing RL Exploration and Reset Strategies:** To mitigate the exploration problem in RL, various strategies have been proposed:
- **Reward shaping and exploration bonuses:** Modifying the reward function to guide the agent towards relevant interactions.
- **Reset distribution design:** Altering the distribution of initial states ($p(s_0)$) for episodes. This includes reverse curriculum generation, resetting to randomly generated or previously visited states, and learned reset samplers tailored for specific tasks like reaching and grasping. While these approaches acknowledge the importance of the reset distribution, they often rely on heuristics, are task-specific, or expand the state visitation distribution only locally. This limits their generalizability, particularly for tasks requiring diverse, complex contact configurations such as object balancing.

**Goal-Conditioned RL and Universal Policies:** A related area focuses on training "universal policies" or goal-conditioned RL agents that can achieve any task within a fixed environment, given any start state and any goal state. Approaches like Hindsight Experience Replay (HER), reward relabeling, and hierarchical goal setting aim to improve sample efficiency. A common implicit assumption in these methods is that start and goal states can be trivially sampled from the problem definition. This assumption breaks down in complex non-prehensile manipulation, where feasible and meaningful start and goal states are themselves difficult to define and generate.

**This work's contribution** positions itself by bridging the gap between model-based insights and model-free RL capabilities. It proposes to leverage the structural understanding of contact physics, typically used in model-based planning, not for direct dynamics optimization but for efficiently *sampling* diverse, feasible manipulation states. These states then serve as goal and reset distributions to guide goal-conditioned RL, addressing the exploration challenge without attempting to solve the complex dynamics explicitly with model-based methods. This integration seeks to combine the strengths of both paradigms to tackle the core challenge of general non-prehensile robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the training of non-prehensile manipulation policies in contact-rich environments using goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL). The authors identify a core limitation in current RL approaches for these complex tasks: the difficulty of sufficient exploration and the discovery of intricate manipulation strategies due to sparse rewards and the need to reach specific, often rare, contact states.

The motivation for this work stems from two main observations:
1. **Effectiveness of Reset Strategies:** Modifying the initial state distribution of RL episodes (reset strategies) has shown promise in improving exploration and learning efficiency. However, existing methods often lack generality, relying on task-specific heuristics or local expansions of the state space that are insufficient for diverse contact-rich scenarios like balancing objects. A general method to specify diverse and relevant contact configurations for non-prehensile manipulation is needed.
2. **Efficiency of Model-Based Sampling:** While full model-based trajectory optimization through contact is computationally demanding, recent work suggests that model-based approaches for sampling states on constrained manifolds can be highly efficient. These approaches can inherently incorporate the physics of contact, allowing for the generation of physically plausible and diverse contact configurations.

Building upon these observations, the research sets out to achieve three specific contributions:
- **Novel Constrained State Sampler:** To introduce a new state sampler specifically designed for contact-rich manipulation. This sampler is intended to explicitly consider the structure of contact to generate a rich and diverse set of feasible contact modes. The generated states serve as both reset and goal distributions for goal-conditioned RL, thereby providing the policy with a comprehensive set of starting points and targets that are meaningful in the context of manipulation.
- **Novel Interpolation and Curriculum Reset Strategy:** To propose additional strategies that complement the constrained state sampler. This includes projected interpolation resets, which aim to bridge the gap between distant sampled states by projecting interpolated states onto the feasible manifold, and an optional curriculum learning approach that gradually increases the difficulty of reset states. These strategies are motivated by the challenge of exploration when random start and goal states, even if feasible, may still be too far apart for efficient learning in high-dimensional spaces.
- **Experimental Validation:** To empirically demonstrate that the proposed integrated approach, combining constrained sampling with the novel reset strategies, significantly improves the performance of RL for non-prehensile manipulation tasks. The goal is to train universal, dynamic, and robust manipulation policies capable of utilizing whole-body and environmental contacts across a broad range of goal states.

Ultimately, the motivation is to mitigate the exploration challenge in RL for non-prehensile tasks by imposing a state visitation prior that encodes domain knowledge about feasible manipulation states. This allows off-the-shelf RL methods to learn a universal policy to connect such states, thereby making complex manipulation skills more accessible to RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a novel framework, **Combined Constrained Sampling and Reinforcement Learning (CSRL)**, which integrates a physics-informed state sampler with off-the-shelf goal-conditioned RL.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The authors define a constrained goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP). This extends a standard goal-conditioned MDP by explicitly incorporating a *constraint space* ($S_c$). This space is defined by a set of inequality ($g_c(s) \leq 0$) and equality ($h_c(s) = 0$) constraint functions over the state space $S$. The goal is to train a goal-conditioned policy $\pi(s, g) \rightarrow a$ that maximizes the expected return, where both the initial state $s$ and the goal state $g$ are sampled uniformly from this constraint space $U(S_c)$. Sampling from this uniform distribution over $S_c$ is non-trivial, requiring the solution of a non-linear constraint problem, thus connecting the MDP formulation with Non-Linear Programming (NLP). This constraint formulation allows for the integration of prior domain knowledge regarding feasible manipulation states.

#### 3.2. State Sampler

The core of the methodology is the novel state sampler, which generates diverse, physically consistent states by formulating contact, collision, and force constraints based on existing manipulation planning literature.

- **State Constraints:**
 - **Non-collision:** For any pair of rigid shapes (i, j), the minimal distance $d_{ij}(s)$ must be non-negative.
 - **Contact Constraints:** For pairs in explicit contact ($c_{ij}=1$), the following conditions are enforced:
 - The shapes must touch at a Point of Attack (POA) $p_{ij}$, meaning distances $d_{ij}$, $d_{p_{ij}}$, and $d_{p_{ji}}$ are zero (Eq. 4).
 - The normal force component must be compressive or zero ($n_{ij}^T f_{ij} \leq 0$, where $n_{ij}$ is the normal pointing outwards from shape $i$), ensuring contact is maintained (Eq. 5). The paper states "normal force is positive", which in common physics context means repulsive/compressive.
 - The tangential force component must lie within a Coulomb friction cone, parameterized by $\mu$ (Eq. 6).
 - **Quasi-static Equilibrium:** For each unactuated shape, the net wrench $F_i$ from contact forces must balance the gravitational wrench $M_i g_{grav_i}$ (Eq. 7). This ensures the sampled states are statically feasible.

- **Non-linear Constrained Sampling:**
 - **Decision Variables:** The sampler operates on discrete contact modes ($c_{ij} \in \{0,1\}$) and continuous variables including generalized coordinates ($s$), POAs ($p_{ij}$), and contact forces ($f_{ij}$).
 - **Hierarchical Strategy:** A hierarchical approach is used. First, discrete contact modes ($c_{ij}$) are sampled. The current implementation assumes a single free object and a finite set of potential support shapes, limiting the number of active support contacts to 1, 2, or 3.
 - **Proximal Problem:** For a fixed set of contact modes, an Augmented Lagrangian method is employed to solve a proximal optimization problem: $\min_{s,f,p} \frac{1}{2} ||s - \bar{s}||^2$ subject to the defined constraints ($g_c, h_c$). $\bar{s}$ is a uniform random sample from box constraints, acting as a prior that is then projected onto the feasible space.
 - **Dataset Generation:** The process of sampling contact modes and solving the NLP is repeated until a dataset $D = \{s_i\}$ of $S$ feasible solutions is collected. The solver handles cases where initial samples or local optima lead to infeasible solutions.

#### 3.3. Additional Reset Strategies

To address the exploration challenge where randomly sampled feasible states might still be too distant for efficient RL, two additional strategies are proposed:

- **Projected Interpolation Resets (PIR):** Instead of directly resetting to a random $s \in D$, PIR involves sampling two states $a, g \in D$. The goal for the episode is set to $g$. The start state $s$ is then found by first computing a convex interpolation of their state vector embeddings $\phi(s)$: $[t\phi(a) + (1-t)\phi(g)]$ for $t \sim U$. This interpolated point is then projected back to the nearest *feasible* state in $D$ (using a kd-tree in $\phi$-space) to become the reset state $s$. This ensures the reset state is a valid, constrained state that lies "between" two sampled states.
- **Optional Reset Curriculum:** This strategy introduces a phase variable $\alpha \in (0,1]$ that linearly increases during an initial training phase. The interpolation for PIR is modified to use $t \sim U[0, \alpha]$, meaning initial resets are closer to the goal state $g$. Additionally, the MDP truncation time is also made proportional to $\alpha$, allowing for shorter rollouts near the goal early in training.

#### 3.4. RL Training Integration

CSRL integrates these components with an off-the-shelf RL algorithm. The TD7 algorithm is used as the default, with modifications to hyperparameters (e.g., larger training batch size, increased replay buffer size for harder problems). Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) is also integrated as an extension for further improvements in goal-conditioned settings. The training process involves an offline phase for generating the constrained state dataset $D$, followed by RL training blocks where start-goal pairs are sampled using either uniform sampling from $D$, PIR, or the curriculum-enhanced PIR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluates CSRL across four simulated domains of increasing complexity, designed to test various non-prehensile manipulation skills: double-sphere, panda-sphere, sphere-cube, and panda-cube. These domains cover scenarios ranging from simple object manipulation to whole-body contact and object orientation control.

#### 4.1. Comparison of Alternative State Samplers and Reset Strategies

- **Constrained Sampling (C) vs. Random Sampling (R):**
 - Table 1 demonstrates that using constrained sampling for both goal and start distributions (CC) significantly outperforms random sampling (RR) across all domains, particularly on the more complex panda-sphere and its sparse variant.
 - For instance, on the sparse panda-sphere domain, RR achieves a success rate of 0.002%, while CC (with interpolation and curriculum, CCic) reaches 0.959%.
 - Even using constrained sampling only for goals (CR, where starts are random) substantially improves performance compared to RR, indicating the critical role of meaningful goal distributions. On the double-sphere domain, CR achieves 0.813% success compared to 0.317% for RR.
 - Random sampling struggles notably in scenarios requiring specific object configurations, such as holding the sphere between robot limbs in the panda-sphere domain.

- **Projected Interpolation (i) and Curriculum Learning (c):**
 - The results consistently show that incorporating projected interpolation (i) and a curriculum (c) into the reset strategy further enhances performance (e.g., CC < CCi < CCic in Table 1). This improvement is observed even when using random sampling resets, highlighting the general utility of these strategies.
 - These strategies are particularly crucial for sparse reward tasks and more demanding environments like the cube domains (Table 3a), where they contribute to more stable training and higher success rates.
 - Figure 2 illustrates that these methods not only improve final policy performance but also accelerate convergence speed and sample efficiency during training.

- **Comparison to Reverse Curricula:**
 - Table 3(b) compares projected interpolation to reverse curricula (RC). The projected interpolation approach (Ci and Cic) generally outperforms RC, even when RC leverages constrained sampling for its initial goals. RC also incurred substantial computational overhead due to on-the-fly state generation.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Alternative RL Methods

- Table 2 evaluates the performance using different RL backbones within CSRL, including TD7, stablebaselines3's TD3, and HER extensions.
- TD7 demonstrated robust training behavior and scalability. TD3 was found to be less robust in this context.
- The HER extension of TD7 (TD7_HER) improved performance, confirming the benefit of HER in goal-conditioned settings.
- Critically, the advantage of constrained sampling for goals and resets held true across all tested RL methods, including HER variants. HER alone (without constrained sampling, e.g., RR with TD7_HER) performed significantly worse than CSRL's best configurations (e.g., 54.7% for RR-TD7_HER vs. 92.3% for CCi-TD7 on the panda-sphere problem). This indicates that constrained sampling is a fundamental enabler rather than a mere additive improvement.

#### 4.3. Character of Found Behaviors

The learned policies demonstrate high versatility, robustness, and dynamic behaviors:
- **Double-sphere:** Policies exhibited diverse skills for reaching quasi-static states, including balancing the sphere and reactive recovery from perturbations.
- **Panda-sphere:** The policies learned complex whole-body contact maneuvers to scoop, transport, and hold the sphere using various robot limbs.
- **Sphere-cube:** The policies were effective in rotating the cube to desired orientations and balancing it on edges or corners, showing dynamic and reactive behaviors, though sometimes with slight deviations from goal precision.
- **Panda-cube:** Strategies included rotating the cube via end-effector squeezes. Precision was sometimes below threshold for challenging edge/corner balanced goals.

#### 4.4. Real-World Transfer

A basic real-world transfer experiment was conducted for cube manipulation using an OptiTrack system for state tracking. A sphere-cube policy (where the simulated sphere robot acts as an abstraction for single contact interaction skills) was successfully transferred to a Franka Emika Panda manipulator equipped with a ball end-effector. This demonstrated the potential for these privileged-trained skills to be deployed in real-world scenarios through inverse kinematics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a central challenge in robotic manipulation: training versatile policies for non-prehensile tasks in contact-rich environments. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Non-Prehensile Manipulation:** The work makes a direct contribution to the field of non-prehensile manipulation by demonstrating an effective method for training universal policies capable of executing complex, dynamic, and robust skills. This includes scenarios like balancing objects and intricate whole-body contact maneuvers, which are traditionally difficult for robotic systems.

- **Bridging Model-Based and Model-Free Paradigms:** A key innovation is the strategic combination of physics-informed, model-based structural knowledge with the learning capabilities of model-free reinforcement learning. Instead of attempting full model-based trajectory optimization (which is computationally prohibitive for contact-rich dynamics), the paper effectively leverages model-based principles for *sampling feasible states*. This hybrid approach harnesses the strengths of both paradigms: using model-based understanding to define meaningful state spaces and using RL to learn the complex, often non-smooth, dynamics within those spaces.

- **Enhanced RL Exploration and Efficiency:** The proposed constrained state sampler, complemented by projected interpolation resets and curriculum learning, directly tackles the severe exploration problem inherent in contact-rich RL. By providing a diverse and physically plausible distribution of start and goal states, the framework allows RL algorithms to converge faster and achieve higher success rates, thereby improving sample efficiency. This is particularly impactful for tasks with sparse rewards, where exploration is most challenging.

- **Generalization to Universal Policies:** The framework facilitates the training of "universal policies" that can map a wide range of start states to a diverse set of goal states on a constraint manifold. This contrasts with task-specific policies and moves towards more generalist robotic manipulation capabilities.

- **Foundational for Sim2Real Transfer:** While the current work focuses on privileged training (full state access), the successful demonstration of basic real-world transfer for a cube manipulation task indicates the potential for deploying these learned skills. The framework provides a strong foundation for future work on sim-to-real transfer, potentially through teacher-student learning paradigms where privileged policies train sensor-based student policies.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also clearly identifies limitations and avenues for future work, notably improving the computational efficiency of constrained sampling for more complex scenes (e.g., via parallelization and specialized solvers) and explicitly addressing the sim-to-real gap for sensor-based student policies. These are crucial steps for translating the demonstrated capabilities into wider real-world applications.

In conclusion, this research provides a robust and effective methodology for training advanced non-prehensile manipulation skills, significantly advancing the capabilities of RL in contact-rich environments and offering a promising direction for developing more autonomous and versatile robotic systems.
