# Do as I Do: Dexterous Manipulation Data from Everyday Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The DO AS I DO algorithm generates dexterous manipulation data for multi-fingered robots by reconstructing 3D hand-object interactions from everyday monocular human videos and retargeting them into executable robot actions. This approach achieved a 71% success rate on reconstructed in-the-wild trajectories and demonstrated real-world execution across diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Generating large-scale, high-quality experiential data is a recognized bottleneck for training dexterous, multi-fingered robotic hands.
- Traditional data acquisition methods (e.g., real-world teleoperation, simulated exploration) are costly, require specific expertise, or are limited by environmental design and reward functions.
- Prior methods for learning from human demonstrations often relied on restrictive assumptions (e.g., simple tasks, known object geometries, specialized sensors), limiting their scalability to diverse, real-world scenarios.

## Method

- A two-part pipeline reconstructs 3D hand-object interactions from monocular RGB human videos and then retargets these to a robotic embodiment to produce dynamically feasible trajectories.
- Reconstruction leverages robust hand tracking, a novel object shape and pose estimation via ==guided diffusion== with adaptive parameters, and a clustering-based approach for efficient per-frame pose selection.
- Retargeting employs a dynamics-aware, sampling-based optimization in a GPU-accelerated physics simulator, enhanced with 'warmup steps', 'random force perturbation', and a 'transition reward' to handle noisy human references.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art 3D hand-object reconstruction on DexYCB and HOI4D, and human raters preferred its object tracking over a strong baseline (FoundationPose) in **67%** of in-the-wild videos.
- The retargeting pipeline yielded a **71%** success rate on 655 reconstructed in-the-wild trajectories, a substantial improvement over the **25%** baseline, with each novel component demonstrating incremental gains.
- Successfully generated 500 human-verified dexterous manipulation trajectories and demonstrated the real-world execution of 10 diverse tasks on a bimanual robot setup with Sharpa Wave hands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The confluence of advanced monocular 3D computer vision (for 4D hand-object estimation) and GPU-accelerated physics simulators enables a scalable pipeline to convert diverse human videos into robot-executable actions.
> - Robust 3D reconstruction from noisy, in-the-wild videos benefits significantly from adaptive guidance during object tracking and efficient clustering for pose selection.
> - Successfully bridging the gap between reconstructed human actions and robot execution requires specific retargeting innovations, such as initial stabilization (warmup), robustness against perturbations (random forces), and explicit encouragement of critical interaction changes (transition reward).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of large-scale, high-quality data is a recognized bottleneck in robot learning, particularly for complex tasks involving dexterous, multi-fingered robotic hands. Traditional methods for acquiring experiential data, such as real-world teleoperation or simulated exploration, are often constrained by operational costs, operator expertise, or the complexities of designing diverse simulation environments and reward functions.

Learning from human demonstrations, often referred to as the "Do as I do" paradigm, has been an area of research in artificial intelligence since the 1970s. This approach aims to allow robots to infer actions by observing expert human behavior. Historically, however, such methods have faced two primary algorithmic challenges: accurately recognizing or reconstructing human behavior, and effectively retargeting this behavior onto a potentially morphologically dissimilar robotic embodiment. Prior research in this area has often relied on simplifying assumptions regarding the observed behaviors (e.g., limiting tasks to pick-and-place), the objects involved (e.g., requiring 3D-scanned objects), or the data modalities used (e.g., necessitating depth information, 3D scans, or hand keypoints collected with specialized hardware). While these assumptions enabled progress, they limited the scalability and applicability of these methods to diverse, in-the-wild scenarios.

Recent advancements in adjacent fields have begun to offer new avenues for addressing these limitations. Specifically, the development of 3D computer vision models capable of reconstructing depth, objects, and hands in 3D from monocular RGB images and videos (thereby enabling 4D hand-object estimation) removes the reliance on specialized sensors. Concurrently, the emergence of GPU-parallel physical simulators (e.g., Mujoco Warp, Isaac) facilitates rapid sampling-based optimization algorithms, allowing for the inference of complex robotic actions in physically plausible environments.

This work, DO AS I DO, positions itself within this evolving landscape by attempting to bridge the gap between abundant, observational human video data and the experiential data required for robots. It seeks to leverage these recent advances in 3D computer vision and simulation to process general monocular RGB human videos and convert them into executable data for dexterous robotic hands, crucially without making restrictive assumptions on the behaviors or object classes, thus expanding the scope and scalability of the "Do as I do" approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a scalable algorithm, named DO AS I DO, capable of generating manipulation data for dexterous multi-fingered robotic hands directly from everyday, in-the-wild monocular RGB human videos. This algorithm aims to reconstruct detailed hand-object interactions from various video sources and subsequently retarget these interactions into a sequence of executable actions for a real-world robotic system.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing the Data Scarcity in Robot Learning:** Robot learning algorithms fundamentally rely on large datasets, yet obtaining experiential data for complex, dexterous manipulation remains a significant challenge. Methods like teleoperation are expensive, require skilled operators, and are limited by hardware transparency. Exploration in simulation, while flexible, demands intricate environment designs and reward functions for diverse tasks. This scarcity creates a bottleneck for advancing robot capabilities.
- **Leveraging Abundant Observational Data:** In contrast to the scarcity of experiential robot data, there is a vast and continuously growing repository of observational human data in the form of monocular RGB videos. Humans effortlessly learn by observing, but current robots struggle to translate "watching" into "doing." The motivation is to unlock this abundant resource, making human videos a primary, scalable data source for robot learning.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Prior "Do as I Do" Approaches:** Previous efforts to infer robot actions from human videos often operated under restrictive conditions. These included assumptions about simple behaviors (e.g., pick-and-place), known object geometries (e.g., 3D-scanned objects), or the availability of specialized sensor data (e.g., depth maps, 3D point clouds). These constraints hindered the application of such methods to general, unstructured, and diverse real-world scenarios captured in everyday videos.
- **Exploiting Recent Technological Breakthroughs:** The paper is motivated by the confluence of two recent technological advancements:
 1. **Monocular 3D Computer Vision:** Modern 3D computer vision models can now reconstruct 4D (3D + time) hand-object poses, object shapes, and depths from purely monocular RGB videos, even under challenging conditions like occlusions and low resolution. This negates the need for specialized 3D sensors.
 2. **GPU-Accelerated Physics Simulators:** The development of fast, GPU-parallel physical simulators (e.g., MuJoCo Warp, Isaac) enables efficient sampling-based optimization. This allows for rapid inference of robot actions that are dynamically consistent and physically plausible, crucial for generating executable trajectories.
- **Enabling General-Purpose Dexterous Manipulation:** The research is specifically motivated to support arbitrary rigid bodies and a wide range of dexterous behaviors, moving beyond task-specific priors or object category restrictions. This generality is critical for developing robots that can perform a diverse set of real-world manipulation tasks.

In summary, the key objective is to establish a complete pipeline that transforms diverse, noisy monocular RGB human videos into effective, real-world executable dexterous robot manipulation data, driven by the need for scalable data generation and empowered by advancements in vision and simulation technologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DO AS I DO algorithm is structured as a two-part pipeline: first, the reconstruction of 3D hand-object interactions from video, and second, the retargeting of these reconstructions onto a robotic embodiment to produce dynamically feasible trajectories.

#### 3.1. Reconstruction

The reconstruction phase focuses on identifying and tracking 3D hand and object poses over time from in-the-wild monocular RGB videos. This is achieved through a modular approach:

- **Hand Tracking:** For human hand tracking, the method leverages existing robust models, specifically HaWoR, which has demonstrated performance under diverse visual conditions.
- **Object Shape and Pose Estimation/Tracking:** This constitutes a core innovation:
 - **Initial Processing:** The process begins with three preparatory steps:
 - **Segmentation:** Hand and object segmentation is performed using SAM 3.
 - **Depth and Camera Intrinsics:** Monocular geometry and camera intrinsics are estimated using MoGe.
 - **Object 3D Mesh Generation:** A 3D mesh of the object is generated from a single image using SAM 3D, a generative model robust to occlusions and low resolution.
 - **Object Tracking via Guided Diffusion:** To ensure temporal coherence in object pose, SAM 3D is repurposed as a video object tracker. The key insight is to fix the object's shape (¯xs) from an anchor frame and then predict subsequent poses (xk_p) for each frame, biasing the prediction toward the previous frame's pose (xp k-1). This is implemented by integrating a flow matching inference process, where Euler steps blend model-predicted updates with target interpolants (zref) guided by the fixed shape and previous pose, using guidance strength parameters αs and αp.
 - **Adaptive Guidance Parameters:** Instead of a fixed αp, which could lead to over-rigidity or spurious flips, αp is dynamically adapted based on the object's estimated 2D rotational velocity, derived from point tracks (e.g., from BootsTAPIR) across consecutive frames. This adds an offline tracking pass and improves pose tracking.
 - **Sampling Per-frame Poses:** Due to the stochastic nature of guided pose sampling, multiple (N) candidate poses are generated for each frame. Rather than using the computationally intensive method of ranking candidates by the model's conditional log-density, a clustering-based heuristic is employed. Candidates are clustered based on a weighted SE(3) distance, and the mode-best pose is selected via consensus filtering and mask-IoU, significantly reducing computational cost while maintaining performance.
- **Hand-Object Alignment:** After independent reconstruction, the hand and object are aligned to a consistent near-metric space. The hand reconstruction scale is treated as ground-truth. Object translation is scaled relative to the hand using depth ratios obtained from MoGe pointmaps. An optimization problem solves for a scalar translation scale to align the object's visible-surface centroid with a target position derived from the hand's centroid. Finally, the entire trajectory is aligned with gravity using GeoCalib.

#### 3.2. Retargeting

The retargeting phase aims to convert the reconstructed (and potentially noisy) hand-object trajectories into physically plausible robot actions:

- **Dynamics-Aware Retargeting Core:** The method builds upon a sampling-based optimization framework (specifically, an MPPI-style algorithm described in Pan et al.) executed in a GPU-accelerated physics simulator (MuJoCo Warp). This framework involves kernel annealing across iterations and the prediction horizon, transitioning from broad exploration to local refinement.
- **Novel Components for Noisy References:** To robustly handle the inherent noise and incompleteness (lack of contact forces, morphological differences) of reconstructed references, DO AS I DO introduces three key innovations:
 - **Warmup Steps:** An initial "warmup" period (H steps) is prepended to the reference trajectory. During this phase, the object is virtually held in place (e.g., welded), allowing the robot hand to freely adjust its pose to achieve a stable initial grasp or configuration before starting to track the actual reference. This helps mitigate issues arising from noisy first frames or an object not being grasped initially.
 - **Random Force Perturbation:** To encourage robustness against minor disturbances and prevent the optimizer from getting trapped in unstable local minima (e.g., balancing an object on fingertips), random forces are applied to sampled rollouts. This implicitly trains the robot controls to be more resilient, drawing inspiration from sim-to-real transfer techniques.
 - **Transition Reward:** A specific penalty term is introduced to explicitly encourage successful object transitions (e.g., picking up or placing down an object). This reward penalizes the absence of object-floor contact during "resting" reference timesteps and the absence of hand-object contact during "in-hand" reference timesteps. This addresses the imprecision of tracking rewards alone in encouraging sharp, step-function-like interaction changes.

The overall approach combines state-of-the-art computer vision and simulation techniques with targeted algorithmic innovations to handle the specific challenges of real-world, noisy human video data for dexterous robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from evaluating DO AS I DO across various stages, including reconstruction, retargeting, and real-world deployment, and offers insights into human data filtering.

#### 4.1. Reconstruction Results

- **In-the-Wild Video Benchmark:** On a custom benchmark of 150 in-the-wild internet videos, human raters preferred the object tracking of DO AS I DO over FoundationPose (a state-of-the-art 6-DoF object tracker) in 67% of cases. The qualitative analysis indicated that DO AS I DO demonstrated superior temporal consistency and robustness to common challenges like motion blur and occlusions, where FoundationPose often lost object lock.
- **Standard Hand-Object Reconstruction Benchmarks:** The method achieved state-of-the-art performance on the DexYCB and HOI4D datasets.
 - On DexYCB, DO AS I DO achieved F-5 of 0.71, F-10 of 0.93, and Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.66, outperforming prior joint hand-object reconstruction methods (e.g., G-HOP: F-5 0.31, F-10 0.49, CD 8.11) and object-only trackers (e.g., FoundationPose: F-5 0.69, F-10 0.89, CD 0.89).
 - Similarly, on HOI4D, the method showed strong performance (F-5 0.72, F-10 0.91, CD 0.49), surpassing baselines.
- **Object Tracking Ablations:** Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of design choices. Adaptive pose guidance, derived from 2D point tracks, consistently improved reconstruction quality. The clustering-based approach for selecting per-frame poses was found to perform comparably to a more computationally intensive log-likelihood selection method but was approximately 30 times faster.

#### 4.2. Retargeting Results

- **Performance on Reconstructed Data:** When applied to the 655 reconstructed in-the-wild trajectories, DO AS I DO achieved a 71% success rate (defined by mean position error < 0.1m and mean rotation error < 0.5 rad), significantly outperforming the baseline Annealed Sampling method (25% success rate).
- **Performance on Clean MoCap Data (OakInk2):** The method was also evaluated on OakInk2, a dataset of 1,352 bimanual human-object task trajectories with clean MoCap data. Here, DO AS I DO achieved an 81% success rate, improving upon the baseline of 72%.
- **Contribution of Novel Components:** The incremental introduction of the proposed retargeting components demonstrated consistent performance gains:
 - Adding "Warmup Steps" to Annealed Sampling substantially increased success rates (from 25% to 66% on reconstructed data, and 72% to 77% on OakInk2). This component was a primary factor in successful initializations.
 - Further adding "Random Force Perturbation" showed a marginal quantitative improvement but was noted to enhance the qualitative naturalness of grasps.
 - The "Transition Reward" further improved success rates (to 71% on reconstructed data and 81% on OakInk2) by encouraging successful picks and places during critical interaction changes.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment

- The complete pipeline generated 500 human-verified dexterous manipulation trajectories of high quality, sourced from internet (53%), egocentric (31%), and generated (16%) videos.
- A representative set of 10 tasks, involving various object geometries and grasp types (e.g., writing tripod, power, ventral, parallel extension grasps), were successfully executed on a real bimanual robot setup comprising Sharpa Wave hands and UR3e arms. The process involved manually aligning the initial pose with the robot workspace in simulation before computing arm inverse kinematics and deployment. The results were visually demonstrated, indicating the practical viability of the system to transfer skills from human videos to real robots.

#### 4.4. Human Data Filtering Playbook

- An analysis performed on 2,000 10-second clips from the 100DOH dataset (pre-filtered for hand-object interaction) revealed significant quality issues. Only 187 clips (9%) contained meaningful interaction, and after further filtering for issues like hands/objects being out of frame, inconsistent activity, camera motion, or model failures, only 83 clips (approximately 4%) remained suitable for the reconstruction pass.
- This analysis suggests a potential 20-fold penalty in efficiency if internet videos are not rigorously preprocessed and filtered for quality when intended for dexterous manipulation learning.

In summary, the main findings indicate that DO AS I DO offers a robust and effective pipeline for generating high-quality dexterous manipulation data from diverse monocular RGB human videos, outperforming existing methods in reconstruction accuracy and achieving significant success rates in dynamics-aware retargeting, with real-world validation. The insights into data quality highlight the practical challenges and necessary preprocessing for leveraging in-the-wild human data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DO AS I DO framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robot learning and manipulation:

- **Scalable Data Generation for Dexterous Robotics:** The most prominent impact is addressing the critical data bottleneck in robot learning. By enabling the scalable generation of dexterous manipulation data from readily available, diverse human videos, the work offers a pathway to expand robot capabilities without relying on expensive, labor-intensive, or constrained data collection methods like teleoperation or specialized lab setups. This scalability is crucial for training complex policies for multi-fingered robot hands.
- **Unlocking Abundant Human Observational Data:** The ability to process in-the-wild monocular RGB videos transforms the vast repository of human observational data into a usable resource for robots. This establishes human videos as "first-class citizens" in the robot learning data landscape, moving beyond prior reliance on specialized sensors or structured environments. This significantly broadens the potential scope of tasks and interactions robots can learn.
- **Generalization Across Diverse Scenarios:** The methodology's robustness across egocentric, exocentric, and online video sources, combined with its ability to handle arbitrary rigid objects without prior assumptions on behavior or object categories, contributes to a more generalizable approach to robot skill learning. This contrasts with previous methods that often required specific task priors or known object models.
- **Robustness to Real-World Data Imperfections:** The innovations in the retargeting phase, particularly the warmup steps, random force perturbations, and transition rewards, demonstrate the system's ability to cope with the inherent noise, incompleteness, and morphological discrepancies present in reconstructed human references. This robustness is vital for the practical application of the framework to uncurated, real-world video data.
- **Advancing the "Do as I Do" Paradigm:** This work pushes the frontier of the long-standing "Do as I do" concept by successfully reconstructing complex dexterous actions from general video inputs and translating them into executable trajectories for real robotic systems. It represents a significant step towards enabling robots to learn skills by simply observing humans in their natural environment.
- **Practical Guidance for Data Curation:** The "human data filtering playbook" derived from analyzing existing datasets provides valuable practical insights for researchers attempting to leverage internet video data. By highlighting common failure modes and quantifying the high ratio of unsuitable clips, it helps practitioners more efficiently curate relevant data, potentially saving significant time and computational resources.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays groundwork for future advancements. Limitations identified, such as the assumption of rigid objects, reliance on semi-accurate depth predictions, and the lack of full scene understanding (environmental constraints, deformable objects, articulated parts), represent clear avenues for subsequent research to build upon this foundation. Improvements in physics simulators and scene-level reasoning could further enhance the real-world performance and applicability of such systems.

In essence, DO AS I DO contributes a comprehensive and practical pipeline that facilitates the transfer of complex dexterous manipulation skills from human observation to robotic execution, offering a significant stride towards more autonomous and versatile robots trained through scaled data.
