# Why DPO is a Misspecified Estimator and How to Fix It

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science, IIT Kanpur, and HP AI Research diagnosed DPO as a misspecified estimator for parametric language models and introduced AuxDPO, a new direct preference optimization algorithm. This method mitigates DPO's inherent limitations by preventing preference reversals and reward degradation, demonstrating consistent performance improvements across various LLM alignment tasks and datasets.

## Problem

- Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), widely used for LLM alignment, relies on an idealized tabular policy class assumption that does not hold for real-world parametric deep neural networks.
- This fundamental misspecification can lead to DPO learning policies that exhibit preference reversals, where a less preferred option is favored, or even decrease the expected reward relative to the base model.
- DPO's outcomes are highly sensitive to the distribution and imbalance of pairwise preference data, potentially yielding inconsistent and suboptimal alignment even with sufficient data.

## Method

- **AuxDPO** introduces ==auxiliary variables== into the DPO loss, operating within the null space of a matrix identified via RLHF's local geometry, and jointly optimizes policy parameters with these variables to expand the effective search space for reward functions.
- For large LLMs, a practical batchwise relaxation is implemented, incorporating soft penalties to guide the auxiliary variables towards the null space while managing computational complexity.
- Theoretical analysis shows that by allowing optimization over the null-space component, AuxDPO bypasses DPO's misspecification and enables it to converge to the true RLHF-optimal policy.

## Results

- In didactic multi-armed bandit examples with imbalanced preference data, DPO, IPO, and DPOP demonstrated failure modes by reversing preference order and decreasing average reward, whereas AuxDPO successfully maintained the correct order and increased reward.
- AuxDPO consistently achieved higher alignment accuracies and percentage gains relative to the base policy compared to DPO, IPO, and DPOP across various LLM models (Llama3.1-**8B**, Llama3.2-**1B**, Qwen3-**0.6B**) and benchmark datasets (MMLU-PRO, REWARDBENCH V2).
- The approach exhibited particular robustness in out-of-domain evaluation settings and when operating with a limited fraction of trainable parameters (e.g., LoRA setups), outperforming DPO and often preventing performance degradation relative to the base policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DPO fundamentally solves a misspecified statistical estimation problem via weighted KL-projection of the true reward onto a restricted implicit-reward manifold, explaining DPO's failure modes (reversing preferences, reducing expected reward) even with infinite clean data.
> - Analysis of RLHF reveals equivalence classes of reward functions yielding the same optimal policy, differing by null-space vectors; DPO's implicit reward is a minimum-norm representative within these classes, failing to capture the true reward function.
> - The theoretical validity of local linear and quadratic approximations for both DPO's implicit reward manifold and the RLHF objective can be guaranteed by choosing a sufficiently large KL regularization parameter "beta".

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical, yet often overlooked, theoretical limitation of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a widely adopted method for aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. The broader research landscape is dominated by the challenge of making LLMs helpful, harmless, and honest, a process broadly termed "alignment."

Traditionally, LLM alignment relies on Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF), a two-stage process. First, a reward model (RM) is trained on human preference data (e.g., chosen vs. rejected responses). Second, a policy (the LLM itself) is fine-tuned using reinforcement learning (typically PPO-style algorithms) to maximize the learned reward, while staying close to a reference policy (e.g., the supervised fine-tuned model) via a KL-divergence penalty. While effective, RLHF is computationally expensive and complex, requiring careful hyperparameter tuning and often suffering from engineering challenges like reward hacking and variance reduction.

The introduction of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) by Rafailov et al. (2023) was a "landmark step" because it bypassed the explicit training of a separate reward model and the complexities of reinforcement learning. DPO reframes the preference alignment problem as a single-stage supervised learning task, directly optimizing the policy based on preference data. This simplicity and computational efficiency led to DPO's widespread adoption in both industrial and open-source communities.

However, the theoretical derivation of DPO relies on a crucial, idealized assumption: that the policy class is *tabular*, meaning it can represent any arbitrary input-output conditional probability distribution. In reality, LLMs are based on deep neural networks (e.g., Transformers), representing *parametric* policy classes with a finite number of parameters, which are far from tabular. This paper highlights this discrepancy as a fundamental source of DPO's limitations.

The current work directly challenges this tabular policy class assumption, positioning itself among a growing body of research that scrutinizes DPO's theoretical underpinnings and empirical behaviors. Other related works have explored different aspects:
- Some question the tabular assumption even in two-stage RLHF (Gao et al., 2024; Swamy et al., 2025).
- Others empirically observe DPO's pathological behaviors, such as reducing the absolute likelihood of chosen responses (Tajwar et al., 2024) or propose ad-hoc fixes (Meng et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024a).
- A line of work investigates DPO's shortcomings related to lack of "coverage" (Xu et al., 2024b; Song et al., 2024), arguing that DPO might fail if the base policy does not adequately cover the optimal policy. This paper, however, demonstrates DPO's failures even with *perfect coverage*.
- Another strand of research focuses on the gradient dynamics of DPO, identifying issues like "likelihood displacement" (Pal et al., 2024; Razin et al., 2024; Jian et al., 2025). This paper differentiates itself by analyzing the *minimizer* of the DPO loss, thereby showing failure modes irrespective of the specific optimization algorithm.
- Shi et al. (2025) present a multi-armed bandit example where DPO fails for a log-linear policy. This paper extends this with a more fine-grained geometric analysis and demonstrates broader failure modes beyond a degenerate scenario.

In essence, this work provides a rigorous, principled, and geometric framework to diagnose DPO's "misspecification" for parametric policies, going beyond empirical observations or specific failure scenarios to offer a fundamental statistical interpretation. By proposing AuxDPO, it also contributes a theoretically grounded solution that aligns DPO's objective more closely with the true RLHF objective, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in LLM alignment algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the widespread adoption of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for LLM alignment, despite its theoretical foundation resting on an idealized assumption (tabular policy class) that does not hold for real-world parametric LLMs. The authors identify a critical gap: if DPO's underlying assumption is violated, how does it behave, and are its performance guarantees still valid?

The key objectives of this paper are:

1. **Systematically Uncover DPO's Geometry:** To introduce a comprehensive framework for analyzing the behavior of DPO when applied to general parametric policy classes, typical of LLMs.
2. **Diagnose Misspecification:** To formally demonstrate that DPO, in the context of parametric policies, inherently solves a *misspecified statistical estimation problem*. Specifically, DPO projects the true, preference-generating reward function onto a potentially much smaller, lower-dimensional manifold of reward functions that are implicitly expressible by the parametric policy class.
3. **Identify and Characterize Failure Modes:** To rigorously show that this misspecification leads to undesirable phenomena even with ample and clean preference data. These include:
 - **Preference order reversal:** The fine-tuned policy may prefer a less desirable response over a more preferred one.
 - **Worsening of policy reward:** The expected reward of the fine-tuned policy can actually decrease compared to the initial base policy.
 - **High sensitivity to data distribution:** DPO's outcome can be highly dependent on the specific frequencies of pairwise preferences in the training data, leading to inconsistent or arbitrary estimates.
4. **Gain Insights from RLHF's Local Geometry:** To study the local behavior of two-stage RLHF for parametric policies, drawing connections to natural gradient steps and uncovering concepts like equivalence classes of reward functions that yield the same optimal policy. This is done to understand the "ideal" alignment target.
5. **Propose a Principled Fix (AuxDPO):** To leverage the theoretical insights gained from both DPO's misspecification and RLHF's local geometry to design a new direct preference optimization algorithm, AuxDPO. This algorithm aims to mitigate DPO's misspecification by introducing auxiliary degrees of freedom in the reward space.
6. **Empirically Validate AuxDPO:** To demonstrate the superior performance and robustness of AuxDPO compared to standard DPO and other baselines, both in didactic multi-armed bandit settings and on real-world LLM alignment tasks using benchmark datasets.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond DPO's empirical success to a deeper theoretical understanding of its limitations and to provide a more robust and theoretically sound direct alignment algorithm for LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper is a rigorous blend of theoretical analysis and empirical validation. The authors systematically dissect DPO's mechanism for parametric policies and then construct a principled solution.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis

1. **DPO as Weighted KL-Projection (Proposition 1):** The authors start by formally showing that minimizing the DPO loss for a given parametric policy class is equivalent to finding a policy whose *implicit reward function* (derived from the KL-regularized objective) performs a *weighted KL-divergence projection* of the true, preference-generating reward function ($r^*$) onto the *manifold of implicit reward functions* ($R_\beta$) expressible by the policy class. The weights in this projection are determined by the observed frequencies of preference pairs in the dataset.
2. **Diagnosis of Misspecification:** The core insight here is that for parametric policy classes (e.g., neural networks), the manifold $R_\beta$ is typically a lower-dimensional subspace of the entire reward space $R^m$ (where $m = |S| \cdot |A|$ is the total number of state-action pairs). If the true reward function $r^*$ does not lie perfectly within this expressible manifold $R_\beta$, then DPO operates in a *misspecified statistical estimation setting*. In such cases, the projection depends heavily on the data distribution, and the resulting policy is not guaranteed to be optimal.
3. **Local Geometry of DPO and Failure Mode Demonstration (Proposition 3):** To analyze the misspecified setting, the authors employ a *local linearization* approach. They approximate the implicit reward function $r_\beta^\theta$ using a first-order Taylor expansion around the base policy $\theta_0$. This linear approximation simplifies the manifold $R_\beta$ to the column space of a matrix $A_{\theta_0}^T$.
 - Crucially, they construct a didactic example (single-prompt, 3-response, 1D linear softmax policy) where, despite having "clean data" (infinite preferences from a true $r^*$), DPO exhibits severe failure modes:
 - **Preference reversal:** The learned policy prefers an action that the true reward model ranks lower.
 - **Reward reduction:** The expected reward of the learned policy is *lower* than that of the base policy.
 - **Sensitivity:** These failures are shown to be contingent on the *imbalance* of pairwise preference counts, demonstrating DPO's sensitivity to data distribution even with perfect coverage.
4. **Local Geometry of RLHF Optimization:** To understand the target for a corrected DPO, the authors analyze the optimal KL-regularized policy from the perspective of two-stage RLHF. They locally approximate the RLHF objective (expected reward minus KL penalty) using Taylor expansions (first-order for reward, second-order for KL).
 - This analysis yields a generalized relationship between the optimal policy $\theta^*$ and the true reward $r^*$, resembling a *natural gradient step* (Equation 5).
 - **Equivalence Classes (Lemma 6, Proposition 7):** A key finding is that the RLHF objective induces "equivalence classes" of reward functions: any two reward functions that differ by an element in the *null space* of a specific matrix ($A_{\rho,\theta_0}$) will lead to the same optimal policy. The DPO's implicit reward (under linearization) is shown to be the *minimum Mahalanobis-norm representative* of such an equivalence class.
5. **Approximation Error Control (Proposition 8):** The validity of the local linear/quadratic approximations is theoretically justified by showing that the approximation errors for both DPO's implicit reward manifold and the RLHF objective can be made arbitrarily small by choosing a sufficiently large KL regularization parameter $\beta$.

#### 3.2. Algorithmic Approach: AuxDPO

1. **Leveraging Nullspace Insight:** The core idea for AuxDPO is derived from the observation that the *true reward function* ($r^*$) and the *RLHF-optimal implicit reward* ($r_\beta^{\theta^*}$) belong to the same equivalence class, meaning they differ by a vector $\delta$ that lies in the *null space* of $A_{\rho,\theta_0}$. DPO fails because it cannot "see" this null-space component $\delta$.
2. **Introducing Auxiliary Variables:** AuxDPO introduces these auxiliary variables $\delta$ directly into the DPO loss function. The objective becomes a joint minimization over both the policy parameters $\theta$ and these auxiliary variables $\delta$, subject to the constraint that $\delta$ must lie in the null space of $A_{\rho,\theta_0}$.
3. **Bypassing Misspecification (Proposition 9):** By allowing optimization over $\delta$ in the null space, the search space for rewards is expanded from the limited manifold $R_\beta$ to the entire reward space $R^m$. This effectively removes the misspecification, enabling AuxDPO to find a combination of $\theta$ and $\delta$ such that the combined reward $r_\beta^\theta + \delta$ can accurately represent $r^*$, leading to the RLHF-optimal policy $\theta^*$.
4. **Practical Implementation:**
 - For smaller parameter counts ($d$), the null-space constraint $\delta \in N(A_{\theta_0})$ can be enforced exactly by reparameterizing $\delta$ using an orthonormal basis for the null space.
 - For large LLMs where forming the full $A_{\theta_0}$ is infeasible, a *batchwise relaxation* is proposed. This involves adding a soft penalty term ($\lambda_{null} ||A_{\theta_0,B} \delta_B||^2$) to the loss, pushing the batch-specific $\delta_B$ towards the null space, along with a small regularization term ($\lambda_{amp} ||\delta_B||^2$). The auxiliary variables $\delta$ are defined per example.

#### 3.3. Empirical Validation

- **Didactic Bandit Settings:** The failure modes of DPO and the effectiveness of AuxDPO are first demonstrated using the previously constructed 3-response, 1D-policy example with imbalanced preference data.
- **LLM Alignment Tasks:** AuxDPO is evaluated on real-world LLM preference tuning tasks using benchmark datasets like REWARDBENCH V2 and MMLU-PRO. It is compared against DPO, IPO, and DPOP on various Llama and Qwen models, under both in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) settings.
- **Ablation Studies:** The paper also includes ablation studies on the fraction of trainable parameters to assess AuxDPO's performance at different model capacities, including low-rank adaptation (LoRA) settings.

This comprehensive approach—from theoretical diagnosis to principled solution and extensive empirical validation—establishes AuxDPO as a robust and theoretically sound improvement over existing direct preference optimization methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant theoretical findings and compelling empirical results:

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

1. **DPO is a Misspecified Estimator (Proposition 1):** The most fundamental finding is that for general parametric policy classes (like those used in LLMs), DPO performs a *weighted KL-projection* of the true, underlying reward function ($r^*$) onto the *manifold of implicit reward functions* ($R_\beta$) that the parametric policy can represent. Since $R_\beta$ is typically a lower-dimensional manifold in the full reward space, this constitutes a *misspecified statistical estimation problem* when $r^*$ lies outside $R_\beta$. The quality of this projection (and thus the resulting policy) becomes dependent on the arbitrary weighting given by preference data frequencies.
2. **Failure Modes of DPO (Proposition 3):** The authors rigorously demonstrate that this misspecification can lead to severe and counter-intuitive failure modes, even in an idealized data-rich setting:
 - **Preference Order Reversal:** DPO can learn a policy that reverses the preference order of responses, favoring a less preferred option over a more preferred one.
 - **Reward Reduction:** The expected reward of the DPO-optimized policy can actually *decrease* compared to the base (unaligned) policy, contradicting the goal of alignment.
 - **High Sensitivity to Data Distribution:** The outcome of DPO is highly sensitive to the relative frequencies of pairwise preference counts in the training data. Different data distributions, all reflecting the same true underlying preferences, can lead to drastically different and often suboptimal DPO policies. This highlights that DPO's issues are not merely due to scarce data but intrinsic to its formulation with parametric policies.
3. **RLHF Equivalence Classes and Null Space (Lemma 6, Proposition 7):** Analysis of RLHF reveals that different reward functions can lead to the same optimal policy if they differ by an element in the null space of a specific matrix $A_{\rho,\theta_0}$. The implicit reward function learned by a linearized DPO is shown to be the minimum-norm representative within these equivalence classes. This insight is crucial for designing AuxDPO.
4. **Auxiliary Variables Mitigate Misspecification (Proposition 9):** By introducing auxiliary variables $\delta$ that operate within this null space and optimizing jointly, AuxDPO effectively expands the search space of reward functions. This allows the combined policy-induced reward ($r_\beta^\theta + \delta$) to accurately represent the true $r^*$, thereby bypassing the misspecification and enabling AuxDPO to find the RLHF-optimal policy.

#### 4.2. Empirical Results

1. **Didactic Bandit Demonstration (Tables 4 & 5):**
 - In the 3-response, 1D-policy example, with imbalanced preference counts (n12=5, n23=5, n31=50), DPO, IPO, and DPOP exhibited failure: they changed the preference order and *decreased* the average reward compared to the base policy (Table 4).
 - AuxDPO, in contrast, successfully maintained the correct preference ordering and significantly *increased* the average reward, demonstrating its robustness.
 - The sensitivity of DPO was further shown by demonstrating that with balanced preference counts (n12=10, n23=10, n31=10), DPO (and IPO) *did not* suffer from these failure modes, highlighting the conditional nature of DPO's issues (Table 5). AuxDPO still outperformed DPO in this balanced setting.
2. **LLM Alignment Performance (Tables 1 & 2):**
 - **Consistent Outperformance:** Across various LLM models (Llama3.1-8B, Llama3.2-1B, Qwen3-0.6B) and benchmark datasets (MMLU-PRO, REWARDBENCH V2), AuxDPO consistently achieves higher accuracies and percentage gains relative to the base policy compared to DPO, IPO, and DPOP (Table 1).
 - **Robustness in OOD Settings:** AuxDPO shows particularly significant gains in Out-Of-Domain (OOD) settings (e.g., Llama3.1-8B on REWARDBENCH V2 OOD: DPO +14.31% vs. AuxDPO +32.44%), indicating improved generalization and transferability.
 - **Avoiding Degradation:** While DPO, IPO, and DPOP sometimes result in performance *degradation* (negative percentage change, marked in red in Table 1) relative to the base policy, AuxDPO generally avoids this, consistently improving model alignment.
 - **Subject-wise Accuracies:** Table 2 provides a detailed breakdown for Llama3.1-8B on MMLU-PRO, showing AuxDPO generally achieving the best or second-best accuracies across diverse subjects (e.g., Biology, Business, Chemistry, Economics, Engineering, Health, History, Law, Math) for both ID and OOD evaluations.
3. **Ablation Studies on Trainable Parameters (Figures 4 & 5):**
 - AuxDPO demonstrates stronger performance, especially at lower fractions of trainable parameters (e.g., 25%, LoRA r=4, Last Layer configurations). This suggests that AuxDPO makes more efficient use of limited model capacity, providing better alignment even when only a small portion of the model is fine-tuned.
 - The gains of AuxDPO over DPO are more pronounced in OOD scenarios and when model capacity is restricted.

In summary, the main findings confirm the theoretical diagnosis of DPO's misspecification and demonstrate AuxDPO as a superior, more robust, and theoretically grounded alternative for LLM alignment, capable of consistently achieving better performance and avoiding critical failure modes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for both the theoretical understanding of LLM alignment and the practical development of more capable and reliable AI systems.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

1. **Fundamental Diagnosis of DPO's Limitations:** The paper provides a rigorous statistical and geometric framework to understand *why* DPO can fail for parametric policy classes. By formally characterizing DPO as a misspecified statistical estimation problem involving a weighted KL-projection onto a restricted reward manifold, it moves beyond empirical observations to a deeper, principled diagnosis of its inherent limitations. This theoretical grounding is crucial for guiding future research.
2. **Bridging DPO and RLHF:** The work explicitly connects the local geometry of DPO's implicit reward functions with the equivalence classes of reward functions induced by RLHF optimization. This geometric characterization offers new insights into the relationship between these two prominent alignment paradigms, showing how DPO's simplified approach fundamentally deviates from the true RLHF objective in the parametric setting.
3. **New Failure Modes Identified:** The theoretical demonstration of DPO leading to preference reversal and a decrease in expected reward, even with infinite, clean data, reveals more profound and insidious failure modes than previously recognized. These are not merely optimization or data sampling issues but stem from the core statistical formulation.

#### 5.2. Practical Significance and Potential Impact

1. **More Robust LLM Alignment:** AuxDPO offers a principled and practically implementable solution to DPO's misspecification problem. Its consistent outperformance of DPO and other baselines across various models and datasets, especially in challenging out-of-domain settings and with limited trainable parameters, suggests it can lead to more robust and higher-quality LLM alignment. This directly translates to LLMs that are more reliably aligned with human preferences, exhibit fewer undesired behaviors, and generalize better.
2. **Enhanced Model Performance and Efficiency:** By making more efficient use of model capacity (as shown in ablation studies at lower trainable parameter counts), AuxDPO could make high-quality alignment more accessible, potentially reducing the computational resources or model sizes required to achieve satisfactory alignment results. This is particularly relevant for the development of smaller, more specialized, or domain-specific LLMs.
3. **Improved Generalization:** The significant gains in out-of-domain evaluation highlight AuxDPO's potential to improve the transferability and generalization capabilities of aligned LLMs. Models aligned with AuxDPO may perform better on unseen tasks or in new environments, which is a critical aspect for real-world deployment.
4. **Guiding Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Broader Application:** Investigating if the misspecification issue and AuxDPO's solution extend to other direct preference optimization algorithms.
 - **Theoretical Extensions:** Further exploring the properties of reward function equivalence classes and their implications for preference learning.
 - **Dynamic Null Space:** Developing adaptive ways to define and optimize within the null space of $A_{\rho,\theta_0}$ during training, potentially improving computational efficiency and scalability for even larger models.
 - **Safety and Reliability:** More reliable alignment algorithms like AuxDPO are crucial for developing safer and more trustworthy AI systems, by mitigating the risk of models learning unintended behaviors or reversing preferences.

In conclusion, "WHY DPO IS A MISSPECIFIED ESTIMATOR AND HOW TO FIX IT" delivers a crucial theoretical critique of a widely used LLM alignment technique and provides a robust, principled solution. Its impact extends from foundational understanding to practical improvements in LLM development, contributing to the creation of more capable, reliable, and human-aligned artificial intelligence.
