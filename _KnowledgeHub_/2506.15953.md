# ViTacFormer: Learning Cross-Modal Representation for Visuo-Tactile Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, Berkeley and Sharpa developed ViTacFormer, a framework that integrates high-resolution vision and tactile data using cross-attention and autoregressive tactile prediction for dexterous manipulation. The system achieved approximately 50% higher success rates on short-horizon tasks and completed very long-horizon tasks autonomously on a real robot.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation systems, largely vision-based or with shallow tactile integration, are limited to simple hand configurations and exhibit poor generalization, especially in unstructured or visually occluded environments.
- A lack of effective models exists for learning deep cross-modal representations that comprehensively integrate visual and tactile information for robust dexterous manipulation.
- Achieving fine-grained, adaptive control for multi-fingered robot hands in complex, contact-rich tasks remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **ViTacFormer**, a ==conditional variational auto-encoder== built on the ==ACT architecture==, integrates high-resolution vision and tactile data.
- It employs ==cross-attention== mechanisms for dynamic, semantic fusion of visual and tactile inputs and incorporates an ==autoregressive tactile prediction head== to forecast future contact dynamics.
- A ==two-phase curriculum learning== strategy stabilizes training by initially using ground-truth tactile inputs and progressively transitioning to predicted signals for enhanced robustness.

## Results

- ViTacFormer achieved approximately **50%** higher success rates on real-world short-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks (e.g., Peg Insertion **100%**, Cap Twist **100%**, Vase Wipe **90%**, Book Flip **90%**) compared to prior state-of-the-art baselines.
- The framework successfully completed a very long-horizon, **11-stage** 'Making Hamburgers' task on a real robot autonomously, achieving an **80%** success rate (**70%** without human intervention) and an overall Human Normalized Score (HNS) of **0.88**, significantly outperforming baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of cross-attention for effective multimodal fusion, autoregressive tactile prediction for temporal contact modeling, and the two-phase curriculum for stable and robust learning against prediction inaccuracies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep cross-modal representations, facilitated by cross-attention between vision and touch, are critical for robustly handling fine-grained manipulation tasks where visual features may be ambiguous.
> - Autoregressive prediction of future tactile states provides a temporal understanding of contact dynamics, enabling more anticipatory and effective action generation for contact-rich manipulation.
> - A structured curriculum learning approach is essential for stable training when integrating predicted sensory inputs, allowing the policy to adapt to minor prediction errors during deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation is a fundamental capability for robotic systems designed to interact with the physical world in ways that resemble human actions. Recent advancements in robotic manipulation have largely been driven by vision-based methods and techniques such as behavior cloning (BC), which show promise for high-precision tasks in real-world environments. However, much of the existing work in this domain has been limited to simplified hand configurations and often exhibits constraints in generalization. A significant contributing factor to these limitations is the underutilization of tactile sensing, which is considered essential for achieving fine-grained control, particularly in unstructured environments or situations involving visual occlusion where direct visual feedback is insufficient or unavailable.

Within the existing body of literature, some approaches have explored multimodal fusion using mechanisms like cross-attention and employed curriculum learning strategies to enhance multi-sensory integration. Concurrently, efforts have been made to apply self-supervised learning principles to develop representations specifically for tactile signals, drawing from the success of such methods in other domains. While certain studies have begun to incorporate tactile feedback into dexterous manipulation tasks, the learned tactile representations in these instances are often characterized as shallow. This implies that these representations may not fully capture the complex, dynamic nuances of physical contact required for advanced manipulation. Consequently, there has been a recognized need for an effective model capable of learning deep cross-modal representations that comprehensively integrate visual and tactile information for robust dexterous manipulation.

ViTacFormer aims to address this identified gap by proposing a unified visuo-tactile framework. It positions itself as a system designed to achieve fine-grained and generalizable manipulation through deep cross-modal representation learning. By explicitly focusing on the fusion of high-resolution vision and touch, coupled with predictive modeling of future tactile signals, this work contributes to the development of robotic systems that can perform complex, contact-rich tasks with a level of precision and adaptiveness beyond current state-of-the-art methods that rely primarily on vision or shallow tactile integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable framework for dexterous robotic manipulation by effectively integrating visual and tactile sensory information. The authors aim to enable fine-grained, adaptive control for multi-fingered robot hands, ultimately allowing robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in real-world settings with enhanced precision and autonomy.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving the development of ViTacFormer are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Manipulation Systems:** Current robotic manipulation systems, particularly those relying predominantly on vision or shallow tactile integration, are often restricted to simple hand configurations and exhibit limited generalization capabilities. Tactile sensing is deemed crucial for fine-grained control, especially in scenarios with visual occlusion or unstructured environments. The research is motivated to overcome these shortcomings by leveraging tactile feedback more effectively.
- **Developing Deep Cross-Modal Representation Learning:** A central motivation is the lack of an effective model that learns deep cross-modal representations for visuo-tactile dexterous manipulation. The authors aim to establish a unified framework that can fuse high-resolution visual and tactile inputs to create a rich, actionable latent space.
- **Enhancing Visuo-Tactile Fusion with Cross-Attention:** Traditional multimodal integration methods often employ naive token fusion, which may not capture the intricate semantic relationships between visual and tactile observations. The researchers are motivated to utilize cross-attention mechanisms to dynamically weigh and integrate information from both modalities, thereby extracting more relevant semantic features.
- **Incorporating Autoregressive Tactile Prediction:** The work is motivated by the hypothesis that predicting future tactile states is more informative for action generation than merely perceiving current ones. By forcing the shared latent space to encode actionable touch dynamics and auto-regressively leveraging these predictions, the system aims to anticipate contact changes and generate more effective actions, particularly in dynamic, contact-rich tasks.
- **Ensuring Robustness and Stability with Curriculum Learning:** Learning representations from predicted tactile signals in an autoregressive manner can be challenging, especially during initial training phases when predictions may be inaccurate. To mitigate this, the research is motivated to devise a two-phase curriculum learning strategy. This curriculum aims to stabilize representation learning by initially using ground-truth tactile inputs and progressively transitioning to predicted signals, thereby promoting robust cross-modal reasoning and adaptation to minor prediction errors.
- **Enabling Long-Horizon, High-Precision Manipulation:** A significant motivation is to enable robotic systems to complete complex, long-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks that demand highly precise control with anthropomorphic hands. This includes tasks involving multiple sequential stages and sustained continuous operation, which have historically been difficult for existing systems.
- **Establishing a Real-World Benchmark:** The researchers are motivated to create a comprehensive real-world benchmark for visuo-tactile dexterous manipulation. This benchmark, encompassing both short- and long-horizon tasks, serves as a standardized evaluation tool for their method and future research, providing a clearer measure of performance and generalization in challenging scenarios.

In summary, the research seeks to advance robotic dexterous manipulation by architecting a novel framework that deeply integrates vision and touch, leverages predictive modeling, and employs a structured training regimen to achieve high precision, adaptiveness, and the ability to perform complex, multi-stage tasks in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ViTacFormer framework is designed as a conditional variational auto-encoder, built upon the Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) architecture, and specifically tailored for visuo-tactile dexterous manipulation.

**A. Hardware Setup and Data Collection**
The system comprises two Realman robot arms, each equipped with a SharpaWave dexterous hand. These anthropomorphic hands feature 5 digits and 17 degrees of freedom (DoFs). Visual observations are captured by a top-mounted ZED Mini stereo camera for global scene awareness and two wrist-mounted fisheye cameras for close-up task views. Tactile sensing is provided by high-resolution (320x240) sensors embedded in the fingertips (Sharpa), from which 3-axis force and torque readings (20 channels total) are extracted per fingertip.

High-quality visuo-tactile demonstrations are collected using a custom exoskeleton-based teleoperation system. An operator wears mechanical exoskeleton gloves coupled to the robot hands, capturing finger joint motions. A VR headset provides an immersive first-person interface, integrating stereo top-down views, wrist-mounted local views, and real-time tactile overlays on the fingertips. All data streams—RGB frames, joint states, and compressed tactile maps—are time-synchronized and logged to create multimodal expert trajectories.

**B. Problem Formulation**
The work addresses imitation learning for dexterous bi-manual manipulation. The goal is to learn a policy $\pi_\theta$ that maps multimodal observations $o_t$ to actions $a_t$, where $o_t$ includes robot proprioception ($j_t$), visual observations ($v_t$), and tactile observations ($h_t$). The policy is trained on $N$ expert trajectories to imitate behavior and is evaluated based on task success rates and a Human Normalized Score (HNS).

**C. Cross-Attention-Based Multimodal Integration**
To effectively fuse visual and tactile information, ViTacFormer employs cross-attention mechanisms within its transformer-based architecture. Unlike naive token fusion, cross-attention enables the model to capture semantic dependencies between modalities. Keys and values from visual observations are computed with queries from tactile signals, and vice versa. This dynamic interaction allows the model to attend to relevant parts of both input sequences, and the resulting cross-attention-based features are then concatenated into hidden states for further processing.

**D. Auto-Regressive Modeling with Tactile Signal Forecasting**
ViTacFormer incorporates an autoregressive tactile prediction head. This component is designed to forecast future tactile signals, motivating the latent representations to encode potential future outcomes and actionable touch dynamics. The action-generating procedure operates in two steps:
1. **Tactile Prediction:** Future tactile tokens are predicted based on a style variable `z`, current robot proprioception (joint states), and the integrated visuo-tactile observations. The model achieves an average normalized L1 error of approximately $0.08 \pm 0.02$ for this prediction, indicating its ability to capture essential contact dynamics.
2. **Action Generation:** The predicted future tactile signals are then concatenated with the current input tokens (which also undergo cross-attention-based visuo-tactile integration) to generate the robot's actions. This approach leverages anticipated contact knowledge to improve action sequencing.

**E. Two-Phase Curriculum Strategy**
To address the challenge of using potentially inaccurate predicted tactile signals during early training, a two-phase curriculum learning strategy is implemented:
1. **Phase 1 (First 75% of epochs):** Ground-truth tactile inputs are used to stabilize the representation learning. This ensures the policy initially learns correct action dependencies based on accurate state information.
2. **Phase 2 (Final 25% of epochs):** The system transitions to using predicted tactile signals. This phase is crucial for adapting the policy to the minor prediction errors inherent in real-world inference, enhancing robustness during deployment.

**F. Neural Network Architecture and Learning Procedure**
The ViTacFormer architecture consists of:
- **Transformer-based Encoder (Left side of Fig. 2):** Maps robot proprioception (joints) and an expert action sequence into a style variable `z`.
- **Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder (Right side of Fig. 2):**
 - Extracts representations from visual and tactile observations using the cross-attention-based multimodal integration framework.
 - Auto-regressively predicts future tactile signals.
 - Generates actions by leveraging these predicted future tactile signals.
The style variable `z` is sampled from expert demonstrations during training and set to zero during inference, following the ACT methodology.

The training loss function is a weighted sum of four components:
$L = w_1 \cdot L_{KL} + w_2 \cdot L_{JA} + w_3 \cdot L_{tactile} + w_4 \cdot L_{arm}$
- $L_{KL}$: KL divergence between the action style variables and a Gaussian distribution.
- $L_{JA}$: L1 loss between predicted and ground-truth joint angle actions.
- $L_{tactile}$: L1 loss between predicted and ground-truth future tactile signals.
- $L_{arm}$: Auxiliary supervision for arm end-effectors, defined as $\lambda_1 \cdot L_{position} + \lambda_2 \cdot L_{rotation}$, where $L_{position}$ is L2 loss on end-effector position and $L_{rotation}$ is L1 loss on end-effector rotation. The $L_{arm}$ component is noted as particularly useful for dexterous manipulation skill acquisition.

**G. Implementation Details**
- **Input Modalities:**
 - **Visual:** Four camera views (stereo pair from ZED Mini, two wrist-mounted fisheye views). Frames are encoded by a vision backbone.
 - **Proprioception:** A 58-dimensional vector representing dual arms, dexterous hands, and neck states. A temporal horizon of 6 frames is used, resulting in a (6, 50) input shape.
 - **Tactile:** 3-axis force and torque data from 10 fingertips (20 channels total). 18 frames of raw signals concatenated with frame-wise deltas, yielding a final tensor of shape.
- **Action Output:** High-frequency action sequences of shape (100, 50) per rollout, covering 50 control dimensions (7-DoF left arm, 17-DoF left hand, 7-DoF right arm, 17-DoF right hand, 2-DoF neck). The policy runs at 10Hz, and temporal smoothing is applied for stable execution.
- **Training:** Each task is trained using 50 expert demonstrations for 100 epochs on 2 NVIDIA H20 GPUs. Short-horizon tasks converge within 12 hours, while long-horizon tasks require up to 2 days. The Adam optimizer is used with a learning rate of 1e-4 and a batch size of 128.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ViTacFormer was evaluated through a series of experiments on both short-horizon and long-horizon real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, comparing its performance against several state-of-the-art imitation learning algorithms and conducting an ablation study on its components.

**A. Benchmark Tasks and Metrics**
The evaluation included four short-horizon tasks: Peg Insertion, Cap Twist, Vase Wipe, and Book Flip, each presenting distinct sensory challenges. A very long-horizon task, "Making Hamburgers," consisting of 11 sequential stages, was also used. All tasks were trained with a limited dataset of 50 expert demonstrations per task, highlighting sample efficiency. Performance was assessed using two metrics: success rates (binary task completion) and a Human Normalized Score (HNS), which provides a detailed, weighted stage-wise evaluation (0-3 raw score per stage). Baselines included Diffusion Policy (DP), HATO (DP with tactile), ACT, and ACTw/T (ACT with tactile).

**B. Algorithm Comparison on Short-Horizon Tasks**
Across the four short-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks, ViTacFormer demonstrated a notable improvement in success rates compared to the baselines.
- **Peg Insertion:** ViTacFormer achieved a 100% (10/10) success rate, outperforming ACTw/T (60%), HATO (40%), ACT (40%), and DP (20%).
- **Cap Twist:** ViTacFormer achieved a 100% (10/10) success rate, significantly higher than ACTw/T (60%), ACT (40%), HATO (10%), and DP (0%).
- **Vase Wipe:** ViTacFormer achieved a 90% (9/10) success rate, compared to ACTw/T (40%), HATO (40%), ACT (30%), and DP (30%).
- **Book Flip:** ViTacFormer achieved a 90% (9/10) success rate, surpassing ACTw/T (40%), HATO (30%), ACT (20%), and DP (10%).

Overall, ViTacFormer achieved approximately 50% higher success rates than the prior state-of-the-art baselines on these tasks, demonstrating its capability to robustly solve tasks requiring fine-grained control and multimodal sensing. The results also indicated that the inclusion of tactile observation input generally improved manipulation performance (e.g., ACTw/T outperformed ACT, and HATO outperformed DP).

**C. Algorithm Comparison on Long-Horizon Task (Making Hamburgers)**
For the 11-stage "Making Hamburgers" task, ViTacFormer demonstrated the capability to complete very long-horizon dexterous manipulation on a real robot autonomously.
- **Human Normalized Score (HNS):** ViTacFormer achieved an overall HNS of 0.88, a substantial improvement over ACTw/T (0.72) and ACT (0.61). ViTacFormer scored higher in most individual stages, indicating superior execution quality throughout the complex sequence.
- **Success Rates:**
 - When success was defined as completing every stage with a non-zero score, ViTacFormer achieved 80% success, compared to 40% for ACTw/T and 10% for ACT.
 - Under the stricter criterion of completing the full task without any human intervention, ViTacFormer achieved 70% success, whereas ACTw/T achieved 10%, and ACT achieved 0%.
Specific stages, such as grasping soft, deformable lettuce (Stage 5) and flipping a sign under occlusion (Stage 11) requiring precise timing and force, were highlighted as instances where tactile sensing, and particularly predictive visuo-tactile modeling, enabled reliable execution that vision-alone methods struggled with.

**D. Ablation Study**
An ablation study was conducted to quantify the contribution of each architectural component of ViTacFormer.
- **Effect of Cross-Attention (w/o CrossAtten):** Removing the cross-attention module and replacing it with naive concatenation led to a significant performance drop, particularly in Peg Insertion (90% success to 70-80% HNS) and Cap Twist. This suggests that the dynamic weighting and dense interaction between visual and tactile modalities are crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained control, especially when visual features are ambiguous.
- **Effect of Tactile Forecasting (w/o AutoRegressive):** Excluding the autoregressive tactile prediction head impaired the temporal modeling of contact dynamics. In tasks like Vase Wipe, the ablated model exhibited inconsistent force application or loss of contact. The forecasting objective was found to effectively regularize the latent representation to encode contact trends, leading to smoother manipulation.
- **Effect of Curriculum Learning (w/o Two-Stage):** Training without the two-phase curriculum resulted in unstable convergence and higher variance in success rates. The curriculum was identified as essential for allowing the policy to first learn valid kinematics with accurate tactile signals before adapting to the inherent noise of predicted tactile signals during real-world inference.
The full ViTacFormer consistently achieved the best results across all ablation scenarios, validating the importance of each proposed component.

**E. Failure Analysis of Baselines**
Analysis of failure modes in baselines (e.g., ACTw/T) provided insights into ViTacFormer's advantages:
- **Peg Insertion Failure:** Baselines often failed to locate the insertion hole accurately or manage temporal force differences, highlighting the benefit of ViTacFormer's future tactile prediction for anticipating contact changes.
- **Cap Twist Failure:** Baselines struggled to discern whether the cap was open or closed, underscoring the importance of ViTacFormer's autoregressive architecture for reasoning about actions in complex contact situations.
- **Book Flip Failure:** Baselines exhibited a lack of awareness of the book or page, failing to fuse visual and tactile observations effectively, which ViTacFormer addresses through cross-attention.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ViTacFormer research presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation, particularly through its principled integration of vision and touch. The work addresses critical limitations of existing manipulation systems by offering a unified framework that leverages deep cross-modal representation learning.

The **significance** of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation Capabilities:** ViTacFormer demonstrates a substantial improvement in the success rates of complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks compared to prior state-of-the-art systems. Its ability to achieve approximately 50% higher success rates indicates a notable leap in the reliability and precision of robotic control for multi-fingered hands.
- **Pioneering Long-Horizon Autonomy:** The research introduces, to the authors' knowledge, the first robotic system capable of autonomously completing very long-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks (e.g., 11 sequential stages over 2.5 minutes of continuous operation) on a real robot. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in more complex, multi-stage real-world scenarios.
- **Robust Cross-Modal Representation Learning:** The core technical contributions—cross-attention for dynamic multimodal fusion, autoregressive tactile prediction for encoding future contact dynamics, and a two-phase curriculum for robust training—collectively yield a more stable and effective latent representation. This approach moves beyond shallow tactile integration, allowing for a deeper understanding and utilization of physical contact information.
- **Improved Generalization with Limited Data:** The framework demonstrates robust generalization to spatial perturbations using a relatively small dataset of 50 expert demonstrations per task. This high sample efficiency is important for real-world deployment, where collecting vast amounts of expert data can be time-consuming and labor-intensive.
- **Comprehensive Real-World Benchmark:** The establishment of a comprehensive real-world benchmark for visuo-tactile dexterous manipulation provides a standardized platform for evaluating future advancements in the field, facilitating objective comparisons and driving progress.

The **potential impact** of ViTacFormer extends to several areas:

- **Human-like Robotic Interaction:** By enabling fine-grained, adaptive control, this work contributes to the development of robotic systems that can interact with the physical world in ways that more closely resemble human dexterity. This can unlock new possibilities for automation in sensitive or complex environments.
- **Applications in Unstructured Environments:** The emphasis on tactile sensing and robust multimodal fusion makes ViTacFormer particularly suitable for tasks in unstructured or visually occluded settings where vision-alone methods fail. This includes applications in manufacturing, service robotics, hazardous environments, and even personal assistance.
- **Advancing Multimodal AI in Robotics:** The methodological innovations in cross-modal representation learning, especially the predictive aspect of tactile signals, offer valuable insights for the broader field of multimodal artificial intelligence, particularly where temporal dynamics and interaction are critical.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a strong foundation for future research directions, such as integrating Sim-to-Real transfer learning to reduce reliance on human demonstrations and exploring more generalizable tactile representations like foundation models for touch. These advancements could further enhance robustness against sensor noise and adaptability to novel objects and tasks.

In conclusion, ViTacFormer represents a significant step towards generalizable, high-precision robotic manipulation by demonstrating a principled and effective integration of vision and touch, thereby opening new avenues for autonomous robotic capabilities in complex real-world scenarios.
