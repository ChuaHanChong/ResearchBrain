# Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models (HWM) introduces a top-down hierarchical planning strategy that operates in learned latent spaces, enabling robust long-horizon control directly from high-dimensional observations. This framework successfully performs zero-shot, non-greedy manipulation on a real robot and enhances efficiency and generalization in complex simulated tasks.

## Problem

- Learned world models struggle with robust long-horizon control due to compounding prediction errors over extended rollouts.
- The planning search space expands exponentially with increasing horizon, making long-term planning computationally intractable.
- Existing hierarchical methods often require low-dimensional state spaces, hand-engineered representations, or task-specific training, limiting their applicability to high-dimensional zero-shot settings.

## Method

- **HWM** employs a ==top-down hierarchical planning== strategy that operates entirely in a shared ==learned latent space== during inference.
- A ==high-level planner==, using a long-horizon world model and learned ==latent macro-actions==, generates immediate subgoals in the latent space.
- A ==low-level planner== utilizes a short-horizon world model and primitive actions to achieve the immediate subgoals, with the entire process using ==receding-horizon Model Predictive Control== (MPC).

## Results

- **HWM** achieved a **70%** success rate on real-robot pick-and-place tasks, outperforming single-level VJEPA2-AC (**0%** success) and strong vision-language-action baselines.
- It improved long-horizon control on the Push-T task, achieving a **61%** success rate at d=75 timesteps compared to **17%** for the flat DINO-WM planner, while requiring approximately **3x** less compute.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution maze layouts, achieving an **83%** success rate on hard mazes versus **44%** for flat PLDM, with up to **4x** less planning compute.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing long-horizon problems into a hierarchy of shorter-horizon subproblems significantly mitigates the accumulation of prediction errors and manages the exponential growth of the planning search space.
> - Learning latent macro-actions for the high-level world model effectively compresses complex action sequences, enabling more efficient and effective long-horizon planning.
> - Operating both high- and low-level planning within a shared latent space, derived from high-dimensional observations, facilitates zero-shot control and generalization without task-specific rewards or explicit policy training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the domain of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on planning for decision-making in complex environments using learned world models. Planning with learned world models has gained attention as a method for enabling zero-shot embodied control by simulating potential action outcomes. Recent advancements in latent-space modeling and self-supervised learning have facilitated the training of such models from large, unlabeled, task-agnostic offline datasets, allowing for planning directly from high-dimensional observations like pixels.

Existing single-level (flat) world model planners exhibit limitations in two primary areas:
1. **Non-greedy tasks:** For tasks requiring intermediate actions that temporarily move away from the goal (e.g., pick-and-place), the goal-matching cost surface can be non-monotone. This can cause local optimizers to converge to greedy, suboptimal shortcuts, leading to task failure.
2. **Long-horizon tasks:** Over extended planning horizons, prediction errors in autoregressive model rollouts tend to compound. Concurrently, the search space for actions increases exponentially with the planning horizon, rendering naive search intractable.

Temporal hierarchy has been explored as a strategy to address these challenges by enabling coarse reasoning over long horizons while retaining fine-grained control for shorter periods. However, prior hierarchical approaches generally do not directly translate to zero-shot planning using visual world models:
- **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL):** Methods based on options, skills, or hierarchical policies often necessitate task-specific rewards or task distributions. Even modern goal-conditioned and zero-shot RL techniques frequently exhibit limited generalization beyond their training distributions.
- **Hierarchical World Models in RL:** While some research has integrated hierarchical world models within RL settings, these remain tied to policy learning over specific task distributions.
- **Classical Hierarchical Model Predictive Control (MPC):** In optimal control, hierarchical MPC is task-agnostic but has typically been applied to low-dimensional states, relied on hand-engineered features, or assumed known dynamics, thereby limiting its applicability to high-dimensional visual inputs.

This research addresses the gap in hierarchical zero-shot planning directly on visual world models, aiming to leverage the benefits of hierarchy to overcome the limitations of flat planning while maintaining task-agnostic, zero-shot capabilities. It proposes an approach that distinguishes itself from contemporaneous work by using latent subgoal matching between different temporal scale world models for both high-level and low-level control, rather than relying on learned low-level policies or explicit action maps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a framework, Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models (HWM), that enables effective zero-shot hierarchical Model Predictive Control (MPC) directly on visual world models. This framework aims to address fundamental limitations observed in single-level world model planners, particularly their failures on non-greedy and long-horizon tasks.

The key motivations and specific objectives are:

- **Solving Non-Greedy Tasks:** Single-level world model planners, such as VJEPA2-AC, have demonstrated difficulty with tasks like pick-and-place, where optimal trajectories require temporary deviation from the immediate goal. The cost function for goal-matching in these scenarios is often non-monotone, trapping local optimizers in suboptimal, greedy actions. HWM seeks to overcome this by introducing hierarchy, allowing the high-level planner to set intermediate subgoals that guide the low-level planner through non-greedy phases without requiring explicit task-specific rewards or policies.
- **Mitigating Compounding Prediction Errors:** On long-horizon tasks, autoregressive rollouts of world models accumulate prediction errors, leading to increasingly inaccurate forecasts. HWM aims to reduce the number of autoregressive steps needed for long-horizon planning by introducing a coarser-grained, high-level world model that predicts dynamics over longer temporal segments, thereby decreasing error accumulation.
- **Managing Exponential Search Space:** The computational complexity of action search grows exponentially with the planning horizon in flat planning. HWM proposes to address this by learning an action encoder that compresses sequences of primitive actions into lower-dimensional latent macro-actions. This compression reduces the dimensionality of the search space for the high-level planner, making long-horizon optimization more tractable and efficient.
- **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalization:** A core motivation is to maintain the zero-shot generalization capabilities of world model planners. Unlike hierarchical RL methods that often require task-specific rewards or extensive training on specific task distributions, HWM is designed to operate solely on world models trained via next-latent prediction from unlabeled, task-agnostic offline trajectories. This allows the system to perform goal-conditioned control from novel start and goal observations without prior exposure to the specific task at inference time.
- **Achieving Computational Efficiency:** The design of HWM is also driven by the goal of improving planning efficiency. By decomposing long-horizon problems into shorter, more manageable subproblems and using compressed macro-actions, the framework aims to achieve comparable or superior performance with reduced computational cost per planning step compared to flat planners.
- **Unified Latent Space for Subgoal Transfer:** A specific objective is to develop an architecture where world models at different temporal scales operate within a shared latent space. This shared space facilitates direct subgoal transfer: predictions from the long-horizon model can serve as subgoals for the short-horizon model via latent matching, eliminating the need for hierarchical policies, explicit skill learning, or complex translation mechanisms between levels.

In essence, HWM aims to introduce temporal hierarchy into world model planning in a way that is compatible with zero-shot, vision-based control, thereby expanding the applicability of world models to more challenging, real-world robotic manipulation and navigation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HWM employs a framework for zero-shot hierarchical Model Predictive Control (MPC) that operates on learned latent world models. The core idea is to train and utilize world models at multiple temporal resolutions within a shared latent space, coupling them during planning to enable efficient long-horizon control.

**Problem Setting:**
The system operates in a goal-conditioned control setting within a Markov Decision Process (MDP). It leverages an offline dataset of state-action trajectories. At inference time, the agent receives a current observation and a goal observation (both raw pixels) and must reach the goal using MPC. Standard latent-space MPC encodes observations into latent states (z) and uses a predictor (F) to forecast future latents under candidate actions. The planner optimizes action sequences by minimizing a goal-reaching objective, typically the L1 norm between the predicted final latent state and the goal latent state.

**Top-Down Hierarchical Planning:**
HWM implements a top-down hierarchical planning strategy executed entirely within the latent space, as illustrated in Figure 2. This strategy involves two main components: a high-level planner and a low-level planner.

1. **High-Level Planning:**
 - **Input:** Current observation ($s_1$) and goal observation ($s_g$), encoded into latent states $z_1 = E(s_1)$ and $z_g = E(s_g)$ respectively.
 - **Objective:** The high-level planner optimizes a sequence of $H$ macro-actions, $\hat{l}_{1:H}$, to minimize a goal-conditioned energy function in latent space: $E_2(\hat{l}_{1:H}; z_1, z_g) \triangleq ||z_g - F^{(2)}(\hat{l}_{1:H}; z_1)||_1$. Here, $F^{(2)}$ is the high-level world model, which autoregressively unrolls predictions using macro-actions.
 - **Output:** The optimal macro-action sequence $l^*_{1:H}$ and a sequence of intermediate latent subgoals $\tilde{z}_i \triangleq F^{(2)}(l^*_{1:i}; z_1)$, for $i=1, \dots, H$. The first predicted latent state, $\tilde{z}_1$, serves as the immediate subgoal for the low-level planner.

2. **Low-Level Planning:**
 - **Input:** Current latent state $z_1$ and the first latent subgoal $\tilde{z}_1$ from the high-level plan.
 - **Objective:** The low-level planner optimizes a sequence of $h$ primitive actions, $\hat{a}_{1:h}$, to reach the first subgoal by minimizing $E_1(\hat{a}_{1:h}; z_1, \tilde{z}_1) \triangleq ||\tilde{z}_1 - F^{(1)}(\hat{a}_{1:h}; z_1)||_1$. Here, $F^{(1)}$ is the low-level world model, which predicts dynamics based on primitive actions.
 - **Execution and Replanning:** The optimal low-level action sequence $a^*_{1:h}$ is determined, and the first $k \le h$ actions are executed. The agent then observes the new state and replans using the hierarchical planner in a receding-horizon MPC fashion.

**Hierarchical World Model Architecture:**
The framework requires two world models operating at different temporal scales in a shared latent space:

1. **Low-Level World Model ($F^{(1)}_\theta$):**
 - **Function:** Predicts the next latent state $z_{t+1}$ given the current latent state $z_t$ and a primitive action $a_t$, i.e., $z_{t+1} = f(z_t, a_t)$.
 - **Training:** It is a standard latent world model trained with a next-latent prediction loss (teacher-forcing $\ell_1$ loss) and, in some cases, a multi-step autoregressive rollout loss to mitigate compounding errors. It encodes raw observations using an encoder ($E$) into latent states.

2. **High-Level World Model ($F^{(2)}_\phi$):**
 - **Function:** Predicts dynamics across temporally extended segments, i.e., $z_{t+h}$ given $z_t$ and a latent macro-action $l_t$. This model uses a learned action encoder to summarize primitive action chunks.
 - **Action Encoder ($A_\psi$):** This component is introduced to compress sequences of primitive actions (e.g., $a_{t_k: t_{k+1}}$ between two waypoint states $z_{t_k}$ and $z_{t_{k+1}}$) into compact latent macro-actions $l_{t_k}$. This reduces the dimensionality of the high-level MPC search space. The action encoder is typically a transformer-based network, where a CLS token is projected to the macro-action space.
 - **Training:** Given a trajectory, $N$ waypoint indices ($1=t_1 < \dots < t_N$) are sampled. Waypoints are encoded as $z_{t_k} = E(s_{t_k})$ and action chunks as $l_{t_k} = A_\psi(a_{t_k:t_{k+1}})$. The high-level model $F^{(2)}_\phi$ is trained to predict the next waypoint latent $\hat{z}_{t_{k+1}}$ from the interleaved sequence $(l_{t_i}, z_{t_i})_{i \le k}$, using a teacher-forcing $\ell_1$ loss: $L_{tf}(\phi, \psi):= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N ||\hat{z}_{t_{k+1}} - z_{t_{k+1}}||_1$.

Both models operate in the same latent space defined by the encoder $E$, which facilitates the direct transfer of latent subgoals between the high-level and low-level planners. The framework leverages existing latent world model architectures (VJEPA2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM) as backbones for the low-level models and adapts them for high-level prediction with macro-actions. For planning, the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) or Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) are used for trajectory optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of HWM across three latent world-model architectures (VJEPA2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM) and various tasks demonstrated consistent improvements in performance, especially for long-horizon and non-greedy tasks, along with enhanced computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Non-Greedy Control (Franka Arm with Robotiq Gripper)

- **Task:** Real-world multi-stage Franka manipulation (pick-and-place, drawer opening/closing), requiring non-greedy behavior.
- **Architecture:** HWM instantiated with VJEPA2-AC as the low-level world model.
- **Results:**
 - The single-level VJEPA2-AC planner achieved 0% success on pick-and-place tasks (cup and box) and 30% on drawer tasks. Its failures were attributed to the non-greedy nature of these tasks.
 - HWM achieved 70% success on pick-and-place cup and 60% on pick-and-place box, and 70% on drawer tasks. This demonstrates HWM's capability to solve non-greedy tasks from a single goal image without manual decomposition.
 - **Analysis (Manual Subgoals):** When the single-level VJEPA2-AC planner was provided with manually defined intermediate subgoals (e.g., grasp point for pick-and-place), its success rate increased to 80% for both pick-and-place cup/box. This indicates that the VJEPA2-AC world model possessed the necessary representational fidelity, and the failure of single-level planning was indeed hierarchical. HWM recovered a substantial portion of this "oracle" performance automatically.
 - **Comparison with VLAs:** HWM was competitive with or outperformed several vision-language-action (VLA) baselines (Octo, π0-FAST-DROID, π0.5-DROID), despite using significantly less training data (approximately 77x less) and image-goal supervision (compared to language goals for some π-models).

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Reasoning and Compute Efficiency (Push-T)

- **Task:** Goal-conditioned Push-T task, extended from 25 to 75 timesteps to stress-test long-horizon planning.
- **Architecture:** HWM instantiated with DINO-WM as the low-level world model.
- **Results:**
 - HWM consistently outperformed the single-level DINO-WM planner and policy-based baselines (GCIQL, HIQL, HILP) as the task horizon increased.
 - At $d=25$ timesteps, HWM achieved 89% success vs. DINO-WM's 84%.
 - At $d=50$ timesteps, HWM achieved 78% success vs. DINO-WM's 55%.
 - At $d=75$ timesteps, HWM achieved 61% success vs. DINO-WM's 17%.
 - **Compute Efficiency:** Hierarchical planning achieved higher success rates while requiring up to 3x less compute per planning step compared to the single-level planner under fixed planning budgets. This was demonstrated through Pareto frontiers of success rate vs. planning time.
 - **Model Size vs. Hierarchy:** Increasing the parameter count of single-level DINO-WM models (from 44M to 98M) did not improve or even degraded performance, suggesting that HWM's gains stem from its hierarchical structure rather than merely increased model capacity.

#### 4.3. OOD Generalization and Compute Efficiency (Diverse Maze)

- **Task:** Diverse Maze navigation, focusing on zero-shot generalization to larger, unseen maze layouts requiring long-horizon planning.
- **Architecture:** HWM instantiated with PLDM as the low-level world model.
- **Results:**
 - HWM consistently improved performance over the single-level PLDM planner as the task horizon (shortest-path distance D) increased.
 - For hard settings ($D \in$), HWM nearly doubled the success rate from 44% (PLDM) to 83%, indicating the benefit of coarse subgoal planning for long-horizon navigation.
 - **Compute Efficiency:** HWM achieved higher success rates with up to 4x less compute than PLDM, particularly for challenging tasks.
 - Both PLDM variants (with and without hierarchy) outperformed goal-conditioned and zero-shot RL policy baselines (GCIQL, HIQL, HILP) when generalizing to out-of-distribution maze layouts, highlighting the robustness of model-based planning to geometric distribution shifts.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Hierarchical Planning Mechanisms

- **High-Level World Models Improve Long-Horizon Prediction:** For horizons $\ge 1.5$ seconds, a single-step prediction from the high-level world model showed lower L1 prediction error than autoregressive rollouts of the low-level model. This supports the hypothesis that multi-timescale models reduce error accumulation for long horizons.
- **Learned Latent Macro-Actions vs. Alternatives:** Learned latent macro-actions consistently outperformed simpler alternatives like concatenating primitive actions or hand-crafted summaries (e.g., net end-effector displacement). Learned macro-actions achieved substantially higher planning performance (e.g., 95% vs. 52% success in medium Diverse Maze) by compressing action sequences and capturing relevant temporal structure.
- **Emergence of Semantic Subgoals:** The dimensionality of the macro-action space influenced the quality of subgoals. A macro-action dimension of 4 was found to strike a balance for Franka tasks: expressive enough to encode useful trajectories but not so expressive as to enable subgoals requiring overly complex, non-greedy execution that the low-level planner could not reliably execute. This suggested that moderate latent dimensionality biases the planner towards reachable, greedy subgoals. Reconstruction fidelity from latent predictions was not strongly correlated with planning success, as coarse semantic structure was sufficient.

In summary, HWM demonstrated the effectiveness of hierarchical planning with latent world models for real-world robotic tasks and simulated environments, providing significant improvements in performance, particularly for non-greedy and long-horizon tasks, while also enhancing computational efficiency. The findings support that hierarchy is a practical mechanism for extending the capabilities of latent world model MPC.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models (HWM), a framework that integrates hierarchical control with learned visual world models for zero-shot decision-making. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood in several dimensions:

- **Enabling Zero-Shot, Non-Greedy Control:** A key contribution is demonstrating the ability to solve non-greedy, multi-stage manipulation tasks on a real robot (Franka arm) directly from visual inputs and a single goal image. This capability, where the corresponding single-level planner fails completely, is a notable advancement. It suggests that HWM can overcome limitations of local optimization traps in non-monotone cost landscapes, which are prevalent in real-world complex tasks. This could significantly broaden the range of tasks addressable by model-based approaches without extensive task-specific engineering.

- **Improved Long-Horizon Planning:** HWM consistently enhances performance on long-horizon tasks across diverse simulated environments (Push-T, Diverse Maze) and different world-model backbones (VJEPA2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM). This is achieved by mitigating compounding prediction errors through multi-temporal scale models and by making the high-level search space more tractable via learned latent macro-actions. The ability to plan effectively over longer horizons is crucial for many real-world applications, from extended robotic manipulation sequences to autonomous navigation in large environments.

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** The framework demonstrates that hierarchical planning can achieve comparable or superior performance with substantially reduced computational cost (up to 3x to 4x less planning compute). This addresses a critical limitation of model predictive control, where the exponential growth of the search space makes long-horizon planning computationally expensive. Increased efficiency could enable faster decision cycles, real-time deployment in resource-constrained environments, and the exploration of more complex planning problems.

- **Robustness to Task Difficulty and Distribution Shift:** The consistent gains across varying task difficulties and generalization to unseen environments (e.g., larger, unseen mazes) underscore the robustness of the hierarchical approach. By building upon task-agnostic, reward-free offline data, HWM retains the zero-shot generalization advantages of latent world model planners, outperforming goal-conditioned and zero-shot RL policy baselines in out-of-distribution settings. This suggests a path toward more general-purpose embodied agents.

- **Advancement in World Model Utility:** The work demonstrates that hierarchy is a practical mechanism for extending the capabilities of latent world-model MPC beyond short, greedy behaviors. It provides a blueprint for leveraging the predictive power of world models more effectively for complex, real-world problems. The finding that high-level world models improve long-horizon prediction by reducing error accumulation reinforces the value of multi-scale modeling.

- **Foundational for Future Research:** The HWM framework offers several avenues for future research. Exploring more sophisticated waypoint sampling or subgoal discovery mechanisms, dynamically chunking actions, or incorporating feedback from the low-level planner into high-level subgoal optimization could further refine the approach. Investigating the benefits of level-specific latent abstractions beyond a single shared latent space for highly open-ended environments is another potential direction.

In conclusion, HWM contributes to the development of more capable and efficient embodied AI systems by addressing fundamental challenges in long-horizon and non-greedy planning through a principled hierarchical framework. Its success on real-world robotics and its computational benefits suggest a significant step towards practical, general-purpose intelligent agents capable of complex, vision-based control.
