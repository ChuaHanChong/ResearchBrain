# WALL-SS: Scaling Long-horizon World Models via Next-Scale Autoregression

> [!summary] Summary
> WALL-SS is a robotic world model that generates visual futures by coarse-to-fine next-scale autoregression over interleaved observations and actions. Scale-aligned action conditioning, scale-compressed bounded time-scale memory with dream forcing, and reward-based on-policy alignment give action-faithful, minute-long streaming rollouts, plus closed-loop policy evaluation whose success rates and checkpoint rankings track the physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing action-conditioned video world models are organized around clip-level prediction, handling memory, variable horizons and reward optimization through separate task-specific interfaces.
- Recursively committing generated observations as context accumulates error, corrupting object identity, persistent state and action history, so long-horizon coherence and action fidelity degrade.
- Success-filtered demonstration corpora teach shortcuts where a grasp command implies attachment, so models render magnet-like grasps instead of counterfactual, action-dependent failure outcomes.

## Method

- **WALL-SS** performs ==action-conditioned next-scale prediction==: deterministic action-to-camera grounding plus scale-aligned causal masks inject matched action prefixes at every coarse-to-fine scale, making transitions action-dependent.
- ==Scale-compressed time-scale KV memory== keeps a fixed identity anchor, fine recent and coarse distant records under a bounded budget, with ==scale-wise dream forcing== training on corrupted, self-generated history.
- ==On-policy alignment== treats next-scale token generation as a stochastic policy, optimizing fresh rollouts by ==group-relative policy optimization== under action-following and long-horizon consistency rewards, with reference-KL and autoregressive replay.

## Results

- On an embodied video benchmark, it reaches **0.539** trajectory accuracy and **0.290** action following versus **0.202** and **0.044** for the strongest baseline, with best interaction quality.
- Reward alignment lifts action following **0.264** to **0.290**, trajectory accuracy **0.512** to **0.539**, cuts cross-clip boundary error **0.118** to **0.104**, and raises real-robot task progress **64.6** to **69.1**.
- Across **600** matched closed-loop episodes, success-rate calibration MAE is **0.062**, checkpoint ranking preserves **89%** of pairwise orderings, and outcome balanced accuracy is **0.88**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning the memory hierarchy with the generative hierarchy lets one mechanism serve both prediction and recall, giving bounded-cost streaming without a separate memory encoder.
> - Rollback-and-replay interventions supply matched contexts with divergent controls, the only supervision that forces a generator to explain futures by physics rather than task priors.
> - Optimizing dynamics-only rewards that exclude task success still improves downstream control, but simulated evaluators stay optimistic exactly at contact-rich transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative world models are a component in robotics, offering predictive capabilities for how environments evolve under interaction. These models are applied in simulation, planning, policy evaluation, and robot learning. Initial advancements in video world models have focused on generating short clips, demonstrating progress in visual realism, temporal coherence, and action controllability.

However, the broader research landscape indicates a demand for world models that extend beyond clip-level prediction. Current systems often lack a unified generative formulation that explicitly links actions to their consequences, supports flexible prediction horizons, maintains a consistent world state during continuous interaction, and provides a probabilistic interface for reward-driven optimization. Many existing approaches are organized around discrete clip generation, requiring separate interfaces for broader capabilities.

WALL-SS distinguishes itself by formulating robotic world evolution as a causal sequence of temporally interleaved observations and actions. This approach contrasts with conventional video diffusion models, which primarily predict future clips from visual history, and action-conditioned diffusion models, which typically condition clip generation on robot actions without a streaming causal structure. The work aims to address a central challenge in autoregressive world modeling: the accumulation of errors over long horizons, which can lead to inconsistencies in persistent world states, object identities, interaction outcomes, and action fidelity. By proposing a next-scale autoregressive framework, WALL-SS positions itself as an effort to integrate perception, action, memory, and optimization within a single evolving generative process to support long-horizon and action-controllable robotic simulation. It builds upon prior work in autoregressive video generation, particularly next-scale models like InfinityStar, and incorporates mechanisms for long-horizon memory management and exposure bias reduction, such as dream forcing techniques. The research also connects to efforts in aligning generative models with reward signals, as seen in diffusion policy optimization methods, by applying similar principles to visual dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general generative model of embodied trajectories that provides action-controllable and long-horizon robotic simulation capabilities. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific limitations identified in existing world models and the requirements for advanced robotic applications:

1. **Explicit Action-Consequence Relation:** Current models often do not explicitly organize actions and their consequences within a shared causal history. A key motivation is to make action-dependent state transitions explicit, allowing the model to represent how specific robot controls transform the world. This is crucial for planning and understanding interaction outcomes.
2. **Flexible Prediction Horizons and Granularity:** Many systems are organized around fixed-length clip prediction. The objective is to support predictions over flexible horizons and at varying levels of granularity, without imposing a fixed output structure. This enables generation of futures of different durations and detail.
3. **Continuous Interaction and Streaming State Maintenance:** Robots operate continuously, necessitating a model that can incrementally maintain world state during ongoing interaction. The goal is to support streaming extension through reusable causal states, allowing the model to propagate context efficiently over extended periods under bounded memory.
4. **Probabilistic Interface for Reward-Driven Optimization:** For applications like reinforcement learning and policy optimization, a probabilistic interface is beneficial for evaluating and improving actions. The model is motivated to expose explicit sequence probabilities and token-level log-probabilities for direct optimization.
5. **Addressing Autoregressive Error Accumulation:** A fundamental challenge in autoregressive generation is that errors can accumulate over time, leading to visual drift and inconsistencies in the world state. A core motivation is to mitigate these errors to maintain long-horizon coherence and action fidelity.
6. **Simulating Diverse Behaviors, Including Failures:** World models should be capable of simulating a broad range of prescribed behaviors, including suboptimal actions and failed outcomes. This enables counterfactual reasoning and allows the model to learn a more comprehensive representation of world dynamics, rather than merely predicting likely successful continuations.
7. **Improving Physical Consistency and Action Fidelity:** The model aims to strengthen the coupling between robot controls and predicted state transitions, reduce long-horizon drift, and enhance the physical consistency of generated futures, ensuring that simulated consequences align with physical reality.
8. **Downstream Utility for Robot Policy Evaluation:** Ultimately, the developed world model should serve as a reliable tool for evaluating external robot policies. The motivation extends to demonstrating that the learned dynamics preserve action-dependent information relevant for calibrated task outcomes and consistent policy-checkpoint rankings in closed-loop deployment.

By addressing these motivations, WALL-SS seeks to provide a unified, flexible, streaming, and reward-optimizable foundation for embodied modeling, thereby advancing the utility of generative world models in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WALL-SS is a next-scale autoregressive world model designed for action-controllable and long-horizon robotic simulation. Its methodology integrates three primary components: action-conditioned next-scale prediction, scale-compressed long-horizon memory, and on-policy alignment of autoregressive visual dynamics.

#### 3.1. Next-Scale Autoregression for Action-Grounded Dynamics

The core of WALL-SS is its next-scale autoregressive visual generator, initialized from InfinityStar. It represents embodied trajectories as causal sequences of temporally interleaved observations and actions. Each future observation is generated in a coarse-to-fine manner, factorizing the prediction into multiple scales of residual visual codes.

- **Action-Conditioned Next-Scale Prediction:** To ensure strong coupling between actions and future states, scale-aligned action representations are introduced throughout the coarse-to-fine generation process.
 - **Action-Video Temporal Grounding:** A deterministic renderer projects planned robot trajectories into the visual timeline for each camera view. A shared causal encoder processes this stream continuously, slicing it by the same clip intervals used for RGB observations. Action features aligned with these intervals are projected at a set of "retained action scales" (S_A) as a synchronized condition. For each visual scale, the finest retained action scale not exceeding it is selected.
 - **Scale-Aligned Causal Conditioning:** Action tokens are materialized as condition prefixes within the Transformer sequence. Causal masks ensure that a query for a visual token only reads actions associated with the current visual interval and its corresponding scale, preventing access to future controls or unrelated views.
- **Autoregressive Learning Objective:** The model is trained by minimizing a balanced negative log-likelihood (L_AR) over clips, scales, and residual repeats. This objective evaluates the probability of generating a visual token given its preceding world history, intervening actions, and task instruction.

#### 3.2. Time–Scale Memory for Long-Horizon Rollout

To support long-horizon rollouts under a bounded memory budget, WALL-SS reuses the next-scale hierarchy for managing historical context.

- **Time–Scale Memory Records:** During generation, synchronized causal states are materialized at retained pre-prediction boundaries. Recent history is kept at finer detail, while older history is progressively compressed into coarser summaries, aligning with the coarse-to-fine generation process. Visual memories are paired with their corresponding action banks, ensuring actions remain linked to the states they produced.
- **Identity-Anchored Distance-Aware Retention:** A persistent anchor, derived from the initial observation (O_0), stabilizes identity and global layout. Rolling records carry intermediate state, and a monotone schedule dictates which precomputed boundary is retained (fine for recent, coarse for distant).
- **Bounded Memory Bank:** The memory bank maintains a fixed size (B_KV) by evicting records after their final scheduled read. This design ensures that memory cost does not grow with rollout length.
- **Scale-wise Dream Forcing:** To improve robustness against self-generated context and reduce exposure bias, the model is trained on a mixture of clean teacher-forced histories, age-dependent corrupted histories (f_M_c), and suffixes sampled from a lagged streaming model (b_Z_c:c+d-1). The objective (L_long) combines L_AR with terms for corrupted context (λ_cor L_cor) and self-generated context (λ_self L_self). This training exposes the model to imperfect histories without changing the underlying autoregressive objective.

#### 3.3. On-Policy Alignment of Visual Dynamics

This component refines the visual generator to improve fidelity to action-conditioned dynamics, without directly training a robot controller.

- **Visual Generator as a Policy:** Next-scale visual-token generation is treated as a stochastic policy (π_V_θ). For a given initial observation, instruction, and fixed prescribed action sequence, a frozen behavior copy of the model samples multiple long-horizon visual rollouts.
- **Dynamics Fidelity Rewards:** Two frozen scorers provide rewards: R_act measures agreement between each transition and its prescribed action, and R_long measures state, boundary, and cross-view consistency over a rolling window. These rewards specifically target visual dynamics fidelity, excluding task progress or terminal success.
- **Group-Relative Policy Optimization:** Returns are aggregated and normalized across multiple rollouts (from the same initial conditions) to provide a group-relative baseline for policy gradient updates. The objective (L_PG) uses a clipped surrogate.
- **Prior-Preserving Regularization:** To prevent drift from the pretrained visual distribution, the optimization objective (L_align) includes a KL divergence term against a frozen reference generator (π_V_ref) and an autoregressive replay term (L_real_AR) from real trajectories.

#### 3.4. World Action Policy (Co-trained Action Expert)

A co-trained action expert (F_Aω) is developed to complement the visual model. It predicts executable control chunks (A_c) from committed world states (Read_V(M_c)), current proprioceptive state (s_c), and task instruction (T_g). This expert uses the same grounding interface as external actions and is trained with its own loss (L_act) alongside the visual loss (L_cotrain = L_long + λ_A L_act). This allows for recursive imagination where action prediction, visual generation, and memory commit alternate.

#### 3.5. Training Data and Recipe

- **Data Composition:** A heterogeneous corpus is used, comprising public robot data (AgiBotWorld-Beta, ManipArena), private X2-Robot and non-embodiment UMI data, and policy-intervention/failure-recovery trajectories.
- **Modality-Aware Curation:** Data is routed based on reliability: samples with synchronized actions and valid calibration contribute to the action-conditioned pool (D_ac), while others contribute to the video-only pool (D_vid).
- **Training Stages:** Training proceeds from an InfinityStar initialization through three main stages: (1) action-grounded autoregressive learning on clean ground-truth contexts, (2) rollout-robust long-horizon adaptation using time-scale memory and dream forcing, and (3) on-policy alignment of visual dynamics.
- **Inference:** During inference, WALL-SS performs action-conditioned streaming: given an initial observation and controls, it sequentially generates future clips, commits them, and advances the bounded memory. For closed-loop rollout, a frozen external robot policy interacts with WALL-SS, consuming generated observations and providing actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate WALL-SS's capabilities across embodied video generation, action-conditioned prediction, long-horizon streaming, and real-robot policy evaluation.

#### 4.1. Embodied Video Generation Evaluation

- **Quantitative Performance:** WALL-SS shows improved overall performance on embodied-relevant dimensions compared to baselines (InfinityStar, Wan2.1/2.2, CogVideoX, Cosmos3-Nano). It achieves higher scores in motion quality, temporal and semantic consistency, and physical and conditional fidelity metrics.
- **Key Metrics:** Notably, WALL-SS leads in Interaction Quality, Instruction Following, Trajectory Accuracy, and Depth Accuracy, indicating a stronger physical prior over robot motion, object interaction, and contact evolution.
- **Qualitative Coherence:** Qualitatively, WALL-SS maintains more coherent and goal-consistent behavior over minute-long rollouts compared to generic video models, which exhibit motion and interaction drift.

#### 4.2. Action-Conditioned Video Generation Evaluation

- **Action Fidelity:** WALL-SS performs favorably on both Action Following (0.290) and Trajectory Accuracy (0.539), indicating that distinct action conditions induce distinguishable future outcomes from the same visual context, and these differences align with the intended robot motion.
- **Counterfactual Reasoning:** The model demonstrates the ability to generate action-faithful video sequences under different action conditions from the same initial visual observation and language instruction, enabling counterfactual analysis. This capability is attributed to scale-aligned action conditioning and the intervention-rich training data.

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Streaming Rollout Evaluation

- **Memory Efficiency and Robustness:** Under a bounded memory budget, WALL-SS supports coherent minute-long streaming rollouts. Compared to memory variants (e.g., "recent-clip only"), it accumulates substantially less trajectory error (Fig. 9a), exhibits lower cross-clip boundary error (Fig. 9b), and maintains higher persistent-state consistency (Fig. 9c).
- **Perceptual Quality over Time:** Over 60-second autoregressive rollouts, WALL-SS maintains higher PSNR and lower FID and FVD values in successive windows compared to other models, indicating sustained visual fidelity.
- **Cross-View Consistency:** The model demonstrates low 3D-probe error and cross-view correspondence error, suggesting that its generated multi-view outputs remain spatially compatible and 3D-aware.

#### 4.4. Reinforcement-Learning Evaluation (On-Policy Alignment)

- **Improved Dynamics Fidelity:** On-policy alignment of the visual generator leads to consistent gains in action following (from 0.264 to 0.290) and trajectory accuracy (from 0.512 to 0.539).
- **Reduced Drift:** Cross-clip boundary error drops from 0.118 to 0.104, and persistent-state consistency improves, indicating that reward optimization mitigates drift and discontinuities.
- **Prior Preservation:** Appearance metrics remain stable, suggesting that alignment reallocates probability mass among dynamics-consistent visual futures without trading off visual quality, attributed to KL regularization and real-trajectory replay.
- **Action Expert Benefits:** The improvements in visual dynamics transfer to the co-trained action expert, increasing average real-robot Task Progress from 64.6 to 69.1, particularly in contact-rich and multi-stage tasks.

#### 4.5. Closed-Loop Policy Consistency

- **Task-Level Calibration:** Across 30 task–checkpoint cells, WALL-SS shows an MAE of 0.062 between generated and real success rates, with a fitted calibration line slope of 0.84, indicating mild overestimation for weaker checkpoints.
- **Within-Task Ranking:** The model preserves 89% of untied real-robot pairwise policy orderings with a mean per-task Spearman ρ of 0.88, demonstrating its utility for policy checkpoint ranking.
- **Episode-Level Agreement:** Balanced accuracy for episode outcomes is 0.88, with success recall 0.90 and failure recall 0.86. The false-positive rate (generated success | real failure) is 0.136.
- **Subtask-Level Bias:** Residual bias (optimism) is concentrated at contact/insertion stages (+0.08), with conditional transition bias peaking at +0.12 for insertion.

#### 4.6. Real-Robot Evaluation

- **Physical Control Performance:** The co-trained action expert of WALL-SS achieves an average Task Progress of 69.1 on diverse tabletop bimanual tasks, outperforming baseline methods (π0.5, DreamZero, LingBot-VA).
- **Task Versatility:** Strong performance is observed in direct manipulation tasks and tasks requiring tracking of previously completed subgoals (e.g., sorting, classification), which benefits from the bounded time–scale memory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of WALL-SS represents an advancement in generative world models for robotics, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Unified Generative Framework for Embodied AI:** WALL-SS introduces a unified modeling formulation that connects perception, action, memory, and optimization within a single evolving generative process. This integration moves beyond clip-level video prediction towards a more comprehensive representation of embodied trajectories, which is relevant for the development of generalist embodied systems.
2. **Scalable Long-Horizon Simulation:** By introducing next-scale autoregression and scale-compressed long-horizon memory, the model enables coherent, minute-long streaming rollouts under a bounded memory budget. This capability is critical for simulating complex, multi-stage robotic tasks that require prolonged interaction and consistent world state tracking, which has been a limitation of many prior models.
3. **Enhanced Action Fidelity and Counterfactual Reasoning:** The model's design, including scale-aligned action conditioning and training on intervention and failure-recovery data, improves the fidelity of action-dependent dynamics. This allows for the simulation of diverse outcomes, including suboptimal and failed behaviors, facilitating counterfactual reasoning. This is significant for robust planning and understanding the boundaries of robot capabilities.
4. **Reliable Policy Evaluation and Calibration:** The demonstrated ability of WALL-SS to calibrate policy evaluation in simulation with real-world task outcomes and policy checkpoint rankings positions it as a potentially reliable tool for evaluating and developing robot policies. Reducing the sim-to-real gap in policy evaluation can accelerate robot learning cycles and reduce the need for extensive physical experimentation.
5. **Robustness to Autoregressive Error Accumulation:** Mechanisms like scale-wise dream forcing and on-policy alignment directly address the challenge of error accumulation in autoregressive models. By training with self-generated and corrupted contexts, and by optimizing visual dynamics based on fidelity rewards, WALL-SS enhances the robustness and physical consistency of its long-horizon predictions.
6. **Action-Relevant Predictive Representations:** The strong performance of the co-trained action expert on physical robots, leveraging the same committed causal state as the visual generator, suggests that WALL-SS learns representations that are directly relevant and useful for long-horizon physical control. This indicates a reciprocal benefit between predictive world modeling and effective robot control.
7. **Foundation for Interactive Understanding:** The work contributes to a shift in the objective of world models from merely synthesizing plausible videos towards creating a coherent account of observations, actions, and outcomes that supports interactive understanding and decision-making. This deeper interactive understanding is a foundational step towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
