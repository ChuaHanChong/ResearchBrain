# Geometry-Aware Motion Latents for Learning Robust Manipulation Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoMoLa learns robust robot manipulation policies by deriving motion latents from four-dimensional (4D) geometric transformations, focusing on predicting the evolution of 3D point clouds. This method achieved an 84.7% overall success rate on RLBench, recorded an average task sequence length of 3.60 on the CALVIN benchmark, and improved real-world task success by 13.3% over baselines using limited demonstrations.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation often relies on motion latents extracted from 2D visual sequences, lacking explicit 3D geometric grounding.
- 2D-centric approaches struggle with generalization across varying viewpoints, object poses, and lighting conditions, leading to compounding errors in real-world scenarios.
- Existing 3D methods typically focus on static representations or incur high computational costs, failing to comprehensively model both spatial structure and temporal dynamics for motion latents.

## Method

- Develops **GeoMoLa**, a framework that learns ==discrete motion latent codes== explicitly encoding ==4D spatiotemporal geometry==, which represents the evolution of 3D structures over time.
- Trains these motion latents through a ==self-supervised== objective of predicting future 3D point cloud observations, supported by an auxiliary RGB prediction.
- Utilizes a ==diffusion-based== model to generate precise 6-DoF end-effector trajectories, conditioned on the learned geometry-aware motion latents and current observations.

## Results

- Achieved an **84.7%** overall success rate across 10 challenging RLBench tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art 3D representation-based policies and 4D dynamic frameworks.
- Demonstrated superior long-horizon task execution on the CALVIN benchmark with an average task sequence length of **3.60**, indicating reduced error accumulation.
- Secured a **53.3%** average success rate in real-world experiments with limited demonstrations (20 trajectories per task), representing a **13.3%** improvement over the 3D Diffuser Actor.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning motion latents from 4D spatiotemporal dynamics, specifically 3D state transitions, enables more accurate and robust action prediction than conditioning on static 3D features or 2D visual appearance.
> - Explicitly modeling 3D geometry, through pointmap prediction, is fundamental for learning transferable manipulation primitives, with significantly greater impact on performance than RGB prediction.
> - The learned geometry-aware motion latents demonstrate improved interpretability, consistently generating semantically meaningful motion patterns across diverse scene configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation often relies on learning motion patterns, referred to as motion latents, to abstract complex continuous movements into discrete, reusable skills. Conventional methods typically extract these motion latents from two-dimensional (2D) visual sequences, such as RGB images or videos. However, robotic manipulation inherently involves interaction with a three-dimensional (3D) physical world, where actions are fundamentally determined by geometric factors like spatial relationships, object poses, and volumetric changes over time.

Existing research approaches in robot learning generally address either spatial structure or temporal dynamics, but often not both comprehensively in a unified representation for motion latents. Some methods focus on static 3D representations, processing frozen point clouds or volumetric maps for scene understanding and action planning, without explicitly modeling how these 3D structures evolve dynamically during manipulation. Examples include diffusion policies that generate trajectories from fixed scene features or voxel-based approaches that can suffer from computational scaling issues. Conversely, other approaches are primarily video-based, learning motion latents in image space without explicit access to depth information or the underlying 3D geometry, which can limit their generalization capabilities across varying viewpoints or object arrangements. Large-scale vision-language models for robot control, such as RT-1/2 or GATO, implicitly learn 3D geometry from extensive 2D demonstrations but require substantial data volumes.

This work, GeoMoLa, positions itself as an advancement in this landscape by proposing to learn motion latents that explicitly encode four-dimensional (4D) dynamics—that is, the evolution of 3D geometry over time. By focusing on predicting future 3D point cloud evolution rather than reconstructing 2D visual observations, GeoMoLa aims to address the representational gap where current methods may produce latents that fail to generalize effectively in real-world scenarios involving occlusions, novel object poses, or precise spatial interactions. The authors argue that this geometry-aware approach provides a more fundamental understanding of physical motion, thereby offering a path towards more robust and transferable manipulation policies compared to methods that primarily operate on pixel-level patterns or static 3D representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a perceived limitation in current robotic manipulation learning paradigms: the insufficient integration of three-dimensional geometric understanding into motion latent representations. While motion latents are crucial for abstracting and transferring manipulation skills, existing methods predominantly derive them from 2D visual inputs. The authors identify several challenges arising from this 2D-centric approach:

- **Lack of Geometric Grounding:** Manipulation tasks inherently depend on precise 3D spatial relationships, approach angles, and the continuous evolution of 3D configurations. Learning from 2D images risks creating representations that encode visual appearance patterns rather than the actual physical motion and its geometric effects.
- **Poor Generalization:** Representations learned without explicit 3D geometry may struggle to generalize across different viewpoints, object poses, lighting conditions, or spatial arrangements in novel scenes. For instance, an occluded object might maintain its geometric relationship to other objects despite visual changes, a nuance that 2D representations may miss.
- **Cascading Failures in Real-World Deployment:** Small spatial errors, if not grounded in 3D understanding, can compound across action sequences, leading to failures in tasks requiring high precision or involving complex interactions with cluttered environments. Robots may not accurately infer that objects maintain their geometric relationships despite visual alterations, potentially leading to unsuccessful actions.
- **Computational and Data Efficiency:** Explicitly modeling 3D geometric transformations is a core challenge. Existing methods often sacrifice either spatial detail (2D-based) or temporal dynamics (static 3D-based) or require prohibitive computational costs or vast amounts of demonstrations to implicitly learn 3D reasoning.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the GeoMoLa research are:

- **To develop a framework for learning reusable, discrete motion latent codes that explicitly encode 3D geometric transformations over time.** This involves moving beyond 2D visual patterns to capture the *four-dimensional spatiotemporal geometry* of manipulation actions.
- **To train these geometry-aware motion latents through a self-supervised objective of predicting future 3D observations.** By forecasting how point clouds evolve, the latents are compelled to capture the causal relationship between actions and their physical effects, leading to motion primitives grounded in transformation rather than appearance.
- **To demonstrate that these geometry-aware motion latents enable robust manipulation policies, particularly with single-view RGB-D input.** The goal is to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding state-of-the-art methods, some of which require multi-view reconstruction or extensive data.
- **To quantitatively validate the significance of 3D geometric prediction over visual appearance for manipulation success through ablation studies.** This aims to provide empirical evidence for the hypothesis that spatial understanding is fundamental to robot control.
- **To show the interpretability and transferability of the learned motion codes**, such that they can produce physically consistent transformations when applied to novel scenes, regardless of specific visual context.
- **To confirm the robustness of the approach in real-world cluttered environments with minimal demonstrations**, where precise spatial reasoning is critical for task completion.

In summary, the research aims to establish that effective motion latents for robot control emerge more effectively from understanding motion through its 3D geometric effects than from pixel-level visual patterns, leading to more generalizable, interpretable, and robust robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GeoMoLa framework is designed to learn robust manipulation policies by grounding motion latents in four-dimensional (4D) spatiotemporal geometry. The approach consists of two main components: a self-supervised pipeline for discovering geometry-aware motion latents and a diffusion-based model that leverages these latents for action generation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system operates on a dataset of robotic manipulation demonstrations, each comprising sequences of RGB-D observations $(I_t, D_t)$ and corresponding actions $(p_t, r_t, g_t)$, along with a natural language instruction $l_i$. Actions specify the end-effector position ($p_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$), 6D rotation ($r_t \in \mathbb{R}^6$), and gripper state ($g_t \in \{0, 1\}$). The objective is to learn a policy $\pi$ that maps the observation history and task instruction to an action chunk $\hat{a}_{t:t+h-1}$ over a prediction horizon $h$.

#### 3.2. Learning Motion Latents

This component aims to discover discrete motion patterns by training a dictionary of 3D latent codes. These codes are learned by predicting future 3D observations, thereby ensuring they capture motion semantics rather than visual appearance.

#### 3.2.1. Encoding Observations into 3D Motion Latent

Given an RGB-D observation $o_t$ and language instruction $l$, a continuous representation $f_t = \phi_{vlm}(o_t, l)$ is first extracted using a vision-language encoder (Mini-GPT). This continuous representation is then discretized into a sequence of $n_s$ discrete codes $z_t = [z_{t,1}, \dots, z_{t,n_s}]$ using Vector Quantization (VQ-VAE) with NSVQ for stable training. Each $z_{t,j}$ is an index into a learned codebook $C = \{c_1, \dots, c_K\}$.

#### 3.2.2. Learning via Future 3D Prediction

The discrete latent codes $z_t$ are trained by conditioning them on a prediction task: forecasting how the 3D scene will evolve over future timesteps.

- **Pointmap Representation:** Each RGB-D observation $o_t$ is converted into a 3D pointmap $P_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ by back-projecting pixels using camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
- **Conditional Diffusion for Future Prediction:** A video latent diffusion architecture (adapted from Blattmann et al., 2023) is employed.
 - **3D-aware VAE:** An encoder $\psi_{enc}$ and decoder $\psi_{dec}$ translate pointmaps to and from a latent space ($h_{t'}^{pm} = \psi_{enc}(P_{t'})$). This VAE is initialized from a pre-trained RGB VAE and fine-tuned on pointmaps through reconstruction.
 - **Diffusion Model:** A diffusion model $\psi_{diff}$ generates future pointmap latents $\hat{h}_{t+1:t+w}^{pm}$ conditioned on an observation window of historical pointmap latents $h_{t-w+1:t}^{pm}$ and the motion latent $z_t$. The model is trained to minimize a denoising objective $L_{pm}^{diff}$ (similar to DDPM), predicting the noise added to the target future latent.
- **Joint RGB Prediction:** An additional branch is trained to predict future RGB images using the same motion latent $z_t$, with a corresponding denoising objective $L_{rgb}^{diff}$.
- **Combined Training Objective:** The overall loss function is $L_{total} = L_{pm}^{diff} + L_{rgb}^{diff} + L_{vq}$, where $L_{vq}$ is the VQ-VAE loss, which includes a commitment loss and encourages latents to cluster similar motions. The VAEs are frozen during latent action learning.

#### 3.3. Action Prediction with Motion Latents

This component uses the learned geometry-aware motion latents to generate executable robot trajectories. The motion latent $z_t$ acts as a high-level guide for the policy.

#### 3.3.1. Overall Pipeline

A 3D denoising transformer $\epsilon_\theta$ (based on Ke et al., 2024) generates 6-DoF end-effector trajectories through conditional diffusion. It iteratively refines noisy action sequences into precise motions, conditioned on the current observation $o_t$, the learned motion latent $z_t$, and the robot's proprioceptive state $c_t$.

**Input Tokenization:** The transformer processes four types of tokens:
- **Motion Latent Tokens:** The discrete motion latent $z_t$ is embedded via learned embeddings.
- **Trajectory Tokens:** Each noisy action ($a_{t+k}$) is encoded via an MLP, with its 3D position concatenated for spatial grounding.
- **Scene Tokens:** Visual features are extracted from RGB images using a frozen CLIP-ResNet50 encoder. These features are lifted to 3D positions using the depth map and camera intrinsics, forming scene tokens that combine appearance and 3D location. A subset of these tokens is selected using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to manage computational load.
- **Proprioception Token:** The robot's current state (joint angles, end-effector pose) is encoded by an MLP and combined with a positional embedding of the end-effector position.

**Attention Mechanisms:** The transformer uses a two-stage attention strategy:
- **Self-Attention with 3D Positional Encoding:** Self-attention is applied across all trajectory, scene, and proprioception tokens, incorporating rotary positional embeddings to encode spatial relationships.
- **Cross-Attention to Motion Latents:** Subsequent cross-attention layers allow trajectory, scene, and proprioception tokens to attend to the motion latent embeddings, enabling the geometry-aware motion patterns in $z_t$ to guide trajectory generation.

The transformer outputs refined trajectory tokens, from which MLPs predict the noise added to 3D translations $\epsilon_{\theta,p}$, rotations $\epsilon_{\theta,r}$, and the gripper state $\hat{g}$.

#### 3.3.2. Training Objective and Inference Process for Action Prediction

The denoising transformer is trained to predict the noise added to a ground-truth action chunk $a_{t:t+h-1}$. The loss $L_\theta$ is a weighted sum of L1 losses for continuous position and rotation components, and binary cross-entropy (BCE) for the discrete gripper state. During inference, a noisy action chunk is iteratively denoised using the learned model and the current observation's motion latent to produce executable 6-DoF poses and gripper commands.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GeoMoLa was conducted across simulation benchmarks (RLBench, CALVIN) and real-world manipulation tasks, including ablation studies, to assess its performance, interpretability, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. RLBench Evaluation

- **Performance:** GeoMoLa achieved an overall success rate of 84.7% across 10 challenging language-conditioned manipulation tasks and 166 variations, outperforming existing 3D representation-based policies (e.g., Act3D (65.3%), 3D Diffuser Actor (77.0%), RVT2 (80.4%)) and 4D dynamic frameworks (ManiGaussian (44.8%)). It ranked first on 8 out of 10 tasks. This performance was achieved using only single-view RGB-D input, whereas some baselines like GNFactor and ManiGaussian required up to 19 additional views for 3D reconstruction during training.
- **Mechanism:** The results indicated that learning motion latents from 4D spatiotemporal dynamics, specifically state transitions, enabled more accurate action prediction compared to conditioning on static 3D feature fields. The use of 3D geometric changes along trajectories contributed to more precise and reliable action generation.
- **Interpretability of Geometry-Aware Motion Latents:** Experiments demonstrated that the learned motion latents encoded semantically meaningful motion primitives. When specific latent codes (e.g., for downward motion, for rotational motion) were applied to diverse initial scene configurations, they consistently generated the corresponding motion patterns in predicted future observations. This consistency suggested that the 4D dynamics pretraining effectively distilled reusable motion primitives.

#### 4.2. CALVIN Evaluation

- **Long-Horizon Task Execution:** On the CALVIN benchmark, which assesses long-horizon task execution and zero-shot generalization to an unseen environment (ABC→D setup), GeoMoLa achieved the highest average task sequence length of 3.60. This indicated superior ability in completing entire long-horizon subtask sequences compared to other methods, including 3D-based policies (e.g., DP3 (0.31), 3D Diffuser Actor (3.35)), video-pretrained models (e.g., GR-1 (3.06), SuSIE (2.69)), and closed-loop methods (e.g., Clover (3.53)).
- **Error Accumulation:** This result supports the hypothesis that explicit 4D dynamics modeling reduces compounding errors that can accumulate over extended task sequences.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Geometric vs. Appearance Modeling:** Ablation studies evaluated the contribution of different modalities in the 4D dynamics learning phase.
 - Removing pointmap prediction (i.e., relying only on RGB prediction) resulted in a substantial performance degradation (CALVIN: -0.22 average length; RLBench: -5.1% success rate).
 - Removing RGB prediction (i.e., relying only on pointmap prediction) had a comparatively minimal impact (CALVIN: -0.10 average length; RLBench: -2.7% success rate).
 - This asymmetry suggests that explicit 3D geometry is fundamental for learning transferable manipulation primitives, while visual appearance provides a less critical auxiliary context.
- **Role of Geometry in Motion Types:** Analysis of RLBench tasks indicated that 3D geometry was particularly important for rotation-heavy tasks (e.g., "sweep to dustpan," "open drawer"), where removing pointmap prediction led to a nearly 9% average reduction in success rates. In contrast, the drop was less than 1% for translation-heavy tasks (e.g., "close jar," "push buttons"). This suggests that geometric awareness is crucial for precise rotational control and complex object interactions.

#### 4.4. Real-World Validation

- **Performance with Limited Data:** In real-world experiments using an ALOHA robot and limited demonstrations (20 trajectories per task), GeoMoLa achieved a 53.3% average success rate across six distinct manipulation tasks. This represented a 13.3% improvement over the 3D Diffuser Actor (40.0% success rate) and significantly surpassed SkillDiffuser (21.7%).
- **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** Consistent gains were observed in tasks involving significant occlusion ("Clean cup") and those requiring precise manipulation ("Stack cups," "Stack cubes"). These improvements highlight the benefit of modeling dynamics and scene geometry for accurate action prediction in complex environments.
- **Qualitative Observations:** The policy successfully executed smooth and collision-free trajectories, demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer. A case study showed GeoMoLa enabling stable cup stacking in a cluttered scene, where a 3D static baseline failed due to misalignment, emphasizing the advantage of 4D spatiotemporal modeling for precise control under visual occlusion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robot learning by demonstrating the efficacy of grounding motion latent learning in four-dimensional (4D) geometric transformations, rather than relying predominantly on two-dimensional visual sequences.

The key significance of GeoMoLa lies in several aspects:
- **Shift in Representation Paradigm:** It quantitatively establishes that explicitly modeling the evolution of 3D geometry over time is more critical for learning robust manipulation skills than relying on pixel-level appearance patterns. The ablation studies provide empirical evidence that geometric prediction contributes substantially to performance, while visual prediction plays a comparatively minor role.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** By encoding actual physical motion and its 3D effects, the learned motion latents demonstrate improved robustness and generalization capabilities across novel scenes, viewpoints, and complex, cluttered environments. This addresses a common limitation of previous 2D-based or static 3D-based methods.
- **Sample Efficiency:** The approach shows effectiveness with limited real-world demonstrations, suggesting that geometry-aware latent representations intrinsically capture fundamental motion primitives, reducing the need for extensive datasets. This is a practical advantage for deploying robots in diverse real-world settings where data collection can be costly and time-consuming.
- **Interpretability:** The learned discrete motion codes exhibit semantic consistency, producing predictable and consistent geometric transformations regardless of specific visual context. This interpretability can facilitate debugging and transfer of skills.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance with Minimal Input:** GeoMoLa achieves strong performance across multiple manipulation benchmarks using only single-view RGB-D input, outperforming methods that may require multi-view reconstruction or larger training datasets.

The potential impact of this work is relevant to several application domains:
- **Assistive Robotics and Healthcare:** More robust and precise manipulation capabilities can improve the performance of robots assisting humans in daily tasks or sterile environments.
- **Logistics and Manufacturing:** Enhanced manipulation policies can lead to more efficient and adaptable robotic systems for object handling, assembly, and sorting in dynamic industrial settings.
- **Domestic Automation:** Reducing reliance on large-scale labeled demonstrations can lower the barriers to deploying robots in home environments, enabling them to perform diverse household chores.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a foundation for extending motion latent learning to more complex scenarios, such as manipulating deformable objects, and for developing hierarchical planning systems that compose learned geometric primitives into longer-horizon tasks.

In summary, GeoMoLa offers an alternative perspective on learning reusable manipulation skills by emphasizing the understanding of how objects move through 3D space over time. This approach has the potential to foster the development of more capable, adaptable, and deployable robotic systems.
