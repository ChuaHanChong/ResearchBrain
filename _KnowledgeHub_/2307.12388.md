# Uncertainty-aware Grounded Action Transformation towards Sim-to-Real Transfer for Traffic Signal Control

> [!summary] Summary
> UGAT is a sim-to-real transfer method for RL-based traffic signal control that extends Grounded Action Transformation with uncertainty quantification. A forward model trained on real data and an inverse model trained in simulation produce a grounded action; evidential deep learning estimates its uncertainty and a dynamically updated threshold rejects unreliable groundings. Across four real-world settings, UGAT cuts travel-time, queue, and delay gaps versus direct transfer.

## Problem

- RL traffic signal policies are trained in simulators with static vehicle acceleration and delay settings, so they degrade when deployed under real-world variability.
- Calibrating internal simulator parameters against real data is impractical, motivating action-level grounding rather than direct modification of the traffic simulator itself.
- Grounded Action Transformation was developed and applied mostly in robotics, and its grounding models give no reliability signal on unseen state-action inputs during exploration.

## Method

- Vanilla ==Grounded Action Transformation (GAT)== for signal control: a neural forward model trained on real trajectories with MSE loss, plus an inverse model trained on simulated data with categorical cross-entropy over discrete phases.
- **UGAT**'s uncertainty-aware grounding: the transformation function emits both a grounded action and an uncertainty value via ==evidential deep learning==; groundings above threshold are rejected and the policy's original action executes.
- ==Dynamic grounding rate==: the threshold is reset each iteration to the mean logged uncertainty over the last epochs, tracking the drifting grounding model instead of a fixed hyperparameter.

## Results

- On the snowy-weather setting, UGAT cut the travel-time gap from **221.25** to **173.03** seconds and the queue gap from **58.42** to **48.43** versus direct transfer.
- Ablation on the light-truck setting: travel-time gap rises from **33.49** with full UGAT to **39.12** without dynamic threshold, **44.87** for vanilla GAT, and **47.71** without grounding.
- Dynamic thresholding beat every static setting tested, with fixed values from **0.2** to **0.8** yielding travel-time gaps of **39.12** to **68.59** against **33.49** dynamic.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action grounding models are themselves uncertain; gating grounded actions by predicted uncertainty prevents corrections that widen rather than close the dynamics gap.
> - A threshold tracking a model that keeps training should adapt with it, since any fixed rejection cutoff misaligns as the grounding model's uncertainty distribution drifts.
> - Gains held across evidential learning, concrete dropout, and deep ensembles, so the benefit comes from gating itself rather than a specific uncertainty estimator.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traffic Signal Control (TSC) is a critical component of modern urban infrastructure, impacting traffic flow, congestion, and the daily lives of millions. The complexity of dynamic traffic scenarios necessitates the development of adaptive control algorithms. Recent advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) have shown promise in optimizing TSC, offering an advantage over traditional methods by enabling agents to learn adaptive signal plans directly from environmental feedback through trial and error.

However, a significant challenge in deploying RL-based TSC solutions in real-world environments is the "sim-to-real" (S2R) transfer gap. RL policies are predominantly trained in simulated environments due to their cost-effectiveness and safety, but these simulations often fail to fully capture the complexities and variabilities of real-world dynamics (e.g., diverse vehicle behaviors, weather conditions). This disparity leads to a performance degradation when a policy trained in simulation is directly applied to reality.

Prior research has attempted to bridge this gap through various approaches. One direction involves enhancing traffic simulators to align more closely with real-world conditions, often by incorporating real-world data. However, altering internal simulator parameters can be a resource-intensive and challenging task. Another line of research, particularly prevalent in robotics, is Grounded Action Transformation (GAT). GAT aims to align simulator transitions with reality by modifying the actions taken in the simulation based on real-world observations. While GAT has demonstrated effectiveness in robotics, its application to complex domains like traffic signal control, where transition dynamics are highly stochastic and multi-agent, has been limited.

This paper addresses the sim-to-real gap in RL-based TSC by adapting and extending the GAT framework. It recognizes that standard GAT might introduce instability during policy learning due to uncertainty in the action transformation model, particularly during exploration. By integrating uncertainty quantification into GAT, the proposed method aims to mitigate these issues and provide a more robust and efficient transfer mechanism for traffic signal control, thereby enhancing the practical applicability of RL-based solutions in intelligent transportation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective method for transferring Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies trained in simulated traffic environments to real-world traffic signal control (TSC) systems, thereby mitigating the performance gap commonly observed in such sim-to-real transfers.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations and challenges:

- **Prevalence and Limitations of Simulation-based Training:** RL policies for TSC are primarily developed and trained in simulated environments. While simulations offer benefits such as safety, reproducibility, and cost-effectiveness, they often simplify complex real-world dynamics. Factors like diverse vehicle acceleration/deceleration profiles, driver behavior variations, and environmental conditions (e.g., weather) are difficult to model perfectly, leading to discrepancies between simulated and real-world transition dynamics. This inherent "domain gap" prevents RL policies from achieving comparable performance when directly deployed in real-world settings.
- **Impact of Performance Degradation:** The performance degradation of RL policies transferred from simulation to reality can lead to suboptimal traffic flow, increased congestion, higher travel times, and reduced system efficiency. This limits the practical utility and adoption of advanced RL-based TSC solutions.
- **Challenges with Existing Sim-to-Real Approaches:**
 - **Simulator Enhancement:** Modifying internal parameters of existing traffic simulators to match real-world conditions more accurately can be complex and labor-intensive, often requiring extensive real-world data collection and calibration.
 - **Vanilla Grounded Action Transformation (GAT):** While GAT (initially from robotics) offers a promising framework by learning to transform simulated actions to align with real-world dynamics, the direct application of vanilla GAT to TSC presents a challenge. The action transformation function itself, being a learned model, can exhibit high uncertainty, especially when encountering novel states or actions during the RL agent's exploration phase. Taking grounded actions based on highly uncertain transformations can inadvertently *enlarge* the transition dynamics gap, destabilizing policy learning and hindering convergence.

Therefore, the key motivation behind proposing Uncertainty-aware Grounded Action Transformation (UGAT) is to address the limitations of vanilla GAT by:
- **Quantifying Model Uncertainty:** Integrating uncertainty quantification mechanisms within the GAT framework to assess the reliability of the action transformation model for any given state-action pair.
- **Dynamic Action Grounding:** Leveraging this uncertainty information to dynamically decide whether to apply the transformed (grounded) action or revert to the original action proposed by the RL policy. This adaptive approach aims to prevent the introduction of unreliable grounded actions that could destabilize training or enlarge the sim-to-real gap.
- **Adaptive Grounding Rate:** Introducing a dynamic grounding rate (threshold for uncertainty) that adjusts iteratively based on the observed model uncertainty, thus reducing the need for manual hyperparameter tuning and enhancing robustness as the transformation model evolves.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to make RL-based TSC policies more robust, efficient, and reliable for real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Uncertainty-aware Grounded Action Transformation (UGAT) to bridge the sim-to-real transfer gap in traffic signal control. The approach is built upon the existing Grounded Action Transformation (GAT) framework, with significant modifications to incorporate uncertainty quantification and dynamic action grounding.

**A. Problem Formulation and Base RL Model**
The Traffic Signal Control (TSC) problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by $M = \langle S, A, P, r, \gamma \rangle$.
- **State Space (S):** Includes the number of vehicles in each lane and the current phase of the intersection (represented as a one-hot code).
- **Action Space (A):** Discrete, representing the chosen phase for the next time interval ($\Delta t$).
- **Transition Dynamics (P):** Maps a state-action pair to a probability distribution over the next states. This is the primary source of the sim-to-real gap.
- **Reward (r):** An immediate scalar return, calculated as the negative sum of queue lengths across all lanes at the intersection, aiming to minimize waiting vehicles.
- **Discount Factor ($\gamma$):** Weights future rewards.
The RL agent learns a policy $\pi_{\theta}: S \rightarrow A$ to maximize the long-term expected discounted reward. For the discrete action space, Deep Q-Network (DQN) is employed as the underlying RL algorithm, trained within a simulated environment ($E_{sim}$).

**B. Grounded Action Transformation (GAT) Framework**
GAT aims to minimize the discrepancy between the transition dynamics of the simulator ($P_{\phi}$) and the real world ($P^*$). It achieves this by learning an action transformation function $g_{\phi}(s_t, a_t) = \hat{a}_t$, which takes the agent's state $s_t$ and policy-predicted action $a_t$ as input and outputs a "grounded action" $\hat{a}_t$ to be executed in $E_{sim}$. This grounded action effectively modifies $E_{sim}$'s dynamics to better reflect $E_{real}$.
GAT consists of two main components:
- **Forward Model ($f_{\phi_+}$):** Trained on real-world data ($D_{real}$), this deep neural network predicts the next possible real-world state ($\hat{s}_{t+1}$) given a current state $s_t$ and action $a_t$: $\hat{s}_{t+1} = f_{\phi_+}(s_t, a_t)$. It is optimized by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between predicted and actual next states: $L(\phi_+) = MSE(f_{\phi_+}(s_i^t, a_i^t), s_i^{t+1})$.
- **Inverse Model ($h_{\phi_-}$):** Trained on simulation data ($D_{sim}$), this deep neural network predicts an action $\hat{a}_t$ that would lead from a current state $s_t$ to a desired next state (specifically, the $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ predicted by the forward model). The inverse model takes $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ and $s_t$ as input: $\hat{a}_t = h_{\phi_-}(\hat{s}_{t+1}, s_t)$. Given that actions are discrete, it is optimized by minimizing the Categorical Cross-Entropy (CCE) loss: $L(\phi_-) = CCE(h_{\phi_-}(s_i^{t+1}, s_i^t), a_i^t)$.
The grounded action $\hat{a}_t$ used in $E_{sim}$ is computed as $\hat{a}_t = h_{\phi_-}(f_{\phi_+}(s_t, a_t), s_t)$.

**C. Uncertainty-aware GAT (UGAT) Enhancements**
The core innovation of UGAT is to address the instability caused by high model uncertainty in the action transformation function $g_{\phi}$.
1. **Model Uncertainty Quantification:**
 - The authors recognize that $g_{\phi}$ can produce unreliable grounded actions when encountering state-action inputs it is uncertain about (e.g., during RL exploration).
 - To counteract this, $g_{\phi}$ is augmented to output an uncertainty value $u_t$ in addition to the grounded action $\hat{a}_t$: $\hat{a}_t, u_t = g_{\phi}(s_t, a_t)$.
 - Various uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods for deep neural networks can be used. The paper primarily adopts Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) due to its observed performance, while also experimenting with Concrete Dropout and Deep Ensembles.
 - A dynamic decision rule is applied: if the uncertainty $u_t$ is below a certain threshold $\alpha$ (grounding rate), the grounded action $\hat{a}_t$ is taken in $E_{sim}$; otherwise, the original action $a_t$ from the policy $\pi_{\theta}$ is used, rejecting the uncertain grounded action.

2. **Dynamic Grounding Rate ($\alpha$):**
 - Instead of a fixed hyperparameter, the threshold $\alpha$ is dynamically adjusted during training.
 - The model uncertainty of $g_{\phi}$ changes as it is updated. A fixed $\alpha$ might not remain optimal throughout training.
 - At the end of each training epoch (or a block of $E$ epochs), $\alpha$ is updated by calculating the mean of the logged uncertainties $u_t$ from the previous $E$ epochs: $\alpha = \frac{\sum_{e=1}^E \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} u_t^e}{T \times E}$. This allows $\alpha$ to adapt to the current state of $g_{\phi}$'s uncertainty.

**D. Training Algorithm (Algorithm 1)**
The UGAT training process is iterative:
1. **Pre-training:** The RL policy $\pi_{\theta}$ is initially pre-trained for $M$ epochs in $E_{sim}$.
2. **Iterative Training (I iterations):**
 - **Data Collection:** Trajectories are collected by rolling out $\pi_{\theta}$ in both $E_{sim}$ and $E_{real}$ (note: $E_{real}$ data can also be offline). These datasets are used to update the transformation models.
 - **Transformation Function Update:** The forward model $f_{\phi_+}$ is updated using $D_{real}$ (Equation 5), and the inverse model $h_{\phi_-}$ is updated using $D_{sim}$ (Equation 6).
 - **Policy Training (E epochs):**
 - For each step in the simulation rollout:
 - An action $a_t$ is obtained from the current policy $\pi_{\theta}(s_t)$.
 - The action transformation function $g_{\phi}(s_t, a_t)$ is queried to obtain $\hat{a}_t$ and its associated uncertainty $u_t$.
 - **Dynamic Grounding Decision:** If $u_t \geq \alpha$, the grounded action $\hat{a}_t$ is rejected, and $a_t$ is executed in $E_{sim}$. Otherwise, $\hat{a}_t$ is executed.
 - The uncertainty $u_t$ is logged.
 - The RL policy $\pi_{\theta}$ is updated based on the experience gained in $E_{sim}$ with either grounded or original actions.
 - **Dynamic $\alpha$ Update:** After $E$ epochs of policy training, the grounding rate $\alpha$ is updated using the mean of the logged uncertainties (Equation 8).

**E. Environment Setup and Evaluation Metrics**
- **Simulators:** LibSignal serves as the framework, with Cityflow as $E_{sim}$ and SUMO as $E_{real}$.
- **Real-world Scenarios:** Four distinct real-world configurations (V1-V4) are defined in SUMO, mimicking heavy industry roads (lighter/heavier loaded vehicles) and special weather conditions (rainy/snowy) by adjusting vehicle parameters (acceleration, deceleration, startup delay).
- **Metrics:** Average Travel Time (ATT), Throughput (TP), Reward (total waiting vehicles), Queue (number of waiting vehicles), and Delay (average delay per vehicle). The primary evaluation focuses on the *gap* ($\Delta$) for each metric, calculated as $\psi_{real} - \psi_{sim}$, which directly quantifies the sim-to-real performance difference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to demonstrate the existence of a sim-to-real performance gap in TSC, assess UGAT's ability to mitigate this gap, and analyze the contributions of its individual components.

1. **Existence of Performance Gap:**
 - The "Direct-Transfer" method (policy trained in $E_{sim}$ using DQN for 300 epochs and then directly transferred to $E_{real}$) was used to evaluate the inherent performance gap.
 - Figure 2 visually represents this gap using radar charts across five metrics (ATT, Throughput, Reward, Queue, Delay) for four distinct $E_{real}$ settings (V1-V4).
 - The results consistently show a notable performance drop when the policy is applied to any of the $E_{real}$ settings compared to its performance in $E_{sim}$. For instance, ATT, Queue, and Delay (where lower values are better) are significantly higher in $E_{real}$, while Throughput and Reward (where higher values are better) are substantially lower. This confirms the presence of a substantial sim-to-real performance gap in TSC.

2. **Gap Mitigation by Uncertainty-aware UGAT:**
 - UGAT was compared against the Direct-Transfer method across the four $E_{real}$ settings (Table II). The performance in $E_{sim}$ for both methods converged to similar stable results (Table III).
 - **Quantified Gap Reduction:** UGAT significantly reduced the performance gap ($\Delta$) across all metrics and real-world scenarios compared to Direct-Transfer.
 - For ATT$_\Delta$, Queue$_\Delta$, and Delay$_\Delta$ (metrics where smaller values are better), UGAT consistently achieved smaller positive differences between $E_{real}$ and $E_{sim}$, indicating a closer alignment of performance. For example, in V1, ATT$_\Delta$ was reduced from 47.69 (Direct-Transfer) to 33.49 (UGAT).
 - For TP$_\Delta$ and Reward$_\Delta$ (metrics where larger values are better, and thus the gap is negative), UGAT achieved values closer to zero (i.e., less negative), signifying improved performance in $E_{real}$ relative to $E_{sim}$. For instance, in V1, TP$_\Delta$ changed from -77 to -52, and Reward$_\Delta$ from -32.11 to -19.94.
 - **Improved Absolute Performance:** Beyond gap reduction, UGAT also improved the absolute performance of the transferred policy in $E_{real}$. It resulted in lower Average Travel Time (ATT) and higher Throughput (TP) values compared to Direct-Transfer.
 - **Robustness:** The consistent improvements observed across diverse real-world conditions (V1-V4, representing varied vehicle loads and adverse weather) indicate UGAT's robustness and effectiveness in complex environments.

3. **Ablation Study:**
 - An ablation study was conducted (Table IV, using V1 setting) to evaluate the impact of UGAT's core modules: dynamic grounding rate, uncertainty quantification, and action grounding.
 - **Impact of Dynamic Grounding Rate:** Removing the dynamic $\alpha$ (fixing it at 0.5) resulted in a larger performance gap across all metrics compared to full UGAT (e.g., ATT$_\Delta$ increased from 33.49 to 39.12).
 - **Impact of Uncertainty Quantification:** Removing both dynamic $\alpha$ and uncertainty quantification (effectively reverting to Vanilla GAT) further increased the performance gap (e.g., ATT$_\Delta$ increased to 44.87), demonstrating the benefit of explicitly quantifying uncertainty.
 - **Impact of Action Grounding:** Completely removing the action grounding module (equivalent to Direct-Transfer, "w/o Grounding") led to the largest performance gaps (e.g., ATT$_\Delta$ at 47.71), underscoring the fundamental necessity of the GAT framework itself.
 - **Static vs. Dynamic $\alpha$ (Table V):** Further experiments with static $\alpha$ values (0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8) while keeping the uncertainty module active showed that UGAT's dynamic $\alpha$ consistently outperformed all fixed $\alpha$ settings in terms of gap reduction. This confirms the advantage of adapting the grounding rate to the evolving model uncertainty.

4. **Comparison of Uncertainty Quantification Methods:**
 - Experiments comparing different uncertainty quantification methods (EDL, Concrete Dropout, Deep Ensembles) with a baseline without uncertainty (Fig. 3, using V1 setting) demonstrated that all UQ methods contribute to narrowing the performance gap.
 - EDL exhibited the most favorable performance, resulting in the smallest ATT$_\Delta$ and largest (least negative) TP$_\Delta$, thereby validating its selection as the default UQ method for UGAT.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in addressing the critical sim-to-real transfer problem for Reinforcement Learning (RL) based Traffic Signal Control (TSC). The proposed Uncertainty-aware Grounded Action Transformation (UGAT) method offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap in TSC:** The most direct significance is UGAT's demonstrated effectiveness in substantially reducing the performance gap between policies trained in simulation and their performance in diverse real-world traffic environments. This directly addresses a major bottleneck hindering the practical deployment of sophisticated RL-based TSC solutions. By allowing policies to transfer more seamlessly, UGAT paves the way for more robust and reliable intelligent traffic management systems.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Stability in RL Training:** By integrating uncertainty quantification into the Grounded Action Transformation (GAT) framework, UGAT makes the transfer process more robust. It intelligently filters out unreliable grounded actions that stem from high model uncertainty during RL exploration. This mechanism prevents the action transformation function from destabilizing policy learning or inadvertently widening the domain gap, leading to more stable training and better-converged policies.

3. **Adaptability with Dynamic Grounding:** The introduction of a dynamic grounding rate ($\alpha$) is a pragmatic advancement. Instead of requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning for a fixed threshold, UGAT automatically adjusts $\alpha$ based on the evolving model uncertainty. This adaptability simplifies deployment and maintenance, as the system can respond to changes in the transformation model's confidence throughout its lifecycle.

4. **Improved Generalizability Across Diverse Conditions:** The experiments across varied real-world scenarios, including different vehicle loads (heavy industry roads) and adverse weather conditions (rainy, snowy), demonstrate UGAT's generalizability. This indicates that the method is not confined to specific traffic patterns or environmental settings, enhancing its applicability to a broader range of urban environments.

5. **Foundation for Safer and More Efficient Transportation Systems:** By enabling more effective sim-to-real transfer, UGAT contributes to the development of RL agents that can manage traffic more efficiently in real-world conditions. This has the potential to lead to reduced average travel times, lower queue lengths, decreased fuel consumption, and fewer emissions, thereby improving urban mobility and environmental sustainability.

6. **Broader Implications for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While focused on TSC, the principles of uncertainty-aware action transformation could be applicable to other domains facing sim-to-real challenges, particularly those where GAT is employed (e.g., robotics). The findings regarding the benefits of dynamic uncertainty quantification methods (like EDL) offer insights that may inform future research in broader transfer learning contexts.

In summary, UGAT represents a step forward in making RL-based traffic signal control not only theoretically promising but also practically deployable, thereby contributing to the evolution of intelligent and adaptive urban infrastructure.
