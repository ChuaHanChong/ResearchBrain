# EEPO: Exploration-Enhanced Policy Optimization via Sample-Then-Forget

> [!summary] Summary
> Exploration-Enhanced Policy Optimization (EEPO) introduces a "sample-then-forget" mechanism within Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to address entropy collapse in large language models (LLMs) for reasoning tasks. It achieved average accuracy improvements of up to 33.0% relative to standard GRPO on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, maintaining higher policy entropy and enhancing generalization without incurring significant computational overhead.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs often suffer from "entropy collapse," where policies converge to a narrow set of behaviors.
- This rapid reduction in response diversity leads to overfitting to the training distribution and diminished generalization capabilities, particularly on out-of-distribution problems.
- Existing exploration techniques often fail to disrupt the self-reinforcing feedback loop where dominant behavioral modes are repeatedly sampled and amplified.

## Method

- **EEPO** modifies the GRPO rollout process by dividing it into two sampling stages, separated by an adaptive unlearning step.
- An unlearning procedure temporarily suppresses the responses sampled in the first stage using a complementary loss and a single gradient *ascend* step on the rollout model's parameters.
- Unlearning is activated adaptively based on an entropy threshold and its effects are temporary, confined to the current rollout iteration, ensuring targeted intervention without accumulation.

## Results

- EEPO consistently improved average accuracy across multiple LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-**3B**, Llama3.2-**3B**-Instruct, Qwen3-**8B**-Base) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, achieving up to a **33.0%** relative gain over standard GRPO.
- The method demonstrated superior generalization, particularly on harder and out-of-distribution benchmarks like AMC23, where it achieved **45.0%** accuracy with Qwen2.5-**3B** compared to GRPO's **30.3%**.
- EEPO maintained consistently higher policy entropy throughout training and incurred minimal computational overhead, resulting in training times comparable to standard GRPO while outperforming other exploration-enhanced baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively and temporarily suppressing previously sampled dominant responses during the rollout phase can effectively force an LLM policy to explore alternative, less probable reasoning paths.
> - A targeted unlearning mechanism, conditioned on token-level entropy and utilizing a complementary loss, provides a lightweight and precise way to mitigate entropy collapse.
> - Sustained exploration, facilitated by preventing early convergence to deterministic behaviors, is crucial for improving generalization on challenging and out-of-distribution reasoning problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) capable of advanced reasoning, exemplified by models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1, has significantly progressed. A contributing factor to this advancement is the application of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), often implemented using methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). RLVR leverages rule-based reward models to provide precise feedback in domains like mathematical and logical reasoning, enabling scalable optimization.

However, a fundamental challenge in applying reinforcement learning, including RLVR, is the exploration-exploitation dilemma. Current RLVR methods frequently prioritize exploitation of high-reward trajectories, which can lead to "entropy collapse." This phenomenon describes a rapid reduction in the diversity of generated responses, where the policy converges to a narrow set of behaviors. This can result in overfitting to the training distribution and diminished generalization capabilities to unseen or out-of-distribution (OOD) problems. Previous research has explored methods to enhance exploration, largely categorized into objective-level modifications (e.g., increased clipping thresholds like in DAPO) and indiscriminate exploration techniques (e.g., increasing sampling temperature or entropy regularization). While these methods aim to increase policy stochasticity, they often fail to disrupt a self-reinforcing loop: dominant behavioral modes continue to be sampled, reinforced, and amplified, thereby perpetuating entropy collapse.

EEPO positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing exploration strategies. It introduces a mechanism that intervenes directly in the rollout process to actively suppress previously sampled dominant responses. This approach aims to provide targeted exploration that steers the model toward alternative reasoning paths, thereby disrupting the self-reinforcing feedback loop that contributes to entropy collapse. By doing so, EEPO seeks to improve the generalization and overall performance of LLMs in reasoning tasks, particularly under RLVR frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to mitigate the issue of insufficient exploration and entropy collapse in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large language models (LLMs). The authors identified several specific motivations for pursuing this objective:

1. **Addressing Entropy Collapse and Overfitting:** The paper observes that existing RLVR methods, particularly those based on GRPO, tend to over-emphasize exploitation. This leads to a rapid decline in policy entropy, indicating a convergence to deterministic behaviors. While in-distribution test accuracy may continue to rise, performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks often deteriorates. This divergence suggests that reduced exploration causes models to overfit to the training data rather than discovering generalizable reasoning patterns. The core motivation is to prevent this early convergence and promote the learning of more robust, transferable skills.

2. **Disrupting the Self-Reinforcing Loop:** The authors hypothesize that entropy collapse is driven by a self-reinforcing mechanism. As entropy declines, the policy forms increasingly confident beliefs about solutions, leading to an output distribution with multiple, often imbalanced, modes. One dominant mode receives a disproportionate share of probability mass. During rollouts, responses are predominantly sampled from this dominant mode. If these responses receive positive feedback (e.g., from a rule-based reward model), the policy further amplifies this mode while suppressing alternative, potentially superior, reasoning strategies. This feedback loop inhibits exploration and traps the policy in local optima, limiting its generalization ability. The work aims to break this cycle.

3. **Limitations of Existing Exploration Techniques:** Current methods to enhance exploration, such as increasing sampling temperature, adding entropy regularization, or techniques like DAPO's "clip higher," primarily aim to flatten the output distribution or relax constraints on low-probability trajectories. However, these approaches often do not fundamentally shift probability mass away from dominant behaviors. They may increase stochasticity but do not disrupt the self-reinforcing loop; the dominant mode remains the most likely to be sampled, hindering the discovery of truly novel and diverse reasoning paths. This motivates the need for a more direct and targeted intervention in the exploration process.

4. **Enabling Targeted Exploration during Rollout:** The central objective is to develop a mechanism that allows the policy to explore plausible behaviors beyond the dominant modes during the rollout phase itself. By preventing repeated sampling from the most probable regions, the aim is to encourage the model to uncover alternative trajectories, thereby steering exploration toward broader regions of the output space and fostering the discovery of more generalizable reasoning patterns.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EEPO introduces a framework that modifies the rollout process of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enhance exploration through a "sample-then-forget" mechanism. This approach aims to prevent the rollout model from repeatedly sampling from dominant modes, thereby promoting the discovery of diverse reasoning paths.

**Core EEPO Framework:**
EEPO modifies the standard GRPO rollout by dividing it into two distinct stages, separated by an unlearning step:
1. **Stage 1 Sampling:** The rollout model ($\pi_{rollout}$) generates the first half (G/2) of the total trajectories required for the GRPO update.
2. **Unlearning Step:** An adaptive unlearning procedure is applied to the $\pi_{rollout}$ model. This step temporarily suppresses the responses just sampled in Stage 1.
3. **Stage 2 Sampling:** The remaining G/2 trajectories are sampled from the *modified* $\pi_{rollout}$ model. This forces the model to explore different regions of the output space, as the previously dominant modes have been suppressed.
After both stages, all G trajectories are combined. Their rewards are computed, and the standard GRPO objective is applied to update the main policy model ($\pi_\theta$). The importance sampling ratio (Equation 3) correctly accounts for the rollout model ($\pi_{rollout}$) that generated each specific trajectory. Crucially, the $\pi_{rollout}$ model is synchronized with the policy model ($\pi_\theta$) at the beginning of each GRPO iteration, making the unlearning effects temporary and confined to that specific rollout.

**Adaptive Unlearning Mechanism:**
The unlearning intervention is designed with three specific desiderata: to be targeted, triggerable, and lightweight.

1. **Entropy-Conditioned Activation:** To ensure the unlearning only intervenes when exploration is genuinely deteriorating, an entropy-based indicator ($I_t$) is introduced. Unlearning is activated only if the *m*-step moving average of token-level entropy ($H^{(m)}_t$) falls below a predefined threshold ($\alpha$). This prevents unnecessary intervention during healthy exploration phases. A short moving average window (e.g., $m=3$) is used for prompt detection of low-entropy states.

2. **Complementary Unlearning Loss ($L_{Comp}$):** To penalize dominant regions more effectively, a custom loss function is employed. Standard negative log-likelihood (NLL) inversely penalizes predictions, meaning low-probability predictions incur higher loss. EEPO uses a complementary loss, $L_{Comp} = -\log(1 - p_{clip})$, where $p_{clip}$ is a clipped version of the rollout probability. This loss imposes stronger penalties on high-probability predictions (i.e., dominant modes), thereby actively suppressing them. The clipping (using a small $\epsilon$) prevents numerical instability when probabilities approach 1.

3. **Temporary Single-Step Updates:** To keep the intervention lightweight and localized, the unlearning involves a single gradient *ascend* step on the complementary loss. This update is applied only to the parameters of the rollout model ($\theta'$) and is not propagated to the main policy model ($\theta$). Because $\theta'$ is re-synchronized with $\theta$ at the start of each GRPO iteration, the unlearning effect does not accumulate across iterations and is temporary for the current rollout process.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology was validated through experiments on three distinct LLMs: Qwen2.5-3B, Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, and Qwen3-8B-Base (with additional experiments on Qwen3-14B-Base in the appendix). Models were trained on the MATH dataset (8.5K hard problems) for 2 epochs using the VERL framework with GRPO. A binary reward (+1 for correct, 0 otherwise) was used. Key hyperparameters included a batch size of 128, a learning rate of 5x10$^{-7}$, and 8 rollouts per question. EEPO-specific parameters were an entropy threshold $\alpha=0.3$ and an unlearning rate $\eta=3 \times 10^{-3}$.

Evaluation was performed on five mathematical reasoning benchmarks: Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, AMC 2023, AIME 2024, and AIME 2025 (for stronger models). Baselines included the base/instruct model, standard GRPO, and GRPO variants with increased entropy term, higher sampling temperature, DAPO's "Clip Higher" technique, and more rollouts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across multiple LLMs and mathematical reasoning benchmarks yielded several key findings regarding EEPO's performance and operational characteristics.

**Overall Performance Improvements:**
EEPO consistently demonstrated performance advantages over standard GRPO and all tested exploration-enhanced GRPO variants.
- On Qwen2.5-3B, EEPO improved average accuracy by 24.3% relative to GRPO (from 21.0% to 26.1%).
- On Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, EEPO achieved a 33.0% relative gain (from 17.6% to 23.4%).
- On Qwen3-8B-Base, EEPO showed a 10.4% relative improvement (from 34.7% to 38.3%).
These results indicate that the sample-then-forget mechanism provides consistent and model-agnostic improvements across different model sizes and types (base and instruction-tuned).

**Comparison with Baselines:**
When compared against various exploration strategies—such as increased sampling temperature, enhanced entropy regularization, DAPO's "clip higher" method, and increasing the number of rollouts—EEPO maintained superior performance. While these baselines could sometimes outperform standard GRPO, their gains were often modest and required careful hyperparameter tuning, which could be brittle. For instance, temperature-based exploration showed a clear trade-off, with performance sharply degrading at higher values. Some baselines also led to substantially longer training times due to inefficient exploration and longer generated responses. EEPO, by contrast, achieved larger and more stable gains through its targeted exploration.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
EEPO exhibited improved generalization, particularly on harder and out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks, such as AMC23, where baseline methods tended to plateau. For example, on AMC23 with Qwen2.5-3B, EEPO achieved 45.0% accuracy compared to GRPO's 30.3%. This is attributed to EEPO's ability to maintain higher policy entropy and sustained exploration throughout training, preventing overfitting to the training distribution and fostering the discovery of reasoning patterns that generalize to diverse problems.

**Exploration Enhancement and Quality Preservation:**
Analysis of training dynamics demonstrated that EEPO maintains consistently higher policy entropy throughout training, contrasting with GRPO's continuous entropy collapse. A notable observation was that EEPO's Stage 2 rollouts consistently achieved higher entropy than Stage 1, indicating that the temporary unlearning step successfully forces the model to explore regions of the output space that were less probable or previously suppressed. Despite this enhanced exploration, generation quality, as measured by mean rewards and response lengths, remained stable and comparable to GRPO. These findings support the hypothesis that targeted suppression of sampled responses facilitates exploration without compromising the model's ability to generate relevant and high-quality outputs. Furthermore, EEPO demonstrated better Pass@k scaling with increased sampling budgets, suggesting more robust reasoning pattern discovery.

**Training Efficiency:**
EEPO's computational overhead was found to be minimal, resulting in training times comparable to standard GRPO. This contrasts with certain baseline exploration methods (e.g., higher sampling temperatures or increased rollouts) that significantly increased training duration due to the generation of longer responses or a larger number of trajectories. This indicates that EEPO achieves performance gains while maintaining the efficiency of the underlying GRPO algorithm.

**Scalability and Complementarity (from Appendices):**
Additional experiments in the appendix further supported EEPO's scalability, showing consistent improvements over GRPO on the larger Qwen3-14B-Base model. The appendix also showed that EEPO provides additional absolute accuracy gains (3.7–5.1 points) when combined with other exploration-enhanced GRPO variants (Increased Entropy, Clip High), suggesting its complementary nature rather than being a substitute for these methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Exploration-Enhanced Policy Optimization (EEPO) addresses a notable limitation in the application of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to large language models (LLMs): the pervasive issue of entropy collapse and insufficient exploration. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Advancement in RL for LLM Reasoning:** EEPO provides a novel mechanism to overcome the exploration-exploitation dilemma in RLVR. By introducing a targeted "sample-then-forget" approach, it offers a method to break the self-reinforcing loops that lead to premature convergence and local optima. This directly contributes to developing more robust and capable LLMs for complex reasoning tasks.

2. **Improved Generalization:** The sustained exploration facilitated by EEPO, which prevents overfitting to training distributions, is critical for real-world applications. LLMs trained with EEPO demonstrate better performance on out-of-distribution and more challenging benchmarks, indicating that they learn more generalizable reasoning patterns rather than memorizing solutions. This improved generalization capability is crucial for deploying LLMs in diverse and unpredictable environments.

3. **Practical and Efficient Solution:** EEPO integrates into existing GRPO frameworks with minimal computational overhead, as evidenced by its comparable training times to standard GRPO. This practical efficiency makes it a viable and attractive method for researchers and practitioners working with large-scale LLM training, avoiding the substantial resource increases often associated with other exploration techniques.

4. **Robustness and Scalability:** The consistent performance gains observed across different LLM architectures (Qwen, Llama), model scales (3B to 14B parameters), and a variety of mathematical reasoning benchmarks underscore the robustness and scalability of EEPO. This suggests that the mechanism is not specific to a particular model or task but represents a broadly applicable improvement to RLVR.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** EEPO's approach of an active, rollout-time intervention, distinct from objective-level modifications or indiscriminate stochasticity, opens new avenues for research in exploration strategies for LLMs. It encourages further investigation into dynamic and context-aware methods for balancing exploration and exploitation, potentially leading to hybrid approaches that combine EEPO's targeted unlearning with other complementary techniques.

In summary, EEPO presents a significant step towards enabling LLMs to learn more diverse and generalizable reasoning abilities by providing a practical, efficient, and robust solution to the challenge of insufficient exploration in RLVR.
