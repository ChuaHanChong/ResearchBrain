# PRIMT: Preference-based Reinforcement Learning with Multimodal Feedback and Trajectory Synthesis from Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PRIMT, a Preference-based Reinforcement Learning framework, integrates Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models to provide multimodal synthetic feedback and actively synthesize trajectories, thereby reducing human input reliance and mitigating query ambiguity and credit assignment challenges. It consistently outperforms existing FM-based baselines across various robotics tasks, achieves performance competitive with oracle methods, and demonstrates practical applicability on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) is limited by its high demand for human input for preference labeling, hindering scalability.
- Key challenges in PbRL include query ambiguity during early training with suboptimal robot behaviors and accurately assigning credit for trajectory-level preferences to specific state-action pairs.
- Prior attempts to use Foundation Models (FMs) in PbRL often relied on single-modality evaluations (e.g., text-only LLMs or vision-only VLMs), leading to incomplete or unreliable synthetic feedback.

## Method

- A Hierarchical ==Neuro-Symbolic Preference Fusion== module integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for comprehensive multimodal synthetic feedback, utilizing structured textual projections, keyframe visual renderings, and Probabilistic Soft Logic (PSL) for robust inter-modal integration.
- A Bidirectional Trajectory Synthesis module actively leverages LLMs: foresight generation provides diverse, task-aligned trajectories for warm-starting the replay buffer.
- Hindsight augmentation uses LLMs to generate counterfactual trajectories through minimal interventions at causally critical steps, combined with a causal auxiliary loss, to enhance granular credit assignment during reward learning.

## Results

- PRIMT consistently surpassed single-modality Foundation Model baselines in task performance and convergence speed across eight diverse robotics tasks, achieving results competitive with, and in some cases exceeding, an oracle using ground-truth rewards.
- The framework improved the accuracy of synthetic preference labels and demonstrated robustness, maintaining reasonable performance even when using weaker Foundation Model backbones, with a **13x** improvement in cost-performance efficiency.
- Successful real-world deployment on a Kinova Jaco robot for block lifting and stacking tasks showed PRIMT outperforming a baseline (e.g., 7/10 successful lifts versus 4/10), validating its practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured hierarchical fusion of multimodal feedback (from LLMs and VLMs) is essential for robust synthetic preference learning, as naive multimodal integration can degrade performance.
> - Foundation Models can actively address intrinsic PbRL challenges by synthesizing diverse foresight trajectories to reduce early-stage query ambiguity and generating counterfactuals for improved credit assignment.
> - Leveraging causal auxiliary loss with counterfactual trajectories allows the reward model to precisely attribute preference differences to specific state-action changes, leading to more accurate and granular reward learning.
