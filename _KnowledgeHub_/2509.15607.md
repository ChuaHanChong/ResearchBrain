# PRIMT: Preference-based Reinforcement Learning with Multimodal Feedback and Trajectory Synthesis from Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PRIMT, a Preference-based Reinforcement Learning framework, integrates Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models to provide multimodal synthetic feedback and actively synthesize trajectories, thereby reducing human input reliance and mitigating query ambiguity and credit assignment challenges. It consistently outperforms existing FM-based baselines across various robotics tasks, achieves performance competitive with oracle methods, and demonstrates practical applicability on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) is limited by its high demand for human input for preference labeling, hindering scalability.
- Key challenges in PbRL include query ambiguity during early training with suboptimal robot behaviors and accurately assigning credit for trajectory-level preferences to specific state-action pairs.
- Prior attempts to use Foundation Models (FMs) in PbRL often relied on single-modality evaluations (e.g., text-only LLMs or vision-only VLMs), leading to incomplete or unreliable synthetic feedback.

## Method

- A Hierarchical ==Neuro-Symbolic Preference Fusion== module integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for comprehensive multimodal synthetic feedback, utilizing structured textual projections, keyframe visual renderings, and Probabilistic Soft Logic (PSL) for robust inter-modal integration.
- A Bidirectional Trajectory Synthesis module actively leverages LLMs: foresight generation provides diverse, task-aligned trajectories for warm-starting the replay buffer.
- Hindsight augmentation uses LLMs to generate counterfactual trajectories through minimal interventions at causally critical steps, combined with a causal auxiliary loss, to enhance granular credit assignment during reward learning.

## Results

- PRIMT consistently surpassed single-modality Foundation Model baselines in task performance and convergence speed across eight diverse robotics tasks, achieving results competitive with, and in some cases exceeding, an oracle using ground-truth rewards.
- The framework improved the accuracy of synthetic preference labels and demonstrated robustness, maintaining reasonable performance even when using weaker Foundation Model backbones, with a **13x** improvement in cost-performance efficiency.
- Successful real-world deployment on a Kinova Jaco robot for block lifting and stacking tasks showed PRIMT outperforming a baseline (e.g., 7/10 successful lifts versus 4/10), validating its practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured hierarchical fusion of multimodal feedback (from LLMs and VLMs) is essential for robust synthetic preference learning, as naive multimodal integration can degrade performance.
> - Foundation Models can actively address intrinsic PbRL challenges by synthesizing diverse foresight trajectories to reduce early-stage query ambiguity and generating counterfactuals for improved credit assignment.
> - Leveraging causal auxiliary loss with counterfactual trajectories allows the reward model to precisely attribute preference differences to specific state-action changes, leading to more accurate and granular reward learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in various robotics domains. However, its widespread applicability is frequently constrained by the requirement for meticulously designed reward functions. The process of reward engineering can be challenging, particularly when task objectives are implicit or multifaceted. Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) offers an alternative paradigm, enabling robots to learn complex behaviors by inferring reward models from human comparative feedback on robot trajectories. This approach aims to align robotic systems more intuitively with human intent, bypassing the need for explicit reward function design.

Despite its advantages, PbRL faces two primary limitations. First, the reliance on extensive human input for preference labeling restricts its scalability. Second, even with human feedback, challenges arise in resolving query ambiguity, especially during early training stages when robot behaviors are suboptimal, and in accurately assigning credit, where overall trajectory preferences must be attributed to specific state-action pairs.

To address the human input bottleneck, recent research has investigated the use of Foundation Models (FMs), such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), as synthetic feedback sources. These FMs leverage their broad world knowledge to evaluate robot behaviors. Prior work has explored using FMs to generate dense reward functions, provide auxiliary contrastive signals, or act as evaluators within the PbRL framework. Existing FM-based PbRL methods, such as PrefCLM, RL-SaLLM-F, and RL-VLM-F, typically rely on single-modality evaluations (e.g., LLMs interpreting textual state-action sequences or VLMs assessing visual renderings of final states or keyframes). This single-modality dependence can lead to incomplete or unreliable feedback. LLMs, while capable of sophisticated temporal reasoning, may hallucinate spatial details from abstract textual descriptions. Conversely, VLMs, proficient in capturing spatial cues, may overlook subtle temporal dynamics.

This paper introduces PRIMT, aiming to integrate the complementary strengths of LLMs and VLMs for a more comprehensive multimodal evaluation. Furthermore, PRIMT moves beyond using FMs solely as passive evaluators. It investigates how FMs can be actively leveraged to mitigate query ambiguity and enhance credit assignment, two intrinsic challenges in PbRL that existing FM-based methods have not fully addressed through active trajectory manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) framework, named PRIMT, that addresses the critical limitations of extensive human input, query ambiguity, and credit assignment within the PbRL paradigm. This is achieved by leveraging the capabilities of foundation models (FMs) for both multimodal synthetic feedback and active trajectory synthesis.

The motivation for this research stems from several challenges inherent to existing PbRL approaches:

1. **Reducing Reliance on Extensive Human Input**: Traditional PbRL requires substantial human effort to label preferences between pairs of robot trajectories. This manual annotation process is time-consuming and expensive, significantly hindering the scalability of PbRL to complex and diverse tasks. The paper is motivated to eliminate or drastically reduce this human dependency by employing FMs as synthetic evaluators.

2. **Improving the Quality and Reliability of Synthetic Feedback**: While FMs have been explored as synthetic feedback sources, current approaches often rely on single-modality evaluations. LLMs, which process structured textual descriptions of trajectories, can perform sophisticated temporal reasoning but may struggle with fine-grained spatial interactions and are prone to hallucinations regarding key visual events. VLMs, which analyze visual renderings, effectively capture spatial goal completion but can overlook subtle temporal dynamics or the procedural logic of actions. This motivates the development of a multimodal evaluation framework that integrates the complementary strengths of both LLMs and VLMs to provide more reliable and comprehensive feedback, moving beyond the limitations of single-modality assessments.

3. **Mitigating Query Ambiguity in Early Training Stages**: In the initial phases of PbRL training, policies are often random or weakly optimized, generating trajectories of uniformly low quality. When comparing such trajectories, it can be difficult for even expert evaluators (human or synthetic) to discern meaningful differences or elicit informative preferences. This phenomenon, known as query ambiguity, impedes efficient reward model learning. The research is motivated to actively leverage FMs to generate diverse and task-aligned trajectories, thereby warm-starting the trajectory buffer with more informative samples that can serve as meaningful anchors for comparison, thus reducing early-stage ambiguity.

4. **Enhancing Credit Assignment in Reward Learning**: Even with reliable trajectory-level preferences, accurately attributing observed preference differences to specific states or actions (i.e., credit assignment) remains a significant challenge. This granularity mismatch can lead to a learned reward model that misaligns with desired behaviors or generalizes poorly to unseen situations. The paper is motivated to utilize FMs' advanced causal reasoning capabilities to generate counterfactual trajectories. By identifying critical causal steps and minimally intervening to reverse a preference, these counterfactuals can provide targeted signals that highlight the precise state-action events responsible for a preference difference, thus improving the granularity and accuracy of credit assignment.

In summary, PRIMT is motivated by the need for a more scalable, robust, and efficient PbRL paradigm that actively harnesses foundation models to overcome the limitations of human annotation, incomplete synthetic feedback, and intrinsic challenges in reward learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRIMT is a Preference-based Reinforcement Learning framework that integrates Foundation Models (FMs) through two synergistic modules: Hierarchical Neuro-Symbolic Preference Fusion and Bidirectional Trajectory Synthesis.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Neuro-Symbolic Preference Fusion

This module is designed to enhance the quality and reliability of synthetic feedback by combining the complementary strengths of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

- **Trajectory Preprocessing**: For any given trajectory pair (σA, σB), two distinct representations are generated:
 - **Textual Projections**: `text(σ)` is created by organizing each trajectory into dimension-specific sequences of state-action pairs. This structured textual format is designed to enhance semantic interpretability for LLMs, capturing temporal patterns.
 - **Keyframe Visual Renderings**: `kvis(σ)` comprises a selection of visual frames for VLM evaluation. Instead of using all frames or only the final state, PRIMT employs a hybrid keyframe extraction method to capture both low-level motion cues and high-level behavioral transitions. This method combines:
 - **Near-Zero Velocity Detection**: Identifies frames where robot motion is minimal, often indicating subgoal completions or transitional pauses.
 - **Smoothing Residual Peaks**: Detects high-curvature or abrupt motion transitions by comparing the raw trajectory to a smoothed version, highlighting significant motion shifts.
 - **Change Point Detection (CPD)**: Segments the trajectory into semantically coherent phases, identifying structural task changes.
 The final keyframe set is the union of frames selected by these three methods, along with the first and last frames of the trajectory.

- **Intra-modal Preference Fusion**: Each modality (LLM and VLM) is queried separately with its respective input (textual projections or keyframe sequences) and a task description. A structured three-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompt guides the evaluation: i) analyze trajectory effectiveness, ii) output a preference label (0 for σA, 1 for σB, -1 for indecision), and iii) verify the decision and assign a confidence score (0-1). To improve reliability and mitigate variance, a crowd-check mechanism is employed, querying each FM multiple times with permuted trajectory orderings. The K predictions from each modality are aggregated into a final modality-specific preference label (Υ_M) via majority voting. Confidence (C_M) is then calculated as a weighted combination of the average confidence of agreeing judgments and the label consistency ratio (vote agreement).

- **Inter-modal Preference Fusion**: This stage integrates the modality-specific preference labels and confidences into a unified decision using Probabilistic Soft Logic (PSL). PSL is a probabilistic framework that represents entities as logical atoms connected by weighted first-order logic rules, allowing for structured and interpretable reasoning. Four primary rules are defined:
 - **Agreement Rule**: If LLM and VLM agree on a label and at least one has high confidence, that label is selected.
 - **Conflict Resolution Rules**: When modalities conflict, PSL resolves the disagreement by considering both confidence and trajectory context.
 - It prioritizes VLM prediction if visual discriminability (VDHigh, measured by Wasserstein distance between CLIP embeddings of keyframes) and VLM confidence are high.
 - It prioritizes LLM prediction if temporal discriminability (TDHigh, measured by trajectory volatility based on mean L2 norm of second-order finite differences) and LLM confidence are high.
 - **Indecision Rule**: If both modalities exhibit low confidence, an indecision label is assigned.
 PSL inference solves a convex optimization problem to infer the final preference label, subject to a one-hot constraint, effectively managing uncertainties and cross-modal conflicts.

#### 3.2. Bidirectional Trajectory Synthesis

This module leverages FMs to actively enhance reward learning in PbRL by addressing query ambiguity and credit assignment.

- **Foresight Trajectory Generation**: Prior to PbRL training, LLMs generate diverse, semantically meaningful, and task-aligned "bootstrapped" trajectories to warm-start the trajectory buffer. This process follows a three-step CoT strategy: i) generate a high-level action plan from the task specification, ii) translate each step into executable code snippets (motion primitives), and iii) execute these programs under varied initial conditions and strategy parameters to synthesize diverse trajectories. These are treated as informative comparison anchors rather than strictly optimal trajectories, reducing early-stage query ambiguity when paired with exploratory samples.

- **Hindsight Trajectory Augmentation with Causal Auxiliary Loss**: During PbRL training, when a clear preference is identified by the multimodal feedback module, LLMs perform hindsight reasoning to generate counterfactual variants of the preferred trajectory. This process is based on a structural causal model and involves three steps:
 - **Abduction**: The LLM identifies a set of critical causal steps within the preferred trajectory that contributed to its superior preference. Keyframe indices serve as potential reference candidates.
 - **Action**: The LLM selects a key causal step for minimal intervention, generating a counterfactual trajectory that reverses the original preference. This involves modifying specific state-action features at the chosen step (e.g., gripper delay, end-effector perturbation), while the rest of the trajectory remains identical, with light smoothing to ensure physical continuity. Counterfactuals are filtered based on a minimal L1 distance deviation threshold from the original.
 - **Prediction**: Each generated counterfactual variant is paired with the original preferred trajectory and fed into the LLM-based intra-modal fusion module to verify that the counterfactual is indeed sub-preferred (i.e., original ≻ counterfactual).
 To exploit these counterfactuals for credit assignment, a **causal auxiliary loss** (`L_aux_cf`) is introduced. This loss encourages reward separation at the minimally edited steps while enforcing reward consistency in the unedited parts of the trajectory. This guides the reward model to precisely attribute preference differences to the causal changes. The final reward learning objective combines this auxiliary loss with the standard trajectory-level preference loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PRIMT was conducted across eight robotics tasks: two locomotion tasks (Hopper Stand, Walker Walk) from the DeepMind Control (DMC) suite and six manipulation tasks (Button Press, Door Open, Sweep Into from MetaWorld; PickSingleYCB, StackCube, PegInsertionSide from ManiSkill). PRIMT's performance was compared against several foundation model-based baselines (RL-VLM-F, RL-SaLLM-F, PrefCLM, PrefMul) and an oracle baseline (PrefGT) using expert-designed rewards. Ablation studies were performed to assess the contribution of individual components.

**Overall Performance and Convergence:**
- **Superior Performance:** PRIMT consistently outperformed all foundation model-based baselines that rely on single-modality feedback (RL-VLM-F, RL-SaLLM-F, PrefCLM) across all evaluated tasks. It demonstrated both superior final task performance and faster convergence during training.
- **Ineffectiveness of Naive Multimodality:** The PrefMul baseline, which directly fed multimodal trajectory inputs to a multimodal FM without a structured fusion strategy, performed poorly. This result indicated that naive integration of multimodal signals can overwhelm FMs and degrade performance, thereby validating the necessity for PRIMT's hierarchical neuro-symbolic fusion design.
- **Competitiveness with Oracle:** PRIMT achieved performance competitive with, and in some cases surpassed, the oracle PrefGT baseline (e.g., on Sweep Into and Peg Insertion Side tasks). This suggests that PRIMT effectively bridges the gap between synthetic and ground-truth feedback quality while addressing intrinsic PbRL challenges.
- **Robustness to FM Backbone:** An ablation study showed that PRIMT maintained reasonable performance even when using a weaker foundation model backbone (gpt-4o-mini), indicating robustness and a substantial improvement in cost-performance efficiency (13x improvement for 16-26% performance drop).

**Impact of Multimodal Feedback Fusion:**
- **Improved Label Quality:** PRIMT produced more accurate synthetic preference labels and significantly reduced the rate of indecisive judgments compared to baselines and ablations.
- **Critical Role of Hierarchical Fusion:** Ablation studies demonstrated that removing either the intra-modal (w/o.Intra) or inter-modal (w/o.Inter) fusion components led to a significant degradation in task performance. The impact of removing inter-modal fusion was particularly severe, underscoring the importance of the Probabilistic Soft Logic (PSL)-based reasoning in robustly integrating heterogeneous preference beliefs.

**Impact of Bidirectional Trajectory Synthesis:**
- **Foresight for Early-Stage Learning:** The foresight trajectory generation module contributed to accelerating early-stage learning, as evidenced by the slower learning curve of the `w/o.ForeGen` ablation, even if it reached a similar final performance. This confirmed its role in mitigating query ambiguity by providing informative preference anchors.
- **Hindsight for Final Performance and Credit Assignment:** Both hindsight trajectory augmentation (`w/o.HindAug`) and the causal auxiliary loss (`w/o.CauAux`) were critical for achieving high final performance. The notable performance drop in `w/o.CauAux` indicated that the causal auxiliary loss effectively leveraged counterfactuals to capture state-action-level preference causation, enhancing credit assignment.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations demonstrated that foresight generation produced diverse, semantically meaningful, and task-aligned trajectories that were superior to early-stage random explorations. Hindsight augmentation generated counterfactual trajectories through minimal interventions at causally critical steps, thereby sharpening the causal signal for the reward model.

**Credit Assignment and Reward Alignment:**
- PRIMT's learned reward models exhibited patterns that closely reflected ground-truth task progress at the state-action level. In contrast, baselines and ablations showed noisy signals or high variance, indicating difficulties in accurate state-action-level credit assignment. Quantitative analysis using R² coefficients confirmed PRIMT's stronger alignment with ground-truth rewards.

**Real-World Deployment:**
- PRIMT was successfully deployed on a Kinova Jaco robot for real-world block lifting and stacking tasks. It outperformed the PrefCLM baseline, achieving 7/10 successful lifts (vs. 4/10 for PrefCLM) and 6/10 successful stacks (vs. 2/10 for PrefCLM), demonstrating its practical applicability.

**Comparison with Dense-Reward RL:**
- When compared to an RL policy trained with ground-truth dense rewards (GT), PRIMT achieved between 1.9% and 17.3% of the performance gap relative to GT. This suggests PRIMT effectively approximates the behavior of dense-reward RL without explicit reward shaping, which is valuable in scenarios where dense reward engineering is impractical.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PRIMT framework represents a significant advancement in Preference-based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) by leveraging Foundation Models (FMs) in a novel and active manner. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows.

### 6. Reduced Human Effort and Enhanced Scalability in PbRL

PRIMT substantially reduces the reliance on extensive human input for preference labeling. By employing FMs as synthetic teachers for multimodal evaluation, the framework mitigates the primary bottleneck of human annotation, which traditionally limits PbRL scalability. This makes PbRL more practical for complex and diverse robotic tasks where human expert feedback is expensive or difficult to obtain. The paper estimates over a 92% reduction in human annotation costs compared to expert-sourced ground truth preferences while maintaining comparable performance.

### 7. Improved Quality and Reliability of Synthetic Feedback

The introduction of a hierarchical neuro-symbolic preference fusion strategy is a key contribution. By integrating the complementary strengths of LLMs (for temporal reasoning and procedural logic from text) and VLMs (for spatial awareness from visuals), PRIMT overcomes the limitations of single-modality FM evaluations. This fusion mechanism, particularly through Probabilistic Soft Logic (PSL)-based inter-modal fusion, provides more reliable, comprehensive, and context-aware synthetic feedback, leading to more robust reward learning.

### 8. Active Mitigation of PbRL's Intrinsic Challenges

PRIMT actively addresses two fundamental challenges in PbRL: query ambiguity and credit assignment.
- **Query Ambiguity:** Foresight trajectory generation leverages LLMs to produce diverse, semantically meaningful trajectories for warm-starting the replay buffer. These bootstrapped samples serve as informative anchors, reducing ambiguity in early-stage preference queries and accelerating reward model learning.
- **Credit Assignment:** Hindsight trajectory augmentation, coupled with a causal auxiliary loss, enables FMs to perform counterfactual reasoning. By minimally editing critical causal steps in preferred trajectories to reverse preferences, PRIMT generates contrastive examples that precisely attribute preference differences to specific state-action events. This improves the granularity and accuracy of the learned reward model, enhancing its generalization and alignment.

### 9. Superior Performance and Robustness

The empirical results demonstrate that PRIMT consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art FM-based PbRL baselines across a wide range of locomotion and manipulation tasks. It achieves superior final task performance and faster convergence. Furthermore, PRIMT exhibits robustness even when utilizing less powerful foundation model backbones, suggesting adaptability to scenarios with computational or cost constraints. Its performance is competitive with, and occasionally surpasses, an oracle using ground-truth rewards, highlighting its effectiveness.

### 10. Real-World Applicability

The successful deployment of PRIMT on a physical Kinova Jaco robot for block lifting and stacking tasks demonstrates the framework's practical viability and potential for real-world robotic applications. This indicates that PRIMT can translate learned policies from simulation to physical systems, offering a promising path towards more autonomous and capable robots in unstructured environments.

### 11. Future Research and Broader Implications

The underlying principles of multimodal feedback fusion and causal trajectory-level reasoning developed in PRIMT are not limited to single-agent robotics. These concepts could potentially be extended to non-robotic domains and multi-agent settings, influencing research in areas such as interactive AI agents, automated task planning, and human-AI collaboration.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations, primarily the increased computational cost associated with making multiple foundation model calls. However, it argues for a strong cost-performance trade-off and highlights significant cost reductions compared to human annotation. The reliance on FMs also inherits potential concerns regarding data privacy, transparency, and fairness, as FMs may carry biases from their training data. The authors advocate for future work in bias mitigation, model auditing, and responsible deployment strategies, particularly for high-stakes or safety-critical applications.
