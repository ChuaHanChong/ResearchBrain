# ContactIPM: A Structure-Exploiting Interior-Point Solver for Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> ContactIPM is a primal-dual interior-point solver for contact-implicit trajectory optimization. It marks complementarity pairs, lifts them via a barrier-coupled elastic relaxation tied to the barrier parameter, eliminates slacks and duals stage-locally, and solves the reduced Newton system by Riccati recursion, with fixed multi-phase recovery. It runs 2.17-8.87x faster than CRISP, passes 150/150 IMPACT robustness cases, and succeeds in all 50 closed-loop Push Box rollouts.

## Problem

- Complementarity constraints violate standard constraint qualifications, making multipliers nonunique and the KKT system singular, so generic primal-dual Newton solvers become unreliable.
- Contact-specific solvers gain robustness by abandoning coupled primal-dual iterates: one is primal-only with convex subproblems, another splits updates into block-coordinate augmented-Lagrangian steps.
- Structure-exploiting optimal-control solvers use Riccati or condensing factorizations but lack complementarity-pair recognition, relaxation, and physical-residual acceptance gates for contact.

## Method

- **Barrier-coupled elastic interior relaxation**: each marked pair gets side slacks plus a product slack with target proportional to the barrier parameter, giving an identity slack Jacobian, ==LICQ==, and no separate penalty weight.
- **Stage-local slack and dual elimination** folds complementarity into modified stage Hessian and gradient terms only, preserving block-banded structure so a backward ==Riccati recursion== solves the coupled Newton system in linear horizon time.
- **Multi-phase MPCC recovery** with continuation from intermediate barrier values then ==Gauss-Newton restart==, plus diagonal trajectory scaling, ==filter line search==, and a termination gate on the unrelaxed physical complementarity residual.

## Results

- Against CRISP on **four** fixed instances over **20** paired repetitions, speedups were **2.168x** to **8.868x**, and robustness improved to **24/25** Push Box and **50/50** Push-T versus **19/25** and **27/50**.
- Against IMPACT, it was **2.96x** faster on Push T and **4.91x** on Cart Transport but **4.46x** slower on Push Box, passing **150/150** suite cases versus **147/150**.
- Closed-loop Push Box: all **50** disturbed rollouts succeeded across **1,104** solves, median **2.08ms**, **99.37%** under the **100ms** deadline, worst final errors **11.9mm** and **2.15mrad**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complementarity is stage-local under multiple shooting, so relaxing it need not destroy the banded optimal-control structure that Riccati recursions exploit.
> - Tying relaxation tightening to the barrier parameter removes the separately escalated penalty weight that augmented-Lagrangian and penalty methods must balance against the objective.
> - Solver convergence and physical feasibility are insufficient success criteria: an exact-complementarity baseline reported feasible zero-contact trajectories that never moved the object.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotics research frequently addresses scenarios involving contact, such as pushing, sliding, grasping, or locomotion. To plan motions in such scenarios, robots must integrate continuous motion planning with discrete contact events. Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO) is a methodology developed to address this by optimizing states, inputs, contact forces, and contact modes concurrently, thereby avoiding the need to pre-specify a contact sequence.

However, CITO problems are numerically challenging because they typically yield Mathematical Programs with Complementarity Constraints (MPCCs). MPCCs are known to violate standard constraint qualifications, which renders conventional nonlinear programming (NLP) solvers unreliable. This degeneracy often results in non-unique multipliers and ill-conditioned or singular Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) systems, particularly problematic for primal-dual methods.

Existing approaches in this domain exhibit a "structure-robustness gap":
- **Contact-specific MPCC solvers** like CRISP and IMPACT prioritize robustness to MPCC degeneracy. CRISP operates by solving a sequence of convex primal subproblems, foregoing a single primal-dual iterate for the original MPCC. IMPACT employs an augmented-Lagrangian outer loop with an inner block-coordinate descent scheme, updating trajectory and complementarity blocks separately rather than through a fully coupled KKT system. While effective for robustness, these methods do not fully leverage the primal-dual consistency and stagewise optimal control structure amenable to Riccati-type recursions.
- **Structure-exploiting optimal control (OCP) solvers** like acados, Hippo, and FilterDDP are designed for efficiency on problems with temporal structure, often using Riccati or block-factorization techniques. However, these solvers are not inherently designed to handle the specific degeneracy issues of MPCCs. For instance, acados, when imposing exact complementarity without special treatment, has been observed to fail in generating desired contact behaviors.

This work addresses this gap by developing a solver, ContactIPM, that aims to integrate the robustness required for MPCCs with the efficiency of structure-exploiting primal-dual optimal control solvers, demonstrating that these capabilities can coexist.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for ContactIPM stems from the observation that the numerical challenges of contact complementarity do not inherently preclude the exploitation of primal-dual and optimal control structure. Specifically, in a multiple-shooting transcription, each complementarity pair, along with its relaxation, slack variables, multipliers, and derivative contributions, is localized to a single stage. Consequently, eliminating these local slack and dual variables should only affect the corresponding stage's gradient and Hessian, preserving the dynamics as the sole coupling between adjacent stages. This structure is crucial for efficient solution methods like Riccati recursions.

Based on this insight, the paper outlines several key objectives:
1. **Unified Stagewise Formulation**: To develop a comprehensive mathematical framework that seamlessly integrates nonlinear dynamics, costs, generic inequalities, bounds, and specifically "marked" complementarity pairs. A central aspect of this objective is the conversion of these marked pairs into "barrier-coupled elastic product rows."
2. **MPCC-Aware Primal-Dual Newton Method**: To design a solver that computes directions from a single, fully coupled, perturbed KKT system, thereby maintaining primal-dual consistency. This method must also preserve the optimal control structure through local slack and dual variable elimination, enabling the use of a Riccati recursion. Additional components include trajectory-scale preconditioning and a multi-phase MPCC recovery strategy to enhance robustness.
3. **Reproducible Experimental Evaluation**: To thoroughly validate ContactIPM through empirical comparisons against established contact-specific MPCC solvers (CRISP and IMPACT) using matched benchmark conditions. This also includes evaluating ContactIPM against an OCP-structured solver (acados) that enforces exact complementarity, and demonstrating its performance in a closed-loop control setting under various disturbances.

The overarching objective is to demonstrate, through both theoretical formulation and experimental results, that the robustness required for MPCCs, primal-dual consistency, and efficient optimal control structure exploitation can be successfully combined within a single practical solver.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ContactIPM is designed as a primal-dual interior-point solver specifically tailored for contact-implicit trajectory optimization problems. The methodology focuses on three main components: an MPCC-specific elastic interior relaxation, a structure-exploiting primal-dual Newton system, and robust globalization and recovery strategies.

#### 3.1. A. MPCC and Elastic Interior Relaxation

The solver takes a multiple-shooting problem formulation, including stage costs, nonlinear dynamics, general inequalities, and complementarity constraints expressed as $0 \le a_{k,p}(x_k, u_k) \perp b_{k,p}(x_k, u_k) \ge 0$. For each identified complementarity pair $(g_a, g_b)$, where $a = -g_a \ge 0$ and $b = -g_b \ge 0$, ContactIPM introduces:
- Positive side slacks: $s_a > 0$ and $s_b > 0$, yielding $g_a + s_a = 0$ and $g_b + s_b = 0$.
- A positive product slack: $s_c > 0$, yielding $g_a g_b - \theta(\mu) + s_c = 0$.
The term $\theta(\mu) = \gamma_{mpcc}\mu$ couples the complementarity tightening directly to the barrier parameter $\mu$, which is also used in the logarithmic barrier objective (e.g., $-\mu (\log s_a + \log s_b + \log s_c)$). This design choice means that complementarity tightening is controlled by the same parameter defining the central path, avoiding the need for an independently tuned and escalated penalty weight.

Crucially, this "elastic lift" construction ensures regularity:
- For any fixed $\mu > 0$, the Jacobian of the relaxed complementarity residuals with respect to the local slack variables $(s_a, s_b, s_c)$ forms an identity matrix. This guarantees that the lifted complementarity rows, along with other slack-lifted inequalities and dynamics defects, satisfy the Linear Independence Constraint Qualification (LICQ) at every interior point. Consequently, the multipliers associated with these constraints are uniquely defined, and the KKT system is regular, circumventing the degeneracy issues of MPCCs.
- Solution acceptance is gated by an "unrelaxed physical complementarity residual" $r_{phys} = \max_{k,p} |a_{k,p}b_{k,p}| \le \epsilon_{mpcc}$, ensuring the final trajectory satisfies the original physical condition, independent of the interior relaxation.

#### 3.2. B. Primal–Dual Newton System

ContactIPM maintains primal-dual consistency by deriving Newton steps from the linearization of a single perturbed KKT system that includes optimality conditions, dynamics, constraints, and slack-multiplier centering conditions ($S_k\lambda_k - \mu \mathbf{1} = 0$). This ensures that all components (primal trajectory, dynamics multipliers, inequality multipliers, slacks) are updated coherently.

A key aspect of the method is its exploitation of optimal control structure:
- Since complementarity pairs and their elastic variables are localized to individual stages, their elimination from the linearized KKT equations only modifies the local stage Hessian and gradient terms ($\tilde{H}_k = H_k + C_k^\top \text{diag}(\lambda_k/s_k) C_k$ and $\tilde{q}_k = q_k + C_k^\top w_k$).
- This local modification preserves the block-banded structure of the equality-constrained OCP, which is then efficiently solved using a backward Riccati recursion followed by a forward substitution. This results in an algorithm with linear complexity in the horizon length $N$.
- The solver uses exact Lagrangian curvature when the stage factorizations are positive definite; otherwise, it defaults to Gauss-Newton curvature.

#### 3.3. C. Scaling and Globalization

To handle varied numerical scales in contact-rich problems, ContactIPM implements a horizon-consistent diagonal coordinate scaling based on stage derivative magnitudes. This scaling is applied consistently across dynamics, costs, constraints, and Riccati computations, with the accepted Newton step mapped back to physical coordinates. Globalization is managed using a filter line search, balancing objective reduction with constraint violation. A fraction-to-boundary rule ensures slacks and multipliers remain positive. The barrier parameter $\mu$ is reduced only after the current subproblem has achieved the required accuracy. A nonlinear dynamics rollout is also evaluated as a candidate step to avoid rejecting otherwise useful steps.

#### 3.4. D. Multi-Phase MPCC Recovery

To enhance robustness when the relaxed central path becomes challenging (e.g., unfavorable basins, conditioning loss), ContactIPM employs a multi-phase recovery schedule. This schedule combines continuation (retaining the current basin) with restart (searching a different basin) through a fixed sequence of up to five solves:
1. An initial solve with user-specified barrier and curvature settings.
2. If unsuccessful, continuation phases are attempted by solving from progressively tighter barrier parameters ($\mu_m = \sqrt{\mu_0 \mu_t}$ and $\mu_t = \min(\mu_0, \mu_{conv})$), retaining the latest primal trajectory.
3. If continuation fails, the original primal guess is restored, and the problem is restarted at a larger barrier parameter ($\mu_r = \max(\mu_{recovery}, \mu_{conv})$) using Gauss-Newton curvature, providing a larger interior margin.
4. A final Gauss-Newton solve at $\mu_t$ is performed.
Each phase requires the complete termination conditions, including the unrelaxed physical residual $r_{phys} \le \epsilon_{mpcc}$, for success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ContactIPM involved extensive comparisons against two contact-specific MPCC solvers (CRISP and IMPACT) and an OCP-structured solver (acados), as well as closed-loop validation.

#### 4.1. A. Matched CRISP Comparison

- **Timing**: On four fixed CRISP benchmark cases (Cartpole, Push Box, Transport, Push T), ContactIPM was consistently faster. Median speedups ranged from 2.168x on Push Box to 8.868x on Cartpole, with all 80 paired timing repetitions passing the common post-solve evaluation.
- **Robustness**: Across multi-instance suites, ContactIPM matched CRISP on Cartpole (15/15) and Transport (8/15) and achieved higher success on Push Box (24/25 vs. 19/25) and Push T (50/50 vs. 27/50). Overall, ContactIPM passed 47/55 cases against 42/55 for CRISP on these suites.
- **Solution Quality**: For jointly successful cases, solutions were comparable. ContactIPM exhibited a slightly higher objective for Push Box (more force, smaller terminal errors) and a lower objective for Push T (lower tracking/effort, smaller translation error). ContactIPM terminated near its $10^{-6}$ physical complementarity tolerance, while CRISP often drove it closer to zero ($10^{-13}$ to $10^{-15}$).

#### 4.2. B. Matched IMPACT Comparison

- **Timing**: For fixed IMPACT benchmark cases, ContactIPM was faster on Push T (2.96x speedup) and Cart Transport (4.91x speedup). However, IMPACT was substantially faster on Push Box (ContactIPM was 4.46x slower).
- **Robustness**: On three 50-case suites, ContactIPM passed all 150 instances. IMPACT passed 49/50 Push Box, 48/50 Push T, and 50/50 Cart Transport cases, for a total of 147/150 successes.
- **Solution Quality**: Varied by benchmark. On Push Box, ContactIPM achieved a slightly lower objective. On Push T, IMPACT yielded a lower objective and less force effort. On Cart Transport, ContactIPM achieved a lower objective, used less force, and reached smaller terminal errors. ContactIPM consistently produced smaller median dynamics defects and physical complementarity residuals compared to IMPACT.

#### 4.3. C. OCP Structure without Elastic Complementarity (acados baseline)

- An acados baseline, which employs full SQP with partial condensing and HPIPM (exploiting OCP structure) but directly enforces exact complementarity ($a \ge 0, b \ge 0, ab \le 0$), was evaluated.
- On 75 Push Box cases, acados passed the physical feasibility check in all instances and reported convergence in 62, but *none* reached the terminal goal. It frequently converged to zero-contact trajectories where the box did not move.
- Similar trends were observed across other contact-rich benchmarks (CRISP Transport, CRISP Push T, IMPACT Push T, IMPACT Cart Transport), where acados produced only one accepted trajectory out of 165 cases, while ContactIPM succeeded in 158.
- This demonstrates that exploiting OCP structure alone is insufficient for robust MPCC performance from naive initializations when exact complementarity is imposed, highlighting the need for specific MPCC treatment (like ContactIPM's elastic lift) to navigate degenerate feasible sets and initiate contact.

#### 4.4. D. Closed-Loop Validation

- ContactIPM was evaluated in 50 closed-loop Push Box rollouts using a quasi-static model under various disturbances (initial pose errors, mass/friction mismatch, isolated state resets, and combined disturbances). The controller performed 19-stage OCP solves every 0.1 seconds using shifted warm starts.
- All 50 rollouts succeeded, demonstrating robustness to model mismatch and state disturbances.
- Median completion times ranged from 19 steps (nominal motion) to 27 steps (combined disturbance). Final pose accuracy remained high, with worst-case errors for combined disturbance being 11.9 mm and 2.15 mrad.
- Solver latency was low: the median solve time across all 1,104 MPC solves was 2.08 ms. 99.37% of all solves and 99.81% of shifted-warm-start solves met a 100 ms control deadline.
- The planned and applied physical complementarity products remained consistently low (max $1.01 \times 10^{-6}$ and $1.00 \times 10^{-6}$, respectively), indicating that closed-loop feedback maintained both primal feasibility and physical complementarity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ContactIPM makes a significant contribution by demonstrating the viability of primal-dual interior point methods for contact-implicit trajectory optimization, an area where they have traditionally faced challenges due to MPCC degeneracy. The solver successfully integrates several key features: explicit complementarity recognition, an elastic interior relaxation mechanism, stagewise primal-dual elimination, and the efficient Riccati recursion for OCP structure exploitation, complemented by robust scaling and multi-phase recovery strategies.

The experimental results indicate that ContactIPM offers competitive or superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art contact-specific solvers across various benchmarks, both in terms of computational speed and solution robustness. Crucially, it addresses the previously identified "structure-robustness gap" by showing that MPCC reliability, primal-dual consistency, and efficient OCP-structured computation can be realized concurrently within a single solver framework.

The closed-loop validation further underscores ContactIPM's practical utility, exhibiting high success rates and low solve times under diverse disturbance conditions. This suggests its potential for deployment in real-time robotic control applications that necessitate robust and efficient handling of contact interactions. The research provides a principled foundation for future development of solvers for problems involving complex contact phenomena and other mathematical programs with complementarity constraints.
