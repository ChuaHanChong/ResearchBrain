# WireCraft: A Simulation Benchmark for Industrial DLO Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> WireCraft introduces a comprehensive simulation benchmark for industrial deformable linear object (DLO) manipulation, featuring configurable tasks, dual DLO physics models, and multi-source trajectory data. The platform evaluates learning policies, revealing challenges in vision-based contact-rich manipulation and sim-to-real transfer for tasks like connector insertion and cable routing.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) is challenging due to their complex deformation and contact dynamics, hindering robust policy learning in industrial assembly.
- Existing robot learning benchmarks for DLOs are often generic or hardware-specific, failing to address industrial context, structured fixtures, or provide reusable data and standardized evaluation.
- A unified simulation environment integrating industrially relevant tasks, diverse physics models, and multi-source data for DLO manipulation was missing.

## Method

- Developed **WireCraft**, a unified simulation benchmark on Isaac Lab, offering GPU-accelerated training for industrial DLO manipulation tasks.
- Integrated two DLO physics models – an articulated model for high-throughput and a ==FEM-based deformable model== for fidelity – alongside configurable industrial assets and tasks like connector insertion and clip routing.
- Established a multi-source data collection framework and a shared evaluation protocol to benchmark various learning policies (RL, IL, VLA) with consistent metrics.

## Results

- Privileged state-based RL policies (PPO, SACfD) achieved over **92%** insertion success for connector tasks, demonstrating the simulation's task fidelity.
- Vision-based policies exhibited a significant drop-off from reaching to insertion success (e.g., ACT with ~**70%** reach but ~10-**20%** insert), highlighting challenges in contact-rich manipulation without privileged information.
- Real-world validation of policies trained only in simulation resulted in zero-shot transfer failures, while policies trained with mixed real-world and simulated data showed improved but still limited real-world task completion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The articulated DLO model demonstrates superior computational scalability for data generation, while the deformable model provides higher fidelity for contact-sensitive scenarios.
> - Vision-based policies face a substantial "reach-insert gap" in connector insertion, struggling with contact-rich final alignment and visual state estimation under occlusion.
> - Sim-only policies fail zero-shot transfer to real robots, and even mixed real-world data policies show challenges with contact uncertainty, underscoring a persistent sim-to-real gap in DLO manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs), such as wires and cables, is a critical component in numerous industrial assembly processes, including wire-harness manufacturing and cable management. Unlike rigid objects, DLOs present significant challenges due to their infinite-dimensional configuration space and continuous deformation under contact with grippers, fixtures, and the workspace. This complexity results in intricate contact dynamics, self-occlusion, and uncertainty stemming from material properties like stiffness and damping, making robust policy learning difficult.

Within the broader robotics research landscape, existing benchmarks for robot learning often do not adequately address DLO manipulation. General benchmarks like RLBench and ManiSkill3 provide task suites for rigid objects but offer limited DLO-specific data. Deformable-object benchmarks such as SoftGym, PlasticineLab, DaXBench, and MoDeSuite include DLOs alongside other deformable categories but frequently lack the structured industrial fixtures (e.g., connectors, clips, channels) relevant to real-world assembly tasks. A more focused line of work, exemplified by DLO-Lab and ReForm, targets canonical DLO behaviors such as shape control and coiling, yet these also do not prioritize industrial assembly contexts.

Parallel efforts have focused on specific industrial DLO tasks using physical testbeds. These studies, while contributing to the understanding of clip routing, connector insertion, and channel seating, are typically tied to particular robot platforms, fixture designs, cable geometries, and sensing setups. This specificity limits the reusability of learned policies and collected data for broader research on sim-to-real transfer or generalizable DLO manipulation. Furthermore, physical task boards like those from NIST and ManipulationNet offer standardized real-world evaluation but do not include accompanying simulation environments.

The current work, WireCraft, addresses a significant gap by proposing a unified simulation benchmark that specifically targets *industrial* DLO manipulation. It aims to integrate industrially grounded tasks with a simulation environment that supports diverse DLO physics models, configurable assets, multi-source trajectory data (both simulated and real-world), and shared evaluation protocols. By doing so, WireCraft seeks to provide a standardized platform that can accelerate the development, comparison, and validation of advanced learning-based policies for complex DLO manipulation tasks in an industrial context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind WireCraft is to bridge the existing gap in robotics research by providing a standardized, comprehensive benchmark for industrial manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs). The pervasive presence of wires and cables in industrial assembly necessitates robotic solutions, but the inherent complexities of DLOs—such as their infinite-dimensional configuration space, continuous deformation under contact, and challenging contact dynamics—make policy development and comparison difficult. Current benchmarks are often insufficient, either focusing on generic deformable objects without industrial context, or being tied to specific hardware setups, which limits reusability and generalizability. There is a notable absence of benchmarks that align simulation environments, real-world data, and shared evaluation protocols for industrial DLO tasks.

To address these limitations, the authors establish the following key objectives:

1. **Introduce a Unified Simulation Benchmark:** The main objective is to establish WireCraft as a comprehensive simulation benchmark for industrial DLO manipulation. This benchmark is designed to cover three distinct task families: connector insertion, clip routing, and channel seating. It aims to feature configurable task assets and difficulty levels, standardized policy interfaces, and support for two complementary DLO physics models—an articulated model for high-throughput and a deformable (FEM-based) model for higher fidelity.
2. **Evaluate Representative Learning Policies:** A core objective is to evaluate and benchmark various policy learning paradigms, including reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning (IL), and vision-language-action (VLA) policies. These evaluations are to be conducted under a shared evaluation protocol and common metrics, providing a consistent baseline and reference point for future research in industrial DLO learning.
3. **Facilitate Open-Source Research and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The final objective is to publicly release the WireCraft benchmark, including its simulation environment, extensive trajectory data, data-generation tools, and 3D-printable task-board components. This release is intended to foster further research by enabling the collection of new demonstrations and facilitating studies on sim-to-real transfer for industrial DLO manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WireCraft is developed as a simulation benchmark designed to evaluate robotic manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) in industrial contexts. The methodology encompasses a robust simulation engine, configurable assets, a suite of industrial tasks, and a multi-source data collection framework.

**Simulation Engine:**
The core of WireCraft is built upon Isaac Lab v2.2.1, leveraging Isaac Sim 4.5 for photo-realistic rendering and physics simulation. This framework supports GPU-accelerated, multi-instance training, enabling scalable policy learning and trajectory generation. The physics timestep is set at 1/120 s, with a control decimation of 2, resulting in a 60 Hz control rate.

**DLO Physics Models:**
WireCraft incorporates two complementary DLO representations to balance fidelity and computational efficiency:
- **Articulated Wire:** Modeled as a chain of rigid capsule segments connected by 6-DoF PhysX D6 joints. These joints are driven by passive implicit spring-damper actuators. This model relies on rigid-body dynamics, offering high-throughput simulation suitable for extensive trajectory generation, though it can exhibit instability under self-contact or accumulated contact forces.
- **Deformable Wire:** Utilizes Isaac Sim’s deformable body API and PhysX FEM (Finite Element Method) simulation. It consists of a slender triangulated cylindrical rod (e.g., decagonal cross-section) that is voxelized into a hexahedral FEM mesh. This model provides higher fidelity in capturing local bending, compression, and contact deformation, but at a greater computational cost.
Both wire types share common connector and task component interfaces, allowing for consistent task instantiation. Contact behaviors are defined by material parameters for connectors, grippers, and the workspace.

**Sensors:**
The platform integrates standard RGB camera data (wrist and third-person views), with optional depth channels. To capture interaction, contact sensors for precise collision detection and force sensors for grasping and insertion state monitoring are included. Real-time robot proprioception (joint positions and velocities) is natively supported.

**WireCraft Assets:**
The benchmark provides a library of configurable, 3D-printable assets:
- **DLO Assets:** Articulated wires are parameterized by length, radius, colors, damping, and stiffness. Deformable wires are defined by Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, density, and mesh resolution.
- **Connector Assets:** Include both reference-based (e.g., USB-A) and geometry-based (e.g., cylinder, cuboid) connectors. These vary in rotational symmetry, allowing for a range of task difficulties from rotation-invariant to orientation-sensitive insertion.
- **Fixture Assets:** Comprise clips, channels, and female ports, which define the geometric constraints of tasks and can be configured for different layouts and difficulty levels.
- **Robot Embodiments:** Supports simulated UR5, Franka, and Trossen Stationary AI robots, each with specific grippers and control interfaces.

**WireCraft Tasks:**
The benchmark features three industrial task families with configurable difficulty:
- **Connector Insertion:** Involves grasping a rigid connector attached to a deformable wire and inserting it into a matching female port. Difficulty is modulated by connector geometry (e.g., cylindrical, cuboid, Ethernet, DisplayPort) and female-port clearance.
- **Clip Routing:** Requires routing a DLO through one or more structured U-slot clips. Difficulty scales with the number and placement of clips, and the clip opening size.
- **Channel Seating:** Focuses on laying a DLO into a geometric channel, emphasizing extended shape control. Difficulty is controlled by channel curvature (e.g., straight vs. curved channels).

**Randomization:**
Tasks incorporate per-episode randomization, including varying DLO spawn position and orientation, female port positions for insertion, clip positions and orientations for routing, and channel geometry for seating.

**Control Modes:**
Three end-effector control modes are available: joint control, Cartesian control (6-DoF delta pose), and Cartesian control with fixed orientation (3-DoF translation). This allows matching the action space to task requirements.

**WireCraft Demonstrations (Data Collection):**
The benchmark supports four data collection methods, all serialized into a common LeRobot-compatible format:
- **Scripted Policies:** Provide scalable, successful demonstrations using privileged simulator state, often incorporating perturbations to diversify outcomes. These are implemented as vectorized finite-state machines.
- **RL Rollouts:** Generate closed-loop interaction data, including states leading to failures.
- **Human Teleoperation (in simulation):** Captures human corrective strategies for contact-rich manipulation, which may involve trial-and-error. Supports SpaceMouse and Meta Quest VR input.
- **Real-World UR5 Demonstrations:** Provide physical validation data, recorded using a ROS 2 + LeRobot stack.
All data includes 7-D robot state, 7-D action, synchronized RGB camera streams, and language instructions.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
Experiments benchmark representative RL, IL, and VLA methods under shared task definitions, randomization, and success criteria. For connector insertion, two metrics are used: Reach Success (SR\_reach) and Insert Success (SR\_insert). For clip routing, success requires the wire to be seated in clips. For channel seating, success is achieved when over 80% of the wire's length lies within the channel. Privileged state-based RL baselines use ground-truth simulator state, while other policies rely on image-based observations and proprioception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using WireCraft reveal several insights regarding DLO manipulation and the performance of various learning policies.

**1. DLO Physics Model Performance:**
Computational scaling benchmarks indicated that the articulated wire model scales efficiently to 8192 parallel environments, demonstrating high aggregate throughput. In contrast, the deformable (FEM-based) wire model, while offering higher fidelity, scaled successfully up to 2048 environments but encountered limitations at 4096 environments due to PhysX deformable-volume constraints, resulting in weaker throughput scaling. This positions the articulated model as suitable for high-throughput training and bulk data generation, and the deformable model for high-fidelity evaluation and contact-sensitive rollouts.

**2. Connector Insertion Performance (Ethernet, Single Configuration):**
- **Privileged State-Based RL:** Both PPO and SACfD, utilizing privileged simulator state, achieved high insertion success rates (SR\_insert ≥ 92%). This result confirms that the Ethernet connector insertion task is well-posed and tractable when the full state information (e.g., socket and cable pose) is directly observable.
- **Vision-Based Policies (RL, IL, VLA):** A substantial "reach–insert gap" was observed for all non-privileged baselines. While vision-based policies could achieve varied success in reaching the socket neighborhood (SR\_reach), they struggled significantly with the subsequent contact-rich final alignment and insertion (SR\_insert).
 - Vision PPO exhibited a low SR\_reach (51.6%), suggesting challenges in early-stage visual state estimation from RGB-only input, particularly under occlusion and tight clearances.
 - ACT and Diffusion Policy demonstrated higher SR\_reach (around 70-75%), but their SR\_insert rates significantly dropped (around 10-20%), indicating a sensitivity to demonstration diversity during the contact phase.
 - VLA baselines (Diffusion Transformer VLA, π0.5) underperformed IL, with π0.5 achieving very low success (6.2% SR\_reach, 0.0% SR\_insert). This was attributed to a limited fine-tuning budget, a frozen vision tower, and a pre-training distribution that might lack fine-grained contact-rich DLO interaction.

**3. Diverse Connector Insertion Performance (Across Geometries):**
- **Privileged RL:** Both PPO and SACfD showed robustness to different connector geometries (Cylinder, Cuboid, Ethernet, DisplayPort). PPO maintained SR\_insert rates above 92% across all types, while SACfD achieved over 77%. Notably, the geometric difficulty did not monotonically correlate with performance, with the rotation-invariant cylinder sometimes performing similarly to or slightly below more complex connectors, possibly due to fixed reward targets penalizing multiple valid yaw configurations.
- **Vision PPO:** Performance degraded sharply across all connectors, indicating that the curriculum primarily stresses the visual observability of orientation rather than control. The Ethernet connector, with a distinct latch, sometimes yielded higher success than the smoother cuboid, suggesting that subtle visual cues can influence yaw estimation from RGB data.

**4. State PPO on Clip Routing and Channel Seating:**
Preliminary evaluations using privileged State PPO on simplified settings demonstrated tractability for the other two task families:
- **1-clip routing:** Achieved a success rate of 95.32%.
- **Straight Channel Seating:** Achieved a success rate of 82.33%.
These results confirm the well-posed nature of these tasks within the simulation environment under privileged state observation.

**5. Real-World Validation (Ethernet Insertion):**
Experiments on a physical UR5 using ACT (which had the highest SR\_reach among image-input baselines in simulation) demonstrated that real-world insertion remains significantly more challenging than simulation.
- **Sim-only policies** (trained only with scripted or RL rollout data) exhibited zero success in zero-shot transfer (0/10).
- **Mixed-source policies** that included real-world demonstrations (e.g., R+S, R+P, R+S+P) achieved non-zero reach and insertion success, with R+S showing the highest insertion count (4/10) at the evaluated checkpoint.
- Qualitative analysis indicated that failures in the real world included grab instability, perception noise, and contact uncertainty, highlighting a persistent sim-to-real gap, particularly in post-grasp control. While mixed data sources improved early-stage behaviors like reaching and grasping, consistent resolution of contact-rich insertion challenges remained an open issue.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WireCraft represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing the complexities of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) within industrial contexts. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

**1. Bridging Critical Research Gaps:** WireCraft is positioned as the first unified simulation benchmark that comprehensively integrates industrially grounded DLO tasks (connector insertion, clip routing, channel seating) with configurable assets, dual DLO physics models (articulated and FEM-based), and multi-source trajectory data, including both simulated and real-world demonstrations. This directly addresses the current fragmentation in DLO manipulation research, where existing benchmarks often lack industrial relevance, reusability, or standardized evaluation protocols across simulation and physical robots.

**2. Standardized Platform for Policy Development and Comparison:** By offering a shared evaluation protocol, consistent task definitions, and a common dataset schema, WireCraft provides a standardized framework for developing, benchmarking, and comparing a diverse range of learning-based policies, including Reinforcement Learning (RL), Imitation Learning (IL), and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) methods. This standardization is crucial for ensuring reproducible research and enabling direct comparisons of algorithmic advancements.

**3. Highlighting Key Challenges in DLO Manipulation:** The experimental results clearly delineate a significant performance gap between policies operating with privileged simulator state and those relying on vision-based inputs. The consistent "reach–insert gap" in connector insertion tasks demonstrates that precise contact-rich final alignment, visual state estimation under occlusion, and controlling continuous deformation remain major bottlenecks for current vision-based learning approaches. This benchmark effectively isolates these difficulties, providing clear targets for future algorithmic development.

**4. Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer Research:** The inclusion of real-world UR5 validation, alongside simulated environments and multi-source data, makes WireCraft an important tool for investigating the sim-to-real transfer problem in DLO manipulation. The finding that simulation-only policies struggle with zero-shot transfer, while mixed data sources improve real-world performance, underscores the existing sim-to-real gap. This provides a foundation for researchers to explore techniques for domain randomization, sim-to-real adaptation, and integrating real-world data effectively to achieve robust robotic performance.

**5. Resource for Future Research and Development:** The planned open-sourcing of WireCraft—including the simulation benchmark, trajectory data, data-generation tools, and 3D-printable task components—will make it a valuable resource for the research community. This will foster advancements in:
 - **Perception:** Developing more robust visual localization and deformation-aware state estimation techniques.
 - **Control:** Engineering policies for fine-grained, contact-rich insertion and fixture-constrained routing.
 - **Feedback Integration:** Investigating the incorporation of force/torque feedback to resolve contact-rich failure modes such as misalignment and jamming.
 - **VLA Capabilities:** Improving VLA policies to handle fine-grained DLO interaction by addressing current limitations in fine-tuning and pre-training distributions.
 - **Diagnostic Tools:** Encouraging the development of richer diagnostic protocols with failure-type labels and detailed contact histories to provide more informative comparisons.

In conclusion, WireCraft's contribution lies in establishing a foundational and comprehensive benchmark that unifies industrial DLO manipulation tasks within a flexible, multi-modal simulation and data framework. By clearly articulating the current limitations of learning-based policies and providing the necessary tools, it aims to accelerate progress towards autonomous DLO manipulation in industrial settings.
