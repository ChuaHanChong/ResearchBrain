# GRPO-CARE: Consistency-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ARC Lab, Tencent PCG, The University of Hong Kong, and The Chinese University of Hong Kong introduce GRPO-CARE, a consistency-aware reinforcement learning framework that enhances both answer correctness and reasoning coherence in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) without explicit process supervision. The method, evaluated on their new SEED-Bench-R1 benchmark, boosts reasoning consistency by 24.5% (to 82.4%) and achieves a 6.7% performance gain on challenging cross-environment and cross-task scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing outcome-supervised reinforcement learning methods for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) improve accuracy but often degrade the logical coherence between reasoning steps and final answers, leading to issues like "Thought Collapse."
- Current MLLM evaluation benchmarks are too narrow, focusing either on perception or reasoning, and lack structured assessment of generalization across diverse real-world scenarios.
- It is challenging to develop MLLMs that can genuinely integrate sophisticated perception with robust logical reasoning while maintaining interpretable Chain-of-Thought outputs.

## Method

- A new comprehensive benchmark, ==SEED-Bench-R1==, is introduced for MLLM post-training on video understanding, featuring real-world inputs, complex questions, large-scale training data, and hierarchical generalization assessment.
- ==GRPO-CARE==, a novel reinforcement learning framework, is proposed to jointly optimize for answer correctness and ==reasoning consistency== in MLLMs without requiring explicit process supervision.
- GRPO-CARE employs a ==two-tiered reward system==, combining a base correctness reward with an ==adaptive consistency bonus== derived from a slowly evolving reference model, while removing restrictive KL divergence penalties.

## Results

- GRPO-CARE significantly outperforms standard GRPO on the SEED-Bench-R1 benchmark, achieving a **6.7%** performance gain on the challenging Level-3 (cross-environment & cross-task) evaluation.
- The method dramatically improves reasoning consistency, boosting the consistency rate from **57.9%** (for standard GRPO) to **82.4%** for MLLMs.
- Ablation studies confirm that removing the standard KL divergence penalty and utilizing GRPO-CARE's sparse, adaptive consistency bonus are crucial for performance and consistency.
- Models trained with GRPO-CARE demonstrate strong transferability, showing significant performance improvements across six diverse general video understanding benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard outcome-supervised reinforcement learning, while improving MLLM accuracy, often compromises the logical coherence between the reasoning steps and the final answer, exhibiting a low consistency rate (e.g., 57.9% for GRPO).
> - Standard KL divergence penalties, typically used in RL, can excessively restrict MLLM exploration and hinder the model's ability to adaptively adjust reasoning paths, thereby limiting performance and interpretability.
> - A sparse, group-relative consistency bonus, adaptively estimated from a slowly evolving reference model, effectively improves reasoning coherence without requiring explicit step-wise supervision, proving more robust than dense or noisy consistency signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses critical challenges at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Advancements in LLMs and CoT Reasoning:** The field has seen remarkable progress in LLMs, particularly in their ability to generate complex Chain-of-Thought reasoning. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and related techniques like Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have been instrumental in refining these reasoning capabilities, allowing LLMs to self-improve based on verifiable rewards and generalize to out-of-distribution tasks.

**Emergence and Challenges of MLLMs:** MLLMs extend the power of LLMs by incorporating multimodal inputs, such as images and videos. While they inherit strong reasoning abilities, adapting RL-based post-training methods to MLLMs presents unique challenges. Existing MLLM evaluations tend to be narrow, focusing either on perception (e.g., detection) or reasoning (e.g., multimodal math problems), or they lack structured generalization assessment across diverse real-world scenarios. This limitation hinders the development of MLLMs that can genuinely integrate sophisticated perception with robust logical reasoning. Specifically, previous outcome-supervised RL methods for MLLMs have been observed to improve final answer accuracy but often compromise the logical coherence of the intermediate reasoning steps, leading to issues like "Thought Collapse" or inconsistent explanations.

**Gap in Benchmarking:** A significant gap identified by the authors is the absence of an ideal benchmark for MLLM post-training that effectively balances perception and logical reasoning while enabling rigorous evaluation of generalization. Current video understanding benchmarks are often limited by narrow tasks, scarce training data, or lack a structured approach to assessing generalization across varying levels of difficulty and domain shift.

**Addressing the Gaps:** This paper directly addresses these landscape gaps through two main contributions:
1. **SEED-Bench-R1:** It introduces a novel, comprehensive benchmark specifically designed for systematic evaluation of post-training methods on video understanding. This benchmark is unique in providing large-scale training data and a hierarchical three-level validation set (in-distribution, cross-environment, cross-environment-task), pushing models to integrate perception, commonsense planning, and long-horizon understanding in complex real-world scenarios. This fills the void for a robust evaluation framework.
2. **GRPO-CARE:** It proposes a novel RL framework that tackles the issue of reasoning inconsistency in outcome-supervised MLLM training. Unlike prior works that rely on costly process supervision or struggle with the trade-off between accuracy and interpretability, GRPO-CARE aims to jointly optimize both without explicit step-wise annotations. By introducing a consistency-aware reward, it seeks to improve the logical coherence of MLLM reasoning, thereby enhancing interpretability and overall performance.

In essence, this work pushes the frontier of MLLM capabilities by providing both a much-needed robust evaluation tool and an innovative training methodology that fosters more interpretable and reliable multimodal reasoning agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research paper is driven by two primary objectives, stemming from identified limitations in the current landscape of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and their post-training methodologies:

**Objective 1: To introduce a comprehensive and challenging benchmark for MLLM post-training methods in video understanding.**
- **Motivation:** Existing MLLM evaluation benchmarks are insufficient for rigorously assessing post-training methods, particularly those involving reinforcement learning (RL). They often focus too narrowly on either perceptual tasks (e.g., object detection) or symbolic reasoning tasks (e.g., visual math problems), failing to integrate both. Furthermore, many benchmarks lack structured generalization assessment, meaning they don't adequately test a model's ability to perform in unseen environments or on novel tasks. This makes it difficult to develop MLLMs that can robustly integrate sophisticated perception with logical reasoning in complex, real-world video scenarios. The authors argue for an "ideal benchmark" that fosters models integrating these capabilities for accurate, interpretable, and robust performance.

**Objective 2: To develop a novel reinforcement learning framework that enhances both answer correctness and reasoning coherence in MLLMs without requiring explicit process supervision.**
- **Motivation:** A pilot study conducted by the authors using a representative outcome-supervised RL method, GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization), revealed a critical limitation when applied to MLLMs: while GRPO improved final answer accuracy, it often degraded the logical coherence between the reasoning steps (Chain-of-Thought) and the final answer. The "consistency rate" was found to be a low 57.9%. This issue stems from two main problems:
 1. **Sole focus on final answer rewards:** Optimizing only for the final answer encourages "shortcut solutions" where models prioritize correctness over maintaining logical consistency in their reasoning, leading to less interpretable outputs.
 2. **Strict KL divergence penalties:** The standard KL (Kullback-Leibler) divergence penalty, used in GRPO to constrain policy updates, excessively restricts model exploration, especially for longer reasoning traces. This hinders the model's ability to adaptively adjust causal relationships between reasoning paths and answers, exacerbating logical inconsistencies.
- The motivation is thus to overcome these limitations by designing an RL framework that encourages MLLMs to generate reasoning that is not only correct but also logically consistent with the final answer, thereby improving both performance and interpretability, crucially without the need for expensive, manual step-wise process supervision annotations.

In summary, the paper aims to provide both the diagnostic tools (SEED-Bench-R1) and the therapeutic solutions (GRPO-CARE) necessary to advance the development of more capable, interpretable, and generalizable MLLMs for complex video understanding tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research paper introduces a new benchmark, SEED-Bench-R1, and a novel reinforcement learning framework, GRPO-CARE.

#### 3.1. SEED-Bench-R1: A Benchmark for Multimodal Video Understanding

**Design Philosophy:** SEED-Bench-R1 is designed for the systematic evaluation of post-training methods for MLLMs on video understanding. It aims to balance perception and logical reasoning, and rigorously assess generalization.

**Key Features:**
- **Real-world Visual Input:** Grounded in realistic egocentric videos from Epic-Kitchens and Ego4D datasets, capturing everyday human activities. This ensures the tasks are highly practical and representative of real-world challenges.
- **Complex Question Design:** Models must comprehend open-form task goals, track long-horizon visual progress, perceive real-time environment states from an egocentric view, and apply world knowledge to reason about the next action plan. Questions are multiple-choice, with ground-truth answers based on actual next actions in the video and negative options sampled from the same video, demanding deep understanding rather than just semantic matching.
- **Large-Scale Training Data:** Provides a substantial training dataset (50,269 samples) automatically constructed from Epic-Kitchens videos, suitable for robust post-training methods like RL.
- **Hierarchical Validation Set for Generalization Assessment:** A crucial aspect is its meticulously partitioned validation set, human-verified for correctness, which assesses generalization across three escalating levels of challenge:
 - **Level-1 (L1 - In-distribution):** Questions from the same video source as the training data (Epic-Kitchens), representing scenarios with visual and task overlap.
 - **Level-2 (L2 - Cross-environment):** Similar daily life tasks but using visual observations from unseen kitchen environments by new participants (Ego4D). This tests environmental generalization.
 - **Level-3 (L3 - Cross-environment & Cross-task):** Utilizes the full Ego4D dataset, including general-domain questions spanning hobbies, recreation, and work, from diverse indoor and outdoor environments. This is the most challenging level, testing both environmental and task generalization.

**Experiment Setup:** The base model used for evaluation is Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B. A pilot study compares outcome-supervised GRPO with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using a subset of SEED-Bench-R1 data. GRPO utilizes rule-based rewards for answer correctness (1 if correct, 0 otherwise), unlike SFT which can be augmented with distilled Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

#### 3.2. GRPO-CARE: Consistency-Aware Reinforcement Learning

**Core Idea:** GRPO-CARE (Consistency-Aware Reward Enhancement) is a novel RL framework designed to jointly optimize for answer correctness and logical consistency of reasoning, crucially *without* requiring explicit process supervision (i.e., step-by-step human annotations for reasoning).

**Addressing Limitations of Standard GRPO:** The approach directly tackles the two identified issues with standard outcome-supervised GRPO for MLLMs:
1. **Reward signal focused on final answers:** GRPO-CARE introduces a two-tiered reward system.
2. **Strict KL divergence penalties:** GRPO-CARE *removes* the KL divergence penalty from the GRPO objective, promoting more effective exploration.

**Two-Tiered Reward System:**
1. **Base Reward:** This is a standard rule-based reward for the correctness of the final answer (e.g., 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect). This ensures the model learns to provide accurate answers.
2. **Adaptive Consistency Bonus (Novel Contribution):** This bonus incentivizes logical coherence between the generated reasoning trace and the final answer.
 - **Reference Model (πφ):** A key component is a "slowly evolving reference model," whose parameters (φ) are updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the online model's parameters (θ). This ensures stability for likelihood estimation while still adapting to the online model's improvements.
 - **Likelihood Calibration:** For each generated reasoning trace (τ) and answer (a), the reference model (conditioned on the multimodal input `x` and reasoning `τ`) computes the likelihood of generating the answer `a`, i.e., πφ(a|x,τ). This "reasoning-to-answer likelihood" serves as a proxy for consistency. The likelihoods are clipped at a maximum threshold (γp) to prevent over-optimization.
 - **Two-Stage Filtering for Bonus Application:**
 - **Stage 1: High-Accuracy Selection:** Only trajectories (reasoning + answer pairs) generated by the online model that achieve a minimum accuracy threshold (γacc) are considered for the consistency bonus. This prevents reinforcing "consistent-but-wrong" reasoning.
 - **Stage 2: Relative Consistency Evaluation:** From the high-accuracy samples, a "group-relative consistency baseline" is calculated (mean clipped likelihood of the group minus a small margin). Trajectories whose reference likelihood exceeds this baseline receive a sparse consistency bonus. The bonus is also weighted by the sample's accuracy.
 - **Total Reward:** The base reward and the adaptive consistency bonus are summed to form the total reward (R_g). This total reward is then used to compute advantages for updating the online model.

**Model Update:** The online policy (πθ) is updated via the GRPO policy gradient, but *without* the KL divergence penalty that is standard in GRPO. This encourages broader exploration guided by the combined accuracy and consistency reward. The reference model (πφ) is updated via EMA periodically (e.g., every 10 steps), ensuring it gradually absorbs the beneficial learning from the online model.

**Summary of Approach:** GRPO-CARE's methodology combines a meticulously designed benchmark for robust evaluation with an innovative, self-supervised (no explicit process annotations) reinforcement learning scheme. By leveraging a dynamic consistency bonus derived from a reference model and removing restrictive KL penalties, it encourages MLLMs to generate more interpretable and robust reasoning for complex video understanding tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of GRPO-CARE on the newly introduced SEED-Bench-R1 and its transferability to other general video understanding benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of RL (GRPO) over SFT for MLLMs

- Initial experiments on SEED-Bench-R1 confirm that outcome-supervised GRPO significantly outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in data efficiency and boosts MLLM performance on both in-distribution (L1) and out-of-distribution (L2, L3) questions. This is achieved despite GRPO using only simple outcome-based rewards without explicit CoT annotations, highlighting RL's power for MLLMs.

#### 4.2. Limitation of Standard Outcome-Supervised GRPO for MLLMs

- A key finding is that while standard GRPO improves perceptual abilities and answer accuracy in MLLMs, it often sacrifices logical coherence between the generated reasoning (CoT) and the final answer. The consistency rate for GRPO was found to be a low **57.9%**. This means models frequently arrive at correct answers through inconsistent or illogical reasoning paths, limiting interpretability and overall performance ceiling. This is attributed to the reward signal focusing solely on final answers and strict KL penalties hindering adaptive reasoning.

#### 4.3. Superiority of GRPO-CARE on SEED-Bench-R1

- GRPO-CARE consistently and significantly outperforms standard GRPO across all three difficulty levels of SEED-Bench-R1.
 - On the most challenging **Level-3 (Cross-Task, Cross-Environment)** evaluation, GRPO-CARE achieves a substantial **6.7% performance gain** over GRPO (53.4% vs. 46.7% overall L3, with notable improvements in "Hobbies" and "Work" domains).
 - Crucially, GRPO-CARE dramatically improves the **consistency rate by 24.5%** (from 57.9% for GRPO to **82.4%** for GRPO-CARE), indicating that it successfully produces reasoning that is both accurate and logically coherent.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Confirm GRPO-CARE's Design Choices

- **KL-Oriented Baselines:**
 - Variants modifying KL penalties (KL-EMA, KL-EMA-HA, SepKL-EMA-HA) generally show some improvement over base GRPO in both accuracy and consistency, but none outperform the "NoKL" baseline (which removes the KL penalty entirely). This strongly suggests that standard KL regularization, in this context, *hinders* the MLLM's performance ceiling and its ability to explore coherent reasoning paths.
- **Reward-Based Alternatives:**
 - "DenseCons" (dense consistency rewards) improved L1 and L2 performance with better consistency but slightly underperformed on L3, possibly due to over-reliance on precise reference model calibration.
 - "RefGen" (reference model regenerating answers for consistency signal) significantly boosted consistency but introduced instability due to sampling noise, leading to overall performance degradation.
 - GRPO-CARE's approach of using **sparse, group-relative consistency rewards** based on adaptive EMA-updated reference likelihoods proved most robust, consistently delivering reliable learning signals and avoiding the pitfalls of dense rewards or noisy sampling. This validates its unique two-stage filtering mechanism.

#### 4.5. Generalization to General Video Understanding Benchmarks

- **Significant Transferability:** GRPO-CARE-trained models (CARE-7B trained on SB-R1) demonstrate strong transferability, achieving significant performance improvements over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model across six diverse, challenging general video understanding benchmarks (VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU, MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME). This validates the quality of SEED-Bench-R1's training data and the robustness of the GRPO-CARE methodology.
- **Competitiveness with Strong Baselines:** When GRPO-CARE was applied to general-domain video data (Video-R1-260k), the resulting model (CARE-7B (Video-R1)) achieved competitive or even superior performance compared to Video-R1-7B, a strong baseline that benefited from explicit temporal order grounding constraints and supplementary supervised fine-tuning. This highlights GRPO-CARE's efficiency and effectiveness even in broader contexts.
- **Implicit Visual Grounding:** The results suggest that by improving reasoning-answer consistency, GRPO-CARE implicitly encourages the model to align its responses with visual grounding results. This indicates that an implicit approach to consistency can be as effective as explicit visual perception constraints, offering a promising alternative for enhancing MLLM performance.

In summary, the main findings highlight the limitations of previous RL methods for MLLMs in maintaining reasoning coherence, while definitively demonstrating that GRPO-CARE effectively addresses this by jointly optimizing accuracy and consistency, leading to significant performance gains and improved interpretability across diverse tasks and generalization levels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions that could have a substantial impact on the development and application of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

#### 5.1. Establishment of a Rigorous MLLM Benchmark (SEED-Bench-R1)

- **Filling a Critical Gap:** SEED-Bench-R1 addresses a long-standing need for a comprehensive benchmark that balances perception and reasoning in MLLMs, particularly for video understanding. Its unique three-level hierarchical generalization evaluation (in-distribution, cross-environment, cross-task) provides a systematic framework to rigorously assess model robustness and transferability.
- **Driving Future Research:** By providing a large-scale training set and a meticulously designed validation set, SEED-Bench-R1 will serve as a vital tool for the research community, enabling more standardized and meaningful evaluation of novel MLLM architectures and post-training methods. This could accelerate progress in building more generalizable and capable MLLMs.

#### 5.2. Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for MLLMs (GRPO-CARE)

- **Solving the Consistency-Accuracy Dilemma:** GRPO-CARE offers a novel and effective solution to the critical problem of maintaining logical coherence between MLLM reasoning and final answers, a common pitfall of outcome-supervised RL. By introducing a consistency-aware reward derived from a slowly evolving reference model, it jointly optimizes for both accuracy and interpretability.
- **Reducing Annotation Burden:** A key strength of GRPO-CARE is its ability to achieve this improved consistency *without* requiring costly and time-consuming explicit process supervision (step-by-step human annotations of reasoning). This makes the approach scalable and more practical for real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Trustworthiness:** By fostering more logically consistent reasoning, GRPO-CARE contributes to the development of more interpretable MLLMs. When a model's "thought process" aligns with its final decision, it becomes more trustworthy and understandable to human users, which is crucial for deployment in sensitive or critical domains.

#### 5.3. Demonstrated Robustness and Generalizability

- The extensive evaluation on SEED-Bench-R1, showing significant performance gains and a remarkable increase in consistency rate, robustly validates GRPO-CARE's effectiveness.
- Furthermore, the strong transferability of GRPO-CARE-trained models to a wide array of general video understanding benchmarks underscores the generality and robustness of the proposed framework. This suggests that the lessons learned and improvements achieved on SEED-Bench-R1 are not confined to that specific benchmark but are broadly applicable.
- The finding that improving reasoning-answer consistency can implicitly encourage better visual grounding (comparable to explicit perception constraints) opens up new avenues for MLLM design, potentially simplifying training pipelines while achieving competitive performance.

**Potential Impact:**
The combined contributions of SEED-Bench-R1 and GRPO-CARE have the potential to:
- **Accelerate MLLM Development:** By providing better tools and methods, researchers can develop MLLMs that are not just accurate but also transparent and reliable.
- **Enable Complex Real-World Applications:** MLLMs capable of robust, consistent multimodal reasoning are essential for tasks requiring deep understanding of dynamic environments, such as autonomous systems (robotics, self-driving cars), intelligent assistants (e.g., in smart homes), video content analysis, and augmented reality.
- **Push Boundaries of AI Interpretability:** By addressing reasoning consistency, this work contributes to the broader effort of making complex AI systems more understandable and debuggable, a key step towards safer and more responsible AI deployment.

Overall, this paper represents a significant step forward in the field of multimodal AI, offering both a new standard for evaluation and a powerful technique for training more capable and coherent MLLMs.
