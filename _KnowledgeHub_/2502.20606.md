# Map Space Belief Prediction for Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping

> [!summary] Summary
> This research develops a framework for robots to build complete semantic maps in cluttered environments by intelligently combining passive observation with active manipulation actions. It introduces Calibrated Neural-Accelerated Belief Updates (CNABUs) within a POMDP framework, enabling a UR5 robot to identify 39% more previously unseen objects in real-world cluttered shelf scenes compared to non-interactive baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic mapping in cluttered, occluded environments often requires active interaction beyond passive observation to reveal all objects and complete the map.
- Existing manipulation-enhanced mapping (MEM) approaches suffer from inefficient action switching, lack of coherent belief updates after manipulation, or rely on restrictive assumptions about environment dynamics.
- Traditional Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) are computationally intractable for high-dimensional semantic map spaces, making decision-making under uncertainty challenging for such tasks.

## Method

- The problem of Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping (MEM) is formalized as a ==Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)== where the belief state is defined over a high-dimensional metric-semantic grid map.
- **Calibrated Neural-Accelerated Belief Updates (CNABUs)** are introduced, utilizing ==evidential deep learning== to efficiently update map beliefs after both observation and manipulation actions while providing calibrated uncertainty estimates.
- A 2-step receding horizon greedy planner, optimizing for ==Volumetric Information Gain==, intelligently decides between observing from new viewpoints and manipulating objects, leveraging the learned belief dynamics.

## Results

- The method significantly outperforms baselines in highly occluded simulation scenes, achieving superior occupancy and semantic mIoU by effectively integrating manipulation actions.
- The POMDP-based push selection strategy consistently yields the best mIoU compared to heuristic or random push alternatives, demonstrating the value of intelligent planning.
- Achieved zero-shot transfer to a real UR5 robot, revealing **39%** of objects previously unseen by non-interactive baselines and leading to a higher count of correctly identified objects with fewer hallucinations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating manipulation and observation actions within a unified POMDP framework, rather than relying on heuristic switching, allows for more intelligent and efficient information gathering in cluttered environments.
> - Learned map-space belief dynamics (CNABUs) with calibrated uncertainty are crucial for robust decision-making in high-dimensional, interactive environments, addressing the computational challenges of traditional POMDPs.
> - Explicit 3D map representations combined with learned belief dynamics offer interpretability, long-term consistency, and robustness to out-of-distribution object shapes, facilitating zero-shot transfer to real robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several key areas in robotics: **active sensing**, **robotic manipulation**, **semantic mapping**, and **decision-making under uncertainty (POMDPs)**.

Traditionally, active sensing in robotics has focused on **Next Best View (NBV) planning**. NBV aims to efficiently explore unknown environments or reconstruct 3D objects by strategically selecting camera viewpoints to maximize information gain, typically by reducing uncertainty about the map or object geometry. However, most NBV approaches assume a *static* environment where the robot only *passively observes*.

The increasing complexity of real-world robotic applications, particularly in household, logistics, and warehouse settings, necessitates robots that can *actively manipulate* their environment to gather information. This is where **Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping (MEM)** comes into play, a problem space this paper directly addresses. MEM is a natural evolution of active sensing, where the robot can not only change its viewpoint but also interact with objects to uncover occluded areas or reveal new information.

MEM is closely related to **mechanical search**, a subfield focused on locating and retrieving target objects from cluttered scenes by manipulating clutter. While mechanical search often deals with similar challenges of occlusion and confined spaces, many prior works in this area have relied on restrictive assumptions, such as:
- **Fixed viewpoints:** Ignoring the robot's ability to navigate and observe from different angles (e.g.,).
- **Full observability of object dynamics/poses:** Assuming perfect knowledge of how objects move or their precise initial states (e.g.,).
- **Limited, predefined object sets:** Restricting the problem to known objects with pre-learned models (e.g.,).
- **Lack of long-term environmental memory:** Rebuilding environmental knowledge from scratch with each observation, leading to inefficiencies (e.g.,).

This paper explicitly tackles these limitations by formulating MEM as a **Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)**. POMDPs are a standard formalism for decision-making under uncertainty, but applying them to unstructured, interactive, high-dimensional worlds like semantic maps is computationally challenging. Prior works, like Dengler et al. (which is a closely related antecedent from the Bonn group), attempted MEM but suffered from inefficient heuristic-based action switching and the critical flaw of not updating the environmental map after a manipulation, leading to outdated beliefs and inefficient re-exploration.

The innovation also extends to **learned world dynamics models**. While other approaches learn latent space models for control or pixel-space simulators for visual fidelity, this work advocates for and develops an approach that uses **explicit 3D maps**. This choice is crucial for *human interpretability*, *long-term temporal consistency*, and critically, *calibrated uncertainty measures* – aspects vital for information gathering tasks where confidence in the map is paramount. By leveraging neural networks for belief propagation in a metric-semantic grid map space, the authors aim to overcome the dimensionality challenge while maintaining meaningful representations. The use of "Evidential Deep Learning" for confidence calibration further distinguishes this work, addressing a common problem of overconfidence in neural network predictions which would be detrimental to an information-gathering planner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the practical challenges robots face in real-world cluttered environments, such as shelves in homes or warehouses. Searching for and identifying all objects in such settings requires more than just passive observation; it demands active interaction to reveal hidden items.

The paper identifies two significant new challenges beyond standard Next Best View (NBV) problems in the context of Manipulation-Enhanced Mapping (MEM):
1. **Reasoning about object movement:** The robot needs to anticipate how its manipulation actions (e.g., pushing) will affect the environment and specifically, previously occluded regions.
2. **Anticipating impact on observed and unobserved objects:** Manipulations can move not only the target object but also other observed or even entirely unobserved objects, propagating uncertainty.

To address these challenges and advance the state of the art, the paper sets out the following key objectives:

- **Formulate the MEM problem as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP):** This provides a formal framework for sequential decision-making under uncertainty. A novel aspect is defining the POMDP's belief state within a high-dimensional *metric-semantic grid map space*, allowing for unstructured and arbitrary numbers of objects.
- **Develop a novel framework for map-space belief updates using neural networks:** To overcome the computational intractability of traditional POMDP belief propagation in high-dimensional map spaces, the authors propose using deep learning. These networks are designed to reason efficiently and simultaneously about object geometries, locations, categories, occlusions, and the physics of manipulation.
- **Ensure calibrated uncertainty estimates:** For accurate information gain analysis, the learned belief updates must provide reliable, confidence-calibrated predictions. This is critical because overconfidence can lead to ineffective exploration or premature termination. The introduction of **Calibrated Neural-Accelerated Belief Updates (CNABUs)**, leveraging evidential deep learning, directly addresses this objective.
- **Improve map completeness and accuracy:** The ultimate goal is for the robot to efficiently identify all objects in a cluttered shelf, leading to a more complete and accurate semantic map of the environment.
- **Demonstrate generalization and zero-shot transfer:** The proposed framework should generalize to novel scenarios not seen during training and ideally transfer directly to real-world robots without extensive fine-tuning.
- **Overcome limitations of prior work:** Specifically, to improve upon approaches like Dengler et al. by enabling more efficient action switching between observation and manipulation, and crucially, by integrating belief updates *after* manipulation actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a POMDP solver operating in a metric-semantic map space, enabled by novel neural network-based belief updates.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formalized as an optimal budgeted mapping problem, where the robot has a maximum action budget ($T$) to create the most accurate representation of a dynamic workspace configuration ($c_t$). The robot's internal representation is a belief over metric-semantic maps ($\Phi_t$), which includes both occupancy (3D voxel belief, $\Phi_O$) and semantic (2D birds-eye belief, $\Phi_S$) information. The objective is to maximize the mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) between the robot's final predicted map ($\phi_T$) and the ground truth map ($\phi_{GT_T}$). The robot's actions include observation actions (moving a camera to a viewpoint $v_t$ to capture RGB-D images) and manipulation actions (interacting with the scene, e.g., pushing).

#### 3.2. Neural Map Belief Dynamics (CNABUs)

Traditional POMDP belief updates are computationally infeasible for high-dimensional map spaces due to inter-cell correlations and the vast space of possible maps. To overcome this, the authors introduce **Calibrated-Neural Accelerated Belief Updates (CNABUs)**.
- **Factored Belief Representation:** The map belief $\Phi_t$ is represented as a Bernoulli distribution for occupancy and a categorical distribution for semantic class for each cell, assuming independence between cells for compactness.
- **Evidential Posterior Networks:** To achieve calibrated uncertainty estimates, the CNABUs operate on *evidential parameters* rather than direct probabilities. For occupancy, a 3D grid of Beta distribution parameters ($\lambda_O$) is used; for semantics, a 2D grid of Dirichlet distribution parameters ($\lambda_S$) is used. Evidential deep learning models are known for improving calibration.
- **Two Types of CNABUs:**
 - **Observation CNABU ($\sigma_o$):** Updates the map belief after an observation action. It takes the current evidential belief ($\lambda_t$), the chosen viewpoint ($a_t$), and the observed RGB-D image with semantic labels ($o_t$) as input. The observation data is projected into the map space to be aligned with the belief.
 - **Manipulation CNABU ($\sigma_m$):** Updates the map belief after a manipulation action. It takes the current evidential belief ($\lambda_t$) and the manipulation action ($a_t$) as input. No immediate observation is generated during manipulation. This CNABU also includes an auxiliary output: a Beta distribution over a voxel grid representing the probability of each voxel being *changed* by the manipulation ($\lambda_{diff}$), which helps in modeling dynamics. The robot's swept volume and start/end points of the trajectory are encoded as inputs.
- **Network Architecture:** Both CNABUs utilize Res-UNet-like architectures, adapted from BEVFusion, which feed voxel heights as additional channels to 2D Res-UNets to process 3D voxel grids.

#### 3.3. POMDP Solver (Action Selection)

The POMDP is solved using a **2-step receding horizon greedy planner**, balancing computational efficiency with action quality.
- **Reward Approximation:** Volumetric Information Gain (VIG) is used as a proxy for the true mIoU reward. VIG is submodular in static scenes, which justifies the greedy approach for bounded suboptimality, and is assumed to hold for dynamic scenes in this context due to the limited lookahead.
- **Action Space:** The robot can choose from a fixed array of camera positions ($V$) for observation actions or sample from a set of feasible manipulation actions ($\Theta_t$).
- **Two-Step Lookahead:** Since manipulation actions do not provide immediate information gain, the minimum search horizon is two steps. The planner compares two action sequences:
 - **Viewpoint Planning (VPP):** Two consecutive observation actions ($(v_t^*, v_{t+1}^*)$) maximizing VIG on the current belief ($\text{IGV}_t$).
 - **Manipulation Planning:** A manipulation action followed by an observation action ($(\theta_t^*, v_{t+1}^*)$) maximizing VIG on the *predicted belief after manipulation* ($\text{IGM}_t$).
- **Action Decision Policy:** The chosen action $a_t$ is decided by comparing $\text{IGV}_t$ with $\text{IGM}_t + \gamma \text{Reg}_t$. $\text{Reg}_t = \Delta H(\Phi_t, \tilde{\Phi}^{\theta_t^*}_ {t+1})$ is a regularization term capturing the entropy difference (reduction in uncertainty) in the semantic map caused by the best push, with $\gamma$ as a discount factor. This regularizes against overly aggressive pushes that might cause unnecessary scene disturbances or introduce too much uncertainty.
- **Execution:** If a manipulation action is chosen, the belief is updated via $\sigma_m$. If an observation action is chosen, the robot obtains an observation $o_t$, and the belief is updated via $\sigma_o$. This loop continues until the budget is expended or a map completeness threshold (95% semantic voxels with >85% certainty) is met.
- **Push Sampling:** Valid push candidates are generated by sampling high-confidence frontier points from the shelf entrance, determining feasible motion plans, and sampling push directions and distances (50-150mm).

#### 3.4. Data Generation and Training

- **Simulation Environment:** All CNABU models are trained on large synthetic datasets generated in PyBullet.
- **Object Set:** 14 categories from the YCB dataset are used, placed on a (0.8x0.4x0.4)m shelf.
- **Stochastic Scene Generation:** Object configurations are sampled considering desired occupancy fraction (30-45%), class affinities, and a regularity parameter ($\rho$) to control object alignment and induce occlusions (larger objects given higher base probability, affinity for smaller objects). Ground truth maps are obtained by dense top-down views.
- **Observation Dataset ($D$):** Comprises ground truth 3D metric-semantic voxel maps and sequences of depth/semantically segmented images from various discrete viewpoints.
- **Manipulation Dataset ($D_a$):** Generated by simulating random pushes in synthesized scenes, providing ground truth maps before and after manipulation, along with pre-manipulation observations.
- **Training Procedure:**
 - **Observation CNABU ($\sigma_o$):** Trained by recursively predicting evidential beliefs from sequences of observations. The loss function is an evidential uncertainty-aware cross-entropy with a Kullback-Liebler divergence regularization term, encouraging calibrated predictions.
 - **Manipulation CNABU ($\sigma_m$):** Trained similarly, but predicts the post-manipulation belief and an additional $\lambda_{diff}$ (voxel change probability). It uses a combination of semantic, occupancy, and difference losses, plus a "consistency loss" (MSE between pre- and post-manipulation evidential parameters in unchanged areas) to regularize.
 - **Robustness:** Simulation data is augmented with noise (salt-and-pepper, Gaussian depth noise) and random transformations for improved real-world transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation in both simulation and real-world settings, demonstrating the efficacy and robustness of their proposed "Ours" method.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on 100 low-occlusion and 25 high-occlusion scenarios. Baselines included a reimplementation of Dengler et al., a purely random approach, "Random + Observation CNABU" (random views but uses our $\sigma_o$), and "Ours w/o Pushing" (our method but only uses observation actions).
- **Key Result (Fig. 5):**
 - "Ours" significantly outperforms all baselines in **highly occluded scenes** in terms of both occupancy and semantic mIoU over time. Its IoU growth is slower initially because pushing itself doesn't provide information until a subsequent observation.
 - Dengler et al. performs well early on but degrades, especially semantically. This is attributed to its inefficient heuristic action switching (pushing too early/often or observing when manipulation is better) and, crucially, its failure to update the map belief after a push, leading to a stale map and inefficient re-exploration.
 - Belief prediction via CNABUs is powerful: "Random + Observation CNABU" and "Ours w/o Pushing" achieve excellent coverage in low occlusion scenes, showing the benefit of accurate belief updates even without manipulation.
 - Pushing is critical for progress in highly occluded scenarios after visible surfaces are mapped.

#### 4.2. Push Selection Alternatives

- **Setup:** Evaluated different push selection strategies, all using the same belief prediction networks where applicable. These included "Random Push Every 5th" (with/without $\sigma_m$), "Random View, Random Push Every 5th", and "Saturation Pushing" (random push when VIG saturates).
- **Key Result (Fig. 6):**
 - The "Ours" POMDP-based push selection strategy achieves the best and most consistent mIoU across all steps, particularly in highly occluded scenes. It avoids uninformative or overly aggressive manipulation actions.
 - Using the manipulation CNABU ($\sigma_m$) for belief updates after pushes is consistently beneficial, even for random pushes. Methods that do not use $\sigma_m$ (blue and purple curves) show degraded performance.
 - Saturation pushing, while avoiding post-push performance drops, is ultimately outperformed, indicating that sophisticated planning beyond simple heuristics is necessary.

#### 4.3. Out-of-Distribution Shape Experiments

- **Setup:** Tested robustness to shape variations by randomly resizing objects (factor 0.8-1.2 on principal axes) in high-occlusion scenes.
- **Key Result (Fig. 8):**
 - Performance decreases when encountering out-of-distribution (OoD) shapes, as expected.
 - However, the long-term performance remains consistent with in-distribution (ID) experiments. This is a notable finding, suggesting that the CNABUs, grounded in observed maps, possess some inherent robustness to moderate shape distribution shifts, even without explicit shape augmentation during training. The authors suggest that adding such augmentation would further reduce this gap.

#### 4.4. Hardware Experiments

- **Setup:** 10 real-world zero-shot transfer experiments on a UR5 robot in handcrafted cluttered shelf scenes (avg. 18 objects). Compared against "Random" and "Ours w/o Pushing" (as other baselines showed comparable performance in simulation). Map quality assessed by classifying objects as Correctly Found, Misclassified But Found, Not Found, or Hallucinated.
- **Key Result (Table I & Fig. 7):**
 - Despite the significant sim-to-real gap, "Ours" retains its edge. It leads to the highest number of "Correctly Found" objects (85 vs. 72 Random, 81 Ours w/o Pushing) and "Misclassified But Found" objects (52), while greatly reducing "Not Found" (35) and "Hallucinated" (7) objects.
 - The complete pipeline reveals 39% of objects previously unseen by non-interactive baselines, validating the necessity of manipulation.
 - A domain gap exists, especially due to Realsense L515 camera noise and inaccuracies of the SAM2+CLIP semantic segmentation pipeline (e.g., poor performance on oblique/partial views, consistent hallucination of a "gelatin box"). This noise explains why the "Ours w/o Pushing" (VPP) showed less advantage over "Random" than in simulation, as the vision pipeline became a bottleneck.
 - Nevertheless, the system successfully performs zero-shot transfer and demonstrates its practical utility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step forward in the field of robotic mapping and manipulation for complex, unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Unified Framework for Active Information Gathering:** The paper provides a novel and coherent POMDP-based framework that unifies observation and manipulation actions for information gain. By treating both types of actions within a single belief-space decision-making process, the robot can intelligently choose the most informative action, whether it's looking around or physically interacting with the scene. This moves beyond heuristic switching mechanisms that have limited prior work.
- **Calibrated Uncertainty for Robust Decision-Making:** The introduction of Calibrated Neural-Accelerated Belief Updates (CNABUs) is critical. By providing well-calibrated estimates of uncertainty using evidential deep learning, the POMDP solver can make more reliable and robust decisions. Overconfidence, a common issue in learned models, is directly addressed, ensuring that the robot's perceived information gain aligns with actual performance. This is paramount for real-world deployment where false confidence can lead to failed tasks.
- **Advancement in Semantic Mapping of Cluttered Scenes:** The method demonstrates superior performance in mapping completeness and accuracy in challenging, highly occluded environments, both in simulation and real-world. This directly addresses a practical bottleneck for robots operating in dense, realistic settings like shelves, where objects frequently obscure each other.
- **Generalization and Zero-Shot Transfer:** The ability of the learned CNABUs to generalize to novel scenarios (including some out-of-distribution object shapes) and transfer in a zero-shot fashion to a real robot is a major achievement. This reduces the need for expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection and fine-tuning, accelerating robot deployment.
- **Foundation for Complex Interactive Robotics:** By providing a robust and generalizable framework for manipulation-enhanced mapping, this work lays the groundwork for more advanced interactive robotic tasks. Future applications could include:
 - **Precise Object Retrieval:** Once a complete and accurate map is built, more targeted retrieval or manipulation plans can be executed.
 - **Automated Inventory Management:** Robots could automatically map and track inventory in cluttered storage areas.
 - **Household Robotics:** Enhanced capabilities for robots to understand and organize human environments.
 - **Search and Rescue:** Robots could systematically explore and map collapsed or cluttered disaster sites to locate victims or hazardous materials.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the reliance on representative simulation data, the need for high-quality semantic segmentation, and current computational costs, the fundamental approach offers a promising direction. The explicit 3D map representation, combined with learned, calibrated belief dynamics, offers a powerful and interpretable solution for robots to effectively perceive and interact with complex real-world environments.
