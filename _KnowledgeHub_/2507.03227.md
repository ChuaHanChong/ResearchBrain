# Dexterous Teleoperation of 20-DoF ByteDexter Hand via Human Motion Retargeting

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance Seed researchers developed ByteDexter, a 20-DoF anthropomorphic robotic hand, and an optimization-based teleoperation system that accurately translates human hand motions to the robot. The integrated system achieved high-fidelity in-hand manipulation and efficiently completed a long-horizon cluttered table organization task in under five minutes.

## Problem

- Robotic systems lack human-level dexterity for versatile manipulation in unstructured, human-centric environments.
- Existing linkage-driven robotic hands often compromise on compactness, robustness, or dexterity due to design limitations and kinematic complexities.
- Teleoperation systems for high-DoF robotic hands struggle with kinematic mismatches between human and robot anatomy, increasing operator cognitive load and hindering high-quality data generation for imitation learning.

## Method

- A compact (1.3 kg), robust, 20-DoF anthropomorphic robotic hand, ByteDexter, was engineered using a linkage-driven mechanism to integrate actuators within the palm.
- A novel thumb mechanism with three actuators for four decoupled DoFs and a real-time kinematic solver (microsecond-level computation) were developed for precise hand control.
- An optimization-based motion retargeting framework translates human hand poses by minimizing keyvector discrepancies between human and robot hands, incorporating distance-dependent weights and joint limits for fidelity.

## Results

- The proposed retargeting method achieved closer fingertip distances in pinch motions and reduced unintended finger collisions compared to a DexPilot baseline, enhancing grasp stability and mitigating mechanical wear.
- The system successfully performed complex in-hand manipulation tasks, including regrasping a bottle, grasping two objects simultaneously, and executing two distinct lid-opening strategies.
- An operator completed a long-horizon task involving organizing nine cluttered cosmetic items into a multi-drawer organizer in under five minutes, demonstrating robust, precise, and efficient manipulation of diverse objects and non-prehensile actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High dexterity (20 DoF) in a compact, linkage-driven robotic hand is achievable through careful mechanical design, including a novel thumb, allowing actuator integration within the palm.
> - Optimization-based motion retargeting, focused on keyvector alignment and weighted by interaction distances, effectively bridges significant kinematic and morphological disparities between human and robot hands.
> - Dynamic adjustment of joint limits and the inclusion of regularization terms in retargeting not only ensure physical feasibility but also enhance temporal smoothness and prevent unintended collisions during teleoperation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of human-level dexterity is a prominent area within robotics research, particularly as robots are increasingly deployed in unstructured, human-centric environments to perform everyday tasks. This work addresses the multidisciplinary challenge of creating versatile and dexterous manipulation capabilities for general-purpose robots, integrating mechanical design, advanced sensing, and control.

Historically, robotic hands have adopted various transmission paradigms: motor-direct-driven, tendon-driven, and linkage-driven mechanisms. Motor-direct-driven hands simplify transmission but constrain miniaturization. Tendon-driven systems can achieve close biomimicry but often require elongated forearms for components, complicating arm integration and necessitating frequent maintenance due to cable wear. Linkage-driven mechanisms, the focus of this work, are favored for their compactness and robustness, integrating actuators within the palm. However, prior linkage-based designs have often involved compromises in dexterity, such as simplified three-finger grippers or reduced degrees of freedom (DoFs) per finger in commercial applications (e.g., Schunk SVH). While some linkage-driven hands, like that by Kim et al., advanced dexterity with modular 20-DoF designs, they faced limitations in kinematic analysis and broader applicability. The ByteDexter hand contributes to this landscape by introducing a 20-DoF linkage-driven design that aims to overcome these prior limitations, particularly through a refined thumb mechanism and a real-time kinematics solver.

A complementary challenge is the control of high-DoF robotic hands, where teleoperation offers a method to leverage human cognitive abilities and generate demonstration data for imitation learning. Existing teleoperation systems, however, encounter difficulties with kinematic mismatches between human and robotic hands, leading to increased cognitive load for operators and potential task execution errors. Vision-based methods (e.g., MediaPipe, UltraLeap) are susceptible to occlusions and lighting variations, while sensorized gloves, despite providing direct joint angle measurements, can involve calibration overhead and operator discomfort. Mapping human poses to robot commands is further complicated by anatomical differences, especially in thumb kinematics and DoF disparities, often failing in contact-rich tasks. Object-centric retargeting methods have been explored, minimizing fingertip-level discrepancies or formulating constrained optimization problems. This paper positions its motion retargeting framework as a method that bridges these kinematic differences more effectively by optimizing keyvector discrepancies, thereby enhancing teleoperation fidelity for complex human hand motions and improving data quality for subsequent imitation learning.

In summary, this research contributes to the development of anthropomorphic robotic hands and their control through teleoperation, specifically by presenting a compact, high-DoF linkage-driven hand and an optimization-based motion retargeting system designed to improve fidelity and address kinematic mismatches prevalent in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance robotic dexterity to a level comparable to human manipulation, facilitating the deployment of robots in dynamic, human-centered environments for everyday tasks. This broad goal is pursued through the development and integration of a high-DoF robotic hand and an intuitive teleoperation system.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **To develop a highly dexterous anthropomorphic robotic hand:** The motivation stems from the recognition that versatile and dexterous manipulation is crucial for robots to transition from structured industrial settings to complex real-world environments. The paper aims to achieve biomimetic dexterity through a specific mechanical design.
 - **Specific objectives for the ByteDexter hand:**
 - **Design a compact, high-DoF linkage-driven hand:** A primary goal was to create a 20-DoF anthropomorphic hand that is compact (255×118×77 mm³; 1.3 Kg) and robust, utilizing linkage-driven mechanisms to integrate actuators within the palm, addressing the miniaturization and robustness challenges faced by other transmission types.
 - **Implement a novel thumb mechanism:** Existing linkage-driven designs for the thumb often compromise mobility or compactness. A specific objective was to develop a thumb kinematic structure with three actuators driving four DoFs, enabling decoupled, human-like thumb mobility critical for diverse grasping and manipulation tasks.
 - **Develop a real-time kinematic solver:** To enable precise motor control and support real-time operation (e.g., a 100 Hz control loop), the paper aimed to create a systematic kinematic analysis for the linkage-driven transmission system, formulating and solving forward and inverse kinematics with microsecond-level computation times.
- **To create a high-fidelity teleoperation system for human motion transfer:** Teleoperation is identified as a promising pathway to leverage human intelligence for robot guidance and to generate high-quality demonstration data, which is essential for imitation learning. The motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing teleoperation systems.
 - **Specific objectives for the teleoperation system:**
 - **Bridge kinematic mismatches between human and robotic hands:** A significant challenge in teleoperation is the anatomical and kinematic disparity between human and robot hands, which can lead to cognitive burden and operational errors. The objective was to develop an optimization-based motion retargeting framework that accounts for these differences.
 - **Achieve real-time, high-fidelity reproduction of intricate human hand motions:** The system aimed to accurately translate complex human hand movements, including subtle finger coordination, to the robotic hand without introducing errors or requiring excessive operator compensation.
 - **Enable seamless hand-arm coordination:** Integrating hand and arm control into a unified, intuitive interface was a goal to reduce operator cognitive load and facilitate complex manipulation tasks that require coordinated full-arm movements.
 - **Generate high-quality demonstration data:** The fidelity and intuitiveness of the teleoperation system are intended to produce superior demonstration data, which can then be used to train more effective control policies for autonomous manipulation via imitation learning.

In essence, the research is motivated by the necessity for robots to perform dexterous tasks in human environments, addressing this by developing both advanced robotic hand hardware and sophisticated teleoperation software that efficiently transfers human dexterity to robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves the design and integration of a 20-DoF anthropomorphic robotic hand, ByteDexter, with a human-interface teleoperation system for real-time motion retargeting and hand-arm coordination.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The teleoperation system integrates human input devices with a robotic arm and hand.
- **Human Interface:** A Meta Quest 3 headset is used for tracking wrist poses, while a Manus Quantum Metaglove tracks hand motions, providing 25 landmarks at 120 Hz. A custom holder secures the Quest controller to the glove's back to synchronize wrist and finger tracking.
- **Robotic System:** A Franka Research 3 (FR3) arm is employed, with the ByteDexter hand mounted at its end-effector. The wrist–fingertip axis of the ByteDexter hand is aligned with the FR3 arm's seventh joint.

Human wrist poses from the Quest headset are directly mapped to the FR3 end-effector. Hand motions from the Manus Glove are processed through an optimization-based retargeting framework to generate joint position commands for the ByteDexter hand.

#### 3.2. Anthropomorphic Hand System (ByteDexter)

ByteDexter is a 20-DoF linkage-driven anthropomorphic hand, prioritizing compactness (255×118×77 mm³; 1.3 Kg) and robustness.
- **Finger Design:** The four long fingers adopt a parallel–serial topology, featuring two actuated, coupled DoFs at the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint, one actuated DoF at the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint, and one underactuated DoF at the distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint.
- **Thumb Mechanism:** A novel kinematic structure was developed for the thumb, providing decoupled, human-like mobility. It utilizes three actuators to drive four DoFs: abduction–adduction, MCP flexion–extension, PIP flexion–extension, and passively coupled DIP flexion–extension. This design aims to maintain full-range MCP and PIP flexion across the thumb's entire abduction span.
- **Kinematic Analysis:** A systematic kinematic analysis of the linkage-driven transmission system was performed. Both forward and inverse kinematics are formulated as sets of constrained nonlinear equations, solved using the Ceres Solver. This approach achieves microsecond-level computation times, which is sufficient for a 100 Hz control loop for all 15 DoAs (degrees of actuation).
- **Control Implementation:** A C++ API leverages multi-threading for efficient bidirectional communication between the host computer and the onboard controller. Motor position commands are computed from joint-space commands, and joint states are derived from motor feedback. A joint-position controller dynamically adjusts DoF limits at each MCP joint based on the position of the other DoF to ensure only feasible commands are translated to motor positions.

#### 3.3. Hand Motion Retargeting

The retargeting method translates human hand pose data from the Manus Glove into ByteDexter joint position references.
- **Keyvector Definition:** The approach utilizes keyvectors, which are 3D vectors between pairs of keypoints, expressed in the robot hand’s base frame. To address morphological discrepancies caused by the ByteDexter's non-anthropomorphic palm size (due to integration constraints), keyvectors are computed from each fingertip to its own MCP joint, rather than to the wrist. An iterative process identifies 15 unique keyvectors that capture a majority of grasping types (e.g., thumb-index for pinch, inter-finger distances for power grasps).
- **Optimization Problem Formulation:** The hand motion retargeting is formulated as a constrained optimization problem (Equation 1):
 ```
    min_qt Σ[i=0 to N] w(di) ||ri(qt) - f(di)v̂i,t||^2 + λ ||qt - qt-1||^2
    s.t. ql ≤ qt ≤ qu
    ```
 - `qt`: Joint position vector of the robotic hand at time `t`.
 - `ri(qt)`: The `i`-th keyvector calculated using forward kinematics on the robotic hand.
 - `vi,t`: The `i`-th keyvector on the operator’s hand.
 - `di = ||vi,t||` and `v̂i,t = vi,t / ||vi,t||`.
 - `w(di)`: A weight function that prioritizes maintaining specific distances. It assigns higher weights (200 or 400) when `di` is below a threshold `ϵ` for vectors pointing from fingers to the thumb (S1) or between fingers (S2), respectively, encouraging closing/separation. Otherwise, `w(di)` is 1.
 - `f(di)`: A distance function. When `di > ϵ`, it scales the human distance by `βi`. When `di ≤ ϵ`, it forces a closing distance `η1` (finger-to-thumb) or a separation distance `η2` (inter-finger) to avoid collisions.
 - `λ ||qt - qt-1||^2`: A regularization term for temporal smoothness.
 - `ql ≤ qt ≤ qu`: Joint limit constraints.
- **Jacobian Calculation:** The optimization requires computing gradients. The nonlinear coupling between the underactuated DIP and PIP joints is handled by applying the chain rule to incorporate the `∂qDIP/∂qPIP` term into the PIP column of the overall Jacobian.

#### 3.4. Robotic Arm Teleoperation

The FR3 arm's movements are controlled by mapping the Meta Quest headset's controller transform.
- **Optimization Problem:** Human wrist pose mapping is formulated as a constrained optimization problem (Equation 2):
 ```
    min_˙q ||Je ˙q - λ∆x||^2_w0 + ||Ja ˙q - ∆α||^2_w1 + ||˙q||^2_w2 + ||˙q - ˙q[t-1]||^2_w3
    s.t. fm ≤ ˙q ≤ fM
    ```
 - `˙q`: Joint velocity vector.
 - `Je`, `∆x`: Tool frame Jacobian and relative transforms of human wrist poses.
 - `λ`: Scaling factor for workspace difference.
 - `Ja`, `∆α`: Arm angle Jacobian and deviation from vertical reference plane.
 - `w{i}`: Weights for different tasks.
 - `fm`, `fM`: Joint position and velocity constraints.
 - `||˙q - ˙q[t-1]||^2_w3`: Term to improve motion smoothness.
- **Control:** The VR teleoperation loop operates at 50 Hz, with the FR3 arm tracking optimized motions using a 1 kHz joint-impedance controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the proposed system involved benchmarking the retargeting pipeline, evaluating dexterous in-hand manipulation tasks, and assessing performance in a long-horizon teleoperation task.

#### 4.1. Retargeting Comparison Results

The retargeting pipeline was compared against a modified DexPilot implementation using thumb–index and thumb–middle pinch motions over five open-close cycles.
- **Pinch Fidelity:** The proposed method demonstrated closer thumb–index and thumb–middle fingertip distances compared to the human reference trajectories, often achieving tighter spacing than the human reference itself. This was interpreted as beneficial for exerting forces into objects and enhancing grasp stability.
- **Joint Coordination:** The retargeting successfully reproduced human-like joint coordination, such as aligning opposing thumb and index fingertips into a parallel posture. This alignment was observed to be advantageous for reliably retrieving slender items (e.g., lipsticks, brushes, tubes).
- **Collision Avoidance:** The method reduced unintended collisions between task-irrelevant fingers compared to DexPilot, which is noted as mitigating mechanical wear during extended teleoperation.

#### 4.2. In-hand Manipulation Results

The system's capabilities in in-hand manipulation were assessed across three primitives—regrasping, sliding, and rotating—instantiated in four benchmark tasks:
- **Regrasping:** The system successfully performed regrasping of a bottle, transitioning from a thumb–index precision grasp to a power grasp. This demonstrated a strategy for enhancing grasp reliability by leveraging precision for acquisition and power for stabilization.
- **Multi-object Grasping:** The operator was able to grasp two objects simultaneously. This involved initially retrieving a cream tube with a precision grasp, then shifting to a power grasp involving the middle, ring, and pinky fingers and the palm to free the thumb and index finger. The hand then rotated palm-up to hold the first object, allowing the index finger to clear, before acquiring a second object with a thumb–index precision grasp. This sequence was identified as demonstrating a level of dexterity previously not observed.
- **Lid Opening:** The system executed two distinct lid-opening strategies: unscrewing via rotational motion and pushing with the thumb. Both tasks required the operator to regulate force or torque to initiate lid movement without compromising the stability of the main object's grasp.

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Teleoperation Results

The system was evaluated in a long-horizon task: organizing a cluttered makeup table with nine randomly arranged cosmetic and skincare items into a multi-drawer organizer.
- **Robustness in Cluttered Environments:** The system demonstrated robust performance in continuous grasping and manipulation of diverse geometries, including serum bottles, cream tubes, lipsticks, brushes, and powder palettes. It was also able to recover from grasp slippage through real-time adjustments.
- **Drawer Interaction:** The system successfully managed to open and close a multi-drawer organizer. Opening involved tilting the hand and using the index fingertip to engage a recessed pull. Closing required distributing the pushing force across multiple fingertips to prevent the organizer from tilting.
- **Non-Prehensile Manipulation:** The operator utilized non-prehensile primitives (pushing, pulling, tilting) to improve task efficiency. Examples included dragging a thin pocket mirror to the table edge to facilitate a pinch grasp, and pushing a palette's edge to flip it for a more stable grip.
- **Precision:** The system exhibited precision in tasks such as closing a lipstick cap without disturbing its pose.
- **Task Completion Efficiency:** An operator completed the nine-object organization task, including drawer interactions, in under five minutes, despite occasional slippage. The report indicates potential for faster completion times with additional operator training, positioning the platform as a tool for continuous data collection for imitation learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation and teleoperation, with several points of significance and potential impacts.

- **Advancement in Anthropomorphic Hand Design:** The development of ByteDexter, a 20-DoF linkage-driven anthropomorphic hand with a novel thumb mechanism and real-time kinematic solver, contributes to the ongoing effort to create robot hands that can approach human-level dexterity. The emphasis on compactness and robustness, while maintaining a high DoF count, addresses practical challenges in integrating such complex mechanisms onto robotic arms for real-world applications. The detailed kinematic analysis and efficient solver could serve as a foundation for future designs and control strategies for linkage-driven hands.

- **High-Fidelity Teleoperation:** The optimization-based hand motion retargeting framework directly addresses the long-standing problem of kinematic mismatches between human and robotic hands. By formulating the problem based on keyvector discrepancies and incorporating mechanisms to handle close-proximity interactions, the system achieves precise reproduction of intricate human hand motions. This enhances the intuitiveness of the teleoperation interface, reduces operator cognitive burden, and improves the reliability of task execution, particularly in contact-rich scenarios.

- **Enabling Data Collection for Imitation Learning:** A key impact of this work is its demonstrated capability to generate high-quality human demonstration data. The system's ability to intuitively control a dexterous hand-arm system through complex manipulation tasks, as shown in the cluttered table organization, means it can serve as a robust platform for collecting large and diverse datasets. Such datasets are critical for training data-driven control policies, including those based on imitation learning, which are essential for the eventual automation of dexterous skills in unstructured environments. This could accelerate the development of autonomous agents capable of general-purpose manipulation.

- **Enhanced Robotic Capabilities in Human Environments:** The empirical validation across dexterous in-hand manipulation tasks (regrasping, multi-object grasping, lid opening) and a long-horizon cluttered table organization task demonstrates the system's potential for real-world deployment. The ability to handle diverse object geometries, recover from slippage, and perform non-prehensile manipulations showcases a versatile platform that could perform household chores, personal assistance, and other tasks in human-centered settings. The reported "previously unseen level of dexterity" in multi-object grasping highlights a significant step towards more adaptable robotic manipulation.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies specific limitations, such as the current thumb's lack of true oppositional capability, susceptibility to vision-based occlusion in wrist tracking, and operator cognitive fatigue. These identified limitations provide clear directions for future research, including developing more anatomically accurate thumb designs, integrating multi-modal sensing for robust pose estimation, and incorporating autonomous low-level grasping strategies. Addressing these areas could further advance the state of the art in dexterous teleoperation and autonomous manipulation.

In conclusion, this research contributes a well-integrated system that pushes the boundaries of dexterous robotic teleoperation, with direct implications for the collection of training data for autonomous systems and the potential for broader robotic application in complex human environments.
