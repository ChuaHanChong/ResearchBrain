# IllumiCraft: Unified Geometry and Illumination Diffusion for Controllable Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from University of Oxford, NEC Labs America, UC Merced, and Google DeepMind present IllumiCraft, a diffusion transformer framework that unifies 3D geometry tracking with HDR illumination control for video relighting, achieving 43% FVD reduction over Light-A-Video baseline and 6x faster inference (105 vs 645 seconds for 49-frame videos) through joint modeling of lighting and geometric cues within a Wan2.1-based architecture, while introducing IllumiPipe data collection pipeline that generates 20,170 video pairs enriched with synchronized HDR maps, 3D point tracks, and relit sequences to address the critical limitation of prior video relighting methods that omit explicit geometric guidance and suffer from lighting inconsistencies when scene geometry changes.

## Problem

- Existing video diffusion models treat illumination as implicit factor without precise control over scene lighting that respects 3D structure
- Prior video relighting methods like RelightVid and Light-A-Video lack explicit geometric guidance, leading to degraded lighting fidelity when scene geometry changes
- Absence of comprehensive datasets pairing videos with HDR environment maps and 3D tracking sequences hinders geometry-guided video editing research
- Traditional inverse rendering techniques require specialized inputs and assume static scenes, making them impractical for dynamic real-world videos

## Method

- Develop unified diffusion framework integrating HDR illumination control with explicit 3D geometry tracking through modified **Wan2**.1 transformer architecture
- Create **IllumiPipe** data collection pipeline extracting six complementary data types: relit foreground videos, background videos, HDR maps, 3D tracking sequences, masks, and captions
- Inject illumination control via MLP-Transformer lighting encoder processing HDR maps with learnable embedding that enables text-only control at inference
- Integrate 3D geometry guidance using **ControlNet-style** conditioning with hierarchical injection of tracking data through cloned transformer blocks and zero-initialized linear layers

## Results

- Outperforms Light-A-Video with 43% FVD reduction, improved LPIPS, PSNR, text alignment, and temporal consistency across text-conditioned and background-conditioned relighting tasks
- Achieves **6x** inference speedup (105.21 vs 645.53 seconds) for 49-frame videos compared to Light-A-Video through efficient spatio-temporal latent encoding
- Demonstrates superior visual quality with sharper details, reduced color shifts, eliminated temporal flicker, and better subject-background separation in qualitative comparisons
- Contributes **20,170** high-quality video pairs dataset with synchronized HDR maps, 3D tracking sequences, and relit versions as public resource for future research

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint geometry-illumination modeling achieves 18% FVD reduction compared to illumination-only approach, confirming geometry's critical role in spatially-aware light-scene interaction
> - Strategic data augmentation with 50% HDR dropout and 30% 3D tracking dropout during training enables robust generalization and text-only control without explicit geometric inputs
> - Temporal consistency of HDR maps achieved by warping initial chrome ball extraction across frames using estimated 3D camera motion rather than per-frame extraction
> - Explicit geometric guidance addresses fundamental limitation where lighting changes must respect evolving scene structure for physical plausibility

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI, particularly diffusion models, has made remarkable strides in creating high-quality images and, more recently, videos. However, controllable generation, especially concerning complex physical phenomena like illumination, remains a significant challenge.

#### 1.1. Current State of Video Generation and Relighting

Diffusion models have become the state-of-the-art for image and video synthesis, capable of generating diverse, high-resolution content from text or image prompts. Models like Stable Diffusion (for images) and more recent video diffusion models (e.g., Wan2.1, Stable Video Diffusion, CogVideoX) have demonstrated impressive fidelity and temporal coherence for general video generation. However, their control over scene lighting and visual appearance across frames is often implicit and limited, treating illumination as an unmodifiable factor.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Existing Approaches

- **Traditional Inverse Rendering:** Historically, physically accurate relighting has been achieved through inverse rendering techniques. These methods decompose a scene into intrinsic properties like albedo, normals, and lighting, enabling precise relighting. However, they typically rely on specialized inputs (e.g., HDR captures, spherical harmonics) and often assume static scenes, making them impractical for dynamic, real-world videos with complex motion.
- **Video Diffusion Models without Explicit Control:** While general video diffusion models can generate visually appealing videos, they often struggle with explicit, user-defined control over scene lighting. The illumination is an emergent property of the generated content rather than a controllable input.
- **Recent Video Relighting Diffusion Models:** More recently, efforts have been made to extend single-image relighting models (like IC-Light) to the video domain. RelightVid and Light-A-Video are notable examples that aim for temporal consistency through various attention mechanisms or progressive fusion steps. While these methods improve frame-to-frame coherence, they predominantly rely on implicit temporal correlations and *omit explicit geometric guidance*. This fundamental limitation means they suffer from a loss of lighting fidelity and coherence, especially when the scene's geometry undergoes significant changes, as they lack an understanding of how light interacts with 3D structures over time.
- **General Video Editing Methods:** Other video editing approaches, such as AnyV2V or Tune-A-Video, achieve impressive results in tasks like style transfer or object insertion but do not explicitly model lighting cues, leading to potential inconsistencies in illumination when changes are applied.

#### 1.3. IllumiCraft's Unique Contribution

IllumiCraft distinguishes itself by addressing the critical gap of integrating *explicit geometric cues* alongside *detailed illumination control* within a unified diffusion framework for video generation. By jointly modeling lighting and geometry, IllumiCraft aims to overcome the shortcomings of prior work, which either lacked temporal consistency, geometric awareness, or both. This explicit joint modeling enables the generation of videos where illumination changes are not only coherent over time but also physically plausible with respect to the evolving scene geometry. The introduction of a dedicated, large-scale dataset featuring paired original videos, synchronized relit videos, HDR maps, and 3D tracking information further marks a significant contribution to the broader research landscape, providing a crucial resource for future advancements in controllable video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of IllumiCraft is to enable **controllable video generation with high-fidelity relighting capabilities**, explicitly integrating geometric cues to ensure physically plausible and temporally coherent illumination changes.

The key motivations behind this work stem from the limitations of existing video generation and relighting approaches:

1. **Lack of Explicit Geometry-Illumination Integration:** Current diffusion-based video generation models, while producing high-quality visuals, treat illumination as an implicit factor. They struggle to precisely control scene lighting in a way that respects the 3D structure of objects and environments. This often leads to "flat" or inconsistent lighting that does not accentuate the scene's three-dimensional properties.
2. **Challenges in Coherent Video Relighting:** Achieving realistic video relighting involves two major hurdles:
 - **Temporal Consistency:** Illumination must remain stable and smooth across frames, preventing distracting flicker or abrupt changes.
 - **Physical Plausibility:** Shadows, highlights, and reflections must move consistently with camera and object motion, correctly interacting with the scene's geometry.
3. **Limitations of Prior Relighting Methods:**
 - Traditional inverse rendering techniques are ill-suited for dynamic, real-world videos due to their reliance on specialized inputs and assumptions of static scenes.
 - More recent diffusion-based video relighting models (e.g., RelightVid, Light-A-Video) have improved temporal consistency but critically **omit explicit geometric guidance**. This oversight means their lighting fidelity and coherence degrade significantly when scene geometry changes, as they lack an understanding of 3D light-object interactions.
4. **Need for Comprehensive Training Data:** The absence of public video datasets that include both HDR environment maps and precise 3D tracking sequences has hindered progress in geometry-guided video editing and relighting.

To address these motivations, IllumiCraft proposes to jointly model lighting and geometry, enabling a system that can generate high-fidelity, relit videos by accepting three complementary inputs: HDR video maps for detailed lighting control, synthetically relit frames (optionally with a static background reference) for appearance cues, and 3D point tracks for precise 3D geometry information. The overarching goal is to achieve superior fidelity, consistency, and controllability compared to existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IllumiCraft proposes an end-to-end diffusion framework built upon a transformer-based video diffusion architecture, specifically Wan2.1. Its methodology can be broken down into two main components: a novel data collection pipeline called IllumiPipe, and the core IllumiCraft model architecture.

#### 3.1. IllumiPipe: The Data Collection Pipeline

Recognizing the scarcity of suitable datasets for geometry- and illumination-aware video relighting, the authors introduce IllumiPipe, an efficient pipeline designed to generate a comprehensive, high-quality dataset from real-world videos. For each input video ($V_{appr}$), IllumiPipe extracts six types of complementary data:
- **Relit Foreground Video ($V_{rf}$):** The foreground of the original video is extracted using Grounded SAM-2 and MatAnyone. This foreground is then relit using Light-A-Video conditioned on 100 diverse, carefully curated lighting prompts. This process generates target relit videos with varied illumination while preserving original motion and viewpoint.
- **Background Video ($V_{bg}$):** The background is generated by inpainting the original video using DiffuEraser, based on the extracted foreground mask. This provides a clean background reference.
- **HDR Environment Maps ($V_{hdr}$):** These maps encode per-direction radiance values for accurate image-based lighting. DiffusionLight is used to extract HDR maps. A key innovation for temporal stability is extracting a chrome ball image only from the first frame, then warping this initial chrome ball to subsequent frames using estimated 3D camera motion (derived from sparse feature tracking and Video Depth Anything) to ensure temporal consistency of HDR data across the sequence.
- **3D Tracking Videos ($V_{geo}$):** For real-world videos lacking ground-truth 3D geometry, SpatialTracker is employed. It detects and localizes salient 3D interest points by initializing a uniform grid and estimating 3D positions and correspondences across consecutive frames. This yields dense, temporally coherent 3D point tracks.
- **Mask Video ($V_{mask}$):** Binary masks corresponding to the extracted foreground video.
- **Video Captions (C):** Detailed descriptions of each video, including content, object motion, lighting conditions, and atmosphere, generated using CogVLM2-Video-LLaMA3-Chat with a specialized prompt to emphasize lighting and motion.

This dataset, comprising 20,170 video pairs, serves as a crucial foundation for training IllumiCraft.

#### 3.2. IllumiCraft Model Architecture

The core of IllumiCraft is built upon the pre-trained Wan2.1 video generation model, which uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone. This leverages existing strong video priors and accelerates training.

- **Latent Feature Extraction:** The VAE encoder ($E_{VAE}$) transforms the appearance video ($V_{appr}$), relit foreground video ($V_{rf}$), and a zero-padded static reference image (first frame of $V_{bg}$) into latent representations. The relit foreground latent and reference latent are concatenated to form a "control latent" ($z_c$). The appearance latent ($z$) is noised ($z_t$) and then concatenated with $z_c$ before being fed into the DiT for denoising.
- **Inject Illumination Control:** HDR maps ($V_{hdr}$) are processed by a compact MLP-Transformer (the "lighting encoder") to produce a feature matrix ($X_{hdr}$). A learnable illumination embedding ($X$) is introduced and updated by adding $X_{hdr}$. Crucially, during training, $X_{hdr}$ is randomly zeroed out with 50% probability. This forces $X$ to learn a robust, standalone representation of lighting priors, enabling illumination control solely via text prompts at inference time, even without explicit HDR input. This updated illumination embedding is then concatenated with the CLIP text prompt embedding ($P$) to form the final prompt embedding ($P'$), which guides the diffusion model.
- **Integrate 3D Geometry Guidance:** IllumiCraft extends the ControlNet concept by using the 3D tracking video ($V_{geo}$) as an additional conditioning signal. $V_{geo}$ is encoded into geometry latents ($z_g$) using the VAE encoder. These signals are injected hierarchically into the main DiT stream by cloning the first four blocks of the 32-block denoising transformer. The output of each cloned block is passed through a zero-initialized linear layer and then added to the corresponding feature map in the main DiT. During fine-tuning, 3D tracking videos are randomly replaced with zero tensors with a 30% probability, simulating inference without 3D tracking input and promoting robust generalization.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference Strategies

The training involves fine-tuning the DiT blocks and the zero-initialized linear layers while freezing the VAE and CLIP text encoder. The model is optimized using a diffusion denoising loss, conditioning on the composite latents (geometry, appearance, and the illumination-aware text prompt). For inference, users provide a text prompt and an input video, with optional reference images. IllumiCraft leverages the learned geometric and illumination cues to generate coherent, illumination-aware videos efficiently.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

IllumiCraft demonstrates significant advancements in controllable video relighting, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in both quantitative metrics and qualitative visual fidelity.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

The evaluations were conducted on two primary tasks: text-conditioned video relighting and background-conditioned video relighting, using a custom 50-video test set. Metrics include FVD (Fréchet Video Distance, lower is better), LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity, lower is better), PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio, higher is better), Text Alignment (CLIP cosine similarity, higher is better), and Temporal Consistency (CLIP cosine similarity across frames, higher is better).

- **Text-Conditioned Video Relighting:** IllumiCraft significantly outperforms baselines such as IC-Light, AnyV2V + IC-Light, and Light-A-Video. For instance, it achieved a 43% reduction in FVD compared to the strongest baseline (Light-A-Video) and improved scores across all other metrics (e.g., LPIPS, PSNR, Text Alignment, Temporal Consistency).
- **Background-Conditioned Video Relighting:** IllumiCraft again shows superior performance. For 49-frame sequences, it reduced FVD by 37% compared to Light-A-Video, while also improving perceptual similarity, prompt alignment, and temporal consistency. When evaluated on 16-frame sequences against RelightVid (which natively supports 16 frames), IllumiCraft also achieved better scores across all metrics, showcasing higher fidelity and detail preservation.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Performance

Visual comparisons highlight IllumiCraft's ability to produce sharper, more consistent, and prompt-faithful results. Competing methods often struggle with color shifts, excessive smoothing, blurring of fine details, or temporal jitters. IllumiCraft, in contrast, preserves fine textures, accurately captures lighting nuances (e.g., spotlight effects, subtle glows), ensures strict adherence to text prompts, and produces flicker-free, temporally coherent videos. It demonstrates superior subject-background separation and realistic textures.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Illumination and Geometry Guidance:** Comparing a model trained with illumination-only (I) versus one with both illumination and geometry guidance (I+G) revealed that incorporating geometry consistently improved all metrics, including an approximate 18% reduction in FVD. This confirms the critical role of geometry in providing spatial context for accurate light-scene interaction, leading to more photorealistic and temporally stable relit videos.
- **Effect of Dropping HDR ($X_{hdr}$):** Varying the probability of dropping HDR inputs during training (40-70%) showed that a 50% drop rate yielded the best trade-off between visual fidelity, text alignment, and temporal consistency. This indicates that a moderate omission rate helps the model generalize better and rely on learned illumination priors for text-only control.
- **Effect of Dropping 3D Tracking Videos:** A 30% drop rate for 3D tracking videos during training was found to be optimal, balancing visual quality, text alignment, and frame consistency in both text- and background-conditioned settings.
- **Effect of Dropping Reference Image:** A 10% drop rate for reference images during training provided the best balance, maximizing visual realism, prompt alignment, and frame consistency across both settings.

#### 4.4. Efficiency

IllumiCraft demonstrates significant efficiency improvements in inference time. For a 49-frame video (720x480 resolution), IllumiCraft completes inference in just 105.21 seconds, dramatically faster than Light-A-Video (645.53 seconds), AnyV2V (1033.21 seconds), and IC-Light (228.31 seconds). This efficiency is attributed to its spatio-temporal latent encoding. For 16-frame videos, it's also faster than RelightVid.

#### 4.5. New Dataset Contribution

The paper introduces a substantial new dataset of 20,170 high-quality video pairs, enriched with HDR maps, 3D tracking sequences, and synchronized relit videos. This dataset is a valuable public resource for future research in video relighting and broader controllable video generation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

IllumiCraft represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable video generation, particularly in the challenging domain of video relighting. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Technical Advancements

- **Unified Geometry and Illumination Modeling:** The core technical breakthrough is the successful integration of explicit 3D geometry cues with detailed illumination information within a single diffusion framework. This addresses a critical limitation of previous video relighting methods that struggled with physically plausible light interactions and temporal consistency in dynamic scenes due to a lack of geometric understanding.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Fidelity:** By providing granular control through HDR maps, relit foregrounds, and 3D tracking data, IllumiCraft enables high-fidelity manipulation of video illumination. The ability to relight videos based on text prompts alone, even without explicit HDR or 3D inputs at inference, showcases the model's learned robust lighting priors.
- **Superior Temporal Coherence:** The quantitative and qualitative results consistently demonstrate IllumiCraft's ability to produce videos with exceptional temporal consistency, mitigating issues like flicker and unnatural light transitions common in prior works. This is crucial for creating realistic and immersive visual experiences.
- **Efficient Inference:** The remarkable speed-up in inference time compared to existing methods makes IllumiCraft a more practical solution for real-world applications and larger-scale video processing.
- **Novel Data Collection Pipeline:** The introduction of IllumiPipe and the curated dataset of 20,170 video pairs (with HDR maps, 3D tracking, and relit versions) is a substantial contribution to the research community. This resource fills a critical gap, providing a foundation for future research in geometry-aware and illumination-controlled video generation.

#### 5.2. Broader Applications

The capabilities of IllumiCraft extend beyond mere video relighting. Its ability to disentangle and control illumination and geometry opens doors for:
- **Advanced Video Editing:** More precise and physically accurate adjustments to existing videos, including scene composition, object insertion, and style transfer that respect environmental lighting.
- **Virtual Production and Filmmaking:** Tools for visual effects artists to easily modify lighting in post-production, match real-world footage with virtual environments, or rapidly prototype lighting scenarios.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** Creating more immersive AR/VR experiences by ensuring virtual objects are lit consistently with the real or virtual environment.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Generating diverse, high-quality synthetic video data with varied lighting conditions for training other computer vision models, especially for tasks sensitive to illumination.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its significant achievements, the authors acknowledge certain limitations:
- **Dependency on Base Generative Prior:** The fidelity of IllumiCraft's output is still influenced by the underlying generative prior of the base diffusion model (Wan2.1). If this prior lacks sufficient detail or accurate shading cues, artifacts like texture blurring or misplaced shadows can occur.
- **Data-Driven Artifacts:** Issues such as oversaturated colors in foliage or mistaking occluded floodlight parts as foreground indicate the need for even more diverse training data, particularly scenes with complex dynamic occlusions and strong directional lighting.

Future work could focus on enhancing the base model's generative capabilities, expanding the training dataset with more challenging lighting scenarios, and exploring explicit physically-based rendering principles within the diffusion framework to minimize artifacts.

#### 5.4. Ethical Considerations

The paper explicitly addresses the ethical implications of its work. By significantly enhancing the realism and temporal coherence of manipulated videos, IllumiCraft could inadvertently contribute to the rise of "deepfakes" – hyper-realistic but fabricated video content. The authors responsibly acknowledge this potential misuse and advocate for concurrent research into safeguards, detection techniques, and ethical guidelines to mitigate such risks. This proactive stance is crucial for fostering responsible AI development.

In conclusion, IllumiCraft presents a pioneering step towards truly controllable video generation by thoughtfully integrating geometry and illumination cues. Its technical innovations, combined with the release of a valuable dataset, promise to accelerate future research in realistic and physically plausible video manipulation.
