# Tempered Sequential Monte Carlo for Trajectory and Policy Optimization with Differentiable Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Tempered Sequential Monte Carlo (TSMC) is presented as a unified framework for finite-horizon trajectory and policy optimization under differentiable dynamics, effectively combining global exploration from sampling with local exploitation from gradients. This approach consistently achieved superior or competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines on various complex robotic control tasks, including challenging swing-up and contact manipulation problems.

## Problem

- Gradient-based optimization methods often struggle with non-convex control problems, frequently converging to sub-optimal local minima.
- Traditional sampling-based methods typically require a large number of samples for satisfactory performance, leading to computational inefficiency.
- A unified algorithm that leverages differentiable dynamics to combine global exploration and efficient local refinement for both trajectory and policy optimization was needed.

## Method

- Reframes trajectory and policy optimization as a ==control-as-inference== problem, seeking to sample from a ==Boltzmann-tilted distribution== over controller parameters.
- Employs **Tempered Sequential Monte Carlo (TSMC)**, which uses ==adaptive tempering==, importance reweighting, resampling, and ==Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC)== rejuvenation to navigate sharp, multimodal energy landscapes.
- Leverages exact gradients derived from differentiable dynamics within HMC for efficient local exploration, with adaptations for policy optimization via deterministic approximation or an extended-space approach to handle expected costs.

## Results

- In trajectory optimization, TSMC significantly outperformed baselines like Parallel-IPOPT and Parallel-MPPI on challenging tasks such as Acrobot swing-up and Pusher Slider, consistently finding lower-cost and successful trajectories.
- For policy optimization, TSMC variants achieved performance comparable to or surpassing deep reinforcement learning methods (PPO, SAC) on tasks like the inverted pendulum with sparse rewards and difficult Acrobot/Double Pendulum swing-ups.
- TSMC successfully learned complex policies for swing-up tasks where standard deep RL algorithms like PPO and SAC struggled to achieve reliable solutions, demonstrating its robustness in high-dimensional and non-trivial control problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Casting optimal control as a probabilistic inference problem enables the application of advanced sampling techniques to find optimal control sequences or policies.
> - Adaptive tempering provides a robust mechanism to sample from complex, potentially multimodal target distributions by gradually annealing from an easy-to-sample prior.
> - The availability of exact gradients from differentiable dynamics is crucial for the efficient local exploration and diversity maintenance provided by Hamiltonian Monte Carlo rejuvenation within the TSMC framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses fundamental problems in optimal control and reinforcement learning: trajectory optimization (TO) and policy optimization (PO). These problems typically involve minimizing a cost function over a sequence of control inputs or policy parameters, respectively, for a dynamical system over a finite horizon. The existing landscape for solving these problems can be broadly categorized into two types:

- **Gradient-based methods:** These methods, often rooted in nonlinear programming for TO (e.g., single or multiple shooting) or policy gradient techniques for PO, exploit the differentiability of the dynamics model to compute gradients and iteratively refine solutions. Recent advancements in differentiable physics simulators and learned differentiable world models have amplified the utility of these approaches. A recognized limitation is their susceptibility to local minima in non-convex landscapes and potential informativeness issues with gradients from non-smooth dynamics.
- **Sampling-based methods:** Approaches like Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) or the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) explore the control space by sampling and evaluate trajectories, making them less sensitive to local minima. However, they often demand a substantial number of samples to achieve satisfactory performance.

The paper identifies a gap: a unified and principled algorithm that can combine the benefits of global exploration offered by sampling methods with the efficiency of gradient-based local optimization, particularly in the context of differentiable dynamics. This work fits into a growing trend of "control-as-inference," which reframes optimal control problems as probabilistic inference tasks, allowing for the application of advanced sampling techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and versatile sampling-based framework for finite-horizon trajectory and policy optimization under differentiable dynamics. This framework aims to integrate the exploratory capabilities of sampling with the local refinement efficiency of gradient-based methods.

The motivation for this approach stems from several points:
- **Addressing Non-convexity and Local Minima:** Traditional gradient-based optimization often struggles with the non-convex nature of many control problems, leading to convergence to sub-optimal local minima. The authors propose reframing the optimization problem (minimizing expected trajectory cost, E[J(τ(x0, θ))]) as an inference problem. This is achieved by introducing a KL-regularized objective:
 ```
    min_p E_{x0~μ, θ~p} [J(τ(x0, θ))] + λ D_KL(p || p0)
    ```
 where `p` is a distribution over controller parameters `θ`, `p0` is a prior, and `λ` is a regularization parameter (temperature).
- **Leveraging "Control-as-Inference":** This KL-regularized problem yields a closed-form optimal distribution `p*(θ) ∝ p0(θ) exp(-E(θ)/λ)`, known as a Boltzmann-tilted distribution (or Gibbs posterior). Here, `E(θ)` is the expected trajectory cost. This formulation transforms the search for optimal parameters `θ` into the task of sampling efficiently from `p*(θ)`. As `λ → 0+`, `p*(θ)` concentrates its mass on the minimizers of the original TPO objective, effectively turning optimization into an inference problem.
- **Challenges of Sampling from Sharp, Multimodal Distributions:** While the "control-as-inference" perspective offers a principled solution, sampling from `p*(θ)`—especially at low temperatures (`λ`) where the distribution becomes sharply concentrated and potentially multimodal—remains challenging. Standard importance sampling methods (like MPPI) can require many samples, and Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods can suffer from long mixing times in such landscapes.
- **Combining Exploration and Exploitation:** The paper aims to design an algorithm that can effectively navigate these complex, multimodal landscapes by combining the global exploration benefits of sampling with the local exploitation capabilities of gradient information, which is available due to the assumption of differentiable dynamics.

The core motivation is to create a unified algorithm that is more robust to initialization and less prone to local minima than pure gradient methods, while being more sample-efficient and offering better diversity than pure sampling methods, all within the context of differentiable dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on **Tempered Sequential Monte Carlo (TSMC)**, a sampling-based framework that integrates tempering, importance sampling, resampling, and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) rejuvenation.

**A. Reframing Optimization as Inference:**
The initial step is to reframe the trajectory and policy optimization problem as an inference problem. By minimizing a KL-regularized expected trajectory cost, the optimal distribution over controller parameters `θ` is shown to be a Boltzmann-tilted distribution:
`p*(θ) ∝ p0(θ) exp(-E(θ)/λ)`, where `E(θ) = E_{x0~μ} [J(τ(x0, θ))]` is the energy function (expected trajectory cost) and `p0(θ)` is a prior. Sampling from this distribution, particularly at low `λ`, allows for the identification of low-cost solutions.

**B. Tempered Sequential Monte Carlo (TSMC) Core Algorithm:**
TSMC addresses the challenge of sampling from sharp, potentially multimodal target distributions by constructing a tempering path. This path gradually deforms an easy-to-sample prior distribution (`p0`) into the target Boltzmann-tilted distribution (`p*`). This deformation is achieved through a sequence of intermediate distributions indexed by `k = 0, ..., K`:
`pk(θ) ∝ p0(θ) exp(-βk/λ E(θ))`, where `0 = β0 < β1 < ... < βK = 1` is an increasing sequence of tempering parameters.

The TSMC algorithm proceeds iteratively through tempering levels `k`:
1. **Importance Reweighting:** Given a weighted particle approximation `{(θ_{k-1}^{(i)}, w_{k-1}^{(i)})}` for `p_{k-1}`, particles are reweighted to approximate `pk`. The incremental weight for each particle `i` is calculated as `Δw_k^{(i)} = exp(-(βk - β_{k-1})/λ E(θ_{k-1}^{(i)}))`, and then normalized to obtain `w_k^{(i)}`. This step preferentially upweights particles with lower energy.
2. **Adaptive Tempering:** To prevent particle degeneracy, the next tempering parameter `βk` is chosen adaptively. This is typically done by targeting a desired Effective Sample Size (ESS) ratio (`ρN`), where ESS measures the variance of the particle weights. `βk` is increased just enough to maintain the ESS above a threshold, ensuring that weight disparity does not become too severe too quickly.
3. **Resampling:** After reweighting, particles are resampled based on their normalized weights. This converts the weighted particle set into an approximately unweighted one, focusing computational effort on high-probability regions and preventing the accumulation of weight disparities. This step, however, can reduce particle diversity.
4. **MCMC Rejuvenation (Hamiltonian Monte Carlo - HMC):** To restore particle diversity and enable exploration of the parameter space, an MCMC transition kernel `T_k(θ'|θ)` is applied to each particle. The key is that `T_k` must leave `p_k` invariant. HMC is chosen for its ability to propose distant moves.
 - HMC operates on an augmented state space `(θ, r)`, where `r` is an auxiliary momentum variable. It defines a Hamiltonian `Hk(θ, r) = Vk(θ) + 0.5r^T M^-1 r`, with `Vk(θ) = βk/λ E(θ) - log p0(θ)` being the negative log-density (potential energy) of `p_k(θ)`.
 - Leapfrog integration is used to approximately simulate Hamiltonian dynamics, proposing new `(θ', r')` states.
 - A Metropolis correction step accepts or rejects the proposed state, correcting for discretization errors and ensuring detailed balance with respect to `p_k`.
 - Parameters for HMC (mass matrix `M`, step size `ε`, number of steps `L`) are tuned, with `M=I` and NUTS (No-U-Turn Sampler) used for adaptive `L`. The gradient `∇θ Vk(θ)` is required for HMC.

**C. TSMC for Trajectory Optimization (TO):**
For TO, the initial state `x0` is fixed (`μ = δ_{x0}`). The energy function becomes `E(θ) = J(τ(x0, θ))`. Crucially, `∇θ E(θ)` can be computed **exactly** by differentiating through the trajectory rollout using the adjoint method (reverse-mode automatic differentiation). This provides precise gradient information for the HMC rejuvenation step. The control parameterization `θ` is typically the sequence of open-loop control inputs `(u0, ..., uT-1)`.

**D. TSMC for Policy Optimization (PO):**
For PO, `E(θ)` involves an expectation over initial states `x0 ~ μ`, making it generally intractable to evaluate exactly. Two practical alternatives are proposed to address the stochasticity:

1. **Deterministic Approximation (TSMC:D):** The initial-state distribution `μ` is approximated by an empirical measure over a fixed batch of `B` initial conditions `{(x0^{(b)})}`. This yields a surrogate energy `E_B(θ) = 1/B Σ J(τ(x0^{(b)}, θ))`. Both `E_B(θ)` and `∇θ E_B(θ)` are then deterministic and computable by averaging individual rollout gradients, allowing the standard TSMC algorithm to be applied without modification.

2. **Extended-Space TSMC (TSMC:E):** This approach augments each particle state to include both controller parameters `θ` and a batch of `B` i.i.d. initial conditions `x0 = (x0^{(1)}, ..., x0^{(B)})`. The tempered target distributions are defined on this extended space `(θ, x0)`:
 `~pk(θ, x0) ∝ p0(θ) Π μ(x0^{(b)}) exp(-βk/λ J_B(θ, x0))`, where `J_B(θ, x0)` is the empirical average rollout cost over the batch.
 - The TSMC steps are adapted: incremental weights use `J_B(θ, x0)`.
 - The rejuvenation step involves two alternating blocks:
 - **Move θ (hold x0 fixed):** An HMC move targets the conditional density `~pk(θ | x0)`, using `∇θ J_B(θ, x0)`.
 - **Refresh x0 (hold θ fixed):** To prevent `x0` from being restricted to initial samples, a Metropolis-Hastings (MH) refresh is applied to `x0`. For each `x0^{(b)}`, a new `x0'^{(b)}` is proposed from `μ`, and accepted based on a probability involving the cost difference `J(τ(x0'^{(b)}, θ)) - J(τ(x0^{(b)}, θ))`.
 The resulting `θ`-marginal distribution from this extended-space approach is a risk-sensitive (exponential utility) perturbation of the true Boltzmann tilt, with the mismatch decreasing as `O(1/B)`.

**E. Extension to Stochastic Dynamics:**
The framework can be extended to stochastic dynamics by redefining the energy function to average over both initial-state and disturbance randomness. The extended-space construction would then augment auxiliary variables with sampled disturbance trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TSMC spans both trajectory optimization (TO) and policy optimization (PO) benchmarks, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art baselines.

**A. Trajectory Optimization (TO) Experiments:**
TSMC was compared against Parallel-NUTS (independent HMC chains), Parallel-MALA (independent Metropolis-adjusted Langevin algorithm chains), Parallel-MPPI (sampling-based control), and Parallel-IPOPT (gradient-based nonlinear programming solver). All methods used N=100 particles/initializations.

- **Inverted Pendulum (Swing-up):**
 - **Setup:** Swing up a pendulum to an upright configuration; `θ ∈ R^30`. A certified lower bound on the optimal cost was used for reference.
 - **Results:** TSMC, Parallel-NUTS, and Parallel-IPOPT achieved comparable, strong performance, with final particle costs close to the certified lower bound, indicating near-global solutions. Parallel-MALA performed reasonably but showed a heavier tail of higher costs. Parallel-MPPI was outperformed by the gradient-based methods, including TSMC.
 - **Observation:** This task highlights that for relatively simpler, well-behaved problems, tempering provides similar benefits to direct HMC, and gradient-based methods can perform well.

- **Acrobot (Swing-up):**
 - **Setup:** Swing up a two-link pendulum to an upright configuration with a single control input; `θ ∈ R^200`.
 - **Results:** TSMC significantly outperformed all baselines. Its best trajectory cost was 2389, while baselines ranged from 2562 (Parallel-IPOPT) to 2967 (Parallel-NUTS, Parallel-MALA). Visualizations confirmed TSMC was the only method achieving a successful swing-up close to the upright configuration.
 - **Observation:** This result suggests TSMC's robustness in handling more complex, potentially multimodal landscapes where other methods may get trapped in local minima.

- **Pusher Slider:**
 - **Setup:** Planar pusher-slider problem involving non-smooth contact dynamics, with a relaxed differentiable model for contact modes; `θ ∈ R^800`.
 - **Results:** TSMC again significantly outperformed all baselines, being the only method to consistently reach the goal pose. The best-cost trajectories from TSMC successfully completed the task.
 - **Observation:** This demonstrates TSMC's effectiveness in scenarios with high-dimensional control spaces and dynamics that are challenging for traditional gradient-based (IPOPT) and purely sampling-based (MPPI) methods. The differentiable relaxation for contact was critical for gradient-based methods.

**B. Policy Optimization (PO) Experiments:**
TSMC (both deterministic approximation TSMC:D and extended-space TSMC:E) was compared against Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), standard deep reinforcement learning algorithms. Policy parameters `θ` were neural network weights.

- **Inverted Pendulum with Sparse Rewards:**
 - **Setup:** Swing up inverted pendulum with a sparse terminal reward; `θ ∈ R^1184`. Initial states uniformly sampled from a 2D box. `B = 3000` for TSMC variants, `N = 32` particles.
 - **Results:** PPO did not reliably improve. SAC attained strong returns. Both TSMC:D and TSMC:E achieved performance similar to SAC, indicating competitive policy learning in this sparse reward setting.
 - **Observation:** TSMC effectively learns policies in sparse reward environments, comparable to strong off-policy RL methods.

- **Acrobot (Swing-up, single initial state):**
 - **Setup:** Swing up acrobot from a fixed initial state; `θ ∈ R^1184`. `N = 16000` particles for TSMC.
 - **Results:** Neither PPO nor SAC reliably reached a successful swing-up within a budget of 10^6 environment steps, nor even with 10^9 steps. TSMC achieved a best trajectory cost of 1338.15, significantly outperforming PPO (2311.14 average) and SAC (2482.44 average) and demonstrating successful swing-up.
 - **Observation:** This highlights TSMC's ability to learn complex, non-trivial policies even when other RL baselines struggle, possibly due to the sharpness of the policy landscape or high variance of policy gradients.

- **Double Pendulum on a Cart (Swing-up, single initial state):**
 - **Setup:** Swing up a two-link double pendulum on a cart from a fixed initial state; `θ ∈ R^1248`. `N = 14000` particles for TSMC.
 - **Results:** PPO and SAC achieved average trajectory costs of 1713.00 and 3121.77, respectively. TSMC achieved a best cost of 1350.50, outperforming both RL baselines.
 - **Observation:** Similar to Acrobot, TSMC demonstrated superior performance in a higher-dimensional and more challenging swing-up task compared to standard RL algorithms.

**C. Runtime and Hyperparameters:**
- **Runtime:** TSMC can be computationally intensive. For TO, it was slower than Parallel-NUTS and Parallel-MALA due to the tempering procedure, but generally faster than Parallel-IPOPT for high-dimensional or non-smooth problems. For PO, TSMC was about 3x slower than PPO and SAC but achieved better policy performance.
- **Hyperparameters:** PO tasks empirically required higher temperatures, smaller NUTS step sizes, and larger particle counts compared to TO, suggesting a "harder" optimization landscape, potentially due to larger gradient magnitudes from policy parameterization.

**D. Energy Distributions:**
Across all experiments, visualizations of energy distributions across tempering steps showed a progressive concentration and shift towards lower energies, consistent with TSMC's goal of sampling from low-cost regions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Tempered Sequential Monte Carlo (TSMC) for trajectory and policy optimization represents a notable contribution to the fields of optimal control and reinforcement learning.

**Significance:**
- **Unified and Principled Framework:** TSMC provides a unified framework that addresses both trajectory and policy optimization problems under differentiable dynamics by casting controller design as inference. This "control-as-inference" perspective offers a principled way to handle complex optimization landscapes.
- **Robustness to Non-convexity and Multimodality:** By combining adaptive tempering and HMC-based rejuvenation, TSMC demonstrates robustness in exploring parameter spaces with sharp, multimodal energy landscapes. This capability is critical for complex control problems where global optimality is hard to achieve with local optimization methods.
- **Integration of Sampling and Gradients:** The framework effectively merges the global exploration benefits of sampling-based methods with the efficiency of gradient-based local exploitation. The use of exact gradients (via adjoint methods) within HMC rejuvenation allows for efficient local search while maintaining diversity.
- **Improved Performance on Challenging Benchmarks:** The experimental results show that TSMC often outperforms or is competitive with specialized state-of-the-art baselines, particularly on tasks with high-dimensional parameter spaces, non-smooth dynamics (Pusher Slider), or complex control objectives (Acrobot, Double Pendulum swing-up) where other methods struggle to find good solutions. This is particularly evident in policy optimization, where TSMC successfully learns policies for tasks that PPO and SAC fail to solve reliably.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advanced Control System Design:** TSMC could facilitate the design of more robust and optimal controllers for complex robotic systems, especially those benefiting from differentiable simulators and requiring fine-grained control over trajectories.
- **Bridging Optimal Control and RL:** The framework strengthens the connection between optimal control and reinforcement learning by applying advanced Bayesian inference techniques to both. This could inspire new algorithms that leverage probabilistic modeling for more effective learning and control.
- **Foundation for Differentiable Physics Applications:** As differentiable physics simulators mature, methods like TSMC that can effectively leverage exact gradients will become increasingly valuable for tasks such as sim-to-real transfer, model-based RL, and robot learning in contact-rich environments.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges that TSMC can be computationally and memory intensive, particularly due to differentiating through rollouts and maintaining many particles. Future work suggested includes:
- Integrating TSMC with mature differentiable contact solvers for contact-rich tasks in RL.
- Accelerating TSMC through customized low-level GPU kernels and hardware acceleration to address its computational demands.

Overall, TSMC offers a methodology for trajectory and policy optimization that is well-suited for problems where differentiable dynamics are available, and where the cost landscape is complex enough to challenge purely gradient-based or purely sampling-based approaches.
