# VLA-GSE: Boosting Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning in VLA with Generalized and Specialized Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research Asia and Tsinghua University developed VLA-GSE, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework that enhances adaptation capacity for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models while preserving multimodal understanding. It achieved an 81.2% average zero-shot success rate on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, outperforming full fine-tuning by 6.3 percentage points and existing PEFT methods by 4.4 percentage points.

## Problem

- Full fine-tuning (FFT) of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robotic control leads to overfitting on limited robotic datasets and catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained vision-language capabilities.
- Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods often lack sufficient adaptation capacity for the precise, fine-grained control required by complex robotic manipulation tasks.
- A core challenge in VLA is to maintain the broad generalization power of the VLM while simultaneously acquiring specialized skills for specific robotic tasks, balancing generalization and specialization.

## Method

![[2605.06175-method.png]]
*The VLA-GSE framework. It uses Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to initialize a "Generalized Expert" for common knowledge and a set of "Specialized Experts" that are dynamically selected by a router for input-specific adaptation. The frozen backbone weights are adjusted to maintain consistency with the pre-trained state at initialization.*

- **VLA-GSE** introduces a ==parameter-efficient fine-tuning== framework with a dual architecture of ==generalized== and ==specialized experts== for adapting pre-trained VLM weights.
- It utilizes ==Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)== of the frozen VLM weight matrix to initialize these experts: top singular components form a generalized expert, and ==disjoint residual components== initialize multiple specialized experts, which are then selected via an input-dependent routing mechanism.
- The framework incorporates ==expert-wise gradient scale balancing== to equalize optimization dynamics across specialized experts and a ==backbone weight adjustment== to preserve the pre-trained VLM's functional behavior in expectation at initialization.

## Results

- **VLA-GSE** achieved an **81.2%** average zero-shot success rate on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, surpassing Full Fine-Tuning by **+6.3 pp** (**74.9%**) and the strongest PEFT baseline (GOAT) by **+4.4 pp** (**76.8%**).
- The framework maintained multimodal understanding capabilities comparable to standard LoRA-based PEFT methods, significantly outperforming both full fine-tuning and co-trained VLA models in knowledge retention.
- Real-world experiments on an AgileX PiPER robot across four manipulation tasks and distribution shifts showed VLA-GSE achieving the highest average success rate of **82.5%**, outperforming other baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing adaptation into generalized and specialized components, initialized through SVD, effectively allocates trainable parameters to capture both common transferable knowledge and input-dependent control refinement.
> - Addressing optimization imbalances through expert-wise gradient scaling and preserving the backbone's initial state with expectation-based weight adjustment are crucial for stable and effective PEFT of complex expert-based architectures.
> - A parameter-efficient approach can achieve superior performance in VLA tasks compared to full fine-tuning, demonstrating that enhanced adaptation capacity for robotic control does not necessitate sacrificing foundational multimodal understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents an ongoing effort to enable general-purpose robotic control by leveraging the rich visual-semantic understanding derived from pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models aim to translate visual observations and language instructions into low-level actions, facilitating advancements in complex manipulation tasks and broad generalization.

However, adapting large, pre-trained VLMs to the specific requirements of robotic control presents several challenges. Full fine-tuning (FFT), while offering high model expressivity, often leads to overfitting on the comparatively limited robotic datasets. It can also result in catastrophic forgetting, where the model degrades its pre-trained vision-language capabilities in favor of task-specific robotic skills.

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), have been explored as an alternative. These methods generally preserve pre-trained knowledge more effectively than FFT by updating only a small subset of parameters. However, existing PEFT approaches often demonstrate insufficient adaptation capacity for the precise control required in many embodied robotic tasks.

The current work addresses this recognized gap by aiming to develop a PEFT framework that can simultaneously enhance adaptation capacity for robotic control and preserve the foundational knowledge of the pre-trained VLM. It builds upon prior research into SVD-based PEFT methods that exploit spectral components for knowledge preservation or task-relevant subspace exposure, and MoE-style methods that improve capacity through routed low-rank experts. VLA-GSE distinguishes itself by tailoring these concepts to the VLA domain, specifically combining a shared generalized expert with routed specialized experts to support both transferable adaptation and input-specific control refinement, along with mechanisms to stabilize optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to propose and validate VLA-GSE, a parameter-efficient VLA fine-tuning framework designed to enhance the adaptation capabilities required for precise robot control tasks while retaining the knowledge-preservation advantages typically associated with PEFT methods.

The motivation for this work arises from several observed limitations in current VLM-to-VLA adaptation strategies:
- **Catastrophic Forgetting in Full Fine-Tuning (FFT):** While VLMs provide robust multimodal priors, fully fine-tuning them on robotic data can lead to overfitting due to data scarcity and catastrophic forgetting. This process overwrites the generalized visual-semantic knowledge, reducing the model's ability to generalize across diverse environments and instructions beyond the training distribution. This degrades the core VLM capability that makes them attractive for VLA in the first place.
- **Insufficient Adaptation Capacity in Existing PEFT:** Conventional PEFT methods, such as LoRA, are more effective at preserving pre-trained VLM knowledge. However, they frequently lack the expressivity and capacity to learn the fine-grained, precise control policies demanded by complex robotic manipulation tasks. This results in under-adaptation, where the model fails to achieve high success rates in environments requiring detailed motor skills.
- **Balancing Generalization and Specialization:** A core challenge in VLA is to maintain the broad generalization power of the VLM while simultaneously acquiring specialized skills for specific robotic tasks. Existing approaches struggle to strike this balance effectively.

To address these motivations, VLA-GSE was designed with specific technical goals:
- **Spectral Decomposition for Capacity Allocation:** Utilize spectral decomposition of the frozen backbone's weight matrix (via Singular Value Decomposition, SVD) to intelligently allocate trainable parameters. Leading singular components are assigned to "generalized experts" to capture common, transferable knowledge, while disjoint residual components are assigned to "specialized experts" to handle input-dependent, fine-grained adaptation. This aims to maximize adaptation capacity within a fixed parameter budget and reduce redundancy.
- **Mitigating Optimization Imbalance:** Spectral decomposition can result in large singular-value imbalances across specialized experts, leading to uneven update magnitudes during training. The objective is to introduce an "expert-wise gradient scale balancing" mechanism to compensate for this imbalance and harmonize optimization scales, ensuring all experts are effectively trained.
- **Preserving Backbone Initialization:** The proposed expert architecture introduces a non-zero low-rank perturbation at initialization. A key objective is to implement a "backbone weight adjustment" mechanism that preserves the backbone-equivalent weight in expectation at initialization, preventing an undesired shift from the pre-trained state before fine-tuning commences.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** Ultimately, the framework aims to improve the VLA model's robustness and generalization capabilities across various real-world and simulated distribution shifts, ensuring both high task success rates and strong retention of VLM's multimodal understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-GSE is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework applied to the Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone within a standard VLM-to-VLA pipeline. The action head, which generates robot actions, is fully fine-tuned using an OpenVLA-OFT-style MLP architecture and parallel decoding. The core of VLA-GSE involves decomposing the adaptation of a pre-trained weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ into a structured low-rank update while freezing most backbone parameters.

The methodology is comprised of three primary components:

#### 3.1. Generalized and Specialized Expert Initialization via SVD

The framework initiates adaptation by performing a Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of the frozen pre-trained weight matrix $W_0 = U \Sigma V^{\top}$. Singular values are sorted in descending order to facilitate a spectral decomposition and strategic allocation of components.

- **Generalized Expert:** The top $r_g$ singular values and their corresponding singular vectors are allocated to a generalized expert. This expert is intended to capture common, foundational knowledge and operates as an always-on component. Its initialization weights, $B_g \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r_g}$ and $A_g \in \mathbb{R}^{r_g \times n}$, are derived from $\Sigma^{1/2}$ and scaled by $s_g$. This design leverages the observation that leading singular components encapsulate the dominant energy of the pre-trained weight matrix.

- **Specialized Experts:** The remaining dominant singular components, indexed from $r_g+1$ to $r_g+dE$, are evenly partitioned to initialize $E$ specialized experts. Each specialized expert is assigned a contiguous block of rank $d$. The $i$-th specialized expert is initialized with $B_i^s \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$ and $A_i^s \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$ from its designated SVD segment, scaled by $s_i^s$. These experts are designed for input-specific adaptation.

- **Specialized Expert Selection Method (Routing):** During the forward pass, a trainable router, $W_z \in \mathbb{R}^{E \times n}$, generates routing logits $z(x) = W_z x$ for an input $x$. A sparse subset of specialized experts (top-k) is selected based on these logits. Routing weights $w_i(x)$ are computed by applying a softmax function exclusively over the selected top-k logits, ensuring dynamic, input-dependent expert composition.

- **Load Balancing Auxiliary Loss:** To counteract the tendency of sparse routing to favor a small subset of experts, an auxiliary load-balancing loss, $L_{bal}$, is introduced. For each GSE block, this loss penalizes imbalances between the empirical selection frequency ($f_i^{(l)}$) and the average routing probability ($P_i^{(l)}$) of each expert $i$. The total loss function is $L_{final} = \|\hat{a} - a\|_1 + \alpha \sum_{l=1}^L L_{bal}^{(l)}$, where the first term is the action regression loss and $\alpha$ is a weighting factor.

#### 3.2. Gradient Scale Balancing

The spectral initialization of specialized experts can result in significant variations in the magnitudes of their singular values, leading to uneven gradient scales during training.
- **Problem Identification:** The expected squared Frobenius norms of the localized gradients for the $i$-th specialized expert ($E[\|g_i^A\|_F^2]$ and $E[\|g_i^B\|_F^2]$) are shown to be jointly determined by the routing coefficient's second moment ($\rho_w$), the expert's scaling factor ($s_i^s$), and projected second-order gradient energy ($\alpha_L^i, \alpha_R^i$) on its corresponding singular subspaces.
- **Proposed Solution:** To equalize the optimization scales, the specialized expert scaling factor $s_i^s$ is set inversely proportional to the trace of its allocated singular value matrix: $s_i^s = s_{base} \cdot \frac{C}{\text{Tr}(\Sigma_i)}$. This rule is justified under the assumption that projected second-order gradient statistics are approximately uniform across experts, which is satisfied under an isotropic global gradient variance assumption.

#### 3.3. Backbone Weight Adjustment

Unlike traditional PEFT methods that ensure zero perturbation at initialization, VLA-GSE's generalized and specialized experts introduce a non-zero low-rank perturbation. To maintain consistency with the original pre-trained weight $W_0$ at initialization:
- **Problem Identification:** The equivalent weight of the GSE block, $W_{eq}(x)$, includes contributions from the generalized and selected specialized experts, causing it to deviate from $W_0$ at initialization.
- **Proposed Solution:** The frozen backbone weight $W_0$ is adjusted to $\tilde{W}_0 = W_0 - W_{res}$, where $W_{res}$ is the expected residual shift introduced by the experts. This $W_{res}$ is calculated as the sum of the generalized expert's contribution and the average expected contribution of all specialized experts (assuming uniform routing in expectation at initialization). This adjustment ensures that $E_x[W_{eq}(x)] = W_0$ at initialization, preserving the pre-trained backbone's functional behavior in expectation.

The VLA-GSE framework uses Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct as the foundation VLM backbone. The low-rank dimension ($r$) is 16, with 1 generalized expert of rank 2 and 7 specialized experts, each of rank 2. A Top-2 gating strategy is used for specialized expert selection. Overall, this configuration involves 356 generalized experts and 2492 specialized experts across the architecture. Decoupled learning rates are used: $1 \times 10^{-5}$ for GSE parameters and $1 \times 10^{-4}$ for the action MLP head.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLA-GSE was conducted across simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, alongside assessments of multimodal understanding.

#### 4.1. Parameter Efficiency

VLA-GSE demonstrates high parameter efficiency. Out of 4,551.85 million total model parameters (for Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct), only 114.04 million parameters are optimized, representing 2.51% of the full model. This includes 48.41 million parameters for the GSE modules and 65.62 million for the action head, with 4,437.82 million non-GSE VLM parameters remaining frozen.

#### 4.2. LIBERO-Plus Benchmark Results (Simulation)

On the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, which assesses VLA models' generalization across seven perturbation dimensions (Camera, Robot, Language, Light, Background, Noise, Layout), VLA-GSE achieved an 81.2% average zero-shot success rate. This performance exceeded strong prior baselines, including ABot-M0 (80.5%) and VLANeXt (80.1%). Compared to fine-tuning strategies with the same Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct backbone and comparable trainable parameter budgets (approx. 2.51% or 114M parameters):
- VLA-GSE outperformed Full Fine-Tuning (FFT) by 6.3 percentage points (74.9% for FFT). FFT exhibited underperformance, attributed to catastrophic forgetting.
- VLA-GSE surpassed the strongest PEFT baseline, GOAT, by 4.4 percentage points (76.8% for GOAT).
- The improvements were broad, with VLA-GSE demonstrating the strongest average robustness on Robot, Language, Light, and Background perturbations, while matching the best performance on Layout.
Qualitative analysis of attention maps in challenging scenarios (e.g., dark lighting) showed VLA-GSE maintaining precise attention on graspable edges of target objects, unlike baselines (FFT, LoRA, $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$) which displayed scattered or mislocalized attention.

#### 4.3. Knowledge Retention after VLA Fine-Tuning in Terms of Multimodal Understanding

Evaluation on a diverse set of multimodal understanding benchmarks (MMMU, MMStar, OCRBench, MMB-en, DocVQA, InfoVQA, AI2D, RealWorldQA) assessed knowledge retention.
- FFT resulted in significant and consistent degradation across benchmarks, indicating severe catastrophic forgetting.
- Co-trained VLA models (e.g., $\pi_{0.5}$) also showed multimodal understanding performance far below general-purpose VLMs.
- VLA-GSE maintained multimodal understanding capabilities comparable to standard LoRA-based PEFT (Qwen3-VL-LoRA), and substantially better than both FFT and co-trained VLA baselines. This suggests that VLA-GSE's enhanced embodied performance does not compromise its original vision-language understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the LIBERO-Plus zero-shot Long suite highlighted the contribution of VLA-GSE's design elements:
- The full GSE framework achieved 74.1% success.
- Removing SVD initialization ("w.o. SVD Initialization") led to the most severe performance degradation (60.9%).
- Replacing generalized experts with specialized ones ("w.o. Generalized Experts," 67.2%) or specialized experts with generalized ones ("w.o. Specialized Experts," 63.1%) resulted in notable drops, indicating the importance of both types of experts.
- Removing the auxiliary load-balancing loss ("w.o. Auxiliary Loss," 72.0%) showed the necessity of encouraging balanced expert utilization.
- Disabling expert-wise gradient scaling ("w.o. Gradient Scaling," 72.1%) demonstrated the importance of balancing optimization dynamics.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments

Real-world experiments on an AgileX PiPER robot across four manipulation tasks and four distribution shifts (lighting, language, clutter layout, background) showed:
- VLA-GSE achieved the highest average success rate of 82.5%.
- It outperformed $\pi_{0.5}$ (74.2%), Qwen3-VL-FFT (65.8%), Qwen3-VL-LoRA (61.7%), and $\pi_0$ (55.8%).
- Qwen3-VL-LoRA consistently underperformed Qwen3-VL-FFT in real-world settings, which is consistent with the simulation findings regarding vanilla LoRA's adaptation capacity for robust action grounding.
- VLA-GSE's clear margin over baselines indicated its effectiveness in handling real-world distribution shifts.
- Inference time analysis showed VLA-GSE to be efficient, incurring only a modest additional overhead compared to Qwen3-VL-FFT, and remaining practical for real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents VLA-GSE as a parameter-efficient framework for adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models to Vision-Language-Action policies. Its significance lies in demonstrating that it is possible to substantially improve adaptation capacity for precise robotic control while simultaneously retaining the knowledge-preservation benefits characteristic of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT).

The core contribution of VLA-GSE, through its generalized-specialized expert decomposition, SVD-based initialization, expert-wise gradient scale balancing, and expectation-based backbone weight adjustment, offers a systematic approach to overcoming the trade-off between knowledge retention and task-specific adaptation that has hindered VLM-to-VLA transfer.

The consistent outperformance of VLA-GSE against strong Full Fine-Tuning (FFT) and existing PEFT baselines in both simulation and real-world manipulation tasks, under comparable trainable-parameter budgets, suggests that this methodology provides a practical and compute-efficient alternative. It reduces the computational cost and data requirements associated with full model fine-tuning or heavy co-training strategies that often require extensive vision-language data to maintain pre-trained knowledge.

The findings position effective PEFT, particularly through architecturally informed designs like VLA-GSE, as a promising direction for VLM-to-VLA adaptation. This could accelerate the development and deployment of general-purpose robot policies by making the adaptation process more efficient and robust. While the current evaluation focuses on robotics, the underlying principles of balancing generalized knowledge and specialized adaptation within a parameter-efficient framework could potentially be extended to other high-precision multimodal domains, such as medical image analysis or complex financial forecasting, where foundational model adaptation requires both broad understanding and granular specialization.
