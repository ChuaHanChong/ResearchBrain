# VLA-GSE: Boosting Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning in VLA with Generalized and Specialized Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research Asia and Tsinghua University developed VLA-GSE, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework that enhances adaptation capacity for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models while preserving multimodal understanding. It achieved an 81.2% average zero-shot success rate on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, outperforming full fine-tuning by 6.3 percentage points and existing PEFT methods by 4.4 percentage points.

## Problem

- Full fine-tuning (FFT) of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robotic control leads to overfitting on limited robotic datasets and catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained vision-language capabilities.
- Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods often lack sufficient adaptation capacity for the precise, fine-grained control required by complex robotic manipulation tasks.
- A core challenge in VLA is to maintain the broad generalization power of the VLM while simultaneously acquiring specialized skills for specific robotic tasks, balancing generalization and specialization.

## Method

![[2605.06175-method.png]]
*The VLA-GSE framework. It uses Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to initialize a "Generalized Expert" for common knowledge and a set of "Specialized Experts" that are dynamically selected by a router for input-specific adaptation. The frozen backbone weights are adjusted to maintain consistency with the pre-trained state at initialization.*

- **VLA-GSE** introduces a ==parameter-efficient fine-tuning== framework with a dual architecture of ==generalized== and ==specialized experts== for adapting pre-trained VLM weights.
- It utilizes ==Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)== of the frozen VLM weight matrix to initialize these experts: top singular components form a generalized expert, and ==disjoint residual components== initialize multiple specialized experts, which are then selected via an input-dependent routing mechanism.
- The framework incorporates ==expert-wise gradient scale balancing== to equalize optimization dynamics across specialized experts and a ==backbone weight adjustment== to preserve the pre-trained VLM's functional behavior in expectation at initialization.

## Results

- **VLA-GSE** achieved an **81.2%** average zero-shot success rate on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, surpassing Full Fine-Tuning by **+6.3 pp** (**74.9%**) and the strongest PEFT baseline (GOAT) by **+4.4 pp** (**76.8%**).
- The framework maintained multimodal understanding capabilities comparable to standard LoRA-based PEFT methods, significantly outperforming both full fine-tuning and co-trained VLA models in knowledge retention.
- Real-world experiments on an AgileX PiPER robot across four manipulation tasks and distribution shifts showed VLA-GSE achieving the highest average success rate of **82.5%**, outperforming other baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing adaptation into generalized and specialized components, initialized through SVD, effectively allocates trainable parameters to capture both common transferable knowledge and input-dependent control refinement.
> - Addressing optimization imbalances through expert-wise gradient scaling and preserving the backbone's initial state with expectation-based weight adjustment are crucial for stable and effective PEFT of complex expert-based architectures.
> - A parameter-efficient approach can achieve superior performance in VLA tasks compared to full fine-tuning, demonstrating that enhanced adaptation capacity for robotic control does not necessitate sacrificing foundational multimodal understanding.
