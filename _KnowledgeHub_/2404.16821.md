# How Far Are We to GPT-4V? Closing the Gap to Commercial Multimodal Models with Open-Source Suites

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces InternVL 1.5, an open-source multimodal large language model that reduces the performance gap with commercial models across multiple dimensions. The model achieves state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks, particularly excelling in OCR, chart understanding, and bilingual (English-Chinese) tasks.

## Problem

- Open-source multimodal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit a significant performance gap compared to proprietary commercial models like GPT-4V, particularly in parameter scale, image resolution handling, and multilingual capabilities.
- Existing open-source MLLMs are often limited by fixed, lower image resolutions, which leads to loss of fine-grained details and poor performance on tasks like document or scene text understanding.
- Many open-source MLLMs are primarily English-centric, resulting in suboptimal performance in non-English tasks, especially those involving OCR or cultural nuances.

## Method

- **InternVL 1.5** employs a large-scale ==InternViT-6B== vision encoder continuously pre-trained for high-resolution processing and OCR capabilities, integrated with an ==InternLM2-20B== language model via an ==MLP projector== (the ==ViT-MLP-LLM== paradigm).
- It implements a ==dynamic high-resolution strategy== that processes images using 448x448 pixel tiles (with a ==pixel shuffle== to cut visual tokens), supporting resolutions up to 4K during testing, and includes a thumbnail for global context.
- The model's multilingual proficiency is enhanced through a meticulously curated, high-quality ==bilingual (English-Chinese) dataset== and a scalable ==data translation pipeline== that converts English datasets into Chinese.

## Results

- InternVL 1.5 achieves state-of-the-art results in **8 out of 18** multimodal benchmarks, notably outperforming proprietary models on ChartQA, OCRBench, MMBench-CN, CCBench, and MathVista.
- It demonstrates superior hallucination reduction (best on HallusionBench) and robust bilingual (English-Chinese) capabilities, excelling in Chinese scene understanding and OCR tasks.
- Qualitative analyses show InternVL 1.5's comparable performance to GPT-4V in general QA and scientific understanding, and superior performance in Chinese traditional culture understanding and multi-image dialogue.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Larger vision foundation models (VFMs) significantly enhance the performance of larger language models (LLMs) in complex multimodal tasks, as observed by InternVL 1.2's performance compared to LLaVA-NeXT.
> - Dynamic high-resolution processing primarily benefits OCR-related and detailed understanding tasks, while general multimodal tasks show stable or slightly reduced performance at higher resolutions, indicating an adaptive capability.
> - The model demonstrates strong zero-shot capabilities for multi-image dialogues despite being trained only on single-image inputs, suggesting robust visual representation learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses one of the most pressing challenges in the field of multimodal artificial intelligence: the significant performance disparity between open-source large multimodal models (MLLMs) and their proprietary, commercially developed counterparts, such as OpenAI's GPT-4V, Google's Gemini series, and Alibaba's Qwen-VL-Max. The authors frame this gap across three critical dimensions:

Firstly, **Parameter Scale**, where proprietary models typically boast upwards of 100 billion parameters, while open-source models often combine smaller language models (7B or 13B) with comparatively tiny vision foundation models (VFMs) of around 300 million parameters. This work aims to narrow this gap by employing a larger VFM (InternViT-6B) alongside a substantial LLM (InternLM2-20B), reaching a combined parameter count more competitive with commercial offerings.

Secondly, **Image Resolution**, where proprietary MLLMs leverage dynamic resolution approaches that preserve original aspect ratios and support high-resolution inputs (e.g., up to 4K) for detailed understanding. In contrast, many open-source models are constrained by fixed, lower resolutions (e.g., 336x336 or 448x448), leading to performance degradation on complex visual tasks like document or scene text understanding. InternVL 1.5 introduces a sophisticated dynamic high-resolution strategy to overcome this limitation.

Thirdly, **Multilingual Capability**, an area where proprietary models often benefit from extensive multilingual training data, enabling robust performance across diverse languages. Open-source alternatives, however, frequently rely on English-centric datasets and zero-shot transfer for other languages, resulting in suboptimal performance, particularly in tasks involving non-English text or cultural nuances. This paper explicitly targets enhanced bilingual proficiency, focusing on English and Chinese, through a meticulously curated dataset and a novel translation pipeline.

The authors position InternVL 1.5 as a direct effort to bridge these specific gaps, building upon the "ViT-MLP-LLM" architectural paradigm prevalent in open-source MLLMs like the LLaVA series and MiniGPT-4. By tackling these three aspects systematically, InternVL 1.5 seeks to raise the bar for open-source MLLM capabilities, offering a viable and competitive alternative to closed-source commercial models and contributing significantly to the democratization of advanced AI research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **bridge the capability gap between open-source and proprietary commercial models in multimodal understanding**. The authors explicitly state their ambition to make InternVL 1.5 a leading open-source MLLM that can compete directly with state-of-the-art commercial systems like GPT-4V, Gemini Pro 1.5, and Claude-3 Opus.

This primary goal is underpinned by three specific, interrelated motivations and objectives, each corresponding to a key improvement introduced in InternVL 1.5:

1. **Develop a Strong Vision Encoder with Continuous Learning:** The motivation here stems from the observation that existing vision foundation models (VFMs) commonly used in open-source MLLMs (like CLIP-ViT) are often trained at fixed, low resolutions (e.g., 224x224) and tend to perform poorly on high-resolution images or domain-specific content like documents. The objective is to enhance the visual understanding capabilities of a large-scale VFM, InternViT-6B, through a continuous learning strategy using high-quality image-text data. A crucial aspect is to ensure the VFM's features are highly transferable and reusable across different large language models, promoting modularity and adaptability in MLLM development.

2. **Implement a Dynamic High-Resolution Strategy:** The authors are motivated by the fixed-resolution limitations of many open-source MLLMs, which often distort aspect ratios or lose fine-grained details in complex images, contrasting sharply with the dynamic, high-resolution capabilities of proprietary models that preserve original image quality. The objective is to enable InternVL 1.5 to support input resolutions up to 4K by intelligently dividing images into tiles of 448x448 pixels, dynamically adapting to aspect ratios, and incorporating a thumbnail for global context. This aims to facilitate detailed scene and document understanding without prohibitive computational costs.

3. **Curate a High-Quality Bilingual (English-Chinese) Dataset:** Recognizing that most open-source MLLMs are primarily English-centric and perform sub-optimally in non-English tasks, particularly those involving OCR or cultural understanding, the motivation is to significantly enhance InternVL 1.5's multilingual proficiency. The objective is to collect and meticulously annotate a diverse, high-quality bilingual dataset covering common scenes, document images, charts, and conversations, augmented by a scalable data translation pipeline. This specifically targets superior performance in OCR- and Chinese-related tasks, aiming to surpass even proprietary models in these areas.

By addressing these three aspects, the project seeks not only to close the performance gap but also to contribute robust, flexible, and accessible open-source MLLM solutions to the research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InternVL 1.5 adopts the widely-used "ViT-MLP-LLM" architectural paradigm, combining a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) with a large language model (LLM) via a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) projector.

**Core Components:**
- **Vision Encoder:** InternViT-6B, a large-scale vision foundation model, serves as the backbone for visual feature extraction. The authors emphasize its strength and transferability.
- **Language Model:** InternLM2-20B, specifically its chat version, provides robust language processing capabilities.
- **MLP Projector:** A randomly initialized MLP connects the vision encoder and the language model, projecting visual features into the LLM's token space.
- **Visual Token Management:** A simple pixel shuffle operation is employed to reduce the number of visual tokens to one-quarter, enhancing scalability for high resolutions. A 448x448 image is thus represented by 256 visual tokens.

**Three Major Improvements Integrated into the Methodology:**

1. **Strong Vision Encoder (InternViT-6B-448px-V1.5):**
 - **Continuous Pre-training:** The development of InternViT-6B-448px-V1.5 is a continuation of pre-training from InternViT-6B-448px-V1.2.
 - **Layer Optimization:** Based on prior observations, features from the fourth-to-last layer of InternViT-6B were found to be optimal for multimodal tasks. Consequently, the last three layers were discarded, reducing the model from 48 to 45 layers.
 - **Resolution Increase:** The base training resolution of InternViT-6B was increased from 224x224 to 448x448 pixels.
 - **Joint Training:** Both the vision encoder and the MLP were activated for training, utilizing a mixed dataset comprising image captioning and OCR-specific data to endow the model with high-resolution processing and OCR capabilities.
 - **Data Scale & Diversity:** For V1.5, the pre-training dataset's scale, quality, and diversity were further enhanced, and the training image resolution was expanded from fixed 448x448 to dynamic 448x448 tiles.
 - **Compatibility:** The InternViT weights demonstrated strong compatibility and portability, even with a change in the LLM backbone (from Nous-Hermes-2-Yi-34B to InternLM2-20B).

2. **Dynamic High-Resolution:**
 - **Inspiration:** Inspired by UReader, this strategy allows the model to adapt to varying resolutions and aspect ratios.
 - **Tile-based Processing:** Images are segmented into tiles of 448x448 pixels. During training, the number of tiles ranges from 1 to 12. Crucially, during testing, the model can zero-shot scale up to 40 tiles (equivalent to 4K resolution).
 - **Dynamic Aspect Ratio Matching:** To minimize distortion, the system dynamically matches the input image's aspect ratio to an optimal ratio from a pre-defined set of 35 combinations (derived from 1 to 12 tiles). For low-resolution images, it prioritizes ratios that don't excessively enlarge the image.
 - **Global Context Thumbnail:** Alongside the tiles, a 448x448 thumbnail of the entire image is included to provide global context, aiding the model's overall scene understanding. This results in visual token counts ranging from 256 to 3,328 during training, and up to 10,496 during testing.

3. **High-Quality Bilingual Dataset:**
 - **Comprehensive Pre-training Dataset (Table 1a):** A diverse collection of publicly available sources was used, covering captioning (Laion-EN/ZH, COYO, GRIT, COCO, TextCaps), object detection (Objects365, GRIT, All-Seeing), and various OCR tasks (Wukong-OCR, LaionCOCO-OCR, Common Crawl PDF, and numerous smaller, specialized OCR datasets). Large-scale OCR data was constructed using PaddleOCR for Chinese and English images.
 - **Fine-tuning Dataset (Table 1b):** This stage utilized a rich set of datasets spanning general QA, scientific understanding, chart interpretation, mathematics, knowledge-based QA, more OCR and document understanding, visual grounding, and multi-modal conversation. Text-only datasets were also included to maintain the LLM's linguistic capabilities.
 - **Data Translation Pipeline (Figure 5):** To boost multilingual capabilities, a pipeline using state-of-the-art open-source LLMs (or GPT-3.5) translates English datasets into Chinese (and potentially other languages). This prompt-based translation ensures natural and fluent Chinese output, enabling the expansion of bilingual data without manual annotation.

**Training Process:**
InternVL 1.5 was trained in two stages:
1. **Pre-training:** Focused on optimizing the InternViT-6B vision encoder and the MLP projector for visual feature extraction.
2. **Fine-tuning:** The entire model (all 26 billion parameters) was fine-tuned to enhance multimodal capabilities.
Both stages used a context length of 4096 and adopted the response formatting prompts from LLaVA 1.5. Evaluation was primarily supported by VLMEvalKit.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InternVL 1.5 demonstrates significant progress in closing the gap between open-source and proprietary MLLMs, achieving competitive performance and, in several key areas, surpassing leading commercial models.

**Quantitative Performance across 18 Benchmarks (Tables 2 & 3):**
- **Overall State-of-the-Art:** InternVL 1.5 achieves state-of-the-art results in 8 out of 18 multimodal benchmarks, showcasing its broad capabilities.
- **OCR-related Image Understanding:**
 - InternVL 1.5 shows strong performance comparable to proprietary models and significantly outperforms its open-source predecessors (LLaVA-NeXT and InternVL 1.2).
 - It achieves **state-of-the-art** results on **ChartQA** and **OCRBench**, outperforming all listed proprietary models (including GPT-4V, Grok-1.5V, Claude-3 Opus, and Gemini Pro 1.5). It also performs very strongly on DocVQA and TextVQA. This highlights the effectiveness of its dynamic high-resolution and high-quality bilingual OCR dataset.
- **General Multimodal Evaluation:**
 - InternVL 1.5 substantially narrows the performance gap with proprietary models compared to other open-source models.
 - It achieves the **best performance on HallusionBench**, indicating superior hallucination reduction.
 - Thanks to its bilingual dataset, it exhibits **robust Chinese language capabilities**, significantly outperforming both open-source and proprietary methods on **MMBench-CN** and **CCBench**.
 - While competitive with models like MM1 and Gemini Pro 1.0 on MMMU, it shows a slight decline from its 40B-parameter predecessor (InternVL 1.2), which the authors attribute to the smaller LLM size (26B vs 40B). A similar trend is observed in MMT-Bench.
- **Mathematical Reasoning (MathVista):** InternVL 1.5 **outperforms all competing models**, including GPT-4V, by a notable margin, demonstrating strong capabilities in complex mathematical and visual reasoning tasks.
- **Multi-Turn Conversation (ConvBench, MMT-Bench):** InternVL 1.5 is a leading performer among open-source models in multi-turn conversational abilities. However, it still lags behind GPT-4V in this domain, indicating an area for future improvement.

**Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Larger LLMs Benefit from Larger VFMs:** A comparison between InternVL 1.2 (40B LLM + 6B VFM) and LLaVA-NeXT (34B LLM + 300M VFM) suggests that for larger LLMs, a commensurately larger VFM can significantly enhance the model's ability to handle complex multimodal tasks, leading to overall improved performance.
- **Dynamic Resolution is Task-Dependent:** The study on dynamic resolution (Figure 6) reveals that while OCR-related tasks (DocVQA, InfoVQA, TextVQA, OCRBench) significantly benefit from increased resolution, other general tasks (AI2D, MMMU, MMBench, HallusionBench) show either stable or slightly reduced performance at higher resolutions. This indicates the model's robustness and ability to adapt resolution based on task demands, balancing efficiency and detail. The zero-shot scaling up to 4K resolution (40 tiles) during testing proved effective.

**Qualitative Results (Figures 7-12):**
- **General QA:** Comparable performance with GPT-4V, though GPT-4V sometimes shows excessive refusal due to privacy concerns.
- **OCR-Related QA:** InternVL 1.5 demonstrates superior ability in Chinese scene understanding (outperforming GPT-4V's translation limitations) and strong performance in chart understanding.
- **Scientific Understanding:** Both InternVL 1.5 and GPT-4V show comparable capabilities, with InternVL 1.5 occasionally providing more precise analyses.
- **Chinese Traditional Culture:** InternVL 1.5 exhibits a deeper cultural understanding and provides more detailed descriptions compared to GPT-4V.
- **Object Localization:** InternVL 1.5 matches GPT-4V's accuracy in localizing objects and understanding spatial relationships.
- **Multi-Image Dialogue:** Surprisingly, despite being trained solely on single-image inputs, InternVL 1.5 displays strong zero-shot capabilities for multi-image dialogues, providing detailed and accurate comparisons.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of InternVL 1.5 carries significant implications for the field of multimodal AI, particularly within the open-source community.

Firstly, its primary significance lies in its **effective reduction of the capability gap between open-source and proprietary MLLMs**. By demonstrating competitive, and in several instances superior, performance against commercial giants like GPT-4V and Gemini Pro 1.5, InternVL 1.5 proves that high-caliber multimodal understanding is achievable with open-source models. This directly challenges the perception that only closed, proprietary systems can reach the frontier of AI capabilities, thus fostering a more democratized and accessible research landscape.

Secondly, the methodological contributions are impactful. The **continuous learning strategy for the large-scale InternViT-6B vision encoder** highlights a path for developing robust, transferable visual representations, moving beyond the limitations of smaller, fixed-resolution VFMs. The successful implementation of a **dynamic high-resolution strategy** signifies a crucial step forward for open-source models to handle real-world image complexities, from fine-grained details in documents to expansive scenes, without compromising on efficiency. Furthermore, the emphasis on a **high-quality bilingual dataset and an innovative data translation pipeline** provides a blueprint for enhancing multilingual capabilities in MLLMs, which is critical for global accessibility and diverse applications. The exceptional performance in Chinese-related and OCR tasks directly validates this approach.

Thirdly, the **open-source release** of InternVL 1.5's code and model weights is a major contribution to the research community. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers, enabling them to build upon cutting-edge technology, reproduce results, and explore new avenues without needing access to vast proprietary datasets or computational resources. This open collaboration is vital for accelerating progress in MLLM development.

Finally, the potential impact extends to various practical applications. InternVL 1.5's demonstrated proficiency in **OCR, document understanding, chart analysis, and mathematical reasoning** makes it highly valuable for tasks requiring precise extraction and interpretation of visual information. Its flexible resolution capability allows users to optimize for specific use cases, balancing computational cost with required detail. While multi-turn conversation remains an area for further refinement, the strong zero-shot performance in multi-image dialogue is promising. Overall, InternVL 1.5 serves as a powerful testament to the advancements possible through focused, collaborative open-source efforts, setting a new benchmark for multimodal AI and encouraging further innovation in the field.
