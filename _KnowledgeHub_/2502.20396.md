# Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning for Vision-Based Dexterous Manipulation on Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> A practical sim-to-real reinforcement learning framework enables vision-based dexterous manipulation on humanoid robots with multi-fingered hands, integrating autotuned robot modeling and hybrid object representations. This method achieves high success rates (e.g., 80% for box lift) and robust zero-shot generalization to unseen objects (60-80% success) on the Fourier GR-1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Current robot manipulation learning relies on costly real-world data and imitation, hindering scalability and versatility for complex tasks.
- Successfully applying sim-to-real reinforcement learning to vision-based, contact-rich, bimanual dexterous manipulation on humanoids is challenging due to inherent complexities and domain gaps.
- Developing generalizable and robust policies for multi-fingered humanoid hands is difficult given noisy hardware, complex coordination, and inefficient exploration in vast state-action spaces.

## Method

- A comprehensive ==sim-to-real reinforcement learning== "recipe" is proposed, integrating ==autotuned robot modeling== for dynamics and extensive ==domain randomization== for perceptual robustness.
- Reward design is structured around contact and object goals, utilizing ==contact stickers== and ==stage-based rewards== for complex bimanual tasks.
- Policy learning efficiency is enhanced through ==task-aware hand pose initialization== from human priors and a ==divide-and-conquer distillation== strategy for generalist policies.

## Results

- Learned policies achieved high success rates on the Fourier GR-1 humanoid, including **80%** for box lift, **62.3%** for grasp-and-reach, and **52.5%** for bimanual handover.
- The policies demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to unseen real-world objects, achieving **60-80% success**, and maintained performance under external disturbances.
- The methodology successfully enabled cross-embodiment generalization across two morphologically different multi-fingered robot hands and proved the importance of autotuned robot modeling and hybrid object representations for transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automated calibration of simulator physics and URDF parameters using minimal real-world data is crucial for bridging the dynamics gap in sim-to-real transfer on low-cost humanoid hardware.
> - Decomposing complex manipulation tasks into contact and object goals, formalized with "contact stickers," provides an effective and generalizable method for reward design in bimanual dexterous tasks.
> - Combining lightweight human priors for task initialization and a "divide-and-conquer" distillation approach for policy learning significantly improves sample efficiency and generalization for high-dimensional, sparse-reward tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is actively striving for generalizable and robust policies, particularly for complex humanoid platforms. Existing paradigms primarily rely on extensive real-world data collection and imitation learning. While these methods have shown success, they suffer from significant drawbacks: they are expensive, labor-intensive, and inherently difficult to scale to a wide variety of tasks, objects, and environmental conditions. The "sim-to-real" (simulation-to-reality) transfer paradigm offers a compelling alternative, promising the ability to leverage the virtually limitless data and parallelization of simulation environments to train policies that then perform effectively in the real world.

However, the application of sim-to-real reinforcement learning (RL) to dexterous manipulation, especially for complex humanoid robots, has historically been limited. Prior successes in sim-to-real RL have predominantly been in simpler domains such as robot navigation, locomotion, or autonomous drone racing. Within manipulation, successes have largely been confined to single-hand setups or tasks relying on state-based observations rather than raw, high-dimensional vision. The challenge intensifies for vision-based, contact-rich, and particularly *bimanual* manipulation tasks on multi-fingered humanoid robots, which present a unique confluence of high-dimensional observation spaces, complex action spaces, intricate contact dynamics, and coordination requirements.

This paper positions itself directly at the frontier of these challenges. It aims to bridge the gap between simpler sim-to-real RL applications and the complexities of vision-based dexterous manipulation on humanoids. The authors explicitly identify and address several critical hurdles that previous works have not thoroughly explored in this context:
1. **Low-cost Manipulation Systems:** Unlike expensive, high-precision industrial robotic arms, many humanoid platforms use lighter, noisier motors. This makes fine-grained, contact-rich dexterous manipulation and bimanual coordination significantly harder for sim-to-real methods.
2. **Complex Coordination:** Bimanual tasks demand precise, complementary actions from two hands, which is difficult to capture with conventional reward functions.
3. **Exploration Challenges:** The vast state and action spaces of bimanual, multi-fingered systems lead to sparse rewards and inefficient exploration.
4. **Robust Object Perception:** Handling diverse objects with varying physical properties while overcoming the sim-to-real domain gap in visual perception is a major hurdle.

By tackling these challenges comprehensively, the paper advances the state-of-the-art beyond single-hand or state-based control. It presents the first robust sim-to-real transfer of bimanual policies on a novel humanoid platform with multi-fingered hands, thereby pushing the boundaries for generalist robot learning and embodied AI. It builds upon foundational work in deep RL, imitation learning, and previous sim-to-real efforts, but innovates by integrating these elements into a practical "recipe" tailored for the specific demands of humanoid dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a practical and scalable "recipe" for applying sim-to-real reinforcement learning to vision-based dexterous manipulation on humanoid robots. This recipe aims to enable humanoid robots equipped with multi-fingered hands to acquire highly generalizable, robust, and dexterous manipulation skills.

The motivations driving this work are multi-faceted:
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Current methods for learning complex robot manipulation, primarily relying on extensive real-world data collection and imitation learning, are inherently expensive, labor-intensive, and challenging to scale. This significantly impedes the deployment of versatile robots in diverse, unstructured environments.
- **Unlocking Sim-to-Real Potential:** While sim-to-real RL has shown promise in other robotics domains (e.g., locomotion, navigation), its successful extension to the intricate demands of vision-based, contact-rich, bimanual manipulation on humanoids remains an open challenge. The authors aim to demonstrate that this extension is not only viable but also highly effective.
- **Addressing Specific Technical Hurdles:** The paper explicitly identifies four critical challenges that hinder the widespread adoption of sim-to-real RL for humanoid dexterous manipulation:
 1. **Environment Modeling Gap:** Accurate modeling of robot dynamics and object physics in simulation is crucial for successful transfer, especially for low-cost, noisier humanoid hardware.
 2. **Reward Design Complexity:** Crafting effective and generalizable reward functions for long-horizon, contact-rich bimanual coordination tasks is non-trivial.
 3. **Exploration Inefficiency:** The high dimensionality and sparse rewards characteristic of multi-fingered bimanual tasks lead to inefficient exploration during RL training.
 4. **Perceptual Domain Shift:** The diversity of real-world objects and the inherent sim-to-real gap in visual perception (RGB-D images) make robust vision-based policy transfer difficult.
- **Enabling Generalizable and Robust Robot Behavior:** The ultimate goal is to enable humanoid robots to perform a wide range of tasks reliably, generalize to unseen objects and scenarios, and maintain robust performance even under external disturbances—qualities essential for real-world deployment.

By addressing these core challenges, the authors intend to establish a robust foundation and a practical framework that makes high-performance dexterous manipulation via sim-to-real RL a more accessible and scalable reality for complex humanoid platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a comprehensive "sim-to-real RL recipe" that integrates several novel and practical techniques across four key modules, as illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper.

#### 3.1. Real-to-Sim Modeling

To minimize the gap between simulation and reality, especially for low-cost, noisy humanoid systems:
- **Autotuned Robot Modeling:** A fast, automated calibration module is introduced. This module jointly optimizes simulator physics parameters (e.g., friction, damping) and URDF constants (e.g., link inertia, joint limits) by sampling parameter sets, executing joint-targeted motions in parallel simulations, and selecting the set that minimizes tracking error (Mean Squared Error) against a single, short (less than 4 minutes) real-world calibration trajectory. This eliminates manual tuning and generalizes to any simulator-exposed kinematic parameters.
- **Approximate Object Modeling:** Following prior work, objects are modeled using simple geometric primitives (cylinders, cubes, spheres) with randomized physical parameters (mass, friction). This approximation is found to be sufficient for learning generalizable policies and balancing training efficiency with transferability.

#### 3.2. Generalizable Reward Design

For complex, contact-rich bimanual tasks, the authors propose a structured reward design:
- **Manipulation as Contact and Object Goals:** Tasks are decomposed into sequences of desired hand-object contact transitions and object state changes.
- **Contact Goals:** Reward terms encourage fingertips to reach "contact stickers" – procedurally generated surface markers on simulated objects representing desirable contact locations. The reward function quantifies the inverse distance between fingertips and these markers, allowing for flexible specification of contact preferences.
- **Object Goals:** Reward terms penalize deviations of the current object state (e.g., 3D position) from a target object state.
- **Stage-Based Rewards:** For multi-stage tasks like bimanual handover, a stage variable is used to activate different reward components sequentially, ensuring appropriate coordination across the task horizon.

#### 3.3. Sample Efficient Policy Learning

To mitigate the exploration problem in high-dimensional, sparse-reward tasks:
- **Task-Aware Hand Pose Initialization:** Instead of full demonstrations, task-relevant hand-object configurations are collected from human teleoperation in simulation (casual "play data," taking less than 30 seconds per task). These recorded states are then randomly sampled as initial conditions for training episodes, significantly improving early-stage exploration.
- **Divide-and-Conquer Distillation:** A multi-object task is decomposed into multiple single-object sub-tasks. Specialist policies are trained for each sub-task. High-quality rollouts from these specialists are then filtered and distilled into a single generalist policy. This approach effectively uses the specialists as "simulated teleoperators" to generate curated data, making generalist policy learning more efficient and robust.

#### 3.4. Vision-Based Sim-to-Real Transfer

To address domain gaps in both dynamics and perception:
- **Hybrid Object Representations:** The policy observes a combination of compact low-dimensional features and expressive high-dimensional features.
 - **Sparse Representation:** 3D object center-of-mass position, derived from a third-person camera using object mask tracking and noisy depth readings.
 - **Dense Representation:** Segmented depth image from an egocentric camera. The Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM2) is used for initial mask generation and tracking.
 - This hybrid approach balances the richness of information with the challenge of the sim-to-real gap.
- **Extensive Domain Randomization:** Applied during training to improve robustness to real-world variations. This includes randomization of object parameters (friction, mass, scale), camera parameters, robot physical properties, observation noise, and the application of random forces to objects.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning Implementation

- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used with an asymmetric actor-critic architecture. The actor receives vision-based and proprioceptive inputs, while the critic benefits from privileged state information (e.g., joint velocities, full fingertip positions, object orientation/velocity, randomization scales).
- **Hardware:** The Fourier GR-1 humanoid robot, featuring two 7-DoF arms and multi-fingered hands (Fourier and Inspire hands), serves as the primary hardware platform. NVIDIA Isaac Gym is used for high-performance GPU-based physics simulation.

These integrated methodologies constitute a comprehensive and practical framework designed to enable complex dexterous manipulation skills on humanoids through sim-to-real RL, moving beyond the limitations of prior work.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates a significant advancement in vision-based dexterous manipulation for humanoid robots through sim-to-real RL, presenting robust, generalizable, and adaptive policies.

- **High Task Success Rates and Generalization:** The proposed recipe enables the humanoid robot to achieve impressive success rates on challenging tasks:
 - **Grasp-and-reach:** 62.3% success rate.
 - **Box lift:** 80% success rate.
 - **Bimanual handover:** 52.5% success rate.
 Crucially, these policies exhibit strong **zero-shot generalization** to unseen real-world objects with diverse physical properties (shape, size, color, material, mass), achieving 60-80% success rates on novel objects, and up to 90% on seen objects. This highlights the practical utility of the approach in varied environments.

- **Robustness to Disturbances:** The learned policies prove to be highly robust against external force perturbations, such as knocks, pulls, pushes, and drags, demonstrating their ability to adapt to unexpected real-world interactions.

- **Hardware Adaptability:** The method's versatility is showcased by successful cross-embodiment generalization to two distinct multi-fingered robot hands (Fourier hands and Inspire hands), which have substantially different morphologies and actuation characteristics.

- **Validation of Autotuned Robot Modeling:** Empirical results confirm the critical role of the automated real-to-sim tuning module. Policies trained with autotuned models (lowest MSE between real and simulated tracking errors) exhibit significantly higher sim-to-real transfer success rates (e.g., 8/10 grasp success for lowest MSE vs. 0/10 for highest MSE), proving its effectiveness in bridging the dynamics gap.

- **Effectiveness of Approximate Object Modeling:** Training with randomized simple geometric primitives (cylinders, cubes, spheres) leads to faster convergence in simulation and strong generalization to diverse unseen objects in the real world, indicating a good balance between simplicity and efficacy.

- **Efficacy of Contact-Based Rewards:** The use of "contact stickers" for reward design effectively shapes desired contact behaviors, with different contact patterns emerging clearly based on the placement of these markers, confirming the principle of structured reward design.

- **Impact of Task-Aware Hand Pose Initialization:** Incorporating lightweight human priors through task-aware hand pose initialization significantly boosts exploration efficiency. For instance, grasp success rates improved from 60% to 80% and handover success rates from 0% to 30% with this initialization, demonstrating its utility in jump-starting learning for challenging RL tasks.

- **Benefits of Divide-and-Conquer Distillation:** This strategy enhances both training efficiency and sim-to-real generalization. Training specialist policies on smaller, more homogeneous object sets (e.g., single object or shape-similar groups) leads to better sample efficiency and superior transfer performance (e.g., 90.0% transfer success for policies trained on mixed object groups after distillation, compared to 23.3% for a single policy trained on all objects).

- **Value of Hybrid Object Representations:** Combining sparse (3D object center-of-mass position) and dense (segmented egocentric depth image) object representations drastically improves sim-to-real transfer. For example, pickup success in grasp-and-reach was 10/10 with the hybrid approach compared to 2/10 with depth-only, especially for tasks demanding precise object geometry understanding.

In summary, the paper provides compelling evidence that its integrated "recipe" effectively addresses the long-standing challenges of sim-to-real transfer for complex, vision-based dexterous manipulation on humanoid robots, yielding highly capable and robust policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial step forward in the field of robot learning and embodied AI, particularly for humanoid robots. Its significance and potential impact can be distilled into several key areas:

- **Advancing Humanoid Dexterous Manipulation:** By demonstrating successful vision-based, contact-rich bimanual manipulation using sim-to-real RL on a humanoid robot with multi-fingered hands, this work pushes the boundaries of what these complex platforms can achieve. It moves beyond simpler single-arm or state-based control, paving the way for more sophisticated and human-like interactions with the environment.

- **Scalability and Reduced Cost of Skill Acquisition:** The proposed "recipe" offers a viable and scalable alternative to traditional methods that rely on extensive, costly, and labor-intensive real-world data collection and imitation learning. By shifting the burden of data generation and exploration to high-fidelity, GPU-accelerated simulations, the approach significantly reduces the time and resources required to develop new robot skills. This makes advanced robotic capabilities more accessible for a wider range of applications and research groups.

- **Practical Solutions to Core Sim-to-Real Challenges:** The paper provides concrete, validated solutions for critical bottlenecks in sim-to-real transfer:
 - **Automated System Identification:** The autotune module addresses the long-standing problem of accurately modeling real-world robot dynamics in simulation, particularly for "noisier" low-cost hardware.
 - **Principled Reward Design:** The decomposition of tasks into contact and object goals, along with the use of "contact stickers," offers a generalizable framework for designing effective rewards for complex, multi-stage, and coordinated manipulation tasks.
 - **Efficient Policy Learning:** Techniques like task-aware initialization and divide-and-conquer distillation provide practical strategies to overcome the exploration challenge in high-dimensional action spaces, leading to faster and more stable policy training.
 - **Robust Vision-Based Transfer:** The hybrid object representation strategy, combined with extensive domain randomization, tackles the notorious problem of the sim-to-real gap in visual perception, enabling policies to generalize to diverse, unseen real-world objects.

- **Towards Generalist Robots:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to novel objects and robustness to external disturbances are crucial characteristics for developing truly generalist robots capable of operating reliably in dynamic, unstructured human environments. This work brings the field closer to robots that can adapt on the fly to unforeseen circumstances and variations.

- **Foundation for Future Research and Development:** The comprehensive "recipe" presented in this paper serves as a valuable framework and toolkit for future research. It highlights effective strategies that can be built upon, refined, or combined with other techniques (e.g., incorporating more advanced human priors, exploring torque control, developing novel dynamics gap reduction methods). The acknowledged limitations, such as the need for more sophisticated hardware and improved dynamics transfer techniques for highly dynamic tasks, also clearly delineate future research directions.

In conclusion, this research not only addresses significant challenges in robot manipulation but also provides a practical and scalable pathway for realizing advanced dexterous capabilities on humanoid robots. Its impact is poised to accelerate the development and deployment of more intelligent, versatile, and adaptable robots in various real-world applications.
