# Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from OpenAI developed InstructGPT models by applying Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to align large language models with human instructions. The study found that even smaller aligned models were preferred by human evaluators over much larger unaligned models, and demonstrated improvements in truthfulness and reduced toxicity while largely maintaining performance on standard NLP tasks.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often generate outputs that are unhelpful, factually incorrect, or fail to precisely follow user instructions, despite their advanced capabilities.
- LLMs can produce toxic, harmful, or biased content, posing risks when deployed in real-world applications.
- The standard language modeling objective (next token prediction) does not inherently ensure alignment with complex human values or nuanced intentions, leading to outputs that don't match user preferences.

## Method

- A three-step ==Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)== process was applied to fine-tune pre-trained ==GPT-3== models on diverse human-written prompts.
- Human-written demonstrations of desired behavior were collected to perform ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== on a pre-trained language model.
- A ==reward model== was trained on human preference data (rankings of model outputs) and then used as a reward function to optimize the policy model via ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==, creating **InstructGPT** models.

## Results

- Human evaluators preferred outputs from the **1.3B parameter** InstructGPT model over the **175B parameter** GPT-3 model **71%** of the time, and the 175B InstructGPT model over the 175B GPT-3 model **85%** of the time.
- InstructGPT models showed improvements in truthfulness on the TruthfulQA benchmark and modest reductions in toxicity when specifically prompted for respectful behavior.
- Performance on standard NLP benchmarks varied; InstructGPT models maintained or improved performance on some tasks (e.g., SQuAD v2) and showed some regression on others (e.g., DROP), but the PPO-ptx variant helped mitigate these drops.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Alignment with human preferences, achieved through RLHF, can be more impactful for user satisfaction than increasing model size alone, with smaller aligned models outperforming larger unaligned ones.
> - Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) can effectively align language models with human intentions without significantly degrading their performance on general language tasks, especially with careful optimization.
> - Aligned models demonstrate generalization capabilities beyond their specific training data, extending to held-out human evaluators, non-English instructions, and different task domains like code.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across a wide range of tasks, from writing code to answering complex questions. However, a fundamental challenge remains: how do we ensure these models reliably follow human instructions and align with human values? This question sits at the heart of the research paper "Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback," developed by a team at OpenAI.

As shown in the figure above, the researchers developed a three-step process to align language models with human intent. This approach represents a significant step forward in addressing what's known as the "alignment problem" in AI research - ensuring that increasingly powerful AI systems act in accordance with human intentions and values.

### 2. The Alignment Problem

The paper tackles a central problem in AI safety: as language models grow more capable, they don't inherently become better at following user intentions. Standard language modeling objectives, like predicting the next token in a sequence, can lead to models that generate outputs that are unhelpful, potentially harmful, or fail to follow instructions.

The researchers identified several key challenges:

1. Models often generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect information
2. Models can produce toxic or harmful content
3. Models don't reliably follow the specific instructions given by users
4. Models may optimize for metrics that don't align with actual user preferences

These issues become increasingly critical as language models are deployed in real-world applications where their outputs can have significant impacts.

### 3. Methodology: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

The core innovation in this research is the application of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to align language models with human intentions. This approach builds on previous work in RLHF but applies it at scale to instruction following across a diverse range of tasks.

The methodology involves three key steps:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Collecting human-written demonstrations of desired behavior and fine-tuning a pre-trained GPT-3 model on this data.

2. **Reward Model Training**: Collecting comparison data where human labelers rank different model outputs from best to worst, then training a reward model to predict these human preferences.

3. **Reinforcement Learning Optimization**: Using the reward model as a reward function to optimize the policy with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

This process creates what the researchers call "InstructGPT" models, which are designed to follow instructions better than their base GPT-3 counterparts.

### 4. The InstructGPT Approach

The researchers implemented their approach through several careful design choices:

#### 4.1. Dataset Collection

The training data consisted of prompts from the OpenAI API and prompts written by human labelers. These prompts covered a diverse range of tasks including creative writing, question answering, summarization, and code generation. The researchers hired contractors who underwent a screening process to ensure sensitivity to potentially harmful outputs.

#### 4.2. Model Training

The team trained models of three different sizes (1.3B, 6B, and 175B parameters) using the GPT-3 architecture. They explored various techniques to optimize the training process:

- Using a modified version of RLHF called PPO-ptx that includes a pretraining loss to avoid performance regressions
- Fine-tuning learning rates with cosine decay
- Employing residual dropout and pretraining mix to prevent overfitting
- Carefully balancing the KL divergence term to maintain output diversity

#### 4.3. Evaluation Methods

The researchers evaluated their models using:
- Human preference ratings on a held-out test set of API prompts
- Automatic metrics on standard NLP benchmarks
- Specific evaluations for truthfulness, toxicity, and bias

### 5. Key Results

The paper reports several remarkable findings about the performance of InstructGPT models:

As shown in the figure above, human evaluators strongly preferred the outputs of InstructGPT models over the original GPT-3 outputs. Even the smallest InstructGPT model (1.3B parameters) was preferred over the largest GPT-3 model (175B parameters), demonstrating that alignment can be more important than raw model size.

Some key statistics from the human evaluations:

- The 1.3B InstructGPT model was preferred to the 175B GPT-3 model 71% of the time
- The full-size 175B InstructGPT model was preferred to the 175B GPT-3 model 85% of the time
- InstructGPT models showed consistent improvements in attempting to follow instructions, following explicit constraints, and reducing hallucinations

These results demonstrate that RLHF is an effective technique for aligning language models with human preferences, and this alignment can be achieved without sacrificing the model's general capabilities.

### 6. Performance on NLP Tasks

One concern with alignment techniques is that they might degrade performance on standard NLP benchmarks. The researchers evaluated their models on a range of public datasets to assess this "alignment tax":

The results were mixed but generally positive:

- On some datasets (like SQuAD v2 and Winograd), InstructGPT models performed as well as or better than GPT-3
- On other tasks (like DROP and translation), there was some performance regression
- The PPO-ptx variant (which includes a pretraining loss term) helped mitigate many of these performance drops

The researchers found that the alignment tax could be substantially reduced through careful optimization of the training process, suggesting that alignment and general capability don't have to be in tension.

### 7. Truthfulness and Toxicity Improvements

Beyond following instructions, the researchers were interested in whether InstructGPT models showed improvements in truthfulness and reductions in toxic outputs:

#### 7.1. Truthfulness

When evaluated on the TruthfulQA benchmark, InstructGPT models demonstrated meaningful improvements in truthfulness over GPT-3. The models were less likely to generate plausible-sounding but false information, particularly when explicitly prompted to be truthful.

#### 7.2. Toxicity

The research also showed small improvements in toxicity metrics:

When specifically prompted to be respectful, InstructGPT models showed reduced toxicity compared to GPT-3. However, the researchers note that these improvements were modest, and significant challenges remain in addressing potential harms from language models.

### 8. Generalization Capabilities

A particularly interesting finding is how InstructGPT models generalized beyond their training distribution:

1. **Generalization to held-out labelers**: The models aligned well with the preferences of labelers who didn't produce any of the training data, suggesting they captured general human preferences rather than overfitting to specific annotators.

2. **Generalization to non-English instructions**: Despite being trained primarily on English data, InstructGPT showed promising capabilities in following instructions in other languages.

3. **Generalization to code tasks**: The models performed well on code-related tasks even though these were not the focus of the RLHF training.

These findings suggest that RLHF produces models that generalize alignment in meaningful ways beyond the specific contexts seen during training.

### 9. Limitations and Open Questions

The researchers identify several important limitations and open questions in their work:

1. **Mitigating Harms**: While InstructGPT shows improvements in reducing toxicity and bias, these improvements are modest. Significant challenges remain in making language models consistently safe and beneficial.

2. **Value Judgments**: The process of alignment inevitably involves value judgments about what constitutes "good" model behavior. The researchers acknowledge the need for greater transparency and stakeholder participation in these decisions.

3. **Evaluation Challenges**: Evaluating alignment is difficult and subjective. The researchers note that their evaluation methods are imperfect and may not capture all relevant aspects of model behavior.

4. **Training Process Limitations**: The alignment technique relies on human feedback, which can be expensive, time-consuming, and may incorporate human biases.

5. **Alignment Gaming**: As models become more capable, there's a risk they might learn to optimize for the reward model in ways that don't actually align with human preferences.

These limitations highlight the ongoing nature of alignment research and the need for continued work in this area.

### 10. Conclusion

The research on training language models to follow instructions with human feedback represents a significant advance in AI alignment. The InstructGPT models demonstrate that it's possible to make large language models substantially more aligned with human intent while maintaining their general capabilities.

Key takeaways from this research include:

1. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback is an effective technique for aligning language models with human preferences
2. Alignment can be more important than model size for producing outputs that humans prefer
3. With careful optimization, alignment can be achieved without significant performance regressions on standard benchmarks
4. Aligned models can generalize beyond their training distribution to new contexts and user groups

This work provides a promising direction for making AI systems more helpful, harmless, and honest as they continue to grow in capability. However, the researchers emphasize that significant challenges remain, and alignment should be seen as an ongoing research program rather than a solved problem.

The techniques developed in this paper have already had a substantial impact on deployed AI systems, and the lessons learned will likely continue to influence how we approach the challenge of making advanced AI systems that reliably act in accordance with human values and intentions.
