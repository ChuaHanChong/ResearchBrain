# Sparse-View 3D Reconstruction: Recent Advances and Open Challenges

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Shenzhen Institute of Advanced Technology, CAS, provide a comprehensive survey on sparse-view 3D reconstruction, systematically categorizing recent advancements across geometry-based, neural implicit (NeRF), and 3D Gaussian Splatting methods. The paper details how generative models and Vision Foundation Models are integrated to address memory and ambiguity challenges, enhancing reconstruction quality and efficiency from limited input images.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D reconstruction methods like Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multiview Stereo (MVS) require a large number of densely overlapping images, which is often impractical in real-world applications.
- Sparse image inputs lead to inherent ambiguities, causing artifacts such as 'floaters,' blurred textures, and unreliable camera pose estimation.
- The rapidly evolving field of sparse-view 3D reconstruction lacks a unified, comprehensive analysis of its diverse deep learning methodologies and their convergence.

## Method

- The survey systematically categorizes advancements into ==geometry-based methods==, ==neural implicit representations (NeRF)==, and ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)==, along with hybrid approaches.
- It provides an in-depth review of cutting-edge ==3DGS techniques==, including core improvements, diffusion-integrated variants, and ==pose-free methods== for sparse-view scenarios.
- The work analyzes the integration of ==diffusion models== and ==Vision Foundation Models (VFMs)== like CLIP and SAM to introduce strong priors, ensure view consistency, and hallucinate plausible geometry from limited data.

## Results

- **Geometry-based methods** are enhanced by neural networks for pose hypotheses and semantic awareness, improving performance in sparse settings.
- **NeRF variants** achieve better sparse-view performance through regularization, integration of geometric priors, and improved generalization beyond per-scene optimization.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting** approaches demonstrate enhanced quality and efficiency via robust initialization, co-regularization, depth normalization, and effective integration with diffusion models, including pose-free reconstruction.
- The integration of **diffusion models and VFMs** significantly aids in synthesizing novel views, infilling missing regions, and providing semantic guidance, thereby resolving ambiguities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep learning has profoundly reshaped sparse-view 3D reconstruction, with NeRF and 3DGS proving effective in learning robust scene representations and synthesizing novel views from minimal inputs.
> - Generative models, especially diffusion models and VFMs, are crucial for hallucinating missing details and providing strong 3D-aware priors, significantly improving reconstruction fidelity and consistency.
> - The move towards 'pose-free' or 'COLMAP-free' frameworks, which jointly optimize geometry and camera parameters, along with hybrid representations, is essential for robust, efficient, and generalizable reconstruction in challenging real-world conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction from two-dimensional (2D) images has been a foundational challenge in computer vision for decades. Traditional approaches, such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multiview Stereo (MVS), typically necessitate a large number of densely overlapping images to reliably construct accurate 3D models. However, real-world applications in robotics, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), autonomous navigation, and digital content creation often present scenarios where acquiring such dense imagery is impractical, costly, or even impossible due to constraints like limited sensor views, occlusions, or dynamic environments. This gap spurred the specialized field of sparse-view 3D reconstruction, aiming to achieve high-quality 3D models from a minimal set of input images.

The inherent ambiguity arising from sparse inputs—leading to issues like "floaters" (spurious geometry), blurred textures, background collapse, and unreliable camera pose estimation—has traditionally hindered the performance of classical methods. The advent of deep learning has profoundly reshaped this landscape, particularly with the emergence of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and, more recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), alongside the integration of powerful generative models like diffusion models. These deep learning-based methods have demonstrated remarkable progress in synthesizing novel views and reconstructing geometry from sparse data.

This survey paper strategically positions itself within this evolving research landscape by addressing a critical need for a unified and comprehensive analysis. While existing surveys have covered broader aspects of 3D reconstruction or focused on specific techniques like 3DGS for sparse views, none have systematically analyzed the convergence of the three major paradigms: geometry-based methods, neural implicit representations (NeRF and its variants), and generative (diffusion-based) approaches, especially in the context of sparse-view scenarios. The paper aims to bridge this analytical gap by providing a consolidated framework, comparative evaluation, and an in-depth review of these disparate yet converging methodologies. Its focus on cutting-edge techniques, including 3DGS and the increasing role of diffusion models and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), makes it a timely and relevant contribution to the community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is the growing demand for robust 3D reconstruction capabilities in applications where dense image acquisition is infeasible. Traditional SfM/MVS pipelines falter in sparse-view settings due to insufficient correspondences, leading to unreliable results. Deep learning has driven significant advancements in overcoming these limitations, yet the field is characterized by a diverse array of methodologies, making it challenging to grasp the overall landscape, trade-offs, and future directions. The authors specifically highlight the "inherently ambiguous" nature of sparse-view reconstruction, which frequently results in artifacts and pose estimation ambiguities, necessitating a shift towards deep learning methods that can jointly optimize or bypass explicit pose estimation.

The survey outlines several key objectives to address these challenges and gaps:

- **Systematic Categorization:** To organize the recent advancements into clear categories: geometry-based, neural implicit (NeRF), 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), and hybrid approaches, detailing their core mechanisms and limitations.
- **In-depth Analysis of 3DGS Methods:** To provide the most extensive and up-to-date review of 3DGS techniques, including core advancements, diffusion-integrated variants, and pose-free methods, specifically evaluating their effectiveness under sparse-view conditions.
- **Integration of Generative Models:** To analyze how diffusion models and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like CLIP, SAM, and DINO are leveraged to introduce strong priors, ensure view consistency, and hallucinate plausible geometry from limited data.
- **Cross-paradigm Comparison:** To offer a critical comparison across different paradigms (SfM, NeRF, 3DGS, diffusion), evaluating their respective trade-offs in terms of accuracy, efficiency, generalizability, and real-world applicability under sparse constraints.
- **Identification of Research Gaps:** To highlight unresolved challenges such as domain generalization, truly pose-free reconstruction, and efficient learning from minimal supervision, thereby charting promising directions for future research.

By achieving these objectives, the survey aims to provide a unified perspective that clarifies the state-of-the-art and guides future research in sparse-view 3D reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic and rigorous methodological review protocol to ensure comprehensive coverage and objective analysis of the literature, as detailed in Figure 3 of the paper. This protocol comprised distinct stages:

- **Search Strategy and Identification:** The initial phase involved extensive searches across major academic databases and repositories, including Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Semantic Scholar, and arXiv (specifically targeting `cs.CV`, `cs.GR`, `cs.AI` categories). A broad range of keywords and search terms was employed, such as "3D reconstruction sparse views," "novel view synthesis sparse," "NeRF sparse input OR NeRF few-shot," "Gaussian Splatting sparse OR 3DGS sparse view," "diffusion models 3D reconstruction OR generative priors 3D," "pose-free 3D reconstruction OR uncalibrated multi-view," and "multi-view stereo sparse OR SfM sparse." To ensure a comprehensive collection, initial searches were supplemented by both backward snowballing (analyzing references of identified papers) and forward snowballing (examining citations to identified papers). Zotero was used for efficient literature management.

- **Screening and Selection:** A two-pass screening process was implemented. The first pass involved reviewing titles and abstracts to filter out irrelevant papers. The second pass entailed a full-text review of the shortlisted articles. Clear inclusion and exclusion criteria guided this process. Papers were included if their primary focus was sparse 3D reconstruction or novel view synthesis, if they employed methodologies within geometry, NeRF, 3DGS, or Diffusion/VFM paradigms, were published between 2020 and 2025 (with foundational earlier works also considered), were in English, and were either peer-reviewed or high-quality preprints. Papers were excluded if they addressed irrelevant topics, focused solely on dense-view reconstruction, were not related to 3D reconstruction, or were duplicates.

- **Data Extraction and Categorization:** For each selected paper, relevant information was systematically extracted. This included publication details (title, authors, year), the specific problem solved, its core innovation or "key trick," the data requirements (e.g., number of input images), reported speed/runtime, identified limitations, and the datasets used for evaluation. This extracted data formed the basis for organizing the methods into a structured taxonomy, continuously refining categories and subcategories (as illustrated in Figures 2 and 4 of the paper).

- **Quality Assessment and Validation:** The final stage focused on ensuring the reliability and consistency of the extracted data and the developed taxonomy. This involved assessing the quality of reported results, ensuring consistent categorization of methods, and validating the taxonomy through what the authors describe as "expert review," implicitly referring to their own specialized knowledge in the field. Discrepancies and ambiguities were resolved to maintain the integrity of the review.

This systematic methodology ensures that the survey provides a robust, well-structured, and authoritative overview of the sparse-view 3D reconstruction landscape.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey comprehensively synthesizes the advancements in sparse-view 3D reconstruction across three major paradigms: geometry-based methods, neural implicit representations (NeRFs), and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), alongside a detailed examination of the integration of diffusion models and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs).

#### 4.1. Geometry-Based Methods

Traditional SfM and MVS methods, while foundational, face significant limitations with sparse views due to insufficient feature correspondences. Recent advancements have focused on augmenting these methods with neural networks and additional cues. Early efforts incorporated Markov Random Fields and Gaussian Process Latent Variable Models (GPLVMs) to leverage geometric relationships and learned shape priors. More modern approaches, such as NOPE-SAC, use neural networks to learn pose hypotheses from minimal planar correspondences, outperforming classical RANSAC. SparseCraft and 3DFIRES demonstrate improvements by integrating stereopsis-guided geometric linearization or transformer-based feature fusion for hidden surface reconstruction, respectively. Semantic awareness, as seen in Wei et al., further enhances accuracy in complex environments.

#### 4.2. Neural Implicit Representations (NeRF and Variants)

The original NeRF, while revolutionary for novel view synthesis, struggles with overfitting and geometric inaccuracies in sparse-view settings. Key strategies have focused on regularization, leveraging geometric and learned priors, and improving generalization:
- **Regularization:** Methods like RegNeRF employ patch-based geometric and color regularization with annealing strategies to mitigate artifacts. FlipNeRF uses flipped reflection rays and uncertainty-aware losses, while SC-NeRF introduces self-correction mechanisms.
- **Geometric Priors:** Integrating external depth priors from SfM (Roessle et al.) or monocular depth estimators (SparseNeRF) significantly constrains NeRF optimization, improving both depth accuracy and image quality.
- **Generalization and Efficiency:** PixelNeRF and IBRNet enabled feed-forward generalization, moving beyond per-scene optimization. ZeroRF achieves fast 360° reconstruction without pre-training. Advances such as 6Img-to-3D and NeRS tackle large-scale outdoor scenes and surface-based object reconstruction, respectively. Many recent NeRF variants integrate various regularization (e.g., SimpleNeRF, ConsistentNeRF), multi-input MLPs (e.g., mi-MLP), or hierarchical guidance (e.g., HG3-NeRF) to enhance quality and efficiency from few shots. Pose-free NeRFs like LEAP and methods for joint pose-geometry optimization (SparsePose, SC-NeuS) represent a crucial evolution.

#### 4.3. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Approaches

3DGS has emerged as a state-of-the-art technique for its real-time rendering capabilities, but its performance also degrades with sparse inputs. Research has focused on robust initialization, regularization, and integration with other models:
- **Core 3DGS Improvements:** SparseGS addresses "floaters" and "background collapse" using novel depth rendering and unseen viewpoint regularization. LoopSparseGS and GaussianObject introduce progressive initialization and floater removal strategies. CoR-GS uses co-regularization by training two 3DGS fields, leveraging their disagreements for self-supervision. DNGaussian optimizes with global-local depth normalization, significantly accelerating training while preserving detail.
- **Generalization and Efficiency:** Speedy-Splat optimizes rendering speed and model size through efficient pruning. TranSplat employs transformers for generalizable 3DGS from sparse views, and UniForward unifies scene and semantic field reconstruction in a feed-forward manner.
- **Geometry-Prioritized and Surface-Aware 3DGS:** Sparse2DGS and PointGS leverage MVS point clouds or stereo foundation models for accurate geometric initialization, improving surface reconstruction. Methods like SPARS3R combine SfM with dense point clouds from modern depth techniques for robust priors.
- **3DGS + Diffusion Hybrids:** This is a rapidly growing area where diffusion models enhance 3DGS. Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS uses diffusion as a "View Densifier" to generate photorealistic pseudo-observations. LM-Gaussian leverages large model priors for robust point cloud initialization and multimodal regularization. MVSplat360 integrates Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) for appearance refinement, while ProSplat combines a 3DGS generator with a one-step diffusion improver.
- **Pose-Free Methods:** InstantSplat and CF-3DGS are prominent examples, combining dense stereo models for initialization with joint optimization of Gaussian attributes and camera poses, enabling rapid, high-quality reconstruction from unposed images without relying on SfM. MV-DUSt3R+ extends this to a single-stage, feedforward network for fast reconstruction.

#### 4.4. Integration of Diffusion Models and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs)

Generative AI, particularly diffusion models, is transforming sparse-view reconstruction by hallucinating missing details and providing strong priors.
- **Diffusion for Generation and Consistency:** Models like GenFusion and Sp2360 use video diffusion or cascaded 2D diffusion to synthesize new views, infill missing regions, and ensure multi-view consistency. VI3DRM explores perspective-disentangled 3D latent spaces for high-fidelity novel view synthesis. Sparse3D distills robust priors from multi-view consistent diffusion models to address blurriness.
- **Integration with VFMs:** Large-scale pretrained models like CLIP, SAM, and DINO provide rich semantic and visual understanding. Their embeddings and masks serve as powerful priors, guiding semantic supervision, object segmentation, and feature matching, thereby resolving ambiguities and enhancing both geometric accuracy and visual fidelity.

#### 4.5. Comparative Evaluation

The paper emphasizes the importance of standardized evaluation using benchmark datasets such as DTU, LLFF/Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks and Temples, CO3D, and ScanNet. Performance is measured using rendering quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID), geometric accuracy metrics (Chamfer Distance, Normal Consistency, ATE), and efficiency metrics (training/inference time, FPS). A visual comparison (Figure 1) and an in-depth analysis table (Table VI) highlight the trade-offs and relative performances across methods, demonstrating that increasing input views generally improves quality, with significant gains observed when moving from extremely sparse to moderately sparse regimes (Figure 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the field of sparse-view 3D reconstruction, serving as a comprehensive benchmark and forward-looking guide. It meticulously consolidates a vast and rapidly evolving body of literature, offering a structured understanding of disparate methodologies and their convergence.

#### 5.1. Current Challenges

The authors delineate persistent challenges that underscore the need for continued innovation:
- **Fundamental Ambiguities and Artifacts:** The ill-posed nature of sparse-view problems leads to pervasive artifacts like "floaters," blurry textures, "background collapse," and the "Janus problem" (inconsistent hallucination of unseen surfaces). Reconstructing occluded geometry remains particularly difficult.
- **Computational Bottlenecks and Scalability:** Despite advances, achieving real-time performance for the *entire* reconstruction pipeline (including training and optimization) for large-scale or high-resolution scenes remains elusive. Many methods demand substantial computational resources and time.
- **Robustness to "In-the-Wild" Conditions:** Real-world scenarios introduce uncalibrated images, noisy poses, dynamic elements, challenging materials (specular, transparent, low-textured), noise, and varying illumination, which current models often struggle to handle robustly.
- **Generalization Across Diverse Scenes and Objects:** Many state-of-the-art methods still rely on per-scene optimization, limiting their practicality. Generalizable approaches often sacrifice fidelity when applied to out-of-distribution data.

#### 5.2. Promising Emerging Trends

The survey identifies several crucial trends shaping the future of the field:
- **Leveraging Advanced Generative Priors:** The integration of diffusion models to synthesize "3D-aware images" and pseudo-observations is vital for densifying sparse inputs and enhancing multi-view consistency. VFMs like CLIP, SAM, and DINO also provide rich semantic and feature-level guidance.
- **Hybrid Representations and Architectures:** Combining explicit structures (point clouds, Gaussians) with implicit neural fields (NeRFs, SDFs) is proving effective in balancing speed, memory efficiency, and geometric accuracy.
- **Joint Learning of Geometry and Pose:** The move towards "pose-free" or "COLMAP-free" frameworks, which jointly optimize scene structure and camera parameters, significantly enhances robustness to poor initializations and speeds up the process.
- **Adaptive and Optimized Training Strategies:** Dynamic control over representation size, sample-space annealing, and iterative refinement loops are making optimization more stable and reliable with minimal inputs.
- **Semantic and Material-Aware Reconstruction:** Enriching reconstructions with high-level semantic labels and material properties enables more informative 3D models and downstream applications like semantic editing and relighting.
- **Real-time Performance and Efficiency Optimization:** Efforts to optimize rendering and training (e.g., Speedy-Splat, DNGaussian) and develop feed-forward generalizable models (e.g., SparSplat) are critical for practical deployment.

#### 5.3. Future Research Directions

The paper concludes by outlining impactful future research avenues:
- **Unified Multi-Modal Generative 3D Priors:** Developing "3D-native" generative foundation models trained directly on diverse 3D datasets, capable of synthesizing full 3D structures (RGB-D, normals, materials) with intrinsic consistency from minimal input (e.g., single image, text).
- **Robustness and Fidelity in Extreme "In-the-Wild" Conditions:** Research into reconstructing dynamic scenes from unstructured video, achieving illumination/material-agnostic inverse rendering, and developing methods resilient to degraded data (motion blur, noise, distortions).
- **Towards Real-time, On-Device, and Continual 3D Understanding:** Focusing on ultra-efficient, lightweight pipelines for edge devices (smartphones, AR/VR headsets), adaptive Level-of-Detail (LoD) streaming, and "living" 3D maps that update incrementally.
- **Intelligent Acquisition and Human-in-the-Loop Reconstruction:** Designing uncertainty-aware active reconstruction systems (e.g., drones) that intelligently acquire data from ambiguous regions, and integrating intuitive user interfaces for interactive refinement and semantic editing.

#### 5.4. Overall Impact

This survey's profound significance lies in its capacity to serve as a definitive, up-to-date reference for both researchers and practitioners. By meticulously dissecting the landscape, highlighting innovations, and forecasting future trajectories, it acts as a catalyst for ongoing innovation. The insights provided will accelerate the development and deployment of robust, intelligent 3D reconstruction systems, ultimately enabling richer, more actionable 3D representations across a wide array of everyday applications, from immersive experiences to autonomous systems and digital content creation. The field is indeed transitioning from a reliance on constrained, dense inputs to flexible, generalizable, and efficient methods capable of operating effectively with minimal, uncalibrated data.
