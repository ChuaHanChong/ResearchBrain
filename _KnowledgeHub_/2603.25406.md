# MMaDA-VLA: Large Diffusion Vision-Language-Action Model with Unified Multi-Modal Instruction and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MMaDA-VLA, a large diffusion Vision-Language-Action model from Westlake University and OpenHelix Robotics, unifies multi-modal understanding and generation for robotic manipulation through a native discrete diffusion framework. It achieves a 98.0% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and an average successful execution length of 4.78 on CALVIN, outperforming previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing hierarchical VLA models suffer from architectural complexity, additional training costs, and information fidelity loss.
- End-to-end autoregressive VLA approaches often lead to weak temporal consistency and compounding errors in long-horizon tasks.
- VLA models generally lack an explicit mechanism to model environment dynamics without auxiliary world models or complex multi-stage generation, leading to increased overhead and error propagation.

## Method

- **MMaDA-VLA** develops a fully native pre-trained ==discrete diffusion== VLA model that embeds all modalities (language, vision, actions) into a single ==token space==.
- It jointly predicts future ==goal visual observations== and a chunk of subsequent actions in parallel through an ==iterative denoising== process.
- A ==hybrid attention mechanism== and a unified ==masked token denoising== objective are used for multi-modal sequence modeling and learning across pre-training and fine-tuning stages.

## Results

- **MMaDA-VLA** achieved a **98.0%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing state-of-the-art vanilla and diffusion-based VLA models.
- It demonstrated an average successful execution length of **4.78** on the CALVIN benchmark (ABC->D setting), a notable improvement over prior methods for long-horizon tasks.
- In real-world evaluations with an AgileX Piper robot, **MMaDA-VLA** achieved success rates ranging from **83.3%** to **93.3%** across various manipulation tasks, showing robustness and error recovery capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling environment dynamics through goal observation prediction within a unified framework substantially improves action generation quality and task success.
> - Parallel iterative denoising for joint goal observation and action chunk prediction enhances long-horizon consistency and mitigates error accumulation compared to sequential or autoregressive methods.
> - Large-scale pre-training on diverse robotic manipulation datasets is crucial for developing VLA models that generalize well across various tasks and environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are designed to enable robots to perform manipulation tasks guided by visual observations and natural language instructions. This field is critical for advancing applications such as household assistance and industrial automation, demanding both generalization and precision in robotic control.

Existing VLA models primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Hierarchical paradigms**: These models augment pre-existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with a dedicated policy head. While leveraging strong VLM representations, this approach introduces architectural overhead, training complexities, and can result in reduced information fidelity across module boundaries.
2. **End-to-end autoregressive approaches**: These models discretize continuous actions and generate them sequentially. Although effective for action prediction and generalization, they often exhibit weak temporal consistency, which can lead to compounding errors over long operational horizons and limit global trajectory planning.

A common limitation across both paradigms is the general absence of an explicit mechanism to model environment dynamics through direct prediction of future visual observations. Prior research has attempted to address these challenges through various methods:
- **Action quantization schemes**: Discretizing continuous actions while aiming to preserve reconstruction accuracy.
- **Multi-step action prediction**: Enabling parallel decoding for otherwise autoregressive models to predict multiple actions in a single pass.
- **Auxiliary modules**: Introducing separate visual generation modules or additional objectives to model environment dynamics by predicting future observations or learning inverse dynamics.

Despite these advancements, many approaches still involve substantial design and computational overhead, complex architectures, and error propagation across multi-stage generation processes. More recently, unified VLA models based on large diffusion backbones have emerged, replacing autoregressive decoding with discrete mask token prediction and incorporating goal image generation to capture environment dynamics. However, these models are frequently fine-tuned from autoregressive counterparts, which can introduce inconsistencies between training and inference procedures, potentially limiting their overall performance.

MMaDA-VLA positions itself within this landscape by proposing a fully native pre-trained large diffusion VLA model. It aims to unify multi-modal understanding and generation within a single framework from its foundational pre-training stage, thereby addressing the architectural, temporal consistency, and dynamics modeling limitations observed in prior VLA approaches without incurring the inconsistencies of fine-tuning diffusion models from autoregressive bases.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of controlling robots for complex manipulation tasks using natural language instructions and visual observations. The motivation stems from several critical limitations inherent in existing VLA paradigms:

1. **Architectural Complexity and Overhead**: Traditional hierarchical VLA models often integrate pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with separate policy heads. This design can lead to increased architectural complexity, higher training costs, and potential loss of information fidelity as data passes between distinct modules. MMaDA-VLA aims to simplify this by unifying multi-modal understanding and generation within a single, end-to-end framework.

2. **Temporal Inconsistency and Error Accumulation**: Autoregressive VLA models, which predict actions sequentially, are susceptible to generating action sequences with weak temporal consistency. Errors can compound over long task horizons, making global trajectory planning difficult and unreliable. The project aims to overcome this by introducing a mechanism for order-free refinement and parallel generation of action chunks, thereby improving long-horizon consistency.

3. **Lack of Environment Dynamics Modeling**: Many existing VLA models do not explicitly model environment dynamics or predict future visual observations. This absence can limit the robot's ability to anticipate task-relevant state evolution, leading to less informed action generation. The research seeks to integrate an implicit "world-model-like" capability by jointly generating future goal observations alongside actions, without requiring auxiliary world model modules or multi-stage training.

4. **Inconsistency in Diffusion-Based VLA Models**: While recent diffusion-based VLA models have shown promise, many are fine-tuned from existing autoregressive models. This adaptation can create discrepancies between the training and inference processes, potentially hindering their optimal performance. MMaDA-VLA is specifically designed to be "fully native pre-trained," ensuring consistency across all stages and maximizing the benefits of a discrete diffusion formulation.

Specifically, the key objectives for MMaDA-VLA are:
- To propose a novel fully native pre-trained large diffusion VLA model that unifies multi-modal instruction understanding and multi-modal generation.
- To develop a native discrete diffusion VLA formulation that embeds language, images, and continuous robot controls into a single discrete token space.
- To train a single backbone model using a masked token denoising objective to jointly generate a future goal observation and a chunk of actions in parallel.
- To leverage iterative denoising for global, order-free refinement, thereby improving long-horizon consistency and implicitly grounding actions in predicted future visual outcomes.
- To establish a complete training pipeline, encompassing large-scale pre-training and task-specific fine-tuning.
- To demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MMaDA-VLA is designed as a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model operating within a natively discrete diffusion framework. The system is structured to integrate multi-modal instruction following and generation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Conventional VLA models learn a policy to predict an action `a_t` given a current visual observation `o_t` and a language instruction `l`. MMaDA-VLA augments this by explicitly predicting a future goal visual observation (`o_t'`) in parallel with a chunk of actions (`a_t:t'-1`), where `t' = t + k` and `k` is the action chunk size. This joint prediction `(o_t'; a_t:t'-1) ∼ π_θ(o_t', a_t:t'-1 | o_t, l)` enables the policy to internalize task-relevant state evolution, thereby providing a form of implicit dynamics modeling without an explicit world model.

#### 3.2. Architecture

The architecture of MMaDA-VLA is built upon a unified discrete tokenization and sequence modeling strategy.

##### 3.2.1. Data Tokenization

A unified discrete tokenization approach maps all input modalities—language, images, and continuous robot actions—into a common discrete token space. This allows a single model backbone to process and generate all types of information.
- **Textual data**: Utilizes the textual tokenizer from LLaDA.
- **Visual data**: Leverages the pre-trained image quantizer MAGVIT-v2, converting images into discrete visual tokens.
- **Robot actions**: Continuous 7-DoF robot actions are discretized by mapping each dimension into one of 256 bins, with bin widths determined by the training data range.
All modalities are thus transformed into discrete token sequences, sharing a common vocabulary.

##### 3.2.2. Multi-Modal Sequence Modeling

A standardized prompting strategy is employed to format inputs and outputs for VLA tasks. Special tokens (`[SOX]`, `[EOX]`) mark the beginning and end of each modality sequence (e.g., `[SOO]` for Start of Observation, `[EOL]` for End of Language). The input sequence is structured as: `[SOO] current_obs_tokens [EOO] [SOL] instruction_tokens [EOL] [SOO] goal_obs_tokens [EOO] [SOA] action_chunk_tokens [EOA]`. During training, tokens in the `goal_obs_tokens` and `action_chunk_tokens` sections are masked for prediction.

##### 3.2.3. Hybrid Attention Mechanism

The model incorporates a hybrid attention mechanism to manage intra-modal and inter-modal interactions:
- **Bidirectional full attention**: Applied for interactions within the same modality (e.g., among image tokens or among action tokens). This allows global information exchange and avoids imposing an arbitrary autoregressive order on inherently unordered dimensions, such as components of an action vector.
- **Causal attention**: Used for interactions across different modalities (e.g., from instruction tokens to action tokens). This enforces a directed flow of information, ensuring that instructions guide subsequent generation.
This design decouples generation targets while providing a stable feature foundation for action prediction and mitigating compounding errors typical of strictly sequential generation.

#### 3.3. Learning Objective

MMaDA-VLA employs a single, unified learning objective across all modalities: masked token prediction. During both pre-training and fine-tuning, tokens within the generation part of the sequence (goal observation and action chunk) are randomly replaced with a special mask token (`[M]`). The number of masked tokens is dynamically determined by a mask ratio sampled according to a cosine schedule. The model is trained to reconstruct the original tokens, minimizing the cross-entropy loss over the masked positions. This objective encourages the model to learn the underlying distributions of multi-modal data and their interdependencies.

#### 3.4. Inference

Inference in MMaDA-VLA involves an iterative denoising process combined with a caching mechanism to ensure real-time performance.

##### 3.4.1. Iterative Denoising

At inference time, all tokens corresponding to the future goal observation and action chunk are initially masked. The MMaDA-VLA model then performs `D` iterative denoising steps. In each step `d` (from `D` down to 0), the model estimates the distribution over the clean sequence. The most likely sequence is obtained via greedy decoding. A confidence-based remasking strategy is then applied: a proportion `β` of tokens with the lowest confidence scores are re-masked, while the remaining tokens are accepted from the predicted clean sequence. This iterative refinement allows the model to progressively recover the masked tokens, leading to the final prediction of the goal observation image and the action chunk.

##### 3.4.2. Key-Value Cache

To mitigate the high inference latency associated with iterative denoising, a training-free key-value caching framework is implemented.
- **Instruction part**: Since the instruction remains constant across denoising steps, its intermediate representations (keys, values, attention outputs, FFN outputs) are computed once and cached for the entire denoising process.
- **Generation part**: The tokens in the generation part evolve. However, this evolution is typically sparse. Therefore, a selective refresh strategy is used: every `λ` denoising steps, only a subset of tokens (specifically, the `⌊ρn'⌋` tokens with the lowest cosine similarity between current and cached value vectors) are recomputed, while the rest are retrieved from the cache. This optimizes computation for real-time robotic control.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The model utilizes MMaDA-8B-Base as its backbone, containing 8 million parameters. Input observations consist of vertically concatenated RGB images from both third-person and wrist-view cameras, resized to 256x256, with the robot's proprioceptive state appended as text.
- **Pre-training**: MMaDA-VLA is pre-trained on a large-scale dataset of 61 million steps from various cross-embodiment robotic manipulation datasets (details provided in Table 2 of the paper). This stage aims to capture general relationships between visual observations and actions.
- **Fine-tuning**: For specific benchmarks, the action chunk size is set to 5 for LIBERO and 10 for CALVIN.
- **Training Infrastructure**: Pre-training leverages DeepSpeed and requires approximately 30 hours using 8 Nvidia 8-H800 GPU nodes. Inference is configured with 24 denoising steps and a refresh interval of 6 steps for the key-value cache.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MMaDA-VLA demonstrates consistent performance across various benchmarks, including simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

MMaDA-VLA consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance when compared against both discrete-action and continuous-action baselines across all evaluated settings.

#### 4.2. LIBERO Simulation Benchmark

On the LIBERO benchmark, which assesses multi-task and lifelong robotic manipulation across spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon generalization categories, MMaDA-VLA achieved an average success rate of **98.0%**. This performance surpasses:
- Leading continuous-action vanilla VLA models, such as VLA-Adapter (97.3%).
- Strongest discrete-action baselines, like Discrete Diffusion VLA (96.3%).
- VLA models incorporating world model attributes via visual prediction, such as F1 (95.7%).
The results suggest that MMaDA-VLA's discrete unified modeling approach enhances the generalization of visual prediction, thereby improving action generation quality even with limited training data for visual prediction components.

#### 4.3. CALVIN Simulation Benchmark

For the CALVIN benchmark, focused on long-horizon, language-conditioned manipulation, MMaDA-VLA achieved an average successful execution length of **4.78** under the ABC→D setting. This indicates:
- A substantial improvement in long-horizon performance compared to other methods.
- The success rate on the fifth sub-task, which represents the longest horizon, increased to approximately 90%.
These findings indicate that MMaDA-VLA not only improves the success of individual sub-tasks but also maintains enhanced consistency over extended action sequences, a critical aspect for complex multi-step tasks.

#### 4.4. Real-World Evaluation

Real-world experiments were conducted using an AgileX Piper 6-DoF robotic arm across four categories of tasks: simple pick-and-place, precision stacking, complex storage, and long-horizon organizing.
- MMaDA-VLA achieved success rates ranging from **83.3% to 93.3%** across these tasks.
- It outperformed the GR00T N1.6 baseline, which showed average success rates below 70% in the same task settings.
The system demonstrated robust execution, even in scenarios with environmental disturbances (e.g., distractors, target displacement) or complex interactions (e.g., drawer manipulation), and often exhibited the ability to self-correct during task execution.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (on CALVIN without pre-training)

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of different architectural components:
- **Excluding World-Model (no goal image prediction)**: Performance dropped significantly from an average length of 4.56 to 4.08, indicating the importance of explicit environment dynamics modeling through goal observation prediction.
- **Excluding Parallel Denoising (sequential goal image then action prediction)**: Performance decreased to an average length of 4.38, highlighting the benefits of parallel generation for leveraging intermediate features and mitigating cumulative errors.
- **Attention Mechanism Variants (Causal or Bidirectional Attention)**: Replacing the hybrid attention with either causal or fully bidirectional attention resulted in minor performance decreases (less than 0.1 average length). This suggests that while hybrid attention is optimal, the overall generative paradigm has a more substantial impact.

#### 4.6. Pre-Training Effectiveness

Comparing MMaDA-VLA with and without large-scale pre-training demonstrated significant benefits:
- **LIBERO**: Pre-training increased the average success rate from 94.5% to 98.0% (a 3.5 percentage point gain).
- **CALVIN**: Pre-training improved the average execution length from 4.56 to 4.78 (an absolute increase of 0.22).
These results confirm that pre-training on diverse, large-scale data substantially enhances model generalization and execution accuracy.

#### 4.7. Visual Generation Quality

Analysis of generated goal observations showed that while the images generally maintained consistency with instructions and high-level task dynamics across various environments, their visual fidelity was limited. Fine-grained details, such as gripper geometry or small objects, often appeared blurred or inaccurate. This limitation is attributed to the use of a compact image representation designed for computational efficiency. Despite reduced pixel-level accuracy, the generated frames were deemed reliable for conveying task progression and providing useful anticipatory cues for downstream control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MMaDA-VLA represents a notable advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Unified and Streamlined Architecture**: MMaDA-VLA introduces a fully native pre-trained discrete diffusion framework that unifies multi-modal understanding and generation within a single model. This approach addresses the architectural overhead and complexities associated with traditional hierarchical VLA designs, which often rely on augmenting existing VLMs with separate policy heads. The single backbone and shared objective simplify the model design and training procedure.

2. **Enhanced Temporal Consistency and Long-Horizon Performance**: By employing an iterative denoising process that enables global, order-free refinement and parallel generation of action chunks, MMaDA-VLA mitigates the issues of weak temporal consistency and compounding errors prevalent in autoregressive action prediction. This is particularly significant for long-horizon tasks, where errors can accumulate rapidly, and the demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like CALVIN underscores this improvement.

3. **Implicit Environment Dynamics Modeling**: The model’s ability to jointly generate future goal observations in parallel with actions provides an implicit mechanism for understanding environment dynamics. This "world-model-like" capability is integrated directly into the policy learning without requiring external generative modules or multi-stage training/inference mismatches, offering a more efficient and tightly coupled approach to grounding actions in anticipated visual outcomes.

4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness**: Extensive experiments across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN) and real-world tasks demonstrate MMaDA-VLA's superior performance and generalization capabilities. The pre-training on large-scale, cross-embodiment datasets further enhances the model's ability to adapt to new tasks and environments, including those with unseen objects or varying layouts. Its robust execution in real-world scenarios, including recovery from mistakes, suggests a step towards more reliable and adaptable robotic systems.

5. **Efficiency in Inference**: The integration of a key-value caching mechanism during iterative denoising addresses the potential high inference latency of diffusion models. This optimization makes the iterative generation process more practical for the real-time requirements of robotic control, which is crucial for practical deployment.

6. **Advancement Towards Generalist Robot Policies**: By effectively tackling challenges related to unification, temporal consistency, and dynamics modeling, MMaDA-VLA contributes to the development of more generalist robot policies. Such policies could enable robots to perform a wider array of complex, language-conditioned manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, impacting fields like domestic robotics, logistics, and assistive technologies.

While the visual fidelity of generated goal observations remains an area for further refinement, the work demonstrates that even approximate visual predictions can provide sufficient anticipatory cues for robust action planning and control. This research provides a foundation for future investigations into more refined visual representations within a unified diffusion framework.
