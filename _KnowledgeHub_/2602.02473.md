# HumanX: Toward Agile and Generalizable Humanoid Interaction Skills from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanX is a full-stack framework that synthesizes physically plausible humanoid-object interaction data from human videos and employs a unified imitation learning approach. It enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to acquire agile, generalizable, and robust interaction skills, demonstrating an 8x improvement in generalization success rate over previous methods in simulation and zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks from single video demonstrations.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to acquire agile and adaptive interaction skills due to the difficulty in obtaining high-quality, physically plausible human-object interaction data from videos.
- Traditional reinforcement learning approaches necessitate meticulously engineered, task-specific reward functions, which limits scalability for diverse skills.
- Policies trained on limited demonstrations often overfit and lack generalization to novel conditions or disturbances, hindering robust real-world deployment.

## Method

- The XGen pipeline synthesizes diverse, physically plausible humanoid-object interaction data from monocular human videos, overcoming issues like occlusion and physical implausibility.
- The XMimic unified imitation learning framework uses a teacher-student training architecture and a comprehensive interaction-imitation reward function to learn generalizable skills without task-specific reward engineering.
- Robustness and sim-to-real transfer are achieved through generalization-first training strategies, including disturbed initialization, interaction termination, and extensive domain randomization for physical properties and external forces.

## Results

- In simulation, HumanX achieved an average generalization success rate (GSR) of over 80%, approximately 8 times higher than previous state-of-the-art methods.
- Policies learned from single video demonstrations transferred zero-shot to a physical Unitree G1 robot, executing five complex basketball skills with 70% to 100% success rates in a perception-free mode.
- The robot demonstrated exceptional robustness and adaptive behaviors in MoCap-based interactive tasks, performing over 10 consecutive human-robot basketball passes and distinguishing feints from genuine attacks in a reactive fighting scenario.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthesizing physically plausible interaction trajectories from human videos, rather than relying solely on exact reconstruction, is crucial for efficient and effective robot skill acquisition.
> - A teacher-student distillation approach, combined with a unified reward scheme, can effectively consolidate multiple skills and patterns into a single, generalizable policy.
> - Robust real-world deployment of policies requires inferring external forces from proprioception and training with simulated sensor noise (e.g., MoCap signal loss) and sustained external disturbances.
