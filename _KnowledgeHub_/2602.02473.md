# HumanX: Toward Agile and Generalizable Humanoid Interaction Skills from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanX is a full-stack framework that synthesizes physically plausible humanoid-object interaction data from human videos and employs a unified imitation learning approach. It enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to acquire agile, generalizable, and robust interaction skills, demonstrating an 8x improvement in generalization success rate over previous methods in simulation and zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks from single video demonstrations.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to acquire agile and adaptive interaction skills due to the difficulty in obtaining high-quality, physically plausible human-object interaction data from videos.
- Traditional reinforcement learning approaches necessitate meticulously engineered, task-specific reward functions, which limits scalability for diverse skills.
- Policies trained on limited demonstrations often overfit and lack generalization to novel conditions or disturbances, hindering robust real-world deployment.

## Method

- The XGen pipeline synthesizes diverse, physically plausible humanoid-object interaction data from monocular human videos, overcoming issues like occlusion and physical implausibility.
- The XMimic unified imitation learning framework uses a teacher-student training architecture and a comprehensive interaction-imitation reward function to learn generalizable skills without task-specific reward engineering.
- Robustness and sim-to-real transfer are achieved through generalization-first training strategies, including disturbed initialization, interaction termination, and extensive domain randomization for physical properties and external forces.

## Results

- In simulation, HumanX achieved an average generalization success rate (GSR) of over 80%, approximately 8 times higher than previous state-of-the-art methods.
- Policies learned from single video demonstrations transferred zero-shot to a physical Unitree G1 robot, executing five complex basketball skills with 70% to 100% success rates in a perception-free mode.
- The robot demonstrated exceptional robustness and adaptive behaviors in MoCap-based interactive tasks, performing over 10 consecutive human-robot basketball passes and distinguishing feints from genuine attacks in a reactive fighting scenario.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthesizing physically plausible interaction trajectories from human videos, rather than relying solely on exact reconstruction, is crucial for efficient and effective robot skill acquisition.
> - A teacher-student distillation approach, combined with a unified reward scheme, can effectively consolidate multiple skills and patterns into a single, generalizable policy.
> - Robust real-world deployment of policies requires inferring external forces from proprioception and training with simulated sensor noise (e.g., MoCap signal loss) and sustained external disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "HumanX" addresses a grand challenge in robotics: enabling humanoid robots to perform agile, adaptive, and interactive tasks seamlessly within human environments. This goal is particularly pertinent given the morphological similarity between humanoids and humans, which theoretically allows them to leverage the vast repertoire of human skills. However, translating human prowess into robotic capabilities has long been hindered by significant bottlenecks, which HumanX directly aims to overcome.

The broader research landscape can be categorized into several interconnected areas:

1. **Humanoid Loco-Manipulation and Motion Retargeting**: Early and ongoing research in this area focuses on transferring human motions to robots. Methods like SFV, SkillMimic, VideoMimic, and GMR have explored estimating human and object motion from videos to train simulated or real-world humanoids for tasks ranging from acrobatics to general motion tracking. HumanX builds upon these by using GVHMR for human pose estimation and GMR for retargeting. However, a key limitation of prior methods is the struggle to accurately estimate physically plausible Human-Object Interaction (HOI) data from monocular video, especially for intricate skills, due to issues like occlusion and depth ambiguity. HumanX distinguishes itself by *synthesizing* physically plausible interaction trajectories rather than relying solely on exact reconstruction, thereby addressing the data quality and efficiency problem.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Humanoid Robots**: RL, often combined with physics simulation, has been instrumental in teaching humanoids complex behaviors like gait learning, getting up, and specific tasks like goalkeeping or box carrying. While powerful, a major drawback of traditional RL is the requirement for "meticulously engineered, task-specific reward functions", which limits scalability across diverse tasks. HumanX explicitly states its ambition to learn skills *without task-specific rewards*, pushing towards a more generalizable and scalable RL paradigm.

3. **Imitation Learning (IL) and Human-Object Interaction (HOI) Imitation**: Inspired by successes in character animation (e.g., DeepMimic, AMP), imitation learning has extended to robotics for acquiring locomotion skills like parkour and martial arts. More recently, HOI imitation, as seen in SkillMimic and HDMI, has focused on dynamic interaction skills, often leveraging contact graphs and unified reward schemes. However, deploying these HOI skills on real humanoids introduces significant challenges: kinematic differences, maintaining physical plausibility, the sim-to-real gap, and overfitting to demonstrations. HumanX positions itself as a solution to these challenges by focusing on:
- **Data Generation (XGen)**: Overcoming the difficulty of obtaining high-quality, physically plausible HOI data, especially from monocular video, by synthesizing it with physical rules and supporting scalable augmentation. This addresses the "low data efficiency" mentioned as a challenge for existing methods.
- **Learning Framework (XMimic)**: Tackling the sim-to-real gap and generalization issues through innovations in perception (inferring forces from proprioception, flexible object perception with simulated noise), a unified reward scheme, and generalization-first training strategies (disturbed initialization, interaction termination, domain randomization).

In essence, HumanX represents a significant step towards a general, scalable pipeline for acquiring a diverse range of interactive skills for humanoids directly from human videos. It bridges the gap between vision-based human motion capture, physics-based simulation, and robust real-world robot control, offering a comprehensive "full-stack" approach that surpasses the limitations of prior work in data quality, reward engineering, and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind HumanX stems from the vast, largely untapped potential of human motion for training humanoid robots. Given their human-like morphology, humanoid robots are ideally suited to operate in human environments and interact with everyday objects. However, current robotic learning paradigms are bottlenecked, preventing them from harnessing the "rich diversity of skills demonstrated in human motion."

The **key objectives** of the HumanX framework are meticulously designed to overcome these fundamental limitations:

1. **Enable Agile and Adaptive Interactive Tasks for Humanoids**: The overarching goal is to equip humanoid robots with the capability to perform dynamic and responsive interactions with objects and humans in a diverse array of scenarios. This includes complex loco-manipulation tasks that demand both mobility and dexterity.

2. **Overcome Data Scarcity and Quality Issues**: Current imitation learning methods often rely on large-scale, costly teleoperated demonstrations, or struggle with the physical implausibility and incompleteness of interaction data extracted directly from monocular human videos (e.g., due to occlusion and depth ambiguity). HumanX aims to address this by developing a robust data generation pipeline that synthesizes *diverse and physically plausible* humanoid interaction data from readily available human videos.

3. **Eliminate the Need for Task-Specific Reward Engineering**: Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) approaches require "meticulously engineered, task-specific reward functions." This is a significant barrier to scalability, as designing rewards for every new skill is time-consuming, expert-dependent, and prone to sub-optimal local optima. A core objective of HumanX is to learn generalizable interaction skills *without any task-specific reward design*, using a unified imitation learning framework.

4. **Achieve Strong Generalization and Robustness**: Policies trained on limited demonstrations often overfit, performing poorly when faced with variations in object state, environment, or disturbances. HumanX seeks to enable skills that generalize significantly beyond the specific demonstration data, allowing for adaptive behaviors in novel situations and robustness against external perturbations, which is critical for real-world deployment.

5. **Facilitate Seamless Real-World Deployment**: The ultimate objective is for the learned skills to transfer effectively and robustly to physical humanoid robots. This necessitates addressing the sim-to-real gap and accommodating real-world perception limitations (e.g., intermittent sensor data, reliance on proprioception).

In summary, HumanX is motivated by the vision of humanoids that can learn a vast repertoire of complex, interactive skills by observing humans, without prohibitive data collection costs or laborious reward engineering. It seeks to provide a scalable, task-agnostic pathway for real-world robot skill acquisition, fundamentally changing how humanoid robots learn and adapt.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HumanX is presented as a "full-stack framework" comprising two synergistically designed components: **XGen** for data generation and **XMimic** for imitation learning.

#### 3.1. XGen: Data Generation Pipeline

XGen's core insight is that *physically plausible* interactions are paramount for robot skill acquisition, overriding the need for photometrically faithful (but potentially implausible) reconstructions from video. It synthesizes diverse and physically consistent humanoid interaction data from monocular human videos.

1. **Extracting Humanoid Motion from Human Video**:
- **Human Pose Estimation**: Uses GVHMR to obtain initial 3D human pose sequences (root pose and joint rotations) from a monocular RGB video.
- **Motion Retargeting**: Employs GMR to transfer the estimated human pose sequence to the target humanoid robot's morphology. This involves keypoint alignment, skeleton scaling, and inverse kinematics (IK)-based optimization to adapt the human motion to the robot's kinematics.

2. **Synthesizing Humanoid-Object Interaction**: The process segments the video into contact and non-contact phases for specialized treatment.
- **Contact Phase**:
- **Anchor Definition**: A predefined anchor (e.g., midpoint between palms for grasping, specific body part for kicking) represents the core of the interaction. The relative pose between this anchor and the object is assumed invariant.
- **Object and Relative Pose Estimation**: The object's mesh and its initial rotation relative to the anchor are estimated from a keyframe using SAM-3D, or can be manually defined.
- **Trajectory Synthesis**: The object's trajectory is synthesized by propagating this relative pose along the anchor's trajectory (derived from robot motion).
- **Force-Closure Optimization**: To ensure physical plausibility, the robot's poses during contact are optimized frame-by-frame under force-closure constraints, refining both robot and object poses.
- **Non-Contact Phase**:
- **Physics-Based Simulation**: A physics simulator (IsaacGym) generates physically consistent object trajectories.
- **Forward Simulation**: For actions like shooting, the object is initialized at the end of contact with a predefined velocity, and its trajectory is simulated forward.
- **Backward Simulation**: For actions like catching, the object is simulated backward from the start of contact, and the sequence is reversed to generate the pre-contact trajectory (e.g., a parabolic ball path). Object damping coefficients are inverted for backward simulation.

3. **Interaction Augmentation**: XGen introduces scalable data augmentation to enhance interaction diversity and data coverage, crucial for generalization.
- **Scaling Object Geometry**: Object meshes can be scaled or replaced, and XGen ensures physically plausible interactions with these varied geometries.
- **Enriching Object Trajectories (Contact Phase)**: Simple geometric transformations (translation, scaling) are applied to the object trajectory within the contact phase, allowing generation of data for similar actions (e.g., lifting a box from different heights).
- **Enriching Object Trajectories (Non-Contact Phase)**: Parametric randomization of the object's initial velocity in physics simulation creates diverse trajectories (e.g., hitting a shuttlecock with different parabolas, shooting a basketball from various distances).

#### 3.2. XMimic: Unified Imitation Learning Framework

XMimic focuses on learning accurate, natural, and generalizable interaction skills through several key innovations.

1. **Teacher-Student Training Architecture**:
- **Policy Formulation**: Policies are parameterized as Gaussian distributions, mapping state observations to action distributions, converted to joint torques via PD controllers.
- **Stage 1: Training Privileged Teacher Policies**: Individual teacher policies ($\pi_{tea}$) are trained on each skill dataset generated by XGen. They receive a *privileged state observation* (proprioception, privileged body info, object state). Training uses PPO to maximize a unified interaction-imitation reward.
- **Stage 2: Distilling Teachers into a Deployable Student Policy**: A single student policy ($\pi_{stu}$) is trained on a combined dataset from all skills. The student receives *realistic perceptual observations* (proprioception, optional object observations, no privileged info). Its objective combines PPO policy gradient with a Behavior Cloning (BC) loss, distilling knowledge from pre-trained teachers, enabling a unified, deployable policy.

2. **Perception Design**:
- **Inferring External Force from Proprioception**: A theoretical analysis (dynamics equation) demonstrates that external joint torques can be inferred from proprioceptive data (joint positions, velocities, commanded torques, and history of velocities). The policy's observation space is designed to include these variables, enabling force-aware interaction without dedicated force/torque sensors.
- **Flexible Object Perception for Deployment**:
- **No External Perception (NEP) Mode**: Object observations are removed during student training. The robot relies solely on proprioception, suitable for skills where the object state is largely inferred (e.g., dribbling, jumpshots).
- **MoCap-based Mode**: Object observations are provided by a MoCap system. Crucially, *realistically simulated frame loss* is introduced during training to enable zero-shot adaptation to real-world MoCap systems with intermittent data loss.

3. **Unified Interaction Imitation Reward**: A composite reward function $r_t = r_{body_t} + r_{obj_t} + r_{rel_t} + r_{c_t} + r_{reg_t}$ is used for both teachers and students.
- **Body Imitation ($r_{body_t}$)**: Tracks body position, rotation, joint positions, velocities, and includes an Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) term for naturalness.
- **Object Imitation ($r_{obj_t}$)**: Ensures accurate tracking of the object's state (position, rotation).
- **Relative Motion ($r_{rel_t}$)**: Encourages correct body-object relative spatial relationships.
- **Contact ($r_{c_t}$)**: Penalizes deviations from a reference contact graph, ensuring precise contact timing and location.
- **Regularization ($r_{reg_t}$)**: Promotes motion smoothness, reduces torque, and improves deployment stability (e.g., penalizing feet slippage, joint limits).

4. **Simulation Settings for Generalization**:
- **Disturbed Initialization (DI)**: Random perturbations to the robot's root, joints, and object pose at episode start. This prevents overfitting and encourages exploration.
- **Interaction Termination (IT)**: When the reference frame involves contact, episodes probabilistically terminate if the relative position error between the object and key body parts exceeds a threshold. This prioritizes successful interaction over mere body motion.
- **Domain Randomization (DR)**: Randomizes physical properties (object size, mass, friction, robot CoM offsets, perception noise) and applies sustained random external forces to the robot during training. This is vital for robustness in real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HumanX demonstrates significant advancements in acquiring agile and generalizable interaction skills for humanoid robots, both in simulation and on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

1. **Superior Generalization**: HumanX consistently outperforms prior HOI imitation methods (SkillMimic, OmniRetarget, HDMI) across diverse tasks like Basketball Catch-Shot, Badminton Hitting, and Cargo Pickup.
- On generalization success rate (GSR), HumanX's final model achieves an average of over 80%, which is approximately **8 times higher** than HDMI (the best prior method at ~10%).
- While HDMI shows reasonable success rates (SR) on original demonstrations, its generalization is limited. HumanX maintains high SR (near perfect in base setting) while dramatically improving GSR. This indicates that HumanX learns policies that extend far beyond simple motion replay from the demonstration.

2. **Effectiveness of Ablation Components**:
- **Disturbed Initialization (+DI)**, **Interaction Termination (+IT)**, and **XGen Data Augmentation (+Data Aug)** all contribute incrementally to improved GSR, validating their role in preventing overfitting and promoting robust learning.
- The **Teacher-Student Scheme (+Tea-Stu)** further boosts GSR, particularly for complex tasks and multi-pattern learning, confirming its efficacy in consolidating diverse skills and improving generalization.

3. **Multi-Pattern Skill Learning**: HumanX, especially with the teacher-student framework, successfully learns multiple distinct interaction patterns for a single skill (e.g., different football kicks or badminton hits) from multiple demonstration videos. The teacher-student scheme showed a substantial gain in GSR for multi-pattern tasks (e.g., Football Kicking from 74.2% to 93.1% GSR).

#### 4.2. Real Robot Experiments (Unitree G1 Humanoid)

HumanX policies were successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot under two perception schemes, demonstrating high practical utility. Each skill was learned from a *single video demonstration*.

1. **No External Perception (NEP) Mode**:
- **Perception-Free Control**: The robot successfully executed five complex basketball skills (Jumpshot, Dribble, Pickup, Layup, Pump-fake turnaround fadeaway) without any external object sensing.
- **High Success Rates**: Achieved high success rates (70% to 100%) across 10 trials per skill, demonstrating reliable proprioceptive control for diverse and dynamic interactions. This validates the theoretical analysis that external forces can be implicitly inferred from proprioception.

2. **MoCap-based Mode**:
- **Sustained Closed-Loop Interactions**: The robot performed four interactive tasks (Cargo Pickup, Basketball Catch-and-Pass, Football Kicking, Reactive Fighting) with a human partner.
- **Exceptional Robustness and Generalization**:
- **Basketball Catch-and-Pass**: Sustained over **10 consecutive successful catch-and-pass cycles** with a human partner, demonstrating stability even after dropped balls and seamless resumption.
- **Football Kicking**: Achieved over **14 consecutive successful return kicks** despite variability in human passes.
- **Adaptive & Emergent Behaviors**:
- During cargo pickup, the robot maintained a stable grasp and compensated for external pushes. If the object was taken and placed elsewhere, the robot autonomously *walked to and re-grasped it*.
- In reactive fighting, the robot *distinguished between feints and genuine attacks*, reacting with a brief startle to feints but executing full defensive and counter maneuvers only for real strikes. This indicates real-time interactive reasoning beyond simple motion replay.
- **Crucial Factors for Deployment**: Ablation studies on the real robot revealed that training with sustained random external forces (part of Domain Randomization) and simulating MoCap signal loss during training are critical for maintaining balance and robustness during dynamic interactions and in the face of sensor occlusions.

In essence, HumanX not only excels in simulation but also translates effectively to the real world, enabling complex, adaptive, and generalized interaction skills from minimal, readily available human video demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HumanX represents a substantial leap forward in the field of humanoid robotics and embodied AI, with far-reaching significance and potential impact:

1. **Breaking Key Bottlenecks in Humanoid Robotics**: HumanX directly addresses two of the most critical challenges hindering the widespread deployment of versatile humanoids:
- **Data Scarcity**: By introducing XGen, the framework overcomes the prohibitive cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale, high-quality, physically plausible robot interaction data. Leveraging monocular human videos, a ubiquitous data source, transforms data acquisition from a bottleneck into a scalable resource.
- **Reward Engineering**: The "task-agnostic pathway" through XMimic's unified imitation learning framework eliminates the need for meticulous, task-specific reward engineering, which is notoriously difficult and time-consuming. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing new humanoid skills.

2. **Enabling Generalizable and Adaptive Skills**: The demonstrated 8x higher generalization success rate compared to prior methods is a landmark achievement. Learning skills from a *single video demonstration* that can then adapt to novel object positions, trajectories, and interactions, and exhibit emergent behaviors, is crucial for real-world robustness. This moves robots beyond mere motion replay to genuinely adaptive and intelligent agents.

3. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap Effectively**: The meticulous design choices within XMimic, such as inferring external forces from proprioception, simulating MoCap signal loss during training, and extensive domain randomization, are highly effective in bridging the sim-to-real gap. This enables robust zero-shot transfer of learned policies from simulation to a physical Unitree G1 robot, a critical step for practical robotics.

4. **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction**: The ability to learn sustained closed-loop interactions (e.g., over 10 consecutive human-robot basketball passes) and exhibit real-time interactive reasoning (e.g., distinguishing feints from real attacks) opens new avenues for natural and intuitive human-robot collaboration. This lays foundational groundwork for humanoids that can truly co-exist and interact dynamically with humans in shared spaces.

5. **Scalability and Democratization of Robot Skill Learning**: By providing a full-stack, scalable framework, HumanX has the potential to democratize the development of humanoid skills. Researchers and practitioners can leverage readily available human videos to imbue robots with a wide array of capabilities, without needing extensive robotics expertise for data collection or reward design. This could accelerate the pace of innovation in humanoid applications.

6. **Future Impact on Generalist Humanoid Robots**: HumanX contributes significantly towards the vision of generalist humanoid robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured environments. The framework's ability to learn multiple skills and patterns and exhibit adaptive behaviors positions it as a key component for developing more autonomous, versatile, and competent humanoids that can perform complex tasks in domestic, industrial, or service settings.

In conclusion, HumanX represents a paradigm shift in how humanoid robots can acquire complex interaction skills. By providing a robust and scalable method for converting human observations into generalizable robot behaviors, it brings us closer to a future where humanoids can seamlessly integrate into our world and tackle a wide range of tasks with agility and intelligence.
