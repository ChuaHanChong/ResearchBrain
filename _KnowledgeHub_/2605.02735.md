# Visual Latents Know More Than They Say: Unsilencing Latent Reasoning in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage inference-time latent optimization framework addresses the "Silenced Visual Latents" phenomenon in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), where semantically rich visual latents are under-utilized. This framework consistently improves MLLM performance across eight diverse benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced visual reasoning without modifying the MLLM backbone.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often exhibit an asymmetric inductive bias towards language, leading to visual hallucination and superficial visual understanding.
- Existing Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) methods, which jointly optimize visual latents and answer prediction, suffer from "Silenced Visual Latents" where semantic enrichment of latents does not translate to improved answers.
- This "silencing" is attributed to the autoregressive answer prediction objective favoring "shortcut" routes, relying on direct visual input rather than intermediate latent processing, leading to under-utilization of informative latents.

## Method

- A ==two-stage inference-time latent optimization== framework is introduced to disentangle the conflicting objectives of latent quality and utilization, without modifying the MLLM backbone parameters.
- Stage I, "Visual Latent Warm-up," enhances latent semantic quality using query-guided relevance scoring and a ==chunk-wise contrastive alignment== objective to assign distinct positive and negative visual evidence to each latent token.
- Stage II, "Latent-to-Answer Reinforcement," enforces the utilization of these warmed-up latents during answer prediction by optimizing them with a confidence-progression reward function, leveraging ==Native Evolutionary Strategy== (**NES**) for gradient estimation.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently achieves best or second-best performance across eight diverse visual reasoning benchmarks (e.g., IQTest, RR, MMVP, ScienceQA) using both Qwen2.5VL-7B and Qwen2.5VL-3B backbones.
- It provides consistent performance gains over vanilla MLLM baselines, with notable improvements such as +**8.66%** on IQTest and +**5.00%** on MM-Vista for Qwen2.5VL-7B.
- Ablation studies confirm the complementary nature of the two-stage optimization, and performance increases with longer latent reasoning lengths (K), with MMVP accuracy improving from **72.33%** at K=2 to **73.67%** at K=10, demonstrating effective latent capacity utilization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The paper identifies and characterizes "Silenced Visual Latents," a pathology where MLLM internal visual latents become semantically capable but are systematically under-utilized due to autoregressive "shortcuts" during joint optimization.
> - Disentangling the optimization objectives of latent quality and latent utilization through an inference-time, frozen-backbone framework is crucial for effectively "unsilencing" latent reasoning pathways.
> - Optimizing latent representations directly at inference time, rather than through backbone parameter updates, enables MLLMs to genuinely leverage their internal visual reasoning capacity for more robust multimodal understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate visual encoders with Large Language Models (LLMs) to enable joint reasoning over both language and visual inputs. While MLLMs have demonstrated advancements in various cross-modal tasks, they often exhibit an asymmetric inductive bias towards language, leading to issues such as visual hallucination and superficial visual understanding.

A common strategy to address these limitations is the adoption of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, where models generate explicit textual reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. This approach aims to provide additional auxiliary reasoning contexts. A related and increasingly explored paradigm is Latent Reasoning, which shifts intermediate reasoning from discrete textual tokens to continuous latent representations. Latent reasoning is posited to offer increased representational bandwidth and reduced autoregressive context length, making it particularly suitable for integrating high-dimensional visual evidence compactly without external tools or complex pixel-level generation.

Existing Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) methods typically instantiate latent representations by aligning them with pre-curated visual evidence (e.g., regions of interest, segmentation maps) and simultaneously optimizing these latents alongside the autoregressive prediction of the final answer. This paper identifies a specific limitation within this common joint optimization scheme, termed "Silenced Visual Latents," where the semantic enrichment of visual latents does not translate effectively into improved answer prediction. The proposed work directly addresses this identified issue, distinguishing itself by disentangling the optimization objectives and introducing an inference-time latent optimization framework that aims to fully leverage the latent reasoning capacity without modifying the MLLM's core parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address and rectify the "Silenced Visual Latents" phenomenon observed in existing Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) methods. The authors' motivation stems from a critical inconsistency identified in the joint optimization of visual latent representations and autoregressive answer prediction in MLLMs.

The specific problem identified is that while visual latents become semantically enriched during training—progressively aligning better with task-relevant visual evidence (Observation #1, Figure 2)—this improvement does not consistently translate into enhanced final answer accuracy. Instead, the performance of jointly optimized models often fluctuates or stagnates (Observation #2, Figure 2). This reveals a dissociation between the quality of latent representations and their effectiveness in influencing the final prediction.

Further investigation into this discrepancy (Figure 3) revealed two key mechanistic insights:
1. **Shortcut Reliance:** Within the shared parameter space for optimization, the autoregressive answer prediction objective tends to favor "shortcut" routes, relying heavily on direct visual input rather than routing through the intermediate visual latents. This is evidenced by attention maps showing that attention gradually drifts towards raw visual tokens rather than the visual latents during training (Figure 3a).
2. **Compromised Latent States:** Concurrently, the latent tokens are driven towards "transition-like" states, specifically evidenced by an overwhelmingly large prediction logit for the `<latent_end>` token (Figure 3b). This suggests that the model is learning to quickly bypass the latent reasoning span rather than processing it for informative content. The balance hyperparameter (λ) between latent alignment and answer autoregression influences this, with a smaller λ alleviating the issue.

The authors term this phenomenon "Silenced Visual Latents" because the visual latents, despite becoming semantically capable, are systematically under-utilized or "silenced" during the final answer prediction due to this optimization pathology. The core motivation is to develop a method that disentangles these conflicting objectives, allowing the semantic quality of visual latents to be fully exploited for improved multimodal reasoning without compromising the MLLM's existing capabilities. This is proposed to be achieved by optimizing the latent reasoning directly at inference time, keeping the backbone MLLM parameters frozen.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology addresses the "Silenced Visual Latents" by disentangling the conflicting objectives of latent quality and latent utilization through a two-stage inference-time latent optimization framework. This approach keeps the MLLM backbone parameters frozen, focusing solely on enhancing the visual latents for each input instance.

**Problem Formulation:**
The standard LVR paradigm optimizes an MLLM (Φ) with a joint objective (Equation 1). This objective combines a visual latent reasoning term, which encourages alignment of latent representations (H) with pre-curated visual reasoning clues (˜V), and an answer autoregression term, which maximizes the probability of generating the correct answer (A) conditioned on visual (V), query (Q), latents (H), and previous tokens. The authors argue that this joint optimization within a shared parameter space leads to the "Silenced Visual Latents" pathology.

**Disentangled Inference-Time Optimization:**
To overcome the limitations of joint training, the proposed framework directly optimizes the visual latents (H) at inference time. This per-instance optimization occurs in two stages:

**Stage I: Visual Latent Warm-up**
This stage aims to enhance the semantic quality of the visual latents (H) before enforcing their contribution to the final answer.
1. **Query-Guided Relevance Scoring:** For a given multimodal input {Vision: V, Query: Q}, the framework first computes a query-guided relevance score (s_n) for each visual token (v_n ∈ V). This score is the average attention that the query tokens (Q) allocate to each visual token (Equation 2), indicating its relevance to the question.
2. **Chunk-wise Contrastive Alignment:** Instead of using a common supervision pool for all latent tokens, which could lead to latent collapse, visual tokens are ranked by their relevance scores (s_n). The ranked tokens are then partitioned into non-overlapping chunks, with distinct positive (P_k) and negative (N_k) sets assigned to each latent token (h_k ∈ H) (Equation 3). This ensures that each latent token aligns with a distinct set of highly relevant visual evidence while being repelled from a distinct set of low-relevance content, thereby preserving diversity across the latent reasoning sequence.
3. **Contrastive Objective:** The latent tokens (H) are then optimized using a contrastive objective (Equation 4). This objective pulls each latent token (h_k) towards its assigned positive visual tokens (P_k) and pushes it away from its assigned negative visual tokens (N_k), using cosine similarity and a temperature parameter (τ). A balancing factor (β) corrects for positive/negative set-size imbalance.
This stage optimizes H for a small number of steps (N_sft, default 5), yielding a warmed-up latent state H∗sft.

**Stage II: Latent-to-Answer Reinforcement**
This stage focuses on enforcing the utilization of the warmed-up latents (H∗sft) during answer prediction, ensuring they actively contribute to the final answer.
1. **Confidence-Progression Reward:** The core idea is to incentivize reasoning progression. A reward function, R(˜H), is defined to encourage the output token distribution of each latent token (h_k) to become progressively more concentrated along the latent span (from h_1 to h_K). This is achieved by penalizing increases in top-δ entropy (E(k)) from one latent position to the next (Equation 5). A higher reward indicates that the latent reasoning is becoming more certain and focused as it progresses.
2. **Stochastic Local Exploration:** Optimization begins from H∗sft and proceeds for N_rl steps (default 15) using Native Evolutionary Strategy (NES) gradient estimation. At each step, a Gaussian perturbation (ϵ_i) is sampled and added to the current latent state (H_i) to form a candidate (˜H_i).
3. **Latent State Update:** The latent state is then updated using the NES gradient estimator (Equation 6), which leverages the reward R(˜H_i) and the perturbation ϵ_i. The step size (α) and the standard deviation of the perturbation (σ_i) are adjusted, with σ_i decaying geometrically to gradually shrink the exploration radius.
4. **Best State Retention:** Since the reward trajectory is non-monotonic, the best-performing latent state (H∗) encountered throughout Stage II is retained for final answer decoding.

The overall procedure (Algorithm 1) outlines these two sequential stages, where Stage I focuses on semantic quality and Stage II on utilization, all performed at inference time with a frozen MLLM backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed two-stage inference-time latent optimization framework across various benchmarks and MLLM backbones.

**Overall Performance (Table 1):**
- **Superiority on Benchmarks:** The proposed method consistently achieves best or second-best performance across eight benchmarks spanning visual perception (Counting, IQ Test, RR), visual reasoning (MMVP, Hull-Bench), comprehensive reasoning (ScienceQA, MM-Star), and mathematical reasoning (MM-Vista). This holds for both Qwen2.5VL-7B and Qwen2.5VL-3B backbones.
- **Consistent Gains:** Compared to the vanilla MLLM baseline, the method provides consistent performance gains across all benchmarks, with particularly notable improvements on IQTest (+8.66%), RR (+5.88%), MMVP (+4.33%), and MM-Vista (+5.00%) for Qwen2.5VL-7B.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Non-Latent Reasoning (CoT-based):** The proposed method consistently outperforms verbal chain-of-thought methods (MCoT, CCoT, ICoT), which sometimes degrade performance on fine-grained visual tasks. This suggests that textual reasoning alone is insufficient for certain challenges, and latent reasoning provides a more direct integration of visual information.
 - **Latent Reasoning Baselines:** The method shows clear advantages over other latent reasoning baselines (DMLR, Monet, CoVT, LVR), especially on perception-intensive benchmarks. Unlike these methods, which may rely on dynamically retrieved visual patches, complex training schemes, or are prone to visual input shortcuts, the proposed method optimizes latent representations directly at inference time without parameter updates, ensuring latents serve as genuine reasoning substrates.

**Ablation Studies (Table 2, Figure 5, Table 3, Table 5):**
- **Two-Stage Optimization Design:** The ablation study (Table 2) confirms the complementary nature of the two stages. Stage I (Visual Latent Warm-up) alone significantly improves performance (e.g., MMVP from 68.67% to 72.00%). The addition of Stage II (Latent-to-Answer Reinforcement) further enhances results (e.g., MMVP to 73.00%), demonstrating that improving latent quality and promoting latent utilization are distinct but synergistic objectives.
- **Optimization Steps (Figure 5a, b):** Performance shows moderate gains with increasing Stage I steps (N_sft) and is more sensitive to Stage II steps (N_rl), peaking at N_rl = 15 before a slight decline. This suggests a balance for efficiency and quality, leading to the chosen defaults (N_sft = 5, N_rl = 15).
- **Latent Reasoning Length (Table 3, Table 5):** The method exhibits increasing performance gains as the latent reasoning length (K) increases (e.g., MMVP improves from 72.33% at K=2 to 73.67% at K=10). This indicates that the method effectively leverages additional latent capacity. In contrast, existing methods like LVR demonstrated weaker utilization of longer latent sequences.
- **Contrastive Patch Configuration (Figure 5c, d):** Optimal performance was observed with a compact positive set (pos_num = 2) and a moderate negative set (neg_num = 4) in Stage I, suggesting that too few negatives provide insufficient signal while too many positives can dilute alignment.

**Attention Visualization (Figure 6, Figure 9):**
- Visualizations of attention maps show that optimized latents receive substantially stronger and more focused attention from the predicted outputs compared to raw visual input tokens. This indicates that the framework successfully redirects the model's focus to the refined latent representations, confirming that the "Silenced Visual Latents" are effectively "unsilenced" and actively utilized.

**Efficiency (Figure 7):**
- The efficiency ratio, which measures performance gain relative to output token count, shows that the proposed method achieves the highest ratio (+0.75). This indicates superior performance gains with competitive token efficiency compared to other latent reasoning methods and non-latent baselines.

**Generalization to Various Models (Table 4):**
- The framework demonstrates model-agnostic effectiveness, consistently improving performance across different MLLM backbones, including VLAA Thinking-7B and R1 OneVision-7B. Significant improvements were observed on tasks like IQTest (+14.00%) and Hull-Bench (+7.41%) for R1 OneVision-7B.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries significant implications for the development and understanding of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in the realm of visual reasoning.

**Theoretical and Practical Significance:**
- **Identification of a Novel Optimization Pathology:** The paper's primary contribution is the identification and empirical characterization of "Silenced Visual Latents." This phenomenon highlights a critical flaw in current joint optimization strategies for LVR, where internal representations become semantically rich but are under-utilized due to autoregressive shortcuts. This insight contributes to a deeper understanding of MLLM training dynamics and the challenges of integrating diverse modalities.
- **Disentanglement of Conflicting Objectives:** The proposed inference-time, frozen-backbone framework provides a novel solution by effectively disentangling the objectives of latent quality and latent utilization. This demonstrates that performance gains can be achieved by optimizing reasoning pathways *post-training* without altering the foundational model parameters, which is a departure from conventional fine-tuning or full retraining approaches.
- **Enhanced MLLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By "unsilencing" the visual latents, the method enables MLLMs to genuinely leverage their internal visual reasoning capacity. This leads to consistent and substantial performance improvements across a wide array of visual reasoning benchmarks, particularly those requiring fine-grained perception and complex inferential steps.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved Reliability and Robustness:** The ability to route predictions through more deliberate latent reasoning pathways, rather than relying on superficial direct visual inputs, could lead to MLLMs that are less prone to visual hallucinations and exhibit more robust, grounded understanding of visual content.
- **Accessibility for MLLM Enhancement:** The inference-time nature of the solution, requiring no retraining of the backbone model, makes it highly accessible. This allows developers and researchers to enhance the reasoning capabilities of pre-trained MLLMs without the substantial computational resources typically required for model fine-tuning or architecture modifications. This is particularly valuable for deploying MLLMs in specialized applications where retraining is impractical.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a new direction for research into MLLM reasoning. Future work could explore more sophisticated reward designs for latent optimization, investigate the interplay between different types of visual reasoning clues, or adapt this disentangled optimization strategy to other modalities or reasoning paradigms within MLLMs. The identified "silencing" phenomenon might also exist in other contexts, prompting broader investigations into how models utilize their internal representations.
- **Applications in Complex Reasoning:** The consistent performance gains on tasks like IQ tests, scientific QA, and mathematical reasoning within visual contexts suggest the potential for MLLMs equipped with this framework to tackle more complex real-world problems requiring deep multimodal understanding, such as in scientific discovery, advanced robotics, or medical diagnostics.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
- The method introduces additional inference-time computation due to per-instance latent optimization.
- Its effectiveness depends on the quality of patch selection and reward design.
- Current evaluation focuses on image-based reasoning, with extensions to video and embodied settings identified as future work.

Despite these limitations, the paper offers a significant step forward in making MLLMs more effective and interpretable by unlocking their inherent, but previously suppressed, visual reasoning capabilities.
