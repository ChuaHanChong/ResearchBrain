# You Need Better Attention Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> GOAT recasts attention as entropic optimal transport regularized by KL divergence to a learnable prior rather than an implicit uniform one, so optimal weights become a softmax over content scores plus an additive log-prior. The prior combines a truncated Fourier relative term with a key-only sink bias, folded into query/key subspaces so one unmodified FlashAttention call computes it, improving perplexity, long-context retrieval, and memory.

## Problem

- Standard softmax attention is heuristically motivated; its transport derivation hides an uninformative uniform prior that penalizes any deviation from flat attention unless content scores justify it.
- Rotary encodings inject position multiplicatively, coupling positional bias magnitude to content norms, so enforcing structural defaults forces inflated content vectors and corrupts semantic representations.
- ALiBi keeps additivity but locks each head to a rigid monotone recency slope, while learned table biases fit the training window and fail to extrapolate.

## Method

- ==KL-regularized transport== generalization: replacing ==Shannon entropy== with ==KL divergence== against an arbitrary prior yields a closed-form optimum, softmax over content scores shifted by an additive log-prior.
- ==Spectral relative prior==: truncated ==Fourier series== with learnable signed coefficients, factorized via ==angle-difference identities== into 2R-dimensional positional query and key features that reproduce the bias exactly through the dot product.
- ==Key-only sink bias==: per-head linear recency term, sinusoidal-feature MLP, and first-token bump occupying one extra key dimension, so pre-scaled composite vectors run through unmodified ==FlashAttention==.

## Results

- 125M models on C4 reach perplexity **30.95** versus **32.49** for ALiBi and **33.24** for RoPE, with stable extrapolation to **16x** the training length.
- At 1.23B parameters on FineWeb, GOAT scores **20.75** versus RoPE **20.82** at 2k context, and **24.77** versus **417.43** at 16k.
- Genome modeling gives validation NLL **1.1294** versus RoPE **1.2054**, with peak CUDA memory **1.83 GB** versus **2.86 GB** at comparable throughput.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Additive, disentangled positional priors beat multiplicative rotation: keeping structure out of content vectors buys extrapolation without sacrificing in-window fit.
> - Attention sinks are predictable transport defaults, not artifacts; a query-independent key bias supplies them, since posteriors collapse to the prior when content signal weakens.
> - Parameterizing a bias as a factorizable continuous function rather than a lookup table keeps it kernel-compatible and extrapolatable past the training length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

Self-attention is the principal mechanism underlying Transformer models used in language modeling, computer vision, diffusion modeling, and sequential decision-making. Standard scaled dot-product attention computes similarities between queries and keys, applies a temperature-scaled softmax, and uses the resulting probabilities to combine value vectors. Although this formulation is effective, its treatment of positional structure and default attention behavior has largely developed through separate heuristic modifications.

The paper addresses three related areas of current research. The first is **attention efficiency**. Methods such as linear attention, sparse attention, and FlashAttention seek to reduce computational or memory costs while preserving the functionality of attention. The second is **positional modeling**. Relative position biases, RoPE, ALiBi, and learned positional embeddings introduce sequence or spatial information into attention, but they differ in their flexibility and extrapolation behavior. The third is **long-context stability**, including the emergence of attention sinks, in which one or a few tokens receive substantial attention probability even when they are not semantically relevant.

The paper places all three issues within an **Entropic Optimal Transport (EOT)** interpretation of attention. Under this view, attention distributes a unit amount of mass from a query across keys while balancing affinity-based transport cost against an entropy-related regularizer. Standard softmax attention is interpreted as using an implicit uniform prior over keys. The authors propose replacing this uniform prior with a learnable, structured prior and introduce **Generalized Optimal transport Attention with Trainable priors (GOAT)**.

GOAT is related algebraically to additive positional biases, but the authors distinguish it conceptually from conventional positional encodings. Its central design principle is to separate content-based similarity from structural routing preferences. This places the method near recent work on relative biases and attention sinks, while also connecting it to optimal transport, Fourier representations of translation-invariant functions, and information-theoretic maximum-entropy principles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper has four primary objectives.

First, it seeks to provide a formal explanation of why standard attention may be structurally limited. The authors argue that the entropy regularizer in ordinary attention is equivalent, up to a constant, to penalizing the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the attention distribution and a uniform distribution. This means that standard attention implicitly assumes that all key positions are equally likely before content evidence is considered. The authors regard this as an overly restrictive prior because many tasks have structural regularities such as locality, recency, periodicity, directionality, or preferred default positions.

Second, the paper aims to construct a trainable prior that is more expressive than fixed schemes such as ALiBi, while retaining the extrapolation properties associated with relative or functional positional encodings. A finite learned position-embedding table may fit the training range but does not define behavior outside that range. GOAT instead uses continuous functions of position, allowing the learned structural pattern to be evaluated at longer sequence lengths.

Third, the authors address attention sinks. Existing models often create sinks indirectly by learning content key vectors with unusually large norms. This can entangle a token’s structural role as a default destination with its semantic representation. GOAT introduces an explicit key-only prior term so that a sink can be learned without changing the content space.

Fourth, the paper evaluates whether the same mechanism can work across modalities. The authors extend the prior from one-dimensional sequence positions to two-dimensional image coordinates and test language, genomic, visual, and synthetic retrieval tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. EOT formulation

For a single query, the paper defines attention as the solution of

\[
\min_{p\in\Delta}\{-\langle p,s\rangle+\tau KL(p\|\pi)\},
\]

where \(s\) contains content-based query–key scores, \(\tau\) is a temperature, and \(\pi\) is a prior distribution over keys. The resulting optimum is

\[
p_j^\ast=\operatorname{softmax}_j(s_j/\tau+\log \pi_j).
\]

When \(\pi\) is uniform, the equation reduces to standard softmax attention. Therefore, the log-prior appears as an additive term in the attention logits.

#### 3.2. Parameterization of the relative prior

GOAT represents the relative log-prior as a truncated Fourier series:

\[
K^{rel}_{ij}=\sum_{r=1}^{R}
[\alpha_r\cos(\omega_r(i-j))+\beta_r\sin(\omega_r(i-j))].
\]

The cosine coefficients model symmetric relationships, while the sine coefficients model asymmetric or directional relationships. The frequencies are fixed geometric frequencies in the main experiments, using a base of 10,000, although the paper also reports frequency ablations.

The authors use trigonometric identities to factorize this relative term into a query vector and a key vector. Each frequency contributes a two-dimensional positional subspace consisting of sine and cosine features. This allows the relative prior to be incorporated into a standard query–key dot product rather than computed as a separate dense \(L\times L\) bias matrix.

The paper further argues that, under finite-dimensional SDPA compatibility, translation equivariance, and boundedness, admissible relative priors must be finite trigonometric functions. This provides a mathematical justification for the Fourier parameterization within the constraints selected by the authors, although it does not establish that this family is optimal for every learning task.

#### 3.3. Explicit sink and absolute-position terms

GOAT adds a query-independent key bias \(u(j)\), parameterized as a combination of a learned linear recency term, a small absolute-position multilayer perceptron, sinusoidal features, normalized position, logarithmic position, and an optional first-token bias:

\[
u^h(j)=\lambda^h j+g^h(\phi^{abs}(j))+b^h\mathbf{1}\{j=0\}.
\]

This term is implemented through one additional query–key dimension. The query component is set to one and the key component is \(u(j)\), producing a key-only logit contribution.

The complete prior is

\[
K_{ij}=K^{rel}_{ij}+u(j).
\]

Content and prior features occupy separate subspaces. The content dimensions are rescaled by the ratio of total head dimension to content dimension, while the prior dimensions are scaled so that the prior enters the SDPA logits without being attenuated by the usual head-dimension normalization. The value vectors remain content-based. Consequently, position affects routing probabilities but does not directly occupy the value representation.

#### 3.4. Theoretical analysis of attention sinks

The paper proves that if the dynamic range of content scores for a query becomes small, the posterior attention distribution approaches the prior. This establishes that every attention head requires some default behavior when semantic evidence is weak. With a uniform prior, mass spreads across the context. With a peaked prior, mass concentrates on designated default keys.

The authors define total context sensitivity as the probability mass assigned to all non-sink context tokens. Under a uniform prior, this quantity approaches one as sequence length increases in the low-signal regime. Under a prior with sink margin \(\delta\), the sensitivity is bounded by

\[
\frac{L-1}{e^\delta+L-1}.
\]

This theoretical result implies that a margin growing logarithmically with sequence length can maintain a bounded default preference.

#### 3.5. Experimental design

The experiments include:

- A synthetic copy-mixture task involving global copying, local copying, and random noise.
- 125-million-parameter C4 language models trained at a context length of 2,048.
- Matched 1.23-billion-parameter Llama-style models trained on FineWeb.
- Passkey retrieval and needle-in-a-haystack long-context tests.
- Human genome next-token modeling.
- Spatial visual question answering.
- ImageNet-1k Vision Transformers trained at 224-pixel resolution and evaluated at higher resolutions.

Baselines include RoPE, ALiBi, learned absolute positional embeddings, sinusoidal encodings, and models without positional encoding in selected experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

On the synthetic copy-mixture task, the learned prior separates two structural patterns: a key-specific preference for the first token and a relative bias concentrated near the previous-token diagonal. The Fourier component also learns negative regions that suppress intervening positions. This supports the claimed ability to represent attraction and repulsion in positional logit space.

For 125-million-parameter language models trained on C4, the paper reports that GOAT improves in-distribution perplexity by **1.55 points relative to ALiBi**. It also maintains stable performance when evaluated at sequence lengths substantially beyond the 2,048-token training window. The reported ablations indicate that increasing the number of Fourier modes mainly improves extrapolation, while changes to the frequency base have limited effect. Zero initialization is reported to be reliable, and an ALiBi initialization provides a small extrapolation benefit.

In the 1.23-billion-parameter FineWeb experiment, the reported perplexities are:

| Model | 2k | 4k | 8k | 16k |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RoPE | 20.82 | 28.53 | 89.45 | 417.43 |
| GOAT | 20.75 | 21.04 | 22.69 | 24.77 |

Thus, GOAT is close to RoPE at the training length but exhibits much slower degradation at longer lengths in this experiment.

On long-context retrieval, GOAT maintains higher passkey and needle-in-a-haystack accuracy as context length increases beyond the training range. The paper attributes this to the ability of the learned prior to encourage structurally useful routing without imposing a fixed distance penalty. In particular, it reports that ALiBi can suppress distant retrieval targets because of its monotonic bias, whereas GOAT can reduce the prior margin when content evidence strongly identifies a distant key.

For genomic modeling, GOAT achieves a validation NLL of **1.1294**, compared with **1.2054** for RoPE. Peak CUDA memory allocation decreases from **2.86 GB to 1.83 GB**, while throughput remains similar, at approximately 138,171 versus 139,886 tokens per second. The paper also reports modest improvements in nucleotide-generation accuracy and in the correlation between generated and true GC-content profiles.

In ImageNet experiments, GOAT learns a two-dimensional local, shift-invariant prior and retains higher accuracy when evaluated at resolutions above the 224-pixel training resolution. In the spatial VQA experiment, GOAT achieves an answer NLL of **0.350** and exact-answer accuracy of **20.3%**, compared with 0.367 and 16.5% for RoPE and 0.359 and 17.2% without positional encoding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper’s main significance is conceptual and architectural. It presents attention as a content-sensitive distribution modulated by an explicit structural prior, rather than as a mechanism whose positional behavior must be indirectly encoded through query and key representations. This framing provides a common explanation for relative biases, recency preferences, and attention sinks.

If the reported results generalize, GOAT could be useful for models requiring long-context inference, variable input lengths, or spatial extrapolation. Its implementation within an ordinary SDPA call is relevant to practical deployment because it avoids custom attention kernels and dense bias matrices. The separate sink channel may also improve interpretability and reduce pressure on semantic representations.

Several limitations remain. The experiments use particular model sizes, datasets, and selected baselines, and the paper does not establish performance across all Transformer architectures or downstream tasks. The claimed computational advantage depends on the reserved positional dimensions and implementation details. The Fourier family is mathematically justified only under stated constraints, and fixed frequencies may limit the representation of nonperiodic structural patterns. Finally, the theoretical results describe behavior in low-signal regimes but do not by themselves guarantee improved optimization or task performance. Further evaluation on larger-scale models, diverse real-world workloads, and more demanding spatial and multimodal settings would be needed to assess the broader applicability of the method.
