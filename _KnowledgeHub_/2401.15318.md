# Gaussian Splashing: Unified Particles for Versatile Motion Synthesis and Rendering

> [!summary] Summary
> Gaussian Splashing (GSP) unifies physics-based animations of solids and fluids with 3D Gaussian Splatting for high-quality novel view synthesis and dynamic scene interaction. The framework achieves realistic rendering of complex material properties, such as fluid specular reflections and surface tension, while robustly handling large solid deformations and two-way fluid-solid coupling.

## Problem

- Existing 3DGS methods struggle to produce high-quality renderings for objects undergoing large rotational or anisotropic deformations, often resulting in fuzzy or 'spiky' artifacts.
- Accurately rendering complex, dynamic phenomena like translucent and specular fluids within 3DGS is challenging due to simplified light field composition and lack of explicit appearance properties.
- A unified framework was needed that could robustly simulate diverse physical interactions (deformable solids, rigid bodies, fluids, and their coupling) and render them with high fidelity in real-time using learning-based scene representations.

## Method

- A unified particle representation leverages 3DGS kernels and Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) particles, employing a decoupling strategy for solids where simulation particles' deformations are interpolated to rendering kernels.
- 3DGS training is enhanced with physically-based rendering (PBR) material parameters, an anisotropy loss to prevent rendering artifacts from large deformations, and normal consistency regularization.
- The system integrates Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) for solids and Position-Based Fluids (PBF) for fluid simulation, incorporating advanced features like surface tension models and dynamic soft shadows for visual realism.

## Results

- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of anisotropy regularization in preventing deformation artifacts and the necessity of PBR materials for realistic fluid specular highlights, significantly enhancing visual quality.
- GSP demonstrates robust two-way fluid-solid coupling, successfully simulating complex scenarios like objects deforming and floating in water, waves crashing, and fluid flooding environments.
- The framework enables novel interactive scene editing, allowing users to introduce diverse physical phenomena such as fluid flooding, object liquefaction, and realistic surface tension effects (e.g., droplets and overflowing liquids) in reconstructed scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent point-based nature of 3DGS kernels and PBD particles offers a natural compatibility for building a unified, efficient framework for physics-driven neural rendering.
> - Decoupling the uniformly sampled simulation particles from the adaptively distributed rendering kernels for solids is essential to maintain both accurate physics and high-quality rendering during deformation.
> - Incorporating PBR material parameters and specific rendering approximations (e.g., Beer's Law for refraction, surface normal assignment for fluids) within the 3DGS framework is crucial for realistic depiction of dynamic fluids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "Gaussian Splashing" operates at the crucial intersection of neural rendering and physics-based simulation, two rapidly evolving fields in computer graphics and vision.

**Evolution of 3D Scene Representation:**
The landscape of 3D scene representation has been significantly reshaped by learning-based techniques. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) revolutionized novel view synthesis by encoding 3D scenes implicitly within an MLP, achieving unprecedented photorealism. However, NeRFs are computationally intensive, limiting their use in real-time or interactive applications. This led to the emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which offers an explicit, point-based representation (a collection of 3D Gaussian kernels). 3DGS achieves comparable visual quality to NeRFs while enabling significantly faster, real-time rendering through rasterization.

**Dynamic Scenes and Physics Integration in NeRF/3DGS:**
Both NeRF and 3DGS have seen rapid extensions to dynamic scenes. Early "dynamic NeRFs" aimed to capture time-varying scenes by learning deformation fields or adding temporal dimensions. Similarly, "4D Gaussian Splatting" extended 3DGS to dynamic content by learning deformations of the Gaussian kernels over time. While these advancements enable the *reconstruction and rendering* of observed dynamic scenes, they typically rely on learning observed motion patterns, rather than *simulating* physically plausible interactions.

**Bridging Physics and Neural Rendering:**
The challenge of integrating explicit physics-based simulation with neural scene representations is a critical frontier. Previous efforts have explored this for NeRFs, such as PIE-NeRF and PAC-NeRF, primarily focusing on deformable solids. However, a significant gap remains in robustly integrating highly dynamic and complex phenomena like fluids, especially with the real-time advantages of 3DGS. Fluids pose unique challenges due to their translucent and specular properties, large-scale positional shifts, and intricate surface details, which vanilla NeRF/3DGS struggle to capture accurately.

**GSP's Unique Position:**
"Gaussian Splashing" directly addresses this gap. It builds upon the explicit, particle-like nature of 3DGS kernels, which naturally aligns with particle-based physics simulation methods like Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) and Position-Based Fluids (PBF). While a recent work, PhysGaussian, also integrated physics simulation with 3DGS, GSP appears to advance this by significantly enhancing rendering quality for fluids (e.g., proper specular reflections) and addressing artifacts from large solid deformations. GSP leverages insights from GaussianShader to augment 3DGS kernels with physically-based rendering (PBR) capabilities, enabling realistic reflections and refractions that were previously problematic for dynamic fluids in 3DGS. By unifying these explicit representations, GSP aims to create a versatile framework for both simulation and high-quality rendering of complex, interactive, physically-driven dynamic scenes. This work represents a significant step towards developing tools that allow users to not only reconstruct static scenes but also actively interact with and manipulate them in a physically consistent and visually realistic manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind "Gaussian Splashing" are multifaceted, aiming to push the boundaries of neural rendering by deeply integrating physics-based simulation:

1. **Demonstrate Feasibility of Physics-Integrated 3DGS:** The primary goal is to conclusively show that it is possible and practical to integrate physics-based animations of both solids and fluids directly into 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes. This integration aims to enable the creation of novel and dynamic effects within virtual environments reconstructed using 3DGS.

2. **Achieve a Unified Framework for Dynamics and Rendering:** The authors seek to establish a cohesive system where rendering, novel view synthesis, and the dynamics of interacting solids and fluids are managed within a single, consistent framework. This is motivated by the inherent coherence between the point-based representation of Gaussian Splatting and particle-based physics simulation (Position-Based Dynamics, PBD).

3. **Overcome Limitations of Vanilla 3DGS in Dynamic Scenarios:**
 - **Solid Deformation Artifacts:** Vanilla 3DGS, primarily designed for static view synthesis, often produces rendering artifacts like "spiky noises" or fuzzy results when Gaussian kernels undergo large rotational or anisotropic deformations. A key motivation is to mitigate these issues to maintain high rendering quality for dynamic solids.
 - **Realistic Fluid Rendering:** Standard 3DGS simplifies light field composition and lacks well-defined appearance properties for complex materials. This makes rendering translucent and specular fluids accurately cumbersome. The paper is motivated to engineer solutions for realistically reproducing surface highlights and refraction effects on dynamic fluids.

4. **Enable Versatile Physical Interactions:** The goal is to build a system flexible enough to handle a broad spectrum of physical phenomena, including deformable bodies, rigid objects, and fluid dynamics, as well as their two-way coupling. This versatility is crucial for creating rich and interactive virtual experiences.

5. **Facilitate Novel Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) Modalities:** Beyond just realistic rendering, the authors are motivated to enable entirely new ways for users to interact with and edit reconstructed scenes. Examples include flooding a scene, floating objects, or even "liquefying" objects in a scene, thereby offering compelling effects not possible with existing NeRF/3DGS systems.

6. **Enhance Visual Realism with Advanced Rendering Features:** A continuous motivation in graphics is realism. GSP aims to enhance visual quality by incorporating features like specular-aware Gaussian shading (building on GaussianShader), surface tension effects for fluids, and dynamic soft shadows, which are critical for convincing dynamic scenes.

In essence, GSP's objectives are to move beyond passive reconstruction and learned dynamics towards active, physically-plausible interaction and highly realistic rendering within the efficient 3DGS framework, thereby expanding the utility and creative potential of neural scene representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Gaussian Splashing (GSP) is an intricately engineered pipeline that combines several state-of-the-art techniques from neural rendering, physics simulation, and computer graphics to achieve its objectives. The core idea revolves around leveraging and enhancing the point-based nature of 3D Gaussian Splatting and Position-Based Dynamics (PBD).

#### 3.1. Unified Particle Representation with Decoupling Strategy

- **Coherence:** GSP starts from the observation that both 3DGS kernels (ellipsoids) and PBD particles are point-based representations, making them inherently compatible for a unified framework.
- **Solid Simulation-Rendering Decoupling:** For solid objects, GSP employs a crucial decoupling strategy.
 - **Simulation Particles:** To ensure robust and accurate physics simulation, particles for solids are sampled *uniformly* from an explicit surface mesh reconstructed using NeuS (a method for implicit surface reconstruction). Poisson disk sampling is used to generate these simulation particles, which accurately describe object boundaries and interiors. This approach is preferred over sampling directly from trained 3DGS kernels, which tend to be unevenly distributed, especially for thin structures, potentially compromising simulation quality.
 - **Rendering Kernels:** The original trained 3DGS kernels are retained for rendering, as their adaptive distribution is optimal for capturing fine-grained appearance.
 - **Interpolation:** The deformations (positions and deformation gradients) computed on the simulation particles via PBD are then smoothly interpolated to the rendering Gaussian kernels using Generalized Moving Least Squares (GMLS). This ensures high quality in both simulation and rendering.
- **Unified Fluid Particles:** For fluids, a single set of Gaussian kernels/particles serves both simulation and rendering, reflecting the continuous and volumetric nature of fluids.

#### 3.2. Enhanced 3DGS Training for Appearance and Robustness

- **Physically-Based Rendering (PBR) Materials:** GSP builds upon GaussianShader by augmenting each Gaussian kernel with additional trainable material parameters: diffuse color (d_p), specular color (s_p), roughness (ρ_p), and surface normal (n_p). A global environment map is also incorporated. This allows for dynamic, physically-plausible shading.
- **Anisotropy Loss (L_aniso):** To counteract rendering artifacts (spiky noises, fuzziness) caused by large rotational deformations of solid objects, an anisotropy loss is introduced during 3DGS training (Equation 6). This loss constrains the ratio of the largest to the second-largest scaling factor of the Gaussian kernel, preventing excessive elongation while allowing deformation along the smallest axis (which typically aligns with the surface normal).
- **Normal Consistency Regularization (L_normal):** Adopted from GaussianShader, this term helps maintain consistent surface normals, which are crucial for accurate PBR.
- **Loss Function:** The overall loss function during training combines color loss (L_color), normal consistency regularization, and the new anisotropy loss (Equation 5).

#### 3.3. Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) for Simulation

- **General PBD Framework:** GSP uses PBD (specifically XPBD) as its underlying simulation engine due to its robustness, stability, and versatility in handling various physical problems. PBD solves for particle positions by iteratively projecting them onto constraints. The solver is parallelized on GPUs using CUDA.
- **Position-Based Fluids (PBF):**
 - **Incompressibility:** PBF is used for fluid simulation, enforcing incompressibility through density constraints (Equation 7) on each particle, computed using the SPH kernel.
 - **Surface Tension:** A position-based tension model is integrated to simulate realistic fluid surface dynamics, essential for effects like droplets and overflowing. This involves:
 - **Surface Detection:** Identifying surface particles based on an occlusion estimation method (cumulative projection area of neighbors on a spherical screen). This step is parallelized on the GPU.
 - **Area Constraint:** Enforcing an area constraint on surface particles to minimize local surface area, which requires calculating surface normals (Equation 8) and local triangulation (Equation 9).
 - **Distance Constraints:** Additional distance constraints (Equation 10) are used to promote a more uniform particle distribution within the fluid.
- **Solid Dynamics:** PBD is also used for simulating deformable and rigid solids, leveraging its versatility to handle various material behaviors through different constraint types.

#### 3.4. Advanced Rendering Pipeline

- **Dynamic Solids Rendering:** For deformed solids, Gaussian kernels are updated with their new positions, covariance matrices (Equation 11 for scaling), and normals (Equation 11 for rotation). These are then shaded using the PBR material parameters.
- **Dynamic Fluids Rendering:**
 - **Spherical Kernels:** Spherical Gaussian kernels are generated for each fluid particle, with initial covariance determined by particle radius.
 - **Surface Normal Assignment:** The normal for each fluid kernel adopts the surface normal of the nearest surface fluid particle from the PBF simulation.
 - **PBR for Fluids:** Specular reflections are modeled using the same PBR formula (Equation 4), with fluid kernels assigned a highly specular material (s_p=1, ρ_p=0.05).
 - **Refraction Modeling:** Refraction is approximated through a thickness-dependent diffuse color (d_p) using Beer's Law (Equation 12). Fluid thickness (τ_p) is obtained via additive splatting. A distortion term (βn_p) is applied to the background back-projection to mimic light path changes due to refraction. Opacity (σ_p) is set to 1.
 - **Foam, Spray, and Bubbles:** These elements are synthesized as a post-processing step and rendered using modified additive splatting with distinct kernels for increased realism.
- **Dynamic Shadows:** GSP re-engineers nearly-soft shadows into the 3DGS pipeline using variance shadow mapping, projecting Gaussians to a light-view camera, and compositing with the rendered image for enhanced visual realism.
- **Inpainting for Exposed Regions:** When objects are displaced, previously occluded scene areas become visible. Since these areas are not in the input images, GSP uses a generative AI inpainting model (LaMa) to fill these missing pixels, assigning the inpainted colors to the background Gaussian kernels.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** An image-space segmentation module allows users to select and manipulate objects of interest, facilitating interactive scene editing.

The entire system is implemented using Python, C++, and CUDA, leveraging PyTorch for the differentiable 3DGS pipeline and CUDA for parallelized PBD/PBF.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by Feng et al. demonstrate that Gaussian Splashing (GSP) successfully integrates versatile physics-based dynamics with 3D Gaussian Splatting, enabling high-quality, realistic rendering of interactive dynamic scenes.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies Confirm Methodological Effectiveness

- **Anisotropy Regularization:** The ablation study (Fig. 4) clearly shows that the proposed anisotropy loss effectively prevents rendering artifacts. Without it, solid objects undergoing large rotational deformations exhibit fuzzy and "spiky" noise, particularly near surfaces. The regularization significantly improves image quality and preserves correct specular effects under deformation.
- **PBR Material for Fluids:** A critical finding is the importance of physically-based rendering for fluids (Fig. 5). Fluids rendered with only diffuse color appear smoke-like and unrealistic. The inclusion of specular highlights, modeled through PBR materials, dramatically enhances the realism and dynamic appearance of fluids, which is a major improvement over prior 3DGS physics integrations that baked lighting.
- **Dynamic Shadow Maps:** The integration of nearly-soft shadows significantly improves visual realism (Fig. 6). Without shadows, objects appear flat and lack depth, while GSP's shadows enhance the perception of spatial relationships and scene depth.
- **Generative AI Inpainting:** The inpainting module effectively addresses the challenge of newly exposed regions (Fig. 7). When objects are moved, vanilla 3DGS leaves black smudges or dirty textures in the previously occluded areas due to missing information. GSP's use of LaMa successfully fills these gaps with plausible textures, ensuring seamless scene editing.

#### 4.2. Evaluation Across Diverse Dynamic Scenarios

GSP's versatility is showcased through a wide array of experiments covering deformable bodies, rigid objects, and fluids, including two-way coupling.
- **Fluid-Solid Two-Way Coupling:**
 - A soft chair falling into a pool deforms, floats due to buoyancy, and generates realistic fluid ripples (Fig. 12).
 - Waves continuously crash against a coastal cliff, accurately modeling splashing, foam, and spray (Fig. 13).
 - Water flooding a garden submerges a table and sweeps a potted plant, demonstrating complex fluid-object interactions (Fig. 14).
 - A LEGO bulldozer is animated to surf on splashing waves, showcasing the two-way coupling's ability to drive complex object motion (Fig. 8, Fig. 15).
- **Surface Tension Effects:**
 - Water flowing from a headset forms droplets that slide down a screen and splash onto a desk, creating a puddle, highlighting realistic low-volume fluid behavior due to surface tension (Fig. 9, Fig. 17).
 - Water droplets accumulate and overflow a can, with surface tension causing the liquid to rise above the rim before spilling (Fig. 18).
 - An astronaut is "liquefied" and transforms into a water sphere in zero-gravity, demonstrating surface tension's role in forming coherent fluid bodies (Fig. 19).
- **Deformable and Rigid Body Dynamics:**
 - A deformable ficus plant waves under external force, showcasing continuous shape changes (Fig. 20).
 - LEGO bulldozers fall into a bowl, colliding and scattering realistically, demonstrating robust rigid body contact dynamics (Fig. 21).
- **Interactive Scene Editing:** The semantic segmentation module enables users to manipulate objects and even "liquefy" them (e.g., transforming a paper cup and toy dog into water that splashes on a table), demonstrating novel HCI possibilities (Fig. 16).

#### 4.3. Performance Metrics

Table 1 provides detailed time performance, showing that the simulation and rendering times per frame are within a reasonable range for dynamic content, though still computationally intensive for very large scenes or highly interactive real-time applications without further optimization. The GPU parallelization of PBF constraints is noted as efficient.

In summary, GSP robustly addresses the challenges of integrating physics-based interactions with 3DGS, yielding visually compelling and physically plausible dynamic scenes. The methodological innovations, particularly the decoupling strategy for solids, enhanced 3DGS training, and specialized fluid rendering, are key to its success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Gaussian Splashing" represents a significant advancement in the fields of computer graphics and vision, particularly at the intersection of neural rendering and physical simulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Unified Framework:** GSP is one of the first comprehensive frameworks to convincingly unify physically-based simulation of both solids and fluids with 3D Gaussian Splatting. This addresses a critical gap in the existing literature, which often focused on either learned dynamics or physics integration with less efficient NeRF-based methods. By demonstrating the feasibility of this integration, GSP opens new avenues for creating dynamic, interactive virtual content.

2. **Enhanced Realism for Dynamic Scenes:** The framework significantly elevates the realism of dynamic scenes generated from learning-based reconstructions. GSP goes beyond simply "playing back" learned motions by incorporating true physical interactions (e.g., fluid-solid coupling, surface tension, rigid body collisions). The emphasis on high-quality rendering, including specular highlights for fluids, realistic deformation handling for solids, and dynamic shadows, results in visually compelling and convincing animations that were previously challenging with 3DGS.

3. **New Capabilities for Scene Editing and Interaction:** GSP empowers users with novel ways to interact with and modify reconstructed 3D scenes. The ability to introduce physical phenomena (like flooding, scattering objects, or even "liquefying" scene elements) into existing 3DGS models transforms static reconstructions into dynamic, editable environments. This facilitates creative applications and opens doors for more immersive human-computer interaction modalities.

4. **Advancements in 3DGS Utility:** The work extends the practical utility of 3D Gaussian Splatting beyond its original scope of static novel view synthesis. By addressing key limitations of vanilla 3DGS, such as artifacts from large deformations and inadequate material rendering for complex substances like fluids, GSP makes 3DGS a more versatile and robust representation for dynamic content. The anisotropy loss and PBR integration are important contributions to the 3DGS ecosystem.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** GSP provides a robust foundation for numerous future research directions:
 - **More Accurate Physics:** While PBD is robust, it is less physically accurate than other methods. Future work could explore integrating more sophisticated meshless simulation techniques (e.g., SPH, MPM) or even hybrid methods for enhanced physical fidelity.
 - **Advanced Light Transport:** The fluid rendering, while impressive, approximates refraction. Future research could integrate more physically accurate light transport models for fluids, potentially leveraging differentiable path tracing within the Gaussian kernel framework.
 - **Broader Physical Phenomena:** The framework could be extended to other complex physical simulations such as cloth, smoke, fire, or fracturing materials.
 - **Real-time Interactive Performance:** Further optimization could push the performance boundaries to enable highly interactive, real-time experiences for complex scenes, crucial for VR/AR applications.

6. **Potential Applications:** The impact of GSP can be felt across various industries and research domains:
 - **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** Creating more dynamic and interactive virtual environments where users can physically manipulate objects and observe realistic reactions.
 - **Gaming and Visual Effects (VFX):** Enabling artists to rapidly prototype and generate realistic physical effects within virtual scenes, reducing manual animation efforts.
 - **Robotics:** Generating synthetic training data for robots that needs to interact with deformable or fluid objects in complex scenarios.
 - **Scientific Visualization:** Simulating and visualizing complex physical processes in an easily understandable and interactive manner.

In conclusion, "Gaussian Splashing" stands as a testament to significant engineering effort and insightful integration, successfully bridging the divide between high-fidelity neural rendering and robust physics simulation. It not only showcases what is possible with unified particle representations but also inspires a future where reconstructed virtual worlds are not just static images but interactive, physically-responsive environments.
