# Learning When to See and When to Feel: Adaptive Vision-Torque Fusion for Contact-Aware Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Texas A&M University developed an adaptive vision-torque fusion strategy for robotic manipulation that dynamically integrates joint torque feedback only during physical interaction, filtering out noise during free motion. The system achieved an 82.0% average success rate across three contact-rich tasks, demonstrating a 14% improvement over the strongest baseline.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies, often vision-based, struggle with contact-rich and force-sensitive tasks where visual information alone is insufficient or prone to occlusion.
- Directly concatenating force/torque (F/T) and visual features can lead to "modality collapse," where policies neglect crucial but sparse F/T signals in favor of rich visual inputs.
- Incorporating F/T data throughout an entire manipulation process introduces sensor noise and inertia-induced measurements during free-motion, potentially degrading policy performance.
- A comprehensive comparative study of diverse F/T-vision integration approaches for contact-aware manipulation has been largely absent in existing literature.

## Method

- The authors propose an adaptive vision-torque fusion strategy within a diffusion policy framework, utilizing vision encoders for RGB images and MLPs for joint torque and robot state measurements.
- A Contact Gating mechanism selectively includes torque information: it uses actual torque features only when contact is detected via external joint torque, otherwise replacing them with a learnable parameter to filter noise during free-space movement.
- A Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)-style fusion mechanism blends noise predictions from separate vision-specialized and torque-specialized U-Nets, with a learned scale predictor dynamically modulating the torque guidance weight based on contact status.

## Results

- The proposed adaptive vision-torque fusion method achieved an 82.0% average success rate across three real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, representing a 14% improvement over the best baseline.
- Simple torque gating, which filters out torque measurements during free-space motion, significantly improved average success rate from 30.0% (vision-only or feature concatenation) to 68.0%.
- The adaptive approach demonstrated higher single-attempt success rates and lower average task execution steps compared to baselines, indicating improved precision and efficiency.
- Analysis revealed that the proposed method explicitly modulated the torque guidance weight based on contact status, allowing for adaptive utilization, unlike other methods where weights remained nearly constant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive concatenation of force/torque features with visual inputs is often ineffective, performing similarly to vision-only policies due to sensor noise and irrelevant data during free-motion phases.
> - Filtering out irrelevant or noisy torque information during free-space movement is crucial for improving multimodal integration performance, as demonstrated by the significant improvement achieved by simple torque gating.
> - Dynamically adapting the contribution of torque information based on the robot's contact status, rather than using constant weights or auxiliary prediction tasks, leads to superior and more robust performance in contact-rich tasks.
> - Explicitly modulating the torque guidance weight to be zero during no contact and learned when contact is detected allows for functionally adaptive utilization of torque information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation policies have frequently relied on visual observations due to their accessibility and richness. However, a significant limitation arises in contact-rich and force-sensitive manipulation tasks, where visual information alone is often insufficient. In these scenarios, signals such as external force/torque (F/T) at the end effector or joint torque measurements become critical, providing data about contact dynamics, alignment quality, and interaction state that vision cannot capture. Examples include connector plug-in, cable manipulation, or assembly tasks where visual occlusion can compromise perception. Furthermore, many real-world tasks necessitate hybrid force and position control, requiring the robot to maintain precise trajectories while regulating contact force.

The effective integration of F/T signals with visual data presents several challenges. A direct concatenation of F/T and visual features can lead to "modality collapse," where policies over-rely on high-fidelity visual inputs and disregard sparse but crucial force signals. Additionally, incorporating F/T data throughout the entire manipulation process can introduce sensor noise and inertia-induced measurements during free-motion phases, potentially degrading policy performance.

Various strategies have been proposed in the literature to address these integration challenges. These include:
- **Auxiliary prediction objectives:** Methods that learn additional tasks, such as predicting future torques or contact status, to regularize multimodal representations. Examples include TA-VLA, ImplicitRDP, and self-supervised frameworks learning multimodal representations.
- **Mixture-of-experts (MoE) or routing-based fusion:** Approaches that dynamically combine modality-specific experts. FACTR and FoAR introduce mechanisms to modulate the contribution of force features, while methods like Chen et al. and ForceVLA employ router networks or MoE modules to blend noise predictions or tokens from specialized subnetworks.
- **Gating mechanisms:** Strategies that regulate force usage based on inferred interaction status, selectively incorporating force feedback only during contact events. FoAR, for instance, uses a future contact predictor for selective haptic utilization.

Despite these advancements, the existing research often evaluates new integration strategies primarily against vision-only policies or simple feature concatenation baselines. A comprehensive comparative study of these diverse integration approaches, designed to identify their practical effectiveness and limitations across different contact-rich scenarios, has been largely absent. This paper aims to fill this gap by benchmarking representative strategies and proposing an adaptive fusion method designed to improve performance by dynamically managing F/T signal integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of purely vision-based robotic manipulation policies in tasks requiring physical interaction. While visual data is abundant and informative for many tasks, it becomes inadequate when precise contact dynamics, force regulation, or tactile feedback are essential. For instance, in tasks like inserting a connector or opening a lid, visual cues may be minimal or occluded, making it difficult for a robot to discern alignment, depth, or the completion of a contact-rich phase. Force/torque signals, in contrast, provide direct and immediate feedback on these critical aspects.

However, the straightforward incorporation of F/T signals into learning-based policies faces two primary issues:
1. **Modality Collapse:** Policies often prioritize rich visual data over sparse F/T signals, leading to insufficient utilization of force information when it is most needed.
2. **Noise and Irrelevance during Free-Motion:** F/T sensors can register noise and inertia-induced measurements during non-contact phases, which can confuse the policy and degrade performance if not properly filtered or managed.

Given these challenges and the existing diversity of proposed fusion strategies, a clear understanding of their comparative performance and specific limitations is lacking in the literature. Most prior work has not provided a direct comparison between various advanced F/T-vision integration techniques.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
- **Benchmarking of F/T and Vision Integration Strategies:** To conduct a comparative evaluation of different F/T and vision integration strategies within diffusion-based manipulation policies. The goal is to assess their performance across various force-aware tasks and identify which approaches are most effective in practice. This objective directly addresses the gap regarding the lack of a comprehensive comparison of existing methods.
- **Development of an Adaptive Integration Strategy:** To propose and implement an adaptive integration strategy that specifically addresses the challenge of F/T signal noise during free motion and ensures robust utilization during contact. This strategy is designed to suppress F/T signals during non-contact phases and dynamically incorporate them during physical interaction, thereby reducing noise interference while maintaining stable visual planning.

These objectives collectively aim to advance the state of the art in contact-aware robotic manipulation by both evaluating existing methods and introducing a novel approach that demonstrates improved performance and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology builds upon the diffusion policy framework for robotic manipulation, integrating vision and joint torque measurements to enable contact-aware manipulation.

**A. Overall Architecture and Sensing Modalities**
The core architecture utilizes:
- **Vision Encoders:** Two ResNet-18 models process RGB images (224 × 224 × 3 pixels) from both an agent-view camera and a wrist-mounted camera.
- **Torque Encoder:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encodes a 7 × 10 vector representing joint torque measurements from the past 10 observation steps. This 10-step window is selected to capture both magnitude and rate of change in external joint torques.
- **Robot State Encoder:** Another MLP encodes a 7-dimensional joint position vector.
The encoded features from these modalities are concatenated to form the conditioning context for a U-Net, which serves as the noise prediction model in the diffusion process. During inference, the policy generates future actions by iteratively refining a trajectory initialized from Gaussian noise.

**B. Proposed Adaptive Vision-Torque Fusion**
The proposed approach introduces two key components for effective vision-torque fusion:

1. **Contact Gating:**
 - **Mechanism:** This component selectively includes torque information based on the robot's contact status. Contact is detected if any joint experiences an external torque exceeding a predefined magnitude.
 - **Functionality:** A learnable parameter, `f*`, represents encoded torque features during free-space movement. When contact is detected (`ϕ = 1`), the actual output of the torque encoder (`f_torque`) is used directly. When no contact is detected (`ϕ = 0`), the torque channel is replaced by `f*`. This gating mechanism, defined by the equation `f_torque-gated = ϕ · f_torque + (1 − ϕ) · f*`, aims to filter out noise and inertial effects from torque data during free-space motion. The gated torque feature is then concatenated with image and proprioceptive features.

2. **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)-Style Adaptive Vision-Torque Fusion:**
 - **Architecture:** Instead of a single denoiser, two modality-specialized U-Nets are trained:
 - A **vision-specialized U-Net** is conditioned on visual and proprioceptive features.
 - A **torque-specialized U-Net** is conditioned on the *contact-gated* torque features and proprioceptive features.
 - **Fusion Mechanism:** Inspired by classifier-free guidance, the noise predictions from these two U-Nets are blended. The final noise term (`ˆϵ_final`) used for denoising is computed as: `ˆϵ_final = ˆϵ_vision + w_torque (ˆϵ_torque − ˆϵ_vision)`.
 - **Scale Predictor:** A three-layer MLP acts as a scale predictor, taking concatenated vision and gated torque features as input to output `w_scale`.
 - **Contact-Dependent Modulation:** The guidance weight `w_torque` is further modulated by the contact status `ϕ`: `w_torque = ϕ · σ(w_scale)`, where `σ(·)` is the softplus function. This ensures that `w_torque` is effectively zero when no contact is detected (`ϕ = 0`), resulting in purely vision-driven actions (`ˆϵ_final = ˆϵ_vision`). During contact (`ϕ = 1`), `w_torque` becomes a learned, positive value (`w_torque ∈ (0, ∞)`), allowing for an adaptive blend of vision and torque information.
 - **Training:** The two U-Nets and the scale predictor are optimized jointly using a standard noise prediction objective: `L = MSE(ˆϵ_final, ϵ)`, where `ϵ` is the ground truth Gaussian noise.

**C. Baseline Methods for Comparison**
To benchmark the proposed approach, the paper evaluates several alternative fusion strategies:
- **Vision-only:** Policy relies solely on visual and proprioceptive inputs.
- **Feature Concatenation:** Naively concatenates torque features with visual and proprioceptive features for conditioning a single U-Net.
- **Torque Gating:** Implements only the contact-gating mechanism, with the gated torque features concatenated to vision features for a single U-Net.
- **Auxiliary Goals:** Augments the standard Diffusion Policy with an auxiliary task of predicting future joint torque signals, using a weighted sum of action prediction loss and torque prediction loss during training. At inference, only actions are extracted.
- **Mixture of Modality-Specific Experts (MoE):** Two independent U-Nets (vision-specific and torque-specific) are trained in parallel. Their noise predictions are blended element-wise at each denoising step using learned routing weights (`w_vision`, `w_tor`) obtained from a small MLP that takes concatenated features as input.
- **MoE w/o torque encoding:** A variation of MoE where torque information is not explicitly encoded.

**D. Experimental Setup and Tasks**
- **Robot Platform:** A Franka Research 3 robot, which provides accessible joint external torque measurements.
- **Cameras:** An agentview RealSense camera and an OAK-D wrist camera.
- **Data Collection:** Demonstrations were collected for each task (110-250 demonstrations per task).
- **Training Details:** Algorithms implemented on Robomimic, optimized with AdamW (learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴, L2 weight decay 1 × 10⁻⁶). Diffusion process used 100 timesteps. Data sampled at 10 Hz.
- **Evaluation Tasks:** Three real-world, contact-rich manipulation tasks:
 - **Egg Boiler Lid Opening:** Requires twisting and lifting, sensitive to resistance changes.
 - **Weight-Based Bottle Placement:** Requires discriminating between visually identical empty and full water bottles based on weight for placement on specific plates.
 - **Twisty Connector Pull Out:** Requires rotation to a limit (sensed via resistance) followed by an axial pulling force.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Success rate per task over 10 or 20 trials. A task is considered failed if it results in robot motion abortion due to collision or running out of time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of various F/T–vision integration strategies demonstrated distinct performance differences, with the proposed adaptive fusion approach achieving the highest success rates.

**A. Overall Performance Comparison**
The proposed method achieved an average success rate of 82.0% across the three contact-rich manipulation tasks, representing a 14% improvement over the strongest baseline. It also attained the highest success rate in each individual task.

**B. Impact of Contact Gating and Noise Filtering**
- **Baseline Performance:**
 - **Vision-only:** Achieved an average success rate of 30.0%. While performing adequately in parts of the bottle placement task (e.g., placing empty bottles), it entirely failed tasks like connector pull-out due to the lack of contact awareness.
 - **Feature Concatenation:** Also yielded an average success rate of 30.0%. This method performed similarly to vision-only, indicating that naive concatenation of F/T features without specific handling of noise and modality balance can be ineffective. The paper attributes this to the introduction of sensor noise and inertia-induced F/T measurements during free-motion phases, which distract from the visual planning.
 - **Torque Gating (Baseline):** This simple strategy, which filters out torque measurements during free-space motion, significantly improved performance, achieving an average success rate of 68.0%. This result highlights that filtering irrelevant or noisy torque information is crucial for multimodal integration.

**C. Performance of Other Integration Strategies**
- **Auxiliary Goals:** This strategy exhibited suboptimal performance, with an average success rate of 28.0%. The paper suggests this is due to the presence of patternless joint torque measurements during free-space movement, making future torque prediction a challenging auxiliary task, especially with limited data.
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE):** The MoE baseline also performed poorly, with an average success rate of 24.0%. A specific variant, "MoE w/o torque encoding," which might imply a simpler form of MoE, performed better at 54.0%, but still less than the Torque Gating baseline.

**D. Adaptive Scaling and Dynamic Weighting**
- **Comparison with Torque-Gated MoE:** An ablation study compared the proposed CFG-style adaptive fusion with a torque-gated MoE. Even when the MoE routing network received contact-gated torque features, it underperformed the proposed method (12/20 vs. 16/20 success rate in the bottle placement task).
- **Interpretability of Weights:** Analysis of routing weights revealed that the MoE model's weights (`w_vision`, `w_torque`) remained nearly constant regardless of the interaction state (e.g., 0.7634 and 0.2366 during non-contact, 0.7714 and 0.2286 during contact). In contrast, the proposed method explicitly modulated `w_torque` to be zero during no contact and dynamically varied it when contact was detected, demonstrating a more interpretable and functionally adaptive utilization of torque information. This explicit regulation of `w_torque` based on contact status was critical.

**E. Efficiency and Precision**
The proposed approach also demonstrated higher single-attempt success rates and lower average task execution steps compared to the contact-gating baseline. For example, in the egg boiler lid opening task, the proposed method achieved a 60.0% single-attempt success rate with an average task horizon of 87.1 steps, compared to the torque-gating baseline's 20.0% single-attempt success rate and 110.7 steps. This indicates that the adaptive fusion not only improves overall success but also enhances the precision and efficiency of manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically evaluating various vision-torque integration strategies and proposing an adaptive method that addresses identified limitations.

The **significance** of this work can be summarized as follows:
- **Addressing a Research Gap:** The paper directly tackles the absence of a comprehensive comparative evaluation of F/T-vision integration strategies within diffusion-based manipulation policies. By benchmarking several representative methods, it clarifies their practical effectiveness and highlights their specific limitations, particularly regarding noise sensitivity and modality collapse.
- **Enhanced Manipulation Performance:** The proposed adaptive contact-aware vision-torque fusion strategy demonstrates a substantial improvement in success rates across diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks. The 14% improvement over the strongest baseline suggests a significant step forward in enabling robots to perform complex tasks requiring nuanced physical interaction.
- **Improved Robustness and Reliability:** By selectively leveraging torque signals only during contact phases and suppressing them during free-space motion, the method effectively mitigates the negative impact of sensor noise and inertia-induced measurements. This contact-aware gating mechanism and dynamic fusion contribute to a more robust and reliable policy that can discern signal from noise.
- **Increased Efficiency and Precision:** The observed higher single-attempt success rates and reduced task execution horizons indicate that the adaptive fusion approach not only improves task completion but also enhances the robot's efficiency and precision during manipulation, reducing the need for multiple attempts.
- **Practical Applicability:** The use of readily available joint torque measurements, rather than specialized external F/T sensors, makes the proposed approach more hardware-efficient and potentially applicable to a broader range of robotic platforms, such as the Franka Research 3.

The **potential impact** of this research extends to various applications in robotics:
- **Advanced Assembly and Disassembly:** The ability to perform delicate, contact-rich tasks with higher precision and reliability could significantly benefit automated assembly lines and specialized disassembly operations, particularly for complex electronics or components.
- **Service Robotics:** Robots capable of more dexterous and sensitive interactions could improve performance in domestic or care settings, handling objects with varying compliance and fragility.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** More robust contact awareness can lead to safer and more effective human-robot interaction, where robots can better understand and respond to physical forces during shared tasks.
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** The insights gained into effective multimodal fusion could inform the development of more capable and generalizable robot learning policies, especially as research moves towards large-scale, generalist robot models.

The paper also acknowledges **limitations** that point to future research directions:
- The evaluation covers a representative subset but not the entire spectrum of existing integration methods.
- The definition of "auxiliary goals" for multimodal learning remains an area for further exploration, specifically identifying goals that are both easily learnable and yield significant performance gains.
- The current approach passively senses F/T data. Future work could focus on actively applying forces to interact with the environment, which is crucial for tasks like surface finishing or high-precision assembly/disassembly. This shift towards active force control represents a significant next step in utilizing contact-aware manipulation.
