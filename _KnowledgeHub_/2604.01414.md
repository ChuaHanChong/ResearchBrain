# Learning When to See and When to Feel: Adaptive Vision-Torque Fusion for Contact-Aware Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Texas A&M University developed an adaptive vision-torque fusion strategy for robotic manipulation that dynamically integrates joint torque feedback only during physical interaction, filtering out noise during free motion. The system achieved an 82.0% average success rate across three contact-rich tasks, demonstrating a 14% improvement over the strongest baseline.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies, often vision-based, struggle with contact-rich and force-sensitive tasks where visual information alone is insufficient or prone to occlusion.
- Directly concatenating force/torque (F/T) and visual features can lead to "modality collapse," where policies neglect crucial but sparse F/T signals in favor of rich visual inputs.
- Incorporating F/T data throughout an entire manipulation process introduces sensor noise and inertia-induced measurements during free-motion, potentially degrading policy performance.
- A comprehensive comparative study of diverse F/T-vision integration approaches for contact-aware manipulation has been largely absent in existing literature.

## Method

- The authors propose an adaptive vision-torque fusion strategy within a diffusion policy framework, utilizing vision encoders for RGB images and MLPs for joint torque and robot state measurements.
- A Contact Gating mechanism selectively includes torque information: it uses actual torque features only when contact is detected via external joint torque, otherwise replacing them with a learnable parameter to filter noise during free-space movement.
- A Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)-style fusion mechanism blends noise predictions from separate vision-specialized and torque-specialized U-Nets, with a learned scale predictor dynamically modulating the torque guidance weight based on contact status.

## Results

- The proposed adaptive vision-torque fusion method achieved an 82.0% average success rate across three real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, representing a 14% improvement over the best baseline.
- Simple torque gating, which filters out torque measurements during free-space motion, significantly improved average success rate from 30.0% (vision-only or feature concatenation) to 68.0%.
- The adaptive approach demonstrated higher single-attempt success rates and lower average task execution steps compared to baselines, indicating improved precision and efficiency.
- Analysis revealed that the proposed method explicitly modulated the torque guidance weight based on contact status, allowing for adaptive utilization, unlike other methods where weights remained nearly constant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive concatenation of force/torque features with visual inputs is often ineffective, performing similarly to vision-only policies due to sensor noise and irrelevant data during free-motion phases.
> - Filtering out irrelevant or noisy torque information during free-space movement is crucial for improving multimodal integration performance, as demonstrated by the significant improvement achieved by simple torque gating.
> - Dynamically adapting the contribution of torque information based on the robot's contact status, rather than using constant weights or auxiliary prediction tasks, leads to superior and more robust performance in contact-rich tasks.
> - Explicitly modulating the torque guidance weight to be zero during no contact and learned when contact is detected allows for functionally adaptive utilization of torque information.
