# Multi-Rate Nonlinear Model Predictive Control for Wall-Supported Bipedal Locomotion of Quadrupedal Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A multi-rate nonlinear model predictive control (MR-NMPC) framework is presented for quadrupedal robots to perform stable wall-supported bipedal locomotion. This approach co-optimizes continuous robot states and discrete foot placements, allowing adaptive navigation of rough terrain and achieving a 2.9 times higher success rate than conventional methods in simulations.

## Problem

- Quadrupedal robots are limited in environments requiring elevation, fine manipulation, or navigation through very narrow passages with traditional gaits.
- Achieving stable wall-supported bipedal locomotion in quadrupedal robots is challenging due to complex multi-contact, hybrid, and multi-rate dynamics.
- Existing MPC methods struggle with the multi-rate dynamics of wall-supported bipedal locomotion, where environmental contact forces change rapidly while limb placements update less frequently.

## Method

- A high-level ==Multi-Rate Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MR-NMPC)== framework is developed to dynamically plan both discrete-time contact points and continuous-time CoM and orientation trajectories using an ==augmented state model==.
- The **MR-NMPC** unifies 'fast' environmental reaction forces and 'slow' limb placement updates within a ==finite-horizon optimization problem== by using ==indicator functions== in an augmented state model.
- A low-level ==Nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC)== operating at 1 kHz tracks the MR-NMPC's optimal trajectories, enforcing full-order robot dynamics through a convex ==Quadratic Program (QP)==.

## Results

- The **MR-NMPC** demonstrated a **2.9 times** higher success rate in negotiating irregular terrain at high speeds compared to a conventional MPC with heuristic foot placement in RaiSim simulations.
- The framework effectively mitigated large external disturbances by planning optimal sequences of smaller, asymmetric steps, maintaining lateral CoM trajectory and respecting kinematic constraints.
- Preliminary hardware experiments on a Unitree A1 robot showed real-time feasibility, achieving and maintaining stable wall-supported bipedal balance for over **60 seconds**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An augmented state model successfully integrates fast and slow control inputs into a single NMPC framework, addressing hybrid multi-rate dynamics for complex locomotion tasks.
> - Proactive, co-optimized planning of asymmetric footstep placements and continuous robot states enhances dynamic stability and enables adaptive support polygon generation over challenging terrain.
> - The layered control architecture (100 Hz MR-NMPC and 1 kHz WBC) enables real-time execution by decoupling reduced-order planning from full-order dynamics tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robotics actively pursues enhanced mobility and adaptability in complex, constrained, and unstructured environments. Quadrupedal robots have demonstrated robustness in various terrains, but their capabilities are often limited in scenarios requiring elevation, fine manipulation, or navigation through very narrow passages. Bipedal locomotion in quadrupedal robots offers a potential solution to these limitations by enabling an upright posture, thereby expanding their reach and maneuverability in height-constrained or spatially limited environments.

Existing approaches to legged locomotion control predominantly leverage Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). MPC, often combined with reduced-order models like the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) or Single Rigid Body (SRB), provides real-time trajectory optimization and feedback control. While Quadratic Programming (QP)-based MPC with linearized models is computationally efficient, it can struggle to capture the full nonlinear dynamics essential for agile maneuvers. Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) addresses these nonlinearities, but its application has primarily focused on quadrupedal gaits, with less exploration in wall-supported bipedal locomotion for quadrupeds, which presents distinct multi-rate control challenges.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise in generating bipedal gaits for quadrupedal robots, demonstrating impressive performance in simulation and hardware for various challenging motions, including jumping, walking on hind legs, and even wall climbing. RL-based methods can learn complex policies without explicit dynamic models, but they often require extensive training data and may lack the guarantees of model-based approaches, particularly concerning constraint satisfaction and explainability.

Wall-supported bipedal locomotion, specifically, introduces a unique set of challenges compared to free bipedal or quadrupedal gaits. It involves multi-contact scenarios (four-contact, three-contact, double-contact phases) where both ground and environmental (wall) contacts contribute to stability and propulsion. This hybrid, multi-rate dynamic structure, coupled with underactuation and unilateral contact constraints, complicates the design of real-time predictive controllers that must simultaneously plan continuous trajectories for the robot's center of mass (CoM) and orientation, discrete contact points, and environmental interaction forces. Traditional MPC formulations typically assume uniform input rates, which is not suitable for such multi-rate dynamics where contact forces change rapidly, but foot placements are updated less frequently. This work aims to bridge this gap by developing a unified optimization framework that explicitly addresses these multi-rate characteristics for stable wall-supported bipedal locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to develop a unified and computationally efficient multi-rate nonlinear model predictive control (MR-NMPC) framework that enables quadrupedal robots to achieve robustly stable wall-supported bipedal locomotion in constrained environments. This objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Extending Quadrupedal Capabilities:** Traditional quadrupedal gaits limit robots in environments requiring elevated reach, negotiation of high obstacles, or manipulation tasks that necessitate an upright posture. Bipedal locomotion, especially with wall assistance, expands the operational envelope of these robots into constrained spaces like narrow corridors, cluttered industrial settings, or disaster sites.
- **Addressing Inherent Challenges of Bipedal Locomotion in Quadrupedal Robots:** Achieving stable bipedal balance in quadrupedal robots is challenging due to their morphology and the limited foothold options in constrained environments. Wall-supported locomotion mitigates this by leveraging environmental contacts for additional stability, but this introduces complex multi-contact dynamics.
- **Managing Hybrid, Unstable, and High-Dimensional Dynamics:** The dynamics of a quadrupedal robot in an upright, wall-supported posture are inherently hybrid, unstable, and high-dimensional. This involves fast-varying reaction forces from both ground and walls, alongside slower variations in foot placement and contact transitions. Standard NMPC formulations, designed for uniform input rates, are not directly applicable to this multi-rate structure.
- **Integrating Locomotion and Environmental Support Forces:** Effective operation in constrained environments requires simultaneous regulation of CoM trajectory, torso orientation, and wall interaction forces. This must be achieved while respecting feasibility constraints such as joint torque limits, kinematic reachability, and friction cones for both ground and wall contacts.
- **Real-Time Predictive Planning and Coordination:** These complex requirements must be met under stringent real-time constraints. Optimization-based control frameworks are necessary for predictive planning and whole-body coordination, but they face computational complexity issues, particularly with high-dimensional and multi-rate dynamics.
- **Improving Stability and Adaptability:** Compared to heuristic foot placement strategies, dynamic planning of contact points within an optimal control framework is expected to enhance dynamic stability, especially on irregular terrain and under disturbances, by proactively adapting to anticipated contact configurations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a layered planning and control framework, comprising a high-level Multi-Rate Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MR-NMPC) and a low-level Nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC).

**A. High-Level MR-NMPC**

The high-level MR-NMPC is formulated to dynamically plan both discrete-time contact point trajectories and continuous-time CoM and orientation trajectories using a Single Rigid Body (SRB) dynamics model.

- **Multi-Rate Dynamics Formulation:** The core of the MR-NMPC addresses the multi-rate nature of the problem. It distinguishes between "fast" control inputs (environment reaction forces, ERFs, which include both ground and wall contact forces) that can be updated at every time step, and "slow" control inputs (limb placements, representing incremental step lengths) that change only at predetermined switching instants (e.g., beginning of a gait cycle).
- **Augmented State Model:** To handle this multi-rate behavior within a unified NMPC framework, an augmented state model is introduced. The original system state `x(t)` (CoM position and velocity, orientation, and angular velocity) is augmented with the Cartesian coordinates of the stance limb end-effectors `v(t)`. The "slow" input `v(t)`'s updates are gated by an indicator function `δ_T(t)`, which is 1 at switching instants and 0 otherwise. This allows the limb placements to evolve as part of the state and be updated only at gait cycle transitions. The augmented control input `u_a(t)` then comprises the fast control inputs `u(t)` (ERFs) and the slow-input updates `Δv(t)` (step lengths).
- **SRB Dynamics:** The SRB template model is used for the high-level dynamics. This model describes the robot's CoM position and orientation dynamics under net forces and torques generated by ERFs. The equations consider gravity and the robot's mass and inertia. The ERFs and the vector from CoM to foot contacts are used to compute net force and torque. Foot positions are augmented into the state, their evolution linked to `Δv(t)`.
- **NMPC Optimization Problem:** A finite-horizon optimal control problem (Problem 2) is formulated. It minimizes a cost function comprising terminal and stage costs for both the augmented state (CoM, orientation, foot positions) and augmented control inputs (ERFs, step lengths) over a prediction horizon `N`. The cost function penalizes deviations from reference trajectories and control effort. Constraints include the augmented SRB dynamics, equality constraints reflecting the availability of control inputs based on contact configuration (enforced by time-varying binary matrices `E_u`, `E_Δv`), and inequality constraints for state feasibility, input admissibility (e.g., friction cones for ERFs), and kinematic limits.
- **Gait and Scheduling:** A multi-contact locomotion pattern `G` is considered, consisting of a periodic sequence of four-contact, three-contact, and double-contact phases. The MR-NMPC is solved at 100 Hz (10 ms sampling time) over a prediction horizon of 20 samples (200 ms), covering multiple gait phases.
- **Computational Aspects:** The MR-NMPC is implemented using the CasADi framework with the IPOPT interior-point solver, achieving a mean execution time of 8.26 ms, enabling 100 Hz operation.

**B. Low-Level Nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC)**

The low-level WBC operates at a higher frequency (1 kHz) and is responsible for tracking the optimal trajectories generated by the MR-NMPC, enforcing full-order robot dynamics.

- **Full-Order Dynamics:** The controller accounts for the full-order floating-base dynamics of the quadrupedal robot using Euler–Lagrange equations, including generalized coordinates, joint torques, and ERFs at stance limbs. Contact constraints (zero acceleration of stance limb end-effectors) are explicitly enforced.
- **Virtual Constraints:** To bridge the gap between the reduced-order SRB model and the full-order robot, a set of outputs (virtual constraints) is defined. These outputs include the CoM position, torso orientation, and swing limb end-effector Cartesian coordinates. The WBC drives these actual outputs `y_a(q)` to track the desired values `y_des(t)` provided by the high-level MR-NMPC. For swing limbs, Bezier polynomials interpolate between current positions and upcoming placements optimized by the MR-NMPC.
- **Quadratic Program (QP) Formulation:** The WBC is formulated as a convex QP that solves for joint-level torques `τ` and ERFs `f`. The objective function minimizes a weighted sum of squared joint torques, tracking error for desired ERFs `f_des` (from MR-NMPC), and a slack variable `δ` for output dynamics feasibility. The QP is subject to equality constraints for desired output dynamics (`¨y + K_D ˙y + K_P y = δ`) and no slippage (`¨r_foot,ℓ = 0`), as well as inequality constraints for joint torque limits and linearized friction cones. The QP's convex nature allows for real-time solution at 1 kHz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed MR-NMPC framework was validated through extensive numerical simulations using a Unitree A1 quadrupedal robot within the RaiSim physics environment.

- **Simulation Environment:** Simulations were conducted in a 45 cm narrow corridor with parallel walls, featuring unknown and rough terrain composed of randomly placed wooden blocks (up to 3 layers, 2 cm tall, 14 cm wide, covering 70% of the track).
- **Nominal Condition Benchmark:** Under nominal conditions (flat terrain, no external disturbances), both the proposed MR-NMPC and a classical Raibert heuristic-based controller achieved comparable reference velocity (0.8 m/s) and base orientation tracking. This established a baseline, suggesting that subsequent performance differences in challenging scenarios could be attributed to the predictive foothold formulation of the MR-NMPC.
- **Adaptive Footstep Placement and Stability on Rough Terrain:**
 - When traversing rough terrain (piled wooden blocks), the MR-NMPC utilized full-state feedback and a 20-step prediction horizon to maintain the robot within a safety envelope and track the reference velocity.
 - Analysis of longitudinal foot offsets (relative to CoM) revealed that MR-NMPC significantly modulated rear foot placement (`r_rx`) compared to the Raibert heuristic, while front foot placement (`r_fx`) remained somewhat comparable.
 - This proactive modulation of rear foot placement allowed the MR-NMPC to dynamically expand or contract the robot's support polygon, enabling the generation of corrective restorative torques. This flexibility, unattainable under the fixed-symmetry constraints of the Raibert heuristic, facilitated navigation over treacherous terrain by effectively "steering" the robot back to the reference path.
- **Comparative and Quantitative Analysis (Success Rate):**
 - A quantitative comparison was performed over 250 randomized 7m tracks with stochastic wooden block placement, ramping the reference velocity up to 0.8 m/s.
 - A "successful trial" was defined by maintaining nominal CoM height, lateral tracking margins, and kinematic safety limits.
 - At low velocities, both planners showed comparable reliability. However, as the reference velocity increased, the Raibert heuristic's success rate sharply degraded.
 - The MR-NMPC demonstrated a **2.9 times higher success rate** in negotiating irregular terrain at high speeds compared to the conventional MPC with heuristic foot placement. This was attributed to the MR-NMPC's ability to co-optimize the full robot state and proactively adapt foothold locations based on anticipated contact configurations, addressing the limitations of reactive feedback at higher speeds.
- **Disturbance Rejection and Constraint Awareness:**
 - The robustness against high-magnitude perturbations was evaluated by applying a 50 N sinusoidal force for 1 second.
 - The Raibert heuristic responded with long step lengths that led to joint-limit saturation and subsequent instability, manifesting as lateral CoM deviation.
 - In contrast, the MR-NMPC leveraged its prediction horizon to identify an optimal sequence of smaller, asymmetric steps. This allowed the controller to mitigate disturbances while respecting safety and kinematic constraints, maintaining lateral CoM trajectory.

- **Preliminary Hardware Experiments:** Preliminary hardware experiments indicated the real-time feasibility of the framework. A Unitree A1 robot was able to transition from a crouched posture to upright wall-supported bipedal locomotion and maintain balance for over 60 seconds. However, velocity estimation noise was identified as a limitation, inducing forward pitching and preventing the stable limit-cycle behavior observed in simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant advancement in the control of legged robots for complex, constrained locomotion tasks. The primary significance lies in the development of a unified MR-NMPC framework that effectively integrates high-level footstep planning and low-level force computation for wall-supported bipedal locomotion.

- **Unified Optimization for Hybrid Dynamics:** By formulating multi-rate dynamics through an augmented state model and indicator functions, the framework addresses a fundamental challenge in controlling hybrid systems with varying control input rates. This allows for the simultaneous optimization of continuous robot states (CoM, orientation) and discrete contact points (foot placements), enhancing dynamic stability and adaptability.
- **Enhanced Stability and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability of the MR-NMPC to proactively plan asymmetric step lengths and dynamically adapt contact configurations (front and rear foot placements) is crucial for navigating rough terrain and rejecting external disturbances. The quantitative evidence of a 2.9 times higher success rate over heuristic methods in challenging environments highlights its practical advantage.
- **Expanded Operational Capabilities for Quadrupedal Robots:** Enabling stable wall-supported bipedal locomotion extends the utility of quadrupedal robots into environments previously challenging for their traditional gaits, such as narrow corridors, stairs, or industrial settings requiring reaching elevated objects. This could lead to new applications in inspection, manipulation, and disaster response.
- **Real-time Feasibility:** The computational efficiency of the MR-NMPC, achieving 100 Hz operation, combined with the 1 kHz low-level WBC, indicates its potential for real-world deployment on robotic hardware. The preliminary hardware experiments support this real-time feasibility, even while identifying areas for future refinement.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a foundation for incorporating more advanced capabilities. Future work on improving state estimation, increasing front-foot contact area for wall support, and integrating perception-aware planning for unknown terrain and contact conditions will build upon this established control structure. Comparative studies against other optimization-based baselines would further solidify its position in the field.

In summary, this work offers a robust and computationally viable control solution for a complex locomotion modality, expanding the capabilities of quadrupedal robots in demanding environments and contributing to the broader field of legged robot control.
