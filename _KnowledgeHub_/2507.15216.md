# Improving Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture with Diffusion Noise

> [!summary] Summary
> N-JEPA enhances the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture by integrating diffusion noise into masked token position embeddings during self-supervised pre-training. This method yields a +1.5% Top-1 accuracy improvement over the I-JEPA baseline on ImageNet-1K linear evaluation and delivers significant gains in low-shot learning settings.

## Problem

- Traditional self-supervised learning methods often rely on hand-crafted data augmentations, which can limit generalization and be sensitive to human prior knowledge.
- Masked Image Modeling (MIM) can sometimes produce lower-level representations that hinder performance in downstream tasks compared to more semantic features.
- Existing efforts to leverage generative diffusion models for representation learning have generally lagged behind dedicated self-supervised methods in learning robust semantic features.

## Method

- **N-JEPA** modifies the JEPA framework by introducing an additional predictor that forecasts ==masked== information from student features augmented with diffusion noise.
- It employs the EDM (Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models) noise schedule to inject multi-level Gaussian noise into the position embeddings of masked tokens, preserving Vision Transformer architecture compatibility.
- The model is trained using a composite loss function, combining the standard JEPA prediction loss with auxiliary noise-to-teacher and context-to-noise denoising objectives.

## Results

- N-JEPA achieved a consistent +**1.5%** Top-1 accuracy improvement over the I-JEPA baseline on ImageNet-**1K** linear evaluation (**73.6%** vs. **72.1%** at 600 epochs).
- The method demonstrated substantial gains in low-shot ImageNet-**1K** classification, improving Top-1 accuracy by +**2.5%** with **1%** labels (**60.0%**) and +**3.1%** with **10%** labels (**66.1%**) compared to the baseline.
- The multi-level noise schedule consistently outperformed the single-level noise schedule, yielding a **0.2**-**0.4%** higher Top-1 accuracy, validating its role in robust feature learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion noise can effectively act as a feature-level augmentation within self-supervised learning frameworks like JEPA, enhancing representation capacity without relying on hand-crafted transformations.
> - A multi-level noise schedule, applying distinct noise samples to each masked block, proves more effective than a single-level schedule for promoting diverse and robust feature learning.
> - Careful weighting of the auxiliary noise-related losses is critical, indicating their role in complementing the primary prediction objective to maximize performance gains.
