# Flow-Based Policy for Online Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowRL introduces a framework for online reinforcement learning that leverages flow-based generative models to represent expressive policies, achieving competitive performance with state-of-the-art methods while significantly reducing computational overhead through one-step policy inference. The approach effectively addresses the objective mismatch between generative modeling and reinforcement learning by using a constrained policy search guided by high-value actions from the replay buffer.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning policies often lack the expressiveness to capture complex, multimodal action distributions required for optimal performance in challenging continuous control tasks.
- Integrating expressive generative models like diffusion models or flow-based models into online reinforcement learning is difficult due to objective mismatch (static data imitation vs. dynamic value-based optimization) and high computational costs (iterative sampling, backpropagation through time).
- Existing generative model-based RL methods are often computationally expensive and sample-inefficient, limiting their scalability and practical application in online settings.

## Method

- FlowRL parameterizes the policy using a ==continuous normalizing flow== (CNF), where actions are generated by integrating a learned, state-dependent velocity field from a simple initial distribution.
- Policy learning is formulated as a constrained optimization problem, maximizing expected returns while regularizing the flow-based policy towards an 'optimal behavior policy' derived from the replay buffer using a tractable Wasserstein-2 distance.
- It employs an implicit guidance mechanism with a Q-value-based weighting function that adaptively guides the policy towards actions that yielded higher historical returns, along with one-step policy inference for computational efficiency.

## Results

- FlowRL demonstrates competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art model-free baselines (e.g., TD3, SAC, QVPO) across various challenging DMControl and HumanoidBench environments.
- The method achieves high performance with significant computational efficiency, utilizing only one flow step (N=1) for both training and evaluation, and does not require sampling-based action selection.
- Ablation studies confirm that the proposed policy constraint is crucial for FlowRL's success and sample efficiency, and that its performance is robust to the number of flow steps, validating the efficiency-focused design.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A constrained policy search, using Wasserstein-2 distance to implicitly guide a flow-based policy towards high-value actions from a replay buffer, effectively addresses the objective mismatch between generative modeling and reinforcement learning.
> - Flow-based policies can achieve strong performance in online RL with highly efficient one-step inference, avoiding the costly iterative sampling and backpropagation through time common in other generative models.
> - The inherent stochasticity of flow-based actors, coupled with value-guided exploitation via a policy constraint, can provide sufficient exploration in complex environments without needing explicit exploration mechanisms.
