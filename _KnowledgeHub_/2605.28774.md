# Agent Explorative Policy Optimization for Multimodal Agentic Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Agent Explorative Policy Optimization (AXPO) is introduced to bridge the "Thinking-Acting Gap" in multimodal agentic reasoning, a challenge where standard reinforcement learning under-trains tool use. This method notably improves vision-language model performance in tool-intensive tasks, with an 8B AXPO-trained model achieving superior Pass@4 scores compared to a 32B baseline.

## Problem

- Current multimodal agentic reasoning models struggle with effectively integrating external tool use, often due to an inherent asymmetry between internal "thinking" and external "acting."
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods like GRPO are suboptimal in addressing this "Thinking-Acting Gap," leading to under-attempted tool usage and suppressed learning signals for tool-calling behaviors.
- Empirical analysis revealed tool use is under-attempted (~30% of rollouts) and tool-using subgroups are frequently "all-wrong" (~40% of cases), hindering effective learning from RL.

## Method

- Introduces Agent Explorative Policy Optimization (**AXPO**), an RL algorithm built upon ==GRPO==, designed to specifically address the identified "Thinking-Acting Gap."
- Employs prefix-fixed resampling to concentrate exploration on the tool-call boundary, generating multiple tool-using continuations from a fixed thinking prefix.
- Utilizes uncertainty-based ranking to prioritize resampling from prefixes with low confidence in their tool-call tokens and applies a binary recovery reward to credit prefixes leading to successful resampled continuations.

## Results

- AXPO consistently improved performance, achieving average Pass@1 gains of +1.1 to +**1.8** percentage points and Pass@4 gains of +1.8 to +**2.8** percentage points across different model sizes (2B, 4B, 8B).
- The 8B AXPO-trained model demonstrated enhanced parameter efficiency, outperforming the 32B Base model on Pass@4 (**75.8%** vs. **75.1%**) and reaching **99%** of its Pass@1 performance.
- AXPO effectively closed the "Thinking-Acting Gap" during training, increasing the tool-use rate by **28** percentage points and reducing the "all-wrong" rate for tool-using subgroups by approximately **17** percentage points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "Thinking-Acting Gap" is characterized by under-attempted and frequently incorrect tool use, where standard RL methods fail to provide adequate learning signals for tool-call tokens.
> - Targeted exploration focused on the tool-call boundary, achieved through prefix-fixed resampling, is more effective than increasing overall rollout budgets or uniform exploration for improving tool-using capabilities.
> - By concentrating stochasticity and applying separate advantage calculations for shared prefixes and resampled continuations, AXPO can effectively restore positive learning signals for tool-using behaviors.
