# Search-Aided Joint Agent-Environment Reinforcement Learning for Robust Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding with Rotations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and NUS introduced Search-Aided Joint Reinforcement Learning (SJRL), a framework for Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding that addresses realistic robust safety and in-place rotation constraints (LMAPF-R2). SJRL jointly optimizes agent and environment policies, outperforming strong search-based baselines and existing learning methods in throughput, and demonstrated efficacy in a mixed-reality warehouse simulation.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based LMAPF planners often rely on oversimplified kinematic models, neglecting real-world constraints such as robust safety distances and in-place rotation capabilities.
- The inclusion of robust safety and in-place rotation constraints (LMAPF-R2) significantly increases coordination complexity, leading to challenges like congestion, deadlocks, and livelocks for learning-based methods.
- A modeling gap exists between search-based planners, which increasingly incorporate realistic constraints, and learning-based LMAPF methods, limiting their practical deployment in complex robotic systems.

## Method

- Introduces **Search-Aided Joint Reinforcement Learning (SJRL)**, a unified reinforcement learning framework for LMAPF-R2 that jointly optimizes both local agent policies and global environment policies.
- Develops an agent policy that predicts high-level subgoals (next vertices) and integrates a synchronized ==Causal PIBT== variant for local collision resolution and robust intention propagation.
- Implements an environment policy as a CNN that learns to generate optimal edge costs for guidance graphs, which are then used to compute minimum-cost heuristic distances for agent guidance via ==Dijkstra search==.

## Results

- SJRL consistently outperformed strong search-based baselines (Causal-PIBT NORL) and state-of-the-art learning methods (SILLM, MAGAT+, HMAGAT) in throughput across various LMAPF-R2 scenarios, particularly at higher agent densities.
- The framework led to an increased Mean Pairwise Distance (MPD) among agents, indicating greater spatial dispersion and reduced congestion, visually supported by wait action heatmaps.
- Validated in a mixed-reality warehouse environment with **8** physical and **248** virtual robots, SJRL achieved the highest throughput of **1.01**, surpassing NORL (**0.72**), SERL (**0.84**), and SARL (**0.87**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint optimization of both agent (local reactive decisions) and environment (global guidance) policies is crucial for effectively managing the increased coordination complexity in LMAPF-R2 scenarios, leading to reduced congestion.
> - Integrating a sophisticated search component like a Causal PIBT variant within the learning framework is essential for robust collision shielding and effective intention propagation, particularly in congested environments with realistic constraints.
> - A policy hierarchy (predicting subgoals rather than primitive actions) combined with intention propagation significantly enhances exploration and overall performance in dense multi-agent reinforcement learning under complex kinematic models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) and its lifelong variant (LMAPF) are fundamental challenges in multi-agent systems, with applications in automated warehouses, smart manufacturing, and virtual environments. Historically, search-based planners have been prominent in this domain, providing robust solutions. However, recent years have seen a growing interest in learning-based approaches due to their potential for enhanced expressiveness and scalability, particularly in large-scale, high-density scenarios characteristic of LMAPF.

Many existing learning-based LMAPF planners, including pioneering works like PRIMAL and recent state-of-the-art methods, often operate under an oversimplified kinematic model. This standard model typically assumes that an agent can move directly to any adjacent vertex in a single discrete timestep. This simplification can overlook crucial motion constraints present in real-world robotic systems, such as robust safety requirements (maintaining minimum safe distances) and in-place rotation constraints (reflecting non-holonomic robot characteristics like differential-drive robots). While search-based planning has increasingly incorporated such realistic constraints, their integration into learning-based LMAPF planners has received limited attention. This discrepancy creates a modeling gap between theoretical planning and practical execution in real-world systems.

This research addresses this gap by studying a more realistic LMAPF model, termed LMAPF-R2, which explicitly includes robust safety and in-place rotation constraints. The work posits that these constraints significantly increase the coordination complexity for learning-based planners. To navigate this increased complexity, the paper draws inspiration from successful techniques in both search-based and learning-based planning. It integrates single-step search algorithms, such as Causal PIBT, for local collision resolution and intention propagation, building upon prior work in collision shielding for neural policies. Furthermore, it incorporates the concept of guidance graphs, previously used for global coordination through edge cost optimization, into a reinforcement learning framework. A distinct aspect of this work is its investigation into the joint optimization of both agent and environment policies, a paradigm less explored in large-scale (L)MAPF compared to co-design studies limited to smaller agent populations. By integrating these elements, the research aims to advance learning-based LMAPF toward more practical and realistic applications while maintaining scalability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and robust learning-based planner for Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding (LMAPF) that can effectively operate under more realistic kinematic constraints than typically assumed in the field. Specifically, the study aims to design a framework capable of handling "LMAPF-R2," a model that incorporates robust safety constraints and in-place rotation capabilities for agents.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **Oversimplified Kinematic Models:** Many contemporary learning-based LMAPF planners, despite their advancements, rely on an oversimplified standard kinematic model. This model assumes that agents can move directly to any adjacent grid cell in a single timestep, neglecting factors like minimum safe distances between agents or the time required for a robot to rotate before moving. Such simplifications can lead to plans that are not directly executable or are inefficient in real-world robotic systems, particularly those with high agent densities and tightly constrained operational spaces, such as automated warehouses.

2. **Real-World Constraints and Their Impact:** The paper highlights two specific constraints critical for real-world performance:
 - **Robust Safety Constraints:** These enforce a minimum safe distance between moving agents, typically by prohibiting an agent from moving into a cell that was occupied by another agent in the previous timestep (following collisions). This ensures safety and allows for reactive responses to delays.
 - **In-Place Rotation Constraints:** These account for the non-holonomic nature of many robots (e.g., differential-drive robots), where reaching an adjacent cell may require multiple timesteps for rotation and then movement.
 These constraints significantly increase the complexity of coordination. They lead to greater congestion, make agents more susceptible to deadlocks, and can induce livelocks, presenting substantial challenges for planners.

3. **Gap in Learning-Based Approaches:** While search-based planners have made progress in addressing these realistic constraints, learning-based LMAPF research has largely not focused on them. This creates a disconnect between the capabilities of learning methods and the demands of practical robot deployments. The motivation is to bridge this gap by bringing more realistic modeling into learning-based LMAPF.

4. **Enhancing Coordination and Exploration:** The increased coordination difficulty under LMAPF-R2 necessitates improved planning strategies. The research is motivated by two design principles to address this:
 - **Leveraging Search and Learning Complementarity:** Combine the local optimality and collision-avoidance guarantees of search-based methods with the generalization and expressiveness potential of learning-based methods.
 - **Joint Agent-Environment Optimization:** Recognize that coordination can be improved by optimizing policies from both local (agent) and global (environment) perspectives. An agent's local reactive decisions can be complemented by global guidance provided by the environment, creating a synergistic effect. This joint optimization is intended to achieve more effective traffic flow and higher throughput.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Search-Aided Joint Reinforcement Learning (SJRL) to address Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding with robust safety and in-place rotation constraints (LMAPF-R2).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation (LMAPF-R2)

LMAPF-R2 is defined on a 4-neighbor grid graph where agents possess an orientation (East, South, West, North). Agents can execute four primitive actions: move forward, rotate 90° clockwise, rotate 90° counterclockwise, or wait. The model incorporates two key constraints:
- **Robust Constraint:** Prohibits "following collisions," meaning an agent cannot move into a vertex occupied by another agent in the previous timestep. This includes edge collisions and guarantees a minimum safe distance.
- **Rotational Constraint:** Accounts for non-holonomic robots, where changing orientation and moving to an adjacent vertex may take multiple timesteps.
The objective in this lifelong setting is to maximize throughput, defined as the average number of goals reached per timestep across all agents.

#### 3.2. MDP Formulation for SJRL

SJRL formulates the problem within a unified Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework for joint optimization.
- **Environment's Role:** At the beginning of a simulation, an "environment policy" generates edge costs for the graph. Backward Dijkstra search is then performed to compute minimum-cost heuristic distances for all state (vertex-orientation) pairs. These heuristics are provided as input to the agent policies.
- **Agent's Role:** Agents, when "free" (not committed to a subgoal), use an "agent policy" to select a "subgoal" (a next vertex). Once a subgoal is determined, the agent becomes "busy" and commits to reaching it via greedy actions.
- **Rewards:**
 - Agents receive a reward of -1 for each action taken. Upon reaching a subgoal, an additional progress reward (`dist(s_prev, g) - dist(s_curr, g)`) is given, measuring progress toward the final goal. A team reward, equal to the average reward of agents within a 5x5 local view, encourages local coordination.
 - The environment policy's reward is the sum of all agents' rewards.

#### 3.3. Agent Policy Learning

The agent policy builds upon the neural architecture of SILLM.
- **Input Features:** Includes binary obstacle maps, ternary agent priority maps (1 for higher, -1 for lower, 0 for others), a channel indicating busy/free status, and a four-channel heuristic map (one per orientation) derived from the environment policy.
- **Network Architecture:** Uses a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), a communication module, and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
- **Skip Connections:** Two skip connections directly integrate heuristic information by concatenating learned feature vectors with heuristic distances from candidate next vertices, strengthening the environment policy's influence.
- **Subgoal Prediction:** The neural policy predicts an intended next vertex (subgoal) rather than primitive actions. This hierarchical approach simplifies exploration in dense multi-agent environments.
- **Search-Aided Collision Shielding (Causal PIBT variant):**
 - A synchronized variant of Causal PIBT (Okumura, Tamura, and Défago 2021) is applied after agents propose subgoals.
 - It resolves collisions among free agents and propagates intentions. Only free agents participate; busy agents' current and subgoal vertices are treated as obstacles.
 - Cyclic movements are prohibited to avoid deadlocks under robust constraints.
 - Agents decide in priority order. Priority inheritance is used when a high-priority agent targets a vertex occupied by a low-priority agent.
 - The decision process prioritizes the neural policy's highest-probability choice, then considers other candidates based on ascending heuristic distances.
 - This mechanism facilitates intention propagation, especially in congested areas like corridors, enabling more coordinated movement and effective exploration during RL training.
 - After Causal PIBT, all agents are assigned a subgoal and become busy, executing greedy actions (rotate, wait if blocked, move).
 - Causal PIBT is treated as part of the environment during training; PPO optimizes the policy without directly accounting for the modifications introduced by Causal PIBT.

#### 3.4. Environment Policy Learning

The environment policy is implemented as a CNN.
- **Input Features:** Binary obstacle map and precomputed simulation statistics (vertex visitation and edge traversal frequencies) generated from a warm-up agent policy. These statistics remain fixed.
- **Output:** For each edge, the CNN outputs the mean and variance of a Gaussian distribution representing its cost. An arctan transformation bounds these costs within.
- **Heuristic Generation:** The sampled edge costs are used in a backward Dijkstra search to compute global minimum-cost distances, which serve as heuristic features for the agent policy.
- **Training:** The environment policy is trained using MAPPO, with all "virtual agents" (edges) sharing the same reward.

#### 3.5. Joint Reinforcement Learning Procedure

- **Warm-up Stage:** To mitigate high variance from random agent policy exploration, the agent policy is first trained with uniform edge costs (5.5) before joint training commences.
- **Joint Training:** Both agent and environment policies are trained concurrently using shared simulations.
- **Inference:** During deployment, the environment policy's edge costs and heuristic distances can be precomputed once per map. Only the agent policy needs to be executed at each timestep, which supports scalability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across six diverse grid maps (Warehouse, Sortation, Paris, Empty, Random-10, Random-20) consistently demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed SJRL framework.

#### 4.1. Benefits of Joint Learning (Section 5.1 & Figure 5, 6)

- **Performance Improvement:** SJRL significantly outperforms the strong search-based baseline, Causal-PIBT (NORL), particularly as agent density increases. This suggests that learning-based approaches, when properly formulated, can exceed traditional search methods in complex LMAPF-R2 scenarios.
- **Complementary Policies:** SJRL consistently outperforms SARL (Search-Aided Agent RL, agent policy only) and SERL (Search-Aided Environment RL, environment policy only). This indicates that the agent and environment policies contribute to LMAPF coordination in a complementary manner, and their joint optimization yields performance gains.
- **RL for Guidance Graphs:** SERL achieves performance comparable to or slightly better than CMA-ES, the original guidance graph optimization method. This demonstrates the potential of optimizing guidance graphs using reinforcement learning with more complex network structures.
- **Reduced Congestion:** Analysis of the Mean Pairwise Distance (MPD) shows that both agent and environment policy learning lead to increased MPD, implying greater spatial dispersion of agents and, consequently, reduced congestion. Wait action heatmaps (Figure 1, Figures 12-17 in Appendix) visually support this, showing that SARL alleviates local congestion, SERL balances global traffic, and SJRL combines both benefits.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods (Section 5.2 & Figure 7, 11)

- **Superiority of LMAPF-R2 Model:** SARL (agent policy trained under LMAPF-R2) outperforms or performs on par with state-of-the-art methods like SILLM, MAGAT+, and HMAGAT, which operate under the standard, simplified LMAPF model. SJRL consistently outperforms all these methods.
- **Importance of Modeling:** This finding suggests that even highly advanced learning-based planners designed for the standard LMAPF model may not translate effectively to more realistic kinematic models like LMAPF-R2. It underscores the necessity of studying and developing planners tailored to such models, rather than relying solely on execution frameworks like Action Dependency Graph (ADG) to bridge the modeling gap.

#### 4.3. Policy Hierarchy and Intention Propagation (Section 5.3 & Figure 8)

- **Impact of Policy Hierarchy:** Naive Action-Based Collision Shielding (NACS), where agents predict primitive actions, generally performs worse than Naive Vertex-Based Collision Shielding (NVCS), where agents predict next vertices (subgoals). This supports the benefit of a policy hierarchy in simplifying exploration.
- **Critical Role of Causal PIBT:** Both NACS and NVCS perform substantially worse than the Causal PIBT variant integrated into SJRL. The performance gap is particularly pronounced on maps with restrictive geometries like long corridors (e.g., Warehouse and Random maps).
- **Effective Exploration:** The Causal PIBT variant, by combining policy hierarchy with intention propagation through depth-first search and priority inheritance, significantly mitigates exploration difficulties. It enables agents to form coordinated movement patterns more effectively, which is crucial in congested environments with robust and rotational constraints.
- **Design Consideration:** These results highlight that straightforward adaptations of collision-shielding mechanisms to complex robot kinematics are insufficient, emphasizing the need for carefully designed and integrated search components within learning-based planners.

#### 4.4. Additional Findings (Appendix)

- **Skip Connections:** Skip connections, which directly inject heuristic information into the neural network, improve the performance of both SARL and SJRL, demonstrating their role in facilitating the optimization of the guidance graph during joint learning.
- **Guidance Graph Initialization:** Expert-informed initialization of edge costs significantly benefits all planners (Causal-PIBT, SARL, SJRL), with weaker planners experiencing larger performance gains. This suggests that while SJRL is effective, it may sometimes converge to suboptimal local optima due to the local nature of PPO optimization.
- **Physical Robot Validation:** SJRL was validated in a mixed-reality warehouse environment with 8 physical and 248 virtual robots. It achieved the highest throughput (1.01) compared to NORL (0.72), SERL (0.84), and SARL (0.87), providing empirical evidence of its practical applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of multi-agent pathfinding by addressing a critical modeling gap between theoretical learning-based approaches and the requirements of real-world robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancing Realistic LMAPF Modeling:** The work introduces and investigates LMAPF-R2, a more realistic yet scalable kinematic model for learning-based planners, incorporating robust safety and in-place rotation constraints. This is the first study of its kind for learning-based LMAPF, advocating for a broader adoption of such models to ensure the practical relevance of learned policies. This encourages future research to move beyond oversimplified models, thereby enhancing the deployability of learning-based planners in industrial and logistical settings.

2. **Novel Search-Aided Joint Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The proposed SJRL framework effectively integrates the complementary strengths of search algorithms and reinforcement learning. By embedding a Causal PIBT variant for local collision resolution and intention propagation, and jointly optimizing both agent and environment policies, SJRL provides a comprehensive approach to complex multi-agent coordination. This framework offers a paradigm for future hybrid planning systems.

3. **Improved Coordination and Throughput in Complex Environments:** The experimental results demonstrate that SJRL achieves substantial performance improvements over strong search-based baselines and existing state-of-the-art learning methods, particularly in high-density and tightly constrained environments. The joint optimization of local agent behaviors and global environment guidance contributes to better traffic flow, reduced congestion, and higher overall system throughput, which are critical metrics for automated warehouse efficiency.

4. **Enhanced Exploration in Multi-Agent RL:** The findings highlight the importance of a policy hierarchy (predicting subgoals) and intention propagation mechanisms within collision-shielding algorithms. This contributes to the understanding of how to design more effective exploration strategies for multi-agent reinforcement learning, especially when dealing with complex kinematic constraints that can easily lead to deadlocks or livelocks.

5. **Validation for Real-World Deployment:** The validation of SJRL in a mixed-reality environment with both physical and virtual robots provides a proof of concept for its practical applicability. This step is crucial for transitioning research from simulations to real-world robotic deployments, such as those in large-scale automated warehouses.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper implicitly opens several avenues for future work, including the development of more effective optimization paradigms for joint learning to avoid local optima, investigating adaptive updates of guidance graphs, and exploring more sophisticated communication mechanisms tailored for LMAPF-R2 and other complex kinematics. This research serves as a foundation for further advancements in robust and scalable multi-agent coordination.
