# Human Hands as Probes for Interactive Object Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Human hands in in-the-wild egocentric video supply both the data and the supervision for interactive object understanding. Contrastive learning ties object crops to co-occurring hand appearance and motion, yielding state-sensitive features; a context-prediction task masks the hand and predicts where interaction happens and which grasp applies. Trained on EPIC-KITCHENS, both beat self-supervised, saliency, and semantically supervised baselines.

## Problem

- No large-scale labelled datasets exist for how objects work; object states are highly contextual, so one object simultaneously carries many state labels depending on intended interaction.
- Manual annotation of where and how to touch objects suffers from bias, capturing imagined use rather than how people actually manipulate objects in daily conduct.
- Prior affordance and hotspot methods rely on action labels, gaze data, or thermal sensors in lab settings, and detection-based segmentation misses small parts like knobs and handles.

## Method

- ==Temporal SimCLR with Object-Hand Consistency== (**TSC+OHC**): a joint contrastive loss where L_temporal pulls together crops from one object-of-interaction track, and L_hand pairs object crops with corresponding hand appearance plus positional-encoded hand motion.
- ==Affordances via Context Prediction== (**ACP**): a ResNet-50 encoder sees an asymmetric context region with the hand patch masked out, then decodes an interaction segmentation mask and multi-label grasp types.
- A data pipeline strings per-frame hand, object-of-interaction and contact detections into paired tracks, with grasp pseudo-labels from a ==GUN71== classifier adapted to EPIC-KITCHENS via a temporal contrastive loss.

## Results

- On EPIC-STATES, TSC+OHC reaches **84.8** mAP versus **83.1** for ImageNet features, with gains widening to **9.5** points on novel objects under **12.5%** training data.
- Self-supervised features beat semantic supervision: **3.3-9.6** points over MIT-States pretraining and **3-10** over EPIC action classification; TCN and plain SimCLR trail by larger margins.
- ACP scores **57.4** AP on EPIC-ROI, above DeepGaze2's **55.7** and hotspots' **43.8** without any mask supervision; fused with Mask R-CNN it hits **68.7**, **+4.7**. Grasp mAP **38.1** versus **30.2** chance, **56.8** supervised.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freely available behavioural signals, hand appearance and hand motion, can substitute for hand-crafted state taxonomies and outperform semantically supervised pretraining on Internet images.
> - Masking the cue that trivially reveals the answer forces context reasoning; without it a model reads the hand instead of learning about the object.
> - Self-supervised affordance signals complement rather than replace supervised detectors: they recover small parts and non-catalogued objects that category-level segmentation systematically misses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of interactive object understanding within computer vision, moving beyond traditional object recognition (identifying "how objects look") to comprehend "how objects work"—specifically, what actions can be performed on them, how to interact, and the potential outcomes of such interactions. This capability is crucial for intelligent agents, particularly in robotics, to effectively manipulate objects in the real world.

Current approaches to object understanding often rely on large-scale labeled datasets. However, for interactive understanding, such datasets are difficult to acquire due to the highly contextual nature of object states and the potential for human annotation biases regarding plausible interactions. This work positions itself as an alternative by leveraging self-supervision from naturally occurring human-object interactions observed in egocentric videos.

The research builds upon several related areas:
- **Understanding hands:** Existing work focuses on classifying grasp types, estimating hand pose, or reconstructing 3D hand-object interactions. This paper utilizes these hand understanding capabilities as a foundation for understanding the objects themselves.
- **Using humans or hands as probes:** Prior studies have explored using human interactions to infer scene affordances, identify contact regions, or detect novel objects. This work distinguishes itself by focusing on fine-grained object affordances (interaction regions and specific grasp types) derived from hands in unscripted, in-the-wild egocentric videos.
- **Interactive Object Understanding:** Various methods have been developed for learning grasping strategies, object states, interaction hotspots, and reward functions through diverse supervision paradigms (strong, weak, imitation, reinforcement learning). This paper diverges by proposing a novel self-supervision source based purely on the observation of human hands in egocentric footage.
- **Self-supervision:** The techniques are inspired by contemporary methods in self-supervised learning, particularly context prediction and contrastive learning, adapting them to extract interactive understanding without explicit semantic labels.

By observing hands, the authors aim to circumvent the limitations of manual annotation and large-scale semantic labeling for interactive object properties, thereby contributing a data-driven approach directly from natural human behavior.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current computer vision systems in enabling robots to interact meaningfully with objects. Merely localizing and categorizing an object (e.g., a "cupboard") is insufficient for a robot to effectively manipulate it. To enable successful interaction, an intelligent agent requires a deeper understanding, specifically:
1. **Identification of plausible interaction sites:** Where on an object can an interaction occur?
2. **Methods of interaction:** How should one interact with a specific site (e.g., pulling, pushing, grasping)?
3. **Consequences of interaction:** What changes or events would result from a particular interaction?

Based on these needs, the paper articulates two specific objectives:
a) **Learning a feature space sensitive to object state:** This feature space should capture characteristics indicative of an object's current state and, consequently, what actions can be performed on it. This goes beyond simple object categorization, aiming for features that can differentiate between, for instance, an open versus a closed cupboard, or a whole versus a cut onion. Such state-sensitive features could serve as reward functions for manipulation tasks or enable detection of pre- and post-interaction states.
b) **Identifying object affordances:** This involves predicting *what hand-grasps objects afford* and *where* those grasps can be applied. This objective aims to provide spatial priors for robotic exploration and manipulation, guiding a robot on how and where to physically engage with an object.

The authors motivate the use of egocentric videos and the observation of human hands as a solution to the challenges of data collection and supervision for these objectives. Egocentric videos, captured from a first-person perspective, inherently center on hand-object interactions. This focus is argued to solve both the *data* problem (providing a rich corpus of interactions) and the *supervision* problem (hands naturally reveal interaction information). Specifically:
- **Localization and Stabilization:** Hands interacting with objects automatically highlight and stabilize the "active" objects in a scene, simplifying the learning process.
- **Interaction Sites:** The locations where hands touch indicate plausible interaction regions.
- **Interaction Methods and Object State:** The specific actions performed by hands (their appearance and motion) implicitly convey information about the object's state and the appropriate ways to interact with it.

This approach is posited to bypass the difficulties and potential biases associated with manually annotating object states and interaction types, allowing for learning directly from natural human behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is divided into two main components, addressing the stated objectives: learning state-sensitive features and predicting object affordances. Both components utilize the EPIC-KITCHENS egocentric video dataset and rely on pre-existing models for initial hand and object detection.

**Data Preparation:**
- The research uses per-frame hand and object-of-interaction bounding box detections, along with hand contact state, from Shan et al..
- Hand grasp-type predictions are obtained from a model trained on the GUN-71 dataset.
- These detections are linked across frames to form paired object-hand tracks.
- Hand motion (`h_m_t`) is represented by concatenating the hand bounding box's location and scale relative to the object over three adjacent frames.

#### 3.1. State Sensitive Features via Temporal and Hand Consistency (TSC+OHC)

This approach aims to learn object features (`ϕ_o`) that are sensitive to an object's state. It is based on two principles: the temporal consistency of object states and consistency with hand pose/motion.
- **Core Learning Paradigm:** Contrastive learning, implemented through a joint loss function: `L_temporal + λL_hand`.
- **`L_temporal` (Temporal SimCLR - TSC):**
 - This component encourages object crops that are close in time within the same object track to have similar feature representations.
 - It leverages "temporal augmentations" by treating `o_i` and a temporally close `o'_i` from the same track as positive pairs.
 - All other object crops (`o_j`, `o'_j`) within the batch are treated as negatives.
- **`L_hand` (Object-Hand Consistency - OHC):**
 - This component utilizes hands as a source of supervision to align object features with interaction patterns.
 - For an object crop `o_i`, its corresponding hand appearance (`h_a_i`) and motion (`h_m_i`) form a positive pair `[h_a_i, h_m_i]`.
 - The loss encourages `o_i` to be similar to `[h_a_i, h_m_i]`, while being dissimilar to other objects (`o_j`) and other hands (`[h_a_j, h_m_j]`) in the batch.
 - This indirectly leads to objects that are interacted with similarly, or are in similar states, having closer feature representations.
- **Technical Details:**
 - Batches consist of quadruples `(o_i, o'_i, h_a_i, h_m_i)`.
 - Object and hand crops are augmented using standard SimCLR techniques (color jitter, grayscale, resized crop, horizontal flip, Gaussian blur).
 - Separate convolutional trunks (`ϕ_o` and `ϕ_h`) are used for objects and hands, respectively.
 - Multiple projection heads are employed: `f_o` for `L_temporal`, and `f_h`, `g_h` for `L_hand`.
 - Hand motion (`h_m_i`) is encoded via positional encoding and concatenated with `ϕ_h(h_a_i)` before being fed into `g_h`.
 - The Normalized Temperature Scaled Cross-Entropy (NT-Xent) loss is used with cosine similarity.
 - The output `ϕ_o` serves as the state-sensitive feature representation.

#### 3.2. Object Affordances via Context Prediction (ACP)

This approach aims to infer regions of interaction (ROI) and applicable hand-grasp types directly from scene context.
- **Problem Addressed:** When a hand is physically present, models might simply learn to detect the hand itself rather than the object's inherent interactive properties.
- **Solution: Context Prediction Task:** The core idea is to mask out the interacting hand and train the model to predict the hand's location and grasp type solely from the surrounding visual context. This forces the model to reason about object affordances.
- **Data Generation:**
 - For hands detected in a "contact" state, an `s × s` patch is sampled around the hand.
 - A larger `2s × 2s` *asymmetric* context region is cropped, placing the `s × s` hand patch at its bottom center.
 - The `s × s` hand patch is then masked out, forming the input image for the model.
 - **Supervision:**
 - **Segmentation Mask:** The ground truth is a segmentation mask for the hand (and optionally the object-of-interaction) within the masked region, derived by "pasting" the detection boxes.
 - **Grasp Type:** The ground truth is the grasp type predicted by the pre-trained GUN-71 classifier for the interacting hand.
 - Additional positive patches are sampled around object-of-interaction detections, and negative patches from other image regions. All patches are resized to `128 × 128`.
- **Model Architecture:**
 - ResNet-50 encoder.
 - **Segmentation Head:** A deconvolutional decoder generates `64 × 64` segmentation masks, trained with binary cross-entropy loss (positive class weighted 4x).
 - **Grasp Type Prediction Head:** Two fully-connected layers predict grasp types as a multi-label problem, using independent binary cross-entropy losses for each grasp type.
- **Inference:**
 - During inference, context regions are sampled densely at three scales across the image.
 - These are reshaped and the `64 × 64` bottom-center region is masked out before being fed to the model.
 - The resulting per-patch predictions are then aggregated and pasted back onto the original image to create per-pixel probability maps for interaction and afforded hand grasps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates its proposed methods on three distinct tasks, utilizing two newly introduced datasets (EPIC-STATES and EPIC-ROI) and an adapted existing one (YCB-Affordance). All experiments maintain a strict participant or object category separation between training and testing splits to ensure generalization.

#### 4.1. State Sensitive Features for Objects (on EPIC-STATES)

- **Task:** Fine-grained object state classification (10 binary state categories like OPEN, CLOSE, INHAND, CUT). Evaluation metric: mean Average Precision (mAP).
- **Key Results:**
 - **Superiority over ImageNet Pre-training:** The proposed self-supervised methods, TSC and TSC+OHC, consistently outperformed features from ImageNet pre-training. For example, TSC+OHC achieved an mAP of 84.8% on the full dataset with 100% training data, compared to 83.1% for ImageNet pre-trained features.
 - **Enhanced Performance in Challenging Settings:** The performance gains of TSC+OHC were more pronounced in scenarios with limited training data (12.5%) or when generalizing to novel object categories. In the novel object setting with 12.5% data, TSC+OHC improved mAP by 9.5% over ImageNet pre-trained features (79.7% vs. 70.2%).
 - **Outperforming Other Self-Supervision:** TSC and TSC+OHC demonstrated superior performance compared to alternative self-supervised learning schemes such as vanilla SimCLR, Time Contrastive Networks (TCN), and their combination. TSC alone was more effective than SimCLR, suggesting the importance of temporal information.
 - **Benefits of Hand Consistency (`L_hand`):** The addition of the `L_hand` term in TSC+OHC consistently improved performance over TSC alone (e.g., 0.6% overall gain, and up to 5.2% in novel category/low-data settings). This supports the hypothesis that observing hand interactions significantly aids in understanding object states.
 - **Outperforming Semantic Supervision:** Notably, TSC and TSC+OHC models surpassed features learned from direct semantic supervision tasks, including action classification on EPIC-KITCHENS and state classification on the MIT States dataset. This suggests the effectiveness of the proposed self-supervision from egocentric video in learning state-sensitive features, potentially due to reduced domain gap and more relevant supervision.
- **Ablation Studies (Supplementary):** Confirmed the utility of tracking "object-of-interaction" patches and the importance of both hand appearance and motion (with motion being more influential) for the TSC+OHC model.

#### 4.2. Regions of Interaction (on EPIC-ROI)

- **Task:** Pixel-level prediction of regions where human hands frequently interact. Evaluation metric: Average Precision (AP), including a "1% SLACK" setting to account for minor boundary inaccuracies.
- **Key Results:**
 - **Competitive Performance without Segmentation Masks:** ACP, despite not being trained on any explicit segmentation masks, achieved an AP of 67.3% (with 1% SLACK), outperforming objectness-based methods (SalGAN 56.4%, DeepGaze2 64.6%) and action classification-based interaction hotspots (52.0%).
 - **Strong Performance on Novel Interaction Sites:** While Mask RCNN (when restricted to relevant categories) provided a strong baseline (70.0% AP with 1% SLACK), its performance suffered on non-COCO objects or object parts. ACP, in contrast, showed strengths in these challenging areas (e.g., stove knobs, pan handles).
 - **Complementary to Instance Segmentation:** When combined with Mask RCNN, ACP led to the strongest overall performance (76.4% AP with 1% SLACK), yielding a 4.7% improvement over Mask RCNN alone. This indicates that ACP effectively learns about objects and interaction sites not typically present or finely represented in large object detection datasets.
- **Ablation Studies (Supplementary):** Validated the design choices of ACP, including the importance of masking out hands, using an asymmetric context window, and filtering based on contact state. The use of hand segmentation masks (rather than bounding box masks) also showed minor improvements.

#### 4.3. Hand Grasps Afforded by Objects (on YCB-Affordance)

- **Task:** Predicting which of 33 hand-grasp types are afforded by an object, given its segmentation mask. Evaluation metric: mAP across 7 grasp types present in the test set.
- **Key Results:**
 - ACP achieved an mAP of 38.1%, which represented a 30% reduction in the performance gap between chance (30.2%) and a supervised ceiling (56.8%).
 - Adapting the GUN71 grasp classifier used for supervision, by incorporating an `L_temporal` loss on EPIC-KITCHENS hand tracks, contributed to improved performance (38.1% vs. 34.3% without adaptation).
 - This demonstrates ACP's capability to provide a more comprehensive interactive understanding by predicting specific grasp types alongside interaction regions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of computer vision by demonstrating that human hands, observed in egocentric videos, serve as effective "probes" for acquiring interactive object understanding. The core insight is that natural human interactions implicitly provide both the necessary data and supervisory signals to learn how objects work, where interactions occur, and what types of grasps are afforded. This self-supervised approach bypasses the labor-intensive and potentially biased process of manual annotation for complex interactive properties.

**Key Significance:**
- **Novel Self-Supervision:** The paper introduces novel self-supervised learning techniques (Temporal SimCLR with Object-Hand Consistency and Affordances via Context Prediction) that derive meaningful information about object states and affordances directly from unscripted egocentric video.
- **Improved Object Understanding:** The learned state-sensitive features demonstrate superior performance compared to traditional ImageNet pre-training and even some forms of semantic supervision, particularly in generalizing to novel objects and low-data regimes.
- **Comprehensive Interaction Maps:** The ability to predict both regions of interaction and applicable hand grasps offers a more holistic understanding of object manipulability, extending beyond simple object detection or classification.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** By leveraging readily available egocentric video data, the methods offer a scalable solution to the challenge of acquiring large-scale labeled datasets for interactive tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
The developed techniques have potential applications in several areas:
- **Robotics:** Enhanced interactive object understanding can enable robots to learn more robust manipulation policies, execute tasks more effectively, and adapt to novel objects. State-sensitive features could serve as reward functions for reinforcement learning, and affordance predictions could guide exploration for grasping.
- **Assistive Technologies:** The ability to understand human-object interactions could inform the development of assistive systems that anticipate user needs or provide guidance for complex manipulation tasks.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Deeper understanding of how humans interact with their environment could lead to more intuitive and responsive interfaces.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- The reliance on off-the-shelf hand and object detectors means the system's performance is constrained by the quality of these upstream models.
- The ACP model's inability to "see" the pixels it predicts on (due to masking) can lead to less localized predictions, suggesting room for architectural improvements.
- The newly introduced EPIC-ROI dataset, while valuable, could benefit from more fine-grained annotations for small objects to enable more precise evaluation.
- The lack of large-scale, in-the-wild datasets for grasp evaluation is a general challenge.
- The different aspects of interactive object understanding (state, affordances) were tackled largely in isolation; a joint learning framework could potentially yield further improvements.

Ethical considerations are also noted, including the use of sensitive egocentric data (managed by relying on consented public datasets like EPIC-KITCHENS) and the potential for inherited biases from the raw video data. The authors also highlight the broader societal implications of advanced interactive object understanding, particularly in the context of automation, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of its use. Future work could explore richer hand representations (e.g., 3D pose, segmentation) to further enhance object understanding.
