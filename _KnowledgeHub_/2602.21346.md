# Alignment-Weighted DPO: A principled reasoning approach to improve safety alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Virginia and Capital One developed Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO), a training method that improves large language model safety against jailbreak attacks by explicitly incorporating reasoning capabilities into the post-training process. This approach consistently enhanced safety across various models while preserving general utility, supported by empirical evidence suggesting current alignment relies on superficial heuristics.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to "jailbreak" attacks, which exploit weaknesses in safety alignment mechanisms by disguising harmful intent.
- Existing alignment techniques often rely on superficial refusal heuristics rather than deep, principled reasoning, leading to generic refusals without true comprehension of harmfulness.
- A lack of openly released Chain-of-Thought (CoT) alignment datasets and insufficient consideration of trade-offs with general model utility hinders progress in reasoning-aware alignment.

## Method

- Conducted ==causal intervention== experiments, deactivating reasoning-critical neurons to empirically demonstrate that current LLM safety alignment is largely independent of deep reasoning.
- Constructed and openly released a novel, long-form Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset by using GPT-4o to generate detailed step-by-step rationales for both safety-related and general-purpose prompts.
- Developed **Alignment-Weighted DPO** (AW-DPO), a reinforcement learning method that assigns distinct preference weights to the reasoning and final-answer segments of an output to address fine-grained misalignments found in CoT fine-tuning.

## Results

- AW-DPO consistently achieved superior safety performance across LLaMA-3.2-**3B**, LLaMA-3.1-**8B**, and Mistral-**7B**-v0.3, reducing the Attack Success Rate (ASR) against diverse jailbreak attacks while maintaining competitive utility.
- Causal intervention experiments showed that deactivating attention heads critical for reasoning degraded reasoning tasks but left safety performance unaffected, providing empirical evidence for superficial alignment.
- The AW-DPO dataset demonstrated strong transferability across different model architectures, enabling robust safety and utility without requiring new dataset generation for each model, thus reducing development costs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Causal interventions revealed that LLM safety alignment is often superficial; models can detect and refuse harmful prompts based on shallow patterns, even when their deep reasoning capabilities are impaired.
> - Standard CoT fine-tuning can improve safety, but error analysis showed misalignments where reasoning was correct but the final answer unsafe, or vice versa, necessitating finer-grained optimization.
> - Explicitly guiding models to produce refusals grounded in step-by-step understanding, rather than pattern recognition, is critical for developing more robust safety alignment against diverse jailbreak strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of large language models (LLMs) in sensitive applications across various sectors, including finance, healthcare, and education, necessitates their alignment with human values to prevent the generation of harmful, undesirable, or disallowed content. Existing alignment techniques, such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), have contributed to enhancing model safety. However, a persistent challenge in the field is the vulnerability of LLMs to "jailbreak" attacks. These attacks exploit weaknesses in alignment mechanisms by disguising harmful intent through indirect or deceptive phrasing, such as role-playing, rhetorical manipulation, prompt obfuscation via ciphers or low-resource languages, and attacks involving formal logic or code injection.

A growing body of research indicates that current LLM alignment is frequently superficial. This superficiality often manifests as alignment signals primarily affecting early tokens of a response, with models potentially generating unsafe content once they deviate from a safe opening. Furthermore, alignment tends to fail when harmful intent is expressed indirectly. Despite the diversity of these attack vectors, the underlying mechanisms that render current alignment superficial and bypassable remain largely unexplored.

This paper addresses this gap by hypothesizing that current alignment methods rely on shallow refusal heuristics rather than deep reasoning. It suggests that models learn to detect superficial markers of harmfulness, leading to generic refusals without a genuine understanding of *why* the content is harmful. Prior studies have explored reasoning-aware alignment, often through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning, to improve safety. However, these efforts often do not release their CoT alignment datasets or fail to consider the trade-offs with general model utility. This work aims to bridge these identified gaps by empirically investigating the nature of current alignment and proposing a targeted enhancement method based on these insights.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed vulnerability of large language models to jailbreak attacks, despite advancements in alignment techniques. The authors contend that current safety mechanisms in LLMs are often superficial, rejecting harmful prompts based on shallow patterns or heuristics rather than a deep, principled understanding of the content's harmful nature. This leads to models that can be easily bypassed by adversarially crafted prompts.

The key objectives of this study are:

1. **Empirical Demonstration of Superficial Alignment**: To provide causal evidence that existing safety alignment in LLMs is largely independent of deep reasoning capabilities. This objective seeks to validate the hypothesis that models can refuse harmful requests without truly comprehending the underlying reasons for their harmfulness, thus operating on "shortcut" patterns.
2. **Enhancing Alignment through Reasoning-Aware Post-training**: To improve safety alignment by explicitly integrating reasoning capabilities into the post-training process. This involves encouraging models to produce refusals that are grounded in a step-by-step understanding of the harmfulness, rather than mere pattern recognition.
3. **Construction and Release of a Novel CoT Dataset**: To address limitations in existing resources, a novel Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning dataset is constructed and openly released. This dataset is designed to include both utility-oriented and safety-critical prompts, each accompanied by detailed step-by-step rationales, to ensure that enhanced safety does not disproportionately compromise general model utility.
4. **Development of Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO)**: To refine the alignment process by introducing a novel reinforcement learning method. This objective is motivated by observed failure patterns in CoT fine-tuning, where misalignments can occur between the reasoning process and the final response. AW-DPO aims to provide finer-grained, targeted optimization by assigning distinct preference weights to the reasoning and final-answer segments of an output.
5. **Improving Robustness and Maintaining Utility**: The overarching goal is to develop a method that consistently improves alignment robustness against diverse jailbreak strategies across various LLMs while concurrently maintaining or competitively preserving the overall utility of the model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-stage approach, beginning with a causal investigation into the nature of current LLM alignment, followed by the development of reasoning-aware post-training techniques.

**Preliminary Experiments: Causal Intervention**
To test the hypothesis that current safety alignment is superficial, the authors conducted causal interventions.
- **Identification of Reasoning-Critical Neurons**: Linear probing was used on models (Llama-2-7b-Chat, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, with Llama-2-13B and Llama-3.2-3B also evaluated in supplementary material). For each attention head in each layer, a logistic regression classifier was trained to distinguish between:
 - Safe versus unsafe answers for alignment tasks (using a merged dataset of AdvBench and BeaverTails for malicious prompts, and Natural Questions for benign prompts).
 - True versus false answers for reasoning tasks (using the CommonsenseQA dataset, concatenating questions with correct/incorrect answers).
 High classification accuracy indicated neurons critical for a given task.
- **Intervention**: The top 10% of attention heads with the highest probing accuracy in the first 11 layers (identified as crucial for early-stage reasoning) were selected. These heads were "deactivated" by zeroing out their query, key, and value (Q, K, V) weights.
- **Evaluation**: Model performance was re-evaluated on both reasoning and alignment tasks using the same probing procedure. Additionally, quantitative benchmarks were performed, assessing reasoning ability by comparing generated answers to ground truth and safety ability using Llama Guard 3-8B.

**Method: Teaching Models Why to Say No with Alignment-Weighted DPO**

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Fine-tuning**:
 - **Dataset Construction**: A novel, open-source, long-form CoT dataset was created. It combines 4,000 safety-related samples from `repnoise_beavertail` and 16,000 general-purpose samples from Alpaca. GPT-4o was employed to generate detailed step-by-step rationales for each sample.
 - For harmful examples, the rationale explains *why* the query is harmful and *how* the model's refusal is justified.
 - For benign examples, the rationale explains *why* the question is safe and outlines the reasoning steps leading to the correct answer.
 - **Training Format**: The generated rationales were placed between `<think>` and `</think>` tags, followed by the final response, to train the model to adopt this structured thinking process.

- **Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO)**:
 - **Motivation**: Error analysis of CoT fine-tuning revealed cases where reasoning was correct but the final answer was unsafe, or vice versa (approximately 15% of failure cases). Standard DPO optimizes for full-response preferences, which may not address these fine-grained misalignments.
 - **Pipeline (Figure 2)**:
 1. **Candidate Generation**: The CoT-finetuned LLM generates `k=5` candidate responses per prompt, using adversarial harmful prompts from WildJailbreak.
 2. **Harmfulness Scoring**: An LLM judge (GPT-4o) assigns harmfulness scores (`h`) to three components for each candidate: (i) reasoning trace (`h_rs`), (ii) final response (`h_rp`), and (iii) the full answer (`h_f`).
 3. **Preference Pair Construction**: Preference pairs (`x_chosen`, `x_rejected`) are constructed by selecting candidate pairs where the difference in full harmfulness scores (`h_f`) exceeds a threshold `γ=0.5`.
 4. **Alignment Weight Calculation**: For each chosen/rejected pair, alignment weights for reasoning (`w_reasoning`) and response (`w_respond`) are calculated based on the differences in their respective harmfulness scores (`d_reasoning = h_chosen_rs - h_rejected_rs`, `d_respond = h_chosen_rp - h_rejected_rp`). These weights are normalized.
 5. **AW-DPO Training**: The standard DPO loss is modified to incorporate these fine-grained alignment weights. The reward function `ϕ_AW(x, y)` is decomposed into token-level contributions, weighted by `w_reasoning` or `w_response` based on whether the token belongs to the reasoning or response segment. This results in separate DPO losses for reasoning (`L_rs_DPO`) and response (`L_rp_DPO`). The final loss is a weighted sum: `L_AW-DPO = w_reasoning * L_rs_DPO + w_respond * L_rp_DPO`. This design prioritizes correction of the component exhibiting more harmful behavior.
 - **Hyperparameters**: A scaling factor `α=0.2` was applied to the DPO loss terms for stability. Learning rates were typically `5e-7` or `1e-6` for specific models, DPO temperature `β=0.2`.

**Evaluation Metrics and Baselines**:
- **Safety Evaluation**: SorryBench, encompassing 20 jailbreak attacks and 44 categories of harmful prompts. Metric: Attack Success Rate (ASR), with lower values indicating better safety.
- **Utility Evaluation**: MMLU benchmark for general knowledge and reasoning. Metric: Accuracy.
- **Models**: LLaMA-3.2-3B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and LLaMA-2-7B-Base models were used.
- **Baselines**: Vanilla SFT, Safety SFT, Safety SFT + DPO, Vanilla CoT SFT, Safety CoT SFT, open-source chat models (e.g., LLaMA2-7B-Chat), SAFECHAIN, Representation Rerouting (RR), and STAIR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings, supported by extensive experimentation across multiple models and benchmarks.

**Causal Intervention Results**:
- **Alignment vs. Reasoning Difficulty**: Linear probing revealed that LLMs (Llama-2-7b-Chat, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, and others) achieved nearly 100% accuracy in distinguishing safe from unsafe prompts across all layers, including early ones. In contrast, reasoning task accuracy remained at chance level for the first 11 layers, improving only in later layers. This indicates that the alignment task is significantly simpler for LLMs than the reasoning task.
- **Disconnection of Alignment from Deep Reasoning**: After deactivating reasoning-critical attention heads (top 10% in the first 11 layers), model performance on reasoning tasks degraded substantially (accuracy dropped to approximately 50%). However, performance on safety tasks remained largely unaffected, maintaining nearly 100% accuracy.
- **Conclusion**: These results empirically support the hypothesis that current safety alignment mechanisms are superficial and do not rely on the model's deep reasoning abilities. Models reject harmful prompts without necessarily understanding *why* they are harmful, rendering them susceptible to jailbreak attacks.

**Performance of CoT Fine-tuning**:
- Fine-tuning LLMs with the newly constructed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset significantly improved safety performance compared to standard SFT baselines. This improvement was achieved while maintaining comparable general model utility across various models (Table 1).

**Error Analysis and Motivation for AW-DPO**:
- Qualitative error analysis of CoT fine-tuned models revealed two specific failure modes, accounting for approximately 15% of all safety failures: (i) instances where the model provided correct reasoning but an unsafe final answer, and (ii) instances where reasoning was incorrect but the final answer was safe (Figure 3a). Standard DPO, which optimizes for full-response preferences, was found to be insufficient for addressing these fine-grained reasoning-related misalignments.

**Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO) Performance**:
- **Superior Safety and Competitive Utility**: The proposed Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO) method consistently achieved the best overall safety performance across multiple LLM families and sizes (LLaMA-3.2-3B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3) when compared to various baselines, including SFT, Safety SFT, CoT SFT, and standard DPO (Table 1). AW-DPO also preserved competitive utility.
- **Comparison with Advanced Baselines**: When compared against recent advanced alignment methods (SAFECHAIN, Representation Rerouting, STAIR), AW-DPO demonstrated strong safety performance and competitive utility. While STAIR-DPO-3 showed higher safety, it required a more computationally intensive three-round iterative training process, whereas AW-DPO achieved its results in a single, more efficient round (Table 2).
- **Against Open-Source Aligned LLMs**: AW-DPO demonstrated superior average safety performance compared to several advanced open-source aligned LLMs, despite these models often benefiting from proprietary datasets and extensive computational resources. While utility might be slightly lower, it provided an effective means to enhance safety (Figure 3b, 3c, Table 7).
- **Improvement on Already Aligned Models**: AW-DPO was shown to yield additional safety improvements even on models that had undergone prior alignment (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct), while successfully preserving their original strong utility (Figure 4a, Table 11).

**Transferability of DPO Dataset**:
- The AW-DPO dataset, initially constructed using LLaMA2-7B, exhibited strong transferability. When applied to train AW-DPO models on other architectures (LLaMA3.2-3B, LLaMA3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-V0.3), it achieved robust safety and utility performance, indicating potential for cost reduction in dataset generation (Table 3).

**Ablation Studies**:
- **AW-DPO vs. Standard DPO**: Direct comparison revealed that AW-DPO consistently outperformed standard DPO in both safety and utility, substantiating its effectiveness in correcting fine-grained alignment errors (Figure 4b, 4c, Table 12).
- **Robustness to Scaling Factor α**: Performance remained stable across different values of the scaling factor α (0.05 to 0.5), suggesting that AW-DPO is robust to this hyperparameter (Table 4).
- **Sensitivity to Learning Rate**: Similar to standard DPO, AW-DPO's performance was sensitive to the learning rate, with `1e-6` generally yielding optimal results (Table 5).

**Performance under Prefix Attack**:
- The model trained with AW-DPO maintained its advanced safety and utility performance even when subjected to prefix attacks (e.g., appending `<think></think>`), demonstrating robustness to prompts designed to bypass reasoning (Table 10).

**Comparison with Reasoning LLMs**:
- General reasoning-oriented models (e.g., Phi-4-Reasoning and Qwen Thinking models) performed significantly worse on safety tasks despite their enhanced general reasoning capabilities (Figure 3b, Table 9). This indicated that merely improving general reasoning is insufficient for achieving stronger alignment-specific safety, underscoring the necessity of explicitly targeting reasoning tailored for alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of large language model safety by providing both a diagnostic understanding of current alignment deficiencies and a principled, effective method for improvement.

**Fundamental Understanding of LLM Safety**: The causal intervention experiments offer empirical evidence that existing LLM safety alignment often relies on superficial patterns rather than deep reasoning. This finding is significant because it explains *why* current alignment mechanisms are vulnerable to jailbreak attacks, which often exploit the model's lack of true comprehension of harmful intent. This foundational insight can guide future research towards more robust and interpretable safety mechanisms.

**Advancement in Reasoning-Aware Alignment**: By proposing that safety alignment requires deep reasoning, the paper advocates for a more sophisticated approach than merely rejecting prompts based on surface-level cues. The introduction of the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning dataset, which couples utility-oriented and safety-critical prompts with step-by-step rationales, represents a concrete step towards embedding principled reasoning into LLM behavior. This dataset's open-source release facilitates further community research in this direction.

**Targeted and Fine-grained Optimization**: The development of Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO) addresses a crucial limitation of standard preference optimization methods. By assigning differential weights to the reasoning and final-answer segments of an output, AW-DPO enables a more granular correction of misalignments. This targeted approach is particularly valuable for complex failure modes where, for example, a model might reason correctly but still produce an unsafe final output. Such fine-grained control has the potential to produce more robust and reliable safety alignments.

**Practical Impact on LLM Deployment**:
- **Enhanced Robustness**: The demonstrated ability of AW-DPO to consistently improve safety performance across various models and against diverse jailbreak strategies (writing styles, persuasion, encoding, multilingual attacks) is crucial for real-world deployment. Safer LLMs are essential for maintaining trust and mitigating risks in high-stakes applications.
- **Efficiency and Transferability**: Achieving strong safety improvements with a single round of SFT and DPO, as opposed to multi-round iterative training, makes the method more computationally efficient. Furthermore, the observed transferability of the AW-DPO dataset across different model architectures can significantly reduce the cost and effort of developing safety alignment for new models.
- **Preservation of Utility**: A key challenge in safety alignment is avoiding over-refusal or "safety tax" that degrades general model utility. The research shows that AW-DPO maintains competitive utility, ensuring that models remain helpful while being safer.

The findings collectively suggest that future efforts in LLM alignment should prioritize the development of deep, context-aware reasoning for safety. The methods and insights presented in this paper offer a foundation for building more robust, interpretable, and trustworthy large language models, thereby contributing to their safer integration into society.
