# Alignment-Weighted DPO: A principled reasoning approach to improve safety alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Virginia and Capital One developed Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO), a training method that improves large language model safety against jailbreak attacks by explicitly incorporating reasoning capabilities into the post-training process. This approach consistently enhanced safety across various models while preserving general utility, supported by empirical evidence suggesting current alignment relies on superficial heuristics.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to "jailbreak" attacks, which exploit weaknesses in safety alignment mechanisms by disguising harmful intent.
- Existing alignment techniques often rely on superficial refusal heuristics rather than deep, principled reasoning, leading to generic refusals without true comprehension of harmfulness.
- A lack of openly released Chain-of-Thought (CoT) alignment datasets and insufficient consideration of trade-offs with general model utility hinders progress in reasoning-aware alignment.

## Method

- Conducted ==causal intervention== experiments, deactivating reasoning-critical neurons to empirically demonstrate that current LLM safety alignment is largely independent of deep reasoning.
- Constructed and openly released a novel, long-form Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset by using GPT-4o to generate detailed step-by-step rationales for both safety-related and general-purpose prompts.
- Developed **Alignment-Weighted DPO** (AW-DPO), a reinforcement learning method that assigns distinct preference weights to the reasoning and final-answer segments of an output to address fine-grained misalignments found in CoT fine-tuning.

## Results

- AW-DPO consistently achieved superior safety performance across LLaMA-3.2-**3B**, LLaMA-3.1-**8B**, and Mistral-**7B**-v0.3, reducing the Attack Success Rate (ASR) against diverse jailbreak attacks while maintaining competitive utility.
- Causal intervention experiments showed that deactivating attention heads critical for reasoning degraded reasoning tasks but left safety performance unaffected, providing empirical evidence for superficial alignment.
- The AW-DPO dataset demonstrated strong transferability across different model architectures, enabling robust safety and utility without requiring new dataset generation for each model, thus reducing development costs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Causal interventions revealed that LLM safety alignment is often superficial; models can detect and refuse harmful prompts based on shallow patterns, even when their deep reasoning capabilities are impaired.
> - Standard CoT fine-tuning can improve safety, but error analysis showed misalignments where reasoning was correct but the final answer unsafe, or vice versa, necessitating finer-grained optimization.
> - Explicitly guiding models to produce refusals grounded in step-by-step understanding, rather than pattern recognition, is critical for developing more robust safety alignment against diverse jailbreak strategies.
