# The Image as Its Own Reward: Reinforcement Learning with Adversarial Reward for Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Adv-GRPO, developed by NUS Show Lab and ByteDance, introduces a reinforcement learning framework with an adversarial reward mechanism and visual foundation models like DINO to mitigate reward hacking in text-to-image generation. The method achieves a 70.0% win rate in image quality and 85.3% in aesthetics against Flow-GRPO in human evaluations, while also improving OCR accuracy from 0.59 to 0.69.

## Problem

- Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models optimized with existing scalar reward functions are prone to "reward hacking," generating images that score high but lack genuine human-perceived quality.
- Scalar reward models (e.g., PickScore, aesthetic models) often introduce biases that generators exploit, leading to artifacts or suboptimal visual fidelity.
- Common regularization techniques, like KL divergence penalties, can constrain model optimization and limit performance gains in T2I synthesis.

## Method

- **Adv-GRPO** integrates Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a dynamically optimized ==adversarial reward== model that distinguishes high-quality reference images from generated ones.
- The reward model is trained adversarially using either a trigger-based co-training for human preference models or a combined multi-reward approach for rule-based models.
- Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) like DINO are leveraged as the reward backbone, providing rich global and local visual features to a classification head, trained adversarially to offer dense, unbiased reward signals.

## Results

- Adv-GRPO consistently outperformed Flow-GRPO in human evaluations, achieving a **70.0%** win rate in image quality and **85.3%** in aesthetics, while also showing significant perceptual improvements over the base SD3 model.
- Utilizing DINO as the reward model resulted in a **72.4%** win rate in aesthetics against SD3 and improved task-specific benchmarks, with OCR accuracy increasing from **0.59** to **0.69** and GenEval score from **0.61** to **0.69**.
- The framework successfully demonstrated style customization capabilities by transferring generation styles (e.g., anime, sci-fi) from reference datasets while preserving semantic structure and image quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating the image itself as a reward through adversarial training with high-quality reference images can effectively ground the reward learning process and mitigate reward hacking.
> - Pre-trained visual foundation models (VFMs) can serve as comprehensive and less biased reward signals due to their rich semantic and structural representations, guiding generators towards more holistic visual improvements.
> - An adversarial learning framework can dynamically align the generator and reward model, providing a self-improving reward mechanism that moves beyond static, potentially biased scalar preference models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL), generative AI (specifically text-to-image, T2I diffusion models), and human-centric AI alignment. In recent years, RL has demonstrated remarkable success in aligning large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) with human preferences and complex tasks, exemplified by algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).

The application of RL to T2I generation is a natural progression, aiming to improve the quality, aesthetics, and prompt alignment of generated images. Prior work like DanceGRPO and Flow-GRPO has already adapted GRPO for T2I synthesis, showing promising results by guiding diffusion models with reward signals. However, a fundamental challenge in this domain is the **reliability and fidelity of reward models**. Traditional reward models, such as PickScore, HPS, and various aesthetic models, are often pre-trained to output scalar scores that approximate human preferences. The paper highlights a critical limitation: these models are prone to "reward hacking," where a generator learns to exploit biases in the reward function to achieve higher scores without actually producing genuinely better or more aesthetically pleasing images. For instance, CLIP-based rewards might favor oversaturated colors, while OCR-based rewards might compromise overall aesthetics for text accuracy. Furthermore, common regularization techniques like KL divergence penalties, while mitigating reward hacking, can also constrain model optimization, limiting performance gains.

This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a novel adversarial training paradigm and leveraging visual foundation models. It pushes beyond existing methods that primarily focus on refining reward design or adding regularization. By treating the image itself as a reward and incorporating high-quality reference images, the authors aim to ground the reward learning process in richer, more direct visual signals. This approach represents a significant step towards creating more robust, unbiased, and perceptually aligned T2I generation systems, moving beyond the inherent biases of scalar preference models and the limitations of simple regularization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Adv-GRPO framework are multifold:

1. **Alleviate Reward Hacking in T2I Generation:** The central motivation is to overcome the prevalent issue of reward hacking, where T2I models optimized with existing scalar reward functions (e.g., PickScore, OCR) tend to produce images that score high on metrics but lack genuine human-perceived quality, aesthetics, or accurate prompt alignment. These scalar rewards often introduce biases that generators exploit.
2. **Develop a More Reliable Reward Function:** The authors aim to create a reward mechanism that more accurately reflects human perception and true image quality. They argue that pre-trained preference models are insufficient and that rewards should be derived directly from rich visual information.
3. **Integrate High-Quality Reference Images as Supervision:** A key objective is to leverage high-quality reference images as a direct form of positive supervision for the reward model. This contrasts with purely scalar feedback and aims to anchor the reward model's understanding of "good" images in concrete visual examples, thereby making it more robust against hacking.
4. **Explore Visual Foundation Models as Unbiased Reward Signals:** The paper seeks to harness the rich semantic and structural representations learned by large visual foundation models (e.g., DINO) to provide dense, holistic visual rewards. The motivation here is that these models, pre-trained on vast image datasets, can offer a more comprehensive assessment of image quality, aesthetics, and coherence than specialized scalar reward models.
5. **Enable RL-based Distribution Transfer and Style Customization:** Beyond general quality improvement, the authors aim to demonstrate that their RL framework can facilitate flexible control over generated image properties, specifically enabling distribution transfer and style customization by aligning generations with specific reference visual domains.
6. **Achieve Consistent Gains Across Diverse Metrics:** The overarching goal is to achieve consistent improvements in image quality, aesthetics, and task-specific metrics (e.g., text alignment for OCR, object correctness for GenEval), without sacrificing performance in one area for another.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, **Adv-GRPO**, is an RL framework that combines Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with an adversarial reward mechanism.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: GRPO on Flow Matching and Adversarial Training

- **GRPO on Flow Matching:** The denoising process of a diffusion model is framed as a Markov Decision Process, where each reverse step is an action from a policy. GRPO optimizes this policy by maximizing a clipped objective function (Eq. 2), using normalized group advantages derived from rewards of generated samples. A KL penalty regularizes policy updates.
- **Adversarial Training:** This well-known paradigm involves a generator and a discriminator. The generator creates samples to fool the discriminator, while the discriminator learns to distinguish real data from generated data.

#### 3.2. GRPO with Adversarial Reward

Adv-GRPO extends GRPO into an adversarial setting where the T2I generator ($G_\theta$) and the reward model ($R_\phi$) are jointly optimized:
- **Generator Optimization:** The generator ($G_\theta$, which is a Stable Diffusion 3 model) is optimized using the standard GRPO objective (Eq. 5), maximizing the rewards provided by $R_\phi$.
- **Reward Model as Discriminator:** The reward model ($R_\phi$) is trained adversarially to distinguish high-quality reference images ($D_{ref}$) as positive samples from images generated by $G_\theta$ as negative samples (Eq. 6). This joint training establishes a dynamic equilibrium, forcing $R_\phi$ to learn perceptual quality beyond initial biases and guiding $G_\theta$ towards higher-quality outputs.

The paper details two main strategies for incorporating this adversarial reward:

- **For Human Preference Models (e.g., PickScore, Aesthetic models):** The adversarial co-training is *triggered* when the average reward of generated images ($ \bar{r}_{gen} $) surpasses that of reference images ($ \bar{r}_{ref} $), signaling potential reward hacking. At this point, the reward model is fine-tuned adversarially using reference samples as positives and generated samples as negatives, re-aligning it towards human-preferred quality.
- **For Rule-based Reward Models (e.g., OCR, GenEval):** Since these models are deterministic and non-differentiable, they are not directly amenable to adversarial training. Instead, a multi-reward formulation is used: $R_{combined}(x_g, c) = \lambda R_{rule}(x_g, c) + (1 - \lambda) \text{sim}_{CLIP}(x_g, x_r)$. This combines the task-specific rule-based reward with CLIP similarity to a reference image, balancing task performance with overall visual fidelity.

#### 3.3. Visual Foundation Models As Reward

To address the inherent biases and limited scope of existing reward models, Adv-GRPO further proposes leveraging visual foundation models (VFMs) as reward models:
- **VFM Backbone:** A pre-trained VFM (e.g., DINOv2) has its parameters frozen.
- **Classification Head:** A lightweight binary classification head ($h_\phi$) is attached to the VFM's representations.
- **Global and Local Features:** The reward is computed using both the global `[CLS]` embedding and patch-level features extracted from the VFM (Eq. 9, 10). This allows for capturing both high-level semantics and fine-grained details.
- **Adversarial Training with Hinge Loss:** The reward head ($h_\phi$) is trained to discriminate reference images (positives) from generated images (negatives) using separate hinge losses at the global and local levels (Eq. 12, 13). The final adversarial loss is a weighted combination of these (Eq. 14). This provides dense visual signals that guide the generator toward more coherent and visually refined images.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Base Generator:** Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3).
- **Training Setup:** Uses specific prompt datasets (PickScore, OCR, GenEval). For DINO, the classification head is trained. For OCR, SD3 is jointly optimized with OCR and CLIP similarity rewards. Training involves 10 inference steps, 16 samples per prompt, and specific learning rates for generator and reward model.
- **Reference Images:** 8 reference images per prompt generated with Qwen-Image are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of Adv-GRPO across various evaluation metrics and scenarios:

#### 4.1. Reward Hacking Mitigation

- **Quantitative Performance:** Adv-GRPO achieves benchmark scores comparable to Flow-GRPO (e.g., PickScore ~22.80, OCR Accuracy ~0.91), significantly outperforming the base SD3 model (PickScore 21.70, OCR 0.58). This indicates that the adversarial training maintains strong metric performance.
- **Human Evaluation Superiority:** Critically, Adv-GRPO consistently outperforms Flow-GRPO in human evaluations across image aesthetics, alignment, and quality.
 - Under PickScore, Adv-GRPO achieved a **70.0% win rate in image quality** against Flow-GRPO.
 - Under OCR, it achieved an **85.3% win rate in aesthetics** against Flow-GRPO.
 - Compared to the base SD3 model, Adv-GRPO showed significant perceptual improvements (e.g., 72.6% win rate in aesthetics under PickScore, 77.6% in alignment under OCR).
- **Visualizations:** Qualitative results (Fig. 5, 12) visually confirm that Adv-GRPO produces images with better perceptual quality and fidelity, effectively mitigating the artifacts and biases characteristic of reward hacking observed in Flow-GRPO.

#### 4.2. Vision Foundation Model as Better Rewards (DINO)

- **Comprehensive Improvements:** When using DINO as the reward model, Adv-GRPO consistently improves all human evaluation metrics (aesthetics, alignment, quality) compared to the original SD3 model. A notable result is a **72.4% win rate in aesthetics** against SD3.
- **Outperformance Against Baselines:** Adv-GRPO (DINO reward) achieved a **66.3% win rate in quality and 75.2% in aesthetics** against Flow-GRPO (using DINO similarity as reward). It also showed a remarkable **93.5% win rate in quality** against Flow-GRPO (using PickScore reward).
- **Consistent Benchmark Improvement:** The DINO reward framework consistently improved performance across different task benchmarks: OCR accuracy increased from 0.59 (SD3) to 0.69 (Adv-GRPO DINO), and GenEval score from 0.61 to 0.69.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations (Fig. 7, 13, 14) show that DINO-rewarded images exhibit stronger compositional quality, richer colors, improved aesthetics, and more diverse background details.
- **Generalization to other VFMs:** Experiments with SigLIP (Fig. 15) as an alternative VFM also showed overall improvements in image quality, demonstrating the generalizability of the approach beyond DINO.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Number of Reference Images:** The method showed strong data efficiency, achieving comparable DINO similarity even with only 200 reference images (Tab. 3, Fig. 9a), implying that small datasets are sufficient.
- **Comparison with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Adv-GRPO (DINO reward) significantly outperformed SFT in human evaluations, with over 70% win rates in aesthetics and quality (Fig. 6d, 9b, Tab. 4), and achieved better quantitative metrics, demonstrating the superiority of RL for targeted optimization.
- **KL Regularization:** Adding a KL constraint to Flow-GRPO resulted in lower reward scores and degraded visual quality (Tab. 4, Fig. 9b), highlighting the limitations and sensitivity of KL regularization compared to Adv-GRPO's adversarial approach.
- **Multi-Reward Combination:** Adv-GRPO yielded higher metrics and better visual fidelity than a multi-reward baseline (Tab. 4, Fig. 9b), indicating better stability and effectiveness compared to simply balancing different reward weights.

#### 4.4. Style Customization

- Adv-GRPO successfully demonstrated style transfer capabilities. By fine-tuning SD3 with anime-style or sci-fi themed reference datasets, the method effectively transferred the generation style to the target domains while preserving semantic structure and image quality (Fig. 11, 16), showcasing a novel RL-based framework for style customization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Adv-GRPO framework represents a significant advancement in the field of text-to-image generation and reinforcement learning for generative models, with several potential impacts:

1. **Robust and Reliable Image Generation:** By effectively mitigating reward hacking, Adv-GRPO addresses a critical vulnerability in current RL-driven T2I models. This leads to more reliable and perceptually consistent image generation, where higher scores genuinely correspond to higher quality and better alignment with human preferences. This is crucial for developing trustworthy generative AI systems.
2. **Paradigm Shift in Reward Model Design:** The work introduces an innovative adversarial training paradigm that jointly optimizes the generator and the reward model using high-quality reference images. This shifts away from static, potentially biased pre-trained scalar reward models towards dynamic, self-improving reward functions that are more grounded in visual reality.
3. **Unlocking the Potential of Visual Foundation Models as Rewards:** The most impactful contribution might be demonstrating the efficacy of visual foundation models (like DINO) as comprehensive, unbiased reward signals. By leveraging their rich semantic and structural representations, the method provides dense visual feedback, moving beyond the limited and often biased scalar outputs of traditional preference models. This could set a new standard for how generative models are guided and evaluated, enabling more holistic improvements across various aspects of image quality.
4. **Enhanced Controllability and Customization:** The successful application of Adv-GRPO to style customization showcases its potential for fine-grained control over generation style. This could empower artists, designers, and general users to produce images that adhere to specific aesthetic or domain requirements, opening new avenues for creative applications of generative AI.
5. **Broader Implications for AI Alignment:** The challenges of reward hacking are not unique to image generation; they are pervasive in RL-driven AI systems. The adversarial training strategy and the use of rich, direct sensory feedback (like visual features) as rewards could inspire similar solutions in other domains, contributing to the broader goal of aligning AI systems with complex human preferences and values.
6. **Practical Tool for High-Quality Content Creation:** The consistent gains in image quality, aesthetics, and prompt alignment, validated by extensive human evaluation, indicate that Adv-GRPO can serve as a powerful tool for generating high-quality visual content for various industries, from media and entertainment to e-commerce and marketing.

In conclusion, Adv-GRPO offers a principled and effective approach to overcoming key limitations in RL-based image generation, paving the way for more perceptually aligned, controllable, and robust generative AI systems.
