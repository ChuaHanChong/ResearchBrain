# World Model Failure Classification and Anomaly Detection for Autonomous Inspection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a hybrid framework for real-time failure classification and anomaly detection in autonomous inspection robots, leveraging a world model backbone and conformal prediction. The system achieved over 90% accuracy in distinguishing success, known failures, and out-of-distribution conditions for gauge inspection, often identifying issues earlier than human observation.

## Problem

- Autonomous inspection robots face challenges from environmental factors like occlusions, glare, and limited viewpoints, leading to inaccurate readings or perception failures.
- There is a need to differentiate between known, repeatable failure modes and novel, unexpected out-of-distribution (OOD) anomalies for appropriate robot responses.
- Ensuring real-time, online failure detection is critical for timely corrective actions and enhancing safety in industrial robotic systems.

## Method

- A hybrid framework integrates ==supervised failure classification== with ==anomaly detection==, built upon a ==world model backbone== for architectural simplicity and real-time applicability.
- Two predictive models are trained: one for successful task data and another for known failure data, to predict future observations from current inputs.
- ==Conformal Prediction== (CP) calibrates fixed thresholds on anomaly scores using separate success and failure datasets, enabling the classification of inputs as success, known failure, or OOD.

## Results

- The framework achieved over **90%** classification accuracy for success, known failure, and out-of-distribution cases using the latent prediction error metric at a **90%** conformal prediction quantile.
- Classifications were made, on average, **1 to 3 seconds** earlier than human observers, particularly with the latent prediction error, indicating sensitivity to precursor patterns.
- Successful deployment on a Boston Dynamics Spot robot for gauge inspection demonstrated operational feasibility, with the system capable of prompting corrective actions like camera adjustments for OOD conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The latent prediction error metric consistently delivered robust performance, achieving over 90% accuracy across all three classification categories (success, known failure, OOD) while also providing anticipatory detection.
> - Conformal Prediction effectively calms thresholds for anomaly scores, allowing for reliable separation between nominal, known failure, and OOD conditions without requiring extensive labeled anomaly data.
> - The framework's policy-agnostic design, coupled with a world model backbone, demonstrates adaptability for various autonomous inspection tasks and enables deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
