# Generalizable Geometric Prior and Recurrent Spiking Feature Learning for Humanoid Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, RGMP-S, integrates a Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS) for context-aware skill selection and a Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet) for data-efficient action generation, enabling humanoid robots to perform complex manipulation tasks. It achieves a 5x improvement in data efficiency over prior methods while maintaining a high inference frequency of 75.2 Hz, demonstrating robust generalization to novel objects and long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often fail to bridge the semantic-geometric gap, struggling to infer physical interaction affordances from abstract instructions due to a lack of explicit geometric reasoning.
- Existing data-intensive approaches for robotic manipulation, such as large-scale diffusion and transformer models, are computationally expensive and inefficient in collecting interaction data, particularly in real-world, dynamic environments.
- Generative policy models, while expressive, typically suffer from high inference latency or neglect the intrinsic spatiotemporal mechanisms of manipulation, limiting their real-time application and generalization under domain shifts.

## Method

- ==Introduce RGMP==-S, a unified framework integrating high-level skill reasoning with data-efficient motion synthesis, processing egocentric RGB inputs and natural language commands.
- Develop the Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS) to bridge the semantic-geometric gap by using a VLM, semantic segmentation, and a Geometry-Grounded Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoner to infer and select geometrically feasible manipulation skills.
- Implement a Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet) incorporating dynamic recursive computation, Adaptive Spike Neurons (ASN), and a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for robust, data-efficient spatiotemporal feature learning and action generation.

## Results

- The LGSS component improved skill selection accuracy (Acc_s) by up to **15%** in challenging tasks like handling deformed objects, demonstrating effective geometric reasoning.
- RGMP-S achieved a **0.89** average success rate in zero-shot grasping and maintained a high inference frequency of **75.2** Hz, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods like Dex-VLA by **12%** in success rate.
- The framework demonstrated a **5x** improvement in data efficiency, achieving performance comparable to models trained on 200 trajectories with only 40, and consistently surpassed baselines in long-horizon and simulation tasks, such as a **16%** higher success rate in "Pouring Water."

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit geometric priors derived from 2D visual inputs can effectively guide skill selection, enabling context-aware manipulation without the computational burden or noise sensitivity of explicit 3D reconstruction.
> - Embedding explicit spatiotemporal inductive biases into the policy architecture via dynamic recursive computation and adaptive spiking neurons enhances data efficiency and generalization, allowing robust learning from limited expert demonstrations.
> - Utilizing a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for action generation accurately represents the multimodal nature of robotic actions, improving precision and robustness by avoiding the "regression to the mean" problem prevalent with single Gaussian models.
