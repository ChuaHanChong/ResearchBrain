# Generalizable Geometric Prior and Recurrent Spiking Feature Learning for Humanoid Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, RGMP-S, integrates a Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS) for context-aware skill selection and a Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet) for data-efficient action generation, enabling humanoid robots to perform complex manipulation tasks. It achieves a 5x improvement in data efficiency over prior methods while maintaining a high inference frequency of 75.2 Hz, demonstrating robust generalization to novel objects and long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often fail to bridge the semantic-geometric gap, struggling to infer physical interaction affordances from abstract instructions due to a lack of explicit geometric reasoning.
- Existing data-intensive approaches for robotic manipulation, such as large-scale diffusion and transformer models, are computationally expensive and inefficient in collecting interaction data, particularly in real-world, dynamic environments.
- Generative policy models, while expressive, typically suffer from high inference latency or neglect the intrinsic spatiotemporal mechanisms of manipulation, limiting their real-time application and generalization under domain shifts.

## Method

- ==Introduce RGMP==-S, a unified framework integrating high-level skill reasoning with data-efficient motion synthesis, processing egocentric RGB inputs and natural language commands.
- Develop the Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS) to bridge the semantic-geometric gap by using a VLM, semantic segmentation, and a Geometry-Grounded Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoner to infer and select geometrically feasible manipulation skills.
- Implement a Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet) incorporating dynamic recursive computation, Adaptive Spike Neurons (ASN), and a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for robust, data-efficient spatiotemporal feature learning and action generation.

## Results

- The LGSS component improved skill selection accuracy (Acc_s) by up to **15%** in challenging tasks like handling deformed objects, demonstrating effective geometric reasoning.
- RGMP-S achieved a **0.89** average success rate in zero-shot grasping and maintained a high inference frequency of **75.2** Hz, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods like Dex-VLA by **12%** in success rate.
- The framework demonstrated a **5x** improvement in data efficiency, achieving performance comparable to models trained on 200 trajectories with only 40, and consistently surpassed baselines in long-horizon and simulation tasks, such as a **16%** higher success rate in "Pouring Water."

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit geometric priors derived from 2D visual inputs can effectively guide skill selection, enabling context-aware manipulation without the computational burden or noise sensitivity of explicit 3D reconstruction.
> - Embedding explicit spatiotemporal inductive biases into the policy architecture via dynamic recursive computation and adaptive spiking neurons enhances data efficiency and generalization, allowing robust learning from limited expert demonstrations.
> - Utilizing a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for action generation accurately represents the multimodal nature of robotic actions, improving precision and robustness by avoiding the "regression to the mean" problem prevalent with single Gaussian models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robot manipulation represents a significant area within embodied intelligence, requiring both sophisticated high-level semantic reasoning and precise low-level action generation. Recent advancements, particularly the emergence of foundation models trained on large-scale datasets, have propelled progress in this field. However, significant challenges persist in transitioning these models to real-world applications.

A primary challenge identified in the research landscape is the **semantic-geometric gap**. Vision-Language Models (VLMs), while proficient in parsing semantic information and generating high-level task plans, often struggle to translate abstract instructions into contextually appropriate robotic skills during the execution phase. This deficiency arises because VLMs, despite their ability to identify objects, frequently fail to infer physical interaction affordances dictated by specific geometries. For instance, a VLM might recognize a "crushed Coke can" but still attempt a standard side-grasping primitive, which is physically infeasible for the deformed object, leading to task failure. Existing approaches often rely on explicit 3D reconstruction, which is computationally intensive and sensitive to noise, or coarse semantic embeddings, which lack the precision required for fine-grained manipulation.

Another critical challenge is **sample-efficient learning and data efficiency**. The prevailing "brute-force" scaling paradigm, exemplified by Diffusion Policies and large Transformer-based architectures, relies on training on massive, diverse datasets. While effective at scale, this approach is data-inefficient and computationally burdensome, making it prohibitive for dynamic manipulation tasks in real-world unstructured environments where interaction data is costly to collect. Diffusion models, despite their expressiveness in modeling action space, incur high inference latency due to their iterative denoising process. Transformer-based VLA models offer rapid inference but often treat visual inputs as flattened sequences, thereby neglecting the intrinsic spatiotemporal mechanisms of manipulation and lacking explicit inductive biases for capturing fine-grained spatial relationships between the robotic end-effector and target objects. This can lead to overfitting to spurious correlations in sparse data regimes or poor generalization under domain shifts.

This paper addresses these two core challenges by proposing a unified framework that integrates explicit geometric reasoning with data-efficient spatiotemporal feature learning. It aims to bridge the semantic-geometric gap by inferring implicit geometric structures directly from 2D inputs, thus bypassing the computational cost and noise sensitivity of explicit 3D reconstruction. Simultaneously, it seeks to improve data efficiency and generalization by embedding explicit spatiotemporal inductive biases into the policy architecture, moving beyond brute-force data scaling and iterative denoising approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, data-efficient, and generalizable framework for humanoid robot manipulation, specifically designed to bridge the semantic-geometric gap inherent in current systems. This overarching goal is broken down into two core research questions and their associated motivations:

**I. How can robots effectively exploit geometric reasoning to reliably select feasible manipulation skills?**
- **Motivation:** Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate proficiency in recognizing objects and localizing them within a scene. However, a significant limitation arises in their ability to translate abstract semantic instructions into physically executable robotic skills that account for the nuanced geometric properties of objects. For example, a VLM might semantically identify a "crushed Coke can" but fail to perceive the structural deformation, leading to an attempt at a conventional side-grasp rather than a more appropriate pinching motion. This disconnect stems from a lack of explicit geometric grounding within the VLM's decision-making process. Traditional methods that rely on explicit 3D reconstruction for detailed spatial information often suffer from high computational latency and sensitivity to noise, rendering them impractical for real-time robotic interaction in dynamic environments. Therefore, there is a clear need for a mechanism that can align high-level semantic instructions with low-level spatial and geometric constraints to ensure the selection of physically feasible manipulation skills, even in unseen or deformed object scenarios.

**II. How can robots effectively learn robust data-efficient policies from limited demonstrations?**
- **Motivation:** The development of advanced robotic manipulation policies, particularly those driven by generative models like Diffusion Policies, has shown promise in modeling complex action spaces. However, these expressive models typically come with substantial computational costs, such as the prohibitive inference latency introduced by iterative denoising processes, which hinders their applicability in high-frequency, real-time control tasks. Alternative approaches, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and Transformer-based architectures, aim for rapid, end-to-end action generation but often require massive, diverse datasets for robust generalization. This "brute-force" data scaling is computationally heavy and data-inefficient, especially given the expense of collecting high-fidelity interaction data in robotics. Furthermore, these models often process visual inputs as flattened semantic tokens, neglecting the intrinsic spatiotemporal mechanisms of manipulation and potentially latching onto spurious correlations in sparse data. This makes them prone to poor performance under domain shifts. The motivation is to move beyond mere parameter scaling or inefficient iterative methods by constructing an architecture that naturally embeds the spatiotemporal structure of manipulation tasks, allowing for efficient distillation of robust skills from limited expert demonstrations while mitigating overfitting.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes the **RGMP-S framework**, which integrates a **Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS)** to bridge the semantic-geometric gap through lightweight 2D geometric inductive biases, and a **Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet)** to enable data-efficient, robust policy learning by embedding spatiotemporal inductive biases and leveraging adaptive spiking dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **RGMP-S (Recurrent Geometric-prior Multimodal Policy with Spiking Features)**, is designed to facilitate generalizable robotic manipulation by integrating high-level skill reasoning with data-efficient motion synthesis. It processes egocentric RGB inputs and natural language commands to generate task plans and 6-DoF actions. RGMP-S comprises two principal components: the Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS) and the Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet).

#### 3.1. Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS)

The LGSS aims to translate natural language commands and visual observations into executable skill sequences by incorporating geometric commonsense priors, thereby bridging the semantic instruction and physical execution gap.

- **Mechanism:** The LGSS operates in a sequential manner. First, a Visual-Language Model (VLM), specifically the Qwen-vl API, interprets human instructions and identifies the target object within the visual input, generating bounding box coordinates. This process is enhanced by structured prompt engineering, which incorporates prior knowledge from a predefined contextual set (C) of examples linking instructions, observations, and action plans.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** Following object localization, a YOLOv8n-seg model processes the image region corresponding to the bounding box. This module extracts precise semantic segmentation masks to capture the object's geometric structure.
- **Geometry-Grounded CoT Skill Reasoner:** This module orchestrates the selection of appropriate manipulation strategies. It synthesizes the bounding box coordinates and morphological features (from segmentation) via the Qwen-vl reasoning engine. Instead of simple instruction following, a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) protocol is deployed with a constraint-aware prompt. This prompt embeds task-specific primitives (e.g., Side Grasp, Lift Up, Top Pinch, Pour Water, Delivery) and explicitly maps latent geometric affordances to manipulation strategies. The mechanism resolves operational ambiguities by integrating safety-critical constraints, prioritizing kinematically feasible and collision-free interactions based on environmental context.
- **Skill Library:** The system maintains a library of predefined grasping configurations, parameterized by finger joint angles, supporting core manipulation primitives. The LGSS selects an appropriate skill from this library based on its geometric reasoning, and the corresponding RASNet model parameters are then used for action trajectory generation.
- **Algorithmic Advancement (over preliminary version):** The LGSS includes a hierarchical CoT mechanism, which enables structured decomposition of complex goals into multi-stage sequences, particularly beneficial for long-horizon tasks like towel folding and water pouring.

#### 3.2. Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet)

The RASNet is designed to convert visual inputs into robotic manipulation actions through spatiotemporal memory modeling, enhancing robustness and mitigating overfitting when training data is scarce. This is achieved through three interconnected technical innovations.

- **Architecture Overview:** The RASNet comprises a Stem layer for initial feature extraction, three hierarchical processing stages (each with two RASNet blocks), and a multi-scale feature fusion module integrated with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for action generation.
 - **Stem Layer:** An input RGB image $O$ is initially processed by a 3x3 convolutional layer, batch normalization, Squared ReLU activation, and a max-pooling layer to generate an initial feature map $F_0$ with reduced resolution.
- **Dynamic Recursive Computation (Spatial Mixing Block):**
 - **Adaptive Decay Mechanism (ADM):** $F_0$ is processed by the ADM to generate content-adaptive decay factors ($W$). This involves convolutional layers and a sigmoid activation function to produce a channel-wise decay vector in the range, dynamically modulating historical memory retention.
 - **Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE):** RoPE are applied to the keys ($K_s$) generated from $F_0$. This method encodes positional information through geometric rotations in feature space, enhancing the model's capacity to capture position-sensitive dependencies without learnable position parameters. It inherently reflects relative token distances.
 - **Recurrent WKV Mechanism:** Sequential dependencies among 16x16 image patches are modeled using a recurrent mechanism that accumulates cumulative memory ($n_i$, $d_i$) of key-value products and keys, modulated by the adaptive decay factors $W$.
 - **Channel Mixing Block:** After a gating factor $R_s$ modulates the output of the Spatial Mixing Block, a Channel Mixing Block reallocates channel-wise feature weights.
- **Spiking Dense Feature Extraction (SDFE):**
 - **Dense Block:** In a parallel branch, $F_0$ is processed by a Dense Block to extract rich contextual and textural features, yielding an enriched representation $F_k$.
 - **Spatial Attention Mask:** $F_k$ is used to generate spatial attention features ($B_k$, $D_k$), and a spatial attention mask ($X_k$) is computed from $B_k$ to encode global dependencies between pixels.
 - **Adaptive Spike Neuron (ASN):** The core of the SDFE. Traditional spiking neurons have fixed decay factors and binary signaling, posing training challenges. ASN addresses this by dynamically adjusting the decay factor ($\tau$) at each timestep, computed through a 1x1 convolutional layer and averaging, and integrated into gradient backpropagation. ASN integrates spatial input ($X_t$) from dense visual features with temporal input ($H_{t-1}$) from the previous timestep. It fires a spike ($S_t$) if the membrane potential ($V_t$) exceeds a threshold, with $V_t$ decaying exponentially if no spike is fired. This mechanism selectively amplifies task-relevant features and suppresses noise.
- **Guided Self-attention:** A guided self-attention mechanism merges the dynamically recurrent features from the Spatial Mixing Block and the spiking-based dense features from the SDFE, creating a robust, combined feature representation.
- **Multi-scale Feature Fusion:** Feature maps from the three hierarchical stages ($F_1, F_2, F_3$) are fused using learnable weighting parameters ($\alpha_i$) and up-sampling, preserving both high-level semantics and detailed spatial structure.
- **Action Generation with Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM):**
 - The fused feature map ($F_f$) is passed through a linear layer to generate an initial action prediction ($a_{in}$).
 - To better represent the multi-modal nature of robotic actions and mitigate issues of single Gaussian regression to the mean, a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with six components is employed. GMM parameters (prior weight, mean, covariance) are estimated via the expectation-maximization algorithm from expert demonstrations.
 - The initial action prediction ($a_{in}$) is refined by calculating the Mahalanobis distance to each GMM component. The final action ($a^*$) corresponds to the mean vector of the component with the smallest distance, ensuring statistically consistent action generation, especially with limited data.
- **Architectural Evolution (over preliminary version):** This journal version introduces Spiking Dense Feature Extraction (SDFE) and Adaptive Spike Neurons (ASN) into the visuomotor module. These additions dynamically filter redundant visual noise in cluttered scenes, enhancing robustness and temporal logic for action synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments across the ManiSkill simulation benchmark and three heterogeneous real-world robotic systems (a custom humanoid, a desktop manipulator, and a commercial Aloha robot) to evaluate the RGMP-S framework. The evaluation focused on validating the framework's effectiveness, generalization capacity, and the specific contributions of its LGSS and RASNet modules.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Experiments utilized an upper-body humanoid robot (with egocentric RGB camera, omnidirectional microphone, 6-DOF arms, dexterous hands), a desktop dual-arm manipulator (with overhead RGB camera), and an Aloha robot (with central and wrist-mounted RGB cameras).
- **Dataset:** A dataset of 500 expert trajectories was curated. For specific tasks, training involved 40 expert trajectories per skill category (e.g., interactive bar service), 40 Fanta can trajectories for zero-shot grasping, and 100 trajectories for long-horizon tasks (towel folding, water pouring, bin picking).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance was quantified by skill selection accuracy (Acc_s), execution success rate (Acc_t), comprehensive success rate (Acc = Acc_s × Acc_t), and inference frequency (Hz).
- **Tasks:** Ten manipulation tasks were evaluated: five physical scenarios (Interactive Bar Service, Zero-shot Grasping, Towel Folding, Pouring Water, Bin Picking) and five ManiSkill2 simulation tasks (OpenCabinetDrawer, OpenCabinetDoor, PushChair, MoveBucket, PlugCharger).
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against ResNet, Transformer, Diffusion Policy (DP), ManiSkill2 1st, Octo, OpenVLA, RDT-1B, Dex-VLA, and the preliminary RGMP version.

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Human-Robot Interaction (Table 2):** The LGSS framework consistently improved skill selection accuracy (Acc_s) compared to the standard Qwen-vl baseline across various backbones (ResNet50, Transformer, ManiSkill2-1st, Diffusion Policy). For example, with the Diffusion Policy backbone, LGSS improved the overall accuracy on the "squashed Coke" task from 0.40 to 0.55, a 15% increase. This enhancement was attributed to LGSS's ability to accurately identify object orientations, which mitigated unstable grasps on deformed objects.
- **Generalized Performance (Zero-shot Grasping, Table 3):** RGMP-S exhibited superior zero-shot generalization capabilities. When trained on only 40 Fanta can trajectories, it achieved an average success rate of 0.89 across novel objects (Coke can, spray bottle, human hand) and randomized positions of the Fanta can. This outperformed the best baseline, Dex-VLA, by 12% (0.89 vs. 0.77). Notably, RGMP-S achieved a 0.93 success rate in the challenging human hand handover task. Furthermore, RGMP-S maintained a high inference frequency of 75.2 Hz, significantly surpassing computationally intensive models like OpenVLA (3.6 Hz) and Diffusion Policy (1.01 Hz), demonstrating its suitability for real-time control.
- **Long-horizon Manipulation (Figure 9, Figure 10 left):** In complex long-horizon tasks, RGMP-S consistently outperformed all other baselines. For instance, in the "Pouring Water" scenario, RGMP-S achieved approximately a 16% higher success rate than the Diffusion Policy. For "Towel Folding," it reached a success rate of 0.86 compared to 0.68 for Dex-VLA, demonstrating robustness with deformable objects. In "Bin Picking" under cluttered conditions, RGMP-S achieved the highest success rate of 0.83. The inclusion of Spiking Dense Feature Extraction (SDFE) in RGMP-S (compared to the preliminary RGMP version) was found to significantly enhance robustness and action execution across the temporal dimension for these tasks.
- **Simulation Benchmark (Table 6, Figure 8, Figure 11):** RGMP-S demonstrated superior robustness and performance across all five ManiSkill2 tasks, especially under stochastic physical constraints. In "Open Cabinet Door," RGMP-S achieved 26.48% success, surpassing ManiSkill2-1st (24.68%) and Diffusion Policy (15.32%). For the bimanual "PushChair" task, RGMP-S scored 16.47%, almost double that of ManiSkill2-1st (8.63%), indicating effective handling of stochastic dynamics and precise object positioning. Learning curves further illustrated RGMP-S's stable convergence and superior performance.

#### 4.3. Diagnostic Experiments (Ablation Studies)

- **Spiking Dense Feature Extraction (SDFE) (Figure 10 left):** Removing the SDFE module resulted in a notable performance degradation across long-horizon tasks (Fold, Pour, Bin-pick), with "Pour" task success dropping from 0.92 to approximately 0.65. This indicates SDFE's importance for capturing temporal dependencies and fine-grained spatial features in complex, multi-stage manipulations.
- **RASNet and GMM (Table 4):** The RASNet module demonstrated improved feature representation, outperforming the Diffusion Policy baseline even without the GMM. Integrating the GMM further enhanced performance, with the combination of RASNet and GMM yielding the highest accuracies (e.g., 0.60 for Tissue, 0.69 for Squashed Coke). This validates their synergy in improving policy robustness against object deformability and state uncertainty.
- **Component Contribution (RoPE, SMB, CMB, GSM) (Table 5):** An analysis of individual components within RGMP-S (Rotary Positional Embeddings, Spatial Mixing Block, Channel Mixing Block, Guided Self-attention Module) showed that each contributed to the overall performance. Removing any single component led to a noticeable performance drop, emphasizing their collective importance for robust grasping generalization. For example, omitting the Guided Self-attention Module reduced "Hand" object success from 0.93 to 0.73.

#### 4.4. Data Efficiency Analysis

RGMP-S exhibited superior data efficiency. It achieved a high success rate comparable to the Diffusion Policy (which required approximately 200 training trajectories) using only about 40 trajectories for the Fanta grasping task. This represents a 5x improvement in data efficiency, attributed to the inductive biases introduced by the RASNet, enabling effective learning from sparse data.

#### 4.5. Failure Case Analysis and Limitations

The study also identified boundary conditions where RGMP-S and other baselines struggled (Figure 12). These included scenarios demanding extreme perceptual precision, such as "Fold Towel" when the fabric was completely flush with the table (requiring millimeter-level Z-axis control), and "Plug Charger" tasks with insertion alignment tolerances stricter than 5 mm. In these cases, insufficient manipulation margin, precise Z-axis control limits, and self-occlusion of the gripper hindered fine-grained visual feedback. These failure modes suggest intrinsic complexities of physical interaction, challenging even human operators, and indicate future research directions such as integrating tactile feedback or active vision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, RGMP-S, that contributes to the field of humanoid robot manipulation by addressing two critical limitations: the semantic-geometric gap and data inefficiency.

The **Long-horizon Geometric-prior Skill Selector (LGSS)** offers a method for enhancing high-level task reasoning by integrating lightweight 2D geometric inductive biases into Vision-Language Models. This approach facilitates precise 3D scene understanding, enabling robust skill selection in diverse, unseen environments without relying on computationally expensive 3D reconstruction. This could significantly broaden the applicability of language-guided robotic systems in real-world, unstructured settings where object variations and semantic ambiguities are common.

The **Recursive Adaptive Spiking Network (RASNet)** provides an advancement in low-level action generation by embedding explicit spatiotemporal inductive biases. The inclusion of Adaptive Spike Neurons and a recurrent computational structure allows for efficient distillation of robust skills from limited demonstrations. This addresses the challenge of data scarcity in robotics, making advanced manipulation policies more practical to develop and deploy, as it reduces the reliance on vast datasets. Furthermore, the integration of Gaussian Mixture Models for action refinement improves control precision and robustness, particularly in sparse data regimes.

The empirical results, demonstrating superior performance against state-of-the-art baselines across a range of physical and simulated tasks, underscore the framework's effectiveness. The identified 5x improvement in data efficiency and high inference frequency suggest that RGMP-S can enable more responsive and adaptable robotic systems.

The significance of this work lies in its potential to advance the development of generalizable, data-efficient, and robust humanoid robots capable of performing complex, human-level tasks. By providing a unified framework that bridges the gap between semantic understanding and geometric execution, and by enabling effective learning from limited data, RGMP-S paves the way for future research in embodied AI, particularly in areas requiring fine-grained interaction and adaptation to dynamic environments. The public availability of the source code also fosters reproducibility and further scientific inquiry in this domain.
