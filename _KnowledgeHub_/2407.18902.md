# Lessons from Learning to Spin "Pens"

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC San Diego, Carnegie Mellon University, and UC Berkeley demonstrated continuous pen spinning by a robotic hand using a learning-based system. Their approach trains an oracle policy in simulation and then fine-tunes a proprioceptive policy using limited real-world data obtained from replaying oracle trajectories, achieving robust generalization to various unseen pen-like objects with improved success rates compared to pure open-loop replay.

## Problem

- Enabling a robotic hand to perform continuous, dynamic dexterous in-hand manipulation, specifically the challenging task of pen spinning.
- Overcoming the limitations of existing learning paradigms, such as insufficient high-quality real-world demonstrations via teleoperation and a significant sim-to-real gap for direct policy transfer in dynamic contact tasks.
- Achieving generalization of learned manipulation skills to diverse pen-like objects with varying physical properties (mass, length, diameter, friction) using only proprioceptive feedback.

## Method

- Training an 'oracle policy' in randomized simulation using ==reinforcement learning== (PPO) with privileged information to synthesize high-fidelity, dynamic manipulation trajectories.
- Pre-training a proprioceptive sensorimotor policy in simulation using a large dataset of joint positions and actions collected from rollouts of the trained oracle policy.
- Fine-tuning the pre-trained sensorimotor policy with a small, high-quality real-world dataset, which is collected by replaying successful oracle trajectories on the physical robot ('oracle replay') and manually selecting effective instances.

## Results

- The proposed hybrid method enabled continuous pen spinning, outperforming a pure open-loop oracle replay baseline by 10-**30%** in success rate and radians rotated on unseen objects.
- Policies trained with only simulation pre-training or only real-world behavior cloning performed poorly, demonstrating that both simulation pre-training and real-world fine-tuning are critical for success.
- The method successfully generalizes to various unseen pen-like objects with different physical properties, rotating them for multiple revolutions with smooth finger gaiting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed initial state distribution and a 'z-reward' encouraging horizontal object orientation are crucial for training stable oracle policies for dynamic manipulation in simulation.
> - For highly dynamic and contact-rich tasks like continuous pen spinning, direct sim-to-real policy transfer or distillation is largely ineffective due to the substantial sim-to-real gap.
> - Combining diverse motion priors from large-scale simulation pre-training with efficient real-world fine-tuning using limited, high-quality data is essential for robust generalization and cannot be simply replaced by more real-world data.
