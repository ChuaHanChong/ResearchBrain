# Evaluating and Improving Cultural Awareness of Reward Models for LLM Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology and Peng Cheng Laboratory developed CARB, a new benchmark for evaluating cultural awareness in reward models (RMs) for large language model (LLM) alignment, and introduced "Think-as-Locals," a method enhancing generative RMs by eliciting culturally grounded reasoning. The proposed method improved performance by over 10% compared to base models and significantly reduced reliance on superficial features.

## Problem

- Existing reward model evaluation benchmarks largely overlook multilingual cultural awareness, focusing instead on general capabilities.
- A scarcity of culturally relevant data hinders the assessment of how well RMs can align LLMs with diverse global cultural preferences.
- Reward models may exhibit spurious correlations, relying on superficial linguistic features rather than genuine cultural understanding, potentially leading to reward hacking.

## Method

- A new benchmark, ==Cultural Awareness== Reward modeling Benchmark (**CARB**), was constructed, encompassing 10 cultures, diverse languages, and four cultural domains, structured in a Best-of-N paradigm.
- A method called "Think-as-Locals" was proposed to enhance generative RMs by prompting them to produce explicit, culturally grounded evaluation criteria before making judgments, using a novel reward function for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).
- Extensive evaluation of 70 state-of-the-art RMs was conducted on CARB, alongside a robustness analysis to identify sensitivity to causal versus spurious cultural features.

## Results

- CARB comprises 8,576 query, winner, and loser triplets across diverse cultures and domains, with generative RMs like Qwen3-**235B**-A22B-Instruct-2507 achieving top performance.
- The Think-as-Locals method achieved over **10%** performance improvement compared to base models, surpassed most state-of-the-art classifier-based RMs, and showed increased sensitivity to causal features while mitigating spurious correlations.
- CARB accuracy demonstrated a strong, statistically significant positive correlation (r² > **0.6**, p < **0.001**) with downstream multilingual cultural alignment performance, outperforming M-RewardBench which showed weak correlation (r² < **0.1**, p > **0.05**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative RMs generally outperform classifier-based RMs in culturally aware, multilingual reward modeling, particularly in nuanced cultural contexts.
> - Many RMs are susceptible to spurious correlations, relying on superficial features rather than authentic cultural understanding, which poses a risk for reliable LLM alignment.
> - The CARB benchmark exhibits strong positive correlation with downstream multilingual cultural alignment tasks, making it an effective predictor of an RM's real-world cultural alignment performance.
