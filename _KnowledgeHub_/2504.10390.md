# Teacher Motion Priors: Enhancing Robot Locomotion over Challenging Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> The Teacher Motion Priors (TMP) framework enhances humanoid robot locomotion on challenging terrains by integrating generative adversarial learning and auxiliary tasks within a teacher-student reinforcement learning paradigm. This approach leads to superior learning convergence, improved velocity tracking accuracy (e.g., 44.16% error reduction on discrete obstacles), and lower Cost of Transport on a physical CASBOT SE robot.

## Problem

- Performance degradation in teacher-student paradigms due to distributional shift between teacher's privileged information and student's limited proprioceptive observations.
- Existing teacher-student frameworks often rely on complex, tightly coupled network designs, hindering simplified policy deployment and generalization.
- Training robust locomotion policies for high-dimensional humanoid robots is computationally intensive and struggles with generalization to novel, dynamic terrains.

## Method

- A two-stage training process where a teacher policy, trained with privileged information, guides a student policy that only uses proprioceptive inputs.
- Generative adversarial knowledge transfer utilizing a discriminator to ensure the student's actions closely mimic the teacher's behavior distribution, decoupling network architectures.
- Auxiliary task learning, where an auxiliary network shares layers with the student actor to predict partial state information, enhancing feature representation and accelerating convergence.

## Results

- The TMP framework achieved **26.39%** faster convergence and attained higher average terrain levels compared to the Original Teacher-Student framework in simulation.
- TMP consistently reduced linear velocity tracking errors, with up to **44.16%** improvement on discrete obstacles, and demonstrated the lowest Cost of Transport across all tested terrain types.
- Real-world experiments on the CASBOT SE humanoid robot validated the robustness and adaptability of the learned policy across slopes, rough terrain, and under external disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling teacher and student network designs through adversarial training allows for simpler student policies without sacrificing performance.
> - Generative adversarial imitation effectively bridges the distributional gap between privileged and proprioceptive observation spaces, improving knowledge transfer efficiency.
> - Auxiliary task learning can significantly improve feature extraction from noisy proprioceptive inputs, leading to faster learning and better generalization in complex robot control.
