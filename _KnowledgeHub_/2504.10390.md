# Teacher Motion Priors: Enhancing Robot Locomotion over Challenging Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> The Teacher Motion Priors (TMP) framework enhances humanoid robot locomotion on challenging terrains by integrating generative adversarial learning and auxiliary tasks within a teacher-student reinforcement learning paradigm. This approach leads to superior learning convergence, improved velocity tracking accuracy (e.g., 44.16% error reduction on discrete obstacles), and lower Cost of Transport on a physical CASBOT SE robot.

## Problem

- Performance degradation in teacher-student paradigms due to distributional shift between teacher's privileged information and student's limited proprioceptive observations.
- Existing teacher-student frameworks often rely on complex, tightly coupled network designs, hindering simplified policy deployment and generalization.
- Training robust locomotion policies for high-dimensional humanoid robots is computationally intensive and struggles with generalization to novel, dynamic terrains.

## Method

- A two-stage training process where a teacher policy, trained with privileged information, guides a student policy that only uses proprioceptive inputs.
- Generative adversarial knowledge transfer utilizing a discriminator to ensure the student's actions closely mimic the teacher's behavior distribution, decoupling network architectures.
- Auxiliary task learning, where an auxiliary network shares layers with the student actor to predict partial state information, enhancing feature representation and accelerating convergence.

## Results

- The TMP framework achieved **26.39%** faster convergence and attained higher average terrain levels compared to the Original Teacher-Student framework in simulation.
- TMP consistently reduced linear velocity tracking errors, with up to **44.16%** improvement on discrete obstacles, and demonstrated the lowest Cost of Transport across all tested terrain types.
- Real-world experiments on the CASBOT SE humanoid robot validated the robustness and adaptability of the learned policy across slopes, rough terrain, and under external disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling teacher and student network designs through adversarial training allows for simpler student policies without sacrificing performance.
> - Generative adversarial imitation effectively bridges the distributional gap between privileged and proprioceptive observation spaces, improving knowledge transfer efficiency.
> - Auxiliary task learning can significantly improve feature extraction from noisy proprioceptive inputs, leading to faster learning and better generalization in complex robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Achieving robust and adaptive locomotion for robotic systems, particularly humanoid robots, over complex and uncertain terrains remains a fundamental challenge in robotics. This challenge is attributed to the high-dimensional control requirements and the inherent uncertainties of real-world environments.

Historically, model-based control methods have enabled basic locomotion on challenging terrains and were adapted for humanoid robots. However, these methods typically exhibit limitations in adaptability when faced with novel or dynamic real-world conditions. Recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) have shown potential in addressing complex control problems, including robot locomotion. Despite this, applying RL to humanoid robots is complicated by their high degrees of freedom (DoF) and the need for policies that are robust across various dynamic terrains.

The teacher-student paradigm has emerged as a strategy to tackle these complexities. In this approach, a high-performance teacher policy is trained using privileged information (e.g., precise environment state, internal robot parameters often unavailable in reality), and its knowledge is then transferred to a student policy that relies solely on more realistic, proprioceptive inputs (e.g., joint positions, velocities, IMU data). This paradigm facilitates efficient sim-to-real transfer, but it faces several persistent issues. These include distributional shifts between the teacher's and student's observation spaces and action distributions, as well as dependencies on complex network structures that can hinder generalization. Previous attempts, such as Regularized Online Adaptation (ROA) and Collaborative Training of Teacher-Student Policies (CTS), have sought to improve generalization but have not fully resolved these challenges, particularly regarding distributional shifts and network complexity.

Other approaches like Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) and Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) leverage adversarial training to mitigate distributional shift and decouple the student policy from the teacher's network architecture, enabling more stylized motion generation. Additionally, Multi-Task Learning (MTL) has been integrated into RL to enhance feature representations, accelerate training, and improve generalization.

This work, Teacher Motion Priors (TMP), situates itself within this landscape by proposing a teacher-student framework that specifically addresses the identified limitations of distributional shift, network dependency, and limited generalization. It synthesizes elements of generative adversarial learning and auxiliary task learning within the teacher-student paradigm to develop a more efficient and robust locomotion strategy for humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust locomotion strategy for humanoid robots that can effectively navigate complex and dynamic terrains. The authors identify several specific challenges in current teacher-student reinforcement learning approaches for humanoid locomotion and motivate their proposed framework by aiming to resolve them:

1. **Mitigate Performance Degradation due to Distributional Shift:** Traditional teacher-student paradigms often suffer when transferring knowledge from a teacher policy, which uses privileged information (e.g., ground truth terrain height maps, precise contact detection), to a student policy that operates only with noisy, partial proprioceptive observations. This difference in input information can lead to a significant drop in student performance. The motivation is to bridge this gap effectively so that the student policy can mimic the teacher's high-performance behavior even with limited sensing.

2. **Decouple Network Design and Simplify Policy Deployment:** Many existing teacher-student frameworks rely on encoder-based state embeddings or tightly coupled network structures between the teacher and student. This can lead to increased network complexity, make it difficult to adapt the policy for different robot platforms or sensing modalities, and complicate deployment. The authors are motivated to create a framework that decouples the network design, allowing for a simpler student policy network that is easier to deploy and integrate into existing systems without extensive restructuring.

3. **Enhance Learning Efficiency and Generalization:** Training robust locomotion policies for high-dimensional humanoid robots is computationally intensive and often struggles with generalization to new, unseen terrains or disturbances. The motivation is to accelerate the learning process and improve the student policy's ability to adapt to varying terrains and environmental uncertainties. This includes improving feature representation within the student policy to make it more adaptable.

4. **Provide a Practical Solution for Real-World Deployment:** Ultimately, the goal is to develop a control strategy that can be reliably deployed on a physical humanoid robot. The authors aim to validate their framework on a full-scale humanoid robot to demonstrate significant improvements in locomotion stability and robustness on dynamic terrains, thereby offering a practical and deployable solution.

To achieve these objectives, the paper introduces the Teacher Motion Priors (TMP) framework, which integrates a generative adversarial mechanism for robust knowledge transfer and auxiliary task learning for improved feature representation and training efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Teacher Motion Priors (TMP) framework is a two-stage training process involving a teacher policy and a student policy, utilizing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an actor-critic architecture. The problem is formulated as a partially observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).

**A. Teacher Policy Training**
The teacher policy (π_t) is trained first to acquire robust and generalizable locomotion skills.
- **Observations:** The teacher actor receives N frames of proprioceptive data (o_t) and M frames of privileged information (o_p_t). The critic receives M frames of full state data (s_t = [o_t, o_p_t]), which is noise-free. Gaussian noise is added to the proprioceptive input during training to enhance generalization.
- **Architecture:** An actor-critic network is employed. The actor generates actions (a_t), controlling joint positions via a PD controller.
- **Loss Function:** The teacher optimizes a loss function combining:
 - **Clipped Surrogate Loss (L_clip):** A PPO-specific term for stable policy updates.
 - **Value Function Loss (L_v):** Mean squared error between the critic's predicted value and the target return, computed using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
 - **Entropy Loss (L_e):** Encourages exploration by promoting diverse action distributions.

**B. Student Policy Training**
The student policy (π_s) is designed to learn from the teacher while relying only on proprioceptive data for its actor.
- **Actor Input:** The student actor receives only N frames of proprioceptive data (o_t).
- **Critic Input:** Similar to the teacher's critic, it receives state data.
- **Generative Adversarial Knowledge Transfer:** Instead of direct supervised imitation, a generative adversarial approach (inspired by GAIL) is used to transfer the teacher's behavior distribution.
 - **Discriminator (D):** A discriminator network is trained to distinguish between trajectories generated by the teacher and those by the student. It receives a tuple of (state_frames, action) and outputs a probability p_D indicating the likelihood of the action originating from the teacher.
 - **Discriminator Loss (L_disc):** Includes:
 - **Prediction Loss (L_pred):** Binary cross-entropy to classify teacher vs. student trajectories.
 - **Gradient Regularization (L_grad):** Penalizes large gradients to prevent overfitting.
 - **Weight Regularization (L_weight):** An L2 penalty on discriminator weights for improved generalization.
- **Auxiliary Task Learning:** To enhance feature representation and accelerate convergence, an auxiliary network (aux) is incorporated.
 - **Shared Layers:** The auxiliary network shares its initial layers with the student actor network.
 - **Auxiliary Prediction:** It predicts auxiliary observations (ˆo_aux_t) based on the shared features.
 - **Auxiliary Loss (L_aux):** Mean squared error between predicted and ground truth auxiliary observations. This task guides the student in learning noise distributions in proprioceptive inputs and improves feature extraction.
- **Student Loss Function:** The student policy optimizes a combined loss function:
 - L_clip, L_v, L_e (similar to the teacher's but for the student's parameters).
 - L_aux (from the auxiliary task).
 - L_disc (from the adversarial training, where the student tries to "fool" the discriminator).
- **Deployment:** During real-world deployment, only the student actor policy (π_s) is utilized, without the auxiliary network or the critic.

**C. Training Configuration**
- **Robot Model:** The framework is applied to a humanoid robot, modeling base and foot poses with a 6D vector. A gait cycle involves double and single support phases.
- **Observation Space:**
 - **Proprioceptive (o_t, 47 dims):** Includes sensory data (joint positions/velocities, base angular/linear velocities, Euler angles), a phase clock signal, and command inputs (linear/angular velocities).
 - **Privileged (o_p_t, 213 dims):** Not available at deployment, comprises a height map (187 dims), feet contact detection, disturbance forces/torques, gait phase, body weight, and friction coefficient.
 - **Auxiliary (o_aux_t, 48 dims):** Partial state data used during student training.
- **Network Inputs (Frames):** Teacher actor uses 3 frames of privileged and 15 frames of proprioceptive data. Student actor uses 15 frames of proprioceptive data. Discriminator uses 10 frames of state data and 12 action dimensions.
- **Curriculum Learning:** A game-inspired curriculum is used across four terrain types (slopes, rough, stairs, discrete obstacles) with increasing difficulty levels (0 to 20). Each level contains a balanced set of 20 terrain instances.
- **Domain Randomization:** To address the sim-to-real gap, various parameters are randomized during training, including ground friction, restitution, push intervals/velocities, base mass, COM displacement, stiffness, damping, torque multipliers, link mass, motor offsets, joint friction, damping, armature, and observation lags.

**D. Reward Design**
A unified reward system is designed to promote stable, energy-efficient locomotion that adheres to gait patterns and velocity commands. Key components include:
- **Tracking Rewards:** Penalizes errors in achieving commanded linear and angular velocities and maintaining base orientation/height.
- **Periodic Gait Reward:** Penalizes deviations from desired foot contact patterns.
- **Foot Trajectory Reward:** Ensures proper foot clearance during the swing phase.
- **Regularization Terms:** Penalize undesirable behaviors like large joint torques, accelerations, excessive foot contact forces, and collisions.
- **Discriminator Reward (r_disc):** Introduced during student training, derived from the discriminator's output, encouraging the student to produce actions that are indistinguishable from the teacher's.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the TMP framework was evaluated through simulations and real-world experiments using the CASBOT SE humanoid robot. Comparisons were made against several baseline methods: Oracle (PPO with full state input), Baseline (PPO with proprioceptive actor and privileged critic), Original Teacher-Student (TS) framework, and Regularized Online Adaptation (ROA). All methods were trained with consistent configurations where applicable.

**A. Terrain Level Convergence**
- **Performance:** The student policy trained with TMP, incorporating auxiliary task learning, demonstrated a terrain level convergence curve that closely tracked the high-performance Oracle (teacher) policy.
- **Efficiency:** TMP converged faster and achieved a higher final average terrain level than other comparative methods. Specifically, TMP showed a 26.39% improvement over the Original Teacher-Student (TS) framework and a 17.20% improvement over ROA in final learning performance.
- **Impact of Auxiliary Task:** The student policy trained without auxiliary task learning exhibited a slower convergence rate, indicating the contribution of the auxiliary task to learning efficiency.
- **ROA Performance:** ROA showed a slight performance improvement over the baseline after 5000 iterations, though its terrain level metric was noted as less effective in capturing traversability due to its policy switching mechanism.

**B. Tracking Accuracy**
- **Superiority:** TMP consistently outperformed TS and ROA in linear velocity tracking accuracy across all four tested terrain types: discrete obstacles, rough slopes, slopes, and stairs.
- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - On discrete obstacles, TMP reduced tracking errors by 44.16% compared to TS and 30.25% compared to ROA.
 - On rough slopes, error reductions were 40.53% against TS and 28.16% against ROA.
 - On slopes, error reductions were 39.17% against TS and 23.71% against ROA.
 - On stairs, error reductions were 27.74% against TS and 26.66% against ROA.
- **ROA vs. Baseline:** While ROA had comparable terrain level performance to the baseline, it exhibited higher tracking accuracy.

**C. Cost of Transport (CoT)**
- **Energy Efficiency:** The student policy trained with TMP exhibited the lowest Cost of Transport (CoT), indicating greater energy efficiency, across all four terrain types compared to TS and ROA.
- **Quantitative Reductions:**
 - On discrete obstacles, TMP achieved CoT reductions of 26.67% compared to TS and 2.384% compared to ROA.
 - On rough slopes, CoT reductions were 16.89% against TS and 2.205% against ROA.
 - On slopes, CoT reductions were 5.870% against TS and 14.35% against ROA.
 - On stairs, CoT reductions were 13.65% against TS and 6.604% against ROA.
- **Explanation:** The higher CoT for TS and ROA was attributed to their reliance on supervised learning, which may limit exploration capabilities, whereas TMP allows the student to dynamically learn the teacher's strategy within the simulated environment.

**D. Real-World Experiments**
- **Robot Platform:** Experiments were conducted on the CASBOT SE humanoid robot (1.65m tall, 46kg, 12 controlled DoFs).
- **Scenarios:**
 - **Sloped Surface Traversal:** The robot successfully navigated sloped terrains by dynamically adjusting joint configurations, particularly the ankle pitch joints, to maintain balance. Analysis of joint torques showed stable hip and knee torques, with significant variations in the ankle pitch, indicating effective adaptation. This contributed to stable locomotion, lower CoT, and enhanced terrain adaptability.
 - **Rough Brick-Paved Terrain:** The robot demonstrated stability on uneven surfaces by employing adaptive foot placement and modulating joint stiffness to compensate for irregularities and maintain upper body stability despite unpredictable contact forces.
 - **External Disturbances:** When subjected to sudden pushes, the robot rapidly adjusted its stepping strategy and redistributed its center of mass to regain balance, leveraging proprioceptive feedback for swift recovery and stability maintenance.
- **Validation:** These real-world tests corroborated the effectiveness and robustness of the TMP control strategy in managing complex terrains and resisting external disturbances, thereby demonstrating the generalizability of the learned policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work resides in its methodological contributions and the practical implications for robotic locomotion.

**Methodological Advancements:**
- **Novel Framework Design:** The TMP framework introduces a departure from certain traditional teacher-student paradigms by explicitly decoupling the network design, particularly by eliminating the need for complex encoder structures that might tightly couple teacher and student networks.
- **Generative Adversarial Knowledge Transfer:** The integration of a generative adversarial mechanism for knowledge transfer directly addresses the problem of distributional shift, enabling the student policy to effectively mimic the teacher's behavior distribution even when operating with only proprioceptive information.
- **Auxiliary Task Learning for Feature Representation:** The use of auxiliary task learning enhances the student policy's ability to learn robust feature representations from noisy proprioceptive inputs, contributing to faster convergence and improved generalization capabilities.

**Practical Impact and Implications:**
- **Reduced Development Costs and Effort:** The framework is designed to allow developers to train a high-performance teacher policy and transfer its knowledge to existing student network structures without requiring extensive redesign or restructuring of the student policy. This potentially simplifies the development pipeline for deploying robust locomotion strategies.
- **Enhanced Robot Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate superior learning performance, enhanced tracking accuracy, and reduced Cost of Transport (CoT) compared to other mainstream teacher-student methods. This indicates that robots equipped with TMP-trained policies can achieve more stable, efficient, and adaptable locomotion on complex terrains.
- **Robust Real-World Deployment:** The successful validation on a full-scale humanoid robot, CASBOT SE, across various challenging scenarios (slopes, rough terrain, external disturbances), highlights the framework's capability for robust sim-to-real transfer and reliable operation in real-world environments.
- **Future Extensibility:** The decoupled network structure supports the future integration of exteroception modules, such as vision systems, without necessitating retraining of the base teacher policy. This suggests a modular and extensible architecture that can accommodate future sensing capabilities and task requirements.

In summary, this work provides a practical and effective solution for developing and deploying robust locomotion strategies for humanoid robots, addressing critical challenges in learning efficiency, generalization, and sim-to-real transfer. Its contributions may facilitate the broader adoption of advanced locomotion capabilities in humanoid robotics.
