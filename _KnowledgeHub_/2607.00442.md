# Learning Gait-Aware Quadruped Locomotion with Temporal Logic Specifications

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Southern California developed a reinforcement learning framework where Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications shape rewards, enabling a quadruped robot to learn a single policy that autonomously adapts its gait (walking-trot, trot, bound) to commanded speeds. This approach achieved superior command tracking, higher success rates, and better energy efficiency at high speeds in simulation compared to heuristic baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) reward functions for quadruped locomotion often lack interpretability and explicit control over specific gait characteristics.
- Learning a single policy that smoothly transitions between distinct gait patterns (e.g., walk, trot, bound) across varying speed regimes remains challenging, often leading policies to converge to a single dominant gait.
- Existing methods to induce specific gaits typically rely on external data like motion-capture or hand-designed schedules, providing only indirect control over temporal gait structures and coordination.

## Method

- Integrated ==Signal Temporal Logic (STL)== specifications into a reinforcement learning framework, utilizing mode-dependent ==PSTL templates== to define distinct gaits and safety constraints.
- Developed a data-driven pipeline to learn parameters (thresholds) for the PSTL gait templates from expert locomotion trajectories using a ==quantile-based approach==.
- Trained a single ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== policy to generate walking-trot, trot, and bound gaits by dynamically activating appropriate STL specifications based on commanded speed regimes, incorporating ==hysteresis== for smooth transitions.

## Results

- The STL-based policy achieved perfect survival (**1.0**) and success (**1.0**) rates across the full range of commanded forward velocities (**0.3 to 2.1 m/s**) in straight-line locomotion, outperforming heuristic baselines, particularly at high speeds.
- Demonstrated lower Cost of Transportation (CoT) at speeds greater than or equal to **1.3 m/s**, indicating improved energy efficiency by learning to transition to a bounding gait at higher velocities.
- Maintained superior performance under mixed velocity commands (lateral and yaw), exhibiting higher success rates and competitive CoT compared to heuristic methods, especially at the extremes of the speed range.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formal, interpretable Signal Temporal Logic specifications can effectively guide reinforcement learning to produce complex, gait-aware locomotion behaviors, offering a structured alternative to heuristic reward shaping.
> - Deriving mode-dependent parameters for STL templates directly from expert locomotion data allows for the creation of interpretable and effective gait-specific constraints that align with biomechanical principles.
> - The quantitative robustness semantics of STL provide valuable diagnostic signals, enabling precise attribution of policy failures to specific violated specifications for easier debugging and refinement of complex behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion has advanced significantly, with platforms demonstrating agile movements in complex environments. However, achieving animal-level robustness across diverse speed regimes and gait patterns remains an ongoing challenge. Traditional model-based control methods, such as Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP) and Model Predictive Control (MPC), offer high performance but are reliant on precise system models and carefully tuned cost functions, which can be difficult to implement on physical hardware.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a complementary approach, allowing for direct optimization of closed-loop policies in simulation, supported by advancements in physics engines like MuJoCo and specialized robot platforms. A critical aspect of quadrupedal locomotion is the ability to adapt gait patterns—such as walking, trotting, and bounding—to various speeds, each requiring distinct contact timing, duty factors, and support structures. Current deep RL pipelines primarily focus on command tracking and partially address safety through engineered reward terms and domain randomization. However, these reward functions often lack interpretability and explicit control over specific gait structures and transitions, sometimes leading to policies that favor a single dominant gait. Existing methods to induce distinct gaits frequently involve imitation learning from motion-capture data or hand-designed gait schedules.

Formal specification methods, particularly Signal Temporal Logic (STL), offer an alternative by encoding desired behaviors as logical constraints rather than loosely coupled rewards. STL is used in robotics to express bounded-time constraints over real-valued signals and provides quantitative robustness semantics, indicating the degree of satisfaction or violation of a property. Prior research has demonstrated the utility of STL robustness as a reward signal to accelerate RL and enhance interpretability, particularly in navigation and manipulation tasks. However, its application to complex, multi-gait quadrupedal locomotion, which involves highly dynamic and temporally structured behaviors, has been less explored, with existing logic-driven approaches for quadrupeds often relying on discrete logic rules or contact-level propositions.

This research distinguishes itself by proposing a structured, analyzable, and mode-aware specification-driven framework. It positions itself as an alternative to both black-box latent-representation methods and purely heuristic reward shaping. The work aims to combine the interpretability of formal specification design with the scalability of deep RL to address the challenge of learning a single policy that can adapt its gait structure across multiple speed regimes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables quadruped robots to learn gait-aware locomotion policies using Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for quadrupedal control:

- **Limited Interpretability and Control with Heuristic Rewards:** Conventional RL for quadruped locomotion frequently relies on fixed, hand-crafted, and Markovian reward functions. These functions often lack clear interpretability, making it difficult to understand why a policy behaves in a certain way or to explicitly control specific gait characteristics. This limitation is particularly pronounced in scenarios requiring distinct gait behaviors across varying speed regimes.
- **Challenges in Multi-Gait Locomotion:** Quadrupedal robots need to switch between different gaits (e.g., walking-trot, trot, bound) to operate efficiently across a wide range of speeds. Standard static reward functions, while potentially sufficient for a single gait, often fail to induce distinct gait regimes or smooth transitions between them, sometimes causing the learned policy to converge to a single dominant gait.
- **Indirect Control over Gait Structure:** Current reward engineering methods provide only indirect control over crucial gait characteristics such as footfall patterns, duty factors, and inter-limb coordination. Achieving precise gait-specific objectives often requires external data like motion-capture or hand-designed schedules, adding complexity.
- **Need for Temporal Reasoning:** Many requirements for stable and efficient locomotion, including footfall sequences, support phases, and stability criteria, are inherently temporal. They describe behaviors over segments of a trajectory rather than instantaneous states. Traditional Markovian rewards struggle to effectively capture these temporal properties.
- **Reward Discontinuities and Learning Instability:** Directly switching between different gait-specific objectives based on speed thresholds can introduce discontinuities in the reward landscape, potentially destabilizing the learning process. A mechanism to smooth these transitions is needed.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop Mode-Aware Logic-Based Rewards:** Introduce a framework where distinct gaits are specified using parameterized constraints expressed in Signal Temporal Logic (STL). These specifications encompass safety bounds, gait synchronization, command tracking, and actuation limits, with different specifications becoming active based on the commanded speed regime.
2. **Establish a Data-Driven Parameter Learning Pipeline:** Design a pipeline to fit the parameters of the parametric STL gait templates from expert locomotion trajectories. This approach aims to derive interpretable specifications that align with observed gait statistics from successful demonstrations.
3. **Learn a Unified Single Policy for Multi-Gait Locomotion:** Train a single RL policy capable of generating diverse gait patterns—walking-trot, trot, and bound—according to desired motion objectives and commanded speed, while achieving reduced tracking errors compared to heuristic baselines.
4. **Provide Interpretable Diagnostics for Failure Analysis:** Leverage the quantitative robustness semantics of STL to offer diagnostic signals. This enables attribution of policy errors to specific violated specifications, facilitating debugging and development of complex locomotion behaviors.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide an interpretable, scalable, and robust alternative for learning complex, gait-aware quadrupedal locomotion policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented involves integrating Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications into a reinforcement learning (RL) framework for quadrupedal locomotion. The core idea is to shape the reward function using quantitative robustness values derived from mode-dependent STL templates, thereby providing a structured and interpretable learning signal.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting and Mode-Dependent Gait Selection

The work considers Google's Barkour vb quadruped robot, controlled by a single policy that takes robot observations (proprioception, commands, previous actions, and a mode flag) and outputs joint positions for a PD controller. Recognizing that a single gait is inefficient across varied speeds, the robot transitions between three distinct speed regimes (modes): walking-trot (W), trot (T), and bound (B). These modes are determined by the commanded forward velocity, with transition thresholds (0.7m/s and 1.7m/s) derived from the Froude number, a biomechanical scaling law. Hysteresis is incorporated into the mode selection logic to prevent chattering near transition boundaries, ensuring smoother reward transitions.

#### 3.2. Signal Temporal Logic (STL) and Quantitative Robustness

STL is utilized to formally specify temporal properties over real-valued signals. Formulas are constructed from atomic predicates, Boolean connectives, and time-bounded temporal operators (e.g., "always" $G_{[a,b]}$ and "eventually" $F_{[a,b]}$). The framework employs quantitative semantics, where robustness values $\rho_\phi(t)$ indicate the degree of satisfaction (positive value) or violation (negative value) of a specification $\phi$ at time $t$. Parametric STL (PSTL) is used, allowing constants and interval bounds in STL formulas to be represented by parameters, which are learned from data.

#### 3.3. Data Collection and Feature Extraction

To parameterize the STL templates, trajectory datasets are collected from three specialized, expert models, each trained for a specific speed regime (low, mid-high, high speed). A compact dataset of 50 expert trajectories per regime is deemed sufficient. Each trajectory consists of 500 simulation steps (0.02s timestep), with an initial 100-step (2s) warm-up period discarded to remove transients.
Key features extracted from these trajectories include:
- **Tracking features:** Errors in linear velocity and yaw rate.
- **Safety/Stability features:** Center of Mass (CoM) position and velocity, Center of Pressure (CoP) proxy, base roll/pitch, CoP-CoM distance, and slip proxy.
- **Contact-pattern features:** Foot contacts for each leg, and synchronization errors for diagonal or front/hind leg pairs.
- **Stride-level features:** Estimated stride period, duty factor, and diagonal phase error.
These features are used to inform the design and parameterization of the STL templates.

#### 3.4. Data to STL Specifications

A family of PSTL templates is defined, with each mode $g \in \{W, T, B\}$ having three types of specifications:
1. **Shared Safety Template ($\phi_{safe,g}$):** Includes constraints on actuator torque limits, robot base roll and pitch, CoM height, and minimum support contacts. Thresholds are mode-dependent.
2. **Commanded Velocity Tracking Template ($\phi_{track,g}$):** Specifies bounds on errors in linear and angular base velocities, also with mode-dependent thresholds.
3. **Gait-Structure Template ($\phi_g$):** Captures regime-specific contact patterns and coordination, detailed for each mode.
The parameters (thresholds) for these templates are learned from the collected expert datasets using a quantile-based approach. For instance, thresholds for error magnitudes are set based on high quantiles (e.g., 99th percentile) to allow for minor transient deviations without penalizing them excessively.

#### 3.5. Mode-Dependent STL Specifications for Multi-Gait Locomotion

The reward is derived from the quantitative robustness of the currently active specification over a finite horizon $W_g = [t-H_g, t]$, chosen to cover approximately one gait cycle.
- **Walk-Trot Gait ($\phi_W$):** Emphasizes support-rich diagonal locomotion without flight. Specific predicates include diagonal phase error (G1), diagonal pair fraction (G2), and multi-support moment (G3, requiring at least three feet in contact recently).
- **Trot Gait ($\phi_T$):** Shares G1 and G2 but also includes a two-leg support predicate (G4), reflecting the dominant diagonal two-contact support characteristic of trotting.
- **Bound Gait ($\phi_B$):** Designed for high-speed, pair-synchronized running with aerial phases. Predicates include minimum aerial-phase occupancy (B1), minimum front-only and hind-only support fractions (B2, B3), constraints on all four contact fraction (B4), suppression of trot-like diagonal support (B5), and requirements for front-pair and hind-pair touchdown events (B6).

#### 3.6. Robustness Reward and Reinforcement Learning

Multiple robustness terms are aggregated using a sign-preserving soft-minimum function (smin_sp_β), and then normalized using tanh(ρ) to scale values to [−1, 1] and prevent any single large robustness value from dominating the reward. An additional torque-effort penalty $J_\tau(t)$ is included for energy efficiency. The final scalar reward $r_t$ is a weighted sum of the normalized safety, tracking, and pattern robustness terms, minus the torque penalty.
The policy is trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, leveraging MuJoCo XLA (MJX) for highly parallelized GPU-based simulation. Training incorporates domain randomization (varying friction, actuator gain/bias) to enhance policy robustness. A curriculum learning strategy expands the forward velocity command range during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the STL-based reward framework by comparing its performance against two hand-engineered reward baselines: "heuristic-best" (optimized parameters and velocity ranges) and "heuristic-default" (same training structure but applied to the experimental setup). Evaluation is conducted on Google’s Barkour vb quadruped robot in MuJoCo XLA (MJX), across various forward velocity commands (0.3 to 2.1 m/s), with zero lateral and yaw commands, and also under mixed velocity commands. Metrics include Cost of Transportation (CoT), survival rate, and success rate (average forward speed within ±15% of commanded velocity).

#### 4.1. Comparison Across Straight-Line Forward Velocities (Table 3)

- **Overall Consistency and Robustness:** The STL-based reward policy demonstrated the most consistent performance across the full range of commanded forward velocities (0.3 m/s to 2.1 m/s). It maintained perfect survival (1.0 ± 0.0) and success (1.0 ± 0.0) rates at every tested velocity, including high-speed commands (1.9, 2.0, 2.1 m/s) where heuristic baselines experienced degradation. This indicates that the mode-dependent STL reward provides a more reliable training signal for preserving both command tracking and stability across diverse gait regimes.
- **Energy Efficiency (CoT):**
 - At lower speeds (v$_x$ ≤ 1.0 m/s), the heuristic-best baseline achieved lower CoT, suggesting it was more energy-efficient in walking/trotting regimes.
 - However, from v$_x$ = 1.3 m/s onward, the STL-based policy achieved the lowest CoT while maintaining perfect survival and success. This high-speed advantage is attributed to the STL policy learning to transition to a bound-like gait, which is mechanically more suitable for fast locomotion, whereas the heuristic-best baseline continued to execute a trot-like gait.
- **Baseline Performance:**
 - The heuristic-best baseline, while energy-efficient at low speeds, showed declining success rates at higher speeds (e.g., 0.0 at 1.9 m/s, 0.1 at 2.0 m/s, 0.0 at 2.1 m/s), indicating difficulty in sustaining accurate tracking.
 - The heuristic-default baseline was the least reliable, showing poor success rates across most commands despite sometimes maintaining stability. This highlights the sensitivity of static, manually tuned rewards to implementation details.

#### 4.2. Evaluation Under Mixed Velocity Commands (Table 6)

- When lateral-velocity (v$_y$ ∈ [-0.4, 0.4] m/s) and yaw-rate (ω$_z$ ∈ [-0.4, 0.4] rad/s) commands were additionally sampled, the STL-based unified model generally sustained its superior performance.
- The STL policy maintained perfect survival (1.00) across all tested forward velocities (0.3 to 2.1 m/s). Its success rate was perfect up to 1.9 m/s, then decreased to 0.80 at 2.0 m/s and 0.00 at 2.1 m/s.
- In contrast, the heuristic-best baseline exhibited significantly lower success rates, particularly at low forward velocities (0.00 at 0.3 m/s, 0.35 at 0.5 m/s) and high velocities (0.00 at 1.9, 2.0, 2.1 m/s) under mixed commands.
- The CoT for the STL policy remained competitive, often lower than the heuristic-best baseline, especially at the extremes of the speed range.

#### 4.3. Interpretable Diagnostics

The paper highlights that the STL structure provides interpretable diagnostics for failure analysis. By examining the robustness signals of individual predicates, policy errors can be attributed to specific violations (e.g., insufficient support, actuator limits, poor orientation), a capability difficult to achieve with monolithic reward functions.

#### 4.4. Limitations

The study was conducted entirely in simulation (MJX), despite employing domain randomization. Therefore, the findings primarily demonstrate improved simulation performance and reward design, with sim-to-real transfer yet to be established. Additionally, the current model for gait transitions is primarily speed-dependent and does not integrate terrain information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of quadrupedal locomotion by addressing the limitations of traditional reinforcement learning (RL) reward function design, particularly concerning interpretability and explicit control over diverse gait behaviors.

#### 5.1. Key Significance

- **Interpretable and Structured Reward Design:** The primary significance lies in introducing Signal Temporal Logic (STL) as a formal and interpretable means to specify complex, temporal locomotion behaviors. Unlike opaque, hand-tuned heuristic rewards, STL allows for human-readable requirements (e.g., "always keep two feet in contact," "eventually reach a flight phase"). This transparency improves the understanding of learned policies and facilitates their design and refinement.
- **Enhanced Multi-Gait Locomotion:** The framework enables the learning of a single, unified policy that can autonomously adapt its gait structure (walking-trot, trot, bound) in response to commanded velocities. This is crucial for energy-efficient and robust locomotion across varied speed regimes, a capability often challenging for traditional RL approaches that tend to converge to a dominant gait or require explicit, discontinuous switching mechanisms.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness:** Experimental results demonstrate that the STL-shaped rewards lead to tighter velocity tracking and more stable training compared to heuristic baselines. The ability of the STL-based policy to transition to appropriate gaits (e.g., bounding at high speeds) results in better energy efficiency (lower Cost of Transportation) and higher success rates, particularly in challenging high-speed scenarios and under mixed command inputs.
- **Actionable Diagnostics for Debugging:** The quantitative robustness semantics of STL provide diagnostic signals that can attribute policy failures to specific violated specifications. This "failure attribution" capability is a practical tool for debugging and iteratively improving complex robotic control policies, offering more insight than simply observing a low reward value.
- **Bridge Between Formal Methods and Deep RL:** The work represents a practical middle ground, combining the rigorousness and analytical power of formal methods (STL) with the scalability and learning capabilities of deep reinforcement learning (PPO). This integration can foster the development of more verifiable, reliable, and adaptable autonomous systems.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Development of More Capable Legged Robots:** The ability to learn robust, gait-aware locomotion policies with clear objectives can lead to quadruped robots that are more versatile and reliable in various real-world applications, from inspection and logistics to search and rescue.
- **Reduced Development Time for Complex Behaviors:** The interpretable nature of STL specifications and the diagnostic feedback can significantly reduce the time and effort currently spent on empirical reward tuning and debugging, accelerating the development cycle for advanced robotic skills.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This framework can serve as a foundation for integrating other complex, temporal constraints into RL, such as terrain-adaptive gait selection or interaction with dynamic environments. It opens avenues for exploring how formal methods can imbue learned behaviors with greater safety, predictability, and compliance with high-level objectives.
- **Advances in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While currently evaluated in simulation, the robust performance and domain randomization employed suggest potential for sim-to-real transfer. Interpretable specifications could also aid in understanding discrepancies between simulated and real-world performance.

In conclusion, this research provides a methodology for developing gait-aware quadruped locomotion policies that are not only effective but also more interpretable and debuggable, potentially influencing the design of next-generation robotic control systems.
