# PCPO: Proportionate Credit Policy Optimization for Aligning Image Generation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Proportionate Credit Policy Optimization (PCPO) introduces a framework for aligning image generation models by reformulating the policy gradient objective and enforcing proportional credit assignment across timesteps. The approach led to up to 41.2% faster convergence, improved sample diversity, and generated outputs consistently preferred by human evaluators over baseline methods.

## Problem

- Policy gradient methods for text-to-image (T2I) generation suffer from training instability, high variance, and 'model collapse', leading to reduced sample diversity and image quality degradation.
- These issues are attributed to numerical precision errors in the standard policy gradient objective function and, more critically, disproportionate credit assignment across different timesteps in generative samplers.
- Existing stable alignment methods like DPO have theoretical performance upper bounds, while other policy gradient improvements often fail to fundamentally address the root causes of instability.

## Method

- **PCPO** reformulates the ==policy gradient== objective by replacing the numerically unstable term `ρ_t - 1` with `log ρ_t` in the hinge loss, operating in log-space to enhance numerical precision and stability.
- For diffusion models (DDIM), PCPO re-engineers the DDIM variance schedule `~σ_t` to produce a constant gradient weight `w(t)` across all timesteps, ensuring proportionate credit assignment.
- For flow models, PCPO directly reweights the training objective to make each timestep's contribution proportional to its integration interval `Δt`, thereby enforcing proportionality without altering optimized sampling procedures.

## Results

- PCPO achieved up to **41.2%** faster convergence in epochs to reach target reward levels, demonstrating superior training stability with lower and more consistent clipping fractions.
- The method effectively mitigated model collapse, showing statistically significant improvements in FID, preserved sample diversity (indicated by lower Inception Score), and received consistent human preference for generated image quality and text-image alignment.
- PCPO generalized robustly across various base models, reward functions, and unseen prompt datasets, outperforming baselines on multiple alignment metrics (e.g., CLIPScore, PickScore) without incurring additional computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disproportionate credit assignment, an inherent artifact of generative sampler mathematics, is a primary cause of training instability and model collapse in policy gradient-based T2I alignment.
> - Reformulating the policy gradient objective using `log ρ_t` instead of `ρ_t - 1` significantly improves numerical precision and overall training stability.
> - Ensuring that each timestep contributes to the policy update proportionally to its actual impact leads to a more stable learning signal, accelerating convergence and mitigating model collapse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation, primarily driven by diffusion and flow models, has made significant progress in synthesizing images. However, a persistent challenge is ensuring that generated outputs consistently align with human preferences. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), a paradigm that has achieved state-of-the-art results in aligning large language models (LLMs), has been adapted for T2I models. Among RLHF approaches, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) frameworks have emerged as leading online policy gradient methods for T2I alignment.

Despite their successes, policy gradient methods applied to T2I generation, including GRPO, are commonly affected by training instability, high variance, and a phenomenon known as "model collapse." Model collapse in this context manifests as a loss of sample diversity and degradation in image quality, often due to the model over-optimizing for a reward signal at the expense of general fidelity or exhausting its policy entropy. Early attempts to adapt Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to diffusion models were similarly unstable, leading to the adoption of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for improved stability, though DPO's theoretical performance is upper-bounded by policy-gradient methods. The current work addresses these fundamental limitations within policy gradient methods for T2I models.

The paper identifies "disproportionate credit assignment" as a key underlying cause of instability. This refers to the generative sampler's mathematical structure producing feedback across timesteps that is volatile and non-proportional to each step's actual contribution. While other concurrent research (e.g., TempFlow-GRPO, MixGRPO) has also explored improving alignment efficacy by addressing suboptimal credit assignment, primarily for training acceleration through techniques like trajectory branching or sliding SDE windows, this work posits a more fundamental "proportionality principle" to explain and resolve these issues. By providing a principled explanation and solution, this research aims to enhance the reliability and performance of policy gradient-based alignment for T2I models, potentially influencing future developments in RLHF for generative AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the training instability, high variance, and model collapse observed in state-of-the-art policy gradient methods, such as GRPO, when applied to align text-to-image (T2I) generation models. The authors identify two fundamental limitations contributing to these issues:

1. **Numerical Precision Errors in the Objective Function:** The standard policy gradient objective is susceptible to numerical precision errors that can skew gradient magnitudes, contributing to overall instability.
2. **Disproportionate Credit Assignment:** This is identified as the more critical limitation. The inherent mathematical structure of generative samplers (diffusion and flow models) leads to an inconsistent and non-proportional weighting of feedback across different timesteps. This results in a high-variance learning signal, where some timesteps receive an arbitrarily amplified learning signal, while others receive a diminished one, independent of their actual importance to the final outcome. This disproportionate credit assignment is a significant source of instability that hinders the training process.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose Proportionate Credit Policy Optimization (PCPO), a new framework with the following detailed objectives:

- **Enhance Numerical Stability:** By reformulating the policy gradient objective, PCPO aims to reduce susceptibility to numerical precision errors, thereby stabilizing the gradient computation.
- **Enforce Proportional Credit Assignment:** The core objective is to ensure that each timestep's contribution to the policy update is proportional to its actual impact on the trajectory or the integration interval it represents. This involves re-engineering the credit assignment mechanism to provide a more uniform and consistent learning signal across timesteps.
- **Accelerate Convergence:** By mitigating instability and high variance in the learning signal, PCPO aims to enable faster and more efficient convergence of the T2I models to a preferred policy.
- **Improve Image Quality and Mitigate Model Collapse:** The stabilization of the training process is expected to directly lead to superior image quality by preventing model collapse. This includes preserving sample diversity (avoiding classic mode collapse) and maintaining general fidelity while optimizing for rewards (preventing reward hacking artifacts and unrealistic outputs).

In essence, the motivation is to develop a more robust and efficient policy optimization framework that allows T2I models to achieve higher quality and better alignment with human preferences without succumbing to common training pathologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PCPO (Proportionate Credit Policy Optimization) is introduced as a framework to stabilize policy gradient training for T2I models by addressing numerical instability and disproportionate credit assignment. The methodology is adapted for both diffusion and flow matching models.

**A. Foundation and Objective Reformulation**

PCPO builds upon existing policy gradient methods, specifically using PPO notation for its derivation, as its modifications to the policy ratio `ρ_t` are common to frameworks like GRPO. The approach begins by reformulating the standard hinge loss objective:

- **Stabilizing the Objective:** The numerically unstable term `ρ_t - 1` in the original PPO hinge loss is replaced with `log ρ_t`. This modification is justified by:
 - The interpretation of `ρ_t - 1` as a "classifier" in the hinge loss, allowing for a substitution while maintaining the core mechanism.
 - `log ρ_t` serving as a Taylor approximation of `ρ_t - 1` for small policy updates, a condition typically enforced by the small clipping range `ξ` (e.g., 10^-4) used in these methods.
 - Avoiding the `exp` operation to compute `ρ_t` directly, which improves numerical precision by operating directly in log-space, mitigating floating-point errors.
 - This leads to the `L_PCPO-base` (log-hinge) objective: `E[ Σ_t max(0, ξ|A| - A log ρ_t)]`.

**B. Addressing Disproportionate Credit Assignment**

The core of PCPO lies in rectifying the disproportionate credit assignment caused by the native mathematical structure of generative samplers.

- **Analysis of `log ρ_t`:**
 - **For Diffusion Models (DDIM):** Proposition 1 details the decomposition of `log ρ_t`, revealing that the gradient contribution of each timestep `t` is scaled by a native weight `w(t) = C(t)/σ_t`. This `w(t)` is shown to be highly non-uniform and can span orders of magnitude, contributing to training instability. The objective is transformed into an `ε-matching` loss scaled by `w(t)`.
 - **For Flow Models (SDE):** Equation 8 provides an analogous decomposition for flow models, where `log ρ_t_i` is scaled by `w(t_i) = (√Δt_i / σ_t_i) * (1 + (1-t_i)σ^2_t_i / (2t_i))`. In modern flow-matching models with timestep shifting, `w(t_i)` is highly non-proportional to the integration interval `Δt_i`.

- **Proportionality Principle:** Drawing an analogy to the REINFORCE algorithm's eligibility vector, the authors argue that for proper credit assignment, the gradient contribution from each timestep should be uniformly scaled by the advantage `A`, or proportional to its integration interval `Δt`. The native `w(t)` is an artifact of the sampler's mathematics, not a deliberate credit assignment strategy, introducing high variance.

- **Implementation Strategies:**
 - **For Diffusion Models:** PCPO re-engineers the DDIM variance schedule `~σ_t` to produce a constant weight `w(t) = w*` for all timesteps. This is achieved by solving for the `σ_t` value at each step that yields the desired constant weight, while keeping the standard DDIM schedule `(α_t)` fixed. For a fair comparison, `w*` is rescaled to match the mean of the original non-uniform weights.
 - **For Flow Models:** Direct modification of the variance schedule or timestep shifting strategy would cause significant deviations from optimized sampling procedures. Therefore, PCPO enforces proportionality by directly reweighting the training objective. Proposition 2 defines a new `w(t_i) = ζΔt_i`, where `ζ` is a normalizing coefficient that ensures the sum of new weights matches the sum of original weights.

**C. Experimental Design**

The experimental validation covers a range of scenarios:

- **Baselines:** DDPO (on Stable Diffusion 1.5) and DanceGRPO (on SD 1.4 and FLUX.1-dev flow-matching model).
- **Reward Models:** Aesthetics, BERTScore, HPSv2.1.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Training Dynamics:** Reward acceleration and clipping fractions are tracked throughout training.
 - **Sample Quality:** Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Fréchet Distance DINOv2 (FD_DINO), Inception Score (IS), and LPIPS Diversity are measured at matched reward levels. Linear Mixed Models (LMM) are used for statistical analysis to account for per-prompt variance.
 - **Qualitative Assessment:** Visual comparison of generated images.
 - **Human Preference Study:** Formal study to validate human perception of quality.
 - **Generalization:** Benchmarking on unseen prompts from MSCOCO-2017 and MJHQ-30K datasets, evaluated with a diverse suite of alignment metrics (HPSv2.1, Aesthetics, CLIPScore, PickScore, ImageReward).
 - **Robustness:** Application of PCPO to the Flow-GRPO SDE framework on SD3.5-M, using different rewards (OCR, PickScore) and an auxiliary KL divergence penalty to test generalizability across diverse architectures and training setups.
- **Ablation Study:** Decomposing PCPO into its components (log `ρ_t` objective, `ε-matching` with native `w(t)`, and proportional weighting) to assess individual contributions. Comparison against heuristic acceleration methods like timestep subsampling and prioritizing high-noise timesteps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding PCPO's impact on training stability, efficiency, image quality, and generalizability.

**A. Improved Training Efficiency and Stability:**
PCPO consistently demonstrated superior training stability compared to baselines. It maintained a lower and more stable clipping fraction across all experimental settings, indicating more consistent policy updates. This stability directly translated into accelerated convergence, with PCPO achieving substantial speedups in epochs required to reach target reward levels, ranging from 24.6% to 41.2% (Table 1). Furthermore, PCPO introduces no additional computational overhead, and in some cases, slightly reduces wall-clock time per epoch compared to baselines, as it avoids an additional SDE sampler step required by some baselines. Gradient analysis confirmed PCPO's superior stability, showing gradients with lower mean and variance near convergence (Figure 11).

**B. Mitigation of Model Collapse:**
PCPO's enhanced stability resulted in significant improvements in sample fidelity and diversity, indicating an effective mitigation of model collapse.
- **DDPO Experiments:** For DDPO trained with Aesthetics reward, PCPO achieved a statistically significant improvement in FID. While FD_DINO showed similar trends, its statistical significance was not reached, which the authors attributed to task or base model limitations. An LMM analysis revealed a strong positive interaction between PCPO and larger batch sizes for LPIPS diversity. Critically, PCPO produced a statistically significant reduction in Inception Score (IS). Based on an empirical finding that increasing batch size (a known method to reduce model collapse) decreases IS in this context, the authors interpreted a lower IS as an indicator of mitigated mode collapse rather than degradation, signifying preserved diversity.
- **DanceGRPO Experiments:** PCPO delivered significantly better FID for both SD1.4 and FLUX models, even when evaluated at a higher-reward checkpoint than the baseline (a condition that typically degrades FID). A lower IS for the FLUX model further reinforced the mitigation of model collapse.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual inspection confirmed that PCPO avoids the severe model collapse seen in baselines, generating clear and diverse images instead of blurry or repetitive outputs (Figure 1 and Appendix H).
- **Human Evaluation:** A formal human preference study showed that evaluators consistently preferred PCPO's outputs over the DanceGRPO baseline across text-image alignment, visual quality, and general preference categories, even when PCPO was trained for fewer epochs (Figure 4).

**C. Generalization and Robustness:**
PCPO exhibited robust generalizability across different models, reward functions, and training configurations.
- **Unseen Prompts and Metrics:** When evaluated on unseen prompts from MSCOCO-2017 and MJHQ-30K datasets, PCPO not only excelled on its trained metric (HPSv2.1) but also outperformed baselines across a suite of unseen alignment metrics (Aesthetics, CLIPScore, PickScore, ImageReward) (Table 5). PCPO maintained better text-image alignment (CLIPScore) than baselines, suggesting reduced reward hacking.
- **Flow-GRPO Framework:** PCPO consistently outperformed the baseline when applied to the Flow-GRPO framework on the SD3.5-M model, which involved a distinct noise schedule, different rewards (OCR, PickScore), and an auxiliary KL penalty. PCPO achieved higher rewards, lower KL divergence, and significantly less clipping (Figure 5), confirming its stabilizing properties.

**D. Ablation Study:**
The ablation study confirmed the contribution of each PCPO component. Sequentially adding the `log ρ_t` objective, `ε-matching`, and finally proportional weighting to the DDPO baseline progressively reduced clipping fractions. Only the full PCPO framework achieved a zero on-policy clipping ratio, indicating its design effectively addresses instability. Proportional weighting was identified as the most critical factor for accelerating training (Figure 6). The study also demonstrated that PCPO's principled approach significantly outperformed heuristic acceleration methods (e.g., timestep subsampling degraded quality, while a heuristic uniform weighting strategy for high-noise timesteps harmed performance in DanceGRPO SDE, Figure 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of aligning image generation models, particularly those based on diffusion and flow architectures. The identification of "disproportionate credit assignment" as a root cause of training instability in policy gradient methods is a fundamental insight. PCPO's principled approach to correct this, rather than relying on empirical heuristics, offers a robust solution with several implications.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enhanced Training Stability and Efficiency:** By directly addressing the sources of instability, PCPO enables significantly accelerated convergence and more stable training trajectories for T2I models. This translates to reduced computational resources and time required for fine-tuning, making RLHF more accessible and efficient.
2. **Superior Image Quality and Diversity:** The mitigation of model collapse is a direct outcome of PCPO's stable training. This leads to generated images that are not only of higher fidelity but also exhibit greater diversity, avoiding the blurry, repetitive, or artifact-laden outputs often associated with unstable policy optimization. The sustained quality even with prolonged training, as demonstrated qualitatively, is crucial for practical applications.
3. **Robustness and Generalizability:** PCPO's effectiveness across various base models (Stable Diffusion, FLUX), different policy gradient frameworks (DDPO, DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO), diverse reward functions (Aesthetics, BERTScore, HPSv2.1, OCR, PickScore), and unseen prompt datasets highlights its broad applicability. This suggests PCPO is a model-agnostic principle, which significantly increases its potential for widespread adoption. Its demonstrated ability to reduce "reward hacking" (maintaining text-image alignment on unseen metrics) is also highly valuable.
4. **Foundational Understanding:** The paper's theoretical analysis, which links disproportionate credit assignment to the mathematical structure of generative samplers and draws parallels to the REINFORCE eligibility vector, provides a deeper understanding of the challenges in applying RL to these models. This principled explanation can guide future research in designing more inherently stable RL algorithms for generative tasks.
5. **Future Research Avenues:** PCPO opens up new directions for research, particularly in exploring its synergy with other stabilization techniques (e.g., dynamic clipping, temporal localization) and investigating the fundamental causes of large, unstable gradients in flow models. Addressing these issues could lead to policy gradient methods that are stable by design, further advancing the state of the art.

In conclusion, PCPO offers a comprehensive and effective solution to a critical problem in T2I model alignment. Its ability to accelerate training, improve output quality, and generalize across diverse setups marks a notable advancement, potentially accelerating the development of more aligned and capable generative AI systems.
