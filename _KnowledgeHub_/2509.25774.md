# PCPO: Proportionate Credit Policy Optimization for Aligning Image Generation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Proportionate Credit Policy Optimization (PCPO) introduces a framework for aligning image generation models by reformulating the policy gradient objective and enforcing proportional credit assignment across timesteps. The approach led to up to 41.2% faster convergence, improved sample diversity, and generated outputs consistently preferred by human evaluators over baseline methods.

## Problem

- Policy gradient methods for text-to-image (T2I) generation suffer from training instability, high variance, and 'model collapse', leading to reduced sample diversity and image quality degradation.
- These issues are attributed to numerical precision errors in the standard policy gradient objective function and, more critically, disproportionate credit assignment across different timesteps in generative samplers.
- Existing stable alignment methods like DPO have theoretical performance upper bounds, while other policy gradient improvements often fail to fundamentally address the root causes of instability.

## Method

- **PCPO** reformulates the ==policy gradient== objective by replacing the numerically unstable term `ρ_t - 1` with `log ρ_t` in the hinge loss, operating in log-space to enhance numerical precision and stability.
- For diffusion models (DDIM), PCPO re-engineers the DDIM variance schedule `~σ_t` to produce a constant gradient weight `w(t)` across all timesteps, ensuring proportionate credit assignment.
- For flow models, PCPO directly reweights the training objective to make each timestep's contribution proportional to its integration interval `Δt`, thereby enforcing proportionality without altering optimized sampling procedures.

## Results

- PCPO achieved up to **41.2%** faster convergence in epochs to reach target reward levels, demonstrating superior training stability with lower and more consistent clipping fractions.
- The method effectively mitigated model collapse, showing statistically significant improvements in FID, preserved sample diversity (indicated by lower Inception Score), and received consistent human preference for generated image quality and text-image alignment.
- PCPO generalized robustly across various base models, reward functions, and unseen prompt datasets, outperforming baselines on multiple alignment metrics (e.g., CLIPScore, PickScore) without incurring additional computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disproportionate credit assignment, an inherent artifact of generative sampler mathematics, is a primary cause of training instability and model collapse in policy gradient-based T2I alignment.
> - Reformulating the policy gradient objective using `log ρ_t` instead of `ρ_t - 1` significantly improves numerical precision and overall training stability.
> - Ensuring that each timestep contributes to the policy update proportionally to its actual impact leads to a more stable learning signal, accelerating convergence and mitigating model collapse.
