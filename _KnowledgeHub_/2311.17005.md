# MVBench: A Comprehensive Multi-modal Video Understanding Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces MVBench, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate the temporal comprehension capabilities of multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) in dynamic video tasks. It also develops VideoChat2, a new video MLLM baseline that significantly surpasses existing models, including GPT-4V, on MVBench and other video understanding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Most existing benchmarks for multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) are image-centric, primarily evaluating spatial understanding in static images and overlooking crucial temporal comprehension in videos.
- Prior video-centric MLLM evaluation efforts are often limited to basic tasks, specific domains, or restricted scenes, lacking comprehensive coverage for open-world scenarios.
- Current video benchmarks often rely on labor-intensive manual annotations, making them costly and difficult to scale, and open-ended answer evaluations can introduce biases.

## Method

- Introduces **MVBench**, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating multi-modal large language models' (MLLMs) temporal understanding in videos, featuring 20 diverse temporal tasks derived using a ==static-to-dynamic== method.
- Develops an ==automatic question-answering generation pipeline== leveraging 11 public video datasets and large language models (LLMs) to create ==multiple-choice questions==, ensuring efficiency and unbiased scoring.
- Presents **VideoChat2**, a robust video MLLM baseline trained with 2 million diverse ==instruction-tuning== samples and a ==three-stage progressive multi-modal training== paradigm (with a ==UMT-L== encoder, ==QFormer==, and ==LoRA==) to enhance spatio-temporal reasoning.

## Results

- Existing MLLMs (e.g., VideoChat, VideoLLaMA) achieve low average accuracy (around **30-35%**) on MVBench, demonstrating their limited temporal understanding and minimal improvement over text-only baselines.
- VideoChat2 significantly outperforms leading MLLMs and GPT-4V, achieving an average accuracy of **51.1%** on MVBench, and further outperforming GPT-4V by **16.9%** with a Mistral-based LLM.
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of visual encoder choice (UMT-L outperforms EVA-CLIP-g), data diversity, and progressive multi-modal training for enhancing temporal understanding, with LoRA consistently improving results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing state-of-the-art multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) perform poorly (around 30-35% accuracy) on temporal video understanding tasks, indicating a significant gap in their ability to leverage temporal information.
> - A strong spatio-temporal visual encoder, diverse and video-centric instruction-tuning data, and progressive multi-modal training are critical for improving MLLMs' temporal reasoning capabilities.
> - A simple, effective prompt design using 'Best Option: (' can guarantee 100% extraction accuracy for multiple-choice answers, improving evaluation fairness and reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "MVBench: A Comprehensive Multi-modal Video Understanding Benchmark" positions itself squarely at the intersection of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and video understanding. This work addresses a critical gap in the rapidly evolving field of AI.

**The Rise of MLLMs and the "Image-Centric" Bias:**
In recent years, MLLMs, which combine powerful visual encoders with large language models (LLMs), have driven significant advancements in vision-language learning. Models like Flamingo, PaLM-E, LLaVA, and MiniGPT-4 have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding and generating content across modalities, primarily focusing on text and static images. As these models become more sophisticated, a natural and pressing question arises: how comprehensively can we evaluate their understanding capabilities?

**The Unaddressed Challenge of Temporal Understanding:**
In response to the evaluation need, numerous benchmarks have emerged (e.g., MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench, MM-Vet). However, the authors correctly identify a critical limitation: the vast majority of these benchmarks predominantly assess *spatial understanding* in *static image tasks*. They are designed to evaluate perception in fixed snapshots, largely overlooking the crucial dimension of *temporal understanding* inherent in dynamic videos. Real-world activities are inherently procedural and dynamic, requiring models to reason about events, causality, changes over time, and interactions within a continuous flow of information. The lack of robust temporal evaluation hinders the development of truly comprehensive MLLMs capable of understanding the real world.

**Limitations of Existing Video-Centric Efforts:**
While some attempts have been made to evaluate MLLMs on video tasks (e.g., SEED-Bench, FunQA, Perception Test, VideoChatGPT), the authors argue that these efforts are often limited:
- **Basic Tasks:** Some focus on very basic video tasks (e.g., simple action recognition).
- **Particular Domains/Restricted Scenes:** Others are confined to specific domains (e.g., surprising events) or limited indoor scenes, lacking generalizability to open-world scenarios.
- **Annotation Labor:** A significant drawback is their reliance on labor-intensive manual annotations, making them expensive to scale and update.
- **Evaluation Bias:** Furthermore, evaluations for open-ended video QA often resort to LLM-based scoring or user studies, which can introduce biases or inconsistencies.

**MVBench's Position as a Solution:**
MVBench directly addresses these challenges by introducing a *comprehensive*, *systematic*, and *efficiently generated* benchmark specifically designed for temporal understanding. It aims to bridge the gap between static image comprehension and dynamic video reasoning, offering a robust evaluation standard. By proposing a novel "static-to-dynamic" task definition and an automatic QA generation pipeline, MVBench overcomes the limitations of previous benchmarks regarding scope, efficiency, and fairness.

**Complementary Model Contribution:**
Beyond the benchmark, the paper also contributes "VideoChat2," a strong video MLLM baseline. This is crucial because a robust benchmark necessitates models that can truly test its boundaries. The development of VideoChat2, which leverages diverse instruction-tuning data and progressive multi-modal training, serves as both a proof-of-concept for the benchmark's utility and a significant step forward in video MLLM capabilities. It aims to fill the performance gap observed in existing MLLMs when confronted with true temporal understanding tasks.

In summary, MVBench aims to redefine the evaluation paradigm for MLLMs, shifting the focus towards the dynamic and temporal aspects of multi-modal understanding, which are essential for real-world artificial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the MVBench paper stem from a critical observation of the current state of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and their evaluation.

**Primary Objective:**
The overarching goal of this research is to introduce a **comprehensive and reliable benchmark, MVBench, specifically designed to evaluate the temporal comprehension capabilities of Multi-modal Large Language Models in dynamic video tasks.**

**Key Motivations (Problem Statement):**

1. **Addressing the "Static Image Bias" in MLLM Evaluation:**
 - **Problem:** The rapid advancement of MLLMs has led to a proliferation of benchmarks. However, the authors keenly observe that most of these existing benchmarks (e.g., MME, MMBench, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) are primarily **image-centric**. They are designed to assess spatial perception and understanding in static images, with questions often answerable by analyzing a single frame.
 - **Motivation:** This image-focused evaluation overlooks a fundamental aspect of real-world intelligence: the ability to understand and reason about dynamic events and temporal evolution. To truly comprehend the world, an MLLM must grasp procedural activities, sequences of events, and changes over time. Thus, there is a strong motivation to shift the evaluation paradigm towards dynamic, temporal understanding.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Video Benchmarks:**
 - **Problem:** While some attempts have been made to evaluate MLLMs on video tasks, they suffer from significant limitations. They might be too basic (e.g., simple action recognition), domain-specific (e.g., surprising events), or confined to restricted scenes (e.g., indoor environments), thereby limiting their scope for comprehensive evaluation in open-world scenarios.
 - **Motivation:** A truly comprehensive benchmark needs to cover a wide range of temporal skills, from low-level perception (e.g., object movement, action localization) to high-level cognition (e.g., episodic reasoning, counterfactual inference), across diverse real-world contexts.

3. **Improving Efficiency and Fairness in Benchmark Creation:**
 - **Problem:** Many existing video benchmarks rely heavily on labor-intensive manual annotations, making them expensive to create and difficult to scale or update. Furthermore, methods that rely on LLM-based scoring of open-ended answers or subjective user studies can introduce evaluation bias, compromising fairness and accuracy.
 - **Motivation:** There is a need for an efficient and fair annotation paradigm. MVBench aims to leverage existing public video datasets and automated question-answering (QA) generation techniques to significantly reduce manual intervention, while using a multiple-choice format with ground-truth answers to ensure objective and unbiased scoring.

4. **Developing a Strong Baseline to Highlight the Temporal Understanding Gap:**
 - **Problem:** The authors hypothesize that existing MLLMs, designed predominantly for image understanding, would perform poorly on a truly temporal benchmark. This lack of performance would underscore the need for dedicated improvements in temporal reasoning.
 - **Motivation:** To confirm this hypothesis and also to provide a strong reference point for future research, the paper is motivated to develop a robust video MLLM baseline, VideoChat2. This model aims to bridge the observed performance gap and demonstrate the potential for significantly improved temporal understanding, thereby "paving the path to general video understanding."

In essence, the paper is driven by the ambition to elevate the evaluation and development of MLLMs from static image comprehension to a more holistic and practical understanding of the dynamic world, providing both the tools (MVBench) and a leading example (VideoChat2) to achieve this.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of MVBench is two-fold: first, the design and automatic generation of the benchmark itself, and second, the development of VideoChat2, a strong MLLM baseline.

#### 3.1. MVBench Construction

The core of MVBench lies in its innovative approach to defining and populating temporal understanding tasks.

##### 3.1.1. Temporal Task Definition: Static-to-Dynamic Method

The authors introduce a "static-to-dynamic" method to systematically define temporal tasks, addressing the image-centric bias of existing benchmarks.
- **Reference Point:** They begin by summarizing 9 main spatial understanding tasks commonly found in previous image-based multi-modal benchmarks (e.g., object, action, position, scene, count, attribute, pose, character, cognition).
- **Temporal Augmentation:** Each static task is then enriched with dynamic, video-specific context, transforming it into a "temporal-related" task that inherently requires reasoning over the entire video sequence, not just a single frame. For example, a "position" task in an image ("Is the man on the stage?") becomes a "moving direction" task in a video ("What direction is the man moving?").
- **Comprehensive Coverage:** This systematic adaptation leads to the definition of 20 challenging temporal tasks covering a broad spectrum of understanding skills, from low-level perception (e.g., Action Sequence, Moving Direction, Object Interaction, Fine-grained Pose) to high-level cognition (e.g., Episodic Reasoning, Counterfactual Inference, Egocentric Navigation).

##### 3.1.2. Automatic QA Generation

To ensure efficiency, diversity, and fairness, MVBench employs an automatic QA generation paradigm from existing public video annotations.
- **Data Filtration:**
 - **Video Diversity:** 11 diverse public video datasets (e.g., STAR, CLEVRER, Perception Test, TVQA) are carefully selected. These datasets cover a wide range of domains (real-world, simulated), scenes (indoor, outdoor), and perspectives (first-person, third-person).
 - **Temporal Sensitivity:** To guarantee that tasks genuinely require temporal understanding, short video clips with minimal motion are excluded, as are excessively long videos with overly complex contexts. Videos with intermediate durations (typically 5s to 35s) are preferred.
 - **Question Difficulty:** Criteria are applied to balance question difficulty. For instance, for STAR, event localization complexity is increased by shifting video clip start/end points. For CLEVRER, overly complex questions requiring more than 10 conditions are filtered out.
- **QA Conversion via LLMs:**
 - **Question Generation:** ChatGPT is used to generate a question for each video, guided by the specific task definition.
 - **Answer Option Creation:**
 - **Template-Based Construction:** For many tasks, answer options are directly constructed from ground-truth annotations using predefined templates (e.g., correct action, its antonym, and a "not sure" option for Action Antonym; cardinal directions for Moving Direction).
 - **LLM-Based Generation:** For tasks like "Unexpected Action" that have open-ended annotations, ChatGPT is leveraged to convert these into multiple-choice questions with plausible distractors.
 - **Rationale for Multiple-Choice:** The choice of a multiple-choice format is deliberate to ensure evaluation fairness and accuracy. This avoids the biases and inconsistencies that can arise from scoring open-ended answers via LLMs or labor-intensive human judgment.
- **Answer Option Processing:** To further enhance robustness and prevent "answer leakage" (e.g., longer options being more likely correct), options are randomly sampled (3-5 per question) and shuffled. An LLM is then used to ensure similar and reasonable text lengths across all options for a given question. Each task ultimately contains 200 multiple-choice QA pairs.

#### 3.2. Prompt Design for Evaluation

The paper also details specific prompt engineering strategies crucial for effective evaluation:
- **System Prompt:** A detailed system prompt encourages MLLMs to focus on temporal aspects during inference, guiding them to scrutinize actions, poses, object movements, and other dynamic details.
- **Answer Prompt:** A simple yet highly effective answer prompt, "Best Option: (", is used to reliably extract the model's chosen option from its response. This method guarantees 100% extraction accuracy, a significant improvement over previous complex matching or LLM-based extraction methods that often suffer from lower alignment rates.

#### 3.3. VideoChat2 Development

To serve as a strong baseline and demonstrate the benchmark's utility, the authors develop VideoChat2, a robust video MLLM.

##### 3.3.1. Instruction-Tuning Data

- **Diversity and Scale:** Recognizing the limitations of prior MLLMs due to narrow instruction-tuning data, VideoChat2 leverages an extensive dataset of 2 million samples derived from 34 distinct sources. This includes both image and video data.
- **Categorization:** The data is organized into 6 categories: Conversation, Simple Caption, Detailed Caption, VQA, Reasoning, and Classification, providing comprehensive coverage of vision-language tasks.
- **LLM for Instruction Generation:** ChatGPT is used to generate diverse instructions for each dataset, based on dataset descriptions, task descriptions, and example instructions, streamlining the data preparation process.

##### 3.3.2. Progressive Multi-Modal Training

VideoChat2 employs a three-stage progressive training paradigm to effectively bridge the semantic gap between visual and linguistic representations.
- **Stage 1: Vision-Language Alignment:** The visual encoder (UMT-L, a powerful spatio-temporal representation model) is frozen, and a flexible QFormer (from BLIP-2) is trained. The QFormer compresses visual tokens and aligns them with text tokens using multi-modal losses (Vision-Text Contrastive, Vision-Text Matching, Vision-grounded Text Generation). This stage particularly emphasizes video-language modeling by using large video caption datasets (WebVid-10M).
- **Stage 2: Vision-Language Connection:** The visual encoder is unfrozen, and its outputs (via QFormer) are connected to a pre-trained LLM (Vicuna-7B v0) through a linear projection. The model is further trained for vision-based caption generation (VTG), using an expanded set of image and video captions. Unfreezing the visual encoder at this stage allows for better alignment with the LLM.
- **Stage 3: Instruction Tuning:** The full diverse instruction-tuning dataset is used. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied to the frozen LLM, which is then fine-tuned alongside the visual encoder and QFormer using VTG loss. Crucially, instructions are integrated into the QFormer to enable extraction of instruction-relevant visual tokens, enhancing instruction-following capabilities.

This comprehensive methodology, from benchmark design to model development, ensures that MVBench is a robust tool for evaluating temporal understanding in MLLMs, while VideoChat2 pushes the state-of-the-art in this challenging domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on MVBench yield significant findings regarding the capabilities of current MLLMs in temporal understanding and highlight the superior performance of VideoChat2.

#### 4.1. Performance on MVBench

- **Underperformance of Existing MLLMs:** A surprising and critical finding is that existing state-of-the-art image and video MLLMs (such as mPLUG-Owl, LLaMA-Adapter, BLIP2, Otter, MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP, LLaVA, VideoChatGPT, VideoLLaMA, and VideoChat) perform *far from satisfactorily* on MVBench. Their average accuracy hovers around 30-35%.
- **Minimal Temporal Understanding in Leading Models:** Notably, VideoChat, previously a top-performing video MLLM, only marginally surpasses a text-only baseline (`VideoChat2_text`, which generates responses from text alone without video input) by a mere 0.8% (35.5% vs. 34.7% average accuracy). This starkly reveals that these models, despite handling video input, largely fail to leverage temporal information effectively, often relying on their general language understanding or static image capabilities.
- **VideoChat2's Significant Leap:** In contrast, VideoChat2 demonstrates a remarkable improvement, substantially surpassing the leading models by *over 15%* in average accuracy on MVBench (51.1%). This validates MVBench as a challenging benchmark that truly differentiates models based on their temporal understanding.
- **Task-Specific Performance of VideoChat2:** VideoChat2 particularly excels in tasks related to Action, Object, Scene, Attribute, and Fine-grained Pose recognition, indicating its strength in understanding dynamic changes and detailed visual properties over time.
- **Areas for Improvement in VideoChat2:** Despite its overall strong performance, VideoChat2 still struggles in certain categories, specifically Position, Count, and Character tasks. Its performance in these areas is sometimes even comparable to or worse than the text-only baseline, suggesting that it might not have been sufficiently exposed to relevant tuning data for these specific temporal aspects (e.g., precise localization, counting events over time, or recognizing ordered characters). This points to specific avenues for future research.
- **Comparison with GPT-4V:** When evaluating the powerful GPT-4V, it achieves satisfactory performance (43.5% average accuracy), demonstrating its capacity for temporal understanding. However, the Mistral-based VideoChat2 further *outperforms GPT-4V by 16.9%* (60.4%), underscoring the superiority of the proposed model and methodology on MVBench's diverse temporal tasks.

#### 4.2. More Comparisons (Generalization and Robustness)

Beyond MVBench, VideoChat2 also establishes new state-of-the-art results on other challenging video benchmarks:
- **Video Conversation:** On the video conversation benchmark, VideoChat2 exhibits superior performance across all metrics (correctness, detail, contextual understanding, temporal understanding, consistency) compared to VideoChatGPT and VideoChat. This indicates its enhanced ability to comprehend both spatial and temporal details and provide more consistent and reliable conversational responses.
- **Zero-Shot Video QA:** VideoChat2 significantly outperforms previous methods on typical zero-shot video QA datasets like MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, and ANet-QA. Its exceptional performance on ActivityNet (part of ANet-QA) suggests its robustness in understanding long videos and complex events.
- **Other Challenging Benchmarks:** Further results on NExT-QA, STAR, TVQA, EgoSchema, and IntentQA corroborate VideoChat2's robust performance on complex reasoning tasks, even when directly outputting options without likelihood comparisons.
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative comparisons confirm that VideoChat2 produces more precise and thorough video descriptions, accurately identifying moving object properties, unforeseen actions, and future movements, and providing detailed insights into human actions, camera motions, and background ambiance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Insights into Design Choices)

The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the design decisions for VideoChat2:

- **Instruction Data:** The quantity and diversity of instruction-tuning data are crucial. Increasing data, especially video data, significantly boosts performance (video data contributes more than image data, 50.5% vs. 42.1% accuracy gain). Compressing redundant simple caption data has minimal impact on performance, suggesting that diversity and specific task instructions are more vital than raw volume of basic captions.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** The choice of visual encoder is critical. UMT-L significantly outperforms EVA-CLIP-g, demonstrating its superior spatio-temporal representation learning for video tasks.
 - **LLM:** While larger and newer LLMs offer marginal improvements, the core performance on MVBench appears to rely more on the visual encoder's capabilities, indicating that improving visual understanding is paramount for temporal tasks.
 - **LoRA:** Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) consistently improves results, likely by enhancing the model's capacity to follow instructions effectively.
- **Training Method:** The progressive multi-modal training paradigm is highly effective. Unfreezing QFormer and subsequently the visual encoder during later stages of training leads to substantial performance gains, emphasizing the importance of more learnable parameters for adapting the visual pipeline to the language model.
- **Prompt Design:**
 - **System Prompt:** A comprehensive system prompt that emphasizes temporal aspects and task requirements improves effectiveness.
 - **Answer Prompt:** The simple "Best Option: (" answer prompt is highly effective and reliable, guaranteeing 100% option extraction accuracy across various MLLMs, thus ensuring fair and accurate evaluation. This is a significant practical contribution.
 - **Resolution & Frames:** Increasing the number of video frames processed improves performance, while increasing resolution does not, reinforcing that MVBench primarily assesses temporal rather than just spatial understanding.

In summary, the findings robustly establish MVBench as a challenging and effective benchmark for temporal video understanding, expose the limitations of current MLLMs in this domain, and demonstrate VideoChat2 as a powerful new baseline, with detailed ablations guiding future research on MLLM architecture and training strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "MVBench: A Comprehensive Multi-modal Video Understanding Benchmark" paper carries significant implications for the advancement of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and their application in real-world scenarios.

1. **Addresses a Critical Gap in MLLM Evaluation:** The most immediate and profound significance of MVBench is its pioneering role in addressing the long-standing bias towards static image understanding in MLLM evaluation. By introducing a comprehensive benchmark specifically for *temporal comprehension* in videos, MVBench fills a crucial void. This shift in focus is vital because understanding the real world inherently requires processing dynamic, time-evolving information. Without such a benchmark, the progress of MLLMs towards true general intelligence would remain incomplete.

2. **Establishes a Standardized and Fair Evaluation Protocol:** MVBench provides a systematic and fair method for evaluating MLLMs' temporal understanding.
 - The "static-to-dynamic" task definition method offers a principled way to generate diverse temporal tasks.
 - The automatic QA generation, leveraging existing public datasets and LLMs, dramatically reduces the cost and labor associated with benchmark creation, making it scalable and reproducible.
 - Crucially, the reliance on a multiple-choice format with ground-truth annotations ensures objective and unbiased scoring, avoiding the subjectivity and potential biases of LLM-based evaluations for open-ended answers. This standardization will enable more reliable comparisons and foster healthier competition in the research community.

3. **Provides Actionable Insights for Model Design:** The extensive evaluation of various MLLMs on MVBench clearly exposes their shortcomings in temporal reasoning. The detailed ablation studies on VideoChat2 offer invaluable insights into what makes an MLLM effective for video understanding.
 - It highlights the paramount importance of a strong spatio-temporal visual encoder (like UMT-L).
 - It demonstrates the significant impact of diverse and comprehensive instruction-tuning data, especially video-centric instructions.
 - It underscores the efficacy of progressive multi-modal training paradigms and techniques like LoRA for better instruction following. These findings will directly guide future research in designing more capable and robust video MLLMs.

4. **Catalyst for Advanced Video MLLM Development:** The strong performance of VideoChat2, significantly outperforming existing leading models and even GPT-4V on MVBench, sets a new state-of-the-art. This achievement not only serves as a proof-of-concept for the benchmark's utility but also provides a powerful baseline for future research. The public release of MVBench data and the VideoChat2 model will accelerate innovation in the field, allowing researchers worldwide to build upon this foundation, reproduce results, and develop even more sophisticated video understanding models.

5. **Broadening the Scope of AI Applications:** Improved temporal understanding capabilities in MLLMs have far-reaching implications across numerous domains:
 - **Content Analysis:** More accurate video summarization, content moderation, and event detection for media and entertainment.
 - **Surveillance and Security:** Enhanced anomaly detection and behavioral analysis in security footage.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Better perception and prediction capabilities for self-driving cars and robotics, allowing them to understand dynamic environments and anticipate actions.
 - **Education and Training:** Development of interactive learning platforms that can understand and respond to complex actions in educational videos.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** More natural and intelligent video-based interactions, enabling MLLMs to serve as truly helpful assistants that can reason about ongoing human activities.

In conclusion, MVBench is not merely another dataset; it represents a significant methodological step forward in evaluating the intelligence of MLLMs. By explicitly challenging models on their temporal reasoning, it pushes the entire field towards developing AI systems that can genuinely understand and interact with the dynamic, unfolding narrative of our world.
