# Universal Humanoid Motion Representations for Physics-Based Control

> [!summary] Summary
> PULSE learns a physically plausible universal motion latent space for humanoids by distilling motor skills from a robust motion imitator trained on a large, unstructured dataset. This enables more efficient and natural control across diverse generative and tracking tasks, demonstrating superior performance and faster convergence compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Developing physically simulated humanoids currently requires extensive, task-specific data curation and training new control policies from scratch for each desired movement, leading to high costs and limited generalizability.
- Existing motion representations either cover only narrow, specialized behaviors (physics-based) or lack physical plausibility when applied in simulation (kinematics-based), preventing universal applicability.
- Reward design for natural, human-like motion is often complex, requiring adversarial methods to avoid unnatural behaviors.

## Method

- Develops **PHC+**, an advanced ==physics-based motion imitator==, trained to robustly imitate a large, cleaned motion capture dataset (AMASS) with a 100% success rate.
- Distills the comprehensive motor skills of the frozen PHC+ policy into **PULSE**, a ==probabilistic latent space== model with an ==encoder-decoder== structure and a learned, ==proprioception-conditioned prior==.
- Employs a ==hierarchical control== framework where a high-level policy outputs latent codes, which are then decoded by the frozen PULSE model, utilizing a ==residual action formulation== with the learned prior for efficient and natural exploration in downstream tasks.

## Results

- PULSE, after distillation from PHC+, achieves **97.1%** success in imitating the AMASS test set, demonstrating effective skill compression with high fidelity.
- Policies using PULSE's latent space achieve higher normalized returns and significantly faster convergence across various generative tasks (e.g., speed, reach, strike, terrain traversal) compared to other state-of-the-art baselines.
- PULSE shows strong performance in VR controller tracking, outperforming other latent space models and achieving accuracy comparable to training a specialized policy from scratch, while also enabling faster convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling motor skills from a robust, large-scale motion imitator (PHC+) can create a comprehensive and physically plausible latent space for humanoid control.
> - A learned conditional prior, combined with a residual action formulation, is crucial for enabling efficient exploration and generating natural behaviors in hierarchical reinforcement learning tasks.
> - Regularizing the continuity of latent codes during distillation helps ensure a smooth and navigable latent space, which is essential for stable and effective downstream policy learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of physically simulated humanoids is crucial for various fields, including animation, gaming, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), and robotics. Recent advancements, particularly leveraging motion capture (MoCap) data and reinforcement learning (RL), have enabled humanoids to perform complex motions. However, the broader research landscape is characterized by a significant limitation: each new task or desired movement style typically requires specialized data curation and training a new physics-based policy from scratch. This process is time-consuming, resource-intensive, and prone to issues related to reward design and data quality, hindering the widespread applicability of these simulated humanoids.

This paper addresses a critical gap in the existing literature regarding motion representations for physics-based control. Prior methods for creating reusable motion controllers often involve forming a latent space or skill embedding for hierarchical RL. However, these methods usually rely on small and specialized motion datasets, leading to latent spaces that can only produce a narrow range of preordained behaviors (e.g., specific locomotion styles, boxing, dancing). Examples like ASE (Peng et al., 2022) and CALM (Tessler et al., 2023) fall into this category, effective for curated datasets but struggling with diverse skills. Attempts to scale these methods to larger, more varied datasets like CMU MoCap have yielded unsatisfactory results. Consequently, these motion representations are often limited to stylized animation or specific generative tasks, falling short for free-form motion tracking or tasks requiring broad motor skill coverage.

Another line of research focuses on kinematics-based human motion latent spaces (e.g., MVAE, HuMoR by Rempe et al., 2021). While these models can learn universal motion priors from large datasets like AMASS without direct physics simulation, their outputs often lack physical plausibility and stability when translated into a physics engine. Kinematic motion, due to its high dimensionality and lack of physical constraints, is also a poor sampling space for direct RL.

PULSE (Physics-based Universal motion Latent SpacE) positions itself as a solution to these limitations by aiming to learn a *universal motion representation* that is both comprehensive in its skill coverage and inherently physically plausible. The paper explicitly draws an analogy to a "foundation model for control," suggesting an ambition to create a foundational component that can be reused across a vast array of downstream tasks without significant modification. By leveraging state-of-the-art physics-based motion imitation (PHC+ which itself is a significant recent advancement), PULSE distills motor skills from an *unstructured, large-scale motion dataset* (AMASS) into a probabilistic latent space. This approach bridges the gap between narrow, specialized physics-based control and broad, but often physically unrealistic, kinematic motion priors. It effectively seeks to unify the benefits of large-scale data and physical simulation within a single, reusable framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Universal Humanoid Motion Representations for Physics-Based Control" stems from the inherent inefficiencies and limitations of current approaches to animating and controlling physically simulated humanoids. Despite significant advancements in motion capture and reinforcement learning, the creation of realistic human-like behavior in simulations remains a laborious, engineering-heavy process. Each new application or complex task (e.g., diverse locomotion, interaction, free-form movement) typically necessitates:
1. **Careful Curation of Specialized Motion Data:** Datasets are often narrow in scope, limiting the generalizability of learned behaviors.
2. **Training New Physics-Based Policies from Scratch:** This is time-consuming and computationally expensive.
3. **Complex Reward Design:** Issues in reward function design or learning frameworks can lead to unnatural or jarring motions.

This status quo significantly hampers the "en masse" applicability of physically simulated humanoids across various domains like animation, gaming, AR/VR, and robotics.

To address these challenges, the paper outlines several key objectives for PULSE:

1. **Achieve Universal Motion Representation:** The central objective is to develop a latent space that encompasses a *comprehensive range of motor skills* rather than being limited to specific movement styles. This universal coverage is envisioned to allow the representation to be reused across a wide spectrum of tasks.
2. **Ensure Physics-Based Plausibility:** Unlike purely kinematic motion models, the representation must be grounded in physics, ensuring that generated motions are stable, realistic, and respect physical constraints. This is achieved by operating within a physics-based control framework.
3. **Enable Efficient Reusability via Hierarchical RL:** The learned latent space should serve as a structured, low-dimensional action space for high-level task policies. This hierarchical approach aims to significantly improve sampling efficiency during reinforcement learning for new tasks, as the high-level policy can explore coherent, human-like behaviors instead of raw joint torques.
4. **Generate Diverse and Stable Human Motion:** The learned latent space should be capable of generating long, stable, and diverse human motions, even from random samples, without requiring complex adversarial rewards for naturalness.
5. **Facilitate Downstream Task Solving:** The representation should demonstrate effectiveness in solving a suite of diverse generative tasks (e.g., locomotion, striking, terrain traversal) and motion tracking tasks (e.g., VR controller tracking) with human-like behavior.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to create a "foundation model for control" for humanoids, providing a pre-trained, versatile, and physically intelligent base layer that significantly reduces the burden of developing new controllers for specific applications, thereby making physically simulated humanoids broadly applicable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of PULSE is structured in a three-stage pipeline: first, learning a highly robust and comprehensive motion imitator, then distilling its extensive motor skills into a probabilistic latent space, and finally leveraging this latent space for hierarchical control in various downstream tasks.

#### 3.1. PHC+: One Policy to Imitate Them All

The foundation of PULSE is an advanced motion imitator called PHC+ (Perpetual Humanoid Controller Plus), building upon the prior work of PHC (Luo et al., 2023). The objective of PHC+ is to achieve 100% success rate in imitating all motion sequences from a large, unstructured motion dataset, specifically the cleaned AMASS training set, and to recover robustly from fallen states.

Key modifications and improvements in PHC+ over the original PHC include:
- **Data Cleaning:** Extensive analysis of the AMASS dataset identified and removed sequences with severe penetration and discontinuity, which previously hindered the imitator's performance due to issues in physical simulation (e.g., large ground reactionary forces) and learning from jarring transitions.
- **Improved Progressive Training Strategy:** Instead of immediately initializing a new primitive for hard-to-imitate sequences, PHC+ allows the current primitive to further improve on its own hard negatives before a new primitive is instantiated. This better utilizes the capacity of each primitive.
- **Architectural Changes:** The use of larger 6-layer MLPs with SiLU activation functions (instead of ReLU) contributed to the policy's ability to learn fail-state recovery and achieve 100% imitation success.

PHC+ is trained using goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning, where the goal state for imitation is the difference between the humanoid's current proprioception and a reference pose from the MoCap data.

#### 3.2. Learning Motion Representation via Online Distillation

Once PHC+ is fully trained and frozen, its comprehensive motor skills are distilled into the PULSE latent space model. This distillation uses an encoder-decoder structure with a *conditional variational information bottleneck*.

The components of PULSE's latent space model are:
- **Variational Encoder (E):** $E(z_t | s_p_t, s_{g-mimic_t}) = N(z_t | \mu^e_t, \sigma^e_t)$. The encoder takes the humanoid's proprioception ($s_p_t$) and the goal state for mimicry ($s_{g-mimic_t}$) to compute a probabilistic distribution (mean and variance) for the latent code $z_t$.
- **Decoder (D):** $D(a_t | s_p_t, z_t)$. The decoder takes the proprioception and a sampled latent code $z_t$ to produce the low-level action $a_t$ (PD targets for the humanoid's joints).
- **Learned Conditional Prior (R):** $R(z_t | s_p_t) = N(z_t | \mu^p_t, \sigma^p_t)$. Crucially, instead of a simple fixed Gaussian prior (as in standard VAEs), PULSE learns a prior conditioned on the humanoid's proprioception. This allows the model to learn context-dependent distributions of motor skills, enabling more expressive and efficient sampling later.

The training objective for PULSE is based on the evidence lower bound (ELBO), comprising three loss terms:
- **Action Reconstruction Loss ($L_{action}$):** This is a supervised loss, minimizing the L2 difference between the action produced by PULSE's decoder ($a_t$) and the action produced by the expert PHC+ teacher policy ($a_{PHC+_t}$). This is the core of the distillation process.
- **KL Divergence Loss ($L_{KL}$):** This term encourages the latent distribution output by the encoder to be close to the distribution learned by the conditional prior.
- **Regularization Term ($L_{regu}$):** $L_{regu} = \|\mu^e_t - \mu^e_{t-1}\|_2^2$. This term penalizes large deviations between consecutive latent codes, encouraging a smoother and more continuous latent space. This is critical for making the latent space navigable by downstream RL policies.

The training data for PULSE are pairs of (state, goal, action, teacher action) collected by rolling out the nascent PULSE policy in the environment for the motion imitation task and then querying the frozen PHC+ teacher to provide the expert actions. A progressive training strategy, similar to PHC+, is also used to balance hard and simple motion sequences. The KL divergence coefficient ($\beta$) is annealed during training.

#### 3.3. Hierarchical Control for Downstream Tasks

Once PULSE's encoder, decoder, and prior are trained, the decoder D and prior R are frozen. They then serve as a fixed low-level controller for new downstream tasks. A high-level task-specific policy ($\pi_{task}$) is trained using PPO, where its "action" is now a latent code in the $z_t$ space.

A key aspect of this hierarchical control is the use of the *learnable prior* R for more efficient exploration:
- The high-level policy $\pi_{task}$ generates a residual latent action $z_{task_t}$.
- This residual is *added* to the mean of the prior's latent code ($\mu^p_t$) computed from the humanoid's proprioception: $a_{task_t} = D(\pi_{task}(z_{task_t} | s_p_t, s_{g-task_t}) + \mu^p_t)$.
- This "residual action" formulation ensures that the high-level policy samples coherent, human-like motions (biased by the prior) rather than exploring purely random or physically implausible actions. This significantly improves sampling efficiency and leads to more natural behaviors.

The methodology integrates state-of-the-art imitation learning with a sophisticated latent space design, emphasizing robustness, large-scale data handling, and physically plausible skill transfer for diverse applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling results across various tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness and universality of PULSE.

#### 4.1. Motion Imitation Quality

- **PHC+ Performance:** The improved PHC+ achieves a remarkable 100% success rate on the cleaned AMASS training data (compared to 98.9% for original PHC) and 99.2% on the test set (vs. 97.1%). This indicates that PHC+ is a highly robust and comprehensive teacher policy capable of imitating a vast array of human motions.
- **PULSE Distillation Success:** After distillation from PHC+, PULSE retains most of the motor skills. It achieves a 99.8% success rate on the AMASS training data and 97.1% on the test data. This minor degradation in performance (similar to a VAE's non-zero reconstruction error) suggests that the variational bottleneck effectively compresses motor skills while preserving high fidelity, confirming that the learned latent space (decoder D) inherits the necessary skills for diverse motions.

#### 4.2. Downstream Tasks

**Generative Tasks (Speed, Reach, Strike, Terrain Traversal):**
- **Superior Performance:** Policies trained using PULSE's latent space as the low-level controller consistently outperform all baselines, including policies trained from scratch and other state-of-the-art latent space models like ASE and CALM. PULSE achieves higher normalized returns across the board.
- **Faster Convergence:** PULSE-based policies converge significantly faster compared to baselines, especially for complex tasks like terrain traversal. This highlights the efficiency gained by exploring a meaningful, pre-structured latent space of motor skills.
- **Human-like Behavior Without Adversarial Rewards:** A key qualitative finding is that PULSE enables humanoids to solve complex tasks like terrain traversal using human-like behaviors (e.g., jumping over stairs) even with simplistic rewards, without needing additional style or adversarial rewards (which are often required by other methods like PACER). This is attributed to the learned prior guiding exploration towards natural movements.
- **Frequency Importance:** 30Hz latent spaces (like PULSE and 30Hz versions of baselines) generally outperform 6Hz versions, indicating that finer-grained control in the latent space is beneficial for performance.

**VR Controller Tracking:**
- **Strong Tracking Accuracy:** PULSE significantly outperforms other latent space models (ASE, CALM) in terms of tracking error (E_g-mpjpe, E_mpjpe) across AMASS training, test, and real-world datasets. This demonstrates PULSE's ability to handle free-form, unscripted human motion.
- **Competitive with Training from Scratch:** PULSE's tracking results are comparable to training a specialized policy from scratch. While "scratch" sometimes achieves slightly better raw precision (which is expected for a specialized policy), PULSE maintains high accuracy and good stability.
- **Faster Convergence:** Similar to generative tasks, policies using PULSE's latent space converge faster for VR controller tracking than training from scratch, implying that the universal latent space does not handicap exploration despite its generality.

#### 4.3. Random Motion Generation

- By sampling from the learned prior R and decoding, PULSE can generate long, stable, and physically plausible human motions.
- It qualitatively outperforms kinematics-based models like HuMoR by avoiding physically implausible motions.
- Compared to other physics-based latent spaces (ASE, CALM), PULSE generates more diverse and natural motions, confirming its broader coverage of motor skills.
- The variance of input noise can be controlled to bias the generation process towards smoother or more energetic motions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on the challenging VR controller tracking task reveal the importance of several design choices:
- **Regularization Term ($L_{regu}$):** Penalizing large deviations of consecutive latent codes (R2 vs. R1, R6 vs. R3) is crucial. Without it, the latent space can become discontinuous and difficult for RL policies to explore efficiently.
- **Learned Conditional Prior (R):** The learnable prior (R6 vs. R2) is vital. Without it, sampling for downstream tasks becomes difficult, leading to significantly worse performance.
- **Residual Action Formulation (Eq. 4):** Using the learned prior's mean as a baseline for the high-level policy's action (R6 vs. R5) is essential for effective exploration. Without this "residual action," the latent space is too hard to navigate, akin to issues observed with CALM without its style reward.
- **Pure Distillation:** Mixing RL training objectives with online supervised distillation (R6 vs. R4) has a negative impact. The best results are achieved through pure supervised distillation from the expert teacher PHC+.

Overall, the results strongly support the paper's central claims: PULSE provides a versatile, physically plausible, and broadly applicable motion representation that enhances the efficiency and naturalness of learning humanoid control policies across diverse tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PULSE represents a significant step towards creating truly versatile and reusable intelligent agents in simulation. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating the feasibility of learning a **universal motion representation** for physics-based humanoid control, effectively positioning it as a "foundation model for control." This concept, mirroring the success of large foundation models in natural language processing and computer vision, suggests a paradigm shift in how humanoid controllers are developed.

The potential impacts are far-reaching:

1. **Increased Efficiency and Accessibility:** By providing a pre-trained, comprehensive latent space of motor skills, PULSE drastically reduces the need for extensive task-specific data collection and training from scratch. This democratizes the creation of physically simulated humanoids, making it more accessible to researchers, developers, and animators without requiring deep expertise in reinforcement learning or physics-based simulation.
2. **Enhanced Realism and Naturalness:** The ability to generate human-like behaviors for complex tasks without relying on adversarial rewards is a major advantage. This simplifies reward design and ensures that simulated characters exhibit believable, physically plausible movements, even in novel or challenging scenarios like navigating complex terrains.
3. **Robustness and Recovery:** Building on the PHC+ foundation, PULSE inherits its impressive ability to recover from fallen states and maintain stability across diverse motions. This robustness is critical for applications where humanoids must interact dynamically with environments and users.
4. **Advancement in AR/VR and Gaming:** The direct application to VR controller tracking is particularly impactful for Meta's Reality Labs. PULSE offers a solution for creating highly responsive and realistic avatars that faithfully track sparse user inputs, which is essential for immersive AR/VR experiences. In gaming, it could enable more dynamic and diverse character animations and AI behaviors with less manual effort.
5. **Foundation for Embodied AI and Robotics:** For embodied AI and robotics, a universal motion representation provides a powerful prior for learning complex manipulation and locomotion skills. It could significantly speed up the development of robots that exhibit human-like dexterity and movement patterns, particularly in human-centric environments.
6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper identifies clear avenues for future work, such as learning more human-interpretable motion representations, incorporating scene information, and modeling human-object interactions (including articulated fingers). PULSE provides a strong baseline and framework for these next-generation challenges, propelling the field towards more intelligent and adaptive virtual agents.

In summary, PULSE moves beyond specialized, task-specific controllers to offer a versatile, physically intelligent, and reusable core for humanoid motion. Its successful demonstration across generative and tracking tasks underscores its potential to streamline development, enhance realism, and expand the practical applicability of physically simulated characters across numerous domains.
