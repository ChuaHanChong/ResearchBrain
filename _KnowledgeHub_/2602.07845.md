# Recurrent-Depth VLA: Implicit Test-Time Compute Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models via Latent Iterative Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Recurrent-Depth VLA (RD-VLA) introduces a method for Vision-Language-Action models to implicitly scale test-time computation through iterative latent-space reasoning, employing a weight-tied recurrent core. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance with 93.0% average success on the LIBERO benchmark, up to an 80x inference speedup, and demonstrates robust real-world application, all while maintaining a constant memory footprint.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models traditionally operate with a fixed computational depth, applying the same compute to both simple and complex robotic tasks.
- Existing reasoning-centric VLAs rely on explicit token-based reasoning, which is inefficient for continuous action spaces and leads to linear memory scaling with reasoning length.
- Deliberation in current reasoning VLAs occurs in discrete output space, causing information bottlenecks and quantization noise during cyclical re-projection from continuous latent representations.

## Method

- Introduces Recurrent-Depth VLA (**RD-VLA**), which utilizes a ==weight-tied recurrent transformer== core for implicit, iterative reasoning within a continuous latent space.
- The model employs a latent "scratchpad" that is iteratively refined by the recurrent core, grounded by **VLM** features, ensuring representational stability.
- An adaptive stopping criterion, based on the ==Kullback-Leibler divergence== of action distributions, dynamically scales test-time computation by determining the required number of recurrent iterations.

## Results

- RD-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO benchmark with **93.0%** average success, outperforming larger token-reasoning models.
- The adaptive computation strategy reduces average inference compute by **34%** while maintaining competitive performance, and provides up to an **80x** inference speedup over prior reasoning-based VLAs.
- The model demonstrates superior robustness in real-world bimanual robot tasks and achieves the highest average task chain length of 3.39 on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, indicating enhanced sequential planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic reasoning can be efficiently performed in a continuous latent space through iterative refinement, rather than relying on explicit token generation in output space.
> - A weight-tied recurrent transformer block enables variable computational depth at test time with a constant memory footprint, by repeatedly applying the same neural circuitry.
> - Training with randomized recurrence and truncated backpropagation through time encourages the model to learn a robust iterative refinement process capable of converging from noisy initial states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Recurrent-Depth VLA" positions itself at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and efficient, adaptive computation for robotic control. It directly addresses a critical limitation in current VLA models: their fixed computational depth.

**Evolution of VLA Models:**
The field of VLA models has rapidly evolved, driven by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Language Models (VLMs). Initial efforts focused on developing generalist robot policies, such as RT-1, RT-2, and OpenVLA, which leverage large-scale internet data and transformer architectures to map visual observations and language instructions directly to robot actions. These models, while impressive in their generalization capabilities, typically operate with a fixed computational budget for each control step, treating simple adjustments and complex multi-step manipulations with the same processing intensity.

**Emergence of Reasoning-Centric VLAs:**
To overcome the brittleness and limited latent reasoning of early VLAs, a new wave of "reasoning-centric" VLAs emerged. These models, including CoT-VLA, ThinkAct, MolmoAct, and TraceVLA, incorporate explicit intermediate thinking processes. This reasoning is often achieved through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, generating textual reasoning steps (pseudo-CoT labels) or visual reasoning aids (sub-goal images or 2D traces).

**Limitations of Existing Reasoning Approaches:**
The authors identify several fundamental limitations in these current reasoning-centric VLAs:
1. **Token-level reasoning:** The thinking process is tied to explicit token generation, which is inefficient for the continuous action spaces inherent in robotic control. This requires custom reasoning datasets and linear memory scaling with the length of the reasoning chain.
2. **Output-space reasoning:** Reasoning occurs in the output space (e.g., text, discretized actions). This forces a cyclical re-projection between the model's continuous internal latent space and lossy, discretized output representations. This creates information bottlenecks, quantization noise, and limits reasoning fidelity to the resolution of the output vocabulary rather than the continuous physical dynamics.

**Inspiration from Biology and Recurrent Transformers:**
The paper draws inspiration from biological cognition, which features recurrent neural dynamics that iteratively refine sensory inputs into deliberative representations. This connects to the growing interest in **recurrent transformers**, an architectural paradigm where layers are repeatedly reused to scale computation at test time with a constant memory footprint. While diffusion policies also involve multi-step refinement, they are generative sampling techniques rather than deliberative ones that enrich scene representations.

**RD-VLA's Position:**
RD-VLA proposes a paradigm shift by moving robotic reasoning from the discrete output space to a continuous latent space. It aims to achieve computational adaptivity through *implicit latent iterative refinement* rather than explicit token generation. By employing a recurrent, weight-tied action head, RD-VLA seeks to enable arbitrary inference depth with constant memory, dynamically allocating compute based on task complexity. This work aims to provide a scalable, memory-efficient, and fast alternative to token-based reasoning, bridging the gap between fixed-compute policies and the computational overhead of explicit reasoning models. It directly contributes to the ongoing challenge of developing more intelligent, adaptable, and resource-efficient robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "Recurrent-Depth VLA" is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of **adaptive, test-time computation scaling** for visuomotor control, mimicking the efficiency and flexibility observed in human cognition.

The key motivations driving this research are multi-faceted:

1. **Addressing Inefficient Fixed Computational Budgets:** Current VLA models operate with a fixed computational depth, applying the same amount of compute to both trivial adjustments (e.g., nudging an object) and complex, multi-step manipulations (e.g., high-precision navigation in clutter). This is inherently inefficient. Humans, in contrast, adaptively reallocate cognitive resources, expending low effort for simple tasks and increasing deliberation for complex ones. The paper aims to imbue VLA models with this human-like ability to scale "compute" to task complexity.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Token-Based Reasoning:** While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in reasoning-centric VLAs allows for variable computation, it suffers from several drawbacks:
 - **Memory Scaling:** CoT linearly scales memory requirements with the length of the reasoning chain.
 - **Continuous Action Spaces:** It is ill-suited for the continuous action spaces required for fine-grained robotic control, as physical manipulation is often subtle and less conducive to tokenization.
 - **Overhead:** Tokenizing physical dynamics carries significant overhead, demanding the curation of custom reasoning datasets and increasing computational burden.

3. **Eliminating Information Bottlenecks from Output-Space Reasoning:** Existing reasoning-based VLAs perform deliberation in the output space (e.g., text, depth bins, coordinates). This necessitates iterative transitions between the model's high-dimensional latent space and a discretized, low-bandwidth output space. This cyclical re-projection is fundamentally non-ideal, causing a loss of information and introducing quantization noise, which limits the fidelity of reasoning to the resolution of the output vocabulary rather than the continuous dynamics of the environment. The authors are motivated to keep the reasoning process entirely within a continuous latent space.

4. **Drawing Inspiration from Biological Recurrence and Recurrent Transformers:** The iterative nature of biological systems, where the same neural circuitry is repeatedly recruited for progressively refined representations, serves as a strong motivation. Analogously, the concept of recurrent transformers (or looped transformers) offers an architectural mechanism for iteratively processing information within neural networks, allowing for test-time scaling with constant memory. The paper seeks to apply this principle to the VLA domain.

5. **Achieving Scalable and Resource-Efficient Robotic Control:** Ultimately, the goal is to develop a VLA framework that can provide high-precision robotic control efficiently. This means achieving deep, adaptive computation when needed, without the substantial memory and speed penalties of prior reasoning methods, thus providing a more practical and scalable path for advanced robotics.

In essence, the paper aims to redefine how VLA models "think" – moving away from explicit, tokenized, and memory-intensive output-space reasoning towards implicit, continuous, and memory-constant latent-space refinement, enabling robots to dynamically adjust their cognitive effort based on task demands.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Recurrent-Depth VLA (RD-VLA) lies in its novel architecture that enables implicit, iterative reasoning within a continuous latent space, contrasting with explicit token generation or output-space refinement.

**A. Architectural Backbone and Token Flow:**
RD-VLA is designed to be backbone-agnostic, integrating with any Vision-Language Model (VLM) producing dense latent representations. For this work, it uses a VLM based on Qwen2.5-0.5B (from MiniVLA), which utilizes a frozen DINOv2 and SigLIP fused vision encoder.

1. **Vision Encoder:** Processes image observations (e.g., 256 vision tokens per image, 512 for main and wrist cameras) and projects them into the VLM.
2. **VLM Backbone (Qwen2-0.5B):** A 24-layer LLM fine-tuned via LoRA. Input augmented with 64 learned latent embeddings acting as specialized grounded placeholders that attend to the multimodal context.
3. **Representation Extraction:** After VLM execution, hidden states are partitioned into:
 - **Task/Vision representations (h_vis):** Capturing spatial and semantic scene information.
 - **Latent-specific representations (h_lat):** Compressed, task-aligned features from the latent token positions.
 These are combined to form a static conditioning manifold `[h^(24)_vis+lat; p]`, where `p` is robot proprioception.

**B. Recurrent-Depth Architecture:**
The RD-VLA action head decouples computational depth from fixed VLM constraints by shifting the burden to a weight-tied recurrent transformer core. This head is structured into three functional components:

1. **Prelude (P_ϕ):** A non-recurrent interface that takes `K=8` learned queries. These queries self-attend, then cross-attend to the VLM's *middle-layer* visual features (`h^(12)_vis+lat`) to create a `grounded latent foundation (S_pre)`.
2. **Latent Scratchpad (S):** An initialized `R^(KxD)` tensor, drawn from a high-entropy truncated normal distribution. This `S_0` acts as a "noisy workspace" that the model must iteratively refine, ensuring it learns a stable refinement operator rather than overfitting to an initial state.
3. **Recurrent Core (R_θ):** The heart of the iterative refinement. This is a **weight-tied transformer block** that recursively updates the scratchpad.
 - **Input Injection:** At each iteration `k`, the previous scratchpad state `S_(k-1)` is concatenated with the fixed `S_pre` (from the Prelude) along the feature dimension. This ensures representational stability and prevents "representational collapse" over long unrolls by persistently grounding the latent reasoning in observations.
 - This combined input is then mapped back to the manifold dimension via a learned adapter and RMSNorm to form `x_k`.
 - **Refinement:** `S_k = R_θ(x_k, [h^(24)_vis+lat; p])`. The recurrent block performs bidirectional self-attention on `x_k`, followed by gated cross-attention, where queries are from `x_k` and keys/values are from the concatenated conditioning manifold `[h^(24)_vis+lat; p]` (VLM's *final-layer* features and proprioception).
 - This process iteratively refines internal representations within a fixed-dimensional latent space.
4. **Coda (C_ψ):** A non-recurrent interface that processes the converged scratchpad `S_r` (after `r` iterations). It performs the final decoding by attending to `S_r`, self-attention, and high-level VLM features (`h^(24)_vis+lat`).
5. **Action Projection:** A final linear layer `W_out` maps the refined features from the Coda to the robot's continuous action space `a`.

**C. Training with Randomized Recurrence (TBPTT):**
To encourage convergence to a steady state irrespective of the starting depth, the number of iterations `N` during training is sampled from a heavy-tailed log-normal Poisson distribution. The model is trained using **Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (TBPTT)**, where gradients are propagated through only the final `d=8` iterations, with earlier steps having detached gradients. This forces the network to learn a robust iterative refinement process that can start from a noisy state and converge effectively.

**D. Adaptive Computation (Inference):**
At inference time, RD-VLA dynamically determines the number of recurrent iterations `k*` using an adaptive stopping criterion. This criterion is based on the **Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence** between the action distributions of consecutive iterations, approximated by the Mean Squared Error (MSE) in the action space: `||a_k - a_(k-1)||^2 < δ`. The inference loop terminates when this criterion is met, allowing the model to self-regulate compute based on its internal convergence.

**E. Adaptive Execution:**
To couple reasoning depth with action execution, two strategies are proposed:
1. **Threshold-Based Adaptive Execution:** If the required iterations `k*` exceed a threshold `τ`, the execution horizon is truncated to a shorter duration (`H_short`), assuming higher uncertainty. Otherwise, a longer horizon (`H_long`) is used.
2. **Linear Decay Execution:** The execution horizon decreases linearly with increasing reasoning depth, ensuring the agent replans more frequently in complex states.

This comprehensive methodology allows RD-VLA to achieve flexible, implicit, and efficient reasoning within a compact latent space, a significant departure from prior explicit reasoning approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on challenging manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world provide compelling evidence for the efficacy and efficiency of Recurrent-Depth VLA (RD-VLA).

**A. Performance Scaling via Recurrent Computation (LIBERO):**
- **Log-linear Improvement:** The model's performance on the LIBERO benchmark exhibits a clear log-linear improvement with increased computational depth (number of recurrent iterations).
- **Significant Gains:** Starting from near-random performance at N_inf = 1 (8.4% average success), RD-VLA achieves substantial gains: 40.5% at N_inf = 2, 84.1% at N_inf = 4, and 92.6% at N_inf = 8.
- **Saturation Point:** Performance saturates between 8 and 12 iterations, with a peak of 93.1% at N_inf = 24. Scaling beyond 12 iterations yields diminishing returns, indicating an optimal compute budget for the tasks.
- **Criticality of Recurrence:** Tasks that fail entirely (0% success) with single-iteration inference can achieve over 90% success with four or more iterations, demonstrating that recurrent depth is critical for complex tasks.

**B. Necessity of Task-Dependent Computation:**
- **Distinct Convergence Profiles:** Individual tasks exhibit distinct convergence behaviors, highlighting that different tasks require varying levels of computational complexity.
- **Emergent Adaptivity:** The required number of iterations emerges naturally from the task context, rather than being prescribed. Some tasks achieve high success with just two iterations, while others require three or more before showing meaningful success, motivating the adaptive computation strategy.

**C. Efficacy of Adaptive Computation Strategies:**
- **Comparable Performance, Reduced Compute:** Adaptive computation strategies maintain parity with the best fixed-depth models while significantly reducing average inference cost. For example, the Binary Adaptation strategy with τ = 5 × 10^(-4) achieves a 92.5% success rate (comparable to 93.0% peak of fixed N=12) using an average of only 7.93 iterations, representing a **34% reduction in compute**.
- **Dynamic Adjustment:** The model dynamically adjusts the number of recurrent steps based on the perceived difficulty of the current state, with Spatial tasks requiring more iterations (μ = 11.8) than Object (μ = 9.8) or Goal (μ = 9.0) tasks.
- **Robustness of Adaptive Approaches:** All three adaptive strategies (Binary Adaptation, Linear Decay, Pure KL) perform comparably at matched compute budgets, suggesting that the principle of condition-dependent computation allocation is key.

**D. Performance Against Other Baselines:**
- **State-of-the-Art on LIBERO:** RD-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance of **93.0% average success** on the LIBERO benchmark with fixed recurrence (0.5B parameters), significantly outperforming all prior methods, including larger 7B+ parameter token-reasoning models (e.g., Fast-ThinkAct at 89.7%). The adaptive variant maintains competitive performance (92.5%).
- **Strong Long-Horizon Generalization on CALVIN:** On the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, RD-VLA achieves the highest average task chain length of **3.39**, surpassing OpenVLA (3.27) and all other baselines. This indicates that latent refinement effectively enhances the model's sequential planning capabilities.
- **Inference Speedup:** RD-VLA provides up to **80x inference speedup** over prior reasoning-based VLA models, combined with a constant memory footprint.

**E. Real-World Experiments:**
- **Superior Robustness:** Deployed on a bimanual YAM robot arm, RD-VLA demonstrates superior robustness across challenging household tasks (cube placement, dish wiping, towel folding, bread toasting). It consistently outperforms Diffusion Policy and π0.5 baselines.
- **Viability of Dynamic Computation:** The fixed 8-iteration RD-VLA achieves near-perfect performance on dish wiping, while the adaptive variant (Pure KL with τ = 10^(-4)) maintains competitive performance and even achieves the highest score on cube placement. This validates the viability and robustness of dynamic computation in physical settings.

In summary, the main findings confirm that RD-VLA successfully integrates latent iterative reasoning into VLA models, leading to significant improvements in task success, computational efficiency, and generalization across diverse manipulation benchmarks and real-world scenarios. The ability to dynamically scale compute without increasing memory footprint and to adapt to task complexity represents a critical advancement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Recurrent-Depth VLA (RD-VLA) marks a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics, with profound implications for the design and deployment of intelligent robotic systems.

**A. Novel Paradigm for Robotic Reasoning:**
RD-VLA is presented as the first VLA model to scale test-time computation through implicit latent-space reasoning via a weight-tied recurrent core. This shifts the paradigm of robotic reasoning from explicit, token-based, and often memory-intensive output-space generation to a continuous, iterative refinement within the latent representation space. This fundamental architectural change is highly significant, offering a more natural and efficient way for robots to "think" about their actions.

**B. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:**
One of the most impactful contributions of RD-VLA is its ability to achieve variable computational depth at test time while maintaining a **constant memory footprint**. Unlike Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which scales memory linearly with reasoning length, RD-VLA's weight-tied recurrent block allows for arbitrary inference depth with minimal parameter overhead due to weight reuse. This efficiency translates directly to practical benefits, including an impressive **up to 80x inference speedup** over prior reasoning-based VLA models, making it far more suitable for real-time robotic control.

**C. Adaptive and Human-like Cognition:**
The model's ability to dynamically allocate compute based on task complexity, using an adaptive stopping criterion based on latent convergence, is a crucial step towards human-like cognitive efficiency in robots. This "think longer for harder tasks" capability means that robots can expend minimal computational resources on simple, reflexive actions and dedicate more deliberation to complex, uncertain scenarios. This adaptive behavior emerges naturally from the recurrent architecture, removing the need for explicit supervision of iteration counts or curated reasoning traces.

**D. Improved Performance and Generalization:**
Empirically, RD-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks like LIBERO and CALVIN, outperforming existing end-to-end and token-reasoning VLAs, often with a significantly smaller model backbone (0.5B parameters vs. 7B+). Its strong performance on long-horizon tasks, as demonstrated by the CALVIN benchmark, suggests enhanced sequential planning capabilities. Furthermore, successful deployment on real-world bimanual robot tasks validates its robustness and practical applicability, even outperforming strong baselines like Diffusion Policy and π0.5.

**E. Foundation for Robust and Reliable Robotics:**
The exposure of internal state dynamics within recurrent architectures provides new avenues for **uncertainty quantification**. By monitoring latent convergence, the model can implicitly measure its own certainty, potentially enabling future test-time interventions such as autonomously halting execution or requesting human assistance if uncertainty levels exceed safety thresholds. This capability could be foundational for building more reliable and safe autonomous robotic systems.

**F. Opening New Research Avenues:**
RD-VLA "opens a new design space for efficient, reasoning-capable robotic policies." Future research can delve into:
- **Scaling Laws:** Investigating how latent recurrence scales with larger backbones and more diverse datasets.
- **Optimal Adaptive Compute Regimes:** Fine-tuning and exploring more sophisticated adaptive stopping criteria and execution schedules.
- **Hybrid Approaches:** Combining recurrent depth with token-based CoT reasoning for synergistic benefits.
- **Depth Generalization:** Addressing limitations regarding performance degradation beyond an optimal number of iterations.

In conclusion, RD-VLA represents a pivotal step towards developing the next generation of resource-efficient, robust, and intelligently adaptable foundation models in robotics. By moving reasoning into the continuous latent space, it offers a scalable and effective solution to the long-standing challenge of matching computational effort to task complexity, paving the way for more capable and autonomous robots in diverse real-world applications.
