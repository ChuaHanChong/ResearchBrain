# Walk the PLANC: Physics-Guided RL for Agile Humanoid Locomotion on Constrained Footholds

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named PLANC integrates a physics-guided reduced-order model with reinforcement learning, utilizing Control Lyapunov Function rewards and a teacher-student architecture, to enable agile and robust humanoid locomotion over constrained and discontinuous footholds. The approach achieved 100% success on challenging stepping-stone terrains in simulation and demonstrated zero-shot generalization on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based control methods for bipedal locomotion are brittle to perception noise and modeling errors, limiting their robustness in real-world, unstructured environments.
- Pure reinforcement learning (RL) struggles with sample efficiency and satisfying hard physical constraints for precise foot placement on sparse, constrained footholds, often leading to unnatural gaits or instability.
- Achieving both the precision required for navigating discontinuous terrain and the adaptability to unknown disturbances remains a significant challenge for humanoid robots.

## Method

- A hybrid framework, PLANC, integrates a reduced-order model (==Linear Inverted Pendulum== with virtual slopes) for real-time generation of dynamically consistent reference trajectories (Center of Mass, swing foot, step timing).
- Reinforcement learning is guided by Control Lyapunov Function (CLF)-based rewards that enforce tracking of these physics-guided references, injecting stability guarantees into the learning process.
- A two-stage teacher-student distillation and curriculum learning approach is used, where a privileged teacher policy is distilled into a student, then fine-tuned on procedurally generated terrains with increasing difficulty and perturbations.

## Results

- The framework achieved **100%** success on challenging flat stepping stones and **97.217%** on high-difficulty height-varying terrains in simulation, significantly outperforming an end-to-end RL baseline which failed on complex terrains.
- Hardware experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid demonstrated stable and repeatable stepping patterns, showing successful zero-shot generalization to unseen mixed-terrain courses with irregular stone depths.
- Ablation studies confirmed that dynamic step timing and vertical CoM momentum regulation are crucial components for high performance, with their removal leading to noticeable performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating dynamically consistent model-based references via CLF rewards significantly accelerates RL training and enables precise, stable locomotion on challenging constrained footholds where pure RL struggles.
> - Dynamic adaptation of step timing and vertical CoM momentum regulation, derived from the reduced-order model, are crucial for robust traversal of height-varying and discontinuous terrains.
> - A teacher-student distillation framework combined with curriculum learning allows complex, precise behaviors learned with privileged information to be transferred to a generalizable policy without it, facilitating sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The challenge of robust bipedal locomotion, especially over discontinuous and constrained footholds like stepping stones or beams, represents a long-standing frontier in robotics. This paper positions itself at the intersection of two dominant paradigms in robot control: model-based control and reinforcement learning (RL).

Traditionally, model-based control methods excel at generating precise, physically consistent motions that adhere to kinematic, dynamic, and contact constraints. Approaches rooted in reduced-order models (RoMs) like the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) and centroidal dynamics, combined with optimization techniques (e.g., Mixed-Integer Convex Optimization for footstep planning or Model Predictive Control), have achieved impressive feats. However, as the authors highlight, these methods suffer from significant drawbacks:
- **Brittleness to Perception:** They demand highly accurate mathematical representations of terrain geometry, making them fragile in the face of noisy or incomplete sensor data.
- **Computational Burden:** Unifying footstep planning and full-body control in a single optimization framework for high-DoF humanoids often leads to computationally intensive, non-convex problems, often restricting solutions to quasi-static motions.
- **Modeling Errors:** Reliance on perfect models makes them susceptible to real-world discrepancies (e.g., unmodeled friction, actuator limits).

In contrast, deep reinforcement learning has emerged as a powerful alternative, capable of learning robust and adaptive policies directly from high-dimensional sensor data, often showing strong resilience to disturbances and modeling errors. RL has demonstrated success on continuous rough terrain. Yet, for tasks requiring *precision* on *sparse* footholds, end-to-end RL faces its own set of challenges:
- **Sample Inefficiency:** Discovering precise foot placement and complex step sequencing from scratch, especially for bipedal robots that are inherently unstable, is incredibly sample-inefficient.
- **Difficulty with Hard Constraints:** Standard RL often struggles to satisfy hard physical constraints, tending to prioritize survival over the exact precision needed for discrete footholds.
- **Unnatural Gaits:** Policies might converge to stable but unnatural or period-two gaits that exploit reward structures rather than achieving truly dynamic and agile motion.
- **Lack of Generalization:** Without structured guidance, policies might overfit to specific terrains and fail to generalize.

The current work directly addresses this "dichotomy." It contributes to a growing trend of "guided RL" or "physics-guided RL," which seeks to combine the strengths of both paradigms. Rather than relying solely on reward shaping or purely data-driven exploration, these hybrid approaches leverage model-based priors, human demonstrations, or structured references to "steer" the learning process. Previous guided RL works have used human trajectory references or model-based references to constrain the learning manifold. This paper specifically advances the model-guided RL approach by integrating dynamically consistent references from a reduced-order planner via Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) rewards, a novel and theoretically sound method for injecting stability guarantees into the learning process. This allows RL to focus on refining contact dynamics and disturbance rejection while the model handles the fundamental planning of feasible, constrained motions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and agile locomotion framework for bipedal humanoid robots traversing constrained and discontinuous footholds, such as stepping stones, beams, and stairs. The authors are specifically motivated by the limitations of existing approaches: the fragility of model-based methods to perception noise and modeling inaccuracies, and the difficulty of purely data-driven reinforcement learning in achieving the necessary precision and consistency for sparse contact tasks.

The core motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridge the Gap:** To combine the precision and constraint-handling capabilities of classical model-based control with the robustness and adaptability of reinforcement learning, thereby overcoming the individual limitations of each.
- **Enhance Reliability on Constrained Footholds:** To enable humanoid robots to reliably navigate environments where foot placement errors can lead to catastrophic failure, a critical requirement for deployment in disaster zones or cluttered industrial settings.
- **Reduce Dependency on Perfect Perception:** To develop a system less reliant on highly accurate and noise-free mathematical representations of terrain geometry, allowing for deployment in more unstructured and uncertain environments.
- **Improve Learning Efficiency and Precision for RL:** To guide the reinforcement learning process with physics-based structure, enabling policies to discover precise foothold placement and appropriate step sequencing more efficiently than end-to-end model-free RL, which often struggles with these sparse-contact scenarios.
- **Provide Dynamically Consistent Motion Targets:** To develop a mechanism (specifically, a reduced-order stepping planner) that can supply dynamically consistent motion targets for the RL agent, including precise foot placement, center of mass (CoM) trajectory, and step timing.
- **Validate on Hardware:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework through extensive simulation and, crucially, real-world hardware experiments on a high-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) humanoid robot.
- **Minimize Human Data Dependency:** To remove the need for extensive human motion dataset collections and complex retargeting pipelines, providing a physically grounded, controllable, and transferable prior for RL in severely constrained environments.

In essence, the authors aim to create a hybrid control system that is both intelligent enough to adapt to unknown disturbances and precise enough to execute highly constrained, dynamic movements, ultimately pushing the capabilities of humanoids in challenging unstructured environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, dubbed "PLANC" (Physics-Guided RL for Agile Humanoid LocomotioN on Constrained Footholds), tightly integrates a reduced-order model (RoM) based planner with a reinforcement learning (RL) framework, using Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) based rewards and a teacher-student distillation architecture.

**A. Model-Based Reference Generation (The Physics-Guided Component)**

At the core of the physics guidance is a reduced-order walking model, specifically a Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) model, extended to account for varying height stepping stones by modeling them as virtual slopes. This planner generates dynamically and environmentally consistent reference trajectories in real-time.

1. **Step Timing (T_s):** The LIP model's closed-form solution (Eq. 4) is used to analytically determine the step duration (T_s) required for the CoM to reach a target pre-impact location relative to the upcoming stepping stone. This allows for dynamic adaptation of step timing based on terrain geometry.
2. **Orbital Energy Regulation via Vertical CoM Control:** The post-impact orbital energy (E+) in the sagittal plane is regulated to a desired value (E*=0.6) by modulating the pre-impact vertical CoM velocity (u_des). This is crucial for ensuring the robot's state remains on a viable manifold for forward progression, calculated using momentum transfer laws from the underactuated dynamics (Eq. 5, 12).
3. **CoM Translation Reference:** Instead of complex MPC, smooth cubic splines are used to generate CoM trajectories parameterized by a phasing variable, ensuring time-normalized trajectories. Boundary conditions are set to satisfy momentum regulation, and lateral balance is maintained using an analytical solution of the Horizontal LIP (HLIP) model.
4. **Swing Foot Translation Reference:** B́ezier polynomials are used to generate smooth swing foot trajectories, interpolating between lift-off and the target foothold, strictly determined by the upcoming stepping stone's geometry, with lateral targets from the HLIP reference.
5. **Other References:** Pelvis and swing foot orientations are aligned with local terrain, and arm joints track nominal, phase-synchronized trajectories for angular momentum cancellation and natural gait.

This planner generates a full state trajectory (desired CoM, swing foot, torso, upper body joints, and holonomic constraints) at every time step, which are then used within the RL training loop.

**B. Reinforcement Learning Environment and Architecture**

The RL component builds upon the model-based references using a hierarchical teacher-student framework.

1. **Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) Rewards:** The generated references are directly embedded into the RL training as CLF-based rewards (Eq. 7, 8). A CLF is designed for output tracking (e.g., CoM position, swing foot pose, torso orientation). The CLF decreasing condition (˙V ≤ −cV) encourages exponential convergence of the actual robot outputs to the desired references, effectively injecting stability guarantees and guiding the policy towards physically consistent motions.
2. **Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP):** The task is formulated as a POMDP. The agent's observations include a command vector, proprioception (base angular velocity, projected gravity, joint positions/velocities), exteroception (local heightmap), and the last action.
3. **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** PPO (Eq. 10) is used as the core RL algorithm to optimize both teacher and student policies, chosen for its stability in policy updates.
4. **Teacher-Student Distillation:** A critical two-stage training pipeline is employed to overcome the difficulty of learning precise, sparse-foothold locomotion from scratch under partial observability:
 - **Teacher Training:** A privileged teacher policy is first trained with access to full CLF-based rewards and ground-truth information (terrain, contact phase).
 - **Distillation (Warm Start):** The teacher's competence is distilled into a student policy via behavior cloning (Eq. 11). This removes the dependency on privileged information and provides the student with a stable, non-privileged walking prior.
 - **Curriculum Fine-tuning:** The initialized student is then fine-tuned using PPO on a procedurally generated stepping-stone curriculum with increasing difficulty, and with injected observation noise and external perturbations to enhance generalization and robustness. This stage uses the same rewards but without teacher supervision or privileged inputs.

**C. Terrain Generation and Curriculum Learning**

- **Procedural Generation:** The training environments feature procedurally generated stepping-stone terrains (upstairs, downstairs, flat, height-varying) with randomized properties (gap distance, height variation, stone dimensions).
- **Difficulty-Indexed Sampling:** A curriculum learning strategy gradually increases terrain difficulty. The agent progresses to harder levels only after three consecutive successful traversals, ensuring monotonic skill development and exposure to a mix of difficulties.

**D. Hardware and Simulation Setup**

Training is performed on a 21-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid in IsaacLab with the IsaacSim physics engine. Domain randomization (inertial properties, contact dynamics, observation noise) and base velocity perturbations are applied to facilitate sim-to-real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded significant findings across quantitative comparisons, ablation studies, simulation evaluations, and real-world hardware transfers, strongly supporting the efficacy of the proposed model-guided RL framework.

**A. Quantitative Results and Comparison with Baselines**

- **Superior Performance over End-to-End Baseline:** The proposed model-guided RL policy and its ablations demonstrated consistently stable learning and rapid convergence to high performance, in stark contrast to a direct end-to-end RL baseline. The baseline, even with pre-training on flat ground, struggled immensely with stepping stones, often collapsing to standing or converging to an unstable, asymmetric period-2 gait (consistent with prior work limitations).
- **High Success Rates:** As detailed in Table I, the proposed method achieved 100% success on challenging flat stepping stones, compared to a mere 56% for the end-to-end RL baseline. It also achieved 100% on mid-difficulty height-varying terrains and 97.217% on high-difficulty height-varying terrains, where the baseline achieved 0% success. This clearly illustrates the critical role of model-based structure in enabling precise, height-aware foot placements.

**B. Ablation Analysis**

Two ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contribution of key model-based components:
1. **Fixed Step Timing (Fixed T = 0.4 s):** Removing dynamic step timing adaptation resulted in noticeable performance degradation, slower improvement, and reduced steady-state CLF reward (Fig. 4, left).
2. **No Vertical CoM Momentum Regulation (Fixed ˙z_com = 0):** Disabling the regulation of post-impact orbital energy through vertical CoM velocity also lowered performance compared to the full model, though less severely than fixed timing.

Despite the degradations, the ablated policies still outperformed the end-to-end baseline, suggesting RL's ability to partially compensate for missing structure. However, the full proposed method achieved the highest CLF reward, indicating better satisfaction of the model-based stability objective and superior performance on the most challenging height-varying tasks (Table I).

**C. Simulation Evaluation (Sim-to-Sim Transfer)**

- **Precise Tracking:** The proposed method exhibited precise tracking of swing foot and CoM trajectories in the sagittal direction (Fig. 5), crucial for hitting constrained footholds and improving performance on subsequent stones.
- **Generalization and Robustness:** The learned policies demonstrated robust generalization capabilities through sim-to-sim transfer in a separate MuJoCo environment. The robot successfully traversed an extended, mixed-terrain course including various stairs, height-varying stones, sparse and narrow footholds, and even out-of-distribution mixed sequences (Fig. 6). This highlights that the reduced-order structure not only accelerates learning but also enhances transferability across simulation platforms.
- **Ablation Sensitivity:** Ablated policies showed reduced sim-to-sim transfer reliability, exhibiting gait irregularities, inaccurate foot placements, undesirable flight phases (Fixed T case), and significant lateral drift (Fixed ˙z_com case) (Fig. 7). This further underscores the stabilizing role of both dynamic step timing and vertical CoM regulation.
- **External Perturbation Robustness:** The policy was also robust to external pushes (±100Nm for 0.2s duration) during stepping-stone traversal, indicating strong disturbance rejection capabilities.

**D. Real-World Transfer (Sim-to-Real)**

- **Successful Hardware Deployment:** The learned policy was successfully deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot using a Qualisys motion capture system for pose tracking and ray-casting for elevation maps.
- **Stable and Repeatable Gait:** Hardware rollouts showed stable and repeatable stepping patterns with consistent swing trajectories and smooth torso motion, closely matching simulation behavior (Fig. 1, 8). This confirms that the model-guided structure enables reliable hardware deployment without additional tuning.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** The policy successfully generalized to a mixed stepping-stone course with irregular stone depths, a configuration entirely absent from the training curriculum. This suggests that the model-guided references prevent overfitting and enable effective zero-shot generalization to unstructured terrain topologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a significant advancement in the field of bipedal humanoid locomotion, particularly for navigating challenging, discontinuous terrains. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Model-Reality Gap:** The most impactful contribution is the effective bridging of the gap between the theoretical precision of model-based control and the practical robustness of data-driven reinforcement learning. By leveraging dynamically consistent references from a reduced-order planner within a CLF-rewarded RL framework, the authors demonstrate a powerful synergy that overcomes the limitations of either approach in isolation. This paradigm offers a blueprint for creating highly capable and reliable robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Precision for Humanoids:** The ability to reliably achieve precise foot placement and maintain balance on constrained footholds is crucial for deploying humanoids in real-world applications such as disaster response, industrial inspection, or assistance in complex environments. This work brings humanoids closer to operating effectively in such scenarios where even minor errors can lead to catastrophic failure.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Generalization in RL:** By providing the RL agent with physics-guided priors, the framework drastically improves learning efficiency and helps the policy converge to physically plausible and robust gaits. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen and out-of-distribution terrains is particularly impactful, suggesting that the learned policies are not merely memorizing specific patterns but developing a deep, transferable understanding of dynamic balance and stepping strategy.
- **Reduced Dependency on Human Data:** The removal of reliance on human motion datasets for generating prior behaviors simplifies the development pipeline and makes the approach more scalable and adaptable to various robot morphologies and task requirements. The model-based guidance provides a powerful, physics-grounded alternative.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful deployment on physical hardware, without additional tuning, is a testament to the framework's robustness and the effectiveness of domain randomization and the principled model-guided approach. This is a critical step towards practical applications of learned robot control.
- **Advancing Guided RL Methodologies:** The specific integration of CLF-based rewards with a reduced-order model provides a theoretically sound and empirically validated method for injecting stability and performance guarantees into the RL objective function. This novel application of CLFs within an RL reward structure sets a new benchmark for guided learning in complex dynamical systems.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to open-source the entire codebase will undoubtedly accelerate future research and allow other groups to build upon these advancements, fostering a collaborative environment for progress in humanoid robotics.

In conclusion, "Walk the PLANC" significantly pushes the frontier of humanoid locomotion by demonstrating a robust, agile, and generalizable control framework for constrained environments. Its principled integration of model-based planning with data-driven learning, validated extensively in simulation and on hardware, lays critical groundwork for the next generation of intelligent and capable bipedal robots.
