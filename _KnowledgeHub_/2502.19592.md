# RAMEN: Real-time Asynchronous Multi-agent Neural Implicit Mapping

> [!summary] Summary
> RAMEN introduces a real-time asynchronous multi-agent neural implicit mapping system that enables robust scene reconstruction despite unreliable communication. This system, demonstrated as the first real-world implementation of its kind, achieved over 91% average completion ratio in simulations under 50% communication success and successfully converged in hardware experiments where synchronous methods failed.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent neural implicit mapping methods (e.g., DiNNO) rely on synchronous communication, which is impractical for real-world robotic deployments due to network delays, packet loss, and limited bandwidth.
- Synchronous communication often requires 'barriers' that halt optimization, forcing faster agents to wait for slower ones, leading to significant delays and hindering real-time operation.
- Efficiently merging the compact, neural network-based representations of neural implicit maps across multiple robots is challenging, as parameters cannot be simply averaged.

## Method

- **RAMEN** extends single-agent neural implicit mapping by introducing an uncertainty-weighted decentralized ==Consensus Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (C-ADMM)== algorithm.
- It incorporates weight matrices into the C-ADMM formulation, reflecting the relative reliability and uncertainty of information exchanged between neighboring agents.
- Uncertainty is quantified using a ==frequency-based== method that counts the number of non-zero gradient updates each ==feature grid== parameter receives, indicating how frequently a spatial region has been observed and refined.

## Results

- In simulations, RAMEN consistently achieved the best overall performance, with an average completion ratio over **91%** under **50%** communication success, outperforming synchronous methods like DiNNO.
- RAMEN demonstrated resilience to severe communication disruptions, reconstructing scenes effectively even at a **20%** communication success rate, whereas synchronous approaches largely failed or produced incomplete results.
- The paper presented the first real-world demonstration of multi-robot neural implicit mapping, where RAMEN successfully enabled **two** Turtlebots to reconstruct a complete scene despite highly asynchronous communication and noisy sensor data, a scenario where synchronous methods entirely failed to converge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Communication in real-world multi-robot systems is inherently asynchronous and unreliable, necessitating robust approaches beyond synchronous distributed optimization.
> - Incorporating uncertainty weighting into decentralized Consensus Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (C-ADMM) allows agents to effectively leverage outdated or partial information without degrading map quality.
> - A simple frequency-based uncertainty quantification method, based on counting non-zero gradient updates for feature grid parameters, can effectively guide consensus in neural implicit mapping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of multi-robot systems, real-time mapping, and neural implicit representations.

**Multi-Robot Systems and Collaborative Mapping:** The ability of multiple robots to cooperatively explore and map an environment is fundamental for diverse applications, including autonomous driving, warehouse automation, and search-and-survey missions. Traditional multi-robot mapping typically involves sharing explicit local maps (e.g., point clouds, occupancy grids) or raw sensor data, which can be communication-intensive. The primary challenge here lies in efficiently merging individual observations into a coherent, comprehensive global map.

**Emergence of Neural Implicit Maps:** Recently, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and similar neural implicit maps have emerged as powerful tools for scene representation. Unlike traditional explicit maps, neural implicit maps store complex geometries and appearance information (color, lighting) compactly within neural networks. This offers advantages in terms of storage efficiency and the potential to integrate high-level semantic information. However, integrating these into multi-robot systems presents a unique challenge: neural network parameters cannot be simply averaged or merged like point clouds.

**Distributed Optimization and its Limitations:** Existing approaches for multi-agent neural implicit mapping often leverage distributed optimization algorithms, such as Distributed Stochastic Gradient Descent (DSGD) or Consensus Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (C-ADMM). These algorithms aim to bring individual robot-specific neural networks into consensus, allowing each robot to eventually possess an identical, shared representation of the environment. The critical limitation, as highlighted by the authors, is that these state-of-the-art distributed optimization methods (like DiNNO and Di-NeRF) are predominantly designed for *synchronous communication*. In real-world robotic deployments, communication is inherently *asynchronous* due to factors like limited bandwidth, packet loss, network delays, physical obstacles, and agents moving out of range. Synchronous methods often rely on "barriers" that halt optimization, waiting for the slowest agent, which is impractical for real-time robotic operations. This leads to inaccurate reconstructions or even complete divergence of the mapping process.

**Addressing the Synchronicity Gap:** RAMEN directly addresses this crucial gap. It recognizes that in real-world scenarios, information shared between agents will inevitably be outdated or incomplete. The paper positions itself as a pioneering effort to make multi-agent neural implicit mapping practical by explicitly accounting for asynchronous communication. It builds upon existing frameworks like Co-SLAM (a single-agent neural implicit mapping method) and extends established distributed optimization techniques (C-ADMM) by introducing mechanisms to handle uncertainty and variable communication frequencies. This work moves the field closer to robust, deployable multi-robot mapping solutions that can leverage the rich representational capabilities of neural implicit maps under realistic communication constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the RAMEN research is to develop a **real-time asynchronous multi-agent neural implicit mapping system** that can robustly and accurately reconstruct complex environments despite challenging communication conditions.

The motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

- **Overcoming Real-World Communication Constraints:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent limitations of real-world multi-robot communication. Unlike idealized lab settings, practical deployments face issues like limited bandwidth, unreliable wireless connections, sporadic message loss (due to best-effort protocols like UDP), and agents moving out of communication range or behind obstacles. Existing state-of-the-art multi-agent neural implicit mapping methods, which rely on perfect synchronous communication, are brittle and prone to failure or divergence under these realistic conditions.
- **Enabling Robust Multi-Robot Collaboration:** For multi-robot systems to be truly effective in complex tasks like autonomous exploration, navigation, and coordination, they require a comprehensive and shared understanding of their environment. This necessitates robust map merging capabilities that can function reliably even when individual agents' observations and communication schedules are not perfectly aligned.
- **Leveraging Neural Implicit Maps' Advantages:** Neural implicit maps offer significant advantages over traditional representations, including compact storage, high fidelity in geometric and appearance detail, and the potential for integrating semantic information. The motivation is to unlock these benefits for multi-robot systems by developing a framework that can overcome the challenges of collaboratively building and maintaining such maps in a distributed, asynchronous manner.
- **Avoiding Computational Bottlenecks and Delays:** Relying on synchronous communication or "barrier" mechanisms (where all agents wait for the slowest one) can significantly hinder real-time operation, which is critical for robotic applications requiring continuous planning and action. RAMEN aims to eliminate this bottleneck, enabling continuous and efficient mapping even with varied processing speeds and communication frequencies among agents.
- **Pioneering Real-World Deployment:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the practical viability of multi-robot neural implicit mapping in actual hardware. The paper explicitly aims to present the "first real-world demonstration," highlighting a gap between theoretical advancements and practical robotic implementation. This pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with current multi-robot mapping technologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAMEN's methodology is built upon extending existing neural implicit mapping and distributed optimization techniques to handle asynchronous communication.

**Foundation: Co-SLAM for Single-Agent Mapping:**
RAMEN uses **Co-SLAM** as its underlying single-agent neural implicit mapping framework. Co-SLAM represents a scene using:
1. **Multi-resolution hash-based feature grid (Vα):** This is the primary learnable component, where feature vectors at different resolutions are stored, allowing for compact representation of geometry and fine details.
2. **Geometry MLP decoder (Fτ) and Color MLP decoder (Fϕ):** These MLPs decode the feature vectors from the grid into SDF values (for geometry) and RGB values (for color).
Co-SLAM renders RGB and depth images from its neural implicit map, minimizing the difference between rendered and observed robot camera images (objective function Lobj). Critically, for RAMEN, the MLP decoders (Fτ, Fϕ) are *pretrained and kept fixed* during mapping, meaning only the parameters of the multi-resolution hash grid (α) are optimized. This decision simplifies uncertainty quantification by focusing on parameters with direct spatial correspondence.

**Addressing Asynchronicity with Uncertainty-Weighted Decentralized C-ADMM:**
The core innovation of RAMEN lies in its novel **uncertainty-weighted decentralized Consensus Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (C-ADMM)** algorithm.
- **The Problem with Synchronous ADMM:** Standard C-ADMM (used in methods like DiNNO) assumes perfect synchronization, where agents always have the latest parameters from their neighbors. This is impractical for real-world asynchronous communication.
- **Weighted Augmented Lagrangian:** RAMEN introduces weight matrices (W_ij, W_ji) into the augmented Lagrangian of the ADMM formulation. These weights reflect the *relative reliability* (or inversely, uncertainty) of the information exchanged between agents i and j.
- **Primal and Dual Updates:** The derivation leads to modified ADMM update rules:
 - **Consensus Variable (z_ij) Update:** The local auxiliary variable z_ij, which represents the consensus target between neighbors i and j, becomes a *weighted average* of their current neural network parameters (Θ_i, Θ_j). Parameters with lower uncertainty (higher weight) contribute more to this consensus target. This allows RAMEN to leverage valuable, albeit potentially outdated, information while downplaying uncertain parts.
 - **Primal Update (Θ_i):** Each agent's neural map (Θ_i) is updated by minimizing its local objective (from its own observations) and aligning with the weighted consensus target z_ij. This is approximated by taking multiple steps of stochastic gradient descent.
 - **Dual Update (p_i):** Dual variables, which penalize violations of the consensus constraints, are updated in a way that incorporates the weights, further emphasizing reliable information.
- **Decentralized Nature:** The algorithm remains decentralized, meaning agents only communicate with their direct neighbors, avoiding a single point of failure common in centralized approaches.

**Uncertainty Quantification: Frequency-Based Epistemic Uncertainty:**
To determine the weights (W_ij, W_ji), RAMEN proposes a simple yet effective **frequency-based epistemic uncertainty quantification method**:
- **Epistemic Uncertainty:** This type of uncertainty reflects the model's lack of knowledge, often reduced with more data.
- **Feature Grid Focus:** Given that MLP decoders are fixed, uncertainty is primarily associated with the feature grid parameters (α). Each feature vector in the multi-resolution hash grid corresponds to a unique spatial location.
- **Gradient Update Count:** The key insight is that the uncertainty of a spatial region decreases with the frequency of observations. RAMEN quantifies this by simply counting the number of non-zero gradient updates each feature grid parameter receives over time (u_t). More updates mean a more frequently observed and, thus, lower-uncertainty region.
- **Weight Normalization:** To use these counts as weights in the C-ADMM, a linear scaling and shifting operation is applied (Eq. 19). This normalization is crucial because it ensures that the proportional relationships between the uncertainty values of different agents are preserved, allowing for meaningful relative weighting.

**Overall Workflow (Algorithm 1):**
1. Agents initialize their neural maps.
2. Each agent continuously captures images and adds them to its local database.
3. Agents periodically send their current neural map parameters (Θ_t) and uncertainty values (u_t) to their neighbors.
4. Upon receiving neighbor information, each agent computes the uncertainty-weighted matrices (W_ij, W_ji).
5. Each agent then performs multiple steps of stochastic gradient descent to update its local neural map (Θ_i) based on its own observations and the weighted consensus target.
6. Dual variables are updated.
7. The frequency-based uncertainty (u_t) is updated based on the gradient updates.
This iterative process allows agents to continually refine their shared map, prioritizing information from less uncertain, more frequently observed regions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RAMEN's performance was evaluated through extensive simulated experiments on public datasets (Replica, ScanNet) and challenging real-world hardware experiments using Turtlebots. Quantitative metrics used for evaluation included "Artifacts" (average distance from reconstructed mesh to ground truth, indicating spurious structures), "Holes" (average distance from ground truth to reconstructed mesh, indicating missing structures), and "Completion Ratio" (percentage of ground truth points reconstructed within 5cm of tolerance), with the latter being the primary holistic measure.

**A. Simulated Experiments:**

1. **General Performance (vs. DSGD, DSGT, DiNNO):**
 - RAMEN consistently achieved the **best overall performance** across four diverse indoor scenes (two synthetic from Replica, two real-world from ScanNet) with a simulated 50% communication success rate.
 - It significantly **outperformed DiNNO in Completion Ratio** (e.g., 91.44% average vs. 80.46% for DiNNO), demonstrating its superior ability to reconstruct complete scenes.
 - RAMEN exhibited **significantly smaller standard deviations** across metrics compared to DiNNO, indicating a more stable and robust mapping process.
 - DSGD and DSGT performed adequately on simpler synthetic scenes but struggled, with DSGT failing to converge on the more challenging ScanNet dataset under asynchronous conditions.
 - RAMEN maintained **minimal computational overhead**, running at 8.58 FPS compared to DiNNO's 9.38 FPS on an RTX 4090 GPU.

2. **Performance under Severe Communication Disruptions:**
 - Experiments on ScanNet "scene0169" with decreasing communication success rates (from 100% down to 20%) showed RAMEN's remarkable resilience.
 - Even at a **20% communication success rate**, RAMEN preserved the overall structure and details of the scene, while **DiNNO failed to reconstruct half of the scene** and produced incomplete/blurry results.
 - RAMEN demonstrated **significantly less performance degradation** and maintained **much smaller standard deviations** compared to DiNNO as communication became sparser.
 - Surprisingly, RAMEN **even outperformed DiNNO under synchronous communication (100% success rate)**, suggesting that its uncertainty-weighted approach inherently improves consensus accuracy.

3. **Scalability with More Agents:**
 - Evaluation with 4, 5, and 6 robotic agents on ScanNet "scene0106" (with agents only communicating with immediate neighbors to test information propagation) showed RAMEN's effective scalability.
 - RAMEN effectively **utilized observations from additional agents** to achieve more complete scene reconstructions (e.g., successfully reconstructing a computer on a desk with the 6th agent's input).
 - In contrast, DiNNO struggled, producing **numerous floating artifacts** and incomplete reconstructions, leading to higher artifact scores.
 - RAMEN continued to show better scene completion improvements with more agents, validating the effectiveness of its uncertainty-based weighting in combining distributed information.

**B. Real-World Hardware Experiments:**

- **Setup:** Two Turtlebots with RGBD sensors were manually controlled to explore half of an environment each, streaming data via ROS2 and WiFi to separate PCs. A VICON motion capture system provided ground-truth poses. Crucially, the PCs had substantial differences in processing speed, and the neural implicit maps were exchanged only **every 30 seconds (a mere 3.33% communication success rate)**, creating an extremely challenging asynchronous environment with noisy depth images and motion blur.
- **Outcome:** Under these harsh real-world conditions, **DiNNO failed to converge entirely**.
- **RAMEN's Success:** RAMEN, however, successfully enabled both Turtlebots to **reach consensus and reconstruct the complete scene**, despite each robot only physically visiting half of it.
- **Reconstruction Quality:** While fine details were limited by the noisy depth images from the Turtlebots, RAMEN accurately captured the overall geometry and structure of the scene. The absence of floating artifacts (unlike in some simulated DiNNO results) was attributed partly to human-in-the-loop active perception.
- **Significance:** This marks the **first real-world implementation and demonstration of multi-robot neural implicit mapping**, proving RAMEN's robustness and practical viability in highly constrained and asynchronous environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RAMEN represents a significant step forward in the field of multi-robot mapping and collaborative perception, with far-reaching implications for various applications.

- **Enabling Robust Multi-Robot Operation in Real-World Scenarios:** The most impactful contribution of RAMEN is its ability to overcome the critical bottleneck of synchronous communication in multi-agent neural implicit mapping. By demonstrating robust performance under severe communication disruptions (low success rates, asynchronous updates), RAMEN makes collaborative mapping practical for real-world deployments where perfect communication is an unrealistic assumption. This is crucial for applications in dynamic, cluttered, or large-scale environments where communication infrastructure may be unreliable or non-existent.
- **Advancing Distributed Optimization for Robotics:** RAMEN introduces a novel uncertainty-weighted decentralized C-ADMM algorithm. This is not just a tweak but a fundamental adaptation of distributed optimization techniques to better suit the specific challenges of robotic systems. By biasing consensus towards more reliable information, the method ensures that even outdated or partial information can be leveraged effectively without degrading overall map quality. This methodology could inspire similar approaches for other distributed learning tasks in robotics.
- **Unlocking the Potential of Neural Implicit Maps:** Neural implicit maps offer compelling advantages in terms of compactness, fidelity, and the potential for semantic understanding. By enabling robust multi-robot collaboration, RAMEN allows these advanced representations to be built and maintained collectively, leading to more comprehensive and richer environmental models for robot teams. This could facilitate more intelligent decision-making, better navigation, and enhanced human-robot interaction.
- **Paving the Way for Autonomous Exploration and SLAM:** The demonstrated ability of robots to cooperatively map a full scene while individually exploring only parts of it is a cornerstone for efficient autonomous exploration. RAMEN lays foundational work for future multi-robot Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems that leverage neural implicit representations, potentially leading to faster, more accurate, and more resilient mapping of unknown territories.
- **Broader Impact on Collaborative AI and Robotics:** The principles behind RAMEN – dealing with uncertainty and asynchronicity in distributed learning – are applicable beyond mapping. It could inform the development of robust collaborative AI systems in other domains, such as multi-robot manipulation, decentralized control, or collective learning, where agents need to share information and reach consensus under imperfect communication.
- **Stimulating Future Research:** The paper's identified limitations (e.g., uncertainty quantification for geometric complexity, keyframe database size, depth sensor noise) provide clear avenues for future research, such as integrating active perception strategies or more sophisticated uncertainty models.

In conclusion, RAMEN pushes the boundaries of multi-robot collaboration by addressing a fundamental practical challenge. Its success in real-world hardware experiments under highly constrained communication conditions solidifies its significance, demonstrating a critical step towards truly autonomous and cooperative robotic systems.
