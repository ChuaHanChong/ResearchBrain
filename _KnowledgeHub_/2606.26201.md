# OmniContact: Chaining Meta-Skills via Contact Flow for Generalizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Noitom Robotics and academic institutions introduce OmniContact, a hierarchical framework enabling humanoids to perform long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks by chaining meta-skills through a novel "contact flow" representation. The system achieves high success rates across diverse tasks, demonstrating robust online replanning, and sustained execution for extended durations.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid loco-manipulation methods struggle with robust execution, seamless chaining of meta-skills, and autonomous recovery in dynamic environments.
- Detailed humanoid-object interaction (HOI) representations are too complex for high-level planning, while implicit skill embeddings lack the interpretability needed for structured skill composition.
- Current reinforcement learning paradigms often lack mechanisms for recovery from interaction failures or struggle with generalization due to reliance on dense reference motions.

## Method

- **OmniContact** employs a hierarchical framework with "==contact flow==" as an intermediate representation, explicitly decoupling skill composition from execution.
- CF-Gen, a rule-based planner, synthesizes contact flows from high-level goals and manages skill chaining and online replanning for autonomous recovery.
- CF-Track, a unified low-level policy, is trained via reinforcement learning to robustly execute diverse contact-rich behaviors conditioned on the generated contact flow.

## Results

- OmniContact achieved **98.7%** success for "Carry Box" and **82.5%** for "Push Suitcase," outperforming baselines by an average of **40.9%**, and successfully completed multi-stage tasks where baselines achieved **0%** success.
- Online replanning improved "Push Suitcase" success from **82.5%** to **94.5%** and "Stack Boxes" from **56.6%** to **80.5%**, primarily by refreshing plan segments.
- The framework demonstrated extended long-horizon endurance, surviving for 40 minutes with **100%** success for sequential single-object goals, performing over 1000 skill rounds with minimal object error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Contact flow," consisting of sparse body targets and binary contact signals, serves as an optimal skill representation, balancing expressiveness for diverse behaviors with compactness for efficient planning and composition.
> - A closed-loop hierarchical architecture, with high-frequency failure detection and replanning, enables robust, continuous, and adaptive task performance in dynamic environments.
> - Integrating a unified low-level policy with an adversarial motion prior allows for both accurate task execution and natural humanoid movements, leading to improved stability and generalizability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics aims to enable robots to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks autonomously. This involves coordinating whole-body motion with continuous object interaction over extended periods. Existing approaches to this challenge generally fall into several categories, each with limitations that OmniContact seeks to address.

Behavior cloning (BC) methods, exemplified by works like HumanPlus and VIRAL, can acquire intricate skills from human demonstrations. However, these methods are often limited by the efficiency of data acquisition and may lead to open-loop execution, which hinders autonomous recovery from unexpected events. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise for robust control, but current RL-based methods also exhibit specific constraints.

These RL approaches can be broadly categorized as:
1. **Body Motion Learning:** Methods such as SONIC primarily focus on replicating teleoperated body motion and often handle object interaction in an open-loop manner. This makes robust recovery from interaction failures difficult.
2. **Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) Tracking:** Approaches like HDMI and Omniretarget incorporate object perception but rely on dense, frame-by-frame whole-body references. This dependency can limit generalization capabilities and autonomous recovery, as generating such dense references for novel scenarios is challenging.
3. **Task-Specific HOI Learning:** Methods such as PhysHSI and HumanX demonstrate robustness and recovery for isolated skills. However, their task-dependent nature restricts their applicability to composition in long-horizon tasks.

A key challenge identified in the broader landscape is the absence of an optimal representation for skill reuse that balances expressiveness for contact-rich execution with compactness for high-level planning. Dense HOI states are precise but too complex for efficient planning, while implicit skill embeddings (e.g., LessMimic) are compact but lack the interpretability required for structured skill composition.

OmniContact positions itself by proposing "contact flow" as this intermediate representation. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level semantic planning and low-level whole-body execution, enabling flexible composition and closed-loop robustness that prior methods struggle to achieve. By focusing on the essential contact dynamics, the work attempts to overcome the limitations of both dense, over-specified control signals and overly abstract, uninterpretable skill representations, thereby facilitating generalizable and long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to autonomously perform long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks with robust execution, seamless, closed-loop chaining of meta-skills, and autonomous failure recovery. This overarching goal is driven by a recognized dual challenge in humanoid robotics:
1. **Robust Execution of Meta-Skills:** The robot must be able to perform individual complex interaction skills reliably, even in the presence of environmental variability or perturbations.
2. **Seamless, Closed-Loop Chaining with Autonomous Recovery:** Beyond executing individual skills, the robot needs to compose these skills into longer sequences, adapt to dynamic environments, and recover from failures without human intervention.

The motivation for proposing a new framework, OmniContact, stems from several limitations observed in existing methodologies:
- **Difficulty of High-Level Planning with Detailed HOI Representations:** Current explicit humanoid-object interaction representations, while precise, are highly complex. This complexity makes them difficult to use for high-level task planning, which often requires a more abstract understanding of interaction.
- **Lack of Interpretability in Implicit Skill Embeddings:** Conversely, implicit skill embeddings are compact but offer limited interpretability. This lack of clear semantics makes it challenging to compose skills in a structured and predictable manner for complex, multi-stage tasks.
- **Inadequacy of Existing RL Paradigms for Skill Chaining and Recovery:**
 - Body motion learning methods often lack mechanisms for recovery from interaction failures.
 - HOI tracking approaches, by depending on dense reference motions, face difficulties in generalization and autonomous recovery when these references are not readily available or when the environment changes.
 - Task-specific HOI learning methods, while robust for single skills, typically do not support flexible skill composition for long-horizon tasks.

To address these limitations, the authors posit that the fundamental distinction of loco-manipulation lies in object contact dynamics. This insight motivates the development of "contact flow" as a compact and expressive representation. The core motivation is to design an optimal skill interface that is both expressive enough to capture diverse manipulation intents (e.g., carrying, pushing, kicking) and structured enough to facilitate heuristic synthesis and efficient high-level planning, thereby enabling robust, generalizable, and long-horizon loco-manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OmniContact is presented as a hierarchical framework for closed-loop, long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation. It operates by explicitly decoupling skill composition from skill execution, utilizing "contact flow" as a shared intermediate representation. The system comprises two main modules: CF-Gen (a high-level planner) and CF-Track (a low-level executor).

#### 3.1. Contact Flow (CF)

Contact flow (F_t) is proposed as a compact and expressive intermediate representation designed to capture both whole-body motion intent and interaction structure. It is defined as a sequence of future interaction states, non-uniformly sampled at specific frame offsets (e.g., T = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 50}). For each future step, contact flow includes:
- **Sparse Body Motion Targets (b_t+k):** A minimal set of key body trajectories (e.g., wrists, torso, ankles).
- **Time-Series Binary Contact Signals (c_t+k):** A 4-dimensional binary signal indicating the contact states (0 or 1) of the robot's end-effectors (e.g., hands, feet).

This formulation aims to be expressive enough to encode diverse behaviors (manipulation, locomotion) while remaining compact and structured for easier synthesis and sequencing compared to dense, high-dimensional HOI trajectories.

#### 3.2. CF-Track: Unified Execution of Loco-Manipulation Meta-Skills

CF-Track serves as the low-level executor, a unified policy trained to robustly realize diverse, contact-rich loco-manipulation behaviors conditioned on the contact flow.
- **Policy Input and Output:** At each control step, the policy receives an observation vector (x_t) consisting of the target contact flow (F_t) and a history buffer (H_t) of recent states (proprioceptive state and object state, including relative 6D pose and bounding box). The policy outputs low-level motor actions (a_t) for the humanoid.
- **Learning Objective:** CF-Track is trained via reinforcement learning with a composite reward function (r_t). This includes:
 - **Tracking Reward (λ_track * r_track_t):** Encourages the policy to track the target body and object trajectories specified by the contact flow.
 - **Adversarial Motion Prior (λ_amp * r_amp_t):** Enforces natural humanoid movements, learned through an adversarial discriminator (AMP).
 - **Regularization Reward (λ_reg * r_reg_t):** Penalizes large action rates to promote smooth control.
- **Unified Learning:** A single CF-Track policy is trained on the OmniContact dataset, integrating various interaction modes through the common contact flow input. This design supports generalization to heuristically planned contact flows, translating imperfect plans into stable humanoid execution.

#### 3.3. CF-Gen: Contact-Flow Synthesis and Skill Chaining

CF-Gen acts as a mid-level, rule-based reference synthesizer. It translates high-level object-centric goals into dense reference motion segments, which are then converted online into future contact-flow sequences for CF-Track.
- **Phase-Template Specification:** Meta-skills are decomposed into ordered sequences of hand-designed "phase blocks," each with explicit contact semantics (e.g., "approach," "grasp," "lift," "transport," "place," "recover standing"). For long-horizon tasks, CF-Gen manages a high-level stage state, switching active objects, meta-skills, and goals upon phase completion.
- **Keyframes Generation:** To adapt templates to specific scenes, CF-Gen defines ending poses for each phase using object-centric geometry. Inverse Kinematics (IK) is employed when precise end-effector placement (wrists, ankles) is required for contact. For locomotion phases, it specifies only ankle poses, with other joints assuming a default posture. The IK problem optimizes pelvis height, pelvis pitch, and joint degrees of freedom (excluding waist roll/yaw for stability) to minimize end-effector and posture errors.
- **Trajectory Interpolation:** After keyframe generation, CF-Gen synthesizes continuous motion trajectories for each phase by interpolating between start and end poses. Linear Interpolation (LERP) is used for Euclidean variables (pelvis position, joint DOFs), and Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) is used for pelvis orientation, ensuring smooth transitions.
- **Contact Flow Construction:** At execution time, CF-Gen queries the interpolated dense reference at non-uniform future offsets (T) to construct the sparse contact flow (F_t). This includes sparse body targets (wrists, torso, ankles) and binary end-effector contact states, transformed into the current torso-yaw frame. This allows the low-level policy flexibility in execution.
- **Skill Chaining and Replanning:** Long-horizon tasks are achieved by chaining these synthesized segments. CF-Gen continuously monitors execution at 50 Hz. If the observed object state deviates from the planned state beyond a threshold, it triggers failure detection, aborts the current plan, and immediately replans from the current state. This enables autonomous recovery from perturbations.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Execution

During test time, OmniContact operates in a closed-loop hierarchy: CF-Gen converts task goals into contact-flow segments, CF-Track executes them, and CF-Gen monitors states and updates the plan or replans as needed, continuing until task completion.

#### 3.5. OmniContact Dataset

To support the unified learning setup, the authors collected the OmniContact dataset, a MoCap-based HOI corpus for humanoid loco-manipulation. It features object-constrained whole-body motions, synchronized human motion with 6-DoF object trajectories, and is structured around reusable primitives (carrying, pushing, kicking, relocating, sliding) with task-level language descriptions. It focuses on longer, high-frequency demonstrations with extensive object transport compared to existing datasets like OMOMO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in simulation and with VLM integration demonstrate the capabilities of OmniContact across various loco-manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Meta-Skill Success:** OmniContact achieved high success rates on individual meta-skills: 98.7% for "Carry Box" and 82.5% for "Push Suitcase." These results significantly surpassed baselines, with average margins of 40.9%. For instance, motion-tracking baselines like SONIC exhibited instability during complex actions like bending or squatting and failed to lift objects, while HDMI failed due to overfitting to training states.
- **Long-Horizon Composability:** For multi-stage tasks, OmniContact demonstrated successful completion. In contrast, all baselines registered 0% success on "Stack Boxes" (due to fragile long-horizon execution) and "Push-Stack Boxes" (due to a lack of support for skill transitions), highlighting OmniContact's advantage in skill chaining.
- **Online Replanning:** Online replanning consistently improved success rates. For "Push Suitcase," the success rate increased from 82.5% to 94.5%, and for "Stack Boxes," it rose from 56.6% to 80.5%. The performance gains were primarily attributed to refreshing subsequent plan segments with updated object states, with emergency recoveries being rarely triggered.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Contact Flow Tracking Target Design:** The ablation study on contact flow targets (Table 4) indicated that torso-only tracking was too sparse for interaction, leading to low success (0.5%). Dense targets (e.g., including object pose and all degrees of freedom) over-constrained the policy, resulting in low success rates (e.g., 0% for [T, EE, C, O, Dof]). Crucially, the explicit modeling of binary contact (C) significantly improved success, boosting a [T, EE] baseline from 11.50% to 98.70% with minimal object errors. The optimal configuration, [T, EE, C], balanced constraint for intent learning with compactness for CF-Gen.
- **CF-Gen Synthesis Configuration:** Removing components of CF-Gen's trajectory synthesis pipeline (e.g., contact adaptation, torso adaptation, wrist adaptation, or replanning) led to increased object errors (E_T_obj) and reduced success rates (R_succ) on the "Carry Box" task (Table 5). This validated the importance of each module in planning object-centric motion and adapting to object geometry.
- **CF-Track Reward Balance:** An optimal balance between tracking (W_track) and adversarial motion prior (W_amp) weights was found to be crucial. Low tracking weights (0.5–0.5) yielded high stability (82.70%) but near-zero task success (0.10%), prioritizing naturalness over task completion. Pure tracking (1.0–0.0) minimized torso error but reduced robustness and stability (46.30%). The chosen balance (0.85–0.15) achieved peak task success (98.70%) with minimal object error while preserving motion regularization.
- **Scalability with Data Volume:** Experiments showed a positive correlation between the amount of HOI data used for training (from 10% to 100% of the OmniContact dataset) and improvements in both task success and object accuracy. This indicated the framework's capacity to effectively learn from diverse data distributions.

#### 4.3. Extended Long-Horizon Execution

OmniContact demonstrated robust endurance in extended long-horizon tasks (Table 10):
- **Protocol I (Single-Object Sequential Goals):** The system survived for 40 minutes with 100% success, near-perfect replanning success, and minimal object error, performing over 1000 skill rounds.
- **Protocol II (Single-Object Task Resampling):** While more challenging due to continuous goal changes and lack of environment resets, surviving agents maintained high success rates and low errors over long periods.
- **Protocol III (Multi-Object Sequential Goals):** In this demanding scenario involving 5 varying-sized objects and inter-object interference, OmniContact demonstrated robustness to varying object sizes and environment-aware replanning, though survival rates decreased with duration.

#### 4.4. VLM Integration

Qualitative demonstrations (Figures 5, 8) illustrated OmniContact's compatibility with Vision-Language Models (VLMs). By prompting VLMs to decompose complex semantic tasks (e.g., "arrange scattered boxes into a heart shape" or "Noitom" layout with color-matching) into start-to-goal object poses, CF-Gen could automatically synthesize continuous contact flows. This integration enabled the robot to perform complex, semantically grounded loco-manipulation behaviors, highlighting the framework's ability to bridge high-level semantic planning with mid-level heuristic synthesis and low-level skill execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OmniContact introduces a hierarchical framework that leverages "contact flow" as a compact and expressive interface to bridge high-level task reasoning with low-level whole-body humanoid execution. This approach addresses significant limitations in current humanoid loco-manipulation research, primarily the challenges of robust meta-skill execution, seamless skill chaining, and autonomous recovery in dynamic environments.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:
- **Optimal Skill Representation:** The proposed contact flow, composed of sparse body keypoint trajectories and binary contact signals, offers a balance between expressiveness (for diverse contact-rich behaviors) and compactness (for efficient planning). This design helps overcome the interpretability issues of implicit embeddings and the planning complexity of dense HOI states, providing a more effective interface for skill reuse and composition.
- **Unified Closed-Loop System:** OmniContact's hierarchical architecture, with CF-Gen handling heuristic synthesis and replanning, and CF-Track providing robust, unified skill execution, enables continuous and adaptive task performance. This closed-loop nature, coupled with high-frequency failure detection and replanning, contributes to autonomous recovery capabilities, enhancing the system's robustness against perturbations.
- **Generalizability and Scalability:** The framework demonstrates superior performance across a range of meta-skills and multi-stage chaining tasks, significantly outperforming prior baselines. The positive scaling with increasing HOI data volume suggests potential for learning more complex and diverse behaviors with larger datasets.
- **Long-Horizon Endurance:** The capability to execute continuous tasks for extended durations (e.g., 40 minutes of box-carrying) underscores the system's stability and robustness, a critical aspect for real-world humanoid deployment.
- **VLM Integration for Semantic Tasks:** The natural compatibility with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is a notable impact. By allowing VLMs to focus on high-level semantic decomposition and object-level goal generation, while OmniContact handles the physical grounding, the system can execute complex, language-instructed tasks, such as arranging objects into specific shapes or sorting based on attributes. This demonstrates a pathway towards more intelligent and flexible humanoid robot assistants that can understand and respond to human commands in natural language.

The potential impact of OmniContact extends to advancing humanoid robotics towards more autonomous, versatile, and human-like interaction in unstructured environments. By effectively decoupling the "what" (interaction semantics) from the "how" (physical execution), this research provides a foundation for developing more scalable loco-manipulation systems. Future developments, such as extending contact flow to dexterous hands and replacing rule-based planning with data-driven approaches, could further enhance fine manipulation capabilities and reactivity, potentially enabling learning from in-the-wild human videos for even more natural behaviors.
