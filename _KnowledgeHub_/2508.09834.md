# Speed Always Wins: A Survey on Efficient Architectures for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a systematic, unified taxonomy of efficient architectural designs and optimization strategies for Large Language Models (LLMs), addressing the computational and memory inefficiencies of traditional Transformers. It synthesizes current progress across diverse approaches, including linear and sparse models, optimized full attention, Mixture-of-Experts, and emerging Diffusion LLMs, also demonstrating their applicability to various modalities.

## Problem

- The Transformer architecture's quadratic computational and memory complexity with respect to sequence length creates significant barriers for training and deploying LLMs, especially for long-context and multimodal applications.
- The rapid proliferation of efficiency-enhancing techniques for LLMs has led to a fragmented research landscape, making it difficult to navigate and understand the broader trends.
- There is a critical need to reconcile the immense power of large foundation models with the practical demands of efficiency, scalability, and resource consciousness for sustainable AI development.

## Method

- The authors conduct a comprehensive literature review, categorizing efficient LLM architectures into a systematic taxonomy with seven main categories: ==Linear Sequence Modeling==, ==Sparse Sequence Modeling==, ==Efficient Full Attention==, ==Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)==, ==Hybrid Architectures==, ==Diffusion LLMs==, and Applications to Other Modalities.
- The survey delves into the technical details, ==mathematical formulations==, key innovations, and representative models within each category, providing a structured overview.
- It emphasizes the practical aspects of ==hardware-efficient implementations== and critically analyzes the design principles, performance benefits, and limitations of each approach.

## Results

- The survey maps a trend of 'linearization' of pre-trained Transformer models into efficient recurrent structures, often achieved via finetuning or distillation, to leverage existing model capabilities while gaining efficiency.
- It demonstrates that Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layers are a powerful paradigm for scaling model capacity without a proportional increase in computational cost, detailing advancements in routing and load balancing.
- The research highlights the broad cross-modal transferability of these efficient architectural principles, with SSMs and MoE architectures being effectively adapted for diverse tasks in computer vision, audio processing, and complex multimodal settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Linear Attention, Linear RNNs, and State Space Models (SSMs) show a convergent evolutionary trajectory, increasingly adopting similar mathematical formulations and optimization objectives to achieve linear complexity and constant memory.
> - Significant efficiency gains for standard softmax attention are achieved through hardware-aware optimizations (e.g., IO-aware attention like FlashAttention) and inference-time reductions (e.g., grouped attention, quantization) even without altering quadratic complexity.
> - Hybrid architectures, combining efficient linear models with attention mechanisms, and the emergence of non-autoregressive Diffusion LLMs represent promising directions for balancing performance and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has been a transformative force in AI, pushing boundaries in language understanding, generation, and multimodal capabilities. At the core of this revolution lies the Transformer architecture, which, despite its excellent scaling properties, inherently suffers from quadratic computational and memory complexity with respect to sequence length. This inefficiency poses significant barriers to the large-scale training and practical deployment of LLMs, especially as applications demand increasingly longer contexts (e.g., Retrieval-Augmented Generation, AI agents, complex reasoning) and multimodal data (e.g., high-resolution images, video, audio).

This survey directly addresses this critical challenge by systematically examining innovative architectural designs and optimization strategies aimed at boosting LLM efficiency. It positions itself against existing surveys (e.g., those on Efficient Transformers, State Space Models, or recurrent processing) by offering a "more comprehensive and organized overview." While previous reviews might have focused on specific aspects of efficiency (like linear attention or SSMs), this paper aims to provide a unified taxonomy encompassing a broader spectrum of approaches, including less explored areas like Diffusion LLMs and the crucial practical dimension of hardware-efficient implementations.

The work acknowledges the immense computational costs ("hidden costs") behind the "unrivaled capabilities" of modern LLMs, VLMs, and LRMs, highlighting a fundamental trade-off between model capability and efficiency. By synthesizing current progress and emerging trends across diverse architectural modifications, the survey provides a critical meta-analysis of the field's efforts to break through the Transformer's efficiency ceiling. It reflects a growing consensus in the research community that raw parameter scaling is not the only path forward; rather, intelligent architectural design and optimization are essential for the sustainable and widespread adoption of powerful AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a systematic and comprehensive examination of innovative LLM architectures designed to enhance efficiency and address the inherent limitations of the traditional Transformer model.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** The quadratic complexity of the Transformer's self-attention mechanism, coupled with the growing size of Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs), leads to prohibitively high training and inference costs. This is particularly problematic for emerging long-context scenarios (e.g., RAG, AI agents, reasoning, multimodal applications) and poses practical barriers to widespread adoption.
- **Providing a Unified Taxonomy:** The rapid proliferation of efficiency-enhancing techniques has led to a fragmented research landscape. This survey aims to organize and categorize these diverse approaches into a coherent "blueprint" to help researchers navigate the field.
- **Highlighting Design Principles and Trade-offs:** The authors seek to summarize the core design principles, performance benefits, known limitations, and potential future developments of each category of efficient architecture, thereby offering insights into the trade-offs between efficiency, capacity, and performance.
- **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The survey emphasizes the importance of hardware-efficient implementations, acknowledging that theoretical efficiency gains must translate into practical speed-ups on modern GPUs.
- **Promoting Future Research:** By synthesizing current progress and identifying promising future directions, the authors hope to "motivate future research toward more efficient, versatile AI systems."
- **Expanding Beyond Language:** Recognizing that efficiency principles are transferable, the survey also motivates and explores the application of these architectures to other modalities like vision, audio, and general multimodal tasks.

In essence, the motivation is to offer clarity and guidance in a rapidly evolving field, urging the community to consider not just "what LLMs can do," but "how efficiently and sustainably they can do it."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and taxonomic approach to survey the vast landscape of efficient LLM architectures. Their methodology involves:

1. **Problem Definition:** Clearly identifying the computational and memory inefficiencies of the traditional Transformer architecture as the central problem (quadratic complexity of self-attention, large FFNs, KV cache growth).
2. **Literature Review and Categorization:** Grouping recent studies into a comprehensive taxonomy based on their core technical approach to efficiency. This categorization, visually represented in Figures 1 and 3, serves as the organizational backbone of the survey. The seven main categories are:
 - **Linear Sequence Modeling:** Methods that reduce attention complexity to linear (O(N)), often inspired by RNNs or State Space Models (SSMs), and eliminate KV cache. Sub-categories include Linear Attention, Linear RNN, State Space Model, Test-Time-Training RNN, Unified Linear Sequence Modeling, Linearization, and Hardware-efficient Implementation.
 - **Sparse Sequence Modeling:** Techniques that compute attention only over a subset of token interactions to reduce computation and memory. Sub-categories include Static Sparse Attention, Dynamic Sparse Attention, Training-free Sparse Attention, and Hardware-efficient Implementation.
 - **Efficient Full Attention:** Methods that enhance the standard softmax attention's efficiency while retaining its theoretical quadratic complexity, primarily through hardware-aware optimizations. Sub-categories include IO-Aware Attention (e.g., FlashAttention), Grouped Attention (e.g., MQA, GQA, MLA), Mixture of Attention, and Quantized Attention.
 - **Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):** Conditional computation approaches where only a subset of parameters (experts) are activated per input token, increasing capacity without proportional cost. Sub-categories include Routing Mechanisms, Expert Architectures, and MoE Conversion.
 - **Hybrid Architectures:** Designs that combine linear sequence modeling components with traditional full attention layers. Sub-categories include Inter-layer Hybrid and Intra-layer Hybrid.
 - **Diffusion LLMs:** Emerging non-autoregressive diffusion models for language generation, offering new avenues for efficient and high-quality text synthesis through parallel decoding. Sub-categories include Non-Autoregressive Diffusion LLM, Bridging Diffusion LLM and Autoregressive, and Extending Diffusion LLM to Multimodality.
 - **Applications to Other Modalities:** Demonstrating the adaptability of these efficiency principles beyond text to vision, audio, and multimodal settings. Sub-categories include Vision (Classification, Detection, Segmentation, Enhancement/Restoration, Generation, Domain-specific applications), Audio (Understanding, Enhancement/Generation), and Multimodality (Understanding, Unified).
3. **Detailed Technical Examination:** For each category and sub-category, the authors delve into the technical details, mathematical formulations (where applicable, such as in linear attention recurrence or SSM discretization), key innovations, and representative models.
4. **Comparative Analysis:** Implicitly, the survey compares and contrasts different methods within and across categories, discussing their advantages (e.g., speed, memory reduction) and disadvantages (e.g., approximation fidelity, complexity of implementation, training stability).
5. **Focus on Practicality:** A recurring theme is the emphasis on "hardware-efficient implementation" sections within each major category, underscoring the practical aspects of deploying these architectures on modern accelerators like GPUs.
6. **Identification of Future Directions:** The conclusion synthesizes the findings to propose promising avenues for future research, spanning both architectural design and applications.

This structured approach allows the survey to provide a clear, in-depth, and well-organized overview of a dynamic research area, making it a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, its "findings" are not experimental results but rather a comprehensive synthesis and categorization of the state-of-the-art in efficient LLM architectures. The main findings and insights presented can be summarized as follows:

1. **Convergence in Linear Sequence Modeling:** The survey reveals a convergent evolutionary trajectory among Linear Attention, Linear RNNs, and State Space Models (SSMs). Initially distinct, these approaches have increasingly adopted similar mathematical formulations (e.g., matrix-based memory, data-dependent gating) and optimization objectives, often shifting from simple cumulative updates to more sophisticated Delta learning rules and L2-based regularization to manage memory and achieve better associative recall. Test-Time-Training (TTT) RNNs are presented as an advanced form leveraging explicit online learning paradigms.
2. **Linearization of Transformers:** A significant trend is the "linearization" of pre-trained Transformer models into efficient recurrent structures. This can be achieved via finetuning-based or distillation-based methods, allowing existing powerful LLMs to gain the efficiency benefits of linear complexity and constant memory without the immense cost of training from scratch.
3. **Diverse Sparsity Strategies:** Sparse attention mechanisms are broadly categorized into static (pre-defined patterns), dynamic (content-adaptive routing), and training-free (inference-time optimization) approaches. This highlights the varied ways researchers are pruning the attention matrix to reduce computational load, from global/local patterns to adaptive token selection and KV cache management. Hardware-efficient implementations (e.g., block-sparse FlashAttention) are crucial for realizing these gains.
4. **Optimization of Full Attention:** Even without changing the quadratic complexity, significant efficiency gains have been achieved for standard softmax attention. IO-Aware Attention (e.g., FlashAttention series) dramatically reduces memory traffic by fusing operations and recomputing gradients, achieving substantial wall-clock speedups. Grouped Attention variants (MQA, GQA, MLA) efficiently reduce KV cache size during inference. Mixture of Attention applies MoE principles to attention heads, while Quantized Attention leverages low-precision arithmetic (e.g., INT8, FP8, FP4) for memory and speed benefits.
5. **Scaling with Mixture-of-Experts:** Sparse MoE layers are a powerful paradigm for scaling model capacity without a proportional increase in computational cost. The survey details advancements in routing mechanisms (token-choice vs. expert-choice, adaptive top-k), load balancing techniques (auxiliary losses, expert-wise bias), and expert architectures (fine-grained, shared, Mixture-of-Depths). MoE conversion strategies (splitting, copying, merging FFNs) offer cost-effective ways to create MoE models from existing dense checkpoints.
6. **Prevalence of Hybrid Architectures:** Combining the strengths of efficient linear models with the expressivity of full or sparse attention is a dominant trend. Both inter-layer hybrids (alternating linear and attention layers) and intra-layer hybrids (mixing attention types within a single layer) are widely explored, exemplified by models like Jamba (Mamba, Attention, MoE) and Hymba (Mamba, Softmax Attention heads).
7. **Emergence of Diffusion LLMs:** Diffusion models are presented as a promising non-autoregressive alternative to traditional LLMs. They offer inherent advantages in parallel decoding, controllability, and bidirectionality, which can mitigate fundamental bottlenecks of AR models. Research is actively bridging Diffusion LLMs with AR models and extending them to multimodal domains.
8. **Cross-Modal Transferability:** A critical finding is the wide applicability of these efficient architectural principles beyond natural language. SSMs, RWKV-like models, and MoE architectures are being effectively adapted for diverse tasks in computer vision (classification, detection, segmentation, generation, medical imaging, autonomous driving, remote sensing) and audio processing (understanding, enhancement, generation), as well as complex multimodal understanding and generation.

Overall, the survey demonstrates that the field is rapidly innovating to reconcile the immense power of large foundation models with the practical demands of efficiency, scalability, and resource consciousness, exploring a rich design space that goes far beyond simple Transformer scaling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the broader AI research community and industry, potentially guiding future directions and accelerating the development of more capable and sustainable AI systems.

**For Researchers:**
- **Comprehensive Reference:** The detailed and systematically organized taxonomy serves as an invaluable reference, helping researchers quickly grasp the state-of-the-art across various efficiency paradigms. This reduces redundant efforts and facilitates cross-pollination of ideas between different sub-fields (e.g., drawing inspiration from SSMs for linear attention, or MoE for sparse attention).
- **Identification of Gaps and Opportunities:** By highlighting the strengths and limitations of existing methods, the survey implicitly points to open research questions and promising avenues. For instance, the discussion on linearization and bridging Diffusion LLMs with AR models suggests fruitful inter-architectural research.
- **Motivation for Algorithm-Hardware Co-Design:** The consistent emphasis on hardware-efficient implementations underscores the importance of considering system-level optimizations from the outset of algorithmic design, fostering a crucial co-design philosophy.
- **Guidance for Novel Architectures:** The "blueprint" provided can inspire the design of entirely new hybrid architectures that strategically combine different efficiency techniques to achieve optimal trade-offs for specific applications.

**For Practitioners and Industry:**
- **Informed Decision-Making:** Developers and engineers working on deploying LLMs can use this survey to make informed choices about which efficient architectures are most suitable for their specific computational constraints, latency requirements, and desired model capabilities (e.g., long-context processing, real-time inference).
- **Cost Reduction:** By outlining methods for reducing training and inference costs, the survey directly contributes to making advanced LLMs more accessible and affordable, democratizing access to powerful AI technologies.
- **Enabling New Applications:** The ability to handle longer contexts and process multimodal data efficiently can unlock new application domains for LLMs, such as more sophisticated AI agents, real-time multimodal interaction, and advanced scientific discovery.
- **Sustainable AI Development:** Addressing the high resource consumption of LLMs is critical for environmental sustainability. The survey contributes to a broader effort to develop "resource-aware foundation models," mitigating the carbon footprint and energy demands of AI.

**Broader Impact:**
The survey's concluding "Future Directions" section is particularly impactful, proposing ambitious goals such as:
- **Infinite Long Context:** Essential for advanced RAG, agents, and reasoning over vast amounts of information.
- **Efficient Agentic LLMs:** Crucial for building AI systems that can interact dynamically with environments and tools with minimal latency.
- **Efficient Large Reasoning Models:** Allowing complex cognitive processes to be executed at scale.
- **Efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and Omni-modal Models:** Enabling robust real-time understanding and control across diverse sensory inputs and actions, fundamental for robotics and embodied AI.
- **Continual Adaptation and Lifelong Learning:** Addressing the challenge of models evolving in dynamic environments without costly retraining.

In essence, "Speed Always Wins" serves not just as a retrospective analysis but as a forward-looking manifesto, advocating for a paradigm shift towards truly efficient, versatile, and sustainable AI systems that can seamlessly integrate into real-world applications across all modalities.
