# AM-RADIO: Agglomerative Vision Foundation Model -- Reduce All Domains Into One

> [!summary] Summary
> The AM-RADIO framework unifies diverse Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM into a single student model, achieving superior performance across various visual understanding tasks. It also introduces E-RADIO, a hardware-efficient architecture that delivers comparable quality while offering at least 6x faster inference speeds.

## Problem

- Current Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) are specialized (e.g., CLIP for language, SAM for segmentation, DINOv2 for dense features), leading to a fragmented ecosystem.
- Using multiple distinct VFMs for comprehensive visual intelligence is computationally inefficient and complex.
- Large Foundation Models (FMs) have prohibitive computational and memory demands for many real-world applications.

## Method

- A ==multi-teacher knowledge distillation== framework, AM-RADIO, trains a student model from scratch using ==CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM as teachers==.
- The approach distills both ==summary and spatial feature representations== from teachers via adaptor heads, primarily using cosine similarity loss.
- A novel, hardware-efficient hybrid ==CNN-Transformer architecture called E-RADIO== is developed to optimize throughput and performance during distillation.

## Results

- The flagship RADIO-ViT-H/16 model surpasses its individual teacher models, achieving the best metrics on **6 out of 9** evaluated tasks, including ImageNet-1K k-NN (**86.06%**) and ADE20k semantic segmentation (**51.34%**).
- The E-RADIO-L architecture achieves comparable quality to larger models while demonstrating at least **6x faster** inference speeds compared to teachers at matched resolutions.
- RADIO retains its teachers' specialized functionalities, serving as a drop-in replacement for SAM's vision encoder with comparable COCO mIOU, and probing shows 3D awareness on par with larger DINOv2 models (e.g. depth estimation).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling both summary and spatial feature representations from teachers is essential, leading to an 18% relative improvement on dense tasks like ADE20K; DINOv2's spatial features are particularly effective for dense image understanding, outperforming SAM in this regard for linear probing.
> - Simple, equally-weighted loss balancing performed comparably to more complex automatic methods, indicating robustness of the feature matching approach.
> - Cropped Position Embedding (CPE) effectively enables ViTs to generalize to arbitrary input resolutions, enhancing performance on semantic segmentation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "AM-RADIO" is situated at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas within artificial intelligence and computer vision:

- **Foundation Models (FMs):** The past few years have seen the emergence of large, general-purpose "Foundation Models" across various modalities, from large language models (LLMs) to Vision Foundation Models (VFMs). Models like OpenAI's CLIP, Meta's DINOv2, and SAM have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across a wide array of downstream tasks. However, as the authors note, these VFMs are often trained with distinct objectives and excel in specific areas (e.g., CLIP in vision-language alignment, DINOv2 in dense feature learning, SAM in open-vocabulary segmentation). This has led to a fragmented landscape where multiple specialized models are required for comprehensive visual intelligence. AM-RADIO directly addresses this fragmentation by aiming to unify their strengths.
- **Knowledge Distillation (KD):** The core technical approach employed is multi-teacher knowledge distillation. Knowledge distillation is a well-established technique for transferring knowledge from a large "teacher" model (or an ensemble of teachers) to a smaller, more efficient "student" model. Early work by Hinton et al. (2015) introduced the concept of soft targets. More recently, feature distillation, which involves matching intermediate activations, has gained prominence. AM-RADIO extends this by applying multi-teacher distillation to the context of large, heterogeneous foundation models, which is a novel and complex application of the technique. The paper builds upon prior work in multi-teacher distillation, adapting strategies to handle the diverse feature spaces and objectives of modern VFMs.
- **Efficiency in AI Systems:** As FMs grow in size and complexity, their computational and memory requirements become prohibitive for many real-world applications. The demand for "efficient AI" — models that can deliver high performance with reduced resource consumption — is a major driving force in current research. This paper's development of E-RADIO directly contributes to this area by proposing a novel, hardware-efficient architecture designed specifically for the unique challenges of distilling VFMs. This focus on efficiency is particularly relevant for NVIDIA, as it highlights the performance benefits on their hardware.
- **Multimodal AI and Embodied Intelligence:** The ability to combine vision-language understanding (CLIP), detailed pixel-level understanding (SAM), and robust visual representations (DINOv2) into a single model is crucial for advancing multimodal AI systems and ultimately, more capable embodied AI agents. By enabling a single model to interface with language models like LLaVA, AM-RADIO paves the way for more sophisticated visual reasoning and interaction.

In essence, AM-RADIO attempts to synthesize the best aspects of specialized, high-performing VFMs into a single, cohesive, and efficient model, moving towards a more unified and practical paradigm for general visual intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind AM-RADIO stems from the current landscape of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs). While models like CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM are powerful, they are trained with distinct objectives, leading to specialized capabilities. This specialization, though beneficial for specific tasks, results in a fragmented system where multiple large models are needed to cover a broad range of visual understanding tasks. The authors identify this as a "large hole in capability" when viewed from a holistic perspective.

Based on this, the key objectives are:

- **Unified Visual Intelligence:** To develop a single, "agglomerative" vision foundation model (RADIO) that can effectively combine the unique properties of multiple disparate teacher VFMs, such as CLIP's zero-shot vision-language comprehension, DINOv2's detailed pixel-level understanding, and SAM's open-vocabulary segmentation capabilities.
- **Performance Enhancement:** To demonstrate that this unified student model can not only consolidate features but also *surpass* the performance of its individual teacher models across various representative benchmarks.
- **Maintain Distinct Functionalities:** To ensure the student model retains the ability to perform tasks typically handled by its teachers (e.g., CLIP-ZeroShot applications, Segment-Anything tasks) by serving as a direct drop-in replacement for their vision encoders.
- **Hardware Efficiency and Practicality:** To investigate and develop a novel, highly hardware-efficient model architecture (E-RADIO) within the multi-teacher distillation pipeline. The goal is for E-RADIO to exceed the performance of predecessors while offering significantly faster inference speeds, addressing the computational demands of large foundation models.
- **Generalizable Framework:** To propose a robust and general methodology (AM-RADIO) for distilling multiple distinct foundation models, even those with incompatible input resolutions or architectural differences, into a single student model. This framework should be adaptable to future advancements in VFMs.

The overarching motivation is to move towards a more comprehensive, versatile, and practically deployable general-purpose vision model that reduces complexity and computational overhead by consolidating diverse capabilities into a single, performant backbone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AM-RADIO framework is designed for multi-teacher distillation, training a new vision foundation model (the student) from scratch by learning from an ensemble of pre-trained VFMs (the teachers).

- **Teacher Model Selection:** The authors carefully select three prominent VFMs that offer complementary strengths:
 - **CLIP (OpenCLIP ViT-H/14, OpenAI CLIP ViT-L/14, DFN CLIP ViT-H/14):** Valued for its exceptional zero-shot vision-language understanding and image-level reasoning.
 - **DINOv2 (ViT-g/14-reg):** Chosen for its strong dense feature representations and performance on pixel-level tasks like semantic segmentation.
 - **SAM (ViTDet-H):** Included for its state-of-the-art open-vocabulary instance segmentation capabilities.
 The choice to omit supplemental ground truth guidance and treat datasets purely as image sources reflects the self-supervised or weakly-supervised nature of these teachers and simplifies the multi-teacher problem by focusing on feature learning.

- **Distillation Process - Feature Matching:**
 - Unlike traditional knowledge distillation that often matches logits, AM-RADIO focuses on matching the *feature representations* from the vision encoders of each teacher. This is critical given the heterogeneous nature of the teachers and their different output formats/objectives.
 - Both **summary feature vectors** (e.g., CLIP's class token, DINOv2's class token) and **spatial feature vectors** are distilled.
 - **Loss Function:** A combination of cosine similarity (primary for aligning feature directions) and Smooth-L1 (secondary, to match feature magnitudes) is used for feature matching. Cosine similarity was empirically found to yield better models.
 - **Adaptor Heads:** Simple 2-layer MLPs with LayerNorm and GELU are used as adaptor heads. For each teacher, two such heads project the student's unified feature representation to match the teacher's summary and spatial feature dimensions.
 - **Loss Balancing:** Simple, naive equal weighting for spatial features across teachers, and equal weighting for summary features from CLIP and DINOv2 (SAM doesn't have a summary feature). Extensive experimentation with automatic loss balancing methods (e.g., uncertainty-based, AdaLoss) showed little significant advantage over naive manual balancing.

- **Training Data and Schedule:**
 - **Dataset:** DataComp-1B is selected as the primary training dataset. Ablation studies (Table 2) showed it provided the best balance of quality across various downstream metrics, particularly for zero-shot performance, compared to ImageNet-1K/21K or LAION-400M.
 - **Phased Training:** The training proceeds in two stages:
 1. 300k steps with CLIP and DINOv2 teachers at 256x256 resolution.
 2. Another 300k steps with CLIP and DINOv2 at 432x432 resolution, *plus* SAM at 1024x1024. This staggered approach helps integrate the high-resolution SAM teacher effectively.
 - **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer, batch size 1024, cosine annealing learning rate schedule, base learning rate 0.001.

- **Addressing Heterogeneity and Architectural Challenges:**
 - **Input Resolution Mismatch:**
 - Random scale + cropping applied to both student and teacher inputs.
 - DINOv2 features are bilinearly interpolated to match the student's output resolution, as DINOv2 operates at 224px while the student can train at higher resolutions.
 - **Cropped Position Embedding (CPE):** For ViT student models, CPE (from) is used to enable the model to operate at arbitrary input resolutions, transcending the fixed-resolution limitation of standard ViT positional embeddings. This also regularizes position embeddings (Figure 3).
 - **High-Resolution Training (SAM):** To handle SAM's 1024x1024 inputs efficiently, the authors reformulate the ViTDet architecture into a training augmentation, sampling various window sizes. Spectral reparametrization and weight decay stabilize high-resolution training.
 - **GPU Partitioning:** Teachers are grouped by batch size and input resolution and distributed across different GPUs to optimize memory and throughput.
 - **Feature Summarization:** For the student, the "class token" is preferred over average pooling for summary features, as it yielded better overall performance (Table 6).

- **Student Architecture Exploration:**
 - **Standard ViT:** A ViT-H/16 architecture is used for the flagship RADIO model to directly compare with the ViT-based teachers.
 - **Efficient Architecture (E-RADIO):** A novel hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture is proposed. It features an input stem with strided convolutions, followed by YOLOv8 C2f convolution blocks, and then transformer stages using windowed attention with multi-resolution attention (interleaving local and global attention, inspired by FasterViT and ViTDet). A deconvolutional layer upsamples final feature maps to reduce spatial reduction to 16x, improving dense task performance while maintaining speed. This architecture specifically targets high throughput on GPUs. The authors benchmarked over 10 existing efficient architectures using their distillation framework before developing E-RADIO, highlighting a general deficiency in these models for VFM distillation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AM-RADIO framework yields compelling results, demonstrating the viability and benefits of multi-teacher distillation for Vision Foundation Models.

- **Superior Performance of RADIO:** The flagship RADIO-ViT-H/16 model generally *outperforms* its individual teacher models (DFN CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) across a range of benchmarks. Specifically, it achieved the best metrics on 6 out of 9 evaluated tasks (Table 1), including ImageNet-1K k-NN (86.06% vs. max 85.27%), ADE20k semantic segmentation (51.34% vs. max 48.68%), PascalVOC (84.71% vs. max 82.78%), GQA (63.01% vs. max 62.20%), POPE (86.20% vs. max 86.09%), and VQAv2 (79.28% vs. max 78.78%). This confirms the successful agglomeration and synergistic learning of diverse capabilities.
- **Efficiency of E-RADIO:** The newly proposed E-RADIO architecture represents a significant step forward in hardware efficiency. E-RADIO-L achieves comparable quality to larger, slower models while being *at least 6x faster* than the teacher models at matched resolution (e.g., 468 throughput for E-RADIO-L vs. 170-503 for teachers, Table 1). In some cases, it delivers results 10 times faster with a 20% improvement over teachers (Figure 4). This makes high-quality VFMs much more practical for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
- **Importance of Feature Distillation:** The authors empirically confirm the crucial role of full feature distillation (matching both summary and spatial features) in boosting performance on dense image understanding tasks, yielding an "18% relative improvement on ADE20K."
- **Teacher Contributions and Synergy:**
 - DINOv2 is identified as providing superior spatial features crucial for dense tasks like ADE20k linear probing, significantly outperforming SAM in this regard.
 - SAM excels in instance segmentation (COCO mIOU), and RADIO can effectively drop-in replace SAM's vision encoder with comparable performance (Table 8).
 - CLIP’s influence ensures strong zero-shot vision-language capabilities are retained.
 - Training with all three teachers (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) yielded the best overall results.
- **3D Awareness:** Probing the model's 3D awareness using tasks like monocular depth estimation, surface normals, and multi-view correspondence (Table 9) revealed that RADIO's performance is on par with the larger DINOv2-g-14-reg and significantly better than other comparably-sized teachers, indicating robust 3D feature extraction.
- **Technical Insights:**
 - **Cropped Position Embedding (CPE):** CPE proved effective in enabling ViTs to generalize to arbitrary resolutions, improving semantic segmentation mIOU even when training at fixed resolutions. It also helps regularize position embeddings (Figure 3).
 - **Loss Balancing:** Counter-intuitively, simpler "naive" loss balancing performed as well as or better than more complex automatic balancing schemes (e.g., uncertainty-based, AdaLoss), suggesting the robustness of the feature matching approach.
 - **Dataset Choice:** DataComp-1B was optimal for achieving high quality across diverse tasks.
- **Identified Limitation:** The authors note a "mode switching" phenomenon in RADIO's features at higher resolutions, where the model appears to shift between latent "low resolution" and "high resolution" modes (Figure 5). This points to an area for future improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AM-RADIO research offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact across the AI and computer vision landscape:

- **Toward General-Purpose Visual AI:** The most profound impact is the demonstration that it is possible to unify the diverse strengths of multiple specialized Vision Foundation Models into a single, more versatile, and capable model. This "agglomerative" approach moves the field closer to a true general-purpose visual intelligence that can handle a broad spectrum of tasks without needing a zoo of specialized models. This simplification of the AI stack for visual tasks is highly desirable for developers and practitioners.
- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** By surpassing the performance of individual teacher models on various benchmarks, RADIO proves that synergy through distillation can lead to a *better* overall model, not just a smaller or consolidated one. This implies a more robust and broadly applicable visual backbone for future AI systems.
- **Democratization of Advanced AI:** The development of E-RADIO, a significantly faster and more efficient architecture, directly addresses the practical deployment challenges of large VFMs. By making high-quality vision models accessible with fewer computational resources, AM-RADIO facilitates wider adoption on edge devices, mobile platforms, and in applications where low latency and high throughput are critical. This has direct commercial implications for NVIDIA, as it increases the utility and accessibility of AI models running on their hardware.
- **Blueprint for Future Foundation Model Development:** The AM-RADIO framework provides a robust and general methodology for distilling and fusing capabilities from current and future foundation models. As new VFMs emerge, this framework offers a principled way to integrate their unique strengths, ensuring continuous improvement and expansion of a unified model's capabilities. It encourages a shift from training specialized models from scratch to distilling and integrating knowledge.
- **Advancing Multimodal AI:** The seamless integration of RADIO as a vision encoder for Large Vision-Language Models (LLaVA-1.5) demonstrates its immediate utility for multimodal applications, enabling more sophisticated visual question answering and general visual reasoning that combines perception with language understanding.
- **Innovation in Model Architecture:** The design of E-RADIO, with its hybrid CNN-Transformer approach and multi-resolution attention, offers valuable architectural insights for building efficient yet powerful models. The finding that many existing "efficient" backbones are over-tailored to ImageNet-1K highlights a gap that E-RADIO successfully fills, contributing to the broader field of efficient deep learning.

In conclusion, AM-RADIO represents a pivotal step towards more integrated, powerful, and efficient visual AI. By successfully merging disparate foundation models, it offers a single, performant solution that can reduce complexity, improve overall capabilities, and pave the way for broader deployment of advanced visual intelligence across various applications.
