# AM-RADIO: Agglomerative Vision Foundation Model -- Reduce All Domains Into One

> [!summary] Summary
> The AM-RADIO framework unifies diverse Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM into a single student model, achieving superior performance across various visual understanding tasks. It also introduces E-RADIO, a hardware-efficient architecture that delivers comparable quality while offering at least 6x faster inference speeds.

## Problem

- Current Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) are specialized (e.g., CLIP for language, SAM for segmentation, DINOv2 for dense features), leading to a fragmented ecosystem.
- Using multiple distinct VFMs for comprehensive visual intelligence is computationally inefficient and complex.
- Large Foundation Models (FMs) have prohibitive computational and memory demands for many real-world applications.

## Method

- A ==multi-teacher knowledge distillation== framework, AM-RADIO, trains a student model from scratch using ==CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM as teachers==.
- The approach distills both ==summary and spatial feature representations== from teachers via adaptor heads, primarily using cosine similarity loss.
- A novel, hardware-efficient hybrid ==CNN-Transformer architecture called E-RADIO== is developed to optimize throughput and performance during distillation.

## Results

- The flagship RADIO-ViT-H/16 model surpasses its individual teacher models, achieving the best metrics on **6 out of 9** evaluated tasks, including ImageNet-1K k-NN (**86.06%**) and ADE20k semantic segmentation (**51.34%**).
- The E-RADIO-L architecture achieves comparable quality to larger models while demonstrating at least **6x faster** inference speeds compared to teachers at matched resolutions.
- RADIO effectively retains the specialized functionalities of its teachers, serving as a drop-in replacement for SAM's vision encoder with comparable COCO mIOU.
- Probing revealed that RADIO's 3D awareness is on par with larger DINOv2 models for tasks like monocular depth estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling both summary and spatial feature representations from teachers is essential, leading to an 18% relative improvement on dense tasks like ADE20K.
> - DINOv2's spatial features are particularly effective for dense image understanding, outperforming SAM in this regard for linear probing.
> - Simple, equally-weighted loss balancing performed comparably to more complex automatic methods, indicating robustness of the feature matching approach.
> - Cropped Position Embedding (CPE) effectively enables ViTs to generalize to arbitrary input resolutions, enhancing performance on semantic segmentation.
