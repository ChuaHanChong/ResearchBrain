# Building Cooperative Embodied Agents Modularly with Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces CoELA, a cognitive-inspired modular framework leveraging Large Language Models to enable cooperative embodied agents in decentralized, partially observable environments with costly natural language communication. The system outperforms baseline methods in multi-agent tasks, demonstrates emergent strategic communication and cooperation, and fosters enhanced trust and collaboration in human-AI interactions.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent systems often simplify communication or rely on centralized control, which is unrealistic for complex, decentralized, and partially observable real-world environments.
- Integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) into embodied multi-agent systems, especially for decentralized cooperation with costly communication, remained an unexplored challenge.
- Developing embodied AI agents capable of human-like cooperation and natural language interaction for assistance in everyday tasks presents significant challenges in perception, planning, and communication.

## Method

- ==Introduced CoELA== (Cooperative Embodied Language Agent), a cognitive-inspired modular framework that leverages LLMs for high-level reasoning and natural language communication.
- CoELA integrates five modules: Perception (for sensory processing and semantic map generation), Memory (for world, agent, and episodic knowledge), Communication (LLM-driven message generation), Planning (LLM-driven high-level decision-making), and Execution (low-level action translation).
- Evaluated the framework in two challenging custom-built embodied environments (ThreeDWorld Multi-Agent Transport and Communicative Watch-And-Help) to demonstrate decentralized control, costly communication, and complex interaction.

## Results

- CoELA agents significantly outperformed strong baselines (RHP, MHP) in both TDW-MAT and C-WAH environments, achieving up to **39%** efficiency improvement (TR **0.71**) in multi-agent transport tasks.
- Qualitative analysis showed CoELA agents exhibiting emergent cooperative behaviors, including strategic information sharing, adaptive planning, and selective communication to minimize costs.
- Human subjects rated CoELA agents communicating in natural language as significantly more trustworthy (average trust score **6.3** vs. **4.7** for templated communication), leading to more effective human-AI collaboration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs, when integrated into a modular cognitive architecture, can enable sophisticated multi-agent cooperation and strategic natural language communication in complex embodied environments.
> - Efficient and trustworthy human-AI collaboration is enhanced through natural language communication, with CoELA agents demonstrating increased human trust and more effective joint task completion.
> - Fine-tuning open-source LLMs can yield performance competitive with larger proprietary models, indicating a promising path for accessible development of cooperative embodied agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and challenging frontier in Artificial Intelligence: the development of **cooperative embodied agents** that can operate effectively in complex, realistic environments. It sits at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields:

- **Multi-Agent Systems (MAS):** The long-standing goal of MAS research is to enable multiple agents to coordinate and collaborate to achieve shared objectives. Previous MAS work has explored various communication mechanisms (uninterpretable continuous vectors, limited discrete symbols) and control paradigms (centralized vs. decentralized). However, many prior platforms either neglected communication or assumed cost-free channels, which deviates significantly from real-world constraints. This paper directly tackles these limitations by focusing on decentralized control and *costly natural language communication*.
- **Embodied AI:** This field aims to create intelligent agents that perceive, act, and learn within physical or simulated environments, mirroring human interaction with the world. Challenges include perception from raw sensory data, long-horizon planning, and partial observability. Recent advancements have seen LLMs driving single-agent embodied tasks, but extending this to multi-agent cooperation with complex interactions remains largely unexplored.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs):** LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding, generation, world knowledge, and complex reasoning. Recent research has shown their potential as "language agents" for sequential decision-making, planning, and even simulating human-like behavior in artificial societies. However, their application to *decentralized, embodied, multi-agent cooperation with costly communication*—where planning involves complex interactions with other agents and communication is a strategic decision rather than a free dialogue—is a significant open question.

**The unique contribution of this work** lies in its synthesis of these fields to address a novel problem setting. Unlike prior work that often oversimplifies communication or assumes centralized control, this paper formalizes a **challenging DEC-POMDP (Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process) extension** that incorporates raw sensory observations, decentralized control, and crucially, *costly natural language communication*. By leveraging the strengths of LLMs within a modular, cognitive-inspired architecture, the authors aim to bridge the gap between LLMs' impressive linguistic and reasoning abilities and the practical demands of real-world multi-agent embodiment. This positions the work as a crucial step towards building truly intelligent and collaborative AI partners for human interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the observation that humans are exceptionally adept at cooperation and communication to solve complex tasks. The ambition is to create embodied AI agents that can similarly engage in and assist humans in everyday life. However, this goal is fraught with challenges, including complex perception, partial observation, long-horizon planning, and natural language communication.

The paper identifies a critical gap in existing research: while LLMs have shown promise for single-agent tasks, their utility for *cooperative embodied agents in decentralized settings with costly communication* is largely unknown. Previous multi-agent research often simplifies communication or relies on centralized controllers, which are unrealistic for many real-world scenarios.

Given these challenges and gaps, the **key objectives** of this paper are:

1. **Formalize and Instantiate a Challenging Multi-Agent Embodied Cooperation Problem:** To define a setting that includes decentralized control, complex partial observations, costly communication, and long-horizon, multi-objective tasks. This problem is instantiated in two challenging embodied environments: ThreeDWorld Multi-Agent Transport (TDW-MAT) and Communicative Watch-And-Help (C-WAH).
2. **Investigate the Utility of LLMs for Decentralized Multi-Agent Cooperation:** Specifically, to determine if LLMs possess the necessary abilities for cooperative planning and efficient natural language communication in such complex settings.
3. **Develop a Novel Cognitive-Inspired Modular Framework (CoELA):** To seamlessly integrate the commonsense knowledge, reasoning ability, language comprehension, and text generation prowess of LLMs into a structured cognitive architecture for cooperative embodied agents.
4. **Demonstrate Emergent Efficient Communication and Effective Cooperation:** To show that LLM-driven agents can not only achieve high performance but also exhibit sophisticated cooperative behaviors, including strategic communication.
5. **Evaluate Human-AI Cooperation:** To assess the possibility of achieving effective and trustworthy collaboration between humans and LLM-driven agents through natural language.
6. **Explore the Feasibility of Open-Source LLMs:** To investigate whether open LLMs (like LLAMA-2) can be fine-tuned to achieve promising performance in this challenging domain, reducing reliance on proprietary models.

In essence, the authors are motivated to push the boundaries of embodied AI by leveraging the power of LLMs to enable truly collaborative agents that can reason, communicate, and act effectively in complex, shared environments, ultimately paving the way for more capable and trustworthy human-AI interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is **CoELA (Cooperative Embodied Language Agent)**, a cognitive-inspired modular framework designed to leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for multi-agent cooperation. The overall setting is framed as an extension of the Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (DEC-POMDP) to include costly communication, referred to as DEC-POMDP-COM.

CoELA consists of five interconnected modules that mimic human cognitive processes:

1. **Perception Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To process raw sensory observations (RGB-D images) from the environment into high-level, useful information for reasoning.
 - **Approach:** Employs a pre-trained **Mask-RCNN** (fine-tuned on custom data) to perform instance segmentation from RGB images. This is combined with depth information to construct 3D point clouds, which are then used to build and update a **2D local semantic map**. This map includes information on object states (positions, names, IDs, relationships) and explored areas, crucial for spatial reasoning.

2. **Memory Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To maintain the agent's knowledge and experience of the world, itself, and other agents. Mimics human long-term memory.
 - **Components:**
 - **Semantic Memory:** Stores current world knowledge, including the evolving semantic map, task progress, the agent's own state, and the perceived state of other agents. Notably, it acknowledges that knowledge of others might be inaccurate due to partial observability.
 - **Episodic Memory:** Records the agent's past experiences, specifically action history and dialogue history (keeping the last K actions and D dialogues for efficiency).
 - **Procedural Memory:** Stores knowledge on "how-to" carry out specific high-level plans, implemented as code, and also holds neural model parameters (e.g., LLMs, Mask-RCNN).

3. **Communication Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To decide "what to send" by generating natural language messages to other agents.
 - **Approach:** Utilizes an LLM (e.g., GPT-4) as a message generator. It retrieves relevant information from the Memory Module (semantic map, task progress, agent states, action/dialogue history). This information is converted into textual descriptions using templates and then compiled into a detailed prompt for the LLM. Constraints (e.g., message accuracy, helpfulness, brevity, non-repetition) and "seed messages" are included in the prompt to elicit effective communication behavior.

4. **Planning Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To make high-level decisions, including "when to communicate," by leveraging the LLM's reasoning abilities.
 - **Approach:** An LLM serves as the planner. It retrieves information from the Memory Module (similar to the Communication Module). It then compiles an "Action List" of all currently available high-level plans (e.g., navigation, interaction, *and* sending the message generated by the Communication Module). The LLM is prompted with this information and the action list. **Zero-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting** (Kojima et al., 2022) is used to encourage the LLM to perform more explicit reasoning before selecting a plan.

5. **Execution Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To translate the high-level plans from the Planning Module into primitive actions executable in the environment.
 - **Approach:** Retrieves procedural knowledge from the Memory Module (e.g., A-star-based navigation, object interaction rules). This modular design allows the Planning Module (LLM) to focus on general, high-level strategic reasoning, reducing inference time and making the system more generalizable across environments (only procedural knowledge needs rewriting for new environments).

**Experimental Setup:**
The framework is evaluated on two custom-built, challenging embodied multi-agent environments:
- **ThreeDWorld Multi-Agent Transport (TDW-MAT):** A rearrangement task requiring agents to transport target objects using containers in multi-room indoor environments. Features ego-centric RGB-D observations and low-level navigation/interaction actions, plus natural language communication.
- **Communicative Watch-And-Help (C-WAH):** Extends the Watch-And-Help challenge, focusing on common household activities with support for inter-agent communication. Experiments are conducted under both symbolic and visual observation settings.

**Baselines:**
- **MCTS-based Hierarchical Planner (MHP):** From Watch-And-Help Challenge.
- **Rule-based Hierarchical Planner (RHP):** From ThreeDWorld Transport Challenge.
- **Multi-Agent Transformer (MAT):** A centralized MARL baseline, adapted to the setting (with oracle semantic map and agent states).

**LLM Instantiations:**
- **GPT-4:** The primary, powerful proprietary LLM.
- **LLAMA-2-13b-chat:** An open-source LLM.
- **CoLLAMA:** A fine-tuned version of LLAMA-2-13b-chat using LoRA on a small dataset of human-filtered high-quality trajectories collected from CoELA agents.

This modular, cognitive-inspired architecture, combined with the strategic use of LLMs for high-level reasoning and natural language communication, represents a novel and robust approach to multi-agent embodied cooperation under realistic constraints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across TDW-MAT and C-WAH environments, both with AI-AI and Human-AI collaboration, yielded several significant findings:

#### 4.1. Collaborating with AI Agents

- **Superior Performance over Baselines:**
 - **TDW-MAT:** CoELA driven by GPT-4 significantly outperforms strong planning-based baselines (RHP). When a single RHP agent works alone, its average Transport Rate (TR) is 0.43. Cooperating with another RHP agent increases TR to 0.61 (29% Efficiency Improvement, EI). However, cooperating with CoELA boosts TR to 0.69 (36% EI). The best performance is achieved when two CoELA agents cooperate, reaching 0.71 TR (39% EI). Even with Oracle Perception, CoELA's performance remains dominant (0.85 TR, 41% EI for CoELA+CoELA).
 - **C-WAH:** CoELA also surpasses MCTS-based Hierarchical Planner (MHP). While a single MHP takes an average of 111 steps, MHP+MHP reduces this to 75 steps (33% EI). MHP+CoELA further reduces steps to 59 (45% EI), and CoELA+CoELA achieves the lowest steps at 57 (49% EI).
- **Emergent Efficient Communication and Cooperation:** Qualitative analysis showed CoELA agents exhibiting sophisticated cooperative behaviors:
 - **Information Sharing:** CoELA agents effectively share progress, object locations, and intentions (e.g., "I found 3 target objects in the kitchen, I remember you were holding an empty container, can you come here to pick them up...").
 - **Requesting and Responding to Help:** Agents know when to ask for assistance and effectively respond to partners' requests.
 - **Adaptive Planning:** Agents can adapt their plans based on partners' communications and observed states (e.g., one agent suggests a labor division, and the other suggests an even better plan given its current position).
 - **Strategic Non-Communication:** CoELA agents learn *when not to communicate* if it's more efficient to just perform the task, demonstrating an understanding of communication costs.
- **Viability of Open LLMs:** While LLAMA-2-13b-chat initially underperformed GPT-4, the fine-tuned CoLLAMA achieved competitive performance (0.70 TR on TDW-MAT), sometimes even surpassing GPT-4 on specific subtasks (e.g., "Stuff"). This demonstrates the promising potential of fine-tuning open LLMs for building cooperative embodied agents, reducing reliance on expensive proprietary models.

#### 4.2. Collaborating with Humans

- **Improved Human-AI Cooperation:** CoELA also performs better than MHP when cooperating with human subjects. Disabling communication (CoELA w/o communication) led to a significant performance drop, highlighting the importance of communication in human-AI interaction.
- **Enhanced Trust and Collaboration:** A user study revealed that human subjects preferred collaborating with CoELA (natural language communication) over MHP (template language). Critically, humans rated CoELA communicating in natural language as significantly more trustworthy (average trust score of 6.3 for CoELA vs. 4.7 for CoELA w/o communication, p=0.0003). This increased trust directly correlated with more effective human-AI cooperation. An example showed a human and CoELA perfectly dividing exploration based on natural language exchange.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Limitations Analysis

- **Importance of Strong LLMs:** Replacing GPT-4 with GPT-3.5 led to increased task completion steps, indicating that stronger LLMs are crucial for complex reasoning and avoiding implausible plans or unuseful messages.
- **Role of Communication:** Although critical for human-AI trust and cooperation, disabling communication *among AI agents* did not cause a "significant" performance drop. The authors attribute this to the extreme challenge and cost of robust, efficient communication for current LLMs in their decentralized setting, suggesting they don't *yet* master it robustly enough for performance gains over highly optimized non-communicating planners.
- **Efficacy of Memory and Execution Modules:** The Memory Module proved vital, as its removal nearly doubled the steps required for task completion. The Execution Module was also crucial; attempting to let the Planning Module handle low-level controls directly resulted in poor performance and excessively long inference times.

**Failure Cases and Limitations of LLMs:**
The study also identified current limitations of LLMs:
- **Limited 3D Spatial Reasoning:** LLMs struggle to effectively incorporate precise 3D spatial information, leading to semantically sound but inefficient exploration plans.
- **Lack of Low-Level Action Reasoning:** Abstracting high-level plans for LLMs prevents them from reasoning over low-level actions, which can lead to suboptimal decisions (e.g., misinterpreting a partner's immediate low-level activity).
- **Unstable Performance on Complex Reasoning:** LLMs occasionally make reasoning errors (misunderstanding rules, incorrect counting of goals), causing planning failures.

Overall, the results strongly support the effectiveness of the CoELA framework, demonstrate the profound potential of LLMs in decentralized multi-agent cooperation, and crucially, highlight the transformative impact of natural language communication on human-AI trust and collaboration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly at the confluence of embodied AI, multi-agent systems, and large language models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancing Multi-Agent Cooperation under Realistic Constraints:** The paper formalizes and addresses a highly challenging problem setting that incorporates decentralized control, raw sensory observations, costly natural language communication, and long-horizon, multi-objective tasks. This moves beyond the often simplified assumptions of previous research, bringing multi-agent AI closer to real-world applicability. By directly tackling communication costs, the work contributes to developing more practical and efficient multi-agent coordination strategies.

2. **Unlocking LLM Capabilities for Embodied Collaboration:** It demonstrates that LLMs, particularly powerful models like GPT-4, are not just capable of single-agent planning but can be effectively integrated into modular architectures to drive sophisticated multi-agent cooperation and communication. This validates the potential of LLMs to serve as the "brain" for embodied agents in complex, interactive scenarios.

3. **Novel Cognitive-Inspired Modular Framework (CoELA):** The proposed CoELA framework, with its distinct Perception, Memory, Communication, Planning, and Execution modules, offers a robust and interpretable architecture. This modularity is significant because it allows for independent development and improvement of each component, making the overall system more flexible, debuggable, and extensible compared to monolithic end-to-end approaches. The cognitive inspiration adds a valuable dimension, grounding the AI design in principles of human intelligence.

4. **Demonstration of Emergent Cooperative Behaviors:** The qualitative results showing CoELA agents exhibiting behaviors like strategic information sharing, adaptive planning, requesting/responding to help, and even knowing when *not* to communicate are highly impactful. These are hallmarks of intelligent cooperation and suggest that LLMs can facilitate more nuanced and human-like multi-agent interactions.

5. **Paving the Way for Trustworthy Human-AI Collaboration:** The user study demonstrating that CoELA, when communicating in natural language, earns more trust from humans and leads to more effective cooperation is a crucial finding. This highlights the practical importance of natural language communication in building truly collaborative AI systems that humans are willing and able to work with. It suggests that future embodied AI assistants could be integrated more seamlessly into human teams.

6. **Promising Future for Open-Source LLMs in Embodied AI:** The success of fine-tuning LLAMA-2 into CoLLAMA, achieving competitive performance with a powerful proprietary model like GPT-4, has significant implications. It suggests that it might be possible to develop high-performing cooperative embodied agents using open and more accessible models, democratizing research and development in this area and reducing computational and financial barriers.

7. **Clear Identification of Current LLM Limitations and Future Research Directions:** The paper candidly discusses the current shortcomings of LLMs in areas like 3D spatial reasoning, low-level action reasoning, and occasional reasoning instability. This self-critical analysis is valuable as it provides a clear roadmap for future research, pointing towards the need for multi-modal LLMs, models capable of direct low-level control, and more robust reasoning capabilities.

In summary, this work pushes the boundaries of multi-agent embodied AI by creatively leveraging LLMs, validating their potential for complex cooperation, and offering a modular framework for future development. Its findings have direct implications for creating more intelligent, collaborative, and trustworthy AI agents that can seamlessly integrate into human-centric environments.
