# Self-supervised visual learning in the low-data regime: a comparative evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> A comparative evaluation reveals how Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) methods perform in low-data regimes (50,000-300,000 images), demonstrating that in-domain low-data SSL, particularly DINO, can surpass large-scale generic pretraining for specialized domains like medical or security imaging, while identifying an effectiveness threshold between 50,000 and 65,000 images.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) methods typically require massive unlabeled datasets (millions of images) and significant computational resources for pretraining.
- Specialized real-world applications (e.g., medical imaging, security imaging) often lack the ability to collect millions of relevant unlabeled images, making large-scale SSL impractical.
- A lack of systematic understanding existed regarding the behavior and effectiveness of different SSL categories and architectures when pretraining data is limited.

## Method

- Defined a ==low-data regime== as pretraining datasets ranging from 50,000 to 300,000 images based on empirical observations of SSL effectiveness thresholds.
- Conducted a comprehensive comparative evaluation of four representative SSL methods (==MAE==, ==SimCLR==, ==DINO==, DeepClusterV2) and architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-L/16) across classification and object detection tasks.
- Evaluated the methods on diverse settings including single-dataset, ==transfer learning==, semi-supervised, robustness benchmarks, and crucially, multiple highly distinct domain-specific visual datasets.

## Results

- SimCLR (ResNet-50) consistently achieved the highest accuracy in low-data single-dataset classification scenarios, sometimes outperforming ViT-based DINO.
- MAE and DINO (ViT-L/16) were the most robust low-data SSL approaches across various corruptions, imbalances, and cross-domain shifts.
- In-domain low-data DINO pretraining significantly surpassed DINOv2 and large-scale supervised baselines in medical imaging (ChestX-Det), security imaging (SIXRay-10), and UAV-captured infrared footage (HIT-UAV object detection). An effectiveness threshold was identified between **50,000** and **65,000** images.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For highly specialized visual domains (e.g., medical X-rays, security scans, thermal infrared), in-domain low-data SSL pretraining (especially DINO) outperforms large-scale generic pretraining and even DINOv2.
> - A practical lower bound for SSL effectiveness was identified to be between 50,000 and 65,000 images for single-dataset scenarios, below which pretraining offers minimal benefits.
> - Vision Transformers (ViTs) generally exhibit higher data-hunger than CNNs in very low-data single-dataset settings, but DINO (ViT-based) shows strong generalization in semi-supervised and transfer learning contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) has revolutionized Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) by enabling efficient representation learning from vast amounts of unlabeled data, leading to improved performance in various downstream tasks. The dominant paradigm in recent years has been to scale SSL approaches, employing ever-larger pretraining datasets (often millions or hundreds of millions of images, like ImageNet-1k or LVD-142M for DINOv2) and increasingly complex DNN models (e.g., Vision Transformers). This "bigger is better" philosophy has driven significant advancements but often relies on immense computational resources and the availability of massive generic datasets.

This paper directly challenges and complements this dominant trend by investigating SSL's effectiveness in the **"low-data regime"**, defined as pretraining datasets containing between 50,000 and 300,000 images. The authors motivate this investigation by highlighting real-world limitations: the impracticality or impossibility of collecting millions of unlabeled images in specialized or domain-specific application scenarios (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing) and the computational infeasibility of training large models on such scales for many practitioners.

Therefore, this work fills a critical gap in the literature, which has largely overlooked how SSL methods behave when the assumption of abundant data is not met. It offers a counter-narrative to the prevailing scaling efforts, suggesting that SSL can still be a valuable tool even with constrained data resources, especially if the pretraining data is *in-domain*. By providing a systematic comparative evaluation across different SSL categories (generative, contrastive, clustering, self-distillation) and diverse visual domains, the paper aims to provide practical guidance for researchers and practitioners working with limited data, an increasingly common scenario outside of well-resourced academic and industry labs. It bridges the gap between theoretical SSL advancements and practical, resource-constrained applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the practical challenges associated with applying state-of-the-art Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) in real-world, often resource-constrained, scenarios. While SSL excels with massive datasets and computational power, these are frequently unavailable for specialized or domain-specific applications. Specifically, the authors identify two main bottlenecks:
1. **Data Scarcity**: Collecting millions of relevant, high-quality unlabeled images is often infeasible in specialized domains (e.g., medical, security imaging).
2. **Computational Resources**: Pretraining large DNNs on massive datasets demands significant computational power, which may not be accessible to all research groups or practitioners.

Given these limitations, the paper aims to investigate the effectiveness of common SSL pretext tasks when the pretraining dataset is of relatively limited size. The key objectives of this study are clearly articulated:
- **Identify what is learned via low-data SSL pretraining**: To understand the nature and quality of visual representations acquired by different SSL methods when trained on constrained datasets.
- **Determine how different SSL categories behave in low-data training scenarios**: To conduct a thorough comparative evaluation of representative SSL methods from major categories (contrastive, generative, clustering, self-distillation) to assess their relative performance and characteristics under data scarcity.
- **Provide valuable insights and future research directions**: To extract actionable observations regarding the performance of each SSL category, thereby guiding future research and practical application in low-data settings.

Ultimately, the study seeks to ascertain whether low-data SSL pretraining can still offer significant advantages, especially for domain-specific tasks, compared to traditional supervised pretraining or training from scratch.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a comprehensive comparative evaluation framework designed to rigorously test various SSL methods in a low-data regime.

**Definition of Low-Data Regime:**
The authors define "low-data" as a pretraining dataset size ranging from **50,000 to 300,000 images**. This range was chosen after initial exploratory experiments indicated that SSL pretraining is largely ineffective below 50,000 images, aligning with existing literature on the data requirements for meaningful representation learning without ground-truth labels.

**Selected SSL Methods and Architectures:**
Four widely-used and representative SSL methods, each from a different algorithmic category, were chosen:
- **MAE (Masked Autoencoders)**: Representing the **generative** category.
- **SimCLR (Simple Contrastive Learning of Representations)**: Representing the **contrastive** category.
- **DINO (DIstillation with NO labels)**: Representing the **self-distillation** category.
- **DeepClusterV2**: Representing the **clustering** category.

The neural architectures were chosen based on the original design choices of each method:
- **ResNet-50**: Used for SimCLR and DeepClusterV2.
- **ViT-L/16**: Utilized for MAE and DINO.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
- **Downstream Finetuning**: For whole-image classification tasks, **linear probing** was predominantly used. This involves freezing the pretrained backbone and training only a linear classifier on top of its extracted features, providing a direct measure of the quality of the learnt representations.
- **Object Detection**: For one specific domain, object detection was evaluated using the **YOLOv8** detector, with the pretrained backbones kept frozen and only the neck and detection head modules optimized.
- **Evaluation Metric**: Top-5 classification accuracy (unless specified, like top-1 for low-class datasets) and Mean Average Precision (mAP) for object detection.

**Experimental Settings:** The experiments were divided into three main classes:

1. **Main Experiments (Whole-Image Classification)**:
 - **Granularity**: Object-centric (ImageNet-100) and Scene-centric (Places-100).
 - **Dataset Setup**:
 - **Transfer-learning**: Pretraining and downstream finetuning on *different* datasets (e.g., ImageNet-100 pretraining, COCO/STL-10 finetuning; Places-100 pretraining, ADE20K/SUN finetuning).
 - **Single-dataset**: Pretraining and downstream finetuning on the *same* dataset (e.g., ImageNet-100 pretraining and finetuning).
 - **Semi-supervised Finetuning**: A subset of single-dataset experiments also evaluated semi-supervised finetuning using 5% and 10% of ground-truth labels.

2. **Robustness Experiments**: Evaluated the resilience of learnt representations:
 - **Noisy-ImageNet-100**: Using ImageNet-A, ImageNet-P, and ImageNet-C (derived from ImageNet-1k with various corruptions/adversarial examples).
 - **Imbalanced-ImageNet-100**: Using a long-tail distributed variant of ImageNet-100.
 - **VTAB (Visual Task Adaptation Benchmark)**: Assessing cross-domain transfer learning performance on 19 diverse datasets (natural, specialized, structured tasks).

3. **Domain-Specific Experiments**: Investigated performance in specialized visual domains significantly different from natural RGB images, using a transfer-learning setup.
 - **Domains**: Remote sensing (MLRSNet pretraining, AID finetuning), Medical imaging (MedPix pretraining, ChestX-Det finetuning), Security imaging (SIXRay-100 pretraining, SIXRay-10 finetuning), Microscopy imaging (CEM500K pretraining, RaabinWBC finetuning), and UAV-captured infrared footage (BIRDSAI pretraining, HIT-UAV finetuning for object detection).
 - **Key Aspect**: In-domain SSL pretraining was compared against generic pretraining.

**Baselines:**
- **Supervised Pretraining**: Models pretrained in a supervised manner on ImageNet-1k (large-scale) or ImageNet-100/Places-100/Places-205 (low-data).
- **Training from Scratch**: DNNs initialized randomly and trained directly on the downstream task.
- **DINOv2**: An off-the-shelf, state-of-the-art ViT-L/14 model pretrained on an extremely large dataset (LVD-142M, 142 million images), representing the "scaling up" paradigm.

**Analysis of Learnt Representations:**
To provide deeper insights into the quality of learnt features, quantitative and qualitative analyses were performed:
- **Silhouette Coefficient**: A cluster validity index (median S_m) was used to quantify the compactness and separation of class representations in the latent space.
- **RELAX (REpresentation LeArning eXplainability)**: This method generates attribution maps (importance and uncertainty heatmaps) in pixel space, revealing which image regions are most critical for the feature extractor and the confidence of these explanations.

This comprehensive experimental design allows for a multi-faceted evaluation of low-data SSL across various tasks, domain shifts, and robustness challenges, providing a robust foundation for the derived insights.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation yielded several critical insights into the behavior and effectiveness of SSL in the low-data regime:

**Performance in Main Experiments (Whole-Image Classification):**

- **Single-Dataset Setup (e.g., IN-100↓IN-100, P-100↓P-100):**
 - **SimCLR (ResNet-50) emerged as the best-performing SSL method**, consistently achieving the highest accuracy, even slightly outperforming DINO (ViT-L/16).
 - **Crucially, CNN-based SSL (SimCLR) often outperformed ViT-based SSL (DINO)**. This suggests that Vision Transformers, known for their "data hunger," may be less suitable for very low-data single-dataset scenarios compared to CNNs.
 - **Identified SSL Effectiveness Threshold**: Experiments with subsampled ImageNet-100 revealed a pretraining dataset size limit between **50,000 and 65,000 images** (assuming identical pretraining and downstream domains), below which SSL pretraining becomes largely ineffective for learning useful representations.
- **Transfer-Learning Setup (e.g., IN-100↓COCO, P-100↓ADE):**
 - **DINO (ViT-L/16) was the leading SSL method** within the low-data regime, indicating its superior ability to learn more general image representations.
 - However, **all low-data SSL methods, including DINO, were generally surpassed by conventional supervised baselines** (even low-data supervised pretraining on ImageNet-100/Places-100) and significantly by the large-scale pretrained DINOv2 model. This suggests that for generic transfer tasks, supervised pretraining (even if low-data) or massive-scale pretraining still holds an advantage.
- **Semi-supervised Single-Dataset Setup:**
 - Baseline approaches (random initialization) generally outperformed low-data SSL methods.
 - **DINO (ViT-L/16) was the best-performing SSL method** in object-centric scenarios, especially with 10% labeled data, reinforcing its capability to learn robust, generally applicable embeddings even with limited supervision.

**Robustness Analysis:**

- **MAE (ViT-L/16) and DINO (ViT-L/16) were identified as the most robust low-data SSL approaches** across various corruption (Noisy-ImageNet-100), imbalance (Imbalanced-ImageNet-100), and cross-domain shift (VTAB) benchmarks.
- Despite their relative robustness among low-data SSL methods, **none could surpass the performance of large-scale supervised baselines or DINOv2**.
- **MAE's potential for data efficiency**: MAE exhibited an advantage over DINO in VTAB, suggesting it might require less pretraining data to learn inherently semantically discriminant and generally applicable features. However, this did not translate to overall superiority due to the inherent limitations of the low-data regime.

**Domain-Specific Performance (Key Finding):**

- **In-domain low-data SSL pretraining (specifically DINO) significantly outperformed all other approaches, *including large-scale supervised pretraining on generic datasets and DINOv2***, in highly distinct domains like **medical imaging (ChestX-Det), security imaging (SIXRay-10), and UAV-captured infrared footage (HIT-UAV for object detection)**.
 - This critical finding highlights the **power of domain relevance**: when the target domain substantially differs from conventional RGB natural images (e.g., X-rays, thermal images), in-domain SSL, even with limited data, can provide superior feature representations compared to transferring knowledge from massive but out-of-domain datasets.
 - For the security imaging domain (SIXRay-10), with extreme class imbalance, SSL methods successfully learned generic features from all images, overcoming the limitations faced by large-scale supervised pretraining in cases of feature space discrepancy.
- **Remote Sensing (AID)**: For remote sensing (aerial RGB images), large-scale pretraining baselines (DINOv2, ImageNet-1k supervised) still performed better, although in-domain low-data DINO did outperform low-data ImageNet-100 supervised pretraining. This suggests that for domains still within the RGB spectrum, generic large-scale pretraining might retain its edge.
- **Microscopy Imaging (RaabinWBC)**: DINOv2 achieved the highest performance, followed closely by in-domain low-data DINO pretraining. This suggests that LVD-142M might contain relevant microscopy images, or that the sheer scale of DINOv2's pretraining enables exceptional generalization even to novel domains.

**Insights from Representation Explanations (Silhouette Coefficient & RELAX):**

- **Strong Correlation**: The quantitative silhouette coefficient (S_m) values closely correlated with downstream classification accuracy, confirming that better-performing SSL methods indeed learn to form more distinct, compact, and well-separated class representations in the latent space.
- **DINO's Attention**: RELAX visualizations indicated that DINO tends to diffuse its attention across multiple image regions, leading to features with generally lower uncertainty across the image. This broad, low-uncertainty focus likely contributes to its superior generalization capabilities.
- **MAE's Attention**: MAE showed a more focused attention on a few contiguous key areas, often highly consistent with the visible foreground object. This might explain its competitive robustness in scenarios like VTAB, as it emphasizes object-centric features, potentially needing less pretraining data for semantic discrimination.

**Summary of Key Experimental Insights:**
In essence, while large-scale supervised and DINOv2 pretraining generally dominate generic and robustness benchmarks, low-data in-domain SSL (especially DINO) provides a compelling advantage in highly specialized visual domains where massive, relevant unlabeled data is scarce and transfer from generic datasets is suboptimal due to significant domain shifts. There's also a clear trade-off between CNNs and ViTs in very low-data scenarios, with CNNs sometimes outperforming ViTs in single-dataset settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant practical and theoretical implications for the field of computer vision, particularly for applications operating under data and resource constraints.

**Practical Significance:**

- **Empowering Domain-Specific Applications**: The most impactful finding is the demonstration that **in-domain low-data SSL pretraining can outperform even large-scale supervised pretraining and foundation models like DINOv2 when the target domain diverges significantly from conventional natural RGB images (e.g., medical X-rays, security scans, thermal infrared)**. This is a game-changer for specialized fields (e.g., healthcare, defense, industrial inspection) where collecting millions of annotated images is impractical, expensive, or impossible, and public large-scale datasets offer limited relevance. It validates a viable pathway for leveraging deep learning in these critical areas without massive resources.
- **Guidance for Practitioners**: The study provides concrete guidance on the expected performance of different SSL categories and architectures (CNN vs. ViT) in the low-data regime. Identifying the 50k-65k image lower bound for SSL effectiveness helps practitioners make informed decisions about whether SSL is viable for their specific dataset size. The insights into DINO's generalization and MAE's focused attention offer hints for selecting appropriate methods based on task requirements (e.g., general embeddings vs. object-centric robustness).
- **Reduced Resource Dependence**: By showing that effective visual representation learning is possible with significantly fewer pretraining images, the work encourages the adoption of advanced deep learning techniques in environments with limited computational power and data collection capabilities.

**Theoretical Significance:**

- **Challenging the "Bigger is Better" Paradigm**: This research provides a crucial counterpoint to the prevailing belief that ever-larger datasets and models are always superior for SSL. It highlights the importance of *domain relevance* over sheer data volume, especially when domain shifts are substantial. This could spark further research into more data-efficient SSL methods.
- **Understanding Architectural Nuances**: The observation that CNNs (ResNet with SimCLR) sometimes outperform ViTs (ViT with DINO) in very low-data single-dataset settings sheds light on the "data hunger" of Vision Transformers, suggesting that inductive biases inherent in CNNs might be more beneficial when data is extremely scarce.
- **Interpretable SSL**: The use of silhouette coefficients and RELAX for explaining learnt representations provides a valuable framework for understanding *why* certain SSL methods perform better. This moves beyond pure performance metrics to offer deeper insights into the quality and nature of the learned features, which can guide future algorithm design.

**Future Research Directions (as suggested by authors):**

- **Domain-Specific SSL Adaptations**: Future work should focus on developing general SSL methodologies tailored to specific domain properties (e.g., unique features of X-ray scans, pixel-level overlaps), aiming for higher data efficiency and better domain-specific feature learning, potentially overcoming the identified data size limit.
- **Hybrid SSL Approaches**: Given DINO's generalization capabilities and MAE's robustness and focused feature learning, exploring hybrid methods that combine the strengths of self-distillation and generative masked image modeling could lead to new SSL algorithms that offer tunable robustness-performance trade-offs suitable for small datasets.

In conclusion, this paper significantly advances our understanding of SSL's applicability beyond the realm of abundant data. It provides a robust empirical foundation for leveraging self-supervised learning in data-scarce and domain-specific real-world scenarios, thereby broadening the accessibility and utility of cutting-edge deep learning techniques.
