# EmbodiedBench: Comprehensive Benchmarking Multi-modal Large Language Models for Vision-Driven Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents EMBODIEDBENCH, a new comprehensive benchmark for evaluating Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as vision-driven embodied agents. Experiments with 24 MLLMs reveal that while models perform strongly on high-level planning tasks, they significantly struggle with low-level physical interactions like precise manipulation and long-horizon tasks, with GPT-4o achieving only 28.9% on manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for embodied agents are often tailored for Large Language Models (LLMs), lacking multimodal input capabilities necessary for evaluating Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- Current evaluation frameworks primarily focus on high-level symbolic planning or specific domains, neglecting low-level physical interaction (e.g., precise manipulation and continuous navigation).
- There is a lack of fine-grained evaluation to diagnose specific capabilities (e.g., spatial awareness, visual perception) of MLLMs in embodied contexts, beyond overall task success.

## Method

- Introduces **EMBODIEDBENCH**, a comprehensive benchmark for vision-driven MLLM-based embodied agents, featuring **1,128 tasks** across four distinct environments (==EB-ALFRED==, ==EB-Habitat==, ==EB-Navigation==, ==EB-Manipulation==).
- Covers both ==high-level tasks== (household planning) and ==low-level atomic actions== (precise navigation, robotic arm manipulation) to assess MLLMs across hierarchical action levels.
- Provides a ==fine-grained capability-oriented evaluation== across six dimensions: commonsense reasoning, complex instruction understanding, spatial awareness, visual perception, long-term planning, and basic task solving.

## Results

- MLLMs achieved over **60%** success rates on high-level tasks (EB-ALFRED, EB-Habitat), but the best model (**GPT-4o**) scored only **28.9%** on average for low-level manipulation (EB-Manipulation).
- Removing visual input caused GPT-4o's EB-Navigation performance to drop from **57.7% → 17.4%**, demonstrating vision's criticality for low-level tasks.
- Providing detection boxes boosted EB-Manipulation performance by **+12.5%** for GPT-4o, and visual in-context learning improved manipulation tasks by **+16.7%** for Claude-3.5-Sonnet.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While MLLMs show strong performance in high-level planning and reasoning for embodied tasks, they struggle significantly with low-level, fine-grained physical interaction and manipulation.
> - Visual input is indispensable for low-level embodied tasks (e.g., navigation and manipulation), whereas high-level tasks sometimes rely more heavily on an MLLM's textual understanding and reasoning.
> - Long-horizon planning remains a primary bottleneck for MLLM-based agents across all task complexities, indicating limitations in multi-step reasoning and memory.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied agents capable of navigating and interacting with the real world is a grand challenge in AI. Recent advancements in foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), have shown unprecedented potential in enabling human-like reasoning and task comprehension for these agents. This paper specifically addresses a critical bottleneck in this rapidly evolving field: the lack of comprehensive and standardized evaluation frameworks for MLLM-based embodied agents.

The research landscape has seen various initiatives for evaluating LLM-based embodied agents, such as AgentBench, Lota-bench, and Embodied Agent Interface. These benchmarks have significantly contributed to understanding how LLMs can be used for high-level planning in simulated environments like household tasks (e.g., ALFWorld, ALFRED, Behavior-1K, Language Rearrangement). However, the authors argue that these existing benchmarks suffer from several limitations. Many lack support for multimodal input, focusing predominantly on textual information, which inherently limits their applicability to MLLMs that leverage vision. Furthermore, most existing benchmarks are narrowly focused on high-level symbolic planning or specific domains like household tasks, leaving crucial aspects of embodied intelligence, such as low-level physical interaction (e.g., manipulation, precise navigation), underexplored. For instance, VisualAgentBench was a pioneering effort for MLLM agents but was restricted to high-level planning, neglecting the role of vision in low-level control. Benchmarks like VLMbench and GOAT-bench address low-level control but are specialized for manipulation or navigation, lacking comprehensive multi-domain coverage.

EMBODIEDBENCH significantly advances this landscape by introducing a multi-domain, multi-action-level benchmark with a fine-grained, capability-oriented evaluation framework. It bridges the gap by:
1. **Enabling comprehensive MLLM evaluation:** Unlike many LLM-centric benchmarks, EMBODIEDBENCH is explicitly designed for vision-driven MLLMs.
2. **Covering hierarchical action levels:** It uniquely includes both high-level semantic tasks (e.g., household rearrangements) and low-level atomic actions requiring precise visual perception and spatial reasoning (e.g., robot arm manipulation and continuous navigation). This addresses the critical gap in evaluating how MLLMs translate high-level plans into granular physical actions.
3. **Offering fine-grained capability assessment:** Moving beyond simple overall accuracy, EMBODIEDBENCH dissects agent performance across six essential capabilities: commonsense reasoning, complex instruction understanding, spatial awareness, visual perception, long-term planning, and basic task solving. This allows for a much deeper understanding of MLLMs' strengths and weaknesses in embodied contexts.

By providing a more holistic and diagnostically informative evaluation platform, EMBODIEDBENCH sets a new standard, pushing the field beyond high-level planning toward more robust, versatile, and physically grounded embodied AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **introduce EMBODIEDBENCH, a comprehensive and standardized benchmark for evaluating vision-driven embodied agents powered by Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs).** The authors motivate this objective by highlighting a critical gap in the existing research landscape: while MLLMs show immense promise for real-world applications, their comprehensive evaluation as embodied agents remains largely underexplored due to the lack of appropriate frameworks.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives are:

- **To bridge the evaluation gap for MLLM-based embodied agents:** Current benchmarks are often tailored for Large Language Models (LLMs) and lack multimodal input capabilities, or they are too narrowly focused on specific high-level tasks, leaving the full potential of MLLMs in vision-driven embodied scenarios unassessed.
- **To provide diverse tasks with hierarchical action levels:** The benchmark aims to evaluate agents not only on high-level semantic planning (e.g., household tasks) but also on low-level, atomic actions requiring precise control, navigation, and manipulation. This is crucial for understanding how MLLMs perform across the full spectrum of embodied tasks.
- **To enable capability-oriented, fine-grained evaluation:** Beyond overall task success, the benchmark seeks to assess specific, essential capabilities of embodied agents, such as commonsense reasoning, understanding complex instructions, spatial awareness, visual perception, and long-term planning. This fine-grained analysis is intended to pinpoint the strengths and weaknesses of current MLLMs, providing diagnostic insights for future model development.
- **To establish a standardized platform:** By offering a unified agent framework and a diverse set of testing instances across multiple environments, EMBODIEDBENCH aims to facilitate consistent and reproducible comparisons among different MLLM architectures and agent designs.
- **To highlight existing challenges and provide valuable insights:** Through extensive experimentation with state-of-the-art MLLMs, the authors intend to reveal the current limitations and bottlenecks in MLLM-based embodied agents, thereby guiding future research directions and inspiring advancements in the field.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond mere "task success" to a deeper understanding of *why* agents succeed or fail, and to illuminate the path forward for developing more capable and robust embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on designing and implementing EMBODIEDBENCH, a multifaceted benchmark, and then evaluating a wide array of MLLMs using a unified agent pipeline.

#### 3.1. Definition of Action Levels

The paper distinguishes between two levels of actions in embodied AI:
- **Low-level actions:** These are atomic commands directly executable by robots, specifying precise translational or rotational displacements (e.g., a 7-dimensional vector for a robotic arm or "move forward 0.1m").
- **High-level actions:** These are sequences of low-level primitives, representing abstract commands that require decomposition (e.g., "find a HandTowel").

#### 3.2. Vision-driven Agent Formulation

The problem is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) augmented with language instructions. The agent's goal is to maximize the probability of task success by selecting actions through a policy $\pi(a_t|L, h_t)$, where $L$ is the language instruction and $h_t$ is the interaction history including visual observations ($I_t$).

#### 3.3. EMBODIEDBENCH Design

The benchmark comprises 1,128 testing tasks across four distinct environments, carefully chosen to cover both high-level and low-level actions and diverse scenarios.

- **High-level Tasks:**
 - **EB-ALFRED:** Based on the ALFRED dataset and AI2-THOR simulator, focusing on household tasks (e.g., "put a book on the desk"). Improvements include supporting multiple instances of the same object, streamlining the action space, and correcting simulator errors.
 - **EB-Habitat:** Built on the Language Rearrangement benchmark and Habitat 2.0 simulator, featuring 282 language instruction templates for robotic rearrangement. It complements EB-ALFRED with more constrained navigation (only to receptacle objects) and broader high-level skills.

- **Low-level Tasks:**
 - **EB-Navigation:** An AI2-THOR-based suite assessing navigation abilities. Agents rely solely on visual observations and textual feedback to locate target objects using 8 low-level actions (move, rotate, tilt camera). Includes automatic task generation for customization.
 - **EB-Manipulation:** Extends VLMBench using the CoppeliaSim simulator and a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robotic arm. It involves low-level object manipulation. To facilitate MLLM control, the action space is discretized (e.g., 7D vector with binned coordinates), and additional information like YOLO detection boxes with index markers and object pose estimations are provided.

#### 3.4. Capability-oriented Data Collection

The benchmark introduces six meticulously curated subsets for fine-grained evaluation:
- **Base:** Evaluates fundamental task-solving skills.
- **Common Sense:** Assesses reasoning with indirect object references using common knowledge.
- **Complex Instruction:** Measures understanding in the presence of long, potentially irrelevant contexts.
- **Spatial Awareness:** Tests understanding of objects' relative locations.
- **Visual Appearance:** Evaluates object identification based on visual attributes (color, shape).
- **Long Horizon:** Contains tasks requiring extended action sequences.

Data collection strategies varied by environment, combining manual annotation, GPT-4o-based augmentation, and adaptation of existing datasets to align with the specific capability definitions.

#### 3.5. Vision-driven Agent Design

A unified agent pipeline is designed to interface MLLMs with the environments:

- **Agent Input:** Integrates language instructions, egocentric visual perceptions (current step image primarily, but multi-step images explored), few-shot in-context demonstrations, interaction history, and environment feedback. Task-specific information (e.g., valid skill sets, action format descriptions, detection boxes, object positions for EB-Manipulation) is also provided.
- **Task Planner:** At each step, the MLLM performs five key functions structured in JSON output:
 1. Generates a textual description of the current visual input.
 2. Reflects on past actions and environment feedback.
 3. Reasons about how to achieve the goal.
 4. Formulates a language-based plan.
 5. Converts the language plan into an executable action sequence.
The pipeline supports *multi-step planning*, allowing the agent to generate multiple atomic actions per inference call, thereby reducing API calls and improving efficiency. If a plan fails or an invalid action occurs, planning restarts from the latest state.

This comprehensive methodological approach ensures a robust and diagnostic evaluation of MLLMs in embodied AI settings, covering a wide range of task complexities and agent capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments by evaluating 24 leading proprietary and open-source MLLMs within EMBODIEDBENCH, yielding several key findings:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Trends

- **High-level vs. Low-level Tasks:** MLLMs demonstrate strong performance on high-level tasks (e.g., household planning in EB-ALFRED and EB-Habitat), with top models achieving success rates over 60%. However, they significantly struggle with low-level tasks, particularly fine-grained manipulation (EB-Manipulation), where the best model, GPT-4o, scored only 28.9% on average. This highlights a considerable gap between abstract planning and concrete physical execution.
- **Model Performance:** Among proprietary models, Claude-3.5-Sonnet generally excelled at high-level tasks (64.0% on EB-ALFRED, 68.0% on EB-Habitat), while GPT-4o led in low-level tasks (57.7% on EB-Navigation, 28.9% on EB-Manipulation). For open-source models, InternVL3-78B showed the strongest overall performance, sometimes nearing GPT-4o's low-level performance, and demonstrating a clear scaling trend with larger models performing better. However, a noticeable performance gap persists between top proprietary and open-source models in reasoning-intensive high-level tasks.

#### 4.2. The Crucial Role of Vision

- **Low-level Tasks' Reliance on Vision:** Visual input is critically important for low-level tasks. For instance, removing vision caused GPT-4o's EB-Navigation performance to plummet from 57.7% to 17.4%, with long-horizon navigation tasks completely failing (0%). This underscores that visual perception is indispensable for precise control and navigation.
- **High-level Tasks' Lower Reliance on Vision:** Surprisingly, high-level tasks showed less dependence on visual input. GPT-4o (Lang) and GPT-4o-mini (Lang) sometimes performed on par with or even slightly better than their vision-enabled counterparts in EB-ALFRED and EB-Habitat. This suggests that these tasks might lean more heavily on the MLLMs' powerful textual understanding and reasoning abilities, rather than detailed visual processing, for overall planning.

#### 4.3. Fine-grained Capability Analysis

- **Varying Performance Across Subsets:** Models exhibited varied strengths across the six capability subsets, emphasizing the importance of fine-grained evaluation. For example, Claude-3.5-Sonnet outperformed GPT-4o on specific subsets like complex instruction and visual appearance in EB-Manipulation, despite GPT-4o's higher overall average.
- **Long-Horizon Planning as the Most Challenging:** Consistently across both high-level and low-level tasks, the "Long-Horizon" subset proved to be the most difficult. Models showed significant performance drops (e.g., Claude-3.5-Sonnet from 96% to 58% in EB-Habitat base vs. long-horizon), indicating that planning and executing extended action sequences remains a major bottleneck for current MLLM-based agents.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Insights

- **Language-centric Ablations (High-level Tasks):** Removing environment feedback significantly reduced success rates (e.g., 10% for GPT-4o in EB-ALFRED), and reducing the number of in-context examples severely impacted performance (down to ~40% in 0-shot settings). This confirms that high-level tasks rely heavily on explicit textual information and structured prompting.
- **Visual-centric Ablations (Low-level Tasks):**
 - **Camera Resolution:** Mid-range resolutions (500x500) generally performed best for low-level tasks, suggesting a trade-off between detail and complexity. Too low resolution lacks information, while excessively high resolution might introduce noise or overwhelm the model.
 - **Detection Boxes:** Providing detection boxes and visual markers significantly improved performance in EB-Manipulation (e.g., 12.5% boost for GPT-4o), aiding object localization. However, they surprisingly hindered performance in EB-Navigation, possibly by cluttering visual cues.
 - **Multi-step/Multi-view Images:** Integrating multiple historical images or multiple camera views (front and wrist/top-down) often did *not* improve performance; instead, it sometimes led to a decline. This indicates that current MLLMs struggle to effectively integrate and leverage complex temporal or multi-perspective visual information.
 - **Visual In-context Learning (ICL):** Including image observations as part of in-context examples significantly boosted performance, especially in manipulation tasks (e.g., 16.7% for Claude-3.5-Sonnet). This highlights visual ICL as a promising avenue for improving MLLM's understanding of action-perception relationships.

#### 4.5. Error Analysis (GPT-4o)

- **Dominant Error Types:** Planning errors were the most common failure mode in both high-level (55% in EB-ALFRED) and low-level (44% in EB-Manipulation) tasks. Reasoning errors (41% in EB-ALFRED) were also significant.
- **Specific Error Manifestations:**
 - **Planning Errors:** Often involved "missing steps" or "invalid actions" (EB-ALFRED) and "inaccurate actions" (EB-Manipulation), indicating difficulties in generating complete, valid, and precise action sequences.
 - **Reasoning Errors:** Included "reflection errors" (failure to learn from feedback) and "wrong termination decisions" (premature task completion).
 - **Perception Errors:** More prevalent in low-level tasks (33% in EB-Manipulation), primarily "wrong recognition" of object attributes, even with aids like detection boxes.

Overall, the findings underscore that while MLLMs possess impressive high-level reasoning, significant challenges remain in grounding these capabilities in precise physical interaction and integrating complex visual streams effectively, especially for long-horizon and low-level tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EMBODIEDBENCH represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and the evaluation of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Comprehensive and Standardized Evaluation

The primary contribution is the introduction of a benchmark suite that is far more comprehensive than its predecessors. By encompassing tasks with diverse action levels (high-level household, low-level navigation, and manipulation) and enabling fine-grained, capability-oriented evaluation across six distinct dimensions (commonsense, complex instruction, spatial awareness, visual perception, long-horizon planning, and base task-solving), EMBODIEDBENCH provides a much-needed standardized platform. This standardization is crucial for:
- **Fair Comparison:** Allowing researchers to compare different MLLM architectures and embodied agent designs on an equitable footing.
- **Reproducibility:** Providing a clear, well-defined environment and dataset for experiments, fostering reproducible research.
- **Diagnostic Insights:** Moving beyond a single success metric to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of MLLMs, enabling targeted improvements.

#### 5.2. Highlighting Key Challenges and Guiding Research

The extensive evaluation of 24 leading MLLMs within EMBODIEDBENCH yielded critical insights that directly inform future research directions:
- **Low-level Control Gap:** The stark finding that MLLMs excel at high-level planning but struggle significantly with low-level manipulation (e.g., GPT-4o's 28.9% average in EB-Manipulation) highlights a major research frontier. Future work must focus on better grounding MLLMs in physical realities and enabling more precise action generation.
- **Long-Horizon Planning Bottleneck:** The consistent difficulty with long-horizon tasks across all environments indicates a need for advancements in multi-step reasoning, memory, and hierarchical planning within MLLM-based agents.
- **Nuanced Role of Vision:** The finding that vision is crucial for low-level tasks but less so for high-level ones (where language dominates) suggests that MLLMs need more sophisticated mechanisms for integrating and prioritizing multimodal information based on task requirements. Furthermore, the challenges with multi-step/multi-view image inputs point to a need for more effective temporal and spatial fusion techniques.
- **Promise of Visual In-Context Learning:** The demonstrated effectiveness of visual ICL in boosting performance offers a promising direction for improving model adaptability without extensive fine-tuning, emphasizing the importance of visual exemplars in grounding MLLMs for embodied tasks.
- **Error Analysis for Targeted Improvement:** The detailed error taxonomy and analysis (planning errors, reasoning errors, perception errors) provide actionable insights for developers to address specific failure modes, from improving action precision to enhancing object recognition and reflection capabilities.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications and Future Impact

While the current work is limited to simulated environments, its findings are highly relevant for the development of real-world embodied agents in applications such as household robotics, logistics, and autonomous systems. By identifying the current limitations and offering diagnostic tools, EMBODIEDBENCH will inspire the development of:
- **More Capable Agents:** Agents that can not only understand complex human instructions but also execute them precisely in physical environments.
- **Robust Agents:** Agents that can better handle visual ambiguities, learn from feedback, and generalize across diverse scenarios.
- **Efficient Agent Architectures:** Insights from the multi-step planning and ablation studies can guide the design of more computationally efficient agent frameworks for MLLMs.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors transparently acknowledge the primary limitation: evaluation solely in simulated environments. While simulations offer benefits like reproducibility and safety, bridging the "sim-to-real" gap remains a crucial next step. Future work should explore more complex and realistic simulations, and eventually, standardized real-world test suites. The paper also outlines several promising future research directions, including expanding task diversity to more complex scenarios (e.g., autonomous driving, multi-agent collaboration), enhancing low-level and spatial reasoning, improving long-horizon planning via memory and world models, developing better multi-image understanding, optimizing visual ICL, and training dedicated multimodal embodied agents through various learning paradigms. Addressing these challenges, guided by EMBODIEDBENCH, will be vital for realizing the full potential of MLLM-based embodied agents in practical applications.

In conclusion, EMBODIEDBENCH is a timely and impactful contribution that significantly advances the evaluation paradigm for MLLM-based embodied agents, setting a clear agenda for future research in this exciting and challenging domain.
