# MemoryVLA: Perceptual-Cognitive Memory in Vision-Language-Action Models for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Dexmal developed MemoryVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for robotic manipulation that integrates a bio-inspired dual-memory system. This system allows robots to leverage both short-term perceptual details and long-term cognitive semantics, achieving up to a +26 percentage point gain on real-world long-horizon temporal tasks compared to prior VLA models.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation primarily rely on instantaneous observations, neglecting temporal context.
- This leads to poor performance in long-horizon, temporally dependent, and non-Markovian tasks where past events are crucial for decision-making (e.g., tracking progress or avoiding redundant actions).
- Existing temporal modeling attempts often suffer from computational inefficiency (frame concatenation), loss of fine-grained detail (latent propagation), or superficial reasoning (chain-of-thought).

## Method

- A ==Cognition-Memory-Action framework== (**MemoryVLA**) is introduced, inspired by the human dual-memory system, integrating vision-language processing with structured memory.
- A ==Perceptual–Cognitive Memory Bank (PCMB)== is designed to store both ==low-level perceptual details== and ==high-level cognitive semantics== over extended horizons.
- The PCMB features mechanisms for ==retrieving decision-relevant historical context==, adaptively fusing it with current observations via a ==gate mechanism==, and ==consolidating== new experiences to maintain a compact memory.

## Results

- **MemoryVLA** achieved an average success rate of **71.9%** on SimplerEnv-Bridge, outperforming the CogACT-Large baseline by **+14.6** percentage points.
- On real-world long-horizon temporal tasks, it demonstrated an **83%** success score, marking a substantial **+26** percentage point gain over CogACT and significant improvements on tasks like "Seq. Push Buttons" (**+43**).
- The model showed strong robustness and generalization across various out-of-distribution conditions and introduced minimal inference overhead (**+3.6%** latency, **+0.8 GB** GPU memory) compared to a baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adopting a dual-memory system (working and episodic-like memory) allows VLA models to effectively manage both immediate and long-term temporal information for complex tasks.
> - Explicitly distinguishing and storing perceptual details and cognitive semantics enables a more comprehensive understanding of task progress and context over time.
> - Adaptive gate fusion dynamically balances the influence of current observations and retrieved historical context, while redundancy-aware consolidation efficiently manages memory capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on MemoryVLA is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation**. This domain has seen remarkable progress, primarily driven by the synergy of large-scale cross-embodiment robotic datasets (e.g., Open-X Embodiment, Agibot) and powerful pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Prismatic VLM, LLaMA, and Qwen. Mainstream VLA models, such as OpenVLA, RT-2, and π0, have demonstrated capabilities in various manipulation tasks by tokenizing actions and leveraging VLMs for policy learning.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation: most contemporary VLA models tend to **overlook temporal context**, relying predominantly on the current observation. This leads to significant performance degradation in **long-horizon, temporally dependent, and inherently non-Markovian robotic tasks**. For instance, tasks like pushing buttons may not exhibit clear visual changes before and after the action, making it difficult for models without memory to track progress or avoid redundant actions.

Existing attempts to incorporate temporal modeling in robotics have faced challenges:
- **Naive frame concatenation:** Suffers from the quadratic complexity of self-attention in Transformers, severely limiting the usable temporal context length, and is often misaligned with the single-frame pretraining distribution of many VLMs.
- **Sequential video models (e.g., Octo, RoboVLMs, Interleave-VLA):** Adapt VLM paradigms to handle interleaved image-text video data, but can be computationally expensive and complex to implement.
- **Latent representation propagation (e.g., RoboFlamingo):** Uses LSTMs to propagate compressed vision-language representations but may discard fine-grained perceptual details, leading to coarser temporal understanding.
- **Trajectory painting (e.g., TraceVLA):** Encodes historical states by "painting" trajectories on the current frame, but sacrifices rich semantic details.
- **Chain-of-Thought approaches (e.g., UniVLA):** Incorporates past actions into prompts but often serves as a superficial reasoning process without effectively leveraging historical information for decision-making.

MemoryVLA addresses this gap by drawing inspiration from **human cognitive science, specifically the dual-memory system** (working memory and episodic memory). This bio-inspired approach seeks to explicitly model and integrate both **low-level perceptual details and high-level cognitive semantics** over extended horizons, a capability largely missing or inefficiently handled by prior methods. By proposing a structured memory mechanism that can retrieve, fuse, and consolidate information, MemoryVLA aims to equip VLA models with a more robust and human-like understanding of temporal dynamics in complex robotic manipulation. It positions itself as a significant advancement in building more capable and intelligent embodied AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for MemoryVLA stems from a fundamental limitation in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their **inability to effectively handle temporal dependencies in robotic manipulation tasks**. The authors highlight that real-world manipulation is often non-Markovian, meaning the optimal action at any given moment depends not just on the current observation but also on past events, states, and actions. This oversight leads to significant failures in long-horizon tasks where a robot might struggle to track progress, avoid redundant actions, or complete a sequence of context-dependent steps. A concrete example provided is the "Push Buttons" task, where the visual state before and after pushing a button can be nearly identical, rendering a current-observation-only policy ineffective.

Inspired by insights from cognitive science, the authors motivate their approach by observing how humans tackle similar challenges. Human cognition employs a sophisticated **dual-memory system**:
1. **Working memory:** Acts as a short-term buffer for transient neural activities, holding immediate perceptual and cognitive representations for current control.
2. **Episodic memory (supported by the hippocampus):** A long-term system that stores past experiences, including both precise "verbatim" details and abstract "gist" representations, temporally indexed. This system retrieves decision-relevant context and integrates it with current perceptions to guide actions, while also consolidating new experiences.

Translating this biological inspiration into an AI framework, the key objectives of the MemoryVLA project are:
- **To propose a Cognition-Memory-Action framework (MemoryVLA)** that explicitly models and leverages temporal dependencies for robust robotic manipulation.
- **To design a Perceptual–Cognitive Memory Bank (PCMB)** inspired by the hippocampus, capable of storing both low-level perceptual details and high-level cognitive semantics over long horizons.
- **To integrate this PCMB with working memory**, allowing the system to:
 - **Retrieve** decision-relevant historical contexts from the bank.
 - **Adaptively fuse** these retrieved contexts with current observations.
 - **Consolidate** new experiences into the bank by merging redundancies, thereby maintaining a compact yet informative memory.
- **To condition a diffusion action expert** with these memory-augmented representations to generate temporally aware and multi-step robotic action sequences.
- **To achieve state-of-the-art performance** on challenging long-horizon and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, specifically outperforming existing VLA models that lack explicit temporal memory.
- **To demonstrate strong robustness and generalization** under diverse out-of-distribution conditions, showcasing the practical utility of the proposed memory mechanism.

In essence, MemoryVLA aims to imbue robotic agents with a more human-like capacity for temporal reasoning, allowing them to effectively plan and execute complex, multi-stage tasks in dynamic environments by intelligently recalling and integrating past experiences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MemoryVLA proposes an end-to-end Cognition-Memory-Action framework that integrates VLM capabilities with a bio-inspired dual-memory system for robust robotic manipulation. The methodology is structured into three main modules: Vision-Language Cognition, Perceptual–Cognitive Memory, and Memory-Conditioned Action Expert.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as a sequential decision-making process where a policy $\pi$ maps a current RGB image $I$ and a language instruction $L$ to a sequence of future actions $A = (a_1, \dots, a_T)$. Each action $a_t$ is a 7-DoF vector comprising relative translation, relative rotation (Euler angles), and a binary gripper state.

#### 3.2. Overall Architecture

As illustrated in Figure 2, the framework begins by encoding visual observations and language instructions into perceptual and cognitive tokens, forming a short-term **working memory**. This working memory interacts with a **Perceptual–Cognitive Memory Bank (PCMB)**, which stores long-term high-level semantics and fine-grained perceptual details. The working memory retrieves relevant history from the PCMB, adaptively fuses it with current representations, and updates the PCMB through consolidation. The resulting memory-augmented representations then condition a **memory-conditioned diffusion action expert** to generate future action sequences.

#### 3.3. Vision-Language Cognition Module

This module is responsible for extracting current perceptual and cognitive representations.
1. **VLM Backbone:** Utilizes a 7B-parameter Prismatic VLM, which is further pretrained on the large-scale Open-X Embodiment dataset.
2. **Visual Encoding:** Parallel DINOv2 and SigLIP backbones process the current third-person RGB image $I$ to generate raw visual tokens.
3. **Perceptual Compression:** A SE-bottleneck module compresses these raw visual tokens into a compact set of $N_p=256$ **perceptual tokens** $p \in \mathbb{R}^{N_p \times d_p}$.
4. **Cognitive Token Generation:** Raw visual tokens are projected into the language embedding space and concatenated with the tokenized language instruction $L$. This combined input is fed into an LLaMA-7B model. The output at the end-of-sentence (EOS) position is taken as the **cognitive token** $c \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_c}$, summarizing high-level cognitive semantics.
5. **Working Memory:** The generated perceptual tokens $p$ and cognitive token $c$ together constitute the short-term working memory $M_{wk} = \{p, c\}$.

#### 3.4. Perceptual–Cognitive Memory Module (PCMB)

Inspired by the hippocampus, the PCMB stores and manages long-term temporal context.
1. **Memory Bank Structure:** $M_{pcmb} = \{m_x | x \in \{per, cog\}\}$, where $m_x = \{m_{x_i} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x \times d_x}\}_{i=1}^L$ stores up to $L$ entries for both perceptual and cognitive streams.
2. **Memory Retrieval (Figure 3a):**
 - The current working memory ($p$, $c$) acts as a dual query.
 - Each memory entry in the PCMB is associated with a sinusoidal episode timestep encoding TE($\cdot$).
 - Stacked perceptual memories (with TE) and cognitive memories (with TE) form key-value pairs for cross-attention.
 - Scaled dot-product attention is applied between current tokens ($q_x \in \{p, c\}$) and memory tensors to retrieve raw historical outputs $\hat{H}_x$.
 - Two Transformer layers further refine these into final retrieved embeddings $H_p$ and $H_c$.
3. **Memory Gate Fusion (Figure 3b):**
 - This mechanism adaptively integrates retrieved historical embeddings with current working memory.
 - For each stream (perceptual $x=p$ and cognitive $x=c$), a gating vector $g_x = \sigma(\text{MLP}(\text{concat}[x, H_x]))$ is computed.
 - The memory-augmented representation $\tilde{x}$ is then obtained as $\tilde{x} = g_x \odot H_x + (1 - g_x) \odot x$. This allows the model to dynamically weigh the importance of current versus retrieved historical information.
4. **Memory Consolidation (Figure 3c):**
 - After gate fusion, the memory-augmented representations ($\tilde{p}$, $\tilde{c}$) are added to the PCMB.
 - To prevent memory bloat when the bank reaches its capacity $L$, a consolidation strategy is employed. Cosine similarities are computed between adjacent entries within each stream. The pair with the highest similarity is merged by averaging their vectors: $m_{x_{i^*_x}} \leftarrow \frac{1}{2}(\tilde{x}_{i^*_x} + \tilde{x}_{i^*_x+1})$. This maintains a compact yet essential representation of long-term history.

#### 3.5. Memory-Conditioned Action Expert

This module generates continuous robotic actions based on the memory-augmented context.
1. **Diffusion Policy:** A Diffusion-based Transformer (DiT) implemented with Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) is used to predict a sequence of $T=16$ future 7-DoF actions. This choice addresses the continuous and multimodal nature of robotic control.
2. **Conditional Generation:** At each denoising step, noisy action tokens are conditioned on the sinusoidal encoding of the denoising timestep and the high-level cognitive representation $\tilde{c}$ via a cognition-attention layer. Fine-grained visual details from the perceptual features $\tilde{p}$ are then integrated through a perception-attention layer.
3. **Action Prediction:** The combined and refined representation is processed through a feed-forward network to yield the denoised action for that step. The final denoised vectors are passed through an MLP to output continuous 7-DoF actions.
4. **Training:** The model is trained using a mean squared error (MSE) loss between predicted and target actions.

This comprehensive methodology allows MemoryVLA to capture and leverage temporal context at both abstract and detailed levels, translating it into robust, temporally-aware action sequences for complex robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MemoryVLA underwent extensive evaluation across a diverse range of simulation and real-world tasks, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance, particularly on long-horizon and temporally-dependent challenges. The experiments involved 3 robots (WidowX, Google robot, Franka), 6 benchmarks, 150+ tasks, and 500+ variations.

#### 4.1. Simulated Evaluation

1. **SimplerEnv-Bridge (WidowX robot):** MemoryVLA achieved an average success rate of **71.9%**, significantly outperforming the CogACT-Large baseline by **+14.6 percentage points** and surpassing other state-of-the-art VLAs like $\pi^0$.
2. **SimplerEnv-Fractal (Google robot):** It recorded an overall success rate of **72.7%**, a **+4.6 point gain over CogACT**. This performance was strong across both Visual Matching (VM, 77.7%) and Visual Aggregation (VA, 67.7%) settings, demonstrating robustness to visual variations. Notable gains were seen in tasks like Open/Close Drawer (VM: +12.9; VA: +24.9 over CogACT).
3. **LIBERO (Franka robot):** MemoryVLA achieved an impressive overall success rate of **96.5%** across five suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long-10, Long-90), exceeding CogACT by **+3.3 percentage points**. This was accomplished using only third-person RGB, without additional wrist views or proprioceptive states, which some baselines utilize.
4. **Mikasa-Robo (Franka robot):** On this memory-dependent benchmark, MemoryVLA attained **41.2% success**, surpassing $\pi^0$ by **+11.8 percentage points**. A remarkable **+41.0% improvement** was observed on the ShellGameTouch task, highlighting its ability to leverage past information effectively.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

MemoryVLA was evaluated on 12 real-world tasks using Franka and WidowX robots, divided into General and Long-horizon Temporal suites.
1. **General Tasks:** Achieved an **85% success score**, outperforming CogACT by **+9 percentage points**. It surpassed the strongest baseline on every general task, with notable gains on "Egg in Pan" (+13) and "Egg in Oven" (+20).
2. **Long-horizon Temporal Tasks:** Achieved an **83% success score**, a substantial **+26 percentage point gain over CogACT**, and significantly outperformed $\pi^0$. Improvements were particularly pronounced on tasks requiring strong temporal understanding, such as "Seq. Push Buttons" (+43), "Change Food" (+38), "Guess Where" (+32), and "Clean Table & Count" (+17).

#### 4.3. Robustness and Generalization

MemoryVLA demonstrated strong robustness and generalization under various out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions in both real-world and simulation, including unseen backgrounds, distractors, objects, containers, lighting, and occlusion. It generally maintained high success rates, although performance experienced larger degradation under severe changes like unseen camera views in simulation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Detailed ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of MemoryVLA's proposed components:
1. **Memory Type:** Combining both perceptual and cognitive memory (71.9%) significantly outperformed using cognitive-only (63.5%) or perceptual-only (64.6%) memory on SimplerEnv-Bridge.
2. **Memory Length:** An optimal memory length (e.g., 16 for SimplerEnv-Bridge, 256 for real-world temporal tasks) was crucial for performance.
3. **Memory Retrieval:** Incorporating timestep positional encoding improved retrieval performance (from 69.8% to 71.9%).
4. **Memory Gate Fusion:** The adaptive gate fusion mechanism (71.9%) outperformed simple additive fusion (67.7%).
5. **Memory Consolidation:** The token-merge consolidation strategy (71.9%) was superior to a basic FIFO (First-In-First-Out) strategy (66.7%).
6. **Number of Cognitive Tokens:** Increasing cognitive tokens from 1 to 4 showed no gain, suggesting the single 4096-dim EOS token was sufficient.

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency and Zero-Shot Generalization

The memory module was found to be lightweight, introducing only a **minor overhead** in inference latency (+3.6%) and GPU memory (+0.8 GB) compared to a baseline, ensuring practical deployability. Furthermore, MemoryVLA demonstrated **good zero-shot task generalization** to unseen task categories.

#### 4.6. Comparison with Temporal Context VLA Baselines

MemoryVLA consistently outperformed other VLA models that explicitly incorporate temporal context, such as TraceVLA, RoboVLMs, CronusVLA, 4D-VLA, MAP-VLA, and TTF-VLA across different benchmarks, further cementing its state-of-the-art status in temporal manipulation.

In summary, MemoryVLA achieves remarkable performance gains across a wide spectrum of robotic manipulation tasks, particularly excelling where temporal understanding is paramount. Its biologically inspired memory architecture is key to these advancements, providing robust, generalizable, and efficient decision-making capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MemoryVLA marks a significant step forward in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, addressing a critical limitation that has long plagued these systems: the effective handling of temporal dependencies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Novelty and Conceptual Advancement

- **Bio-Inspired Architecture:** MemoryVLA introduces a novel framework inspired by the human dual-memory system (working memory and hippocampal-episodic memory). This allows for a principled approach to integrating short-term, immediate context with long-term, detailed historical information, a more sophisticated mechanism than prior attempts at temporal modeling in robotics.
- **Perceptual–Cognitive Memory Bank (PCMB):** The core innovation lies in the PCMB, which explicitly distinguishes and manages both low-level perceptual details and high-level cognitive semantics. The mechanisms for **retrieval, adaptive gate fusion, and redundancy-aware consolidation** are crucial for maintaining a compact yet highly effective memory. This structured approach contrasts with simpler concatenation or coarse latent propagation methods.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Robotic Capabilities

- **Solving Non-Markovian Tasks:** By explicitly modeling temporal context, MemoryVLA empowers robots to tackle inherently non-Markovian, long-horizon tasks that are difficult or impossible for current VLA models that rely solely on instantaneous observations. This is evident in its substantial performance gains on tasks requiring tracking past actions, recognizing progress, and making context-dependent decisions (e.g., "Seq. Push Buttons," "Change Food," "Guess Where").
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The ability to leverage historical context makes the robot more robust to momentary visual ambiguities and changes in the environment. Its demonstrated strong performance under various out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions in both simulation and real-world settings highlights its potential for real-world deployment in dynamic and unpredictable environments.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Consistently outperforming leading VLA baselines (CogACT, $\pi^0$) and other temporal-context-aware VLA models across a broad range of challenging benchmarks (SimplerEnv, LIBERO, Mikasa-Robo, and custom real-world tasks) underscores its efficacy and establishes a new benchmark for temporal robotic manipulation.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications

- **Real-World Applicability:** The impressive gains observed in real-world experiments, particularly on long-horizon temporal tasks, indicate MemoryVLA's readiness for practical applications. This could enable robots to perform more complex assembly tasks, multi-step service operations, or intricate logistics in various industries.
- **Efficiency:** Despite its sophisticated memory mechanisms, MemoryVLA is shown to be computationally lightweight, incurring only minor inference overhead. This makes it suitable for deployment on hardware with limited computational resources, further enhancing its practical utility.
- **Data Efficiency:** While not explicitly highlighted as a contribution, a more intelligent use of temporal context might implicitly lead to better learning from existing datasets, as the model can make better sense of sequential demonstrations.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The authors outline exciting future work that builds directly on MemoryVLA's foundation:
- **Memory Reflection:** Aligning long-term memory to the LLM input space to enable "embedding-space chain-of-thought reasoning." This could allow robots to perform higher-level cognitive planning by explicitly reasoning over their past experiences.
- **Lifelong Memory:** Developing biologically inspired consolidation mechanisms to distill frequently reused experiences into permanent representations. This could pave the way for true lifelong learning in robotics, where robots accumulate knowledge over extended periods and generalize it across new scenes, tasks, and embodiments.

In conclusion, MemoryVLA represents a critical advancement towards more intelligent and autonomous robotic agents. By successfully integrating bio-inspired memory systems into VLA models, it not only pushes the boundaries of current robotic capabilities but also lays robust groundwork for future research into human-like reasoning, lifelong learning, and versatile real-world robotic applications.
