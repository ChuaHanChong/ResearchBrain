# KerJEPA: Kernel Discrepancies for Euclidean Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> KERJEPA introduces a generalized framework for Euclidean self-supervised learning, building on LeJEPA by employing various kernel discrepancies for regularization. It reveals that LeJEPA's slicing implicitly yields heavy-tailed, dimension-dependent kernels and empirically demonstrates that analytically derived discrepancies improve training stability and convergence, with KSD leveraging non-Gaussian priors effectively and an IMQ kernel achieving 91.90% accuracy on ImageNette.

## Problem

- Existing Euclidean self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, like LeJEPA, suffer from high estimation variance and computational costs due to stochastic projections in their sliced Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) regularization.
- These methods offer limited flexibility, being constrained to specific Gaussian kernels and priors, which restricts the exploration of diverse feature space geometries.
- Approximations involved in integral calculations for regularization introduce additional hyperparameters and potential biases.

## Method

- The KERJEPA framework generalizes regularization in ==Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)== by employing a flexible family of ==kernel discrepancies==, including MMD and Kernel Stein Discrepancy (KSD).
- Closed-form analytical expressions are derived for 'infinitely' sliced MMD and KSD, eliminating the ==Monte Carlo variance== associated with stochastic projections.
- ==KSD== is introduced as a novel regularizer that utilizes only the score function of the prior distribution, enabling the use of a wider range of non-Gaussian priors (e.g., Laplace) and alternative kernels like Inverse Multiquadric (IMQ).

## Results

- Various KERJEPA variants achieved performance comparable to or superior to LeJEPA on ImageNette, with an unsliced KSD using an IMQ kernel and Gaussian prior reaching the highest top-1 accuracy of **91.90%** (compared to LeJEPA's **91.13%**).
- Analytically sliced MMD and KSD variants consistently exhibited improved training stability and faster convergence rates across different projector dimensions due to the elimination of Monte Carlo variance.
- Unsliced KSD demonstrated its effectiveness with non-Gaussian priors (e.g., Laplace), outperforming finite-sliced MMD in these scenarios by directly leveraging the prior's score function.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LeJEPA's SIGReg regularizer is theoretically equivalent to an MMD using a specific heavy-tailed, dimension-dependent kernel, demonstrating that finite slicing implicitly induces a unique feature space geometry.
> - The variance from finite Monte Carlo slicing significantly impacts training stability and convergence, especially in higher embedding dimensions, which is consistently alleviated by using analytically derived discrepancies.
> - KSD offers a powerful and flexible approach for enforcing diverse target prior distributions on embeddings, particularly for non-Gaussian priors, without requiring approximations from integral estimation or target sampling.
