# GAN-based Reactive Motion Synthesis with Class-aware Discriminators for Human-human Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> A semi-supervised GAN synthesizes one character's reactive motion from another's observed action. The generator pairs a part-based hierarchical LSTM encoder with a seq2seq temporal-attention decoder, while a dual discriminator judges both realism and interaction class. Trained with bone, continuity and contractive losses, it aligns the two characters in time and cuts frame error on SBU, HHOI and 2C interactions.

## Problem

- Kinematic and Markov-based reaction methods sample or blend stored clips, generalizing poorly to large-scale motion where diverse user-driven variation is needed.
- Prior GAN motion work models single characters only, and recurrent interaction models accumulate sampling error that drifts the generated pair off course.
- Existing synthesis lacks interactive feature modelling, so the two characters become temporally misaligned and reactions fire at the wrong moment.

## Method

- ==Part-based hierarchical LSTM== encoder splits the skeleton into four limbs plus trunk, encoding each separately before concatenating into one spatial state per frame.
- ==Seq2seq global-attention decoder== builds a per-step context vector over all encoder states, feeding LSTM gates so the reaction aligns temporally with the input action.
- ==Dual discriminator== shares bidirectional LSTM layers between a binary real/fake head and an N+1-way class head; bone, continuity and L1 contractive losses regularize output.

## Results

- Adding the multi-class discriminator lowered Average Frame Distance in most SBU classes, hug **0.81** to **0.72**, shake hands **0.50** to **0.44**, kick **0.58** to **0.53**.
- Against NN, HMM, DMDP, kernel RL and max-entropy inverse optimal control, the method won half the SBU classes, kick **0.53** and punch **0.45** being lowest.
- Loss ablation raised HHOI recognition of synthesized reactions from **0.7352** adversarial-only to **0.9573** with all three losses, near the **0.9782** real-data prototype.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A class-aware discriminator can inject label supervision without conditioning the generator, so the system infers interaction type at inference instead of requiring annotation.
> - Temporal attention over the partner's frames is what fixes action-reaction timing; spatial part decomposition alone reduces artifacts but still fires reactions early.
> - Data-driven reaction synthesis inherits capture noise: occlusion-heavy close-contact classes like hugging degrade most, and unobserved locomotion appears as foot floating.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion synthesis and generation is an area of study in computer graphics and animation, with applications in virtual reality, games, and human-robot interaction. A specific challenge within this field is the synthesis of reactive motions, where a character's movement is generated in response to the actions of another character or user. This becomes particularly complex in human-human interactions due to the diverse and nuanced ways individuals can interact.

Existing approaches to reactive motion synthesis include physical simulation, which is often suitable for motions involving direct body contact, and kinematic-based methods, which aim to preserve context in close interactions. Non-parametric algorithms such as Markov Decision Processes (MDP) and motion blending have also been employed, often relying on interaction history. However, these methods can face challenges in capturing the intricate spatial and temporal synchronizations required for realistic and diverse human-human interactions, particularly those without direct contact.

Deep learning-based models, especially Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), have demonstrated success in synthesizing various single-human actions by learning from large datasets and generating vivid, diverse samples. Conditional GANs further enable the generation of specific types of activities through supervised training. Despite these advancements in single-character motion synthesis, the application of adversarial training to model human-human interactions has been less extensively explored. This is attributed to the inherent difficulties in effectively encoding complex spatial-temporal information and maintaining synchronization between two interacting entities, as well as managing the wide variety of reactive patterns.

This research addresses this gap by proposing a semi-supervised GAN system specifically designed for reactive motion synthesis in human-human interactions. It aims to develop a deep learning framework that can generate a character's reactive motion in response to an active character's movement, while accounting for the spatial and temporal complexities and the diverse nature of different interaction types. The work positions itself as an advancement in applying deep learning, particularly GANs, to the challenging domain of multi-character reactive motion generation, moving beyond single-character synthesis and traditional kinematic or statistical approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a system capable of synthesizing realistic reactive motion for a character (referred to as character B) given the active motion of another character (character A) within human-human interaction scenarios. This capability is intended to enhance the realism of virtual characters in applications such as computer graphics, games, and virtual reality.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and observations:

- **Complexity of Reactive Motion:** Synthesizing reactive motions in human-human interactions is inherently difficult due to the multitude of ways two humans can interact, encompassing both physical contact and subtle non-contact cues.
- **Limitations of Existing Methods:** While physical simulation is effective for contact-based reactions, it is less suitable for the broader range of human-human interactions. Kinematic-based and non-parametric approaches, while capable of preserving context, often struggle with the precise spatial and temporal synchronization required for natural-looking reactive movements.
- **Underutilization of Deep Learning for Interaction:** Deep learning models, particularly GANs, have demonstrated considerable success in synthesizing single-human actions. However, their application to modeling the complexities of human-human interaction, which necessitates understanding dynamic relationships between two subjects, has been less explored.
- **Encoding Spatial-Temporal Information:** Human motion involves intricate spatial relationships between body parts and complex temporal dynamics. Effectively encoding this multi-faceted information for reactive synthesis is a significant challenge. Misalignment of temporal movements between interacting characters is a common issue in existing models.
- **Diversity of Reactive Motions:** Different types of interactions (e.g., kicking, hugging, shaking hands) elicit significantly different reactive motions. A robust synthesis system should be able to generate reactions that are consistent with the interaction type, rather than generic or monotonous responses.
- **Semi-Supervised Learning Opportunity:** The researchers observe that leveraging interaction class labels can enhance the quality and diversity of generated reactive motions. A semi-supervised GAN framework can integrate this information into the discriminator to guide the generator, even if the generator itself does not explicitly receive the class label as input. This approach aims to create a more versatile animation system where the interaction nature might not be pre-defined.

To address these motivations and objectives, the research proposes two key insights:

1. **Generator Design for Spatial-Temporal Encoding:** To model the complicated spatial-temporal information, the generator incorporates a part-based Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) module. This allows for separate modeling of the temporal movement of different body limbs, capturing spatial detail. Additionally, an attention module is included to learn the temporal significance of the interaction, aiming to enhance the temporal alignment between the active and reactive motion pair.
2. **Class-Aware Discriminator:** To account for the diverse nature of reactive motions across different interaction types, a discriminator is introduced that performs two functions: determining if a generated movement is realistic, and classifying the interaction's class label. This class-specific feedback from the discriminator supervises the generator's training, encouraging it to produce high-quality reactive motions with diverse, type-specific characteristics.

These insights form the core motivation for the proposed semi-supervised GAN system, aiming to generate realistic, synchronized, and contextually appropriate reactive motions for virtual characters.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system is a semi-supervised Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) designed for reactive motion synthesis in human-human interactions. It consists of a specifically designed generator and a dual discriminator.

#### 3.1. Data Pre-processing

Interactions involving two characters, A (active) and B (reactive), are normalized. This involves rotating the entire interaction according to character A's facing direction and translating the origin of the coordinate system to character A's pelvis joint. Character B's joint locations are then represented relative to this transformed coordinate system. Motion sequences are represented as 3D joint positions in a Cartesian coordinate system.

#### 3.2. The Part-based Attentive Recurrent Generator (G)

The generator is designed to synthesize the reactive motion of character B without requiring explicit input of the interaction's class label. It is composed of a part-aware encoder and an attentive decoder.

- **Part-aware Encoder:**
 - The human skeleton is segmented into five main body parts: four limbs and the trunk.
 - For each body part `p`, an individual LSTM layer processes the 3D joint positions `x_A_p_s` of character A at each frame `s`. This hierarchical approach allows for modeling of local, part-level dynamics.
 - The LSTM cell updates input, forget, output, interim, and cell gates using character A's part-specific pose and the previous hidden state.
 - The five local hidden states (`h_p_s`) from each body part are then concatenated to form a single integrated spatial state `h_s` for the entire body at frame `s`.

- **Attentive Decoder:**
 - The decoder incorporates a seq2seq attention mechanism with an LSTM layer to facilitate temporal alignment.
 - At each decoding step `t`, a context vector `r_t` is computed. This vector is a weighted sum of all encoded states `{h_s}` from the encoder.
 - The attention weights `α(s,t)` are calculated based on the general score between each encoder state `h_s` and the previous decoder state `h_t-1`, using a content-based addressing function (softmax of a tanh-activated linear transformation). This mechanism allows the decoder to focus on crucial information across the temporal input sequence.
 - The context vector `r_t`, along with the previous generated pose `x_B_t-1` and the previous decoder hidden state `h_t-1`, updates the LSTM gates and cell state of the decoder.
 - A linear layer follows the attentive LSTM to restore the 3D joint positions for character B at timestep `t`. The decoder is configured to output the same number of frames as the input motion from character A, ensuring complete interaction sequences.

#### 3.3. The Class-aware Discriminator (D)

A dual discriminator is proposed to evaluate the generated reactive motion: it judges realism and classifies the interaction type. This is motivated by the observation that reactive motions vary significantly between interaction types.

- **Architecture:** The discriminator receives only the synthesized reactive motion `x_B` as input. This design choice aims to force the discriminator to focus on the reactive patterns rather than relying on features from character A's motion.
- **Shared Layers:** Bidirectional LSTM layers are employed as shared backbone layers between the two classification heads. These layers process the reactive motion sequence to extract high-level contextual features from both forward and backward temporal directions.
- **Binary Classifier (D_b):** One head acts as a standard binary classifier, `D_b`, which is responsible for distinguishing between real reactive motions and fake (generated) ones. This component contributes to maintaining the authenticity of the synthesized motions.
- **Multi-class Classifier (D_m):** The second head is a multi-class classifier, `D_m`. It classifies the input reactive motion into one of `N` real interaction classes or an additional `N+1` class representing fake samples. This component enhances the generator's capacity by promoting the synthesis of diverse, type-specific reactive patterns, preventing monotonous generations.

#### 3.4. Loss Functions

The system is trained using a semi-supervised adversarial loss, augmented with three auxiliary losses.

- **Adversarial Losses:**
 - `L_sup`: Supervised loss for `D_m`. It aims to correctly classify real reactive motions into their respective `N` classes and distinguish generated motions from these real classes (assigning them to the `N+1` fake class). This loss also encourages `D_m` to classify synthesized reactions into their intended real class (if appropriate), guiding the generator.
 - `L_unsup`: Unsupervised loss for `D_b`. This is the standard GAN objective, where `D_b` tries to maximize the log-likelihood of correctly classifying real data as real and generated data as fake, while `G` tries to minimize `D_b`'s ability to distinguish generated data from real data.

- **Auxiliary Constraints for the Generator:** These losses reinforce physical plausibility, smoothness, and adherence to ground truth.
 - `L_skl` (Bone loss): An L1 norm-based loss that penalizes deviations of synthesized bone lengths from a predefined reference skeleton length. This ensures the physical validity of the generated character's structure.
 - `L_con` (Continuity loss): Designed to encourage smooth and stable motion. It penalizes large differences in joint locations between close frames compared to more distant frames, using a temporal gradient.
 - `L_1` (Contractive loss): An L1 norm between the generated reactive motion `x_B` and the ground truth `x_B`. This strongly guides the generator to mimic specific reactive patterns and reduces ambiguity in predictions.

- **Overall Objective Function:** The final training objective is a min-max optimization problem that combines all these losses with weighting coefficients (`α`, `β`, `γ`):
 `min_G max_D (L_sup + L_unsup + αL_skl + βL_con + γL_1)`

#### 3.5. Datasets

The method is evaluated on three 3D joint-based datasets:
- **SBU Kinect Interaction dataset (SBU):** Contains 8 interaction categories, used for 6 relevant types (excluding 'approach' and 'depart'). Provides active/inactive agent annotations.
- **Human-Human-Object Interaction dataset (HHOI):** Used for 'shake hands' and 'high-five' interactions. Data is expanded via a sliding window.
- **Character-Character dataset (2C):** A high-quality Motion Capture-based dataset with 'kicking' and 'punching' interactions, converted from joint angles to positions.

Leave-one-subject-out cross-validation is used for evaluation.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The system is implemented using Keras with a TensorFlow backend. RMSprop is the optimizer. Specific LSTM neuron counts are configured for encoder spatial slices and the attentive layer, varying by dataset. Parameters for `L_con` (`k`, `∆t`, `λ`) are set. Training typically converges around 1000 epochs, with a specified training time per epoch and inference time per interaction. Loss weights are set as `α = β = 0.01` and `γ = 1`, with lower weights for `L_skl` and `L_con` to prevent over-constraining physical properties. One-side label smoothing is used for adversarial training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented both qualitative and quantitative evaluations to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed GAN-based reactive motion synthesis system.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Evaluations

The system demonstrated the ability to generate realistic reactive motions that are spatially and temporally consistent with the observed active motion.
- **Diverse Reactions:** Examples showed synthesized characters performing appropriate defensive actions like dodging in response to punching, pushing, and kicking. This involved specific body part movements such as leaning back the upper body or pulling back the lower body. For neutral interactions (hugging, shaking hands, exchanging objects), the synthesized motions exhibited consistent relative distance changes between characters.
- **Discriminative Details:** The inclusion of the multi-class discriminator (`D_m`) was observed to promote the generation of more discriminative details within each interaction class, preventing generic responses.
- **Generalization and Noise Handling:** The generator exhibited generalization capabilities by producing natural reactive motions even when the ground truth data contained noise or unnatural configurations (e.g., flickering joints in the SBU dataset). This suggested the model learned intrinsic human movement patterns rather than simply replicating noisy input.
- **High-Quality Motion Data:** On the high-quality 2C dataset, the synthesized reactions were highly consistent with the ground truth, preserving intricate motion details such as squat-to-stand transitions and initial body contact during a punch.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluations

- **Effectiveness of `D_m` (Class-aware Discriminator):** Measured using Average Frame Distance (AFD), which quantifies geometric similarity between synthesized and ground truth skeletons. Table 1 shows that including `D_m` generally resulted in lower AFD values across most interaction categories of the SBU dataset, indicating improved quality of synthesized reactive motions through class discrimination.
- **Comparison with Prior Work:** The proposed method was compared against Nearest Neighbour (NN), Hidden Markov Model (HMM), Discrete Markov Decision Process (DMDP), Kernel-based Reinforcement Learning (KRL), and Maximum-Entropy Inverse Optimal Control (ME-IOC) on the SBU dataset. As shown in Table 2, the proposed method achieved comparable performance, demonstrating the lowest prediction errors in half of the interaction categories. For 'push' and 'shake hands', the AFD differences with the best competing models (DMDP and ME-IOC, respectively) were minimal. The 'hug' interaction showed a relatively higher AFD, which the authors attributed to high diversity and frequent self-occlusions in the training data, making feature learning challenging.
- **Ablation Study on Loss Functions (Recognition Accuracy):** The contribution of the auxiliary loss functions was evaluated by measuring the recognition accuracy of a baseline classifier on synthesized motions under different loss combinations. Tables 3 and 4 (for SBU and HHOI, respectively) indicated that discriminability generally increased with the inclusion of `L_skl` (bone loss), `L_con` (continuity loss), and `L_1` (contractive loss). The model trained with all three constraints yielded the highest recognition accuracy, approaching the prototype (real data) baseline more closely. Specifically, `L_1` was noted as crucial for differentiating visually similar actions like pushing and punching. Challenges in neutral interactions like hugging due to dataset noise and ambiguity between 'shake hands' and 'exchange objects' were also observed, though deemed less impactful on visual quality.

#### 4.3. Interaction Alignment Evaluations

An ablation study on generator components investigated their roles in motion alignment:
- **Seq2seq Generator (Baseline):** A basic LSTM encoder-decoder.
- **Seq2seq Part-based Generator:** Incorporating hierarchical part-based LSTMs reduced spatial artifacts (e.g., floating limbs) but still exhibited temporal misalignment, where the reactive character might dodge prematurely.
- **Seq2seq Part-based Attentive Generator (Proposed G):** The addition of the attention mechanism significantly improved temporal alignment. The decoder learned to focus on critical interaction stages, enabling the reactive character to respond at appropriate times (e.g., dodging synchronously with a push).
- **High-five Sequence Analysis:** The system demonstrated synchronized arm movements throughout different phases of a high-five interaction (raise arm, high-five, put down arm), indicating its ability to generate real-time and context-aware feedback based on observed spatial patterns.
- **Attention Map Visualization:** Visualizations of attention weights confirmed that the attention module identifies active periods of interaction and contributes to temporal alignment. For instance, shaking interactions showed a wide range of non-zero weights along the diagonal, while exchanging objects showed concentrated weights over a shorter period.

#### 4.4. Generalization Tests

The system's generalization was tested by feeding reactive motions as input (e.g., a dodging action). The model successfully generated appropriate active actions (e.g., kicking or punching) and could also generate a high-five in response to a high-five reaction. This indicated that the discriminator helps prevent the generation of averaged or ambiguous actions, promoting specific responses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer animation and virtual character interaction by proposing a deep learning framework for synthesizing reactive human-human motions. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Research Gap:** The work provides a robust deep learning solution using Generative Adversarial Networks for the complex task of human-human reactive motion synthesis, an area where GANs have been less explored compared to single-character motion generation. It addresses the challenges of encoding intricate spatial-temporal information and ensuring synchronization between interacting characters.
- **Enhanced Realism in Virtual Environments:** The ability to generate realistic, synchronized, and contextually appropriate reactive motions has direct implications for improving the authenticity of virtual characters in various applications. This includes:
 - **Computer Graphics and Animation:** Animators can more efficiently create believable interactions without manually keyframing complex reactive movements.
 - **Games:** Non-player characters (NPCs) can exhibit more dynamic and natural responses to player actions, enhancing immersion and gameplay.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR):** Users can experience more convincing interactions with virtual agents, leading to more engaging and believable VR experiences.
- **Novel Generator Architecture:** The proposed part-based attentive recurrent generator contributes a novel approach to modeling human motion. By decomposing the skeleton into parts for separate LSTM processing and integrating an attention mechanism, it effectively captures both spatial detail and crucial temporal alignments, which are critical for natural interactions.
- **Class-Aware Discriminative Learning:** The introduction of a dual discriminator with both binary and multi-class classification capabilities is a key innovation. This allows the system to not only produce realistic motions but also to generate diverse reactions that are specific to different interaction types. The semi-supervised nature of this approach means the generator can learn these type-specific characteristics without requiring explicit labels as input during inference, enhancing its practical usability.
- **Improved Motion Quality:** The integration of auxiliary losses (bone, continuity, and contractive) ensures that the synthesized motions adhere to physical properties (e.g., consistent bone lengths), exhibit smoothness, and closely follow real reactive patterns. This multi-faceted loss structure contributes to the high quality and naturalness of the generated movements.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** This research lays a foundation for further exploration in multi-character motion synthesis. The authors identify future directions such as increasing the diversity of generated reactions by disentangling basic patterns from styles, accommodating random noise, exploring online reactive motion synthesis with local temporal attention, and leveraging larger datasets for evaluation with distribution-based metrics like FID. These suggest avenues for more sophisticated and versatile interaction systems.
- **Potential for Human-Robot Interaction:** The capability to synthesize natural human reactions could inform the development of robots that can perceive and respond to human actions more intuitively and contextually, fostering more natural human-robot interactions.

In summary, this work represents a technical advancement in creating dynamic and realistic human-human interactions in virtual settings, providing a robust deep learning framework with implications across multiple computational graphics and interaction domains.
