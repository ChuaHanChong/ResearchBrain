# GentleHumanoid: Learning Upper-body Compliance for Contact-rich Human and Object Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed GentleHumanoid, a framework that integrates impedance control into a reinforcement learning-based whole-body motion tracking policy, enabling humanoid robots to achieve tunable upper-body compliance. The system demonstrated significantly reduced and stable contact forces across diverse physical interaction tasks, leading to safer and more natural human-robot interactions on a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Current humanoid control policies, particularly RL-based ones, often prioritize rigid motion tracking, treating external forces as disturbances, which results in unsafe and unnatural physical interactions.
- Many physical interactions involve coordinated contact across multiple body parts, requiring distributed compliance beyond single end-effector or base control methods.
- There is a need for a general method to learn tunable upper-body compliance for whole-body control to safely and naturally interact with humans and objects across various scenarios.

## Method

- A novel framework integrates impedance control into a whole-body motion tracking policy, modeling upper-body link motion with both driving and interaction forces.
- A unified simulated interaction force model, distinguishing between resistive and guiding contact, is used during RL training, where guiding forces are sampled from kinematically consistent human motion data.
- An adaptive, safety-aware force thresholding mechanism is incorporated during training, alongside a teacher-student reinforcement learning architecture, to achieve tunable compliance and facilitate sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- GentleHumanoid consistently reduced peak contact forces (e.g., hand forces stabilized around 10 N compared to over 20 N for rigid baselines) and maintained specified safety thresholds in both simulation and real-world experiments.
- On a Unitree G1 humanoid, the policy exhibited smooth, posture-invariant, and tunable compliance during wrist interactions, requiring lower forces for movement (5-15 N range) and maintaining moderate, bounded pressures during mannequin hugs.
- The robot successfully performed delicate tasks like holding a balloon without damage and adapted to human body shapes for autonomous, personalized hugging, demonstrating superior adaptability compared to rigid policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating impedance control with whole-body reinforcement learning effectively enables tunable compliance across multiple robotic body links, rather than just end-effectors.
> - Modeling interaction forces by sampling from human motion datasets ensures kinematically consistent and natural compliant responses throughout the robot's upper body.
> - Explicitly training with adaptive safety force thresholds allows the robot to learn to modulate its gentleness, providing predictable and user-controllable safety in physical interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in human-centered environments necessitates safe, natural, and compliant physical interaction. This paper addresses a critical gap in the current robotics landscape, which broadly encompasses three main areas:

**a. Humanoid Whole-Body Control:** Traditional model-based approaches, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), have long tackled the high-dimensional dynamics of humanoids. While capable of stable behaviors, they demand extensive expert design and meticulous tuning. More recently, learning-based methods, particularly Reinforcement Learning (RL) trained on human motion data, have achieved impressive results in generating dynamic whole-body locomotion and manipulation. However, as the authors note, most of these RL policies prioritize rigid position or velocity tracking and tend to suppress external forces as disturbances. This rigidity limits their applicability in scenarios requiring adaptive compliance, such as physical human-robot interaction (HRI) or handling deformable objects. GentleHumanoid aims to overcome this by introducing compliance directly into the learning framework, moving beyond purely tracking predefined motions to *adapting* those motions based on physical contact.

**b. Force-Adaptive Control:** To address the robustness and safety concerns of contact, classical force-adaptive methods like impedance and admittance control have been developed and extended to whole-body frameworks. More recent RL-based approaches have incorporated these methods or implicitly learned robustness to external forces. However, these prior works typically focus on end-effector interactions (e.g., a robot arm pushing a button or manipulating a tool). They often emphasize resisting extreme forces rather than enabling *gentle, distributed compliance* across the entire kinematic chain. GentleHumanoid fills this gap by explicitly modeling compliance across multiple upper-body links (shoulders, elbows, hands), acknowledging that human-robot and robot-object interactions often involve broader contact surfaces and coordinated force distribution beyond just the robot's "hand."

**c. Human-Humanoid Interaction:** As humanoids move towards deployment, their ability to physically interact with people safely and comfortably is paramount. Previous work has explored human-in-the-loop strategies, haptic feedback, and task-specific control methods for assistance tasks like sit-to-stand. However, these approaches are often tailored to single scenarios and lack generalization. Some recent works focus on collision avoidance using vision-based criteria. GentleHumanoid distinguishes itself by proposing a *general motion-tracking policy* that inherently integrates compliance, making it adaptable to diverse interaction contexts (hugging, support, manipulation). Furthermore, it combines this compliant control with vision-based human shape estimation for personalized interactions, such as autonomous, shape-aware hugging, demonstrating a holistic approach to safe and natural HRI.

In essence, GentleHumanoid advances the field by demonstrating a robust method for learning *universal whole-body control with tunable upper-body compliance*, allowing humanoids to move beyond rigid imitation and safely engage in a wide array of physical interactions that are essential for their effective integration into human environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to perform **safe and natural physical interactions** in human-centered environments. The authors identify that current robotic control policies, especially those based on reinforcement learning, often prioritize rigid motion tracking, treating external forces as mere disturbances to be suppressed. This rigidity leads to unsafe and unnatural behaviors when robots inevitably encounter humans or objects in their workspace.

The core motivations driving GentleHumanoid are:

- **Necessity of Compliance:** For humanoids to operate effectively alongside humans, they must be able to adapt their movements in response to physical contact. This includes yielding gently to external forces (e.g., during a handshake or hug), providing firm but non-damaging support (e.g., for sit-to-stand assistance), and delicately handling fragile objects (e.g., a balloon). The lack of such compliance in existing systems is a major barrier to real-world deployment.
- **Whole-Body, Multi-Link Interaction:** Many physical interactions, particularly those with humans, are not confined to a single end-effector but involve coordinated contact across multiple body parts (e.g., shoulder, elbow, wrist). The paper aims to develop a framework that can manage and coordinate compliant force responses across the entire upper-body kinematic chain, unlike prior methods focused only on base or end-effector control.
- **Diverse Contact Scenarios:** The desired compliance must range from very gentle yielding to firm, supportive assistance, all while adhering to safety thresholds. The policy needs to be robust and adaptable to a wide spectrum of interaction forces and situations.
- **Safety Assurance:** A paramount concern in HRI is safety. The robot's interactions must remain within acceptable force limits to prevent injury or discomfort to humans and damage to objects. This necessitates a mechanism for regulating and bounding interaction forces.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The ultimate goal is to bridge the sim-to-real gap, enabling the learned compliant behaviors to transfer effectively from simulation to physical humanoid robots, demonstrating their practical utility in various real-world scenarios.

In summary, the motivation is to make humanoids "gentle" – capable of intuitive, safe, and effective physical collaboration and manipulation, thereby paving the way for their broader acceptance and utility in human society.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GentleHumanoid introduces a novel framework that integrates **impedance control into a whole-body motion tracking policy** to achieve tunable upper-body compliance. The approach can be broken down into several key components:

**a. Problem Formulation and Reference Dynamics:**
The core idea is to model the motion of each upper-body link (shoulder, elbow, hand) as being influenced by two types of forces:
1. **Driving Force ($\mathbf{f}_{\text{drive}}$):** Modeled as a virtual spring-damper system, this force pulls the link towards a target motion, akin to classical impedance control. It ensures the robot attempts to follow a desired trajectory.
2. **Interaction Force ($\mathbf{f}_{\text{interact}}$):** This force represents physical contact with humans or objects, introducing compliance.
These forces are combined to define a "reference dynamics" model, which the RL policy is trained to emulate.

**b. Unified Interaction Force Modeling:**
Since collecting diverse real-world interaction data is challenging, the authors propose a simulated interaction force model during RL training. This model uses a unified spring formulation but distinguishes between two critical cases:
- **Resistive Contact:** When the robot presses against a surface, the spring anchor is fixed at the initial contact point. This generates restoring forces that resist further deviation, mimicking pressing against a rigid object.
- **Guiding Contact:** When the robot is pushed or pulled by an external agent (e.g., a human), the spring anchor is sampled from *upper-body postures in human motion datasets*. This is a crucial innovation: sampling from complete postures ensures that forces are kinematically consistent across the entire kinematic chain (shoulder, elbow, wrist), preventing unrealistic, uncoordinated forces on individual links.
To enhance diversity, stiffness values for the interaction springs and the set of active contact links are randomized during training.

**c. Safety-Aware Force Thresholding:**
To ensure safe interactions, an adaptive force thresholding mechanism is introduced. A piecewise-constant safety limit ($\tau_{\text{safe}}$) is sampled during training, capping the maximum allowable driving force applied by the robot. This threshold is also provided to the RL policy as an observation, allowing it to learn to modulate its forces. The tunable nature of $\tau_{\text{safe}}$ (e.g., 5-15 N) allows the robot to adapt from gentle (hugging) to firm (sit-to-stand) interactions, benchmarking against ISO safety standards and comfort studies.

**d. RL-based Control Policy:**
The system uses a **teacher-student training framework** for sim-to-real transfer, typical in advanced RL for robotics:
- **Teacher Policy:** Trained in simulation, it receives comprehensive "privileged information" (e.g., reference dynamics states, actual and reference interaction forces, cumulative tracking error) in addition to standard observations. This allows it to learn optimal control.
- **Student Policy:** Designed for real-world deployment, it observes only information available on the physical robot (proprioception, target motion, current $\tau_{\text{safe}}$, etc.) and is trained to imitate the teacher's behavior.
Both policies output joint position targets for low-level PD controllers.

**e. Motion Datasets and Reward Design:**
- **Motion Data:** Diverse human motion datasets (AMASS, InterX, LAFAN) are retargeted to the humanoid model, providing rich target motions and realistic posture distributions for guiding contact forces.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function encourages accurate motion tracking and locomotion stability (standard for whole-body control). Crucially, GentleHumanoid introduces a "compliance reward" with three components:
 - **Reference Dynamics Tracking:** Minimizes the discrepancy between the robot's actual link states (position, velocity) in simulation and the compliant reference states derived from the impedance model.
 - **Reference Force Tracking:** Penalizes discrepancies between the interaction forces predicted by the reference dynamics and those measured in the simulator, explicitly regulating force magnitudes.
 - **Unsafe Force Penalty:** A hard penalty for interaction forces exceeding the safety margin ($\tau_{\text{safe}}$), further reinforcing safe behavior.

**f. Autonomous Hugging Pipeline:**
For a specific application, the policy is integrated into an autonomous hugging pipeline. This involves:
- **Human Shape Estimation:** Using an RGB camera on the robot's head, an existing human mesh estimation method (BEDLAM) reconstructs the human's body shape, scaled by height from a motion capture system.
- **Posture Optimization:** The humanoid's hugging posture is optimized to align its hands with the estimated waist points of the human, allowing for personalized, shape-aware hugs.
- **Locomotion & Interaction:** A separate locomotion policy guides the robot to a proper stance in front of the human, after which the GentleHumanoid policy executes the compliant hug.

This comprehensive methodology, spanning advanced force modeling, safety mechanisms, and a robust RL training pipeline, allows GentleHumanoid to learn nuanced, safe, and adaptable physical interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GentleHumanoid, conducted in both simulation and on the Unitree G1 humanoid, consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to baseline policies, particularly in reducing peak contact forces and enabling smoother, more natural interactions.

**a. Simulation Results (Hugging Motion with External Pull):**
- **Reduced and Stable Forces:** When an external pulling force attempted to move the robot away from a hugging posture, GentleHumanoid maintained significantly lower and more stable interaction forces across the hand, elbow, and shoulder links. For instance, hand forces stabilized around 10 N, whereas Vanilla-RL (rigid tracking) exceeded 20 N, and Extreme-RL (end-effector force-adaptive) settled above 13 N.
- **Smooth Adaptation:** GentleHumanoid adapted smoothly to external interactions, exhibiting compliant motions, while baselines responded stiffly with higher peak forces.

**b. Real-World Experiments (Unitree G1 Humanoid):**

- **Static Pose with External Force (Wrist):**
 - **Posture-Invariant Compliance:** GentleHumanoid allowed the arm to yield softly to external forces applied at the wrist, requiring significantly lower forces to reposition the arm (effective range 5-15 N) while maintaining balance. Crucially, it demonstrated consistent compliance across different arm configurations.
 - **Tunable Force Limits:** The robot's compliance level directly matched the user-specified force limit ($\tau_{\text{safe}}$), showcasing predictable and user-controllable gentleness.
 - **Baseline Rigidity:** Baselines (Vanilla-RL, Extreme-RL) resisted stiffly, often shifting the torso or losing balance, requiring much higher peak forces (e.g., Extreme-RL: 51.14 N, Vanilla-RL: 24.59 N) to move the arm.

- **Hugging a Mannequin (with Custom Pressure-Sensing Pads):**
 - **Moderate and Bounded Pressures:** Using customized pressure-sensing pads, GentleHumanoid maintained moderate and bounded contact pressures during hugging, even under deliberate mannequin misalignment.
 - **Safety under Misalignment:** Unlike baselines that generated higher, less predictable, and often highly localized force peaks (especially Vanilla-RL) or failed to sustain the motion under misalignment, GentleHumanoid consistently kept forces within safe and comfortable limits.

- **Handling Deformable Objects (Balloons):**
 - **Safe Object Manipulation:** With $\tau_{\text{safe}}$ set to 5 N, GentleHumanoid successfully held a balloon without damage, applying just enough force to stabilize it.
 - **Baseline Failure:** Both baselines applied excessive pressure, leading to the balloon being squeezed and eventually dropped due to the robot losing balance.

**c. Other Applications:**
- **Teleoperation Integration:** GentleHumanoid was integrated with a locomotion teleoperation framework for the Unitree G1, allowing users to trigger compliant reference motions (hugging, sit-to-stand, object handling) via a joystick. The inherent compliance promises safer interactions during teleoperation.
- **Autonomous Shape-Aware Hugging:** The paper demonstrates a complete pipeline where the robot uses vision-based human shape estimation to reconstruct a personalized body mesh, extracts waist points, and optimizes its hugging motion to adapt to individuals of different body shapes. This system generated stable and comfortable hugging motions for various participants.

In summary, the key findings confirm that GentleHumanoid successfully achieves its objectives by consistently reducing peak contact forces, maintaining safety thresholds, and exhibiting smoother, more adaptable, and natural physical interactions across a wide range of tasks and interaction partners compared to existing rigid and force-resistant policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GentleHumanoid framework represents a significant step forward in humanoid robotics, particularly in advancing their capability for safe, natural, and effective physical interaction with humans and the environment. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

**a. Enhanced Safety and Comfort in HRI:** The most direct and critical impact is the improvement of safety and comfort during human-robot interaction. By enabling tunable compliance and maintaining interaction forces within predefined safety thresholds, GentleHumanoid directly addresses a major barrier to the widespread deployment of humanoids in human-centric spaces. Robots controlled by this framework are less likely to cause injury or discomfort, fostering greater trust and acceptance among human users. This is particularly vital for assistive robotics in healthcare (e.g., sit-to-stand assistance) and elderly care.

**b. Broader Application Scope for Humanoids:** The ability to handle diverse contact scenarios, from gentle hugs to firm support and delicate object manipulation, significantly broadens the range of tasks humanoids can perform. Instead of being confined to structured industrial settings or purely observational roles, compliant humanoids can engage in collaborative tasks, provide physical assistance, and operate with fragile items, making them more versatile and useful in domestic, social, and service environments.

**c. More Natural and Intuitive Interactions:** The kinematic consistency of interaction forces, learned by sampling from human motion datasets, leads to interactions that feel more natural and intuitive to humans. This moves away from the "uncanny valley" of rigid, jerky robot movements towards behaviors that mimic human-human physical contact, enhancing the overall user experience.

**d. Advancements in Robot Learning and Control:** The methodological contributions—the unified spring-based interaction force modeling, the integration of impedance control with whole-body RL, and the safety-aware force thresholding—provide a robust template for future research in compliant robot control. The teacher-student architecture coupled with carefully designed compliance rewards offers a promising pathway for learning complex physical interaction skills that are difficult to program manually.

**e. Foundation for Autonomous Human-Aware Robots:** The demonstration of an autonomous, shape-aware hugging pipeline showcases the potential for humanoids to adapt their physical interactions to individual human characteristics. This capability, integrating perception (human shape estimation) with compliant control, lays foundational work for truly human-aware robots that can personalize their behavior for better social and physical engagement.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that highlight avenues for future research:
- **Dataset Constraints:** The reliance on human motion data, while ensuring kinematic consistency, might limit the diversity of force distributions, particularly for less common interactions (e.g., shoulder forces). Expanding datasets to include more varied motions (like dancing) could address this.
- **Simulated Interaction Complexity:** The spring-based force model, while effective, does not fully capture the intricacies of real human contact, such as friction or viscoelastic properties of tissues. Future work could incorporate more sophisticated models or real-world tactile sensing.
- **Sim-to-Real Discrepancies:** Occasional force overshoots in real-world experiments indicate remaining sim-to-real gaps, suggesting a need for more precise force regulation, potentially through additional tactile sensing.
- **Human Localization and Perception:** Current reliance on motion capture for human localization and height for the autonomous hugging pipeline limits autonomy. Integrating fully vision-based pipelines for these aspects would enhance practicality.
- **Long-Horizon Dynamic Interactions:** The current focus is on specific interaction scenarios. Future work will extend evaluations to long-horizon, dynamic interactions where the humanoid must continuously adapt to evolving human behaviors.

Despite these limitations, GentleHumanoid provides a compelling vision for the future of humanoid robotics—one where robots are not just capable but also gentle, making them valuable and trustworthy companions in human society.
