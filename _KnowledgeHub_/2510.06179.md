# Differentiable Model Predictive Control on the GPU

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Toyota Research Institute developed DiffMPC, a GPU-accelerated differentiable Model Predictive Control (DMPC) tool that achieves over 4x speedups compared to existing DMPC baselines by exploiting the time-induced sparsity of optimal control problems. This tool was successfully deployed on a physical Toyota Supra to learn robust drifting behaviors through water puddles, demonstrating practical utility in challenging real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- The practical adoption of differentiable Model Predictive Control (DMPC) was limited by severe computational inefficiency, especially when attempting to parallelize inherently sequential optimal control algorithms on GPUs.
- Existing differentiable solvers either lacked efficiency for Optimal Control Problems (OCPs) by not exploiting their specific structure or were primarily CPU-bound, hindering scalability for large-scale learning applications.
- The inability to efficiently compute gradients through MPC policies on GPUs created a computational bottleneck for integrating model-based control with modern machine learning techniques like reinforcement and imitation learning.

## Method

- Developed **DiffMPC**, a specialized GPU-accelerated ==differentiable optimization== tool for OCPs that explicitly leverages their time-induced sparsity to enable efficient parallel execution.
- Employed a custom Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) routine with a symmetric stair preconditioner to efficiently solve the large, sparse KKT linear systems arising from Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) on the GPU.
- Implemented the framework in JAX, allowing for seamless integration into machine learning workflows, automatic differentiation, and leveraging JIT compilation and GPU acceleration for both forward and backward passes.

## Results

- DiffMPC achieved over **4x** speedups compared to the fastest differentiable MPC baselines (mpc.pytorch, trajax) across various benchmarks, with up to **7.3x** speedup for the backward pass on a nonlinear attitude stabilization task.
- The framework accelerated imitation learning tasks by approximately 2 times and demonstrated benefits of warm-starting for both forward and backward passes, leading to further speedups.
- A learned MPC policy, trained with DiffMPC and domain randomization, successfully controlled a physical Toyota Supra to robustly drift through water puddles, achieving **100%** success in simulation versus **70%** for a manually tuned baseline controller.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient GPU parallelization of inherently sequential optimal control problems is possible by carefully exploiting their sparse-in-time structure and designing custom linear solvers, such as the symmetric stair preconditioned PCG.
> - Tailoring optimization solvers specifically for OCPs, rather than using general-purpose differentiable optimization frameworks, yields substantial performance gains, enabling DMPC for practical learning-based control.
> - Integrating efficient GPU-based DMPC allows for tighter coupling of model-based control with deep learning, leading to adaptive controllers that can learn optimal control parameters and physical model properties from data while maintaining robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of optimal control, machine learning, and high-performance computing, specifically addressing a critical bottleneck in the field of differentiable optimization.

**Differentiable Optimization (DO) and Differentiable Model Predictive Control (DMPC):** In recent years, differentiable optimization has emerged as a powerful paradigm, allowing the outputs of optimization algorithms to be treated as differentiable components within larger machine learning (ML) models. This enables end-to-end training, where optimization solvers can serve as inductive biases, reducing data requirements and enforcing complex constraints implicitly. Differentiable Model Predictive Control (DMPC) is a specialized instance of DO where the optimization problem is an optimal control problem (OCP). DMPC has found diverse applications across motion planning, parameter estimation, reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning (IL), and end-to-end planning and control for autonomous systems. Its promise lies in combining the structured, physics-informed nature of model-based control with the data-driven adaptability of machine learning.

**The Computational Bottleneck:** Despite its theoretical appeal and broad applicability, the practical adoption of DMPC has been severely limited by computational efficiency. Traditional optimization algorithms, particularly those for optimal control, are inherently sequential (e.g., Riccati recursions used in Iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator or iLQR). This sequential nature makes them difficult to parallelize efficiently on modern Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), which are the workhorses of deep learning and crucial for scaling to large datasets and complex neural network architectures. Existing differentiable solvers either (1) are general-purpose but do not exploit the specific structure of OCPs, leading to significant slowdowns (e.g., Theseus), or (2) are OCP-specific and efficient but typically run on CPUs (e.g., many iLQR-based methods) or rely on large batch sizes to amortize the cost of sequential GPU operations (e.g., mpc.pytorch, trajax). This effectively creates a computational chasm between the needs of modern ML and the capabilities of current DMPC tools.

**Bridging the Gap:** "Differentiable Model Predictive Control on the GPU" directly addresses this gap. It introduces a novel GPU-accelerated differentiable optimization tool for MPC that *explicitly exploits the time-induced sparsity and structure of OCPs to enable efficient parallelization on GPUs*. By doing so, this work aims to unlock the full potential of DMPC for large-scale learning applications, allowing researchers and practitioners to seamlessly integrate advanced control strategies with cutting-edge machine learning techniques without being constrained by computational performance. This represents a significant step towards more robust, adaptable, and data-efficient intelligent systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are clearly articulated and stem from the identified computational bottlenecks hindering the widespread adoption of differentiable model predictive control:

1. **Overcome the Parallelization Challenge:** The foremost objective is to resolve the fundamental challenge of efficiently parallelizing the inherently sequential operations of differentiable optimal control algorithms on modern GPU hardware. This is the central computational bottleneck limiting DMPC's scalability.
2. **Develop a GPU-Accelerated Differentiable MPC Tool:** The authors aim to create a novel, specialized software tool, named DiffMPC, that is explicitly designed for high-performance execution on GPUs for both solving (forward pass) and differentiating through (backward pass) optimal control problems.
3. **Exploit Problem Structure for Efficiency:** A core motivation is to achieve this acceleration by intelligently leveraging the sparse-in-time structure inherent to optimal control problems, rather than relying on general-purpose solvers that might be less efficient for this specific class of problems.
4. **Enable Scalable Integration of Learning and Control:** By providing efficient GPU-based DMPC, the authors seek to facilitate the scaling of learning-based control methods (such as reinforcement learning and imitation learning) to larger datasets, more complex environments, and more expressive policy architectures, thereby fully realizing the benefits of combining model-based control with deep learning.
5. **Demonstrate Practical Utility in Challenging Real-world Scenarios:** The work is motivated by the need for robust controllers in complex, safety-critical applications. By showcasing DiffMPC on the challenging task of learning to drift an autonomous vehicle at the limits of handling under model uncertainty, the authors aim to validate its practical relevance and potential impact.

In essence, the motivation is to make differentiable MPC a truly practical and scalable tool for the machine learning and control communities, especially for applications where real-time performance and data-driven adaptation are crucial.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffMPC proposes a comprehensive methodology for solving and differentiating through Optimal Control Problems (OCPs) on the GPU, built around Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) and a custom GPU-optimized linear solver.

**Problem Formulation:** The target OCP involves minimizing a sum of time-separable state and control costs subject to equality constraints that describe system dynamics and initial conditions. Both costs and constraints can be parameterized by `θ`, allowing for learning.

**Forward Pass: Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP):**
To solve the generally non-convex OCP, DiffMPC employs an SQP scheme. In each iteration, the non-linear OCP is approximated by a Quadratic Program (QP) by linearizing the dynamics constraints and approximating the cost functions as linear-quadratic. The key aspect here is the parallel computation of the QP matrices (e.g., `Q`, `R` for costs, and `A`, `B`, `C` for constraints) across different OCP problem instances and time steps `t`. To ensure well-posedness, the cost matrices are projected onto the positive definite cone. The resulting QP's Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions lead to a large linear system, which must be solved to find the optimal state-control trajectory and Lagrange multipliers.

**Backward Pass: Implicit Function Theorem (IFT):**
After obtaining a solution `z` to the OCP via the forward pass, DiffMPC computes the sensitivities (gradients) of this solution with respect to the parameters `θ` using the Implicit Function Theorem. In the context of machine learning, computing the Vector-Jacobian Product (VJP) is typically more efficient than Jacobian-Vector Products (JVP) for scalar loss functions. The VJP computation requires solving a linear system that is structurally identical to the KKT system from the forward pass, using the same KKT matrix but with a different right-hand side. This allows for significant computational reuse.

**Core Linear Solver: Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) with Tridiagonal Preconditioning:**
The most innovative aspect of DiffMPC's methodology lies in its approach to efficiently solve the large, sparse linear systems arising from the KKT conditions in both the forward and backward passes.
1. **Schur Complement:** The KKT system is first condensed by forming its Schur complement `S = -HG⁻¹Hᵀ`. This reduces the size of the primary linear system to be solved for the Lagrange multipliers `λ`.
2. **Symmetric Stair Preconditioning:** For solving `Sλ = γ`, DiffMPC employs a custom Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) routine leveraging a *symmetric stair preconditioner* `Φ⁻¹` (introduced in Adabag et al., 2024). This preconditioner is crucial because it reduces the condition number of `S` (accelerating PCG convergence) while *retaining its parallel-friendly block-tridiagonal structure*. This design choice directly enables efficient parallel execution on GPUs.
3. **Parallel Variable Computation:** Once `λ` is obtained, the state `x_t` and control `u_t` variables for all time steps `t` are computed *in parallel* using simple matrix inversions and multiplications, again exploiting the problem's sparse structure and maximizing GPU utilization.

**Implementation Details:**
- **JAX Framework:** DiffMPC is implemented in JAX, a Python library for high-performance numerical computing and automatic differentiation. This choice simplifies its integration into machine learning workflows and leverages JAX's capabilities for JIT compilation and GPU acceleration.
- **Line Search:** A standard line search procedure (Nocedal & Wright, 2006) is used to ensure global convergence of the SQP algorithm. Critically, the evaluation of the merit function for different step sizes during the line search is also performed *in parallel* to exploit GPU capabilities further.
- **Warm-starting and Computation Reuse:** The solver supports warm-starting both the SQP and PCG iterations with solutions from previous steps, which is highly beneficial in MPC settings where problems are solved sequentially. Furthermore, the pre-computed KKT matrix from the forward pass is reused for the backward pass, reducing redundant computations.
- **Approximation vs. Exact SQP:** Following common practice (e.g., iLQR), DiffMPC approximates the QP cost matrices using only second-order derivatives of the immediate costs (`∇²c`), neglecting the curvature of the dynamics constraints (`∇²f`). This simplifies the implementation and often works well in practice, relying on the line search for robustness, though it might impact gradient accuracy.

By combining these elements, DiffMPC offers a GPU-native approach to differentiable optimal control, specifically engineered to maximize parallelism for improved computational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings across several benchmarks and a challenging real-world application, demonstrating the significant advantages of DiffMPC.

### 5. Significant GPU Acceleration on Reinforcement Learning Benchmarks

- **Speedup over Baselines:** DiffMPC consistently achieved substantial speedups over state-of-the-art differentiable optimization libraries. On GPU, it demonstrated speedups of *more than 4x* compared to the fastest baselines (mpc.pytorch and trajax) across various linear and nonlinear optimal control problems configured for RL tasks. For a nonlinear attitude stabilization task, DiffMPC achieved a remarkable *7.3x speedup* for the backward pass.
- **Comparative Performance:**
 - **Theseus:** Was found to be orders of magnitude slower, especially on CPU, highlighting the necessity of exploiting OCP-specific structure.
 - **mpc.pytorch and trajax:** While supporting GPU execution and exploiting some structure, these iLQR-based methods were not significantly faster on the GPU than on the CPU for the tested problems. This is attributed to their reliance on sequential Riccati recursions, which limit GPU parallelization unless batch sizes are exceptionally large.
 - **DiffMPC on CPU:** While its primary strength is on the GPU, DiffMPC's CPU performance was competitive with, or in some cases slower than, CPU-optimized iLQR methods like trajax, underscoring its tailored design for parallel architectures.
- **Source of Speedup:** The substantial speedups are attributed primarily to DiffMPC's custom Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) routine and its design choices that effectively exploit the time-induced sparsity of OCPs for parallel execution on GPUs.

### 6. Benefits of Warm-starting

- DiffMPC's PCG routine enables warm-starting for both forward and backward passes. While initial results at a very strict PCG tolerance (10⁻¹²) showed modest speedups (~4%), these benefits became more pronounced (up to 11% for forward, 9% for backward) when a more relaxed, yet still practical, tolerance (10⁻⁴) was used. This indicates potential for further performance gains in high-frequency MPC applications.

### 7. Accelerated Imitation Learning (IL)

- On a cart-pole imitation learning benchmark with nonlinear dynamics, DiffMPC enabled end-to-end training that was *approximately 2 times faster* than trajax. This demonstrates its efficiency for learning policies in data-driven settings, where repeated optimization and gradient computations are required.

### 8. Real-World Application: Robust Drifting of a Toyota Supra via Domain Randomization and RL

- **Challenging Task:** DiffMPC was successfully applied to the highly challenging task of learning a robust controller for driving a Toyota Supra at the limits of handling (i.e., drifting) through simulated water puddles and varying initial conditions using reinforcement learning and domain randomization. This task is notoriously difficult due to unstable dynamics and sensitivity to model mismatch.
- **Learned Parameters:** The RL training process automatically tuned key MPC parameters, including cost weights and physical model parameters (like tire friction coefficients). The learned policy exhibited a physically reasonable but non-trivial *13% reduction in rear tire friction coefficient* in its prediction model and a *58% decrease in the cost term associated with sideslip angle errors*. These adaptations would be difficult and time-consuming to obtain through manual tuning.
- **Improved Robustness in Simulation:** The learned policy demonstrated significantly enhanced robustness in simulations, achieving a *100% success rate* in drifting through water puddles compared to a 70% success rate for a manually tuned baseline policy. The learned policy achieved this by selecting actions that resulted in lower sideslip and wheel speeds, providing a larger buffer against instability.
- **Hardware Validation and Transferability:** Crucially, the learned MPC policy was successfully deployed on a *physical Toyota Supra*. It robustly drifted a donut trajectory through water puddles, a task where the baseline controller consistently spun out. This impressive result highlights the transferability of the learned policy (trained on figure-8 trajectories) and the practical utility of DiffMPC for developing robust, data-driven controllers in complex, safety-critical domains.

Overall, the findings strongly validate DiffMPC as an efficient, scalable, and practically impactful tool for differentiable model predictive control, particularly when integrated with learning pipelines on GPU architectures.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Differentiable Model Predictive Control on the GPU (DiffMPC) represents a significant advancement in the fields of optimal control, machine learning, and robotics, with profound potential impacts:

1. **Unlocking Scalability for Differentiable Optimization in Control:** DiffMPC directly addresses a major computational bottleneck that has limited the widespread adoption of DMPC. By enabling efficient parallelization on GPUs, it allows researchers and practitioners to scale differentiable control to larger problem sizes, longer prediction horizons, and significantly larger batch sizes. This scalability is essential for training complex deep learning models that integrate control logic.

2. **Bridging Model-Based and Model-Free Learning:** The ability to efficiently compute gradients through MPC policies on the GPU fosters a tighter integration between physics-informed, model-based control and data-driven, learning-based approaches. This synergy allows for policies that leverage the strong inductive biases of system dynamics and constraints (reducing data requirements and improving safety) while still being adaptable and tunable through end-to-end learning. This is a critical step towards more robust and generalizable intelligent systems.

3. **Accelerating Research in Learning-Based Control:** By substantially reducing computation times for DMPC, DiffMPC will accelerate research in reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and other data-driven control paradigms. Researchers can now iterate faster, explore more complex policy architectures, and train on larger, more diverse datasets, leading to more sophisticated and capable controllers.

4. **Enabling Robust Autonomous Systems in Challenging Environments:** The practical demonstration on a Toyota Supra, learning to drift robustly through water puddles, directly showcases DiffMPC's impact on safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. The ability to learn control strategies for scenarios at the limits of handling, where traditional control methods struggle with model mismatch and uncertainty, is crucial for developing truly resilient autonomous vehicles. This also implies potential for improving robustness against various disturbances and system degradations.

5. **Facilitating Development of Adaptive and Self-Tuning Controllers:** The paper demonstrates learning parameters for the MPC cost function and vehicle model. This capability can lead to controllers that self-tune or adapt to changing environments, vehicle conditions (e.g., tire wear, payload changes), or even driver preferences, reducing the burden of manual expert-driven parameter tuning, which is often time-consuming and expensive.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also highlight avenues for future research:
- **Inequality Constraints:** DiffMPC currently focuses on equality constraints. Incorporating inequality constraints (e.g., control limits, state bounds) via methods like Augmented Lagrangian or interior-point methods, while challenging for differentiability, could lead to higher-quality and more reliable solutions.
- **CPU Performance:** While GPU-optimized, DiffMPC currently runs slower on CPUs compared to highly optimized CPU-specific solvers. Further optimization or hybrid approaches could address this.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Automated tuning of solver hyperparameters (e.g., max iterations, PCG tolerance) within the differentiable pipeline is not explicitly supported, though GPU parallelism could aid in parallel hyperparameter search.
- **Robust Initialization:** Poor initial guesses for solver variables can still lead to divergence, underscoring the need for research into robust initialization strategies for differentiable optimization.

Despite these limitations, DiffMPC represents a significant milestone, transforming differentiable model predictive control into a computationally practical framework for integrating learning and control, poised to influence a wide range of applications from robotics to autonomous vehicles.
