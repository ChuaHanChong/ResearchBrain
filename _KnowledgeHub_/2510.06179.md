# Differentiable Model Predictive Control on the GPU

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Toyota Research Institute developed DiffMPC, a GPU-accelerated differentiable Model Predictive Control (DMPC) tool that achieves over 4x speedups compared to existing DMPC baselines by exploiting the time-induced sparsity of optimal control problems. This tool was successfully deployed on a physical Toyota Supra to learn robust drifting behaviors through water puddles, demonstrating practical utility in challenging real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- The practical adoption of differentiable Model Predictive Control (DMPC) was limited by severe computational inefficiency, especially when attempting to parallelize inherently sequential optimal control algorithms on GPUs.
- Existing differentiable solvers either lacked efficiency for Optimal Control Problems (OCPs) by not exploiting their specific structure or were primarily CPU-bound, hindering scalability for large-scale learning applications.
- The inability to efficiently compute gradients through MPC policies on GPUs created a computational bottleneck for integrating model-based control with modern machine learning techniques like reinforcement and imitation learning.

## Method

- Developed **DiffMPC**, a specialized GPU-accelerated ==differentiable optimization== tool for OCPs that explicitly leverages their time-induced sparsity to enable efficient parallel execution.
- Employed a custom Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) routine with a symmetric stair preconditioner to efficiently solve the large, sparse KKT linear systems arising from Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) on the GPU.
- Implemented the framework in JAX, allowing for seamless integration into machine learning workflows, automatic differentiation, and leveraging JIT compilation and GPU acceleration for both forward and backward passes.

## Results

- DiffMPC achieved over **4x** speedups compared to the fastest differentiable MPC baselines (mpc.pytorch, trajax) across various benchmarks, with up to **7.3x** speedup for the backward pass on a nonlinear attitude stabilization task.
- The framework accelerated imitation learning tasks by approximately 2 times and demonstrated benefits of warm-starting for both forward and backward passes, leading to further speedups.
- A learned MPC policy, trained with DiffMPC and domain randomization, successfully controlled a physical Toyota Supra to robustly drift through water puddles, achieving **100%** success in simulation versus **70%** for a manually tuned baseline controller.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient GPU parallelization of inherently sequential optimal control problems is possible by carefully exploiting their sparse-in-time structure and designing custom linear solvers, such as the symmetric stair preconditioned PCG.
> - Tailoring optimization solvers specifically for OCPs, rather than using general-purpose differentiable optimization frameworks, yields substantial performance gains, enabling DMPC for practical learning-based control.
> - Integrating efficient GPU-based DMPC allows for tighter coupling of model-based control with deep learning, leading to adaptive controllers that can learn optimal control parameters and physical model properties from data while maintaining robustness.
