# Pure Vision Language Action (VLA) Models: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Lanzhou University, NUS, and the Chinese Academy of Sciences conducted a comprehensive survey classifying pure Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models into five paradigms based on action generation strategies, reviewing over three hundred studies to map the field's advancements and future directions for generalized robotics. The survey identifies critical challenges in data scarcity and real-time inference, alongside opportunities for world modeling and causal reasoning to bridge the perception-understanding-action gap in robotics.

## Problem

- Traditional robotics faced limitations in adaptability and intuitive human interaction due to reliance on pre-programmed instructions.
- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) were primarily passive observers, capable of understanding but not actively interacting or making decisions in the physical world.
- The rapidly growing field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lacked a consolidated, systematic review and taxonomy to guide research and development.

## Method

- Systematically review over **300** recent studies focusing on =="pure Vision-Language-Action (VLA) methods"== to synthesize insights into this emerging field.
- Develop a structured taxonomy that classifies VLA approaches based on their ==action-generation strategies== (e.g., ==autoregression==, ==diffusion==, ==reinforcement==, ==hybrid==).
- Detail the motivations, core strategies, implementations, and application domains of various VLA models, along with an overview of foundational ==datasets== and ==simulation platforms==.

## Results

- The survey established a comprehensive taxonomy for VLA models (autoregression-, diffusion-, reinforcement-, hybrid-, and specialized-based approaches) and cataloged foundational resources, including datasets like OXE, simulation platforms (CARLA, Isaac Gym), and robotic hardware.
- Key challenges to VLA development were identified, notably data scarcity, architectural heterogeneity, real-time inference constraints, and the need for more robust evaluation methods.
- Future opportunities were outlined, focusing on advancements in world modeling, causal reasoning, virtual-real integration, and developing trustworthy VLA systems for societal embedding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pure VLA models extend Vision-Language Models from passive understanding to active physical interaction and decision-making, unifying perception, language, and executable control.
> - A structured taxonomy identifies autoregression-based, diffusion-based, reinforcement-based, hybrid, and specialized methods as core paradigms for action generation in VLA.
> - Significant challenges include the scarcity of diverse real-world robotic data, architectural complexity, real-time inference constraints, and limitations in current evaluation benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey is situated at the crucial intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: traditional robotics, deep learning, large language models (LLMs), and vision-language models (VLMs), ultimately aiming for generalized robotics and embodied AI.

Historically, robotics has relied on meticulously pre-programmed instructions and engineered control policies, limiting robots to specific, often repetitive tasks in structured environments (e.g., factory assembly lines). The advent of deep learning brought capabilities like feature extraction and trajectory prediction across diverse modalities, pushing the boundaries of embodied intelligence and autonomous driving. However, these robots largely functioned as isolated agents, lacking intuitive interaction with humans and dynamic external environments.

The emergence of LLMs and VLMs revolutionized natural language processing and multimodal understanding, respectively. VLMs, in particular, demonstrated advanced capabilities in grounding language in visual perception, enabling tasks like image captioning and visual question answering. However, these models were largely "passive sequence generators," primarily focusing on understanding and generating content, not actively interacting with the physical world.

The paper argues that Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm shift, transforming VLMs from passive observers into "active agents for manipulation and decision-making." VLA models are designed to unify visual perception, language understanding, and executable physical control within a single framework. This allows robots to not only "see" and "understand" but also "act" meaningfully based on complex natural language instructions in dynamic, unstructured environments.

This survey directly addresses a significant gap in the existing literature. While there are surveys on VLM foundational models and broad overviews of robotic manipulation, "reviews of pure VLA methods remain scarce." Existing surveys either categorize VLA approaches by their underlying foundational models or offer comprehensive historical analyses of robotics, often neglecting the nuances of emerging VLA techniques. This paper fills this void by focusing specifically on "pure VLA methods," providing a structured taxonomy, and synthesizing insights from over three hundred recent studies, thereby mapping the contours of this rapidly evolving field. By doing so, it provides a much-needed consolidated reference and roadmap for researchers navigating the complexities of developing scalable, general-purpose VLA methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this comprehensive survey are clearly articulated by the authors:

1. **Establish a Clear Taxonomy and Systematic Review:** The authors aim to provide a well-structured taxonomy of pure VLA methods, classifying approaches based on their action-generation strategies. This is crucial for systematizing a nascent field where a consensus methodological landscape is still forming.
2. **Summarize Existing VLA Research:** The survey seeks to offer a comprehensive review of current VLA methods, elucidating their development trajectory and highlighting the unique contributions and distinctive features of previous studies.
3. **Analyze Methodological Paradigms:** A core objective is to classify VLA approaches into several paradigms (autoregression-based, diffusion-based, reinforcement-based, hybrid, and specialized methods) and examine their motivations, core strategies, and implementations in detail.
4. **Assess Application Domains:** The authors intend to provide a comprehensive assessment of VLA deployment across diverse scenarios, including robotic arms, quadruped robots, humanoids, and autonomous vehicles.
5. **Overview Foundational Resources:** Given the data-intensive nature of VLA models, the survey aims to introduce and categorize foundational datasets, benchmarks, and simulation platforms essential for training and evaluation.
6. **Propose Challenges and Future Directions:** Building on the current VLA landscape, a critical objective is to identify key challenges (e.g., data limitations, inference speed, safety) and outline future research directions to advance the field toward generalizable robotics.
7. **Synthesize Recent Studies:** By synthesizing insights from over three hundred recent studies, the survey intends to map the opportunities and challenges that will shape the development of scalable, general-purpose VLA methods.

The overarching motivation is to bridge the "perception–understanding–action" gap in robotics, leveraging the advancements in LLMs and VLMs to create active agents capable of flexible and accurate manipulation in complex, dynamic environments. The authors explicitly state their goal is to provide a "consolidated reference and a forward-looking roadmap" for the VLA community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a systematic, comprehensive review approach to analyze the vast and rapidly evolving literature on VLA models. Their methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Literature Collection and Scope:** The survey synthesizes insights from "over three hundred recent studies," indicating an extensive review of the most current research in the field. The focus is specifically on "pure VLA methods," distinguishing them from broader VLM or general robotics surveys.
2. **Taxonomy Development:** A central methodological contribution is the creation of a structured taxonomy for VLA methods. The authors classify approaches into five main paradigms based on their action-generation strategies:
 - **Autoregression-based Models:** These models treat action sequences as temporally dependent processes, generating actions step-by-step conditioned on prior context, perceptual inputs, and task prompts.
 - **Diffusion-based Models:** These models reframe action generation as a conditional denoising process, enabling probabilistic generation of diverse action distributions.
 - **Reinforcement-based Fine-Tune Models:** These methods integrate vision-language foundation models with reinforcement learning to enhance perception, reasoning, and decision-making, often using reward strategies.
 - **Hybrid Architectures:** These approaches combine multiple generation paradigms (e.g., diffusion with autoregression) to leverage their complementary strengths.
 - **Specialized Methods:** This category encompasses advanced multi-modal fusion, domain adaptations, foundation models, and practical deployment strategies.
3. **Detailed Analysis within Categories:** For each category, the authors delve into:
 - **Motivations:** Why this particular approach is adopted.
 - **Core Strategies:** The underlying principles and mechanisms.
 - **Implementations:** How these strategies are realized in specific models.
 - **Chronological Organization:** The taxonomy and discussion of representative works are organized along a chronological timeline to highlight evolution.
4. **Resource Compilation:** The survey includes a dedicated section on "associated resources," cataloging foundational datasets (real-world and simulation), benchmarks, and simulation platforms crucial for training and evaluating VLA models.
5. **Application Scenario Review:** The paper examines VLA applications across various robotic embodiments, including robotic arms, quadruped robots, humanoids, and wheeled robots (autonomous vehicles).
6. **Challenges and Future Directions:** The authors conclude by identifying key challenges (e.g., data scarcity, architectural heterogeneity, inference constraints, pseudo-interaction, evaluation limitations) and proposing future research opportunities (e.g., world modeling, causal reasoning, virtual-real integration, societal embedding).
7. **Contribution Summarization:** The methodology is framed by four key contributions: a well-structured taxonomy, emphasis on defining characteristics, a comprehensive overview of associated resources, and identification of key limitations and future avenues.

This structured approach ensures a comprehensive, systematic, and insightful analysis of the VLA field, providing both a historical perspective and a forward-looking outlook.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's main findings are presented through its comprehensive taxonomy and analysis of VLA models, along with discussions on supporting resources and future outlook.

#### 4.1. VLA Model Paradigms: Innovations and Limitations

**A. Autoregression-Based Models:**
- **Innovations:** These models unify multimodal perception, language reasoning, and sequential action generation using scalable Transformer architectures. They excel in generalist agents, semantic planning through LLM integration, and long-horizon trajectory generation. Structural optimizations like token compression and parallel decoding enhance efficiency.
- **Limitations:** Suffer from error accumulation and latency due to sequential decoding. Multimodal alignment can be brittle with noisy inputs, and large models demand prohibitive computational resources. Reasoning-driven approaches face challenges like hallucination, stability, and interpretability.

**B. Diffusion-Based Models:**
- **Innovations:** Fundamentally reframe robotic control as a probabilistic generative modeling problem, enabling diverse action generation and geometric grounding. They incorporate multimodal architectural fusion and cognitively inspired deployment strategies. Efficiency-focused designs (e.g., TinyVLA, SmolVLA) make real-world deployment more feasible.
- **Limitations:** Temporal coherence remains fragile in dynamic environments. Large-scale models require immense computational resources and datasets. Safety-critical reliability under uncertain conditions is underexplored. Multimodal fusion can dilute modality-specific strengths, and specialized adaptations may reduce transferability.

**C. Reinforcement-Based Fine-Tune Models:**
- **Innovations:** Leverage visual and language signals to generate dense, transferable reward proxies. Combining offline behavioral cloning with online reinforcement learning stabilizes policy optimization and improves generalization. Safety-focused approaches use constrained optimization to mitigate high-risk actions. Adaptations to diverse robots (quadruped, humanoid) and autonomous driving demonstrate versatility.
- **Limitations:** Reward engineering is often indirect and noisy. Training stability can be hampered by the interplay of supervised fine-tuning and exploration. Scaling to high-dimensional, real-world environments is computationally expensive, and ensuring reliable generalization under ambiguous instructions remains challenging.

**D. Other Advanced Researches (Hybrid and Specialized):**
- **Innovations:**
 - **Hybrid Architectures:** Combine complementary paradigms (e.g., diffusion for smooth trajectories, autoregression for reasoning) to integrate continuous action generation with discrete planning.
 - **Advanced Multi-Modal Fusion & Spatial Understanding:** Move beyond simple concatenation to explicitly model geometry, affordances, and 3D spatial relationships (e.g., CLIPort, 3D-VLA).
 - **Specialized Domain Adaptations:** Extend VLA to diverse applications like autonomous driving (CoVLA), GUI interaction (ShowUI), humanoid control (LeVERB, Helix), and robot orchestration (AutoRT), validating VLA generality.
 - **Foundation Models & Large-Scale Training:** Utilize massive multimodal datasets and scalable architectures (e.g., DROID, ViSA-Flow) to build generalist embodied agents with broad capabilities.
 - **Practical Deployment (Efficiency, Safety, Human-Robot Collaboration):** Focus on real-time inference (EdgeVLA, RTC), robustness (SAFE, DyWA), and seamless human-robot interaction (CrayonRobo, collaborative frameworks).
- **Limitations:** Hybrid systems are computationally complex. Multimodal fusion struggles with noisy or incomplete real-world inputs. Domain-specific adaptations risk overfitting. Foundation models require prohibitive data/resource investments. Deployment efforts face challenges in robustness, interpretability, and reliability in dynamic/adversarial conditions.

#### 4.2. Foundational Resources

- **Datasets and Benchmarks:**
 - **Real-world:** Essential but costly and labor-intensive to collect. Examples include MIME, RoboNet, BridgeData, RT-1, OXE (Open X-Embodiment – a large collaborative dataset of >1M episodes across 160k+ tasks). Autonomous driving datasets (e.g., Cityscapes, Waymo Open Dataset) exist but mostly in open-loop settings, limiting coverage of rare corner cases.
 - **Simulation:** Cost-effective for generating large-scale data and testing (e.g., ROBOTURK, Meta-World, VIMA-BENCH, CARLA). They provide diverse, controllable environments but face the persistent "sim-to-real" gap.
 - **Evaluation:** Primarily uses Success Rate, Language Following Rate, and in driving, metrics like L2 distance and Infraction Score.
- **Simulators:** Indispensable for VLA development, providing physics engines, sensor models, and task logic in safe, reproducible settings. Key examples include THOR, Habitat, MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, and CARLA.
- **Robot Hardware:** The physical foundation for VLA, comprising sensors, actuators, power systems, and control units. Hardware design balances performance, energy efficiency, weight, and durability for task-specific requirements.

#### 4.3. Identified Challenges

The survey highlights critical bottlenecks for VLA models:
1. **Scarcity of Robotic Data:** Insufficient scale and diversity in real-world data, and persistent sim-to-real gap.
2. **Architectural Heterogeneity:** Diverse backbones (vision, language, action heads) and loose coupling of perception, reasoning, and control hinder comparison and generalization.
3. **Real-Time Inference Constraints and Cost:** Large Transformer architectures and autoregressive decoding limit speed and efficiency on physical robots, leading to high latency and computational demands.
4. **Pseudo-Interaction in Human-Robot Interaction:** Current VLA systems often rely on statistical co-occurrence rather than genuine causal reasoning or dynamic environmental feedback, leading to failures in novel situations.
5. **Evaluation and Benchmarking Limitations:** Narrow scope of current benchmarks (laboratory/structured simulation) fails to capture generalization and robustness in open-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Opportunities

Despite challenges, the authors identify significant opportunities for VLA models:
1. **World Modeling and Cross-Modal Unification:** Tighter coupling of language, vision, and action to enable VLA to evolve into proto-world models, unifying understanding and physical execution.
2. **Breakthroughs in Causal Reasoning and Genuine Interaction:** Moving beyond static correlations to causal modeling and interactive reasoning for adaptive behavior.
3. **Virtual-Real Integration and Large-Scale Data Generation:** Creating massive datasets through high-fidelity simulation, synthetic data, and multi-robot sharing to achieve internet-scale generalization for embodied AI.
4. **Societal Embedding and Trustworthy Ecosystems:** Integrating safety, trustworthiness, and ethical alignment into VLA design to enable their widespread adoption in real-world applications (healthcare, industry, etc.).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey holds significant value for the scientific research community, particularly in robotics, artificial intelligence, and machine learning. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Field Consolidation and Standardization:** By providing the first "clear taxonomy and a systematic, comprehensive review" of "pure VLA methods," the survey acts as a critical reference point. It brings order to a rapidly expanding and somewhat fragmented field, helping researchers understand the landscape, identify core approaches, and articulate their work within a common framework. This can foster standardized discussions and accelerate cumulative progress.
2. **Identification of Gaps and Bottlenecks:** The detailed analysis of existing methodologies, datasets, and platforms clearly highlights current limitations and challenges. This includes data scarcity, architectural heterogeneity, real-time inference constraints, superficial human-robot interaction, and inadequate evaluation benchmarks. By explicitly naming these bottlenecks, the survey guides researchers toward high-impact problem areas.
3. **Roadmap for Future Research:** Beyond challenges, the survey outlines concrete "opportunities" and "future directions." This forward-looking roadmap, covering areas like world modeling, causal reasoning, virtual-real integration, and societal embedding, can inspire new research avenues and collaborative efforts, pushing the boundaries of what VLA models can achieve.
4. **Promotion of Generalizable Robotics:** The core thesis of VLA models—reframing VLMs into active, general-purpose agents—is central to achieving truly intelligent robots capable of operating in diverse, unstructured environments. This survey contributes to this goal by systematically reviewing the progress and potential, thus accelerating the shift from specialized, policy-based control to more generalized, adaptable robotic systems.
5. **Facilitation of Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration:** The survey integrates insights from computer vision, natural language processing, reinforcement learning, and robotics. Its multi-institutional authorship further underscores the interdisciplinary nature of VLA. By synthesizing knowledge across these domains, it can encourage greater collaboration and the cross-pollination of ideas, which are essential for complex embodied AI problems.
6. **Guidance for Practitioners and Developers:** The overview of datasets, benchmarks, simulators, and hardware components provides practical guidance for researchers and engineers looking to build, train, and deploy VLA models. It helps them choose appropriate tools and resources, understand evaluation criteria, and consider real-world deployment challenges from the outset.
7. **Advancing Trustworthy AI:** The emphasis on challenges like safety, interpretability, and ethical alignment, alongside opportunities for societal embedding, highlights the growing importance of "trustworthy AI" in robotics. The survey implicitly calls for VLA research to not only focus on capability but also on responsible development and deployment.

In conclusion, this survey is a timely and valuable contribution that maps the state-of-the-art in pure VLA models, critically assesses their strengths and weaknesses, and charts a strategic course for their future development. Its significance lies in its capacity to organize, inspire, and guide the research community toward the realization of truly intelligent, adaptable, and trustworthy embodied artificial intelligence.
