# SimScale: Learning to Drive via Real-World Simulation at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> The SimScale framework, developed by researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, The University of Hong Kong, and Xiaomi EV, creates scalable, photorealistic simulation data from real-world driving logs to enhance end-to-end autonomous driving systems. It consistently improves robustness and generalization across various planner architectures, with one model achieving a new EPDMS score of 48.0 on the navhard benchmark and weaker baselines seeing relative gains over 20%.

## Problem

- End-to-end (E2E) planners struggle with safety-critical or out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios due to their scarcity in real-world human driving datasets.
- Scaling real-world data alone is inefficient and costly for developing deploy-ready autonomous driving systems, especially for rare challenging events.
- Existing neural rendering simulations can generate OOD observations but lack effective methods for generating corresponding feasible expert demonstrations (actions/trajectories) for training E2E planners.

## Method

- A ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== simulation data engine reconstructs real-world assets to render high-fidelity, multi-view RGB observations, allowing dynamic placement of foreground vehicles.
- A ==pseudo-expert scene simulation pipeline== generates diverse, perturbed OOD states and corresponding feasible trajectories by coupling reactive behavior simulation (==Intelligent Driver Model== for agents, ==LQR== for ego) with trajectory perturbation and rigorous filtering.
- A scalable ==sim-real co-training== strategy integrates synthesized simulation data with real-world data, enabling progressive scaling of synthetic data to train various E2E planners while keeping real data fixed, and offers ==reward-only supervision== for scoring-based models.

## Results

- All evaluated end-to-end planning models (**LTF**, **DiffusionDrive**, **GTRS-Dense**) showed consistent improvements in robustness (navhard) and generalization (navtest), with weaker baselines achieving relative gains exceeding **20%**.
- The **GTRS-Dense (V2-99)** model achieved a new reported **EPDMS score of 48.0** on the navhard benchmark, demonstrating enhanced performance with the framework's data.
- The sim-real learning system exhibited predictable scaling trends, with performance generally increasing with more simulation data, notably showing approximately **linear improvement** for diffusion-based planners.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Planner-based pseudo-experts, which generate more exploratory trajectories, offer better performance and scaling compared to recovery-based experts, highlighting the importance of diversity and exploration in synthetic supervision.
> - Multimodal planners (e.g., diffusion-based) exhibit superior scaling properties with increasing diverse simulation data, outperforming single-mode regression planners which can suffer from mode confusion and saturation.
> - Reactive agent dynamics within simulation significantly enhance realism and diversity of traffic interactions, leading to better scaling performance and EPDMS gains than non-reactive simulations.
