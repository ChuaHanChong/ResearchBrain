# SimScale: Learning to Drive via Real-World Simulation at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> The SimScale framework, developed by researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, The University of Hong Kong, and Xiaomi EV, creates scalable, photorealistic simulation data from real-world driving logs to enhance end-to-end autonomous driving systems. It consistently improves robustness and generalization across various planner architectures, with one model achieving a new EPDMS score of 48.0 on the navhard benchmark and weaker baselines seeing relative gains over 20%.

## Problem

- End-to-end (E2E) planners struggle with safety-critical or out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios due to their scarcity in real-world human driving datasets.
- Scaling real-world data alone is inefficient and costly for developing deploy-ready autonomous driving systems, especially for rare challenging events.
- Existing neural rendering simulations can generate OOD observations but lack effective methods for generating corresponding feasible expert demonstrations (actions/trajectories) for training E2E planners.

## Method

- A ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== simulation data engine reconstructs real-world assets to render high-fidelity, multi-view RGB observations, allowing dynamic placement of foreground vehicles.
- A ==pseudo-expert scene simulation pipeline== generates diverse, perturbed OOD states and corresponding feasible trajectories by coupling reactive behavior simulation (==Intelligent Driver Model== for agents, ==LQR== for ego) with trajectory perturbation and rigorous filtering.
- A scalable ==sim-real co-training== strategy integrates synthesized simulation data with real-world data, enabling progressive scaling of synthetic data to train various E2E planners while keeping real data fixed, and offers ==reward-only supervision== for scoring-based models.

## Results

- All evaluated end-to-end planning models (**LTF**, **DiffusionDrive**, **GTRS-Dense**) showed consistent improvements in robustness (navhard) and generalization (navtest), with weaker baselines achieving relative gains exceeding **20%**.
- The **GTRS-Dense (V2-99)** model achieved a new reported **EPDMS score of 48.0** on the navhard benchmark, demonstrating enhanced performance with the framework's data.
- The sim-real learning system exhibited predictable scaling trends, with performance generally increasing with more simulation data, notably showing approximately **linear improvement** for diffusion-based planners.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Planner-based pseudo-experts, which generate more exploratory trajectories, offer better performance and scaling compared to recovery-based experts, highlighting the importance of diversity and exploration in synthetic supervision.
> - Multimodal planners (e.g., diffusion-based) exhibit superior scaling properties with increasing diverse simulation data, outperforming single-mode regression planners which can suffer from mode confusion and saturation.
> - Reactive agent dynamics within simulation significantly enhance realism and diversity of traffic interactions, leading to better scaling performance and EPDMS gains than non-reactive simulations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems require the ability to make rational decisions across a diverse range of scenarios, particularly those that are safety-critical or represent out-of-distribution (OOD) cases. End-to-end (E2E) planning, which maps raw observations directly to actions, is a method for leveraging large-scale driving data to achieve this. However, real-world datasets collected from human expert demonstrations are predominantly composed of common driving situations, leading to an underrepresentation of non-trivial and safety-critical events. Consequently, planners trained solely on such data may struggle to generalize to rare or unforeseen circumstances, which can result in distribution shifts and causal confusion during deployment. Scaling real-world data alone is often considered inefficient for developing deploy-ready autonomous driving systems due to the rarity of challenging scenarios.

Simulation methods, particularly those employing neural rendering, offer a potential solution by generating high-fidelity driving scenarios that include OOD states at scale. While such simulations can expand the diversity of observation data, a significant challenge remains in effectively generating corresponding feasible demonstrations (i.e., appropriate actions or trajectories) for these synthetic OOD states, which are necessary for training E2E planners. Furthermore, a comprehensive analysis of the impact of scaling simulation data on planner performance has been lacking.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a systematic framework, SimScale, for generating scalable simulation data from limited real-world scenarios for E2E planning. It extends existing expert distributions to cover OOD states and provides a method for generating pseudo-expert supervision. By combining this synthetic data with real-world data through a co-training strategy, the work aims to enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of various E2E planners, offering an alternative to solely relying on extensive and costly real-world data collection for challenging scenarios. This work contributes to the field by investigating the scalability of simulation data and its predictable effects on end-to-end autonomous driving systems, bridging the gap between abstract traffic simulation and raw sensory domain learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance fully autonomous driving systems by enabling them to learn rational decisions across a wide spectrum of driving scenarios, particularly those that are safety-critical and out-of-distribution (OOD). The paper highlights a fundamental limitation in current autonomous driving development: real-world data collected from human experts predominantly covers common driving situations, leading to a scarcity of data for critical, rare, or OOD events. This data imbalance constrains the robustness and generalization capabilities of end-to-end (E2E) planners, making it inefficient to achieve deploy-ready autonomy by solely scaling real-world data.

The key motivations driving this work are:
1. **Addressing Data Scarcity for OOD Scenarios:** Real-world data collection for safety-critical and OOD situations is difficult, costly, and time-consuming. Simulation is identified as a promising avenue to synthesize massive amounts of these underrepresented states.
2. **Generating Feasible Demonstrations in Simulation:** While neural rendering can produce high-fidelity visual observations for OOD states, existing simulation methods often lack the capability to effectively generate corresponding feasible actions or expert trajectories that planners can learn from. This work aims to develop a mechanism for generating such "pseudo-expert" supervision.
3. **Systematic Analysis of Simulation Data Scaling:** There has been a lack of in-depth analysis regarding the impact and scaling properties of increasing simulation data, particularly when combined with a fixed real-world corpus. The research seeks to systematically investigate how planners benefit from scaled simulation data and whether this benefit scales predictably.

To address these motivations, the study formulates its research around three specific questions:
1. What constitutes effective simulation data for E2E planning? This involves defining the characteristics of synthetic data, including how to generate diverse and plausible OOD states, and how to create corresponding expert demonstrations.
2. How well do E2E planners benefit from this effective simulation data? This involves evaluating the performance improvements in terms of robustness and generalization across different planner architectures.
3. Does this sim-real learning system scale predictably upon a fixed real-world corpus? This entails analyzing the relationship between increasing simulation data volume and planner performance, specifically looking for scaling trends and potential plateaus.

By answering these questions, the authors aim to derive a systematic approach for leveraging simulation data to bootstrap and scale E2E autonomous driving systems, thereby unlocking the potential of existing real-world driving logs and mitigating their inherent limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SimScale framework is designed to generate scalable simulation data and integrate it with real-world data through a co-training strategy for end-to-end autonomous driving. The methodology involves three main components: a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) simulation data engine, a pseudo-expert scene simulation pipeline, and a scalable sim-real co-training strategy.

#### 3.1. 3DGS Simulation Data Engine

The core of the simulation environment is a photorealistic data engine built upon 3DGS assets, which are reconstructed from real-world datasets. This engine, denoted as Φ, is capable of rendering high-fidelity multi-view RGB observations from the ego vehicle's perspective. It takes camera intrinsics and extrinsics, along with the positions and yaw angles of both the ego and non-ego vehicles, as input.

- **Preprocessing:** Exposure alignment is applied across simultaneously captured multi-view images, guided by projected LiDAR points. 3D bounding boxes from annotations (NAVSIM) are used to divide colored LiDAR points into groups (static background, multiple vehicles) for initializing Gaussians, which improves reconstruction performance.
- **Block-wise Reconstruction:** To manage computational costs associated with a large number of images, reconstruction is performed in a block-wise manner, covering specific spatio-temporal ranges. Backgrounds and foregrounds (vehicles) are reconstructed as separate models, allowing movable vehicle assets to be placed at desired locations and headings during rendering. Blocks with low average PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio) in novel-view synthesis are excluded to maintain data quality.

#### 3.2. Pseudo-Expert Scene Simulation

This pipeline generates diverse simulation data, including OOD states and their corresponding feasible demonstrations, from existing real-world driving logs.

- **Reactive Scene Reconstruction:** For each real-world training clip, two sequential simulations, each of duration H, are performed. The first explores perturbed states, and the second generates expert trajectories. The ego vehicle's trajectory during simulation is governed by an LQR controller, while other agents are modeled using the Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) to ensure reactive interaction with the ego. This produces future states and corresponding multi-view videos rendered by the 3DGS engine. Decoupling behavior simulation from sensor rendering allows for dynamic, responsive environments, enhancing realism and diversity.
- **Trajectory Perturbation:** At the start of the first simulation phase (timestep T), the ego trajectory is perturbed. The objective is to sample diverse yet plausible OOD states. Perturbations are drawn from a clustered human-trajectory vocabulary. Plausibility is enforced by thresholding longitudinal/lateral shifts and heading changes, and by filtering out physically invalid trajectories (e.g., collisions, off-road excursions, unstable renderings). Spatially sparse sampling on trajectory endpoints ensures uniform coverage of the state space. Infeasible trajectories are pre-filtered non-reactively before resource-intensive reactive checks.
- **Pseudo-Expert Trajectory Generation:** For each perturbed state at timestep T+H, a non-human "pseudo-expert" generates a feasible corresponding trajectory for the subsequent H duration (T+H to T+2H). Stricter filtering is applied at this stage, incorporating physical constraints, traffic rules, and vehicle kinematic limits to ensure the quality of supervision. Two strategies for pseudo-expert generation are compared:
 - **Recovery-based Expert:** This expert retrieves a human trajectory from a large vocabulary that best matches the ego vehicle's projected final state. This approach aims for human-like and conservative fallback behaviors, guiding the policy back towards the human trajectory manifold.
 - **Planner-based Expert:** This expert utilizes a privileged planner (PDM-Closed, a rule-based planner) that leverages ground-truth states to generate optimized and reactive trajectory rollouts. This strategy is more exploratory, potentially sacrificing some human-likeness or realism for strong optimization and diverse OOD solutions.

#### 3.3. Scalable Sim-Real Co-training

SimScale employs a co-training strategy that integrates both real-world data (D) and synthesized simulation data (D_sim).

- **Co-training Strategy:** During training, samples are randomly drawn from a mixture of D and D_sim. This approach aims to maintain the human driving distribution while mitigating potential visual domain degradation introduced by simulation artifacts. The framework allows for scaling the total training data by progressively adding non-overlapping simulation samples while keeping the amount of real-world data fixed.
- **Planners for Co-training:** The effectiveness of simulation data is evaluated across three representative paradigms of E2E planners:
 - **Regression-based Planners:** Represented by LTF, these models directly regress future waypoints.
 - **Diffusion-based Planners:** Represented by DiffusionDrive, these models generate diverse trajectories through an iterative denoising process.
 - **Vocabulary Scoring-based Planners:** Represented by GTRS-Dense, these models rank candidate trajectories from a predefined vocabulary based on predicted scores.
- **Learning Objectives:**
 - **With Pseudo-Expert Trajectory:** For regression and diffusion planners, co-training uses an imitation loss based on human expert trajectories in D and pseudo-expert trajectories in D_sim. For scoring-based planners, a reward loss is added to distill evaluation metrics.
 - **With Rewards Only (for Scoring-based):** For scoring-based planners, the learning objective can rely purely on reward signals from both real and simulated data, exploring directions to maximize reward without being restricted to a specific expert trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several findings derived from experiments conducted on the NAVSIMv2 benchmarks (navhard for safety-critical scenarios and navtest for generalization) using the EPDMS metric.

#### 4.1. Leaderboard Performance Improvements

- **Robustness and Generalization:** All evaluated end-to-end planning models (LTF, DiffusionDrive, GTRS-Dense) exhibited improvements in both robustness (navhard) and generalization (navtest) when trained with SimScale's simulation data and co-training strategy.
- **Quantitative Gains:** On the navhard benchmark, the GTRS-Dense (V2-99) model achieved an EPDMS score of 48.0, setting a new reported performance. Weaker baseline models, such as LTF and DiffusionDrive, showed relative gains exceeding 20%, indicating that the simulation data enabled these models to better exploit existing dataset knowledge. On the navtest benchmark, all models demonstrated consistent improvements, up to +2.9 EPDMS, signifying enhanced performance under diverse real-world conditions.
- **Model-Agnostic Nature:** The consistent improvements across different planner architectures (regression, diffusion, scoring-based) suggest that the generated simulation data and the co-training approach are largely model-agnostic.

#### 4.2. Ablation and Data Scaling Analysis

The study investigates how performance scales with increasing simulation data while keeping the real-world data fixed, using a log-quadratic function to model the relationship.

- **Pseudo-Expert Strategy:**
 - **Exploratory vs. Conservative:** Planner-based pseudo-experts, which generate more exploratory and diverse trajectories, consistently led to higher performance and better scaling properties compared to recovery-based experts. Recovery-based experts, which steer the policy towards the human data manifold, showed earlier saturation and lower ultimate performance, though they exhibited advantages in small-data regimes due to better alignment with human-like behavior. This highlights the importance of exploratory behavior in pseudo-experts for maximizing the value of simulation data under scaling.
- **Multimodality Modeling:**
 - **Scaling Differences:** Diffusion-based planners (e.g., DiffusionDrive), which inherently support multimodal output distributions, exhibited more favorable scaling properties (approximately linear improvement) compared to single-mode regression planners (e.g., LTF). The latter tended to saturate or degrade in performance when the simulation-to-real data ratio reached 1:1, attributed to mode confusion from increasingly diverse, multi-modal supervision provided by the expanded simulation data. This observation underscores the relevance of multimodal modeling for scalable E2E autonomous driving, as real-world driving inherently involves multiple plausible actions.
- **Reward-Only Supervision for Scoring-based Planners:**
 - For scoring-based planners like GTRS-Dense, utilizing reward signals alone from simulation data, without explicit pseudo-expert trajectories, yielded superior performance. This suggests that when a model has sufficient initial expert supervision from real data to stabilize its optimization, reward guidance allows for more effective exploration and interaction within the environment, ultimately leading to better performance.
- **Effect of Reactive Simulation:**
 - Comparisons between reactive and non-reactive simulation data demonstrated that reactive agent dynamics (where surrounding agents respond plausibly to the ego vehicle) consistently enhanced the realism and diversity of traffic interactions. This led to superior scaling performance and EPDMS gains, even when non-reactive simulations produced a larger volume of "valid" samples.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative visualizations of the simulation scenes demonstrated the high fidelity of the generated data and the effectiveness of the pseudo-expert supervision. The simulated scenarios included typical out-of-distribution challenges such as off-center lane drifts, near-collision events, departures from the drivable area, and cutting-in situations. These visualizations showed the perturbed ego trajectories, the pseudo-expert's corrective trajectory, and the synthetic multi-view sensory inputs, confirming the pipeline's ability to generate relevant training data for challenging real-world driving conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SimScale framework contributes to the field of autonomous driving by offering a methodology for leveraging scalable real-world simulation to augment existing datasets for end-to-end autonomy. The research demonstrates that this approach can yield consistent performance improvements across various end-to-end planner architectures.

### 6. Significance of the Work

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The study provides evidence that combining real-world data with scalable simulation data through co-training leads to synergistic improvements in both the robustness and generalization capabilities of diverse end-to-end planners. This addresses a critical challenge in autonomous driving related to handling safety-critical and out-of-distribution scenarios, which are often underrepresented in real-world data.
- **Predictable Data Scaling:** The research reveals that the proposed sim-real learning system exhibits a clear and predictable scaling trend with increased simulation data, even when the real-world data corpus remains fixed. This predictability offers a framework for understanding and planning data acquisition and generation strategies.
- **Insights into Simulation Design:** The findings highlight several crucial aspects of designing effective simulation systems for autonomous driving. Specifically, the importance of exploratory behavior in pseudo-experts, the benefits of reactive environments for enhancing realism and diversity, and the superior scaling properties of multimodal planners are demonstrated.
- **Model-Agnostic Framework:** The observed performance gains across different types of end-to-end planners (regression, diffusion, scoring-based) indicate the broad applicability and model-agnostic nature of the SimScale framework.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Development of Autonomous Systems:** By enabling the generation of vast amounts of relevant training data for challenging scenarios, SimScale could help mitigate the costly and time-consuming process of collecting diverse real-world data. This has the potential to accelerate the development and validation of more robust and generalizable autonomous driving systems.
- **Addressing Long-Tail Scenarios:** The ability to synthesize safety-critical and OOD states directly addresses the "long-tail problem" in autonomous driving, where rare but critical events can lead to significant safety concerns if not adequately handled by the driving policy.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The release of the simulation data and code is intended to encourage further academic and industrial exploration into real-world simulation for data scaling. This could foster research in areas such as more sophisticated pseudo-expert generation, advanced traffic behavior modeling, and improved sensor simulation fidelity, potentially leading to more advanced, safe, and reliable autonomous systems.
- **Broader Applicability:** While focused on autonomous driving, the principles of scalable, photorealistic simulation with pseudo-expert supervision and sim-real co-training may have implications for other domains in robotics and embodied AI where data scarcity and generalization across diverse, complex environments are challenges.
