# Latent Collaboration in Multi-Agent Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> LatentMAS, a framework by researchers from Princeton, UIUC, and Stanford, enables large language model agents to collaborate purely within their continuous latent space, rather than using text. This approach substantially improves problem-solving accuracy by up to 14.6% and achieves 4x to 4.3x faster inference speeds while reducing output token usage by 70-80% compared to text-based multi-agent systems.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems (MAS) primarily use text for communication and reasoning, which is computationally expensive and leads to slow inference.
- Text-based communication creates information bottlenecks, as rich continuous latent information is compressed and potentially lost when converted into discrete tokens.
- Errors in reasoning can easily propagate and amplify in sequential text-based MAS, as subsequent agents explicitly inherit textual mistakes.

## Method

- LatentMAS introduces an end-to-end, training-free framework where LLM agents generate and communicate continuous =="latent thoughts"== (last-layer hidden states) auto-regressively.
- It preserves and transfers an agent's complete =="latent working memory"== (accumulated KV caches from all transformer layers) directly to subsequent agents, enabling ==lossless information transfer==.
- An ==input-output alignment operator== is employed to ensure distributional consistency when feeding latent states as inputs, preventing hidden state drift.

## Results

- LatentMAS achieved an average of **+13.3%** higher accuracy compared to single-model baselines and up to **+14.6%** improvement over text-based MAS on various benchmarks.
- It reduced total output token usage by **70.8%** to **83.7%** and accelerated end-to-end inference speed by **4x** to **4.3x** compared to text-based MAS.
- Empirical analysis validated that latent thoughts maintain semantic consistency, offer broader expressive capacity, and the alignment operator is critical for performance, improving accuracy by **2.3%** to **5.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent thoughts are theoretically more expressive and carry significantly more information than discrete text tokens, offering a richer communication medium.
> - Direct transfer of Key-Value (KV) caches facilitates "lossless information transfer" between agents, preserving the full context of prior reasoning steps without re-encoding.
> - The framework inherently reduces computational complexity compared to text-based methods, bypassing tokenization and decoding overheads, as supported by theoretical analysis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses advancements in multi-agent systems (MAS) composed of large language models (LLMs), a growing area in the field of Agentic AI. Traditional LLM-based MAS primarily rely on natural language or text for inter-agent communication and internal reasoning, functioning as a common medium for thought processes and information exchange. This paradigm, while effective, can introduce inefficiencies related to token usage, inference speed, and potential loss of information fidelity during text-based re-encoding.

Previous research has explored the use of LLMs' continuous latent space in several ways: for a single model's internal latent chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning (e.g., using hidden representations), or for information exchange between two models (e.g., using KV caches or layer embeddings). However, a comprehensive framework that integrates both latent reasoning within individual agents and latent communication across multiple agents in an end-to-end manner has been largely unexplored.

This paper positions LatentMAS as a contribution that aims to unify latent reasoning and latent communication within a multi-agent framework. It seeks to transition model collaboration from explicit text-level coordination to direct interaction within latent representations, thus potentially offering a more expressive and efficient mode of operation for MAS. The research builds upon existing advanced text-based MAS methods by proposing a system where agents are treated as tightly integrated components operating purely in the latent space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in current LLM-based multi-agent systems, where text-based mediation serves as the foundation for both internal reasoning and inter-agent communication. While text provides a human-readable interface, it introduces overheads in terms of computational resources (token usage, inference speed) and can potentially constrain the expressiveness and fidelity of information exchange between agents due to the discrete nature of tokens.

The central objective is to investigate the question: "Can MAS achieve pure latent collaboration?" To address this, the authors propose LatentMAS, an end-to-end framework designed to enable LLM agents to collaborate entirely within the continuous latent space, rather than relying on discrete text tokens.

This core objective is underpinned by three foundational principles:

- **Reasoning Expressiveness:** The work aims to leverage the inherent richness of hidden representations, hypothesizing that each latent step can encode more semantic information and continuous thoughts compared to discrete tokens. This would allow individual agents to engage in deeper and more nuanced internal reasoning processes.
- **Communication Fidelity:** A key motivation is to overcome potential information loss and re-encoding overhead associated with text-based communication. The objective is to design a mechanism for transferring internal representations and latent thoughts between agents that ensures lossless information preservation, ideally without requiring re-encoding into text.
- **Collaboration Complexity:** The research seeks to demonstrate that moving collaboration into the latent space can lead to a more favorable trade-off between expressive power and computational efficiency. The aim is to achieve higher collaborative expressiveness than text-based MAS while substantially reducing inference complexity and computational overhead.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to develop a training-free framework that enhances system-level reasoning quality, significantly reduces computational costs (token usage and inference time), and improves the efficiency and fidelity of information exchange in multi-agent systems, all by operating directly within the continuous latent space of LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LatentMAS is presented as an end-to-end, training-free framework designed for multi-agent collaboration entirely within the continuous latent space of LLMs. It integrates two primary mechanisms: auto-regressive latent thought generation and latent working memory transfer. The framework is designed to be agnostic to specific MAS architectures, and for evaluation, it is applied to two common settings: sequential MAS and hierarchical MAS.

**Auto-regressive Latent Thoughts Generation:**
Each LLM agent performs reasoning by auto-regressively generating a sequence of last-layer hidden representations (referred to as "latent thoughts") instead of decoding explicit tokens.
1. **Process:** Given input embeddings, an agent processes them through its transformer layers to compute the last-layer hidden representation at the current step. This hidden representation is then inserted as the input embedding for the next latent step, bypassing the standard token decoding and embedding process. This is repeated for a specified number of latent steps (`m`).
2. **Input-Output Distribution Alignment:** To prevent out-of-distribution activations that could arise from feeding last-layer hidden states directly into shallow input layers, a linear alignment operator (W_a) is introduced. This operator maps the generated last-layer hidden states back to the valid input embedding space. Theoretically, W_a is derived to minimize the Wasserstein distance between the aligned embedding distribution and the original token embedding distribution, approximated using the pseudo-inverse of the output embedding layer (W_out) and the input embedding layer (W_in). This matrix W_a is computed once and reused, making its computational cost negligible.
3. **Theoretical Justification:** Theorem 3.1 formally establishes that latent thoughts exhibit substantially higher expressive capacity, scaling with the model's hidden dimension (d_h), implying that they can convey richer semantic information more efficiently than discrete tokens.

**Thoughts Transfer via Latent Working Memory:**
To facilitate communication between agents without text, LatentMAS utilizes a latent working memory transfer mechanism.
1. **Memory Extraction:** After an agent completes its latent thought generation, its KV (Key-Value) caches from all transformer layers are extracted. This collection of layer-wise caches (M_A1) encapsulates both the initial input context and the newly generated latent thoughts.
2. **Memory Integration:** A succeeding agent integrates this working memory by performing a layer-wise concatenation of the predecessor's KV caches (K_cache, V_cache) with its own existing caches. This process ensures that the new latent thought generation in the subsequent agent is conditioned on the complete internal representations of the preceding agent, without re-encoding.
3. **Lossless Information Transfer:** Theorem 3.3 asserts that this latent working memory transfer mechanism ensures information fidelity equivalent to directly inputting the preceding agents' outputs, effectively achieving lossless information preservation.

**End-to-End Pipeline with Complexity Analyses:**
The entire MAS operates by iteratively applying these two mechanisms: each agent generates latent thoughts, then transfers its updated latent working memory to the next agent. Only the final agent decodes the ultimate answer into text.
- **Complexity Analysis:** Theorem 3.4 demonstrates that LatentMAS exhibits significantly lower time complexity (O((d_h^2*m + d_h*m^2 + d_h*t*m)L)) compared to text-based MAS, especially given the expressive efficiency of latent thoughts.

**MAS Settings for Evaluation:**
- **Sequential MAS:** A chain-of-agents design (Planner, Critic, Refiner, Solver) where outputs are passed sequentially.
- **Hierarchical MAS:** A domain-specialized design (e.g., Math, Science, Code agents) that reason independently, with a Summarizer agent aggregating responses.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks and Datasets:** Evaluated across 9 benchmarks, including Math & Science Reasoning (GSM8K, AIME24/25, GPQA-Diamond, MedQA), Commonsense Reasoning (ARC-Easy/Challenge), and Code Generation (MBPP-Plus, HumanEval-Plus).
- **Models and Baselines:** 5 off-the-shelf LLMs from Qwen3 (4B, 8B, 14B) and Llama3 (3B, 8B) families. Baselines included Single LLM agents, Sequential text-based MAS, and Hierarchical text-based MAS.
- **Implementation Details:** Used HuggingFace Transformers with an optional vLLM backend. Each agent performed a variable number of latent steps (`m` from 0 to 80). Temperature and top-p settings were standardized, and max output lengths were adjusted per task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations across 9 benchmarks and 5 LLM backbone models provided several key findings:

**Overall Performance:**
- **Accuracy Improvement:** LatentMAS consistently improved task accuracy compared to single-model baselines (average 14.6% in sequential, 13.3% in hierarchical settings) and text-based MAS baselines (average 2.8% in sequential, 4.6% in hierarchical settings). In some cases, it achieved up to 14.6% higher accuracy.
- **Efficiency Gains:** The framework demonstrated substantial computational efficiency improvements:
 - **Token Usage Reduction:** Reduced output token usage by 70.8%-83.7% compared to text-based MAS. It also showed 15.0%-60.3% lower token usage than single agents.
 - **Inference Speed:** Provided 4x-4.3x faster end-to-end inference speed on average compared to text-based MAS. Even against vLLM-optimized text-based baselines, LatentMAS achieved a 2.6x-7x speedup.

**In-depth Analyses:**
- **Latent Thoughts Reflect Text Reasoning:** Analysis of last-layer embeddings revealed that LatentMAS's generated latent thoughts largely cover the embedding space of text-based generated tokens, indicating semantic consistency. Moreover, they offered greater diversity and expressive capacity, suggesting richer semantic representations than discrete tokens. Quantitatively, LatentMAS consistently achieved lower average pairwise cosine similarity among generated embeddings, indicating less collapsed and more diverse representations.
- **Effectiveness of Input-Output Alignment:** Empirical validation of the linear alignment operator (W_a) showed that unaligned output embeddings drifted significantly from the input embedding space. Applying W_a effectively realigned these vectors, preserving geometric and statistical structure and mitigating representation drift. This alignment contributed to consistent accuracy gains of 2.3%-5.3% across different tasks.
- **Optimal Latent Step Depth:** Performance generally improved with an increasing number of latent steps, peaking around 40-80 steps before plateauing or slightly declining. This suggests that a moderate latent step budget provides the optimal accuracy-efficiency trade-off.
- **Ablation Studies:** Hybrid variants (Latent Reasoning + Text Communication, or Text Reasoning + Latent Communication) consistently underperformed the full LatentMAS framework. This indicated that both latent reasoning (for expressive internal computation) and latent communication (for lossless information transfer) are crucial components contributing to the overall gains.
- **Final-Agent Token Usage:** Further analysis showed that the final answering agent in LatentMAS generated fewer text tokens (average 29.1% reduction) compared to its counterpart in text-based MAS. This implied that token savings were not only from eliminating intermediate textual communication but also from a more concise final answer decoding process, as latent memory allowed for more direct aggregation of prior thoughts.
- **Intermediate Error Tracking (Debug Mode):** A "debug mode" that allows simultaneous generation of latent thoughts and human-readable text was introduced for interpretability. A correlation study confirmed high consistency (e.g., 96.2% correct debug text when final answer was correct, 90.0% incorrect debug text when final answer was incorrect) between the correctness of intermediate debug text and the final answer, validating its utility as a diagnostic tool. Case studies illustrated how latent collaboration mitigated error propagation compared to text-based methods.
- **Generalizability:** The observed trends in accuracy and efficiency gains were consistent across both Qwen3 and Llama3 model families, demonstrating the generalizability of LatentMAS across different LLM architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LatentMAS presents a notable advancement in the field of multi-agent systems and large language model collaboration. By enabling purely latent reasoning and communication, the work proposes a shift from text-based interaction, which has several implications:

- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The framework demonstrates consistent improvements in system-level reasoning accuracy while simultaneously achieving substantial reductions in computational overhead. This includes a significant decrease in output token usage (70.8%-83.7% less) and a notable acceleration in end-to-end inference speed (4x-4.3x faster) compared to conventional text-based MAS. These efficiency gains are achieved without requiring additional training, which could make LatentMAS a practical and appealing solution for resource-constrained environments or applications requiring high throughput.
- **Richer Expressiveness and Fidelity:** Operating directly within the continuous latent space allows for the representation and transfer of richer semantic structures than discrete tokens. This "lossless" information exchange, as supported by theoretical analysis, can lead to more coherent and robust multi-agent reasoning, potentially mitigating issues like error propagation seen in text-based systems.
- **Scalability for Complex Tasks:** The reduced computational complexity of latent collaboration, as theoretically analyzed, suggests that LatentMAS could offer a more scalable paradigm for orchestrating numerous LLM agents on complex, multi-step problems. This could enable more intricate and extensive collaborative reasoning workflows that would be prohibitively expensive with text-based communication.
- **Generalizable Framework:** The demonstrated effectiveness across various benchmarks (math, science, commonsense, code generation) and different LLM families (Qwen3, Llama3) suggests that LatentMAS is a generalizable approach. Its training-free nature further contributes to its adaptability across diverse applications and models.
- **New Paradigm for Agentic AI:** LatentMAS establishes a foundation for exploring agentic collaboration beyond the constraints of natural language. This opens new research avenues for designing and optimizing multi-agent interactions, potentially leading to more tightly integrated and sophisticated AI systems that reason and communicate at a deeper, continuous level. Future work might explore its extension to heterogeneous agents through adaptive layer mapping.

In summary, LatentMAS represents a step towards more efficient, expressive, and scalable multi-agent intelligence by leveraging the continuous latent space of LLMs. This could significantly impact the development of advanced AI agents capable of complex problem-solving with reduced resource consumption.
