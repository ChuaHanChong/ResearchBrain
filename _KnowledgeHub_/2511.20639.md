# Latent Collaboration in Multi-Agent Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> LatentMAS, a framework by researchers from Princeton, UIUC, and Stanford, enables large language model agents to collaborate purely within their continuous latent space, rather than using text. This approach substantially improves problem-solving accuracy by up to 14.6% and achieves 4x to 4.3x faster inference speeds while reducing output token usage by 70-80% compared to text-based multi-agent systems.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems (MAS) primarily use text for communication and reasoning, which is computationally expensive and leads to slow inference.
- Text-based communication creates information bottlenecks, as rich continuous latent information is compressed and potentially lost when converted into discrete tokens.
- Errors in reasoning can easily propagate and amplify in sequential text-based MAS, as subsequent agents explicitly inherit textual mistakes.

## Method

- LatentMAS introduces an end-to-end, training-free framework where LLM agents generate and communicate continuous =="latent thoughts"== (last-layer hidden states) auto-regressively.
- It preserves and transfers an agent's complete =="latent working memory"== (accumulated KV caches from all transformer layers) directly to subsequent agents, enabling ==lossless information transfer==.
- An ==input-output alignment operator== is employed to ensure distributional consistency when feeding latent states as inputs, preventing hidden state drift.

## Results

- LatentMAS achieved an average of **+13.3%** higher accuracy compared to single-model baselines and up to **+14.6%** improvement over text-based MAS on various benchmarks.
- It reduced total output token usage by **70.8%** to **83.7%** and accelerated end-to-end inference speed by **4x** to **4.3x** compared to text-based MAS.
- Empirical analysis validated that latent thoughts maintain semantic consistency, offer broader expressive capacity, and the alignment operator is critical for performance, improving accuracy by **2.3%** to **5.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent thoughts are theoretically more expressive and carry significantly more information than discrete text tokens, offering a richer communication medium.
> - Direct transfer of Key-Value (KV) caches facilitates "lossless information transfer" between agents, preserving the full context of prior reasoning steps without re-encoding.
> - The framework inherently reduces computational complexity compared to text-based methods, bypassing tokenization and decoding overheads, as supported by theoretical analysis.
