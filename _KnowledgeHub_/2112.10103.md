# SAGA: Stochastic Whole-Body Grasping with Contact

> [!summary] Summary
> SAGA synthesizes stochastic whole-body human motions that approach and grasp a randomly placed 3D object. A multi-task WholeGrasp-VAE jointly generates sparse body-marker positions and mutual human-object contact maps for the grasping end pose; a two-part MotionFill-VAE stochastically infills global root trajectory and local articulation between start and end. Contact-aware optimization recovers meshes, yielding diverse realistic grasps on unseen objects.

## Problem

- Prior grasp synthesis models only the hand, ignoring the full body that must approach the object, so whole-body grasping motion remained unstudied.
- Whole-body motion synthesis largely ignores objects, and skeleton or parametric-latent body representations accumulate kinematic-chain error, causing hand-object interpenetration and unrealistic contact.
- Existing infilling models are deterministic or predict trajectory then pose sequentially, so they capture neither trajectory diversity nor local articulation diversity between two given poses.

## Method

- **WholeGrasp-VAE**: a multi-task conditional VAE over sparse surface markers that jointly samples whole-body grasping poses, per-marker contact labels, and an object contact map, trained with a marker-object consistency loss.
- **MotionFill-VAE**: two concatenated CVAEs, ==TrajFill== sampling root-trajectory deviation from a straight interpolation, ==LocalMotionFill== sampling trajectory-conditioned local motion plus foot-ground contacts from a four-channel motion image.
- **GraspPose-Opt** and **GraspMotion-Opt**: ==contact-aware SMPL-X optimization== combining marker fitting, mutual contact, signed-distance collision, ground contact, and a palm-facing term to recover realistic meshes and smooth motion.

## Results

- On GRAB, SAGA reaches **0.94** contact ratio with **0.48 cm3** interpenetration volume and **0.16 cm** depth, versus GrabNet-SMPLX at **0.65** ratio and **14.15 cm3**.
- Motion infilling beats the Route+PoseNet baseline: ADE **0.083** vs **0.219**, skating **0.394** vs **0.575**; the local-only variant tops LEMO with ADE **0.079** and PSKL-J **0.377**.
- Ablation shows dropping the learned mutual contact loss collapses contact ratio to **0.24**; AMT raters scored generated motions **3.15** versus **4.04** for ground truth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparse surface markers beat parametric latent body codes as a learning target: they avoid kinematic-chain error accumulation that wrecks physical plausibility at the hands.
> - Jointly generating pose and mutual contact beats sequential prediction or fixed heuristic contact patterns; collision losses alone destroy contact unless paired with explicit contact terms.
> - Factoring motion into a sampled global trajectory plus trajectory-conditioned local articulation captures diversity deterministic infillers miss, while explicit foot-ground labels suppress skating artifacts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human-computer interaction and animation has seen significant advancements in synthesizing realistic human motions and interactions. Existing research has predominantly focused on two distinct areas: synthesizing realistic hand-object interactions for grasping and manipulation, and synthesizing realistic whole-body human motions.

Within hand-object interaction, methods such as Grasping Field, GrabNet, GraspTTA, and ContactOpt have been developed to generate plausible hand poses and refine hand-object contact. However, these approaches typically concentrate solely on the hand, often without considering the broader context of full-body movements that precede or accompany the grasp. The dexterous finger movements and precise hand-object contacts are modeled, but the global body dynamics leading up to the interaction are largely ignored.

Conversely, works in whole-body motion synthesis have made progress in generating human movements in 3D space, addressing tasks like motion prediction, in-betweening, and character control. These methods often utilize parametric body models or marker-based representations and sometimes incorporate scene context or ground contact. However, a significant limitation of these methods is their general disregard for explicit object interaction. They focus on overall body kinematics and dynamics, but do not typically address the specific requirements of approaching and grasping a target object.

The current work, SAGA, aims to bridge the gap between these two research streams. It addresses the synthesis of *whole-body grasping motions*, a task that necessitates the integration of both full-body dynamics and dexterous hand movements, along with complex body-scene and hand-object contacts. By focusing on generating diverse and natural whole-body motions to approach and grasp objects, SAGA positions itself as a framework that extends beyond hand-centric grasping or object-agnostic whole-body motion, offering a more comprehensive solution for human-object interaction synthesis in 3D environments. It also distinguishes itself by emphasizing stochasticity to capture the inherent diversity of human movements for a given task, an aspect often overlooked by deterministic models. A concurrent work, GOAL, approaches a similar problem but is noted to employ a deterministic model for motion infilling, contrasting with SAGA's stochastic approach to both end poses and in-between motions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize diverse and natural whole-body human grasping motions. Specifically, the authors aim to generate realistic 3D human motions that start from an arbitrary initial pose, approach a target object placed in 3D space, and then grasp it. This overarching goal is driven by the potential applications across various fields, including augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), video games, and robotics, where automated generation of such complex human-object interactions is valuable.

The motivation for developing a novel framework to address this task stems from several significant challenges:

1. **High-Dimensionality and Complexity of Whole-Body Dynamics and Dexterous Finger Movements**: Whole-body grasping involves coordinating numerous degrees of freedom, from large-scale body movements to intricate finger articulations. This high-dimensional control space makes the synthesis of coherent and realistic grasping motions inherently complicated. Existing methods typically simplify this problem by focusing only on the hand or ignoring objects entirely.

2. **Modeling Complex and Frequent Contact Interactions**: Realistic whole-body grasping requires precise and plausible physical contacts. This includes steady and stable foot-ground interaction to prevent artifacts like foot-skating, as well as natural and conforming hand-object contact during the grasp. Synthesizing these complex and varied contact scenarios in a perceptually realistic manner is a major technical hurdle.

3. **Capturing Motion Diversity and Uncertainty**: Given an initial human pose and a target object, there is an "infinite number of ways" for a person to approach and grasp that object. This diversity is further amplified by variations in object shape and pose. Developing a generative model that can capture this wide range of plausible and realistic motions, rather than just a single deterministic outcome, is a crucial but unsolved problem for creating expressive and lifelike animations or robotic behaviors.

To address these challenges, the authors propose SAGA, driven by two key insights:
- **Markers-based Representation**: Instead of directly learning the latent parameters of parametric body models (e.g., SMPL-X), SAGA utilizes a markers-based representation. This is motivated by previous work suggesting that neural networks can learn more realistic motions from sparse 3D markers, particularly for fine-grained interactions where cumulative errors in kinematic chains (common with parametric models) can lead to unrealistic hand-object interpenetration.
- **Central Role of Contact**: Explicitly modeling and learning contact representations from data is considered fundamental. The framework aims to leverage predicted contact information to guide both the generation of static grasping poses and the synthesis of dynamic motions, thereby improving the physical plausibility and realism of interactions with the ground and the object.

These motivations collectively highlight the need for a comprehensive generative framework that can tackle the complexity, contact dynamics, and inherent variability of whole-body grasping motions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAGA (StochAstic whole-body Grasping with contAct) is designed as a two-stage generative framework to synthesize diverse and realistic whole-body motions for approaching and grasping 3D objects. The human body is represented by sparse 3D marker locations, and 3D objects by centered point cloud data and height.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Stochastic Whole-Body Grasping Pose Generation

The first stage focuses on generating diverse static whole-body grasping end poses and associated human-object contacts.

- **WholeGrasp-VAE Model**: A multi-task conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) is developed to jointly learn whole-body marker positions ($M$) and fine-grained marker-object contact labels ($C_M$, $C_O$).
 - **Inputs**: The model is conditioned on the object information (point cloud $O$ and height $t_O$). For training, it also takes body marker positions, body marker contacts, and object contacts.
 - **Outputs**: The decoder generates diverse sets of consistent full-body marker locations ($\hat{M}$) and corresponding contact labels on both body markers ($\hat{C}_M$) and the object surface ($\hat{C}_O$).
 - **Body Representation**: A markers-based representation is chosen over parametric SMPL-X models due to its perceived advantage in learning realistic motion and avoiding error accumulation, especially for hand grasps.
 - **Training Losses**:
 - **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$)**: L1 loss for marker positions and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for contact probabilities (weighted for class imbalance).
 - **KL-Divergence Loss ($L_{KL}$)**: A robust KL-divergence term is used to prevent posterior collapse in the VAE.
 - **Consistency Loss ($L_c$)**: This loss encourages consistency between predicted marker positions and contact maps. It penalizes discrepancies in distances between contacting markers and object surfaces, and vice-versa, when contact is predicted.

- **GraspPose-Opt (Contact-Aware Pose Optimization)**: Following the generation of markers and contact labels, an optimization module recovers a parametric SMPL-X body mesh and refines the pose for realistic interaction.
 - **Optimization Objective ($E_{opt}$)**: Minimizes a sum of specialized loss terms.
 - **Marker Fitting Loss ($E_{fit}$)**: Minimizes the L1 distance between the generated markers ($\hat{M}$) and markers queried from the SMPL-X mesh ($M(\Theta)$), with regularization on pose parameters.
 - **Object Contact Loss ($E_{o\_cont}$)**: A mutual contact loss that encourages body markers with high predicted contact probability to be close to the object surface, and object points with high predicted contact probability to be close to the body surface.
 - **Collision Loss ($E_{colli}$)**: Uses signed-distance functions to penalize interpenetration between the body (specifically hands) and the object.
 - **Ground Contact Loss ($E_{g\_cont}$)**: Penalizes the height of feet vertices to enforce plausible foot-ground interaction.
 - **Optimization Strategy**: A multi-stage, global-to-local optimization is employed to facilitate gradual convergence of the high-dimensional SMPL-X parameters.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Stochastic Grasp-Oriented Motion Infilling

The second stage generates diverse intermediate motions between an initial pose and the grasping end pose from Stage 1.

- **MotionFill-VAE Model**: A novel generative motion infilling model is constructed using two concatenated CVAEs to capture uncertainty in both global root trajectory and local body poses.
 - **Hierarchical Motion Representation**: Global motion ($M_{0:T}$) is decomposed into global root velocity ($v_{0:T}$) and trajectory-conditioned local motion ($M^l_{0:T}$).
 - **TrajFill Component**: This CVAE learns the conditional intermediate global root trajectory latent space.
 - **Inputs**: Start and end root states ($\Gamma_0$, $\Gamma_T$).
 - **Outputs**: It learns the deviation ($\Delta\Gamma_{0:T+1}$) from a linearly interpolated and extrapolated straight trajectory ($\bar{\Gamma}_{0:T+1}$), and then computes the root velocity ($v_{0:T}$).
 - **LocalMotionFill Component**: This CVAE learns the conditional intermediate local motion latent space.
 - **Inputs**: Global trajectory information (root velocity $v_{0:T}$ from TrajFill), start local pose ($M^l_0$), and end local pose ($M^l_T$). It uses a 4-channel image representation which concatenates local motion with foot-ground contact labels and root velocity.
 - **Outputs**: Generates trajectory-conditioned local motion sequences ($\hat{M}^l_{0:T}$) and foot-ground contact probabilities ($\hat{C}^F_{0:T}$).
 - **Training Losses**:
 - **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$)**: Includes terms for global trajectory, root velocity, foot-ground contact labels (BCE), local motion, and local motion velocity.
 - **KL-Divergence Loss ($L_{KL}$)**: Robust KL-divergence for both TrajFill and LocalMotionFill.

- **GraspMotion-Opt (Contact-Aware Grasping Motion Optimization)**: This module recovers smooth and realistic SMPL-X motions from the generated marker sequences, integrating all predicted contact information.
 - **Optimization Objective**: Minimizes a combination of loss terms over the entire motion sequence.
 - **Basic Loss ($E_{basic}$)**: Sum of marker fitting and object collision losses for all frames, and object contact loss for the last 5 frames (to focus on the actual grasp).
 - **Grasp Loss ($E_g$)**: Encourages the palm to face the object's surface during approach, specifically when palm vertices are close to the object.
 - **Cross-frame Smoothness Loss ($E_{smooth}$)**: Enforces smoothness on the motion latent space (encoded by a pre-trained autoencoder) and directly on hand vertices to prevent jerky movements.
 - **Foot Skating Loss ($E_{skat}$)**: Reduces foot-skating artifacts by penalizing foot velocity when feet are predicted to be in contact with the ground, leveraging foot-ground contact labels from MotionFill-VAE.
 - **Optimization Strategy**: A two-stage optimization is used, first fitting body meshes to markers, then refining the sequences with all loss terms.

#### 3.3. Body Markers Placement

The system uses 143 markers for WholeGrasp-VAE (49 body, 14 face, 6 hands, 30 fingers, 44 palms). For MotionFill-VAE, a sparser set is used, including only 10 fingertip markers, in addition to the body markers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experiments to validate the effectiveness of the SAGA framework, evaluating both individual stages and the complete pipeline.

#### 4.1. Stochastic Whole-body Grasp Pose Synthesis (Stage 1)

- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - The proposed `WholeGrasp` (without optimization) was compared against an extended `GrabNet-SMPLX` baseline, which directly learns SMPL-X parameters for whole-body grasping. `WholeGrasp` demonstrated higher diversity (APD: 2.92 vs. 0.33) and better initial contact (Contact Ratio: 0.96 vs. 0.65), with slightly higher interpenetration volume but comparable depth, indicating that the markers-based representation is more effective than directly learning SMPL-X parameters for plausible human-object interaction.
 - Applying the `GraspPose-Opt` optimization (Our `WholeGrasp w/ opt`) significantly improved results, drastically reducing interpenetration volume (from 12.20 cm³ to 0.48 cm³) and depth (from 0.85 cm to 0.16 cm) while maintaining a high contact ratio (0.94). This highlights the critical role of the contact-aware optimization module.

- **Ablation Study on Multi-task Design**:
 - A single-task `WholeGrasp-single` model (learning only marker positions) with a heuristic contact optimization produced inferior results (Contact Ratio: 0.81, Inter. Vol: 0.21, Inter. Depth: 0.12) compared to the multi-task `WholeGrasp w/ opt` (Contact Ratio: 0.94, Inter. Vol: 0.48, Inter. Depth: 0.16) and even the multi-task model without explicit contact optimization (`WholeGrasp w/o opt`). This suggests that jointly learning mutual human-object contact labels explicitly in the `WholeGrasp-VAE` contributes to more realistic and robust human-object interaction during subsequent optimization.

- **Ablation Study on GraspPose-Opt Components**:
 - Evaluations using both ground truth and sampled markers demonstrated that the `E_colli` (collision) and `E_o_cont` (object contact) loss terms are crucial for recovering realistic human-object interactions. Without them, fitting body meshes resulted in high interpenetration. With `E_colli` and `E_o_cont`, the results for both ground truth and sampled markers achieved a high contact ratio (0.94) and low interpenetration (Vol: 0.52/0.48, Depth: 0.17/0.16), comparable to the ground truth mesh.

- **Qualitative Results**: Generated diverse and natural grasping poses with varying body shapes and whole-body configurations for unseen objects, accompanied by plausible object contact maps.

#### 4.2. Stochastic Motion Infilling (Stage 2)

- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - For global and local motion infilling, SAGA (`Ours`) was compared against the deterministic `Route+PoseNet` baseline. SAGA achieved a significantly lower Average L2 Distance Error (ADE) (0.083 vs. 0.219), less foot skating (0.394 vs. 0.575), and lower Power Spectrum KL-divergence (PSKL-J) in both directions (0.772/0.609 vs. 0.955/0.884), indicating generation of more accurate and natural motions.
 - For local motion infilling (given ground truth trajectories), SAGA's `Ours-Local` component outperformed deterministic baselines (`CNN-AE`, `LEMO`, `PoseNet`) in ADE (0.079), foot skating (0.137), and PSKL-J (0.377/0.327), demonstrating superior capture of human motion patterns.
 - The generated motions maintained a high non-collision score with the ground (0.9743), effectively preventing foot-ground interpenetration.

- **Qualitative Results**: The model demonstrated the ability to generate diverse in-between motions, including different global trajectories and varying local body articulations for the same start and end poses. The `MotionFill-VAE` also showed generalization capabilities to different motion lengths (2s, 4s, 6s) when trained with corresponding datasets.

#### 4.3. Whole-body Grasp Motion Synthesis (Full Pipeline)

- **User Study (Amazon Mechanical Turk)**:
 - A perceptual study on 14 unseen objects (from GRAB and HO3D datasets) evaluated the realism of generated 2-second grasping motions.
 - Ground truth sequences received an average score of 4.04 (around "agree"), while SAGA's synthesized sequences received an average score of 3.15 (above "slightly agree") on a five-point scale. This indicates that the proposed pipeline is capable of synthesizing high-fidelity grasping motions, though with a noticeable gap to real human performance.

- **Limitations**: The framework exhibited some failure cases, particularly with complex objects like a frying pan. While contact with the object was generally maintained, the synthesized human sometimes failed to grasp the handle, leading to perceptually unrealistic results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SAGA framework represents a notable advancement in the synthesis of human-object interaction, specifically addressing the challenging task of generating realistic and diverse whole-body grasping motions. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Comprehensive Human-Object Interaction**: By integrating whole-body dynamics with dexterous hand movements and explicit contact modeling, SAGA moves beyond prior work that largely isolated hand grasping from full-body motion. This holistic approach enables the generation of more complete and believable human behaviors in interactive environments.

2. **Generative Stochasticity**: The use of CVAE-based models at both the static pose generation and motion infilling stages allows SAGA to synthesize diverse and stochastic motions. This is crucial for applications requiring natural variability, as humans exhibit numerous plausible ways to perform a grasping task. The ability to generate varied trajectories and local articulations from a single start pose makes the synthesized motions more expressive and less repetitive.

3. **Robust Contact Handling**: The explicit learning and optimization of human-object and foot-ground contacts are central to SAGA's methodology. This focus on physical plausibility significantly reduces common artifacts such as interpenetration and foot-skating, leading to perceptually more realistic interactions. The multi-task learning for contacts in `WholeGrasp-VAE` and the contact-aware optimization modules (`GraspPose-Opt`, `GraspMotion-Opt`) are key innovations in this regard.

4. **Novel Architectural Components**: The introduction of `WholeGrasp-VAE` for jointly learning static whole-body grasping poses and interactions, and `MotionFill-VAE` for stochastic infilling of both global trajectories and local pose articulations, represents novel architectural contributions to the field of human motion synthesis. The marker-based representation further offers an alternative to parametric model learning for improved realism.

5. **Broad Applicability**: The ability to automatically synthesize realistic whole-body grasping motions has direct and significant implications for:
 - **AR/VR and Video Games**: Enhancing the realism and immersion of virtual characters by providing complex, natural, and diverse interactions with virtual objects.
 - **Robotics**: Informing the design of more robust and human-like grasping strategies for humanoid robots, enabling them to adapt to diverse objects and environmental contexts.
 - **Computer Vision**: Providing synthetic data for training models that understand human-object interaction from real-world observations.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: While SAGA focuses on grasping, it lays a strong foundation for future work on more complex human-object manipulation tasks. The authors identify hand-object manipulation beyond simple grasping, incorporating object affordance, physics, and interaction goals, as a promising direction. This suggests SAGA serves as a crucial step towards creating fully automated systems that can generate rich and context-aware human-object interactions.
