# TA-VLA: Elucidating the Design Space of Torque-aware Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence and Tsinghua University systematically investigated methods for integrating motor current-derived torque signals into pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Their work significantly enhanced robot performance and robustness in contact-rich manipulation tasks by enabling models to respond to physical interactions.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, despite strong visual and language understanding, cannot effectively integrate and respond to subtle physical feedback like force or torque.
- The lack of physical interaction awareness limits VLA models' applicability and robustness in real-world robotic tasks involving physical contact, often leading to failures that are visually imperceptible.
- Existing approaches using force/torque information either rely on costly external sensors or fail to leverage the generalization benefits of pretrained VLA models.

## Method

- A systematic exploration of the design space for integrating ==proprioceptive torque signals== into pretrained VLA architectures, focusing on 'when' (immediate/historical), 'where' (encoder/decoder), and 'how' (single/multi-token) they are used.
- Developed strategies to embed past and present torque signals as observations, identifying that decoder-side integration of aggregated historical torques in a single token is most effective.
- Proposed an auxiliary task of predicting future torques alongside actions, training a unified action-torque ==diffusion model== to foster a physically grounded understanding of interaction dynamics.

## Results

- The combined observation and objective-based torque integration (`π0 + obs + obj`) achieved substantial success rate increases in contact-rich tasks, improving 'Charger Plugging' from 0/20 (baseline) to 17/20 and 'Button Pushing' from 5/20 to 18/20.
- The proposed methods demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, improving performance on another VLA model (RDT, e.g., Button Pushing success rate increased from 4/20 to 16/20) and generalizing to a different robotic arm (ROKAE SR) for cross-embodiment tasks.
- Leveraging joint torques estimated from motor currents ('sensorless' approach) eliminates the need for expensive external force/torque sensors, offering a cost-effective solution without significantly impacting training or inference times.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating torque signals into the decoder, particularly by prepending an encoded torque token to the decoder's inputs, consistently outperforms encoder-side integration.
> - While historical torque information is crucial for dynamic contact events, summarizing the entire history into a single aggregated token within the decoder is more effective than using multiple tokens, balancing informativeness with architectural stability.
> - Predicting future torques as an auxiliary objective encourages the VLA model to develop a physically grounded internal representation by learning the causal relationship between actions and their physical consequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen rapid advancements with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, exemplified by architectures like Octo, RDT, and π0, leverage large-scale datasets, visual perception, and language understanding to generate actions, exhibiting impressive generalizability across diverse tasks and devices. However, a significant limitation of current VLA models is their inability to effectively integrate and respond to subtle physical feedback, such as force or torque signals. This gap is particularly problematic for **contact-rich manipulation tasks**, where assessing successful completion and enabling closed-loop control critically depends on sensing physical interactions.

Prior efforts in imitation learning have explored the use of force/torque information, often relying on external force/torque (F/T) sensors. While effective, these approaches incur higher economic costs and face limitations in harsh operating conditions. Furthermore, many such methods train policies from scratch, failing to capitalize on the substantial benefits of **pretrained VLA models**, which offer a robust foundation in cross-modal learning and shared feature representations. FACTR, for instance, requires complex training pipelines for modality alignment.

This paper explicitly aims to bridge this critical gap. It distinguishes itself by systematically investigating *how* to embed proprioceptive torque signals, derived directly from motor currents (thus "sensorless"), into *existing, pretrained VLA architectures*. By doing so, it seeks to enable contact-sensitive decision-making without sacrificing the generalization and scalability advantages inherent in VLA models. The work positions itself as a methodical exploration of the "design space" for integrating physical modalities, moving beyond simple input concatenation to derive generalizable principles for future VLA development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the observation that while VLA models excel at high-level reasoning and visual understanding, they fundamentally lack an intuitive grasp of physical interactions. Joint torque, as a proprioceptive signal, provides invaluable information about end-effector contact dynamics, which is often imperceptible from visual (RGB) observations alone (as vividly illustrated in Figure 1a). This oversight severely limits VLA models' applicability and robustness in real-world robotic tasks that involve physical contact.

The paper identifies two primary objectives:
1. **Identify the most effective design choices for torque-aware VLA models:** This involves systematically exploring the various ways torque signals can be integrated into existing VLA architectures. The authors delineate a "design space" based on three axes: *when* (immediate, historical, or predictive), *where* (conditioning encoder or denoising decoder), and *how* (single token vs. multi-tokens) torque signals are used.
2. **Derive generalizable principles that guide future integration of physical modalities:** Beyond specific architectural tweaks, the research aims to uncover fundamental insights that can inform the broader community on how to effectively incorporate rich physical feedback into general-purpose robot policies. This pursuit of generalizable principles ensures the findings are not merely applicable to one model but can guide the development of future VLA systems.

By addressing these objectives, the authors seek to equip VLA models with a "physically grounded internal representation of interaction dynamics," ultimately leading to more robust, reliable, and contact-sensitive robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic, empirical approach to explore the design space of torque integration, using the state-of-the-art π0 VLA model as their primary platform for experimentation.

#### 3.1. Sensing Past and Present: Torques as Observations

The initial phase focuses on integrating torque signals as an additional observation, exploring:

- **Where to Embed (Encoder vs. Decoder):** They investigate three architectural strategies for incorporating the current torque signal (τt):
 - **Encoder Embedding (Enc):** Torque is processed via an adapter (MLP) and concatenated with image and language features as an extra conditioning input to the encoder (Figure 2a).
 - **Decoder Pre-Concatenation Embedding (DePre):** Torque is directly integrated by concatenating it with the robot's joint angle state (qt), forming a single combined token for the decoder (Figure 2b).
 - **Decoder Post-Concatenation Embedding (DePost):** Torque is encoded via an adapter and prepended as a separate token to the decoder's state inputs (Figure 2c).
 - **Justification:** Empirical results (Table 1) and analysis using Normalized Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) (Figure 3) reveal that decoder-side integration (DePost) is superior. This is attributed to better proprioceptive alignment with joint angles and the decoder's higher sensitivity to fine-grained input variations (Table 2).

- **How to Leverage Torque History (Single vs. Multi-Tokens):** Recognizing that single-frame torque input is insufficient for dynamic contact events, they explore historical torque information (τt-H+1:t).
 - **Strategies:** Frame-wise tokenization (each historical frame as a separate token) vs. aggregate tokenization (entire history summarized into a single token). Both are tested for encoder and decoder integration (Figure 4).
 - **Justification:** Experiments (Table 3) show that encoding the *entire torque history into a single token in the decoder* (Figure 4c) yields the best performance. This is because multiple history tokens can disrupt the decoder's learned input patterns, which are highly sensitive (Table 4). A single, aggregated token balances informativeness with architectural stability.

#### 3.2. Anticipating Future: Torques as Objectives

Inspired by multi-task planning in autonomous driving, the authors propose an auxiliary task: predicting future torques alongside future actions.

- **Unified Action-Torque Diffusion Model:** Instead of treating torque purely as an observation, they train the VLA model to predict a joint action-torque chunk [At; Tt] using a diffusion model (Figure 5).
- **Loss Function:** A combined loss Ljoint(θ) = Laction(θ) + β Ltorque(θ) is used, sharing diffusion weights. This encourages the model to build a physically grounded latent space by learning the causal relationship between actions and their resulting torque responses.
- **Implementation:** A single linear layer outputs concatenated action and torque predictions, which are then split for their respective MSE losses. Pre-trained weights are leveraged, with newly added dimensions for torque prediction initialized with smaller values.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Cobot Magic ALOHA dual-arm robot (7-DoF per arm) equipped with three D435 depth cameras. Joint torques are estimated from motor currents using manufacturer-provided constants (sensorless approach).
- **Baselines:** Strong VLA models: ACT, RDT, and π0. All models are fine-tuned on a collected dataset of 400 demonstrations per task.
- **Tasks:** 10 diverse tasks, comprising 5 contact-rich tasks (e.g., Button Pushing, Charger Plugging) and 5 regular tasks (e.g., Bottle Pick and Place, Liquid Pouring).
- **Evaluation:** Quantitative (success rates over 20 trials) and qualitative (visualizations of task execution and torque profiles).
- **Generalization:** Cross-model evaluation (on RDT) and cross-embodiment evaluation (on ROKAE SR robotic arm) are performed to assess the robustness of the proposed methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields three key insights and demonstrates significant performance improvements across various experiments:

1. **Decoder-side Torque Integration is Superior:**
 - **Finding:** Integrating torque signals into the *decoder* consistently outperforms placing them in the encoder. Specifically, the **DePost** architecture (encoding torque via an adapter and prepending it as a separate token to the decoder's inputs) yielded the best results among the observation-only methods (Table 1).
 - **Justification:** HSIC analysis (Figure 3) confirmed that torque signals are more strongly aligned with proprioceptive joint angle signals, making decoder integration more suitable. Furthermore, the decoder was found to be more sensitive to input variations (Table 2), allowing for finer utilization of subtle torque changes crucial in contact-rich tasks.

2. **Aggregated Torque History in a Single Token is Most Effective:**
 - **Finding:** Historical torque information is crucial, but embedding the *entire history into a single token within the decoder* is more effective than using multiple tokens or integrating history into the encoder (Table 3).
 - **Justification:** While more history tokens provide richer information, they can disrupt the decoder's learned input patterns and reduce performance (Table 4). Summarizing the history into a single token (e.g., using an MLP) balances informativeness with architectural stability, preserving the decoder's original input pattern completeness.

3. **Predicting Future Torques as an Auxiliary Task Enhances Performance:**
 - **Finding:** Predicting future torques alongside actions, through a unified action-torque diffusion model, further improves manipulation performance (Figure 6).
 - **Justification:** This auxiliary objective encourages the model to develop a physically grounded internal representation by learning the causal relationship between actions and their physical consequences. The model successfully predicts future torque profiles, implying a deeper understanding of interaction dynamics.

**Quantitative Results (Table 5):**
- The proposed methods (`π0 + obs`, `π0 + obj`, and `π0 + obs + obj`) consistently outperform strong VLA baselines (ACT, RDT, π0).
- The combined approach, `π0 + obs + obj`, achieves the best overall performance, demonstrating significant gains, especially in contact-rich tasks. For instance, in "Button Pushing," success rate increased from 5/20 (π0) to 18/20 (`π0 + obs + obj`); in "Charger Plugging," from 0/20 to 17/20.
- Performance improvements were observed not only in contact-rich tasks but also in some regular tasks, suggesting broader utility of torque signals.

**Qualitative Results (Figure 7, Figure 9, Figure 10):**
- Visualizations show that with torque feedback, the robot can detect failed attempts due to misalignment or slippage in contact-rich tasks (e.g., Button Pushing, Door Handle Turning). It then autonomously retries the motion, successfully completing the task. This highlights improved robustness and task success.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
- **Cross-Model (Table 6):** The torque integration strategies generalize well to other VLA models, demonstrating significant performance improvements on RDT (e.g., Button Pushing success rate increased from 4/20 to 16/20).
- **Cross-Embodiment (Figure 8, Figure 12):** The method successfully generalized to a different robotic arm (ROKAE SR) for charger insertion tasks, adapting to different connector types without architectural modifications, showcasing robust cross-embodiment performance.

**System Efficiency (Tables 7 and 8 in Appendix):**
- The proposed enhancements do not significantly impact training or inference times, ensuring practical applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact for the field of robotics and AI:

1. **Bridging the Physical Interaction Gap for VLAs:** The most immediate impact is enabling state-of-the-art VLA models to effectively perceive and respond to physical interactions, particularly force and torque. This capability is crucial for moving robotics beyond purely visual tasks into the realm of complex, real-world manipulation where contact is inevitable.
2. **Systematic Design Principles:** The paper offers a rigorous and systematic exploration of the design space for integrating proprioceptive signals. The derived principles—decoder-side integration, single-token history summarization, and auxiliary torque prediction—provide concrete guidelines for future VLA architecture development, saving significant trial-and-error for researchers.
3. **Enhanced Robustness and Success in Contact-Rich Tasks:** By leveraging torque feedback, robots can detect and recover from failed manipulation attempts, leading to significantly higher success rates and increased robustness in tasks requiring physical contact. This is a critical step towards deploying robots in less structured and more dynamic environments.
4. **Cost-Effective and Practical Solution:** The reliance on joint torques derived from motor currents (a "sensorless" approach) eliminates the need for expensive external force/torque sensors. This significantly reduces the economic cost and complexity of robotic systems, making advanced manipulation capabilities more accessible.
5. **Improved Generalization:** The demonstrated cross-model and cross-embodiment generalization capabilities underscore the robustness of the proposed torque integration strategies. This suggests that the learned physical understanding is not task- or robot-specific, but rather a fundamental enhancement to the VLA model's perception of the world.
6. **Towards Physically Grounded AI:** By encouraging VLA models to predict physical consequences (future torques), the work pushes towards creating AI agents with a more "physically grounded" understanding of their environment and actions. This could pave the way for more intelligent and adaptable robotic behaviors, potentially leading to safer human-robot interaction and more autonomous operation in complex scenarios.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors, such as reliance on accurate torque estimation (susceptible to calibration, noise, thermal drift) and scalability to other physical modalities (tactile, temperature), also point to clear avenues for future research, further highlighting the foundational nature of this work. Overall, "TA-VLA" represents a critical advancement in endowing VLA models with a more comprehensive understanding of the physical world, moving closer to truly intelligent and capable robotic agents.
