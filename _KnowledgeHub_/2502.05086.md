# REASSEMBLE: A Multimodal Dataset for Contact-rich Robotic Assembly and Disassembly

> [!summary] Summary
> The REASSEMBLE dataset provides a comprehensive, multimodal resource for contact-rich, long-horizon robotic assembly and disassembly, utilizing the standardized NIST Assembly Task Board #1. It includes a unique combination of RGB, proprioceptive, force-torque, audio, and event camera data, along with detailed hierarchical annotations for multi-task learning and anomaly detection.

## Problem

- Existing robotic datasets primarily focus on simple, short-horizon tasks (e.g., pick-and-place) and lack the complexity of intricate physical interactions required for real-world assembly and disassembly.
- Current datasets often lack comprehensive multimodal sensor data, particularly high-quality force-torque measurements and event camera data, which are crucial for understanding contact-rich manipulation.
- There is a need for standardized, comprehensive datasets that support long-horizon tasks, task inversion learning (disassembly), multi-task learning, and anomaly detection in robotics.

## Method

- The REASSEMBLE dataset was created using the standardized NIST Assembly Task Board #1 for collecting assembly and disassembly demonstrations.
- Data was collected via haptic teleoperation of a Franka Emika FR3 robotic arm, recording synchronized multimodal sensor data including RGB cameras, robot proprioception, audio, 6-axis force-torque, and event camera streams.
- Demonstrations are comprehensively annotated with hierarchical temporal action segmentation labels, success/anomaly flags, and structured for motion policy learning and task inversion learning (TIL).

## Results

- REASSEMBLE comprises 4,551 high-level action demonstrations (781 minutes of data), including 4,035 successful and 516 failed attempts, covering 4 actions and 17 objects with balanced distribution.
- A state-of-the-art Temporal Action Segmentation (TAS) model achieved an F1@50 of 44.1% on REASSEMBLE, demonstrating the dataset's increased complexity compared to human-centric datasets.
- Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) demonstrated success rates of 70-100% across pick, insert, remove, and place tasks, with insertions being the most challenging (70% success) for the large gear assembly/disassembly.
- An execution monitoring pipeline achieved 96% accuracy, precision, and recall in anomaly detection using REASSEMBLE's annotations, showing the dataset's utility for building robust systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'Insert' action is the most challenging, accounting for the highest number of failures due to its multi-step nature and demand for precise alignment and force application.
> - Force-torque data reveals distinct patterns corresponding to different action phases (e.g., free-space movement, contact, pushing, twisting), underscoring its value for understanding contact dynamics.
> - Randomizing the NIST Assembly Task Board #1 and object poses for each trial successfully created diverse interaction points, which is essential for training generalizable robotic policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation remains a fundamental and challenging problem, especially when tasks involve intricate physical interactions, often referred to as "contact-rich manipulation." The field of robot learning has seen significant advancements, largely propelled by the availability of large-scale datasets, akin to those that revolutionized deep learning in computer vision and natural language processing. However, the authors argue that most existing datasets, while valuable for developing foundational robotic capabilities, predominantly focus on simpler, short-horizon tasks such as object rearrangement, picking, and placing. Examples include BC-Z, RT-1, BridgeDatav2, RoboSet, and DROID, which primarily support Motion Policy Learning (MPL) for basic primitives.

These prevalent datasets fall short in capturing the complexity and physical dynamics inherent in industrial assembly and disassembly processes. For instance, datasets like FurnitureBench address longer-horizon tasks, but lack the high-quality force-torque data crucial for operations requiring tight tolerances. Similarly, while RH20T includes multimodal data like audio and force-torque, it does not specifically target the long-horizon sequences typical of complex assemblies. Furthermore, many datasets for Temporal Action Segmentation (TAS), such as 50Salads, are human-centric and lack direct relevance or the necessary multimodal robot-specific data for robotic action understanding. Datasets focusing on anomaly detection, like (Im)PerfectPour, often do not target contact-rich manipulation.

REASSEMBLE positions itself as a critical bridge over these gaps. It addresses the scarcity of comprehensive datasets for contact-rich, long-horizon robotic manipulation by:
- **Focusing on challenging tasks:** Unlike simple pick-and-place, REASSEMBLE centers on assembly and disassembly using the standardized NIST Assembly Task Board #1, which involves precise peg insertions, gear meshing, and electrical connector assemblies, mimicking real-world industrial and domestic challenges.
- **Providing rich multimodal data:** Beyond common RGB camera views and robot proprioception, REASSEMBLE uniquely incorporates less common but highly valuable sensor modalities such as six-axis force-torque measurements, microphones, and, significantly, event camera data. The inclusion of event camera data is highlighted as a novel contribution for manipulation-focused datasets, offering low-latency, high-precision information about motion critical for fine-grained contact understanding.
- **Offering multi-task annotations:** The dataset supports a variety of downstream applications by providing hierarchical temporal action segmentation labels, motion policy learning data, and success/anomaly detection labels, as well as data structured for task inversion learning (TIL), which is crucial for disassembly.
- **Including failure modes:** Critically, REASSEMBLE contains both successful and unsuccessful demonstrations, which is vital for training robust systems capable of detecting and recovering from anomalies.

By offering a standardized benchmark with diverse, synchronized, and precisely annotated multimodal data for complex manipulation, REASSEMBLE directly facilitates the development of advanced learning algorithms that can generalize to intricate, real-world robotic scenarios, thereby pushing the boundaries of robot autonomy beyond highly structured industrial settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the REASSEMBLE dataset is to **bridge the significant gap in existing robot learning datasets by providing a comprehensive, multimodal resource specifically designed for long-horizon and contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks, particularly assembly and disassembly.**

The motivation for creating REASSEMBLE stems from several pressing challenges and needs in the field:

1. **Complexity of Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Many real-world robotic applications, such as industrial assembly, necessitate precise physical interaction with objects, often involving tight tolerances, significant contact forces, and dynamic changes. Existing datasets, largely focused on simpler tasks, do not adequately capture these complexities, leading to poor generalization of learned policies in real-world scenarios.
2. **Lack of Suitable Data for Long-Horizon Tasks:** Complex tasks like assembling a product or disassembling electronic waste involve long sequences of actions and intricate sub-skills. Current datasets often focus on short, primitive skills, making it difficult to train models for multi-step, sequential manipulation.
3. **Importance of Disassembly for Sustainability:** Beyond assembly, the authors highlight the critical, yet underexplored, need for robotic disassembly capabilities. Disassembly is a cornerstone of the circular economy, aligning with United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 12 to drastically improve resource efficiency and recycling rates. Automating the dismantling of complex objects (e.g., cars, electronics, EV batteries) can reduce manual labor and environmental impact. The concept of "task inversion learning" (TIL), where disassembly policies can be learned from assembly demonstrations, further emphasizes this need.
4. **Need for Multimodal Data for Holistic Understanding:** Relying solely on visual data is insufficient for contact-rich tasks where subtle physical interactions are key. Force-torque measurements provide crucial contact information, while audio and event camera data can offer insights into dynamic events (e.g., slip, impact, precise movements). A comprehensive dataset needs to integrate these diverse modalities for robust and adaptive learning.
5. **Facilitating Multi-Task Learning and Robustness:** To build truly autonomous and adaptable robots, it's essential to not only learn manipulation policies but also to understand task conditions, segment actions, and detect/recover from failures. The motivation includes providing annotations that support hierarchical temporal action segmentation, motion policy learning, and anomaly detection, thus fostering research into more robust and versatile robotic systems.
6. **Standardized Benchmarking:** By leveraging the well-established NIST Assembly Task Board #1 benchmark, REASSEMBLE aims to provide a standardized platform for reproducible research and comparison across different research institutions, facilitating the deployment and evaluation of learned algorithms.

In essence, REASSEMBLE is motivated by the ambition to move robot learning beyond simple pick-and-place into the realm of dexterous, intelligent, and adaptable manipulation required for complex real-world assembly and disassembly challenges, contributing both to scientific advancement and sustainable practices.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The REASSEMBLE dataset was meticulously designed and collected to address the challenges of contact-rich, long-horizon manipulation.

#### 3.1. Task Design and Objects

Instead of custom tasks, the authors leveraged the **NIST Assembly Task Board #1**, a standardized benchmark recognized for its realistic manipulation challenges. This board includes tasks such as peg and connector insertions, gear meshing, and fastening nuts. Minor physical modifications were made to the board (e.g., PVC base, redesigned Ethernet/USB holders, 3D-printed custom object holders) to enhance compatibility with the robotic setup and simplify certain picking tasks for a top-down manipulator, while maintaining original tolerances.

The dataset covers both assembly and disassembly processes, encompassing four fundamental actions: **pick, insert, remove, and place**. These were chosen as the minimal set to comprehensively describe the tasks while managing the cognitive load for human annotators. The NIST board features 20 objects, but 3 nuts were excluded due to robot/teleoperation constraints, resulting in **17 objects** being used across these four actions.

#### 3.2. Sensor Modalities

A comprehensive array of sensors was utilized to capture diverse sensory information, ensuring a rich multimodal dataset:
- **Multi-view RGB Cameras:** Two external HAMA C-600 Pro webcams and one wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D435i camera capture visual data from various perspectives.
- **Robot Proprioception:** Standard robot data, including joint positions, velocities, efforts, end-effector position and orientation, and gripper width, were recorded.
- **Audio:** Three microphones were used: two mounted on external cameras and one wireless microphone attached to the robot's gripper, to capture sounds related to contact and interaction.
- **Force-Torque (FT) Sensor:** A wrist-mounted 6-axis AIDIN ROBOTICS AFT200-D80-C FT sensor was used to measure interaction forces and torques. A calibration procedure was performed to ensure accurate readings in the robot's base frame.
- **Event Camera:** A crucial distinguishing feature, an external DAVIS event camera was included. Unlike traditional cameras, event cameras asynchronously record pixel intensity changes, providing high temporal resolution and low latency data that is particularly useful for detecting fine-grained motion events and contact, like a peg dislodging from a socket.
- **Motion Capture System:** Used for precise initial estimation of camera and board poses relative to the robot's base frame. Continuous tracking was avoided due to interference with the event camera.

#### 3.3. Teleoperation and Data Collection

Data was collected through **haptic teleoperation** using an omega.6 haptic device (master) controlling a Franka Emika FR3 robotic arm (remote). Haptic feedback was deemed essential for demonstrating contact-rich tasks, allowing operators to perceive interaction forces and adjust motions, preventing damage or misalignments. The teleoperation system employed a bidirectional control scheme, relaying force feedback from the robot to the master device. A Cartesian impedance controller governed robot motion, complemented by nullspace optimization to avoid kinematic singularities and joint limits, ensuring smooth and intuitive operation.

During data collection:
- **Randomization:** The position and orientation of the board and objects were randomized for each trial to ensure high diversity in interaction points across demonstrations.
- **Annotation:** Operators simultaneously performed actions by teleoperating the robot and provided **verbal narration** of their activity, marking temporal boundaries of task segments by key presses.
- **Post-processing and Validation:** Raw sensor recordings were saved as ROS bags and then post-processed into HDF5 files. Video and audio were encoded (MP4/MP3) to optimize memory. Importantly, data was saved with native timestamps, allowing for flexible synchronization and downsampling by users for specific downstream tasks. The verbal narrations were transcribed using Whisper ASR and then manually validated and corrected for accuracy.
- **Hierarchical Annotations:** Beyond high-level actions, **manual skill annotations** were added after recording. Nine common skills (Grasp, Lift, Approach, Align, Release, Twist, Push, Pull, Nudge) were identified, providing a hierarchical structure (actions composed of skills). The dataset includes both successful and unsuccessful demonstrations (4,035 successful, 516 failed), crucial for learning anomaly detection.

This meticulous data collection and annotation process ensures that REASSEMBLE is a comprehensive, high-quality multimodal dataset suitable for a wide range of robot learning research in complex manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The REASSEMBLE paper presents extensive statistics about the collected dataset and benchmark results for three key robot learning tasks, demonstrating the dataset's utility and the challenges it poses.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Characteristics

- **Demonstration Count and Duration:** The dataset comprises **4,551 high-level action demonstrations**, which decompose into **10,195 low-level skills**, spanning a total of **781 minutes** of recorded data. Out of the high-level demonstrations, 4,035 were successful, and 516 were failures.
- **Action and Object Distribution:** REASSEMBLE features **4 actions** (pick, insert, remove, place) and **17 objects**, resulting in **68 unique action-object pairs**. The distribution of demonstrations per pair is remarkably balanced, ranging from a minimum of 55 ("Remove square peg 2") to a maximum of 86 ("Pick USB" and "Insert BNC"), ensuring no significant bias towards particular actions or objects.
- **Skill Composition:** On average, each high-level action consists of 2.2 skills. The "Pick" action typically involves three skills, while the "Insert" action, being the most complex, shows the highest variability, sometimes requiring up to ten steps (e.g., for BNC connector or nut due to alignment and twisting). Uncommon skill-action combinations are often observed during failure recovery.
- **Action Difficulty and Failure Modes:** The "Insert" action is identified as the most difficult, exhibiting the highest number of failures. This is attributed to its multi-step nature, requiring precise approach, search for alignment, and force application. Inserting the BNC and Nut 4 are particularly challenging due to rotational requirements. "Pick" failures often result from missed grasps or objects slipping, while "Remove" failures stem from improper gripper alignment leading to jamming. "Place" actions have the fewest failures, primarily due to objects slipping prematurely onto the task board instead of the designated holder.
- **Interaction Point Diversity:** By randomizing the board and object poses for each trial, the dataset achieves high diversity in the robot's end-effector interaction points. This spatial variance is crucial for improving the generalization capabilities of learned policies.
- **Force and Torque Patterns:** Analysis of force and torque profiles reveals distinct patterns correlated with different action phases. For instance, the "Insert waterproof" action clearly shows free-space movement, initial contact/alignment, and a final pushing phase in the Z-component force. The "Place round peg 4" action distinctly shows the object's weight being released as force drops to near zero. The "Remove small gear" action displays periodic Z-axis torque changes, corresponding to the operator twisting the gear to loosen it. These patterns highlight the value of force-torque data for understanding and learning contact dynamics.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Results

The authors provide initial benchmark results for three critical robot learning tasks:

- **Temporal Action Segmentation (TAS):**
 - **Approach:** Evaluated DiffAct, a state-of-the-art visual TAS model based on diffusion processes, using only wrist camera RGB and optical flow features.
 - **Performance:** Achieved Accuracy: 61.5%, EDIT: 47.8%, F1@10: 63.3%, F1@25: 58.4%, F1@50: 44.1%.
 - **Analysis:** These results are significantly lower than DiffAct's performance on the 50Salads dataset (e.g., F1@50 of 83.7%). The authors attribute this to REASSEMBLE's increased complexity: a much higher number of unique actions (69 vs. 19), less visually distinguishable objects (e.g., similar pegs and gears), and a higher median number of actions per video. DiffAct often missed very short actions. This benchmark highlights the challenging nature of REASSEMBLE for TAS and the potential for research into multimodal fusion (preliminary results with multimodal fusion showed improvement) and common-sense reasoning for action segmentation.

- **Motion Policy Learning (MPL):**
 - **Approach:** Initial experimentation with language- and goal-conditioned diffusion models for MPL yielded a goal-point error of ~1.3 cm, which is too high for high-precision assembly. For more robust benchmarking, Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) were adopted, integrating with an RGB-D object state estimation pipeline (YOLO for detection, PCA for orientation). DMPs learn motion trajectories that can adapt to varying start and goal points, executed using an impedance controller. A spiral search was incorporated for insertion tasks, leveraging learned force thresholds.
 - **Performance:** Evaluated on large gear assembly and disassembly over 10 trials per action:
 - **Pick:** 8/10 successful (failures due to slipping).
 - **Insert:** 7/10 successful (lowest success, failures when spiral search completed without full insertion).
 - **Remove:** 8/10 successful (failures when gripper grasped the holding plate instead of the gear).
 - **Place:** 10/10 successful.
 - **Analysis:** DMPs demonstrated adaptability for complex tasks. The results reflect the inherent difficulty of precise insertion tasks, even for human teleoperators. Failures suggest areas for improvement in perception (grasping wrong part) and control (tracking errors).

- **Task Execution and Monitoring (Anomaly Detection):**
 - **Approach:** A ConditionNet-like execution monitoring pipeline was trained to detect anomalies by identifying precondition and effect states of actions. It used observations from the wrist-mounted camera and was trained on defined ranges within the ground truth action segmentation. Behavior trees were used to express robot reactions to anomalies.
 - **Performance:** Achieved an Accuracy, Precision, and Recall of **96%**.
 - **Analysis:** The results demonstrate that REASSEMBLE's annotations, particularly the success/failure flags and temporal segments, can effectively train models for real-time anomaly detection. The system could differentiate between states like "precondition satisfied" (gripper empty), "effects met" (object grasped), and "unsatisfied" (object dropped), showcasing its potential for building robust and reactive robotic systems.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

#### 5.1. Significance and Impact

The REASSEMBLE dataset represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning and manipulation, with far-reaching potential impacts:

- **Advancing Contact-Rich Manipulation:** By providing a unique, comprehensive dataset for contact-rich, high-precision assembly and disassembly tasks, REASSEMBLE directly addresses a critical gap in current robot learning research. It enables the development of algorithms that can handle complex physical interactions, which are ubiquitous in real-world scenarios beyond idealized lab settings.
- **Enabling Robust and Generalizable Robot Learning:** The inclusion of diverse multimodal data (RGB, proprioception, F/T, audio, event cameras) allows for more holistic understanding of robot-environment interactions. This richness is expected to foster research into more robust and generalizable policies, reducing reliance on single modalities and improving performance in dynamic, uncertain environments. The event camera data, in particular, is a novel addition that can be transformative for tasks requiring high temporal precision and motion detection.
- **Facilitating Multi-Task and Hierarchical Learning:** The multi-task annotations (TAS, MPL, AD, TIL) provide a versatile foundation for various robot learning paradigms. Hierarchical action segmentation, for instance, can simplify learning complex, long-horizon tasks by breaking them down into manageable sub-skills, promoting generalization and skill reuse.
- **Driving Research in Task Inversion and Sustainability:** By supporting both assembly and disassembly, REASSEMBLE is uniquely positioned to accelerate research in task inversion learning. This directly contributes to the development of robots capable of efficiently disassembling products, which is crucial for recycling, resource conservation, and advancing the circular economy. This aspect has significant societal and environmental implications.
- **Standardized Benchmarking:** Leveraging the well-established NIST Task Board #1 provides a standardized benchmark, allowing researchers to compare algorithms across institutions on a common, realistic set of tasks, fostering collaboration and progress.
- **Boosting Anomaly Detection and Recovery:** The inclusion of failed demonstrations and comprehensive state annotations is invaluable for training models that can effectively monitor task execution, detect anomalies, and potentially trigger recovery strategies, enhancing the reliability and autonomy of robotic systems.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its significant contributions, REASSEMBLE, like any dataset, has certain limitations that also point towards future research directions:

- **Object Visual Similarity vs. Proprioceptive Distinctiveness:** The NIST Task Board #1 features geometrically similar objects (e.g., round/square pegs, gears) that can be visually challenging to distinguish but differ proprioceptively. This suggests that future research on REASSEMBLE may need to prioritize the fusion and intelligent use of proprioceptive data over purely visual cues for object identification in some tasks.
- **Lack of Depth Information:** The dataset intentionally excludes depth data to keep its size manageable (avoiding a 12x increase). While current approaches are exploring RGB-only manipulation, depth information provides crucial 3D spatial context for accurate perception, distance estimation, and precise trajectory planning. This remains a limitation for methods that rely heavily on 3D geometry. Future work could explore incorporating sparse depth information or developing efficient representations to mitigate this.
- **Scope of NIST Boards:** Currently, the dataset focuses solely on NIST Task Board #1. This limits the diversity of manipulation challenges, particularly those involving deformable objects or highly visually distinct items.
- **Future Expansion:** The authors explicitly state plans to expand the dataset to include data from other NIST boards, such as Task Board #4, which features objects like cables. These objects are more similar proprioceptively but visually distinct, offering a complementary challenge and broader coverage of real-world manipulation scenarios. This expansion would further enhance the dataset's utility for diverse robotic tasks.
- **Advanced Control and Policy Learning:** While initial benchmarks demonstrate the dataset's use, the performance on high-precision tasks like insertion highlights that current learning methods (e.g., diffusion models) still struggle with the fine-grained control required. This indicates a need for continued research into more precise motion policy learning algorithms tailored for contact-rich interactions.

In conclusion, REASSEMBLE is a meticulously crafted, multimodal dataset poised to significantly advance robot learning for complex, real-world assembly and disassembly tasks. Its unique features, especially the inclusion of event camera data and comprehensive multi-task annotations, open new avenues for research in robust, intelligent, and sustainable robotic manipulation, while its identified limitations provide clear directions for future development within the robotics community.
