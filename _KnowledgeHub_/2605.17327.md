# Efficient Feature-Free Initialization for Monocular Visual-Inertial Systems Using a Feed-Forward 3D Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence developed an initialization method for monocular visual-inertial systems that replaces traditional visual feature tracking with up-to-scale 3D point clouds from feed-forward models. This approach reduces the required initialization data duration to approximately 1.2 seconds, achieving high success rates and robust performance in challenging visual environments.

## Problem

- Traditional monocular VINS initialization methods rely on visual features, making them fragile in challenging visual conditions (e.g., textureless, low-parallax, motion blur) and susceptible to failure.
- Existing initialization processes typically require 3-4 seconds of sensory data, limiting VINS applicability in time-sensitive scenarios.
- Feed-forward 3D models can predict scene-level 3D point clouds from images but inherently suffer from metric scale ambiguity, making them unsuitable for direct VINS integration.

## Method

- The method integrates pre-trained ==feed-forward 3D foundation models== (e.g., VGGT) to directly infer up-to-scale 3D point clouds, bypassing the need for explicit visual feature extraction and tracking.
- A two-stage pipeline is employed: first, a robust ==closed-form linear system== estimates initial velocity, gravity direction, and a global metric scale from the predicted point clouds.
- This is followed by a ==nonlinear visual-inertial bundle adjustment==, available in **feature-free** (FF) and scale-constrained (SC) variants, to refine the initial states and recover the true metric scale.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a significantly reduced average initialization data duration of **~1.2 seconds** (vs. **3-4 seconds** for baselines), with an **81.2%** success rate on EuRoC and **94.7%** on TUM-VI datasets for the FF variant.
- It demonstrated improved initial state estimation, including a lower average gravity error of **3.54 degrees** on EuRoC, and produced more stable subsequent VINS trajectories with lower absolute trajectory errors.
- In a self-collected dataset covering challenging indoor-degraded scenarios, the FF variant achieved a **90%** success rate, outperforming baselines (**40-50%**) in environments with sparse features or repetitive textures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging feed-forward 3D models enables 'feature-free' VINS initialization, significantly enhancing robustness in visually degraded environments where traditional methods struggle.
> - A compact, linear system formulation effectively resolves the metric scale ambiguity inherent in 3D foundation models by integrating their up-to-scale geometry with metric IMU measurements.
> - The model-agnostic nature of the framework allows it to benefit directly from advancements in underlying 3D foundation models, ensuring continuous performance improvements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Monocular Visual-Inertial Navigation Systems (VINS), which integrate a single camera with an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU), are widely employed for robust and accurate pose estimation across various applications, including augmented/virtual reality and robotics. A fundamental requirement for VINS operation is a reliable initialization process. This process aims to determine initial linear velocity, gravity direction, and the metric scale, which is unobservable in visual-only systems. Inaccurate initialization can lead to significant estimation errors or system divergence.

Traditional monocular VINS initialization methods typically rely on visual feature tracking and the accurate initialization of 3D landmarks from 2D feature observations. These approaches establish geometric constraints between camera frames, but they are susceptible to failure or degraded performance in challenging visual conditions such as textureless environments, rapid camera motion, low-parallax trajectories, motion blur, poor illumination, or sensor noise. These limitations often result in prolonged initialization times, restricting VINS applicability in time-sensitive scenarios.

Recent advancements in deep learning have introduced feed-forward 3D models capable of directly predicting scene-level 3D point clouds from images, such as DUSt3R, MASt3R, and VGGT. These models demonstrate strong spatial understanding but often produce geometry only up to an unknown similarity transformation, presenting an inherent scale ambiguity. While some learning-aided VINS approaches leverage learned single-view depth for initialization, they often implicitly or explicitly retain reliance on feature correspondences.

This research contributes to the intersection of VINS and learning-based 3D geometry. It differentiates itself by proposing the first approach to integrate feed-forward 3D foundation models directly into monocular VINS initialization. By bypassing the traditional visual feature extraction and tracking process, the work aims to address the inherent fragilities of feature-based methods while simultaneously resolving the metric scale ambiguity common in feed-forward 3D models. It positions itself as an augmentation of traditional pipelines with learned priors, aiming to improve robustness and efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a fast and reliable initialization framework for monocular VINS. The authors identify several critical motivations for this work:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Feature-Based Initialization:** A significant motivation stems from the inherent fragility of existing VINS initialization methods, which predominantly rely on visual feature correspondences. These methods are susceptible to failure or reduced accuracy under challenging conditions such as environments with limited texture, rapid camera motion, low parallax, motion blur, or poor illumination. The goal is to overcome these limitations.
2. **Reducing Initialization Time:** Most current methods typically require 3-4 seconds of sensory data for successful initialization. This prolonged duration limits their practical applicability in real-world scenarios like on-device augmented reality, agile robotic deployment, or emergency navigation, where quick system readiness is crucial. The aim is to substantially reduce this data duration.
3. **Leveraging Advances in Feed-Forward 3D Models:** The advent of feed-forward 3D models that can directly predict point clouds from images presents an opportunity to re-evaluate the VINS initialization problem. These models offer direct 3D geometry estimation, potentially obviating the need for complex and error-prone visual feature tracking. The motivation is to explore and harness this capability.
4. **Simplifying System Complexity and Improving Reliability:** By leveraging predicted point clouds, the authors aim to propose a "feature-free" initialization framework. This design is intended to reduce the overall system complexity by eliminating a common failure point (feature tracking) and inherently improve the reliability of the initialization process, especially in visually degraded environments.
5. **Recovering Metric Scale:** While feed-forward 3D models can predict up-to-scale point clouds, they often suffer from scale ambiguity. A key objective is to effectively recover the metric scale of the underlying 3D scene by integrating these scale-ambiguous 3D predictions with metric-scale IMU measurements.
6. **Developing Robust and Efficient Optimization Formulations:** The work aims to derive a compact, feature-free closed-form linear system for an initial guess, followed by a robust nonlinear visual-inertial bundle adjustment. This involves formulating both a purely feature-free variant for maximum efficiency and a scale-constrained variant for potentially higher accuracy, thereby offering flexible solutions based on application needs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed "Efficient Feature-Free Initialization for Monocular Visual-Inertial Systems," introduces a novel two-stage pipeline: a closed-form linear system for initial state estimation, followed by a nonlinear visual-inertial bundle adjustment (VI-BA) for refinement. The core innovation lies in replacing traditional visual feature correspondences with up-to-scale point clouds generated by a feed-forward 3D model.

**A. Feed-Forward 3D Models:**
The framework incorporates pre-trained feed-forward 3D foundation models (e.g., VGGT, DAv3, π3) to infer point clouds. Given a sequence of N input images, the model outputs up-to-scale 3D positions (`C0_p_o_i,k`) and associated confidence scores (`c_i,k`) for each pixel. A critical aspect is that these point clouds are expressed in a consistent reference camera frame (`C0`), which allows for direct geometric constraint formulation without requiring explicit inter-frame feature tracking. To manage computational load, low-confidence predictions are filtered, and a fixed number (K) of high-confidence points are sampled from uniform patches within each frame.

**B. Robust Feature-Free Closed-Form Solution (Linear System):**
Unlike traditional methods that estimate numerous 3D feature positions, this approach represents all 3D points in the scene using a single, unknown global scale parameter (`s`). The 3D position of a point (`I0_p_o_i,k`) in the initial IMU frame (`I0`) is expressed as a scaled version of the predicted `C0`-frame point (`C0_p_o_i,k`) transformed into `I0` coordinates.
This formulation significantly reduces the "feature-related" state vector to a single scalar (`s`). The full state vector (`x`) for the linear system is compact, comprising only the scale, initial linear velocity (`I0_v_I0`), and initial gravity vector (`I0_g`), resulting in a 7-dimensional state `[s, I0_v_I0_transpose, I0_g_transpose]_transpose`.
By substituting this scaled point representation into the standard visual-inertial constraint equations and stacking measurements from multiple frames and sampled points, a linear system `A_bar * x = b_bar` is formed. This system is solved using weighted constrained least-squares, where the weights are derived from the predicted confidence scores (`c_i,k`). RANSAC is integrated into this solving process to identify and reject outliers in the predicted point cloud, enhancing robustness. Observability analysis in the supplementary material indicates that a minimum of three image frames is required for the system to achieve full column rank and be solvable.

**C. Nonlinear Refinement (Visual-Inertial Bundle Adjustment):**
Following the linear system, the initial estimates are refined using a nonlinear optimization. Two variants of VI-BA are proposed, both leveraging the point cloud data, specifically the inlier set determined by RANSAC:

1. **Scale-Constrained (SC) Formulation:** This approach augments a traditional feature-based BA by introducing additional scale parameters (`s`) into the optimization state. It retains the standard feature correspondences but integrates a "scale residual" that connects the 3D feature positions to the shared scale parameter and the `C0`-frame predicted points. This formulation aims to leverage the geometric constraints from predicted point clouds while preserving the potential accuracy benefits of explicit feature correspondences.

2. **Feature-Free (FF) Formulation:** This variant completely eliminates explicit 3D feature states from the optimization. The state vector comprises IMU states (orientation, velocity, position, and biases) and the scale factor(s). The reprojection residual is directly parameterized by the scale factor `s` and the `C0`-frame predicted points (`C0_p_o_i,k`), without requiring data association across views (i.e., `z_C_i,k` can be any pixel coordinate of a sampled point). This design significantly reduces the state dimension (from O(3M) for M features to O(1) for scale variables), leading to a more compact optimization problem. To prevent numerical instability where the scale factor might collapse towards zero, the scale `s` is reparameterized using a softplus-based function (`s = ε + log(exp(s_tilde) + 1)`). Furthermore, to account for potential variations in prediction accuracy across the scene, the point cloud is partitioned into a 2D grid, and independent scale parameters are assigned to each region, connected by smoothness constraints.

The entire methodology aims to improve efficiency and robustness by decoupling VINS initialization from the challenges of visual feature tracking and providing a robust mechanism to recover the metric scale from up-to-scale 3D model predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments on public datasets (EuRoC MAV, TUM-VI) and a self-collected dataset to evaluate the proposed feature-free initialization method. Performance was assessed using gravity direction error, linear velocity error, absolute trajectory error (ATE) for both initialization window (Win. ATE) and subsequent VINS tracking (Traj. ATE), total data duration for successful initialization (T_tot), and success rate (SR).

**A. Evaluation on EuRoC Dataset (5 keyframes, 0.5 s window):**
- **Linear System Performance:** The proposed feature-free linear system demonstrated improved performance in estimating gravity direction and linear velocity compared to Dong-Si and Dep.(AB) baselines, achieving the lowest average gravity error (3.54 degrees). The inclusion of RANSAC provided consistent, albeit modest, improvements.
- **Overall Performance:** The **Ours (FF)** variant achieved the highest success rate (81.2%), requiring an average of only 1.19 seconds of data (`T_tot`) for successful initialization, which is substantially less than baselines (e.g., Dong-Si: 2.48 s, Dep.(Const.): 3.30 s). It also yielded the lowest subsequent VINS tracking error (`Traj. ATE`), indicating robust and stable initialization quality. The **Ours (SC)** variant achieved the best `Win. ATE` (short-window accuracy) but had a slightly lower success rate (68.8%) than Ours (FF).

**B. Evaluation on TUM-VI Dataset (5 keyframes, 0.5 s window):**
- **Linear System Performance:** Under the richer motion excitation of TUM-VI, the proposed linear system showed a more pronounced advantage, achieving approximately a 25% reduction in gravity direction error compared to Dep.(AB).
- **Overall Performance:** Both **Ours (FF)** and **Ours (SC)** variants achieved high success rates (94.7% and 92.0% respectively) with a short average `T_tot` of approximately 1.1 seconds. Ours (SC) maintained a lower `Win. ATE` (1.04/0.023) compared to Ours (FF) (3.22/0.067).
- **Failure Analysis:** An analysis of failure sources indicated that baselines frequently failed due to insufficient observations or rank-deficient linear systems. Learning-based baselines (Dep.(AB) and Dep.(Const.)) also showed issues with covariance recovery and nonlinear optimization. In contrast, the proposed methods significantly reduced failures across all categories, contributing to their lower `T_tot`.
- **Runtime:** Per-attempt runtime for learning-based methods (including ours) was dominated by 3D model inference (~0.24s), making them slower than Dong-Si (0.100s). However, due to significantly fewer attempts needed for successful initialization, the proposed methods achieved lower overall computational time.

**C. Ablation Study (TUM-VI):**
- **Number of Sampled Points (K):** Increasing K initially improved accuracy, but too many points (K=1000) led to longer `T_tot` and reduced `SR`. K=100 was chosen for the optimal balance, achieving the highest `SR`.
- **RANSAC:** Its inclusion provided a modest improvement in both `SR` and accuracy.
- **Global vs. Regional Scale:** Using a single global scale factor yielded comparable performance to region-partitioned scales, suggesting consistent scale predictions from VGGT.
- **Scale-Constrained (SC) vs. Feature-Free (FF):** The SC formulation achieved a significantly lower scale error after refinement (8.80% vs. 25.72% for FF) and better `Win. ATE`. Both maintained high success rates.
- **Window Size:** Increasing the initialization window length consistently improved accuracy, reduced errors (gravity, velocity, scale), and increased success rates for both FF and SC variants, with Ours (FF) achieving 100% `SR` for windows >= 1.5s.
- **Feed-Forward 3D Model:** Replacing VGGT with alternative models like DAv3 or π3 (specifically π3X) consistently resulted in lower errors, confirming the method's model-agnostic nature and its ability to benefit from improved underlying 3D geometry predictions. π3 yielded the best overall performance in linear system evaluation.

**D. Evaluation on Self-Collected Dataset:**
- **Generalization and Robustness:** On a self-collected dataset covering indoor-structured, indoor-degraded, and outdoor scenarios, **Ours (FF)** achieved a 90% success rate, outperforming all baselines (40-50%).
- **Challenging Scenarios:** In visually degraded indoor environments (sparse features, repetitive textures), Ours (FF) reliably succeeded, whereas most baselines failed or produced divergent trajectories.
- **Limitations:** In extremely degraded environments (e.g., blank white walls) or open-sky outdoor scenes with ambiguous visual cues, the 3D model's predictions could be unreliable, leading to initialization failures for all learning-based methods, including the proposed one.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in monocular visual-inertial navigation systems, offering a paradigm shift from traditional feature-dependent initialization. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The primary impact lies in overcoming the fundamental fragility of feature-based VINS initialization in visually degraded scenarios. By eliminating the reliance on visual feature tracking, the proposed method demonstrates robust performance in textureless environments, low-parallax conditions, or scenes with motion blur, where traditional methods often fail. This capability expands the operational envelope for VINS.
2. **Significant Improvement in Efficiency:** The method substantially reduces the data duration required for successful initialization, typically to under 1.2 seconds, compared to the 3-4 seconds often needed by existing approaches. This efficiency is critical for real-time applications such such as augmented reality, virtual reality, agile robotics, and emergency response, where rapid system readiness is paramount.
3. **Simplified System Design and Numerical Stability:** The feature-free formulation simplifies the VINS initialization pipeline by removing complex feature extraction and association modules. The compact state space, particularly in the feature-free (FF) nonlinear refinement, leads to better-conditioned bundle adjustment problems, enhancing numerical stability and convergence properties.
4. **Leveraging Advanced 3D Foundation Models:** This work establishes a novel integration of feed-forward 3D foundation models into VINS initialization. Its model-agnostic design ensures that future improvements in 3D geometry prediction capabilities of these foundation models can directly translate into enhanced initialization performance, allowing the framework to evolve with advancements in deep learning.
5. **Reliable Metric Scale Recovery:** The framework successfully addresses the inherent scale ambiguity of feed-forward 3D models by fusing their up-to-scale geometric predictions with metric-scale inertial measurements. The achieved low scale error indicates that the metric scale of the scene can be reliably recovered, which is crucial for practical VINS operation.
6. **Broader Applicability and Deployment:** By offering a more robust and faster initialization, the research contributes to making monocular VINS a more practical and deployable technology across a wider range of real-world scenarios, particularly those that are challenging for conventional visual algorithms.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the feature-free formulation potentially yielding slightly lower pose accuracy compared to its scale-constrained counterpart. A more fundamental limitation remains the generalization of current feed-forward 3D models in scenes with sparse or ambiguous visual cues (e.g., open-sky scenarios), where the predicted geometry can become unreliable. Future work aims to address these issues by further refining the optimization for higher accuracy and exploring fully differentiable, feed-forward VINS architectures for even greater robustness.
