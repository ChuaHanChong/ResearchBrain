# Visual Instruction Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a method for instruction tuning large multimodal models, addressing the scarcity of high-quality vision-language instruction data by using GPT-4 to generate a comprehensive dataset. The resulting LLaVA model demonstrates strong instruction-following capabilities in multimodal chat and achieves competitive performance on benchmarks like ScienceQA, setting a new state-of-the-art with a GPT-4 ensembling strategy.

## Problem

- Existing large multimodal models (LMMs) lacked explicit instruction tuning, limiting their performance in multimodal tasks compared to language-only ones and resulting in fixed interfaces.
- Developing general-purpose visual assistants was hindered by the scarcity of high-quality, diverse vision-language instruction-following data.
- Prior vision-language models often operated with implicitly defined tasks, making them less adaptable to diverse user commands beyond basic image description or recognition.

## Method

- A novel pipeline was developed to automatically generate 158K vision-language ==instruction-following== samples (conversation, detailed description, complex reasoning) by leveraging GPT-4 as a 'strong teacher' with symbolic image representations.
- An end-to-end trained **LLaVA** (Large Language and Vision Assistant) model was introduced, connecting a pre-trained ==CLIP vision encoder== to a ==Vicuna LLM== via a lightweight ==linear projection layer==.
- A ==two-stage training procedure== was employed: first, ==pre-training the projection layer for feature alignment== on image-text pairs (e.g., CC3M), then ==fine-tuning== the projection layer and LLM end-to-end on the GPT-generated instruction data.

## Results

- LLaVA demonstrated strong multimodal chatbot capabilities, exhibiting reasoning abilities comparable to multimodal GPT-4 on out-of-domain images and consistently following user instructions.
- On the LLaVA-Bench evaluation, LLaVA significantly outperformed OpenFlamingo (**+48%**) and BLIP-2 (**+29%**) in instruction-following, achieving an **81.7%** relative score on complex reasoning questions.
- LLaVA achieved **90.92%** accuracy on the ScienceQA multimodal QA dataset, and a novel 'GPT-4 as the judge' ensembling strategy further improved performance to a new state-of-the-art accuracy of **92.53%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instruction tuning, a paradigm successful in text-only LLMs, can be effectively extended to multimodal models, drastically improving their ability to follow diverse human instructions.
> - Large language models like GPT-4 can be leveraged to automatically generate high-quality, large-scale vision-language instruction-following data, overcoming a major data scarcity challenge.
> - A simple two-stage training approach, involving initial visual feature alignment and subsequent end-to-end fine-tuning, is effective for integrating pre-trained vision encoders with LLMs while preserving their capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence is rapidly advancing towards building general-purpose assistants capable of understanding and interacting with the world through multiple modalities, particularly vision and language. This paper critically addresses a key challenge in this pursuit: the effective integration of visual and linguistic understanding into models that can follow human instructions.

#### 1.1. State of Vision-Language Models

Prior to this work, significant progress had been made in developing language-augmented foundation vision models, such as CLIP and BLIP, which excel in open-world visual understanding tasks like classification, object detection, segmentation, and image captioning. These models typically utilize language to describe image content or to guide visual tasks. However, these models often operate with implicitly defined tasks, meaning that the user's intent or instructions are hard-coded into the model's design or inferencing pipeline. This results in systems with fixed interfaces and limited adaptability to diverse user commands, as language primarily serves as a descriptor rather than a universal interface for task specification. Furthermore, while there are existing multimodal instruction-following agents, they tend to fall into two categories: highly specialized end-to-end models for specific tasks (e.g., vision-language navigation, image editing) or systems that orchestrate multiple distinct models via large language models (e.g., Visual ChatGPT). While effective for their specific niches, these approaches lack the unified, general-purpose instruction-following capability desired for a truly versatile assistant.

#### 1.2. Rise of Instruction-Tuned Large Language Models

Concurrently, the natural language processing (NLP) community witnessed a paradigm shift with the advent of instruction tuning for large language models. Models like ChatGPT and GPT-4 demonstrated unprecedented ability to follow diverse human instructions and exhibit strong zero-shot generalization to new tasks, primarily by fine-tuning on vast amounts of machine-generated instruction-following data. This success spurred the development of open-source counterparts, such as LLaMA, Alpaca, and Vicuna, which leveraged similar instruction-tuning techniques to achieve remarkable performance comparable to proprietary LLMs. However, a critical limitation of this highly successful line of work was its text-only nature, restricting it from interacting directly with visual inputs.

#### 1.3. The Gap Addressed by This Work

The "Visual Instruction Tuning" paper identifies and aims to bridge a crucial gap: the extension of the highly effective instruction-tuning paradigm from the text-only domain to the language-image multimodal space. While existing large multimodal models (LMMs) like Flamingo and BLIP-2 have shown promising task transfer generalization by training on image-text pairs, they "are not explicitly tuned with vision-language instruction data, and their performance in multimodal tasks usually falls short compared to language-only tasks." This work seeks to directly address this deficiency by systematically exploring and demonstrating the effectiveness of instruction-tuning LMMs with explicitly formatted visual-language instructional data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the development of a general-purpose visual assistant that can seamlessly integrate vision and language to follow human instructions across a wide array of tasks. The authors are motivated by the transformative success of instruction tuning in text-based LLMs and aim to replicate this success in the multimodal domain.

Specifically, the paper outlines several key contributions and motivations:

1. **Bridging the Modality Gap with Instruction Tuning:** The primary motivation is to extend the powerful concept of instruction tuning, which has revolutionized text-based AI, to the multimodal vision-and-language space. This is seen as a crucial step towards building truly general-purpose visual assistants that can interpret and act upon human intent in a visually rich world.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity:** A significant hurdle in multimodal instruction following is the lack of high-quality, diverse vision-language instruction-following data. The authors' objective is to overcome this by developing an innovative and scalable method for automatically generating such data, leveraging the advanced capabilities of language-only LLMs like GPT-4.
3. **Developing a Unified Multimodal Model:** The paper aims to introduce LLaVA (Large Language and Vision Assistant), an end-to-end trained large multimodal model. This involves effectively connecting a pre-trained vision encoder with a pre-trained LLM and fine-tuning them on the newly generated instruction-following data. The goal is to demonstrate the empirical effectiveness of this approach.
4. **Establishing Evaluation Benchmarks:** To facilitate rigorous and standardized research in visual instruction following, a key objective is to construct new, diverse, and challenging evaluation benchmarks (LLaVA-Bench). These benchmarks are designed to assess a model's ability to handle application-oriented visual and language tasks.
5. **Promoting Open Science:** A strong motivation for the authors is to contribute to the open-source community. They explicitly state their commitment to making the GPT-4 generated visual instruction tuning data, their LLaVA model, and the associated code publicly available. This open-source release is intended to accelerate future research and foster collaborative development in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "Visual Instruction Tuning" is innovative, focusing on a data-centric approach and a two-stage training strategy for their Large Language and Vision Assistant (LLaVA) model.

#### 3.1. GPT-Assisted Visual Instruction Data Generation

The core challenge addressed is the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale multimodal instruction-following data. The authors propose a novel pipeline to generate this data by leveraging the powerful reasoning and generation capabilities of language-only GPT-4 (and ChatGPT in early experiments) as a "strong teacher."
- **Symbolic Representation of Images:** Since GPT-4 is text-only, images must be represented symbolically. The authors use two main types of representations:
 - **Captions:** Diverse natural language descriptions of the visual scene from various perspectives.
 - **Bounding Boxes:** Localized object concepts and their spatial coordinates, providing precise object information.
- **Data Reformation:** Existing image-text pairs (specifically from the COCO dataset) are converted into instruction-following formats. This involves prompting GPT-4 with a combination of captions and bounding box information (not the image itself) to generate various types of question-answer pairs.
- **Three Types of Instruction-Following Data:**
 - **Conversation:** Multi-turn dialogues where the assistant answers diverse questions about visual content (e.g., object types, counts, actions, locations, relative positions) and engages in more complex reasoning. Only questions with definite answers from the provided symbolic visual context are included.
 - **Detailed Description:** Comprehensive, rich descriptions of images, generated by prompting GPT-4 with an intent to provide extensive detail.
 - **Complex Reasoning:** In-depth questions requiring step-by-step logical reasoning based on the visual information.
- **Scale and Quality:** The pipeline generated 158K unique language-image instruction-following samples, demonstrating that GPT-4 consistently produced higher quality data, especially for spatial reasoning, compared to ChatGPT.

#### 3.2. LLaVA Architecture

LLaVA is designed to effectively combine the strengths of pre-trained large language models and vision encoders.
- **Components:**
 - **Vision Encoder:** Pre-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 is chosen for its open-set visual encoding capabilities. It processes an input image `X_v` to produce visual features `Z_v`.
 - **Language Model (LLM):** Vicuna is selected as the LLM `f_φ(·)` due to its strong instruction-following performance among publicly available models.
- **Projection Layer:** A simple, lightweight linear projection layer `W` connects the vision encoder and LLM. This trainable matrix converts the CLIP visual features `Z_v` into language embedding tokens `H_v` that have the same dimensionality as the LLM's word embedding space. This simple scheme allows for rapid experimentation.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Procedure

LLaVA's training involves a carefully designed two-stage instruction-tuning process:
- **Stage 1: Pre-training for Feature Alignment:**
 - **Data:** A filtered subset of CC3M (595K image-text pairs) is used. These are converted into single-turn instruction-following samples (e.g., "Describe the image concisely" as instruction, original caption as answer).
 - **Objective:** To align the newly introduced visual features from the CLIP encoder with the pre-trained LLM's word embeddings.
 - **Trainable Parameters:** Only the projection matrix `W` is trained, while both the visual encoder and the LLM weights are kept frozen. This stage acts as a crucial "visual tokenizer" for the frozen LLM.
 - **Efficiency:** This stage is computationally efficient, completing within 4 hours on 8 A100 GPUs.
- **Stage 2: Fine-tuning End-to-End:**
 - **Trainable Parameters:** The projection layer `W` and the LLM weights `φ` are updated, while the visual encoder remains frozen.
 - **Use Cases:**
 - **Multimodal Chatbot:** Fine-tuned on the 158K GPT-generated language-image instruction-following data. All three data types (conversation, detailed description, complex reasoning) are uniformly sampled during training.
 - **ScienceQA:** Fine-tuned on the ScienceQA benchmark, where questions and contexts form the instruction, and reasoning and answers form the response.
 - **Training Details:** Models are trained using 8x A100 GPUs, following Vicuna's hyperparameters (Adam optimizer, cosine learning rate with warmup, FSDP, gradient checkpointing). This stage takes about 10 hours for the chatbot and 4 hours for ScienceQA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate LLaVA's impressive capabilities in multimodal understanding and instruction following, both qualitatively and quantitatively.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Chatbot Capabilities

Qualitative evaluations highlight LLaVA's strong performance in visual chat. When tested on examples from the original GPT-4 paper, LLaVA demonstrated reasoning abilities surprisingly similar to multimodal GPT-4, even on out-of-domain images. Crucially, LLaVA consistently followed user instructions (e.g., identifying "unusual aspects" of an image) rather than simply providing a descriptive caption, a common limitation of models like BLIP-2 and OpenFlamingo. LLaVA often provided more comprehensive responses than GPT-4 itself in some cases. The model also exhibited emergent behaviors, such as recognizing unseen visual concepts (e.g., Elon Musk in various contexts) and possessing impressive Optical Character Recognition (OCR) abilities, despite these not being explicitly covered in its training data.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation on LLaVA-Bench

To systematically evaluate LLaVA's instruction-following capabilities, the authors introduced LLaVA-Bench, comprising two benchmarks, and employed GPT-4 as an automated judge to score responses (on helpfulness, relevance, accuracy, and detail, relative to a text-only GPT-4 using ground-truth descriptions).

- **LLaVA-Bench (COCO):** An ablation study using 30 COCO-Val images with 90 generated questions.
 - Instruction tuning drastically improved the model's ability to follow instructions by over 50 points compared to a model without it.
 - The inclusion of a small amount of detailed description and complex reasoning data led to a significant 7-point improvement in overall capability and positively impacted conversational performance.
 - Using all three types of generated data yielded the best performance, achieving an 85.1% relative score against the text-only GPT-4 baseline.
- **LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild):** A more challenging benchmark with 24 diverse, manually curated images (including memes, paintings, sketches) and 60 questions, designed to test generalizability.
 - LLaVA significantly outperformed OpenFlamingo (+48%) and BLIP-2 (+29%) in instruction-following.
 - It achieved an impressive 81.7% relative score on complex reasoning questions and a 67.3% overall relative score, demonstrating strong generalization to novel domains.

#### 4.3. Performance on ScienceQA and GPT-4 Ensembling

LLaVA was also evaluated on the ScienceQA multimodal multiple-choice question answering dataset, a benchmark requiring detailed reasoning.
- LLaVA achieved 90.92% accuracy, performing competitively with the then-state-of-the-art (MM-CoT Large at 91.68%).
- A novel "GPT-4 as the judge" ensembling strategy was introduced. In this approach, when LLaVA and a text-only GPT-4 (which cannot directly "see" images but can reason from text) produced different answers, GPT-4 was prompted again to act as a judge, using both model outputs and the question/context to determine the final answer. This unique scheme achieved a *new state-of-the-art accuracy of 92.53%*. This is particularly notable as it demonstrated that a text-only GPT-4 could even improve performance on image-context questions by identifying instances where the image was not strictly necessary for a correct answer or by correcting LLaVA's errors through its superior textual reasoning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on ScienceQA provided insights into design choices:
- **Visual Features:** Using features *before* the last Transformer layer of CLIP (90.92%) performed better than the last layer's features (89.96%), suggesting that more localized visual properties are beneficial for detailed understanding.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** While a reasoning-first strategy could accelerate convergence, it did not significantly improve the final accuracy, indicating that the LLM's inherent reasoning capabilities are robust.
- **Pre-training:** Skipping the initial feature alignment pre-training stage resulted in a substantial 5.11% drop in accuracy, underscoring the critical role of this stage in aligning multimodal features while preserving pre-trained knowledge.
- **Model Size:** The 13B parameter LLaVA model (90.92%) outperformed its 7B counterpart (89.84%), confirming the benefits of model scale.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Visual Instruction Tuning" paper marks a significant advancement in the field of multimodal artificial intelligence, with profound implications for research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Multimodal AI

This work pioneers the systematic application of instruction tuning to the vision-language domain. By demonstrating that multimodal models can be trained to follow explicit human instructions, LLaVA moves beyond static captioning or task-specific pipelines towards truly general-purpose visual assistants. The impressive performance on multimodal chat and SOTA results on ScienceQA underscore the power of this paradigm shift, suggesting that LLaVA can exhibit emergent behaviors, generalize to unseen visual concepts, and engage in complex reasoning.

#### 5.2. Methodological Contributions

The paper's most impactful methodological contribution is its innovative, scalable, and automatic pipeline for generating high-quality vision-language instruction-following data. Leveraging GPT-4 to "teach" a multimodal model to understand instructions is a creative solution to the data scarcity problem that previously hindered progress. This data generation technique can be widely adopted and adapted by other researchers, accelerating the development of new multimodal models. Furthermore, the two-stage training approach, particularly the pre-training for feature alignment, provides a robust and effective method for integrating pre-trained vision encoders with large language models, preserving their vast pre-trained knowledge. The "GPT-4 as a judge" ensembling strategy also introduces a novel paradigm for leveraging powerful text-only LLMs to enhance the performance of multimodal models, opening new avenues for model collaboration and error correction.

#### 5.3. Practical and Open-Source Impact

The decision to publicly release the GPT-4 generated instruction data, the LLaVA model checkpoints, and the codebase is a crucial contribution to the open-source community. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers interested in building multimodal AI, fostering rapid experimentation and iteration. The LLaVA-Bench evaluation benchmarks also provide standardized tools for comparing future models, promoting rigorous scientific inquiry and reproducible research. The impressive visual chat capabilities showcased by LLaVA hint at a future where AI assistants can engage in nuanced, natural conversations about images, offering potential for applications in education, accessibility, content creation, and more.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Limitations

While groundbreaking, the paper also candidly discusses limitations and outlines future research directions. Challenges include model hallucination (generating factually incorrect information), biases inherited from base models, and the significant energy consumption associated with scaling such large models. The evaluation complexities of multimodal models are also highlighted, emphasizing the need for more comprehensive metrics beyond accuracy. The paper notes LLaVA's occasional failure to grasp complex semantics, perceiving images as "bags of patches," and areas needing improvement in fine-grained visual understanding and multilingual capabilities. These identified limitations and the emphasis on investigating emergent behaviors provide a clear roadmap for the research community to build even more capable, robust, and aligned multimodal models.

In conclusion, "Visual Instruction Tuning" introduces a pivotal methodology for creating and training large multimodal models capable of following human instructions, making a substantial stride towards the realization of truly general-purpose visual AI assistants.
