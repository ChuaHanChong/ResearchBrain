# TransDreamer: Reinforcement Learning with Transformer World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> TransDreamer is a model-based RL agent that swaps Dreamer's recurrent world model for the Transformer State-Space Model (TSSM), a stochastic latent dynamics model whose transformer attends directly to past states and actions while a myopic per-step posterior keeps training parallel. Sharing this frozen world model with a transformer policy trains stably, and it beats DreamerV2 on long-memory Hidden Order Discovery tasks while matching it on DMC and Atari.

## Problem

- Dreamer's recurrent state-space model compresses history into one hidden vector, so it cannot directly access past states for memory-based reasoning over long horizons.
- Recurrent dynamics force sequential updates across timesteps, blocking the parallel training that makes transformers computationally efficient on long sequences.
- Existing transformers are deterministic and reward-only transformer policies train unstably, so no stochastic transformer world model suitable for latent imagination existed.

## Method

- ==Transformer State-Space Model== (**TSSM**) replaces the GRU update with a transformer over all past stochastic states and actions, giving direct history access plus parallel computation of every deterministic state.
- A ==myopic representation model== conditions the posterior only on the current observation, removing the hidden-state input that would otherwise feed a transformer output back as its own input.
- Policy learning keeps Dreamer's ==actor-critic== on ==latent imagination== with the world model frozen, plus reward-prioritized replay sampling and imagination from a small subset of start states.

## Results

- On 2D 4-Ball Hidden Order Discovery, episode reward reaches about **7** versus Dreamer's **4**, and full-order success rate is **23%** versus **7%**.
- World-model foreground image MSE is lower at every context length, for example **245.2** versus **300.9** on 3D 5-Ball Dense with 60 context frames.
- Nonzero reward prediction accuracy improves with context for TransDreamer (**46.9** to **73.2** on 4-Ball Dense) while DreamerV2 lags (**28.2** to **50.5**) and stalls on 5-Ball.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Attention pays off only when the task genuinely needs long-range memory; on near-fully-observable DMC and Atari the recurrent inductive bias converges faster.
> - Freezing the world model during policy updates removes the instability that motivated gating tricks in earlier transformer RL agents, so no extra gating is needed.
> - Dropping history from the posterior costs little when the deterministic state already carries temporal context, buying full parallel training almost for free.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and the rapidly evolving field of Transformer neural networks, addressing a critical limitation in current MBRL agents.

**Evolution of Reinforcement Learning:** The field of reinforcement learning has seen immense progress, with model-free approaches like Q-learning and policy gradients achieving superhuman performance in various domains. However, model-free methods often suffer from poor sample efficiency, requiring vast amounts of interaction with the environment. MBRL emerged as a powerful alternative, aiming to learn a predictive model of the environment (a "world model") to simulate future outcomes and plan more efficiently. This internal simulation allows agents to gather more experience without real-world interaction, leading to significantly better sample efficiency.

**The Rise of Dreamer:** Among MBRL advancements, the Dreamer agent (Hafner et al., 2019; 2020) stands out as a pioneering visual MBRL model. Dreamer learns a world model in a compact latent representation space, enabling it to achieve performance and sample efficiency competitive with or superior to leading model-free agents in complex visual tasks like Atari games. The core of Dreamer's world model is the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), which relies on Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) like LSTMs or GRUs to model temporal dynamics and handle partial observability.

**The Transformer Revolution:** Concurrently, the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) has revolutionized sequence modeling across diverse fields, including Natural Language Processing (NLP), computer vision, and time series prediction. Transformers, with their attention mechanisms, excel at capturing long-range dependencies and directly accessing historical information, overcoming the inherent limitations of RNNs in processing very long sequences.

**Identifying the Gap:** Despite Dreamer's success, its reliance on RNNs for the RSSM means it inherits their limitations, particularly in tasks requiring extensive long-term memory or complex, non-local temporal reasoning. While some prior work explored Transformers in RL (e.g., GTrXL by Parisotto et al., 2020), these efforts primarily focused on replacing RNNs in model-free policy networks and often encountered training stability issues, especially with sparse rewards. Moreover, a robust transformer-based *world model* that is both stochastic and parallelizable for latent dynamics prediction, specifically within an MBRL framework, has been conspicuously absent.

**TransDreamer's Contribution:** This paper directly addresses this gap by proposing TransDreamer, the first MBRL agent to integrate a transformer-based world model. It aims to combine the sample efficiency and planning benefits of Dreamer with the superior long-term dependency modeling capabilities of Transformers. By introducing the Transformer State-Space Model (TSSM), TransDreamer seeks to bring the advantages of direct memory access and parallel processing inherent in Transformers to the critical world model component of MBRL, thereby extending its applicability to tasks demanding more sophisticated memory-based reasoning. This work pushes the frontier of general-purpose intelligent agents by leveraging architectural innovations for more effective environment modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a novel model-based reinforcement learning agent, named TransDreamer, that integrates the powerful Transformer architecture into the established Dreamer framework. Specifically, the authors aim to introduce a transformer-based world model capable of dynamics prediction and subsequently share this model with a transformer-based policy network to achieve stable training in reinforcement learning settings.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the perceived limitations of existing MBRL agents, particularly Dreamer, and the untapped potential of Transformers in this domain:

- **Overcoming RNN Limitations in World Models:** Dreamer's success relies heavily on its Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), which uses Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to model environmental dynamics. While effective for many tasks, RNNs inherently struggle with capturing very long-term dependencies and providing direct, non-sequential access to past states. Tasks requiring "memory-based reasoning" (e.g., remembering a sequence of events over an extended period) pose a significant challenge for RNNs. The authors hypothesize that Transformers, with their self-attention mechanisms, can address these limitations by enabling direct access and complex interaction with historical states.
- **Leveraging Transformers for Enhanced World Modeling:** Transformers have demonstrated superior performance in various sequence-to-sequence tasks, excelling at both understanding and generating complex temporal patterns. These capabilities are highly desirable for a robust world model, which needs to accurately predict future states and rewards based on past observations and actions. The motivation is to harness this generative and memory-aware power of Transformers for more accurate and comprehensive environmental modeling.
- **Facilitating Transformer-based Policy Learning:** Previous attempts to use Transformers directly for policy networks in RL have often faced stability issues, particularly with sparse reward signals. The authors posit that learning a transformer-based *world model*, which receives a more diverse training signal (reconstructing observations, rewards, and dynamics), could provide a more stable foundation. Once a stable world model is learned, the policy network can then be trained on imagined trajectories, potentially leading to more robust transformer-based RL agents.
- **Addressing Architectural Challenges:** Integrating Transformers into a stochastic latent-variable world model like RSSM is not a simple "plug-and-play" replacement. The authors identify critical challenges:
 - Developing a transformer-based world model (TSSM) that supports effective *stochastic, action-conditioned transitions* in latent space, crucial for both prior and posterior dynamics modeling.
 - Ensuring the new architecture preserves the *parallel trainability* of Transformers for computational efficiency during training, which conflicts with sequential dependencies often present in recurrent models.
 - Finding appropriate architectural design choices and hyperparameters to achieve a *working and stable* Transformer-based RL agent.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to build more intelligent and sample-efficient RL agents capable of handling complex, partially observable environments that demand sophisticated long-term memory and reasoning, by integrating the latest advancements in deep learning architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TransDreamer's methodology builds upon the established Dreamer framework while introducing a core innovation: the Transformer State-Space Model (TSSM). The overall approach maintains Dreamer's three cyclical steps: world model learning, policy learning (in imagination), and environment interaction.

#### 3.1. Transformer State-Space Model (TSSM) Design

The central contribution is the TSSM, which replaces Dreamer's Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM). The TSSM aims to satisfy four desiderata: (i) direct access to past states, (ii) parallel state updates during training, (iii) sequential rollout for imagination at test time, and (iv) being a stochastic latent variable model.

- **Replacing the RNN:** In RSSM, the deterministic hidden state `h_t` is updated recurrently via `h_t = gru(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})`. TransDreamer replaces this sequential GRU update with a Transformer. Specifically, `h_t = f_transformer(z_{1:t-1}, a_{1:t-1})`, allowing `h_t` to directly attend to and integrate information from all previous stochastic states (`z`) and actions (`a`).
- **Enabling Parallel Trainability with Myopic Representation Model:** A key challenge for parallel training is that the RSSM's representation model `q(z_t|h_t, x_t)` depends on `h_t`, which itself depends on past `z`s and `a`s. To break this sequential dependency and enable parallel computation of all `h_t`s, TransDreamer modifies the representation model to `q(z_t|x_t)`. This "myopic" representation model infers the stochastic state `z_t` solely from the current observation `x_t`, making `z_t` computations independent across timesteps. The authors hypothesize that the deterministic state `h_t` (generated by the Transformer) can sufficiently capture temporal information, and the full state `s_t = (h_t, z_t)` still retains comprehensive temporal context. This allows all `z_{1:T}` to be computed in parallel, then a single Transformer forward pass can compute `h_{1:T}`.
- **Stochasticity and Imagination:** The TSSM remains a stochastic latent variable model, crucial for capturing environmental uncertainty and generating diverse imagined trajectories. For imagination (during policy learning), the model operates autoregressively: `h_t` is computed, then a prior stochastic state `z_t ~ p(z|h_t)` is sampled and fed back into the transformer to predict `h_{t+1}`. This ensures that the agent can "dream" about future scenarios entirely within its learned latent space.
- **Loss Function:** The TSSM is trained by maximizing a modified Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), similar to RSSM. The key difference in the loss is that the posterior representation `p(z_{1:t}|x_{1:t})` is approximated by `Q_t q_phi(z_tau|x_tau)` instead of `Q_t q_phi(z_tau|z_{1:tau-1}, x_{1:tau})`. The loss includes terms for reconstructing observations (`x_t`), rewards (`r_t`), and discounts (`gamma_t`), along with a KL divergence term between the posterior `q(z_t|x_t)` and the prior `p(z_t|z_{1:t-1}, a_{1:t-1})`.

#### 3.2. Policy Learning and Implementation Details

- **Dreamer-like Policy Learning:** TransDreamer largely adopts Dreamer's policy learning mechanism. An actor-critic policy `pi_phi(a_t|s_t)` and value model `v_psi(s_t)` are trained on imagined trajectories rolled out by the learned TSSM. The objective function for the policy is based on the discounted sum of predicted rewards and bootstrapped values from these imagined sequences. Gradients are computed using a combination of REINFORCE and direct backpropagation through the differentiable world model.
- **Training Stability for Transformers:** A critical insight is how TransDreamer handles the known instability of Transformers in RL. Unlike approaches that directly train a transformer policy with sparse rewards, TransDreamer *fixes the world model parameters* (TSSM) during agent (policy) training. The TSSM itself is trained via reconstruction losses, which are more dense and stable. This effectively provides a stable "imagination environment" for the policy, circumventing common instability issues without requiring additional gating layers like GTrXL.
- **Prioritized Replay:** To accelerate learning in sparse reward environments, TransDreamer optionally uses a prioritized replay buffer. Trajectories with non-zero rewards are sampled with a higher probability (`alpha`-percentage of the time), ensuring the agent focuses on meaningful experiences.
- **Reduced Imagination Trajectories:** Due to the higher memory footprint of Transformers compared to RNNs, TransDreamer generates imagined trajectories from a smaller, randomly chosen subset (`K`) of starting states from the replay buffer, rather than from every sampled state. Despite this reduction, the agent still achieves comparable or superior performance.

By meticulously designing the TSSM and carefully integrating it into the Dreamer framework with stability considerations, TransDreamer offers a practical and powerful method for leveraging Transformers in MBRL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors provide clear evidence for TransDreamer's capabilities, particularly in tasks requiring long-term memory and complex reasoning. The findings address three key questions: performance on memory-intensive tasks, quality of the learned world model, and comparable performance on short-term memory tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Hidden Order Discovery Tasks (Long-Term Memory)

- **Novel Task Introduction:** To rigorously test long-term memory and reasoning, the authors introduced "Hidden Order Discovery" tasks in both 2D (Minigrid) and 3D (Unity, first-person view) environments. In these tasks, an agent must discover a hidden sequence of colored balls by trial and error, remembering past failed attempts to avoid repeating mistakes.
- **Superior Performance:** TransDreamer consistently and significantly outperforms Dreamer (specifically DreamerV2) across all configurations of 2D (4, 5, 6 balls) and 3D (4-Ball Dense/Sparse, 5-Ball Dense) Hidden Order Discovery tasks.
 - For the 2D 4-Ball task, TransDreamer achieved an average episode reward of around 7, meaning it typically collected two balls in the correct order, compared to Dreamer's average of 4 (one ball). TransDreamer also showed a 23% success rate (completing at least one full sequence) versus Dreamer's 7%.
 - This performance gap widened with increasing task complexity (more balls or sparser environments), demonstrating TransDreamer's superior ability to leverage long-term memory. Dreamer's performance degraded more sharply as the number of balls increased, nearly failing in 5-Ball 2D and 5-Ball Dense 3D.
- **Validation of Hypothesis:** These results strongly support the hypothesis that a transformer-based architecture is more effective than RNN-based models for tasks demanding complex long-term memory interactions.

#### 4.2. World Model Quality (TSSM vs. RSSM)

The authors performed both quantitative and qualitative analyses of the world models (TSSM and RSSM) learned by TransDreamer and Dreamer, respectively, in the 3D Hidden Order Discovery environments.

- **Quantitative Analysis:**
 - **Image Generation (Foreground MSE):** TSSM generally achieved lower or comparable Mean Squared Error (MSE) in predicting future images, especially for foreground objects crucial to the task. This indicates that TSSM forms a more accurate and robust latent representation and dynamics model, particularly when provided with longer historical contexts (e.g., 80 contexts).
 - **Reward Prediction Accuracy (Non-zero Rewards):** TransDreamer's TSSM demonstrated significantly higher accuracy in predicting non-zero rewards compared to Dreamer's RSSM, particularly in the 4-Ball Dense and 5-Ball Dense environments (e.g., 32.3% vs. 15.3% for 5-Ball Dense with 80 contexts). Crucially, TransDreamer's reward prediction continued to improve with longer contexts, indicating its ability to effectively utilize extended historical information, whereas Dreamer's improvement was minimal or stalled.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Imagined Trajectories):** Visual inspection of imagined trajectories revealed that TransDreamer produced clearer, more coherent, and more accurate sequences of predicted observations and rewards. Dreamer's imaginations were often blurrier, and its predictions for ball collection and associated rewards were frequently incorrect or inconsistent with the observed context. This qualitative difference underscores TSSM's superior capacity for long-term predictive modeling.

#### 4.3. Short-Term Memory Tasks (DMC and Atari)

- **Comparable Performance, Slower Convergence:** On simpler tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) and Atari (e.g., Cartpole, Walker, Pong), which primarily require short-term memory, TransDreamer eventually reached performance levels comparable to Dreamer. However, TransDreamer typically exhibited a slower convergence rate.
- **Expected Outcome:** This result was largely expected. RNNs have a specific inductive bias towards processing recent history efficiently, making them faster to learn in scenarios where long-term dependencies are not critical. The fact that TransDreamer can eventually match Dreamer's performance, even without this specific inductive bias, demonstrates its general applicability. An interesting exception was DMC Cheetah Run, where TransDreamer showed slightly better performance and faster convergence.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that TransDreamer, powered by its Transformer State-Space Model, provides a significant advantage in reinforcement learning tasks that require sophisticated long-term memory and reasoning, while maintaining competitive performance on simpler tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TransDreamer and the Transformer State-Space Model (TSSM) represents a significant advancement in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning, with profound implications for the development of more capable and generalizable AI agents.

**1. First Transformer-based MBRL Agent and Stochastic World Model:** TransDreamer is the first documented instance of a fully transformer-based MBRL agent, and TSSM is the first transformer-based stochastic world model. This marks a crucial step in integrating the powerful architecture of Transformers, which have revolutionized other AI fields, into the domain of MBRL. It pioneers a new pathway for designing world models that can leverage attention mechanisms for complex dynamics modeling.

**2. Enhanced Long-Term Reasoning and Memory:** The most compelling finding is TransDreamer's superior performance on tasks requiring long-term memory and complex reasoning (Hidden Order Discovery). This demonstrates that Transformers are indeed more effective than traditional RNNs in modeling the intricate temporal dependencies necessary for agents to make informed decisions over extended periods. This capability is vital for solving real-world problems in environments with high partial observability or where strategic planning requires remembering and processing sequences of events far into the past.

**3. Improved World Model Quality:** The quantitative and qualitative evidence showing TSSM's superior image generation and reward prediction capabilities means that the imagined trajectories used for policy learning are more accurate and reliable. This directly translates to more effective policy learning, as the agent is essentially practicing in a higher-fidelity simulated environment. This improved world model quality could be a game-changer for deploying MBRL agents in complex, unstructured environments.

**4. Stable Integration of Transformers in RL:** The paper addresses the notorious training instability of Transformers in reinforcement learning settings. By training the Transformer-based world model primarily on reconstruction losses (which are dense and stable) and then fixing its parameters during policy optimization, TransDreamer provides a robust framework for leveraging Transformers. This "decoupling" strategy offers a promising blueprint for future research aiming to incorporate advanced architectures into challenging RL problems without encountering convergence issues.

**5. Broader Applicability and Generalization:** While demonstrating clear advantages in memory-intensive tasks, TransDreamer also performs comparably on simpler tasks, suggesting its general applicability. The ability to handle both short- and long-term dependencies within a single framework points towards agents that are more versatile and general-purpose, reducing the need for task-specific architectural choices.

**6. Future Research Directions:** This work opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Scaling to More Complex Environments:** The authors suggest validating TransDreamer on even more complex tasks like Crafter, which demands hierarchical reasoning and sophisticated environmental interaction. This would further test its limits and potential.
 - **Exploring More Advanced Stochastic Transformers:** TSSM's "myopic" representation model is a simplification made for parallelization. Future work could explore more complex transformer architectures for the posterior, potentially conditioning on past observations directly while maintaining computational efficiency.
 - **Adaptive Imagination Strategies:** Given the memory constraints of Transformers, optimizing the selection and length of imagined trajectories could further boost efficiency and performance.
 - **Transfer Learning and Meta-RL:** A robust world model capable of complex dynamics could be highly valuable for transfer learning or meta-reinforcement learning, where knowledge about environment dynamics can be quickly adapted to new, related tasks.

In conclusion, TransDreamer represents a significant step towards creating more intelligent, sample-efficient, and generally capable reinforcement learning agents by effectively harnessing the power of Transformer networks to build superior world models. Its implications could extend to various challenging real-world applications where memory, planning, and understanding complex dynamics are paramount, from robotics and autonomous systems to scientific discovery.
