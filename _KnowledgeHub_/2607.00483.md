# VLM-AR3L: Vision-Language Models for Absolute and Relative Rewards in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM-AR3L presents a framework that leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to create both absolute and relative reward signals for reinforcement learning, providing stable and robust supervision. This dual-reward approach addresses the limitations of single-reward formulations in complex visual environments and allows RL agents to learn policies without manual reward engineering, matching or exceeding human-designed reward performance in diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Designing effective reward functions for reinforcement learning in visually complex, abstract, or open-ended environments is challenging due to ambiguous objectives and sparse feedback.
- Existing VLM-based absolute reward models suffer from inconsistency across training steps and struggle in tasks with cyclic structures or ambiguous state orderings.
- Direct similarity-based VLM reward methods often yield noisy and fragile signals, requiring extensive task-specific fine-tuning for reliable performance.

## Method

- A **dual-reward framework** is introduced, combining an ==absolute reward model== and a ==relative reward model==, both trained using ==VLM-generated preference labels==.
- An absolute reward model assigns scalar values to individual states, while a ==Siamese neural network== acts as a relative reward model to evaluate progress between state pairs, incorporating a ==temporal offset== and ==symmetric confidence check==.
- The two reward types are integrated into a **composite dual-reward** signal to guide policy optimization, enabling efficient and robust reward generation without task-specific VLM fine-tuning or privileged information.

## Results

- VLM-AR3L consistently outperformed all prior VLM-based reward learning methods across **ten** diverse tasks in Gym, SoftGym, MetaWorld, and MineDojo environments.
- The framework achieved performance comparable to or superior to human-designed dense ground-truth rewards, and significantly surpassed sparse ground-truth rewards in challenging long-horizon tasks.
- Gemini-2.0-Flash demonstrated over **70%** accuracy in VLM-generated preference labels, while ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of both absolute and relative reward components and the symmetric confidence check.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern VLMs, such as Gemini-2.0-Flash, reliably generate high-accuracy preference labels for complex visual observations and language goals, outperforming similarity-based models.
> - Explicitly training a dedicated Siamese network for relative rewards is crucial for stable comparison and computational efficiency, surpassing direct VLM querying or absolute reward differences.
> - The combination of absolute and relative reward signals provides complementary benefits, particularly with relative rewards proving effective in tasks with ambiguous state ordering or cyclic structures where absolute rewards are less robust.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The design of effective reward functions represents a significant challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in environments characterized by visual complexity, abstract task goals, and open-endedness. Traditional methods, which often rely on hand-crafted reward functions, become impractical in such settings due to ambiguous objectives, sparse feedback, and a lack of privileged state information.

Recent advancements have explored leveraging large pre-trained models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), to address this reward engineering bottleneck. LLM-based approaches have demonstrated success in providing structured feedback or code for RL agents in text-based or programmatic environments. However, these methods typically require privileged state access or specialized interfaces, limiting their applicability in real-world visual domains.

For visual observations, contrastive Vision-Language Models (cVLMs) like CLIP have been utilized to compute similarity scores between observations and language goals for reward shaping. While straightforward, these similarity-based methods often produce noisy and fragile reward signals, showing sensitivity to goal phrasing and frequently requiring task-specific fine-tuning to achieve nuanced and reliable performance across different domains.

An alternative approach, preference-based reward modeling, learns reward functions from pairwise comparisons of observations or trajectory segments. This paradigm, which can derive preferences from human feedback or large pre-trained models, enables finer-grained feedback and improved robustness in visually diverse or ambiguous environments. Previous work, such as RL-VLM-F, has extended this by using generative VLMs to produce pairwise preferences, which are then used to train an *absolute reward model* that assigns scalar values to individual states.

The current work, VLM-AR3L, builds upon preference-based VLM methods but identifies a limitation in existing absolute reward formulations. The authors observe that absolute reward signals can be inconsistent across training steps due to the continuous introduction of new states and can fundamentally struggle in environments with cyclic task structures or ambiguous state orderings where a consistent global ordering of states is not well-defined. VLM-AR3L addresses these specific limitations by proposing a dual-reward framework that integrates both absolute and relative rewards, aiming to provide more stable and robust supervision for RL agents without requiring task-specific fine-tuning or privileged environment access.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the effectiveness and robustness of reward function design in reinforcement learning by leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to provide both absolute and relative reward signals. The motivation for this dual-reward approach stems from identified limitations in existing VLM-based reward learning methods, particularly those that rely solely on absolute, state-based reward formulations.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Inconsistency in Absolute Rewards:** Prior VLM-based reward models, which assign a scalar value to each individual state, can exhibit instability. As an agent explores new states and the reward model undergoes continuous updates, the absolute reward values assigned to previously visited states may fluctuate significantly (illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper). This inconsistency can hinder stable policy optimization, especially in long-horizon tasks where a consistent signal of progress is crucial. VLM-AR3L aims to mitigate this by complementing absolute rewards with relative rewards that focus on state transitions rather than fixed state values.

2. **Overcoming Limitations in Cyclic and Ambiguous Task Structures:** Absolute reward formulations often face fundamental challenges in environments where progress cannot be uniquely defined by a global ordering of states. For instance, in cyclic tasks (e.g., navigating a ring-world, as shown in Figure 2), returning to a "previous" state might represent progress rather than regression, rendering a fixed scalar value for that state ambiguous or misleading. VLM-AR3L is motivated to introduce relative rewards that evaluate progress or regression between consecutive observations, which can provide a more robust signal in such scenarios.

3. **Enhancing Robustness and Scalability in Complex Environments:** Open-ended and visually complex environments, such as Minecraft, often involve abstract goals and require long-horizon decision-making. Hand-crafting rewards for these settings is impractical, and direct similarity-based VLM methods (like CLIP) have been shown to be fragile and noisy. The integration of both absolute and relative rewards aims to combine the stability of state-based evaluation (when applicable) with the robustness of comparative, transition-based supervision, thereby offering a more scalable and adaptable solution for diverse, challenging domains.

4. **Improving Computational Efficiency:** While VLMs are powerful for generating preferences, repeatedly querying them during policy optimization can be computationally intensive. A specific objective of VLM-AR3L is to train a dedicated relative reward model (a Siamese network) using VLM-generated preference labels. This approach avoids repeated VLM inference during policy learning, contributing to computational efficiency and improved training performance.

5. **Broad Applicability without Fine-tuning or Privileged Information:** The research seeks to develop a framework that learns effective reward functions using only natural language task goals and visual observations, without requiring task-specific fine-tuning of the VLM or access to privileged internal environment information. This objective targets making the method broadly applicable across different domains, from structured robotic control to open-world embodied tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VLM-AR3L framework is designed to leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for generating both absolute and relative reward signals to guide reinforcement learning agents. The overall architecture involves two interconnected phases: reward model training and policy learning.

#### 3.1. Reward Model Training (Blue Path in Figure 3)

1. **Observation Pair Sampling:** During training, pairs of observations ($\sigma_i, \sigma_j$) are sampled from the agent's replay buffer. Each pair is associated with a high-level natural language goal (g) describing the task.
2. **VLM-Generated Preference Labels:** A frozen VLM (e.g., Gemini-2.0-Flash) is used as an automated annotator. For each triplet ($\sigma_i, \sigma_j, g$), the VLM evaluates which observation better aligns with the task goal and outputs a preference label (y). The label indicates whether $\sigma_i$ is preferred, $\sigma_j$ is preferred, or they are equally preferable/incomparable. The VLM prompting strategy involves using different prompt templates (single-stage, two-stage, chain-of-thought, binary) based on empirical performance for each VLM (Appendix A.1).
3. **Absolute Reward Model ($F^{abs}_{\psi}$):**
 - This model is trained to assign scalar values $r_{\psi}(s_t)$ to individual states $s_t$, where higher values indicate closer proximity to the goal. The absolute reward for state $s_t$ is given by $R^{abs}_{\psi}(s_t) = F^{abs}_{\psi}(s_t)$.
 - The model is trained using the Bradley-Terry model (Eq. 1) to compute preference probabilities and a cross-entropy loss (Eq. 2) over the VLM-generated preference labels.
 - This formulation provides a state-based evaluation signal, which is effective when a global ordering over the state space is well-defined.
4. **Relative Reward Model ($F^{rel}_{\phi}$):**
 - This model is designed to evaluate progress or regression between pairs of states. It aims to be robust in partially observable, long-horizon, or cyclic scenarios where absolute state values might be inconsistent.
 - A **Siamese neural network** is employed for this model. It processes a pair of states ($s_i, s_j$) by feeding each image through a shared CNN encoder to produce embeddings. These embeddings are then concatenated and passed through an MLP head to output the preference probability $P_{\phi}[\sigma_i \succ \sigma_j]$ (Eq. 5).
 - The relative model is trained using the same cross-entropy loss (Eq. 2) as the absolute model.
 - **Temporal Offset (k):** To enhance sensitivity to subtle changes, particularly between visually similar consecutive observations, the relative reward compares the current observation $s_t$ with a past observation $s_{t-k}$ (where $k \geq 1$).
 - **Symmetric Confidence Check:** To ensure the reliability of the relative reward, a bidirectional confidence check is applied. A positive reward (1) is assigned only if the model's confidence in preferring $s_t$ over $s_{t-k}$ exceeds a threshold $\tau$, *and* the reverse prediction indicates that $s_{t-k}$ is *not* preferred over $s_t$ with confidence exceeding $\tau$. Conversely for a negative reward (-1). Otherwise, a zero reward is assigned (Eq. 6). This filters inconsistent VLM judgments and prevents biased rewards.
 - Training a Siamese network avoids repeated online VLM inference, thus reducing computational overhead during policy learning.

#### 3.2. Dual-Reward Design and Policy Learning (Purple Path in Figure 3)

1. **Dual-Reward Integration:** The absolute and relative reward signals are combined to form a composite dual-reward that guides policy optimization. This dual-reward is computed as:
 $R^{dual}_{\psi,\phi}(s_t, s_{t-k}) = \alpha R^{abs}_{\psi}(s_t) + (1-\alpha) R^{rel}_{\phi}(s_t, s_{t-k})$ (Eq. 7).
 - $\alpha$ is a weighting coefficient (set to 0.5 in experiments) that balances the contribution of absolute and relative rewards. Both reward types are normalized to a consistent bounded range.
 - This combination leverages the strengths of both reward types: the stability of state-based evaluation (absolute) and the robustness of transition-based supervision (relative).
2. **Policy Optimization:** The agent's policy is then optimized using standard reinforcement learning algorithms based on this dense, informative dual-reward signal.
 - Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is used for Gym, SoftGym, and MetaWorld tasks.
 - Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for MineDojo tasks.
 - This approach eliminates the need for manual reward engineering or privileged state information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLM-AR3L was conducted across ten tasks spanning four diverse environments: Gym (Cart Pole), SoftGym (Straighten Rope, Pass Water), MetaWorld (Soccer, Sweep Into, Drawer Open), and MineDojo (Combat Spider, Milk Cow, Shear Sheep, Hunt Cow).

#### 4.1. VLM's Label Accuracy

An initial assessment evaluated the accuracy of various VLMs in generating preference labels.
- **Gemini-2.0-Flash** consistently achieved the highest accuracy, exceeding 70% in all tasks (Table 1), indicating robust multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- **GPT-4.1-nano** generally underperformed Gemini-2.0-Flash.
- **Open-source VLMs** like Phi-3.5-Vision-Instruct and MiniCPM-o-2.6 showed competitive, task-specific strengths.
- **CLIP and MineCLIP** exhibited low accuracy, suggesting limitations in modeling multi-step interactions and temporal dependencies.
- Prompt design significantly influenced VLM performance, with different VLMs benefiting from varying prompt complexities (Table 3). Gemini-2.0-Flash performed well even with simpler prompts (S1), while GPT-4.1-nano improved with Chain-of-Thought (S2+CoT), and Phi-3.5 favored binary single-stage prompts (S1$^2$).

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

VLM-AR3L, using Gemini-2.0-Flash as the VLM, was compared against several baselines (Table 2, Figure 5).
- **Similarity-based methods (CLIP, MineCLIP)**: Performed poorly in complex environments. MineCLIP, despite being fine-tuned on Minecraft videos, failed to generate reliable reward signals in MineDojo tasks. These methods were noted to lack the granularity for subtle visual distinctions, leading to noisy rewards.
- **Absolute preference-based methods (RL-VLM-F)**: Achieved performance comparable to VLM-AR3L in simpler tasks. However, its efficacy significantly degraded in long-horizon and visually complex domains like MineDojo. This was attributed to its reliance on absolute state-based rewards, which struggled with evolving state distributions and the need for a global understanding of the task space.
- **VLM-AR3L (Ours)**: Consistently outperformed all prior VLM-based reward learning methods across diverse tasks.
- **Oracle baselines (GT Dense Reward, GT Sparse Reward)**: VLM-AR3L matched the performance of GT Dense Reward in most tasks. It significantly outperformed GT Sparse Reward in tasks such as Milk Cow, Shear Sheep, and Hunt Cow, where sparse feedback was insufficient for learning. This demonstrates VLM-AR3L's capability to handle sparse feedback and challenging long-horizon scenarios without human-engineered rewards.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of VLM choice**: The choice of VLM for supervision broadly correlated with end-to-end RL performance (Figure 6, Table 15). Gemini-2.0-Flash resulted in the best overall performance, but open-source models like Phi-3.5 and MiniCPM-o-2.6 showed task-specific strengths, suggesting potential for broader adoption.
- **Impact of Relative Reward Architecture**:
 - Explicitly training a Siamese network for relative rewards (Siamese) consistently outperformed direct VLM querying during rollouts (VLM-Direct) and deriving relative rewards from absolute reward predictions (Abs-Diff) (Figure 7). This highlights the benefits of learning a stable comparison function and reducing computational overhead.
 - Computing relative rewards from absolute differences (Abs-Diff) led to inferior performance due to the propagation of noise from unreliable absolute state scores.
 - The symmetric confidence check in the relative reward computation (Eq. 6) was crucial; its removal (Siamese w/o Sym) resulted in noticeable performance drops.
- **Impact of Absolute and Relative Rewards**: Ablations confirmed the complementary nature of the two reward types (Figure 8, Figure 12). Absolute rewards were most beneficial in tasks with clear global progress measures, while relative rewards were critical for tasks with ambiguous state ordering, cyclic structures, or long-horizon exploration. Combining both consistently led to further improvements, supporting their synergistic effect.
- **Hyper-parameter Robustness**: The method demonstrated robustness to variations in the temporal offset $k$ and the weighting coefficient $\alpha$ (Figures 9 and 10), indicating that VLM-AR3L is not highly sensitive to these parameters and performs well with a shared set of values without task-specific tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLM-AR3L framework introduces a significant advancement in addressing the reward engineering problem in reinforcement learning, particularly for complex and open-ended visual environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Alleviation of the Reward Engineering Bottleneck**: The primary impact of VLM-AR3L is its ability to generate effective reward functions from natural language task goals and visual observations, substantially reducing the reliance on manual reward engineering. This is crucial for environments where hand-crafting rewards is prohibitively difficult due to abstract goals, sparse feedback, or complex visual dynamics.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Stability**: By introducing a dual-reward design that combines absolute and relative reward signals, VLM-AR3L addresses key limitations of prior VLM-based methods. The relative reward component provides a more stable and consistent signal of progress during training, particularly in scenarios where absolute state values are inconsistent or a global ordering of states is ambiguous (e.g., cyclic tasks, long-horizon exploration). This robustness allows for more reliable learning in challenging domains.

3. **Superior Performance Compared to Existing VLM-based Methods**: The empirical results demonstrate that VLM-AR3L consistently outperforms current state-of-the-art VLM-based reward learning techniques. This indicates a more effective utilization of VLM capabilities for guiding RL agents.

4. **Competitiveness with Human-Designed Rewards**: In several tasks, VLM-AR3L achieves performance comparable to, or even surpasses, carefully human-designed dense reward functions. Furthermore, it significantly outperforms sparse ground-truth rewards in challenging long-horizon tasks, highlighting its practical utility as a viable alternative where manual reward design is infeasible.

5. **Efficient Utilization of Foundational Models**: The framework effectively leverages the multimodal reasoning capabilities of large pre-trained VLMs without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. By training a specialized Siamese network for relative rewards, it also optimizes computational resources by avoiding repeated, expensive VLM inferences during policy optimization.

6. **Broad Applicability**: VLM-AR3L's evaluation across diverse domains, from structured control to open-world embodied tasks like Minecraft, suggests its generalizability and adaptability. This broad applicability can accelerate the development of RL agents in various real-world scenarios, including robotics, autonomous navigation, and game AI.

**Potential Negative Implications and Mitigations (as identified by the authors):**
The reliance on VLMs for reward labeling introduces potential risks.
- **Bias Propagation**: Biases inherent in the training data of VLMs could be propagated to the RL agents, leading to biased or undesirable behaviors. Mitigation strategies would involve rigorous bias detection in VLM outputs and careful selection of VLMs.
- **Unintended Applications**: The method's effectiveness could be exploited for harmful objectives. This necessitates careful consideration of ethical guidelines, restricted application to controlled environments, and transparent development practices.

In conclusion, VLM-AR3L contributes a robust and efficient framework for reward learning in complex RL settings, pushing the capabilities of VLM-based methods while offering a scalable solution to a fundamental challenge in the field.
