# Helix: Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning for Open-Ended Scientific Problem Solving

> [!summary] Summary
> HELIX integrates an evolutionary search mechanism with reinforcement learning to enable large language models (LLMs) to effectively solve open-ended scientific problems. The framework demonstrated superior performance on 17 out of 20 diverse scientific tasks, outperforming advanced models like GPT-4o and achieving new state-of-the-art results in areas such as circle packing.

## Problem

- Complex scientific problems are characterized by being domain-specific, open-ended, and unbounded, requiring extensive exploration without a known global optimum.
- Existing LLM-based approaches (fine-tuning or workflow-driven) struggle with limited exploration efficiency, entropy collapse, and rely on static, pretrained knowledge, hindering the discovery of fundamentally new solutions.
- Traditional reinforcement learning for LLMs lacks the memory to leverage previously discovered solutions, making continuous improvement challenging in complex, unbounded domains.

## Method

- HELIX proposes a hybrid framework combining a reinforcement learning mechanism for ==iterative== LLM policy updates with a multi-objective evolutionary mechanism to balance solution quality and diversity.
- An in-context learning mechanism is employed to construct prompts that incorporate multiple past trials, allowing the LLM to build upon a lineage of ancestral solutions for guided exploration.
- The LLM policy is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), chosen for its ability to enhance reasoning and generate diverse multi-sample outputs that feed the evolutionary process.

## Results

- HELIX achieved the best performance on 17 of 20 benchmark tasks, consistently outperforming Direct Prompting, OpenEvolve, and GPT-4o across diverse scientific domains.
- The framework set a new record for packing 26 circles in a unit square (sum of radii 2.63598308) and achieved the second-highest score on the Transparent Conductors Kaggle leaderboard (RMSLE of **0.04915**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both explicit diversity-quality balancing and the iterative interplay between reinforcement learning and evolutionary guidance are critical for sustained performance and avoiding local optima.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of reinforcement learning with an evolutionary algorithm (NSGA-II) effectively addresses the exploration-exploitation trade-off, preventing premature convergence and maintaining solution diversity.
> - Leveraging a 'shoulder of giants' approach through in-context learning from a solution's lineage tree allows the LLM to learn from past successes and failures, accelerating the search in unbounded spaces.
> - Dynamically adapting the LLM's policy via RL while concurrently maintaining a diverse, high-quality solution population through evolution enables smaller models to achieve competitive or superior performance compared to larger, proprietary LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) to complex scientific problem-solving, an area experiencing increased research activity. Scientific problems are characterized by being domain-specific, open-ended, and unbounded, often requiring exploration across extensive and flexible solution spaces without a known global optimum.

Existing LLM-based approaches broadly fall into two categories:
- **Post-training methods**: These involve fine-tuning LLMs on domain-specific datasets using techniques like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). Examples include AlphaCode for code generation and Deepseek-R1 for mathematical reasoning. Acknowledged limitations of these methods include entropy collapse and a tendency to operate within the base model's inherent capabilities, thus restricting the discovery of fundamentally new solutions, especially in contexts with sparse rewards.
- **Workflow-driven approaches**: These embed LLMs within predefined pipelines, such as integrating genetic algorithms for enzyme discovery (Nana Teukam et al., 2025), LLM-driven evolutionary loops like LLaMEA (van Stein & Bäck, 2024), or applying evolutionary strategies to prompts (Agrawal et al., 2025). While effective for certain tasks, these methods are often sensitive to the specific workflow design and rely on static, pretrained knowledge, hindering their ability to reuse past discoveries to guide iterative search.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs, including RL from Human Feedback (RLHF) and RLVR, has gained attention for aligning models with human preferences and enhancing reasoning. Methods like GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and its variants (DAPO, Dr.GRPO, KL-Cov) aim to improve generalization and address diversity issues. However, the paper notes that these "memory-less" RL methods struggle to leverage previously discovered solutions for continuous improvement in complex scientific domains.

Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) are a classic optimization approach. Recent work, such as AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., 2025) and its open-source implementation OpenEvolve (Sharma, 2025), integrate LLMs as "mutation" agents for code evolution in tasks like CUDA optimization, drug discovery, and scientific software usage. The limitation here is the requirement for highly problem-specific workflow logic and prompt design, which can restrict generalization.

HELIX positions itself by proposing a framework that synthesizes reinforcement learning with evolutionary search, specifically addressing the aforementioned limitations. It aims to overcome the exploration efficiency and generalization challenges of existing methods by explicitly enabling task-specific policy adaptation, balancing solution quality with diversity, and facilitating iterative learning from past discoveries in unbounded, open-ended scientific problem spaces.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to effectively address complex scientific problems, which are intrinsically characterized as domain-specific, open-ended, and unbounded. The current limitations of existing LLM approaches, including restricted exploration efficiency and poor generalization, motivate the proposed solution.

The authors identify three core challenges inherent to complex scientific problems and propose corresponding abilities an effective LLM system must possess:

1. **Domain-Specificity**: Scientific tasks often feature unique environments and problem-specific constraints.
 - **Motivation for HELIX's approach**: To address this, the system must be capable of *learning from experience*, which involves task-specific policy adaptation by incorporating feedback from previous trials. This allows the LLM to adjust its problem-solving strategy to the nuances of each domain.

2. **Open-Endedness**: These problems require exploration across vast and flexible solution spaces, often without a predefined endpoint or single correct answer.
 - **Motivation for HELIX's approach**: The system needs to *balance quality and diversity* in its solution generation. Maintaining a diverse population of candidate solutions is crucial for thoroughly exploring the expansive solution spaces and avoiding premature convergence to suboptimal local optima. Simultaneously, ensuring high quality within this diverse set provides strong starting points for further refinement.

3. **Unboundedness**: Many scientific problems do not have a known or guaranteed global optimum, implying a continuous search for improvement.
 - **Motivation for HELIX's approach**: To tackle this, the system should enable *exploration based on the "shoulder of giants"*, meaning it must iteratively build upon existing high-quality solutions. This allows for progressive advancement by leveraging prior discoveries as foundations for new insights and more sophisticated solutions.

To fulfill these objectives, HELIX introduces two primary novelties:
- The creation of a diverse yet high-quality pool of candidate solutions, which is intended to broaden exploration through in-context learning.
- The application of reinforcement learning for iterative policy refinement, designed to progressively elevate the quality of generated solutions.

The synergy between these components is intended to allow for the discovery of more advanced solutions than current methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HELIX is presented as a hybrid framework that integrates reinforcement learning (RL) with evolutionary search, designed to enable LLMs to explore large and flexible solution spaces efficiently while maintaining diversity and leveraging high-quality past solutions. The framework comprises three interacting modules:

1. **Reinforcement Learning Framework**: This module updates the LLM's policy parameters based on verifiable reward signals. The objective is to progressively improve the LLM’s reasoning capability and the quality of generated solutions. The candidate solutions generated by the model form a population for evolutionary processes.

2. **Multi-Objective Evolutionary Mechanism**: This component balances solution quality and diversity within the population. It is inspired by NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II) to ensure that both high-performing and diverse candidates are retained for future expansion.

3. **In-Context Learning Mechanism**: This module constructs prompts for the LLM by incorporating multiple past trials. This enables the model to build upon previously discovered solutions, leveraging a "shoulder of giants" approach for exploration.

**Problem Formulation**:
The task is defined as an optimization problem where `s ∈ S` represents a candidate solution (e.g., code in Python or a Domain-Specific Language (DSL)). The objective is to maximize a reward function `R(s)`. The LLM, parameterized by policy `π_θ`, iteratively mutates or improves current solutions. Given a solution `s_t` from the candidate pool `P_t`, the LLM generates an action `a_t` which modifies `s_t` to `s_{t+1}`. The goal is to maximize the expected reward of these new solutions.

**Policy Optimization Aligned with Evolutionary Search**:
HELIX updates the LLM policy `π_θ` using a reinforcement learning algorithm called Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024). GRPO is selected for its effectiveness in enhancing LLM reasoning and its ability to generate diverse multi-sample outputs, which contribute to the evolutionary dataset.

The GRPO objective (Equation 4) involves:
- Expected value over sampled solutions `s_t` and generated actions `a_j`.
- A clipped policy ratio `r_j,k(θ)` (ratio of new to old policy probabilities for a token `a_j,k`).
- A token-level advantage `A_j,k`, normalized by the mean and standard deviation of rewards within a group of rollouts `G`.
- A KL divergence penalty `D_KL(π_θ || π_ref)` against a reference policy `π_ref`, controlled by `β`, to maintain policy stability.

The prompt `q` (Equation 5) for the LLM is constructed to leverage in-context learning:
- It includes the `problem description (p)`.
- It provides the `current solution (s_t)`, its `reward R(s_t)`, and `auxiliary feedback F(s_t)`.
- Crucially, it incorporates `n-1` *ancestral solutions* `f^(k)(s_t)` from the lineage tree of `s_t`, along with their respective `rewards R(f^(k)(s_t))` and `feedback F(f^(k)(s_t))`. This lineage traces the iterative refinement of solutions, allowing the model to learn from past successes and failures.

**Evolutionary Mechanism for Balancing Quality and Diversity**:
To address the exploration-exploitation trade-off and prevent entropy collapse, HELIX integrates an evolutionary algorithm, specifically NSGA-II (Deb et al., 2002), for selecting candidate solutions. This mechanism maintains a population that improves in reward while preserving diversity.

- **Diversity Measurement**:
 - Each solution (code) is normalized into a canonical format and encoded into an embedding vector using a pretrained language embedding model.
 - The diversity score `Div(s_i)` for a solution `s_i` is computed as `1 - (average cosine similarity to its k-nearest neighbors)` in the embedding space (Equation 6). A higher `Div` indicates greater distinctness.
- **NSGA-II Based Selection**:
 - Each candidate solution is mapped to a two-dimensional objective space based on its `reward R(s)` and `diversity score Div(s)`.
 - NSGA-II applies a nondominated sorting procedure to partition solutions into Pareto fronts, where `s_a` dominates `s_b` if it is superior in at least one objective and no worse in others.
 - Within each front, a crowding-distance measure is computed to select representative samples that are well-spread in the objective space, further ensuring diversity.
The overall workflow (Algorithm 1, Figure 2) involves iterating these steps: samples are drawn from the current population, prompts are constructed, the LLM generates modifications, these new solutions are evaluated for reward and diversity, and NSGA-II selects the next generation's population. Concurrently, the collected data is used to update the LLM's policy via GRPO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HELIX was evaluated on 20 tasks across five categories: Machine Learning, Physics Simulation, Circle Packing, Function Minimization, and Symbolic Regression. These tasks were chosen for their unbounded, open-ended, and domain-specific characteristics. The primary models used were DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (14B for most tasks, 32B for physics simulation). Baselines included Direct Prompting (test-time scaling with 64 trials), Open Evolve (an AlphaEvolve reproduction), task-specific methods, and proprietary models like GPT-4o.

**Overall Performance**:
- HELIX achieved the best performance on 17 of the 20 benchmark tasks, surpassing all competing baselines.
- It consistently outperformed Direct Prompting across all benchmarks under identical model settings, indicating effective policy improvement and in-context learning.
- Against OpenEvolve, HELIX demonstrated superior results on 19 tasks. An in-depth analysis (Appendix E) indicated that naive evolutionary methods often fail due to initialization bias and the rejection of novel, initially "destructive" changes, issues that HELIX's explicit diversity accounting and RL parameter learning address.
- When compared against GPT-4o, including setups with multi-role collaborative reasoning, HELIX outperformed GPT-4o on 18 tasks. This suggests that the framework enables smaller models to effectively leverage prior knowledge through RL, achieving competitive or superior results with potentially lower computational costs.
- Compared to human-crafted task-specific methods, HELIX achieved superior performance on 17 tasks. Notably, it set a new record in the circle packing task with a sum of radii of 2.63598308 using a 14B model and achieved the second-highest score on the Transparent Conductors Kaggle leaderboard (RMSLE of 0.04915), previously derived from human-participation.

**Specific Task Highlights**:
- **Circle Packing**: Achieved a new state-of-the-art result for packing 26 circles in a unit square (sum of radii 2.63598308) and a high sum of radii (4.664465) for packing in a unit disk.
- **Machine Learning**: On the Adult and Bank Marketing datasets, HELIX delivered an average F1 improvement of 5.95 points over GPT-4o with a carefully engineered pipeline. For the Boston Housing dataset, it achieved an RMSE of 1.747, outperforming baselines.
- **Physics Simulation**: Achieved high rewards on Inductor (9.609), Beam Bending (17.298), Magnetic Torque (11.045), and Periodic Heat (1.278) tasks, demonstrating capability in geometric reasoning and physical knowledge application.
- **Function Minimization**: Attained perfect scores (1.000) on Eggholder, Mishra’s Bird, and several Keanes Bump function variants.
- **Symbolic Regression**: Achieved very low Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) values across chemistry, biology, physics, and material science benchmarks, indicating strong ability to uncover mathematical expressions from data.

**Convergence Analysis**:
Analysis on Inductor design and Adult income prediction tasks (Figure 3) showed consistent upward trends in both average reward and validity rate of model outputs over epochs. This indicates that reinforcement learning progressively strengthens the model’s reasoning abilities and that the quality of the evolving population continually improves.

**Ablation Studies**:
Ablation studies on Boston Housing and Circle Packing tasks (Figure 4) confirmed the critical contribution of each HELIX component:
- **Diversity and Quality Balance**: For Circle Packing, variants that eliminated explicit diversity mechanisms (TopScore) performed worse, collapsing into narrow solution modes. Approaches focused solely on diversity (TopDiv, Random) were unstable. This highlighted the necessity of balancing diversity with solution quality for stable optimization and avoiding local optima.
- **Reinforcement Learning and Evolutionary Guidance**: For Boston Housing, disabling either RL (EvoOnly, fixed parameters) or the evolutionary mechanism (TrainOnly, pure GRPO) severely limited performance. This demonstrated that both dynamic policy updates via RL and in-context evolutionary guidance are essential for accumulating expertise and refining solutions over time.

**Scaling Experiments**:
Evaluations with different model sizes (1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B) on Magnetic Torque and Inductor Design tasks (Figure 5) showed that maximum and mean rewards generally increased with model scale. This suggests that HELIX benefits from stronger underlying LLMs, exhibiting a positive scaling property in its ability to push the boundaries of scientific discovery.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HELIX represents an advancement in the application of Large Language Models to complex, open-ended scientific problems. The framework’s demonstrated capabilities underscore its significance across several dimensions:

**Addressing Core Challenges**: The work effectively addresses the inherent difficulties of scientific problem-solving, namely its domain-specific, open-ended, and unbounded nature. By integrating hierarchical reinforcement learning with a multi-objective evolutionary mechanism and in-context learning, HELIX provides a principled approach to balance exploration and exploitation, learn from experience, and build upon prior discoveries.

**Demonstrated Performance**: The consistent outperformance of HELIX against strong task-specific baselines, leading proprietary models like GPT-4o, and even human-crafted solutions (e.g., in the circle packing task where it achieved a new record and in Transparent Conductors where it reached a top leaderboard position) indicates its robustness and efficacy. This suggests a potential for autonomous discovery of novel solutions that can exceed those developed through conventional or specialized methods.

**Efficiency and Resource Utilization**: The ability of HELIX to achieve superior results with comparatively smaller models (e.g., a 14B model outperforming GPT-4o in many tasks) highlights its efficiency. This can lead to more cost-effective and accessible solutions for scientific discovery, especially for research institutions or projects with limited computational resources.

**Advancing Autonomous Research Systems**: By enabling iterative, diversity-aware exploration and continuous policy refinement, HELIX lays a foundation for more sophisticated autonomous research systems. Such systems could independently generate, evaluate, and refine hypotheses or designs in various scientific and engineering domains, potentially accelerating the pace of discovery.

**Theoretical Foundation**: The theoretical analysis provided, modeling the algorithm dynamics through drift-diffusion and selection, offers insights into why HELIX can converge more effectively and concentrate its search around optimal solutions compared to purely evolutionary or RL-only approaches. This theoretical grounding strengthens the understanding of the framework's operational advantages.

**Broader Applications**: The diverse range of tasks successfully tackled—from machine learning optimization and physics simulations to geometric packing and symbolic regression—suggests the generalizability of HELIX. This implies a broad potential impact on various scientific and engineering fields, including materials science, drug discovery, and computational design, where complex optimization and discovery problems are prevalent.

In summary, HELIX offers a promising paradigm for integrating advanced AI capabilities with scientific inquiry, capable of pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in complex, real-world scientific and engineering challenges.
