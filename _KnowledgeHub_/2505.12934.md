# Granular Loco-Manipulation: Repositioning Rocks Through Strategic Sand Avalanche

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Southern California developed DiffusiveGRAIN, a learning-based method that enables a multi-legged robot to strategically reposition rocks on granular slopes by inducing sand avalanches. The system integrates predictors for both environment and robot state changes, achieving an 80% success rate in manipulation tasks and a 70% success rate in combined loco-manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing obstacle-aided locomotion strategies rely on precise contact positions and are limited by the unpredictable distribution of obstacles, often assuming independent obstacle movements.
- Granular manipulation methods often fail to account for multi-obstacle interference, where the movement of one obstacle affects others, especially on dense granular slopes.
- Prior methods typically assume the robot's state remains unaffected by its leg excavation actions, but experiments show these actions significantly alter robot position and orientation, requiring integrated planning.

## Method

- **DiffusiveGRAIN**, a learning-based system, employs two ==U-Net== predictors: one for environment state (a ==diffusion model== predicting sand slope changes and obstacle movements) and another for robot state (predicting robot position and orientation changes).
- An ==Effective Action Adjustment (EAA)== method compensates for differences in sand avalanche behavior between a fixed manipulator (for data collection) and a moving robot by adjusting action representations based on predicted robot state changes.
- A ==receding-horizon planning== approach minimizes task-specific costs over a 4-step horizon, jointly considering locomotion (robot CoM to target, safety) and manipulation (obstacle to target) goals.

## Results

- In manipulation tasks, DiffusiveGRAIN achieved an **80%** success rate with an average obstacle displacement error (MAE) of **2.03 cm**, outperforming the GRAIN baseline (**60%** success, **2.71 cm** MAE).
- For combined loco-manipulation tasks, DiffusiveGRAIN achieved a **70%** success rate with an MAE of **2.31 cm**, a substantial improvement over the GRAIN baseline's **20%** success rate and **3.24 cm** MAE.
- The method demonstrated robustness and generalization by maintaining high success rates (**65-70%**) and low prediction errors (around **2.6 cm** MAE) when tested with out-of-distribution real rocks and varying slope angles (16 and 24 degrees) without fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The movement of multiple obstacles on granular slopes is not independent; proximity to other obstacles, particularly along the slope, significantly reduces individual obstacle displacement.
> - Robot leg excavation actions are not merely environmental modifications but also induce considerable changes in the robot's own position and orientation, necessitating a coupled prediction and planning strategy.
> - Decoupling environment and robot state prediction into separate U-Net models within DiffusiveGRAIN proved more effective than an end-to-end approach or pose-only inputs for accurately modeling complex granular and robot dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Terrestrial robot locomotion in natural environments, characterized by deformable sand, steep inclines, and varied obstacles such as rocks and boulders, presents considerable challenges for autonomous systems. A significant area of ongoing research explores bio-inspired "obstacle-aided locomotion" strategies. These methods enable robots to utilize interactions and collisions with large environmental features to enhance mobility on complex terrains, such offering potential for navigation on challenging landscapes.

However, existing obstacle-aided locomotion strategies exhibit certain limitations. Their effectiveness often relies on precise leg-obstacle contact positions; incorrect contacts can lead to failures such as slipping, the robot becoming stuck, or even overturning. This dependency means that performance is highly contingent on the unpredictable availability and spatial distribution of rocks and boulders in natural environments.

Recent advancements have begun to explore indirect manipulation of obstacles. A prior study demonstrated that a robotic leg could strategically induce localized sand avalanches, generating granular flow to reposition rocks to desired locations. This introduced a new paradigm for environmental modification. While promising, most granular manipulation methods published to date primarily address homogeneous granular media or operate under assumptions that obstacle movements are independent of both robot locomotion and the presence of other obstacles. The application of granular flow-based manipulation to locomoting robots operating on substrates with densely-distributed obstacles remained largely unexplored.

This research addresses these gaps by investigating multi-obstacle manipulation on granular slopes using a locomoting multi-legged robot. It extends the concept of indirect obstacle manipulation to scenarios involving multiple obstacles and integrates this capability with the robot's locomotion, acknowledging the coupled nature of robot and environment state changes. This work contributes to the field by providing a method for robots to actively modify their environment for improved traversability, moving beyond passive adaptation to the terrain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that enables a multi-legged robot to efficiently reposition obstacles (rocks) to desired locations on granular slopes by strategically inducing localized sand avalanches. This capability is intended to enhance the robot's mobility in challenging natural environments where obstacle distribution might otherwise impede progress.

The authors identify two critical challenges that motivate their approach, stemming from preliminary experiments that evaluated the limitations of existing granular manipulation methods, specifically the GRAIN framework, in multi-obstacle, multi-leg scenarios:

1. **Interference in Multi-Obstacle Movement:** Prior work, such as GRAIN, often assumes that the movement of individual obstacles under granular flow is independent and unaffected by nearby obstacles or the granular flow they induce. Preliminary experiments conducted by the authors revealed that when obstacles are closely spaced on a sand slope, their movements exhibit significant interference. Specifically, the displacement of an obstacle was observed to decrease when adjacent obstacles were present, particularly when positioned in the direction of sand inclination. This indicated that an approach capable of modeling and resolving simultaneous movements of multiple, interacting obstacles under leg excavation was necessary.

2. **Coupled Robot State and Environment Changes:** Existing granular manipulation methods often assume that a robot's state (its position and orientation) remains largely unaffected by its leg excavation actions. However, the authors' experiments with different multi-leg excavation patterns demonstrated that these actions can substantially alter the robot's own state, causing changes in its fore-aft position, lateral position, and orientation. For instance, certain actions (e.g., "All Four") resulted in significant fore-aft displacement, while others (e.g., "Right Pair", "Left Pair") primarily affected orientation and lateral position. Such state changes can impact subsequent manipulation possibilities and overall locomotion. Therefore, a method that can predict and plan for both environmental changes (obstacle repositioning) and corresponding robot state changes in an integrated manner is motivated.

To address these challenges, the authors propose DiffusiveGRAIN, a learning-based method with the following specific objectives:
- To develop a novel manipulation predictor that uses a U-Net with a diffusion model to accurately forecast avalanche dynamics and obstacle movements on a sand slope, specifically accounting for multi-obstacle interactions.
- To create a novel locomotion predictor, also based on a U-Net, that estimates the robot's state change on a sand slope in response to its multi-leg excavation actions.
- To integrate these two predictors to enable a legged robot to effectively plan and execute loco-manipulation tasks, where both desired robot movement and obstacle repositioning goals are achieved collaboratively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the presented methodology is DiffusiveGRAIN, a learning-based system designed to enable multi-legged robots to perform granular loco-manipulation tasks.

**Experiment Setup:**
Experiments were conducted on a granular trackway (60 cm L × 60 cm W × 20 cm D) filled with 0.3 mm glass beads, which model natural sand. The trackway could be tilted up to 35 degrees, and a slope angle of 20 degrees was primarily used to facilitate the study of avalanche dynamics. A top-down RealSense 435i RGBD camera recorded granular flow and obstacle movement.

**Manipulator vs. Robot:**
To enable scalable data collection for avalanche dynamics and obstacle movement, a gantry system with two C-shape robot leg motors (6.0 cm diameter, 1.5 cm width) was used. This "manipulator" could execute the same excavation actions as the actual quadrupedal robot's front legs but operated from a fixed gantry, allowing for controlled and safe data collection. The quadrupedal robot, weighing 849.1g, was used for deployment experiments.

**Action Space:**
The robot's action space comprised six discrete multi-leg excavation actions, each involving specific combinations of the four legs rotating backward at a constant angular speed of 1π rad/s: Left Front Excavation (LFE), Right Front Excavation (RFE), Left Pair (LP), Right Pair (RP), Front Pair (FP), and All Four (AF).

**State Representation:**
The environment state at time `t` was represented by a depth image `I_t`. The robot's state `x_t` was a 3D vector comprising its 2D position and 1D orientation. Obstacle states `o_t` were represented as 2D positions for each obstacle. Robot actions `A_t` were represented as RGB images using a gradient color region to indicate the leg's interaction area with the granular slope.

**DiffusiveGRAIN Architecture:**
DiffusiveGRAIN consists of two main predictors:
1. **Environment State Predictor (`f_e`):** This model is a U-Net based diffusion model. It takes the current depth image (`I_t`) and the RGB action image (`A_t`) as input. Its output is a predicted change in the granular slope surface depth image (`f_e(I_t, A_t)`). This predicted change is then added to the original depth image to forecast the next environment state (`I_t + f_e(I_t, A_t)`). The model was trained using data collected from the gantry manipulator with multiple obstacles.
2. **Robot State Predictor (`f_r`):** This is a separate U-Net model. It also takes the depth image (`I_t`) and the RGB action image (`A_t`) as input. Its output is the predicted robot state change (`f_r(I_t, A_t)`). This model was trained on data collected from the quadruped robot. The training data for `f_r` was augmented with additional obstacles in the depth images to improve robustness, under the observation that obstacles do not significantly affect robot state change unless directly contacted.

**Effective Action Adjustment (EAA):**
To compensate for the difference in sand avalanche behavior triggered by a fixed manipulator versus a moving robot (where leg excavations cause robot state changes), an Effective Action Adjustment (EAA) method was introduced. During policy execution, `f_r` first predicts the robot's final state `x_2` after an action. The EAA then adjusts the action image `A_t` by effectively re-centering the action representation based on an assumed intermediate robot state `x_1 = (x_0 + x_2) / 2`, where `x_0` is the initial robot state. This adjusted action representation (`A'_t`) is then passed to the environment predictor `f_e`.

**Cost Functions:**
Task-specific cost functions guided the robot's planning:
- **Locomotion Cost (`C_l`):** A weighted sum of the Euclidean distance between the robot's center of mass (CoM) and its target location (`C_rt`), and a robot safety cost (`C_rs`). The safety cost penalized obstacles within a defined "danger zone" based on their distance and relative angle to the robot's heading.
- **Manipulation Cost (`C_m`):** A weighted sum of the Euclidean distance between obstacle positions and their respective targets (`C_o`), and the same robot safety cost (`C_rs`).
- **Loco-Manipulation Cost (`C_lo`):** A linear combination of the locomotion and manipulation costs (`C_l` and `C_m`) for integrated tasks.

**Policy Planning and Execution:**
A receding-horizon planning method was employed, minimizing the cumulative cost over a 4-step horizon with a discount factor (`γ = 0.8`). During deployment, the robot iterates through all possible 4-step action sequences. For each sequence, `f_r` predicts robot state changes, EAA adjusts the action representation, `f_e` predicts environment changes, and then these predictions are combined to form the predicted next depth image (`I_hat_t+1`). This allows for joint consideration of locomotion and manipulation goals.

**Data Collection:**
Training data was collected in two phases: 240 trials (24,590 images) for leg manipulation using the gantry system, covering various obstacle orientations and manipulator actions; and 60 trials (13,480 images) for robot locomotion using the quadrupedal robot, covering various robot orientations and multi-leg actions.

**Baselines:**
Performance was compared against several baselines:
- **GRAIN:** Adapted for multi-obstacle and robot state prediction.
- **E2E-Diffusion:** A single diffusion model attempting to predict both environment and robot state changes end-to-end.
- **Pose-Diffusion:** A network that used only robot and obstacle pose vectors as input to predict future poses.
- **DiffusiveGRAIN (without EAA):** An ablation of the proposed method to assess the impact of the Effective Action Adjustment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings, both from preliminary investigations validating the need for the proposed method and from comprehensive evaluations of DiffusiveGRAIN.

**Preliminary Experiments:**
- **Multi-obstacle Interference:** Controlled experiments varying the distance between two obstacles demonstrated that their movements were not independent. As the distance between obstacles decreased, particularly in the fore-aft direction (along the slope inclination), the displacement of an obstacle was observed to reduce significantly (to 67% and 42% compared to a single obstacle). This confirmed the inadequacy of models like GRAIN, which assume independent obstacle dynamics.
- **Robot State Changes from Multi-leg Actions:** Investigations into the effects of different multi-leg excavation actions showed varied impacts on robot state. The "All Four" (AF) action resulted in the largest fore-aft displacement of the robot. "Right Pair" (RP) and "Left Pair" (LP) actions caused significant changes in robot orientation and lateral position, while having less effect on fore-aft movement. "Front Pair" (FP), "Left Front Excavation" (LFE), and "Right Front Excavation" (RFE) triggered avalanches with minimal robot position change. These results underscored the necessity for integrated planning of robot locomotion and manipulation.

**Evaluation of DiffusiveGRAIN:**
DiffusiveGRAIN was evaluated against baselines across manipulation, locomotion, and combined loco-manipulation tasks, using Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in centimeters and success rates.

- **Manipulation Task:**
 - DiffusiveGRAIN achieved an 80% success rate (8 out of 10 trials) with an MAE of 2.03 cm (±0.84).
 - The GRAIN baseline achieved a 60% success rate (6 out of 10 trials) with an MAE of 2.71 cm (±0.91).
 - This improvement suggests that incorporating training data that accounts for multiple obstacle interactions is beneficial.

- **Locomotion Task:**
 - DiffusiveGRAIN achieved a 90% success rate (9 out of 10 trials) with an MAE of 1.21 cm (±0.38).
 - The GRAIN baseline achieved an 80% success rate (8 out of 10 trials) with an MAE of 1.34 cm (±0.46).

- **Loco-Manipulation Task (In-Distribution):**
 - DiffusiveGRAIN achieved a 70% success rate (7 out of 10 trials) with an MAE of 2.31 cm (±0.98).
 - The GRAIN baseline achieved a 20% success rate (2 out of 10 trials) with an MAE of 3.24 cm (±1.24).
 - This significant performance difference highlights the importance of jointly considering and planning both environment and robot state changes for successful loco-manipulation, particularly as mischosen leg excavations can detrimentally affect future manipulation opportunities.

- **Loco-Manipulation Task (Out-of-Distribution):**
 - To assess generalization, DiffusiveGRAIN was tested with real rocks of varying shapes and angularity, and at different slope inclination angles (16 and 24 degrees in addition to the trained 20 degrees), without fine-tuning.
 - The method maintained high success rates (65-70%) and low prediction errors across these varied conditions (e.g., MAE of 2.61 cm at 16 degrees, 2.57 cm at 24 degrees), indicating robustness and generalization capability to unseen environmental variations and obstacles.

**Ablation Studies and Baseline Comparisons (Appendix C):**
- **DiffusiveGRAIN (without EAA):** This ablation resulted in an MAE of 2.80 cm (±0.84) for obstacle state prediction, which was higher than the full DiffusiveGRAIN (2.44 cm). This supports the efficacy of the Effective Action Adjustment in improving prediction accuracy by accounting for robot state changes during excavation.
- **E2E-Diffusion:** This end-to-end diffusion model showed significantly lower performance (Obstacle MAE 3.81 cm ±1.25, Robot Position MAE 2.25 cm ±0.96). This suggests that decoupling the environment and robot state predictors, as done in DiffusiveGRAIN, aids in learning complex dynamics, possibly due to increased data efficiency when learning less complex sub-problems.
- **Pose-Diffusion:** This model, relying solely on pose inputs, performed the worst (Obstacle MAE 5.04 cm ±1.66, Robot Position MAE 2.94 cm ±1.03). This indicates that raw depth image information, captured by the U-Net backbones, is crucial for modeling granular dynamics and obstacle interactions, which simple pose representations cannot adequately capture.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DiffusiveGRAIN, a method that enables a locomoting legged robot to strategically utilize sand avalanche dynamics for repositioning rocks on granular slopes. This capability represents a significant advancement in robotic interaction with challenging deformable terrains.

The work's primary significance lies in its departure from traditional paradigms where robots passively adapt to their environment. Instead, DiffusiveGRAIN empowers robots to actively manipulate their surroundings to improve their own mobility. This active environmental modification capability can substantially enhance traversability on granular slopes, which are common in natural environments and extraterrestrial landscapes.

The integration of a diffusion-based environment state predictor and a dedicated robot state predictor is a novel contribution. This architecture effectively addresses the previously unaddressed challenges of multi-obstacle interference in granular flow and the dynamic changes in a robot's own state during multi-leg excavation. By planning these coupled effects jointly, the robot can achieve complex loco-manipulation tasks that were not feasible with prior methods. The demonstrated high success rates and generalization capabilities to varied real-world rocks and different slope inclinations, without explicit fine-tuning, underscore the robustness and practical potential of DiffusiveGRAIN.

The potential impact of this research extends to various domains:
- **Enhanced Robotic Mobility:** Robots can gain improved autonomy and effectiveness in navigating highly challenging granular terrains, such as those encountered in planetary exploration, disaster response, and search-and-rescue missions.
- **Indirect Manipulation Strategies:** The study showcases the viability of indirect manipulation through granular flow, opening new avenues for environmental modification where direct interaction with large or complex obstacles is difficult or unsafe.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for future research in granular robotics, particularly in integrating complex physical phenomena with learning-based control for practical applications.

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the vision-focused nature of the model, which does not account for obstacle properties like mass, and the computational intensity of exhaustive policy evaluation for larger problem spaces. Addressing these aspects in future work could further broaden the applicability and efficiency of granular loco-manipulation. Overall, DiffusiveGRAIN represents a step towards robots that can not only move through but also purposefully shape their complex environments.
