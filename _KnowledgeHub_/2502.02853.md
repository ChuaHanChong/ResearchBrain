# Rethinking Latent Redundancy in Behavior Cloning: An Information Bottleneck Approach for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> An Information Bottleneck (IB) approach applied to Behavior Cloning (BC) for robot manipulation mitigates latent representation redundancy, improving policy generalization. This method consistently yields higher success rates across various simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks, particularly for multimodal and multi-task settings.

## Problem

- Existing Behavior Cloning (BC) methods in robot manipulation, despite using large datasets and multimodal inputs, often neglect inherent redundancy in learned latent representations.
- This latent redundancy can hinder policy performance and generalization, especially when dealing with high-dimensional and multimodal sensory inputs.
- Many BC approaches primarily rely on empirical improvements and lack a solid theoretical foundation to guide representation learning strategies.

## Method

- ==The Information Bottleneck (IB)== principle is integrated into Behavior Cloning to reduce latent redundancy by finding a compressed representation that retains maximal information about the target action while minimizing information about the original input.
- The BC-IB objective function combines the standard Behavior Cloning loss with a mutual information regularization term, I(X;Z)I(X; Z)I(X;Z), to explicitly quantify and minimize redundancy.
- Mutual Information Neural Estimation (MINE) is employed to estimate the intractable mutual information, and a unified multimodal input is created by concatenating features from all modalities (visual, proprioceptive, language) before applying the IB bottleneck.

## Results

- Integrating IB consistently improves the success rates of various BC methods across diverse simulation benchmarks (CortexBench, LIBERO) and real-world robot manipulation tasks.
- Mutual information visualizations demonstrate that BC+IB significantly reduces I(X,Z)I(X, Z)I(X,Z) (information from input to latent representation) compared to vanilla BC, correlating with higher task success rates.
- The method proves effective in few-shot learning scenarios and improves model focus on task-relevant features in attention maps, while also showing a **6%** improvement for strong Diffusion Policy baselines on complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent representation redundancy is a significant and often unaddressed challenge in Behavior Cloning for robot manipulation, directly impacting model generalization and robustness.
> - The Information Bottleneck principle provides a theoretically grounded framework to explicitly address this redundancy, yielding more compact and task-relevant representations.
> - Applying IB at the intermediate, fused feature level of multimodal inputs is both theoretically justified and practically effective, allowing for unified redundancy reduction across diverse sensory data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Behavior Cloning (BC) is a fundamental and widely used method in Imitation Learning (IL) for robot manipulation, where a policy is learned by mimicking expert demonstrations. The current landscape of BC research largely focuses on two main avenues to improve generalization:
1. **Scaling up data:** Utilizing massive datasets of human or robot videos.
2. **Multimodal learning:** Integrating additional information sources like text, visual trajectories, and 3D visual data.

Prominent works in this area include generalizable visual encoders pre-trained on large video datasets (e.g., VC-1, R3M, Voltron) and large-scale vision-language-action models (e.g., RT-2, GR-2). While these methods have achieved remarkable progress by capturing more diverse information, the authors identify a critical oversight: they often neglect the inherent **redundancy in the learned latent representations** and **lack a solid theoretical foundation** to guide the learning process. This redundancy can hinder policy performance and generalization, especially when inputs are high-dimensional and multimodal.

This paper directly addresses this gap by introducing an **information-theoretic perspective** to quantify and mitigate latent redundancy in BC for robot manipulation. Specifically, it extends the **Information Bottleneck (IB) principle** to this domain. The IB principle, originally proposed by Tishby et al. (1999), aims to find a compressed representation of an input that is maximally informative about a target variable, effectively balancing complexity and predictive power.

Previous applications of IB in robotics learning have primarily focused on Reinforcement Learning (RL) to extract task-relevant features (e.g., for robust self-supervised exploration or to address sim-to-real gaps) or in Imitation Learning from observation histories to mitigate the "copycat" problem. This work distinguishes itself by:
- Being the **first comprehensive study on redundancy in latent representations specifically for Behavior Cloning** in robot manipulation.
- Applying IB in a **multimodal context**, where features from diverse inputs (visual, proprioceptive, language) are concatenated and then compressed, rather than applying IB to individual modalities. This is a crucial practical distinction given the complexity of robotic sensory data.
- Providing **detailed theoretical analyses**, including generalization error bounds, which were largely absent in prior BC methods that focused solely on empirical improvements.

By integrating IB into BC, the authors aim to enhance generalizability and robustness by forcing the model to learn more compact and task-relevant representations, thereby contributing a theoretically grounded approach to address a significant limitation in contemporary imitation learning for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations of this research stem from the identified limitations of existing Behavior Cloning (BC) methods in robot manipulation.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Quantify Latent Redundancy:** To formally characterize and measure the amount of redundant information present in the latent representations learned by BC policies. This is achieved using the concept of mutual information, specifically I(X, Z), which quantifies the information shared between the high-dimensional input representation (X) and its compressed latent representation (Z).
2. **Mitigate Latent Redundancy:** To develop a theoretically grounded approach to reduce this redundancy. The Information Bottleneck (IB) principle is proposed for this purpose, aiming to compress irrelevant information from the input while preserving task-relevant features necessary for predicting actions.
3. **Enhance Generalization and Performance:** To empirically demonstrate that reducing latent redundancy through the IB principle leads to consistent improvements in generalization performance across various robot manipulation tasks, backbones, and experimental settings.
4. **Provide Theoretical Foundation:** To offer a solid theoretical analysis, including generalization error bounds, to explain *why* the Information Bottleneck approach enhances the transferability and robustness of BC methods.
5. **Study IB Applicability:** To investigate how IB can be effectively applied to different BC architectures, particularly those employing spatial and temporal feature fusion strategies, and to explore its benefits across different stages of learning (pre-training, fine-tuning) and dataset sizes (full-shot, few-shot).

#### 2.2. Motivation

- **Unexplored Challenge:** Despite significant advancements in BC through large datasets and multimodal inputs, the inherent challenges of **input data redundancy in latent representations remain largely unexplored** in robot manipulation. High-dimensional sensory data often contains irrelevant or repetitive information that can make learning inefficient and hinder the model's ability to generalize to new, unseen situations.
- **Lack of Theoretical Grounding:** Many existing BC methods are primarily empirical, lacking a **solid theoretical foundation** to guide the design of learning objectives and representation learning strategies. This makes it difficult to understand *why* certain methods work or to systematically improve them. The information-theoretic perspective offers a rigorous framework to address this.
- **Improved Robustness and Efficiency:** Redundant representations can lead to overfitting, require larger models, and make policies less robust to variations in the environment. By compressing these representations, models can learn more efficient and robust features, leading to better performance, especially in real-world scenarios where data is noisy and demonstrations might be limited.
- **Practical Applicability:** The authors are motivated to develop a method that is applicable across a broad range of existing BC architectures and can enhance their performance without requiring fundamental changes to the underlying policy networks.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to make Behavior Cloning more principled, efficient, and robust by explicitly tackling the problem of information redundancy in its latent representations using the powerful framework of the Information Bottleneck principle.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around integrating the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle into Behavior Cloning (BC) to address the issue of latent representation redundancy.

#### 3.1. Behavior Cloning Problem Setting

The paper frames BC within the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, where a policy $\pi$ maps states $x_t = (o_t, s_t, l)$ (visual observation, proprioceptive state, optional language instruction) to actions $a_t$. The standard BC objective is to minimize the mean squared error (MSE) between predicted actions and expert actions: $L_{BC} = E_{(x_t,a_t)\sim D_e} [||\pi(x_t) - a_t||^2]$.

#### 3.2. Information Bottleneck Principle Integration

The IB principle aims to find a compressed representation $Z$ of an input $X$ that retains maximal information about a target variable $A$. This is achieved by minimizing a Lagrangian:
$L = \beta I(X; Z) - I(Z; A)$
where:
- $I(X; Z)$ is the mutual information between the input $X$ and the latent representation $Z$, which quantifies the amount of information $Z$ preserves from $X$. Minimizing this term encourages compression and redundancy reduction.
- $I(Z; A)$ is the mutual information between the latent representation $Z$ and the action $A$, which quantifies how much information $Z$ contains about $A$. Maximizing this term ensures predictive power is maintained.
- $\beta$ is a Lagrange multiplier balancing compression and predictive power.

In the context of BC, the standard MSE loss implicitly maximizes $I(Z; A)$ by training $Z$ to predict $A$. Thus, the modified BC-IB objective becomes:
$L_{BC-IB} = E_{(x_t,a_t)\sim D_e} [\beta I(x_t, z_t) + ||\pi(x_t) - a_t||^2]$
Here, $z_t = F(x_t)$ where $F(\cdot)$ is the fusion module. A positive $\beta$ value actively reduces the information from $X$ to $Z$.

#### 3.3. Mutual Information Estimation

Directly calculating mutual information is often intractable. The paper employs **Mutual Information Neural Estimation (MINE)**, a neural-network-based approach that estimates a lower bound of mutual information. MINE trains a discriminator network $T_\theta: X \times Z \to \mathbb{R}$ to distinguish between samples from the joint distribution $P_{XZ}$ and the product of marginals $P_X \otimes P_Z$. The MINE objective is:
$I(X; Z) \ge E_{P_{XZ}} [T_\theta(x, z)] - \log E_{P_X \otimes P_Z} [e^{T_\theta(x,z)}]$
For $P_X \otimes P_Z$, samples are shuffled along the batch axis to create decorrelated pairs.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture and Input Representation

A critical design choice is how the input $X$ is formed for the IB principle, especially given the multimodal nature of robot manipulation data. Instead of applying IB to each modality separately (e.g., $O \to Z \to A$), which would be complex and unable to capture cross-modal associations, the authors propose concatenating features extracted from **all modalities** into a unified input $X$.
$x_t = \text{concat}(\text{Enc}_o(o_t), \text{Enc}_s(s_t), \text{Enc}_l(l))$
This forms an information flow $O \to X \to Z \to A$, offering advantages like unified redundancy reduction and better scalability.

The paper categorizes BC methods based on their **feature fusion strategies** from $X$ to $Z$:
- **Spatial Fusion:** Processes aggregated features at a given time step or concatenates features across multiple time steps along feature dimensions. Implemented using MLPs, CNNs, or Spatial Transformers. Primarily used with pre-trained visual encoders.
- **Temporal Fusion:** Captures dynamic relationships and dependencies across time steps using RNNs, LSTMs, or Temporal Transformers. Commonly used with Transformer-based backbones.
The latent representation $Z$ from these fusion modules is then passed to an MLP-based policy head to generate actions.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical backing for its approach:
- **Theorem 4.1 (adapted from Shwartz-Ziv et al., 2019):** Shows that the generalization error in supervised learning (like BC) is upper-bounded by $2I(X; Z) + \log(2/\delta) / (2n)$. Minimizing $I(X; Z)$ directly tightens this bound.
- **Theorem 4.2 (adapted from Kawaguchi et al., 2023):** Provides an approximate bound on generalization error that is positively correlated with $I(X; Z | A)$ and $I(\phi_S; S)$, where $\phi_S$ is the encoder from $X$ to $Z$. This supports the idea of compressing $X$ into $Z$ while preserving $A$-relevant information.
- **Theorem 4.3 (Novel contribution):** Justifies applying IB at the intermediate feature level $X$ (composed of concatenated modality features) rather than the raw observation $O$. It proves that if $X$ preserves the essential structure of $O$, optimizing $I(X; Z)$ is effectively controlling $I(O; Z)$ with a bounded difference. This is crucial for the practical multimodal input design.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Benchmarks:**
 - **CortexBench:** Single-task benchmark covering 14 tasks across Adroit, Meta-World, DMControl, and TriFinger.
 - **LIBERO:** Language-conditioned multi-task benchmark with 40 tasks across LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Spatial, and LIBERO-Long suites.
- **Real-world Evaluation:** A 6-DOF UR5 arm with Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and RealSense L515 camera. Tasks include single-task (Pick, Put) and challenging language-conditioned multi-task (Pick and Place with distractors/randomization).
- **Baselines:** A wide range of visual imitation learning models (R3M, Voltron, VC-1, MPI, ResNet, ViT) for CortexBench; vision-language policy networks (BC-MLP, BC-RNN, BC-Transformer, BC-VILT) for LIBERO; and VC-1 and CogAct for real-world tests. All baselines are augmented with IB (denoted as BC+IB).
- **Implementation Details:** Specifics on training epochs, learning rates, batch sizes, optimizers, and data augmentation are provided, including a detailed setup for the MINE model and the Lagrange multiplier $\beta$ (typically between $1e-4$ and $1e-2$).

This comprehensive methodology, combining theoretical grounding with extensive empirical validation across diverse settings, provides strong support for the proposed Information Bottleneck approach in Behavior Cloning for robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments across simulation and real-world benchmarks yield several consistent and significant findings:

1. **Consistent Performance Improvements with IB:** The most prominent finding is that incorporating the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle consistently improves the success rate of various Behavior Cloning (BC) methods. This holds true across different visual backbones (ResNet, ViT), fusion architectures (spatial and temporal), and experimental settings (CortexBench, LIBERO, real-world).
 - **CortexBench:** All vanilla BC methods, whether using full fine-tuning (ResNet, ViT) or partial fine-tuning (R3M, Voltron, VC-1, MPI), show improved performance when augmented with IB. For instance, ResNet+IB improved by 10.01% on DMControl, and VC-1+IB gained 4.80% on Meta-World.
 - **LIBERO:** In complex language-conditioned multi-task scenarios, all IB-enhanced baselines consistently exhibit performance gains across all suites. BC-VILT+IB, for example, achieved 7.66% and 9.00% gains on LIBERO-Goal and LIBERO-Object respectively.
 - **Real-world Experiments:** IB consistently improves success rates in both single-task (VC1+IB vs. VC1) and language-conditioned multi-task (CogAct+IB vs. CogAct) real-world settings, demonstrating enhanced generalization capabilities for unseen object-bowl combinations.

2. **Evidence of Latent Redundancy:** The consistent improvements observed with IB indirectly highlight the considerable redundancy present in current robot trajectory datasets and the latent representations learned by existing BC models. By reducing $I(X, Z)$, the models achieve better performance, validating the premise that redundant information was indeed hindering generalization. Visualizations of $I(X, Z)$ confirm that BC+IB significantly reduces mutual information compared to vanilla BC, correlating with higher success rates (e.g., a 7.7% success rate increase on LIBERO-Goal while reducing $I(X, Z)$ to one-quarter).

3. **Effectiveness of Fusion Methods:**
 - **Spatial Fusion:** Found to be more efficient and effective for *simpler single-task scenarios* (e.g., CortexBench), with faster loss reduction.
 - **Temporal Fusion (especially Temporal Transformers):** Proved more effective for *complex language-conditioned multi-task scenarios* with longer history and multiple input modalities (e.g., LIBERO). Temporal Transformers (BC-Transformer, BC-VILT) showed over 30% higher average success rates than MLP or RNN-based fusion in LIBERO, indicating their superior ability to capture dynamic dependencies in intricate data.

4. **Impact of Lagrange Multiplier ($\beta$):** The Lagrange multiplier $\beta$ is crucial for balancing compression and predictive power. Experiments showed that while an optimal $\beta$ value can lead to peak performance, values around $1e-4$ to $1e-2$ consistently yield stable improvements across different settings.

5. **Effectiveness in Few-Shot Settings:** IB proves effective even with limited demonstrations. In few-shot scenarios on LIBERO, BC-VILT+IB consistently outperformed vanilla BC-VILT, underscoring its potential in real-world applications where data collection is costly.

6. **Insights on Task Complexity:**
 - Minimal improvements in benchmarks like TriFinger are attributed to inherently *limited redundancy* in the visual input (e.g., simple robot arm and single object), suggesting less room for IB to optimize.
 - IB is particularly effective for tasks requiring diverse feature extraction, like distinguishing task objectives (LIBERO-Goal) or objects (LIBERO-Object), where filtering irrelevant information aids generalization.
 - Less pronounced impact on spatial tasks (LIBERO-Spatial) where structural information could be excessively compressed.
 - For highly complex, long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long), the performance bottleneck often lies in the limited capacity of lightweight baseline models. However, even with stronger Diffusion Policy baselines, IB still showed a 6% improvement (78.0% to 84.0%), demonstrating its general utility.

7. **Improved Feature Learning (Attention Maps):** Visualizations of attention maps suggest that IB encourages the model to focus more selectively on task-relevant regions (e.g., robot arm, target object) while suppressing attention to redundant background features, confirming the effective reduction of irrelevant information.

These findings collectively validate the information-theoretic approach, demonstrating that explicitly addressing latent representation redundancy through the Information Bottleneck principle is a powerful strategy for enhancing the generalization and robustness of Behavior Cloning in robot manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes significant contributions to the field of robot manipulation and imitation learning, with substantial potential impact across theoretical and practical domains.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **First Comprehensive Study of Redundancy in BC:** This paper marks the first comprehensive investigation into latent representation redundancy specifically within the context of Behavior Cloning for robot manipulation. This addresses a critical, yet overlooked, aspect of improving BC performance.
- **Information-Theoretic Foundation for BC:** By introducing the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle, the authors provide a rigorous information-theoretic framework to guide representation learning in BC. This moves beyond purely empirical approaches, offering a solid theoretical basis for understanding and designing more robust imitation learning algorithms.
- **Justification for Multimodal Input Compression:** The theoretical analysis, particularly Theorem 4.3, formally justifies the practical approach of concatenating multimodal features (visual, proprioceptive, language) into a unified input $X$ before applying the IB bottleneck. This provides a principled way to handle complex, multimodal sensory data in robotics.
- **Generalization Error Bounds:** By leveraging and adapting generalization bounds related to mutual information, the work strengthens the theoretical understanding of why reducing redundancy can improve a model's ability to generalize to unseen data.

#### 5.2. Practical Significance and Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent empirical improvements across diverse simulation benchmarks (CortexBench, LIBERO) and real-world tasks demonstrate that IB significantly enhances the generalization and robustness of BC policies. This is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **More Efficient Feature Learning:** By forcing models to learn compressed, task-relevant representations, IB can lead to more efficient use of model capacity and potentially reduce the data requirements for achieving high performance, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios. This is vital for robotics, where collecting large, high-quality demonstration datasets can be expensive and time-consuming.
- **Improved Interpretability (Implicitly):** The attention map visualizations suggest that by reducing redundancy, IB helps models focus on salient task-relevant features. While not directly a claim of interpretability, this indicates that the learned representations are more targeted and less influenced by irrelevant background noise, which can indirectly aid in understanding model behavior.
- **Applicability Across Architectures and Settings:** The method's effectiveness across various backbone networks (ResNet, ViT), fusion strategies (spatial, temporal), dataset sizes (full-shot, few-shot), and even different stages of learning (pre-trained vs. fine-tuned models) highlights its broad applicability and potential as a plug-and-play enhancement for existing BC systems.
- **Insights into Data Quality:** The findings implicitly suggest that current robot trajectory datasets often contain considerable redundancy. This insight can guide future data collection and curation efforts, encouraging the creation of more information-dense and less redundant datasets.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work is positioned to inspire further research into integrating information-theoretic principles into robotics, not merely as an optimization tool but as a guiding framework for fundamental theoretical understanding and innovative model design. Future work could explore scaling IB to larger Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, integrating it with more advanced policy heads (e.g., transformer-based or diffusion policies), and systematically evaluating its robustness to extreme domain shifts.

In conclusion, "Rethinking Latent Redundancy in Behavior Cloning" offers a powerful and principled approach to improving robot manipulation policies. By addressing a fundamental limitation in current BC methods with a strong theoretical foundation and extensive empirical validation, it significantly advances the state-of-the-art in imitation learning for robotics, paving the way for more robust, generalizable, and efficient robot behaviors in complex real-world scenarios.
