# Rethinking Latent Redundancy in Behavior Cloning: An Information Bottleneck Approach for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> An Information Bottleneck (IB) approach applied to Behavior Cloning (BC) for robot manipulation mitigates latent representation redundancy, improving policy generalization. This method consistently yields higher success rates across various simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks, particularly for multimodal and multi-task settings.

## Problem

- Existing Behavior Cloning (BC) methods in robot manipulation, despite using large datasets and multimodal inputs, often neglect inherent redundancy in learned latent representations.
- This latent redundancy can hinder policy performance and generalization, especially when dealing with high-dimensional and multimodal sensory inputs.
- Many BC approaches primarily rely on empirical improvements and lack a solid theoretical foundation to guide representation learning strategies.

## Method

- ==The Information Bottleneck (IB)== principle is integrated into Behavior Cloning to reduce latent redundancy by finding a compressed representation that retains maximal information about the target action while minimizing information about the original input.
- The BC-IB objective function combines the standard Behavior Cloning loss with a mutual information regularization term, I(X;Z)I(X; Z)I(X;Z), to explicitly quantify and minimize redundancy.
- Mutual Information Neural Estimation (MINE) is employed to estimate the intractable mutual information, and a unified multimodal input is created by concatenating features from all modalities (visual, proprioceptive, language) before applying the IB bottleneck.

## Results

- Integrating IB consistently improves the success rates of various BC methods across diverse simulation benchmarks (CortexBench, LIBERO) and real-world robot manipulation tasks.
- Mutual information visualizations demonstrate that BC+IB significantly reduces I(X,Z)I(X, Z)I(X,Z) (information from input to latent representation) compared to vanilla BC, correlating with higher task success rates.
- The method proves effective in few-shot learning scenarios and improves model focus on task-relevant features in attention maps, while also showing a **6%** improvement for strong Diffusion Policy baselines on complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent representation redundancy is a significant and often unaddressed challenge in Behavior Cloning for robot manipulation, directly impacting model generalization and robustness.
> - The Information Bottleneck principle provides a theoretically grounded framework to explicitly address this redundancy, yielding more compact and task-relevant representations.
> - Applying IB at the intermediate, fused feature level of multimodal inputs is both theoretically justified and practically effective, allowing for unified redundancy reduction across diverse sensory data.
