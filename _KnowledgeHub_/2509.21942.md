# Structural Information-based Hierarchical Diffusion for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beihang University developed SIHD, a hierarchical diffusion framework for offline Reinforcement Learning that adaptively constructs a multi-scale hierarchy from structural information. This framework mitigates extrapolation errors through a structural entropy regularizer and achieved superior average rewards on D4RL benchmarks, including an 8.3% improvement in single-task Maze2D while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Offline RL policy training on static datasets leads to extrapolation errors when encountering out-of-distribution states and actions.
- Current hierarchical diffusion methods rely on rigid, fixed two-layer architectures and single temporal scales, limiting adaptability to diverse temporal patterns and task complexities.
- Existing approaches often depend on local reward predictions for subgoals, which can be unreliable in sparse reward settings, and policies can over-rely on offline data, leading to poor generalization.

## Method

- The **SIHD** framework adaptively constructs a ==multi-scale diffusion hierarchy== by building a similarity-guided topological graph and an optimal structural entropy encoding tree to partition state communities and segment trajectories.
- A conditional diffusion model uses the structural information gain of state communities as a direct conditioning signal for lower layers, reducing dependence on local reward predictions.
- A structural entropy regularizer promotes exploration of underrepresented states while constraining it to low-level communities to prevent significant distributional shifts and mitigate extrapolation errors.

## Results

- SIHD achieved the highest average rewards across D4RL Gym-MuJoCo tasks, demonstrating an average performance improvement of **1.6%** on Medium-Expert and up to **3.9%** on Medium-Replay datasets.
- The framework showed superior decision-making in long-horizon, sparse-reward Maze2D and AntMaze tasks, with average reward improvements of **8.3%** in single-task Maze2D and **4.4%** in AntMaze.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all core modules (multi-scale hierarchy, conditional diffusion, structural entropy regularizer) contribute to performance gains, and SIHD maintained comparable training and planning efficiency to existing hierarchical diffusion baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptively structuring policy hierarchies based on intrinsic structural information in offline trajectories allows for flexible modeling across multiple temporal scales, enhancing performance in complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Leveraging structural information gain as a conditioning signal provides a robust alternative to direct reward predictions for guiding lower-level diffusion processes, particularly beneficial in sparse reward environments.
> - Regularizing exploration using structural entropy encourages the generation of diverse yet constrained behaviors, effectively mitigating overreliance on offline data and reducing extrapolation errors without severe distributional shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL), also known as batch RL, is a paradigm where policies are trained exclusively on pre-collected datasets without further interaction with the environment. This approach is particularly relevant in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, education, and robotic control, where online data collection is costly or infeasible. However, offline RL presents several challenges. Temporal-difference learning methods can suffer from severe extrapolation errors when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) states and actions, which are underrepresented or absent in the static offline datasets. Furthermore, the diverse and often suboptimal nature of historical trajectories leads to a mode-covering challenge, where conventional unimodal policies struggle to capture the multi-modal distribution of optimal behavioral strategies.

Recent advancements have seen the integration of diffusion models into offline RL. Diffusion models, known for their strong generative capabilities, have been employed to build expressive diffusion-based policies and reframe offline sequential decision-making as a trajectory generation task. These models can be conditioned on reward-related signals to generate high-return sequences, thereby mitigating issues like extrapolation errors and poor mode coverage.

Despite these developments, deploying diffusion models in long-horizon tasks remains difficult. Challenges include the exponential increase in value estimate variance over extended trajectories and the high computational cost associated with iterative denoising steps. To address efficiency in long-horizon decision-making, hierarchical policies have been introduced into diffusion-based offline RL. These hierarchical approaches decompose complex tasks into more manageable subproblems guided by intermediate subgoals. Early methods incorporated manually predefined skills into diffusion models, limiting scalability. Subsequent frameworks advanced by learning reward-conditioned subgoals and generating goal-directed trajectories using hierarchical diffusion models, or by utilizing lightweight trajectory segmentation for streamlined subgoal inference.

However, a prevalent limitation in existing hierarchical diffusion methods is their reliance on a fixed two-layer diffusion hierarchy, typically comprising subgoal and action layers, and their assumption of a single predefined temporal scale for trajectory segmentation. Such rigid structures restrict adaptability to diverse temporal patterns and task-specific complexities, thereby limiting decision-making performance and flexibility. Previous research has indicated that multi-layer policy hierarchies, structured from state transition dynamics, can enhance generalization in compositional long-horizon tasks. This observation highlights an open challenge in offline RL: the need for a systematic method to analyze historical trajectories and construct a diffusion hierarchy that is both generalizable and task-aware. This paper addresses this gap by proposing an adaptive, multi-scale hierarchical diffusion framework informed by structural information principles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce SIHD (Structural Information-based Hierarchical Diffusion), a novel framework designed for effective and stable offline policy learning, particularly in challenging long-horizon environments characterized by sparse rewards. The framework aims to overcome several limitations inherent in existing diffusion-based offline RL approaches, especially those with hierarchical structures.

The motivation behind SIHD stems from the following specific challenges identified in the broader research landscape:
1. **Rigid Hierarchical Structures:** Current hierarchical diffusion models typically assume a fixed two-layer architecture (subgoal and action layers) and operate with a single, predefined temporal scale for segmenting trajectories. This rigidity limits their adaptability to tasks with varying temporal patterns and diverse complexities, consequently constraining both decision-making performance and policy flexibility. SIHD aims to address this by adaptively constructing a multi-scale diffusion hierarchy based on the structural information embedded within offline trajectories. This allows for flexible trajectory modeling across multiple temporal scales.
2. **Reliance on Local Reward Predictions for Subgoals:** Existing hierarchical methods often depend on reward predictions from localized sub-trajectories to condition their diffusion processes. This can be problematic in sparse reward settings or when local rewards do not accurately reflect global task objectives. SIHD seeks to mitigate this by quantifying the structural information gain of each state community. This information gain is then utilized as a conditioning signal within the corresponding diffusion layer, thereby reducing the reliance on direct reward predictions over local segments.
3. **Overreliance on Offline Datasets and Extrapolation Errors:** Training policies exclusively on static offline datasets can lead to an overreliance on the observed data distribution. This makes policies susceptible to extrapolation errors when attempting to explore states or actions that are underrepresented or entirely absent in the dataset, leading to poor generalization. To counter this, SIHD introduces a structural entropy regularizer. This regularizer is designed to encourage exploration of underrepresented states within the dataset, thereby reducing overreliance on historical data, while simultaneously constraining this exploration to low-level communities to prevent significant distributional shifts and associated extrapolation errors.

In summary, the key motivation is to develop a more adaptive, robust, and generalizable offline RL framework that can effectively learn policies in complex, long-horizon scenarios with sparse rewards by leveraging novel structural information principles to construct flexible hierarchies and guide exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed SIHD framework integrates three core modules: hierarchy construction, conditional diffusion, and regularized exploration.

#### 3.1. Multi-Scale Diffusion Hierarchy Construction

Unlike methods that employ fixed two-layer hierarchies, SIHD adaptively constructs a multi-scale diffusion hierarchy. This process begins by building a similarity-guided topological graph, $G_s$, over all unique state elements extracted from the offline dataset $D_{\pi_b}$. Weighted edges are established between each state and its top-k nearest neighbors based on feature similarity (e.g., cosine similarity). The parameter $k$ is chosen to maximize the one-dimensional structural entropy ($H_1(G_s)$), promoting structural expressiveness.

Subsequently, the HCSE optimization algorithm is applied to $G_s$ to derive an optimal K-dimensional structural entropy encoding tree, $T^*_s$. This tree provides a tree-structured partitioning of $G_s$ into state communities, where each node $\alpha$ corresponds to a state community $V_\alpha \subseteq S$ at a specific granularity level, and parent-child relationships reflect hierarchical inclusion.

Based on this community partitioning, offline trajectories $\tau_0$ are adaptively segmented. For each diffusion layer $h$, the set of nodes $U_h$ at height $h$ from $T^*_s$ is used to segment $\tau_0$. Each resulting segment $\tau_{h,i}^{sa}$ is a temporally continuous sequence of states belonging to the same community, with its final state designated as a subgoal $g_h^i$. The temporal scale $l_{h,i}^{sa}$ (sequence length) of these segments is inferred from $T^*_s$ and the reference trajectory, rather than being predefined.

The K-layer subgoals extracted from this segmentation hierarchy are integrated into a control-as-inference framework. A subgoal sequence $\tau_g^h$ of length $l_g^h$ is defined for each layer $h$. For a subgoal $g_h^i$, the corresponding lower-layer subgoal sequence $\tau_{g}^{h,i}$ is recursively defined, with the base case ($h=1$) corresponding to the state-action segment $\tau_{sa}^{h,i}$. A binary optimality variable $O_i$ indicates whether a trajectory segment is optimal with respect to a subgoal, modeled by a Boltzmann distribution over cumulative rewards. Theorem 4.1 formalizes the factorization of the conditional probability of a full trajectory $p(\tau_0|y(\tau_0))$ into a hierarchical diffusion process across these dynamic temporal scales and multiple layers. The highest-layer conditioning $y(\tau_g^{K,1})$ captures cumulative reward, while lower-layer $y(\tau_g^{h,i})$ impose subgoal satisfaction constraints via Dirac delta functions.

#### 3.2. Conditional Diffusion Model

A shared diffusion model $\epsilon_{\theta_h}$ is employed for each layer $h$. The forward diffusion process progressively adds Gaussian noise to target sequences $\tau_{g,0}^{h,i}$ (subgoal sequences for $h > 1$, state-action sequences for $h=1$). The reverse process, trained to approximate reverse transitions, uses classifier-free guidance to jointly predict the noise term $\epsilon$ and estimate the reverse-step posterior distribution $p_{\theta_h}(\tau_{g,k-1}^{h,i}|\tau_{g,k}^{h,i})$.

The conditional input $y(\tau_{g,k}^{h,i})$ is defined differently based on the layer. For the highest-level diffusion model, it is the exponential of the cumulative reward over the full trajectory. For lower layers, instead of cumulative rewards, $y(\tau_{g}^{h,i})$ is defined as the structural information gain of the state community (node $\alpha$) in $T^*_s$ to which the states in $\tau_{sa}^{h,i}$ belong. This gain quantifies the information required to infer a local state transition given higher-level subgoal knowledge. To maintain hierarchical consistency, the terminal state of each denoised trajectory is replaced with its corresponding subgoal.

#### 3.3. Structural Entropy Regularizer

To mitigate overreliance on offline datasets and reduce extrapolation errors, a structural entropy-based exploration regularizer is introduced. This involves constructing a new complete weighted state graph $G'_s$ where edge weights reflect diffusion-inferred transition probabilities $p_{\theta_1}(s_{t+1}, s_t)$ from the base-layer diffusion model. The weighted degree of each state in $G'_s$ represents its diffusion-based visitation probability.

The structural entropy of $G'_s$ under $T^*_s$, denoted $H_{T^*_s}(G'_s)$, is computed. Theorem 4.2 provides a variational lower bound for this structural entropy, relating it to the Shannon entropy $H(S)$ of the state distribution $p_{\theta_1}$ over $S$. The regularization objective is to maximize $H(S)$ to promote exploration of underrepresented states, thereby enhancing generative diversity and reducing dependence on historical trajectories. Concurrently, it minimizes $H(U_h)$ (the structural entropy of the community partition at each layer $h$), weighted by $\eta_h$, to preserve the decision-making hierarchy encoded in $T^*_s$. This discourages large deviations from the behavioral policy $\pi_b$ and mitigates extrapolation errors. This regularization is specifically applied to the lowest-level communities ($h=1$). #### 3.4. SIHD Planning

During inference (Algorithm 1), the SIHD planner initializes hierarchical subgoal buffers and the base-layer state-action sequence. In each planning step, it samples an initial noisy latent sequence, revises the top-level goal if its terminal criterion is met, and executes a reverse-diffusion rollout. Within each diffuser step, conditional information (structural information gain) is calculated, and the next latent sequence is sampled. Finally, the base-layer state-action sequence is decoded and updated. After $H$ iterations, the complete action trajectory is output.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SIHD framework was extensively evaluated on the D4RL benchmark, encompassing Gym-MuJoCo tasks (dense rewards, short horizons) and Maze2D and AntMaze tasks (sparse rewards, long horizons).

#### 4.1. Offline Reinforcement Learning (Gym-MuJoCo Tasks)

- SIHD, configured with K=3 diffusion layers, consistently achieved the highest average reward across all Gym-MuJoCo tasks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2D) and datasets (Medium-Expert, Medium, Medium-Replay).
- It outperformed both non-hierarchical baselines (CQL, IQL, Decision Transformer, MoReL, Trajectory Transformer, Diffuser) and hierarchical diffusion baselines (HDMI, Hierarchical Diffuser).
- Specifically, SIHD demonstrated an average performance improvement of 1.6% on the high-quality Medium-Expert dataset. The performance advantage was more pronounced on datasets reflecting average data quality, with improvements of 3.8% on Medium and 3.9% on Medium-Replay datasets. These results suggest that SIHD's adaptive hierarchy and structural entropy-regularized exploration reduce dependence on high-quality offline data.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Planning (Maze2D and AntMaze Tasks)

- Configured with K=4 diffusion layers, SIHD exhibited superior decision-making performance in long-horizon, sparse-reward navigation tasks. It consistently achieved the highest task rewards across all Maze2D and AntMaze datasets, including multi-task variants.
- Average reward improvements were 8.3% in single-task Maze2D, 7.4% in multi-task Maze2D, and 4.4% in AntMaze.
- The more pronounced performance advantage in navigation tasks indicates that a deeper diffusion hierarchy (K=4) effectively supports the long-term decision-making requirements of these environments.
- SIHD also demonstrated greater stability across varying data qualities, showing smaller performance drops compared to the second-best performing Hierarchical Diffuser baseline, attributed to the structural entropy regularizer promoting exploration and generalization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of each core module: multi-scale hierarchy, conditional diffusion, and structural entropy regularizer. The full SIHD framework consistently outperformed variants with disabled components (SIHD-DH, SIHD-CG, SIHD-ER).
- The largest performance degradation was observed when replacing the multi-scale hierarchy with a rigid two-layer, single-scale diffuser (SIHD-DH), particularly in long-horizon tasks. This underscores the critical role of adaptive hierarchy construction.
- An additional ablation study (Table 6) demonstrated that SIHD's performance gains were due to its hierarchical structure rather than merely increased parameter count, as it still outperformed baselines when total parameters were matched.
- Replacing structural entropy-based partitioning with fixed-interval temporal partitioning (SIHD-FT) resulted in a clear performance drop (Table 7), highlighting the importance of structural entropy in identifying key states for effective hierarchical construction.

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency

- SIHD maintained training and planning efficiency comparable to the 2-layer Hierarchical Diffuser baseline, despite incorporating additional diffusion layers.
- It achieved significant reductions in training time (82.5%) and planning time (54.8%) compared to the non-hierarchical Diffuser baseline, preserving the computational benefits of hierarchical diffusion.
- The computational overhead compared to Hierarchical Diffuser was modest and did not scale significantly with longer planning horizons, indicating the practical scalability of the approach.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Comparison

- Visualizations in Maze2D tasks showed that SIHD's adaptive subgoal extraction effectively identified task-critical states, such as turning points, leading to more efficient navigation paths compared to the fixed subgoals of the HDMI baseline.
- Compared to Diffuser and Hierarchical Diffuser, SIHD generated diverse trajectories while substantially reducing repeated exploration of invalid states present in the offline dataset. This improvement was attributed to the structural entropy regularizer, which mitigated overreliance on the offline dataset and enhanced long-horizon decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a significant advancement in offline reinforcement learning by proposing SIHD, a novel hierarchical diffusion framework. The framework's core contributions address critical limitations of existing methods, leading to notable improvements in policy learning.

The significance and potential impact of SIHD include:
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Flexibility:** By leveraging structural information from historical trajectories, SIHD adaptively constructs a multi-scale diffusion hierarchy. This overcomes the rigid, predefined structures and single temporal scales common in previous hierarchical diffusion models, allowing for greater flexibility and adaptability to diverse temporal patterns and task complexities in decision-making. This flexible hierarchy is particularly beneficial for long-horizon tasks.
- **Improved Decision-Making and Generalization:** Extensive evaluations demonstrate that SIHD significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across a range of challenging offline RL tasks, including those with sparse rewards and long planning horizons. The framework's ability to achieve higher average rewards and exhibit superior generalization capabilities suggests a more robust and effective approach to policy learning from static datasets.
- **Novel Conditioning Mechanism:** The quantification of structural information gain within state communities and its use as a conditional signal for diffusion layers represents a novel approach. This reduces reliance on potentially unreliable reward predictions from localized sub-trajectories, especially in sparse reward environments, leading to more stable and effective conditional sequence generation.
- **Mitigated Overreliance and Extrapolation Errors:** The introduction of a structural entropy regularizer is a key innovation for offline RL. By encouraging exploration of underrepresented states while constraining it to low-level communities, SIHD reduces the problematic overreliance on limited offline datasets. This directly addresses the critical issues of extrapolation errors and distributional shifts, making the learned policies more robust and capable of handling states not explicitly seen during training.
- **Broad Applicability:** The advancements presented by SIHD are relevant to high-stakes real-world domains where online interaction is not feasible, such as healthcare (e.g., treatment planning), education (e.g., personalized learning paths), and complex robotic control. The framework’s improved stability and generalization could accelerate the deployment of RL solutions in these areas.
- **Foundational Contributions:** The integration of structural information principles into the design of diffusion models for hierarchical planning provides a new theoretical and algorithmic direction for generative modeling in sequential decision-making.

Future work aims to further refine how subgoal constraints are represented and integrated as conditional information within the diffusion framework, and to extend SIHD to more complex offline RL environments and broader diffusion-based generative modeling domains, potentially expanding its impact beyond the current scope.
