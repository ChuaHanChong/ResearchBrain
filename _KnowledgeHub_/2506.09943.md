# CausalVQA: A Physically Grounded Causal Reasoning Benchmark for Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CausalVQA, a new benchmark from FAIR at Meta, evaluates video models on physically grounded causal reasoning in real-world scenarios. The benchmark, based on egocentric videos, reveals that current state-of-the-art models achieve 61.66% accuracy, lagging human performance by more than 22 percentage points on these tasks.

## Problem

- Current multimodal models demonstrate limited ability to perform physically grounded causal reasoning in real-world video scenarios.
- Existing video question answering (VQA) benchmarks often focus on surface-level perceptual understanding or mental states, lacking deep physical causal relationships.
- Physical reasoning benchmarks typically rely on synthetic environments, which limits their applicability due to a lack of real-world diversity and complexity.

## Method

- Researchers from **FAIR** at Meta introduced **CausalVQA**, a benchmark for evaluating physical causal reasoning using 1,586 items derived from 298 egocentric videos of real-world activities.
- The benchmark focuses on four primary reasoning question types: Counterfactual, Hypothetical, Anticipation, and Planning, in addition to descriptive questions.
- **CausalVQA** employs a sophisticated multi-stage quality control pipeline, including automated distractor generation, language refinement, visual groundedness filtering, and a paired evaluation approach, to ensure robustness against shortcut learning.

## Results

- Humans achieved 84.78% overall paired accuracy on CausalVQA, showing a clear stratification across difficulty levels (95.05% easy, 77.32% medium, 60.05% hard).
- The best-performing model, Gemini 2.5 Flash, attained 61.66% paired accuracy, lagging human performance by over 22 percentage points.
- Models showed particular difficulty with anticipation questions (39.34% average accuracy) and hypothetical questions (39.55% average accuracy), contrasting sharply with human scores of 83.54% and 75.92% respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial performance gap exists between state-of-the-art multimodal models and human performance in physical causal reasoning, especially in complex tasks.
> - Current models struggle with anticipating future outcomes and reasoning about hypothetical scenarios based on physical principles, achieving low accuracy on these question types.
> - The multi-stage quality control pipeline and paired evaluation approach in CausalVQA effectively prevent models from exploiting superficial cues, ensuring genuine causal understanding is tested.
