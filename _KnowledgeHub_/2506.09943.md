# CausalVQA: A Physically Grounded Causal Reasoning Benchmark for Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CausalVQA, a new benchmark from FAIR at Meta, evaluates video models on physically grounded causal reasoning in real-world scenarios. The benchmark, based on egocentric videos, reveals that current state-of-the-art models achieve 61.66% accuracy, lagging human performance by more than 22 percentage points on these tasks.

## Problem

- Current multimodal models demonstrate limited ability to perform physically grounded causal reasoning in real-world video scenarios.
- Existing video question answering (VQA) benchmarks often focus on surface-level perceptual understanding or mental states, lacking deep physical causal relationships.
- Physical reasoning benchmarks typically rely on synthetic environments, which limits their applicability due to a lack of real-world diversity and complexity.

## Method

- Researchers from **FAIR** at Meta introduced **CausalVQA**, a benchmark for evaluating physical causal reasoning using 1,586 items derived from 298 egocentric videos of real-world activities.
- The benchmark focuses on four primary reasoning question types: Counterfactual, Hypothetical, Anticipation, and Planning, in addition to descriptive questions.
- **CausalVQA** employs a sophisticated multi-stage quality control pipeline, including automated distractor generation, language refinement, visual groundedness filtering, and a paired evaluation approach, to ensure robustness against shortcut learning.

## Results

- Humans achieved 84.78% overall paired accuracy on CausalVQA, showing a clear stratification across difficulty levels (95.05% easy, 77.32% medium, 60.05% hard).
- The best-performing model, Gemini 2.5 Flash, attained 61.66% paired accuracy, lagging human performance by over 22 percentage points.
- Models showed particular difficulty with anticipation questions (39.34% average accuracy) and hypothetical questions (39.55% average accuracy), contrasting sharply with human scores of 83.54% and 75.92% respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial performance gap exists between state-of-the-art multimodal models and human performance in physical causal reasoning, especially in complex tasks.
> - Current models struggle with anticipating future outcomes and reasoning about hypothetical scenarios based on physical principles, achieving low accuracy on these question types.
> - The multi-stage quality control pipeline and paired evaluation approach in CausalVQA effectively prevent models from exploiting superficial cues, ensuring genuine causal understanding is tested.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Understanding cause and effect in the physical world is fundamental to human intelligence. When we see a vase teetering on the edge of a table, we instinctively predict it might fall if disturbed. This ability to reason about physical causality enables us to navigate complex environments, anticipate consequences, and make informed decisions. However, despite significant advances in artificial intelligence, current multimodal models struggle with this type of physically grounded causal reasoning in real-world scenarios.

*Distribution of question types across difficulty levels in CausalVQA. The benchmark contains 1,586 items spanning five question categories, with reasoning questions (Anticipation, Planning, Counterfactual, Hypothetical) comprising the majority of the dataset.*

Researchers from FAIR at Meta have introduced CausalVQA, a new benchmark that addresses this critical gap by evaluating how well video models can perform causal reasoning in real-world, physically grounded scenarios. Unlike existing benchmarks that either focus on synthetic environments or lack rigorous causal reasoning requirements, CausalVQA combines the complexity of real-world egocentric videos with strict evaluation of physical causal understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Background and Motivation

The development of CausalVQA stems from a fundamental limitation in current AI evaluation methods. Existing video question answering benchmarks typically fall into two categories, each with significant drawbacks. Traditional VQA datasets like TGIF-QA and ActivityNet-QA focus primarily on surface-level perceptual understanding of real-world videos, asking questions about actions performed or object locations rather than deeper causal relationships. While some benchmarks like NExT-QA incorporate elements of causal reasoning, they often probe mental states and intentions rather than physical principles.

On the other hand, benchmarks specifically designed for physical reasoning, such as PHYRE and CLEVRER, use fully controlled synthetic environments. While these allow for precise evaluation of causal understanding, they lack the diversity and complexity of real-world scenarios, limiting their applicability to practical AI systems.

CausalVQA bridges this gap by creating a benchmark that is both grounded in diverse, real-world scenarios and strictly focused on physical causal reasoning. The benchmark addresses the growing need for AI systems that can understand and predict physical interactions as they move into wearable devices and robotics applications where proactive assistance and consequence prediction are essential.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Dataset Creation Methodology

The creation of CausalVQA involved a sophisticated multi-stage pipeline combining human expertise with automated processing to ensure high quality and robustness against shortcut exploitation.

#### 3.2. Video Selection and Question Types

The benchmark is built upon 298 egocentric videos from the EgoExo4D dataset, totaling approximately 12 hours of footage. These videos were selected for their diversity of familiar real-world scenarios and rich interactions between the camera-wearer and the physical environment, focusing on goal-directed activities like sports and cooking.

The benchmark defines five question types, with primary emphasis on four "reasoning" categories:

- **Counterfactual**: Questions about what could have happened if something had changed
- **Hypothetical**: Questions about alternative possibilities in the same scenario
- **Anticipation**: Predictions about what is likely to happen next based on physics
- **Planning**: Questions about steps needed to achieve objectives
- **Descriptive**: Factual questions about events for comparison purposes

#### 3.3. Multi-Stage Quality Control Pipeline

The dataset creation process involved six critical stages:

1. **Human Question Generation**: Sixteen annotators generated 3,764 initial question-answer pairs following detailed guidelines, with each video reviewed by three different annotators.

2. **Automated Distractor Generation**: A vision-language model created plausible incorrect answer choices by analyzing both the main clip and a post-clip segment.

3. **Language Refinement**: Llama 3.1-70B-Instruct refined questions and answers for consistency while removing words that might hint at correct answers.

4. **Perturbation and Pairing**: Each question was converted into two versions (MCQ and MCQ') with paraphrased distractors and shuffled answer orders, enabling robust evaluation through paired accuracy metrics.

5. **Visual Groundedness Filtering**: A text-only language model attempted to answer questions without video access. Questions answerable from text alone were removed, eliminating approximately 39% of items.

6. **Human Quality Assurance**: Final human review by two annotators per question removed errors and unclear items, resulting in the removal of 53% of remaining questions.

#### 3.4. Benchmark Design and Quality Control

The final CausalVQA benchmark comprises 1,586 items (793 paired questions) across 779 video segments, with an average clip duration of 24.8 seconds. A key innovation is the paired evaluation approach, where models must answer both versions of each question correctly to demonstrate robust understanding rather than exploitation of superficial cues.

#### 3.5. Difficulty Calibration

Human performance data from 273 annotators (15 per item) established empirical difficulty levels:
- **Easy**: >86.67% human agreement (57.2% of items)
- **Medium**: 70-86.67% agreement (26.6% of items)
- **Hard**: 36.67-70% agreement (16.2% of items)

Items where humans disagreed with the ground truth were removed, ensuring the benchmark reflects genuine human intuition about physical causality.

#### 3.6. Robustness Measures

The benchmark includes several features to prevent shortcut exploitation:
- Paired evaluation requiring consistency across linguistic perturbations
- Removal of questions answerable without visual input
- Diverse real-world scenarios preventing memorization of specific patterns
- Multiple-choice format with carefully crafted distractors

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The evaluation reveals a substantial gap between current state-of-the-art models and human performance, particularly in complex causal reasoning tasks.

*Human performance distribution across question types and difficulty levels. Humans demonstrate consistently high performance with clear difficulty stratification, achieving 84.78% overall paired accuracy.*

#### 4.2. Human Baseline Performance

Humans achieved 84.78% overall paired accuracy, with performance varying by difficulty: 95.05% on easy questions, 77.32% on medium, and 60.05% on hard questions. Performance was highest on descriptive questions (90.63%) and lower but still strong on counterfactual (76.57%) and hypothetical (75.92%) questions.

#### 4.3. Model Performance Gap

The highest-performing model, Gemini 2.5 Flash, achieved only 61.66% paired accuracy—more than 22 percentage points below human performance. On reasoning questions specifically, the gap widened to 27 percentage points (53.46% vs 81.43% for humans).

#### 4.4. Question Type Analysis

Models showed particularly poor performance on:
- **Anticipation questions**: 39.34% model average vs 83.54% human performance
- **Hypothetical questions**: 39.55% model average vs 75.92% human performance

This suggests that while models can describe what happens in videos, they struggle significantly with reasoning about alternative outcomes or future possibilities based on physical principles.

*Distribution of video clip durations across question types. The benchmark includes clips of varying lengths, with most questions based on clips under 50 seconds to focus on specific causal reasoning scenarios.*

#### 4.5. Robustness Validation

Ablation studies confirmed the benchmark's resistance to shortcuts:
- **Blind evaluation**: Text-only models achieved only ~25% accuracy
- **Single-frame evaluation**: Models performed similarly poorly (21-26%), indicating that temporal understanding is crucial
- **Paired vs unpaired**: Models scored 10-12% higher on unpaired metrics, validating the robustness of the paired evaluation approach

*Distribution of clip durations across difficulty levels. The benchmark maintains consistent temporal complexity across difficulty categories, ensuring that difficulty stems from reasoning complexity rather than video length.*

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Implications and Future Directions

CausalVQA represents a significant contribution to AI evaluation by exposing fundamental limitations in current multimodal models' understanding of physical causality. The benchmark reveals that despite impressive capabilities in many domains, frontier models lack the intuitive physics understanding that humans take for granted.

#### 5.2. Research Impact

The benchmark's findings highlight several critical areas for future research:

1. **Spatial-temporal reasoning**: Models need better understanding of how objects move and interact over time
2. **Physical principles**: Integration of fundamental physical laws into model reasoning
3. **Counterfactual simulation**: Ability to imagine alternative scenarios and outcomes

#### 5.3. Practical Applications

The capabilities CausalVQA evaluates are essential for next-generation AI applications:
- **Wearable devices** that can anticipate user needs and prevent accidents
- **Robotics systems** that understand consequences of actions in physical environments
- **Assistive AI** that can provide proactive guidance based on physical understanding

#### 5.4. Methodological Contributions

The benchmark's creation methodology, particularly its multi-stage quality control pipeline and paired evaluation approach, establishes new standards for robust AI evaluation. The techniques for preventing shortcut exploitation and ensuring visual groundedness provide valuable blueprints for future benchmark development.

CausalVQA ultimately serves as both a diagnostic tool for current AI limitations and a call to action for developing models with more human-like physical intelligence. By quantifying the gap between human and machine understanding of physical causality, it provides a clear target for future research aimed at creating AI systems that can truly understand and navigate the physical world.
