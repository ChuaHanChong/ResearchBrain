# Teacher-Student Representational Alignment for Reinforcement Learning-Driven Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Örebro University developed a method that addresses the imitation gap in reinforcement learning-driven imitation learning by learning a shared latent representation between teacher and student observation spaces during teacher policy training. This approach ensures the teacher's behavior is inherently imitable by the student without altering the teacher's original reward function, achieving a 0.00 imitation gap in a TunnelVision environment and 0.01 in a CollectHealth environment.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) policies trained on low-dimensional 'privileged' states often exploit information unavailable to imitation learning (IL) student policies operating on high-dimensional sensory inputs, creating an 'imitation gap'.
- Existing solutions, such as post-hoc RL finetuning, reintroduce RL complexities, while modifying teacher rewards or actions can degrade teacher optimality or require extensive hyperparameter tuning.
- The inability to construct teacher policies that are inherently imitable from the outset necessitates reliance on complex post-hoc adjustments.

## Method

- A two-phase iterative training process is employed, alternating between learning a shared embedding space and training the teacher policy.
- A common latent embedding space is learned using self-supervised contrastive learning, augmented with explicit alignment and stability loss terms, from paired teacher and student observations.
- The teacher policy is trained using PPO on these shared latent embeddings, with policy gradients explicitly prevented from updating the encoder networks, thus forcing the teacher to rely solely on information shared with the student.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **0.00** imitation gap in the TunnelVision environment and **0.01** in the CollectHealth environment, with both teacher and student policies reaching near-perfect success rates (e.g., **1.00** in TunnelVision).
- It significantly outperformed standard Behavior Cloning (BC), which exhibited large imitation gaps (**0.81** in TunnelVision, **0.53** in CollectHealth), and the SITT baseline which showed smaller but still notable gaps (**0.19** and **0.43** respectively).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the explicit alignment and stability loss terms, as their removal led to noticeable drops in student performance and increased imitation gaps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By explicitly separating the training of observation encoders and the teacher policy, and carefully controlling gradient flow, the teacher can be compelled to learn policies based only on information accessible to the student.
> - A multi-component loss function for embedding learning (contrastive, alignment, stability) is crucial for robustly filtering private agent-specific information and ensuring stable, imitable representations.
> - Preventing the imitation gap from forming *during* teacher training through representational alignment is more effective than attempting to bridge it after the teacher has learned non-imitable behaviors.
