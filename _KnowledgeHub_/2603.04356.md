# RoboCasa365: A Large-Scale Simulation Framework for Training and Benchmarking Generalist Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboCasa365 is a large-scale simulation framework designed for training and benchmarking generalist robots, offering 365 everyday mobile manipulation tasks across 2,500 distinct kitchen scenes and over 2,000 hours of robot interaction data. The framework facilitates a 3x improvement in data efficiency for foundation models through pretraining and enhances real-world success rates by 18% via sim-and-real co-training.

## Problem

- Developing generalist robots is hampered by the scarcity of sufficiently diverse and large-scale robot experience data.
- Real-world evaluation and benchmarking of generalist robot policies are resource-intensive, time-consuming, and lack reproducibility.
- Existing simulation platforms are limited in task diversity, environment complexity, and data scale, hindering comprehensive research into robot generalization.

## Method

- Introduced **RoboCasa365**, a simulation framework built on ==RoboCasa== with 2,500 diverse kitchen scenes and a suite of 365 everyday mobile manipulation tasks (65 atomic, 300 composite).
- Generated over 2,000 hours of robot interaction data, including human demonstrations and large-scale synthetic data using ==MimicGen==, to support varied learning paradigms.
- Established a comprehensive benchmarking protocol for state-of-the-art vision-based robot policies (==Diffusion Policy==, ==π0==/π0.5, ==GR00T N1.5==) across multi-task, foundation model, and lifelong learning settings.

## Results

- Foundation models pretrained on RoboCasa365 data demonstrated a **3x improvement** in data efficiency when fine-tuning on target tasks compared to training from scratch.
- Sim-and-real co-training, incorporating RoboCasa365 data, boosted real-world robot task success rates from **61.8%** to **79.8%** on average, confirming its practical utility for real-world deployment.
- Experiments revealed that current state-of-the-art models, while showing some generalization, still achieve relatively low overall success rates (e.g., GR00T N1.5 at **20%** on all tasks), indicating significant room for improvement in generalist capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretraining on diverse, large-scale simulated data significantly improves data efficiency (approximately 3x) and generalization capabilities for foundation models in downstream tasks.
> - Lifelong learning in robotics faces severe challenges such as catastrophic forgetting, as evidenced by degraded performance on previously learned tasks with sequential new learning.
> - The quality and diversity of pretraining data, particularly human demonstrations and varied scene layouts, are crucial for robust generalization and optimal performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of RoboCasa365 is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of generalist robots and robot foundation models. The field is experiencing a significant push towards creating robots capable of performing a wide array of everyday tasks in complex, human-centric environments. This ambition is fueled by recent advancements in robot learning, particularly with the advent of large-scale real-world robot datasets (e.g., Brohan et al., 2022; Open X-Embodiment Collaboration et al., 2023) and high-capacity robotic foundation models (e.g., Black et al., 2024; NVIDIA et al., 2025). These models have shown promising generalization capabilities to novel objects, environments, and tasks.

However, the paper identifies two critical bottlenecks that RoboCasa365 aims to address. Firstly, training truly generalist robots demands immense volumes of diverse robot experience data. While real-world datasets are growing, they often remain limited in their diversity and task coverage, hindering the training of robust, general-purpose policies. Secondly, real-world evaluation and benchmarking are inherently resource-intensive, time-consuming, and prone to experimental noise, making reproducible and systematic comparisons across different methods exceedingly difficult (Atreya et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2023).

Simulation offers a practical and scalable solution to these challenges. It provides an environment where researchers can generate vast interaction datasets with effectively infinite variations in tasks and environments (Mandlekar et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2025). Furthermore, simulation enables rapid experimentation, precise control over variables, and reproducible benchmarking, capabilities that are largely infeasible in physical robotics.

The existing simulation landscape, however, falls short of meeting the demands of generalist robot research. Many current simulation frameworks and benchmarks are constrained to limited tasks (e.g., simple object manipulation) or narrow environments (e.g., tabletop settings, single rooms) (Zhu et al., 2020; James et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2023). The datasets derived from these platforms are often too small in scope and complexity compared to real-world challenges. RoboCasa365 explicitly positions itself as a comprehensive solution to these limitations, offering unprecedented scale and diversity to facilitate the systematic study of generalization in robot learning, moving beyond frameworks like Maniskill (Gu et al., 2023) or Habitat (Szot et al., 2021) in terms of the specific blend of task complexity, environment diversity, and data scale for mobile manipulation in household settings. It aims to be the first simulation framework to concurrently satisfy the criteria of hundreds of tasks across thousands of unique scenes, large-scale high-quality demonstration datasets, and comprehensive benchmarking capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **RoboCasa365**, a comprehensive, large-scale simulation framework specifically designed for training and benchmarking generalist robots in everyday household mobile manipulation tasks.

The motivations behind RoboCasa365 stem directly from the challenges identified in the broader research landscape:
1. **Bridging the Data Gap:** The accelerating progress toward generalist robots is bottlenecked by the scarcity of sufficiently diverse and large-scale robot experience data. RoboCasa365 aims to address this by offering a vast collection of human and synthetic demonstration data spanning numerous tasks and environments.
2. **Enabling Systematic and Reproducible Evaluation:** The difficulties in conducting reproducible, systematic, and large-scale evaluations in the real world impede objective comparisons and progress assessment. RoboCasa365 is motivated by the need for a controlled, reproducible simulation environment that allows for rigorous benchmarking of generalist robot policies.
3. **Facilitating Research on Generalization:** A crucial aspect of generalist robots is their ability to generalize to novel tasks and environments. The framework is specifically designed to enable researchers to systematically investigate the impact of factors such as task diversity, environment variation, and dataset scale on policy generalization, thereby informing strategies for future progress.
4. **Supporting Key Learning Paradigms:** RoboCasa365 aims to serve as a versatile testbed for various cutting-edge learning paradigms, including massively multi-task learning, foundation model training (pretraining and fine-tuning), and lifelong learning, all of which are central to the development of broadly capable robots.

In essence, the project is driven by the ambition to accelerate the development of truly generalist robots by providing the foundational data and evaluation infrastructure currently lacking in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboCasa365 is built upon the existing RoboCasa simulation framework (Nasiriany et al., 2024) but introduces significant scaling and enhancements across four core components: assets, scenes, tasks, and datasets. The simulation uses the MuJoCo physics engine (Todorov et al., 2012) and GPU-based rendering, operating at 20 Hz (approximately real-time). The robot is controlled via an Operational Space Controller (Khatib, 1995), providing 7 degrees of freedom for the arm and 5 for the mobile base.

1. **Expanding the Scope of Assets:**
 - **Objects:** The framework leverages an existing library of 2,509 objects and augments it with an additional collection of high-quality 3D assets across 57 new object categories (totaling over 3200 objects).
 - **Interactive Fixtures and Appliances:** This is significantly expanded from 20 instances across 4 categories to 456 instances spanning 12 categories (e.g., blenders, toasters, stand mixers, articulated fridges/ovens/dishwashers). Each category includes 20-50 instances to ensure diversity for generalization.

2. **Diverse Kitchen Scenes:**
 - **Scale:** RoboCasa365 features 2,500 unique kitchen scenes. These are generated from 50 distinct layouts combined with 50 distinct styles.
 - **Realism:** The layouts are modeled as "digital cousins" (Dai et al., 2024) from 50 real-world homes listed on Zillow.com across various US geographic locations, ensuring realistic floor plans and spatial arrangements.
 - **Splits:** Scenes are categorized into 2,500 "pretraining" scenes for large-scale data collection and 10 distinct "target" scenes (from RoboCasa) for targeted data collection and evaluation, with styles non-overlapping to test generalization.

3. **Suite of 365 Everyday Tasks:**
 - **Atomic Tasks:** 65 atomic tasks are defined, building on RoboCasa's 8 foundational skills (e.g., pick-and-place, opening/closing, turning knobs, navigation). 40 new atomic tasks support new appliances and behaviors.
 - **Composite Tasks:** 300 composite tasks are generated using a two-stage LLM-driven process (following Nasiriany et al., 2024). LLMs first suggest 60 high-level "activities" (e.g., boiling water, brewing coffee) across 6 families, then provide "task blueprints" (name, description, objects, skill sequence) for each activity. This includes 83 existing and 217 new composite tasks.
 - **Mobility:** 220 tasks require mobile manipulation, while 145 can be performed without mobility.

4. **Large-Scale Datasets:** RoboCasa365 provides over 2,000 hours of robot interaction data, divided into pretraining and target datasets.
 - **Pretraining Datasets:** Covers 300 tasks (65 atomic, 235 composite) across 2,500 scenes.
 - **Human Demonstrations:** 30,000 demonstrations (100 per task), totaling 404 hours, collected via robot teleoperation (Franka Panda Emika with Omron mobile base).
 - **Synthetic Demonstrations:** 1,615 hours of synthetic data for 60 atomic tasks (10,000 demos per task, 100x scaling) using the MimicGen system, seeded by human demos.
 - **Target Datasets:** Covers 50 representative tasks (18 atomic, 16 composite-seen, 16 composite-unseen) across 10 scenes.
 - **Human Demonstrations:** 25,000 demonstrations (500 per task), totaling 208 hours.
 - **Data Structure:** Each demonstration includes language instruction, proprioceptive info (robot pose, gripper state), images from three cameras (wrist, two third-person, 256x256 resolution), and actions.

5. **Benchmarking Experiments:** The paper conducts extensive experiments using state-of-the-art vision-based models: Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), π0 (Black et al., 2024), π0.5 (Physical Intelligence et al., 2025), and GR00T N1.5 (NVIDIA et al., 2025). The evaluation protocol involves 30 trials per task, with success rates reported.
 - **Learning Paradigms:** Experiments cover multi-task training, foundation model training (pretraining then fine-tuning), and lifelong learning.
 - **Studies:** Investigations include the impact of task diversity, environment variation, dataset scale, and synthetic data quality.
 - **Real-World Transfer:** An additional set of experiments examines sim-to-real transfer on four real-world tasks using a DROID Panda arm, comparing training on real data only versus sim-and-real co-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on RoboCasa365 yield several key insights into the training and benchmarking of generalist robot policies:

1. **Multi-task Training Performance:**
 - Learning atomic (short-horizon) tasks is significantly easier than composite (long-horizon) tasks.
 - Generalization to "Composite-Unseen" tasks (not in pretraining data) is challenging, showing lower success rates, though state-of-the-art models exhibit non-zero generalization.
 - Among the tested methods, GR00T N1.5 consistently performs best, followed by π0.5, π0, and Diffusion Policy, highlighting the benefit of high-capacity vision-language-action models for diverse multi-task datasets. However, overall success rates (e.g., GR00T N1.5 averaging 20%) indicate significant room for improvement in generalist capabilities.

2. **Foundation Model Training (Pretraining + Fine-tuning):**
 - Pretraining on large, diverse datasets significantly improves downstream task learning, particularly in data-efficient regimes. The experiments show roughly a **3x improvement in data efficiency**, meaning pretraining allows models to achieve similar performance with three times less target task data compared to training on target data alone.
 - This benefit is especially pronounced for "Composite-Unseen" tasks, indicating that pretraining helps in learning more robust and transferable representations for novel situations.
 - A critical finding is that a two-stage approach (pretrain then fine-tune) is substantially more effective than joint co-training of pretraining and target data in a single phase, emphasizing the importance of a dedicated fine-tuning stage for target task specialization.

3. **Lifelong Learning Challenges:**
 - As robots learn progressively longer-horizon tasks in sequential phases, task success rates for newly learned tasks tend to drop.
 - Performance on previously learned tasks steadily degrades with each new learning phase, vividly demonstrating the "catastrophic forgetting" problem. This highlights lifelong learning as a significant open challenge in generalist robotics.

4. **Impact of Pretraining Data Composition:**
 - **Data Quality:** Training with only human demonstrations (Human300) yielded better downstream results compared to a mixture including synthetic data from MimicGen (Human300 + MG60). This suggests that while MimicGen scales data, the quality or fidelity of the synthetically generated trajectories can vary, presenting a challenge for effective leverage.
 - **Task Diversity:** Increasing the number of pretraining tasks (e.g., from Human50 to Human300) significantly improves downstream performance, particularly for "Composite-Unseen" tasks in low-data regimes. This underscores the importance of broad task coverage for learning robust, generalizable skills.
 - **Scene Diversity:** Evaluations (Appendix H.1) confirm that increasing the diversity of pretraining scenes leads to notable performance gains in zero-shot generalization and subsequent fine-tuning on target tasks.

5. **Robustness and Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - Models are robust to semantic variations in language instructions.
 - However, the models show vulnerability to perturbations in camera poses, initial robot joint angles, and initial base poses (Appendix H.2), indicating areas where sim-to-real alignment or domain randomization could be further improved.
 - Crucially, real-world experiments demonstrate that "Sim-and-Real" co-training (mid-training on simulation data followed by co-fine-tuning on real and corresponding sim data) substantially outperforms training on real-world data alone (79.8% vs. 61.8% average success rate). This validates RoboCasa365's utility in boosting real-world robot policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboCasa365 represents a significant advancement in the tools available for robot learning research, with substantial potential impact on the development of generalist robots:

1. **Establishes a New Benchmark Standard:** By offering an unprecedented scale of tasks (365), environments (2,500 diverse kitchens), and data (over 2,000 hours), RoboCasa365 fills a critical gap for a comprehensive, large-scale, and reproducible simulation benchmark. It sets a new standard for evaluating generalist robot capabilities, moving beyond the limitations of existing narrower benchmarks.

2. **Accelerates Generalist Robot Research:** The framework provides a much-needed testbed for systematically developing and evaluating algorithms for generalist robots. Researchers can now rigorously study how various factors—task diversity, environment variation, data scale, learning paradigms—influence generalization, leading to more informed algorithm design and training strategies. This will undoubtedly accelerate progress in robot learning.

3. **Informs Data Collection and Model Design:** The experimental findings offer concrete insights that can guide future research. For instance, the observation that human-only data sometimes outperforms combined human and synthetic data highlights the ongoing challenge of synthetic data quality. The benefits of task and scene diversity emphasize the importance of broad data collection. The struggles with lifelong learning point to critical areas for algorithmic innovation.

4. **Enhances Real-World Deployment Potential:** The successful demonstration of sim-to-real transfer, where RoboCasa365 data significantly improves real-world policy performance, showcases its practical utility. This bridge between simulation and reality is crucial for moving generalist robots from research labs to practical applications.

5. **Fosters Open Science and Collaboration:** The commitment to open-sourcing all models and the framework itself encourages community-wide benchmarking, collaboration, and faster collective progress. This open approach can democratize access to large-scale robotics research, allowing more groups to contribute and innovate.

6. **Paves the Way for Future Innovations:** While currently focused on kitchen environments, RoboCasa365 provides a robust foundation for expansion into other household settings or broader domains. By identifying current limitations (e.g., sim-to-real gap, lifelong learning), the paper also naturally points towards fertile ground for future research, ensuring its ongoing relevance and impact in the evolving field of embodied AI and robotics.
