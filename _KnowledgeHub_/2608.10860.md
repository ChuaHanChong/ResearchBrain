# Flex-$π$: A Multi-Stream World-Action Model with Compute Flexibility

> [!summary] Summary
> FLEX-π is a 6B-parameter world-action model that supervises future 3D pointmaps and DINO object semantics alongside RGB, exploiting the finding that a frozen video-generation VAE encodes pointmaps almost losslessly. A Mixture-of-Transformers backbone denoises all streams jointly with actions, while per-stream dropout with cross-modality forcing yields one checkpoint spanning action-only to full joint generation, beating strong baselines by 2-6× on real bimanual manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing generalist world-action models predict only RGB latents trained for pixel reconstruction, capturing appearance but not the 3D geometry or object semantics manipulation needs.
- Supervising geometry and semantics normally costs extra sensor modalities, newly trained visual priors, or slower inference, so prior policies simply skip that signal.
- Prior 3D policies demand explicit 3D inputs at deployment, and the speed-versus-performance operating point is fixed during training rather than chosen at deployment.

## Method

- **Shared latent encoding**: one frozen ==Wan-2.2 video VAE== encodes both RGB and ==Depth-Anything-3== pointmaps into the same space, while frozen ==DINOv3== tokens, folded 2×2, add object semantics.
- **Mixture-of-Transformers** backbone with a 5B shared visual trunk plus a narrower 1B ==action expert==, ==cross-stream attention== confined to the middle 16 of 30 blocks, trained by ==flow matching==.
- **Visual stream dropout** plus ==cross-modality forcing==: independent input and output masks make every future stream be denoised even when its input was dropped, yielding one compute-flexible checkpoint.

## Results

- Real bimanual YAM tasks: full joint generation averages **83%** task completion versus **58%** for ManiFlow, **3.5×** the binary success of the VLA baseline, with action-only leading all baselines at **60ms**.
- RoboTwin **50**-task success reaches **94.6%**, matching a VLA pretrained on **76×** more data; at **50** demos per task it scores **78.8%** versus **41.9%** for the best WAM, and LIBERO **99.2%**.
- Ablations: adding DINO inputs gains **6.8** points and pointmaps a further **20**; removing cross-modality forcing costs **21** points; distribution shift drops it **4.7** points versus ManiFlow's **26.7**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent spaces from RGB-trained video VAEs transfer to other image-shaped tensors like pointmaps almost losslessly, so auxiliary modalities can reuse pretrained priors for free.
> - Auxiliary prediction targets pay off even when never generated at test time, meaning richer training supervision buys representation quality without any deployment latency.
> - Requiring each modality to be predictable from the others regularizes more strongly than plain dropout, though extra streams slow convergence and full generation stays slower.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of generalist robot policies, which aim to develop robotic systems capable of performing a wide range of tasks and generalizing to new scenarios with minimal retraining. The paper specifically positions itself within the context of World-Action Models (WAMs), contrasting them with Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs).

WAMs, which jointly predict future observations and actions, have demonstrated effectiveness in terms of demonstration efficiency and generalization. This is attributed to their ability to learn robust representations through joint prediction during training and their inheritance of spatiotemporal priors from video-generation backbones pre-trained on large-scale video data. Existing generalist WAMs predominantly predict future RGB image latents, which are derived from pixel reconstruction objectives. While effective for capturing appearance details, these latents may not inherently align with the 3D geometric structure or object-level semantics that are often critical for precise robot manipulation tasks.

The current work addresses a gap in this landscape by seeking to enhance WAMs with explicit 3D geometry and object semantics. Previous attempts to incorporate such additional modalities typically incurred costs, such as the need for new sensor hardware, extensive training of new visual priors, or an increase in inference latency, which could hinder real-world deployment.

FLEX-π distinguishes itself from other multimodal policy learning efforts that use explicit 3D inputs or 3D world models. Instead, it projects 3D features directly into a shared latent space with DINO and RGB features, leveraging an existing video world model's Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encoder to process 3D pointmaps. A key aspect of this approach is its ability to operate even without 3D input during inference, unlike methods that strictly require it. The paper also relates to research on masking input modalities during training to improve generalization or enable variable capabilities at inference, by introducing a "cross-modality forcing" mechanism that trains the model to synthesize missing modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalist robot policy, termed FLEX-π, that exhibits enhanced demonstration efficiency and generalization capabilities compared to existing models, while simultaneously remaining fast enough for practical deployment.

The motivation for FLEX-π stems from several identified limitations and opportunities within the field of robot learning:

- **Improving upon existing WAMs**: Current generalist WAMs primarily predict future RGB image latents. While beneficial for appearance, these latents may not fully capture the 3D geometric structure and object semantics necessary for precise and robust robotic manipulation. The authors hypothesize that integrating these explicit signals would provide a stronger joint prediction training signal, leading to improved policy performance.
- **Avoiding prohibitive costs**: Historically, incorporating additional modalities like 3D geometry or object semantics has presented challenges. These challenges include:
 - **Additional sensor requirements**: Relying on sensors beyond standard RGB cameras, which can increase system complexity and cost.
 - **Training new visual priors**: The need for extensive pre-training of new visual encoders specifically for geometry or semantics, which is computationally intensive.
 - **Increased inference latency**: The overhead of processing multiple modalities, potentially slowing down the policy execution and making it unsuitable for real-time control.
 The authors explicitly motivate FLEX-π by aiming to overcome these barriers, seeking a method that amplifies WAM strengths without incurring these costs.
- **Leveraging existing large-scale models**: A central motivation is the discovery that a frozen VAE from a pre-trained video generation model, originally trained purely for pixel reconstruction, can encode 3D pointmaps with minimal loss. This "free lunch" allows FLEX-π to supervise on 3D geometry and object-centric DINO semantics alongside RGB without the need for new sensors or new visual prior training.
- **Enabling compute flexibility at deployment**: The project aims to develop a single trained model checkpoint that can adapt to varying computational budgets and sensor availability at inference time. This means the model should be able to run in different modes, from a fast, action-only mode to a full joint generation mode that predicts all visual streams. This flexibility is intended to allow practitioners to choose a suitable speed-performance trade-off post-training.
- **Enhancing internal representation learning**: By employing "cross-modality forcing" during training, where the model is compelled to generate visual streams even if they were not provided as input, the goal is to foster a robust internal representation. This mechanism is intended to encourage the model to internalize and mutually predict different visual representations (appearance, geometry, semantics), thereby improving policy learning.

In summary, the motivation is to create a more capable and versatile WAM for robot manipulation by enriching its visual understanding with 3D geometry and object semantics, while ensuring practical deployability through cost avoidance and computational flexibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FLEX-π is implemented as a 6-billion parameter world-action model designed to predict future 3D geometry and object semantics in addition to RGB observations, without incurring significant costs.

**Input Visual Streams and Encoding:**
FLEX-π processes three complementary visual token streams, all derived from RGB images and fed into a shared latent space:
- **RGB Images ($o_t$)**: Current RGB images are encoded into latent token streams ($z^o_t$) using a single, frozen Variational Autoencoder (VAE) from a pre-trained video generation model (Wan-2.2-5B). This leverages spatiotemporal priors from large-scale video pretraining.
- **3D Pointmaps ($p_t$)**: 3D pointmaps are generated from the same RGB images using Depth Anything 3. A key finding is that the *same frozen Wan-2.2 VAE* used for RGB encoding can encode these pointmaps into the same latent space ($z^p_t$) almost losslessly, despite being trained solely on RGB pixels. This allows for 3D geometric information without new sensors or specialized 3D VAE training.
- **Object-centric DINO Features ($d_t$)**: Object-level semantic tokens ($d_t$) are extracted from the RGB images using a frozen DINOv3 encoder. To manage computational load, DINOv3's patch tokens are folded (each 2x2 neighborhood of patches concatenated into a single token), reducing the token count by a factor of four without losing spatial detail.
- **Global Conditioning**: Proprioception ($s_t$, robot joint states) and language instruction ($l$, task specification) are encoded and concatenated to serve as global conditioning vectors for the latent prediction model.

**Model Backbone - Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT):**
- The core of FLEX-π is a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone, initialized from the same Wan-2.2-5B video generation model. This MoT processes all visual modality tokens ($z^o_t$, $z^p_t$, $d_t$).
- It consists of a 5-billion parameter Visual Transformer Backbone, shared across visual streams, and a separate 1-billion parameter Action Expert Transformer for action generation. The action expert is initialized by resampling parameters from the pre-trained Wan-2.2 backbone.
- Cross-stream attention, enabling fusion of information across RGB, pointmap, and DINO streams, is applied in the middle 16 of the 30 MoT blocks. Early and late blocks maintain stream-specific processing.
- Stream-specific feedforward projection networks and adapters map each modality into and out of the shared trunk.

**Causal Joint Generation:**
The model is designed to jointly generate future actions ($a_t$) and future visual latent tokens ($z^o_{t+1}$, $z^p_{t+1}$, $d_{t+1}$). Action tokens attend to current observation tokens and cross-attend to whichever future visual output tokens are active, meaning action generation can benefit from the model's evolving prediction of the future. Attention is unidirectional; visual tokens do not attend to the action stream.

**Flexible Training via Visual Stream Dropout and Cross-Modality Forcing:**
A core aspect of FLEX-π is enabling a single trained checkpoint to support any combination of input and output streams at inference. This is achieved through:
- **Visual Stream Dropout**: During training, two independent, per-sample binary masks are applied:
 - An **input presence mask ($m_{in}$)** randomly selects which visual streams are provided as conditioning at time t (with a constraint that at least one stream must always be present).
 - An **output attention mask ($m_{out}$)** determines which future visual streams are mutually visible to each other and to the action tokens during joint generation.
- **Cross-Modality Forcing**: Even if a visual stream is omitted from the input ($m_{in}$), its future is still predicted and supervised through the loss function. This mechanism forces the model to synthesize the missing modality's future from the available streams, promoting a more robust and internalized representation of appearance, geometry, and semantics.

**Training Objectives, Data, and Inference:**
- **Loss Function**: The training objective is a sum of per-stream flow-matching losses for the action stream and all three visual streams. All loss weights ($\lambda_a, \lambda_o, \lambda_d, \lambda_p$) are set to 1. The output mask ($m_{out}$) is solely an attention mask and does not mask the loss; every future stream is always denoised and contributes to the loss.
- **DINO x-Prediction**: For the folded DINO stream, "x-prediction" is employed, where the head estimates the clean features directly rather than the velocity, which is then converted for the standard flow matching loss.
- **Pre-training and Fine-tuning**: FLEX-π is pre-trained on approximately 500 hours of bimanual manipulation data from AGIBOT World-Beta. Pointmaps for this dataset are generated offline using Depth Anything 3. After pre-training, the model undergoes domain-specific fine-tuning for various benchmarks and real-world tasks.
- **Inference**: At inference time, based on the chosen input/output configuration, FLEX-π runs K Euler steps of the flow-matching ODE over the active output streams and generates an action chunk of length H.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FLEX-π involved extensive experimentation across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, addressing its effectiveness, generalization, data efficiency, and the impact of its design choices.

**Real-World Bimanual Manipulation (Q1):**
- **Superior Performance**: FLEX-π consistently outperformed strong baselines such as $\pi^{0.5}$, ManiFlow, and Fast-WAM across five dexterous, precise, real-world bimanual manipulation tasks on a YAM robot. The performance margin increased with task difficulty; for instance, on "Self-Repair Gripper," FLEX-π (full joint generation) achieved a 42.7 point increase in task completion over the strongest baseline.
- **Average Success Rate**: Averaged over all tasks, FLEX-π achieved 3.5 times higher success rate than $\pi^{0.5}$ and 2.3 times higher than ManiFlow.
- **Inference Speed vs. Task Completion**: The action-only variant of FLEX-π operated at 60 ms per call, faster than all baselines, while still outperforming them on every task. Engaging full joint generation increased latency but resulted in an average of 13% higher success rates, indicating a performance-latency trade-off at deployment.

**Real-World OOD Generalization and Demonstration Efficiency (Q2):**
- **Robustness to Distribution Shift**: FLEX-π exhibited robustness to visual distribution shifts, such as unseen objects, extreme clutter, and distractors. Its performance drop under significant visual shifts was minimal (average 4.1-4.7 points for action-only and full joint, respectively) compared to ManiFlow (26.7 points) and $\pi^{0.5}$ (25.6 points).
- **Data Efficiency**: When fine-tuned with half the real-world data, FLEX-π maintained the highest success rates. The full-joint prediction mode with half data outperformed all baselines trained on the full dataset, demonstrating that the world-action objective provides substantial supervision.
- **RoboTwin Data Scaling**: In simulation (RoboTwin), FLEX-π was found to be significantly more data-efficient than $\pi^{0.5}$, LingBot-VA, and Fast-WAM across various demonstration budgets (50, 100, 500 demos per task), showing 1.9-4.5 times higher success in lower data regimes.

**Large-Scale Comparison against VLAs and WAMs (Q3):**
- **RoboTwin Benchmark**: FLEX-π achieved a 94.6% success rate in action-only mode, marginally surpassing Qwen-RobotManip (93.9%), a VLA baseline with a significantly larger pre-training corpus (76 times larger). Full joint prediction also outperformed all WAM baselines including Motus and LingBot-VA 2.0. This performance suggests near-saturation on this benchmark.
- **LIBERO Benchmark**: In LIBERO, FLEX-π* (a variant fine-tuned without stream dropout for fixed inference modes) with full joint prediction achieved a 99.2% overall success rate, matching the top VLA, Qwen-RobotManip, and outperforming all other VLA and WAM baselines. The flexible FLEX-π (with dropout) also performed competitively.
- **LIBERO-Plus Generalization**: On LIBERO-Plus, which introduces various perturbations, FLEX-π with full joint generation either matched or outperformed the action-only mode across every perturbation type. This indicates that generating future visual streams at inference time can enhance generalization under distribution shifts.

**Impact of Additional Inputs and Outputs (Q4):**
- **Contribution of Input Modalities**: Ablation studies on RoboTwin demonstrated that each additional visual stream improved performance. Adding DINO features to RGB-only input increased average success by 6.8%. Further adding 3D pointmaps resulted in an additional 20% increase in success, indicating the importance of explicit geometric and semantic grounding.
- **Latency vs. Success Trade-offs with Output Modalities**: A single FLEX-π checkpoint allowed for flexible trade-offs. With RGB-only input, action-only generation achieved 40.2% success at approximately 60 ms. Adding video generation increased success to 60.4%. Generating all visual streams (video, DINO, and pointmaps) yielded 63.8% success at approximately 193 ms.
- **Effectiveness of Cross-Modality Forcing**: Removing the cross-modality forcing mechanism during training resulted in a 21% decrease in success rates on RoboTwin. This suggests that compelling the model to synthesize missing modalities from others during training forces it to learn a more robust and mutually predictive representation of appearance, geometry, and semantics, benefiting action prediction.
- **Few-Step Inference**: Experiments on the number of Euler steps (K) for flow matching showed that success peaked at K=4 steps and remained stable for K ≥ 2, while significantly degrading at K=1. This indicates that latent space prediction allows for effective inference with a low number of denoising steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FLEX-π presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robot learning and embodied AI:

- **Cost-Effective Multimodal Integration**: A primary significance of this work is demonstrating a method for integrating rich 3D geometry and object-level semantics into World-Action Models without incurring the traditional costs of additional sensors, new visual prior training, or increased inference latency. The discovery that a VAE pre-trained for RGB can effectively encode pointmaps provides a practical pathway for enhancing visual understanding in robotic systems, which could lower barriers to adopting more sophisticated visual inputs.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance and Generalization**: FLEX-π achieves notable performance improvements on challenging real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, often outperforming strong baselines by substantial margins. Its demonstrated robustness to visual distribution shifts and superior data efficiency in both real-world and simulated environments indicate a step towards more capable and adaptable generalist robot policies. This can lead to robots that require fewer demonstrations for new tasks and can operate more reliably in varied conditions.
- **Practical Deployment Flexibility**: The inherent compute flexibility of FLEX-π, allowing a single checkpoint to operate across a spectrum of input and output streams and computational budgets, is significant for real-world applications. This feature enables practitioners to dynamically balance real-time performance requirements with the depth of visual understanding, making the policy more versatile for diverse deployment scenarios.
- **Improved Representation Learning through Cross-Modality Forcing**: The empirical evidence supporting the benefits of "cross-modality forcing" during training suggests a novel mechanism for learning robust, internalized representations. By forcing the model to infer missing modalities, it develops a deeper understanding of the relationships between appearance, geometry, and semantics. This principle could influence future architectural designs and training methodologies for multimodal learning in robotics and beyond.
- **Advancement of World-Action Models**: The work reinforces the potential of WAMs as a strong alternative to VLAs, particularly in terms of demonstration efficiency and generalization. By addressing the limitations of prior WAMs that focused solely on RGB latents, FLEX-π pushes the boundaries of what WAMs can achieve in complex manipulation tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The paper identifies clear avenues for future work, such as combining FLEX-π with even stronger Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones or significantly larger robot pre-training datasets. This suggests that the proposed multi-stream, compute-flexible architecture can serve as a strong foundation for further advancements in generalist robot control, potentially leading to robots with enhanced semantic reasoning and broader capabilities.
