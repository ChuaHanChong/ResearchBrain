# WHALE: A Simple Recipe for Joint Harness-Weight Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> WHALE (Weight-Harness Alternating LEarning) jointly optimizes an LLM agent's model weights and its executable harness code by alternating two phases: online rejection-sampling fine-tuning under the current harness, then Meta-Harness search over harness programs under the updated model, switched by fixed budgets or an adaptive patience rule. Across search QA, math, and chess it beats weight-only, harness-only, and prompt-only joint baselines by 4.15-24.38 accuracy points.

## Problem

- Prior joint-adaptation methods co-train weights only with textual prompts, leaving the broader executable harness of tools, context management, error handling and termination fixed.
- Optimizing weights alone or harness alone leaves the system bottlenecked by its frozen counterpart, since each update changes which capabilities or harness designs are effective.
- Weight training and harness search run on mismatched timescales, so concurrent updates confound credit assignment, and no prior work settles when to switch between them.

## Method

- **WHALE** alternates a ==ModelUpdate== phase then a ==HarnessSearch== phase per cycle, each optimizing one component with the other frozen, needing only black-box interfaces so any algorithm plugs in.
- The weight phase uses ==online rejection-sampling fine-tuning==: sample eight trajectories per prompt, keep only verifier-accepted ones, ascend the ==token-normalized supervised log-likelihood==, resync rollout workers each step.
- The harness phase uses **Meta-Harness**, a ==propose-evaluate-select loop== over an archive of executable harnesses; adaptive **WHALE** replaces fixed phase budgets with a ==training-signal patience stopping rule==.

## Results

- Best test mean@8 accuracy reaches **48.3%** on SearchQA, **24.8%** on Math and **29.8%** on Chess, beating the strongest single-component baseline by **7.67-10.05** points and prompt-restricted Fast-Slow by **4.15-13.00**.
- Alternating beats stagewise optimization by **5.32** points on SearchQA and **9.16** on Math, and passes the final stagewise accuracy after only **29%** and **49%** of its rollouts.
- Adaptive scheduling hits **52.82%** on SearchQA, **2.73** points above the best hand-tuned schedule with **23%** fewer rollouts, but lands **1.87** points below it on Math.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bottlenecks are domain-dependent: harness search fixed retrieval in search QA, while math improved only after a weight update, so run cheap harness search as a diagnostic first.
> - Alternation dominates one long pass per component, because a long phase over-optimizes against a counterpart that then changes, settling at a local rather than joint optimum.
> - Phase length sits between two failures: too short accepts noise-inflated candidates, too long overfits the frozen counterpart; training signals alone can schedule the switch.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of agentic language-model systems typically involves two primary components: the model parameters (weights) and the executable "harness" code. The harness manages essential functionalities such as context handling, control flow, tool exposure and invocation, and error management. Prior research has explored joint adaptation of model weights with their surrounding context, primarily focusing on textual prompts. Examples include works by Soylu et al. (2024), Zhang et al. (2026), and Tiwari et al. (2026), which explore prompt and weight co-optimization or continual adaptation.

However, executable tool-using agents present a broader scope for adaptation, encompassing the entire harness code, not just prompts. Recent advancements in LLM-based optimizers, such as those by Hu et al. (2025), Lin et al. (2026), and Lee et al. (2026), have made direct search and optimization over this more extensive harness code feasible. While both model weights and harness code can now be optimized, a clear methodology for coordinating model training with full-harness search has remained less explored. This coordination is complicated by the differing operational timescales of these two components: harness search typically involves longer proposal-and-acceptance cycles based on numerous rollouts, whereas online fine-tuning uses more frequent gradient updates interspersed with rollout collection.

Existing methods for training reasoning agents often keep the harness fixed or abstract it away. Conversely, research on optimizing systems around fixed models has increasingly explored more expressive artifacts, ranging from natural-language instructions and modular prompt programs (Yang et al., 2024a; Khattab et al., 2024; Agrawal et al., 2026) to agent architectures and workflows (Hu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024; Zhuge et al., 2024; Shang et al., 2025). More recent work has extended this optimization to executable harnesses and their components through source-code editing and trajectory-driven updates (Lin et al., 2026; Sengupta & Wang, 2026; Pan et al., 2026; Park et al., 2026).

This work addresses the coordination challenge by proposing an alternating optimization strategy that links reward-filtered model training with executable harness search. It aims to bridge the gap between optimizing model weights and optimizing comprehensive harness logic, building on the recognition that non-prompt harness elements—such as tool interfaces, orchestration, memory, and middleware—can substantially influence fixed-model behavior (Yang et al., 2024b; Zhuge et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2026).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that agent performance is a joint function of both the model parameters ($\theta$) and the executable harness code ($h$). Optimizing either component in isolation can lead to a bottleneck, where the performance of the entire system is limited by its frozen counterpart. For instance, updates to model weights might render the existing harness suboptimal, or changes in the harness might expose new model capabilities or highlight deficiencies. This interdependence creates a "shifting bottleneck" problem.

The primary objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a Joint Optimization Framework:** To propose a methodology that enables the simultaneous and coordinated optimization of both model weights and the broader executable harness, addressing the limitations of single-component optimization.
2. **Overcome Coordination Challenges:** To devise a strategy that manages the different timescales and update cadences of model training and harness search, avoiding issues like confounded credit assignment when components change concurrently. The alternating approach aims to decompose the coupled problem into conditional updates, where each component is optimized with respect to a fixed counterpart.
3. **Investigate Scheduling Decisions:** To examine how the duration of each optimization phase (weight update vs. harness search) should be determined. A key design question is identifying the appropriate balance: each phase must accumulate sufficient evidence to ensure genuine improvements, but also conclude before over-optimizing against a component that will subsequently change. This involves avoiding both "noisy" updates (insufficient evidence) and "over-optimized" updates (excessive adaptation to a temporary fixed counterpart).
4. **Evaluate Against Baselines:** To demonstrate whether the proposed joint optimization method, specifically involving full-harness search, yields performance gains compared to optimizing only weights, only the harness, or prompt-restricted joint optimization methods.
5. **Analyze Component Interactions:** To understand the specific ways in which model weights and harness code interact across different problem domains, identifying scenarios where one component acts as the primary bottleneck and how updates to one can unlock gains in the other.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Weight-Harness Alternating LEarning (WHALE), is a modular recipe that alternates between two distinct optimization phases. It operates on the principle of conditional updates, wherein one component is optimized while the other is held constant.

### 4. Core Alternating Phases

Starting with an initial model-harness pair $(\theta_0, h_0)$, each cycle $k$ involves:
1. **Model Update Phase:** The model parameters $\theta$ are updated to $\theta_{k+1}$ under the current harness $h_k$.
2. **Harness Search Phase:** A search is performed to find a better harness $h_{k+1}$ under the newly updated model $\theta_{k+1}$.

### 5. Instantiation of Optimization Phases

- **Weight Updates (ModelUpdate): Online Rejection-Sampling Fine-Tuning (RSFT)**
 - This is an online variant of RSFT, where the model is fine-tuned using supervised learning exclusively on its own verifier-accepted rollouts.
 - Given a fixed harness $h$, the model $\theta_{old}$ generates $G$ trajectories per prompt from a batch $B_s$. Only trajectories $(x, \tau)$ that receive a reward of 1 from the binary verifier $R(x, \tau)$ are collected into $S_s^+$.
 - The model $\theta$ is then updated by maximizing the token-normalized supervised log-likelihood (Equation 2) over minibatches drawn from $S_s^+$.
 - Gradient updates are applied, and the updated weights are synchronized with the rollout workers.
- **Harness Search (HarnessSearch): Meta-Harness (MH)**
 - MH is an iterative proposal-evaluation-selection process for executable harness programs.
 - Given fixed model parameters $\theta$, MH maintains an archive $A_j$ of harnesses and their evaluation artifacts $E_j$.
 - An LLM-based proposer inspects these artifacts and source files to generate $M$ candidate harnesses $C_j$.
 - Each candidate harness $h \in C_j$ is evaluated on a dedicated harness-search training dataset $D_{harness}$ using one rollout per example to compute an empirical score (Equation 3).
 - The archive is updated ($A_{j+1} = A_j \cup C_j$), and the harness $h_{accept}$ with the highest score in the expanded archive is selected for the next cycle.

### 6. Phase Scheduling

- **Fixed Phase Durations:** The default approach uses fixed hyperparameters for the duration of each phase: $E$ epochs for weight updates and $I$ iterations for harness search. $M$ is the number of candidates proposed per harness-search iteration.
- **Adaptive WHALE:** An alternative adaptive scheduling mechanism is introduced, which dynamically adjusts phase durations.
 - The **weight-update phase** stops if its training reward (averaged over a sliding window $W$) does not improve for a fixed number of steps ($P_w$) after a minimum duration ($T_{min}$).
 - The **harness-search phase** stops if the best training score in its archive does not improve for a fixed number of iterations ($P_h$) after a minimum number of iterations ($J_{min}$). This mechanism aims to eliminate the need for manual tuning of phase budgets.

### 7. Experimental Setup

- **Domains:** Three diverse tool-using domains were selected:
 - **SearchQA:** Question answering involving a retrieval tool from Wikipedia.
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** Problem-solving requiring a Python code execution tool.
 - **Chess Puzzles:** Generating chess moves in UCI notation against an environment.
- **Base Models:** Qwen3.5-2B for SearchQA and Math, Qwen3.5-4B for Chess Puzzles.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Weight-only:** Model fine-tuned with the initial harness $h_0$ fixed.
 - **Harness-only:** Harness searched with the initial model $\theta_0$ fixed.
 - **Fast–Slow Training (FST) Control:** A prompt-restricted version of WHALE, where harness search is limited to modifying system and user prompts, isolating the impact of broader harness expressivity.
 - **Stagewise Optimization:** Full weight training budget applied first, followed by the full harness search budget.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Test mean@8 accuracy (average accuracy over 8 trajectories per example), rollout costs, and domain-specific behavior metrics (e.g., format compliance, retrieval accuracy, truncation rate).
- **Budget Matching:** All baselines received budgets matching the cumulative budgets of WHALE and FST runs within each domain to ensure fair comparison.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness and mechanics of joint weight-harness optimization.

### 9. Superior Performance of WHALE

- WHALE consistently achieved higher performance across all three evaluation domains compared to single-component baselines and prompt-restricted joint adaptation (FST).
- **Accuracy Improvements:** WHALE improved test mean@8 accuracy by 7.67–24.38 percentage points over the stronger single-component baseline and by 4.15–13.00 points over FST. These gains were observed across every individual benchmark within each domain.
- The results indicate that adapting the broader executable harness (as in WHALE) provides substantial benefits beyond optimizing only textual prompts.

### 10. Domain-Dependent Bottleneck Regimes

- **SearchQA (Harness-Dominant):** In SearchQA, harness search alone matched the peak accuracy of weight-only optimization using significantly fewer rollouts (5.79% of the cost). Harness search was more efficient at improving format compliance and retrieval accuracy. This suggests that in some domains, the harness can be the primary bottleneck, and its optimization offers a low-cost diagnostic before extensive model training.
- **Mathematical Reasoning (Model-Dominant):** In contrast, for mathematical reasoning, harness search with the initial model yielded almost no improvement (0.42% accuracy) until a small weight update made the same harness search effective. This indicates a model-dominant regime where model capabilities limit initial harness effectiveness, and model improvement "unlocks" gains from harness optimization.
- These findings demonstrate that the bottleneck can shift between the harness and the model, underscoring the importance of joint optimization.

### 11. Alternation Outperforms Stagewise Optimization

- WHALE’s small, interleaved updates consistently outperformed a stagewise optimization approach (full weight training followed by full harness search).
- **Accuracy and Cost Efficiency:** In SearchQA and Math, WHALE achieved 5.32 and 9.16 percentage points higher accuracy, respectively, and surpassed the stagewise result after only 29% and 49% of the stagewise rollouts.
- The weaker performance of stagewise optimization is attributed to "conditional over-optimization," where each phase optimizes against a fixed counterpart that subsequently changes, leading to suboptimal adaptation for the overall system.

### 12. Balancing Phase Durations

- A study of fixed schedule hyperparameters (E, I) revealed that effective schedules avoid both "noisy" and "over-optimized" extremes.
- Too short phases (e.g., Math (0.2, 2)) resulted in unstable training and poor performance due to insufficient evidence.
- Too long phases led to over-optimization against a fixed counterpart, diminishing overall gains.
- The optimal schedule identified (0.2 epochs for weight updates, 6 iterations for harness search) was the strongest in both SearchQA and Math, suggesting a balance where small weight updates frequently refresh the opportunities for harness search.

### 13. Effectiveness of Adaptive Scheduling

- Adaptive WHALE, which dynamically determines phase durations using a patience-based stopping rule on training signals, demonstrated strong performance without requiring fixed budgets.
- In SearchQA, adaptive WHALE achieved the highest accuracy (52.82%) across all comparisons, surpassing the best hand-tuned fixed schedule by 2.73 points with 23% fewer rollouts.
- In Math, it achieved competitive performance, outperforming the main fixed schedule but slightly underperforming the best hand-tuned schedule.
- The realized phase lengths of adaptive WHALE fell within the range identified by the ablation study, confirming its ability to automatically determine suitable phase durations.

### 14. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents WHALE as a framework for integrating model weight updates with executable harness search, offering a unified approach to optimizing agentic systems.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Paradigm Shift:** It encourages a shift from treating model parameters and harness code as separately engineered components to viewing them as a single, jointly trainable system. This holistic perspective may lead to more robust and performant AI agents.
- **Enhanced Agent Capabilities:** By demonstrating consistent and substantial performance gains in diverse, tool-using environments, WHALE indicates a pathway to developing more capable and efficient language model agents.
- **Improved Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** The findings highlight that alternating small, targeted updates can be more rollout-efficient than extensive single-component optimization or stagewise approaches. This could reduce computational costs associated with agent development and fine-tuning.
- **Diagnostic Utility:** The observation of domain-dependent bottleneck regimes suggests that a brief harness search phase could serve as an effective, low-cost diagnostic tool to identify whether the harness or the model is the primary limiting factor, guiding subsequent optimization efforts.
- **Modularity and Generalizability:** The modular design of WHALE allows for the integration of various weight-update and harness-search algorithms. This flexibility ensures the framework's adaptability to future advancements in either domain, promoting continued research and development in joint optimization.
- **Practical Applicability:** The introduction of adaptive scheduling reduces the reliance on manual hyperparameter tuning for phase durations, simplifying the deployment and maintenance of jointly optimized agents in practical applications.

Future work outlined includes exploring alternative weight-update and harness-search algorithms within the WHALE framework, as well as scaling the study to larger models and additional application domains. The results from this paper establish a foundation for further research into the co-adaptation of model intelligence and its operational context.
