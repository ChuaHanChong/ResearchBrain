# A 21-DOF Humanoid Dexterous Hand with Hybrid SMA-Motor Actuation: CYJ Hand-0

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Science and Technology of China developed the CYJ Hand-0, a 21-DOF humanoid dexterous hand featuring a hybrid actuation system of shape memory alloys and DC motors. This hand, fabricated with AlSi10Mg metal 3D printing for biomimetic structure, demonstrated a single-finger load capacity of 1.2 kgf, an 8 kgf overall hand load, and the ability to perform 32 distinct human-like gestures and various grasping tasks.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid dexterous hands often lack sufficient degrees of freedom (DOFs) and detailed structural biomimicry to achieve human-like dexterity and range of motion.
- Many robotic hands face limitations in sensory capabilities, flexibility, coordination, load-bearing capacity, impact resistance, and are constrained by high manufacturing costs.
- Designing an actuation system that simultaneously provides stable force output, adjustable response frequency, and high displacement accuracy in a compact and lightweight form remains a challenge.

## Method

- Developed the ==CYJ Hand==-0, a 1:1 scale biomimetic robotic hand with 21 DOFs, utilizing AlSi10Mg metal 3D printing for its lightweight (380g) and intricate skeletal structure.
- Integrated a novel hybrid actuation system comprising 15 DC motor linear drive modules for strong, precise finger flexion and 17 shape memory alloy (SMA) linear drive modules for rapid extension and abduction.
- Implemented a tendon-driven system using high-strength fishing line as artificial tendons, with actuators housed in a custom 3D-printed robotic arm for compact integration and efficient force transmission.

## Results

- The CYJ Hand-0 successfully performed all 10 Kapandji tests for thumb dexterity, reproduced 32 common human hand gestures, and completed 5 out of 6 Schlesinger and 9 out of 16 Cutkosky grasping classifications.
- A single universal finger demonstrated a load capacity of **1.2** kgf at its distal phalanx, with the entire hand estimated to support an overall load of 8 kgf.
- The hand's joint ranges of motion (e.g., 0-90° MCP, 0-110° PIP) and customized thumb structure enabled human-comparable articulation and a naturally cupped palm configuration for enhanced grasping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of 1:1 scale biomimetic design and AlSi10Mg metal 3D printing enables the creation of a lightweight (380g) yet robust robotic hand with 21 DOFs, capable of supporting up to an 8 kgf load.
> - A hybrid SMA-motor actuation system effectively leverages the distinct strengths of each technology, providing adjustable response frequency, a wide range of output forces, and high displacement accuracy for versatile human-like movements.
> - Customizing the thumb's carpometacarpal (CMC) joint into separate adduction/abduction and flexion/extension modules simplifies kinematic control while achieving the complex multi-axial mobility essential for high dexterity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid dexterous hands (HDHs) represents a significant area within robotics, aiming to replicate the complex form, structure, and functionality of the human hand for versatile manipulation tasks. Historically, robotic manipulators have evolved from simple industrial tools to systems capable of precise and critical roles. The concept of prosthetic hands dates back centuries, with the 19th century marking the advent of tendon-driven, single-degree-of-freedom designs.

Major milestones in HDH research include the Belgrade hand (1962), followed by advancements such as the Okada hand, Stanford/JPL hand, Utah/MIT hand, and Robonaut hand. The 21st century has seen further evolution with innovations like the Cyber hand, Tokyo hand, and UT hand, driven by progress in sensor technology, human-computer interaction, bionics, and artificial intelligence.

Despite these advancements, existing HDHs frequently encounter limitations across several dimensions:
- **Structural Biomimicry:** Often lacking detailed replication of human anatomical structures.
- **Degrees of Freedom (DOF):** Insufficient DOFs to achieve human-like dexterity.
- **Sensory Capabilities:** Inadequate integration of sensing for precise interaction.
- **Flexibility and Coordination:** Restricted range of motion and limited coordinated movements.
- **Load-Bearing Capacity:** Inability to handle substantial loads.
- **Impact Resistance:** Fragile designs susceptible to damage.
- **Manufacturing Costs:** High production expenses, limiting accessibility and widespread adoption.

The CYJ Hand-0 research endeavors to address these identified limitations by integrating advanced materials, a hybrid actuation system, and a highly biomimetic design. This work positions itself as a contribution towards developing HDHs that offer increased dexterity, higher load capacity, reduced weight, and improved manufacturability, thereby expanding their potential applications across diverse domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research was to develop a humanoid dexterous hand that closely replicates the intricate form, structure, and function of the human hand, thereby enabling flexible and delicate manipulation capabilities in robotic systems. This goal is motivated by the persistent challenges observed in existing HDHs, which limit their practical utility and widespread deployment.

Specifically, the motivations for designing the CYJ Hand-0 included:

- **Enhancing Structural Biomimicry and DOFs:** To overcome limitations in the anatomical accuracy and kinematic complexity of previous designs, aiming for a highly biomimetic skeletal and joint structure with 21 DOFs (excluding the wrist). This objective sought to achieve human-like articulation and range of motion.
- **Improving Flexibility and Coordination:** To implement a tendon-driven system that provides flexibility and responsiveness, utilizing high-strength fishing line as artificial tendons to mimic natural tendon-muscle dynamics.
- **Developing an Efficient and Versatile Actuation System:** To introduce a novel hybrid actuation scheme combining shape memory alloy (SMA) linear drive modules with DC brush motor linear drive modules. This aimed to leverage the distinct advantages of each technology—SMA for rapid extension and abduction, and DC motors for strong, precise flexion—to achieve a comprehensive range of force outputs, response frequencies, and displacement accuracies.
- **Optimizing Integration and Compactness:** To integrate the actuation system into a custom-designed robotic arm, which serves as both the power source and support structure, thereby ensuring a compact and functional design that can be attached to external manipulators.
- **Reducing Manufacturing Cost and Weight:** To utilize cost-effective and easily manufacturable AlSi10Mg metal 3D printing for all structural components. This approach aimed to produce a lightweight hand (total mass of 380 grams) while maintaining structural integrity and reducing production expenses.
- **Establishing Fundamental Control:** To implement a basic control system based on the Arduino Mega2560 microcontroller, enabling foundational control for single-finger and full-hand operations.
- **Achieving High Load-Bearing Capacity:** To design the hand for a significant load capacity, targeting a single-finger capacity of 1.2 kgf and an overall hand load capacity of 8 kgf, which would enable the manipulation of heavier objects.
- **Demonstrating Extensive Dexterity:** To validate the hand's dexterity through various performance evaluations, including its ability to perform all Kapandji tests, execute 32 distinct gesture actions, and successfully complete over 30 grasping experiments, reflecting the versatility of human hand movements.

These objectives collectively aimed to push the boundaries of HDH design, contributing to the development of more capable, adaptable, and economically viable robotic hands for a wide range of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of the CYJ Hand-0 involved a comprehensive methodology encompassing biomimetic design, advanced manufacturing, a hybrid actuation scheme, kinematic modeling, and experimental validation.

**A. Biomimetic Design and Structural Fabrication:**
The design principle focused on replicating the human hand's anatomical structure at a 1:1 scale, including bones, joints, and tendon-muscle pathways.
- **Degrees of Freedom:** The hand was designed with 21 DOFs, excluding the wrist.
- **Material and Manufacturing:** All structural components, including phalanges, metacarpals, and carpals, were fabricated using AlSi10Mg metal 3D printing. This method was selected for its ability to produce complex geometries, reduce weight (total hand mass of 380 g), and facilitate cost-effective manufacturing.
- **Finger Design:** A "universal finger" structure was developed for the index, middle, and ring fingers, comprising 9 component types and 12 individual parts, incorporating sliding bearings and biomimetic interphalangeal joint shapes. The little finger shared this universal structure with minor dimensional adjustments.
- **Thumb Design:** The thumb, critical for dexterity, featured a customized two-segment metacarpal at the carpometacarpal (CMC) joint. This design partitioned the CMC joint functions into an adduction/abduction module and a flexion/extension module, simplifying the kinematic implementation while approximating the thumb's multi-axial mobility.
- **Palmar Structure:** To mimic the human palm's cupped configuration for enhanced grasping, the metacarpal bones were designed with an interchangeable structure and integrated with carpal bones at specific non-parallel angles.
- **Joint Kinematics:** Joint rotation ranges for the MCP, PIP, and DIP joints were designed to align with those of the human hand, allowing for a wide range of flexion, extension, and lateral deviation.

**B. Tendon-Driven System:**
- **Tendons:** High-strength 12-braid fishing line (0.55 mm diameter, 40 kg nominal maximum pull) was chosen as artificial tendons due to its strength, light weight, and impact absorption properties. Tendons were secured using figure-eight loops and double fisherman's knots.
- **Tendon Routing:** Tendons followed fixed paths within the finger joints, with grease applied at contact points to minimize friction against the alloy components. Each universal finger used seven tendons: three for flexion (proximal, middle, distal phalanges), two for extension (proximal, middle phalanges, with distal phalanx reset by a pre-tensioned spring), and two for lateral abduction/adduction.

**C. Hybrid Actuation System:**
The hand employed a hybrid SMA-motor actuation system integrated into a custom robotic arm.
- **DC Motor Drive Modules:** Finger flexion was primarily driven by screw-nut motor modules (GA12-N20-298). Each module measured 73.25mm x 33mm x 25mm, weighed 40g, offered a 20mm working stroke, and provided a rated output force of 400N. These motors offered stable output, self-locking capability, and high displacement accuracy for flexion.
- **SMA Linear Drive Modules:** Finger extension and lateral abduction were facilitated by SMA linear drive modules utilizing Ni-Ti alloy wires from DYNALLOY. These modules, controlled by energized heating, provided up to 18mm displacement and 784N maximum output force. Springs assisted SMA wires in returning to their original length upon deactivation for extension and lateral movements. The hybrid system was designed to combine the advantages of adjustable response frequency, wide output force range, and high displacement accuracy.
- **Actuator Integration:** A total of 32 actuators (15 motor-driven and 17 SMA-driven) were integrated into a 3D-printed white resin robotic arm (780g). The motor modules were arranged in three stacked layers of five, and SMA modules were placed in the gaps between them. This arm was designed to connect to the hand's wrist and an external manipulator (UR10), with a hollow structure for tendon routing.

**D. Kinematic Modeling:**
- **Finger Kinematics:** Each finger was modeled as an open-chain, rotationally articulated robot. The D-H (Denavit-Hartenberg) method was applied to establish a coordinate system for a universal finger, defining its kinematic parameters (Table II).
- **Forward Kinematics:** Homogeneous transformation matrices were used to derive equations (Eq. 2-5) for determining the fingertip's position coordinates in a fixed reference system based on known joint angles.
- **Inverse Kinematics:** Equations (Eq. 6-8) were derived to solve for joint angles given the fingertip's position coordinates. Numerical methods, including Newton's iteration, were employed to solve for specific joint angles, especially considering the intra-finger coupling where the DIP joint flexes with the PIP joint (Eq. 1).
- **Tendon Analysis:** A numerical simulation using GeoGebra analyzed tendon length and moment arm changes with joint angle for the proximal joint. This showed a tendon shortening of approximately 16.86 mm during 0° to 110° flexion and an increasing moment arm, contributing to efficient force transmission.

**E. Control System:**
- **Microcontroller:** An Arduino Mega 2560 served as the central control unit.
- **Motor Control:** 15 Tonkeys TKS-M8-P0 DC motor controllers were used to manage the motor-driven modules, enabling software-controlled forward/reverse rotation and start/stop functionality.
- **SMA Control:** A PWM-relay control system regulated the SMA-driven modules. Relays, managed by high and low-level PWM signals from the Arduino, controlled the power supply to each SMA actuator for heating and cooling cycles.

**F. Experimental Validation:**
A series of experiments evaluated the hand's structural feasibility, actuation performance, and dexterity.
- **Finger Flexion:** Video processing software (Tracker) analyzed joint angles during flexion.
- **Finger Tension:** Load tests measured the pulling force at the distal phalanx, gradually increasing load from 200 g to 1200 g.
- **Gesture Simulation:** The Kapandji Test was performed to assess thumb dexterity, and the hand’s ability to mimic 32 common human hand gestures was evaluated.
- **Grasping Experiments:** The hand's grasping capabilities were tested against Schlesinger's (5 out of 6 types) and Cutkosky's (9 out of 16 types) classification methods, along with practical tasks such as grasping a pen and a full 550 ml water bottle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the CYJ Hand-0 provided validation for its design, actuation system, and dexterity.

**A. Structural and Kinematic Performance:**
- **Biomimicry and DOFs:** The CYJ Hand-0 successfully achieved a highly biomimetic skeletal and joint structure, replicating the human hand at a 1:1 scale with 21 DOFs (excluding the wrist). The use of AlSi10Mg metal 3D printing resulted in a lightweight design, with a total mass of 380 g.
- **Joint Ranges:** The designed joint rotation angles for the MCP, PIP, and DIP joints (e.g., 0-90° MCP, 0-110° PIP, 0-90° DIP) were comparable to or exceeded those of the human hand and other existing robotic hands, as shown in Table I. The customized thumb structure enabled a wide range of motion for adduction/abduction (0-53°) and flexion/extension (0-107°).
- **Palmar Curvature:** The integrated carpal bone structure and angled metacarpal arrangement successfully created a naturally concave geometry, allowing the hand to achieve a cupped palm configuration that enhances grasping performance.
- **Kinematic Modeling Validation:** Numerical simulations confirmed the functionality of the tendon system. For a 110° joint angle change, the tendon length shortened by approximately 16.86 mm, indicating the required linear actuator stroke. The analysis also showed an increase in the tendon's force arm as the joint flexed, which was observed to reduce the stretching force on the tendon during loaded bending.

**B. Actuation System Performance:**
- **Hybrid Advantages:** The integrated SMA-motor hybrid drive system demonstrated the ability to combine the strengths of both technologies, offering adjustable response frequency, a wide output force range, and high output displacement accuracy.
- **Actuator Integration:** The custom 3D-printed robotic arm effectively housed 15 motor-driven modules and 17 SMA-driven modules (32 actuators total), facilitating force and displacement transmission via tendons to the hand.
- **Motor Module Performance:** Each motor module provided a 20 mm working stroke and a rated output force of 400N for finger flexion.
- **SMA Module Performance:** Each SMA module delivered up to 18 mm displacement and a maximum output force of 784N for finger extension and lateral movements.

**C. Load-Bearing Capacity:**
- **Single-Finger Load:** Finger tension experiments showed that a single universal finger could successfully bear a load of 1.2 kg at its distal phalanx. The structural integrity of a single finger was observed to exceed 4 kg, though the motor experienced overload and burnout when loads exceeded 1.5 kg.
- **Overall Hand Load:** Based on single-finger capacity, the entire CYJ Hand-0 was estimated to support an overall load of approximately 8 kg.

**D. Dexterity and Grasping Capabilities:**
- **Thumb Dexterity (Kapandji Test):** The hand successfully performed all 10 combinations of movements described in the Kapandji Test, indicating a high level of thumb dexterity comparable to a human hand.
- **Gesture Simulation:** The CYJ Hand-0 was able to reproduce all 32 common hand gestures, including 11 specific index finger gestures, demonstrating its capability for a broad range of communicative and manipulative postures.
- **Grasping Performance:**
 - The hand successfully achieved 5 out of 6 types of grasping postures categorized by Schlesinger, including cylindrical, spherical, three-finger pinch, side pinch, and two-finger pinch.
 - Out of 16 specific grasping modes classified by Cutkosky, the CYJ Hand-0 completed 9 postures.
 - Practical grasping experiments included successfully gripping a pen and a full 550 ml water bottle, further verifying its ability to handle objects with a certain load.

**E. Control System Functionality:**
The Arduino Mega 2560-based control system, integrated with DC motor controllers and PWM-relay circuits for SMA modules, proved effective for basic control of individual actuators, enabling the execution of the tested movements and grasping tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CYJ Hand-0 represents a notable contribution to the field of humanoid dexterous robotics, addressing several long-standing challenges in the design and performance of robotic hands.

**A. Advancements in Robotic Hand Design:**
The integration of 21 DOFs, a highly biomimetic structure, and a hybrid SMA-motor actuation system in a lightweight (380g), 3D-printed metal frame distinguishes this work. It demonstrates a holistic approach to enhancing the structural fidelity and functional capabilities of robotic hands beyond previous iterations.

**B. Innovation in Hybrid Actuation:**
The proposed SMA-motor hybrid drive module is a key innovation. By combining the strengths of DC motors (precision, sustained force, self-locking) with SMAs (rapid response for extension and abduction, high force-to-weight ratio), the system offers a versatile solution with adjustable response frequency, a wide output force range, and high output displacement accuracy. This hybrid approach has the potential to overcome limitations inherent in single-actuator systems, providing a more adaptable and dynamic manipulation platform.

**C. Impact of Advanced Manufacturing:**
The extensive use of AlSi10Mg metal 3D printing for all structural components showcases a viable and efficient manufacturing paradigm for complex robotic systems. This method facilitates the creation of intricate, lightweight, and anatomically precise designs, potentially reducing manufacturing costs and accelerating prototyping cycles compared to traditional fabrication techniques. This could make advanced robotic hands more accessible for research and diverse applications.

**D. High Performance and Versatility:**
The demonstrated high load-bearing capacity (8 kgf for the hand) coupled with extensive dexterity (performing Kapandji tests, 32 gestures, and various grasping modes) indicates that the CYJ Hand-0 possesses the robustness and adaptability required for a wide array of practical tasks. This performance level surpasses many existing HDHs in specific metrics, particularly the combination of dexterity and strength in a lightweight package.

**E. Broad Potential Applications:**
The capabilities exhibited by the CYJ Hand-0 open pathways for its application in numerous fields:
- **Medical Assistance:** As advanced prosthetics or components for surgical robots, offering more natural and precise manipulation.
- **Domestic Services:** For assisting with household tasks, providing enhanced dexterity for elderly or disabled individuals.
- **Intelligent Manufacturing:** In assembly lines or for handling delicate components, where human-like manipulation is advantageous.
- **Hazardous Environments:** For tasks in space exploration, underwater operations, or public safety scenarios, where direct human intervention is impractical or unsafe.
- **Research Platform:** The hand provides a robust platform for further research into advanced control algorithms, human-robot interaction, sensory integration, and machine learning for manipulation.

**F. Future Development Trajectory:**
The authors acknowledge future work focusing on optimizing the structural design and actuation scheme, improving sensor systems, and developing closed-loop control. This indicates a commitment to further enhancing the hand's autonomy, adaptability, and overall application value, leveraging the foundation established by this study.
