# A Contact-Safe Reinforcement Learning Framework for Contact-Rich Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University developed a contact-safe reinforcement learning framework for contact-rich robot manipulation, integrating a high-rate controller with a low-rate RL policy to ensure low contact forces on both the end-effector and the robot arm during interaction and unexpected collisions. The framework achieved end-effector contact forces below 5N and reduced unexpected arm collision forces by approximately three times compared to a baseline in real-world robot experiments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) for robot manipulation lacks inherent safety, leading to dangerous collisions during exploration and unpredictable behaviors in unseen scenarios.
- Existing safety solutions primarily focus on either end-effector compliance or simple collision avoidance for the robot arm, often failing to limit contact force during inevitable or unexpected arm contacts.
- RL policies are vulnerable to external disturbances, causing task deviations and failures, highlighting the need for robust contact management beyond collision avoidance.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical framework== combines a low-rate (60Hz) ==image-based RL policy== with a high-rate (1kHz) ==contact-aware controller== to manage both task-space and joint-space safety.
- A modified ==momentum observer== is employed to estimate and distinguish external contact torques specifically on the robot arm, enabling precise collision detection.
- The controller dynamically switches between a free-space mode and a contact-aware mode, using ==variable impedance control== for task compliance and specialized ==null-space projection== for compliant arm behavior during unexpected contacts.

## Results

- The framework successfully performed surface wiping with end-effector contact forces consistently below **5N**, maintaining compliance within a **40N** safety threshold.
- During unexpected robot arm collisions, the method reduced contact forces by approximately **3x** compared to a baseline, preventing visible deformation of the contacted object.
- The approach demonstrated improved disturbance rejection, maintaining end-effector position error below **1 cm** across all directions when the robot arm was pushed, compared to **4 cm** for the baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A combined hierarchical control strategy allows for both high-level task learning via RL and low-level, real-time safety enforcement against diverse contacts.
> - Estimating external torques on specific robot links using a momentum observer, distinct from end-effector forces, enables targeted compliant responses to unexpected arm collisions.
> - Advanced null-space projection techniques can effectively integrate compliant posture control with primary manipulation tasks, ensuring safety without compromising task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The increasing demand for automation in both industrial and daily applications necessitates robots capable of performing complex manipulation tasks, often involving rich contacts with diverse, potentially unknown objects in unstructured environments. Traditional robot control methods frequently face challenges in modeling and controlling robots in such complex scenarios. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising model-free paradigm that allows robots to learn complex tasks through self-exploration and reinforcement, demonstrating potential in contact-rich tasks like grasping, insertion, and assembly.

However, a significant barrier to the widespread adoption of RL in real-world robotic applications is its inherent lack of safety. During the exploration phase, RL agents commonly employ random actions or strategies to visit unexperienced states, which can inadvertently lead to dangerous collisions. Even after training, RL policies may exhibit unexpected or unsafe behaviors when encountering unseen scenarios or external disturbances. Such unpredictable interactions can result in high contact forces, causing damage to the robot, its environment, or even humans.

The existing research landscape addresses robot safety from several angles, typically categorized into task space safety and joint space safety:
- **Task Space Safety:** This area focuses on ensuring compliant contact between the robot's end-effector and the objects it manipulates. Impedance control, particularly Variable Impedance Control (VIC), is a common technique, allowing dynamic adjustment of stiffness to maintain compliance. Works such as have shown that using VIC in the end-effector space as an action space for RL can improve sample efficiency and lead to more compliant end-effector contacts. Other studies combine VIC with RL for high-precision assembly or human-robot collaboration, ensuring safety at the interaction point. However, these methods often concentrate solely on end-effector safety, overlooking potential safety issues across the robot arm itself.
- **Joint Space Safety:** This category addresses dangerous contacts occurring along the robot arm, which are relevant in human-robot interaction or cluttered environments. Prior research in this domain often aims to *avoid* contact entirely when using RL. Methods include learning policies in the null space of higher-priority constraints to ensure collision avoidance, or combining VIC with Riemannian motion policies (RMPs) to generate collision-free joint actions based on geometric information. Similarly, works in Human-Robot Collaboration, prioritize finding collision-free motions. A limitation of these approaches is the requirement for precise geometric information, which can be expensive or difficult to obtain in unknown environments. Furthermore, in cluttered settings, unexpected contacts may be unavoidable, and in some tasks, deliberate contact may even be beneficial. In such cases, safety should not solely rely on collision avoidance but on limiting the magnitude of contact force.

This paper addresses a perceived gap in the existing literature by proposing a framework that simultaneously considers *both* task space and joint space contact safety within an RL context. Specifically, it extends joint space safety beyond mere collision avoidance to actively limiting contact force during inevitable or unexpected collisions on the robot arm, a aspect not comprehensively covered by prior reinforcement learning research. The work thus contributes to enhancing the robustness and deployability of RL for complex contact-rich robotic manipulation in real-world, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a contact-safe reinforcement learning framework designed for contact-rich robot manipulation tasks. This framework aims to ensure safety across both the robot's end-effector (task space) and its arm (joint space) during various operational phases, including training exploration and deployment in unseen scenarios.

The specific objectives of the proposed framework are:
1. **Ensuring Task Space Safety:** The framework aims to enable the robot end-effector to perform contact-rich tasks compliantly, maintaining small contact forces with target objects.
2. **Ensuring Joint Space Safety:** It seeks to immediately detect unexpected collisions between the robot arm and the environment and to ensure that the resulting contact force remains small. This objective is particularly relevant when collisions are unavoidable or unexpected.
3. **Robustness to Disturbances:** The framework intends to enable the robot to reject external disturbances while maintaining performance on its primary manipulation task.
4. **Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The approach targets training the RL policy solely in simulation and demonstrating its zero-shot transferability and effectiveness on a real robot system.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges associated with applying Reinforcement Learning to real-world robot manipulation:
- **Risk During RL Exploration:** Standard RL algorithms often rely on extensive exploration, which can involve taking random or novel actions. In physical systems, these actions carry a substantial risk of unexpected and dangerous collisions, potentially causing damage to expensive robot hardware, the environment, or even injury to human operators.
- **Unsafe Behavior in Unseen Scenarios:** Even well-trained RL policies can exhibit unpredictable or unsafe behaviors when confronted with environments or objects not encountered during training. This lack of generalization safety is a significant barrier to real-world deployment. The paper provides an illustrative example where a robot trained for wiping fails and collides with an unseen object, leading to high contact forces.
- **Inadequate Existing Safety Solutions:**
 - Prior work on task space safety, typically using impedance control, focuses primarily on compliant end-effector interaction but largely overlooks safety issues along the robot arm.
 - Existing approaches to joint space safety predominantly aim for *collision avoidance*, which requires precise geometric models of the environment. Such models are often unavailable in unstructured settings, and perfect collision avoidance is not always feasible or desirable (as some tasks might require contact). There is a lack of RL frameworks that specifically address *limiting contact force* when unexpected or inevitable collisions occur on the robot arm.
- **Vulnerability to External Disturbances:** RL policies can be susceptible to external disturbances, leading to task deviations or failures, further highlighting the need for robust control strategies that can mitigate these effects while maintaining safety.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to enhance the reliability and safety of RL-driven robot manipulation, paving the way for its broader application in complex, contact-rich scenarios in both industrial and human-centric environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates a low-rate Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy with a high-rate controller in a hierarchical structure to achieve contact safety in both task space and joint space for contact-rich robot manipulation.

**A. Framework Overview**
The system comprises two main components:
1. **RL Policy (60Hz):** This component takes visual observations (images) and robot state as input and outputs high-level task parameters.
2. **Controller (1kHz):** This component receives the RL policy's outputs and converts them into robot joint torques, while actively monitoring for and responding to contacts.

**B. Image-Based RL Policy**
The RL policy is designed to be image-based and uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for training.
- **Input:** The policy receives an observation image and the robot's end-effector 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) pose.
- **Image Feature Extraction:** To improve sample efficiency, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is pre-trained in an unsupervised manner to extract a latent embedding (s_img) from the input image. This embedding is then concatenated with the end-effector pose (s_pose) to form the complete state (s) for the PPO agent.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs actions (a) that define parameters for Cartesian Variable Impedance Control (VIC). These actions include:
 - Increment of the equilibrium point (x_d) for the end-effector pose.
 - Diagonal stiffness matrix (K1).
 - Damping matrix (D1).
- **Training:** The RL policy is trained in a simulated environment using PPO, with a reward function designed to maximize ink wiped off, provide additional rewards for full ink removal, encourage compliant contact (below 40N), and penalize collisions with the table. A large penalty and episode termination occur if the robot arm collides with the table.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The trained RL policy is transferred to a real robot without further fine-tuning (zero-shot transfer).

**C. Contact Estimator**
To detect unexpected collisions on the robot arm, a modified momentum observer, adapted from previous work, is employed.
- **Principle:** The observer estimates the external joint torque (τ_ext) purely from joint measurements (generalized momentum p = M(q)q̇). It approximates τ_ext using a residual vector (γ).
- **Distinction between End-effector and Arm Contact:** The total estimated external torque (τ_ext) includes torques from both the end-effector contact (τ_eff) and the arm contact (τ_c). The end-effector contact force (F_eff) can often be measured directly by a force/torque sensor. By canceling out this measured end-effector force in the residual vector calculation, the estimator can isolate the torque specifically induced by contact on the robot arm (τ_c).
- **Collision Detection:** A contact condition is triggered when the magnitude of the residual vector (γ) exceeds a predefined threshold (δ). The specific link in contact is identified as the one corresponding to the coefficient of γ that is greatest and furthest from the robot base.

**D. Controller**
The controller operates at a high frequency (1000Hz) and dynamically switches its behavior based on contact detection.

1. **Free Space Controller (No Contact Detected):**
 - **Main Task (Task Space Safety):** The primary task involves executing the Cartesian variable impedance control defined by the RL policy's outputs (x_d, K1, D1). The control force (F_t) is calculated to maintain compliance at the end-effector.
 - **Posture Task (Joint Regulation):** To prevent unpredictable joint motions (e.g., unintended acceleration) in free space, an additional posture task (Γ_pose) is introduced. This task aims to keep the robot in a reference configuration (q_pose), which is derived from the RL policy's desired end-effector pose (x_d) via inverse kinematics (IK).
 - **Null Space Projection:** The posture task (Γ_pose) is projected into the null space of the task Jacobian (N_t) using dynamic consistent null space projection. This ensures that the posture task is executed without interfering with the higher-priority end-effector task.
 - **Total Torque:** The command joint torque is given by τ_m = J_t^T(q)F_t + N_t^T(q)Γ_pose.

2. **Contact-Aware Controller (Contact Detected on Arm):**
 - **Disturbance Compensation Impedance:** When contact is detected on the robot arm, the controller incorporates the estimated arm contact torque (γ) into the task control force calculation (F_t-c). This term aims to actively compensate for and reject the disturbance caused by the unexpected arm contact from affecting the primary end-effector task.
 - **Contact-Aware Null Space Projection:** To maintain joint space safety by limiting contact force, the posture task (Γ_pose) is projected into a "task-consistent null space of the reduced contact Jacobian" (N_c|t). This specialized projection ensures that the posture task does not violate the contact constraints or the primary end-effector task, promoting compliant behavior at the contact point on the arm. The contact direction (u_c) is derived from the contact force (F_c) and its Jacobian (J_c(q)).
 - **Total Torque:** The command joint torque is given by τ_m = J_t^T(q)F_t-c + N_c|t^TΓ_pose.

**E. Controller Switching and Jitter Mitigation**
- **Switching Mechanism:** The controller switches between the Free Space Controller and the Contact-Aware Controller based on the contact estimator's detection.
- **Jitter Mitigation:** To prevent jitter caused by residual vector oscillation and rapid switching:
 - A low-pass filter is applied to the residual vector.
 - The gain K_I of the residual vector estimation is kept small.
 - A threshold (t_c) is set for the minimum execution time of the contact-aware controller, preventing immediate switching back to free space until the contact situation has stabilized.

**F. Experimental Implementation**
- **Hardware:** Franka Panda 7 DoF robot arm, Intel RealSense D455 camera.
- **Software:** Pybullet simulator for training, TRAC-IK for inverse kinematics.
- **Task:** Surface wiping, where the robot's goal is to wipe ink from a whiteboard.
- **Baseline:** The VICES framework, augmented with a posture task for fair comparison, as VICES primarily focuses on task space safety.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on a real Franka Panda 7 DoF robot, with the RL policy trained in simulation and transferred zero-shot to the real environment. The primary task was surface wiping.

**A. Task Space Safety**
The evaluation of task space safety focused on the end-effector contact force during the wiping task.
- **Performance:** The robot successfully performed the wiping task.
- **Contact Force Magnitude:** The maximum end-effector contact force observed with the proposed method was approximately 5N.
- **Safety Threshold:** This value remained significantly below the predefined unsafe contact threshold of 40N, indicating compliant operation at the end-effector.
- **Comparison with Baseline:** The proposed method demonstrated similar performance to the baseline (VICES) in terms of end-effector contact force. This suggests that the introduced posture task did not negatively impact the main task's compliance, which is consistent with the baseline also considering task space safety.

**B. Joint Space Safety**
Joint space safety was assessed by intentionally causing an unexpected collision on the robot arm with an unseen object while the RL policy was performing the wiping task.
- **Experimental Setup:** An unseen black styrofoam disturbance caused the RL policy to command the robot to move right, leading to a collision with a plastic water bottle held in the robot's path.
- **Visual Observation:** The proposed method resulted in no visible deformation of the plastic water bottle. In contrast, the baseline method caused significant deformation of the bottle.
- **Quantitative Force Measurement:** Using a thrust meter at the contact point, the actual contact force for the proposed method was measured to be approximately 3 times less than that of the baseline.
- **Estimated External Joint Contact Force:** The estimated external joint contact force on the robot arm was less than 5 Nm for the proposed method, whereas the baseline reached 10 Nm.
- **Conclusion:** The proposed method generated significantly lower contact forces and exhibited a more compliant behavior during unexpected robot arm collisions. This demonstrates enhanced safety guarantees for both the robot and the surrounding environment, particularly under unforeseen circumstances or during the exploration phase of RL where unexpected actions may occur.

**C. Task Disturbance Rejection**
This experiment evaluated the robot's ability to maintain its primary task trajectory when subjected to external disturbances on its arm.
- **Experimental Setup:** The robot was randomly pushed by a human while performing the wiping task.
- **End-effector Position Error:** The baseline method showed a maximum end-effector position error of 4 cm in the y-direction when subjected to disturbances. In contrast, the proposed method maintained an end-effector position error of less than 1 cm in all directions.
- **Conclusion:** The results indicate that the proposed framework is more effective at rejecting impact disturbances caused by unexpected joint contacts, allowing the robot to maintain the precision of its primary task while still exhibiting compliant behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to addressing a critical limitation in the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to real-world robotic systems: the lack of comprehensive safety guarantees, particularly in contact-rich manipulation tasks. The proposed framework enhances the safety of RL-driven robots in several significant ways:

- **Comprehensive Contact Safety:** By explicitly addressing and integrating both task space (end-effector) and joint space (robot arm) contact safety, the framework provides a more holistic safety solution compared to prior work that often focuses on only one aspect or relies solely on collision avoidance for the arm. This is particularly important as contacts are often inevitable or even desired in complex manipulation.
- **Robustness to Unexpected Events:** The ability to immediately detect unexpected collisions on the robot arm and enforce small contact forces, even when the RL policy generates unforeseen behaviors (e.g., due to unseen objects), significantly improves the robustness and reliability of RL agents during both training and deployment in unstructured environments.
- **Improved Disturbance Rejection:** The framework's capacity to reject external disturbances while maintaining compliant interaction and accurate task performance is crucial for real-world applications where robots must operate in dynamic and unpredictable settings.
- **Enhanced Deployability of RL:** The demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transferability, combined with robust safety features, lowers the barrier to deploying RL policies in physical robots. This could accelerate the adoption of RL for a wider range of industrial automation, service robotics, and human-robot collaboration scenarios where safety is paramount.
- **Generic Applicability:** Although demonstrated with a specific wiping task and RL algorithm (PPO with VAE), the framework's design principles (hierarchical control, momentum observer for contact estimation, null space projection for safety) are presented as generic. This suggests potential for application across various contact-rich manipulation tasks and compatibility with different RL algorithms.
- **Protection of Assets:** By limiting contact forces, the framework helps prevent damage to the robot hardware itself, the manipulated objects, and the surrounding environment, which can include humans. This has economic implications (reduced repair costs) and ethical considerations (human safety).

In conclusion, this work offers a practical and robust approach to integrating safety directly into reinforcement learning frameworks for robot manipulation. Its potential impact lies in facilitating the transition of advanced RL techniques from simulation to safe, reliable, and compliant operation in diverse real-world contact-rich applications.
