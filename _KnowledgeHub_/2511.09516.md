# MAP-VLA: Memory-Augmented Prompting for Vision-Language-Action Model in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MAP-VLA equips a frozen pretrained VLA model (pi-0) with demonstration-derived episodic memory: stage-specific soft prompts retrieved via trajectory similarity and dynamically ensembled with the base prompt at inference. It boosts long-horizon manipulation success by up to 7.0% absolute in simulation and 25.0% on real robots, without touching model weights.

## Problem

- Pretrained VLA models act statelessly, relying only on immediate sensory inputs, so they cannot recall how an expert handled a similar stage in a long-horizon task.
- Training demonstrations' rich experience is baked into offline model weights but not explicitly accessible as retrievable episodic memory at execution time.
- Existing in-context learning approaches for robots need architecture-specific training or are limited to LLMs, leaving pretrained VLA models without native memory support.

## Method

- **Memory Prompt Construction** segments demonstrations into stages via ==RDP key-pose extraction== and ==DTW alignment==, then tunes a learnable ==soft prompt== per stage via ==flow-matching loss== to build a memory library.
- **Memory-Augmented Action Generation** retrieves the closest stage-specific prompt through ==hierarchical sliding-window trajectory similarity matching== against the ongoing robot state.
- Memory-aware ==prompt ensembling== dynamically weights base-prompt and memory-prompt action predictions via ==softmax== over their distance to the retrieved demonstration action prior.

## Results

- MAP-VLA reaches **83.4%** average success on LIBERO-Long versus **76.4%** for pi-0 and **54.0%** for OpenVLA, a **7.0%** absolute and **9.2%** relative gain over the strongest baseline.
- Real-robot long-horizon tasks improve complete success rate from **23.3%** to **48.3%** (**25.0%** absolute, **107.1%** relative gain) over pi-0 across three manipulation tasks.
- Ablation shows each component adds value: base VLA **76.4%** to universal prompt **76.9%** to task prompt **79.3%** to stage prompt **81.4%** to full MAP-VLA **83.4%** with lowest variance (**0.7%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Retrieval-augmented soft prompting can inject episodic, stage-specific memory into a frozen policy without any weight updates, staying lightweight and plug-and-play.
> - Blending a general base signal with a retrieved specific signal via prediction-agreement weighting guards against retrieval errors better than trusting retrieval alone.
> - Explicit memory retrieval improves robustness under visual perturbations and low-data few-shot regimes, suggesting memory compensates for both perception noise and data scarcity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation stands as a foundational challenge within embodied artificial intelligence, demanding sophisticated capabilities in perception, task understanding, and sequential action generation. Traditionally, robotic control relied heavily on task-specific pipelines, requiring extensive engineering and manual tuning, which inherently limited their adaptability and scalability across diverse scenarios.

The advent of large-scale pre-trained models, particularly in vision and language, has catalyzed a paradigm shift in robotics. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi_0$, represent a significant leap forward. These models leverage the vast knowledge acquired from Internet-scale vision-language pre-training and extend it to the action domain, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and map raw visual observations directly to control actions in an end-to-end manner. By integrating perception, language comprehension, and action generation into a unified policy, VLA models offer a powerful foundation for general-purpose robotic manipulation, promising enhanced robustness and generalization.

Despite these breakthroughs, a critical limitation persists: current VLA models largely operate in a "memoryless" fashion during task execution. Once trained, they rely solely on immediate sensory inputs and the overarching task instruction to determine subsequent actions. The rich experiential data embedded in their training demonstrations, while instrumental in shaping their parameters offline, is not explicitly accessible or utilized as episodic memory during real-time execution. This statelessness is particularly detrimental for long-horizon tasks, where a robot must execute a sequence of actions over an extended period, potentially requiring recall of past steps, successful strategies, or handling of similar challenging situations encountered previously. Without such memory, robots may deviate from optimal trajectories or struggle with complex, multi-stage tasks that require maintaining context and adapting based on prior internal states or observations.

Existing attempts to introduce "in-context learning" for robots, often by feeding a few demonstration episodes as part of the policy input, have either demanded extensive architectural modifications and retraining or were primarily tailored for large language models (LLMs) without direct applicability to pre-trained VLA models. This leaves a significant gap between human problem-solving, which heavily relies on recalling past experiences, and the operational mode of most contemporary VLA models.

MAP-VLA directly addresses this crucial gap. It positions itself as a timely and necessary advancement by endowing pre-trained VLA models with a lightweight, dynamic form of episodic memory. By leveraging prompt tuning, a highly efficient technique for adapting large foundation models without modifying their core parameters, MAP-VLA offers a plug-and-play solution. This approach aligns with the broader trend in AI of enhancing pre-trained models with domain-specific or contextual information via prompts, rather than resource-intensive full fine-tuning. By doing so, MAP-VLA aims to unlock the full potential of VLA models for complex, multi-step tasks, moving towards more intelligent, adaptive, and human-like robotic capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for MAP-VLA stems from a fundamental limitation observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their inability to effectively leverage historical knowledge and episodic memory during the execution of long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. While VLA models exhibit impressive generalization from large-scale pre-training, their reliance on immediate sensory inputs renders them "stateless" at test time. This means they cannot explicitly recall "how an expert accomplished a similar stage" if they encounter a challenging situation, leading to potential deviations or failures in multi-step scenarios.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the MAP-VLA framework are:

1. **Empower VLA Models with Memory:** The primary objective is to equip pre-trained, and crucially, *frozen*, VLA models with a mechanism to access and utilize demonstration-derived memory prompts. This aims to bridge the gap between human-like episodic recall and the current stateless execution of robots.
2. **Improve Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:** Specifically target long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks, where the absence of memory is most detrimental. The goal is to enhance robustness, accuracy, and overall success rates for complex, multi-stage operations.
3. **Develop a Lightweight and Flexible Solution:** Design the memory augmentation as a "plug-and-play" module that does not require retraining or modifying the internal weights of the underlying VLA model. This ensures scalability, computational efficiency, and ease of integration with diverse pre-trained models.
4. **Enable Dynamic Memory Retrieval and Integration:** Implement a system that can intelligently retrieve the most relevant stage-specific memory from a library of past demonstrations and dynamically integrate it into the VLA model's action generation process in real-time.
5. **Enhance Robustness to Variability:** Aim to make robotic policies more robust to potential challenges such as perceptual variations (e.g., changes in lighting, blurring) and ambiguities in task stages, by providing historical context that can guide more stable behavior.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to push VLA models beyond merely mapping current observations to actions, towards a more sophisticated form of intelligence that can learn from and dynamically adapt using its past experiences, akin to how humans approach sequential tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MAP-VLA introduces a novel memory-augmented framework designed to enhance pre-trained VLA models, particularly for long-horizon tasks, without altering their core parameters. The approach is bifurcated into two main components: **Memory Prompt Construction (MPC)**, an offline process, and **Memory-Augmented Action Generation (MAAG)**, an online execution process. The underlying VLA model for this work is $\pi_0$, which uses a conditional flow matching process to predict future action chunks from multi-modal observations.

#### 3.1. A. Memory Prompt Construction (MPC)

This offline component is responsible for building a library of stage-specific memory prompts from expert demonstrations.

1. **Stage Segmentation and Alignment:**
 - **Goal:** To break down long demonstrations into semantically meaningful, stage-level segments.
 - **Process:** A well-performed demonstration is selected as a reference. Its end-effector states are processed using the Ramer–Douglas–Peucker (RDP) algorithm to identify "key poses" – points marking salient transitions (e.g., grasping, changing direction). Stage boundaries are then defined at the midpoints between consecutive key poses.
 - **Consistency:** To ensure that corresponding stages across different training demonstrations refer to the same part of the task, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is employed. Each training trajectory is aligned to the RDP-segmented reference trajectory, adjusting for variations in execution speed and duration while preserving semantic task structure. This results in $K$ consistently aligned stage segments across all demonstrations.

2. **Stage-Specific Prompt Tuning:**
 - **Goal:** To encode the expert memory for each specific task stage into a learnable soft prompt.
 - **Process:** For each task stage $S_k$, a unique learnable soft prompt vector sequence $V_k$ is assigned. At any given timestep $t$, the VLA model's base prompt $P_{base}$ (derived from immediate visual and textual inputs) is augmented by element-wise addition with $V_k$, creating a stage-specific memory prompt $P_k = P_{base} + V_k$.
 - **Optimization:** The soft prompt $V_k$ is optimized via prompt tuning (using standard backpropagation) to minimize the flow matching loss, aligning the VLA model's predicted action tokens with the expert actions within that stage $S_k$. This process effectively "teaches" the prompt to guide the frozen VLA model's behavior for that specific stage.
 - **Memory Library:** Upon completion, a memory library is constructed, containing all stage-specific prompts $\{V_k\}_{k=1}^K$, alongside the original segmented demonstration trajectories and action sequences.

#### 3.2. B. Memory-Augmented Action Generation (MAAG)

This online component leverages the constructed memory library to augment action generation during real-time task execution.

1. **Memory Retrieval:**
 - **Goal:** To find the most relevant stage-specific memory prompt from the library based on the robot's current trajectory.
 - **Process:** During execution, a fixed-size sliding window $W$ is used to capture the robot's recent trajectory $S_{cur}$. This $S_{cur}$ is then compared against all candidate segments within the stored training demonstrations ($S_i$).
 - **Efficiency:** To mitigate computational cost and ensure relevance, a hierarchical strategy is used: candidate segments are restricted to those whose associated stage labels are within one step of the most recently identified current task stage $k_{cur}$ (i.e., $|k_{i,j} - k_{cur}| \leq 1$).
 - **Selection:** The $\ell_2$ distance is used to quantify similarity, and the demonstration $i^*$ and segment $j^*$ that minimize this distance are identified. The corresponding stage label $k_{cur}$ is updated, and its associated prompt $V_{k_{cur}}$ is retrieved from the memory library, along with the expert's future action chunk $A_{i^*j^*}$.

2. **Memory-Aware Prompt Ensembling:**
 - **Goal:** To intelligently combine the stage-specific guidance from the retrieved memory prompt with the broad generalization of the base VLA model.
 - **Process:** The frozen VLA model performs two parallel action predictions:
 - $A_{base_t}$: using only the base prompt $P_{base}$ from immediate observations.
 - $A_{mem_t}$: using the retrieved stage-specific memory prompt $P_{k^*}$.
 - **Dynamic Weighting:** The retrieved expert action chunk $A_{i^*j^*}$ (from the best-matching demonstration) serves as an action prior. A dynamic weighting coefficient $\alpha_t$ is computed via softmax normalization, comparing how closely $A_{base_t}$ and $A_{mem_t}$ align with $A_{i^*j^*}$. This ensures that the policy favors the prediction that is more consistent with expert behavior in a similar context.
 - **Final Action:** The final memory-augmented action $A_{MemAug_t}$ is a weighted sum: $A_{MemAug_t} = \alpha_t A_{mem_t} + (1-\alpha_t) A_{base_t}$. This dynamic ensembling strategy is crucial for maintaining robustness against retrieval inaccuracies or ambiguities, allowing the model to adapt while leveraging learned stage-specific knowledge.

In summary, MAP-VLA creates a system where a frozen VLA model can "look up" past expert behavior for the current task stage and dynamically incorporate this memory into its action generation, significantly enhancing its performance on complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate that MAP-VLA consistently and significantly outperforms state-of-the-art memoryless VLA models across a range of challenging robotic manipulation tasks, both in simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. A. Comparison on Long-Horizon Tasks

1. **LIBERO-Long Simulation Benchmark:**
 - MAP-VLA achieved an average success rate of **83.4%** across 10 long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long), substantially surpassing OpenVLA (54.0%) and the stronger baseline $\pi_0$ (76.4%).
 - This represents an absolute performance gain of **7.0%** and a relative gain of **9.2%** over $\pi_0$.
 - MAP-VLA also exhibited greater consistency, with a lower standard deviation in success rate ($\pm0.7\%$) compared to $\pi_0$ ($\pm2.3\%$), indicating improved robustness and reliability.
 - On the full LIBERO benchmark, MAP-VLA consistently outperformed all baselines across all task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), reinforcing its general superior capability.

2. **Real Robot Evaluations:**
 - Evaluations on a 6-DoF Galaxea A1 robotic arm across three real-world long-horizon tasks confirmed the simulation findings.
 - For "Partial Success" (first sub-task completed), MAP-VLA achieved an average of **68.3%** compared to $\pi_0$'s 53.3%, an absolute gain of 15.0%.
 - For "Complete Success" (both sub-tasks completed), MAP-VLA attained **48.3%** versus $\pi_0$'s 23.3%. This is a remarkable absolute gain of **25.0%** and a relative gain of **107.1%**, underscoring the critical role of memory augmentation for sustaining correct behavior over extended action sequences in real-world settings.
 - A case study highlighted MAP-VLA's ability to maintain coherent trajectories and consistent object alignment, especially during critical pick-and-place phases, where the memoryless $\pi_0$ often showed inconsistent behavior leading to failure.

#### 4.2. B. Comparison with Visual Variations

MAP-VLA's robustness was tested under various visual perturbations: smudge, defocus, gray table, cold, and warm illumination.
- Even with these challenging visual shifts, MAP-VLA consistently maintained significantly higher success rates than $\pi_0$. For instance, under the smudge condition, MAP-VLA achieved 72.3% success rate compared to $\pi_0$'s 65.4%.
- Overall, MAP-VLA demonstrated an average relative gain of 9.6% under visual variations, slightly higher than its gain without variations, suggesting strong generalization capabilities across diverse visual domains.

#### 4.3. C. Comparison under Few-Shot Setting

To evaluate adaptability with limited data, experiments were conducted with only 10 or 20 demonstrations per task.
- MAP-VLA consistently outperformed $\pi_0$ in both 10-shot (55.8% vs 53.6%) and 20-shot (75.9% vs 72.1%) settings on LIBERO-Long.
- Crucially, MAP-VLA also exhibited lower standard deviation in these few-shot scenarios ($\pm0.9\%$ and $\pm0.8\%$ respectively), demonstrating improved robustness even with limited training data.

#### 4.4. D. Ablation Study

An ablation study on LIBERO-Long quantified the contribution of each component:
- **Base VLA ($\pi_0$)**: 76.4% SR.
- **Universal Prompt**: A single prompt for all tasks; marginal gain (76.9% SR).
- **Task Prompt**: A distinct prompt per task; improved (79.3% SR).
- **Stage Prompt**: Stage-specific prompts *without* ensembling; further improved (81.4% SR), highlighting the benefit of fine-grained, retrieval-augmented memory.
- **MAP-VLA (Complete Framework)**: The full framework with memory-aware prompt ensembling achieved the highest success rate of **83.4%** and the lowest standard deviation ($\pm0.7\%$). This confirms that the dynamic ensembling mechanism is vital for balancing stage-specific guidance with task-level generalization, leading to optimal performance and robustness.

#### 4.5. E. Retrieval Latency

The memory retrieval process in MAP-VLA was shown to be computationally efficient. The average retrieval time on LIBERO-Long (with ~37.8 demonstrations per task) was only **21.6 ms**, making it suitable for real-time online execution without significant overhead.

In summary, the findings robustly establish MAP-VLA as a new state-of-the-art for long-horizon robotic manipulation, demonstrating superior success rates, enhanced robustness to variations, and efficient operation, all while acting as a lightweight, plug-and-play augmentation to existing VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "MAP-VLA: Memory-Augmented Prompting for Vision-Language-Action Model in Robotic Manipulation" holds significant implications for the field of embodied artificial intelligence and practical robotics.

**1. Addressing a Critical Limitation of VLA Models:** The most significant contribution of MAP-VLA is its elegant solution to the inherent "memorylessness" of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. By enabling VLA models to dynamically access and integrate demonstration-derived episodic memory, MAP-VLA fundamentally improves their capacity for sequential decision-making. This moves VLA models closer to how intelligent agents, including humans, tackle complex, multi-step tasks—by recalling and adapting based on past experiences.

**2. State-of-the-Art Performance for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The empirical results, particularly the substantial performance gains on the challenging LIBERO-Long benchmark in simulation (up to 7.0% absolute gain) and even more remarkably in real-world robot evaluations (25.0% absolute gain for complete success), firmly establish MAP-VLA as a new state-of-the-art. This improvement is not merely incremental but demonstrates a qualitative leap in handling tasks that demand sustained coherence and context over extended periods.

**3. Lightweight and Flexible Integration:** The design of MAP-VLA as a plug-and-play module is a major practical advantage. By employing prompt tuning and operating on a frozen pre-trained VLA model, it avoids the computational expense and data requirements of full fine-tuning or architectural modifications. This flexibility makes MAP-VLA highly adaptable to various existing VLA models and allows for efficient deployment in diverse robotic systems, accelerating the development of more capable robots.

**4. Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** The consistently lower standard deviations in success rates, even under visual variations and in few-shot settings, highlight MAP-VLA's improved robustness. By providing a grounding in expert demonstrations through retrieved memory, the system can better navigate ambiguous situations, resist perceptual noise, and maintain task integrity, leading to more reliable robotic behavior. The memory-aware prompt ensembling mechanism is crucial here, allowing for a dynamic balance between task-level generalization and stage-specific guidance, thus mitigating potential inaccuracies from retrieval.

**5. Advancing the Utility of Foundation Models in Robotics:** MAP-VLA exemplifies a powerful strategy for harnessing the vast capabilities of pre-trained foundation models (like VLA models) by efficiently injecting task-specific or episodic knowledge without altering their core learned representations. This approach is highly relevant in an era dominated by large models, offering a pathway to specialize them for real-world robotic tasks with minimal overhead.

**6. Broadening Practical Applications:** The enhanced capabilities offered by MAP-VLA have significant potential impact across various domains. More robust and adaptable assistive robots could be deployed in household support, aiding with chores or personal care, particularly for the elderly. In industrial automation, robots capable of handling longer, more complex assembly or manipulation sequences with greater reliability could revolutionize manufacturing processes. The ability to learn from fewer demonstrations also makes it appealing for specialized or low-data environments.

**7. Future Research Directions:** The paper also lays out a promising direction for future research: developing generalized and informative memory prompts that can be reused flexibly across a broader spectrum of tasks. This ambition points towards an even more generalist and adaptable robotic intelligence, reducing the need for extensive stage-specific memory construction and further democratizing advanced robotic capabilities.

In conclusion, MAP-VLA represents a pivotal step towards realizing truly intelligent and autonomous robots capable of performing complex, long-horizon tasks in dynamic and uncertain environments. Its innovative blend of memory augmentation, prompt tuning, and retrieval-augmented generation provides a robust, efficient, and highly impactful framework for the next generation of robotic systems.
