# Affordance2Action: Task-Conditioned Scene-level Affordance Grounding for Real-Time Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Affordance2Action (A2A) framework provides a real-time solution for task-conditioned scene-level part affordance grounding, enabling robots to interpret language instructions for manipulation. Its policy variant, A2A-Explicit, increased average manipulation success rates to 94.4% in simulation and achieved 26/40 successful trials in real-world tasks, improving upon Diffusion Policy baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation agents struggle to interpret human language instructions and translate them into specific physical actions in complex, cluttered multi-object environments.
- Existing affordance datasets often focus on specific interaction types (e.g., grasping) or operate at the object level, rather than identifying distinct functional parts based on a task's intent, and frequently rely on synthetic scenes.
- Current approaches typically assume a one-to-one correspondence between an instruction and a target region, failing to account for multiple valid functional regions for a single instruction in realistic multi-object scenes.
- General-purpose vision foundation models localize explicitly described visual concepts but lack the ability to infer implicit functional regions based on a task’s intent, leading to less interpretable robotic systems.

## Method

- The **Affordance2Action** (A2A) framework was developed, comprising A2A-Bench (a benchmark), A2A-AffordGen (an agent-assisted annotation pipeline), A2A-GroundingModel (a real-time grounding model), and A2A-Policy (a manipulation policy integration).
- A2A-AffordGen, an agent-assisted annotation pipeline, was introduced to construct A2A-Bench by scaling multi-object affordance annotation using a dual-regime approach (Single-Instance and Multi-Instance Affordance Generation) and VLM-based iterative refinement.
- A2A-GroundingModel was designed to convert benchmark supervision into a real-time functional-region grounding model using a frozen SAM3 backbone, staged instruction adaptation (ORPS-to-TRPS), and text-conditioned visual prompt injection.
- A2A-Policy integrates A2A-GroundingModel's predictions into a ==Diffusion Policy== backbone through two variants: explicit augmentation via RGB highlighting of affordance regions and implicit augmentation via feature-level injection.

## Results

- A2A-AffordGen achieved a +**13.7** gIoU improvement over the strongest baseline (finetuned LISA-7B) in single-instance annotation and a +**13.6** sIoU / +**19.8** P@50 improvement over SAM3+text in multi-instance scene-level annotation.
- A2A-GroundingModel attained **55.41%** gIoU and **49.52%** cIoU on TRPS instructions from A2A-Bench, surpassing SAM3-LoRA and SAM3-I, while operating in real-time at **9.7** FPS.
- The A2A-Policy with explicit affordance highlighting (A2A-Explicit) increased average success rates from **87.8%** (DP-RGB) to **94.4%** in LIBERO-object simulation and improved total successful trials from 22/40 (DP-RGB) to 26/40 in real-world manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agent-assisted annotation pipeline (A2A-AffordGen) can effectively scale the creation of high-quality, scene-level, multi-part affordance datasets that account for one-to-many instruction-region correspondences, outperforming traditional VLM-box pipelines.
> - Integrating staged instruction adaptation with text-conditioned visual prompt injection into a foundation model (A2A-GroundingModel) enables real-time, fine-grained, task-aware functional-region localization, bridging explicit part descriptions and implicit task semantics.
> - Explicitly highlighting grounded affordance regions as spatial priors in RGB observations (A2A-Explicit) provides a robust and policy-compatible interface for downstream manipulation policies, leading to improved task success rates compared to implicit feature injection or less stable heatmap-based approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation often requires agents to interpret human language instructions and translate them into physical actions within complex environments. A critical component of this process is "affordance grounding"—identifying functional regions of objects that support specific interactions. This research addresses several limitations in the existing landscape of affordance learning and robot manipulation.

Current affordance datasets and benchmarks frequently focus on specific interaction types, such as grasping, or operate at the object level, rather than identifying distinct functional parts. Many existing datasets also rely on synthetic scenes, which often lack the visual complexity and clutter characteristic of real-world environments. A significant limitation highlighted by the authors is the prevailing assumption of a one-to-one correspondence between an instruction and a target region. In realistic multi-object scenes, a single instruction may correspond to multiple valid functional regions, necessitating a "one-to-many" understanding. For example, "sit on the couch" could refer to various seating spots.

While part-level datasets, such as InstructPart, exist, they often lack sufficient object diversity, scene complexity, and scale to be broadly applicable to open-world manipulation tasks. Distillation methods, like UAD, aim to leverage knowledge from large foundation models but frequently depend on simplified object representations, limiting their robustness when applied to cluttered real-world scenarios.

Furthermore, general-purpose vision foundation models (e.g., SAM3) can perform open-vocabulary segmentation, but they typically localize explicitly described visual concepts. Affordance grounding, however, demands the inference of implicit functional regions based on a task’s intent (e.g., inferring that "open the microwave" refers to the handle, not the entire appliance). Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often learn these task-relevant regions implicitly, which can reduce the interpretability and adaptability of the robotic system. The authors propose that affordances should function as a structured intermediate representation, explicitly bridging language understanding and action prediction.

This work introduces a benchmark-centered learning framework designed to address these gaps by providing a scalable, real-time solution for task-conditioned, scene-level part affordance grounding that explicitly accounts for the one-to-many nature of affordances in diverse, cluttered environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive framework, Affordance2Action (A2A), for real-time, task-conditioned grounding of part affordances in complex, multi-object scenes. This framework aims to facilitate more effective and robust robotic manipulation.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Address Scene-Level, Task-Conditioned Affordance Complexity**: The core motivation stems from the recognition that real-world manipulation instructions often require identifying specific functional parts of objects, rather than just object categories. This grounding is inherently dependent on the task and the scene layout. For instance, "open a drawer" and "move the nightstand" target different parts of the same object. The framework aims to capture this granularity.
- **Account for One-to-Many Instruction-Region Correspondence**: A critical limitation in previous work is the assumption of a single target region for a given instruction. In cluttered, realistic environments, a single instruction may admit multiple valid functional regions (e.g., several possible places to "sit on" a couch). The project aims to explicitly model and support this one-to-many structure, which is crucial for evaluating a model's comprehensive scene-level task understanding.
- **Overcome Limitations of Existing Affordance Data and Benchmarks**: Existing datasets are often criticized for being grasp-centric, object-level, relying on synthetic scenes, or lacking the scale and diversity required for robust real-world applications. A key objective is to construct a new benchmark, A2A-Bench, that explicitly covers single-region and multi-region correspondences in everyday, natural scenes, providing more realistic supervision.
- **Bridge the Gap between Language and Functional Regions**: Generic segmentation models struggle with inferring implicit functional regions from task intent. The work seeks to develop a grounding model that can infer these functional parts from manipulation instructions, going beyond simply localizing explicitly described visual concepts. This involves linking explicit part descriptions with implicit task semantics.
- **Develop a Scalable Annotation Pipeline**: Manually annotating scene-level, multi-object, multi-part affordances is a labor-intensive and expensive process. A significant motivation is to develop an agent-assisted annotation pipeline, A2A-AffordGen, to construct the A2A-Bench at scale, reducing the reliance on full manual labeling through automation and human verification.
- **Provide Interpretable and Policy-Useful Spatial Priors**: Rather than relying on VLA policies to implicitly learn task-relevant regions, the framework aims to explicitly model affordances as a structured intermediate representation. This allows for direct use of grounded affordance masks as spatial priors to condition downstream robotic action prediction, potentially enhancing policy interpretability, adaptability, and performance.
- **Achieve Real-Time Performance**: For practical robotic applications, the affordance grounding model must operate in real-time within the policy loop. An objective is to ensure that the proposed grounding model can deliver predictions with low latency.

In summary, the motivation is to advance robotic manipulation by providing a robust, scalable, and interpretable framework for understanding task-conditioned affordances in complex real-world environments, thereby enabling more intelligent and effective robot behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Affordance2Action (A2A) framework is a benchmark-centered learning system designed for scene-level, task-conditioned part affordance grounding for real-time manipulation. It comprises three main components: A2A-Bench (the benchmark), A2A-AffordGen (the annotation pipeline), A2A-GroundingModel (the grounding model), and A2A-Policy (the manipulation policy integration).

**A2A-Bench: Scene-Level Task-Conditioned Affordance Construction**
A2A-Bench is a manipulation-oriented benchmark designed to capture both single-region and multi-region instruction correspondences in natural, multi-object scenes. Its construction addresses the challenge of annotating complex, scene-level affordances.

- **A2A-AffordGen (Agent-Assisted Annotation Pipeline)**: This pipeline scales multi-object affordance annotation using a dual-regime approach:
 - **Data Curation**: The process begins with large-scale in-the-wild images from Objects365. A VLM-based filtering mechanism discards unsuitable images (e.g., pure scenes, crowds) to retain object-centric, daily interactable items.
 - **Single-Instance Affordance Generation (SAG)**: This regime casts part segmentation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) over point prompts (clicks) applied to a frozen SAM3 model. A policy, instantiated as a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3.5-9B VLM, is trained by behavior cloning to mimic a SimpleClick distance-maximizing oracle. This oracle drops positive or negative point prompts at the chamfer-center of residual errors between the current and target mask, iteratively refining the mask. Rollouts terminate when an IoU threshold or incremental gain threshold is met, or a step cap is reached. This process allows precise part mask refinement for isolated objects.
 - **Multi-Instance Affordance Generation (MAG)**: MAG extends SAG to cluttered scenes with multiple interactable objects. For a multi-instance scene and an ORPS (Oracle Referring Part Segmentation) prompt, SAM3's text-prompt grounding first generates candidate masks with confidence scores.
 - **Direct Accept**: If SAM3's confidence (s) is high (s ≥ τ), the mask is accepted as-is.
 - **SAG-Refine**: If confidence is low (s < τ), SAM3's mask is used as a seed for a SAG rollout. A tight bounding box from Grounding DINO crops the object, and iterative mask-out (morphological dilation and gray-filling of surrounding pixels) is applied to suppress neighboring clutter, allowing SAG to operate in its native single-instance mode.
 - **SAG-from-Scratch**: If SAM3 fails entirely, Grounding DINO provides object boxes, and SAG performs rollouts initialized from an empty mask.
 - The refined masks are then mapped back to the original image and unioned to create scene-level annotations.
 - **Instruction Generation**: After mask generation, a VLM (Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) is used to generate diverse TRPS (Task-Reasoning Part Segmentation) instructions from the resulting masks, linking manipulation intent to the segmented functional regions.
 - **A2A-Bench Scale**: The benchmark combines a corpus of approximately 40K single-object part masks from various sources (RAGNet, HANDAL, InstructPart) for point-prompting policy training. A human-verified core of 5,000 multi-object, multi-part scenes (with ~18K single-part masks) from Objects365 provides initial supervision. The trained A2A-AffordGen then automatically generates annotations for unseen Objects365 data, with an initial batch of 5,000 generated multi-instance scenes released after human quality verification.

**A2A-GroundingModel: Real-time Task-Conditioned Grounding**
This model converts the A2A-Bench supervision into a real-time functional-region grounding model that predicts an affordance mask from an image-instruction pair.
- **Architecture**: It adapts a frozen SAM3 backbone with lightweight modules.
- **Staged Instruction Adaptation**: To bridge explicit part descriptions (ORPS) and implicit task semantics (TRPS), the model employs an ORPS-to-TRPS adaptation mechanism. Separate but connected bottleneck adapters (A^(ℓ)\_o for oracle queries, and a two-stage path A^(ℓ)\_o→t followed by A^(ℓ)\_t for task instructions) are used in selected language, fusion, decoder, and mask prediction blocks. This allows the model to first learn stable part-level localization and then transfer this knowledge to task-level affordance reasoning.
- **Text-Conditioned Visual Prompt Injection**: This mechanism makes the visual encoding process task-aware. The model generates hierarchical visual prompts from the image and task instruction, which are then injected into later visual encoder blocks. This biases the visual representation towards task-relevant fine-grained functional parts, especially crucial for small or weakly textured affordance regions.
- **Training Curriculum**: A three-stage ORPS-to-TRPS curriculum is used: (I) ORPS grounding, (II) TRPS affordance adaptation, and (III) joint ORPS–TRPS alignment. Only the new adaptation modules are optimized, while the SAM3 backbone remains frozen. A consistency regularization (bidirectional KL divergence and a disagreement-focused hard-region loss) aligns spatial predictions from ORPS and TRPS instructions.

**A2A-Policy: Policy Learning with Affordance Grounding**
A2A-GroundingModel's predictions are integrated into a downstream manipulation policy, a Diffusion Policy backbone, using two complementary variants:
- **Explicit Augmentation via Highlighting Affordance Region**: The highest-confidence grounded affordance region from A2A-GroundingModel is rendered as a colored alpha overlay on the raw RGB observation. This augmented observation is then fed into the action head (a frozen DINOv2 encoder). This method directly injects a visible spatial prior.
- **Implicit Augmentation via Feature-Level Injection**: A2A-GroundingModel itself functions as the visual-text encoder for the policy. Each frame is processed by its image encoder with text-conditioned visual prompt injection. The resulting multi-scale FPN features, along with the full instruction-token sequence, are fed to a shared multimodal transformer. This aggregates them into a single conditioning vector for the diffusion head, influencing action prediction through feature modulation rather than explicit visual overlay.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across A2A-AffordGen, A2A-GroundingModel, and A2A-Policy demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in various aspects of affordance grounding and robotic manipulation.

**A2A-AffordGen Evaluation (Benchmark Construction)**
A2A-AffordGen was evaluated on the A2A-Bench validation split under both single-instance (N=200) and multi-instance scene-level (N=64) protocols, using metrics such as generalized IoU (gIoU), cumulative IoU (cIoU), Precision at 50 (P@50), and set IoU (sIoU) for multi-instance.
- **Superior Performance**: A2A-AffordGen consistently outperformed six strong baselines, including SAM3+text, GroundingDINO+SAM3, various Qwen-VL+SAM3 combinations, SegAgent, and LISA-7B. It achieved a +13.7 gIoU improvement over the strongest baseline (finetuned LISA-7B) in the single-instance setting and a +13.6 sIoU / +19.8 P@50 improvement over SAM3+text in the multi-instance scene-level setting.
- **Addressing One-to-Many**: VLM-box pipelines performed well in single-instance scenarios but significantly degraded in multi-instance settings due to their inability to enumerate multiple valid functional regions. SAM3+text, while strong for multi-instance due to native proposal generation, still trailed A2A-AffordGen, indicating that text-only prompting inadequately binds affordance semantics. The iterative click-and-mask-out strategy of A2A-AffordGen effectively addressed these limitations.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer**: A2A-AffordGen exhibited strong zero-shot transfer capabilities on standard referring segmentation benchmarks (RefCOCO/+/g), outperforming the GroundingDINO→SAM3 baseline by notable margins (e.g., +9.28 cIoU on RefCOCO). This indicated that the iterative click primitive learned by A2A-AffordGen is task-agnostic.
- **Training Efficiency**: A2A-AffordGen demonstrated faster convergence during training compared to the LISA-7B finetune baseline, owing to its token-level prediction for clicks and offloading pixel synthesis to a frozen, pre-trained SAM3.

**A2A-GroundingModel Evaluation (Real-time Grounding)**
This model was evaluated on TRPS instructions from A2A-Bench for grounding accuracy and streaming inference cost.
- **Enhanced Grounding Accuracy**: A2A-GroundingModel surpassed SAM3-LoRA and SAM3-I across all grounding metrics (gIoU, cIoU, P@50, P@50-95). For instance, it achieved 55.41% gIoU, 49.52% cIoU, 56.79% P@50, and 39.55% P@50-95. This improvement was attributed to the text-conditioned visual prompt injection, which made visual feature formation task-aware beyond just adapter-based instruction tuning.
- **Instruction Consistency**: Qualitatively, A2A-GroundingModel produced more compact and instruction-consistent masks compared to the UAD baseline, which sometimes attended to incorrect regions for a given task.
- **Real-time Performance**: The model achieved a real-time inference speed of 9.7 FPS at 640x480 resolution, comparable to SAM3-I and SAM3-LoRA, indicating that the proposed adapters and prompt injection introduced minimal overhead.

**A2A-Policy Evaluation (Manipulation Performance)**
The impact of A2A-GroundingModel on robotic manipulation was assessed through two variants (explicit and implicit augmentation) against Diffusion Policy (DP) baselines in both simulation (LIBERO-object suite) and real-world (Piper arm, 4 tasks) settings.
- **Superior Explicit Affordance Highlighting**: A2A-Explicit, which used affordance masks as an explicit RGB highlight, achieved the strongest overall performance.
 - In simulation on LIBERO-object, it increased the average success rate from 87.8% (DP-RGB) to 94.4%, significantly outperforming UAD-DP (74.4%).
 - In real-world experiments, it improved the total successful trials from 22/40 (DP-RGB) and 16/40 (UAD-DP) to 26/40. These results suggest that task-conditioned affordance masks provide a useful and policy-compatible spatial prior when explicitly highlighted.
- **UAD-DP Limitations**: UAD-DP exhibited persistent failure modes due to unstable heatmaps under real-world distribution shifts, which could lead to incorrect functional parts or weak responses. This noise was observed to degrade policy performance. A2A-GroundingModel’s consistent masks offered a more stable prior.
- **Implicit Augmentation Performance**: The A2A-Implicit variant, which consumed intermediate features from A2A-GroundingModel, did not consistently outperform DP-RGB and remained weaker than A2A-Explicit. This indicates that features useful for affordance localization are not automatically optimal for action prediction without additional policy-aware alignment. The explicit affordance-mask highlighting was deemed a more stable interface for downstream manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, Affordance2Action (A2A), that addresses fundamental challenges in integrating language understanding with robotic manipulation, yielding several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Affordance Grounding**: The work moves beyond traditional object-level or grasp-centric affordance learning by providing a robust framework for *scene-level, task-conditioned part affordance grounding*. This aligns with the complexity of real-world human-robot interaction where instructions often imply specific functional regions rather than entire objects.
- **Novel Benchmark for Realistic Evaluation**: The introduction of A2A-Bench fills a gap in existing datasets. By explicitly incorporating "one-to-many" instruction-region correspondences in natural, cluttered scenes, the benchmark provides a more realistic and comprehensive environment for evaluating models' understanding of task-relevant affordances. This contributes to more rigorous and ecologically valid assessment of robotic perception systems.
- **Scalable Data Annotation Methodology**: The A2A-AffordGen pipeline significantly reduces the cost and time associated with creating high-quality, scene-level affordance annotations. Its agent-assisted approach, combining language model filtering, interactive segmentation, and iterative refinement, offers a scalable solution for building large-scale datasets, which is crucial for training complex deep learning models in robotics. This methodology can potentially be adapted for annotating other types of semantic information in robotics datasets.
- **Real-Time, Task-Aware Perception for Robots**: A2A-GroundingModel demonstrates that integrating staged instruction adaptation and text-conditioned visual prompt injection into existing foundation models (like SAM3) can yield real-time, fine-grained, task-aware functional-region localization. This capability is essential for closed-loop robotic control, where low-latency perception directly impacts responsiveness and effectiveness.
- **Improved Robotic Manipulation Policies**: The integration of A2A-GroundingModel's predictions as spatial priors into diffusion-based manipulation policies (A2A-Policy) shows tangible improvements in task success rates in both simulation and real-world environments. The effectiveness of explicit affordance mask highlighting suggests a practical and robust method for providing structured visual cues to visuomotor policies, enhancing their performance and stability compared to less specific visual inputs or less reliable heatmap-based approaches.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Adaptability**: By explicitly modeling and grounding functional regions, A2A offers a more interpretable intermediate representation between high-level language instructions and low-level robot actions. This can be beneficial for debugging robotic systems, understanding their decision-making process, and potentially for adapting policies to novel tasks or environments by simply updating the affordance definitions.
- **Foundational for Future Robotics Research**: The framework lays groundwork for future research in several areas:
 - **Mobile Manipulation and Whole-Body Control**: The task-conditioned affordance maps could be extended to inform navigation, base placement, and whole-body coordination for robots operating in larger, more diverse environments beyond tabletop tasks.
 - **Dynamic Affordance Grounding**: Future work could focus on addressing how affordance prediction remains robust during robot interaction, handling occlusions, object motion, or contact disturbances in closed-loop settings.
 - **Multi-modal Integration**: Further research might explore more sophisticated ways of implicitly integrating affordance features into policies to fully leverage their potential for action prediction.

In summary, A2A represents a substantial step towards building more intelligent and capable robotic systems that can robustly understand and execute human instructions in complex, unstructured environments by providing a principled and scalable approach to task-conditioned affordance grounding.
