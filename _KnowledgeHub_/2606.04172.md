# Affordance2Action: Task-Conditioned Scene-level Affordance Grounding for Real-Time Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Affordance2Action (A2A) framework provides a real-time solution for task-conditioned scene-level part affordance grounding, enabling robots to interpret language instructions for manipulation. Its policy variant, A2A-Explicit, increased average manipulation success rates to 94.4% in simulation and achieved 26/40 successful trials in real-world tasks, improving upon Diffusion Policy baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation agents struggle to interpret human language instructions and translate them into specific physical actions in complex, cluttered multi-object environments.
- Existing affordance datasets often focus on specific interaction types (e.g., grasping) or operate at the object level, rather than identifying distinct functional parts based on a task's intent, and frequently rely on synthetic scenes.
- Current approaches typically assume a one-to-one correspondence between an instruction and a target region, failing to account for multiple valid functional regions for a single instruction in realistic multi-object scenes.
- General-purpose vision foundation models localize explicitly described visual concepts but lack the ability to infer implicit functional regions based on a task’s intent, leading to less interpretable robotic systems.

## Method

- The **Affordance2Action** (A2A) framework was developed, comprising A2A-Bench (a benchmark), A2A-AffordGen (an agent-assisted annotation pipeline), A2A-GroundingModel (a real-time grounding model), and A2A-Policy (a manipulation policy integration).
- A2A-AffordGen, an agent-assisted annotation pipeline, was introduced to construct A2A-Bench by scaling multi-object affordance annotation using a dual-regime approach (Single-Instance and Multi-Instance Affordance Generation) and VLM-based iterative refinement.
- A2A-GroundingModel was designed to convert benchmark supervision into a real-time functional-region grounding model using a frozen SAM3 backbone, staged instruction adaptation (ORPS-to-TRPS), and text-conditioned visual prompt injection.
- A2A-Policy integrates A2A-GroundingModel's predictions into a ==Diffusion Policy== backbone through two variants: explicit augmentation via RGB highlighting of affordance regions and implicit augmentation via feature-level injection.

## Results

- A2A-AffordGen achieved a +**13.7** gIoU improvement over the strongest baseline (finetuned LISA-7B) in single-instance annotation and a +**13.6** sIoU / +**19.8** P@50 improvement over SAM3+text in multi-instance scene-level annotation.
- A2A-GroundingModel attained **55.41%** gIoU and **49.52%** cIoU on TRPS instructions from A2A-Bench, surpassing SAM3-LoRA and SAM3-I, while operating in real-time at **9.7** FPS.
- The A2A-Policy with explicit affordance highlighting (A2A-Explicit) increased average success rates from **87.8%** (DP-RGB) to **94.4%** in LIBERO-object simulation and improved total successful trials from 22/40 (DP-RGB) to 26/40 in real-world manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agent-assisted annotation pipeline (A2A-AffordGen) can effectively scale the creation of high-quality, scene-level, multi-part affordance datasets that account for one-to-many instruction-region correspondences, outperforming traditional VLM-box pipelines.
> - Integrating staged instruction adaptation with text-conditioned visual prompt injection into a foundation model (A2A-GroundingModel) enables real-time, fine-grained, task-aware functional-region localization, bridging explicit part descriptions and implicit task semantics.
> - Explicitly highlighting grounded affordance regions as spatial priors in RGB observations (A2A-Explicit) provides a robust and policy-compatible interface for downstream manipulation policies, leading to improved task success rates compared to implicit feature injection or less stable heatmap-based approaches.
