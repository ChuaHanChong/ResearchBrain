# Guiding Cross-Modal Representations with MLLM Priors via Preference Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> The MAPLE (Modality-Aligned Preference Learning for Embeddings) framework enhances cross-modal representation learning for MLLM-based retrieval models. It leverages the fine-grained alignment capabilities of off-the-shelf MLLMs by constructing preference data and using a novel Relative Preference Alignment (RPA) loss, achieving superior performance on both general and fine-grained retrieval benchmarks.

## Problem

- The persistent "modality gap" in cross-modal retrieval models leads to misaligned visual and textual representations, limiting retrieval effectiveness.
- Existing MLLM-based retrievers, adapted from generative architectures, often diminish their inherent fine-grained alignment capabilities due to coarse alignment mechanisms.
- A lack of thorough investigation into the intrinsic fine-grained alignment properties of logit-based MLLMs for leveraging them in discriminative retrieval tasks.

## Method

- The **MAPLE** framework converts a pre-trained MLLM into a ==discriminative retriever== by modifying its attention mechanism and adding mean-pooling for feature aggregation.
- Automatically constructs fine-grained preference data in a two-stage process: offline candidate generation (semantic deduplication, hard negative mining, discriminative captioning) and online scoring by an MLLM as a reward model.
- Introduces the Relative Preference Alignment (RPA) loss, an adaptation of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for embedding models, which is combined with InfoNCE-style contrastive loss for regularization, with both pairwise and listwise variants.

## Results

- MAPLE (Qwen2-VL-**7B**) achieved R@1 scores of 75.5 (Text) on COCO and 94.3 (Text) on Flickr30K, surpassing strong baselines such as SigLIPv2 (g/16-**2B**) and EVA-CLIP (**18B**).
- Substantial improvements were observed on fine-grained retrieval tasks, with MAPLE (Qwen2-VL-**7B**) achieving 56.0 (Text) on Winoground, significantly outperforming SigLIPv2's 39.8.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the listwise RPA loss consistently outperformed its pairwise counterpart, and combining RPA with InfoNCE regularization was crucial for maintaining balanced performance across general and fine-grained retrieval.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-the-shelf MLLMs possess stronger inherent fine-grained modality alignment capabilities compared to contrastively pre-trained models like CLIP, quantifiable through a unified 1-Wasserstein Distance metric.
> - Leveraging MLLM-derived preferences via a listwise preference alignment loss effectively guides cross-modal representation learning, proving particularly beneficial for nuanced semantic distinctions.
> - The unified metric based on the 1-Wasserstein Distance provides a robust tool for quantitatively assessing the modality gap across both logit-based MLLMs and embedding-based models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Cross-modal retrieval, which involves querying content in one modality (e.g., text) to retrieve relevant content in another (e.g., images), represents a foundational area within vision-language research. It underpins various downstream applications, including visual question answering, retrieval-augmented generation, and advanced multi-modal agent systems.

A significant development in this field has been the advent of large-scale contrastive pre-training frameworks like Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training (CLIP). While these models have demonstrated capabilities in cross-modal content retrieval, a persistent challenge is the "modality gap." This gap refers to discrepancies in feature representations between visual and textual modalities, often manifesting as a spatial separation between image and text embeddings in the shared latent space. This misalignment has been observed to limit the effectiveness of learned representations in various retrieval tasks and has been attributed to factors such as architectural choices, training dynamics, and input information imbalance.

Recent research has primarily focused on measuring this modality gap using explicit embeddings. However, the alignment properties of models operating directly via logits, such as Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), have received less exploration. MLLMs, with their unified architectures, have recently been adapted for retrieval tasks. Yet, a transition from their generative architectures to retriever-specific designs, even with fine-tuning, has been observed to diminish their inherent alignment capabilities. This suggests that current MLLM-based retrievers often rely on coarse modality alignment mechanisms, potentially limiting their full potential for fine-grained semantic understanding. The present work positions itself within this context by investigating how to leverage the inherent, fine-grained alignment strengths of off-the-shelf MLLMs to enhance cross-modal representation learning in MLLM-based retrieval models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to guide cross-modal representation learning in MLLM-based retrieval models by leveraging the fine-grained alignment priors observed in off-the-shelf MLLMs. This aims to mitigate the "modality gap" that continues to affect retrieval performance, particularly in tasks requiring nuanced semantic distinctions.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and existing challenges:

- **Persistent Modality Gap:** Despite the success of contrastive learning frameworks like CLIP, a fundamental challenge remains the "modality gap." This gap manifests as a misalignment in the feature spaces of different modalities, hindering the model's ability to precisely match semantically similar content across modalities. Prior work has documented this issue, but effective strategies for its comprehensive mitigation, especially for fine-grained distinctions, are still under development.
- **Underexplored MLLM Alignment Properties:** While the modality gap has been extensively studied in embedding-based models, the inherent alignment properties of logit-based models, such as off-the-shelf MLLMs, have not been thoroughly investigated. The authors developed a unified metric based on the 1-Wasserstein Distance to quantify this gap across different model architectures. Through this metric, they observed that MLLMs like Qwen2-VL exhibit strong inherent modality alignment capabilities, even without relying on explicit embedding representations. This intrinsic alignment capacity of MLLMs serves as a key motivation, suggesting a valuable source of fine-grained semantic knowledge.
- **Limitations of Current MLLM-based Retrievers:** Recent efforts have fine-tuned MLLMs for retrieval by adapting their architectures for cross-modal representation learning. However, these models, while achieving a degree of alignment, often lose some of the native fine-grained alignment present in their original generative forms. This degradation is attributed to their reliance on coarse modality alignment mechanisms, such as InfoNCE-style contrastive loss, which treats all negative samples uniformly without considering nuanced semantic similarities. The goal is to adapt MLLMs for retrieval tasks while preserving and transferring their strong, fine-grained cross-modal alignment strengths, rather than diminishing them.
- **Need for Fine-Grained Discrimination:** Current alignment strategies struggle with subtle semantic differences, particularly when distinguishing between semantically similar negative samples. This limitation is pronounced in fine-grained retrieval tasks where nuanced understanding is critical. The authors aim to enable retrieval models to capture these subtle distinctions, moving beyond coarse alignment to a more precise understanding of cross-modal relationships.

In summary, the research is motivated by the recognized limitations of existing retrieval models in addressing the modality gap, particularly at a fine-grained level, and by the discovery of a largely untapped source of fine-grained alignment knowledge within off-the-shelf MLLMs. The objective is to design a framework that can effectively transfer this implicit knowledge into explicit, enhanced cross-modal representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **MAPLE (Modality-Aligned Preference Learning for Embeddings)**, is designed to leverage the fine-grained alignment priors of off-the-shelf MLLMs to guide cross-modal representation learning. This is achieved through a reinforcement learning-like process involving two main components: automatic preference data construction and a novel preference alignment loss.

#### 3.1. MLLM-based Retriever Architecture

The policy model in MAPLE is initialized from a pretrained MLLM backbone (e.g., Qwen2-VL). To convert the autoregressive MLLM into a discriminative retrieval paradigm, two modifications are made: replacing the causal attention mask with a bidirectional attention mask and adding mean-pooling over the final hidden states to aggregate features for retrieval. The model is then fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on the LLM component.

#### 3.2. Unified Modality Gap Metric

To quantitatively assess the modality gap in both logit-based MLLMs and embedding-based models like CLIP, the authors introduce a unified metric based on the 1-Wasserstein Distance (WD).
- For MLLMs, pairwise alignment scores are computed using the "Yes"/"No" token logits from a relevance query (e.g., "Does the image align with the text?").
- For embedding models, cosine similarities are used.
- WD measures the discrepancy between similarity distributions. It considers two aspects:
 - **Distributional Gap (W_dist-gap):** Measures alignment between intra-modal and cross-modal similarity distributions. A lower value indicates better alignment but risks representation collapse if too close to zero.
 - **Discriminative Gap (W_disc-gap):** Assesses the model's ability to distinguish between matching and non-matching pairs. A higher value indicates better discriminative ability.
- The combined metric, **∆_gap = W_dist-gap / W_disc-gap**, provides a comprehensive measure, with a lower value indicating a superior model.

#### 3.3. Preference Data Construction

This component involves a two-stage process to create preference data from a curated dataset based on OpenImage.

- **Offline Stage: Candidate Generation:**
 1. **Semantic Deduplication:** DINOv2 embeddings are extracted from images, and Semantic Deduplication (SemDeup) is applied to filter near-duplicate images and group similar ones.
 2. **Hard Negative Mining:** For each anchor image, top-K nearest neighbors (hard negative images) are retrieved based on DINOv2 embedding similarity.
 3. **Caption Generation:** An off-the-shelf, powerful MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is used to generate *discriminative captions*. For each anchor image and its hard negatives, the MLLM generates captions that explicitly highlight the visual differences between image pairs. This process generates diverse and challenging textual negatives.

- **Online Stage: Scoring and Structuring Preferences:**
 1. **Alignment Score Computation:** During training, an off-the-shelf MLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B) acts as a reward model. It dynamically computes fine-grained alignment scores (α) for anchor-candidate pairs. This is done by prompting the MLLM with a relevance query ("<image> Does the image align with the text <text>? Answer Yes or No") and calculating the softmax over the "Yes" and "No" token logits.
 2. **Preference Structuring:** The candidates are sorted based on these MLLM-derived alignment scores. The ranked list is then used to construct preference data in two ways:
 - **Pairwise Preferences:** Pairs (x_a, x_b) are formed such that x_a is preferred over x_b if its alignment score (α) with the anchor is higher.
 - **Listwise Preferences:** The entire ranked list (x_0, x_1..., x_K) is utilized. For each element x_k, it is considered preferred over all subsequent elements in its suffix ({x_j | j > k}).

#### 3.4. Preference Alignment (Relative Preference Alignment - RPA Loss)

Inspired by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), MAPLE introduces a novel **Relative Preference Alignment (RPA)** loss adapted for embedding models.

- **DPO Adaptation:**
 - The need for a separate reference model, as in standard DPO, is eliminated by assuming a uniform prior for the reference model, similar to Contrastive Preference Optimization (CPO). This reduces computational and memory overhead.
 - Log probabilities log π_θ(y|x) are replaced with scaled similarity scores β(z_anchor · z_candidate) between embeddings produced by the MLLM-based retrieval model.

- **RPA Loss Variants:**
 - **Pairwise RPA Loss (L_RPA-Pairwise):** Optimizes for pairwise preferences. For an anchor and a preferred/dispreferred candidate pair, it maximizes the difference in similarity scores, weighted by the MLLM's preference margin (difference in α scores) for that pair.
 - **Listwise RPA Loss (L_RPA-Listwise):** Optimizes for listwise preferences. It encourages the top-ranked item within any suffix of the MLLM's ranked list to have the highest similarity score. Each term in the loss is weighted by the average MLLM alignment score difference between the top-ranked item and all less preferred items in that suffix. This loss is designed to capture the holistic ranking structure more effectively.

- **Regularized Relative Preference Alignment:**
 To prevent feature collapse and maintain general cross-modal alignment capabilities, the chosen RPA loss (L_RPA) is combined with a standard InfoNCE-style contrastive loss (L_contrast), typically computed on original anchor pairs within the batch. The final training objective is:
 **L = λL_RPA + (1 - λ)L_contrast**
 where λ is a hyperparameter balancing the strength of preference alignment against regularization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate MAPLE across general and fine-grained cross-modal retrieval benchmarks, providing insights into its performance and the contributions of its components.

#### 4.1. MLLMs' Inherent Modality Alignment

Quantitative comparison using the proposed unified 1-Wasserstein Distance (WD) metric revealed that off-the-shelf MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B) exhibit stronger inherent modality alignment capabilities compared to CLIP. Specifically, Qwen2-VL-7B demonstrated a significantly lower ∆_gap (1.09 on MMVP, 3.58 on Winoground) compared to OpenCLIP (ViT-H/14) (47.28 on MMVP, 36.1 on Winoground). This indicated MLLMs' superior discriminative capability (higher W_disc-gap) while maintaining a low distributional gap (W_dist-gap).

#### 4.2. Superior Retrieval Performance

MAPLE consistently outperformed both CLIP-based and other MLLM-based retrieval models across a range of benchmarks:
- **General Retrieval (COCO, Flickr30K):** MAPLE (Qwen2-VL-7B) achieved R@1 scores of 75.5 (Text) and 60.3 (Image) on COCO, and 94.3 (Text) and 86.1 (Image) on Flickr30K. These scores generally surpassed strong baselines like SigLIPv2 (g/16-2B) and EVA-CLIP (18B).
- **Fine-grained Retrieval (Winoground, NaturalBench, MMVP, BiVLC):** The improvements were particularly pronounced on fine-grained tasks.
 - On Winoground, MAPLE (Qwen2-VL-7B) achieved 56.0 (Text) and 32.7 (Image), substantially outperforming SigLIPv2 (39.8 Text, 17.0 Image) and VladVA (40.5 Text, 17.5 Image).
 - On NaturalBench, MAPLE (Qwen2-VL-7B) achieved 76.1 (Text) and 76.8 (Image), exceeding SigLIPv2 (65.5 Text, 68.7 Image).
These results indicate the effectiveness of the preference-guided alignment approach in capturing nuanced cross-modal relationships.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Loss Components

- **Relative Preference Alignment (RPA):** Using only RPA losses (L_RPA-Pairwise, L_RPA-Listwise) on preference data, while sometimes reducing general retrieval performance when used alone, resulted in significantly better fine-grained retrieval compared to using only a standard contrastive loss on preference examples (L_contrast-pref). This highlighted the importance of preference alignment for nuanced distinctions.
- **Regularization:** Combining RPA losses with the standard contrastive loss (L_contrast) as a regularizer mitigated the performance degradation on general retrieval tasks while maintaining strong fine-grained performance.
- **Listwise vs. Pairwise RPA:** L_RPA-Listwise consistently achieved better results than L_RPA-Pairwise across nearly all scenarios. This was attributed to the listwise loss's ability to align preferences across the entire ranked list, providing a more comprehensive signal than independent pairwise comparisons.
- **Impact of Balancing Hyperparameter λ:** Analysis of the balancing weight λ between L_RRPA and L_contrast showed a trade-off: increasing λ improved fine-grained discrimination but could slightly degrade general retrieval abilities. Optimal λ values were identified to balance these objectives.

#### 4.4. Impact of Expanded Negative Pool

An efficient strategy to implicitly enlarge the effective batch size for contrastive loss, by incorporating readily available hard negatives, yielded consistent performance improvements across both general and fine-grained retrieval tasks without incurring substantial additional computational overhead for MLLMs.

#### 4.5. Modality Gap Reduction with MAPLE

MAPLE with RPA loss substantially reduced the distributional gap (W_dist-gap) while improving discriminative power (W_disc-gap). For instance, on MMVP, MAPLE (with L_RPA-Listwise) achieved a W_dist-gap of 5.32 and W_disc-gap of 0.94, leading to a ∆_gap of 5.66, which was an improvement over the baseline (19.15) and OpenCLIP (47.28). This indicated that the framework effectively brought the modality representations closer while enhancing their ability to differentiate between matching and non-matching pairs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MAPLE, a framework that addresses a critical challenge in cross-modal retrieval by systematically leveraging the inherent fine-grained alignment capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

**Novel Approach to Modality Alignment:** The work proposes a novel method for transferring the implicit alignment knowledge from powerful off-the-shelf MLLMs into the embedding spaces of MLLM-based retrieval models. This contrasts with previous approaches that often diminished MLLMs' inherent alignment during adaptation for retrieval. By formulating the learning as a preference alignment problem with automatic data construction and a tailored DPO-inspired loss (RPA), the research offers a distinct paradigm for enhancing cross-modal representations.

**Advancement in Fine-grained Retrieval:** The demonstrated significant performance improvements, particularly on fine-grained retrieval tasks (e.g., Winoground, NaturalBench), highlight the framework's effectiveness in handling nuanced semantic distinctions. This has implications for applications requiring precise understanding of complex visual-linguistic compositions, moving beyond coarse similarity matching.

**Unified Modality Gap Metric:** The introduction of a unified metric based on the 1-Wasserstein Distance provides a valuable tool for quantitatively assessing the modality gap across diverse model architectures, including both logit-based MLLMs and embedding-based models like CLIP. This metric can facilitate more robust analysis and comparison in future research on multimodal representation learning.

**Implications for MLLM Utilization:** The findings suggest that MLLMs are not merely powerful generative models but also possess intrinsic, valuable alignment priors that can be extracted and utilized to improve other vision-language tasks. This opens new avenues for exploring MLLMs as "reward models" or "teacher models" for guiding the training of more efficient or specialized discriminative models.

**Broader Impact on Preference-Guided Optimization:** By adapting Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for cross-modal embedding learning, the research extends the applicability of preference-guided optimization techniques beyond traditional language generation tasks. This could inspire further exploration of reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) or similar methods in multimodal contexts.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:** The authors acknowledge limitations, including potential biases inherited from the MLLM used for preference generation and the need for validation on more complex tasks such as composed retrieval. Addressing these could lead to even more robust and versatile cross-modal systems. This work lays a foundation for future research in several areas, including the development of more sophisticated MLLM-based data curation strategies and the application of MAPLE to a wider array of multimodal reasoning challenges.
