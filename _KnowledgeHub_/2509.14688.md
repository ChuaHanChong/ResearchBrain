# exUMI: Extensible Robot Teaching System with Action-aware Task-agnostic Tactile Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced exUMI, an extensible robot teaching system, and Tactile Predictive Pretraining (TPP), an algorithm for learning action-aware tactile representations. The system enables efficient collection of large-scale, contact-rich multimodal datasets and significantly improves robot manipulation performance on force-sensitive tasks, achieving up to 55% higher success rates compared to vision-only policies.

## Problem

- Existing portable robot teaching systems (e.g., UMI) suffer from unreliable proprioception, leading to low data usability, and lack robust integrated tactile sensing.
- Tactile-aware robot learning is hindered by data scarcity, sparsity (valid contacts <10% of trajectories), and limitations in representation learning.
- Current methods struggle to model the dynamic, action-aware nature of touch, which is crucial for contact-rich manipulation.

## Method

- Developed **exUMI**, a low-cost, open-source hardware system with robust ==proprioception== (AR Motion Capture, magnetic rotary encoder) and integrated ==visuo-tactile sensors== for efficient, high-quality multimodal data collection.
- Introduced **Tactile Predictive Pretraining (TPP)**, an action-aware, task-agnostic framework that casts tactile representation learning as a ==conditional future prediction== problem using a ==diffusion model==.
- Collected a large-scale, contact-rich "human play data" dataset (over 1 million frames) using exUMI to pretrain the TPP tactile encoder.

## Results

- The exUMI hardware achieves nearly **100%** data usability and **10x** higher efficiency in collecting contact-rich data compared to teleoperation, enabling a dataset of over **1 million** frames.
- TPP-pretrained tactile embeddings, when integrated into a diffusion policy, consistently and significantly improve success rates on various contact-sensitive robot manipulation tasks (e.g., **15-55%** gains over vision-only baselines).
- Ablation studies confirm that multimodal conditioning (vision and action sequences) is crucial for accurate tactile prediction within the TPP framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A co-design approach, integrating hardware improvements for robust data collection with novel algorithmic approaches for representation learning, is effective for advancing tactile robot learning.
> - Modeling tactile perception as an action-conditioned temporal prediction problem allows the learning of rich, dynamic, and generalizable tactile representations that capture contact mechanics.
> - Efficient collection of large-scale, contact-rich "human play data" significantly mitigates the issues of tactile data scarcity and sparsity for pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "exUMI" addresses critical challenges at the intersection of robot data collection, multimodal sensing, and representation learning for dexterous manipulation. The broader research landscape is currently intensely focused on enabling robots to learn complex skills from diverse data sources, particularly human demonstrations.

**Evolution of Robot Data Collection:**
Historically, robot data collection has been dominated by two extremes:
1. **Teleoperation:** Provides high-quality, precise robot trajectories but is labor-intensive, expensive, and scales poorly.
2. **Learning from Human Demonstration (in the wild):** Offers massive, cheap data but often lacks the precision and embodiment-specific accuracy required for direct robot transfer.

Recent advancements have introduced **portable hand-held devices and physical twins** like UMI and AirExo, which bridge this gap by allowing humans to kinesthetically teach robots directly. These systems aim for efficient, in-the-wild data collection that is more amenable to robot embodiment. exUMI directly builds upon and significantly extends the UMI paradigm, indicating a clear progression in this area. It seeks to rectify known limitations of UMI, such as its vulnerability to proprioception errors (e.g., SLAM failures) and its lack of integrated, robust tactile sensing.

**Challenges in Tactile-Aware Robot Learning:**
While vision-based robot learning has made significant strides, integrating tactile feedback remains a formidable challenge, crucial for contact-rich tasks (e.g., grasping deformable objects, precise insertion, force-sensitive assembly). The paper highlights several core problems:
1. **Data Scarcity and Sparsity:** Tactile signals are often sparse, with valid contacts occurring in less than 10% of typical manipulation trajectories. This severely limits the effectiveness of direct imitation learning approaches.
2. **Representation Learning Limitations:**
 - **Direct Imitation Learning:** Suffers from data scarcity and often fails to generalize.
 - **Spatial Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Often borrows inductive biases from vision (e.g., translation invariance) that may not apply to dynamic tactile signals, potentially learning incorrect representations.
 - **Visual-Tactile Alignment:** Assumes a coarse one-to-one mapping between vision and touch, overlooking the one-to-many relationship where varying contact forces can produce different tactile signals for similar visual scenes. Moreover, it treats vision and tactile as strictly aligned rather than complementary.

**The exUMI Contribution to the Landscape:**
This work contributes to the landscape by:
- **Advancing Data Collection Systems:** It pushes the boundaries of human-centric robot teaching by providing a more robust, extensible, and tactile-integrated hardware platform. By achieving nearly 100% data usability and higher efficiency, exUMI tackles a major bottleneck in robot learning.
- **Novel Representation Learning:** It introduces a new paradigm, Tactile Prediction Pretraining (TPP), which explicitly models the *dynamic* and *action-aware* nature of touch. This shifts from static tactile observations to understanding contact mechanics in relation to intended motion, mirroring human haptic perception. This is a significant conceptual leap compared to previous SSL or alignment methods for tactile data.
- **Addressing Data Gaps:** The creation of a large-scale, contact-rich tactile-action aligned dataset (over 1 million frames) is a crucial resource for the community, which has historically lacked diverse and extensive tactile datasets compared to visual data.

In essence, exUMI positions itself as a dual-pronged solution that simultaneously improves the *means* of acquiring high-quality multimodal robot learning data (hardware) and the *methods* for extracting meaningful, actionable insights from that data (algorithm), particularly for the under-explored domain of dynamic tactile perception. It aims to make contact-rich manipulation more accessible and robust for autonomous robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **bridge the gap between human tactile intuition and robot learning** by overcoming fundamental bottlenecks in tactile data collection and representation. This is crucial for enabling robots to perform complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks that currently elude them due to a lack of sophisticated tactile understanding.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

**Hardware-Centric Objectives (Motivated by existing system limitations):**
1. **Develop an Extensible and Robust Data Collection System:** The primary motivation here is to address the shortcomings of existing portable robot teaching systems like UMI. UMI, while innovative, suffered from vulnerable proprioception (visual SLAM and ArUco markers were prone to failure) and lacked integrated, robust tactile sensing.
 - **Goal 1.1: Robust Proprioception:** Achieve nearly 100% data usability by replacing unreliable tracking systems with robust alternatives for end-effector 6D pose (AR Motion Capture) and gripper width (magnetic rotary encoder). This is motivated by the need for high-quality, clean input data for robot learning.
 - **Goal 1.2: Modularity and Extensibility:** Design a system that can seamlessly integrate various additional sensors, especially visuo-tactile sensors, through a centralized controller. This is motivated by the increasing need for multimodal data in robotics and the desire for a future-proof platform.
 - **Goal 1.3: Durable and Consistent Tactile Sensing:** Embed high-quality, low-cost visuo-tactile sensors (an upgraded 9DTact design) on the gripper fingertips, ensuring stability, durability, and consistent performance for reliable tactile data acquisition. This is driven by the general fragility and inconsistency often associated with tactile sensors.
 - **Goal 1.4: Portability and Affordability:** Maintain the "in-the-wild" data collection capability without relying on fixed infrastructure, and ensure the system is low-cost and DIY-friendly to democratize access to advanced tactile robotics research.

**Algorithm-Centric Objectives (Motivated by limitations in tactile representation learning):**
2. **Propose an Action-aware, Task-agnostic Tactile Representation Learning Framework:** The core motivation here is to overcome the limitations of current tactile learning paradigms, which struggle with data scarcity, sparsity, and accurately modeling the dynamic nature of touch.
 - **Goal 2.1: Model Contact Dynamics:** Reformulate tactile learning as an action-conditioned temporal prediction problem (Tactile Prediction Pretraining - TPP). This is motivated by the human intuition that tactile perception is intrinsically linked to motion intent and future contact outcomes.
 - **Goal 2.2: Mitigate Tactile Sparsity and Scarcity:** Leverage efficiently collected, large-scale, contact-rich "human play data" to pretrain a tactile encoder, allowing it to learn rich representations even when task-specific contact data is limited. This addresses the bottleneck of insufficient diverse tactile data.
 - **Goal 2.3: Learn Generalizable Representations:** Develop representations that capture fundamental contact mechanics rather than task-specific features, promoting transferability to novel manipulation tasks. This avoids the pitfalls of direct imitation learning or spatial SSL methods that might impose incorrect inductive biases.

**Validation Objective:**
3. **Empirically Validate the Co-designed System:** Demonstrate the combined effectiveness of the exUMI hardware and TPP algorithm in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, showing significant performance gains, especially in force-sensitive scenarios, over existing baselines.

In essence, the authors are motivated by the clear need for better tools and methods to bring the sense of touch to robot learning, arguing that a holistic co-design approach for both data acquisition and representation learning is essential for progress.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is characterized by a **co-design philosophy**, where hardware and algorithmic innovations are developed synergistically to address the challenges of tactile-aware robot learning.

#### 3.1. Hardware System: exUMI (Extensible Universal Manipulation Interface)

exUMI is presented as an extensible upgrade to the original UMI system, meticulously designed around principles of precise proprioception, extensibility, and portability.

- **Robust Proprioception:**
 - **AR Motion Capture System:** Replaces the vulnerable visual SLAM of UMI with a Meta Quest 3 headset for robust and accurate 6D pose tracking of the end-effector. The VR controller is integrated via a custom mount, and the headset tracks its 6D pose. This system is chosen for its accuracy, robustness to occlusion, and potential for future AR-assisted user interfaces.
 - **Magnetic Rotary Encoder:** Addresses the inaccuracy and occlusion issues of ArUco markers for gripper state estimation. A low-cost AS5600 magnetic rotary encoder is integrated to provide precise and robust gripper width tracking.
- **Extensibility and Centralized Control:**
 - An Orange Pi controller serves as a universal sensor hub, synchronously capturing data from the AR headset, rotary encoder, and any additional sensors, like tactile sensors. This architecture supports modularity, allowing easy integration of new modalities.
 - **Non-Parallel Gripper Mechanical Design:** An additional mechanical system is provided to support non-parallel grippers (e.g., Flexiv Grav, Robotiq 2F), enhancing the system's adaptability to various robotic platforms.
- **Fingertip Visuo-tactile Sensors:**
 - A low-cost visuo-tactile sensor based on the 9DTact design is embedded in the fingertips. The authors redesigned the sensor with a contact protection structure (bevelled shell to secure silicone gel) and optimized cable/power connections for enhanced durability and stability. A customized mold ensures consistent silicone gel thickness for stable tactile sensing quality.
- **Visual Input:** A GoPro camera with a fisheye lens is used for a wide and clear view, positioned to minimize body occlusion.
- **Cost and Accessibility:** The system is designed to be low-cost ($698 starting) and DIY-friendly, with all CAD files intended for open-source release.

#### 3.2. Data Processing

The exUMI system incorporates streamlined calibration protocols for high data usability:
- **System Calibration (One-time):**
 - **AR Controller Calibration:** Aligns exUMI with robot base coordinates in AR space.
 - **Gripper State Calibration:** Maps AS5600 readings to absolute gripper width through incremental positioning.
- **Latency Calibration:** A novel protocol involves horizontal sweeping above an ArUco marker. X-axis movement of AR MoCap and marker trajectories are extracted, and an MSE minimization algorithm determines the latency offset between the two systems, ensuring tight synchronization (<50 ms error).

#### 3.3. Algorithmic Approach: Tactile Predictive Pretraining (TPP)

TPP is an action-aware, task-agnostic tactile representation learning framework designed to model contact dynamics.

- **Action-aware Tactile Data Collection:**
 - Leveraging exUMI's portability, a large-scale "human play data" dataset is efficiently collected. Collectors randomly interact with diverse objects (300+) across various environments (10), accumulating over 1 million frames of aligned image-tactile-action data. This dataset is "contact-rich," with over 60% active tactile frames, enhancing efficiency (480K tactile frames from 5 hours of interaction, 10x faster than teleoperation).
- **Tactile Predictive Pretraining (TPP):**
 - **Problem Formulation:** Tactile representation learning is cast as a conditional future prediction task: `p_θ(T_{t+1:t+n} | E_T(T_{t-n+1:t}), E_V(V_t), E_A(A_{t-n+1:t+n}))`. The goal is for the tactile encoder `E_T` to learn informative representations encoding tactile dynamics.
 - **Architecture:** The framework adopts a diffusion model and masked autoencoder structure, similar to.
 - **Multimodal Encoding:** A learnable VAE model acts as the encoder (`E_T`) and decoder (`D_T`) for tactile images, converting them into patch embeddings.
 - **Tactile Prediction:** History tactile patch embeddings and action features are fused using a transformer. These inputs are then fed into a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) to predict future tactile latents.
 - **Conditions:** The LDM's denoising process is conditioned on the *future action sequence* (`A_{t+1:t+n}`) and the *current RGB image* (`V_t`). This is the "action-aware" component, allowing the model to reason about contact dynamics given intended future movements.
 - **Losses:** The model is trained with a hybrid loss comprising: (1) `L_diff`: the standard diffusion loss, and (2) `L_recon`: an MSE loss for reconstructing the tactile images.
- **Policy Learning:** After pretraining, the tactile encoder `E_T` is **frozen** and integrated into a standard imitation learning policy (specifically, a diffusion policy). This allows the policy to leverage the rich tactile dynamics knowledge acquired during pretraining without further finetuning the encoder, addressing data scarcity in downstream tasks.

The overall approach is a robust and comprehensive strategy to enhance robot learning with tactile feedback, addressing both the practical challenges of data acquisition and the theoretical challenges of dynamic tactile representation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields significant findings across hardware validation, tactile representation learning, and real-world robot manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. exUMI Hardware System Validation

- **Proprioception Precision:** The AR Motion Capture system demonstrates high accuracy, achieving mean position errors of 5.4 mm (X-axis), 2.3 mm (Y-axis), and 1.7 mm (Z-axis) compared to ground truth, with rotation errors below 1 degree. This significantly outperforms previous vision-based SLAM systems in robustness.
- **Data Usability and Efficiency:** The robust proprioception and streamlined calibration pipeline lead to a nearly **100% data processing success rate**, a substantial improvement over the <60% success rate reported for the vanilla UMI. For a simple pick-and-place task, 100 demonstrations can be collected in just 20 minutes with high usability. The contact-rich data collection strategy enabled by exUMI produces 480K active tactile frames from only 5 hours of human interaction, demonstrating a **10x efficiency gain** over teleoperation.
- **Large-scale Dataset:** exUMI facilitates the creation of a dataset with over 1 million frames of aligned images, tactile signals, and actions, making it significantly larger and more contact-rich than any existing real-world tactile dataset (e.g., TVL has 43.7K frames).

#### 4.2. Tactile Predictive Pretraining (TPP) Results

- **Effectiveness of Multimodal Conditioning:** Ablation studies (Table 1) show that multimodal conditioning (visual and action sequences) is crucial for accurate tactile prediction. The full conditioning (history tactile, history action, current RGB image, future action) achieves the lowest prediction MSE error of 0.0099, outperforming settings with fewer inputs. This validates the "action-aware" aspect of TPP.
- **Learning Contact Dynamics:** Visualizations (Fig. 7) demonstrate that TPP implicitly learns forward tactile dynamics. For instance, the model can infer from future actions that tactile contact will cease, a capability impossible for models not reasoning about action-informed tactile dynamics.

#### 4.3. Real-World Imitation Learning Evaluation

The pretrained tactile encoder from TPP is integrated into a diffusion policy and evaluated on a Flexiv Rizon 4 robot arm with a Flexiv Grav gripper.

- **Non-Tactile Task Performance:** A vision-only diffusion policy trained with exUMI data achieves decent success rates on various general manipulation tasks (e.g., 85% for "Pick Cube," 60% for "Stack Cube," 65% for "Insert Pen"). This confirms the high quality and sufficient spatial accuracy of the data collected by exUMI. Tasks requiring higher precision or dealing with irregular geometry (e.g., "Pick Broccoli") show slightly lower but still acceptable performance.
- **Tactile-Aware Task Performance (Key Contribution):**
 - **Significant Performance Gains:** Policies augmented with tactile sensing, especially those using TPP-pretrained embeddings, consistently and significantly outperform both vision-only and vanilla tactile imitation learning policies on contact-sensitive tasks (Table 3).
 - **Examples of Improvement:**
 - "Put Ball": Vision-only 70% -> Vision & Tactile w/ TPP 85% (15% gain).
 - "Open Bottle": Vision-only 20% -> Vision & Tactile w/ TPP 60% (40% gain).
 - "Pull Drawer (Random)": Vision-only 40% -> Vision & Tactile w/ TPP 95% (55% gain).
 - "Peg in Hole (Insert)": Vision-only 50% -> Vision & Tactile w/ TPP 80% (30% gain).
 - **Qualitative Observations:** The tactile-aware policies demonstrate more nuanced adjustments, such as refining grasp poses for better object centering ("Put Ball") and applying appropriate forces/directions for complex interactions ("Pull Drawer," "Peg in Hole"). The attention map (Fig. 17) shows the model focuses on critical contact areas and inferred tangent forces.
- **Comparison with Baselines (Table 6 in Appendix):** TPP demonstrates superior success rates compared to direct learning and a spatial self-supervised learning method (BYOL) on contact-rich tasks like "Put Ball" and "Peg in Hole," further validating the effectiveness of action-aware temporal prediction for tactile representation.

These results collectively demonstrate that the co-designed exUMI hardware and TPP algorithm effectively address the challenges in tactile robot learning, leading to substantial improvements in robot manipulation capabilities, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained contact understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a significant step forward in the field of robot manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring dexterous, contact-rich interactions. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Advancing Tactile Robot Learning:** The paper fundamentally redefines how robots can learn from tactile data. By introducing **Tactile Predictive Pretraining (TPP)**, it shifts the paradigm from treating tactile signals as static observations to modeling them as action-aware, dynamic processes. This approach, grounded in predicting future tactile states conditioned on robot actions, allows robots to reason about contact dynamics similar to human haptic perception. This is crucial for tasks requiring fine force control, slip detection, and precise alignment, enabling capabilities beyond what vision alone can provide.

2. **Democratization of Robotics Research:** The **exUMI hardware system** is designed to be **low-cost, DIY-friendly, and open-source**. With a starting cost of $698 (and a target below $500), it drastically lowers the barrier to entry for research in advanced tactile robotics. This accessibility will empower smaller labs, educational institutions, and individual researchers globally to engage in cutting-edge multimodal robot learning, accelerating innovation and fostering a broader, more diverse community of contributors.

3. **Robust and Efficient Data Collection:** exUMI solves critical practical problems associated with robot data collection. Its robust proprioception (AR MoCap and magnetic encoder) achieves a **nearly 100% data usability rate**, eliminating the significant data loss and recalibration headaches common in previous systems like vanilla UMI. The ability to efficiently collect a **large-scale (1M+ frames), contact-rich, multimodal dataset** is invaluable. High-quality data is the lifeblood of modern robot learning, and exUMI provides an unparalleled tool for its acquisition, filling a major void in publicly available tactile datasets.

4. **Enabling More Capable Robots:** The empirical results demonstrate that policies leveraging TPP-pretrained tactile representations achieve **significant performance gains** (e.g., 15-55% success rate improvements) on complex, force-sensitive tasks such as "pulling a weighted drawer," "opening a bottle," or "peg-in-hole insertion." This directly translates to robots that are more dexterous, adaptable, and capable of performing a wider range of real-world tasks with greater reliability and precision. This paves the way for robots that can operate effectively in unstructured and dynamic environments.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for future research directions.
 - **Hardware Extensions:** The modular design of exUMI facilitates the integration of even more advanced sensors, such as force-torque measurements and multi-view vision, addressing current limitations and expanding the system's capabilities.
 - **Algorithmic Advancements:** The success of action-aware temporal prediction can inspire new approaches to model multi-modal dynamics and create more sophisticated, human-like robot intelligence.
 - **Real-world Applications:** The enhanced capabilities of tactile-aware robots have profound implications for assistive robotics (e.g., helping elderly or disabled individuals with delicate tasks), industrial automation (e.g., handling fragile components, flexible assembly), and general-purpose service robotics, where safe and intelligent physical interaction is paramount.

In conclusion, "exUMI" offers a comprehensive co-designed solution that pushes the boundaries of robot learning by making high-quality tactile data collection and effective tactile representation learning more accessible and robust. Its impact will be felt across research labs and potentially in real-world applications, accelerating the development of truly dexterous and intelligent robots.
