# Unified Multimodal Understanding via Byte-Pair Visual Encoding

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework unifies multimodal understanding by applying a byte-pair encoding (BPE) strategy to visual tokens, which are derived from quantized images and incorporate spatial consistency. This approach achieves performance competitive with leading continuous representation MLLMs on diverse vision-language tasks and contributes to reduced visual hallucinations.

## Problem

- Conventional Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) using continuous visual representations suffer from a "modality gap" with discrete text tokens and an "information bottleneck" leading to visual hallucinations.
- Existing discrete visual tokenization methods often quantize images into fixed-size tokens without fully capturing semantic structure or incorporating crucial spatial relationships.
- Prior work on visual BPE was primarily frequency-based, overlooking spatial consistency, and lacked effective training procedures for practical multimodal models.

## Method

- Transforms images into discrete BPE visual tokens using a pre-trained ==VQ-GAN== for initial quantization, followed by a ==BPE== process that adapts text-based BPE for visual data.
- Introduces a priority-guided encoding scheme for visual BPE that considers both co-occurrence frequency and a novel measure of spatial consistency to form semantically meaningful tokens.
- Employs a ==multi-stage training pipeline== with ==curriculum-based data composition== (progressing from foundation to instruction data) and progressive parameter unfreezing to optimize learning with hierarchical BPE tokens.

- Proposed models (Being-VL-0.5 and Being-VL-0.5
+
) achieve performance comparable to leading continuous embedding-based MLLMs (e.g., Being-VL-0.5
+
reaches **80.6 on VQAv2** and **72.1 on MMBench**).
- The approach significantly outperforms previous discrete token methods (Being-VL-0) across benchmarks and shows reduced visual hallucination on POPE (**84.3** for Being-VL-0.5).
- Ablation studies demonstrate that both curriculum-based data composition and progressive parameter unfreezing are essential, with the multi-stage strategy outperforming single-stage training by **over 12%** on perception and reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying byte-pair encoding (BPE) to visual data, guided by both frequency and spatial consistency, helps bridge the representation gap between visual and textual modalities.
> - A multi-stage training strategy, combining curriculum-based data composition and progressive parameter unfreezing, is crucial for effectively training models with hierarchical BPE visual tokens.
> - BPE tokenization enables a more unified multimodal knowledge space by balancing the activation magnitudes of visual and text token embeddings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen rapid advancements, aiming to integrate information from various modalities, primarily vision and language. The core challenge in MLLMs lies in effectively representing visual information and seamlessly aligning it with textual data to enable joint reasoning and understanding. The paper positions itself within this vibrant and active research area, acknowledging the significant progress made by existing MLLMs.

#### 1.2. Continuous vs. Discrete Representation Methods

The current MLLM landscape is broadly characterized by two main approaches to multimodal representation:
- **Continuous Token Representation Methods:** This is the mainstream paradigm, adopted by models like LLaVA, Emu, DeepSeek-VL, and Qwen-VL. These models typically employ pre-trained visual encoders (e.g., CLIP) to convert images into high-dimensional continuous vector representations. These continuous features are then aligned with language models via projection layers. While successful, these methods face challenges such as the "modality gap" (discrepancy between continuous visual features and discrete text tokens) and the "information bottleneck problem" (loss of fine-grained visual details during compression), which can lead to issues like visual hallucinations.
- **Discrete Token Representation Methods:** Gaining increasing attention, this approach discretizes images into sequences of tokens, enabling a more unified processing mechanism similar to text. Examples include Unicode, Unified-IO, and Chameleon. The advantage is a potentially more unified input processing, reducing inter-modal differences. However, existing discrete methods often quantize images into fixed-size tokens without fully capturing semantic structure, leading to information loss and unbalanced representation of visual concepts.

#### 1.3. BPE Tokenization for Visual Data

This paper specifically focuses on advancing discrete token representation, drawing inspiration from Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) in natural language processing (NLP). In NLP, BPE forms semantically meaningful tokens by combining frequently co-occurring character sequences, which has proven highly beneficial for Transformer models. Extending this concept to the visual domain is an emerging research direction.

The authors explicitly build upon their prior theoretical work, Being-VL-0, which first proposed applying BPE to quantized visual data. That prior work demonstrated the theoretical benefits of structurally integrating discrete tokens for information preservation in 2D visual data. However, the current paper critically notes that Being-VL-0's implementation was primarily frequency-based, overlooking crucial spatial relationships in visual data. It also identified challenges in data composition and effective training procedures for practical multimodal models. This current work directly addresses these practical challenges, aiming to translate the theoretical promise of visual BPE into a high-performing MLLM.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **unify multimodal understanding by effectively applying byte-pair encoding to visual tokens**. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of conventional MLLMs, particularly those relying on continuous visual embeddings, and to significantly improve upon existing discrete token-based approaches.

The core motivations driving this work are:
1. **Bridging the Modality Gap:** To reduce the inherent discrepancy between continuous visual features and discrete textual tokens, which is a fundamental challenge in MLLMs. By transforming visual information into discrete, BPE-encoded tokens, the goal is to create a more unified representation space that can be seamlessly processed by a single language model architecture.
2. **Addressing Information Bottleneck and Hallucinations:** Traditional continuous encoders can lose fine-grained visual details, leading to the "information bottleneck" and propensity for visual hallucinations. The paper aims to mitigate this by better preserving structural information through hierarchical BPE tokenization.
3. **Enhancing Visual Tokenization Strategies:** To move beyond simple frequency-based visual BPE, as explored in prior theoretical work, by incorporating sophisticated encoding strategies that account for crucial spatial relationships in visual data. This includes devising a "priority-guided encoding scheme."
4. **Optimizing Data Composition and Training:** To identify effective data composition strategies and training procedures that maximize visual-language alignment and fully leverage the unique properties of BPE-encoded visual tokens. This is crucial for translating theoretical benefits into practical performance gains.
5. **Advancing Unified Foundation Models:** Ultimately, the work aims to contribute to the development of more capable, efficient, and truly unified multimodal foundation models that can process various modalities in a coherent and integrated manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework unifies multimodal understanding through a meticulously designed pipeline encompassing visual tokenization, model expansion, and a multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Visual Tokenization Pipeline

The process begins by transforming visual information into token sequences:
1. **Vector Quantization (VQ):** An input image `I` is first divided into patches. A pre-trained VQ model (specifically, a VQ-GAN with an 8192-entry codebook) maps each image patch to its closest codebook entry, producing a grid of discrete indices `Q(I)`. This step discretizes the continuous image data.
2. **BPE Visual Tokenization:** The core innovation. The BPE process, denoted `T(Q(I))`, identifies frequently co-occurring patterns in the quantized representation `Q(I)`. These patterns are then encoded into new, larger tokens, extending the initial VQ codebook into a comprehensive vocabulary `D`. This adapts text-based BPE for visual data by incorporating spatial relationships, reducing the sequence length while preserving structural information.

#### 3.2. Token-Based MLLM

The model formalizes multimodal learning as a conditional probability distribution over unified token sequences. Text input `X` and the BPE-tokenized image `T(Q(I))` are combined into a single sequence `S`, delimited by special tokens (`[BOI]`, `[EOI]`). A Transformer-based language model then autoregressively predicts the next token. The training objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the target output sequence.

#### 3.3. Framework Overview

The overall framework operates in two main phases:
1. **Vocabulary Construction (Offline):** A BPE vocabulary is trained using the proposed priority-guided encoding scheme on a large corpus of quantized images. This phase does not involve any textual data, focusing purely on image statistics.
2. **Application Phase (Online):** Input images are first quantized by VQ-GAN, then encoded using the pre-trained BPE vocabulary. The resulting visual tokens are seamlessly integrated with text tokens and fed into the language model.

#### 3.4. Vocabulary Construction: Priority-Guided Encoding

This is a critical enhancement over simple frequency-based BPE. The authors introduce a **priority function `P(a, b)`** that guides the encoding of token pairs `(a, b)`, considering both:
- **Co-occurrence frequency `F(a, b)`:** The normalized frequency of adjacent occurrences of tokens `a` and `b`.
- **Spatial consistency `S(a, b)`:** A novel measure of how consistently the token pair maintains its relative spatial relationship across different image instances. This is calculated using an average relative positioning (`u(a, b)`) and a spatial distance function (Euclidean distance, transformed by an exponential kernel) to prioritize pairs that form stable visual patterns.

The priority function is `P(a, b) = F(a, b) + α · S(a, b)`, where `α` is a weighting parameter determined via cross-validation (default `α=0.3`, `σ=2.0`). This dual-factor approach ensures that encoded tokens represent not only frequent but also semantically meaningful and spatially consistent visual structures. The process iteratively selects the highest-priority pair to merge, extending the vocabulary until a target size is reached (e.g., 8K or 16K additional BPE tokens).

#### 3.5. Model Expanding

To accommodate the new visual tokens, the pre-trained text-only language model (Llama-3.1-8B in this case) is expanded. Its embedding layer, initially `E_text`, is extended to `E_expanded = [E_text, E_visual]`, where `E_visual` represents new embeddings for the BPE tokens. These new embeddings are initialized using He initialization to maintain signal magnitude during early training. The visual part of the embedding layer is designed to accommodate both the base VQ codebook tokens (8K) and the newly created BPE tokens (8K or 16K).

#### 3.6. Multi-Stage Training

A strategic multi-stage training pipeline is introduced, combining curriculum-based data composition with progressive parameter unfreezing to optimize learning with BPE visual tokens:

##### 3.6.1. Curriculum-Based Data Composition

Recognizing the hierarchical nature of BPE tokens, the training data is categorized into four types based on complexity:
- **Foundation Data (FD):** Basic image-caption pairs (e.g., CC-3M, LCS, LAION-2B-en subset).
- **Perception Data (PD):** Detailed visual attribute tasks (e.g., RefCOCO, AOKVQA).
- **Reasoning Data (RD):** Complex visual question answering (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision general QA/reasoning).
- **Instruction Data (ID):** Multi-turn interactions and complex instruction following (e.g., ShareGPT4o, ALLaVA Inst, Infinity-MM).

A curriculum shifts the emphasis across stages:
- **Stage 1:** Focuses heavily on FD, with some PD (80% FD, 20% PD).
- **Stage 2:** Balances FD, PD, RD (40% FD, 30% PD, 20% RD, 10% ID).
- **Stage 3:** Prioritizes ID and RD (15% FD, 15% PD, 30% RD, 40% ID).
This curriculum ensures that the model first learns basic visual-text associations before tackling more complex reasoning.

##### 3.6.2. Progressive Parameter Unfreezing

This strategy controls which model components are updated at each stage:
- **Stage 1: Embedding Alignment:** Only the newly extended visual token embeddings are trained. The rest of the model (including the pre-trained LLM parameters) remains frozen. This establishes initial visual-linguistic associations without disrupting existing language capabilities. (Learning Rate: 1e-3, Epochs: 2)
- **Stage 2: Selective Fine-tuning:** Early Transformer layers (25% of total layers) are unfrozen, alongside the visual embeddings. This enhances cross-modal interactions in feature integration layers using a broader range of data types. (Learning Rate: 3e-5, Epochs: 3)
- **Stage 3: Full Fine-tuning:** All model parameters are unfrozen. The training emphasizes complex reasoning and instruction-following data, refining the model’s advanced capabilities building on the foundations laid in previous stages. (Learning Rate: 5e-5, Epochs: 3)
All stages use AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule with 3% warmup, and DeepSpeed Stage 2 for efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments confirm the effectiveness of the proposed framework and training methodologies.

#### 4.1. General Visual Understanding Performance

- **Competitive with Continuous Models:** The proposed models, Being-VL-0.5 (8K BPE tokens) and Being-VL-0.5$^+$ (16K BPE tokens), achieve performance comparable to leading continuous embedding-based MLLMs across various benchmarks. Being-VL-0.5$^+$ reaches 80.6 on VQAv2 and 72.1 on MMBench, closely matching VILA-1.5 (80.9 and 72.3). It also shows strong perception capabilities (1536.3 on MME-P), often outperforming continuous models.
- **Outperforming Previous Discrete Models:** Critically, Being-VL-0.5 significantly outperforms Being-VL-0 (their own prior work on visual BPE) across all benchmarks, with substantial gains on MMBench (71.8 vs. 44.0) and MME-P (1525.8 vs. 1316.2). This validates the improvements from their enhanced vocabulary construction and optimized training strategies.
- **Necessity of BPE:** The variant "Being-VL-0.5 w/o BPE" (which only uses base VQ tokens) shows a significant performance drop across all benchmarks, confirming the crucial role of the BPE vocabulary in the framework.
- **Reduced Hallucination:** The method achieves an optimal balance between low-level detail and high-level semantics, showing reduced hallucination on POPE (84.3 for Being-VL-0.5, 86.0 for Being-VL-0.5$^+$) compared to earlier discrete token approaches.

#### 4.2. BPE Token Activation Mechanism Analysis

Visualization of embedding weight distributions reveals that BPE tokenization helps create a more unified multimodal knowledge space.
- In the base LLM+VQ model (without BPE), text tokens exhibit predominantly low-magnitude weights, while visual (VQ) tokens show significantly higher magnitudes, indicating an imbalance and struggle to unify representations.
- With BPE tokenization (Being-VL-0.5 and Being-VL-0.5$^+$), the weight distributions become more balanced, suggesting BPE effectively bridges the representation gap between modalities.
- Some BPE tokens, especially in the 16K variant, show minimal activation, possibly due to insufficient training data to fully utilize the larger vocabulary, which might explain why Being-VL-0.5$^+$ doesn't consistently outperform Being-VL-0.5 on all tasks.

#### 4.3. Scaling Potential and Training Efficiency

- **Trade-offs between Vocabulary Size, Performance, and Efficiency:** While the 16K BPE vocabulary shows superior scaling potential with increased training data (beyond 1.5x standard data), its efficiency (performance-to-cost ratio) remains consistently lower than smaller vocabularies.
- **Optimal Balance at 8K:** The 8K BPE vocabulary achieves a better balance, demonstrating strong performance scaling while maintaining reasonable training efficiency.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Multi-Stage Training Components

Ablation studies highlight the importance of both curriculum-based data composition and progressive parameter unfreezing:
- **Overall Strategy Effectiveness:** The standard multi-stage approach significantly outperforms single-stage training (12.8% higher on perception, 14.1% higher on reasoning tasks).
- **Curriculum-Based Data Composition:** This component shows a stronger impact, with the proposed progressive data mix (FD → PD → RD → ID) outperforming reverse ordering or random mixing strategies, aligning with the hierarchical learning needed for BPE tokens.
- **Progressive Parameter Unfreezing:** The three-stage approach (embedding alignment → selective fine-tuning → full fine-tuning) is more effective. Simplified two-stage (align → full) or reverse unfreezing leads to notable performance drops, confirming the importance of the ordered parameter updates.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Future Work

#### 5.1. Significance and Contributions

This paper makes several significant contributions to the field of multimodal AI:
- **Unified Visual Representation:** It introduces a novel, discrete tokenization approach for visual data that effectively preserves structural information in images through a priority-guided encoding scheme. This provides a unified representation consistent with textual tokens, directly tackling the modality gap challenge.
- **Enhanced Training Strategies:** The development of a progressive training strategy, combining curriculum-based data composition and stage-specific parameter freezing, is crucial for optimizing the learning process with hierarchical BPE visual tokens. This strategy enables models to build better visual understanding incrementally.
- **Empirical Validation and Analysis:** Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks, coupled with detailed analyses of token patterns, computational efficiency, and ablation studies, provide strong empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed methods and elucidate *why* they advance visual-language understanding.
- **Advancement of Discrete Token Methods:** The work significantly pushes the state-of-the-art for discrete token-based MLLMs, demonstrating that they can achieve competitive performance with leading continuous embedding-based models while maintaining the inherent advantages of unified representation.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The advancements presented in this paper have several positive potential impacts:
- **Improved Accessibility:** More robust visual-language models could enhance accessibility technologies for visually impaired individuals, providing more accurate and detailed descriptions of visual content.
- **Natural Human-Computer Interaction:** A truly unified multimodal understanding could lead to more intuitive and natural interactions between humans and AI systems.
- **Educational Applications:** Better comprehension of multimodal learning materials could support more effective educational tools and platforms.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A unified token representation framework may lead to more computationally efficient models in the long run, as distinct modality-specific encoders and complex alignment layers might be streamlined, potentially reducing the environmental footprint of large AI systems.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors openly acknowledge limitations and outline promising avenues for future research:
- **Model Scale:** The current study focused on 8B parameter models due to computational constraints. While promising results were achieved, scaling analysis indicates potential for further improvements with larger models and more training data. The observed relationship between vocabulary size and performance suggests that larger BPE vocabularies (e.g., 16K) might unlock even stronger capabilities with sufficient data.
- **Multimodal Generation:** A natural extension of this unified token-based approach is multimodal generation. Since the framework allows the model to "speak" visual tokens in the same manner as text tokens, it establishes a paradigm for truly unified generation (e.g., text-to-image generation within the same model). The current work primarily focuses on understanding tasks, but extending it to incorporate generative capabilities is identified as a critical direction for future research.
- **Broader Societal Implications:** As with all powerful AI models, the authors highlight potential risks, including the misuse for generating misleading content and the amplification of biases present in training data. They advocate for responsible deployment with appropriate safeguards, content filtering, fairness evaluations, and clear guidelines. The growing computational requirements also raise sustainability concerns that necessitate continued efficiency optimizations.
