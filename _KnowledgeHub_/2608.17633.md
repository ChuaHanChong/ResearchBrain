# OVIP-SG: Open-Vocabulary Instance-Preserving Scene Graphs for Mapping and Retrieval of Small, Fine-Grained Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> OVIP-SG builds open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs that keep small fine-grained objects as separate instances instead of merging them into furniture, via symmetric-IoU association, label-aware penalty, and area-weighted feature fusion. It adds functional room segmentation and a four-tier cascaded retrieval that finds unmapped objects or returns a coverage-conditioned absence decision. Outperforms ConceptGraphs by 6.31 mAcc on Replica.

## Problem

- Open-vocabulary detectors recover long-tail small objects but fragment large surfaces or merge small objects into larger neighbors, breaking instance-level mapping consistency.
- Existing containment-based association (e.g. ConceptGraphs) scores a small detection inside a large object as full overlap, silently absorbing it into furniture.
- Prior systems cannot retrieve unmapped targets or decide whether a queried object is truly absent, blocking robust open-world navigation and exploration.

## Method

- ==Enumerate-to-Ground (E2G)== front-end with ==Frame-Adaptive Vocabulary Gate (FAV-Gate)== uses a VLM to enumerate visible categories per frame, filtering to a stable frequency-thresholded vocabulary before grounding and CLIP feature extraction.
- **Instance-Preserving Association (IPA)** combines ==symmetric 3D IoU merge (IPSM)== and a ==Label-Aware Merge Penalty (LAMP)== to stop small instances being absorbed by containment, paired with ==Area-Weighted Fusion (AWF)== that favors large centered views when updating object features.
- ==Knowledge-Guided Semantic Watershed (KGSW)== partitions the map into functional regions via VLM-inferred object function plus geometric watershed, feeding ==Cascaded Active Retrieval with Absence Assessment (CARA)==, a four-tier lookup-to-active-search pipeline with ==Voxel-Vote Confirmation== and a coverage-conditioned null decision.

## Results

- On Replica under a shared evaluator, OVIP-SG improves mAcc from **45.00 to 51.31** and F-mIoU from **56.30 to 61.45** over ConceptGraphs, leading on five of six scenes.
- Class-agnostic native-instance Panoptic Quality reaches **0.398** versus 0.338 for ConceptGraphs and 0.163 for HOV-SG, with SQ **0.776** and RQ **0.514** both highest.
- KGSW reduces the oracle search area to **21.8%** of floor space (versus 34.2% HOV-SG, 71.3% SysNav) and the balanced Tier-4 absence test yields **0.773** balanced accuracy on 44 queries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symmetric containment scoring (detection-volume over union rather than one-sided overlap) is a cheap, general fix for small-object-swallowing errors in any box-association pipeline.
> - Reserving expensive VLM calls only for ambiguous or fine-grained decisions, while routing routine cases through geometry and CLIP, keeps open-vocabulary perception both accurate and tractable at scale.
> - A retrieval system needs an explicit coverage-conditioned negative branch; without one, absence of evidence is indistinguishable from failure to search.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses challenges within the domain of open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs and semantic mapping for embodied agents. Existing open-vocabulary mapping systems, such as ConceptGraphs, ConceptFusion, and HOV-SG, have advanced semantic representations for robots but exhibit limitations in instance fidelity and retrieval reliability, particularly concerning small, fine-grained objects. These limitations manifest in several ways: small objects may be incorrectly merged into larger furniture or fragmented into multiple pieces; large surfaces can be mislabeled; and instances, once lost during map construction, become unlocatable or unretrievable. Furthermore, current methods often struggle with determining whether a queried object is truly absent from an environment, which hinders robust embodied open-world navigation and exploration.

The integration of open-vocabulary perception models, particularly vision-language models (VLMs), offers the potential to recognize a broader range of categories, including long-tail and fine-grained objects often missed by closed-set models. However, this integration can also introduce issues like the fragmentation of large surfaces or the merging of small objects into neighboring larger ones, thereby compromising instance-level consistency and map fidelity. OVIP-SG aims to mitigate these drawbacks by developing mechanisms that preserve object instances more accurately and enhance retrieval capabilities, including the ability to confirm object absence. This positions OVIP-SG as an effort to improve the practical applicability of open-vocabulary scene graphs for autonomous robots operating in complex, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the OVIP-SG framework are threefold: to achieve high-fidelity semantic mapping that preserves small, fine-grained object instances, to enable robust language-guided retrieval and navigation for both mapped and unmapped targets, and to partition scenes into functional regions for improved search efficiency. These objectives are motivated by the identified shortcomings of current open-vocabulary mapping systems in enabling reliable and comprehensive robotic interaction with real-world environments.

### 3. Task A: High-Fidelity Semantic Mapping

The first key objective is to address specific failure modes in semantic mapping. Existing methods often suffer from:
- **Asymmetric Containment:** Small objects are entirely absorbed into larger, enclosing furniture items. For example, a small decorative vase might be incorrectly mapped as part of a table.
- **Count-Averaged Box-Crop CLIP Fusion:** When fusing features, frequent small detections or peripheral crops can disproportionately influence the feature representation of a large object, leading to mislabeling or feature dilution.
To counter these, OVIP-SG aims to ensure that small objects are maintained as distinct instances and that large objects are labeled accurately without feature corruption.

### 4. Task B: Small, Fine-Grained Object Retrieval and Navigation

The second core objective focuses on improving robot capabilities for object interaction:
- **Grounding Free-Form Queries:** To translate natural language queries for specific objects into precise 3D goals and navigation paths. This includes fine-grained targets that might not be explicitly present in a map.
- **Handling Unmapped Targets:** To enable active exploration when a queried object is not found in the existing map, allowing the robot to search for previously unseen or unrecorded items.
- **Coverage-Conditioned Negative Decision:** To provide a reliable "absence" decision when an object cannot be located after an exhaustive search within the robot's explorable space. This is critical for practical applications, as robots must be able to confirm non-existence rather than endlessly searching.

### 5. Semantic Room Segmentation

A related objective is to segment environments into compact, semantically meaningful "functional search regions." Unlike traditional room segmentation based solely on architectural geometry (e.g., walls), OVIP-SG aims to partition spaces based on the functions implied by the objects within them. This provides more intuitive and efficient search priors for the retrieval system (Task B), especially in open-plan layouts.

Ultimately, the motivation behind OVIP-SG is to overcome the limitations that compromise instance-level consistency and retrieval reliability, thereby paving the way for more robust and capable embodied agents in open-world scenarios like domestic, office, or hotel environments.

### 6. Methodology and Approach

OVIP-SG is structured as a unified framework composed of several interconnected modules for instance-preserving semantic mapping, functional scene partitioning, and language-guided object retrieval.

#### 6.1. E2G (Enumerate-to-Ground) Open-Vocabulary Perception with Selective VLM Invocation

The front-end of OVIP-SG, E2G, processes RGB-D streams to generate open-vocabulary detections.
- **Enumeration:** For each frame, a VLM (Kimi-k2.6) enumerates visible object types. This harnesses the VLM's capabilities for open-world naming, fine-grained vision-language judgment, and commonsense reasoning.
- **FAV-Gate (Frame-Adaptive Vocabulary Gate):** To manage VLM noise and ensure a stable vocabulary, FAV-Gate filters the enumerated object types. Only words appearing in a minimum number of frames (n_min) are admitted into a stable global vocabulary. The intersection of the current frame's VLM tags and this global vocabulary forms the query set for the grounder.
- **Grounding and Feature Extraction:** LocateAnything is used to generate 2D bounding boxes for queried words. SAM2 refines these masks, and a CLIP encoder extracts a normalized feature vector for each detection.
- **Selective VLM Invocation:** To optimize computational cost, VLM calls are reserved for tasks requiring fine-grained semantic decisions (e.g., in KGSW and CARA's later tiers), while routine cases are handled by on-device geometric and CLIP evidence.

#### 6.2. Instance-Preserving Object Update

This module focuses on maintaining instance integrity and accurate feature representation in the 3D map.
- **IPA (Instance-Preserving Association):**
 - **IPSM (Instance-Preserving Symmetric-IoU Merge):** Traditional containment scores can incorrectly merge small objects into larger ones. IPSM addresses this by using symmetric 3D Intersection over Union (IoU) for association. If a small detection (b_i) is contained within a larger object (B_j), the spatial score becomes Vol(b_i)/Vol(B_j) rather than 1, preventing complete absorption.
 - **LAMP (Label-Aware Merge Penalty):** This component introduces a penalty for mismatches between a detection's label (ℓ_i) and the existing object's majority label (ℓ*_j). The association decision considers both spatial IoU, CLIP feature similarity, and this label mismatch penalty. If no existing object meets the similarity threshold, a new object instance is created. This protects the semantic purity of instances.
- **AWF (Area-Weighted Fusion):** To update the CLIP feature (g_j) of an associated object, AWF employs a view-area weighting scheme. This weighting prioritizes large, centrally observed detections and discounts truncated or off-axis observations. This mechanism prevents smaller, frequently detected parts from unduly influencing the overall semantic feature of a larger object.

#### 6.3. KGSW (Knowledge-Guided Semantic Watershed) Room Segmentation

KGSW transforms the object map into a functional decomposition of the environment.
- **Geometric Foundation:** Free space and object centers are projected onto a bird's-eye-view (BEV) grid. A distance field measures clearance to non-free space.
- **Core Construction:** The algorithm uses connected components of free space and a watershed algorithm on the inverse distance field to define geometry-respecting core regions.
- **VLM-Guided Functional Grouping:** The VLM assigns a functional type to each object based on its semantic label and BEV position (e.g., classifying objects as "kitchen items" or "lounge items").
- **Semantic Subdivision:** If a geometric core contains objects implying multiple functional types, it is subdivided. Objects are grouped by functional type and then by BEV proximity using DBSCAN. This ensures that spatially distinct areas, even with the same function, do not share a marker.
- **Watershed Expansion:** These functional groups seed markers for a final watershed algorithm, expanding into the remaining free space to create functional regions. This approach ensures that geometry defines boundaries, while VLM semantics resolves ambiguities in open areas.

#### 6.4. CARA (Cascaded Active Retrieval with Absence Assessment)

CARA is a four-tier pipeline designed for robust language-guided object retrieval and navigation. Each tier is activated sequentially only if prior, less resource-intensive tiers fail to identify the target.
- **Tier 1 (Map Lookup):** The system first checks if the target object exists in the constructed 3D map by matching the query's class name or CLIP similarity. If a match is found, the object's 3D centroid is returned as the goal.
- **Tier 2 (2D-Tag Retrieval):** If the object is not on the map, CARA searches the historical VLM tags associated with past frames. CLIP scores the query against tag features, and if relevant "hot frames" are identified, a VLM disambiguates fine-grained attributes (e.g., "floral towel").
- **Tier 3 (History-Frame VVC - Voxel-Vote Confirmation):** If the object was never tagged, a VLM uses commonsense priors (e.g., "coffee machine is likely in kitchen") to suggest a probable search region. A strong grounder is then re-run on history frames within this region. Detections are localized through multi-view voxel voting (requiring consensus from a minimum number of frames, k), and survivors are verified by a VLM.
- **Tier 4 (Active Search + ∅-Decision):** If all previous tiers fail, the robot engages in active search. It explores sampled waypoints, rotating through multiple headings and navigating along shortest paths. Regions are prioritized by a balance of VLM semantic prior and geodesic cost. If, after exhausting all coverage viewpoints, no sufficiently voted and VLM-verified voxel is found, CARA returns a coverage-conditioned negative decision, indicating the target's absence.
- **Trigger Legitimacy:** This cascaded design ensures that more expensive VLM calls and active exploration are only initiated when cheaper, map-based or history-based evidence is insufficient.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OVIP-SG was conducted across simulated (Replica, ReplicaCAD, HM3D) and real-world robotic environments, demonstrating its performance in mapping fidelity, functional segmentation, and object retrieval.

#### 7.1. Task A: Mapping Fidelity (Replica Dataset)

- **Semantic Accuracy:** OVIP-SG improved class-mean accuracy (mAcc) by 6.31 points (from 45.00 to 51.31) and frequency-weighted mIoU (F-mIoU) by 5.15 points (from 56.30 to 61.45) compared to the ConceptGraphs baseline under a unified evaluation protocol. It led in mAcc on five of six Replica scenes.
- **Instance Quality:** The framework achieved a class-agnostic native-instance Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 0.398, an improvement over ConceptGraphs (0.338) and HOV-SG (0.163). OVIP-SG also showed higher Segmentation Quality (SQ) (0.776) and Recognition Quality (RQ) (0.514).
- **Size-Stratified Performance:** Gains were observed for both small and large instances. Specifically, targeted recall for instances typically "swallowed" by other objects (e.g., "pot," "vase") increased from 15.8% to 43.2% with the Instance-Preserving Association (IPA) mechanism.
- **Ablation Study:** The Enumerate-to-Ground (E2G) front-end contributed the largest mAcc gain (+3.99). The instance-preserving association (IPSM + LAMP) and Area-Weighted Fusion (AWF) modules cumulatively added an additional +2.32 mAcc, primarily by improving instance fidelity and frequency-weighted quality.
- **VLM Robustness:** The results were robust to changes in the underlying VLM backbone used for per-frame enumeration, with a limited mAcc range of 1.71 points across different VLMs (GPT-5.5, Claude-Sonnet-5, Kimi-K2.6, Qwen3.7-Plus).
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visual comparisons indicated that OVIP-SG successfully preserved small, fine-grained instances (e.g., switches, wall-plugs, pictures, vases) while maintaining the integrity of large furniture items, addressing issues like fragmentation or absorption observed in other methods.

#### 7.2. KGSW: Functional Region Segmentation (HM3D Dataset)

- **Segmentation Quality:** KGSW achieved a mean acc@0.5 of 0.593 and an average precision (AP) of 0.698, along with the lowest scene variance, outperforming geometric-only baselines like SysNav (0.351 mAcc@0.5). It demonstrated stability in elongated or half-open layouts where geometry-driven methods collapsed.
- **Search Area Reduction:** For 217 GT-object queries, the oracle target-containing region identified by KGSW occupied 21.8% of the indoor floor space. This represents a 1.6x reduction compared to HOV-SG and a 3.3x reduction compared to SysNav, at comparable target containment rates (approximately 0.78). This translates to materially smaller search spaces for the robot.

#### 7.3. Task B: Small, Fine-Grained Object Retrieval and Navigation (ReplicaCAD, HM3D, Real-Robot)

- **Cascaded Retrieval Coverage:** The four-tiered CARA system demonstrated coverage across different retrieval scenarios, handling mapped objects (Tier 1), tagged-but-unmapped objects (Tier 2), history-only objects through Voxel-Vote Confirmation (Tier 3), and actively searching for objects or determining their absence (Tier 4).
- **Retrieval Success Rate (RSR):** On HM3D, CARA achieved a Top-1 RSR@1m of 0.57, outperforming the reproduced HOV-SG (0.51) and FSR-VLN (0.49). Its Top-5 RSR@3m reached 0.93. The gains were attributed to Tiers 2 and 3, which enabled recovery of off-map or never-tagged categories.
- **Closed-Loop Navigation:** In closed-loop HM3D simulations, CARA yielded a higher mean Success Rate (SR) of 0.605 compared to a CLIP-based policy (0.576) for present queries.
- **Absence Assessment:** In a balanced Tier-4 stress test (22 present, 22 absent queries), CARA demonstrated a sensitivity of 0.909 (20 true positives) and an absent accuracy of 0.636 (14 true negatives). The balanced accuracy for object-presence classification was 0.773. The system returned a coverage-conditioned negative decision when objects were not found after an exhaustive search.

#### 7.4. Real-Robot Deployment

- The full OVIP-SG stack (mapping and CARA) was deployed online and in closed-loop on an AgiBot Expedition A3 humanoid robot in office and hotel environments.
- The robot incrementally built semantic maps, grounded free-form service requests, and navigated to 3D goals in live execution.
- On small-object retrieval tasks, OVIP-SG improved the Success Rate over FSR-VLN by an average of 0.08, indicating practical effectiveness in real-world scenarios.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

OVIP-SG addresses several critical limitations in current open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding and robotic navigation, positioning it as a notable advancement in the field.

**Enhanced Mapping Fidelity:** The introduction of Instance-Preserving Symmetric-IoU Merge (IPSM), Label-Aware Merge Penalty (LAMP), and Area-Weighted Fusion (AWF) significantly improves the fidelity of semantic maps. By preventing the fragmentation of large objects and the erroneous absorption of small, fine-grained objects, OVIP-SG enables robots to build more accurate and perceptually consistent representations of their environment. This is crucial for precise object interaction and robust long-term autonomy.

**Improved Object Retrieval and Navigation:** The multi-tiered CARA (Cascaded Active Retrieval with Absence Assessment) pipeline offers a more comprehensive and robust approach to object retrieval than prior methods. Its ability to handle unmapped objects, disambiguate fine-grained attributes from historical data, and actively search for targets, coupled with its coverage-conditioned negative decision, moves beyond the assumption of object presence. This capability for reliable absence detection is particularly impactful for practical robotic applications, where knowing an object is truly missing is as important as locating it.

**Functional Scene Understanding:** KGSW (Knowledge-Guided Semantic Watershed) contributes to a more functionally aware understanding of indoor spaces. By partitioning environments based on VLM-inferred object functions rather than purely geometric boundaries, OVIP-SG generates semantically coherent and compact search regions. This significantly reduces the search space for robots, leading to more efficient and targeted exploration.

**Practical Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment and qualitative validation of OVIP-SG on a real humanoid robot in diverse environments (office, hotel) demonstrate its practical effectiveness. This indicates the potential for OVIP-SG to be integrated into various service robotics applications, such as assistive robots, inspection robots, or logistics robots, where robust perception, precise localization, and intelligent search capabilities are paramount.

**A Unified Framework:** OVIP-SG provides a unified architecture that seamlessly integrates open-vocabulary perception, instance-preserving mapping, functional scene partitioning, and a sophisticated cascaded retrieval system. This holistic approach offers a comprehensive solution for embodied agents operating in open-world, complex environments. The work contributes towards closing the gap between advanced vision-language models and the practical requirements of autonomous robotic systems.
