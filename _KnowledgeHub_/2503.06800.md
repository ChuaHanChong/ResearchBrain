# VideoPhy-2: A Challenging Action-Centric Physical Commonsense Evaluation in Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> UCLA and Google Research introduce VIDEOPHY-2, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating physical commonsense in video generation models, revealing significant limitations in current models' ability to generate physically plausible videos, with even top performers achieving only 32.6% joint performance on semantic and physical commonsense adherence.

## Problem

- Existing video generation benchmarks lack rigorous evaluation of physical plausibility and commonsense reasoning
- No standardized way to assess whether generated videos follow basic laws of physics
- Prior benchmarks are limited in size, lack human evaluation, suffer sim-to-real gaps, and provide no fine-grained analysis of which specific physical rules are violated

## Method

- Created **VideoPhy-2**, a benchmark focusing on ==action-centric physical commonsense== evaluation
- Curated seed actions from existing video datasets, used Gemini 2.0-Flash-Exp to generate detailed prompts, and had ==vision-language models== compile candidate physical rules each video must satisfy
- Developed **VideoPhy-2-AutoEval**, an ==automatic evaluation model== trained on ==human annotations== to assess physical accuracy

## Results

- Best models achieved only **32.6%** joint performance on full dataset, dropping to **22%** on hard subset
- **VideoPhy-2-AutoEval** showed **47.4%** and **49%** relative gains on unseen prompts and video models
- Human evaluation revealed systematic physical-reasoning failures across all tested models, motivating a curated dataset of challenging scenarios where current models consistently fail

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conservation laws (mass, momentum) pose particular challenges for current video generation models
> - No strong correlation exists between physical accuracy and aesthetic/motion quality metrics
> - Closed-source models don't necessarily outperform open-source ones in physics accuracy

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of video generative models. While significant progress has been made in generating visually appealing and semantically coherent videos, the ability of these models to adhere to physical laws and exhibit commonsense reasoning about the physical world remains largely unexplored.

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** The authors explicitly identify and address the limitations of existing video generation benchmarks, such as limited size, lack of human evaluation, sim-to-real gaps, and absence of fine-grained physical rule analysis.
- **Focus on Physical Commonsense:** The work contributes to the growing body of research on physical reasoning in AI, which aims to imbue AI systems with the ability to understand and predict the behavior of objects and agents in the physical world.
- **Action-Centric Approach:** By focusing on real-world actions, the paper aligns with the trend of developing AI systems that can interact with and understand the world around them, rather than just passively processing information.
- **Evaluation as a Key Challenge:** The work highlights the importance of rigorous evaluation methodologies for AI systems, particularly in domains where safety and reliability are critical. The development of VIDEOPHY-2 and its associated automatic evaluation model contributes to the broader effort of creating more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

The paper is grounded in and builds upon existing research in:
- **Video Generation:** Diffusion models, autoregressive models, and other approaches to text-to-video synthesis.
- **Video Evaluation:** Metrics for assessing video quality, semantic coherence, and temporal consistency.
- **Physical Reasoning:** Cognitive science and AI research on intuitive physics, object interactions, and causal reasoning.
- **Benchmark Development:** Methodologies for creating challenging and informative datasets for evaluating AI models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **To create a challenging benchmark (VIDEOPHY-2) for evaluating physical commonsense in video generative models.** This benchmark should overcome the limitations of existing datasets and provide a more rigorous assessment of model capabilities.
- **To assess the ability of state-of-the-art video generative models to adhere to physical laws and exhibit commonsense reasoning about the physical world.**
- **To develop an automatic evaluation model (VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL) for fast and reliable assessment of physical commonsense in generated videos.** This model should be aligned with human judgments and provide a scalable solution for evaluating video generative models.
- **To provide insights into the types of physical laws that video generative models struggle with.** This can guide future research efforts in improving the physical reasoning capabilities of these models.

The motivation behind this research stems from the recognition that video generative models have the potential to serve as general-purpose physical world simulators. However, before this potential can be realized, it is crucial to ensure that these models can generate videos that are not only visually appealing and semantically coherent but also physically plausible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employed a multi-stage methodology that combines data curation, video generation, human evaluation, and automatic evaluation.

1. **Data Curation:**
 - **Seed Actions:** A diverse list of actions was curated from popular video datasets, focusing on those relevant to physical commonsense evaluation. The list was filtered to remove actions that did not elicit significant motion or were unlikely to be compelling for physical commonsense evaluation in videos.
 - **LLM-Generated Prompts:** A large language model (LLM) was used to generate detailed prompts from the seed actions, focusing on visible physical interactions between objects. Gemini 2.0-Flash-Exp was used to generate 20 prompts for each action in the data set.
 - **Candidate Physical Rules and Laws:** Vision-language models (VLMs) were used to compile a list of candidate physical rules and associated laws that should be satisfied in the generated videos.

2. **Video Generation:** Modern video generative models were used to synthesize videos from the generated prompts. These included open source and closed source video models.
3. **Human Evaluation:** Human annotators were recruited to score each video based on semantic adherence, physical commonsense, and compliance with various physical rules. This involved a 5-point scale based on the likelihood of the video being likely or unlikely.
4. **Construction of Hard Subset:** Actions for which the model failed to generate videos that accurately adhere to the prompts and follow physical commonsense were selected to form a subset to further challenge existing models. CogVideoX-5B was used to select these actions.
5. **Automatic Evaluation:** An automatic evaluation model (VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL) was trained to perform a wide range of tasks, including scoring semantic adherence, physical commonsense, and classifying physical rule grounding in the generated video. VideoCon-Physics was fine-tuned on 50K human annotations to perform three tasks using a shared backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings of this research are:

- **Video generative models struggle to generate videos that adhere to physical laws and exhibit commonsense reasoning about the physical world.** Even the best-performing model achieved only 32.6% joint performance (high semantic and physical commonsense adherence) on the full dataset and 22% on the hard subset.
- **Models particularly struggle with conservation laws, such as those governing mass and momentum.** This suggests that future research should focus on improving the ability of video generative models to reason about these fundamental physical principles.
- **The VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL model outperforms existing video-language models in assessing semantic adherence and physical commonsense.** This demonstrates the potential of automatic evaluation models for providing fast and reliable assessments of video generative models. The authors found that VideoCon-Physics achieved relative gains of 47.4% and 49% on unseen prompts and unseen video models respectively.
- **There is no strong correlation between the physical accuracy of the video, and the metrics of asthetics and motion quality.**
- **Closed models are not necessarily better than open source models in generating accurate physics.**

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has significant implications for the development of video generative models and AI systems in general.

- **Provides a valuable benchmark for evaluating and improving the physical reasoning capabilities of video generative models.** VIDEOPHY-2 can be used by researchers and developers to assess the progress of their models and identify areas for improvement.
- **Guides future research in physically-grounded video generation.** The insights gained from this research can inform the development of new models and training techniques that are better able to adhere to physical laws and exhibit commonsense reasoning.
- **Facilitates the development of AI systems that can interact with and understand the world around them.** By improving the ability of AI systems to reason about the physical world, this research contributes to the development of more capable and reliable AI systems for various applications, such as robotics, autonomous driving, and game playing.
- **Advances the development of general-purpose physical world simulators.** Video generative models that can generate physically plausible videos have the potential to serve as powerful tools for simulating and understanding the physical world.
- **Raises awareness of the importance of physical commonsense in AI.** This research highlights the need to go beyond visual appeal and semantic coherence and ensure that AI systems are also grounded in the fundamental principles of physics.

In conclusion, the VIDEOPHY-2 paper makes a valuable contribution to the field of video generation and AI by addressing the critical issue of physical commonsense. The benchmark, evaluation methodology, and findings presented in this paper will have a lasting impact on the development of more capable and reliable AI systems.
