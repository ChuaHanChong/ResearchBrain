# DragNUWA: Fine-grained Control in Video Generation by Integrating Text, Image, and Trajectory

> [!summary] Summary
> DRAGNUWA, developed by a collaboration including the University of Science and Technology of China and Microsoft Research Asia, presents a diffusion-based model for fine-grained video generation by simultaneously integrating text, an initial image, and object/camera trajectories. The system achieves robust open-domain trajectory control for arbitrary objects and complex motions, addressing limitations of prior fragmented approaches.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models lack sufficient control granularity, typically focusing on only one or two modalities (text, image, or trajectory), making it challenging to achieve comprehensive control over complex video content.
- Current trajectory-based video generation methods are often limited to constrained datasets or specific domains (e.g., human poses), struggling with open-domain images, complex curved trajectories, and multiple objects.
- Previous trajectory control techniques frequently rely on predicting optical flow maps which can lead to unnatural distortions or are constrained by predefined keypoints, limiting their real-world applicability.

## Method

- **DRAGNUWA** proposes an end-to-end latent ==diffusion model== integrating text, an initial image, and trajectory information as simultaneous control signals within a UNet architecture.
- A novel trajectory modeling framework is introduced, comprising a Trajectory Sampler for open-domain trajectory extraction from video ==optical flow==, a Multiscale Fusion mechanism for deep integration of diverse controls, and an Adaptive Training strategy.
- The Adaptive Training employs a two-stage process, first conditioning on dense optical flow for stability, then adapting to sparser, user-friendly trajectories to ensure consistent video generation.

## Results

- DRAGNUWA demonstrates superior open-domain trajectory controllability, generating complex curved paths for objects, handling multiple objects concurrently, and allowing intuitive control over camera movements (zoom, pan) via trajectory modification.
- The integration of all three control modalities (text, image, trajectory) is shown to be essential, enabling precise semantic, spatial, and temporal control over generated videos, outperforming methods with fewer inputs.
- The model supports variable trajectory lengths, where longer input trajectories directly correspond to greater motion amplitudes in the generated video, providing an intuitive way to dictate movement extent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained video generation necessitates the concurrent integration of semantic (text), spatial (image), and temporal (trajectory) controls, as each modality provides unique and complementary information.
> - Open-domain trajectory control can be effectively achieved by dynamically sampling and enhancing trajectories from video optical flow, combined with a two-stage adaptive training approach that leverages dense information for stability before adapting to sparser inputs.
> - The model inherently learns to generate various camera movements (e.g., zoom, pan) by simply modifying dragging trajectories, demonstrating an implicit understanding of spatiotemporal transformations beyond explicit motion modeling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of controllable video generation has rapidly evolved, driven by advancements in deep learning, especially diffusion models. DragNUWA positions itself at the intersection of several established paradigms, aiming to overcome their individual limitations.

Historically, early research focused on **image-to-video generation**, where an initial image served as a spatial control to predict subsequent frames. While this offered some control over appearance, it lacked the ability to dictate dynamic or semantic changes in the video (e.g., Lotter et al., 2016; Srivastava et al., 2015). The subsequent rise of **text-to-video generation** (e.g., GODIVA, NUWA, CogVideo, Make-A-Video, Imagen Video) introduced semantic control, allowing users to describe desired video content through text prompts. However, text alone often falls short in precisely defining spatial layouts or intricate temporal movements.

To enhance control, hybrid approaches like **text-image-to-video generation** emerged (e.g., MAGE, GEN-1, Phenaki, NUWA-XL), combining semantic (text) and spatial (image) information. While these methods offered more precise control over content and style, they still struggled with explicit control over motion, such as camera movements or complex object trajectories.

This is where **trajectory-based control** enters the scene, aiming to directly manipulate temporal information. Previous works in this domain, like CVG (Hao et al., 2018) and C2M (Ardino et al., 2021), often relied on predicting optical flow maps but sometimes suffered from unnatural distortions due to warp operations. More advanced methods, such as II2V (Blattmann et al., 2021b) and iPOKE (Blattmann et al., 2021a), operated in a dense latent space, while MCDiff (Chen et al., 2023) used diffusion latents for autoregressive frame prediction. However, these trajectory-focused methods often came with their own constraints:
- **Domain Specificity:** Many, like MCDiff, relied on keypoint extraction (e.g., HRNet for human poses) from specific datasets (e.g., Human3.6M), limiting their applicability to open-domain images and arbitrary objects.
- **Simplicity of Motion:** Some models, such as Video Composer (Wang et al., 2023), used low-level motion vectors that could only control simple object movements, lacking the high-level semantic understanding needed for complex interactions.
- **Lack of Integration:** Crucially, most trajectory control research neglected the integration of semantic (text) and rich spatial (image) information, leading to less comprehensive control over the generated video.

DragNUWA directly addresses these pervasive limitations by proposing a unified framework that *simultaneously* integrates text, image, and trajectory information. It argues that each of these controls is indispensable for fine-grained video generation, contributing semantic, spatial, and temporal perspectives respectively. By doing so, DragNUWA seeks to push the boundaries of controllability beyond existing fragmented approaches, enabling users to dictate not only *what* is in the video and *how it looks*, but also *how it moves* in complex, open-domain scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DragNUWA stems from two critical limitations observed in the contemporary landscape of controllable video generation:

1. **Insufficient Control Granularity:** Existing models typically focus on one or two control modalities, such as text, image, or trajectory, but rarely integrate all three comprehensively. The authors argue that text, image, and trajectory controls are fundamental and complementary, each offering unique information (semantic, spatial, and temporal, respectively). For instance, text provides high-level semantic context, an initial image establishes spatial layout and appearance, and a trajectory precisely dictates motion paths for objects or cameras. Without their combined power, fine-grained control over complex video content remains elusive. An example cited is the ambiguity of an image without text (sea vs. snow surfing) or the lack of specific motion without a trajectory.

2. **Limited Open-Domain Trajectory Control:** Current research in trajectory control is in its nascent stages and predominantly relies on constrained datasets (e.g., Human3.6M) or specific domains (e.g., human poses). This restricts models' ability to handle diverse, open-domain images and execute complex, curved trajectories involving multiple objects or intricate camera movements. The reliance on predefined keypoints or simple motion vectors limits adaptability to real-world, varied video scenarios.

Based on these observations, DragNUWA sets forth the following key objectives:
- **To propose DragNUWA**, an open-domain diffusion-based video generation model capable of synthesizing realistic and contextually consistent videos.
- **To achieve fine-grained control** in video generation by simultaneously integrating text, image, and trajectory information. This aims to provide users with unprecedented control over video content from semantic, spatial, and temporal perspectives.
- **To resolve the problem of limited open-domain trajectory control** by introducing a novel trajectory modeling framework. This framework comprises three essential components: a Trajectory Sampler (TS) for open-domain trajectory extraction, a Multiscale Fusion (MF) mechanism for integrating trajectories at different granularities, and an Adaptive Training (AT) strategy to ensure consistent video generation guided by trajectories.

Ultimately, DragNUWA aims to empower users with a powerful and intuitive tool for creating complex video content, moving beyond the fragmented control capabilities of prior works.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DragNUWA is designed as an end-to-end diffusion-based video generation model that seamlessly integrates text, image, and trajectory inputs. Its methodology revolves around a UNet architecture augmented with sophisticated mechanisms for encoding and fusing these diverse control signals, with a particular emphasis on robust trajectory modeling.

**Overall Architecture:**
DragNUWA operates within the framework of a latent diffusion model. During training, an input video `v` is encoded into a latent representation `x0` using a pre-trained image autoencoder. Noise `ϵ` is progressively added to `x0` to obtain `xt` at different diffusion steps. The UNet model `ϵθ` is then trained to predict this noise, conditioned on the text `p`, initial image `s`, and trajectory `g` controls.

**Key Aspects of Trajectory Modeling:**

1. **Trajectory Sampler (TS):**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional methods often rely on pre-extracted keypoint trajectories (e.g., human poses), which are domain-specific and create a mismatch between training (dense, specific) and inference (sparse, user-defined).
 - **Approach:** TS directly samples trajectories from open-domain video optical flow during training. Given a video `v`, it first extracts dense optical flow `f` using UniMatch. To ensure diverse learning across objects with varying motion magnitudes, anchor points are uniformly distributed with random perturbations. A maximum number of trajectories `N` is defined, and `n` anchor tracking points are sampled from the anchored optical flow `f^a_0` based on a multinomial distribution weighted by the flow intensity. These sparse tracking points are then iteratively updated across frames to form full trajectories `f^s`. Finally, a Gaussian Filter is applied to `f^s` to create an enhanced trajectory map `f^g`, which improves robustness and helps the model better capture trajectory information. This dynamic sampling allows the model to learn from a wide range of open-domain motions.

2. **Multiscale Fusion (MF):**
 - **Motivation:** To effectively integrate multiple control signals (text, image, trajectory) at different levels of abstraction and resolution within the UNet architecture.
 - **Approach:**
 - **Encoding Controls:**
 - **Video:** The input video `v` is encoded frame-by-frame into latents `x0` using a pre-trained image autoencoder.
 - **Text:** Text prompts `p` are encoded using a CLIP Text Encoder.
 - **Image:** The first frame of the video is used as a spatial condition `s`, repeated `L` times (for `L` video frames), and encoded through the image autoencoder and convolutional layers.
 - **Trajectory:** The enhanced trajectory map `f^g` (from TS) is padded with a zero frame at the beginning and encoded using convolutional layers to obtain `g`.
 - **Fusion Mechanism:** MF integrates these controls deeply within the UNet architecture, which consists of multiscale downblocks and upblocks. Image `s` and trajectory `g` conditions are downsampled to various scales (`s^(l)`, `g^(l)`) corresponding to the UNet's resolution levels. These scaled conditions are fused into the hidden state `h` of each UNet block via linear projections, using scale `w` and shift `b` parameters derived from zero-initialized convolutional layers. Text `p` is injected into `h` via Prompt Cross-Attention, where `h` acts as the query and `p` as key/value.
 - **Mixed Conditions Training:** To support flexibility during inference, the model is trained by randomly omitting text, images, and trajectories with a probability of 0.1. Empty strings replace dropped text, while zero-filled frames replace dropped images and trajectories. This strategy enables the model to handle various combinations of input controls.

3. **Adaptive Training (AT):**
 - **Motivation:** To address the challenge of generating visually and dynamically consistent videos when conditioned on sparse trajectory inputs, while benefiting from richer motion information during initial learning.
 - **Approach:** A two-stage training strategy:
 - **Stage 1 (Dense Flow Conditioning):** The model is initially optimized using prompt `p`, the *dense optical flow* `f` (not the sparse `f^g` from TS), and the repeated first frame `s` as conditions. The loss `Lθ` minimizes the squared error between the predicted noise `ϵθ` and the actual added noise `ϵ`. Conditioning on dense optical flow at this stage helps stabilize video generation, ensuring strong dynamic consistency and preservation of the initial frame.
 - **Stage 2 (Sparse Trajectory Adaptation):** After initial stabilization, training continues by conditioning on the *sparse trajectory* `f^g` (generated by TS from `f`). This stage adapts the model from the rich information of dense flow to the sparser, user-friendly trajectory inputs. Despite the increased sparsity, the stability learned in Stage 1 allows the model to generate consistent dynamics following the given trajectories.

**Inference:**
During inference, given user-provided text `p`, image `s`, and trajectory `g`, these inputs are encoded and processed through the Multiscale Fusion component within the UNet. The UNet iteratively denoises a pure Gaussian noise `xT` to sample the latent code `x0`, which is then decoded into the final video pixels `v` by the image autoencoder.

**Datasets and Implementation:**
DragNUWA is trained on a combination of WebVid (10 million diverse web videos with captions) and a custom-built VideoHD dataset (75K high-resolution, high-quality, BLIP2-annotated, and manually filtered videos). Two versions are implemented: DragNUWA-LD (8 frames, 320x192 resolution) and DragNUWA-HD (16 frames, 576x320 resolution), both utilizing a total of 1.60 billion parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on DragNUWA validate its effectiveness in achieving fine-grained control in video generation, particularly emphasizing its novel trajectory modeling capabilities and the necessity of integrating all three control modalities.

#### 4.1. Trajectory Controllability

A core contribution of DragNUWA is its superior ability to handle complex and open-domain trajectories, moving beyond the limitations of previous works. The model's performance was evaluated across two key aspects:

- **Camera Movements:** Despite not being explicitly modeled, DragNUWA demonstrated an inherent capacity to learn and generate various camera movements (e.g., zoom-in, zoom-out, horizontal/vertical panning) by simply modifying the dragging trajectories (as illustrated in Figure 4). This indicates that the Trajectory Sampler and Adaptive Training enable the model to infer and produce intricate spatiotemporal transformations from open-domain video data. Users can intuitively guide camera actions by drawing directional trajectories at desired zoom or pan locations.
- **Complex Trajectories:** DragNUWA excels at modeling intricate motions, a significant challenge for prior models. Experiments showed its ability to:
 - **Support complex curved trajectories:** Objects can be guided along specific, non-linear paths, as seen with a cat running on a lawn following a curved arrow (Figure 1, Row 1).
 - **Handle variable trajectory lengths:** Longer input trajectories result in greater motion amplitudes in the generated video, providing a direct and intuitive way to control the extent of movement (Figure 5, Row 7-8).
 - **Control multiple objects concurrently:** DragNUWA can simultaneously control the independent trajectories of several objects within the same scene (Figure 5). This represents a substantial leap in interactive video generation.

The authors claim that DragNUWA is a pioneering approach in achieving fine-grained open-domain video generation by allowing users to "drag" any object, manage multiple objects, and accommodate complex trajectories and camera movements.

#### 4.2. Essentiality of Three Controls

DragNUWA fundamentally argues for the necessity of integrating text, image, and trajectory controls, each representing semantic, spatial, and temporal perspectives, respectively. Figure 6 provides compelling evidence by showcasing results from different combinations of these controls:

- **Image-to-Video (s2v):** When only an image is provided, the generated video lacks precise control over the background and character movement, highlighting the limited information content of a single image for dynamic scenes.
- **Text-to-Video (p2v):** With only text, the model generates content semantically relevant to the prompt, but the visual appearance and dynamic movements remain entirely uncontrollable and often generic.
- **Image + Trajectory-to-Video (gs2v):** While combining image and trajectory provides some spatial and temporal control, semantic ambiguity persists. For example, an image of a person on a board with a trajectory might not specify whether they are "surfing on the sea" or "surfing on snow" without explicit text.
- **Text + Image-to-Video (ps2v):** Adding text resolves semantic ambiguity and provides better content generation. However, without a trajectory, the model might make default assumptions about motion (e.g., character automatically moving to the left), losing specific temporal control.
- **Text + Image + Trajectory-to-Video (pgs2v):** This combination demonstrates the full power of DragNUWA, enabling fine-grained control over all aspects. The example shows a "man surfing on snow" and explicitly moving to the right, illustrating complete control over semantics, appearance, and motion.

The paper further contrasts its approach with methods that use an entire video as a condition, arguing that such conditions often provide excessive information, primarily serve for style transfer, and significantly constrain creative freedom while demanding specific video inputs from users. DragNUWA focuses on fundamental, user-friendly conditions for creative synthesis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DragNUWA represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable video generation, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with current generative AI models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Unprecedented Fine-Grained Control:** DragNUWA pioneers the seamless integration of text, image, and trajectory inputs, offering a truly fine-grained control mechanism from semantic, spatial, and temporal perspectives. This holistic approach resolves a critical limitation of previous works that often focused on only one or two modalities, enabling users to dictate complex video narratives with high precision.
- **Robust Open-Domain Trajectory Modeling:** The proposed trajectory modeling framework (Trajectory Sampler, Multiscale Fusion, and Adaptive Training) effectively addresses the long-standing challenge of limited open-domain trajectory control. By moving beyond human-specific keypoints or simple motion vectors, DragNUWA can handle arbitrary objects, complex curved paths, multiple moving entities, and even implicitly learn various camera movements in diverse, real-world scenarios.
- **Methodological Innovation:** The three components of trajectory modeling – TS for dynamic, open-domain trajectory extraction, MF for deep, multiscale integration of diverse controls, and AT for stable learning from sparse cues – represent substantial methodological contributions that could inspire future work in multimodal generative models. The two-stage adaptive training is particularly clever in leveraging dense information for stability before adapting to sparser user inputs.
- **Empirical Validation:** Extensive experiments vividly demonstrate DragNUWA's superior performance in practical scenarios, from controlling detailed object motions to manipulating camera perspectives with intuitive trajectory inputs. The visual examples in the paper clearly articulate the model's capabilities.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Empowering Creative Professionals:** DragNUWA has the potential to revolutionize various creative industries, including animation, filmmaking, video game development, and content creation. Animators and artists could generate intricate scenes with precise control over character and object movements, significantly reducing manual effort. Filmmakers could rapidly prototype complex shots and camera movements.
- **Democratizing Video Production:** By offering intuitive "drag-and-drop" like control over video elements, DragNUWA could lower the barrier to entry for high-quality video production, enabling individuals without extensive technical skills or resources to create compelling visual content.
- **Advancing Human-AI Interaction:** The model's ability to interpret and respond to a combination of high-level semantic (text) and low-level spatial/temporal (image, trajectory) cues provides a blueprint for more natural and powerful human-AI interfaces in creative tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of DragNUWA in fusing disparate control signals and managing open-domain dynamics lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
 - **Longer and Higher-Fidelity Videos:** Extending DragNUWA to generate longer videos with even higher resolution and temporal consistency.
 - **Interactive Editing:** Developing more interactive interfaces that allow real-time modification of generated videos through dragging or text prompts.
 - **Conditional Generation beyond Motion:** Exploring other forms of conditional control, such as lighting, style transfer, or object deformation, within a unified framework.
 - **Broader Multimodal AI:** The principles of multiscale fusion and adaptive training could be adapted to other complex multimodal generation tasks.

In conclusion, DragNUWA represents a significant stride towards truly controllable and versatile video generation. By unifying essential control modalities and tackling the complexities of open-domain trajectory modeling, it not only pushes the boundaries of generative AI but also holds immense promise for practical applications across various creative and industrial sectors.
