# Unseen from Seen: Rewriting Observation-Instruction Using Foundation Models for Augmenting Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Rewriting-driven AugMentation (RAM) paradigm leverages foundation models to rewrite existing human-annotated Vision-Language Navigation data, synthesizing new observation-instruction pairs. This approach enhances agent generalization to unseen environments, showing improved performance across diverse VLN benchmarks like R2R, REVERIE, and R4R.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents struggle to generalize to diverse, unseen environments due to a scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated training data.
- Existing simulator-based data augmentation methods are limited by the diversity of virtual environments and often produce uninformative instructions.
- Web-based augmentation offers diversity but requires extensive, labor-intensive manual cleaning for noisy real-world data, while template-based instruction generation lacks flexibility.

## Method

- A Rewriting-driven AugMentation (RAM) paradigm is proposed, utilizing a combination of ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)==, Large Language Models (LLMs), and Text-to-Image Generation Models (T2IMs) to rewrite existing human-annotated data.
- The framework includes 'Object-Enriched Observation Rewriting' for synthesizing diverse visual scenes with new objects, and 'Observation-Contrast Instruction Rewriting' for generating contextually aligned and linguistically flexible instructions.
- A 'Mixing-then-Focusing' two-stage training strategy is employed to effectively integrate the generated data while mitigating potential noise from the T2IMs, by initially training on mixed data with random observation cropping and then finetuning on original data.

## Results

- RAM consistently improves generalization across R2R, REVERIE, and R4R benchmarks, outperforming baseline DUET on Val Unseen and Test Unseen splits; for instance, on REVERIE Val Unseen, it achieved approximately **3.1%** higher Success Rate (SR) and **2.2%** higher Remote Grounding Success Rate (RGS).
- The method demonstrates cross-environment generalization, showing improvements over the baseline on the continuous R2R-CE dataset despite being primarily developed for discrete environments.
- In low-resource settings, RAM significantly boosts performance; with only **60%** of the original training data, it achieves results comparable to the baseline trained on the full dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Foundation models can be synergistically combined to perform sophisticated rewriting of existing training data, effectively generating diverse observation-instruction pairs without relying on simulators or extensive manual labor.
> - Rewriting original human-annotated data to introduce novel object compositions in observations and contrastive elements in instructions significantly enhances the generalization capabilities of VLN agents.
> - A dedicated two-stage training mechanism, incorporating random observation cropping, is crucial for effectively leveraging augmented data while managing potential noise introduced by generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a domain within embodied artificial intelligence that requires an agent to navigate through a 3D environment based on natural language instructions. A persistent challenge in this field is data scarcity, which substantially limits the ability of VLN agents to generalize effectively to environments not encountered during training. Existing approaches to address this data scarcity broadly fall into two categories: simulator-based and web-based data augmentation.

Simulator-based methods often generate additional trajectories and instructions within existing or new simulator environments (e.g., Matterport3D, HM3D, Gibson). While these methods can produce large datasets, they are inherently constrained by the diversity of the simulator environments themselves. Instructions generated by "Speaker" models in these settings can also be uninformative. Conversely, web-based methods collect diverse real-world images or videos, but this data typically contains significant noise, requiring extensive and labor-intensive cleaning processes. Furthermore, template-based instruction generation used with web data often lacks flexibility.

More recently, research has begun to explore the application of large foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), for VLN tasks. Some studies directly leverage these models for navigation decisions, while others use them for data generation.

This paper introduces a "Rewriting-driven AugMentation (RAM)" paradigm that differentiates itself by directly rewriting existing human-annotated training data using a combination of foundation models (VLMs, LLMs, and Text-to-Image Generation Models or T2IMs). This approach is distinct from prior work as it aims to create unseen observation-instruction pairs in a simulator-free and labor-saving manner. By leveraging the generative capabilities of foundation models for data augmentation *prior* to training, RAM avoids the limitations of simulator-constrained diversity and the labor intensity of web data cleaning, while also generating more informative and flexible instructions than previous automated methods. This positions the work as an advancement in developing robust and generalizable VLN agents by addressing data limitations through sophisticated data synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents, particularly to unseen and diverse environments. This overarching goal is motivated by the persistent challenge of data scarcity in VLN, where the limited availability of high-quality, human-annotated trajectory-instruction pairs significantly hinders agents' performance in novel settings characterized by varied spatial layouts and object compositions.

The authors identify several limitations in existing data augmentation strategies that further underscore the motivation for their proposed approach:

1. **Limited Diversity in Simulator-based Augmentation**: Previous methods that rely on simulators (e.g., Matterport3D, HM3D) for generating additional training data are restricted by the inherent diversity of these virtual environments. While they can expand the quantity of trajectories, they often fail to introduce fundamentally new types of scenes, objects, or spatial arrangements beyond those already present in the simulators. Furthermore, instructions automatically generated by "Speaker" models in these contexts often lack richness and informativeness.
2. **Noise and Labor Intensity in Web-based Augmentation**: Approaches that collect data from the web (e.g., Airbnb images, YouTube videos) offer greater environmental diversity. However, these real-world sources are prone to significant noise, necessitating laborious and time-consuming manual data cleaning to ensure quality suitable for training. Additionally, instruction generation in these methods often relies on rigid, template-based strategies, limiting linguistic flexibility.
3. **Domain Gap in Direct Foundation Model Use**: While recent research explores directly employing LLMs for navigation decisions, such zero-shot or few-shot approaches can suffer from a domain gap between the general knowledge of foundation models and the specific requirements of embodied navigation tasks. Frequent querying of these models during inference also incurs significant computational costs.

To address these issues, the key objectives of the proposed Rewriting-driven AugMentation (RAM) paradigm are:

- **Simulator-Free Data Generation**: To create new observation-instruction pairs without requiring access to or dependence on specific 3D simulator environments, thereby overcoming their inherent diversity limitations.
- **Labor-Saving Process**: To minimize the manual effort associated with data collection, cleaning, and annotation, making the augmentation process more efficient and scalable than web-based methods.
- **Diverse Observation Synthesis**: To generate novel observations that feature a greater variety of objects and spatial layouts, thereby enriching the training data's environmental diversity.
- **Informative and Flexible Instruction Generation**: To produce new natural language instructions that are precisely aligned with the synthesized observations and exhibit more linguistic flexibility and detail compared to automatically generated instructions from previous methods.
- **Effective Integration of Foundation Models**: To demonstrate a synergistic combination of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Large Language Models (LLMs), and Text-to-Image Generation Models (T2IMs) to perform sophisticated rewriting of existing human-annotated data.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to provide a robust solution to data scarcity, enabling VLN agents to acquire more generalized observation-instruction alignment knowledge and navigation skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Rewriting-driven AugMentation (RAM)** paradigm aims to generate unseen observation-instruction pairs by rewriting existing human-annotated training data. This is achieved through a multi-stage process involving several foundation models. The overall framework consists of two core modules: Object-Enriched Observation Rewriting and Observation-Contrast Instruction Rewriting, complemented by a specialized training strategy.

#### 3.1. Object-Enriched Observation Rewriting

This module focuses on synthesizing new observations with diverse objects and spatial layouts, independent of simulators. It comprises two main procedures:

1. **Object-Enriched Scene Description Rewriting**:
 - For an original panoramic observation $O_t$ at a given navigation timestep $t$, a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Tag2Text, is used to generate an initial scene description $C_t$.
 - An LLM, ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo-1106), is then prompted with $C_t$ and a scene description rewriting prompt ($P_c$). This prompt instructs the LLM to generate rewritten, object-enriched scene descriptions ($C_r_t$). The LLM is specifically asked to:
 - Add possible co-occurring objects to the scene description.
 - Explicitly list these newly added objects.
 - Modify the representation of the original scene description to highlight different salient objects, aiming for increased diversity.
2. **Panorama-to-View Observation Generation**:
 - The rewritten object-enriched scene description $C_r_t$ is fed into a panoramic Text-to-Image Generation Model (T2IM), MultiDiffusion, to synthesize a new panoramic image ($O_r_t$). This approach ensures natural view consistency within the panorama.
 - The generated panoramic image is then discretized into $K$ single-view observations ($O_r_t,k$) using the Equirec2Perspec algorithm, which aligns with the standard view representation in VLN environments like Matterport3D.

#### 3.2. Observation-Contrast Instruction Rewriting

This module generates observation-aligned instructions for the newly synthesized observations, ensuring they are informative and contextually relevant. It involves three key steps:

1. **Sequential Landmark Grounding**:
 - An LLM extracts a sequence of landmarks $U = \{U_k\}_{k=1}^M$ from the original human-annotated instruction $I$.
 - For each ground-truth action (observation) $G_t$ in the original trajectory, a VLM (CLIP) is used to identify the most similar landmark $U_t$ from the extracted list $U$. This establishes a sequential grounding of landmarks to original observations.
2. **New Observation Description Collection**:
 - From the rewritten panoramic observations $O_r_t$, the ground-truth action (observation) $G'_t$ corresponding to the same position as $G_t$ is extracted.
 - The VLM (Tag2Text) then generates a scene description $C'_t$ for this new ground-truth observation $G'_t$.
3. **Instruction Rewriting by Observation Contrast**:
 - The LLM (ChatGPT) is given the original instruction $I$, the sequential grounded landmark information $\{U_t\}_{t=1}^T$, the new observation descriptions $\{C'_t\}_{t=1}^T$, and an instruction rewriting prompt ($P_i$).
 - The prompt guides the LLM to:
 - Contrast the original landmarks ($U_t$) with the descriptions of the new observations ($C'_t$).
 - Replace the objects/scenes mentioned in the original instruction $I$ with relevant landmarks identified in $C'_t$ at corresponding timesteps.
 - Modify the actional representations (e.g., "walk," "turn") in the original instruction to their synonyms, enhancing linguistic diversity.
 - This process yields the rewritten instruction $I_r$, which is aligned with the new observations and is more varied in its linguistic expression.

#### 3.3. Mixing-then-Focusing Training Mechanism

To effectively integrate the generated data while mitigating potential noise from T2IMs (e.g., repeated object generation), a two-stage training strategy is developed:

1. **Stage 1 (Mixing)**: The navigation agent is trained using a combination of the original human-annotated data and the newly generated rewritten data. During this stage, a *random observation cropping scheme* is applied to the rewritten panoramas. This scheme randomly crops large patches from panoramas and combines patches from different panoramas to form new, diverse panoramas. This enhances data distribution diversity and helps suppress the "repeated object generation" issue of T2IMs.
2. **Stage 2 (Focusing)**: The agent is finetuned exclusively on the original human-annotated data. This stage is designed to refine the agent's performance and reduce any residual negative impact of noise present in the augmented data.

The RAM paradigm is implemented by plugging into a pretraining-based baseline model, DUET, and utilizes CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 for visual features. Foundation models for augmentation (VLM, LLM, T2IM) are used in a zero-shot manner without additional training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across multiple VLN benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization ability of the Rewriting-driven AugMentation (RAM) paradigm.

#### 4.1. Performance on R2R (Discrete Environments)

- **Superior Generalization**: RAM, particularly when using CLIP ViT-L/14 features, generally outperforms existing methods that do not rely on large-scale additional data for training.
- **Efficiency vs. Scale**: While ScaleVLN achieves higher overall performance by utilizing significantly more (352 times) external simulator data, RAM introduces only a small-scale (3 times) augmentation data and still achieves comparable or even superior performance on the "Val Seen" split. Crucially, RAM consistently outperforms the baseline DUET on the "Val Unseen" split, indicating improved generalization to novel environments.

#### 4.2. Performance on REVERIE (Discrete, Object Grounding)

- **Enhanced Navigation and Grounding**: RAM significantly surpasses the baseline DUET on both navigation (Success Rate, SR) and object grounding (Remote Grounding Success Rate, RGS) metrics on "Val Unseen" and "Test Unseen" splits. For instance, on Val Unseen, it shows approximately 3.1% improvement in SR and 2.2% in RGS.
- **Outperforming Large-Scale Methods**: RAM also demonstrates stronger performance than methods like AutoVLN and ScaleVLN, which introduce extensive additional simulator data, in terms of both navigation and object grounding on "Test Unseen." This highlights RAM's capability to improve generalization without the need for massive external datasets.

#### 4.3. Performance on R4R (Discrete, Longer Trajectories/Instructions)

- **Robustness to Complexity**: For R4R, which features longer instructions and trajectories, RAM outperforms the baseline DUET across all navigation (NE, SR, SPL) and instruction following (nDTW, sDTW, CLS) metrics on "Val Unseen."
- **Advantage over T2IM-based Augmentation**: RAM also shows superiority over PanoGen, another method utilizing T2IMs for environment generation. This comparison indicates the effectiveness of RAM's "object-enriched observation rewriting" for diversifying scenes and its "observation-contrast instruction rewriting" for generating more aligned and informative instructions, compared to PanoGen's Speaker-based instruction augmentation.

#### 4.4. Transfer to R2R-CE (Continuous Environments)

- **Cross-Environment Generalization**: Despite primarily being developed for discrete environments, RAM shows notable improvements over the baseline DUET on the R2R-CE dataset, which involves continuous 3D scans. This suggests RAM's potential for transferring learned knowledge to more realistic continuous navigation scenarios.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Rewriting Components**: Both "object-enriched observation rewriting" and "observation-contrast instruction rewriting" contribute to performance gains. Generating observations based on object-enriched scene descriptions (rather than original ones) significantly boosts performance. The rewritten instructions are found to be more informative and diverse than those generated by traditional Speaker models.
- **Mixing-then-Focusing Training Mechanism**: The proposed two-stage training strategy proves effective, with "random observation cropping" further enhancing data diversity and mitigating T2IM-induced noise. The studies indicate that simply increasing the volume of rewritten data does not lead to linear performance gains, emphasizing the importance of sophisticated data fusion mechanisms.
- **Impact of Training Phases**: Integrating RAM augmentation data during both pretraining and finetuning phases yields the best overall performance, demonstrating a cumulative positive impact across training stages.

#### 4.6. Low-Resource Settings

- RAM consistently outperforms the baseline in scenarios with limited original training data (20%, 40%, and 60% of the full dataset). In the 60% setting, RAM achieves performance comparable to the baseline trained on the full original dataset, highlighting its practical utility in real-world applications where high-quality annotated data is scarce.

#### 4.7. Visualization

- Visual examples confirm that RAM generates diverse scenes with new objects and spatial layouts, and the rewritten instructions are precisely aligned with these new visual contexts and linguistically flexible. The generated panoramas exhibit reasonable cross-step semantic consistency. Comparisons to Speaker-model instructions further underscore the higher informativeness and alignment of RAM's generated instructions. The work also demonstrates the possibility of "controlled object generation" by adding attribute constraints to LLM prompts, opening avenues for targeted scenario creation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) by introducing the Rewriting-driven AugMentation (RAM) paradigm, directly addressing the persistent challenge of data scarcity. The primary significance of this work lies in its novel approach to data augmentation and its demonstrated effectiveness:

1. **Novel Data Augmentation Paradigm**: RAM offers an innovative alternative to traditional simulator-based and web-based data augmentation methods. By directly rewriting human-annotated data, it circumvents the limitations of environmental diversity in simulators and the extensive labor required for cleaning noisy web-collected data. This "unseen from seen" approach provides a new conceptual framework for expanding training datasets in embodied AI.

2. **Enhanced Generalization to Unseen Environments**: The experimental results consistently demonstrate that RAM significantly improves the generalization ability of VLN agents, particularly in unseen scenarios across various discrete and continuous benchmarks (R2R, REVERIE, R4R, R2R-CE). This is crucial for real-world deployment, where agents must operate in novel and unpredictable environments.

3. **Efficiency and Practicality**: The simulator-free and labor-saving nature of RAM makes it a highly efficient and practical solution for generating high-quality augmented data. By leveraging pre-trained foundation models in a zero-shot manner for data generation, it minimizes the need for specialized simulator setups or tedious manual annotation and cleaning, thereby reducing the cost and effort associated with expanding VLN datasets.

4. **Effective Integration of Foundation Models**: The research showcases a sophisticated and synergistic integration of multiple foundation models—Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Large Language Models (LLMs), and Text-to-Image Generation Models (T2IMs)—to perform complex observation-instruction rewriting. This demonstrates the potential of foundation models not just for direct task execution but also as powerful tools for generating diverse and high-quality training data. The ability to generate object-enriched observations and contextually aligned, flexible instructions highlights the advanced capabilities unlocked by this integration.

5. **Robustness in Low-Resource Settings**: The demonstrated effectiveness of RAM in low-resource experimental settings underscores its practical utility. This is particularly relevant for emerging embodied AI tasks where initial annotated datasets are typically small, allowing for rapid and effective expansion of training data.

6. **Inspiring Future Research**: This work contributes new insights into how augmentation data can be created and leveraged. The proposed mixing-then-focusing training strategy and the random observation cropping scheme provide valuable lessons on managing the noise inherent in generated data while maximizing its benefits. This could inspire future research into more sophisticated data fusion mechanisms and training strategies for generated data across various embodied AI domains.

In conclusion, RAM represents a significant step towards addressing the data scarcity challenge in VLN, offering a generalizable, efficient, and powerful method for augmenting training data. Its successful application highlights the transformative potential of foundation models in advancing the capabilities and practical applicability of embodied AI systems. Future research may explore parameter-efficient finetuning, feedback-based learning mechanisms, and even more controlled generation capabilities to further refine the use of foundation models for VLN data augmentation.
