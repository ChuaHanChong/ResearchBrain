# From Language to Locomotion: Retargeting-free Humanoid Control via Motion Latent Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Sydney, BAAI, and collaborators developed RoboGhost, a retargeting-free framework for language-guided humanoid control that directly conditions a diffusion-based policy on motion latents. This approach reduced deployment latency from 17.85 seconds to 5.84 seconds, improving tracking performance and generalization in both simulation and real-world robot deployment.

## Problem

- Existing language-guided humanoid locomotion pipelines suffer from cumulative errors across distinct stages of motion decoding, retargeting, and trajectory tracking.
- Conventional multi-stage approaches introduce high computational latency, hindering real-time, reactive human-robot interaction.
- The loose coupling between high-level language semantics and low-level robot control actions in current systems leads to a disconnect between intended meaning and executed behavior.

## Method

- RoboGhost proposes a 'retargeting-free' framework that bypasses explicit motion decoding and kinematic retargeting, directly utilizing compact latent motion representations.
- A diffusion-based student policy, conditioned by language-grounded motion latents, generates actions executable on the physical humanoid, streamlining the sim-to-real pipeline.
- The framework integrates a continuous autoregressive motion generator, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) based teacher policy, and a latent-driven diffusion-based student policy, trained with a causality-aware adaptive sampling mechanism.

## Results

- The latent-driven pipeline reduced deployment latency from **17.85** seconds (explicit) to **5.84** seconds (implicit), a **67%** reduction, while consistently outperforming explicit baselines in success rate and tracking accuracy.
- RoboGhost achieved a **0.97** success rate and **0.12** mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe) on the HumanML dataset in IsaacGym, demonstrating robust tracking and control.
- The framework demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to the MuJoCo physics engine and real-world execution on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, performing complex motions like backflips and boxing moves from text input without manual tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating motion latents as first-class conditioning signals enables a more direct and robust pathway from language to robot actions, preserving semantic intent and reducing errors.
> - Diffusion-based policies inherently offer superior generalization capabilities, robustly handling diverse action distributions and adapting to varying or imperfect latent conditions for real-world deployment.
> - Causal adaptive sampling during reinforcement learning efficiently focuses on challenging motion segments, significantly boosting the teacher policy's ability to master complex, long-horizon movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape: Current Challenges in Language-Guided Humanoid Locomotion

The field of language-guided humanoid locomotion aims to enable robots to interpret natural language commands and translate them into physically plausible movements. While recent advances in text-to-motion (T2M) models have demonstrated the ability to generate diverse and semantically meaningful human motions, deploying these models on real robots remains a significant challenge. Existing pipelines typically follow a hierarchical, multi-stage process: first decoding human motion from language, then retargeting it to the specific robot's morphology, and finally tracking the resulting trajectory with a physics-based controller.

The paper identifies three systemic drawbacks with this conventional approach:
1. **Cumulative Errors:** Errors and inconsistencies can accumulate across the distinct stages of motion decoding, retargeting, and trajectory tracking, ultimately degrading both the semantic fidelity of the instruction and the physical feasibility of the robot's motion.
2. **High Latency:** The sequential nature of multiple processing stages introduces substantial computational delays, making real-time, reactive human-robot interaction difficult and inefficient.
3. **Loose Coupling:** Each stage in the pipeline is often optimized in isolation, leading to a weak overall coupling between the high-level language semantics and the low-level robot control actions. This lack of end-to-end optimization can result in a disconnect between the intended meaning of a command and the robot's executed behavior.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Prior Work

While various T2M models (e.g., those by Li et al., Chen et al., Guo et al., Tevet et al.) have excelled in generating human motions, their direct application to robotics is hindered by the pipeline issues. Similarly, refinements to specific components of the hierarchical pipeline (e.g., by Yue et al., Serifi et al., Shao et al.) have provided local improvements but have not fundamentally addressed the inherent fragility and inefficiency of the multi-stage architecture. For instance, methods like LangWBC (Shao et al., 2025) struggle with scalability and generalization, while RLPF (Yue et al., 2025) faces issues with catastrophic forgetting and limited output diversity. In the realm of whole-body control (WBC), approaches like OmniH2O (He et al., 2024) and HumanPlus (Fu et al., 2024) prioritize specific robustness at the expense of generality, and others such as Hover (He et al., 2025b) and ExBody2 (Ji et al., 2024) improve adaptability but do not guarantee broad generalization. The core problem remains the indirect, error-prone translation from abstract language to concrete robot actions.

#### 1.3. RoboGhost's Contribution to the Landscape

RoboGhost introduces a paradigm shift by proposing a "retargeting-free" framework that bypasses the problematic intermediate stages. Its key insight is to treat "motion latents as first-class conditioning signals," directly using them to generate humanoid actions without explicit motion decoding and kinematic retargeting. This novel approach represents a more direct pathway from language to action, aiming to achieve robust, real-time interaction rather than fragile pose imitation. By integrating a diffusion-based policy driven by these language-grounded motion latents, RoboGhost seeks to preserve semantic intent, enable fast reactive control, and provide a generalizable foundation for future humanoid vision-language-action systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RoboGhost is to overcome the systemic limitations of existing language-guided humanoid locomotion pipelines, which are characterized by their cumbersome nature, unreliability, high latency, and weak coupling between high-level semantics and low-level control. The authors aim to establish a more direct and robust pathway from natural language instructions to physically feasible humanoid actions.

The key objectives of the RoboGhost framework are:

1. **Eliminate Intermediate Stages:** To fundamentally simplify the language-to-locomotion pipeline by bypassing explicit motion decoding and retargeting processes. This is driven by the need to reduce the accumulation of errors and the associated computational overhead.
2. **Reduce Deployment Latency:** To enable faster, more reactive control of humanoid robots by streamlining the action generation process, making real-time human-robot interaction feasible and efficient.
3. **Enhance Semantic-Control Coupling:** To create a tighter, more direct link between the semantic intent conveyed by natural language and the precise control actions executed by the robot. This involves using language-grounded motion latents as direct conditioning signals for the robot's policy.
4. **Improve Robustness and Generalization:** To develop policies that can reliably execute diverse and dynamic motions, adapt to novel instructions, and generalize across different environments and even different physics engines (sim-to-sim transfer) and real hardware (sim-to-real transfer).
5. **Achieve Smooth and Semantically Aligned Locomotion:** To ensure that the generated robot movements are not only physically plausible and stable but also accurately reflect the nuances of the language commands.
6. **Provide a Generalizable Multimodal Foundation:** To design a framework that can naturally extend beyond text inputs to incorporate other modalities such as images, audio, and music, thereby laying groundwork for comprehensive vision-language-action humanoid systems.

In essence, RoboGhost is motivated by the desire to transition language-driven humanoid control from a state of "fragile pose imitation" to one of "robust, real-time interaction," making humanoid robots more intuitive, responsive, and deployable in complex, dynamic real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboGhost framework is a novel, retargeting-free, latent-driven reinforcement learning architecture for language-guided humanoid control, designed to fundamentally diverge from conventional multi-stage motion-tracking pipelines. It comprises three core components: a continuous autoregressive motion generator, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) based teacher policy, and a latent-driven diffusion-based student policy, all integrated through a two-stage training and inference process.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

As depicted in Figure 2, the process begins with a textual prompt, `T`, which is fed into the continuous autoregressive motion generator. Crucially, this generator produces a compact *latent motion representation* (`l_ref`) rather than an explicit kinematic sequence. This bypasses the error-prone and computationally intensive steps of motion decoding and retargeting. This `l_ref`, combined with proprioceptive states and historical observations, then directly conditions a diffusion-based student policy (`π_s`), which denoises and outputs actions executable on the physical humanoid. This design eliminates dependency on privileged information or explicit reference motions during deployment, significantly streamlining the sim-to-real pipeline.

#### 3.2. Continuous Autoregressive Motion Generator

The motion generator is a hybrid causal transformer–diffusion architecture that leverages continuous autoregression.
- **Architecture:** It combines a causal autoencoder with a continuous masked autoregressive architecture, featuring a causal attention mask. This design aims to capture long-horizon temporal dependencies and ensure global consistency, while mitigating typical drift and information loss in autoregressive models.
- **Masked Modeling:** During training, motion tokens are randomly masked following a cosine-based mask ratio schedule (Equation 1), similar to practices in language models. However, unlike prior masked autoregressive methods, RoboGhost avoids random token shuffling or batch-token prediction and employs causal attention masking instead of bidirectional masks to enhance expressiveness.
- **Text Encoding:** For textual prompts, the LaMP (Li et al., 2024d) text transformer is utilized to extract high-dimensional, semantically rich linguistic features, providing essential guidance for motion generation.
- **Output:** The generator produces conditioned latent representations that guide the subsequent diffusion process, yielding accurate and semantically rich latents (`l_ref`) for downstream action generation, rather than human-like poses. The training objective is reformulated from noise prediction to velocity prediction for improved motion quality.

#### 3.3. Latent-Driven Diffusion Policy for Retargeting-Free Deployment: MoE-Based Teacher Policy

To handle the open-endedness of language and ensure generalization, an oracle teacher policy (`π_t`) is trained in simulation using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Training:** An initial policy (`π_0`) is trained on a diverse motion dataset. This dataset is then filtered, retaining only sequences with tracking errors below a threshold (`e(s) < 0.6`), forming a refined dataset `D`.
- **Privileged Information:** The teacher policy benefits from privileged simulator-state information, including ground-truth root velocity, global joint positions, physical properties (e.g., friction), proprioceptive state, and *explicit reference motion*.
- **Architecture:** To enhance expressive power and generalization, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module is incorporated into the teacher policy network. This network comprises multiple expert networks and a gating network. Experts take both robot state observations and *reference motion* as input, while the gating network produces a probability distribution over experts. The final action is a weighted combination of expert outputs.
- **Output:** The policy outputs target joint positions for proportional-derivative (PD) control, maximizing cumulative rewards for accurate motion tracking and robust behavior.

#### 3.3.2. Diffusion-Based Student Policy

The student policy (`π_s`) represents the core of the retargeting-free deployment.
- **Latent-Driven Input:** Unlike prior methods that use explicit reference motion, the student policy takes the compact motion latent representation (`l_ref`) from the motion generator as input, alongside observation history and proprioceptive states. This eliminates the need for retargeted explicit motion during inference.
- **Denoising Process:** Following a DAgger-like approach, the student policy is rolled out in simulation, and the teacher provides optimal actions for visited states. Gaussian noise (`ϵ_t`) is progressively injected into the teacher’s actions, and the diffusion model is trained to denoise these actions.
- **Forward and Reverse Process:** The forward noising process is modeled as a Markov process (Equation 3). The denoiser (`ϵ_θ`) is trained using an `x_0`-prediction strategy, minimizing the mean squared error loss (`L = ∥a - ˆa_t∥^2_2`).
- **Intelligent Conditioning:** A trainable latent encoder conditions the policy, translating raw (potentially imperfect) latent proposals into stable and actionable commands. This enables the policy to generate diverse and robust actions even from imperfect guidance, which is a crucial advantage.

#### 3.3.3. Inference Pipeline

The inference pipeline is designed for real-time, retargeting-free deployment.
- **Streamlined Flow:** Textual descriptions are input into the motion generator to obtain `l_ref`. Crucially, this latent is *not* decoded into explicit motion sequences, thereby eliminating retargeting.
- **Fast Denoising:** A random noise is sampled and fed into the student policy, where the diffusion model, conditioned via AdaLN on `l_ref`, proprioceptive states, and historical observations, denoises it to produce a clean action.
- **DDIM Sampling:** To ensure motion fluency and smoothness with minimal latency, DDIM sampling (Song et al., 2020) is adopted, along with an MLP-based diffusion model, producing actions directly executable by the robot (Equation 4). This pipeline mitigates issues related to low-quality motion generation, retargeting errors, and insufficient action diversity.

#### 3.4. Causal Adaptive Sampling

To enhance the teacher policy's ability to master challenging and long-horizon motions, RoboGhost employs a novel causality-aware adaptive sampling mechanism during RL training.
- **Addressing Heterogeneity:** Standard uniform sampling across trajectories can lead to oversampling trivial segments and undersampling challenging ones, resulting in high reward variance and suboptimal sample efficiency.
- **Failure Attribution:** The motion sequence is divided into equal-length intervals. When a rollout terminates due to failure in interval `k_t`, the method hypothesizes that the root cause often originates `s` steps prior.
- **Dynamic Probability Adjustment:** An exponential decay kernel (Equation 5) is applied to assign higher sampling weights to time steps leading up to termination. This increases the likelihood of sampling antecedent intervals that contain the "causal" missteps.
- **Efficient Learning:** The sampling probabilities for intervals are dynamically updated and renormalized. This strategy enables selective focus on high-difficulty segments, allowing the policy to efficiently learn agile motor skills by prioritizing corrective learning on challenging motion segments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboGhost undergoes extensive empirical evaluation across various settings, including large-scale reinforcement learning in IsaacGym, zero-shot transfer to MuJoCo, and real-world execution on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot. The evaluation systematically addresses the key research questions posed by the authors.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation: Advantages of Latent-driven Pipeline (Q1)

The ablation study comparing RoboGhost's implicit (retargeting-free, latent-driven) pipeline with an explicit pipeline (involving motion decoding and PHC retargeting) definitively demonstrates the benefits of the proposed approach.
- **Reduced Deployment Latency:** The implicit pipeline significantly cuts down the time cost from motion generation to humanoid deployment, reducing it from 17.85 seconds for the explicit version to a mere 5.84 seconds (Table 3). This is a substantial improvement for real-time applications.
- **Improved Performance:** RoboGhost's implicit approach consistently outperforms the explicit baseline in terms of success rate and tracking accuracy across both IsaacGym and MuJoCo. For instance, on the HumanML dataset, the implicit pipeline achieves a success rate of 0.97 (vs. 0.93 for explicit) in IsaacGym and 0.74 (vs. 0.66) in MuJoCo. Mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe) and mean per-keypoint error (E_mpkpe) are also significantly lower (e.g., IsaacGym E_mpjpe: 0.12 vs. 0.21). This validates the efficiency and performance advantages of bypassing error-prone decoding and retargeting stages.

#### 4.1.2. Generalization Gains via Diffusion Policy (Q2)

An ablation study replacing the diffusion policy with a conventional MLP-based policy highlights the superior generalization capabilities of the diffusion model.
- **Enhanced Tracking:** The diffusion-based policy consistently achieves better tracking performance on the HumanML dataset compared to its MLP counterpart (IsaacGym Success: 0.97 vs. 0.96; E_mpjpe: 0.12 vs. 0.17) (Table 4, left).
- **Robust Generalization to Unseen Data:** When tested on 10 randomly sampled motions from *unseen* MotionMillion subsets (fitness, perform, 100style, haa), the diffusion policy demonstrates significantly better tracking and robustness, even when operating with suboptimal latents from a motion generator not trained on these specific subsets (IsaacGym Success: 0.68 vs. 0.54; E_mpjpe: 0.42 vs. 0.48) (Table 4, right). This confirms that diffusion policies are better equipped to capture diverse action distributions and adapt robustly to varying or imperfect latent conditions, crucial for real-world deployment.

#### 4.1.3. Motion Generator Architecture (Q3)

An ablation on the diffusion backbone within the motion generator, comparing a 16-layer MLP and a 4-layer DiT (Diffusion Transformer), provides insights into architectural trade-offs.
- **Performance vs. Efficiency:** While DiT offered slight, marginal gains in some motion generation metrics (e.g., R@3: 0.870 vs. 0.867; FID: 11.697 vs. 11.706), it introduced significantly higher latency (14.28s vs. 5.84s) without leading to measurable improvements in tracking success or joint accuracy (Table 5). This led the authors to adopt the more efficient 16-layer MLP as the default backbone, balancing effectiveness with real-time deployment constraints.

#### 4.1.4. Motion Generation and Tracking Performance

- **Motion Generation:** RoboGhost's continuous autoregressive motion generation model (Ours-DDPM, Ours-SiT) achieves competitive performance against state-of-the-art text-to-motion methods on HumanML3D and MotionMillion (HumanML subset). Metrics like R-Precision, FID, and MM-Dist demonstrate robustness to representation variation and enhanced dynamic consistency due to the velocity prediction objective (Table 1).
- **Motion Tracking:** RoboGhost achieves high success rates and low joint/keypoint errors on both HumanML and Kungfu datasets in both IsaacGym and MuJoCo simulations. For example, on HumanML in IsaacGym, success rate is 0.97 (Ours-DDPM) with E_mpjpe of 0.12, consistently outperforming the baseline which shows a success rate of 0.92 and E_mpjpe of 0.23 (Table 2). This indicates robust alignment between the generated motion semantics and physically executable trajectories.
- **Causal Adaptive Sampling:** Ablation studies confirm that causal adaptive sampling (CAS) significantly boosts tracking performance and generalization, improving success rates and reducing errors on both HumanML and Kungfu datasets (Table 11). For instance, on HumanML in IsaacGym, CAS improves success from 0.92 to 0.97 and reduces E_mpjpe from 0.18 to 0.12.
- **MoE Experts:** The optimal number of experts in the teacher policy's MoE architecture was found to be 5, maximizing tracking performance (Figure 8).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative results visually reinforce the quantitative findings.
- **Simulation & Transfer:** Figures 1 and 3 illustrate RoboGhost's capability to execute complex motions like backflips, dancing, and various Kungfu sequences in IsaacGym and MuJoCo. The visualizations demonstrate semantic fidelity, balance maintenance under dynamic transitions, and effective generalization across physics engines.
- **Real-World Deployment:** Figure 5 provides snapshots of the Unitree G1 humanoid robot performing tasks such as squats, boxing moves, and jumping jacks based on text input. These real-robot demonstrations confirm that the latent-driven, retargeting-free framework enables smooth, temporally coherent motion execution without manual tuning, validating its practical readiness for embodied applications. The supplementary video further showcases the real-world performance.
- **Motion Generator:** Figure 6 showcases the diversity and quality of motions generated by the continuous autoregressive motion generator, reflecting its ability to interpret various text prompts accurately.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that RoboGhost substantially improves deployment latency, enhances success rates and tracking accuracy, and yields smooth, semantically aligned locomotion on both simulated and real humanoids. The framework’s retargeting-free, latent-driven design and diffusion-based policy offer significant advantages in efficiency, robustness, and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboGhost represents a significant advancement in the field of language-guided humanoid locomotion, offering a compelling solution to long-standing challenges in deploying expressive, semantically rich motions on physical robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Humanoid Control:** By introducing a truly retargeting-free and latent-driven framework, RoboGhost fundamentally redefines the pipeline from language to robot action. It moves beyond the traditional hierarchical, error-prone process of motion decoding, retargeting, and tracking, establishing a more direct and robust connection between semantic intent and physical execution. This shift from "fragile pose imitation to robust, real-time interaction" is a crucial step towards more capable and autonomous humanoid robots.

2. **Enhanced Real-World Deployability:** The substantial reduction in deployment latency (from 17.85s to 5.84s) makes real-time, responsive human-robot interaction a tangible reality. This efficiency, combined with improved robustness and tracking accuracy, is vital for practical applications where robots must react dynamically to fluid human commands and complex environments. The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to the Unitree G1 humanoid robot further validates its practical readiness.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The diffusion-based student policy, combined with the MoE teacher and causal adaptive sampling, demonstrates superior generalization capabilities. The ability to perform well even with suboptimal latents from unseen motion categories highlights a critical strength for real-world scenarios, where robots will encounter novel instructions and variations in motion styles. This reduces the need for extensive, robot-specific data collection and tuning.

4. **Stronger Semantic-Control Coupling:** By directly conditioning the robot policy on language-grounded motion latents, RoboGhost ensures that the robot's actions are more deeply aligned with the semantic meaning of the commands. This tight coupling mitigates the loss of intent that often occurs in multi-stage pipelines, leading to more intelligent and intuitive robot behaviors.

5. **Foundation for Multimodal Humanoid Systems:** The framework's inherent extensibility to other input modalities, such as images, audio, and music, positions RoboGhost as a foundational architecture for future vision-language-action (VLA) humanoid systems. This opens up vast possibilities for robots to understand and respond to a richer spectrum of human communication and environmental cues, enabling more natural and versatile interactions.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** RoboGhost's novel approach will likely inspire further research into latent-space control, end-to-end learning for robotics, and alternative paradigms for bridging high-level intelligence with low-level motor control. It challenges the conventional wisdom regarding the necessity of explicit kinematic representations for robot control.

In conclusion, RoboGhost is more than just an incremental improvement; it represents a methodological breakthrough that addresses fundamental bottlenecks in humanoid control. Its focus on efficiency, robustness, and direct semantic grounding paves the way for a new generation of humanoid robots that are not only more agile and capable but also more intuitive and seamlessly integrated into human-centric environments.
