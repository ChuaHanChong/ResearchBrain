# From Language to Locomotion: Retargeting-free Humanoid Control via Motion Latent Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Sydney, BAAI, and collaborators developed RoboGhost, a retargeting-free framework for language-guided humanoid control that directly conditions a diffusion-based policy on motion latents. This approach reduced deployment latency from 17.85 seconds to 5.84 seconds, improving tracking performance and generalization in both simulation and real-world robot deployment.

## Problem

- Existing language-guided humanoid locomotion pipelines suffer from cumulative errors across distinct stages of motion decoding, retargeting, and trajectory tracking.
- Conventional multi-stage approaches introduce high computational latency, hindering real-time, reactive human-robot interaction.
- The loose coupling between high-level language semantics and low-level robot control actions in current systems leads to a disconnect between intended meaning and executed behavior.

## Method

- RoboGhost proposes a 'retargeting-free' framework that bypasses explicit motion decoding and kinematic retargeting, directly utilizing compact latent motion representations.
- A diffusion-based student policy, conditioned by language-grounded motion latents, generates actions executable on the physical humanoid, streamlining the sim-to-real pipeline.
- The framework integrates a continuous autoregressive motion generator, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) based teacher policy, and a latent-driven diffusion-based student policy, trained with a causality-aware adaptive sampling mechanism.

## Results

- The latent-driven pipeline reduced deployment latency from **17.85** seconds (explicit) to **5.84** seconds (implicit), a **67%** reduction, while consistently outperforming explicit baselines in success rate and tracking accuracy.
- RoboGhost achieved a **0.97** success rate and **0.12** mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe) on the HumanML dataset in IsaacGym, demonstrating robust tracking and control.
- The framework demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to the MuJoCo physics engine and real-world execution on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, performing complex motions like backflips and boxing moves from text input without manual tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating motion latents as first-class conditioning signals enables a more direct and robust pathway from language to robot actions, preserving semantic intent and reducing errors.
> - Diffusion-based policies inherently offer superior generalization capabilities, robustly handling diverse action distributions and adapting to varying or imperfect latent conditions for real-world deployment.
> - Causal adaptive sampling during reinforcement learning efficiently focuses on challenging motion segments, significantly boosting the teacher policy's ability to master complex, long-horizon movements.
