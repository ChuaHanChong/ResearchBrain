# asRoBallet: Closing the Sim2Real Gap via Friction-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Underactuated Spherical Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> ASROBALLET introduces a cost-effective humanoid ballbot developed using subtractive reconfiguration, which achieves zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for robust whole-body locomotion. This is enabled by a high-fidelity, friction-aware MuJoCo simulation and an intuitive iOS teleoperation interface, demonstrating superior stability and velocity tracking compared to LQR in real-world tests.

## Problem

- Underactuated and nonholonomic dynamics make ballbots inherently challenging to control and stabilize.
- High hardware costs and mechanical complexity limit the accessibility and widespread adoption of ballbot research platforms.
- The Sim2Real gap, often due to simplified friction and contact models in simulation, prevents effective transfer of reinforcement learning policies to physical ballbots.

## Method

- Developed **asRoBallet**, a ==reconfigurable humanoid ballbot== built by ==subtractive reconfiguration== of an open-source quadruped, significantly reducing hardware cost and complexity.
- Created a high-fidelity ==MuJoCo simulation== that explicitly models complex ==tribological phenomena== (rolling, lateral, torsional friction, discrete omni-roller mechanics) and actuator friction for robust ==RL training==.
- Integrated a consumer-grade ==iOS ecosystem== for low-latency ==visual-inertial odometry (VIO)== and intuitive, markerless whole-body teleoperation using ubiquitous Apple devices.

## Results

- The RL policy achieved **100% success** in velocity tracking and station keeping in simulation, consistently outperforming a **Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR)** baseline, especially under random friction and initial orientations.
- Demonstrated successful **zero-shot Sim2Real transfer** to the physical asRoBallet hardware, exhibiting smooth locomotion across diverse indoor and outdoor surfaces and recovering from pushes up to **0.3 meters**.
- Achieved real-world station keeping with position variations within **3-5 cm** and velocity tracking with a mean absolute error of **0.05 m/s**, showcasing robust and precise control on hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cost-effective humanoid ballbots can be realized through 'subtractive reconfiguration,' leveraging existing open-source hardware to democratize access to advanced robotic platforms.
> - Explicit and granular modeling of multi-channel friction and micro-dynamics in simulation is critical for bridging the Sim2Real gap in underactuated, contact-rich systems like ballbots.
> - Friction-aware reinforcement learning, when combined with high-fidelity simulation and domain randomization, enables robust whole-body control and zero-shot transfer for unstable, underactuated robotic systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Ballbots, or single-ball-wheel robots, represent a robotic morphology for human-centric environments due to their omnidirectional mobility and slender profile, which facilitate agile navigation and compliant physical interaction. Despite these advantages, their widespread deployment has been limited by two primary barriers: hardware integration complexity and the Sim2Real gap in controlling underactuated spherical dynamics.

Historically, ballbot platforms have relied on expensive, custom-built drivetrains and industrial-grade perception systems, resulting in rigid, mechanically complex, and costly robots. This contrasts with recent advancements in legged robotics, which have seen a convergence towards cost-effective, high-torque-density, and back-drivable actuation systems (quasi-direct-drive) and smartphone-based computing. Applying such compliant and cost-effective architectures to the inherently unstable ballbot dynamics has remained largely unexplored.

The second critical barrier is the "Reality Gap" in control. Classical control methods like Linear Quadratic Regulators (LQR) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) for ballbots typically assume idealized conditions, such as infinite friction (no slip), negligible rolling resistance, and perfect actuation. In practice, the interaction between rubber-coated balls and omni-directional rollers involves complex tribology, characterized by coupled rolling resistance, lateral slip, and torsional friction, further compounded by the passive dynamics of the rollers and the floating ball. A single omni-wheel can introduce multiple passive degrees of freedom, which standard model-based controllers struggle to account for.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated success in developing end-to-end Sim2Real locomotion policies for other robot types (e.g., quadrupeds, bipeds). However, its application to ballbot locomotion has been less extensive. Previous RL studies for ballbots have been confined to restricted control settings, such as refining iLQG-based controllers with model-based initialization, augmenting feedback controllers in simulation only, or controlling only specific sub-systems (e.g., arms during falls). Some work has introduced ballbot simulations for RL, but without demonstrating Sim2Real transfer.

Finally, a usability gap exists in teleoperating high-Degree-of-Freedom ballbot systems, which often require complex, multi-operator interfaces. The lack of lightweight interfaces leveraging ubiquitous consumer hardware for unified, whole-body control represents an additional challenge. This work aims to address these identified gaps in hardware accessibility, control robustness, and interface usability for humanoid ballbots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and deploy an end-to-end reinforcement learning locomotion policy on a humanoid ballbot hardware platform, addressing the Sim2Real gap and other practical challenges. The motivation behind this objective stems from the identified limitations in current ballbot research and deployment:

1. **Hardware Accessibility and Cost:** Traditional ballbots are expensive and complex to build. The researchers aimed to develop a cost-effective and impact-resilient humanoid ballbot platform. This was motivated by the observation that modern quadrupedal robots leverage proprioceptive quasi-direct-drive actuators and smartphone-based computing for high performance at lower costs, an approach largely unapplied to ballbots.
2. **Control Robustness and Sim2Real Gap:** The inherent underactuated nature and complex tribological interactions (rolling, lateral, and torsional friction) at the wheel-ball-floor interfaces create a significant reality gap for conventional control methods. The objective was to overcome this gap by developing a control framework that accounts for these complex microdynamics, enabling zero-shot Sim2Real transfer. This was motivated by the limitations of classical controllers that rely on idealized models and the restricted scope of previous RL applications to ballbots.
3. **High-Fidelity Simulation:** A key enabler for robust RL policies in robotics is an accurate simulation environment. The researchers sought to create a MuJoCo simulation that explicitly models the discrete roller mechanics of omni-wheels, including parasitic vibrations and contact discontinuities, which are critical for physical balancing stability and often ignored in simpler models.
4. **Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** Teleoperation of complex humanoid systems typically requires specialized and often non-intuitive interfaces. A goal was to develop a lightweight, intuitive interface that leverages consumer electronics for whole-body teleoperation, making ballbots more accessible and easier to control for expressive maneuvers.

In summary, the research aims to introduce a holistic ballbot system, "asRoBallet," by addressing the gaps in hardware design, high-fidelity simulation, friction-aware reinforcement learning, and intuitive human-robot interaction, thereby enabling robust humanoid balancing and locomotion in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved a multi-faceted approach encompassing hardware design, high-fidelity simulation, a friction-aware reinforcement learning framework, and a consumer-grade user interface.

**A. Reconfigurable Design of asRoBallet**
The asRoBallet platform was constructed using a subtractive reconfiguration methodology, repurposing components from the open-source asOverDog quadruped.
- **Mechanical Architecture:** The core components, including high-bandwidth actuators, computation, and perception, were retained, while the chassis and four limbs of the quadruped were replaced with new structures.
 - **Spherical Wheel Base:** Three ETH-type omni-wheels, each with 12 rollers, were arranged at 120-degree offsets to drive a 220 mm SBR-coated hollow steel ball. Lower-torque actuators (RobStride 05, 5.5 Nm) were used for the omni-wheels.
 - **Humanoid Upper Body & Frame:** Higher-torque actuators (RobStride 01, 17 Nm) were configured for a 2-DoF head and two 4-DoF arms. These were mounted on a lightweight T-shaped sheet-metal frame with a 3D-printed shoulder, designed to minimize rotational inertia. The system has 49 degrees of freedom (13 active, 36 passive).
- **Proprioceptive & Computational Core:** The existing electro-mechanical stack was directly transplanted. This included quasi-direct-drive actuators for current-based torque estimation, a DJI TB47S battery, a 2 kHz industrial IMU (XSense MTI630), an Intel NUC for real-time control, and a Jetson Orin for neural inference.
- **Consumer-Grade Perception & Interaction:** Industrial sensing was replaced by a unified consumer-grade ecosystem.
 - **Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO):** An iPhone 12 Pro was mounted on the spine to provide low-drift 6-DoF localization via its Neural Engine, eliminating the need for discrete LiDARs.
 - **Scalable Teleoperation:** A custom iOS application was developed using Wi-Fi Aware for low-latency peer-to-peer communication. This interface supports gamepad control and embodied telepresence via markerless human motion capture from Apple devices (Watch, AirPods, iPhone).

**B. Friction-Aware Modeling in MuJoCo**
A high-fidelity MuJoCo multibody simulation was developed to address the reality gap by explicitly modeling tribological phenomena and uncertainties often ignored by analytical models.
- **Floating Base and Virtual Ball Joint:** The robot was modeled with a floating base. A virtual, low-damped ball joint was added between the torso and the ball during training. This joint maintained stable contact between the wheels and the ball, ensuring the total contact force equaled the torso's weight, which was found to be crucial for successful initial training and policy improvement. This joint was removed for evaluation and hardware deployment.
- **Omni-wheel Modeling:** Each omni-wheel was modeled with its physical geometry: a ring of 12 free-spinning rollers attached via hinge joints. This geometric fidelity was intended to capture lateral compliance. MuJoCo's soft-constraint convex solver with an elliptic friction cone and high constraint impedance ratio was used to resolve the overconstrained drive system. The Newton solver with `noslip_iterations=1` was enabled for stick-slip transitions.
- **Solver & Contact Formulation:**
 - **Ball–Floor Contact:** Modeled with full six-dimensional contact (normal, two tangential, torsional spin, two rolling resistances). Parameters specified were high sliding friction (μ_slide = 1), moderate spin friction (μ_torsion = 0.01), and small rolling resistance (μ_roll = 0.0001).
 - **Wheel-Ball Interface:** Standard three-dimensional contact was used. A soft rubber-like layer on the ball was modeled with increasing stiffness upon penetration (`solimp="0.85 0.99 0.003"`). Lateral compliance was derived from the rollers' free joints.
- **Actuator Frictions:** Viscous friction coefficient (D_v) and Coulomb friction torque (D_c) for the omni-wheel actuators were identified. D_c was measured to be 80-120 mNm, notably higher than other ballbots, creating a dead zone that contributes to limit-cycle jitter.
- **Actuation Types:** Omni-wheels were actuated using direct motor torques, while arm and neck joints used position servos.

**C. Reinforcement Learning**
- **Task Formulation:** Two locomotion tasks were defined: Velocity Tracking and Station Keeping. Upper-body manipulation was decoupled from base stabilization; arms and head were treated as passive inertial payloads with randomized configurations during locomotion training.
- **State and Action Space:**
 - **Action Space (A ∈ R^3):** Continuous torque commands for the three omni-wheel motors.
 - **Observation Vector (o_t):**
 - **Velocity Tracking (o_VT_t ∈ R^16):** Proprioceptive measurements relative to the body coordinate: linear velocity (v_B), commanded velocity (v_cmd), roll/pitch tilt (q_tilt), CoM in body frame (x_COM), gyroscope angular rate (ω_B), and previous action history (a_t-1). v_B was approximated via non-slip forward kinematics in real experiments.
 - **Station Keeping (o_SK_t ∈ R^17):** Augmented proprioceptive state with exteroceptive position data: v_B, planar position error in world frame (p_W), sine/cosine of yaw error (sin ψ, cos ψ), q_tilt, ω_B, and a_t-1.
- **Reward Design (Table I):**
 - **Velocity Tracking:** Rewards for tracking linear and angular velocities (exponential decay), penalties for roll/pitch angular velocities, uprightness, torque effort, and action rate.
 - **Station Keeping:** Rewards for minimizing distance from goal position and yaw error, and penalties for angular velocity, body tilt, torque effort, and action rate.
- **Episode Reset & Termination:** Randomized initial yaw, roll/pitch, linear/angular velocities, and head/arm configurations. Episodes terminated if roll or pitch exceeded 20°, or truncated at 1,000 steps (10 seconds) for velocity tracking, 2,000 steps for station keeping.
- **Domain Randomization & Robustness:** To improve Sim2Real transfer, the following parameters were randomized during training: ball-floor tangential friction coefficient (U(0.6, 1.2)), motor Coulomb friction torque (U(0.08, 0.12) Nm), and gyroscope sensor noise (N(0, 0.009) rad/s).
- **Learning Algorithm & Training Setup:** PPO (Stable-Baselines3) was used with 8 parallel MuJoCo environments, 2048 rollout steps per environment, a mini-batch size of 512, and 10 epochs per update. Control frequency was 100 Hz (frame_skip=5, simulator time step=0.002 s). Policies were trained for 4.0 × 10^6 environment steps. Actor and critic networks were MLPs with two hidden layers of 64 units and Tanh activations.

**D. Humanoid Interaction with an iOS App (asMagic)**
A custom iOS application was developed to leverage consumer-grade devices for interaction:
- **All-in-One Real-time Sensing Suite:** The iPhone Pro's embedded sensors were used for pose estimation (VIO) with reported relative errors within 5% for position and 1.5% for orientation. Peer-to-peer communication latency was measured at approximately 1.2 ms.
- **Scalable Interface for Humanoid Interaction:** The app offers three interaction levels: joystick control, wearable motion capture via IMU suit (Apple Watch, AirPods), and markerless whole-body pose tracking (iPhone).
- **General Purpose Visualization:** The app provides user interfaces for visualizing human-robot interaction, multi-modal data streaming, and custom simulation environments with OpenUSD models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the asRoBallet system's performance in simulation and hardware, as well as the contributions of specific design choices.

**A. Benchmark Whole-Body Performance of asRoBallet (Simulation)**
The RL policy was compared against a fixed-gain LQR controller under four increasing levels of task difficulty, progressively violating the assumptions of the nominal linear model:
1. **Fixed default arms (P0):** Nominal condition with all arm joints fixed at zero.
2. **Fixed P0 & random friction:** Random torsional friction between ball and floor (U[0.01, 0.5]).
3. **Fixed P0 & random orientation:** Random initial body tilt within 5 degrees.
4. **Random arm pose:** Random arm configurations causing significant variations in system inertia and CoM.

- **Velocity Tracking (Table II):**
 - Under nominal conditions (Fixed P0), both controllers achieved 100% success. However, the RL policy exhibited a mean absolute error (MAE) of 4.0 cm/s, significantly lower than LQR's 7.7 cm/s, with lower variance.
 - As task difficulty increased, the performance gap widened. With random torsional friction, LQR's success rate dropped to 96%, while RL maintained high accuracy.
 - With random initial orientations, LQR's success rate degraded to 56%, whereas RL maintained 100% success.
 - In the most challenging scenario (random arm configurations), LQR showed large tracking errors (MAE > 23 cm/s) and high variability. RL maintained 100% success with substantially lower errors (MAE 6.6 cm/s).
- **Station Keeping (Table II):**
 - Under nominal conditions, both performed well.
 - With random friction or orientation perturbations, LQR's success rate dropped sharply (to 18% and 0%, respectively), failing entirely in some cases.
 - In contrast, the RL controller consistently achieved 100% success across all configurations, maintaining a smaller steady-state position range and lower residual velocity, even under random arm configurations.

**B. Ablation Study (Simulation)**
Ablation studies were conducted to evaluate the contributions of observation/reward shaping and domain randomization (Table III, Figure 7). All evaluations were performed without the virtual ball joint.

- **Observation/Reward Design:**
 - **`arm-obs` (raw arm joint positions vs. CoM-related features):** Replacing CoM-related state with raw arm joint positions significantly degraded performance (velocity-tracking success dropped to 92%, larger MAE/STD; station-keeping showed greater drift). This indicates the importance of a compact, task-relevant global representation.
 - **Reward Shaping (Tanh-reward, Exp-reward, Track-sigma-0.1):** The exponential tracking reward (used in the full model) achieved the best tracking accuracy (MAE 5.8/6.3/1.7 cm/s). Tanh-shaped reward slightly reduced tracking accuracy but yielded better station keeping (100% success, 4.5 cm range). An overly sharp exponential reward (σ=0.1) negatively impacted tracking.
- **Domain Randomization:**
 - **`w/o-imu-noise`:** Removing injected IMU noise during training reduced tracking success to 78%.
 - **`w/o-friction-randomization`:** Removing friction randomization reduced tracking success to 82%.
 - These results highlight that realistic sensing and contact perturbations (domain randomization) are essential for reliable generalization and robustness.
- **Virtual Ball Joint:** The virtual ball joint, used only during training, was found to be a crucial training-time stabilizer. Policies failed to converge from scratch without it, and removing it after initial convergence destabilized further training. This suggests it guides the agent through sparse reward landscapes in the initial exploration phase.

**C. Sim2Real Transfer of Underactuated Spherical Dynamics**
The RL policy demonstrated zero-shot Sim2Real transfer, successfully deploying on the asRoBallet hardware without any fine-tuning in the real world (Figure 8, Figure 9).

- **Robust Locomotion:** asRoBallet moved smoothly across various indoor and outdoor floor textures and lighting conditions, including ceramic tiles, yoga mats, office carpet, and even transitioning over a 5-degree slope and a slight aluminum bump.
- **Push Recovery:** The station-keeping policy on a yoga mat showed position variation within 3 cm (5 cm on ceramic tiles) and average residual speeds of 4.4 cm/s (5.1 cm/s on tiles). It managed to return to the starting point after being pushed up to 0.3 meters away in 7 trials.
- **Velocity Tracking (Hardware):** When controlled by a human operator via joystick on office carpet, the robot achieved a mean absolute error of 0.05 m/s.
- **Hardware Robustness:** The system exhibited robustness to strong impacts, remaining operational after falls, although the 3D-printed neck connector was identified as a vulnerable component.

**D. iOS App Performance**
- **Sensing Suite:** iPhone 17 Pro's ARKit pose estimation showed relative errors within 5% for position and 1.5% for orientation, deemed sufficient for advanced robotics. Peer-to-peer communication latency was approximately 1.2 ms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents asRoBallet as a comprehensive system that addresses multiple barriers to the deployment of reinforcement learning on underactuated spherical robots. The work is reported as the first instance of an end-to-end RL locomotion policy successfully deployed on a humanoid ballbot hardware platform.

The **significance** of this work stems from several key contributions:
- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** The development of a high-fidelity MuJoCo simulation that explicitly models discrete roller mechanics, parasitic vibrations, and complex tribological interactions (friction-aware framework) enabled robust zero-shot hardware deployment. This indicates that accurate contact modeling is a critical bottleneck for Sim2Real transfer in ballbots, more so than the learning algorithm itself.
- **Cost-Effective Hardware Design:** The "subtractive reconfiguration" methodology, repurposing proprioceptive actuation, computational, and perceptual cores from an existing quadruped, demonstrates a viable strategy for constructing high-performance humanoid research platforms at a reduced cost. This contributes to making advanced robotics more accessible for research and development.
- **Robust Control:** The friction-aware RL policy consistently outperformed classical LQR baselines in robustness to varied surface textures, calibration errors, and significant CoM shifts caused by random arm configurations. This suggests a pathway for ballbots to operate reliably in diverse and unstructured environments.
- **Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** The integration of consumer-grade mobile computing into the control loop via the iOS app ecosystem provides a low-latency, high-bandwidth interface for whole-body telepresence. This democratizes access to advanced humanoid control, potentially simplifying interaction for a broader user base.

**Limitations** of the current implementation include:
- The reliance on a virtual ball joint during RL training for policy convergence, suggesting a sparse reward landscape for spherical balancing that requires artificial constraints for initial exploration.
- The presence of residual jitter in stabilization due to the Coulomb friction dead zone in the current drivetrain, which the RL policy manages but cannot entirely eliminate.

**Potential Impact** and **Future Work** include:
- **Mobile Manipulation:** Extending the system from robust locomotion to active mobile manipulation by incorporating the upper-body joints into the control loop for dynamic tasks like door opening or object transport.
- **Vision-to-Torque Policies:** Developing policies that learn directly from raw iPhone visual inputs to adapt implicitly to surface properties, further reducing the reality gap.
- **Multi-Agent Collaboration:** Utilizing the peer-to-peer Wi-Fi Aware capabilities of the iOS ecosystem to explore multi-agent collaborative balancing for tasks such as large-object transport or navigation in crowded social environments.

Overall, this research contributes to advancing the state of the art in ballbot control, hardware design, and human-robot interaction, laying a foundation for more capable and accessible spherical mobile robots.
