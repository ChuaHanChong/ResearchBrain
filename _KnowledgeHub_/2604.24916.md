# asRoBallet: Closing the Sim2Real Gap via Friction-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Underactuated Spherical Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> ASROBALLET introduces a cost-effective humanoid ballbot developed using subtractive reconfiguration, which achieves zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for robust whole-body locomotion. This is enabled by a high-fidelity, friction-aware MuJoCo simulation and an intuitive iOS teleoperation interface, demonstrating superior stability and velocity tracking compared to LQR in real-world tests.

## Problem

- Underactuated and nonholonomic dynamics make ballbots inherently challenging to control and stabilize.
- High hardware costs and mechanical complexity limit the accessibility and widespread adoption of ballbot research platforms.
- The Sim2Real gap, often due to simplified friction and contact models in simulation, prevents effective transfer of reinforcement learning policies to physical ballbots.

## Method

- Developed **asRoBallet**, a ==reconfigurable humanoid ballbot== built by ==subtractive reconfiguration== of an open-source quadruped, significantly reducing hardware cost and complexity.
- Created a high-fidelity ==MuJoCo simulation== that explicitly models complex ==tribological phenomena== (rolling, lateral, torsional friction, discrete omni-roller mechanics) and actuator friction for robust ==RL training==.
- Integrated a consumer-grade ==iOS ecosystem== for low-latency ==visual-inertial odometry (VIO)== and intuitive, markerless whole-body teleoperation using ubiquitous Apple devices.

## Results

- The RL policy achieved **100% success** in velocity tracking and station keeping in simulation, consistently outperforming a **Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR)** baseline, especially under random friction and initial orientations.
- Demonstrated successful **zero-shot Sim2Real transfer** to the physical asRoBallet hardware, exhibiting smooth locomotion across diverse indoor and outdoor surfaces and recovering from pushes up to **0.3 meters**.
- Achieved real-world station keeping with position variations within **3-5 cm** and velocity tracking with a mean absolute error of **0.05 m/s**, showcasing robust and precise control on hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cost-effective humanoid ballbots can be realized through 'subtractive reconfiguration,' leveraging existing open-source hardware to democratize access to advanced robotic platforms.
> - Explicit and granular modeling of multi-channel friction and micro-dynamics in simulation is critical for bridging the Sim2Real gap in underactuated, contact-rich systems like ballbots.
> - Friction-aware reinforcement learning, when combined with high-fidelity simulation and domain randomization, enables robust whole-body control and zero-shot transfer for unstable, underactuated robotic systems.
