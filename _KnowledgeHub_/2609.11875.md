# UniMPA: A Unified Memory-Prediction-Action Model via Action-Grounded Transition Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> UniMPA is a three-stream vision-language-action model that unifies memory, prediction, and action through an action-grounded transition interface. Zero-initialized World-Expert queries become future-supervised transition tokens via persistent latent plus trigger-gated pixel prediction; bidirectional visual-action memory banks retrieve executable experience, and Prototype-Biased Flow refines the retrieved action prior. It reaches 98.6% on LIBERO and 85.3% on LIBERO-Plus using 25-50% of baseline training epochs.

## Problem

- Observation-conditioned policies suffer transition ambiguity: visually similar frames belong to different manipulation phases needing different transitions, so static observations cannot encode phase and control intent together.
- Future-prediction methods pick one level only; pixel prediction wastes capacity on static background, latent prediction discards contact and geometric constraints, and neither ties predictions to executable actions.
- Memory-augmented policies store observations, keyframes, or implicit histories, ignoring transition-action correspondence, so retrieved action patterns may be unrealizable under current poses and contacts.

## Method

- ==Persistent-Selective Future Prediction== supervises content-free ==World-Expert== transition tokens with continuous latent future targets plus a ==Latent+Action Trigger Gate== that decodes pixels only at transition-critical contact and release moments.
- Bidirectional memory: a ==Visual-Action Bank== queried by the predicted transition returns executable evidence, an ==Action-Visual Bank== queried by action history returns visually grounded prototypes, both built with ==Vision-Mamba==/==Action-Mamba== temporal encoders and ==coarse-to-fine retrieval==.
- ==Prototype-Biased Flow== shifts the flow-matching source distribution toward the retrieved historically supported action manifold, which the ==Action Expert== then refines against current vision, language, and proprioceptive state.

## Results

- LIBERO average **98.6%** beats X-VLA **98.1%** and pi-0.5 **96.9%** at **25%** of the training epochs; LIBERO-Plus zero-shot **85.3%** exceeds GuidedVLA **75.4%** and pi-0.5 **73.6%**.
- RoboTwin 2.0 Randomized-Hard zero-shot average **58.2%** leads HALO and pi-0.5 by **25.7** and **18.5** points, ranking first on **8 of 11** tasks; real-world AgileX gives **74.9%**/**86.4%** TSR/CSR versus **62.3%**/**74.9%**.
- Ablations: removing memory costs **4.1** LIBERO and **13.8**/**10.7** real-world points; nearest-neighbor action copying loses **22.9**/**18.9** real-world points; random trigger gating loses **2.6** and **9.8**/**7.3** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prediction, memory, and action should share one representation; coupling them through an action-grounded transition converts auxiliary future prediction into a retrieval interface rather than a side objective.
> - Retrieved experience works best as a shifted generative source distribution that the policy refines, not as an executed trajectory or a mere conditioning feature.
> - Memory pretrained on future reconstruction beats current-state reconstruction by 12.0/9.7 real-world points, indicating retrieval keys should encode expected state change, not appearance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which translate visual observations into robotic actions conditioned by natural language instructions. However, a central challenge in these models is the "transition realizability gap." This gap refers to the difficulty in inferring a task-relevant visual transition and subsequently executing an action that can physically realize that predicted change in the current environment. Existing VLA models, often categorized as single-system or dual-system architectures, typically learn a direct mapping from visual observations to actions, leaving the intended transition implicit. While some methods incorporate future prediction or memory mechanisms, these are often treated as auxiliary components rather than being fundamentally integrated to model the relationship between intended state evolution and executable action generation.

Prediction-based VLA/World Action Models (WAMs) aim to anticipate future states, but these predictions are frequently used as auxiliary or cascaded objectives and may not be explicitly grounded in historically executable experience. Similarly, memory-augmented VLA models store historical information, but retrieval is often observation-centric, neglecting the crucial correspondence between visual transitions and the actions that produce them. This can lead to issues when visually similar scenes require different actions due to underlying phase changes.

UniMPA addresses these limitations by proposing a unified framework that integrates prediction, memory, and action generation through a shared "action-grounded transition interface." It extends the dual-system VLA paradigm by introducing a "World Expert," forming a three-system architecture that explicitly bridges high-level vision-language understanding with low-level action control by modeling anticipated physical changes and leveraging executable historical experience. This approach distinguishes UniMPA from prior work by fundamentally shifting future modeling from generic reconstruction to explicit transition modeling and by coupling action generation with these anticipated transitions through temporal alignment with previously realized, executable experiences. The goal is to establish a more coherent and adaptable mechanism for robots to understand and execute complex manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the "transition realizability gap" in robotic manipulation, which is identified as a fundamental bottleneck in observation-to-action learning for VLA models. This high-level problem manifests in three interconnected issues that UniMPA aims to resolve:

1. **Transition Ambiguity:** The motivation stems from scenarios where visually similar current observations may correspond to different phases of a manipulation task, implying distinct subsequent transitions and requiring different action patterns. For instance, in T-shirt folding, nearly identical visual layouts might represent the start of a fold, a continuation of a fold, or an adjustment phase, each demanding a unique action sequence. Existing observation-conditioned policies often fail to resolve this ambiguity because they primarily map static observations to actions without explicitly modeling the task's temporal evolution or identifying the intended next transition. UniMPA seeks to resolve this by modeling the intended future state evolution, ensuring that the policy can distinguish between visually similar states that require different action outcomes.

2. **Prediction–Execution Mismatch:** A predicted future observation, even if visually plausible, does not inherently guarantee that it can be physically realized through a coherent and executable action sequence by the robot. Current future prediction methods typically operate at either the pixel level (which can be dominated by static content and computationally intensive) or the latent level (which might lose fine-grained details critical for interaction). Neither approach alone reliably connects the predicted visual transition to the specific action evolution required to achieve it. The objective here is to bridge this gap by enabling the model to assess the physical executability of an anticipated transition, ensuring that predicted future states correspond to physically realizable actions.

3. **Experience–Realization Mismatch:** Historical action patterns, though successful in past scenes, may not be directly transferable or executable in the current scene due to variations in object poses, contact conditions, or geometric constraints. Directly replaying past actions without contextual adaptation can lead to failures. Traditional memory mechanisms often store observations or general implicit histories without explicitly capturing the precise correspondence between a visual transition and the action sequence that physically realized it in a specific context. The motivation is to adapt historically executable experience to the current scene's context, ensuring that retrieved action knowledge can be refined rather than merely replayed.

To address these, UniMPA proposes a unified "anticipate–ground–refine" process. This aims to create a cohesive interface between the intended state evolution and executable action generation, where prediction disambiguates intent and forms retrieval queries, memory grounds future predictions in executable visual-action evolution, and retrieved experience provides an adaptable action prior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniMPA is structured as a Unified Memory-Prediction-Action model that integrates three core components to address the identified transition realizability gaps. The model operates on an input context $X_t = \{V_t, \ell, q_t, H_t\}$ (current visual observations, language instruction, proprioceptive state, and historical actions) to produce a future action chunk $A_t$. It employs a three-system architecture consisting of a VLM backbone, a World Expert, and an Action Expert, interacting through hierarchical cross-expert attention.

1. **Persistent-Selective Future Prediction:** This component is designed to resolve transition ambiguity by explicitly modeling intended future state evolution.
 - **Future-Supervised Latent Transition Modeling:** The World Expert transforms zero-initialized transition queries into latent transition tokens ($Z_{tr,t}$) conditioned on the current context. During training, these tokens are continuously supervised (persistent) by projecting them into a frozen V-JEPA 2 latent space of the target future observation ($V_{t+\Delta}$). This latent prediction loss ($L_{lat}$) guides the World Expert to encode manipulation progress and long-term state evolution into $Z_{tr,t}$. These transition tokens directly condition the Action Expert during both training and inference.
 - **Transition-Critical Trigger Gate:** To incorporate fine-grained interaction changes selectively, a training-only Trigger Gate activates pixel-level future prediction only at critical moments. The gate combines a semantic transition score (computed from the predicted latent displacement) with action-derived indicators (magnitudes of translation, rotation, and gripper state changes over the action horizon). Pixel prediction is activated if the latent change exceeds a threshold OR any action-derived indicator is active. This approach avoids the redundancy and reconstruction bias of dense pixel prediction, focusing supervision on contact and release moments.
 - **Memory-Grounded Pixel Future Calibration:** The future latent representation decoded from $Z_{tr,t}$ is used to construct a transition-aware query. This query is then used to retrieve action-aware transition experience ($\bar{u}^\nu_{v,t}$) from the Visual-Action Memory Bank. A pixel future predictor uses the World Expert's tokens as queries and the retrieved visual values as decoder memory. The pixel prediction loss ($L_{pix}$) is then applied only when the Trigger Gate is active, calibrating $Z_{tr,t}$ around critical visual transitions with memory-grounded evidence.

2. **Transition-Aligned Bidirectional Memory Pretraining:** This component addresses the prediction-execution mismatch and experience-realization mismatch by grounding future prediction in executable evidence. The memory is pretrained in Stage 1 and frozen during policy training (Stage 2).
 - **Visual and Action Key Construction:** For each camera view, visual keys ($k^\nu_{v,t}$) are derived from frozen V-JEPA 2 features. Action keys ($k_{a,t}$) are derived from the recent action history. These keys are optimized to facilitate future-oriented retrieval.
 - **Temporal Value Encoding:** Stateful Mamba modules are employed to encode episode-level temporal dynamics for both visual ($u^\nu_{v,t}$) and action ($u_{a,t}$) streams. This enables memory values to distinguish visually similar observations based on their interaction history.
 - **Cross-Stream Transition Grounding:** Visual temporal states are conditioned on action states and semantic visual tokens, injecting action-conditioned dynamics into visual memories. Conversely, action temporal states are grounded in multi-view visual dynamics, imbuing action memories with visual-transition semantics.
 - **Future-Oriented Memory Reconstruction:** Memory values are trained to decode into future images ($\hat{I}^\nu_{t+\Delta}$) and future action chunks ($\hat{A}_t$) through an $L_{rec}$ loss, ensuring they retain information about future evolution.
 - **Retrieval-Simulation Training:** During pretraining, retrieval is simulated using soft-retrieval distributions over candidate sets. The $L_{ret}$ loss ensures that the retrieved values predict the correct future, optimizing key and value spaces for effective retrieval.
 - **Cross-Modal Pairing Objective:** An $L_{pair}$ loss explicitly aligns visual and action values that correspond to the same physical transition, fostering a vision-action correspondence.
 - **Coarse-to-Fine Temporal Indexing:** For efficiency and consistency, retrieval first identifies the most relevant episode (coarse stage) and then performs soft retrieval only within that episode (fine stage), preventing the mixing of incompatible task phases across demonstrations.

3. **Action-Manifold Prototype Refinement:** This component addresses the experience-realization mismatch by adapting retrieved executable experience to the current scene.
 - **Action Proposer:** Uses the recent action history to compute an action query ($q_{a,t}$), which represents the current execution tendency. This query retrieves a visually grounded action prototype ($\bar{u}_{a,t}$) from the Action-Visual Memory Bank using the coarse-to-fine temporal retrieval strategy.
 - **Prototype-Biased Flow:** Instead of directly executing the retrieved prototype, it is fused with a projection of the recent action history to form prior tokens ($P_{a,t}$). This prior biases the initial Gaussian source distribution for the flow-matching process, translating it towards a historically supported and executable region of the action space. This is formalized as $\tilde{\epsilon}_t = \epsilon_t + \lambda_p P_{a,t}$.
 - **Action Expert Refinement:** The Action Expert then refines these prior-biased samples through iterative flow matching. It conditions its velocity field prediction on the vision-language prefix ($C_t$), the future-supervised transition representation ($Z_{tr,t}$), and the prototype-conditioned action context ($P_{a,t}$). An $L_{act}$ loss guides this refinement.

The overall training objective for UniMPA ($L_{UniMPA}$) combines the action flow-matching loss, persistent latent prediction loss, and trigger-gated pixel prediction loss. During inference, the World Expert and Action-Visual Memory Bank remain active, generating the transition representation and biasing action generation, while the explicit prediction heads are removed to optimize computation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniMPA demonstrates robust performance, generalization, and training efficiency across a range of simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

**Simulation Benchmarks:**

- **LIBERO (Table III):** UniMPA achieved an average success rate of 98.6%, which surpassed the performance of the strongest reported baseline, X-VLA (98.1%). This outcome was achieved using only 25% of the training epochs required by baselines such as $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$, indicating substantial training efficiency.
- **LIBERO-Plus (Table II & V):** For zero-shot generalization under seven perturbation dimensions (camera, robot, language, illumination, background, observation noise, and object layout), UniMPA attained an average success rate of 85.3%. This ranked it first among all compared methods, exceeding the next best average (79.7%) by 5.6 percentage points and outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ by 11.7 points. The model also exhibited the smallest performance drop (13.3 points) from its original performance, indicating superior robustness to distribution shifts. Performance under visual noise, camera, background, and layout shifts showed that the model's reliance on latent future representations and state transitions contributed to its resilience.
- **RoboTwin 2.0 (Table IV & XXI):** When evaluated on zero-shot generalization under the Randomized (Hard) setting after training on Clean demonstrations, UniMPA achieved an average success rate of 58.2% across 11 tasks. This result surpassed HALO and $\pi_{0.5}$ by 25.7 and 18.5 percentage points, respectively, securing the top rank on 8 out of 11 tasks. Detailed evaluation across 44 tasks confirmed an average success rate of 35.84% compared to $\pi_{0.5}$'s 27.57%.
- **VLABench (Table VI):** On language-conditioned manipulation tasks requiring semantic understanding, UniMPA achieved the highest average success rate of 44.0%, outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ by 4.3 percentage points. It achieved the best or tied-best performance on four of the six tasks, indicating effective generalization beyond visually explicit goals due to its task-conditioned future-transition prediction and executable priors.

**Real-World Tasks:**

- **GALAXEA R1 Lite (Table VII):** Across 21 tasks organized into seven manipulation suites, UniMPA achieved 77.7% Task Success Rate (TSR) and 86.3% Cumulative Success Rate (CSR). These figures significantly surpassed those of $\pi_{0.5}$ (65.3% TSR / 75.6% CSR) and OpenVLA-OFT (45.7% TSR / 58.1% CSR).
- **AgileX Cobot Magic (Table VIII):** On seven representative tasks, UniMPA achieved an average 74.9% TSR and 86.4% CSR, outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ by 12.6 and 11.5 percentage points, respectively.
- **Dynamic and Long-Horizon Tasks:** UniMPA exhibited clear advantages in tasks requiring temporal coordination and adaptation. It achieved 82.7% TSR / 86.7% CSR on bimanual coordination tasks, 70.7% TSR / 79.2% CSR on dynamic and reactive tasks, and 72.0% TSR / 83.3% CSR on long-horizon composition and recovery tasks. These results indicate the efficacy of persistent future modeling and transition-aligned memory in maintaining phase-consistent execution, particularly in recovery scenarios.

**Ablation Studies:**

A series of ablation studies provided evidence for the contributions of UniMPA's design choices:
- **Persistent-Selective Future Prediction:** The full persistent latent plus selectively triggered pixel prediction outperformed variants with only latent, only pixel, or dense pixel prediction, highlighting the complementary roles of capturing task progress and fine-grained interaction cues.
- **Transition-Critical Trigger Gate:** The combined latent and action-based trigger gate performed better than random, action-only, or latent-only gates, demonstrating the value of multimodal cues for identifying critical transition moments.
- **Transition-Aligned Bidirectional Memory:** Removing either the Visual-Action or Action-Visual Memory Bank, or the entire memory system, resulted in performance degradation, supporting the necessity of both banks for grounding predictions in executable experience and providing visually grounded action prototypes.
- **Temporal Retrieval Strategies:** Coarse-to-fine temporal retrieval proved superior to a fixed local window search or no temporal modeling, underscoring the importance of phase-consistent retrieval.
- **Future-Oriented Memory Pretraining:** Training the memory with future-oriented reconstruction and cross-modal pairing was more effective than current-state reconstruction or removing these objectives, confirming that the memory effectively learns transition semantics.
- **Action-Manifold Prototype Refinement:** Prototype-Biased Flow significantly outperformed direct nearest-neighbor action copying and using the prototype only as a conditioning feature, indicating that biasing the flow source with retrieved experience, followed by refinement, is an effective strategy.

**Qualitative Analysis:**

Visualizations of temporal memory structure, cross-modal retrieval, executable action prototypes, pixel-level future predictions, and World-Expert attention maps provided qualitative support for the model's mechanisms. These visualizations illustrated temporally coherent memory organization, accurate capture of motion trends by prototypes, informative pixel predictions at critical moments, and focused attention on manipulation-relevant regions that evolve with task progression.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniMPA research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by identifying and systematically addressing the "transition realizability gap" in Vision-Language-Action models. The introduction of a unified "anticipate–ground–refine" process, centered on an action-grounded transition interface, represents a conceptual shift from treating prediction, memory, and action generation as separate components to integrating them within a cohesive framework.

The work demonstrates that explicitly modeling intended visual changes, grounding them in historically executable experience, and refining actions with retrieved prototypes leads to improvements in manipulation robustness, generalization, and training efficiency. The observed state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0, VLABench) and real-world tasks (on both AgileX Cobot Magic and GALAXEA R1 Lite platforms) supports the effectiveness of this unified approach. The significantly reduced training epochs required by UniMPA for superior performance suggests that structured learning of transitions provides a more efficient learning signal compared to direct observation-to-action mapping.

The enhanced robustness to various distribution shifts (e.g., visual noise, camera/background changes) and strong generalization capabilities in zero-shot settings indicate that UniMPA can operate effectively in varied and novel environments beyond its training data. The model's ability to handle dynamic interactions, bimanual coordination, and long-horizon tasks with recovery capabilities suggests applicability to complex real-world scenarios.

Potentially, UniMPA's approach could inspire future embodied AI research to move beyond merely predicting future states or recalling past events. Instead, it encourages focusing on how intended changes in the world can be precisely grounded and realized through adaptable, executable actions. This transition-centric modeling paradigm could serve as a foundation for developing more adaptive, intelligent, and continually improving robotic agents capable of performing complex tasks in unstructured environments.
