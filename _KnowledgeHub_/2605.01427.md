# SixthSense: Task-Agnostic Proprioception-Only Whole-Body Wrench Estimation for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> SixthSense infers whole-body external contact wrenches on humanoids from proprioception and IMU alone, with no force, tactile, or vision sensors. It discretizes the robot surface into per-link regions, predicts a joint contact-probability mask plus a 6-D wrench field, and samples them with conditional flow matching over 50-step windows. On a Unitree G1 it reaches 84.2% detection while standing and generalizes zero-shot to multi-contact.

## Problem

- Reinforcement-learning humanoid controllers treat contact as a disturbance to reject, entangling contact information with task objectives so it is never explicitly estimated or reused across tasks.
- Analytical observers like the generalized momentum observer and contact particle filter need accurate rigid-body models, well-conditioned Jacobians, and known contact cardinality, which floating-base dynamics break.
- Prior learning-based sensorless estimators cover only pre-defined end-effector contacts, and dense whole-body force or tactile skin remains too costly and calibration-heavy to scale.

## Method

- A ==unified whole-body contact representation== divides the surface into N per-link sub-regions, jointly predicting a probabilistic mask and a per-region 6-D wrench field, gated by a threshold product.
- ==Conditional flow matching== learns a velocity field transporting Gaussian noise to a 50-step contact chunk conditioned on tokenized proprioceptive history, with a shared backbone and dual mask/wrench attention heads.
- A composite loss adds mask cross-entropy, mask-aware wrench regression, consistency and sparsity priors; a **contact-resilient controller** trained with domain randomization supplies aligned, task-agnostic normalized-torque observations.

## Results

- Detection reaches **84.2%** standing, **85.5%** walking, **78.1%** tracking; tolerant timing hits **98.1/85.5/93.3%**, with force magnitude errors of **2.1, 1.9, 1.7 N** respectively.
- Trained only on single contacts, flow matching reaches **89.31%** Top-1 multi-contact detection versus **17.24%** for an MLP that matched it at **99.69%** on single contacts.
- Against the contact particle filter under 1-5 simultaneous contacts, detection rises from **45.3%** to **60.0%** at one contact while false alarms drop from **12.6%** to **1.9%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distributional generative modeling, not raw network capacity, is what lets a single-contact-trained estimator hold multiple hypotheses and extrapolate to overlapping, ambiguous multi-contact configurations.
> - Controller robustness is an upstream data-quality knob: more robust policies leave cleaner contact signatures, improving false-alarm rate, localization, and force accuracy far more than detection rate.
> - Dropping controller commands and using impedance-normalized torque as an action surrogate buys cross-task reuse, at a measurable accuracy cost worst on reference-dependent motion tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are increasingly deployed in complex environments, capable of agile locomotion and whole-body motion tracking. However, their ability to robustly interact with physical environments and understand contact forces remains limited. Current approaches often treat physical contact as a disturbance to be rejected rather than a phenomenon to be perceived and understood. This limitation restricts humanoids' practical utility in scenarios requiring physical interaction, such as human-robot collaboration or manipulation in uncertain environments.

Existing methods for external wrench estimation can be categorized into several areas:

- **Implicit Contact-Aware Control:** Reinforcement learning (RL)-based controllers for locomotion or motion tracking implicitly handle contacts. They embed floating-base state estimation within learned networks, allowing for disturbance recovery and stable behaviors without explicit contact force handling. However, contact information in these approaches is typically entangled with specific control objectives, limiting its reusability and generalization across different tasks or interaction regimes.
- **Sensor-Based Contact-Rich Control:** Direct force, torque, and tactile sensors provide explicit contact information, extensively used in dexterous manipulation for grasp stability and fine interaction control. However, scaling dense force sensing to entire humanoid bodies presents challenges due to hardware cost, integration complexity, and calibration requirements. Visual cues can also be used to anticipate contact geometry but do not directly capture body-level force interactions during execution.
- **Sensorless Contact Force Estimation (Model-Based):** For fixed-base manipulators, model-based observers, such as the Generalized Momentum Observer (GMO) or Contact Particle Filter (CPF), infer external wrenches from dynamics residuals. These methods, however, face significant challenges when extended to whole-body humanoid interaction. The floating base and numerous potential contact locations render the problem under-determined and nearly unobservable, leading to non-identifiable mappings and requiring idealistic assumptions or highly restricted contact models. Such assumptions (e.g., accurate rigid-body models, well-conditioned Jacobians, precise foot-force models, known contact cardinality) often limit their practical applicability in real-world humanoid deployment.

This research aims to bridge the gap by proposing a task-agnostic, proprioception-only approach to estimate whole-body external wrenches without requiring additional force, vision, or tactile sensors. It moves beyond implicit contact handling in controllers and addresses the limitations of model-based approaches by formulating the problem as a generative inference task over a spatiotemporal distribution of external wrenches, leveraging modern deep learning techniques. This represents a shift from explicit contact localization and inverse dynamics solutions to a data-driven, probabilistic inference framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a task-agnostic method, named SixthSense, for inferring whole-body contact timing, location, and wrenches in humanoid robots using only proprioceptive and Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data.

The motivation for this research stems from several limitations in current humanoid robotics:

- **Lack of Force-Interaction Capabilities:** While humanoids can execute complex motions, they often lack reliable contact perception. They are typically unaware of *when*, *where*, and *how* external forces are applied to their bodies. This deficiency limits their ability to engage in meaningful physical interaction with their environment or humans, hindering their practical utility beyond controlled demonstrations.
- **Limitations of Existing Analytical Frameworks:**
 - **Floating-Base Dynamics and Indeterminate Contact Locations:** For humanoids, the presence of a floating base and a multitude of potential contact points makes external wrench estimation physically under-determined and nearly unobservable.
 - **Idealistic Assumptions and Hard-to-Obtain Measurements:** Analytical solutions often rely on oversimplified dynamics models or highly restricted contact assumptions, which are difficult to meet in practical, unstructured environments. They can also be fragile to modeling errors and sensor noise.
 - **Non-Identifiable Mappings:** When contact information is uncertain, the mapping from contact wrenches to dynamics residuals can be non-injective, leading to multiple plausible solutions and making wrench recovery structurally non-identifiable.
- **Limitations of Learning-Based Control:** While some learning-based controllers implicitly handle disturbances and contacts for stability, the contact information remains tightly coupled to specific tasks and control objectives. This limits the reusability and generalization of such contact awareness across different tasks or interaction regimes. Explicitly estimating contacts could enable broader applications.
- **Need for Task-Agnostic Perception:** A perception module that can provide explicit, reusable whole-body wrench estimates, independent of the specific control policy or task, is crucial for enabling force-aware reasoning and diverse interactions.

To address these motivations, the paper formulates whole-body contact perception as an inference problem over a spatiotemporal distribution of external wrenches. It seeks to answer specific questions:
- How to represent spatiotemporally sparse and uncertain external wrenches when multi-point contacts occur across the entire body?
- How to infer external wrenches under a fundamentally non-identifiable mapping, where different contacts can produce similar proprioceptive responses, when contacts are only indirectly observed?
- How to maintain task-agnosticism and generalizability in whole-body external wrench estimation despite varying dominant dynamics and contact regimes across different tasks and motion phases?

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SixthSense framework adopts a generative inference approach, modeling external contact wrenches as a stochastic spatiotemporal process conditioned on closed-loop proprioceptive signals.

**A. Contact Representation and Learning Objective**
Instead of a modular three-step pipeline (detecting binary state, identifying location, computing wrench), the robot surface is treated as a single interaction entity.
- **Discretization:** The robot surface is divided into *N* sub-regions (e.g., *N*=30 for Unitree G1, corresponding to its links).
- **Output:** At each time step *t*, the model predicts:
 - A probabilistic contact mask *M*<sub>*t*</sub> ∈<sup>*N*</sup>, indicating the likelihood of contact for each sub-region.
 - A per-subregion 6-D wrench field *F*<sub>*t*</sub> ∈ R<sup>*N*×6</sup>, representing the wrench (force and torque) applied at the center of mass of each sub-region.
- **Wrench Selection:** A threshold *δ* is applied to the contact mask to select active contact regions, and their corresponding wrenches are reported (Eq. 4, 5).
- **Learning Objective:** A composite loss function (Eq. 6) is minimized to learn whole-body contact under severe spatio-temporal sparsity:
 - *L*<sub>mask</sub>: Binary cross-entropy loss for contact mask prediction.
 - *L*<sub>wrench</sub>: Mask-aware regression loss for wrench estimation, prioritizing errors on predicted contact regions.
 - *L*<sub>consistency</sub>: A prior to suppress spurious forces when no contact is predicted.
 - *L*<sub>sparsity</sub>: A prior to encourage sparsity in the predicted contacts.

**B. Proprioceptive Signal Construction and Alignment**
- **Observation Window:** The proprioceptive observation at time *t* consists of joint positions, velocities, torques, base orientation, and angular velocity from the IMU (Eq. 7). A history of *H* consecutive observations forms an observation window *c* = {*o*<sub>*t*</sub>..., *o*<sub>*t+H-1*</sub>}. This sequence-to-sequence formulation leverages temporal correlations.
- **Hypotheses for Observability:** The approach is built on two hypotheses:
 1. If a humanoid remains stable under external contacts, the contact information is weakly observable to the controller, which exploits it in a closed loop to compensate for disturbances.
 2. Greater robustness to external contacts makes contact cues more readily observable to the controller by yielding a more consistent proprioceptive response, thus reducing uncertainty of the latent contact variable.
- **Contact-Resilient Controller Training:** To align proprioceptive signals and make contact cues clearer, a contact-resilient controller is trained using domain randomization and curriculum learning. This ensures that contacts leave strong, informative temporal signatures in the proprioceptive stream, which are then easier for the estimator to decode.
- **Unified Proprioceptive Representation:** To achieve task-agnosticism, a standardized input is defined (Eq. 9), using only proprioceptive signals common across controllers. This involves:
 - Omitting explicit high-level commands, as closed-loop proprioception implicitly reflects them.
 - Using normalized torques as a task-consistent surrogate for actions (Eq. 10), accounting for controller-dependent stiffness and damping.
 - Fixing a single set of normalization weights across tasks for raw signal scales.

**C. CFM-Based Contact Inference**
- **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM):** CFM is employed as a generative inference framework. It learns a time-dependent velocity field to transport a noisy sample towards a target conditional data distribution. This allows the model to capture the multi-modal nature of contact events and address the non-identifiable mapping from proprioception to contacts.
- **Iterative Refinement:** A contact chunk *x* is initialized with Gaussian noise and iteratively refined using the learned velocity field *v*<sub>*θ*</sub>(*x*<sub>*k*</sub>, *t*<sub>*k*</sub>; *c*) conditioned on the proprioceptive observation *c* (Eq. 8). This process is interpreted as an iterative solver that updates the contact estimate to satisfy physical constraints implied by the proprioceptive history.
- **Network Architecture:** A shared-backbone, dual-head design is used. A single CFM backbone encodes the conditioning context and shared contact semantics, while two lightweight attention heads separately decode per-link contact probabilities (mask) and per-link wrenches. This structure promotes feature reuse, improves generalization, and encourages coherent mask-wrench outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SixthSense framework was evaluated using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in both simulation and real-world environments.

**A. Results on the Simulation Contact Dataset**
- **Data Collection:** Datasets were created across three representative behaviors: static balancing, locomotion (RL policy), and whole-body motion tracking (TWIST2 policy). Contacts were injected as impulsive interactions (30-100 N, 0.1-0.4 s duration) causing state deviations. A large dataset (over 1,000,000 rollouts) was compiled.
- **Metrics:** Performance was assessed across four dimensions: (i) whether non-foot contact occurs, (ii) where it happens, (iii) when it happens, and (iv) what wrench is applied. Both strict and tolerant success rates were reported.
- **Contact Awareness (Table I):**
 - **Detection Rate (whether):** High (84.2% standing, 85.5% walking, 78.1% tracking).
 - **False Alarm Rate:** Low (0.4% standing, 1.9% walking, 15.2% tracking), increasing with task complexity.
 - **Location (where):** Target link accuracy varied (61.7% standing, 58.0% walking, 36.9% tracking), but tolerant accuracy (±1 link) was higher (76.4% standing, 72.8% walking, 70.7% tracking).
 - **Timing (when):** Target timestamp accuracy was variable (77.3% standing, 33.6% walking, 75.9% tracking), but tolerant accuracy (±0.1s) was consistently high (98.1% standing, 85.5% walking, 93.3% tracking).
- **Contact Estimation Errors (Table II):** Mean errors for location, timing, force magnitude, force direction, torque magnitude, and torque direction were quantified. The errors were generally low (e.g., force magnitude errors ~1.7-2.1 N).
- **General Performance:** While performance decreased with increased task complexity (e.g., motion tracking being harder), the framework maintained performance levels comparable to low-cost force-torque sensors.

**B. Cross-Task Generalization**
- **Unified Representation:** The use of a unified proprioceptive input (Equation 9) allowed for a task-agnostic estimator.
- **Comparison (Fig. 6):** A task-agnostic estimator (trained on data from all tasks) was compared against task-specific estimators and a single-task baseline. The task-agnostic model showed performance degradation compared to task-specific models (especially for tracking) primarily due to the removal of controller command/reference information, which provides disambiguating context. However, it outperformed the single-task baseline, indicating the benefit of multi-task training and increased data diversity.

**C. Zero-Shot Generalization to Multi-Contact Estimation**
- **Capability:** The estimator, trained *solely* on single-contact data, demonstrated the ability to generalize zero-shot to scenarios with simultaneous contacts at multiple locations (Fig. 7, 8). This is attributed to CFM's ability to model non-uniform distributions and learn transferable contact signatures.
- **Comparison with MLP Baseline (Table III):** CFM significantly outperformed an MLP baseline on multi-contact detection metrics. While the MLP achieved comparable single-contact accuracy, its deterministic mapping failed under multi-contact ambiguity, whereas CFM's flow-based sampling maintained diverse hypotheses.

**D. Comparison with Model-Based Approaches**
- **GMO and CPF:** SixthSense was compared against Generalized Momentum Observer (GMO) and Contact Particle Filter (CPF).
- **Noise Sensitivity (Fig. 9):** SixthSense maintained localization accuracy with increasing Gaussian noise, while GMO and CPF degraded significantly.
- **Multi-Contact Disambiguation (Table IV):** SixthSense showed higher detection rates and lower false alarm rates than CPF for multi-contact scenarios, even though it was slower. Its performance degraded more gracefully with increasing contact points, indicating better scalability. GMO was not applicable in these multi-contact scenarios.
- **Challenging Cases (Fig. 10):** SixthSense demonstrated better matching to ground truth in non-identifiable, rapid motion, and ill-conditioned cases due to its leveraging of pre/post-contact context, learning implicit dynamics, and capturing complex posterior distributions via CFM.

**E. Ablation Study on Controllers with Different Robustness Levels**
- **Hypothesis Validation (Table V):** Empirical evidence supported the hypotheses that more robust controllers make contact information easier to infer. Controllers with higher success rates, lower tracking errors (ITAE), lower constraint violations (VioMag), and higher recovery rates (RVR) resulted in improved contact estimation. Stronger robustness led to minor changes in detection rate and timing but marked improvements in false-alarm rates, location accuracy, and force estimation.

**F. Real-World Validation and Sim-to-Real Transfer**
- **Robustness:** Sim-to-real transfer was enhanced by domain randomization during data collection (varying link properties, joint parameters, ground friction) and proprioceptive input augmentation with noise during training.
- **Real-time Performance:** The estimator, with ~100M parameters and 10 CFM refinement steps, achieved an inference time of 0.5 s per forward pass, demonstrating plug-and-play and real-time capability.
- **Visualization (Fig. 11, 12):** Real-time contact estimates captured applied forces on target links, with correlated responses on neighboring links reflecting kinematic connectivity.

**G. Applications: Physical Human–Robot Interaction (pHRI)**
- **Safety (Case 4, Fig. 13):** Improved whole-body contact perception enabled earlier identification of hazardous situations.
- **Contact as Command (Case 1, Fig. 13):** Reliably estimated contacts could serve as an intuitive command channel (e.g., pulling the robot's arm to signal standing up).
- **Feedback for Planning (All cases, Fig. 13):** The per-step contact mask and wrench field provided structured, high-dimensional feedback for high-level planners, enabling environment-aware decision-making in pHRI through state-machine triggering.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SixthSense represents a contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by introducing a task-agnostic, proprioception-only approach for whole-body external wrench estimation. By reframing contact estimation as a spatiotemporal sequence inference problem and utilizing conditional flow matching (CFM), the work moves beyond the limitations of traditional model-based methods and implicitly contact-aware controllers.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

- **Enhanced Humanoid Capabilities:** It provides humanoids with a "sixth sense" of reliable contact perception, which is currently a fundamental missing capability for robust physical interaction. This can enable robots to understand *when*, *where*, and *how* external forces are applied to their bodies, moving them beyond predefined, contact-free operations.
- **Task-Agnostic and Plug-and-Play Module:** The design allows SixthSense to be readily integrated into existing controllers as a perception module, independent of the specific control policy or task. This promotes reusability and generalizability across diverse behaviors like balancing, walking, and motion tracking, without requiring additional external sensors.
- **Robustness to Uncertainty:** By modeling external wrenches as a sparse, time-varying contact event distribution, rather than point estimates, the framework inherently handles ambiguities and uncertainties arising from floating-base dynamics, indeterminate contact locations, and sensor noise, which are challenges for analytical methods. The zero-shot generalization to multi-contact scenarios further highlights this robustness.
- **Foundation for Advanced Interactions:** The explicit, reusable whole-body wrench estimates can serve as a robust perception foundation for a range of downstream applications, including:
 - **Collision Detection:** Earlier and more accurate identification of unexpected contacts.
 - **Physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI):** Enabling safer interactions, allowing contact to be used as an intuitive command channel, and providing structured feedback for higher-level planning.
 - **Force-Feedback Teleoperation:** Providing rich haptic feedback to human operators.
 - **Manipulation in Contact-Rich Environments:** Laying groundwork for more dexterous and force-aware manipulation tasks.
- **Leveraging Data-Driven Generative Models:** The work demonstrates the effectiveness of conditional flow matching in addressing complex, multi-modal inference problems in robotics, especially where direct observation is challenging and underlying dynamics are intricate.

While the work acknowledges limitations such as the scale of real-robot data collection, reliance on proprioception-only input, and spatial resolution of the robot surface discretization, it provides a foundational step towards equipping next-generation humanoid robots with advanced physical interaction intelligence.
