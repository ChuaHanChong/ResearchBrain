# VideoChat-M1: Collaborative Policy Planning for Video Understanding via Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoChat-M1 introduces a multi-agent framework that uses Collaborative Policy Planning (CPP) and Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) to dynamically generate and refine tool-use strategies for video understanding. The system achieved state-of-the-art performance across eight challenging benchmarks, surpassing leading closed-source models while significantly improving parameter and inference efficiency.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with comprehending videos possessing long temporal contexts or complex spatial structures due to the sheer volume of frames.
- Existing agent-based video understanding frameworks rely on static and pre-defined tool invocation policies, which restricts adaptability and the discovery of diverse clues.
- Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) research has primarily focused on unimodal text domains, often struggling with co-training heterogeneous agents for multimodal tasks like video analysis.

## Method

- **VideoChat-M1** proposes a ==Collaborative Policy Planning== (CPP) paradigm where multiple policy agents dynamically generate, execute, and refine their tool-use policies through inter-agent communication and a shared memory buffer.
- It integrates Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) to jointly optimize the agent team, guided by a hybrid reward system that includes result accuracy, format correctness, and a dedicated collaboration reward.
- The framework provides agents with a comprehensive toolkit for information extraction, spanning coarse-grained retrieval (e.g., global sampling, video retrieval) to fine-grained perceptual analysis (e.g., spatial tool, temporal grounding tool).

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across eight benchmarks in long video QA, video reasoning, spatial intelligence, and temporal grounding, outperforming Gemini 2.5 Pro by **3.6%** and GPT-4o by **15.6%** on LongVideoBench.
- Demonstrated superior parameter efficiency, with a **37B** agent group achieving comparable or better performance than models up to **235B** parameters (e.g., Qwen3-VL-**235B**) on several benchmarks.
- Significantly improved efficiency by processing an average of 69.9 frames per video (12.3-**18.2%** of baselines) and reducing average inference time to 19.8 seconds (8.7-**21.9%** of baselines).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic and learnable policy planning with inter-agent communication (CPP) is crucial for overcoming the limitations of static tool invocation, enabling adaptive strategies and diverse clue discovery in complex videos.
> - Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) with a hybrid reward system effectively optimizes agent collaboration, demonstrating superior performance compared to untrained foundation LLM teams for video understanding.
> - Architectural diversity among agents and the application of agent dropout during MARL training are key for robust learning and developing flexible communication strategies, mitigating co-adaptation.
