# VideoChat-M1: Collaborative Policy Planning for Video Understanding via Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoChat-M1 introduces a multi-agent framework that uses Collaborative Policy Planning (CPP) and Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) to dynamically generate and refine tool-use strategies for video understanding. The system achieved state-of-the-art performance across eight challenging benchmarks, surpassing leading closed-source models while significantly improving parameter and inference efficiency.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with comprehending videos possessing long temporal contexts or complex spatial structures due to the sheer volume of frames.
- Existing agent-based video understanding frameworks rely on static and pre-defined tool invocation policies, which restricts adaptability and the discovery of diverse clues.
- Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) research has primarily focused on unimodal text domains, often struggling with co-training heterogeneous agents for multimodal tasks like video analysis.

## Method

- **VideoChat-M1** proposes a ==Collaborative Policy Planning== (CPP) paradigm where multiple policy agents dynamically generate, execute, and refine their tool-use policies through inter-agent communication and a shared memory buffer.
- It integrates Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) to jointly optimize the agent team, guided by a hybrid reward system that includes result accuracy, format correctness, and a dedicated collaboration reward.
- The framework provides agents with a comprehensive toolkit for information extraction, spanning coarse-grained retrieval (e.g., global sampling, video retrieval) to fine-grained perceptual analysis (e.g., spatial tool, temporal grounding tool).

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across eight benchmarks in long video QA, video reasoning, spatial intelligence, and temporal grounding, outperforming Gemini 2.5 Pro by **3.6%** and GPT-4o by **15.6%** on LongVideoBench.
- Demonstrated superior parameter efficiency, with a **37B** agent group achieving comparable or better performance than models up to **235B** parameters (e.g., Qwen3-VL-**235B**) on several benchmarks.
- Significantly improved efficiency by processing an average of 69.9 frames per video (12.3-**18.2%** of baselines) and reducing average inference time to 19.8 seconds (8.7-**21.9%** of baselines).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic and learnable policy planning with inter-agent communication (CPP) is crucial for overcoming the limitations of static tool invocation, enabling adaptive strategies and diverse clue discovery in complex videos.
> - Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) with a hybrid reward system effectively optimizes agent collaboration, demonstrating superior performance compared to untrained foundation LLM teams for video understanding.
> - Architectural diversity among agents and the application of agent dropout during MARL training are key for robust learning and developing flexible communication strategies, mitigating co-adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video understanding is a critical area in computer vision, with applications ranging from content analysis to intelligent surveillance. Recent advancements have been significantly influenced by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models have demonstrated capabilities in processing video content, particularly short clips. However, a notable challenge for most MLLMs lies in effectively comprehending videos that possess long temporal contexts or complex spatial structures. The sheer volume of frames in such videos often overwhelms conventional MLLM architectures.

To address this limitation, agent-based frameworks have emerged as a promising approach. These frameworks typically do not feed raw video frames directly into MLLMs. Instead, they enhance video understanding by invoking various specialized tools to extract pertinent clues from the video. These tools can operate in an off-the-shelf manner or through iterative processes. A common limitation observed in many existing agent-based frameworks is their reliance on static and pre-defined tool invocation policies. Such rigid policies restrict the system's ability to adapt to diverse video content and queries, thus limiting the discovery of varied clues essential for robust perception and complex reasoning.

Concurrently, the field of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has seen growth, largely driven by advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs). MARL systems typically fall into two categories: training-free systems, which depend on engineered logic and fixed roles, and RL-based paradigms, which train collaborative policies to optimize agent behaviors or interaction architectures. However, much of the sophisticated MARL research has primarily focused on unimodal text domains. These methods often struggle with co-training heterogeneous agents and have not fully addressed the temporal and perceptual challenges inherent in multimodal tasks like long-form video understanding.

VideoChat-M1 positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the inflexibility of static tool invocation policies in agent-based video understanding and to extend MARL capabilities to the complex, multimodal domain of video analysis. It aims to develop a system where multiple agents can dynamically generate, adapt, and refine their tool-use strategies through a collaborative, learning-based approach, thereby improving performance on challenging long and complex video understanding tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind VideoChat-M1 is to address the limitations of existing multi-agent video understanding frameworks, which primarily adopt static and non-learnable tool invocation mechanisms. These rigid mechanisms constrain the system's ability to discover diverse and essential clues needed for robust perception and complex reasoning, especially in videos characterized by long temporal contexts or intricate spatial structures.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Dynamic Collaborative Policy Planning (CPP) Paradigm:** To move beyond fixed tool invocation policies, the primary objective is to introduce a paradigm where multiple agents can autonomously generate and adaptively update their policies for enhanced video understanding. This paradigm is intended to allow agents to refine their tool-use strategies dynamically based on intermediate insights and contextual information.
2. **Enable Policy Communication and Refinement:** A specific objective within the CPP paradigm is to facilitate interaction among agents. This communication allows agents to exchange intermediate results and contextual insights, synthesize information from peers, and consequently revise their original policies into more optimal ones, fostering a collaborative refinement process.
3. **Integrate Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):** To ensure the robustness and effectiveness of the collaborative policy planning, a key objective is to equip the CPP paradigm with a MARL method. This involves jointly optimizing policy agents to improve performance, guided by a comprehensive reward system. This represents an endeavor to introduce the first policy learning framework supporting joint RL training among multiple agents for video understanding.
4. **Design a Hybrid Reward System for MARL:** A specific objective within the MARL framework is to define a reward system that effectively incentivizes both correct final answers and efficient, coherent intermediate collaborative processes. This includes leveraging an LLM as a reward model to evaluate the quality of intermediate collaboration.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance across Diverse Video Understanding Tasks:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that the proposed VideoChat-M1 framework, with its collaborative and learning-based approach, can achieve high performance across a range of challenging benchmarks and tasks, including long video question answering, video reasoning, spatial intelligence, and temporal grounding, surpassing existing closed-source and open-source models.
6. **Improve Efficiency in Video Understanding:** Beyond performance, the research aims to achieve this with superior parameter efficiency compared to substantially larger models and optimized inference time by minimizing the number of processed frames.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoChat-M1 proposes a Multi-agent system for video understanding, built upon a novel **Collaborative Policy Planning (CPP)** paradigm enhanced by **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)**.

#### 3.1. Collaborative Policy Planning Pipeline (CPP)

The CPP paradigm is designed to overcome the limitations of static tool invocation policies. It involves a set of policy agents G = {G_i}, a set of video perception tools T = {T_j}, and a shared memory buffer M = {M_i}. The memory buffer records historical information and supports policy updates through inter-agent communication. The workflow is iterative and comprises three core phases:

1. **Policy Generation:** Each agent G_i initially generates a unique policy P_i, which is an explicit sequence of tool invocations from toolkit T designed to address the user query Q. This process aims to decompose the complex video understanding task into manageable sub-tasks.
2. **Policy Execution:** Following generation, each agent G_i executes its policy P_i. At each step 'n' of the policy, the agent uses the specified tool P_i,n to analyze the input video V, generating an intermediate answer A_i,n based on the previous step's answer A_i,n-1. This process continues until a final answer for the query is derived.
3. **Policy Communication:** Integrated with policy execution, this stage is crucial for dynamic policy updates. After each execution step, the entire agent team G shares its intermediate results (A) and reasoning states via the shared memory buffer M. Each agent G_i then references its initial policy and the collective memory (M) to determine whether to update its policy P_i into P'_i for subsequent steps. If the current policy is deemed optimal, it continues; otherwise, it revises the policy. This iterative communication and refinement allow agents to leverage team experience and adapt their tool invocation strategies.

Once all tools are executed or the maximum number of iterations is reached, each agent summarizes its results. For multiple-choice questions, the final answer is determined by majority voting among agents. For open-ended or temporal grounding queries, a designated, best-performing agent (Qwen3-8B in this work) consolidates the answers.

#### 3.2. Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)

The MARL framework guides and optimizes the team of policy agents, enhancing VideoChat-M1's adaptability and collaborative capabilities. It consists of two main stages:

1. **Policy Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This warm-up stage equips each agent with foundational abilities for policy plan generation. A high-quality policy dataset is constructed from open-source video datasets. Since these datasets lack pre-existing policy plans, the CPP paradigm itself, utilizing high-performance external models (GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1), is used to automatically annotate policy plans. Only plans that yield correct answers and do not require modification during execution are selected. Each agent is then fine-tuned using cross-entropy loss to maximize the likelihood of generating these ground-truth plans, establishing a basis for structured policy generation.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** This stage further optimizes the agent team for effective collaboration using a hybrid reward system R = R_res + R_format + R_col:
 - **Result Reward (R_res):** A positive reward is assigned for correct final answers to the user query, and a negative penalty for incorrect ones.
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** This reward incentivizes syntactically correct actions, such as parsable plans and valid tool calls, by granting positive rewards and imposing penalties for errors.
 - **Collaboration Reward (R_col):** To encourage effective intermediate collaboration, an external LLM (GPT-4o) acts as a reward model. It evaluates the quality of each agent's planning trajectory, assessing feasibility, tool call appropriateness, and step management soundness. This reward is binary (1 for coherent trajectories, 0 otherwise), with an additional penalty for trajectories exceeding five tool calls to promote conciseness. As agents' memories are influenced by team communication, this reward encourages group cooperation.

The agent team is optimized using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. Each agent generates K policy plans, producing K candidate outputs. The advantage of each output is computed by standardizing its reward against the statistics of all outputs in the group. The GRPO objective function is maximized, balancing a reward-seeking term with a KL-divergence penalty. This penalty regularizes the optimized policy, keeping it close to a reference policy (π_ref) to ensure training stability and prevent large, destabilizing updates. Agent dropout is applied during training, where a random communication topology is sampled, encouraging agents to develop robust and flexible communication strategies and mitigating co-adaptation.

#### 3.3. Tool Set

The framework provides agents access to a comprehensive toolkit for information extraction, spanning coarse-grained retrieval to fine-grained perceptual analysis. Key tools include:
- **Global Sampling:** Uniformly samples frames for holistic understanding.
- **Video Retrieval:** Uses ASP-CLIP to identify and retrieve semantically relevant video clips.
- **Time Stamp Retrieval:** Extracts video segments around a specified timestamp.
- **Image Retrieval:** Matches textual descriptions to individual frames using CLIP.
- **Rough/Fine Browser:** Processes sparse or dense sets of frames with MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) for general or detailed analysis.
- **Spatial Tool:** Employs models like InternVL-3.5-8B for spatial reasoning queries.
- **Grounding Tool:** Utilizes models like Eagle2.5-7B for temporal grounding tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoChat-M1 demonstrates strong performance across multiple challenging video understanding benchmarks, often achieving state-of-the-art results.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison with State-of-the-Art Models

- **Overall SOTA:** The model achieves leading performance across eight benchmarks spanning long video QA, video reasoning, spatial intelligence, and temporal grounding tasks, particularly among models under the 80B parameter scale.
- **Long Video QA:** On the LongVideoBench dataset, VideoChat-M1 outperforms Gemini 2.5 Pro by 3.6% and GPT-4o by 15.6%.
- **Video Reasoning:** For Video-Holmes and MMR-V benchmarks, the method shows gains of 14.8% and 14.3%, respectively. On VideoMMMU, the 37B agent group yields results comparable to the Qwen3-VL-235B model, using approximately 15% of its parameters.
- **Spatial Intelligence:** On the VSIBench spatial intelligence task, VideoChat-M1 achieves a 2.4% improvement over Gemini 1.5 Pro.
- **Temporal Grounding:** For the Charades-STA dataset, it shows a 1.8% improvement over Seed 1.5VL.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** Despite having a relatively smaller parameter count (37B for the agent group), VideoChat-M1 performs comparably to or exceeds much larger models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B, Gemini 2.5 Pro) on several benchmarks including Video-MME and MLVU.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Comparison

- VideoChat-M1 processes an average of 69.9 frames per video, which is between 12.3% and 18.2% of the frames used by other models.
- Its average inference time is 19.8 seconds, approximately 8.7% to 21.9% of the baselines. This indicates a favorable efficiency-performance trade-off, achieving high scores on LongVideoBench (82.3%) and VideoMME (83.2%) with reduced computational cost.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effects of the Number of Homogeneous Agents:** Performance generally improved as the number of agents increased from one to four, using the Qwen3-8B architecture. Beyond four agents, performance gains were negligible, indicating saturation.
- **Effects of Agent Group Composition and Scale:** Increasing the total number of agents and using agents with larger parameter capacities generally led to improved performance.
- **Impact of Architectural Diversity:** Heterogeneous agent groups (composed of distinct architectures) demonstrated greater collaborative gains than homogeneous groups, even with a smaller total parameter count. Structural redundancy among agents was observed to reduce discussion diversity.
- **Comparison with Foundation LLM Agent Groups:** VideoChat-M1, trained with MARL, significantly outperformed untrained closed-source foundation LLM teams (e.g., GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1) following the same CPP protocol, with margins of at least 4.3 to 6.4 points on tested benchmarks. This suggests that fine-tuning injects task-specific coordination patterns not discoverable through zero-shot reasoning alone.
- **Ablation Study on Key Components of MARL:** All reward components (result, format, and collaboration rewards) and agent dropout contribute positively to performance. Agent dropout was identified as the most critical regularizer, implying the importance of dynamic communication topologies for robust learning.
- **Ablation on SFT and RFT (MARL):** Both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT, i.e., MARL) contribute additively to the model's performance. SFT establishes initial reliable planning priors, while RFT refines inter-agent coordination, both being necessary for maximum performance.
- **Ablation on Fine-tuning Strategies:** Full-parameter fine-tuning yielded slightly better performance than LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), but the marginal difference suggests that collaborative policy knowledge can be effectively implanted with a small subset of tuned parameters, supporting lightweight deployment options.
- **Ablation on Discussion Mechanisms:** Among different methods for aggregating individual agent conclusions, majority voting achieved the highest performance, outperforming strategies based on best-score selection or designated agent authority. This suggests that leveraging the diversity of independent agent planning through consensus is effective.
- **Tool Reliance Ablation (Supplemental Material):** An ablation study on VSIBench showed that even when replacing a specialized 'Spatial Tool' (InternVL) with a general-purpose MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), VideoChat-M1 maintained strong performance, outperforming much larger baselines. This indicates that the effectiveness primarily stems from the CPP framework and MARL-driven planning, rather than sole reliance on specific state-of-the-art tools.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoChat-M1 introduces a novel approach to video understanding by addressing key limitations of existing methods. Its primary significance lies in being the first multi-agent framework for video understanding that replaces conventional static, fixed tool invocation policies with a dynamic, learnable **Collaborative Policy Planning (CPP)** paradigm. This shift allows agents to autonomously generate, adapt, and refine their tool-use strategies through active communication and collaboration, moving beyond pre-defined rules.

The integration of a pioneering **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)** method is another significant contribution. By jointly optimizing the agent group with a hybrid reward system that considers both final answer accuracy and the quality of intermediate collaborative processes, VideoChat-M1 offers a robust and adaptive learning framework. This marks a new direction for policy learning in complex, multimodal video understanding tasks.

The empirical results demonstrate that VideoChat-M1 achieves state-of-the-art performance across eight challenging benchmarks in long-form video QA, video reasoning, spatial intelligence, and temporal grounding. Its ability to outperform leading closed-source models such as GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro, while exhibiting superior parameter efficiency, indicates a significant advancement in the field. The model's efficiency in terms of frames processed and inference time further highlights its practical potential.

The findings from ablation studies, particularly regarding the benefits of architectural diversity in agent groups and the critical role of MARL components like agent dropout, provide insights into effective multi-agent system design for complex tasks. The success of majority voting as a discussion mechanism also suggests practical strategies for aggregating diverse agent outputs.

The potential impact of VideoChat-M1 is substantial. By enabling more adaptive and intelligent video understanding, it could contribute to:
- **Enhanced AI Capabilities:** Developing more sophisticated AI agents capable of complex reasoning over long and intricate visual data, bridging the gap between current MLLM capabilities and real-world video analysis requirements.
- **Broader Applications:** Improving performance in various downstream applications, including automated video content analysis, intelligent surveillance, educational tools, and advanced human-computer interaction scenarios that rely on deep video comprehension.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Providing a strong foundation and a novel framework for future research in multi-agent systems, collaborative AI, and reinforcement learning applied to multimodal understanding, especially in dynamic, tool-augmented environments.
