# RWKV-CLIP: A Robust Vision-Language Representation Learner

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Sydney and DeepGlint developed RWKV-CLIP, a vision-language representation model that employs a Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV) architecture and a diverse description generation framework to enhance data quality. This model achieved an average linear probe performance improvement of up to 11.1% and increased Recall@1 by up to 8.7% for image-to-text retrieval compared to baseline models.

## Problem

- Web-scale image-text datasets, crucial for models like CLIP, suffer from inherent noise, abstract descriptions, and semantic discrepancies, which existing LLM-based augmentation methods struggle to fully resolve due to hallucinations and coarse-grained object recognition.
- Transformer models, while effective, face limitations due to their quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length, creating a bottleneck for processing high-resolution images or extended text sequences.
- The lack of efficient architectural alternatives that maintain performance while reducing computational overhead, especially during inference, restricts the scalability and deployment of large vision-language models.

## Method

- A ==diverse description generation framework== that synthesizes information from noisy web texts, synthetic captions (OFA_base), and fine-grained image tags (RAM++) via a fine-tuned LLaMA3 model, aiming to reduce hallucinations and semantically enrich descriptions.
- **RWKV-CLIP**, the first vision-language representation learning model to integrate the ==Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV) architecture== for both its image and text encoders, combining parallel training capabilities with efficient RNN-like inference.
- The model is trained using a ==contrastive loss== function, leveraging the augmented datasets, with careful control of parameters and FLOPs to ensure fair comparison with existing Transformer-based baselines.

## Results

- RWKV-CLIP-B/32 demonstrated an average performance improvement of **1.9% to 11.1%** in linear probing across **10** downstream datasets compared to baselines like ALIP-ViT-B/32 on YFCC15M.
- Achieved new state-of-the-art results in zero-shot image-text retrieval, with Recall@1 gains of **5.5%** (Image-to-Text) and **8.7%** (Text-to-Image) on Flickr30K, and **3.5%** (I2T) and **4.7%** (T2I) on MSCOCO, over ALIP.
- Showcased enhanced zero-shot classification performance with an average improvement of **2.6% to 14.4%** across **11** datasets and consistently outperformed baselines in zero-shot robustness evaluations with an average **2.0%** gain across various robustness datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating fine-grained object detection tags as semantic constraints during LLM-based text augmentation significantly reduces hallucinations and improves description accuracy, leading to more robust pre-training data.
> - The RWKV architecture can be effectively applied to both vision and language modalities within a dual-encoder framework, offering a more efficient and robust alternative to Transformers for large-scale vision-language representation learning.
> - Consistent application of the RWKV architecture across both modalities enhances cross-modal alignment and leads to more discriminative feature embeddings compared to Transformer-based or hybrid architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of vision-language representation learning has experienced significant advancements, largely driven by Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP). CLIP leverages large-scale, web-scraped image-text pairs to train distinct unimodal encoders for images and text, using a contrastive loss function. This approach has demonstrated strong transferability and zero-shot recognition capabilities across various vision-language tasks.

The broader research landscape for CLIP-like models is characterized by two main areas of ongoing development: **data quality enhancement** and **model architectural innovation**.

Regarding data, the proliferation of large-scale datasets such as LAION400M and LAION5B, curated from the internet, has been crucial for pre-training. However, a persistent challenge is the inherent noise, abstract text representations, and semantic discrepancies present in web-crawled data, even after filtering. Previous efforts like DataComp have explored filtering strategies, and methods like LaCLIP and CapsFusion have utilized Large Language Models (LLMs) for text augmentation. These LLM-based approaches aim to rewrite or merge captions to improve quality, but face limitations such as LLM hallucination and the coarse-grained nature of object categories identifiable by standard captioning models.

Architecturally, Transformer models have become dominant in large-scale representation learning across modalities, including vision, language, and speech. Despite their success, Transformers suffer from quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length, which poses challenges for processing high-resolution images or extended text sequences. This limitation has spurred research into more efficient architectures. The Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV) model emerged as an alternative, designed for linear scaling while retaining parallel training capabilities and robust scalability similar to Transformers, but with efficient RNN-like inference. RWKV has been successfully applied in natural language processing and more recently adapted to vision tasks (Vision-RWKV, PointRWKV, Diffusion-RWKV), showing promise in efficiency and performance.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these two areas. It aims to address the data quality issues in large-scale image-text datasets through a novel text augmentation framework utilizing LLMs and fine-grained image tags. Concurrently, it explores an alternative to the Transformer architecture by proposing the first RWKV-driven model for vision-language representation learning, seeking to combine the parallel training benefits of Transformers with the inference efficiency of RNNs within the CLIP paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research presented in this paper is motivated by two primary challenges in current vision-language representation learning, leading to a set of specific objectives.

**Motivation 1: Addressing Data Quality Issues in Web-Scale Image-Text Datasets**
Large-scale image-text datasets, vital for pre-training models like CLIP, are typically collected from the internet. While abundant, these datasets inherently contain significant noise, including mismatched image-text pairs, abstract or grammatically flawed text descriptions, and semantic misalignments between images and their associated captions. Even filtering mechanisms, such as those used for LAION datasets, do not entirely eliminate these issues. Existing text augmentation methods that leverage LLMs, such as LaCLIP and CapsFusion, have shown potential but are constrained by several factors:
- **LLM Hallucinations:** LLMs can generate plausible but factually incorrect information, especially when guided by limited context.
- **Coarse-grained Object Recognition:** Conventional caption generation models (e.g., OFA, BLIP) are often limited by their training data and tend to identify only coarse-grained object categories, thus failing to capture detailed semantic information present in images.
The motivation here is to develop a more robust data augmentation strategy that can mitigate noise, reduce hallucinations, and enrich semantic content in image-text pairs.

**Motivation 2: Overcoming Architectural Limitations of Transformers in Vision-Language Models**
Transformer models, while highly effective, suffer from quadratic computational complexity with respect to the input sequence length. This characteristic becomes a bottleneck when dealing with high-resolution images or very long text sequences, limiting their applicability and scalability. The need for more efficient architectures that can maintain performance while reducing computational overhead, particularly during inference, is a significant motivation. The Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV) architecture offers a potential solution by providing linear scaling properties for efficiency while preserving key advantages like parallel training, typically associated with Transformers. The successful adaptation of RWKV to other domains (NLP, vision tasks) suggests its promise for multimodal vision-language learning.

**Key Objectives:**
Based on these motivations, the authors establish the following objectives:
1. **Develop a Diverse Description Generation Framework:** To create more accurate and semantically enriched descriptions by integrating information from noisy web-based texts, synthetically generated captions, and fine-grained object detection tags, leveraging the capabilities of LLMs to synthesize and refine this diverse information.
2. **Propose RWKV-CLIP: An RWKV-driven Vision-Language Model:** To introduce the first vision-language representation learning model that incorporates the RWKV architecture, aiming to combine the effective parallel training capabilities of Transformers with the efficient inference characteristics of RNNs.
3. **Demonstrate Robustness and Effectiveness:** To conduct extensive experiments across various model scales and pre-training datasets to establish RWKV-CLIP as a robust and efficient vision-language representation learner, evaluating its performance on downstream tasks such as linear probing, zero-shot classification, and zero-shot image-text retrieval.
4. **Facilitate Future Research:** To release the code and pre-trained models to support further exploration and development in vision-language representation learning, particularly concerning RWKV-like architectures and advanced data augmentation techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research encompasses two main components: a novel diverse description generation framework and the proposed RWKV-CLIP model architecture.

#### 3.1. Diverse Description Generation Framework

This framework aims to enhance the quality of image-text data by generating semantically enriched and accurate descriptions. The process involves three main sources of textual information and a refinement stage:
1. **Synthetic Caption Generation:** To mitigate the effects of mismatched image-text pairs, the OFA\_base model (as used in ALIP) is initially employed to generate a synthetic caption for each image. These captions aim for high semantic alignment with the image content but are often simplistic and limited to coarse-grained object categories due to OFA's training data.
2. **Open-Set Image Tagging:** To capture more detailed and finer-grained semantic information from images, the open-set image tagging model RAM++ is utilized. This model extracts specific object detection tags from each image, providing granular details beyond what typical captioning models offer.
3. **LLM-based Information Synthesis and Refinement:**
 - **Instruction Dataset Creation:** An instruction dataset is constructed using interactions with ChatGPT-35-turbo. For this, 70,000 image-text pairs are selected from the YFCC15M dataset, specifically those with more than 10 detection tags. The raw text, synthetic caption, and detection tags for these pairs are fed into ChatGPT with a detailed prompt (provided in the supplementary material) instructing it to merge and refine the information. The prompt emphasizes structuring sentences well, retaining real-world detail from raw captions, avoiding simple concatenation, preventing external information addition, and correcting/simplifying sentences.
 - **Fine-tuning and Inference with LLaMA3:** The instruction dataset generated by ChatGPT is then used to fine-tune the open-source LLaMA3-8B model using LLaMA Factory. The fine-tuned LLaMA3 model is subsequently leveraged for large-scale inference to generate diverse descriptions for the entire dataset, with vLLM accelerating this process. This step addresses the computational cost of using ChatGPT directly for large-scale generation.

This framework produces three types of text for each image: raw text ($T_r$), synthetic caption ($T_s$), and the newly generated diverse description ($T_g$).

#### 3.2. RWKV-CLIP Model Architecture

RWKV-CLIP adopts a dual-tower architecture, similar to the original CLIP, consisting of separate encoders for images and text. The core innovation is the integration of RWKV-driven blocks, combining elements of Transformer-like parallel processing with RNN-like efficient inference.

1. **Input Augmentation:** For text inputs, the model randomly selects one of the three available text types ($T_r$, $T_s$, or $T_g$) for augmentation. The input image is transformed into patches, similar to Vision Transformers.
2. **Encoder Design (Image and Text):** Both the image and text encoders are block-stacked, where each block comprises a spatial mixing module and a channel mixing module.
 - **Spatial Mixing Module:** This module acts as an attention mechanism, performing global attention computation with linear complexity.
 - **Lerp (Linear Interpolation):** Input data (image or text) is shifted and passed through four parallel linear layers to derive multi-head vectors ($G_s^x$, $R_s^x$, $K_s^x$, $V_s^x$). For images, Q-Lerp (quad-directional shift) is used, concatenating shifted versions of the input along different spatial directions. For text, B-Lerp (bi-directional shift) is used, concatenating forward and backward shifted versions. These shifts enhance channel-level feature interaction.
 - **Time-varying Decay ($w_s^x$):** A dynamic decay factor $w_s^x$ is calculated, which varies for each channel based on a mix of current and prior tokens, enhancing flexibility.
 - **Bidirectional WKV (Bi-WKV):** The vectors ($w_s^x$, $R_s^x$, $K_s^x$, $V_s^x$) are used to compute the global attention result $wkv_t$ via a linear complexity bidirectional attention mechanism.
 - **Gate Mechanism:** The output $O_s^x$ is controlled by a SiLU (Sigmoid-weighted Linear Unit) gate function applied to $G_s^x$, multiplied element-wise with the layer-normalized $wkv_t$.
 - **Channel Mixing Module:** Following spatial mixing, this module further processes the features.
 - It obtains $R_c^x$ and $K_c^x$ using Lerp.
 - A linear projection and a gate mechanism (involving SiLU on $R_c^x$ and squaredReLU on $K_c^x$) are applied to produce the final output $O_c^x$.
3. **Loss Function:** After passing through the stacked RWKV-based image encoder ($E_I$) and text encoder ($E_T$), the image embeddings ($\hat{I}$) and text embeddings ($\hat{T}$) are obtained. The model is trained using a contrastive loss function (similar to InfoNCE), which minimizes the distance between corresponding image-text pairs while maximizing distances for non-matching pairs.

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings

- **Pre-training Datasets:** YFCC15M (a subset of YFCC100M filtered by DeCLIP), and subsets of LAION400M (10M and 30M) for scalability evaluation.
- **Hyperparameters:** AdamW optimizer (learning rate 1e-3, weight decay 0.2, β1=0.9, β2=0.98), input image size 224x224, text sequence length 77, initial temperature τ=0.07.
- **Training:** 32 epochs with a batch size of 4096 on 8 NVIDIA A100 (80G) GPUs.
- **Fair Comparison:** Parameters and FLOPs of RWKV-CLIP are meticulously controlled to ensure comparability with baseline models (e.g., CLIP, ALIP).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of RWKV-CLIP across various vision-language tasks and model scales.

#### 4.1. Linear Probe Performance

- **YFCC15M:** RWKV-CLIP-B/32, pre-trained on YFCC15M, achieved an average performance improvement of 1.9% to 11.1% over baseline models across 10 downstream datasets (Table 1). It outperformed ALIP-ViT-B/32 on 8 out of 10 datasets. This improvement is attributed to the enhanced descriptions from the proposed generation framework and the superior representation learning capabilities of the RWKV-driven architecture.
- **LAION Subsets (Scaling):** Experiments on LAION10M and LAION30M confirmed the scalability and robustness of RWKV-CLIP (Figure 5, Table 11). RWKV-CLIP-B/32 showed a 2.6% average improvement over ALIP-ViT-B/32 on LAION10M and a 1.4% improvement on LAION30M. RWKV-CLIP-B/16 surpassed ALIP-ViT-B/16 by 2.1% on LAION10M.

#### 4.2. Zero-shot Image-text Retrieval

- RWKV-CLIP achieved new state-of-the-art results on both Flickr30k and MSCOCO datasets (Table 2).
- On Flickr30K, RWKV-CLIP-B/32 obtained 76.0% Recall@1 for Image-to-Text (I2T) retrieval and 57.6% for Text-to-Image (T2I) retrieval, surpassing ALIP by 5.5% and 8.7%, respectively.
- Similar improvements were observed on MSCOCO, with 3.5% (I2T Recall@1) and 4.7% (T2I Recall@1) gains over ALIP. These results indicate robust representations and enhanced cross-modal alignment.

#### 4.3. Zero-shot Classification

- On 11 downstream datasets, RWKV-CLIP achieved an average performance improvement of 2.6% to 14.4% over baseline models (Table 3).
- It outperformed ALIP in 10 out of 11 datasets, showing notable enhancements on instance discrimination datasets like Food101 and ImageNet. This is attributed to the fine-grained semantic information provided by the diverse descriptions.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Robustness Evaluation

- RWKV-CLIP consistently outperformed ALIP across all evaluated robustness datasets (IN-V2, IN-A, IN-R, IN-Sketch) with an average improvement of 2.0% (Table 4), indicating the RWKV-driven model's robustness as a representation learner.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study: Diverse Description Generation Framework

- **Comparison with CapsFusion:** The proposed diverse description generation framework, which incorporates detection tags, yielded a 0.9% improvement in average linear probe and a 2.1% improvement in average zero-shot classification performance compared to using CapsFusion-generated texts (Table 5). This advantage is attributed to the detection tags providing more semantic constraints to LLMs, thereby reducing hallucinations (Figure 4 illustrates this with examples where CapsFusion hallucinates information while the proposed framework provides more accurate details).
- **Effect of Text Types:** Ablation on different text categories (raw, synthetic, generated diverse description) showed that synthetic captions and generated diverse descriptions led to superior linear probe performance compared to raw texts (Table 6). This is linked to the high incidence of mismatched image-text pairs in raw data. Analysis of cosine similarity and token counts (Figure 6) revealed that synthetic and generated descriptions had higher average similarity and token counts. However, raw texts achieved superior zero-shot classification, potentially due to constraints imposed by prompt templates.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study: Model Architecture

- A comparative ablation study combining RWKV and Transformer architectures (Table 7) revealed that the full RWKV-CLIP (RWKV for both image and text encoders) achieved 3.2% and 2.7% improvements in average linear probe and zero-shot classification, respectively, compared to a pure Transformer-based architecture.
- Combinations of RWKV and Transformer (e.g., RWKV image encoder with Transformer text encoder) showed reduced compatibility, with RWKV_I and Transformer_T achieving a 2.7% linear probe improvement but a 10.8% zero-shot classification decline, suggesting that the RWKV architecture performs more effectively when consistently applied across modalities in this context.

#### 4.7. Feature Embedding Analysis and Visualization

- UMAP visualization of modality gaps (Figure 7) indicated that RWKV-CLIP learned representations with clearer discriminability within the same modality and closer distances in the image-text modality space compared to ALIP, suggesting superior cross-modal alignment.
- Class Activation Maps (CAMs) (Figure 8) demonstrated that RWKV-CLIP more accurately aligned image patches with textual semantic entities.
- Cross-modal cosine similarity matrices (Figure 9) showed clearer diagonals for RWKV-CLIP, confirming greater distinctiveness and improved cross-modal alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of vision-language representation learning by addressing both data quality and architectural efficiency, with several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Data Quality and Augmentation:** The proposed diverse description generation framework provides a method for refining noisy web-scale image-text data. By effectively combining web-based texts, synthetic captions, and fine-grained detection tags through LLMs, the framework generates semantically richer and more accurate descriptions. This approach can lead to more robust pre-training, mitigating the issues of data quality that often plague large-scale multimodal datasets. This framework has broader applicability for improving training data in other multimodal learning tasks.

2. **Architectural Diversification and Efficiency:** The introduction of RWKV-CLIP, the first RWKV-driven vision-language representation learning model, offers a viable alternative to the predominantly Transformer-based architectures. By combining the parallel training effectiveness of Transformers with the inference efficiency of RNNs, RWKV-CLIP presents a path toward more computationally efficient models, particularly crucial for deploying large models in resource-constrained environments or for processing very long sequences. This work validates the potential of RWKV-like architectures beyond traditional NLP and unimodal vision tasks.

3. **Improved Performance and Robustness:** The consistent and significant performance improvements demonstrated across various downstream tasks—including linear probing, zero-shot classification, and zero-shot image-text retrieval—establish RWKV-CLIP as a highly effective model. The improved zero-shot robustness further highlights its generalization capabilities, suggesting that the learned representations are more resilient to domain shifts and variations. These performance gains can translate into more capable vision-language applications.

4. **Foundation for Future Research:** By open-sourcing the code and pre-trained models, the authors facilitate further research into RWKV architectures for multimodal learning. This can encourage the community to explore novel model designs that balance performance with computational efficiency, moving beyond the current Transformer paradigm. It also provides a benchmark for further advancements in data augmentation techniques for vision-language models.

In summary, RWKV-CLIP represents a step forward in robust vision-language representation learning by addressing critical limitations in data quality and model architecture, offering a new direction for developing more efficient and effective multimodal AI systems.
