# InfinityDrive: Breaking Time Limits in Driving World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> InfinityDrive is a driving world model generating consistent, high-resolution (576x1024) videos beyond 1500 frames (2 minutes). It combines efficient spatio-temporal co-modeling, memory injection/retention with a forgetting-curve-inspired adaptive loss, and joint image/text-to-video training, reaching state-of-the-art fidelity, consistency, and scenario diversity.

## Problem

- Existing driving world models operate within short temporal windows (under 30 frames), limited by GPU memory needed for joint high-resolution and long-sequence spatio-temporal modeling.
- Autoregressive rollout methods reset condition images from prior clips, so small prediction errors compound over time and cause significant drift in long sequences.
- Current models produce nearly identical outputs given the same initial frame and noise, showing limited scenario diversity due to lacking text-conditioned generation.

## Method

- ==Efficient Spatio-Temporal Co-Modeling== module with dynamic information density adjustment, trading spatial detail for temporal density via resolution scaling, plus ==skip-ROPE positional embedding== for consistent temporal encoding.
- ==Extended Temporal Scope Training== strategy, a ==curriculum== that progressively grows the training window from 16 to 128 frames to teach long-horizon dependencies.
- ==Memory Injection and Retention Mechanism== paired with a ==Memory Curve Adaptive Loss==, an Ebbinghaus-forgetting-curve-inspired exponential decay weighting that prioritizes near-future memory fidelity while allowing distant-future generative freedom. Together these form **InfinityDrive**.

## Results

- In long-term evaluation (100 frames), InfinityDrive achieves FID **14.92** and FVD **113.91**, outperforming SVD-XT (**42.99**/**275.08**) and Vista (**34.61**/**234.60**).
- Ablations show progressive gains: baseline FID **23.88**/FVD **175.17** drops to **17.51**/**117.31** with extended temporal training, then **14.92**/**113.91** with co-modeling and memory curve loss added.
- Qualitatively, competing methods (SVD-XT, Vista, StreamingT2V) degrade into blur by **80** frames, while InfinityDrive sustains high-fidelity generation up to **1200** frames trained on **1745** hours of data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling spatial and temporal information density by resolution lets a model learn both fine spatial detail and long-range temporal coherence under fixed memory budgets.
> - Borrowing a psychological forgetting-curve prior as a loss-weighting schedule is a generalizable trick for balancing memory fidelity against generative freedom in sequential generation.
> - Explicit memory retention mechanisms, not just longer training windows, are necessary to prevent autoregressive drift; scope extension alone is insufficient for multi-minute consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving remains a grand challenge in AI, primarily due to the infinite complexity of real-world scenarios and the difficulty of acquiring sufficiently diverse, extensive, and safety-critical data, particularly for rare or "corner" cases. **World models** have emerged as a promising paradigm to address this data scarcity by generating synthetic yet realistic driving environments. These models can simulate future states based on observations and actions, enabling autonomous systems to anticipate, plan, and react more effectively.

However, existing driving world models face several critical limitations that InfinityDrive aims to overcome:
- **Limited Temporal Horizon:** Many current models, such as DriveGAN, DriveDreamer, WoVoGen, and Drive-WM, are restricted to generating very short video sequences (e.g., <30 frames, lasting only a few seconds). This short duration is insufficient for long-term planning and anticipation, where complex dependencies and behavioral trends unfold over much longer periods. While models like GAIA-1 attempt longer generation (over 1 minute), they often do so at significantly lower resolutions, sacrificing crucial visual detail. Vista, another high-resolution model, can only predict up to 15 seconds before visual quality degrades rapidly.
- **Poor Spatio-Temporal Resolution:** There's a persistent trade-off: models achieving high spatial resolution often cannot sustain it over long temporal windows due to immense computational and memory demands. Conversely, models capable of longer sequences tend to operate at lower resolutions, hindering their ability to represent the fine-grained details vital for autonomous driving (e.g., road markings, small obstacles, distant vehicles).
- **Autoregressive Error Accumulation:** Most long-duration video generation methods rely on autoregressive prediction, where future frames are predicted based on previous generated frames. Small inaccuracies at each step accumulate over time, leading to significant visual degradation, loss of consistency, and divergence from realistic physics or initial conditions, as seen in methods like SVD-XT and StreamingT2V.
- **Lack of Diversity:** Many models struggle to generate a wide variety of scenarios, often producing outputs that are too similar even with different random noise inputs or failing to introduce novel contextual elements beyond the initial conditions. This limits their utility for robust training and testing of autonomous systems against diverse, out-of-distribution events.

Beyond driving-specific models, the broader field of **long video generation** has seen advancements with diffusion models (e.g., Imagen Video, Emu Video) and autoregressive approaches (e.g., FreeNoise, StreamingT2V, Loong). However, these general video generation methods are often object-centric, lack explicit controllability crucial for driving simulations, and are not specifically designed to capture the intricate dynamics and environmental constraints of driving scenarios.

InfinityDrive positions itself at the forefront of this landscape by directly confronting these multifaceted challenges. It seeks to develop the first driving world model capable of **minute-scale (over 2 minutes) video generation at high resolution (576x1024), while simultaneously ensuring high fidelity, temporal consistency, and diverse scenario outputs**. This ambitious goal directly addresses the most pressing needs for data generation in advanced autonomous driving research and development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of InfinityDrive is to establish a **next-generation driving world model** that transcends the current limitations of existing solutions. The core motivation stems from the critical need of autonomous driving systems for vast quantities of diverse, realistic, and temporally extended data to safely navigate complex, real-world environments, especially out-of-distribution scenarios that are difficult and dangerous to collect in reality.

Specifically, the paper articulates the following key objectives and motivations:

- **Breaking Time Limits:** To extend the temporal horizon of generated driving videos from mere seconds to **minute-scale durations (over 2 minutes or 1500+ frames)**. This is motivated by the need for autonomous vehicles to anticipate future events, plan long-term trajectories, and react proactively with sufficient lead time, which short video clips cannot provide.
- **Achieving High Spatio-Temporal Resolution:** To generate videos at a **high spatial resolution (576x1024)** while maintaining **consistent temporal coherence** over these extended durations. This addresses the problem where previous models either offer low resolution for long videos or sacrifice temporal length for high spatial detail. High resolution is crucial for preserving the fine-grained details of driving environments (e.g., lane markings, traffic signs, distant objects) that are vital for accurate perception and decision-making.
- **Countering Autoregressive Error Accumulation:** To develop mechanisms that prevent the cumulative degradation of video quality and consistency during long-term autoregressive rollouts. This is motivated by the observed phenomenon where small prediction errors in iterative generation amplify over time, leading to unrealistic or incoherent sequences in existing models.
- **Enhancing Scenario Diversity:** To enable the generation of a **wide range of complex and varied driving scenarios**, moving beyond the limited diversity of current models that often produce repetitive or minimally varied outputs. This objective is driven by the need to train and test autonomous systems against an expansive spectrum of conditions, including different weather, lighting, traffic situations, and unexpected events, to improve their robustness and adaptability.
- **Building a Foundational Model:** To serve as a robust foundational model for future advancements in autonomous driving world models, particularly for generating multi-view, controllable, and richly annotated synthetic driving videos. Such capabilities are essential for training perception models and reducing the reliance on expensive and labor-intensive real-world data collection and annotation.

By addressing these intertwined challenges, InfinityDrive aims to create a powerful simulation tool that can significantly accelerate the development and validation of safer and more capable autonomous driving systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InfinityDrive introduces a series of innovative modules and training strategies built upon a **diffusion transformer (DiT) model** to achieve its ambitious objectives. The methodology focuses on three core areas: efficient spatio-temporal co-modeling, mechanisms for long-term consistency, and enhanced scenario diversity.

#### 3.1. Efficient Spatio-Temporal Co-Modeling (STCM)

To tackle the challenge of simultaneously achieving high spatial resolution and long temporal duration, InfinityDrive proposes the STCM module, comprising:

- **Dynamic Information Density Adjustment:**
 - **Adaptive Resolution and Sequence Length:** During training, the model dynamically adjusts the resolution. For a base resolution (H, W), a random scaling factor `α ∈` is chosen, scaling the resolution to `(H × α, W × α)`. Crucially, to manage GPU memory constraints, the permitted sequence length `L_curr` is proportionally reduced to `L / (α^2)`. This means higher resolutions are trained with shorter, more spatially dense sequences, while lower resolutions allow for longer, temporally denser sequences.
 - **Variable Temporal Density Training:** Frames are uniformly sampled from the original `L`-frame sequence according to `L_curr`, retaining their original indices. This effectively reduces temporal density at higher resolutions, allowing the model to focus on critical spatial details without processing redundant temporal information in every frame. Conversely, temporal density is increased at lower resolutions, enabling the model to track changes over time more effectively.
 - **Skip-ROPE Positional Embedding:** A novel `skip-ROPE` positional embedding is introduced. Instead of encoding frames based on their downsampled indices, `skip-ROPE` encodes them at the *original* `K` frame rate (e.g., 10Hz). This ensures consistent temporal embeddings across different resolutions, allowing the model to build a coherent understanding of time and accurately interpret temporal information even in high-resolution, long-horizon scenarios that cannot be directly trained.

- **Extended Temporal Scope Training Strategy (Curriculum Learning):**
 - To gradually ease the difficulty of long-term learning, a curriculum learning approach is employed. Training starts with shorter sequences (e.g., `L = 16` frames) to establish a stable foundation. The temporal window is then progressively expanded to `32, 64`, and eventually `128` frames per iteration. This allows the model to learn extended dependencies and behavioral patterns step-by-step, building on its extrapolative capacity from shorter sequences. Batch sizes are adjusted, reducing as frame counts increase to manage memory.

#### 3.2. Long-Term Rollouts (Addressing Error Accumulation)

To combat autoregressive error accumulation and maintain consistency over minute-scale generations, InfinityDrive integrates:

- **Memory Injection and Retention Mechanisms:**
 - The input latent sequence `x` is divided into a `memory segment` (`x_mem = {x_1, ..., x_M}`) and a `future prediction segment` (`x_future = {x_{M+1}, ..., x_L}`).
 - For frames within the memory segment (`T ≤ M`), the diffusion timestep `t` is set to `0`. This means *no noise is added*, preserving the original, pure historical information. The model "remembers" these frames accurately without attempting to predict or denoise them.
 - For the future prediction segment (`T > M`), the full diffusion denoising process is applied, with `t` sampled from `U(0, 1000)`.
 - The choice of `M` (memory length) is dynamic, adjusting based on resolution to ensure a consistent memory *duration* across different configurations, effectively providing a stable anchor.

- **Memory Curve Adaptive Loss (MCAL):**
 - Inspired by the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve, which describes how memory retention decays over time, MCAL applies a decaying weight to the loss function.
 - The weight `w(T) = e^(-λT)` is applied to the combined MSE and VB loss for each frame `T`.
 - **Near-future frames** (small `T` values, closer to the memory segment) receive **higher loss weights**. This forces the model to prioritize retaining detailed historical information, minimizing errors and maintaining consistency where fidelity to the past is most critical.
 - **Distant-future frames** (larger `T` values) receive **gradually decreasing loss weights**. This encourages the model to rely less on direct memory and more on its generative capabilities, fostering greater flexibility and diversity in predicting events further into the future, where strict adherence to past details might be less realistic or desirable.

#### 3.3. Enhanced Scenario Diversity

To address the lack of diversity, InfinityDrive employs:

- **Joint Image-to-Video (I2V) and Text-to-Video (T2V) Training:**
 - Leveraging the greater variability offered by text data compared to images, the model is trained to generate videos conditioned on both initial images and descriptive text prompts. This allows for more flexible and varied outputs.
- **Extensive Text Augmentation:**
 - Autonomous driving datasets are re-captioned using a powerful multi-modal foundation model (e.g., InternLM-XComposer-2.5).
 - Prompts are carefully curated to include camera position as a prefix and then describe detailed scene information: weather, time, location, lane layout, objects, and dynamic actions of both the ego vehicle and other traffic participants. This rich textual conditioning guides the model to generate highly diverse and contextually relevant scenarios.
- **Training Data Scale:** The model is trained on an extensive dataset of over **1700 hours** of driving data (specifically mentioned as 1745 hours), a scale comparable to leading models like Vista (1740 hours), indicating significant data resources critical for learning complex real-world distributions.

By combining these methodological innovations, InfinityDrive constructs a powerful framework capable of generating high-fidelity, consistent, and diverse driving videos over unprecedented minute-scale durations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InfinityDrive demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, fundamentally advancing the capabilities of driving world models across fidelity, consistency, and diversity, particularly in minute-scale video generation.

- **Minute-Scale Video Generation:** InfinityDrive is the first driving world model capable of generating videos lasting **over 1500 frames (more than 2 minutes)** at high resolution (576x1024), maintaining exceptional quality throughout. This is a significant breakthrough, as previous models were largely limited to seconds-long clips. Visual evidence in Figures 1 and 5 strikingly showcases long-duration, continuously evolving driving scenarios.

- **State-of-the-Art Quantitative Performance:**
 - **Short-term Generation:** The model achieves competitive performance, registering the lowest Frechet Video Distance (FVD) score among compared methods, indicating high visual quality and realism for shorter clips.
 - **Long-term Generation:** InfinityDrive significantly surpasses all other approaches in long-term settings (e.g., 100 frames), achieving the lowest Frechet Inception Distance (FID) and FVD scores (Table 2). This demonstrates its superior ability to maintain realism and avoid degradation over extended periods.
 - **Temporal Stability:** Crucially, quantitative evaluations over increasing frame counts (e.g., at 40, 80, 120 frames) show that InfinityDrive maintains **stable FID and FVD scores**, whereas other models like Vista, SVD-XT, and StreamingT2V exhibit a clear upward trend, indicating rapid quality degradation as generation length increases (Figure 3).

- **High Fidelity and Resolution:**
 - The model consistently generates high-resolution videos (576x1024) that preserve sharp details of road textures, vehicle appearances, background elements, and environmental conditions. Visual examples confirm consistent color saturation, lighting stability, and overall visual richness across frames, even over minute-long durations (Figure 7, Figure 4d).

- **Exceptional Temporal Consistency:**
 - InfinityDrive ensures coherence across both short and long temporal windows. In short-term rollouts, fine details like approaching vehicles and background elements (buildings, parked cars, traffic cones) maintain continuity and natural progression (Figures 4d, 4e). Over minute-scale durations, the movement of large structures and vehicles remains consistently synchronized and realistic, preventing the common "hallucination" or object disappearance issues seen in other models (Figure 1). This is partly attributed to the Memory Injection and Retention Mechanisms and the Memory Curve Adaptive Loss.

- **Enhanced Scenario Diversity:**
 - **Image-to-Video (I2V) Diversity:** When conditioned on identical historical frames but with different noise inputs, InfinityDrive generates distinct and varied future scenarios. It consistently adheres to the initial conditions for the first few frames but then diverges to produce different building structures, varied traffic participants, and new motions, unlike other models (Vista, SVD-XT) that remain largely unchanged (Figure 8).
 - **Text-to-Video (T2V) Diversity:** Using the same text condition with varying noise inputs, the model maintains consistent weather and lighting conditions as specified by the text but generates diverse building layouts and traffic participants, showcasing its ability to interpret and expand upon textual prompts while ensuring visual variety (Figure 9).

- **Learning Extended Behavioral Trends:**
 - The model's long-term prediction capacity enables it to capture and learn complex extended dependencies and behavioral trends. Examples include nuanced vehicle interactions, gradual speed adjustments (e.g., deceleration near intersections), and adherence to traffic rules (e.g., stopping on red, yielding to turning vehicles) over longer timeframes, which are challenging to model in shorter windows (Figure 6).

- **Validation through Ablation Studies:**
 - Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each proposed component (Extended Temporal Scope Training - ETST, Spatio-Temporal Co-Modeling - STCM, and Memory Curve Adaptive Loss - MCAL). Each module contributes significantly to reducing FID and FVD, demonstrating their individual and combined importance for achieving the overall performance (Table 3).
 - Specifically, STCM is crucial for preserving fine spatial details (traffic lights, poles) and enhancing temporal coherence for distant objects, preventing inconsistencies like vehicle type changes (Figure 10). MCAL is shown to preserve coherence with historical states and enhance temporal consistency while allowing dynamic evolution (Figure 11).

In summary, InfinityDrive successfully breaks previous time and resolution barriers in driving world models, delivering a highly capable system that generates long, high-fidelity, consistent, and diverse driving videos, supported by robust quantitative and qualitative evidence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InfinityDrive represents a significant leap forward in the field of autonomous driving world models, with profound significance and potential impact across research, development, and deployment:

- **Enabling Next-Generation Autonomous Driving Systems:** By generating minute-scale, high-fidelity, and consistent driving videos, InfinityDrive provides autonomous vehicles with an unprecedented ability to **anticipate, plan, and react proactively** to future events over extended horizons. This long-term foresight is absolutely critical for safe navigation, especially in complex, dynamic scenarios, allowing for smoother maneuvers and more informed decision-making with sufficient reaction time.

- **Addressing the Data Scarcity Challenge:** One of the biggest bottlenecks in autonomous driving is the immense cost and difficulty of collecting diverse, extensive, and out-of-distribution (OOD) real-world data. InfinityDrive offers a powerful solution by **synthesizing realistic and varied driving scenarios**, including rare or hazardous events that are difficult or dangerous to encounter in real-world testing. This dramatically reduces reliance on real-world data collection, making the development process faster, safer, and more cost-effective.

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The model's ability to generate a wide array of diverse scenarios, guided by rich text prompts and initial images, is instrumental in training autonomous systems that are **more robust and adaptable** to varied environmental conditions (weather, lighting, traffic density) and unexpected events. This directly contributes to improving the generalization capabilities of self-driving perception and planning modules.

- **Foundation for Multi-view and Controllable World Models:** The paper explicitly positions InfinityDrive as a foundational model for future advancements, specifically mentioning **multi-view and controllable driving world models**. This indicates a clear pathway toward even more sophisticated simulation environments where not only is the future predicted, but specific elements (e.g., traffic light changes, pedestrian behavior, ego vehicle actions) can be controlled, and generated from multiple camera perspectives. Such capabilities are crucial for generating richly annotated synthetic datasets that can directly train downstream perception models (e.g., object detection, segmentation) without manual labeling.

- **Accelerating Research and Development Cycles:** By providing high-quality, long-duration synthetic data, InfinityDrive can significantly **accelerate the iterative cycles of training, testing, and refining autonomous driving algorithms**. Developers can quickly generate new scenarios to validate hypotheses, test system limits, and fine-tune models, speeding up innovation.

- **Advancement in Generative AI:** Beyond autonomous driving, the methodological innovations introduced, such as the Efficient Spatio-Temporal Co-Modeling module, Memory Injection and Retention Mechanisms, and Memory Curve Adaptive Loss, represent significant contributions to the broader field of **long video generation and diffusion models**. These techniques could inspire similar advancements in other domains requiring consistent, high-fidelity, and long-duration video synthesis.

- **Industry Validation:** As a product of SenseAuto (SenseTime), a major industry player in AI and autonomous driving, InfinityDrive's development reflects a direct response to real-world engineering challenges. Its success underscores the commercial viability and practical utility of advanced world models in an industry striving for safer and more reliable self-driving technology.

In conclusion, InfinityDrive pushes the boundaries of what is possible with driving world models, offering a tangible pathway toward more capable and trustworthy autonomous driving systems by simulating the future with unprecedented fidelity and duration.
