# Loop, Think, & Generalize: Implicit Reasoning in Recurrent-Depth Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Ohio State University investigated recurrent-depth transformers for implicit multi-hop reasoning, demonstrating these architectures achieve systematic generalization and depth extrapolation, outperforming vanilla transformers on synthetic knowledge graph tasks. A three-stage "grokking" process for generalization was observed, with performance gains linked to training-time recurrence and dynamic iteration strategies, alongside a method to mitigate "overthinking" during inference.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) based on vanilla transformer architectures often struggle with compositional generalization and implicit multi-hop reasoning.
- Knowledge in standard transformers is frequently tied to specific layers, hindering flexible access and composition of information across multiple reasoning steps.
- Previous work had not systematically investigated recurrent-depth transformers' capacity to overcome these specific compositional generalization limitations in a controlled setting.

## Method

- The authors developed ==decoder-only recurrent-depth transformers==, employing a ==zero-initialization strategy== for output projection matrices to ensure stable training with ==shared weights==.
- They utilized ==synthetic knowledge graphs== to create controlled experimental setups for evaluating ==systematic generalization== (combining unseen facts) and ==depth extrapolation== (generalizing to deeper reasoning chains).
- Both ==fixed and dynamic recurrent iteration strategies== were explored during training to assess their impact on the models' learning and generalization capabilities.

## Results

- Recurrent-depth transformers (even with R=2) demonstrated non-trivial systematic generalization, whereas vanilla transformers (R=1) did not, with increased recurrence accelerating convergence in terms of epochs and wall-clock time.
- Models trained with dynamic iteration strategies achieved a greater "learnable recursion depth," enabling them to learn more complex multi-hop tasks and leading to superior extrapolation performance.
- An adaptive halting strategy combining KL-divergence and entropy of the output distribution effectively mitigated "overthinking" at inference time, improving efficiency and allocating compute proportionate to task complexity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recurrent-depth transformers exhibited systematic generalization and depth extrapolation capabilities, which vanilla transformers consistently failed to achieve.
> - Systematic generalization emerged through a distinct three-stage "grokking" dynamic: initial memorization, followed by in-distribution generalization, and finally systematic generalization on out-of-distribution inputs.
> - Increasing the number of inference-time recurrent iterations effectively unlocked depth extrapolation, allowing models to generalize to tasks significantly deeper than those seen during training.
