# Loop, Think, & Generalize: Implicit Reasoning in Recurrent-Depth Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Ohio State University investigated recurrent-depth transformers for implicit multi-hop reasoning, demonstrating these architectures achieve systematic generalization and depth extrapolation, outperforming vanilla transformers on synthetic knowledge graph tasks. A three-stage "grokking" process for generalization was observed, with performance gains linked to training-time recurrence and dynamic iteration strategies, alongside a method to mitigate "overthinking" during inference.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) based on vanilla transformer architectures often struggle with compositional generalization and implicit multi-hop reasoning.
- Knowledge in standard transformers is frequently tied to specific layers, hindering flexible access and composition of information across multiple reasoning steps.
- Previous work had not systematically investigated recurrent-depth transformers' capacity to overcome these specific compositional generalization limitations in a controlled setting.

## Method

- The authors developed ==decoder-only recurrent-depth transformers==, employing a ==zero-initialization strategy== for output projection matrices to ensure stable training with ==shared weights==.
- They utilized ==synthetic knowledge graphs== to create controlled experimental setups for evaluating ==systematic generalization== (combining unseen facts) and ==depth extrapolation== (generalizing to deeper reasoning chains).
- Both ==fixed and dynamic recurrent iteration strategies== were explored during training to assess their impact on the models' learning and generalization capabilities.

## Results

- Recurrent-depth transformers (even with R=2) demonstrated non-trivial systematic generalization, whereas vanilla transformers (R=1) did not, with increased recurrence accelerating convergence in terms of epochs and wall-clock time.
- Models trained with dynamic iteration strategies achieved a greater "learnable recursion depth," enabling them to learn more complex multi-hop tasks and leading to superior extrapolation performance.
- An adaptive halting strategy combining KL-divergence and entropy of the output distribution effectively mitigated "overthinking" at inference time, improving efficiency and allocating compute proportionate to task complexity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recurrent-depth transformers exhibited systematic generalization and depth extrapolation capabilities, which vanilla transformers consistently failed to achieve.
> - Systematic generalization emerged through a distinct three-stage "grokking" dynamic: initial memorization, followed by in-distribution generalization, and finally systematic generalization on out-of-distribution inputs.
> - Increasing the number of inference-time recurrent iterations effectively unlocked depth extrapolation, allowing models to generalize to tasks significantly deeper than those seen during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs), typically based on transformer architectures, have demonstrated a substantial capacity to acquire and store factual knowledge during pretraining. However, the ability of these models to effectively compose this knowledge for complex reasoning, particularly in implicit reasoning scenarios (i.e., within a single forward pass without explicit chain-of-thought prompting), remains a challenge. Existing transformer-based LLMs frequently exhibit limitations in compositional generalization, struggling to combine previously encountered knowledge in novel ways or to perform multi-hop reasoning. This limitation suggests that while knowledge may be parametrically stored, its flexible and iterative application is not guaranteed.

The core issue identified in current transformer architectures is that knowledge is often tied to specific layers, hindering flexible access and composition when information needs to be chained across multiple reasoning steps. For example, resolving a two-hop query might require an intermediate fact retrieved in a shallow layer to be accessible for a subsequent step in a deeper layer, which is not inherently supported by fixed-depth, non-shared parameters. Previous work has indicated that vanilla transformers can struggle with systematic generalization (combining unfamiliar knowledge) and depth extrapolation (generalizing to deeper reasoning chains).

Recurrent-depth transformers, also known as looped transformers, represent an architectural modification wherein the same set of transformer layers is applied iteratively. This design allows the output of one iteration to serve as the input for the next, theoretically enabling more flexible access to and composition of parametric knowledge within the same set of weights. Prior studies have explored recurrent-depth transformers for improvements in length generalization and their inductive biases towards reasoning over memorization. However, their capacity to overcome specific compositional generalization limitations, particularly in implicit reasoning over parametric knowledge, has not been systematically investigated in a controlled setting. This paper addresses that gap by studying recurrent-depth transformers in a controlled environment, training models from scratch on synthetic datasets to isolate architectural effects from the complexities of web-scale pretraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the capacity of recurrent-depth transformers to compositionally combine their parametric knowledge for implicit multi-hop reasoning. This involves assessing whether these architectures can address known limitations of vanilla transformers in compositional generalization.

The motivation for this study stems from several observations:
- **Limitations of Vanilla Transformers in Implicit Reasoning:** Despite their extensive knowledge storage, large language models built on vanilla transformer architectures often fail at implicit multi-hop reasoning. This failure is attributed to a lack of compositional generalization, where models struggle to combine existing parametric knowledge for novel or deeper reasoning paths.
- **Architectural Constraints:** In a standard transformer, knowledge retrieval and composition are often distributed across distinct layers. If a piece of information required for a subsequent reasoning step is primarily encoded in an earlier layer, deeper layers may not have direct access to it without parameter sharing. This layer-specific knowledge storage limits the model's ability to flexibly combine information across reasoning hops.
- **Need for Controlled Study:** Analyzing compositional reasoning in large, pre-trained LLMs is challenging due to the vast and opaque nature of their training data and the confounding factors introduced during pretraining. To isolate the effects of architectural design, the authors motivate the use of synthetic datasets and models trained from scratch. This controlled environment allows for a clear attribution of performance differences to specific architectural choices and training strategies.

Specifically, the paper identifies and focuses on two distinct compositional generalization challenges:
1. **Systematic Generalization:** This challenge evaluates a model's ability to combine atomic facts that were never observed in any composition during training. It simulates scenarios where knowledge might be present in a model's parameters (e.g., as plain text) but has not been composed into reasoning queries during training. Vanilla transformers have been shown to struggle significantly with this.
2. **Depth Extrapolation:** This challenge assesses a model's capability to generalize reasoning to depths (number of hops) greater than those it encountered during training. This mirrors real-world situations where training data complexity is bounded by computational or data availability constraints, yet models are expected to generalize to more complex, deeper reasoning paths.

By addressing these challenges, the research aims to provide insights into whether recurrent-depth transformers offer a viable architectural solution for building more robust and compositionally capable implicit reasoning models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a controlled experimental setup using synthetic knowledge graphs and decoder-only recurrent-depth transformers trained from scratch.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The core model is a decoder-only transformer based on a GPT-2 style block. This block, consisting of `L` layers, is reused `R` times during a forward pass, resulting in an effective rolled-out depth of `D = L × R` layers. The embedding layer and language model head have tied weights.
To ensure stable training under repeated application of shared weights, a **zero-initialization strategy** is adopted. Specifically, the output projection matrices (c proj) of both the multi-head attention and feed-forward blocks are initialized to zero. This ensures that each recurrent block functions as an identity mapping at initialization, preventing instability in the input-output Jacobian during unrolling.

#### 3.2. Stopping Strategies for Recurrent Iterations

Two strategies are explored for determining the number of recurrent iterations during training:
- **Fixed Iteration:** The number of recurrent iterations (`R`) is constant for all training instances.
- **Dynamic Iteration:** The number of recurrent iterations is sampled independently for each training batch from a clipped Poisson distribution (`R ∼ clip(Poisson(λ), R_min, R_max)`). This strategy aims to reflect practical scenarios where task complexity is unknown a priori.

#### 3.3. Task Formulation: Synthetic Multi-Hop Reasoning

The implicit reasoning task is defined using a directed knowledge graph (KG) composed of entities (`E`) and relations (`R`). Atomic (1-hop) facts are triplets `(h, r, t)`. A `k`-hop inferred fact represents a chain of `k` atomic facts connecting a head entity `h` to a final tail entity `t` via intermediate entities. The model is given `<e_h> <r_1>... <r_k>` as input and must predict `<e_t>`.

#### 3.4. Generalization Challenges Definition

The atomic fact set `C` is partitioned into `C_ID` (in-distribution) and `C_OOD` (out-of-distribution). Training inferred facts `I_train` are derived from `C_ID` up to a maximum depth `k_train`.
- **In-distribution (ID) generalization:** Evaluated on inferred facts from `I_k(C_ID)` not seen in training.
- **Systematic generalization:** Evaluated on inferred facts from `I_k(C_OOD)`, derived from atomic facts never used in compositions during training.
- **Depth extrapolation:** Evaluated on inferred facts from `I_k(C_ID)` where `k > k_train`.

#### 3.5. Experiment Setups

- **Systematic Generalization (2-hop task):**
 - **Dataset:** KG with |E|=2000, |R|=200. All 40k atomic facts (95% `C_ID`, 5% `C_OOD`) included in training, along with 273.6k 2-hop inferred facts from `C_ID`. Test sets: 3k held-out 2-hop ID facts and 2k 2-hop OOD facts (from `C_OOD`).
 - **Model:** Recurrent-depth transformer with `L=4` layers, trained with fixed recurrence `R ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}`. `R=1` serves as a vanilla transformer baseline. Absolute position embeddings (APE) were used.
 - **Analysis:** OOD accuracy over epochs and wall-clock time, and a "logit lens" technique to analyze the accuracy of predicting intermediate ("bridge") and final ("target") entities at different effective depths and training stages.

- **Depth Extrapolation (Multi-hop task):**
 - **Dataset:** Permutation-based KG (|E|=200, |R|=10, average out-degree 10) to prevent models from learning shortcut solutions based on relation suffixes. 2k atomic facts and 15k `k`-hop inferred facts for each `k ∈` were pre-generated.
 - **Curriculum Training:** Models were trained progressively. Training started with atomic and 2-hop facts until 95% accuracy was reached on a held-out 2-hop test set. Then, 3-hop data was added, and so on, up to `N_max`. Joint training on all previously introduced facts was maintained. The "learnable recursion depth" was defined as the largest `k` for which the model achieved the accuracy threshold.
 - **Model:** `L=4` layers, with fixed `R ∈ {1, 2..., 8}` and dynamic recurrence. No positional embeddings (NoPE) were used.
 - **Evaluation:** 750 held-out `k`-hop facts for each `k`. ID test set `k ≤ learnable recursion depth`, extrapolation test set `k > learnable recursion depth`.
 - **Analysis:** Accuracy as a function of inference-time recurrence, cumulative gradient updates to reach generalization thresholds, comparison of fixed vs. dynamic training strategies, analysis of "overthinking" (performance degradation with excessive inference iterations) using logit margin, and evaluation of adaptive halting strategies (KL divergence + entropy vs. KL-only).

#### 3.6. General Training Details

All experiments utilized the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 10<sup>-4</sup>, weight decay of 0.01, and a linear warmup schedule of 2000 steps. Batch sizes were 512 for systematicity experiments and 128 for extrapolation experiments. For adaptive halting, thresholds `ε_KL = 0.01` and `H_thresh = 3.00` were used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the implicit reasoning capabilities of recurrent-depth transformers across systematic generalization and depth extrapolation challenges.

#### 4.1. Systematic Generalization

- **Recurrent-Depth vs. Vanilla Transformers:** Recurrent-depth transformers exhibited systematic generalization capabilities, while vanilla transformers (R=1) did not. Even with minimal recurrence (R=2), models achieved non-trivial generalization performance. Increasing recurrence during training (e.g., R=4) accelerated convergence in terms of both epochs and wall-clock time.
- **Three-Stage Grokking Dynamic:** Systematic generalization emerged through a distinct three-stage "grokking" process:
 1. **Stage 1 (Memorization):** The model overfit the training set, with only training accuracy improving, and predictions often occurring without reliable decoding of intermediate (bridge) entities.
 2. **Stage 2 (In-Distribution Generalization):** After prolonged training, in-distribution generalization emerged, characterized by the decodability of the bridge entity followed by correct target prediction for in-distribution data at deeper effective depths.
 3. **Stage 3 (Systematic Generalization):** Systematic generalization on OOD inputs arose only after near-perfect in-distribution accuracy was achieved, marking a transition from rote learning to systematic composition.
- **Logit Lens Analysis:** This analysis supported the three-stage dynamic. Recurrent-depth models transitioned from memorizing targets to correctly predicting bridge and then target entities for ID data, and subsequently for OOD data. In contrast, vanilla transformers, while able to recover bridge entities on Test OOD inputs, consistently failed to perform the second-hop reasoning, indicating a lack of incentive to encode OOD facts in deeper layers.

#### 4.2. Depth Extrapolation

- **Increased Learnable Recursion Depth:** Scaling up training-time recurrent iterations (`R`) directly improved the in-distribution (ID) generalization, increasing the learnable recursion depth of the looped transformers. Dynamic iteration strategies further enhanced this, allowing models to learn more complex multi-hop tasks compared to fixed iteration. However, this scaling was not strictly linear; for instance, R=7 and R=8 models learned up to 16-hop tasks.
- **Phase Transition in Learning:** Models demonstrated a phase transition in learning: a prolonged training phase was required for low-hop tasks, followed by rapid generalization to much more complex samples. This suggested that discovering the underlying compositional rule was the main challenge, after which the rule could be quickly extended.
- **Unlocking Extrapolation with Inference-Time Scaling:** While models struggled to generalize to deeper tasks using the same recurrence as training, increasing the number of inference-time recurrent iterations ("inference-time compute") effectively unlocked depth extrapolation. This allowed models to generalize to progressively harder tasks beyond their training depth. This effect was observed to be more prominent for models trained with R > 4.
- **Effect of Training Iteration Strategy:** The maximum number of iterations used during training was found to determine the potential extrapolation range. Dynamic iteration strategies were shown to be effective in exploiting this range, leading to larger learnable recursion depths and superior extrapolation performance compared to fixed-iteration strategies with the same maximum iteration budget, especially when training data was sufficiently complex.
- **Limitation: Overthinking:** A key limitation identified was "overthinking," where excessively large inference-time iterations led to performance degradation. The logit margin analysis revealed that prediction confidence (margin) increased to a peak and then consistently declined with continued iterations, regardless of task complexity. Dynamic models exhibited slower margin decay, indicating greater robustness to overthinking. Furthermore, the peak margin decreased with increasing task complexity, suggesting that more complex tasks are inherently less confident and more susceptible to overthinking.
- **Adaptive Halting for Efficiency:** To mitigate overthinking and improve inference efficiency, an adaptive halting strategy combining KL-divergence and entropy of the output distribution was proposed. This method was found to provide a more reliable halting signal and better allocate inference-time compute proportionate to task complexity, outperforming a KL-divergence-only halting criterion which often halted prematurely.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this research highlight recurrent-depth transformers as a promising architectural approach for addressing fundamental challenges in compositional reasoning for language models.

**Enhanced Compositional Generalization:** The demonstration that recurrent-depth transformers can achieve both systematic generalization and depth extrapolation, where vanilla transformers struggle, signifies a substantial advancement. This suggests a pathway for models to flexibly combine parametric knowledge in novel or deeper reasoning chains, moving beyond rote memorization or mere in-distribution generalization.

**Insights into Learning Dynamics:** The observation of a three-stage grokking process for systematic generalization provides valuable mechanistic insights into how generalization emerges in these architectures. This progression from memorization to in-distribution generalization and finally to systematic generalization offers a refined understanding of the learning trajectory and the computational resources (e.g., training epochs) required for robust reasoning capabilities.

**Architectural Efficiency and Flexibility:** The ability to scale reasoning depth at inference time by simply increasing recurrent iterations, without requiring architectural changes or retraining, represents a significant architectural advantage. This flexibility allows for dynamic allocation of computational resources based on query complexity, potentially leading to more efficient and adaptable reasoning systems.

**Guidance for Model Design and Training:** The study provides practical guidance on effective training strategies for recurrent-depth transformers, including the benefits of dynamic recurrence schedules and specific initialization techniques (zero-initialization) for stability. The analysis of overthinking and the development of an improved adaptive halting mechanism offer concrete methods to optimize performance and computational efficiency in deployment.

**Foundation for Future LLMs:** While conducted in a controlled, synthetic setting, this research provides foundational insights that can inform the design of future large language models. The identified strengths of recurrent-depth architectures, coupled with an understanding of their limitations (like overthinking) and effective mitigation strategies, could contribute to the development of LLMs with enhanced compositional reasoning, especially for implicit multi-hop queries where explicit chain-of-thought is not desirable or feasible. This work lays the groundwork for bridging the gap between LLMs' vast knowledge storage and their current limitations in flexible, compositional application of that knowledge.
