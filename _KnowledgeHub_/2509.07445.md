# Text2Touch: Tactile In-Hand Manipulation with LLM-Designed Reward Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Bristol and Bristol Robotics Laboratory developed a framework for Large Language Models to design reward functions for complex, real-world tactile in-hand manipulation tasks. The approach resulted in policies that consistently outperformed human-engineered baselines on a robotic hand with tactile sensors, achieving up to a 38% increase in rotations and 25% longer episode durations, while also producing simpler reward function code.

## Problem

- Designing effective reward functions for reinforcement learning in dexterous robotic manipulation is a complex and time-consuming process.
- Integrating high-dimensional data from vision-based tactile sensors into reward functions significantly amplifies the difficulty of manual design.
- Previous applications of LLMs for reward design primarily focused on conventional sensing modalities (vision, proprioception), lacking validation for tactile-based real-world tasks.

## Method

- An iterative LLM-based reward design framework, adapted from Eureka, which generates and refines reward functions by providing the LLM with task descriptions, environment context, and performance feedback.
- Enhanced LLM prompting strategies that include scalable success bonuses and early-termination penalties, along with detailed environment variable signatures, to improve reward function validity and efficacy in complex environments.
- A sim-to-real transfer pipeline using teacher-student distillation to deploy policies trained with LLM-designed rewards from simulation to a real Allegro Hand equipped with TacTip tactile sensors.

## Results

- LLM-generated reward functions produced significantly simpler code (e.g., one-tenth the variables, one-eighth the Halstead Volume) while achieving superior performance in simulation compared to a human-engineered baseline.
- On a real robotic hand, LLM-designed policies consistently outperformed the human-engineered baseline, with the best LLM policy achieving a 38% increase in rotations per episode and 25% longer episode durations.
- The LLM-based policies demonstrated enhanced real-world stability, hypothesized to be due to their optimized sensitivity to contact changes that translated to more effective regrasping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively reason over complex, high-dimensional tactile data to formulate concise and interpretable reward functions for dexterous manipulation.
> - Providing scalable success/termination variables and explicit environment context in LLM prompts is critical for successful reward generation in complex robotic tasks.
> - LLM-designed reward functions can lead to policies that are more robust and exhibit greater stability in real-world tactile manipulation compared to meticulously human-engineered baselines.
