# Text2Touch: Tactile In-Hand Manipulation with LLM-Designed Reward Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Bristol and Bristol Robotics Laboratory developed a framework for Large Language Models to design reward functions for complex, real-world tactile in-hand manipulation tasks. The approach resulted in policies that consistently outperformed human-engineered baselines on a robotic hand with tactile sensors, achieving up to a 38% increase in rotations and 25% longer episode durations, while also producing simpler reward function code.

## Problem

- Designing effective reward functions for reinforcement learning in dexterous robotic manipulation is a complex and time-consuming process.
- Integrating high-dimensional data from vision-based tactile sensors into reward functions significantly amplifies the difficulty of manual design.
- Previous applications of LLMs for reward design primarily focused on conventional sensing modalities (vision, proprioception), lacking validation for tactile-based real-world tasks.

## Method

- An iterative LLM-based reward design framework, adapted from Eureka, which generates and refines reward functions by providing the LLM with task descriptions, environment context, and performance feedback.
- Enhanced LLM prompting strategies that include scalable success bonuses and early-termination penalties, along with detailed environment variable signatures, to improve reward function validity and efficacy in complex environments.
- A sim-to-real transfer pipeline using teacher-student distillation to deploy policies trained with LLM-designed rewards from simulation to a real Allegro Hand equipped with TacTip tactile sensors.

## Results

- LLM-generated reward functions produced significantly simpler code (e.g., one-tenth the variables, one-eighth the Halstead Volume) while achieving superior performance in simulation compared to a human-engineered baseline.
- On a real robotic hand, LLM-designed policies consistently outperformed the human-engineered baseline, with the best LLM policy achieving a 38% increase in rotations per episode and 25% longer episode durations.
- The LLM-based policies demonstrated enhanced real-world stability, hypothesized to be due to their optimized sensitivity to contact changes that translated to more effective regrasping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively reason over complex, high-dimensional tactile data to formulate concise and interpretable reward functions for dexterous manipulation.
> - Providing scalable success/termination variables and explicit environment context in LLM prompts is critical for successful reward generation in complex robotic tasks.
> - LLM-designed reward functions can lead to policies that are more robust and exhibit greater stability in real-world tactile manipulation compared to meticulously human-engineered baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: robotic manipulation, tactile sensing, reinforcement learning (RL), and large language models (LLMs).

**a. LLMs for Reward and Policy Design:** The past few years have seen a surge in interest in leveraging LLMs to automate various aspects of robot learning. Traditional RL often struggles with the "reward design problem," where painstakingly hand-crafting effective reward functions is complex, time-consuming, and prone to suboptimal outcomes. Recent works like Eureka have demonstrated that LLMs can generate and refine reward functions for robotic tasks, significantly reducing the human engineering effort. However, the authors correctly point out a critical gap: "these breakthroughs have primarily focused on conventional sensing modalities (vision, proprioception) for real-world validation." *Text2Touch* directly addresses this by being the first to integrate LLM-crafted rewards with *tactile sensing*.

**b. Advances in Tactile Robotic Manipulation:** Tactile sensing, particularly high-resolution vision-based sensors like GelSight, DIGIT, and the TacTip (utilized here), is crucial for human-like dexterity. These sensors provide rich information about contact, slip, and forces that traditional vision or proprioception alone cannot capture. This detailed feedback has enabled significant progress in complex tasks like in-hand object manipulation and rotation. However, even these advanced tactile manipulation successes still rely on "carefully hand-tuned reward functions," which are difficult to transfer across tasks, objects, or sensor suites. *Text2Touch* aims to simplify this by automating the reward design process for tactile systems.

**c. Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Bridging the "reality gap" between simulation and the real world remains a core challenge in robotics. While techniques like domain randomization and teacher-student distillation have made progress in transferring policies learned in simulation to hardware, applying these methods to automatically generated reward structures, especially for highly sensitive tactile tasks, is an open question. Previous LLM-based sim-to-real efforts were limited to proprioceptive sensing. *Text2Touch* extends this by successfully demonstrating sim-to-real transfer for LLM-designed rewards in a complex *tactile* manipulation task, showcasing the robustness of their approach despite inherent differences between simulated and real tactile feedback.

In summary, *Text2Touch* bridges a crucial gap by demonstrating that LLMs can successfully design reward functions for complex, real-world *tactile* dexterous manipulation tasks, a capability previously unexplored. It pushes the frontier of automated robot learning by making the sophisticated capabilities of tactile sensing more accessible and scalable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the *Text2Touch* research can be distilled into several key points:

- **Automate Reward Function Design for Tactile Robotics:** The overarching goal is to mitigate the "formidable challenge" and "painstaking" nature of designing reinforcement learning reward functions for dexterous in-hand manipulation. Specifically, the authors aim to extend the capabilities of LLMs, which have shown promise in automating reward design for conventional sensors, to incorporate the complex and high-dimensional data from vision-based tactile sensors.
- **Unlock Robust Policies for Advanced Manipulation:** Tactile sensing is "critical for human-like dexterity" and provides "detailed contact and force signals that visual sensing alone often fails to capture." However, integrating such complex sensor modalities "amplifies the difficulties of reward design." The motivation is that if LLMs can autonomously generate effective tactile-based rewards, it could "unlock robust policies for advanced manipulation."
- **Tackle Challenging In-Hand Rotation Tasks:** The paper targets a particularly demanding task: "gravity-invariant, multi-axis in-hand object rotation" under both "palm-up and palm-down configurations." This task, involving precise control of a multi-fingered hand to rotate an object about three perpendicular axes while keeping it aloft, is chosen to demonstrate the adaptive capabilities and high dexterity achievable with LLM-designed tactile policies.
- **Reduce Time from Concept to Deployment:** By enabling LLMs to autonomously design "concise, interpretable reward functions," the authors aim to "significantly reduce the time from concept to deployable dexterous tactile skills." This would support "more rapid and scalable multi-modal robot learning" by reducing reliance on expert tuning and repetitive manual engineering.
- **Validate LLM-Designed Rewards in the Real World:** A critical objective is to validate the effectiveness of LLM-generated reward functions on a real-world, tactile-enabled robotic system (the Allegro Hand with TacTip sensors), moving beyond purely simulated environments that most prior LLM-robotics work focused on.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The *Text2Touch* framework introduces an innovative approach that marries iterative LLM-based reward design with a robust sim-to-real transfer pipeline for tactile manipulation.

**a. Iterative LLM Reward Design (Building on Eureka):**
The core of *Text2Touch* is an iterative loop for reward generation, adapted from the Eureka framework. This process involves:
- **Initial Prompting:** An LLM (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Flash) is provided with a "natural language task description" (e.g., "reposition and reorient objects to a target position and orientation by regrasping or finger gaiting") and a comprehensive "environment context" (M).
- **Reward Candidate Generation:** The LLM proposes Python code for reward functions (R).
- **Policy Training and Evaluation:** A PPO learning agent trains policies (π) using these generated reward functions. The performance is measured by a fitness function (F), which in this case, is a running count of "sub-goal incremental rotations" achieved.
- **Reward Reflection and Refinement:** The best-performing reward function is selected, and its training metrics are fed back to the LLM as "feedback." The LLM then uses this feedback to iteratively refine and improve subsequent reward candidates. This loop continues for a preset number of iterations.
- **Environment Curriculum:** The task is structured with incremental sub-goals: each time a small rotation target is met, the environment updates the target orientation, allowing continuous learning and "multiple successive ‘successes’ per rollout."

**b. Enhanced Prompting for High-Complexity Environments:**
Recognizing the limitations of existing LLM prompting strategies when dealing with a large number of environment variables (over 70 in this case), the authors introduce two key modifications:
- **Scalable Bonuses and Penalties:** Instead of a fixed scalar success bonus (B) and an early-termination penalty (P) added *externally*, these terms are explicitly provided to the LLM as *scalable variables* within its context. This allows the LLM to integrate and weight them appropriately within its generated reward expression, which empirically proved essential for task success (Table 1).
- **Modified Prompt Structuring:** The LLM is provided with a "detailed reward function signature" that explicitly lists all available environment variables along with their data types. This contrasts with the generic signatures used in prior work and significantly reduces "syntactic errors or mismatched variable types," leading to more valid and effective reward functions.

**c. Sim-to-Real Pipeline with Teacher-Student Distillation:**
Once the iterative reward design process identifies an optimal reward function (R*), the methodology shifts to deployment:
- **Reward Discovery Training (Short Runs):** To efficiently evaluate many reward candidates, initial training runs for each LLM-generated reward are relatively short (150 million simulation steps).
- **Teacher Policy Training:** The single best LLM-generated reward function (R*) is then used to train a "Teacher" policy in simulation for a longer duration (8 billion steps). This Teacher policy has access to *privileged information* (e.g., precise object pose and velocity).
- **Student Policy Distillation:** The Teacher policy's behavior is then distilled into a "Student" policy. Crucially, the Student policy is trained to mimic the Teacher's actions but only observes *proprioceptive and tactile information*—the modalities available on the real robot. This minimizes the mean-squared distance to the Teacher's actions.
- **Real-World Deployment:** The trained Student policy is transferred directly to the hardware without further tuning. The robotic platform used is an Allegro Hand, a fully actuated four-fingered dexterous robot hand, equipped with vision-based TacTip tactile sensors, operating at 20Hz.

**d. Evaluation:**
Performance is measured by the number of complete rotations per episode and the time to termination. Evaluation covers ten household objects, three rotation axes, and palm-up/palm-down hand orientations. Out-of-distribution (OOD) testing with varying object masses and shapes is also conducted in simulation. The performance is rigorously compared against a carefully tuned human-engineered baseline (from AnyRotate).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings across its three main stages, demonstrating the superior performance and efficiency of LLM-designed reward functions for tactile in-hand manipulation.

**a. Prompting Strategy Verification (Stage 1):**
- **Necessity of Scalable Rewards:** The authors conclusively show that providing the success bonus and early-termination penalty as *scalable variables* to the LLM (denoted M(B,P)modified) was essential for any success. Without this, LLMs consistently failed to guide policies to task completion, highlighting a critical improvement over previous LLM reward design frameworks like Eureka when applied to complex tasks (Table 1).
- **Reduced Code Failures:** The "modified prompt structuring" (Mmodified), which involves explicitly listing all 70+ environment variables in the reward function signature, significantly reduced syntactic errors and improved the generation rate of runnable code, making the LLM-driven process more reliable.
- **Superior Simulation Performance (Privileged Information):** Policies trained with LLM-generated rewards (using privileged information in simulation) consistently achieved a higher number of rotations per episode compared to the human-engineered baseline (AnyRotate). Gemini-1.5-Flash, GPT-4o, and Llama3.1-405B all showed significant improvements (Table 2).
- **Improved Code Quality and Interpretability:** The LLM-generated reward functions were strikingly simpler than the human baseline. They used approximately one-tenth the variables, one-quarter the lines of code, and one-eighth the Halstead Volume, leading to more interpretable and computationally less expensive reward structures while still outperforming the baseline (Table 2).
- **Diverse and Concise Reward Composition:** LLMs discovered succinct reward components focused on hand-object contact, object position/orientation, and sparse success bonuses. Notably, models like o3-mini provided in-line commentary explaining design choices, and Deepseek-R1-671B effectively leveraged keypoint data as a unified position-orientation signal, showcasing the LLMs' ability to find novel and efficient reward formulations.

**b. Model Distillation to Tactile Observations (Stage 2):**
- **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Data:** Student policies, distilled from the LLM-trained Teachers and relying solely on tactile and proprioceptive observations, maintained their performance advantage. They consistently outperformed the human baseline when tested with out-of-distribution objects (heavier masses and novel shapes) in simulation (Table 3). This indicates good generalization and robustness of the LLM-designed reward functions even with restricted sensory inputs.
- **Effective Use of Restricted Modalities:** Interestingly, some LLM-based Students (e.g., Deepseek-based) exhibited longer episode durations with tactile inputs than with privileged observations. This suggests that the focused tactile modality could lead to robust solutions by emphasizing task-relevant signals, preventing the policy from being overwhelmed by the full state space.

**c. Real-World Comparison of Distilled Models (Stage 3):**
- **Real-World Outperformance:** All three selected LLM-based policies (Gemini-1.5-Flash, GPT-4o, Deepseek-R1-671B) deployed on the real tactile Allegro Hand significantly outperformed the human-engineered baseline in both average rotations per episode and sustained episode lengths (Table 4). Deepseek-R1-671B, for instance, achieved a 38% increase in rotations and a 25% increase in episode duration.
- **Enhanced Real-World Stability:** Contrary to simulation observations where LLM-policies were sometimes less stable, in the real world, they demonstrated *greater* stability. The authors hypothesize this is due to the inherent differences between simulated rigid-body contacts and real-world smooth, continuous tactile feedback. The LLM-trained agents' higher sensitivity to contact changes (optimized for faster motions in simulation) translated to earlier instability detection and more effective regrasping in the real world, giving them an advantage over the slower, more cautious human-engineered baseline.

In essence, *Text2Touch* successfully demonstrates that LLMs can automatically generate high-quality, simpler, and more effective reward functions for complex, real-world tactile manipulation tasks than those meticulously engineered by humans, proving crucial for superior performance and stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The *Text2Touch* research represents a pivotal advancement in robot learning and has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Bridging a Critical Gap in Robot Learning:** This work is the *first demonstration* of LLM-generated reward functions successfully guiding a *tactile-based* in-hand manipulation task in the real world. This bridges a significant gap in the literature, which previously focused on conventional sensing modalities for LLM-driven robot control. It proves that LLMs can reason over complex, high-dimensional tactile data to formulate effective reward signals.
- **Accelerated Development of Dexterous Skills:** By largely automating the "painstaking" and expert-dependent process of reward engineering, *Text2Touch* promises to "lower the barrier to tactile robotics research." This will enable "rapid prototyping of complex in-hand manipulations" and a "faster translation of novel behaviours into reliable real-world systems." Researchers can iterate on tasks and robotic capabilities much quicker without getting bogged down in reward tuning.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Dexterity:** The superior performance of LLM-generated policies in both simulation and real-world tactile manipulation (surpassing human-engineered baselines in speed and stability) indicates a pathway toward more autonomous and dexterous robots. This capability is vital for applications requiring fine-grained object handling, such as manufacturing, assistive robotics, and exploration.
- **Improved Interpretability of Reward Functions:** The finding that LLM-generated rewards are significantly shorter and simpler than human-engineered counterparts is highly impactful. Simpler reward functions are easier to understand, debug, and potentially adapt, which is crucial for building trust in autonomous systems and facilitating further research.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer for Tactile Systems:** The successful sim-to-real distillation, even with the inherent domain gap in tactile sensing, demonstrates the robustness of the LLM-designed rewards. This suggests that LLMs can help in designing policies that generalize well from simulated environments to the complexities of the physical world, especially for contact-rich tasks.
- **Foundation for Multi-Modal Robot Learning:** *Text2Touch* paves the way for more nuanced and sophisticated in-hand manipulation by effectively grounding LLM-generated rewards in rich tactile feedback. This could be extended to integrate other modalities like force, temperature, or slip, leading to truly multi-modal robot learning systems.
- **Practical Tool for Robotics Engineers:** The framework positions LLMs not just as theoretical curiosities but as "practical tools" for robotics engineers, empowering them to tackle complex manipulation problems with reduced manual effort and potentially achieve higher performance.

In conclusion, *Text2Touch* marks a significant step forward in making advanced robotic dexterity more accessible and scalable through the intelligent application of large language models. It opens up exciting avenues for future research in autonomous robot learning and the development of highly adaptable robotic systems.
