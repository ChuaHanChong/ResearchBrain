# LabRobFail: A Benchmark for Robotic Failure Analysis in Chemical Self-driving Laboratory

> [!summary] Summary
> LabRobFail is a failure-centric framework for chemical-lab robotics: a simulator injecting control/physics/semantic perturbations, a 20,000+ trajectory dataset spanning 5 failure categories and 11 types, a six-dimensional benchmark, and LabRobFail-VLM, a Qwen3-VL model hybrid-fine-tuned to detect, localize, diagnose, and correct failures, reaching 90.83% detection accuracy and boosting downstream policy recovery by 4-16 points.

## Problem

- Existing lab simulators like LabUtopia and AutoBio emphasize successful execution only and lack systematic failure-injection pipelines, biasing training data toward ideal trajectories.
- Real-world laboratory failure collection is costly and hazardous, leaving agents with insufficient coverage of diverse chemical-lab anomalies for learning discriminative boundaries.
- Existing failure benchmarks target household manipulation with binary or coarse detection, lacking fine-grained localization, severity assessment, and precise recovery for irreversible chemical protocols.

## Method

- **LabRobFail-Sim**: a multi-level ==FailureGenerator== applying control-level Gaussian/Lie-algebra noise, physics-level dynamics parameter scaling, and semantic-level sequence permutation or object-substitution perturbations to synthesize failure trajectories.
- ==LLM-driven semantic annotation pipeline== using GPT-5.4 with rule-based filtering and human sampling to generate temporal and semantic instruction-answer pairs aligned to six benchmark dimensions from simulation metadata.
- **LabRobFail-VLM**: a ==Spatio-Temporal Keyframe Grid== representation feeding a hybrid-fine-tuned Qwen3-VL, with full fine-tuning on vision components and LoRA adaptation on the language decoder for structured failure diagnosis and correction.

## Results

- On seen environments LabRobFail-VLM reaches **90.83%** failure-detection and **77.21%** temporal-localization accuracy, far exceeding GPT-5.4 (**52.34%**, **15.88%**) and other general-purpose VLMs.
- Deployed as a real-time supervisor for OpenVLA and ACT policies, corrections raise downstream task success rates by **4-16** percentage points, with the largest gain on Pour under ACT (**32%** to **48%**).
- Ablations show removing temporal indices drops localization accuracy by **6.63** points and freezing vision components costs **17.00** points on Q3, confirming asymmetric fine-tuning and temporal indexing are load-bearing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured, recovery-oriented failure prediction that jointly outputs detection, localization, severity, and correction generalizes better than treating failure recognition as isolated binary classification.
> - Paired success/failure trajectories under identical conditions sharpen the discriminative boundary a model learns between normal operation and subtle anomaly, a transferable data-design principle.
> - Compound distribution shifts (novel objects plus novel scenes together) degrade localization and classification far more than either shift alone, exposing robustness as still unresolved even with domain-specialized training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "LabRobFail" is situated at the intersection of embodied artificial intelligence, robotics, and scientific automation, specifically within the context of Self-Driving Laboratories (SDLs). SDLs represent an evolving frontier in scientific discovery, aiming to accelerate research through autonomous experimentation. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated generalist manipulation capabilities, enabling robots to perform a range of tasks. However, the deployment of such agents in chemical laboratories faces specific constraints due to the inherent characteristics of chemical experiments: they are often irreversible, require strict adherence to protocols, and carry substantial safety risks. Minor deviations can lead to contamination, invalidate long-horizon workflows, or cause hazardous incidents.

Current research in robotic failure understanding has largely focused on household manipulation tasks, where failures are frequently reversible. Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have been applied to failure detection, but the benchmarks and datasets available are typically designed for less critical environments. For instance, datasets like ARMBench Video Defect, ViFailback, RLBench-Fail, BridgeDataV2-Fail, UR5-Fail, SMF-DROID, RoboFAC, FAILURE, and AHA contribute to robotic failure analysis but primarily lack support for chemical laboratory environments, specialized instruments, systematic failure injection, or provide only coarse evaluation metrics such as binary detection or broad failure categories. The feedback provided by these systems for correction is often ambiguous, which is insufficient for the precise, risk-aware recovery required in irreversible chemical procedures.

Scientific simulators such as LabUtopia and AutoBio have advanced high-fidelity simulation for biological and chemical interactions. However, these platforms predominantly emphasize successful task execution and do not incorporate systematic failure injection pipelines. Consequently, agents are often trained on successful trajectories, which limits their exposure to diverse laboratory anomalies and hinders their ability to detect, diagnose, and recover from failures reliably.

LabRobFail addresses these identified gaps by introducing a framework specifically designed for robotic failure analysis in safety-critical chemical laboratory settings. It aims to provide a platform for systematic failure generation, a large-scale dataset covering fine-grained failure types, and a multi-dimensional benchmark capable of evaluating not only failure detection but also precise temporal localization, severity assessment, and actionable correction planning. This work contributes to bridging the gap between generalist robotic manipulation and the specific demands of reliable autonomous operation in scientific laboratories.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for introducing LabRobFail stems from the challenges associated with deploying embodied agents in Self-Driving Laboratories (SDLs) to accelerate scientific discovery. While the potential of SDLs is recognized, their practical implementation is constrained by the reliability of robotic systems, particularly in chemical experimentation. Chemical experiments are characterized by their irreversible nature and significant safety implications, meaning even minor operational deviations can lead to severe consequences, such as sample contamination, invalidation of entire experimental workflows, or hazardous incidents. Therefore, for laboratory autonomy to be truly reliable, agents must possess capabilities beyond mere task execution; they must also be able to perceive, diagnose, and recover from failures in real-time.

The authors identify two key gaps that impede progress in this domain:

1. **Scarcity of large-scale, diverse failure data**: Collecting real-world failure data in chemical laboratories is often costly, time-consuming, and potentially hazardous. Existing scientific simulators, such as LabUtopia and AutoBio, primarily focus on simulating successful executions and lack robust, systematic pipelines for injecting and annotating diverse failure modes. This results in training datasets that are heavily biased towards successful trajectories, leaving current robotic agents underexposed to the wide spectrum of anomalies that can occur in a laboratory setting. Consequently, models struggle to learn the precise discriminative boundaries between normal operations and subtle failures.

2. **Coarseness of existing evaluation benchmarks**: Current robotic failure analysis benchmarks are largely designed for less critical household manipulation tasks. They typically assess only binary failure detection or classify failures into broad categories. These benchmarks often lack the granularity required for fine-grained temporal localization (identifying *when* a failure occurs), severity assessment (understanding the *impact* of a failure), and actionable corrective feedback (providing *how* to recover). In chemical procedures, where precision and risk awareness are paramount, ambiguous or coarse corrections are insufficient for effective recovery.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of the LabRobFail project are:

- **To introduce LabRobFail-Sim**: A failure-centric simulation framework built upon a physics engine, extended with an automated, multi-level failure injection pipeline. This system aims to generate controllable and scalable failure trajectories by perturbing control signals, physical dynamics, and semantic task conditions.
- **To construct LabRobFail-Data**: A large-scale, annotated dataset derived from LabRobFail-Sim, comprising over 20,000 trajectories across 70+ task scenarios, covering five major failure categories and 11 fine-grained failure types specific to chemical laboratories. The dataset is designed to include paired success and failure trajectories under identical conditions to facilitate the learning of anomaly detection.
- **To establish LabRobFail-Bench**: The first multi-dimensional benchmark tailored for chemical laboratories, evaluating agents across six capabilities: task understanding, failure detection, temporal localization, severity assessment, failure classification, and fine-grained correction planning. This benchmark is intended to directly support the stringent safety requirements of laboratory automation.
- **To develop LabRobFail-VLM**: A domain-specialized vision-language model designed to perform structured failure analysis. This model is intended to generate semantically grounded, recovery-oriented diagnoses and serve as a task-specific supervisor, linking failure understanding to actionable recovery instructions. The objective is to demonstrate that such a specialized model can outperform general-purpose VLMs and improve downstream policy recovery in closed-loop systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LabRobFail framework integrates a simulation platform, a dataset, a benchmark, and a specialized vision-language model to address robotic failure analysis in chemical self-driving laboratories.

**LabRobFail-Sim: Simulation Platform for Failure Generation**
Built upon the physics engine of LabUtopia, LabRobFail-Sim extends existing high-fidelity chemical simulations with an automated, multi-level failure injection pipeline and an LLM-driven semantic annotation process.

- **Multi-level Failure Trajectory Synthesis**: The system generates failure trajectories by applying systematic perturbation functions (Φ) across three hierarchical levels:
 - **Control-level Perturbation (Φ_ctrl)**: Simulates execution errors by introducing stochastic Gaussian noise to the end-effector's translation and Lie algebra noise to its rotation. Gripper commands are also perturbed with a defined failure probability to model actuator faults.
 - **Physics-level Perturbation (Φ_phy)**: Induces emergent failures by dynamically adjusting simulation dynamics parameters such as friction, mass, and viscosity. Parameters are uniformly scaled to deviate from nominal values, testing the agent’s physical robustness.
 - **Semantic-level Perturbation (Φ_sem)**: Introduces task logic errors through permutation operators on sequence indices (e.g., step reversals or skips) and mapping functions for target object substitution (e.g., selecting the incorrect reagent), leading to potential safety constraint violations.
 The perturbed trajectories are executed under dynamically altered physics, enabling the generation of labeled failure data.
- **LLM-driven Semantic Annotation**: To enable scalable and consistent annotation, a GPT-5.4-based VQA (Vision-Question Answering) pipeline is utilized. This pipeline leverages simulation ground-truth metadata to instantiate query and correction templates aligned with the six dimensions of LabRobFail-Bench. It generates context-specific instruction–answer pairs, which are then validated through rule-based filtering and human sampling before integration into LabRobFail-Data.

**LabRobFail-Data: Large-scale Dataset for Laboratory Failure Analysis**
The dataset comprises over 20,000 interaction trajectories from 70+ distinct laboratory task scenarios.

- **Task Complexity Levels**: Tasks are categorized into three levels:
 - **Short-horizon Tasks (≤10 frames)**: Focus on atomic operations like pick, place, pour, primarily capturing instantaneous execution errors.
 - **Medium-horizon Tasks (10-30 frames)**: Involve chaining 2-3 atomic operations (e.g., stirring a beaker), designed to examine failure detection during critical action transitions.
 - **Long-horizon Tasks (>50 frames)**: Represent complex multi-stage workflows (e.g., placing a beaker in a dryer), requiring hierarchical planning and precise fault localization. The dataset oversamples medium and long-horizon tasks.
- **Fine-grained Failure Taxonomy**: The dataset employs a systematic taxonomy of 5 major categories and 11 fine-grained failure types:
 - **Perception Failure (PF)**: Includes Target Positioning Deviation, Operation Position Error, and Grasp Target Misalignment, often arising from challenging optical properties of lab materials.
 - **Grasping Failure (GF)**: Covers Object Slippage and Gripper Control Failure, reflecting end-effector control deficiencies.
 - **Motion Failure (MF)**: Involves Pose Control Error and Incomplete Trajectory, relating to anomalies in trajectory execution.
 - **Logic Failure (LF)**: Encompasses Step Omission and Sequence Reversal, capturing semantic planning errors.
 - **Safety Failure (SF)**: Addresses Improper Handling and Protocol Violation, which carry risks in chemical settings.
- **Paired Contrastive Data**: Success and failure trajectories are generated under identical initial conditions, allowing models to learn the discriminative boundaries between normal and anomalous operations.

**LabRobFail-Bench: Multi-dimensional Evaluation Benchmark**
This benchmark is designed for comprehensive evaluation of robotic resilience in chemical laboratories, organized into three cognitive levels:

- **L1: Task Understanding (Q1)**: Assesses the ability to decompose videos into atomic action sequences and their temporal dependencies.
- **L2: Failure Detection & Localization (Q2-Q3)**: Evaluates binary failure detection (Q2) and precise temporal localization to a specific frame number (Q3).
- **L3: Failure Analysis & Correction (Q4-Q6)**: Targets deeper reasoning by assessing failure severity (Q4, four levels: Minor, Recoverable, Critical, Catastrophic), failure type classification (Q5), and the generation of fine-grained, executable correction plans (Q6) for closed-loop recovery.

**LabRobFail-VLM: Domain-Specialized Vision-Language Model**
Built upon Qwen3-VL, LabRobFail-VLM is adapted for laboratory failure analysis, treating it as a structured, recovery-oriented prediction task.

- **Spatio-Temporal Input Representation**: To manage long-horizon, multi-view robotic videos, the model uses a compact Spatio-Temporal Keyframe Grid. Key timestamps are sampled based on action transitions from multiple synchronized viewpoints (K), arranged into an M × K image grid, with temporal indices explicitly rendered on each image to aid localization.
- **Architecture and Hybrid Fine-tuning**: The model consists of a vision encoder (Eϕ), a modality projector (Pψ), and a language decoder (Dθ). It processes a grid of images and a task instruction to autoregressively predict a composite failure state. A hybrid fine-tuning strategy is employed:
 - **Full fine-tuning** is applied to vision-related components (Eϕ and Pψ) to adapt them to laboratory-specific visual cues (e.g., transparent glassware, reflective liquids, subtle errors).
 - **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)** is applied to the language decoder (Dθ) to adapt it to the structured diagnostic and correction format while retaining its general reasoning capabilities. This asymmetric optimization aims to improve domain adaptation without compromising broad language understanding.
 - The model's generated corrections are designed to be translatable into low-level control primitives for a Franka robot via an Action Dictionary, facilitating closed-loop recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative experimental results evaluating LabRobFail-VLM and baseline models on the proposed benchmark, both in seen and unseen environments.

**Performance on Seen Environments (Table 2)**
LabRobFail-VLM consistently surpassed general-purpose Vision-Language Models across all six evaluation dimensions.
- **Failure Detection (Q2)**: Achieved 90.83% accuracy, indicating a high capability in identifying the presence of a failure.
- **Temporal Localization (Q3)**: Demonstrated 77.21% accuracy, showing proficiency in pinpointing the exact frame where a failure occurs.
- **Severity Assessment (Q4)**: Attained 83.18% accuracy in classifying the risk level of failures.
- **Failure Classification (Q5)**: Reached 73.21% accuracy in categorizing the specific type of failure.
- **Task Understanding (Q1)**: Recorded 94.45% accuracy in decomposing tasks into atomic action sequences.
- **Correction Strategy Generation (Q6)**: Achieved a BLEU-4 score of 0.7362 and a ROUGE-L score of 0.7542, indicating that the generated corrections align well lexically and semantically with ground-truth references. These metrics collectively suggest that the domain-specific adaptation incorporated into LabRobFail-VLM enables accurate diagnosis and actionable recovery instructions.

**Generalization to Unseen Environments (Table 3)**
The model's generalization capabilities were tested in progressively challenging settings involving novel objects, novel scene backgrounds, and a combination of both.
- LabRobFail-VLM maintained superior performance compared to generalist VLMs across all unseen settings.
- In the most challenging "Both" setting (novel objects and scenes), LabRobFail-VLM achieved 71.02% accuracy on Q2 (failure detection), notably outperforming baselines. Its Q6 correction quality remained significantly better, with a BLEU-4 of 0.3269 compared to at most 0.0257 for baselines.
- However, the results also indicated a reduction in performance for Q3 (temporal localization) from 74.39% (Scene) to 41.41% (Both) and Q5 (failure classification) from 72.63% (Scene) to 48.72% (Both) under combined distribution shifts, acknowledging robustness under such shifts as an area for further research.

**Ablation Studies (Table 4)**
An ablation study on the Seen split investigated the impact of key design choices.
- **Hybrid Fine-tuning**: Full fine-tuning of the entire model resulted in degraded performance across Q2, Q3, Q5, and Q6. Conversely, freezing both the vision encoder and projector led to the worst performance, including a 17.00-point drop in Q3, thereby validating the effectiveness of the asymmetric optimization strategy.
- **Temporal Indices**: Removing the explicit temporal indices from the spatio-temporal input representation caused a 6.63-point drop in Q3 accuracy, confirming their importance for frame-level failure localization.

**Downstream Policy Recovery (Table 5)**
LabRobFail-VLM was integrated as an external supervisor with existing robotic manipulation policies (OpenVLA and ACT) to evaluate its impact on closed-loop recovery.
- The integration resulted in an improvement of 4–16 percentage points in downstream task success rates across eight different tasks.
- The largest improvement was observed for the "Pour" task under the ACT policy, increasing from 32% to 48%.
- Despite these improvements, the absolute success rates for harder tasks remained low, indicating that fully reliable laboratory recovery is still an open challenge.

**Transferability Value of LabRobFail-Data (Table 6)**
To assess the utility of the collected dataset, the AHA model (a baseline) was pre-trained using LabRobFail-Data.
- Pre-training with LabRobFail-Data consistently led to performance improvements: a +4.1% increase in accuracy when used in isolation (59.87% to 63.96%) and a +3.9% increase even when combined with large-scale auxiliary datasets (68.85% to 72.74%).
- This suggests that LabRobFail-Data provides unique and complementary failure semantics, particularly regarding liquid dynamics and other lab-specific phenomena, which contributes critical prior knowledge for enhancing generalizable robustness in failure analysis models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LabRobFail framework represents a comprehensive contribution to the field of robotic autonomy in scientific laboratories. Its significance lies in addressing critical limitations present in existing research, particularly the scarcity of failure data and the lack of fine-grained evaluation protocols tailored for safety-critical environments.

The development of **LabRobFail-Sim** provides a systematic and scalable approach to generating diverse and annotated failure trajectories. By injecting controllable perturbations at control, physics, and semantic levels, the framework enables the creation of large-scale datasets that are otherwise difficult or hazardous to obtain in the real world. This capability is crucial for training robust embodied agents that can identify and respond to a wide range of anomalies.

**LabRobFail-Data**, with its over 20,000 trajectories, 70+ task scenarios, and a detailed taxonomy of 11 fine-grained failure types across five major categories, establishes a novel and extensive resource for the research community. The inclusion of paired contrastive data (success and failure under identical conditions) is particularly valuable for enabling models to learn the subtle distinctions between normal and anomalous operations.

The introduction of **LabRobFail-Bench** is a key contribution, as it defines the first multi-dimensional evaluation standard for robotic failure reasoning in chemical laboratories. Its six dimensions, spanning task understanding, failure detection, precise temporal localization, severity assessment, failure classification, and fine-grained correction planning, move beyond coarse evaluation metrics. This benchmark directly supports the development of agents capable of risk-aware, timely, and precise interventions required in irreversible chemical processes.

The **LabRobFail-VLM** demonstrates the efficacy of domain-specialized models for complex failure analysis. Its architecture, employing spatio-temporal input representation and a hybrid fine-tuning strategy, achieves significantly higher accuracy in failure detection, localization, and classification compared to general-purpose VLMs. Critically, its ability to generate actionable and fine-grained correction strategies directly addresses the need for closed-loop recovery, improving downstream task success rates in manipulation tasks.

The **potential impact** of this work is substantial for advancing reliable autonomous discovery in scientific domains. By providing the tools and methodologies for comprehensive failure analysis, LabRobFail can facilitate the development of more trustworthy and robust robotic systems for SDLs. This could accelerate scientific research by enabling safer, more efficient, and less error-prone experimentation, reducing material waste, and preventing hazardous incidents. The benchmark and dataset serve as foundational resources for future research, promoting the development of next-generation embodied agents that can effectively perceive, diagnose, and recover from failures in complex, high-stakes environments.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors include that the training data is entirely synthetic, which may result in a visual domain gap when deploying models in real-world environments. Additionally, the reliance on a deterministic Action Dictionary for converting semantic correction instructions into robot control primitives restricts recovery flexibility. Future work aims to bridge this gap by extending VLA experiments and enabling policies to directly interpret open-ended language corrections. Despite these limitations, LabRobFail represents a significant step towards achieving reliable autonomy in chemical self-driving laboratories.
