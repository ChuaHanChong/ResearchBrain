# Grounding Driving VLA via Inverse Kinematics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST developed an inverse kinematics-inspired architecture for Driving Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that predicts future visual states to ground trajectory planning. This approach enables a 0.5B-parameter model to achieve planning performance comparable to or exceeding larger 7B-8B VLAs on NAVSIM and nuScenes benchmarks, significantly improving visual grounding and reaction to dynamic scenes.

## Problem

- Existing Driving Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit 'blind planning,' where they largely ignore visual input and primarily rely on ego status and text commands for trajectory prediction.
- The task formulation is structurally ill-posed, lacking explicit incentives for models to infer or utilize future visual states, which leads to a reliance on non-visual shortcuts for trajectory generation.
- The problem manifests as both a supervision-side gap (absence of dense visual supervision for future states) and an architecture-side bypass, allowing the trajectory decoder to disregard visual tokens.

## Method

- The methodology re-designs Driving VLA by incorporating a next visual state prediction objective, where the Large Language Model (LLM) is trained to output future visual tokens at a defined time horizon.
- An Inverse Kinematics (IK) Network is introduced specifically for trajectory prediction, taking only the current visual state and the LLM's predicted future visual state as inputs, thereby creating an architectural bottleneck that enforces visual grounding.
- The IK Network is implemented as a ==cross-attention==-based conditional ==diffusion model== and is trained jointly with the next visual state prediction and a question-answering task to enhance overall scene understanding.

## Results

- The 0.5B model achieved a PDMS of 92.2 on NAVSIM-v1 and 90.6 on NAVSIM-v2, representing improvements of +19.0 and +20.4 over the OpenDriveVLA* baseline, and performing comparably to or better than 7B-8B VLAs.
- Visual grounding was significantly enhanced; in counterfactual obstacle stitching experiments, the model decelerated in **63.8%** of cases (compared to **44.2%** for OpenDriveVLA-0.5B) and showed superior GradCAM–Object Overlap scores.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Inverse Kinematics Network and the next visual state prediction objective are critical for maintaining visual grounding and achieving high trajectory accuracy, especially in dynamic driving scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'blind planning' observed in Driving VLAs stems from a fundamental task formulation issue, where models are not explicitly incentivized to reason about future visual states.
> - Drawing an analogy to inverse kinematics, enforcing future visual state prediction as a necessary boundary condition for trajectory decoding, coupled with an architectural bottleneck, compels models to utilize visual information effectively.
> - Structural innovations that ensure visual grounding can lead to substantial performance gains, allowing smaller VLA models to achieve or surpass the planning capabilities of much larger models by better leveraging visual data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving research has seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to unify perception, prediction, and planning into a single end-to-end policy. This approach offers advantages in simplicity and scalability compared to traditional modular pipelines. Driving VLAs typically take camera images and textual commands as input and generate future driving trajectories.

However, existing Driving VLAs often exhibit a phenomenon termed "blind planning," where they largely ignore visual input, relying instead on ego status and text commands to determine trajectories. This behavior has been observed in representative VLAs, where synthesized obstacles in the visual scene minimally alter the predicted path. Previous work identified "ego status sticking" as a partial aspect of this issue; this paper extends that observation to a more systematic disregard for visual tokens.

The current work positions itself as a response to this identified limitation in Driving VLAs. It re-frames the trajectory generation task by drawing an analogy to inverse kinematics, a concept typically used in robotics to determine the joint parameters required to achieve a desired end-effector position. By proposing a new task formulation and architectural design, the authors aim to restore the causal influence of visual features on trajectory planning, thereby enhancing the models' grounding in the driving environment. The work contributes to the broader field of end-to-end learning by addressing the general problem where models tend to disregard input modalities not directly involved in the supervision target. It contrasts with existing world model-policy combinations by making future visual prediction a mandatory boundary condition for trajectory decoding, rather than an auxiliary signal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is the recovery of visual grounding in Driving Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors identify that existing Driving VLAs often predict trajectories without effectively utilizing their visual inputs, a phenomenon they term "blind planning." This is not attributed to insufficient training data or model capacity, but rather to a "structurally ill-posed task formulation."

The core motivation stems from an analogy to inverse kinematics. In classical inverse kinematics, determining a trajectory requires both a starting configuration and a target configuration as boundary conditions. The authors observe that existing Driving VLAs are typically provided only with the current visual state (V_t) and are directly supervised on the trajectory output (T_t). This formulation, they argue, provides no explicit incentive for the model to infer or utilize a future visual state. Consequently, the model learns a "shortcut" path, relying predominantly on readily available ego status (E_t) and textual commands (C_t) to fit the trajectory, as these inputs are often sufficient for predicting plausible trajectories in many routine driving scenarios. This aligns with a known pitfall in end-to-end learning: if an input modality (e.g., visual scene) is not explicitly part of the output space or directly rewarded, the model may bypass it.

The problem is thus characterized by two facets:
1. **Supervision-side gap:** The model is not explicitly tasked with reasoning about future visual scenes, thus lacking dense visual supervision related to future states.
2. **Architecture-side bypass:** The trajectory decoder is free to disregard visual tokens entirely, instead leveraging ego status and text commands directly.

To address these issues, the research aims to fundamentally redesign the Driving VLA. The goal is to mathematically enforce the utilization of visual features, making them an essential element in optimizing the loss function and thus compelling the model to perform vision-grounded planning. The proposed solution involves introducing a mechanism to predict a future visual state, serving as an explicit terminal condition, and restructuring the trajectory decoding process to be solely conditioned on the current and predicted future visual states, thereby blocking direct non-visual shortcuts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology re-designs Driving VLA inspired by inverse kinematics, involving two primary modifications to existing VLA architectures: a next visual state prediction objective and a dedicated Inverse Kinematics (IK) Network. The overall VLA architecture comprises a Visual Encoder, a Large Language Model (LLM), and the novel IK Network.

**Visual Feature Extraction and LLM Input:**
- Multi-camera inputs (front, front-left, front-right images) are fed into a DINOv3 ViT-S/16 visual encoder.
- The extracted visual features are then passed through an MLP projector to obtain visual tokens (V_t).
- These visual tokens, along with MLP-encoded ego status (E_t) and text instructions (C_t), are input to the LLM.
- Questions for a Question and Answering (QA) task are also provided to the LLM.

**Next Visual State Prediction:**
- The LLM's primary task, in addition to QA, is to predict a future visual state (ˆV_t+∆) at a defined horizon (N seconds ahead).
- The LLM is trained to output these predicted future visual tokens in a specified format (e.g., between `<next_start>` and `<next_end>` tokens).
- Supervision is provided by an MSE loss (L_visual) comparing the LLM's predicted hidden states for ˆV_t+∆ against ground-truth future visual tokens (V_t+∆). These ground-truth tokens are obtained by passing actual future images through the same visual encoder and projector.
- This objective provides dense visual supervision, forcing the LLM to learn and reason about future visual scenes. The theoretical diagnosis indicates this objective provides a learning signal for visual parameters even in non-vision-critical situations, which constitute the majority of data.

**Inverse Kinematics (IK) Network:**
- A separate network, the IK Network, is introduced to perform trajectory prediction.
- Crucially, this network takes *only* the current visual state (V_t) and the future visual state (ˆV_t+∆) predicted by the LLM as its inputs. It does not directly receive ego status (E_t) or text commands (C_t).
- This design creates an architectural bottleneck, compelling the model to derive the trajectory solely from the visual changes between the current and predicted future scenes. This is intended to suppress direct shortcuts through ego status or text commands.
- The IK Network is implemented as a cross-attention-based conditional diffusion model. A noisy trajectory (x_t) acts as the query, while the current (Z_t) and predicted next (ˆZ_t+∆t) BEV tokens serve as keys and values in two stacked cross-attention layers.
- The model is trained using a DDPM objective (L_ik) with a cosine β-schedule. At inference, 50 steps of DDPM sampling are used to generate the final trajectory.
- The next visual state prediction loss (L_visual) and the IK Network's trajectory prediction loss (L_ik) are jointly trained, with specified weights (λ_state and λ_traj).

**Question and Answering (QA):**
- In parallel with next visual state prediction, the LLM is also trained on a QA task using scene description datasets like nuCaption and NuX.
- This multi-task training is intended to enhance the LLM's general scene understanding capabilities.

**Architectural and Training Details:**
- **LLM Backbone:** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct.
- **Visual Encoder:** DINOv3 ViT-S/16.
- **IK Network:** Hidden dimension 256, 8 attention heads, 2 cross-attention blocks.
- **Datasets:** NAVSIM-v1/v2 (closed-loop) and nuScenes (open-loop, including nu-Caption, nu-X, nuScenes-QA).
- **Training:** AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule, linear warmup, gradient clipping. Different learning rates for LLM backbone and IK Network.
 - NAVSIM: 2-stage SFT → GRPO, 4s future horizon, 8 waypoints.
 - nuScenes: 4 epochs, joint training, 3s future horizon, 6 waypoints.
- **Baselines for comparison:** OpenDriveVLA* (a re-implementation of OpenDriveVLA for fair architectural comparison on NAVSIM), and various larger-scale (2B to 10B) VLA models like ARTEMIS, DriveVLA-W0, ReCogDrive, DriveFine, Alpamayo-R1, and Impromptu-VLA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results across two benchmarks and several analyses to support its claims regarding recovered visual grounding and improved planning performance.

**Performance on NAVSIM Benchmarks (Closed-Loop):**
- **NAVSIM-v1:** The 0.5B model achieved a PDMS (Planning-Driven Metrics Score) of 92.2. This represented an improvement of +19.0 over the OpenDriveVLA* baseline (73.2) sharing the same backbone and training parameters. This score was comparable to or surpassed several larger (7B–8B) VLA models, including ARTEMIS (7B), DriveVLA-W0 (7B), and even exceeded DriveFine (8B) by 0.4 points. Safety metrics (No-Collision (NC) 99.1 and Time-To-Collision (TTC) 99.1) were also comparable to or better than those of larger baselines.
- **NAVSIM-v2:** On this more challenging benchmark, the 0.5B model achieved an EPDMS of 90.6, showing a large improvement of +20.4 over the OpenDriveVLA* baseline (70.2). This placed its performance on par with or above DriveFine-8B (89.7) and DriveVLA-W0-7B (86.5). Detailed metrics like NC (99.2), TLC (99.9), TTC (99.2), and Lane Keeping (LK, 97.4) were also competitive with or superior to 7B–8B baselines.

**Performance on nuScenes Benchmark (Open-Loop):**
- The 0.5B model achieved an Average Displacement Error (ADE) of 0.06, which represented a reduction of −0.27 compared to the 7B-scale OpenDriveVLA. The collision rate was 0.09, comparable to or better than larger models. This suggests that the visual grounding established through the proposed method contributes to trajectory accuracy.

**Visual Grounding Analysis:**
- **Counterfactual Obstacle Stitching:** In an experiment where a vehicle sprite was synthetically placed in front of the ego vehicle on nuScenes, the proposed 0.5B model decelerated on 63.8% of perturbed samples, with an average 3-second endpoint shortening of −1.04 m. This significantly exceeded OpenDriveVLA-0.5B (44.2% deceleration, −0.06 m shortening) and Alpamayo-R1-10B (54.5% deceleration, −0.12 m shortening). Further analysis confirmed that the response was position-aware, with greater trajectory alteration when the sprite was on the road versus in the sky, even for sprites of the same scale.
- **GradCAM–Object Overlap:** Evaluation using GradCAM on nuScenes showed that the proposed method achieved the highest object alignment. Pointing Mass was 0.268 (+31% over OpenDriveVLA), Average Precision was 0.281 (+16% over OpenDriveVLA), and IoU@top20 was 0.145, along with F1@top20 of 0.236. Qualitative visualizations indicated that GradCAM saliency maps from the proposed model concentrated on planning-relevant objects (vehicles, pedestrians), unlike baselines that exhibited more dispersed attention.

**Ablation Study (on nuScenes):**
- **Without IK Network (direct MLP trajectory prediction):** Removing the IK Network and using a direct MLP head for trajectory prediction caused a substantial degradation in ADE from 0.06 to 0.31 (a nearly 5-fold increase), indicating the critical role of the IK Network's input bottleneck in maintaining visual grounding.
- **Without Next State Prediction (current state reconstruction instead):** Replacing the next visual state prediction objective with a current visual state reconstruction objective resulted in a small average ADE degradation (from 0.06 to 0.07). However, a detailed analysis revealed that this gap was concentrated in dynamic driving situations, specifically turns, where ego angular velocity was significantly higher (p = 1.2 × 10^-14). This suggests that predicting future states is particularly beneficial in complex, visually dynamic scenarios.
- **Without Diffusion in IK (cross-attention pooling + MLP):** Replacing the diffusion-based IK Network with a deterministic cross-attention pooling head and MLP regression resulted in an ADE of 0.16. While still better than removing the IK Network entirely (0.31), it indicated that the generative diffusion process further refined trajectory precision.

**Question and Answering (QA) Performance:**
- **nu-Caption:** The model outperformed baselines, including OpenDriveVLA, across BLEU metrics and achieved a higher BERT-Score (97.6 vs. 91.9), suggesting improved semantic understanding.
- **nu-X:** Showed improvements in CIDEr (35.1), BLEU-4 (5.8), and METEOR (20.2) over OpenDriveVLA, indicating more meaningful reasoning.
- **nuScenes-QA:** Achieved overall accuracy (56.9) slightly below OpenDriveVLA (58.4). Performance was comparable in "Existence" and "Comparison" categories but showed larger gaps in categories requiring fine-grained object identification (e.g., Counting, Object, Status), which is attributed to the use of perspective-view features compared to OpenDriveVLA's BEV encoder pretrained on nuScenes.

**Inference Speed:**
- The 0.5B model demonstrated an end-to-end inference latency of approximately 344 ms (2.9 FPS) on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU. This was competitive and notably faster than OpenDriveVLA-0.5B reported on an A100.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving: the tendency for "blind planning," where models disregard visual input and rely on non-visual shortcuts. By theoretically diagnosing this as a structurally ill-posed task formulation and proposing a novel inverse kinematics-inspired design, the work offers a remedy that could significantly enhance the reliability and safety of autonomous driving systems.

The primary impact is the demonstration that explicit architectural and training modifications focused on enforcing visual grounding can yield substantial performance improvements in trajectory planning. The fact that a relatively compact 0.5B-scale model achieved or surpassed the planning performance of much larger (7B–8B) VLAs on both closed-loop (NAVSIM) and open-loop (nuScenes) benchmarks is noteworthy. This suggests that structural innovation aimed at better utilizing visual data may be more impactful than simply scaling up the size of the underlying Large Language Model. Such efficiency gains are crucial for deploying advanced autonomous driving capabilities on resource-constrained platforms.

The recovery of visual grounding, as quantitatively verified through counterfactual stitching experiments and GradCAM analysis, indicates that the model is more attuned to dynamic visual cues in the environment. This is particularly relevant for safety-critical scenarios, as evidenced by improved No-Collision and Time-To-Collision scores in closed-loop simulations, and the model's ability to react appropriately to stitched obstacles.

Beyond trajectory planning, the observed improvements in scene understanding tasks like nu-Caption and nu-X suggest that the dense visual supervision from next-state prediction enhances the LLM's general comprehension of driving environments. While a minor trade-off in fine-grained object identification was observed on nuScenes-QA due to the choice of a generic perspective-view visual encoder (DINOv3) for broader applicability, the overall gains in core driving tasks remain significant.

Finally, the work highlights a broader principle in end-to-end learning: models may disregard input modalities that are not directly tied to the supervision target. The proposed inverse kinematics formulation, by explicitly requiring the prediction of a future visual state as a boundary condition for trajectory decoding, offers a potential blueprint for other embodied AI policies where actions are derived from desired state transitions. The competitive inference speed also suggests the practical viability of this approach for real-world, latency-sensitive applications.
