# HoloScene: Simulation-Ready Interactive 3D Worlds from a Single Video

> [!summary] Summary
> HoloScene, developed by researchers at UIUC and Intel, generates comprehensive, simulation-ready interactive 3D digital twins from a single video input by unifying geometry completeness, physical plausibility, and photorealistic rendering. The framework achieves significantly higher physical stability (e.g., an 81.7% stable ratio on the Replica dataset) and robustly completes object geometry, enabling diverse applications in AR/VR, gaming, and robotics.

## Problem

- Existing 3D reconstruction techniques struggle to create physically plausible and interactive digital twins, often producing static models with incomplete geometry, especially for occluded regions.
- Prior "Real2Sim" methods frequently result in physically unstable environments or object interpenetrations, hindering reliable dynamic simulations for robotics and interactive applications.
- A lack of a unified framework capable of simultaneously achieving geometry completeness, object interactivity, physical plausibility, photorealistic rendering, and accurate physical properties from a single video.

## Method

- Represents scenes using an interactive 3D scene graph, defining objects with neural SDFs, Gaussian splats for appearance, and physical properties, while edges encode inter-object relationships like support and collision.
- Formulates reconstruction as an energy minimization problem, combining observation terms from the input video with regularization terms for geometry completeness (using generative priors), inter-object penetration, and physical stability.
- Implements a three-stage inference strategy: gradient-based initialization, generative shape sampling with a physics-aware structured tree search for plausibility, and final appearance finetuning, leveraging a non-differentiable rigid-body simulator.

## Results

- The system achieves a 100% object reconstruction ratio and an 81.7% stable ratio on the Replica dataset, demonstrating superior physical plausibility and stability compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- It produces highly competitive geometry accuracy and consistent photorealistic rendering quality (e.g., high PSNR, SSIM, low LPIPS), effectively completing occluded and invisible geometry using generative priors.
- HoloScene's output facilitates diverse interactive applications, including real-time game environments, physically plausible 3D editing, and re-simulation of real-world interactions, showcasing its functional versatility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustly generating simulation-ready digital twins requires unifying observational data, generative priors for shape completion, and explicit physical constraints within an energy-based optimization framework.
> - An interactive 3D scene graph, encoding object geometry, appearance, physics, and inter-object relationships, is essential for modeling complex physical interactions and achieving structural stability.
> - A hybrid inference strategy, combining differentiable optimization with generative sampling, structured tree search, and a non-differentiable physics simulator, is effective for handling the challenges of achieving physical realism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The digitization of the physical world into virtual environments is a rapidly evolving field, central to advancements in augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR), gaming, robotics, and digital content creation. HoloScene contributes significantly to this landscape by addressing several critical, long-standing challenges that current 3D reconstruction and scene understanding methods often fail to overcome simultaneously.

Historically, 3D reconstruction techniques like photogrammetry and more recent neural radiance fields (NeRFs) have excelled at generating photorealistic renderings from novel viewpoints. However, these methods typically produce static, non-interactive representations that lack complete geometry (especially for occluded regions) and inherent physical properties, rendering them unsuitable for dynamic simulations or interactive manipulation. Similarly, neural implicit surfaces (e.g., SDFs) improve geometry representation but still struggle with interactivity and physical plausibility.

In parallel, research in "Real2Sim" (real-to-simulation) environments aims to create virtual replicas from real-world data for robotics and policy learning. While these approaches enable interactable environments, they often compromise on physical realism or struggle with unstable physics. Other methods, like amodal reconstruction or those focusing on individual objects, provide geometry completion but neglect inter-object relationships or physical stability. Works like PhyRecon incorporate differentiable physics but are limited to object-ground interactions, overlooking complex inter-object dynamics. Generative models, such as those leveraging diffusion priors (e.g., DP-Recon, Video2Game, Drawer), can complete shapes but may lack physical stability or high fidelity to the specific input scene.

HoloScene distinguishes itself by offering a unified framework that *simultaneously* achieves geometry completeness (even for occluded parts), object interactivity, physical plausibility, photorealistic rendering, and accurate physical properties—all from a single video input. It bridges the gap between high-fidelity visual reconstruction and functional, simulation-ready digital twins. By representing scenes as interactive 3D scene graphs and integrating observational data, generative priors (for shape completion), and physical constraints (for stability) into a unified energy-based optimization, HoloScene moves beyond the limitations of prior work. Its novel hybrid inference strategy, combining generative sampling with structured tree search and non-differentiable physics simulation (Isaac Sim), specifically tackles the complexities of discrete and continuous variables and non-differentiable terms in physical realism, making it a robust advancement in creating truly interactive and dynamic virtual copies of the real world. This positions HoloScene at the forefront of creating practical, high-fidelity digital twins capable of supporting advanced applications in AR/VR, gaming, and robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of HoloScene is to transform a single input video of a physical environment into an accurate, simulation-ready, and interactive 3D digital twin. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations in existing 3D reconstruction and scene understanding methods:

1. **Comprehensive Digital Twin Creation:** The ultimate goal is to enable users to "digitize our environment into a fully interactive digital twin: complete, composable, photorealistic, and manipulable just like the real world." This moves beyond merely capturing visual appearance to creating a functional replica.

2. **Unifying Disparate Requirements:** Current methods typically excel in one or two aspects but fall short in others. HoloScene aims to be the first framework that *simultaneously* achieves:
 - **Geometry Completeness:** Reconstructing the full 3D shape of objects, including occluded or unobserved regions, which is crucial for realistic interaction.
 - **Object Interactivity:** Allowing users or autonomous agents to manipulate individual objects within the scene.
 - **Physical Plausibility and Stability:** Ensuring that the reconstructed objects behave according to real-world physics, maintaining static equilibrium, and interacting realistically during dynamic simulations.
 - **Photorealistic Rendering:** Producing high-quality visual output from novel viewpoints, essential for immersive experiences.
 - **Accurate Physical Properties:** Inferring properties like mass, friction, and restitution, necessary for reliable physical simulation engines.

3. **Addressing Limitations of Current Approaches:**
 - **Incomplete Geometry:** Many methods produce incomplete shapes, especially in occluded areas, making digital twins unusable for robust interaction.
 - **Lack of Interactivity and Physicality:** Standard 3D models (e.g., from photogrammetry or basic NeRFs) are often static and lack physical properties, making objects "lifeless."
 - **Unstable Physics:** Existing "Real2Sim" methods sometimes produce models that are physically unstable or suffer from interpenetrations, leading to unrealistic or broken simulations.
 - **Reliance on External Assets:** Some approaches depend on retrieving pre-existing 3D assets, which sacrifices fidelity to the specific captured scene.

4. **Enabling Downstream Applications:** By creating such a comprehensive digital twin, HoloScene seeks to unlock significant opportunities across various fields:
 - **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Creating immersive and interactive experiences.
 - **Gaming:** Generating realistic, interactive game environments from real-world footage.
 - **Robotics:** Providing high-fidelity, physically accurate simulation environments for robot learning and testing, reducing the "sim-to-real" gap.
 - **3D Content Creation and Editing:** Offering tools for dynamic visual effects and real-time scene manipulation.

In essence, HoloScene's motivation stems from the desire to create truly functional and indistinguishable digital counterparts of real-world environments, moving beyond passive visual representations to active, interactive, and physically consistent virtual spaces.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HoloScene's methodology revolves around a novel interactive 3D scene graph representation and a multi-stage, energy-based optimization framework designed to integrate observational data, physical constraints, and generative priors.

**Input:** The system takes a single input video sequence, along with visual cues such as camera poses (inferred or ground truth) and instance masks (inferred or ground truth). It also leverages monocular depth and normal priors.

**Core Scene Representation: Interactive 3D Scene Graph (G = (V, E))**
The scene is represented as a graph where:
- **Nodes (V):** Each node `v_i` represents either the background or one of the `N` objects. Each node comprises:
 - **Geometry (`g_i`):** An instance-level neural Signed Distance Function (SDF) provides continuous 3D shape. A mesh representation (`M_i`) is extracted from the SDF using Marching Cubes for physical simulation and efficient rendering.
 - **Appearance (`f_i`):** Encoded as Gaussian splats for real-time, high-quality rendering. A "Gaussians-on-Mesh" (GoM) approach ensures alignment between appearance, geometry, and physical interactions.
 - **Physical Properties (`p_i`):** Defined for rigid bodies, including mass (`m_i`), friction (`κ_i`), damping (`ζ_i`), and restitution (`r_i`). These parameters govern physical interactions.
 - **Dynamic States (`T_i`):** A rigid transform from object-centric to world coordinates, representing the object's pose.
- **Edges (E):** Edges `e_i,j` encode object-object relationships:
 - **Support:** A hierarchical relationship where one object rests stably on another (forms a tree structure).
 - **Beside:** Siblings with touching surfaces, indicating occlusion without hierarchy.
 - **Collide:** Contacts leading to dynamic effects, used in simulation.
This scene graph fundamentally supports physical interactions, allowing a rigid-body simulator (`Sim`) to compute next states based on current states and actions (Eq. 1).

**Problem Formulation: Structured Energy Minimization (Eq. 2)**
HoloScene formulates the reconstruction as an energy minimization problem, combining several terms:
- **Observation Terms:** Quantify the fidelity to the input video:
 - `E_rgb`: Combines MSE and LPIPS losses between rendered and input RGB images.
 - `E_mask`: Cross-entropy loss for instance masks.
 - `E_mono`: L2 loss for rendered depth and normal maps against monocular priors.
- **Regularization Terms:** Impose generative, geometric, and physical priors:
 - **`E_comp` (Completeness Energy):** Encourages full shape reconstruction. For each object, virtual observations are synthesized using a pretrained multi-view diffusion model (Wonder3D), with occluded regions first inpainted using LaMa. The completeness energy is then measured by observation losses against these synthetic views (Eq. 3).
 - **`E_geo` (Geometry Energy):** Ensures geometry compatibility and prevents inter-object penetration. It includes an SDF-penetration term (`E_pene_sdf`) and a mesh-penetration term (`E_pene_mesh`) (Eq. 4).
 - **`E_physics` (Physics Energy):** Ensures physical plausibility and simulability:
 - `E_stable`: Quantifies deviation after a single step of physical simulation under gravity, ensuring static equilibrium using a non-differentiable simulator (Isaac Sim).
 - `E_touch`: Encourages contact between supporting object pairs using Chamfer distance between meshes (Eq. 5).

**Inference Strategy: Three-Stage Divide-and-Conquer Approach**
Optimizing the complex energy function is addressed by a hybrid approach:
1. **Stage 1: Gradient-Based Optimization for Initialization:**
 - Initializes each object's appearance and geometry by minimizing observation terms and SDF-penetration regularization.
 - Uses differentiable volume rendering, similar to neural SDF methods, to get per-instance SDFs.
 - Recovers small objects by balancing training samples across instances.
 - Extracts initial meshes and refines Gaussians via splat rendering.
2. **Stage 2: Generative Sampling & Tree Search for Geometry and Physical Plausibility:**
 - **Scene Graph Edges Creation:** Infers the scene graph topology by analyzing physical contact and support relationships between initial instance meshes, building a support tree rooted at the background.
 - **Generative Sampling:** For each instance, diverse, complete shape candidates are generated by prompting Wonder3D with real-world observations and resampled with different seeds, leading to multiple 3D shape hypotheses.
 - **Structured Tree Search:** A breadth-first traversal on the scene graph selects the most physically plausible combination of sampled shapes. At each node, the `E_physics` is evaluated for samples, retaining the one with the lowest energy. The object's state and physical parameters are adjusted to enforce stability and prevent interpenetration (e.g., pruning SDF values, dynamically optimizing vertex locations after marching cubes for collision avoidance). This step uses a non-differentiable simulator (Isaac Sim).
3. **Stage 3: Final Finetuning for Realism:**
 - Refines Gaussians globally for all objects using splat rendering by minimizing the observation terms via gradient descent, ensuring complete and realistic appearance after shape and pose adjustments in Stage 2.

This comprehensive approach allows HoloScene to robustly reconstruct simulation-ready interactive 3D worlds from limited visual input, integrating diverse aspects like generative priors, neural rendering, and rigid-body physics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HoloScene demonstrates superior performance across various metrics related to geometry accuracy, physical plausibility, and rendering quality, while also showcasing its broad applicability in interactive environments.

**Quantitative Scene-Level Evaluations (Table 2):**
HoloScene was evaluated against state-of-the-art baselines (ObjectSDF++, PhyRecon, DP-Recon) on Replica, Scannet++, and iGibson datasets.
- **Physical Plausibility:** HoloScene achieves significantly higher **Object Reconstruction Ratio (OR%)** and **Stable Ratio (Stable %)**. For instance, on Replica, it achieves 100% OR% and 81.7% Stable % (all instances), vastly outperforming baselines (e.g., DP-Recon: 56.3% OR%, 8.5% Stable %). This highlights HoloScene's effectiveness in generating complete objects that remain physically stable under gravity within a simulator.
- **Geometry Accuracy:** While other methods might show slightly better metrics on certain datasets for some geometry measures (e.g., DP-Recon on Replica's F1), HoloScene generally maintains comparable or highly competitive Chamfer Distance (CD), F-Score (F1), and Normal Consistency (NC), often ranking as the second best or very close to the best, especially when considering its superior physical stability. This indicates that HoloScene's physics-aware optimization does not heavily compromise geometric fidelity.
- **Rendering Quality:** HoloScene consistently provides high PSNR, SSIM, and low LPIPS scores, demonstrating its ability to render photorealistic images from novel viewpoints. Its rendering performance is competitive with, and often superior to, baselines capable of real-time rendering. The use of Gaussians-on-Mesh proves effective for high-quality, real-time visuals.

**Quantitative Object-Level Evaluations (Table 2 - iGibson Object):**
On the iGibson dataset, which offers ground truth for individual objects (including occluded regions), HoloScene again shows strong performance, particularly in reconstructing invisible and occluded parts. It outperforms baselines in both geometry and appearance metrics for individual objects, validating the effectiveness of its generative priors and completion approach.

**Qualitative Results (Figures 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8):**
- **Geometry and Appearance:** HoloScene produces superior reconstructions by smoothly inpainting occluded regions using LaMa and completing invisible back-facing geometry with Wonder3D. Unlike baselines that suffer from object interpenetration or incomplete shapes, HoloScene's reconstructions are visually and geometrically complete.
- **Physical Stability:** Visual comparisons vividly demonstrate that HoloScene's geometry layouts remain stable when gravity is applied in a physics simulator, while baselines often exhibit object interpenetration, instability, or objects falling due to incorrect contact modeling. The Gaussians-on-Mesh approach ensures high-quality rendering throughout these simulations.

**Ablation Study (Table 3):**
The ablation study confirms the crucial contributions of HoloScene's key components:
- Switching from textured mesh to Gaussian rendering improves visual quality.
- The inclusion of physics energy combined with generative priors significantly enhances physical stability.
- The scene graph, by modeling inter-object relationships, further improves physics performance, especially for objects in support relationships.
- A discovered trade-off indicates that while optimizing for physical plausibility dramatically increases stability, it might occasionally lead to minor deviations in pixel-alignment with original observations compared to purely visual reconstruction methods.

**Failure Rate Analysis (Table 4):**
HoloScene achieves remarkably lower failure rates in both geometry and physics compared to baselines.
- **Geometry Failure (iGibson):** HoloScene eliminates "invalid geometry" failures (0 out of 97 instances), significantly reducing "partial" and "oversized" shape failures compared to baselines that lack robust shape priors.
- **Physics Failure (All Datasets):** HoloScene has the lowest total physics failure rate (24% overall compared to 60-90% for baselines). Notably, it completely eliminates "invalid shapes" and "overlap" failures (0 instances each), and drastically reduces "penetration" failures. Most remaining failures are "drifting," indicating minor positional instability rather than catastrophic collapse. This robust performance is attributed to the integration of generative priors and physics-aware constraints.

**Interactive Environment Applications:**
HoloScene's output enables diverse applications:
- **Real-Time Interactive Game:** Reconstructed environments and objects can be imported into game engines (e.g., Unreal Engine), allowing agents to interact with physically plausible objects.
- **Interactive 3D Editing:** Users can manipulate objects (e.g., reposition a chair) with their underlying physical mesh geometry and Gaussians, resulting in coherent scene modifications.
- **Immersive Experience Recording:** The reconstructed 3D objects facilitate recovering 6D object poses from videos of real-world interactions and re-simulating their motions in virtual scenes.
- **Dynamic Visual Effects:** Augmented scenes with rigid body simulations, character animations, and particle effects (e.g., dropping objects, adding fires) blend naturally with the physically consistent environment.

In summary, HoloScene successfully delivers on its ambitious goal of creating highly functional, simulation-ready, and interactive digital twins from single videos, showcasing robust performance across fidelity, physics, and applicability metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HoloScene represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D reconstruction and scene understanding, with profound implications across various domains. Its core significance lies in its ability to generate truly "simulation-ready" interactive 3D worlds from a single video, overcoming limitations that have long hindered the creation of functional digital twins.

1. **Unified Solution for Digital Twin Creation:** HoloScene provides a unified framework that simultaneously addresses geometry completeness, physical plausibility, object interactivity, and photorealistic rendering. This stands in stark contrast to previous methods that often excel in one area but fail in others, making them unsuitable for comprehensive digital twin applications. By integrating a rich scene graph representation, energy-based optimization, and a novel hybrid inference strategy, HoloScene pushes the boundaries of what's achievable from limited visual input.

2. **Bridging the Reality-Simulation Gap:** For fields like robotics, where the "sim-to-real" gap is a major challenge, HoloScene's ability to create physically stable and accurate simulation environments directly from real-world videos is transformative. Robots can learn and be tested in virtual replicas that closely mimic real-world physics, accelerating development and deployment. The explicit enforcement of physical stability via simulator-in-the-loop optimization (using Isaac Sim) is a key innovation for robust real-world applications.

3. **Enhanced Immersive Experiences:** In AR/VR and gaming, HoloScene enables the creation of highly immersive and interactive virtual environments. Users can interact with digital objects that not only look realistic but also behave physically accurately, leading to more engaging and believable experiences. The real-time rendering capabilities afforded by the Gaussians-on-Mesh representation further enhance this immersion.

4. **Robustness and Reliability:** The detailed failure analysis demonstrates HoloScene's superior robustness. By eliminating common issues like invalid geometry, inter-object penetration, and instability, the framework produces digital twins that are more reliable for downstream tasks, reducing the need for extensive manual cleanup or re-creation. The generative priors play a crucial role in completing occluded geometry, ensuring structural integrity where observations are sparse.

5. **Democratization of 3D Content Creation:** By simplifying the process of creating complex, interactive 3D scenes from a single video, HoloScene has the potential to democratize 3D content creation. Artists, developers, and researchers can more easily digitize their surroundings and transform them into editable, dynamic virtual assets without requiring specialized 3D scanning equipment or extensive manual modeling expertise. This opens new avenues for personalized virtual spaces and rapid prototyping.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** While HoloScene currently focuses on static indoor scenes, its robust framework lays a strong foundation for future research. The authors acknowledge limitations such as handling dynamic scenes, large outdoor environments, relightable reconstruction, and deformable/articulated objects. Addressing these challenges in subsequent work, building upon HoloScene's success, promises even more versatile and powerful digital twin capabilities.

In conclusion, HoloScene offers a foundational leap forward in creating digital twins that are not just visually compelling but also physically functional and highly interactive. Its rigorous methodology and impressive empirical results position it as a critical enabler for the next generation of applications in immersive computing, robotics, and generative AI.
