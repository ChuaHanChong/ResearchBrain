# Humanoid World Models: Open World Foundation Models for Humanoid Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Waterloo developed Humanoid World Models (HWMs), a family of lightweight, open-source generative models designed to predict future egocentric video observations for humanoid robots based on their actions. These models enable physically plausible simulations while being trainable and deployable on modest hardware (1-2 GPUs).

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack robust internal simulators needed for autonomous reasoning and long-horizon planning in complex, open-world environments.
- Existing large multimodal and vision-language-action models often lack the physical plausibility, accuracy, and robustness required for safe real-world robotic action.
- Current state-of-the-art video generation models for robotics are often closed-source, computationally prohibitive, or not specifically tailored for humanoids with ego-centric and action conditioning.

## Method

- Introduces **Humanoid World Models** (HWMs), a family of lightweight, open-source generative models that forecast future egocentric video observations conditioned on humanoid control tokens (actions).
- Explores two generative modeling paradigms: ==Masked Video Modelling (Masked-HWM)== and Flow Matching (Flow-HWM), trained on 100 hours of egocentric video from the Humanoid EVE Android robot.
- Investigates various transformer block designs, including parameter-sharing strategies (Full Sharing, Modality Sharing) and split attention, to optimize for computational efficiency and performance.

## Results

- Masked-HWM Base Block achieved the best visual quality with an FID of **10.13** and a PSNR of **29.02** dB, demonstrating superior realism.
- The Masked-HWM Full Sharing variant achieved the smallest model footprint (0.195B parameters), lowest peak GPU memory (**2.12** GB), and fastest inference speed (**2.36** samples/sec), providing significant efficiency gains.
- Generated videos from Masked-HWM accurately captured scene structures and large robot parts, showing robust physical plausibility, whereas Flow-HWM outputs suffered from blurriness and artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Masked Transformer-based models (Masked-HWM) consistently outperform Flow Matching models (Flow-HWM) in visual quality (FID) and sampling speed for action-conditioned humanoid video prediction.
> - Parameter-sharing strategies effectively reduce model size by 33-53% and lower computational resource requirements (GPU memory, inference speed) with minimal acceptable degradation in visual fidelity.
> - The Modality Sharing and Full Sharing variants of Masked-HWM provide strong efficiency-performance trade-offs, making advanced world models practical for academic and small-lab settings with limited GPUs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of humanoid robotics, world models, and generative AI, specifically addressing a critical gap in enabling humanoid robots to operate autonomously in complex, open-world environments.

**Humanoid Robotics:** Humanoid robots possess a human-like morphology, making them uniquely suited for environments built for people, natural human interaction, and learning from teleoperated demonstrations. However, equipping them with advanced reasoning and planning capabilities for unpredictable real-world scenarios remains a significant challenge. This paper directly contributes to addressing this by providing a mechanism for humanoids to simulate and understand the consequences of their actions.

**World Models in Embodied AI:** The concept of "world models"—predictive models that forecast future outcomes based on observations and actions—is a cornerstone for intelligent agents. In the context of embodied AI, particularly robotics, world models can serve as internal simulators, enabling long-horizon planning and generating synthetic data for more efficient policy learning. The authors highlight the limitations of current large multimodal models (LMMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) models, which, despite showing promise in reasoning, often lack the accuracy, reliability, and robustness required for safe and effective action in dynamic, unstructured environments. Video generation models, as used in HWM, offer a more physically grounded alternative, capturing fine-grained spatiotemporal dynamics that language-only models often miss.

**Video Generation Landscape:** The field of video generation has seen rapid advancements, moving from early GANs to more stable and higher-quality diffusion models and transformer-based approaches, often operating in compressed latent spaces of VAEs. However, the authors identify a critical disconnect: most state-of-the-art video generation models are designed for entertainment (e.g., Sora, CogVideoX, MovieGen), prioritizing visual appeal over physical plausibility or the specific needs of embodied agents. Crucially, many existing world models for robotics (e.g., UniSim, IraSim, Navigation World Models, NVIDIA Cosmos) are either closed-source, computationally prohibitive (requiring multiple high-end GPUs like H100s for inference alone), not specifically designed for humanoids, or lack support for critical ego-centric conditioning (past video, actions).

**Bridging the Gap: Efficiency and Accessibility:** This paper directly addresses the "clear gap" for open-source, physically grounded, humanoid-specific world models that are lightweight enough for practical academic and small-lab settings (e.g., 2-3 GPUs). By focusing on resource efficiency, the authors aim to democratize access to advanced robotic capabilities, moving beyond the large-scale compute requirements that often limit cutting-edge research to well-funded industrial labs. This work stands out by explicitly tackling the computational and accessibility barriers, providing a practical tool for the broader robotics community. The architectural explorations of attention mechanisms and parameter-sharing strategies directly reflect this commitment to efficiency while maintaining performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **enable humanoid robots to effectively reason, plan, and act in complex, open-world settings**. This is a fundamental challenge in robotics, particularly for humanoids whose unique morphology makes them ideal for human-centric environments but also necessitates sophisticated cognitive and control capabilities.

More specifically, the paper is driven by several key motivations and aims:

1. **Develop Physically Plausible, Humanoid-Specific World Models:** The primary aim is to create predictive models that can accurately forecast future egocentric video observations conditioned on humanoid control tokens (actions). This physical grounding is critical for real-world robotic deployment, where visual fidelity must align with plausible physical interactions.
2. **Address the Limitations of Existing Generative Models for Embodied AI:** The authors observe that while general video generation models achieve impressive visual quality, they often lack the physical plausibility and conditioning capabilities (e.g., on past video and actions) necessary for embodied agents. Furthermore, existing world models for robotics are often either closed-source, too resource-intensive, or not tailored for humanoid platforms.
3. **Ensure Computational Accessibility and Efficiency:** A central motivation is to design and implement these world models to be lightweight, open-source, and trainable/deployable on modest computational hardware (e.g., 1-2 GPUs). This directly counters the trend of increasingly large foundation models, making advanced robotics research accessible to academic institutions and small labs with limited resources. The core research question posed is: "Can we build a physically plausible, humanoid-specific world model that can be trained and deployed on as little as two GPUs?"
4. **Explore and Optimize Architectural Designs for Efficiency and Performance:** To achieve the above goals, the authors are motivated to systematically investigate different generative modeling paradigms (Masked Transformers and Flow-Matching) and architectural variations (joint vs. split attention, shared vs. separate parameters across token streams). This exploration aims to identify the most effective and efficient architectural choices for humanoid video prediction under resource constraints.
5. **Facilitate Downstream Robotic Applications:** By providing robust action-conditioned video prediction, these world models are intended to serve as dynamics models for long-horizon planning and as synthetic data generators for more data-efficient policy learning, thereby enhancing the autonomy and learning capabilities of humanoids.

In essence, the research aims to provide a practical, high-quality, and computationally feasible "imagination" capability for humanoid robots, thereby advancing their ability to operate intelligently in complex, human-centric environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce Humanoid World Models (HWM), a family of lightweight, open-source models designed to forecast future egocentric video conditioned on humanoid control tokens. Their methodology involves training two distinct types of generative models and exploring various architectural configurations to optimize for both performance and computational efficiency.

#### 3.1. Dataset

The models are trained on the **1xGPT dataset** (1X Technologies, 2024), comprising 100 hours of egocentric video demonstrations captured from the Humanoid EVE Android robot. Videos are recorded at 30 Hz, with each frame paired with a 25-dimensional action vector encoding movement velocities, hand closure states, and pitch-yaw-roll angles of various joints (wrist, knee, elbow, shoulder, neck, hip). The models predict 8 future frames conditioned on 9 past frames at 256x256 resolution.

#### 3.2. Generative Modeling Paradigms

The core of the methodology lies in exploring two prevalent generative modeling frameworks:

- **Masked Humanoid World Model (Masked-HWM):** This variant employs **Masked Video Modelling (MVM)**, inspired by MaskGIT and MAGVIT.
 - **Tokenization:** Video frames are first encoded into discrete latent tokens using a **VQ-VAE** (NVIDIA Cosmos DV8x8x8 tokenizer), which provides 8x spatial and 8x temporal compression. Action sequences are embedded into the same dimensional space via an MLP.
 - **Training:** Past and future latent tokens are concatenated. The future latent sequence is then corrupted with random token replacements (at a rate `U(0, ρ_max)`) and masked using a per-frame thresholding strategy. The model is trained to reconstruct the original tokens at these masked positions using a cross-entropy loss.
 - **Inference:** All future latent tokens are initially masked. Generation proceeds frame by frame, with tokens within each frame predicted in parallel over `K=2` refinement steps, where a subset of tokens is iteratively re-masked and re-predicted.

- **Flow Humanoid World Model (Flow-HWM):** This variant uses the **Flow Matching (FM)** framework, which formulates video generation as a continuous transformation from Gaussian noise to data samples.
 - **Tokenization:** A **continuous VAE** (Cosmos Continuous 8x16x16 tokenizer) is used, performing 16x spatial and 8x temporal compression. Action sequences are embedded via an MLP, and the timestep is encoded using sinusoidal embeddings.
 - **Training:** The model learns a time-dependent velocity field that transforms samples from a Gaussian prior into target video samples. The objective is to minimize the expected mean squared error between the predicted and ground-truth velocity field, conditioned on past video, past actions, future actions, and time. Classifier-free guidance is used.
 - **Inference:** Generation involves integrating the learned velocity field from `t=0` to `t=1`, starting from pure Gaussian noise, using a first-order Euler ODE solver.

#### 3.3. Transformer Block Design and Architectural Variants

For both Masked-HWM and Flow-HWM, a stack of transformer blocks processes four token streams: past video (`v_p`), future video (`v_f`), past actions (`a_p`), and future actions (`a_f`). The authors evaluate several architectural variants focused on efficiency:

- **Base Block:**
 - **Masked-HWM:** Uses factorized spatio-temporal attention (separate spatial and temporal attention). Temporal attention is joint across all streams `[a_p, a_f, L]`. Spatial attention is applied only to video tokens. RoPE is used (2D for spatial, 1D for temporal). Each stream uses distinct MLP weights.
 - **Flow-HWM:** Inspired by Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3). Each stream is modulated by timestep embeddings. Stream-specific QKV projections are computed, followed by a joint multi-stream attention operation across all tokens. 3D RoPE for video tokens, 1D RoPE for action tokens. Stream-specific MLPs are used in the feedforward stage.

- **Parameter Sharing:** These variants modify the Base Block to reduce parameter count:
 - **Full Sharing:** Modulation scalars, QKV projections, and MLPs are shared across all four token streams.
 - **Modality Sharing:** Parameters are shared within each modality (video streams share weights, action streams share weights).
 - A hybrid scheme is used: the first four layers are Base Block (fully separate parameters), and the remaining layers implement parameter sharing.

- **Split Attention:** This variant, primarily for computational efficiency in long sequences, uses a two-stage attention mechanism:
 - Each stream first undergoes independent self-attention.
 - Then, future video tokens (`v_f`) query the keys and values from all other streams (`v_p, a_p, a_f`) via cross-attention.
 - Timestep-dependent scaling and shifting are applied to both self- and cross-attention layers.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

The models are evaluated quantitatively using:
- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** Measures the realism and quality of generated images.
- **PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio):** Assesses pixel-level fidelity.
- **Model Parameter Count:** Direct measure of model size.
- **Video Latent Decoding Speed:** Measured in samples per second (SPS).
- **Peak GPU Memory Usage:** Indicates computational resource requirements.

Qualitative evaluation is conducted through sample video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide a comprehensive comparison between the two generative paradigms and their architectural variants, yielding crucial insights into efficiency and performance trade-offs for humanoid world models.

#### 4.1. Comparison of Generative Paradigms (Masked-HWM vs. Flow-HWM)

A key finding is that **Masked Transformers (Masked-HWM) consistently outperformed Flow-Matching models (Flow-HWM)** in terms of both visual quality (lower FID) and sampling speed. This was observed even when Flow-HWM variants had more parameters and were trained for longer periods. This suggests that for the given dataset and compute constraints, masked video modeling is a more effective paradigm for generating physically plausible humanoid egocentric videos.

#### 4.2. Masked-HWM Variants Performance (Table 1)

- **Base Block:** Achieved the **best FID score (10.13)**, indicating superior visual quality and realism among Masked-HWM configurations. Its PSNR was competitive at 29.02 dB.
- **Split Attention:** Yielded the **highest PSNR (29.37 dB)**, suggesting better pixel-level accuracy. However, its FID was slightly higher (15.31), implying less realistic global structure despite better pixel matching.
- **Parameter Sharing Variants:** Demonstrated significant efficiency gains with minimal performance degradation.
 - **Modality Sharing:** Reduced model size by approximately 26% (0.237B vs. 0.321B) compared to the Base Block while maintaining highly competitive FID (11.67) and PSNR (28.97 dB). This indicates that sharing parameters within modalities (video and actions separately) is an effective strategy.
 - **Full Sharing:** Achieved the **smallest model footprint (0.195B parameters)**, **lowest peak GPU memory (2.12 GB)**, and **fastest inference speed (2.36 samples/sec)**. While its FID (14.21) and PSNR (28.66 dB) were slightly lower than the Base Block, the efficiency benefits are substantial, making it ideal for highly resource-constrained environments.
- **Qualitative Results:** Masked-HWM learned scene structures (furniture, objects) and overall textures well. Larger robot parts (arms, wheels) were generally accurate, but precision elements like fingers often appeared blurry or entangled. The visual quality was robust to varying lighting conditions.

#### 4.3. Flow-HWM Variants Performance (Table 2)

- **Full Sharing:** Within the Flow-HWM family, this variant offered the **best overall trade-off**, achieving the **lowest FID (110.73)**, fastest inference (1.91 samples/sec), and minimal memory usage (3.25 GB) while halving the parameter count compared to the Base Block.
- **Split Attention:** Delivered the highest PSNR (20.50 dB), matching Modality Sharing, but was slower and required more memory.
- **General Observation:** The FIDs for Flow-HWM models were significantly higher (worse) than Masked-HWM models, indicating a lower visual quality overall.
- **Qualitative Results:** Flow-HWM successfully captured general scene structure, but the outputs suffered from noticeable blurriness, artifacting (spotted patches), and degradation in quality in later frames. The model struggled to preserve sharp edges and rounded shapes and sometimes defaulted to canonical arm appearances instead of specific, unusual configurations.

#### 4.4. General Conclusions on Efficiency

The research consistently showed that **parameter-sharing strategies, especially "Full Sharing," provide substantial efficiency gains** (reducing parameter counts by 33–53% and lowering memory/increasing speed) with minimal or acceptable loss in visual fidelity across both generative paradigms. This validates the core hypothesis that lightweight, performant world models can be built using architectural and efficiency trade-offs, making them practical for academic and small-lab settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Humanoid World Models (HWM) carries significant implications for the future of humanoid robotics and embodied AI research, particularly due to its dual emphasis on physical plausibility and computational accessibility.

#### 5.1. Democratizing Advanced Robotics Research

Perhaps the most impactful contribution of HWM is its commitment to being **open-source and lightweight**, designed for training and deployment on modest hardware (1-2 GPUs). This directly addresses a critical barrier in cutting-edge AI research, where the trend towards increasingly large and computationally intensive models often excludes academic institutions and smaller labs. By making robust, humanoid-specific world models accessible, HWM empowers a broader community of researchers to experiment with and advance embodied AI, fostering innovation and diversity in the field.

#### 5.2. Enhancing Humanoid Autonomy and Intelligence

HWMs serve as powerful internal simulators for humanoid robots. By accurately predicting future egocentric video observations conditioned on actions, they provide humanoids with a crucial capability:
- **Long-Horizon Planning:** Robots can "imagine" the consequences of potential action sequences, allowing them to plan complex, multi-step tasks without physically executing every trial-and-error in the real world. This is essential for navigating unpredictable and unstructured environments.
- **Data-Efficient Policy Learning:** The synthetic rollouts generated by HWMs can augment real-world data, enabling robots to learn new policies more efficiently and with less reliance on costly and time-consuming physical demonstrations. This accelerates the development of more capable and adaptable humanoid agents.
- **Counterfactual Reasoning:** The ability to simulate different outcomes given different actions can also support counterfactual reasoning, allowing robots to understand why certain actions lead to success or failure.

#### 5.3. Advancing Physically Grounded Generative Models

The paper emphasizes the need for physically plausible video generation, a distinction from entertainment-focused models. HWM contributes to this sub-field by demonstrating how generative models can be specifically tailored for embodied agents, where the visual output must correspond to realistic physical interactions and robot kinematics. The egocentric perspective and conditioning on detailed humanoid control tokens ensure that the generated futures are relevant and useful for robot decision-making.

#### 5.4. Architectural Innovation for Efficiency

The systematic exploration of attention mechanisms (joint vs. split) and parameter-sharing strategies within transformer architectures provides valuable architectural insights. The finding that parameter-sharing techniques can drastically reduce model size (33–53%) with minimal impact on performance is a significant contribution to efficient deep learning. These techniques can be adopted in other domains where computational resources are a constraint, paving the way for more compact and deployable AI models.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Future Research and Applications

HWM provides a solid foundation for several future research directions, including:
- Integration into full planning and control systems for real-world humanoid tasks.
- Exploration of even more data-efficient learning strategies leveraging the synthetic experiences.
- Extension to other robot embodiments beyond humanoids.
- Development of more sophisticated metrics that go beyond visual fidelity to evaluate physical consistency.

In summary, Humanoid World Models represents a significant step towards practical, intelligent humanoid robots. By making advanced predictive capabilities accessible and demonstrating robust performance under resource constraints, this work not only advances the state of the art in embodied AI but also broadens the participation in this critical research area. The authors appropriately acknowledge the ethical considerations, emphasizing that these models are for scientific progress in embodied AI and not for unsafe or unethical deployment.
