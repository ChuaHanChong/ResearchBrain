# Humanoid World Models: Open World Foundation Models for Humanoid Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Waterloo developed Humanoid World Models (HWMs), a family of lightweight, open-source generative models designed to predict future egocentric video observations for humanoid robots based on their actions. These models enable physically plausible simulations while being trainable and deployable on modest hardware (1-2 GPUs).

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack robust internal simulators needed for autonomous reasoning and long-horizon planning in complex, open-world environments.
- Existing large multimodal and vision-language-action models often lack the physical plausibility, accuracy, and robustness required for safe real-world robotic action.
- Current state-of-the-art video generation models for robotics are often closed-source, computationally prohibitive, or not specifically tailored for humanoids with ego-centric and action conditioning.

## Method

- Introduces **Humanoid World Models** (HWMs), a family of lightweight, open-source generative models that forecast future egocentric video observations conditioned on humanoid control tokens (actions).
- Explores two generative modeling paradigms: ==Masked Video Modelling (Masked-HWM)== and Flow Matching (Flow-HWM), trained on 100 hours of egocentric video from the Humanoid EVE Android robot.
- Investigates various transformer block designs, including parameter-sharing strategies (Full Sharing, Modality Sharing) and split attention, to optimize for computational efficiency and performance.

## Results

- Masked-HWM Base Block achieved the best visual quality with an FID of **10.13** and a PSNR of **29.02** dB, demonstrating superior realism.
- The Masked-HWM Full Sharing variant achieved the smallest model footprint (0.195B parameters), lowest peak GPU memory (**2.12** GB), and fastest inference speed (**2.36** samples/sec), providing significant efficiency gains.
- Generated videos from Masked-HWM accurately captured scene structures and large robot parts, showing robust physical plausibility, whereas Flow-HWM outputs suffered from blurriness and artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Masked Transformer-based models (Masked-HWM) consistently outperform Flow Matching models (Flow-HWM) in visual quality (FID) and sampling speed for action-conditioned humanoid video prediction.
> - Parameter-sharing strategies effectively reduce model size by 33-53% and lower computational resource requirements (GPU memory, inference speed) with minimal acceptable degradation in visual fidelity.
> - The Modality Sharing and Full Sharing variants of Masked-HWM provide strong efficiency-performance trade-offs, making advanced world models practical for academic and small-lab settings with limited GPUs.
