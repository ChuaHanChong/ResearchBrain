# FlowS: One-Step Motion Prediction via Local Transport Conditioning

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowS introduces a one-step, anchor-conditioned flow-matching framework for motion prediction that addresses latency constraints in autonomous driving. It achieves state-of-the-art performance with a Soft mAP of 0.4804 on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset while operating at 75 frames per second on a single A100 GPU.

## Problem

- Existing generative motion prediction models, like diffusion models, achieve high accuracy and multimodality but require many iterative steps, leading to high latency unsuitable for real-time autonomous driving.
- Current flow matching methods suffer significant accuracy degradation when performing single-step prediction, particularly when starting from a scene-agnostic base distribution, due to the compounded challenge of mode discovery and long-range displacement.
- Autonomous driving systems require motion forecasts that are not only accurate and diverse but also delivered with strictly bounded, real-time latency.

## Method

- **FlowS** replaces the standard ==scene-agnostic Gaussian base distribution== with a learned, ==scene-conditioned multimodal prior== that emits calibrated anchor trajectories already proximate to plausible futures, thus localizing the transport problem.
- Introduces a ==step-consistent displacement field== that is trained to admit a true single-step limit by enforcing ==semigroup self-consistency==, allowing a single large step to accurately approximate multi-step dynamics.
- Anchors all displacement field updates at the prior-generated anchor trajectories along ==straight-line paths==, providing a stable and fixed training target for the ==consistency loss==, unlike prior methods relying on curved diffusion paths.

## Results

- Achieved a **Soft mAP of 0.4804** and an **mAP of 0.4703** on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset leaderboard, surpassing all other published entries.
- Operates at approximately **75 FPS (13.2 ms/scene)** on a single A100 GPU with single-step inference, demonstrating real-time capability.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the learned prior and the step-consistent displacement field synergistically contribute to performance, with the prior reducing mean transport distance by **36%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate single-step integration is achievable when the generative transport problem is conditioned to be local, converting a complex, long-range prediction into a short-range refinement task.
> - The learned scene-conditioned prior and the step-consistent displacement field provide complementary gains, addressing distinct sources of single-step error from mode ambiguity and discretization.
> - Enforcing semigroup self-consistency in the displacement field effectively distills multi-step accuracy into a single inference step with negligible performance degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Motion prediction is a critical component for autonomous driving systems, requiring forecasts that are accurate, diverse in their multimodal representations of future possibilities, and delivered with strictly bounded latency. The challenge lies in simultaneously satisfying these three requirements, as many existing methods prioritize some criteria at the expense of others.

The field has seen significant advancements with generative models, which are effective at capturing the complex, multi-agent future behaviors prevalent in driving scenarios. Diffusion models, such as MotionDiffuser, Leapfrog, and Scenario Dreamer, have demonstrated strong performance in terms of accuracy and the diversity of generated futures. However, a primary limitation of these models is their high computational cost during inference, typically requiring tens to hundreds of iterative denoising steps. This iterative refinement process leads to latency that often exceeds the real-time constraints of autonomous driving stacks, where decisions cannot wait for prolonged computations. Efforts to accelerate diffusion models, like Diffusion-Drive and BridgeDrive, have reduced the number of steps but generally still require multiple refinement cycles, failing to guarantee single-cycle, real-time latency.

Another class of generative models, flow matching methods (e.g., TrajFlow, FlowDrive, GoalFlow), offers a more direct approach by learning velocity fields for deterministic trajectory transport. These methods can achieve strong accuracy with fewer steps than diffusion models. However, they typically suffer degradation under aggressive step reduction, particularly when initialized from a scene-agnostic Gaussian distribution. In such cases, a single integration step requires the velocity field to both identify the correct behavioral mode and traverse a significant displacement, leading to amplified discretization errors and reduced accuracy for one-step prediction.

FlowS addresses this specific gap by proposing a method that aims to overcome the latency limitations of diffusion models and the single-step degradation of current flow matching approaches. It posits that accurate single-step integration is achievable if the underlying transport problem is conditioned to be local, thereby converting a complex, long-range prediction into a short-range refinement task. This work seeks to unify high accuracy and multimodal coverage with strictly bounded, single-step latency, a combination that has remained an open challenge in the field of motion prediction for safety-critical autonomous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the FlowS research is to develop a motion prediction framework that can simultaneously achieve high accuracy, generate diverse multimodal future trajectories, and operate with strictly bounded, single-step inference latency. This addresses a critical unmet need in real-world autonomous driving, where a prediction delivered too late is functionally ineffective.

The motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in existing state-of-the-art motion prediction techniques:
- **Latency of Generative Models:** Diffusion models, while excelling in accuracy and multimodal diversity, are computationally intensive during inference. Their requirement for multiple (50-100) iterative denoising steps makes them unsuitable for real-time applications where control updates demand forecasts within milliseconds. Even accelerated variants still require several steps, precluding true single-cycle latency.
- **Single-Step Degradation in Flow Matching:** While flow matching models offer a more direct path to trajectory generation, they often degrade significantly when attempting single-step inference. This is attributed to the challenge of starting from a scene-agnostic base distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise). In this scenario, a single velocity field update must perform a compound task: first, discovering the correct behavioral mode among many possibilities, and second, traversing a potentially long displacement from this arbitrary starting point to the target future. This combined task amplifies discretization errors, making a single Euler step insufficient for accurate prediction.
- **Hypothesis for Single-Step Accuracy:** The core motivation for FlowS is the hypothesis that "single-step integration is accurate when the transport problem is conditioned to be local." If the initial state for the flow matching process is already close to a plausible future trajectory, the task of the velocity field reduces to short-range refinement rather than global mode discovery and long-range traversal. This short-range refinement is a regime where a single integration step is theoretically more accurate.

To operationalize this "local transport conditioning" strategy, FlowS is motivated to introduce two complementary mechanisms:
1. **Scene-Conditioned Learned Prior:** To convert the global mode-discovery problem into local correction, a mechanism is needed to provide an intelligent "warm start." The motivation is to replace the standard scene-agnostic Gaussian base distribution with an online, multimodal prior that generates anchor trajectories already proximate to plausible future paths, tailored to the specific scene context.
2. **Step-Consistent Displacement Field:** Even with localized starting points, residual effective curvature in the marginal velocity field can still introduce discretization errors in a single step. The motivation here is to develop a displacement field that inherently enforces semigroup self-consistency, meaning that a single large step accurately approximates the cumulative effect of multiple smaller, locally accurate steps. This ensures that the single-step output faithfully inherits multi-step accuracy by implicitly absorbing the effects of marginal curvature. A further motivation for the design of this field is to ensure a stable training target, addressing instability observed in prior self-consistency methods that rely on curved diffusion paths.

In essence, FlowS is motivated by the need for generative motion prediction models that are both performant and deployable in latency-critical autonomous systems, aiming to achieve this by fundamentally altering how the transport problem is set up and integrated.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowS is designed as a one-step, anchor-conditioned flow-matching framework for multi-agent motion prediction, specifically engineered for strict latency requirements. The methodology is structured around three main stages: a symmetric scene encoder, a learned scene-conditioned prior, and a step-consistent displacement field.

#### 3.1. A. Overview of FlowS Pipeline

1. **Scene Encoder:** A Transformer-based encoder (MTR++ architecture) processes agent histories and HD-map polylines, aggregating them into per-agent context features `c`. These features condition all subsequent modules.
2. **Learned Scene-Conditioned Prior:** This module replaces the standard Gaussian base distribution. It emits `K` calibrated anchor trajectories per agent, each intended to be close to a plausible future.
3. **Step-Consistent Displacement Field:** This field is trained along straight-line paths derived from the anchors. It is designed to admit a true single-step limit by enforcing semigroup self-consistency.
4. **Multi-shot Decoder:** Produces final candidate trajectories and calibrated scores, followed by Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) for pruning.

#### 3.2. B. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) Preliminaries

FlowS builds upon Conditional Flow Matching (CFM), which learns a time-indexed flow model `v_ϕ` that estimates the expected velocity `E[u_t | X_t, c]`. CFM minimizes the difference between `v_ϕ` and a known velocity target `u_t` along probability paths `p_t(· | Z, Y)` that interpolate between a base distribution `p_0` (e.g., `N(0, I)`) and the data distribution `p_data`.
A common choice for these paths is the optimal transport (OT) straight-line path: `X_t = (1 − t)Z + tY`, where `u_t = Y − Z`. This yields a time-independent target velocity. In a one-step regime, the prediction is `Y_pred = Z + v_ϕ(Z, 0, c)`.

#### 3.3. C. Learned Scene-Conditioned Prior

The core innovation begins by replacing the `Z ∼ N(0, I)` initialization of standard CFM.
- **Problem Statement:** Starting from `N(0, I)` forces the flow model to simultaneously discover the correct behavioral mode and traverse a long displacement in a single step, leading to high truncation error.
- **Solution:** FlowS learns an online, scene-conditioned multimodal prior. Given scene features `c`, this prior emits `K` calibrated anchor trajectories, `A_k`, for each agent. These anchors are designed to be already near plausible futures, reducing the transport problem to a short local correction (i.e., `∥Y_GT − A_k∥₂ ≪ ∥Y_GT − Z∥₂`).
- **Prior Architecture:** The prior network outputs `K` components, each defined by a mean trajectory `μ_k(c) ∈ ℝ^(T×2)`, per-timestep uncertainty `σ_k(c) ∈ ℝ^(T×2) (≥0)`, and a mode weight `π_k(c)`. Sampling an anchor `A_k` involves adding reparameterized Gaussian noise: `A_k = μ_k(c) + √(τ σ_k(c)) ⊙ ε_k`, where `ε_k ∼ N(0, I₂T)`.
- **Anchor-Conditioned CFM:** With these anchors, the flow model `v_ϕ` is trained along straight-line paths from `A_k` to the ground truth `Y_GT`: `X_t = (1 − t)A_k + tY_GT`, with the target velocity `u_t = Y_GT − A_k`. The one-step predictor becomes `Y_k = A_k + v_ϕ(A_k, t, c)`.
- **Prior Training:** The prior is trained with a composite objective `L_prior`:
 - `L_nll`: A per-mode Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood, minimized across `K` modes (`min_k NLL_k`).
 - `L_mix`: A KL divergence between predicted mode weights `π_k` and soft targets `ˆπ_k` (derived from `NLL_k`), ensuring calibration.
 - `L_ent`: A negative-entropy penalty to prevent variance collapse.
 - `L_div`: A bounded hinge repulsion loss to enforce mode diversity.

#### 3.4. D. Step-Consistent Displacement Field

This component addresses residual single-step discretization error arising from effective curvature in the marginal velocity field.
- **Problem Statement:** Even with scene-adapted anchors, the averaged conditional paths can induce curvature in `v_ϕ`, causing error in a single large step.
- **Solution:** A step-conditioned displacement field `s_ψ(X_t, t, d, c)` is introduced. This field predicts the displacement over a fraction `d ∈` of the remaining path. It satisfies `lim_(d→0) s_ψ = v_ϕ`, meaning it recovers the flow model output for infinitesimal steps and predicts a scale-aware finite displacement for `d > 0`.
- **Semigroup Consistency:** The field `s_ψ` is trained to be compositionally self-consistent: two consecutive `d`-sized updates should match a single `2d` update (`T_∆t ◦ T_∆t ≈ T_2∆t`).
- **Training Objective (`L_step`):** Blends two terms:
 - `L_flow`: Anchors `s_ψ(·, ·, 0, ·)` to the closed-form straight-line target (`Y_GT − A_k`) used by `v_ϕ`, ensuring the infinitesimal limit.
 - `L_cons`: Enforces large-step coherence via a consistency loss between a `2d` step prediction and a composite of two `d` steps. The consistency target (`s_target`) is computed using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of `s_ψ` for stability during training.
- **Anchored Straight-Line Paths:** Crucially, all `s_ψ` updates are anchored at prior anchors `A_k`, not Gaussian noise. `X_t` is sampled along `(1 − t)A_k + tY_GT`. This provides a stable, fixed target `(Y_GT − A_k)` for the consistency loss, resolving training instability issues reported in prior work on curved diffusion paths.
- **Synergy:** The prior reduces transport distance, mitigating Euler error, while `s_ψ` absorbs marginal curvature via semigroup consistency, addressing distinct sources of single-step error.
- **Inference:** At inference, `d=1` is set, yielding the full one-step prediction: `Y_pred,k = A_k + s_ψ(A_k, 0, 1, c)`.

#### 3.5. E. Backbone and Decoder

- **Encoder:** An MTR++ encoder processes vectorized agent histories and HD-map polylines using query-centric self-attention to generate per-agent context features `c`.
- **Decoder:** A multi-shot decoder uses `N_q` learnable query tokens to produce candidate trajectories (`Ŷ_k`), confidence scores (`S_k`), and ranking scores (`r_k`). NMS prunes redundant proposals, and a Plackett-Luce (PL) ranking loss calibrates selection.

#### 3.6. F. Implementation Details

The approach is evaluated on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) using standard metrics (minADE, minFDE, Miss Rate, Overlap Rate, soft mAP, mAP). Training uses ADAM optimizer on A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FlowS was evaluated on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD), focusing on its performance in terms of accuracy, multimodality, and inference latency.

#### 4.1. A. Quantitative Performance

- **State-of-the-Art mAP:** On the official WOMD test leaderboard, FlowS (Ens.) achieved a Soft mAP of 0.4804 and an mAP of 0.4703. These scores surpass all other published entries, including other ensemble methods. The single-model FlowS variant also demonstrated competitive performance with an mAP of 0.4512.
- **Convergence Reliability:** Validation metrics aggregated over seven independently trained runs showed low standard deviations (e.g., mAP = 0.4508 ± 0.0017), indicating reliable convergence of the model.
- **Competitive Secondary Metrics:** FlowS maintained competitive performance across other standard metrics, including minADE, minFDE, and Miss Rate. Overlap Rate also remained competitive, suggesting that the multimodal predictions, despite being generated per-agent, do not disproportionately lead to collisions.
- **Per-Class Performance:** On the validation set, FlowS (Ens.) achieved strong mAP across different agent categories: 0.4963 for Vehicles, 0.5015 for Pedestrians, and 0.4152 for Cyclists.

#### 4.2. B. Latency

- **Real-time Single-Step Inference:** FlowS operates at approximately 75 Frames Per Second (FPS), corresponding to about 13.2 ms per scene on a single A100 GPU with single-step inference. This represents a 34% reduction in latency compared to MTR (19.96 ms/scene) and is faster than TrajFlow (16.44 ms/scene), establishing its capability for real-time operation.
- **Ensemble Latency:** While the ensemble configuration achieves the highest accuracy, its latency would be higher due to aggregating predictions from multiple models.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Studies

- **Component Contributions:** An ablation study demonstrated that both core components – the learned prior and the step-consistent displacement field – contribute complementary gains to performance:
 - Starting from a Gaussian base without either component resulted in 0.4376 mAP.
 - Adding the learned prior alone improved mAP by +0.0082.
 - Adding the step-consistent field alone improved mAP by +0.0056.
 - Combining both components yielded the highest single-model performance (0.4508 mAP), confirming their synergistic effect.
- **Prior–Displacement Field Synergy:** The learned prior reduced the mean transport distance from the base sample to ground truth by 36% (from 1.23 to 0.78). This reduction in transport length for the displacement field reduced the mean velocity magnitude from 1.72 to 1.10, making the step-consistent field more effective and stable in the short-range refinement regime.
- **Step Resilience:** The semigroup constraint effectively distilled multi-step accuracy into a single step. Varying inference steps from 1 to 16 showed a negligible change in mAP (∆mAP = 0.0017), while latency increased linearly by 9.5x, highlighting the efficiency of single-step inference without significant accuracy degradation.
- **Drift by Scene Density:** The learned prior provided consistent relative improvements (~2.99% reduction in minADE/minFDE) across scenes of varying agent densities. The largest absolute gains were observed in dense scenes (≥6 agents), where mode ambiguity is typically highest.
- **Scale-Aware Displacement Analysis:** Analysis of the displacement field `s_ψ` showed that at a full step (`d=1`, `t=0`), the predicted displacement magnitude (0.729) closely matched the target magnitude (0.730) with high cosine alignment (0.96). The `ℓ₂`-to-target error remained consistently low (~0.16) across different points along the flow path, indicating stable endpoint prediction.
- **Per-Horizon Error:** Displacement error analysis across different prediction horizons confirmed that single-step generation maintains accuracy over longer horizons. The mean error at 1s was 0.16m, and the cumulative ADE at 8s was 0.58m, which aligns with the overall model performance.

#### 4.4. D. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples showed that FlowS predictions for dense intersections, yielding maneuvers, and branching multi-agent scenarios were smooth, lane-compliant, and accurately captured multimodal branching behaviors, despite being generated in a single step.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "FlowS: One-Step Motion Prediction via Local Transport Conditioning" has several significant implications for the field of motion prediction in autonomous systems:

- **Enabling Real-time Generative Prediction:** The most prominent significance is the demonstration of practical, real-time, one-step generative motion prediction. By achieving state-of-the-art accuracy (Soft mAP and mAP on WOMD) at a high inference rate (~75 FPS) with a single forward pass, FlowS directly addresses the critical latency bottleneck that has historically limited the deployment of powerful generative models like diffusion models in safety-critical autonomous driving applications. This makes high-fidelity multimodal forecasting viable for immediate decision-making.

- **Introduction of Local Transport Conditioning:** The paper introduces "local transport conditioning" as a principled strategy for overcoming the limitations of single-step integration. This conceptual core, which states that accurate one-step integration is possible when the transport problem is reduced to local refinement, offers a new paradigm for designing efficient generative models. This strategy could extend beyond motion prediction to other domains requiring fast, high-quality generation.

- **Novel Complementary Mechanisms:** The proposed two core mechanisms – the scene-conditioned learned prior and the semigroup-consistent displacement field – are novel contributions that synergistically achieve the objective.
 - The **learned prior** effectively transforms the challenging global mode discovery problem into a more manageable local correction task. This not only improves accuracy but also serves to stabilize the training of the displacement field by providing a less noisy starting point.
 - The **step-consistent displacement field** directly tackles the one-step discretization error by enforcing compositional self-consistency. This mechanism ensures that a single large step accurately reflects the dynamics of multiple smaller, coherent steps, implicitly absorbing the effects of marginal curvature.

- **Stable Training for Consistency Methods:** A methodological improvement lies in anchoring the displacement field at learned priors along straight-line paths. This design provides a stable, low-variance training target for the self-consistency loss, addressing instability issues encountered by prior formulations that bootstrap from curved diffusion paths or rely on noisy moving targets. This enhancement in training stability is crucial for developing robust one-step integrators.

- **High Performance and Robustness:** The comprehensive experimental results, including state-of-the-art metrics on a challenging dataset like WOMD, extensive ablation studies, and analyses of step resilience and performance across scene densities, collectively underscore the robustness and effectiveness of FlowS. The low standard deviations in multiple training runs indicate reliable convergence and performance.

- **Potential for Autonomous System Integration:** The combination of high accuracy, multimodal capabilities, and extremely low latency positions FlowS as a strong candidate for integration into the perception and planning modules of autonomous vehicles. Its ability to provide quick, diverse, and plausible future trajectories is invaluable for collision avoidance, smooth navigation, and safe interaction in complex traffic environments.

- **Future Research Directions:** The work also opens avenues for future research, such as extending local transport conditioning to joint multi-agent flows to explicitly handle interaction consistency, exploring closed-loop simulation integration, and investigating reverse mappings to anchor space for further regularization. These directions suggest that the principles established by FlowS could serve as a foundation for even more sophisticated and robust autonomous driving systems.
