# General Flow as Foundation Affordance for Scalable Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University and affiliated institutes developed "General Flow," a language-conditioned 3D flow prediction model trained on large-scale human RGBD video datasets, to enable scalable robot learning. The framework achieves an 81% average success rate in zero-shot human-to-robot skill transfer across 18 diverse real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning struggles with acquiring versatile manipulation skills at scale due to the high cost and difficulty of collecting diverse real-world robot data.
- A significant "sim-to-real" domain gap hinders the effective transfer of policies learned in simulation to physical robots.
- Existing flow-based methods for robot learning often suffer from limited scalability or are confined to simulation environments, making real-world deployment challenging.

## Method

- Introduces "==General Flow==," a universal 3D flow prediction target representing future trajectories of points on objects, inspired by Gibson’s theory of affordances.
- Leverages large-scale, cross-embodiment RGBD human video datasets to directly extract 3D flow labels, circumventing the sim-to-real gap.
- Develops ScaleFlow, a language-conditioned 3D flow prediction model trained on this data, incorporating scale-awareness and robustness augmentations like Hand Mask Augmentation.

## Results

- The ScaleFlow model achieves superior quantitative performance in 3D flow prediction, with an Average Displacement Error (ADE) of 3.55cm and Final Displacement Error (FDE) of 4.70cm on a held-out test set.
- The framework successfully achieves an **81%** average success rate in zero-shot human-to-robot skill transfer across 18 diverse manipulation tasks in 6 real-world scenes.
- It demonstrates robust generalization to novel object instances (e.g., an atypical "Safe") and adaptability to variations in gripper position, grasp manner, and segmentation imperfections.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on diverse, large-scale human video data endows the General Flow model with emergent properties like robustness to hand occlusion and label noise.
> - The model exhibits language-driven semantic control, generating distinct actions for identical visual inputs based solely on language instructions.
> - ScaleFlow dynamically adapts predicted trajectory lengths based on the spatial context of the object, showing "scale spatial adaptation."

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is grappling with the fundamental challenge of enabling robots to acquire versatile manipulation skills in the real world at scale. This paper positions itself at the intersection of several critical research directions, drawing inspiration from the successes of large language models (LLMs) and their observed "scaling laws."

**Challenges in Robot Learning:** Current robotic systems face two significant hurdles: 1) the prohibitive cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale, diverse real-world robot data, and 2) the persistent "sim-to-real" domain gap, where policies learned in simulation often fail to transfer effectively to physical robots. These limitations hinder the development of generalist robots capable of performing a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments.

**Inspiration from LLMs:** The authors explicitly draw parallels with LLMs, which achieve remarkable generalization through two key elements: vast training datasets (e.g., internet text) with a minimal inference domain gap, and a foundational, universal prediction task (e.g., text-token prediction). The core idea is to translate these principles to robot learning.

**Existing Approaches and Their Limitations:**
- **Embodiment-Agnostic Frameworks**: Researchers have explored leveraging large-scale, cross-embodiment data, particularly human videos, as a scalable resource for robot learning.
- **Visual Pre-training**: Many methods employ large-scale visual pre-training to learn embodied-aware representations. However, these often exhibit limited generalization to novel tasks or environments.
- **Video Generation for Actions**: Another direction involves generating action signals from videos, aiming to extract implicit motion patterns.
- **Affordances from Simulators**: Some works focus on extracting object affordances (possible actions) from simulation environments. A major drawback here is the aforementioned sim-to-real domain gap, as simulated physics and visual fidelity often deviate from reality, especially for complex 3D interactions.
- **Direct Geometric Information from Human Videos**: More recent efforts attempt to directly extract geometric-aware structured information from human videos. While promising, these often require extensive in-lab training or demonstrate unstable performance in real-world scenarios.
- **Keypoints and Flow-based Methods**: Prior studies have used optical flow or 3D flow as action descriptors for robot learning. However, these methods typically suffer from either limited scalability due to reliance on embodiment-specific robot data or are simulation-based, inheriting the sim-to-real transfer issues. Specifically, Flowbot3D and ToolFlowNet, which also leverage 3D flow, have primarily operated in simulation or with limited real robot data, respectively. Other 2D flow methods often require in-domain finetuning.

**This Work's Contribution to the Landscape:** This paper aims to overcome these limitations by proposing "General Flow" as a foundational affordance. It uniquely leverages *large-scale RGBD human video datasets* directly, bypassing the sim-to-real gap entirely and providing a scalable data source. By training a *language-conditioned 3D flow prediction model* on this data, the work aims to create a universal and actionable representation that facilitates *zero-shot human-to-robot skill transfer* across diverse object categories and tasks. This approach directly addresses the data scalability and domain gap problems, pushing the frontier towards more generalist and adaptable robotic manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a novel framework that enables **scalable learning for robot manipulation**, with the long-term vision of progressively enhancing robot capabilities through increasing data and model sizes, akin to the scaling laws observed in LLMs.

To achieve this, the authors identify several key, interconnected objectives:
1. **Identify a Universal Prediction Target**: A primary motivation is to find a prediction target that can effectively leverage large-scale datasets and offer efficient, universal learning. The paper proposes **3D flow**, representing the future trajectories of 3D points on objects of interest, as this ideal target, terming it "General Flow." This choice is rooted in Gibson’s theory of affordances, which focuses on object-centric actions independent of the manipulator, making it suitable for human-to-robot transfer.
2. **Exploit Scalable Data Resources**: To address the scarcity of real-world robot data and the sim-to-real gap, the authors aim to **leverage large-scale human RGBD video datasets**. This strategy ensures scalability and a "small inference domain-gap" by utilizing real-world data rich in diverse physics interactions and dynamic behaviors.
3. **Develop a Language-Conditioned 3D Flow Prediction Model**: A crucial objective is to, for the first time, develop a model capable of directly predicting language-conditioned 3D flow from these large-scale human video datasets. This model needs to handle variability in trajectory scales and maintain robustness in zero-shot scenarios.
4. **Achieve Zero-Shot Skill Transfer**: The ultimate goal is to demonstrate that the predicted general flow can provide sufficiently actionable guidance to facilitate **zero-shot human-to-robot skill transfer** in real-world environments, without any in-domain finetuning.

The motivation stems from the belief that foundational models for robotics can emerge by combining vast, diverse, real-world data with a universal, actionable prediction task. By focusing on 3D flow, the authors argue for a representation that is scalable, broadly applicable across different object types (rigid, articulated, soft), and provides stable, actionable guidance for robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, "General Flow as Foundation Affordance," consists of three main components: General Flow Label Acquisition, a Scale-Aware Flow Prediction Model, and a Zero-shot Real World Manipulation strategy.

#### 3.1. General Flow Label Acquisition

The authors develop pipelines to extract 3D flow labels directly from two types of cross-embodiment RGBD human video datasets:
- **From HOI4D Dataset**: For rigid and articulated objects, the HOI4D dataset (44.4 hours, 16 categories, 800 objects) provides detailed 3D labels (object pose, camera parameters). The pipeline samples points on active objects and calculates their future 3D positions based on ground truth poses and camera data. Filtering techniques are applied to reduce annotation noise.
- **From Collected RGBD Videos**: For soft object manipulation (e.g., "fold clothes"), the authors collected their own RGBD videos. This pipeline utilizes Human-Object-Interaction (HOI) segmentation (e.g., FastSAM) to obtain active object masks. Points within these masks are then tracked in 2D using Tracking Any Point (TAP) models (e.g., Co-Tracker), and their 3D trajectories are computed via back-projection. Noise reduction and handling of occlusion are incorporated.
A key aspect is that these pipelines are designed to function automatically, without manual intervention, enabling large-scale label extraction directly from real-world human data, thus circumventing the sim-to-real gap.

#### 3.2. Scale-Aware Flow Prediction Model (ScaleFlow)

The core of the methodology is a language-conditioned 3D flow prediction model that is embodiment-agnostic.
- **General Flow Definition**: Formally, given a perception observation (S) and a task instruction (I), for N_q 3D query points (Q), the general flow (F) represents the trajectories of these points over T future timestamps. The model predicts relative displacements (∆p_i_t) rather than absolute positions, which is empirically shown to improve performance.
- **Architecture (Figure 3)**:
 - **Language Conditioning**: A CLIP encoder processes natural language instructions (I) into semantic features, which are then aligned in dimension via MLPs.
 - **Point Cloud Feature Extraction**: Scene point clouds (XYZ+RGB features) are concatenated with the aligned text features. Query points (Q) are also integrated. These merged features are then fed into a PointNeXt backbone (an improved PointNet++) equipped with a segmentation head to extract geometric features.
 - **Multimodal Prediction**: To capture the diversity of human actions for a given task, a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) is employed. The features of the query points serve as condition variables for the CVAE, generating the final flow predictions.
- **Scale-Awareness**: A "Total Length Normalization (TLN)" strategy is introduced to address the significant variance in trajectory lengths (scales) across different points and tasks. The model separately predicts the total trajectory scale (L_i) and a normalized trajectory (∆n_i_t), which are then combined.
- **Loss Function**: The total loss combines: 1) MSE for normalized trajectory prediction (L_traj), 2) MSE for scale regression (L_scale), 3) KL-divergence for the VAE (L_KL), and 4) an MSE for the recovered accumulative shift (L_acc) to minimize cumulative error.
- **Robustness Augmentations**: To boost zero-shot generalization robustness in complex real-world environments, two key augmentations are used during training:
 - **Hand Mask Augmentation (HMA)**: Simulates various robot arm occlusions by selectively dropping out or sampling hand points in the input scene point clouds.
 - **Query Points Sampling (QPS)**: Augments training by switching query point distributions (random vs. anchor-based sampling) to adapt to different downstream application needs.
 - **Scale Rebalance**: To prevent bias towards static predictions, especially for tasks with many static points, a scale rebalance strategy is implemented where data points are resampled based on scale clusters.

#### 3.3. Zero-shot Real-World Manipulation

The predicted general flow is used as actionable guidance for a Franka-Emika robot in a zero-shot, real-world setting, without any in-domain finetuning.
- **Ego-View Setup**: A RealSense D455 RGBD camera is positioned behind the robot arm to capture an ego-view stream, minimizing the inference domain gap from human video data.
- **Heuristic Policy (Closed-Loop)**:
 1. **Scene Segmentation**: The FastSAM model segments the robot from the incoming RGBD stream, using the robot's static base as a prompt (optional additional prompt points via GUI).
 2. **Point Cloud Reconstruction**: 3D scene point clouds are reconstructed.
 3. **Query Point Selection**: Query points are selected from the scene point clouds near the robot's gripper (within 10cm radius).
 4. **Flow Prediction**: The ScaleFlow model predicts the general flow for these query points based on the scene point cloud and task instruction.
 5. **Transformation Calculation**: An ICP (Iterative Closest Point) problem is solved using the SVD algorithm to align the robot arm's end-effector transformation with the predicted flow. A weighting scheme inversely proportional to the distance from the gripper is applied to prioritize relevant points.
 6. **Robot Control**: The derived SE(3) transformation is executed by an impedance controller (Deoxys library) in the operation space. For short trajectories (<5cm), all steps are consolidated and scaled to 5cm to enhance control precision.
- **Task Initiation**: The robotic arm is manually positioned for task initiation (pre-grasp), as the focus is on post-grasp motion guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings across both model prediction performance and real-world robot manipulation.

#### 4.1. General Flow Prediction Performance

- **Superior Accuracy**: Quantitative evaluation on a held-out test set (Table 1) demonstrates that the proposed "ScaleFlow" model achieves superior performance across all metrics (Average Displacement Error - ADE, Final Displacement Error - FDE) compared to various baselines, including 2D models (ResNet, ViT, R3M), a point cloud affordance model (VAT-MART), and other 3D models (PointNeXt variants). For instance, ScaleFlow-L achieves ADE of 3.55cm and FDE of 4.70cm, outperforming a similarly sized PointNeXt-L model (ADE 3.83cm, FDE 5.16cm) with fewer parameters.

#### 4.2. Emergent Properties from Large-Scale Training

The scale of training on diverse human video data endows the General Flow model with notable emergent properties (Figures 8, 9; Table 3):
- **Robustness to Hand Occlusion**: The model maintains robust prediction performance even when hand points (simulating robot arm occlusion) are included or varied in the input, largely due to Hand Mask Augmentation during training.
- **Language-driven Semantic Control**: The model can generate distinct, semantically correct flows for identical visual inputs purely by changing the language instruction (e.g., "open Safe" vs. "close Safe"). This was validated on the real robot for tasks like opening/closing a drawer or refrigerator, and picking up/putting down a mug.
- **Resilience to Label Noise**: The model demonstrates accurate prediction of general flow trends even when trained on data exhibiting significant label noise.
- **Scale Spatial Adaption**: The model dynamically adjusts the predicted trajectory length based on the spatial context of the object. For instance, the "putdown Mug" task shows longer predicted trajectories when the mug starts from a higher position, adapting its scale to reach the table.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Real-World Manipulation Performance

The most striking finding is the model's performance in real-world robot experiments (Table 2):
- **High Success Rate**: Without any in-domain finetuning, the framework achieves an impressive **81% average success rate** across 18 diverse manipulation tasks, spanning 6 distinct real-world scenes. This includes objects of varying types: rigid (Mug, Toy Car), articulated (Safe, Box, Laptop, Refrigerator, Drawer), and soft bodies (Clothes).
- **Stable Skill Transfer**: The results demonstrate stable zero-shot human-to-robot skill transfer, indicating the strong actionability and generalization capacity of General Flow. This represents a significant advancement for flow-based robot learning in real-world settings.
- **Robustness to Segmentation Error**: Real-robot experiments confirm that Hand Mask Augmentation significantly enhances the system's robustness to imperfections in FastSAM robot segmentation, allowing task completion even with partially failed segmentations (Figure 5a).
- **Generalization to Novel Shapes**: The model successfully manipulates a "Box" (an atypical "Safe"), achieving a 100% success rate, even outperforming its success on a conventional "Safe." This highlights the strong generalization of the learned affordance to novel object instances and variations.
- **Robustness to Grasp Manner**: As an object-centric method, General Flow is robust to variations in gripper position and grasp manner (e.g., pulling vs. grasping a refrigerator handle), successfully completing tasks regardless of how the robot initiates interaction.
- **Adaptation to Diverse Scenes and Directions**: The system operates effectively across six diverse scene backgrounds and can handle variations in object manipulation directions (e.g., pushing a toy car in different directions).

#### 4.4. Inference Latency

The system operates with a total inference latency of approximately 405.7ms (2.5Hz) when FastSAM segmentation is included, with FastSAM accounting for the majority (347.6ms). Without FastSAM, the latency drops to 58.1ms (17.5Hz), indicating significant room for improvement by optimizing the segmentation component.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions to the field of robot learning and holds substantial potential impact:

- **Paving the Way for Scalable Robot Learning**: By demonstrating a successful paradigm that leverages large-scale, cross-embodiment human video data, the paper offers a viable pathway to overcome the data scarcity bottleneck in robot learning. This approach directly addresses the challenge of moving towards generalist robots that can acquire skills at scale, much like LLMs acquire language capabilities.
- **Introducing "General Flow" as a Foundational Affordance**: The concept of "General Flow" as a universal, actionable, 3D trajectory-based affordance is a novel and powerful representation. Its embodiment-agnostic and object-centric nature allows it to serve as a versatile bridge for human-to-robot skill transfer, applicable across rigid, articulated, and soft object categories.
- **Achieving Unprecedented Zero-Shot Transfer**: The impressive 81% success rate in zero-shot human-to-robot skill transfer across a diverse set of 18 tasks and 6 scenes marks a significant milestone. This level of generalization, without any in-domain finetuning, is a crucial step towards truly autonomous and adaptable robots that can perform new tasks on unseen objects in novel environments.
- **Bridging LLMs and Robotics with Foundational Principles**: The explicit connection to LLM scaling laws and the successful translation of foundational prediction tasks from text to 3D object dynamics provides a compelling blueprint for how to build more capable embodied AI agents.
- **Practicality and Robustness**: The framework's ability to operate robustly in diverse, unstructured real-world scenes, tolerating segmentation errors and variations in grasp manner, underscores its practical utility. This makes it a strong candidate for real-world deployment scenarios where complex, clean object segmentation or extensive robot-specific data collection is not feasible.
- **Opening New Research Avenues**: The work highlights the potential for future research directions, including:
 - Leveraging even larger RGBD or RGB-D datasets for broader data diversity.
 - Exploring more expressive generative models (e.g., Diffusion models) to capture multimodal human behaviors more comprehensively.
 - Automating the grasping process, which is currently done manually.
 - Extending the representation from object-centric affordance to universal keypoint motions.
 - Integrating General Flow into few-shot imitation learning or reinforcement learning frameworks as a strong prior or action constraint to further enhance dexterity and adaptability.

By making code, data, and supplementary materials publicly available, the authors also contribute to the open-science ethos, facilitating reproducibility and encouraging further innovation in this critical area of robotics.
