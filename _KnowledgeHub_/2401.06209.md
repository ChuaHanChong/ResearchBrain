# Eyes Wide Shut? Exploring the Visual Shortcomings of Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Current multimodal large language models often exhibit systematic visual shortcomings, primarily due to limitations in their CLIP-based visual encoders. Integrating features from vision-only self-supervised learning models can enhance visual grounding without compromising language capabilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) demonstrate impressive reasoning but exhibit elementary visual shortcomings, struggling with fine-grained visual understanding and prone to visual hallucinations.
- The root cause of these MLLM failures is unclear, with a hypothesis that limitations in the visual representations from pre-trained vision models (like CLIP) might be a fundamental bottleneck.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks for MLLMs primarily focus on general Visual Question Answering (VQA) or language-centric aspects of robustness, lacking specific tools to diagnose fundamental visual grounding issues.

## Method

- The study introduces the concept of ==CLIP-blind pairs== (images visually distinct but similar in CLIP embeddings) and constructs the **Multimodal Visual Patterns (MMVP)** benchmark to systematically probe MLLMs' visual shortcomings.
- A comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs and various scaled ==CLIP== models on the MMVP benchmarks was conducted to diagnose the origin of visual errors, specifically linking them to the CLIP visual encoder's limitations.
- The authors propose the **Mixture-of-Features (MoF)** approach, particularly ==Interleaved MoF==, which integrates vision-centric representations from ==self-supervised learning (SSL)== models (like ==DINOv2==) with CLIP features into MLLMs to enhance visual grounding.

## Results

- State-of-the-art MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4V at **38.7%**, LLaVA-1.5 at **24.7%**) perform significantly worse than humans (**95.7%**) on the newly introduced Multimodal Visual Patterns (MMVP) benchmark, indicating a systematic visual incapability.
- Large-scale CLIP models consistently fail on **7 out of 9** identified systematic visual patterns on MMVP-VLM, and their performance correlates strongly with MLLM failures (e.g., Pearson Correlation **>0.7** for LLaVA-1.5), confirming CLIP as a bottleneck.
- The Interleaved Mixture-of-Features (I-MoF) strategy improved visual grounding on MMVP by **+10.7%** for LLaVA, while maintaining or slightly improving instruction-following abilities and reducing hallucination on other standard benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit profound 'visual blind spots' for subtle visual differences that are obvious to humans, scoring significantly lower on targeted benchmarks.
> - The Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) model, commonly used as a visual backbone, is a core bottleneck; scaling CLIP models does not resolve its fundamental inability to capture fine-grained visual patterns, and its weaknesses correlate strongly with MLLM failures.
> - Combining CLIP's semantic understanding with vision-only self-supervised learning (SSL) models' detailed visual grounding via Interleaved Mixture-of-Features (MoF) can synergistically improve MLLMs' visual accuracy while maintaining or enhancing instruction following.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and evolving challenge in the field of artificial intelligence: the robustness and fidelity of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Over the past few years, MLLMs, such as GPT-4V, Gemini, LLaVA, and InstructBLIP, have demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in tasks that integrate vision and language, like image understanding, visual question answering (VQA), and instruction following. These advancements are largely attributed to the powerful reasoning abilities of large language models (LLMs) combined with visual encoders, most notably those built on Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP).

However, despite their impressive performance, a growing body of research has begun to expose shortcomings in these models, particularly concerning "hallucinations" (generating factually incorrect or unsupported information) and a lack of fine-grained visual understanding. This paper positions itself squarely within this critical examination. It argues that while LLMs provide powerful reasoning, the visual component, often reliant on instance-level contrastive pre-training like CLIP, might be a fundamental bottleneck, leading to "systematic shortcomings" in visual grounding.

The paper differentiates itself from prior evaluation benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2, GQA, TextVQA) that focus on general VQA capabilities, and even from more recent benchmarks designed for MLLMs (e.g., MM-Vet, POPE, MM-Bench) that primarily assess reasoning, robustness, or hallucination from a language perspective. Instead, "Eyes Wide Shut?" delves into the *roots* of these errors, hypothesizing that they stem from deficiencies in the visual representations themselves, rather than merely from the alignment or language understanding components. This focus on the visual encoder's limitations marks a significant shift, challenging the prevailing assumption that scaling up training data and model size (a common panacea in deep learning) is sufficient to resolve these visual deficiencies.

Furthermore, the work connects to ongoing discussions in visual representation learning, particularly the distinction between vision-and-language models (like CLIP) and vision-only self-supervised learning (SSL) models (like DINOv2, MAE, MoCoV3). It suggests that these two paradigms capture complementary features, and the paper's proposed "Mixture-of-Features" (MoF) approach is an initial step towards combining their strengths. By demonstrating that even state-of-the-art MLLMs struggle with "seemingly simple questions due to inaccurate visual grounding," the authors highlight a fundamental gap that needs to be addressed for the future development of truly robust and accurate multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research stems from a critical observation: despite the remarkable progress and impressive demonstrations of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), they still exhibit surprising and elementary visual shortcomings. The authors explicitly ask: "Where do these problems originate? Is it a deficiency in visual modality, language understanding, or their alignment?" This foundational question drives the entire research agenda.

The key objectives of the paper are threefold:

1. **Systematically Identify and Characterize Visual Shortcomings in MLLMs:** The primary objective is to move beyond anecdotal failures and rigorously demonstrate that MLLMs, including leading models like GPT-4V, struggle with basic visual grounding tasks. This involves creating a novel benchmark that specifically targets these deficiencies.
2. **Uncover the Root Causes of These Shortcomings, Focusing on the Visual Encoder:** The authors hypothesize that these failures might stem from limitations in the visual representations provided by the pre-trained vision models, particularly CLIP, which serves as the visual backbone for many open-source MLLMs. A crucial objective is to empirically investigate this hypothesis and show a correlation between CLIP's weaknesses and MLLMs' failures.
3. **Propose and Validate Initial Approaches to Enhance Visual Grounding:** Based on their diagnosis, the paper aims to explore methods to improve the visual capabilities of MLLMs. Specifically, they investigate whether integrating features from vision-only self-supervised learning (SSL) models, which are known to capture rich visual details, can address the observed deficiencies without compromising the strong language capabilities of MLLMs.

In essence, the paper aims to critically evaluate the "vision" component of MLLMs, arguing that the success of these models currently disproportionately relies on the "language" (LLM) aspect, potentially overlooking fundamental limitations in how they truly "see" and interpret images at a fine-grained level. The motivation is to pinpoint these bottlenecks and guide future research towards developing more robust and visually accurate multimodal AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic, multi-stage methodology to identify, diagnose, and propose solutions for the visual shortcomings of MLLMs.

#### 3.1. Identifying CLIP-blind Pairs

The core of the methodology begins with identifying instances where the Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) model, a widely used visual encoder in MLLMs, fails to distinguish visually distinct images. The authors introduce the concept of "CLIP-blind pairs": images that CLIP perceives as highly similar (cosine similarity > 0.95 in CLIP embedding space) but are visually different according to a vision-only self-supervised learning (SSL) model (cosine similarity < 0.6 in DINOv2 embedding space). DINOv2 is chosen as the reference SSL model due to its demonstrated ability to capture fine-grained visual details without language supervision. These pairs are collected from large datasets like ImageNet-1K and LAION-Aesthetics.

#### 3.2. Constructing the Multimodal Visual Patterns (MMVP) Benchmark

Using the identified CLIP-blind pairs, the authors construct two benchmarks:

- **MMVP-MLLM Benchmark:** This is a Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmark designed to probe MLLMs' understanding of the subtle visual differences overlooked by CLIP. For each CLIP-blind pair (e.g., a dog facing left vs. a dog facing right), human annotators meticulously identify the visual discrepancies and formulate straightforward, unambiguous questions (e.g., "Is the dog facing left or right?"). The benchmark comprises 150 such pairs, totaling 300 questions. A crucial scoring criterion is that a model is considered correct *only if it accurately answers both questions* associated with a CLIP-blind pair, ensuring it truly grasps the visual difference.
- **MMVP-VLM Benchmark:** To directly assess the visual understanding capabilities of various CLIP-based models (Vision-Language Models), a subset of MMVP questions is distilled into simpler language descriptions. This benchmark categorizes the questions into nine "visual patterns" (detailed below) and evaluates if CLIP models can correctly match image-text combinations based on these patterns. Each pattern is represented by 15 text-image pairs.

#### 3.3. Evaluating State-of-the-Art MLLMs and CLIP Models

- **MLLM Evaluation:** A comprehensive evaluation is performed on state-of-the-art open-source MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Mini-GPT4) and closed-source models (GPT-4V, Gemini, Bard, Bing Chat). Each question from MMVP-MLLM is queried independently. Human performance on the same benchmark is also assessed via a user study to establish a baseline for "ease" of questions.
- **CLIP Model Evaluation:** Various scaled-up CLIP models (different sizes, training data, and methodologies like OpenAI CLIP, SigLIP, DFN, MetaCLIP, EVA-CLIP) are evaluated on the MMVP-VLM benchmark to determine if scaling alone resolves the identified visual patterns.

#### 3.4. Identifying Systematic Visual Patterns

To understand *why* CLIP and MLLMs fail, the authors use GPT-4 to categorize the MMVP questions and options into nine systematic visual patterns that CLIP vision encoders consistently misinterpret. These patterns include: "Orientation and Direction," "Presence of Specific Features," "State and Condition," "Quantity and Count," "Positional and Relational Context," "Color and Appearance," "Structural and Physical Characteristics," "Texts," and "Viewpoint and Perspective." This categorization allows for a more granular analysis of failure modes. A Pearson Correlation Coefficient is computed to quantify the relationship between CLIP's performance on these patterns and MLLMs' performance.

#### 3.5. Proposing and Evaluating Mixture-of-Features (MoF) Approach

As an initial effort to address the identified shortcomings, the authors propose MoF, integrating vision-centric representations from SSL models (DINOv2) into MLLMs. LLaVA is adopted as the base MLLM framework.
- **Additive MoF (A-MoF):** Linearly mixes CLIP and DINOv2 features with varying proportions (controlled by coefficient α) before feeding them to the adapter and LLM. This explores the trade-off between visual grounding (DINOv2) and instruction following (CLIP).
- **Interleaved MoF (I-MoF):** A more sophisticated approach where images pass through both CLIP and DINOv2 encoders. Their respective features are then individually processed by adapters, and the resulting visual tokens are spatially interleaved before being fed to the LLM. This aims to leverage the strengths of both encoders simultaneously without adverse trade-offs.
MoF approaches are evaluated on both the MMVP benchmark (for visual grounding) and the LLaVA benchmark (for instruction-following ability), as well as POPE for hallucination testing and other standard VQA benchmarks like MMBench and GQA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings that challenge conventional wisdom regarding the visual capabilities of current MLLMs and their underlying vision encoders.

#### 4.1. MLLMs' Systematic Visual Shortcomings

- **Significant Performance Gap:** The MMVP-MLLM benchmark reveals a substantial performance disparity between humans and even state-of-the-art MLLMs. Human participants achieved an average accuracy of 95.7% on the "straightforward" questions, whereas GPT-4V, the best-performing MLLM, only reached 38.7% accuracy. Other prominent MLLMs like Gemini Pro (40.7%) and LLaVA-1.5 (24.7%) performed even worse, with many models scoring below random guess levels (25%). This indicates a profound struggle with basic visual grounding.
- **Visual Incapability vs. Hallucination:** Ablation studies confirmed that these errors primarily stem from genuine visual incapability, rather than issues with language model hallucination or question phrasing. Swapping options or changing notations had minimal impact on performance.

#### 4.2. CLIP as a Bottleneck and the Limits of Scaling

- **Persistent Failures in CLIP:** The MMVP-VLM benchmark demonstrated that various CLIP-based models, even those significantly scaled up in size and training data, consistently struggle with specific visual patterns. Out of the nine identified patterns, 7 (including "orientation," "quantity," "presence of specific features," "positional context," "structural characteristics," "texts," and "viewpoint") could not be resolved by any large-scale CLIP-based model. Only "color and appearance" and "state and condition" showed some improvement with scaling.
- **Weak Correlation with ImageNet Zero-Shot:** The study found that high ImageNet-1K zero-shot accuracy, a common metric for evaluating visual encoders, does not necessarily correlate with better performance on MMVP-VLM for models with superior ImageNet accuracy. This suggests that ImageNet accuracy alone is insufficient to assess a model's true visual grounding capabilities.
- **Strong Correlation Between CLIP and MLLM Failures:** A crucial finding is the strong positive Pearson Correlation Coefficient (e.g., >0.7 for LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP) between the visual patterns that challenge CLIP models and those that challenge MLLMs. This empirically supports the hypothesis that weaknesses in the CLIP vision encoder are directly transferred to downstream MLLMs, making CLIP a significant bottleneck.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Mixture-of-Features (MoF) Approach

- **Additive MoF Trade-off:** Experiments with Additive MoF (A-MoF) revealed a trade-off: increasing the proportion of DINOv2 (vision-only SSL) features improved visual grounding on MMVP but led to a decline in instruction-following capability on the LLaVA benchmark. Conversely, more CLIP features enhanced language comprehension but reduced visual grounding.
- **Interleaved MoF for Synergistic Improvement:** The Interleaved MoF (I-MoF) strategy successfully leveraged the strengths of both CLIP and DINOv2. Under the LLaVA setting, I-MoF significantly enhanced visual grounding (e.g., +10.7% on MMVP) while maintaining or even slightly improving instruction-following abilities and reducing hallucination (on POPE benchmark). This improvement was consistent across different base MLLM versions (LLaVA and LLaVA-1.5) and various vision-only SSL models (MAE, MoCoV3, DINOv2). Increasing token count merely by higher resolution did not yield similar gains, emphasizing that feature *interleaving* from complementary sources is key.

In summary, the results paint a clear picture: current MLLMs possess a significant "visual blind spot" rooted in the limitations of their CLIP-based vision encoders. This issue is not resolved by mere scaling, but can be mitigated by strategically integrating features from vision-only self-supervised learning models that excel at capturing fine-grained visual details.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries substantial significance for the future direction of multimodal AI development, particularly for MLLMs.

1. **Challenges the "Scaling is a Panacea" Narrative:** The most impactful finding is the demonstration that simply scaling up CLIP models (in terms of parameters or training data) does not inherently resolve their fundamental inability to capture fine-grained visual details and specific visual patterns. This directly challenges the prevailing belief in the AI community that more data and larger models will automatically overcome all limitations. It suggests that architectural changes or novel training paradigms for vision encoders are necessary.

2. **Highlights the Criticality of the Visual Encoder:** The paper conclusively shows that the visual encoder, often overlooked in the hype surrounding large language models, is a critical bottleneck for MLLMs. This shifts attention from solely optimizing LLM architectures and language-vision alignment mechanisms to also fundamentally improving how these models "see." For developers and researchers building MLLMs, this implies a need to re-evaluate the visual backbone as a first-order problem.

3. **Proposes a Concrete Direction for Improvement:** The Mixture-of-Features (MoF), particularly the Interleaved MoF, offers a practical and effective initial solution. By demonstrating that combining CLIP's semantic understanding with vision-only SSL models' detailed visual grounding leads to synergistic performance gains, the paper provides a clear pathway for improving current MLLMs. This could inspire further research into hybrid visual encoders or novel pre-training objectives that integrate diverse visual representation learning paradigms.

4. **Advocates for New Evaluation Metrics:** The creation of the MMVP benchmark is significant. It reveals that traditional benchmarks like ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, while useful, do not adequately capture the visual nuances required for robust MLLM performance. The MMVP's focus on "CLIP-blind pairs" and specific visual patterns provides a more diagnostic tool for evaluating true visual grounding, encouraging the development of more comprehensive evaluation frameworks beyond simple classification tasks.

5. **Impact on AI Safety and Robustness:** MLLMs are increasingly deployed in real-world applications where accurate visual perception is paramount (e.g., robotics, autonomous systems, medical imaging interpretation). If these models systematically fail on "elementary" visual questions, it raises significant concerns about their reliability and safety. By exposing these vulnerabilities, the research contributes to the broader effort of building more robust, trustworthy, and less "hallucinatory" AI systems.

6. **Motivates Fundamental Research in Vision Models:** By pinpointing the limitations of current visual representations, especially CLIP, the paper issues a call to action for the computer vision community. It underscores the need for continued innovation in visual representation learning algorithms that can capture both high-level semantics and fine-grained visual details, truly enabling machines to "see" with precision. The involvement of a figure like Yann LeCun further amplifies this call for fundamental advancements in vision models.

In conclusion, "Eyes Wide Shut?" serves as a crucial wake-up call, urging the AI community to critically re-evaluate the visual foundation of powerful multimodal models. Its findings and proposed solutions have the potential to significantly influence the design, training, and evaluation of future MLLMs, paving the way for more visually intelligent and reliable AI.
