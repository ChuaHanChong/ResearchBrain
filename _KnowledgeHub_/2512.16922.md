# Next-Embedding Prediction Makes Strong Vision Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Michigan, NYU, Princeton, and University of Virginia developed Next-Embedding Predictive Autoregression (NEPA), a self-supervised learning approach that adapts NLP's generative pretraining to vision. NEPA achieves competitive results, such as 83.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with a ViT-B and 54.0% mIoU on ADE20K with a ViT-L, using a simplified autoregressive Transformer architecture for next-embedding prediction.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning methods in computer vision often rely on complex pretext tasks or auxiliary architectural components.
- A need existed to transfer the success of generative pretraining from natural language processing to the visual domain.
- Current visual SSL approaches typically focus on learning static feature representations rather than modeling data distribution directly.

## Method

- Developed ==Next-Embedding Predictive Autoregressive (NEPA)== pretraining, which applies next-element prediction from NLP to continuous patch embeddings in vision.
- A standard Vision Transformer, equipped with ==causal attention masking==, is trained to autoregressively predict the embedding of the next image patch.
- Utilizes a ==similarity-based loss function== with a ==stop-gradient operation== on the target embeddings to ensure robust and non-trivial learning.

## Results

- NEPA achieves **83.8%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with a ViT-B and **85.3%** with a ViT-L, matching or exceeding leading self-supervised methods.
- The method demonstrates strong transferability to dense prediction tasks, scoring **48.3%** mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation with ViT-B and **54.0%** with ViT-L.
- Qualitative analyses show NEPA learns long-range, object-centric attention patterns and semantically coherent embeddings, indicating a global understanding of image structure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A simple, causal next-embedding prediction objective can drive powerful self-supervised learning in vision without complex auxiliary components.
> - Masking input embeddings in NEPA's autoregressive setup degrades performance, indicating the objective inherently avoids shortcut solutions.
> - The core components of autoregressive shifting, causal masking, and stop-gradient are essential for preventing model collapse and achieving strong performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental question in computer vision: how to effectively pretrain visual models without large amounts of labeled data. Self-supervised learning (SSL) has become the dominant paradigm for visual pretraining, enabling scalable learners by designing pretext tasks that generate supervisory signals from the data itself.

The current landscape of visual SSL is primarily characterized by three main approaches:
1. **Contrastive Learning:** Methods like MoCo and DINO train models to learn invariant representations by maximizing agreement between different augmented views of the same image while distinguishing them from negative samples. These methods often involve Siamese networks, momentum encoders, and large batch sizes or memory banks.
2. **Masked Image Modeling (MIM):** Approaches such as MAE and BEiT borrow inspiration from BERT in NLP. They mask portions of an input image (or its discrete tokens) and train a Vision Transformer (ViT) to reconstruct the missing information, often using a lightweight decoder.
3. **Representation Prediction:** Emerging methods like JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) move beyond pixel-level reconstruction to predict latent semantic targets. However, JEPA still typically relies on auxiliary encoders and prediction heads, and often predicts in parallel rather than a purely causal, autoregressive manner.

The authors argue that a "fundamentally different paradigm" has driven the success of modern natural language processing (NLP), specifically **generative pretraining** (e.g., GPT, LLMs). In NLP, models are trained as generative and predictive systems to model the data distribution through simple, causal objectives (e.g., next-token prediction). This contrasts with the prevalent "representation learning" philosophy in vision, where models are optimized to extract static features for downstream tasks.

NEPA aims to bridge this philosophical gap by applying the core principles of NLP's generative pretraining directly to vision. It draws a direct analogy to next-token prediction in language models but operates on continuous **patch embeddings** rather than discrete tokens or raw pixels. While previous visual generative pretraining efforts exist (e.g., iGPT for pixel-level autoregression, AIM for continuous patch values, or methods using discrete tokenizers like VQ-VAE for image generation), NEPA distinguishes itself by:
- Operating directly in the continuous embedding space, avoiding the need for pixel decoders or discrete visual tokenizers.
- Formulating the task as a purely causal *next-embedding prediction*, focusing on the predictive power of the model itself.
- Adopting a deliberately minimalist architecture without the complex auxiliary components (e.g., momentum encoders, projection heads, multi-branch designs) common in contrastive or representation prediction methods.

By doing so, NEPA challenges the notion that visual SSL requires complex pretext tasks or specialized architectural components, proposing a simpler, scalable, and potentially modality-agnostic alternative. It positions itself as a direct exploration of whether "minimal causal pretraining might also give rise to strong vision learners," mirroring the success of sequence-to-sequence prediction in LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to ascertain whether the foundational principles of generative pretraining, which have revolutionized natural language processing, can be successfully adapted to and yield powerful self-supervised learners in the domain of computer vision. The authors specifically question if the shift from "learning representations to learning models" observed in NLP can be effectively translated to visual tasks.

The core motivation behind NEPA is driven by several key aspects:

1. **Emulating NLP's Generative Success:** Modern language models achieve their remarkable capabilities by being trained as generative and predictive systems, rather than mere feature extractors. The authors hypothesize that a similar causal, next-element prediction objective applied to visual data could compel models to internalize rich semantic and conditional dependencies within images, much like LLMs do for language.

2. **Simplifying Pretraining Paradigms:** Current self-supervised visual learning methods often involve complex designs:
 - **Pixel Reconstruction:** (e.g., MAE) requires a decoder to reconstruct masked pixels, adding architectural complexity and sometimes focusing on low-level details.
 - **Discrete Tokens:** (e.g., BEiT) necessitate a visual tokenizer to convert images into discrete codes, introducing an additional model and potential quantization errors.
 - **Contrastive Learning:** (e.g., MoCo, DINO) relies on carefully engineered data augmentations, negative pairs, momentum encoders, or self-distillation, which can be sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and require specific training strategies.
 The authors aim to eliminate these complexities, proposing a "deliberately minimalist" approach that derives its entire learning signal from a simple predictive task.

3. **Achieving Scalability and Architectural Simplicity:** By formulating the task as next-embedding prediction within a standard Transformer architecture and leveraging modern stabilization techniques, NEPA seeks to retain the inherent scalability of Transformers without introducing task-specific heads, auxiliary branches, or complex loss functions during pretraining. This simplicity is crucial for efficiently scaling models to larger datasets and architectures.

4. **Developing a Potentially Modality-Agnostic Approach:** The concept of predicting the "next embedding" in a latent space closely mirrors the tied embeddings mechanism in large language models. This suggests that if successful, NEPA could offer a unifying framework for pretraining across different modalities, with embeddings serving as a common representational currency. This potential for modality-agnostic pretraining is a significant long-term vision.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a desire to find a more elegant, efficient, and universally applicable self-supervised pretraining method for vision, directly inspired by the proven success of generative prediction in language.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of NEPA centers on a straightforward yet powerful objective: **Next-Embedding Predictive Autoregression**.

#### 3.1. Next-Embedding Prediction Objective

1. **Patch Embedding:** An input image `x` is initially divided into `T` non-overlapping patches. Each patch `x_t` is then mapped to a continuous embedding `z_t` using a shared encoder `f` (a convolutional patch embedding layer), forming a sequence of embeddings `z = {z_1..., z_T}`.
2. **Autoregressive Prediction:** An autoregressive predictor `h_θ` (a Vision Transformer) is trained to predict the embedding of the *next* patch `bz_{t+1}` based on all *previous* embeddings `z_<=t`. This directly mimics the next-token prediction task in language models.
3. **Loss Function:** A similarity-based loss function, inspired by SimSiam, is employed. The normalized predicted embedding `bz_{t+1}` is compared to the normalized ground-truth embedding `z_{t+1}` (from the encoder `f`) using negative cosine similarity.
4. **Stop-Gradient:** Crucially, a `stop-gradient` operation is applied to the target embeddings `z_{t+1}`. This prevents a trivial solution where the model could simply copy its input or collapse all embeddings to an identical value, ensuring the model learns meaningful predictive relationships. The entire training process involves a single forward pass of the Transformer.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

NEPA utilizes a **standard Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone** as its autoregressive predictor.
- **Causal Attention Masking:** During pretraining, the Transformer's self-attention mechanism is configured with a causal mask, allowing each patch embedding to attend only to preceding embeddings in the sequence. This enforces the autoregressive nature of the prediction.
- **No Separate Decoder:** Unlike reconstruction-based methods (e.g., MAE), NEPA does not require an additional decoder network to reconstruct pixels or tokens. The Transformer directly outputs the predicted embeddings.
- **Modern Stabilization Components:** To enhance training stability and scalability, especially with large Transformers, NEPA incorporates several modern architectural best practices, inspired by DINOv3 and VisionLLaMA:
 - **Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE):** Used at all layers to encode relative positional information, improving generalization and handling varying sequence lengths.
 - **LayerScale:** Learnable per-channel scales are applied to residual branches to stabilize training and accelerate convergence.
 - **SwiGLU Activation:** The standard GeLU activation in the feed-forward networks is replaced with SwiGLU, aligning with recent state-of-the-art models.
 - **QK-Norm (Query-Key Normalization):** Applied to query and key projections to prevent gradient explosion or collapse, especially in deeper Transformers.
These components are considered orthogonal to the core NEPA objective but contribute significantly to its robust performance.

#### 3.3. Task-Specific Heads for Downstream Evaluation

After pretraining, the NEPA model is fine-tuned for specific downstream vision tasks using lightweight, standard heads:
- **Image Classification:** A linear classification head is appended to the Transformer's output. For classification, the last embedding `z_T` (or the class token, if used) is typically used as the compact image representation.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** A UPerNet head, which operates on multi-scale features extracted from intermediate Transformer blocks, is attached. During fine-tuning for segmentation, the causal masking is typically disabled, allowing for bidirectional attention to leverage full spatial context.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **Pretraining Data:** ImageNet-1K dataset, used without labels for self-supervision.
- **Backbones:** Experiments conducted with ViT-Base (ViT-B) and ViT-Large (ViT-L) models, with a patch size of 14.
- **Batch Size:** Global batch size of 4096.
- **Training Infrastructure:** HuggingFace Trainer with distributed data parallelism (DDP) on NVIDIA H100 GPUs.
- **EMA:** Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is used for the model weights to improve stability and performance.
- **Fine-tuning:** Standard fine-tuning protocols are followed, including layer-wise learning rate decay, RandAugment, label smoothing, mixup, cutmix, and DropPath.

This meticulously designed methodology allows NEPA to rigorously test its hypothesis of simple, next-embedding prediction as a powerful visual learning objective, while leveraging modern Transformer best practices for optimal performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on NEPA comprehensively demonstrate the effectiveness and simplicity of next-embedding prediction as a self-supervised learning objective for vision.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies on Core Algorithm Design

The core components of NEPA (shifting, causal masking, stop-gradient) are shown to be essential:
- **Autoregressive Shifting:** Removing the shift (reducing the objective to an identity mapping) causes the model to diverge during fine-tuning. This confirms that predicting the *next* embedding is crucial for a meaningful learning signal.
- **Causal Masking:** Without causal masking (i.e., allowing bidirectional attention during pretraining), the model's performance drops significantly (73.6% vs 76.8% for 50k steps on ViT-B). This highlights that enforcing a causal structure is key to preventing trivial reconstruction and encouraging more complex understanding.
- **Stop-Gradient:** Eliminating the stop-gradient operation leads to a complete collapse of training, where all embeddings become identical. This validates its critical role in preventing degenerate solutions, similar to its function in SimSiam.
- **Random Masking:** Contrary to masked image modeling (MAE), introducing random masking to the input embeddings *degrades* performance (e.g., 0% masking: 78.2%, 40% masking: 76.4%, 60% masking: 75.7% for 100k steps on ViT-B). This is a significant finding, indicating that the autoregressive prediction setup inherently avoids shortcut solutions, and masking simply corrupts the input sequence, disrupting the learning signal rather than enhancing it.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on Orthogonal Components

The modern architectural components (LayerScale, RoPE, QK-Norm, SwiGLU) incrementally improve performance:
- **RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding)** and **QK-Norm (Query-Key Normalization)** contribute significantly to performance gains and training stability, especially when combined with SwiGLU. QK-Norm prevents gradient explosion when SwiGLU is used.
- **LayerScale** improves pretraining stability and convergence speed, although it might slightly slow down fine-tuning if not managed (e.g., by freezing the patch embedding layer).
- **SwiGLU** shows marginal improvements but is retained for architectural alignment with state-of-the-art vision and language models.

#### 4.3. Performance on ImageNet-1K Classification

NEPA demonstrates highly competitive performance on ImageNet-1K image classification after fine-tuning:
- **ViT-B:** Achieves 83.8% top-1 accuracy.
- **ViT-L:** Achieves 85.3% top-1 accuracy.
These results are comparable to or slightly outperform leading methods like MAE (ViT-B: 83.6%, ViT-L: 85.6%), BEiT (ViT-B: 83.4%, ViT-L: 85.2%), and MoCo v3 (ViT-B: 83.2%, ViT-L: 84.1%), despite NEPA's simpler pretraining paradigm (single forward pass, no separate decoder, no auxiliary losses, no specific data augmentations beyond standard). The ability to achieve competitive results even with causal attention during fine-tuning (82.5% for ViT-B) underscores the richness of features learned.

#### 4.4. Performance on ADE20K Semantic Segmentation

NEPA effectively transfers to dense prediction tasks:
- **ViT-B:** Achieves 48.3% mIoU.
- **ViT-L:** Achieves 54.0% mIoU.
These scores are competitive with or surpass methods like supervised pretraining, MoCo v3, BEiT, and MAE. This finding is crucial as it demonstrates that representations learned purely through next-embedding prediction, without any pixel-level objective during pretraining, are highly transferable to tasks requiring detailed spatial understanding.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis (Attention Maps and Embedding Similarity)

- **Attention Maps:** Visualizations show that NEPA learns long-range, object-centric attention patterns. When predicting a future patch, the model focuses its attention on semantically related regions, often other parts of the same object, even if spatially distant. This indicates that the predictive objective fosters a global understanding of object structure.
- **Embedding Similarity:** Predicted embeddings exhibit high cosine similarity to patches belonging to the same object or semantic region, including occluded parts, and low similarity to unrelated background areas. This suggests that NEPA learns semantically coherent and organized representations at an object level.

#### 4.6. Limitations and Failure Cases

- **Linear Probing:** NEPA performs poorly under linear probing (e.g., 11.3% for ViT-B), which is expected because its output embeddings are inherently close to the input features, making a shallow linear classifier insufficient to capture the model's full learned capacity.
- **Complex Scenes:** NEPA struggles with scenes requiring complex physical understanding (e.g., reflections, shading, shadows) or containing many small/overlapping objects. This might be a limitation of the ImageNet-1K dataset's complexity or the current model scale.

In summary, NEPA successfully validates its core hypothesis, demonstrating that simple next-embedding prediction can yield strong, scalable vision learners that achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks, while also providing compelling qualitative evidence for the learned semantic organization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Next-Embedding Prediction Makes Strong Vision Learners" paper introduces a highly significant contribution to the field of self-supervised learning in computer vision, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Visual Self-Supervised Learning

NEPA represents a pivotal shift from the dominant "representation learning" paradigm in vision, which focuses on training models as feature extractors for downstream use, to a "generative and predictive model" paradigm. By directly applying the causal next-token prediction principle from NLP to continuous visual embeddings, NEPA demonstrates that vision models can be trained to deeply understand image structure by modeling the data distribution itself. This fundamentally re-conceptualizes how visual intelligence might emerge in artificial systems.

#### 5.2. Architectural Simplicity and Scalability

One of the most profound impacts of NEPA is its architectural minimalism. Unlike many leading self-supervised methods that require complex components like pixel decoders, discrete tokenizers, momentum encoders, or multi-branch architectures, NEPA achieves competitive (and often superior) performance with a simple, single-stream autoregressive Transformer. This simplicity significantly reduces architectural complexity, making models easier to design, train, and scale. The single forward pass during pretraining also offers computational efficiency advantages, paving the way for even larger and more efficient vision models.

#### 5.3. Unifying Modality-Agnostic Pretraining

The paper highlights a crucial theoretical insight: the mechanism of "predicting the next embedding in the latent space" directly parallels the concept of tied embeddings used in Large Language Models (LLMs). This suggests that NEPA is not just a new vision model but a potential blueprint for a **modality-agnostic pretraining objective**. If vision and other modalities (e.g., audio, 3D data) can be uniformly represented as sequences of continuous embeddings, then the same next-embedding prediction objective could potentially unify pretraining across all these domains. This has immense potential for developing truly multimodal foundation models.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Generative Models

NEPA's autoregressive embedding prediction inherently positions it as a strong foundation for generative modeling. By learning to anticipate future embeddings, the model implicitly learns the underlying generative process of images. Future work could easily couple a pretrained NEPA backbone with a suitable image decoder (e.g., based on diffusion or GANs) to enable high-quality image synthesis, editing, or other generative tasks. This could lead to unified models that simultaneously learn robust representations and generate new content from a single pretraining objective.

#### 5.5. Strong Empirical Performance

Beyond its conceptual elegance, NEPA delivers strong empirical results on standard benchmarks like ImageNet-1K for classification and ADE20K for semantic segmentation. Matching or exceeding the performance of more complex methods with a simpler setup reinforces the efficacy of its core idea. The qualitative analyses of attention maps and embedding similarity further support that NEPA learns semantically rich, object-centric, and globally coherent representations without explicit supervision.

#### 5.6. Directions for Future Research

NEPA opens several exciting avenues for future exploration:
- **Scaling with Larger and More Diverse Datasets:** Investigating NEPA's performance and generalization capabilities when pretrained on larger, more diverse datasets beyond ImageNet-1K (e.g., JFT, LAION) could reveal its full potential.
- **Full Generative Capabilities:** Developing and integrating explicit image decoders to unlock NEPA's generative abilities for tasks like image synthesis and editing.
- **Cross-Modal Integration:** Exploring its application to other modalities and combinations thereof, to fully test the hypothesis of a modality-agnostic pretraining framework.
- **Understanding Predictive Dynamics:** Further theoretical and empirical work to understand *why* next-embedding prediction is such a powerful learning signal for visual semantics.

In conclusion, NEPA is a significant step towards simpler, more scalable, and potentially more unified self-supervised learning. By boldly re-imagining visual pretraining through the lens of generative prediction, it offers a compelling alternative to existing paradigms and sets the stage for future advancements in AI's ability to understand and generate diverse forms of data.
