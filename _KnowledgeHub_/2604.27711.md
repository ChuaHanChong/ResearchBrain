# ExoActor: Exocentric Video Generation as Generalizable Interactive Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from BAAI developed ExoActor, a framework leveraging exocentric video generation to enable humanoid robots to perform complex, interaction-rich tasks from high-level instructions. This method synthesizes task-consistent videos as imagined demonstrations, which are then translated into physical actions for the robot, reducing the need for extensive task-specific data.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with performing fluent, interaction-rich behaviors in unstructured human environments.
- Current control systems find it difficult to jointly model spatial context, temporal dynamics, robot actions, and task intent at scale.
- Generating diverse robot behaviors typically requires extensive task-specific data collection or fine-tuning, limiting generalization to new scenarios.

## Method

- **ExoActor** proposes a unified, end-to-end framework using ==third-person video generation== as an intermediate interface to synthesize ==imagined demonstrations==.
- Pipeline: ==robot-to-human embodiment transfer==, decomposition of high-level instructions into step-wise action prompts, and ==task-consistent video generation==.
- Generated videos undergo ==3D whole-body and hand motion estimation==, deployed on a general motion tracking controller for physical execution without explicit retargeting.

## Results

- Reliable execution of **Level B** (basic locomotion) and **Level A** (coordinated navigation, coarse interaction) tasks in real-world experiments.
- Challenging **Level S** fine-grained multi-step manipulation tasks achieved, sometimes requiring minor physical scene adjustments.
- Ablations confirmed **Kling 3**'s superiority for video generation; direct motion feeding into the controller is preferred over retargeting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale video generation models can effectively be leveraged to implicitly encode and model complex interaction dynamics for generalizable humanoid control.
> - Reframing the challenge of complex humanoid behavior generation as a video synthesis problem provides a flexible and expressive interface for capturing long-horizon behaviors.
> - A modular pipeline, separating video generation, motion estimation, and motion execution, enables independent advancements and promotes overall system scalability and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "ExoActor" addresses a fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics: enabling fluent, interaction-rich behaviors between a robot, its environment, and task-relevant objects while maintaining generalization across diverse scenarios. Despite advancements in learning-based control and whole-body coordination, current systems often struggle with jointly modeling spatial context, temporal dynamics, robot actions, and task intent at scale. This limitation typically necessitates extensive, task-specific data collection, domain-specific tuning, or carefully curated demonstrations, leading to policies that may fail in new, unseen environments.

ExoActor positions itself within a burgeoning area of research that leverages the generalization capabilities of large-scale generative models, particularly video generation models, to overcome these limitations. This approach contrasts with traditional methods that focus on direct policy learning from demonstrations or explicit world models for prediction. Instead, ExoActor proposes using third-person video generation as a unified intermediate interface to synthesize "imagined demonstrations" of interaction dynamics. This strategy decouples the high-level task understanding and interaction modeling from the low-level robot control, allowing humanoid systems to benefit from the broad visual priors learned by large video models.

The paper situates its contributions in relation to several related fields:
- **World Action Models for Robotics:** These models aim to predict future observations based on agent actions, providing predictive structure for control and policy learning. While sharing an interest in visually grounded predictive modeling, ExoActor differs by using generative video models to *synthesize* plausible execution processes as an interface, rather than focusing on learning an explicit predictive model of the world's dynamics for planning.
- **Physics Simulation Based on World Models:** This area explores learned simulators as alternatives to classical physics engines for robot learning, enabling planning and policy adaptation in visually rich settings. ExoActor, however, bypasses the need for an explicit learned world model or simulation loop for prediction, instead leveraging generative video models to directly produce action demonstrations.
- **Generalizable Humanoid Control:** This field seeks robust whole-body behaviors across varied tasks, environments, and robot embodiments. Previous efforts include grounding high-level semantics into executable behaviors and improving controller generalization through human-centric supervision or dynamic motion tracking. ExoActor contributes by offering a scalable method for transferring the implicit interaction knowledge contained in large video models to humanoid control, reducing reliance on expensive robot-specific data.

In essence, ExoActor introduces a novel pipeline that reframes the challenge of complex humanoid behavior generation as a video synthesis problem, followed by a translation into physical actions, thereby offering a distinct approach to achieving generalizable interactive humanoid control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to operate effectively in unstructured human environments and execute a wide array of tasks without requiring extensive task-specific engineering or human supervision. The authors identify a significant bottleneck in current humanoid control systems: the difficulty in modeling fluent, interaction-rich behaviors that demand a joint understanding of spatial context, temporal dynamics, robot actions, and task intent at scale. Conventional supervision methods are deemed insufficient for this complexity and fail to generalize across diverse environments and tasks, often leading to performance degradation in novel scenarios and necessitating expensive data collection or fine-tuning.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes ExoActor, a framework motivated by the observation that large-scale video generation models exhibit strong generalization capabilities. The core motivation is to harness this generalization by employing third-person video generation as a unified interface for implicitly encoding and modeling complex interaction dynamics. The key insight is that if a video generation model can synthesize a plausible execution process for a given task instruction and scene context, this synthesized video inherently captures the coordinated interactions between the robot, its environment, and relevant objects.

The specific objectives and anticipated advantages of the ExoActor framework are:
1. **Eliminate Task-Specific Data Collection:** By leveraging the implicit knowledge embedded in large pretrained video models, the framework aims to reduce or remove the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection for each new task.
2. **Provide a Flexible and Expressive Interface:** Third-person video is considered a natural and intuitive interface for capturing long-horizon behaviors and rich interaction contexts, which can be difficult to represent with symbolic planning or low-level control commands alone.
3. **Enhance System Extensibility and Adaptability:** The modular pipeline, consisting of distinct video generation, motion estimation, and motion execution components, is designed to allow independent improvements to each stage, thereby promoting overall system evolution and robustness.
4. **Advance General-Purpose Humanoid Intelligence:** The ultimate goal is to offer a scalable approach for modeling interaction-rich humanoid behaviors, thereby contributing to the development of more generalizable humanoid intelligence by leveraging generative models beyond perception or planning, directly for synthesizing novel behaviors.

In summary, the research aims to bridge the gap between high-level task understanding and low-level robot control by utilizing the emergent capabilities of video generation models to "imagine" and then execute complex, interactive robot behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ExoActor is designed as a unified, end-to-end framework that translates high-level task instructions into executable humanoid behaviors by employing third-person video generation as an intermediate representation. The methodology is structured into three primary stages: Video Generation, Motion Estimation, and Motion Execution.

#### 3.1. Overall Design

The pipeline begins with a task instruction and an initial third-person observation.
1. **Video Generation:** The system synthesizes task-consistent videos depicting the execution process. This involves transforming the robot observation into a human-like representation (embodiment transfer) and decomposing the task instruction into step-wise action prompts.
2. **Motion Estimation:** The generated videos are processed to recover structured motion representations, specifically 3D human kinematics, without explicit robot-specific retargeting at this stage.
3. **Motion Execution:** A general motion executor consumes these estimated motions to produce dynamically feasible and stable robot behaviors.

#### 3.2. Third-Person Video-Action Generation

This stage aims to generate fixed-camera, third-person action videos that are task-consistent and compatible with the Unitree G1 robot's embodiment.
- **Robot-to-Human Embodiment Transfer:** To address the mismatch between humanoid robots and the human-centric priors of large video generation and motion estimation models, the system converts a robot's third-person view observation into a human-like representation. This transformation strictly preserves the original scene layout, camera viewpoint, body pose, orientation, scale, and robot-body proportions. The implementation utilizes Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3.1 Pro) via a prompt-based image editing interface, guided by a prompt (detailed in Appendix A.2) that enforces strict preservation constraints. This step is intended to enhance temporal consistency, reduce visual artifacts, improve physical plausibility in generated videos, and increase motion estimation accuracy.
- **Task-to-Action Decomposition and Prompt Construction:** High-level, abstract task instructions are decomposed into explicit sequences of intermediate, atomic, and visually observable actions. For example, "Pick up the brown box in front of you and stand up" is decomposed into "approach the box," "bend down," "grasp the box," "lift the box," and "stand upright." This decomposition, performed using GPT-5.4 Thinking (detailed in Appendix A.1), aims to provide structured guidance for video generation. An observation-aware generation prompt is then constructed by grounding this action chain within the initial visual observation, fusing temporal structure with scene layout and spatial constraints (Appendix A.3).
- **Task- and Environment-Generalizable Video Generation:** Using the transferred human-like reference frame, task-consistent third-person videos are generated. To mitigate issues like hallucinated content or inconsistent behaviors, structured prompt templates are designed. These templates explicitly encode scene constraints, motion requirements, and task execution details across fields such as "Shot," "Scene," "Motion," "Execution," and "End State." These constraints enforce fixed camera viewpoints, preserve scene geometry, and encourage natural, physically plausible, and robot-aligned motion patterns. The system primarily employs Kling as the video generation model due to its observed superior stability and consistency (Appendix A.4, A.5).

#### 3.3. Interaction-Aware Motion Estimation

This module converts the pixel-level video synthesis into structured robot control by recovering 3D kinematic trajectories. The estimated motions inherently capture interaction dynamics (e.g., object contacts, spatial constraints).
- **Whole-body Motion Estimation:** The GENMO model (Li et al., 2025), a diffusion-based approach, is used to infer 3D human motion trajectories from the generated third-person videos. GENMO formulates motion estimation as a constrained generation process, leveraging video features and 2D keypoints to produce temporally consistent and physically plausible 3D motion sequences. The output is represented using SMPL parameters (joint rotations, global positions).
- **Hand Motion Estimation:** To capture dexterous manipulation details, WiLoR (Potamias et al., 2025) is applied frame by frame to estimate bilateral hand poses and discrete interaction states (open, half-open, closed). Hand poses and interaction states are synchronized with the whole-body motion and mapped frame-wise to robot end-effector commands. The combined output is an interaction-aware motion representation (M̃) encoding global body motion, bilateral hand poses, and discrete manipulation states.

#### 3.4. General Motion Tracking Deployment

The final stage transforms the estimated motions into physically consistent and dynamically feasible control policies for the robot.
- **Motion Tracking Controller:** SONIC (Luo et al., 2025) is employed as the motion tracking controller. It takes the current robot state and a temporal window of reference motions (derived from the video-to-robot pipeline) as input. SONIC's architecture is intended to "physics-filter" the "imagined" demonstrations, ensuring dynamic stability and stylistic fidelity on the Unitree G1 robot without requiring task-specific reward engineering.
- **No Retargeting:** The system directly feeds the estimated motion into the controller without an intermediate motion retargeting stage from human to robot. This decision prioritizes geometric fidelity and positional accuracy over smoothness, as retargeting was found to introduce spatial deviations due to noise and embodiment mismatch.
- **Hand Motion Deployment:** Estimated hand states are mapped to Unitree’s Dex3-1 compatible 7-DoF joint targets and streamed to the robot's controllers along with the SMPL body trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates ExoActor through real-world experiments, demonstrating its capacity for generating and executing interaction-rich humanoid behaviors across various tasks.

#### 4.1. Difficulty-Level Case Studies

The framework's generalization capability was assessed using a set of zero-shot tasks categorized into three difficulty levels:
- **Level B (Easy):** Tasks involving basic locomotion and goal-directed navigation with minimal interaction (e.g., approaching objects, navigating around obstacles). The robot consistently demonstrated reliable execution, confirming the system's ability to translate generated motion into stable locomotion and spatially consistent goal-reaching behaviors (Figure 6).
- **Level A (Moderate):** Tasks requiring coordinated navigation and coarse interactions with objects or the environment (e.g., sweeping items, sitting on a chair, lifting a box). The robot successfully performed multi-step behaviors combining locomotion with whole-body interactions, without demanding precise dexterous manipulation (Figure 7).
- **Level S (Challenging):** Tasks necessitating fine-grained, multi-step manipulation and precise coordination between locomotion and manipulation (e.g., picking up objects and placing them into containers). The robot achieved these tasks, though minor physical adjustments, such as placing small supporting bases under target items, were occasionally required to compensate for residual inaccuracies in motion estimation, particularly concerning hand height (Figure 8).

#### 4.2. Failure Case Studies

The evaluation also identified several limitations and failure modes within the pipeline:
- **Video Generation Failures:** The video generation stage occasionally produced hallucinations (e.g., extra objects, incorrect scale), inconsistent action sequences, unrealistic environments, or physically implausible ending postures (Figure 9). These issues could propagate downstream, but careful prompt engineering and action decomposition were found to mitigate their frequency.
- **Motion Estimation Failures:** Inaccuracies were observed in fine-grained details, especially hand orientation, and with partially occluded body parts, leading to incomplete or inaccurate motion trajectories (Figure 10). Rear camera views also reduced reliability. Wrist motion estimation, crucial for grasping, frequently failed to infer correct orientations (e.g., vertical grasp becoming horizontal), suggesting visual ambiguity in monocular views for such rotations.
- **Whole-body Execution Failures:** Mismatches between estimated motion and the robot's physical constraints (e.g., inaccurate hand height, deviations in movement distance) impacted task completion, particularly for tasks requiring precise spatial alignment or contact.

#### 4.3. Ablation Case Studies

- **Video Generation Models:** Kling 3 consistently outperformed Veo 3.1 and Wan 2.6, generating more coherent, physically plausible motion with fewer visual artifacts and better adherence to prompts. Consequently, Kling 3 was adopted as the primary model.
- **Retargeting Ablation:** Directly feeding the estimated human motion into the controller was preferred over using intermediate retargeting methods (e.g., GMR, OmniRetarget). While retargeting offered smoother motion, it introduced spatial deviations and reduced positional accuracy due to noisy global positions and embodiment mismatches. Prioritizing precision, the system omitted explicit retargeting, with the SONIC backend designed to handle reconstruction noise.
- **Third-person Perspective Ablation:** Different camera viewpoints proved more effective for specific tasks: back-to-front exocentric views for navigation and front-facing views for manipulation, suggesting the importance of task-aware viewpoint selection.
- **Motion Estimation Ablation:** GENMO was selected over CRISP for whole-body motion estimation due to its comparable quality, significantly higher efficiency, and greater stability in practice.

#### 4.4. Latency Analysis

The ExoActor framework operates as an offline pipeline. The overall latency is dominated by the cumulative time of the upstream processing steps (Table 1):
- Robot-to-Human Embodiment Transfer: ~10.7 seconds per request.
- Task-to-Action Decomposition and Prompt Construction: ~2.5 seconds per request.
- Task- and Environment-Generalizable Video Generation: ~13.2 seconds per second of video.
- Whole-body Motion Estimation: ~2.9 seconds per second of video.
- Hand Motion Estimation: ~16.4 seconds per second of video.
Video generation and hand motion estimation were identified as the primary bottlenecks, highlighting areas for future optimization towards real-time applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ExoActor presents a notable shift in the paradigm for humanoid robot control by leveraging large-scale exocentric video generation models as a core interface. This approach offers several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of robotics:

- **Novel Framework for Generalizable Humanoid Control:** The paper establishes a viable end-to-end framework that demonstrates the feasibility of generating complex, interaction-rich humanoid behaviors from high-level instructions, directly translating visual "imagination" into physical execution. This is a step towards more general-purpose humanoid intelligence, potentially unlocking a new avenue for generative models beyond their traditional roles in perception or planning.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** A key challenge in robotics is the high cost and labor associated with collecting task-specific real-world data. ExoActor's reliance on the implicit knowledge embedded in pretrained large-scale video models offers a method to significantly reduce or even eliminate this dependency for new tasks, accelerating the deployment of humanoid robots in diverse scenarios.
- **Scalability and Extensibility:** The modular design of ExoActor—separating video generation, motion estimation, and motion execution—allows for independent advancements in each component. As video generation models, motion estimation techniques, and robot control systems continue to improve, the overall capabilities of the ExoActor framework are expected to scale proportionally, fostering a continuous path for enhancement.
- **Unified Interface for Interaction Dynamics:** Using third-person video as a unified interface provides a flexible and expressive representation that naturally captures long-horizon behaviors and rich interaction contexts, which are difficult to define or parameterize through conventional programming or reward functions. This abstraction layer simplifies the problem of instructing complex robot behaviors.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work not only presents a functional system but also explicitly outlines critical limitations and promising directions for future research. These include the need for more physically realistic video generation, closed-loop and scene-aware control, real-time task imagination, and more accurate video-to-motion translation. This roadmap is valuable for guiding subsequent efforts in the research community.
- **Bridging AI Generative Models and Embodied AI:** ExoActor exemplifies a deeper integration between advanced generative AI models and embodied robotics. It suggests that generative models can serve as a form of "motor cortex" for robots, synthesizing the underlying motor plans for interaction, thereby pushing the boundaries of what is possible with current AI capabilities in physical domains.

In conclusion, ExoActor contributes a conceptually distinct and empirically demonstrated method for enhancing humanoid robot capabilities. By leveraging the generalization power of video generation, it paves the way for robots that can understand, imagine, and execute a wider range of complex tasks with reduced reliance on specialized data, thereby accelerating progress towards more autonomous and versatile humanoid systems.
