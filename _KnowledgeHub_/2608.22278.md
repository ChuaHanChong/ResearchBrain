# DreamMimic: Learning Visuomotor Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation via World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamMimic distills privileged specialist teachers into a vision-based humanoid loco-manipulation policy using a Dreamer-style RSSM repurposed for representation and supervision rather than planning. It adds auxiliary heads predicting reward, privileged state, contact, and object state, an action-conditioned multi-step latent consistency loss, and Performance-Conditioned Guidance that adapts teacher rollout share. It reaches 92.2% success on OMOMO.

## Problem

- Prior humanoid whole-body methods emphasize locomotion without complex object interaction, or depend on privileged object pose, contact labels, and interaction graphs unavailable at deployment.
- Existing visuomotor frameworks lack temporally structured predictive representations, so small perceptual errors compound over long-horizon contact-rich rollouts under partial observability.
- World-model control results come from low-dimensional action spaces or light contact settings; Dreamer-style agents struggle on high-dimensional visual humanoid tasks.

## Method

- **DreamMimic** uses a Dreamer-style ==RSSM== to encode depth, segmentation, and proprioception into deterministic recurrent plus stochastic latents, repurposed as policy features and supervision rather than for imagined planning.
- ==Auxiliary prediction heads== for reward, privileged teacher vector, target contact, and 13-D object state, whose outputs are fed to the policy alongside proprioception and a goal condition.
- ==Multi-step latent distillation== branches frozen-prior rollouts (H=3) under student and teacher mean actions, plus ==Performance-Conditioned Guidance== decaying teacher-environment ratio by EMA reward ratio.

## Results

- On OMOMO, DreamMimic reaches **92.2%** success, **5.4 cm** robot error, **8.8 cm** object error, versus **72.6-76.5%** for ResNet-18, ViT, and CNN students.
- Removing multi-step latent distillation drops OMOMO success from **92.2%** to **70.6%**; replacing the deterministic recurrent state with stochastic-only features drops it to **74.5%**.
- On BEHAVE, PCG matches naive annealing's **72.7%** success but cuts robot error from **15.2** to **10.2 cm** and extends tracked duration to **289.53 steps**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A world model can pay off as a representation and supervision backbone for distillation even when its imagined rollouts are never used for planning or control.
> - Aligning future latent states under teacher versus student actions supplies credit that single-timestep action matching misses in contact-rich long-horizon control.
> - Auxiliary heads help most when their predictions also condition the policy; training them as losses only lost 8 success points, and evaluation stayed simulation-only.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics aims to enable robots to perform complex tasks in diverse environments, often requiring whole-body loco-manipulation under visual feedback. However, achieving robust visuomotor policies for such tasks presents several challenges. These include the inherent partial observability of visual observations, the discontinuous and complex nature of contact-rich dynamics, the high dimensionality of humanoid action spaces, and the difficulty of learning long-horizon behaviors from high-dimensional visual inputs.

Prior research has contributed to humanoid control and motion transfer. Many approaches have focused on locomotion without extensive object interaction or have relied on privileged information, such as ground-truth object poses, interaction geometries, or contact states, or pre-computed state estimators. While these privileged signals are crucial for disambiguating contact timing and agent-object coupling during training, they are typically unavailable to a deployable visuomotor controller. Existing visuomotor frameworks have also been noted for their limitations in capturing temporally structured, predictive representations, which can lead to compounding errors in long-horizon, contact-rich scenarios.

More recently, world models, which learn predictive latent dynamics, have demonstrated potential in visual control across domains such as legged locomotion, autonomous driving, and arm control. These models improve sample efficiency by learning compact, predictive representations and optimizing policies through imagined rollouts. However, most evaluations of world models have been conducted in settings with lower-dimensional action spaces or less complex contact interactions. Furthermore, Dreamer-style agents have reportedly struggled with high-dimensional visual humanoid tasks, and prior studies primarily employ world models for planning or direct policy optimization, with less emphasis on their role in stabilizing visual policy distillation.

Teacher-student paradigms have also emerged as a strategy for distilling complex skills into vision-based policies for manipulation and quadruped loco-manipulation. Approaches such as student-aware teacher optimization and adaptive combinations of imitation and reinforcement learning have also been explored to address the mismatch between teacher and student observability. This work builds upon existing teacher-student frameworks, specifically InterMimic, which facilitates imitation learning from large, imperfect datasets.

DreamMimic addresses a gap by focusing on end-to-end visual distillation for high-degree-of-freedom humanoid loco-manipulation. In this context, the authors contend that rich contact dynamics and the need for precise temporal coordination make predictive representations and multi-step supervision particularly critical. The paper aims to leverage a world model not for planning, but as a mechanism to learn predictive state representations and provide multi-step action-conditioned supervision, thereby stabilizing the distillation process for complex humanoid behaviors under partial observability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named DreamMimic, that stabilizes vision-based policy distillation for whole-body loco-manipulation tasks performed by humanoid robots. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims to address key limitations in current approaches.

Firstly, humanoid robots are envisioned to play transformative roles in various sectors, from factory automation to daily-life assistance, necessitating capabilities for whole-body loco-manipulation under visual feedback. The inherent challenges associated with learning such skills—including discontinuous contact dynamics, high-dimensional action spaces, and partial observability from visual inputs—motivate the need for more robust learning frameworks.

A core motivation is to overcome the reliance on privileged information (e.g., ground-truth object pose, interaction geometry, contact states) during robot deployment. While such information is invaluable for training complex contact behaviors in simulation, it is typically unavailable in real-world scenarios. DreamMimic aims to distill policies trained with privileged teachers into vision-based student controllers that operate solely on onboard sensing and infer necessary interaction-relevant variables from their observations.

The work is motivated by the observation that existing visuomotor control frameworks often lack mechanisms to capture temporally structured, predictive representations. This deficiency can lead to compounding errors over long task horizons, particularly in contact-rich tasks where small perceptual uncertainties or control inaccuracies can accumulate and destabilize performance. The authors specifically repurpose a Dreamer-style Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) to learn predictive latent dynamics, not for planning, but to serve as a compact representation space and provide action-conditioned multi-step supervision, thereby mitigating long-term drift.

Furthermore, to enhance the utility of the world model for contact-rich loco-manipulation, the research is motivated to augment it with interaction-aware auxiliary objectives. These objectives guide the latent representation to retain signals useful for task progress and agent-object interaction, such as reward, contact, and object state estimations, which are typically part of privileged information. By predicting these cues from student-side observations, the framework aims to provide essential context to the policy without exposing ground-truth states online.

Finally, the authors are motivated to introduce an adaptive distillation schedule to improve the stability of policy optimization. Standard distillation approaches often struggle with prematurely annealing teacher guidance or, conversely, excessive teacher interference. Performance-Conditioned Guidance (PCG) is introduced to dynamically balance teacher supervision and student exploration based on their relative task performance, thereby preventing premature decay of guidance in challenging visual settings and avoiding over-anchoring once the student becomes more competent.

In summary, the key objectives are to enable robust vision-based whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoids by developing a world-model-assisted distillation framework that addresses partial observability and compounding errors through predictive latent representations, interaction-aware auxiliary supervision, and an adaptive guidance mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamMimic is a framework designed to enable vision-based whole-body loco-manipulation on humanoid robots by distilling privileged teacher policies into student controllers through a world-model-assisted mechanism. The approach addresses the challenges of partial observability, contact-rich dynamics, and learning long-horizon behaviors from high-dimensional visual inputs.

The problem is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the student robot receives visual (depth and segmentation images) and proprioceptive observations, along with a compact goal condition. Unlike the teacher, the student does not have access to ground-truth object pose, interaction geometry, or contact states during deployment; instead, it must infer these interaction-relevant variables from its observation history.

**Privileged Teacher Policy**:
The training process begins with a privileged teacher policy. This teacher has access to the full simulator state, including ground-truth object pose, an interaction graph, and object contact signals. It is trained via reinforcement learning to track reference motions while maintaining physically plausible dynamics. The reward function for the teacher is composed of terms that encourage whole-body tracking, end-effector alignment, object consistency, and contact awareness. For large-scale human-object interaction (HOI) datasets, multiple specialist teachers are constructed and consolidated into a unified privileged teacher, following the InterMimic framework.

**Visuomotor Student Distillation**:
The student policy is a goal-conditioned visuomotor policy that maps onboard sensing and task commands to actions. The student's policy input, denoted as $c_t$, consists of:
1. **Non-privileged proprioception** ($p_t$): Standard robot joint positions, velocities, etc., devoid of simulator-only object or contact information.
2. **Compact goal condition** ($g_t$): Combines target object pose and short-horizon robot trajectory cues.
3. **World-model features** ($\phi_{wm_t}$): Inferred from visual-proprioceptive histories by the world model. These features include the deterministic RSSM state ($h_t$) and auxiliary predictions for reward ($\hat{r}_t$), privileged state ($\hat{x}_{priv_t}$), contact ($\hat{c}_{contact_t}$), and object state ($\hat{x}_{obj_t}$). These are *predictions* made by the world model, not ground-truth inputs.
The policy network uses a lightweight Transformer to fuse projected tokens from these three input modalities before feeding them to an actor-critic MLP. Action distillation is performed using supervised imitation as the primary learning signal, complemented by PPO regularization as an auxiliary stabilizer on student-driven rollouts.

**Performance-Conditioned Guidance (PCG)**:
PCG is an adaptive distillation schedule designed to balance teacher supervision and student exploration. During training, environments are allocated to either teacher-driven or student-driven rollouts. A ratio $\rho$ (fraction of teacher-driven environments) is maintained. Exponential moving averages of shaped rewards for both teacher ($\hat{r}_T$) and student ($\hat{r}_S$) rollouts are tracked. A relative performance score $\pi = \hat{r}_S / (\hat{r}_T + \epsilon)$ is computed. As the student's performance relative to the teacher approaches a target ratio, $\rho$ is gradually decayed from a maximum to a minimum value. This dynamic adjustment prevents premature reduction of teacher guidance when the student is still learning and avoids excessive teacher interference once the student becomes more competent under its own state distribution, while keeping the imitation coefficient fixed.

**Reference-Buffer Curriculum**:
An InterMimic-style physical reference-buffer curriculum is employed. Failed student-driven rollouts update a small set of replayed reference states, particularly around difficult time windows. Future rollouts then revisit these challenging states, ensuring that the curriculum is tailored to the student's specific learning difficulties and state distribution.

**World Model for Predictive Representation**:
The core of DreamMimic is a Dreamer-style Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), repurposed for predictive representation learning rather than planning. The world model's architecture consists of:
- **Encoder ($E_\xi$)**: Maps current observation (visual and proprioceptive) and previous action to an embedding. Visual inputs are processed by a convolutional encoder, and proprioceptive inputs by an MLP.
- **RSSM Latent Dynamics**: Maintains a latent state $z_t = (h_t, s_t)$, comprising a deterministic recurrent state ($h_t$, updated by a GRU-based transition function $f_\xi$) and a stochastic state ($s_t$, inferred by a posterior function $q_\xi$ conditioned on $h_t$ and the observation embedding). A prior function $p_\xi$ also defines a distribution over $s_t$ for open-loop prediction.
- **Decoding and Auxiliary Objectives**:
 - **Reconstruction Head (RecH)**: A decoder $D_\xi$ reconstructs both visual (depth and segmentation images via MSE loss) and proprioceptive observations (via symlog-MSE loss) from the latent state. This helps preserve sensory fidelity in the latent space but is not directly fed to the policy.
 - **Auxiliary Prediction Heads**: These heads predict task-relevant information from the latent state and include:
 - Reward predictor: Estimates instantaneous task reward.
 - Privileged-state predictor: Estimates the teacher-side interaction target (e.g., concatenated object state, interaction graph, and target contact label).
 - Contact predictor: Estimates the target contact label.
 - Object-state predictor: Estimates the 13-D target object state.
 These auxiliary signals are derived from simulator annotations during training. At test time, only their world-model predictions are provided to the student policy. The latent state $z_t$ thus provides a temporally structured summary of visuoproprioceptive history and supports inference of otherwise unobservable interaction variables.

**Multi-step Latent Distillation**:
To address compounding errors over time, an action-conditioned latent consistency loss is introduced. Starting from a posterior latent state $z_t$ inferred from the student's current observation, two imagined rollouts are branched through the RSSM prior (with frozen world model parameters). One branch is conditioned on the student policy's mean action ($\mu^{(S)}_t$), and the other on the teacher policy's mean action ($\mu^{(T)}_t$) for $H$ steps ($H=3$ in experiments). A loss $L_{latent}$ minimizes the squared difference between the deterministic and stochastic latent states induced by student and teacher actions across these $H$ imagined steps. This ensures alignment not just in instantaneous actions but also in their predicted future consequences.

**Training Objective**:
The world model is trained periodically on student experience using an objective $L_{wm}$ that combines KL-regularized representation learning ($L_{KL}$ with free-bits mechanism), reconstruction loss ($L_{rec}$), and the auxiliary prediction losses ($L_{rew}$, $L_{priv}$, $L_{con}$, $L_{obj}$).
The student policy's total loss ($L_{total}$) combines mean-action matching ($L_{action}$), multi-step latent supervision ($L_{latent}$), and PPO regularization ($L_{ppo}$). The optimization is imitation-dominant, with PPO acting as a lower-weight regularizer after a warm-up phase. The world model's parameters are frozen during policy updates for the multi-step latent distillation loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates DreamMimic on two large-scale Human-Object Interaction (HOI) datasets, OMOMO and BEHAVE, focusing on long-horizon, contact-rich loco-manipulation sequences. The robot morphology used is SMPL-X, with additional qualitative tests on the Unitree G1 humanoid. Metrics include Success Rate (Succ.), average Duration (Time), Robot Tracking Error ($E_r$), and Object Tracking Error ($E_o$).

**Quantitative Evaluation**:
- **Overall Performance**: DreamMimic consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to various vision-based baselines.
 - On the OMOMO dataset, DreamMimic achieved a 92.2% success rate with an average tracking duration of 184.18 steps, and robot/object tracking errors of 5.4 cm and 8.8 cm, respectively. This performance was notably superior to direct visual-encoder students (e.g., ResNet-18 + DAgger+RL: 72.6% success, 169.49 steps) and single-stage Dreamer (0.0% success, 28.73 steps), which struggled significantly with these contact-rich humanoid tasks.
 - Similar trends were observed on the BEHAVE dataset, where DreamMimic with Performance-Conditioned Guidance (PCG) achieved 72.7% success and 289.53 average steps, with lower tracking errors compared to other baselines.
 - Under stress tests with increased object mass (e.g., OMOMO w/ x5 mass), DreamMimic maintained a higher success rate and better object tracking error compared to baselines, although the performance gap was less pronounced.
- **Teacher-guided distillation** frameworks, including DreamMimic, generally performed substantially better than RL-only visual baselines, highlighting the importance of supervision from privileged teachers for complex skills.

**Ablation Study**:
- **Temporal Latent Supervision**: Removing the multi-step latent distillation component significantly reduced performance. On OMOMO, success dropped from 92.2% to 70.6%, and both robot and object tracking errors increased. This indicates that aligning future latent dynamics, in addition to instantaneous actions, is crucial for maintaining temporally coherent interaction states under partial observability.
- **Auxiliary Interaction Prediction**:
 - A world model trained with only reconstruction (RecH-only WM) achieved 86.3% success but had larger object errors (12.7 cm) compared to the full DreamMimic model (8.8 cm).
 - Adding object-state prediction reduced object errors, and the full model with all auxiliary heads (reward, privileged state, contact, object state) further improved object tracking error to 8.8 cm on OMOMO and 14.8 cm under increased object weight. This suggests that these auxiliary predictions help shape latent representations to be more useful for interaction-rich behaviors.
- **Predictive Features for Policy Conditioning**: When the outputs of the auxiliary prediction heads were used only as losses for world model training but not fed as inputs to the student policy, the success rate decreased (e.g., to 84.3% on OMOMO). This demonstrates that providing these predicted interaction cues explicitly to the policy is beneficial for acting under partial observability, complementing the compact goal condition.
- **Recurrent Dynamics Conditioning**:
 - Replacing the deterministic recurrent state in the RSSM with only a current-step stochastic feature led to a substantial drop in success (from 92.2% to 74.5%), underscoring the importance of a history-aware latent summary.
 - Removing action conditioning from the RSSM caused a moderate performance drop (92.2% to 90.2%), while removing the recurrent deterministic state reduced success to 88.2%. These ablations highlight that both action-conditioned prediction and recurrent memory contribute, with the deterministic recurrent state being central for maintaining stable interaction dynamics.
- **Distillation Schedule (PCG vs. Naive Annealing)**: On BEHAVE, PCG achieved comparable success rates to a carefully tuned naive annealing schedule but demonstrated superior tracking errors and sustained interaction duration. PCG's adaptive nature, based on reward-driven relative performance, reduces the sensitivity to manual decay schedules.
- **Visual Input Modality**: Depth combined with segmentation proved to be the most effective visual input, achieving the highest success rate (92.2%) on OMOMO, outperforming segmentation-only, depth-only, and RGB inputs.

**Qualitative Evaluation**:
- **Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation Behaviors**: DreamMimic-controlled humanoids successfully performed complex tasks such as lifting and transporting a large table, and dragging a container, maintaining balance and coordinated motion. In contrast, baseline policies often failed to establish stable contact or maintain balance, leading to task failure (Figure 4).
- **Cross-Embodiment Simulation**: On the Unitree G1 humanoid, DreamMimic exhibited stable pushing behaviors with consistent balance, while the Dreamer baseline became unstable during contact and fell (Figure 5). This provides qualitative evidence for potential transferability across different robot morphologies in simulation.
- **Cross-Simulator Test**: Policies trained in Isaac Gym and evaluated in Isaac Lab for selected OMOMO sequences successfully completed the tasks (Figure 6). This suggests that the learned latent dynamics can capture interaction-relevant structure that generalizes to some extent across simulators.

**Failure Case Analysis**:
Despite improvements, DreamMimic still encountered failures, particularly under severe occlusion or visually ambiguous contact scenarios, indicating limitations in perception. In G1 experiments, manipulation precision was sometimes insufficient for forceful interaction, leading to pushing rather than lifting, possibly due to limitations in motion retargeting for fine-grained hand-object dynamics. The evaluation was confined to simulation using ground-truth depth and segmentation, with no real-robot experiments conducted.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DreamMimic, a world-model-assisted framework that demonstrates a method for stabilizing vision-based policy distillation in complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks. The findings contribute to the field of robotics by addressing several long-standing challenges associated with learning high-degree-of-freedom, contact-rich behaviors directly from visual observations.

The primary significance of this work lies in its novel repurposing of a Dreamer-style RSSM. Instead of using the world model primarily for planning, DreamMimic leverages it as a backbone for learning predictive state representations and enforcing multi-step action-conditioned latent consistency. This approach is shown to be effective in mitigating compounding errors that typically arise in long-horizon tasks under partial observability, a critical factor for robotic systems operating with visual feedback. The ability to learn temporally coherent behaviors is particularly impactful for contact-rich interactions where small errors can quickly escalate.

Furthermore, the integration of interaction-aware auxiliary objectives within the world model to predict reward, contact, privileged state, and object state is a significant contribution. These predictions provide the student policy with compact, inferred interaction cues, effectively bridging the gap between privileged information available to the teacher and the partial observability of the student. This design allows the student to benefit from task-relevant signals without requiring ground-truth states online, making the deployed policy more self-sufficient and robust.

The introduction of Performance-Conditioned Guidance (PCG) as an adaptive distillation schedule also holds notable significance. By dynamically balancing teacher supervision and student exploration based on real-time relative performance, PCG offers a more robust and less hyperparameter-sensitive alternative to fixed annealing schedules. This adaptive mechanism can prevent premature decay of guidance for under-trained visual policies and avoid over-interference when the student becomes more competent, thereby stabilizing the overall learning process.

The potential impact of DreamMimic is multifaceted:
- **Advancement in Humanoid Robotics**: The framework represents a step towards developing more capable and robust humanoid robots that can perform complex physical interactions in unstructured environments using only visual sensing. This could accelerate the deployment of humanoids in various applications, from logistics and manufacturing to domestic assistance.
- **Reduced Reliance on Privileged Information**: By demonstrating that complex, contact-rich behaviors can be distilled into vision-based policies without requiring online privileged state information, DreamMimic contributes to overcoming a major barrier to real-world robot deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The world-model-assisted distillation paradigm provides a robust foundation for future research. This could include integrating more sophisticated real-world perception systems (e.g., handling noisy RGB-D data), incorporating tactile sensing for fine-grained contact control, developing advanced recovery behaviors for unexpected occlusions or task failures, and exploring more dynamic motion retargeting methods for improved hand-object interaction.
- **Generalization and Adaptability**: The predictive features learned by the world model could inherently lead to better generalization capabilities, allowing robots to adapt more effectively to novel objects, slight variations in environmental conditions, or different robot morphologies, as hinted by the qualitative cross-embodiment and cross-simulator tests.

In conclusion, DreamMimic offers a principled approach to overcoming key challenges in visuomotor learning for humanoid loco-manipulation, presenting a pathway towards more autonomous and versatile robotic systems.
