# RHINO: Learning Real-Time Humanoid-Human-Object Interaction from Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed RHINO, a hierarchical learning framework for real-time, interruptible humanoid-human-object interaction learned from human demonstrations. It achieves high mean Average Precision in intention prediction and robust manipulation, demonstrating enhanced adaptability and scalability compared to end-to-end models on a Unitree H1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with real-time, interruptible interactions, often completing tasks in isolation without dynamic human feedback or task switching.
- Current HRI systems typically rely on specialized modules for individual aspects like object handover or motion generation, lacking a unified framework for complex human-humanoid-object interactions.
- High latency in large language and vision-language models makes them impractical for responsive, real-time human-robot communication and command execution.

## Method

- **RHINO** introduces a hierarchical learning framework, decoupling interaction into a high-level reactive planner for intention inference and a low-level controller for executing interruptible skills.
- The system learns from a combination of human-object-human interaction data (for reactive motion) and VR-based teleoperation data (for precise object manipulation and in-skill interruption handling).
- A critical safety supervisor continuously monitors collision zones between the robot and human, immediately halting robot motion if an unsafe distance is detected.

## Results

- The reactive planner achieves high real-time human intention prediction with a mean Average Precision (mAP) of **0.787** for dining and **0.643** for office scenarios on a Unitree H1 robot.
- Motion generation using a multi-body motion diffusion model produces higher quality and more diverse movements compared to baselines, closely matching human motion diversity.
- The manipulation module shows strong success rates, boosted by in-skill interruption training data (e.g., **85%** for "Pick Can"), with the hierarchical framework maintaining **0.97** success across 5 skills and superior OOD generalization over end-to-end approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Including granular human hand details (3D position, nearest object, IOU) significantly improves the accuracy of real-time human intention prediction.
> - Training with in-skill interruption data is essential for enabling robots to safely and flexibly respond to unexpected human interference during manipulation tasks.
> - A hierarchical framework provides greater scalability and robustness in complex HRI tasks compared to end-to-end models, especially for varied skill sets and out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is rapidly advancing, with significant progress in fundamental capabilities such as bipedal locomotion, dexterous object manipulation, and expressive motion generation. However, the authors identify critical gaps in enabling humanoids to act as truly valuable assistants in human daily life. Existing research often falls short in three key areas:
1. **Real-time Interaction:** Most prior works focus on multi-stage interactions, treating each task in isolation without accounting for real-time human feedback or dynamic task switching. Once a task begins, robots typically cannot be interrupted, requiring completion before new commands are processed.
2. **Unified Frameworks:** Studies often specialize in a single aspect, such as object handover, interactive motion generation, or intention recognition, rather than integrating these diverse capabilities into a cohesive system.
3. **Latency of Foundation Models:** While large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have shown promise in understanding complex human instructions, their inherent latency often makes them unsuitable for real-time, responsive human-robot interaction (HRI). End-to-end models, while attempting integration, struggle with sample efficiency and scalability to a wide range of tasks.

RHINO directly addresses these limitations by proposing a unified, hierarchical framework designed for real-time, interruptible humanoid-human-object interaction. It aims to bridge the gap between basic robotic abilities and the nuanced, dynamic demands of human-centric environments. By focusing on low-latency intention recognition, responsive multi-skill execution, and integrated safety measures, RHINO positions itself as a significant step towards more natural and effective human-robot collaboration, moving beyond sequential task execution to truly interactive assistance. The emphasis on learning from human demonstrations further aligns with a growing trend in robotics to leverage rich human data for more natural and generalizable robot behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RHINO is to empower humanoid robots with the ability to **understand human instructions quickly and react immediately based on real-time human interaction signals, allowing for interruption and dynamic task switching.** This overarching goal is motivated by the desire to make humanoids valuable assistants in human daily life, requiring them to possess three fundamental capabilities:
- **Skill Proficiency:** Equipping the robot with diverse and essential skills for various tasks.
- **Intention Recognition:** Enabling the robot to discern human intentions from multiple modalities (motion, language, object interactions).
- **Instant Feedback:** Ensuring the robot can respond in real-time with feasible and appropriate actions, including the ability to be interrupted and switch tasks seamlessly.

The authors highlight the shortcomings of existing approaches that treat interactions as separate, alternating stages, lacking real-time feedback and the ability to handle interruptions. RHINO is designed to provide a "unified view of reactive motion, instruction-based manipulation, and safety concerns" over various human signal modalities. This unified approach, coupled with its hierarchical learning framework, aims to overcome the latency issues and scalability challenges prevalent in current HRI systems, ultimately making humanoid robots more intuitive, flexible, and safer partners in dynamic human environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RHINO (Real-time Humanoid-human Interaction and Object manipulation) is a **hierarchical learning framework** that decouples the complex interaction process into two main levels:

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Architecture

- **High-Level Reactive Planner:** This module infers human intentions from real-time human behaviors (languages, images, motions) and decides the next appropriate skill. It operates at a high frequency (30Hz) and is designed for low-latency intention prediction.
- **Low-Level Controller:** This component executes reactive motion behaviors and object manipulation skills based on the intentions predicted by the high-level planner. It is designed to be interruptible, allowing it to react to new high-level commands at any time.

#### 3.2. Data Collection

To ensure scalability and cover a wide range of skills, RHINO relies on two types of demonstration data:
- **Human-Object-Human (HOH) Interaction Data:** Collected without a real robot, this dataset captures interactions between two people performing daily tasks with objects. It includes motion capture data and first-person RGB-D camera views of the follower, labeled with human intentions and follower skills. This data is primarily used for learning reactive motion skills.
- **Teleoperation Data:** For skills requiring precise object manipulation, demonstrations are collected using a VR-based teleoperation system where a human controls the robot's arms and neck. This data records control commands, robot proprioception, and stereo videos, and is used to train manipulation skills, including success signals for terminal conditions. In-skill interruption data is also collected to train the robot to handle disturbances.

#### 3.3. Key Modules

- **Reactive Planner:**
 - Implemented as a **Transformer model**.
 - Inputs: Human's body and hand postures (extracted from RGB-D images using models like ZED API and HaMeR), human's hand and head positions, nearest object to hands, and robot's hand occupancy. This avoids direct image input to enhance generalization.
 - Output: Predicted human intention (ID integer).
 - **Skill Transition Logic:** Switches to a corresponding skill if an intention is consistently predicted for *n* frames. Skills have defined start conditions (required hand occupancy) and end transitions. Supports interruption of ongoing skills with "reverse skills" for manipulation tasks. An occupancy graph guides the robot through necessary prerequisite skills to satisfy start conditions.
- **Interactive Motion Skills:**
 - Uses a **multi-body motion diffusion model** (Transformer-based).
 - Generates smooth, consistent, and expressive non-manipulation motions (e.g., waving, shaking hands).
 - Inputs: History of human motion, history of humanoid motion, current hand occupancy, and predicted human intention.
 - Outputs: Future humanoid motion frames (5 frames, predicted at 3Hz to generate 30 frames/second).
- **Manipulation Skills:**
 - Each manipulation skill (e.g., picking up a cup, stamping a paper) is trained as an independent **Action Chunking Transformer (ACT)** model.
 - Inputs: Cropped RGB-D images focusing on the robot-object interaction area (to ensure robustness to human appearance/behavior changes) and robot proprioception.
 - Output: Robot's joint positions.
 - **Terminal Conditions:** Each ACT model predicts an additional "success signal" to determine when a skill is completed, trained with a cross-entropy loss.
 - **Robustness:** Incorporates in-skill interruption data to teach the robot to pause or withdraw when human interference or unreachable objects are detected, enhancing safety and adaptability.
- **Safety Supervisor:**
 - A critical component that ensures safe robot actions.
 - Continuously calculates collision boxes for robot arms and monitors the global coordinates of human hands using the depth camera.
 - If the distance between robot and human body parts falls below a safe threshold (0.1 meters), an "unsafe signal" is sent to immediately halt the robot until safety is restored. This operates at 30Hz.

#### 3.4. Real Robot Platform

RHINO is implemented on a **Unitree H1 humanoid robot** equipped with an active head (2-DoF yaw and pitch) and a ZED mini RGB-D stereo camera. Each arm of the H1 has 5-DoFs, augmented with a 6-DoF end-effector (dexterous hands). Human body posture is detected using ZED API, hand posture using HaMeR, and object detection with a fine-tuned YoloV11 model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RHINO was conducted on a real Unitree H1 humanoid robot across two scenarios: a dining waiter and an office assistant, encompassing over 20 diverse tasks.

#### 4.1. Human Intention Prediction

- **Performance:** The reactive planner achieved high mean Average Precision (mAP) scores of 0.787 for Scene 1 (Dining) and 0.643 for Scene 2 (Office) on a diverse test set with three different human participants. This demonstrates robust real-time intention recognition.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** RHINO significantly outperformed baselines, including variants without "human hand details" (mAP 0.729 / 0.587) and large vision-language models (VLMs). Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct, even after fine-tuning, showed poor performance (mAP 0.228 / 0.159), while GPT-4o-mini achieved reasonable mAP (0.573 / 0.564) but suffered from prohibitively high inference latency (≈0.46 Hz vs. RHINO's 30Hz).
- **Key Insight:** The inclusion of "human hand details" (3D position, nearest object, IOU) was crucial for differentiating intentions, highlighting the importance of granular interaction cues.

#### 4.2. Motion Generation

- **Quality & Diversity:** RHINO's motion generation module, utilizing a diffusion model, significantly outperformed baselines (Zero Velocity, Transformer without diffusion, Transformer without human motion input) in terms of motion quality (lower FID and JPE scores). It also demonstrated higher motion diversity, aligning closely with real human motion diversity, thanks to the stochasticity of the diffusion process.
- **Importance of Components:** The diffusion process was shown to be critical for synthesizing better motions, and detailed human motion guidance was essential for generating expressive, accurate, and reactive movements, surpassing models relying solely on intention labels.

#### 4.3. Object Manipulation

- **Success Rate & Time:** The manipulation module exhibited good performance, with success rates comparable to or even exceeding human teleoperators for simpler tasks (e.g., Pick Can: 1.00 success vs. human 0.97). For more delicate operations (e.g., Brush with Sponge, Stamp the Paper), the robot's success rate was slightly lower, attributed to the robot's limited degrees of freedom and lack of haptic sensing. Average operation times were generally slightly longer than human teleoperation, often due to prediction noise, friction, or constrained periodic motions.
- **In-Skill Interruption Robustness:** Experiments showed that training with in-skill interruption data significantly improved the robot's ability to handle human disturbances. With 20% of the training data containing interruptions, the success rate for properly withdrawing/adapting increased substantially (e.g., Pick Can: 0.85 success rate vs. 0.00 without interruption data), proving its effectiveness for safe and flexible interaction.

#### 4.4. Framework Structure (RHINO vs. End-to-End Models)

- **Scalability & Robustness:** RHINO's hierarchical and decoupled design proved superior to end-to-end (E2E) baselines, especially as the number of skills increased and in out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) scenarios. While E2E models performed well for a single skill in in-distribution (I.D.) scenes (1.00 success rate), their performance drastically dropped with more skills (e.g., 5 skills: 0.84 I.D., 0.67 O.O.D.). RHINO maintained high success rates (5 skills: 0.97), demonstrating better generalization and robustness to unseen human clothing or object arrangements. This highlights the benefits of separating perception, planning, and control for complex HRI tasks.

#### 4.5. Analysis of System Failure

The authors openly discussed limitations and causes of system failure:
- **Sensor Errors:** Reliance on a single RGB-D camera led to potential cumulative 3D position errors and occlusions, impacting intention understanding and safety.
- **Hardware Stability:** Small shifts in robot arm zero position and electronic aging could affect precise control, crucial for manipulation.
- **Model Generalization:** Despite efforts, the models might struggle with extreme O.O.D. scenarios (e.g., highly unusual human clothing, non-standard postures) due to limited training data. However, the interruptible nature of RHINO allows human intervention to mitigate complete system breakdown.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RHINO represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid-human-object interaction (HHOI), primarily by introducing a **real-time, hierarchical, and interruptible learning framework** that addresses critical limitations of prior work. Its key contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Natural Human-Robot Interaction:** RHINO moves beyond rigid, sequential task execution to truly dynamic and responsive interaction. The ability for humans to interrupt ongoing robot tasks and for the robot to immediately adapt is fundamental for natural collaboration, making humanoids much more intuitive and user-friendly. This is a crucial step towards humanoids becoming effective "assistants" rather than just "tools."
- **Unified and Scalable Framework:** By integrating real-time intention recognition, diverse reactive motion skills, and precise object manipulation within a single hierarchical architecture, RHINO offers a unified solution to HHOI. The modular design, coupled with a scalable learning pipeline from human demonstrations, allows for easier extension to new tasks and scenarios without needing to retrain an entire end-to-end system from scratch, addressing a major challenge in robotics.
- **Robustness and Safety in Dynamic Environments:** The system's demonstrated robustness to out-of-distribution data and its integrated safety supervisor (collision detection) are vital for deployment in real-world human environments. The capacity to learn from in-skill interruption data further enhances its ability to safely and flexibly respond to unexpected human behaviors.
- **Foundation for Future Humanoid Applications:** RHINO provides a strong foundation for future humanoid robot applications in daily life assistance (e.g., home care, service robots), industrial automation, and disaster response. The framework's success on a real Unitree H1 robot, coupled with the commitment to open-source code and datasets, facilitates further research and development in these areas.

Despite its promising results, the authors acknowledge limitations, primarily concerning data scalability and the current focus on the upper body within a fixed workspace. Future work will aim to extend RHINO to whole-body control, locomotion, and navigation in dynamic, larger environments, leveraging larger datasets and simulation. Nonetheless, RHINO's demonstration of real-time, interruptible, and robust HHOI marks a substantial leap toward making humanoid robots seamlessly integrated and valuable partners in human-centered tasks.
