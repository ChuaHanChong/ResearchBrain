# No Labels, No Problem: Training Visual Reasoners with Multimodal Verifiers

> [!summary] Summary
> VALOR, developed at Caltech, presents an annotation-free framework that trains visual reasoners by employing multimodal verifiers to jointly tune an LLM for reasoning and specialized vision tools for visual grounding. This approach achieves superior performance on various visual reasoning benchmarks, including a 6.5% average improvement over direct-answer VLMs on OMNI3D-BENCH.

## Problem

- Existing visual reasoning methods, like language-only CoT, often exhibit weak visual understanding and logical errors, requiring extensive and costly labeled supervision data.
- Program-synthesis approaches struggle with faulty program logic and misaligned pre-trained vision specialists, limiting their adaptability to complex spatial reasoning tasks.
- The high cost and labor involved in collecting large-scale, high-quality, and domain-specific labeled data for both reasoning and object annotations bottleneck the development of robust visual reasoning systems.

## Method

- VALOR proposes an ==annotation-free training framework== that jointly optimizes an LLM for complex reasoning (generating plans and executable programs) and vision specialist tools for accurate visual grounding.
- An ==LLM verifier== (Gemini-2.5-Flash) provides structured reward signals based on six criteria (e.g., logic, spatial adherence) to guide the training of the reasoning LLM (Qwen3-8B) via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- A ==VLM verifier== (GPT-5-mini) generates high-quality ==pseudo-annotations== for object detection by filtering and refining initial detector (GroundingDINO) predictions through a three-stage pipeline, enabling scalable, label-free fine-tuning of the grounding model.

## Results

- VALOR achieved an average **6.5%** accuracy improvement on OMNI3D-BENCH compared to state-of-the-art direct-answer VLMs, scoring **44.0%** versus **35.0%** for GPT-4o, demonstrating superior 3D spatial reasoning.
- The framework consistently outperformed other program synthesis methods and RL-tuned VLMs on benchmarks like OMNI3D-BENCH (**44.0%** vs. **38.9%** for VADAR) and ROBOSPATIAL (**69.5%** vs. **64.9%** for ViGoRL).
- The VLM verifier pipeline generated **30,826** high-quality pseudo-annotations from unlabeled images, leading to substantial gains in visual grounding accuracy, with an **8.3%** increase on COUNTBENCHQA and **7.7%** on ROBOSPATIAL compared to reasoning-only training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Powerful generative models, even when imperfect as generators, can serve as highly effective 'critics' or 'verifiers' to provide structured feedback for training without requiring ground truth labels.
> - A synergistic, multimodal approach that uses LLM verifiers to refine high-level reasoning and VLM verifiers to improve low-level visual grounding can lead to robust and accurate visual reasoning capabilities.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically GRPO, is a more effective optimization strategy than supervised fine-tuning for leveraging sparse, verifier-generated reward signals to enhance complex reasoning skills in LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual reasoning is a cornerstone of artificial intelligence, enabling systems to not only identify objects but also comprehend their intricate spatial relationships and attributes within an image. This capability is crucial for advanced applications in robotics, autonomous navigation, and intelligent assistants. However, achieving robust visual reasoning remains a significant challenge.

The current research landscape for visual reasoning primarily comprises two approaches:
1. **Language-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches**: These methods, often employing large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), attempt to generate textual explanations to derive answers. While they can handle simple relationships, they frequently suffer from weak visual understanding, leading to logical errors (e.g., confusing 2D pixel dimensions for 3D object sizes) and requiring vast amounts of meticulously labeled (image, query, answer) supervision data, which is expensive and time-consuming to acquire. Examples include recent iterations of models like GPT-5-Thinking (as critiqued in the paper).
2. **Program-synthesis approaches**: These leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate executable programs that call specialized vision tools (e.g., object detectors, depth estimators) to perform visual tasks. While more interpretable and potentially accurate by offloading visual perception to specialist models, these methods typically rely on proprietary LLMs and pre-trained vision specialists that are often poorly aligned with the specific demands of complex spatial reasoning, leading to flawed program logic or erroneous grounding. Furthermore, they often avoid explicit training, relying on prompting, which limits their adaptability.

VALOR (Verifiers for Annotation-free LOgic and Reasoning) strategically positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms, aiming to overcome their respective limitations. It embraces the interpretable, tool-use nature of program synthesis but addresses the alignment and grounding issues through a novel *annotation-free training framework*. This framework is a significant departure from data-hungry supervised methods, proposing a scalable alternative by leveraging the strengths of modern AI systems: advanced language models for reasoning decomposition and strong vision specialist models enhanced by "critic" VLMs.

The work also contributes to the growing fields of:
- **Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning**: While RL has been successfully applied to text-based reasoning (e.g., mathematics, programming) using verifiable rewards, its application to visual reasoning, particularly for improving tool-use and grounding, is underexplored. VALOR extends this by designing multimodal verifiers to generate structured reward signals for visual reasoning.
- **Training with LLM/VLM Verifiers**: The concept of using powerful, frozen models as verifiers to provide pseudo-supervision in the absence of ground truth labels is gaining traction. VALOR applies this principle bimodally: an LLM verifier refines language-based reasoning, and a VLM verifier strengthens visual grounding. This is a novel integration of verifier-guided training across different modalities and stages of a complex reasoning pipeline, moving beyond simple distillation or filtering.
- **Hard-Negative Mining and Bootstrapping**: The VLM verifier's approach to visual grounding echoes classical computer vision techniques like hard-negative mining, where challenging samples are identified to refine model performance. Applying this with VLM-generated pseudo-labels instead of manual annotations is a modern twist on an effective strategy.

By proposing an annotation-free, verifier-driven training framework, VALOR offers a scalable and robust solution to the core challenges of visual reasoning, bridging the gap between high-level language understanding and precise visual perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the VALOR framework are:

1. **Address the limitations of existing visual reasoning methods**: Current approaches either suffer from weak visual understanding and logical errors (language-only CoT) or struggle with faulty program logic and misaligned pre-trained specialists (program synthesis). The authors aim to build a system that combines the strengths of both while mitigating their weaknesses.
2. **Improve both reasoning and visual grounding**: Visual reasoning inherently requires accurate object localization (grounding) and coherent logical decomposition of complex spatial queries. VALOR seeks to jointly enhance both components within a unified framework.
3. **Develop an annotation-free training paradigm**: A major bottleneck in developing robust visual reasoning systems is the high cost and labor intensity of collecting large-scale, high-quality (image, query, answer) supervision data, as well as domain-specific object annotations. The key motivation is to eliminate this reliance on ground truth labels, making the training process scalable and adaptable to diverse domains.
4. **Leverage modern AI capabilities through multimodal verifiers**: The authors are motivated by the observation that powerful VLMs and LLMs, even if imperfect as generators, can be highly effective as "critics" or "verifiers." They aim to harness this capability by designing specialized verifiers that provide structured feedback to guide the training of both the reasoning LLM and the visual grounding models.
5. **Enable precise 3D spatial understanding**: Many visual reasoning tasks, particularly in embodied AI and robotics, require reasoning about objects in 3D space, not just 2D pixel coordinates. A specific motivation is to develop a system that can accurately infer 3D properties and relationships by integrating depth information.

In essence, VALOR is motivated by the need for a scalable, robust, and accurate visual reasoning system that can learn from unlabeled data by intelligently leveraging the verification capabilities of advanced multimodal AI models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VALOR's core methodology involves a novel annotation-free training framework that jointly tunes an LLM for reasoning and vision specialist tools for grounding, using multimodal verifiers as the primary source of learning signals. The approach is bifurcated into two main components: improving reasoning with LLM verifiers and improving visual grounding with VLM verifiers.

#### 3.1. Improving Reasoning with LLM Verifiers

1. **Plan & Code Generation**: Given a visual query and an image, a base LLM (Qwen3-8B in this work) generates a natural language plan, followed by a Python program. This program invokes a predefined API of three vision specialist tools:
 - `gd_detect(img_pth, prompt)`: Returns bounding boxes for objects specified by a natural language prompt (e.g., "fireplace, coffee table").
 - `depth(img_pth, bbox)`: Returns the estimated depth of an object at a given bounding box.
 - `vqa(img_pth, bbox, prompt)`: Answers a query about an object's attribute within a bounding box, or a holistic image query if `bbox` is `None`.
 The LLM is prompted with API specifications and few-shot examples to guide correct tool usage and program structure.

2. **Reward Model**: A sophisticated reward model verifies the quality of the generated plan and code. This model decomposes program quality into six binary components, each contributing to a weighted sum:
 - **Format (`r_fmt`)**: Checks if the output adheres to `<plan></plan><answer></answer>` tags (hard constraint, multiplier).
 - **Syntax (`r_sn`)**: Verifies if the Python code executes without errors (using dummy functions for API calls).
 - **Logic (`r_log`)**: Assesses if the plan is reasonable and coherent for the query, using a frozen Gemini-2.5-Flash LLM as a verifier.
 - **Attribute (`r_att`)**: Checks if all object properties (e.g., height, color) are correctly identified and handled in the plan, ensuring 3D-aware computations (LLM verifier).
 - **Spatial (`r_sp`)**: Confirms if all spatial relationships in the query are explicitly and correctly addressed in the plan (LLM verifier).
 - **Adherence (`r_ad`)**: Ensures the generated code faithfully implements the natural language plan (LLM verifier).
 The total reward `R` is a product of the format reward and a weighted sum of the other five components, with weights (`λ_k`) tuned based on error analysis (e.g., `λ_log=0.3` due to its high impact on errors).

3. **Optimization**: The LLM `π_θ` is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm that maximizes expected advantages while maintaining policy stability by preventing drift from the pre-trained base policy.

4. **Generating Training Data**: To enable annotation-free training, image-query pairs are generated:
 - Images are sampled from SA-1B (a large segmentation dataset).
 - Gemini-2.5-Flash is prompted to generate five diverse spatial reasoning queries per image, from which one is uniformly selected.
 - Additional (image, query) pairs are sourced from OMNI3D-BENCH (without answers) to ensure coverage of 3D spatial reasoning.
 The final dataset consists of 800 (image, query) pairs, used solely to generate reward signals, not ground truth answers. The LLM verifier's reliability (87% agreement with human labels) is crucial for this process.

#### 3.2. Improving Visual Grounding with VLM Verifiers

1. **Problem**: Pre-trained object detectors like GroundingDINO, trained on general web data, often struggle to generalize to the specific object distributions and spatial reasoning domains required, leading to inaccurate grounding. Fine-tuning with manual annotations is expensive.

2. **VLM Verifiers as Data Labelers**: VALOR proposes an annotation-free method to improve visual grounding. For each spatial reasoning query processed by the LLM reasoning model, the executed grounding queries (e.g., `gd_detect("HELMET")`) are captured. The pre-trained detector (GroundingDINO-T) is run with lowered confidence thresholds to prioritize high recall, leading to overpredictions.

3. **Three-Stage Verification Pipeline**: A frozen VLM (GPT-5-mini) acts as a verifier to filter these overpredictions and generate high-quality pseudo-annotations:
 - **Coarse Filtering**: The VLM inspects the original image overlaid with all predicted bounding boxes (numbered and labeled). It removes boxes whose content doesn't match the label or are poorly fitted.
 - **Per-Crop Object Check**: For remaining boxes, the image is cropped around each detection, and the VLM verifies if the primary object in the crop clearly matches the predicted label.
 - **De-duplication**: The VLM performs a final pass to eliminate duplicate detections, prioritizing boxes that best cover the full object with minimal background.
 This pipeline achieves 75% precision for generated pseudo-labels without human annotation.

4. **Generating Training Data**: The verified pseudo-annotations, derived from thousands of (image, query) pairs (from spatial reasoning benchmarks and generated from SA-1B), form a new training set. This dataset contains 30,826 bounding box annotations across 7,373 images, allowing scalable, label-free fine-tuning of the GroundingDINO model.

In summary, VALOR establishes a powerful symbiotic relationship: LLM verifiers refine the reasoning logic and tool orchestration, while VLM verifiers iteratively improve the perceptual grounding capabilities, all without requiring manual annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VALOR demonstrates significant improvements across various visual reasoning benchmarks, often outperforming both open-source and proprietary models, as well as alternative program synthesis and RL-tuned VLM approaches.

#### 4.1. VALOR vs. LLMs with Tool Use (Table 1)

- **Impact of Verifier-Improved Reasoning (VALOR-RL vs. Qwen3-8B)**: VALOR-RL (Qwen3-8B trained with LLM verifiers) consistently outperforms its base model, Qwen3-8B, particularly on reasoning-heavy tasks. For instance, it shows a **+6.4% gain on OMNI3D-BENCH (43.9% vs. 37.5%)** and **+3.4% on BLINK (67.3% vs. 63.9%)**. This highlights the effectiveness of the LLM verifier in refining task decomposition and tool utilization without improving the visual grounding module.
- **Impact of Verifier-Improved Visual Grounding (VALOR vs. VALOR-RL)**: VALOR (with both LLM and VLM verifiers) further improves performance, especially on benchmarks heavily reliant on accurate grounding. It achieves **+8.3% on COUNTBENCHQA** and **+7.7% on ROBOSPATIAL** compared to VALOR-RL. This demonstrates that enhancing the visual grounding model through VLM verifiers significantly boosts overall accuracy.
- **VALOR-RL vs. Proprietary LLMs**: Despite using a much smaller, open-source LLM (Qwen3-8B), VALOR-RL performs favorably against larger proprietary models like o4-mini and Gemini-2.5-Flash on several benchmarks (e.g., 70.3% on VSR vs. 68.5% for o4-mini). This suggests that verifier-guided training can make smaller models highly competitive.

#### 4.2. VALOR vs. RL-Tuned VLMs (Table 2)

- VALOR significantly outperforms RL-tuned VLMs like GRIT and ViGoRL on reasoning-heavy spatial tasks. For example, VALOR achieves **44.0% on OMNI3D-BENCH compared to GRIT's 27.3%** and **69.5% on ROBOSPATIAL versus ViGoRL's 64.9%**. This is particularly notable because GRIT and ViGoRL rely on labeled data and reason in text, whereas VALOR is label-free and leverages explicit tool use. The comparison underscores the advantage of VALOR's explicit grounding and tool-use paradigm.

#### 4.3. VALOR vs. Program Synthesis Methods (Table 3)

- VALOR outperforms existing program synthesis methods (VisProg, ViperGPT, VADAR) on both OMNI3D-BENCH and GQA. VALOR achieves **44.0% on OMNI3D-BENCH (vs. 38.9% for VADAR)** and **63.0% on GQA (vs. 46.9% for VisProg)**, despite using a smaller open-source LLM base (Qwen3-8B) compared to GPT-based baselines. This highlights the benefit of VALOR's verifier-driven training over purely prompt-engineered, training-free approaches.

#### 4.4. VALOR vs. Direct-Answer VLMs (Table 4)

- VALOR outperforms larger proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Claude-3.5-Haiku) that directly predict answers, especially on complex reasoning benchmarks like OMNI3D-BENCH (**44.0% for VALOR vs. 35.0% for GPT-4o**) and ROBOSPATIAL (**69.5% for VALOR vs. 60.9% for Gemini-2.0-Flash**). This demonstrates the superiority of a reasoning and tool-use paradigm over direct VLM prediction for intricate spatial understanding.

#### 4.5. Impact of Training Data Size (Table 5 & Fig. 7)

- **Reasoning**: VALOR-RL shows a positive correlation between the number of training samples and performance on OMNI3D-BENCH. It surpasses the base Qwen3-8B with as few as 40 samples and continues to improve up to 800 samples, demonstrating strong data scalability for reasoning.
- **Visual Grounding**: The accuracy on grounding-heavy benchmarks (ROBOSPATIAL, VSR, COUNTBENCHQA) consistently increases with the number of VLM-generated pseudo-annotations, from 5.6k to 30.8k. This confirms the scalability of the verifier-guided grounding training.

#### 4.6. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) vs. Reinforcement Learning (RL) (Table 6)

- The ablation study shows that GRPO-based RL training (VALOR) consistently outperforms SFT on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (e.g., **44.0% vs. 38.3% on OMNI3D-BENCH**). This indicates that RL is more effective at optimizing for the sparse, verifier-generated reward signal to improve complex reasoning skills.

Overall, the findings strongly support VALOR's annotation-free, verifier-driven methodology as a highly effective approach for improving visual reasoning, both in terms of logical decomposition and accurate visual grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VALOR framework represents a significant advancement in visual reasoning, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Annotation-Free Learning for Complex Visual Tasks**: The most significant contribution is the demonstration of an entirely annotation-free training paradigm for visual reasoning. By eliminating the reliance on expensive, manually labeled ground truth data, VALOR drastically reduces the cost and effort associated with developing and deploying robust visual AI systems. This democratizes access to advanced training methodologies, enabling researchers and practitioners to tackle complex problems without extensive data collection budgets.

2. **Scalability and Generalization**: The annotation-free nature makes VALOR inherently scalable. The ability to generate training queries and pseudo-labels from arbitrary image corpora (like SA-1B) means the system can continuously learn and adapt to new domains and types of spatial reasoning without human intervention. This scalable learning approach addresses a major bottleneck in AI development, pushing towards more generalized and adaptable visual intelligence.

3. **Synergistic Multimodal AI**: VALOR highlights a powerful synergy between language models and vision models. By using LLMs to decompose complex queries into executable programs and VLM-based critics to refine both the LLM's reasoning and the vision tools' grounding, it effectively combines the strengths of different AI modalities. This integration points towards a promising direction for building truly multimodal intelligent agents where components continuously improve each other.

4. **Improved Reasoning and Grounding**: The empirical results demonstrate that VALOR consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods—including large proprietary VLMs, RL-tuned VLMs, and other program synthesis approaches—on challenging visual reasoning tasks, especially those requiring precise 3D spatial understanding. This signifies a leap forward in the capabilities of AI systems to process and interpret visual information in a logically sound and perceptually accurate manner.

5. **Verifiers as a Foundation for Trustworthy AI**: The success of using LLMs and VLMs as reliable verifiers, even when their generative capabilities might be flawed, underscores a crucial insight. This "verifier-first" approach could be foundational for building more robust and trustworthy AI systems, where complex decisions are evaluated by dedicated, high-capacity critics rather than relying solely on the primary generative model.

6. **Practical Applications**: The improved capabilities in spatial understanding, counting, and 3D reasoning have direct applications in areas like:
 - **Robotics**: Enabling robots to better understand their environment, infer object relationships, and execute complex manipulation tasks based on natural language commands.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality**: Enhancing scene understanding and object interaction in immersive environments.
 - **Autonomous Systems**: Improving perception and decision-making by accurately interpreting spatial layouts and object properties in real-world scenes.
 - **Content Creation and Analysis**: More precise understanding of visual content for intelligent search, summarization, and generation.

**Future Directions:**
The authors propose several exciting avenues for future work, including:
- Integrating VLM verifiers directly into the reinforcement learning loop for a more tightly coupled training of reasoning and grounding.
- Developing methods for guided query generation to specifically harvest "hard negatives" for reasoning, mirroring the success of VLM verifiers for grounding.
- Exploring the adaptation of powerful VLMs, like Gemini-2.0-Flash, as specialized grounding tools within the VALOR framework, potentially leveraging their strong direct prediction capabilities for initial visual cues.

In conclusion, VALOR offers a robust, scalable, and annotation-free pathway to advanced visual reasoning, establishing a new benchmark for multimodal AI systems and opening new possibilities for intelligent agents that can truly "see" and "reason" about the world.
