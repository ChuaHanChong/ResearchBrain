# LoDA: A Level of Detection Aware Method and a Multimodal Sensing Benchmark for Object Level Change Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> LoDA is a method for object-level 3D change detection in urban LiDAR maps, which integrates a Level of Detection (LoD) to quantify local observability and reduce false positives. It includes a new multimodal benchmark and achieved an 8.7 mIoU point improvement over the best baseline for complex changes.

## Problem

- Existing 3D change detection methods for LiDAR data typically produce point-wise or surface-wise discrepancies, which are not directly actionable object-level changes for high-definition (HD) map maintenance.
- Prior approaches are highly sensitive to residual registration errors, varying point cloud densities, and inherent noise, often leading to false positives or missed detections.
- A lack of dedicated, object-level 3D change detection benchmarks that explicitly incorporate spatially varying observability (detectability) hinders rigorous evaluation and development in this area.

## Method

- Developed **LoDA**, a three-stage pipeline that includes geometric registration with ==Level of Detection (LoD) estimation==, semantic and instance segmentation with object correspondence, and ==LoD-gated object-level change analysis==.
- Introduced a novel, spatially varying "Level of Detection (LoD)" metric that quantifies the minimum detectable displacement by integrating local roughness, sampling density, and residual pose uncertainty.
- Constructed and released the LoDA multimodal benchmark dataset, featuring fused bi-temporal LiDAR maps with comprehensive semantic, instance, and five-class object-level change annotations, independently verified.

## Results

- Achieved an overall accuracy of **95.0%**, a macro F1-score of **90.8%**, and a macro IoU (mIoU) of **83.0%** on the LoDA benchmark, surpassing the strongest baseline by **8.7 mIoU points**.
- Demonstrated significant improvements in detecting "Increased" and "Decreased" object states, with gains of **23.1 and 15.3 IoU points** respectively, over the best baseline.
- An ablation study confirmed that LoD gating was the most impactful component, with its removal causing the largest performance drop (**7.8 mIoU points**), validating the importance of explicit observability modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling local detectability through the Level of Detection (LoD) is crucial for robustly distinguishing true object-level changes from noise or misalignment artifacts, particularly for subtle transformations.
> - A modular pipeline that decouples geometric registration, segmentation, object correspondence, and LoD-gated change inference improves robustness, interpretability, and allows specialized processing at each stage.
> - Delivering structured, instance-level change labels (Added, Removed, Increased, Decreased, Unchanged) directly addresses the practical requirements of HD map maintenance systems, providing more actionable intelligence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the critical challenge of maintaining the accuracy and currency of high-definition (HD) 3D LiDAR maps, which are fundamental components for autonomous driving and smart-city services. These maps encode essential information about road geometry, traffic context, and static scene structures. However, urban environments are dynamic, with frequent changes such as building modifications, vegetation growth or removal, and infrastructure updates. These changes necessitate reliable detection to ensure HD maps remain consistent with the physical world.

Existing approaches to 3D change detection from LiDAR data exhibit several limitations. Traditional methods, like raster height differencing or local point distance comparisons (e.g., M3C2), often operate at a per-point or surface-wise level. They are sensitive to residual registration errors between multi-temporal scans and typically produce raw discrepancy scores that require manual grouping to infer meaningful object-level changes. Raster-based and image-based methods simplify the 3D problem by projecting data onto 2D grids, which inherently discards valuable vertical structural information and rarely accounts for spatially varying observability conditions (i.e., whether a change is genuinely detectable given local data density and noise).

More recent advancements involve 3D deep learning models, which learn geometric representations from point clouds or sparse voxels. While these models have improved geometric representation learning and have been adapted for multi-temporal change detection (e.g., Siamese KPConv-style baselines, MinkUNet variants), they commonly operate on localized tiles. Furthermore, they often rely on fixed thresholds or implicit priors for change inference, producing point-wise predictions rather than structured, object-level labels (e.g., "Added," "Removed," "Increased," "Decreased," or "Unchanged"). This point-wise output necessitates further processing to aggregate into semantically meaningful changes relevant for map maintenance. Crucially, these deep learning approaches often do not explicitly model the local detectability of changes, making them susceptible to false positives caused by residual misalignment, varying point densities, or inherent noise.

This research positions itself as an advancement by moving beyond point-wise or tile-based predictions to provide *object-level* change detection. It explicitly integrates a "Level of Detection (LoD)" concept to quantify local observability, thereby addressing the sensitivity to registration errors and density variations that plague prior methods. By delivering structured, instance-level change labels, this work directly caters to the practical requirements of HD map maintenance systems, aiming to provide more reliable and actionable intelligence for urban data management. Furthermore, the introduction of a new multimodal benchmark dataset serves to facilitate controlled evaluation specifically tailored to this object-level, LoD-aware paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and reliable method for detecting object-level changes in multi-temporal urban LiDAR point clouds and to provide a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating such methods. This is driven by the critical need to maintain high-definition 3D maps for applications like autonomous driving and smart-city services, where static digital representations must be continuously updated to reflect the dynamic physical world.

The key motivations and more specific objectives are:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Raw Data Differences and Actionable Object-Level Changes:** Existing change detection methods primarily output point-wise or surface-wise discrepancies. For practical map maintenance, however, the requirement is to identify semantic changes at the object level, such as whether a building has been "Added," "Removed," "Increased" in size, "Decreased" in size, or remains "Unchanged." This research aims to automatically infer these structured, object-level labels directly from the LiDAR data.

2. **Addressing Sensitivity to Registration Errors and Density Variation:** Traditional and many deep learning-based methods are susceptible to false positives or missed detections caused by slight misalignments between multi-temporal scans or variations in point cloud density. This work is motivated by the need to develop a pipeline that explicitly accounts for and mitigates these issues.

3. **Introducing the Concept of "Level of Detection (LoD)":** A core motivation is to explicitly model whether a detected change is actually observable and significant given the local data quality. This "LoD" quantifies the minimum detectable displacement under local roughness, sampling density, and residual pose uncertainty. The objective is to integrate this LoD into the change inference process to suppress false changes in weakly observed or noisy regions.

4. **Decoupling and Integrating Distinct Processing Stages:** The authors aim to improve robustness and interpretability by decoupling key components of the change detection pipeline: geometric registration, geometry-driven object proxy formation, semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and change inference. This modular design allows for specialized processing at each stage and the propagation of uncertainties (like pose uncertainty into LoD).

5. **Developing a Multimodal Sensing Benchmark (LoDA):** A significant motivation is the absence of a dedicated, object-level change detection benchmark that incorporates explicit observability considerations. To enable rigorous evaluation of object-level change detection methods, the research aims to construct LoDA. This benchmark features fused multi-temporal vehicle LiDAR maps, supported by GNSS and IMU data, with semantic and instance annotations, and five-class object-level change labels that are independently verified through a robust annotation protocol.

In summary, the research is motivated by the practical demands of HD map updating, aiming to overcome the limitations of existing techniques by providing a comprehensive, LoD-aware, object-level change detection framework alongside a tailored benchmark for its evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named LoDA, is an object-level 3D change detection pipeline designed for bi-temporal vehicle LiDAR maps. It operates in three main stages: geometric registration and LoD estimation, semantic and instance segmentation with object correspondence, and LoD-gated change analysis.

#### 3.1. Data Collection and Benchmark Construction (LoDA Benchmark)

The LoDA benchmark was constructed using multi-modal vehicle surveys collected in Subiaco, Australia, in 2023 and 2025.
- **Acquisition:** Data was captured in 21 closed loops using an Ouster OS1-128 LiDAR, dual-antenna RTK GNSS, and a MEMS IMU, with GNSS-PPS synchronization and fixed extrinsic calibration.
- **Preprocessing:** Raw LiDAR packets were decoded and deskewed. Trajectories were estimated using multi-resolution NDT and robust point-to-plane ICP, with loop closures and GNSS/IMU constraints fused in a pose graph optimized in the GDA2020 zone 50 frame.
- **Map Fusion:** The city map was fused into a global 0.25 m voxel grid, and a 2 m ground model was estimated for height normalization.
- **Partitioning:** Both epochs were partitioned into fixed 120 m × 120 m blocks aligned to the GDA2020 grid. Blocks were retained if both epochs met minimum occupancy criteria. Block IDs were split into 60%/10%/30% train, validation, and test sets.
- **Annotation:** Per-point semantic labels ({ground, building, vegetation, mobile}) and per-class instance IDs were defined on the fused map. Building instances were generated by merging planar parts, while vegetation and mobile instances followed connectedness. Object-level annotations for five change labels (Added, Removed, Increased, Decreased, Unchanged) were produced by an independent protocol. Candidate cross-epoch pairs were proposed automatically and then manually verified by annotators using synchronized plan-view renderings, height maps, and occupancy overlays. Ambiguous cases were excluded.

#### 3.2. Geometric Registration and Object-Level Correspondence

This stage processes fused bi-temporal point clouds (P$_{23}$ and P$_{25}$) to refine alignment, estimate spatially varying detection limits, and establish geometry-based object correspondences.
- **Preprocessing:** Point clouds were filtered using Statistical Outlier Removal and downsampled into a voxel pyramid ({1.0, 0.5, 0.25} m leaf sizes). Normals were estimated within a 0.75 m radius.
- **Pose Estimation:** Initial pose was determined by multi-resolution NDT, followed by robust point-to-plane ICP. The ICP objective function minimized signed distances along local normals, incorporating a Tukey biweight robust loss and error weighting based on a fixed sensor term (σ₀) and local roughness (σ_r), estimated via robust standard deviation of point-to-plane residuals.
- **Pose Covariance:** The pose covariance Σ_T was approximated by the inverse normal-equation matrix at the solution, with its translational block Σ_t used for LoD calculation.
- **Vertical Alignment:** Ground planes were fitted by RANSAC on a 2 m grid. Both maps were rotated to align the median ground normal with the z-axis and then height-normalized by subtracting local median ground elevation.
- **Level of Detection (LoD):** The LoD was computed on a 1 m grid and interpolated. For each grid cell, points within a 1.0 m radius were pooled, a cell normal estimated, and the 95% LoD along the normal (LoD$_{95}$) calculated using:
 LoD$_{95}$(g) = 1.96 * sqrt( (σ²$_{23}$(g)/N$_{23}$(g)) + (σ²$_{25}$(g)/N$_{25}$(g)) + nᵀ_g Σ_t n_g )
 This formula integrates local point cloud roughness (σ²), sampling density (N), and the translational pose uncertainty (nᵀ_g Σ_t n_g). Cells with insufficient points (N < 30) were marked as low-observability with a default LoD$_{95}$ of 0.50 m.
- **Geometry-Only Object Proxies:** Ground points were removed. Non-ground points were voxelized (0.5 m), processed with 3D morphological closing, and connected components were extracted as proxies. Each proxy stored its centroid, oriented bounding box (OBB), 95th percentile height, and a normalized 10-bin height histogram. Proxies with fewer than 200 voxels were discarded.
- **Cross-Epoch Proxy Association:** Candidate pairs (centroid distance < 2.0 m, OBB IoU > 0.10) were scored by a cost function combining centroid distance, OBB IoU, and histogram D_χ² distance. The Hungarian algorithm was used for final bipartite assignment.

#### 3.3. Semantic and Instance Segmentation

This stage segments each epoch into semantic classes and object instances using geometry cues on a 0.25 m voxel grid.
- **Point Features:** For each point, local covariance eigenvalues (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃) were used to derive linearity, planarity, and sphericity. These, along with the absolute vertical normal component and height above ground, formed a 5-dimensional feature vector.
- **Superpoint Formation:** Superpoints were created by cut-pursuit on a k-NN graph (k=20, edge-length cap 1.2 m), clustering points with similar features.
- **Semantic Labeling (Rule-based):** Superpoints were classified into four classes ({ground, building, vegetation, mobile}) using rules based on median distance to ground, normal orientation, planarity, sphericity, plane-fit RMSE, planar support area, median height above ground, normal azimuth spread, and OBB dimensions/volume. A fixed priority order was applied.
- **Instance Extraction:**
 - **Buildings:** Planar patches were extracted by region growing on superpoints, then merged based on normal deviation and footprint IoU. Small patches were absorbed or discarded.
 - **Vegetation:** Euclidean clustering was applied to non-ground, non-building points using an adaptive radius (max(0.8 m, 1.8 / ρ^(1/3)), where ρ is local density).
 - **Mobile:** DBSCAN (ε=0.7 m, minPts=30) was used in the horizontal plane, followed by eroding the ground mask to remove clusters connected to curbs.

#### 3.4. Change Analysis

This final stage assigns one of five change labels (Added, Removed, Increased, Decreased, Unchanged) for each object pair.
- **Geometric Cues:** For each matched pair (o$_{23}$, o$_{25}$), geometric differences were computed on a 0.5 m voxel grid within their OBB union. These included occupied volumes (V$_{23}$, V$_{25}$), 3D occupancy IoU (IoU$_{3D}$), centroid shift (Δc), 95th percentile height difference (Δh), volume change (ΔV), and normal direction displacement (D_⊥). The pairwise LoD$_{95}$ for the object was taken as the median grid LoD$_{95}$ over the OBB intersection.
- **LoD-Gated Decisions:** Height difference (Δh) and normal direction displacement (D_⊥) cues were only considered "informative" if their absolute values exceeded α_LoD × LoD$_{95}$ (with α_LoD = 1.10).
- **Label Assignment Rules:**
 - **Added/Removed:** Unmatched instances from 2025 were "Added," from 2023 were "Removed."
 - **Matched Buildings:** "Increased" if Δh > 0.60 m or ΔV/V$_{23}$ > 0.12. "Decreased" if Δh < -0.60 m or ΔV/V$_{23}$ < -0.12. "Unchanged" if IoU$_{3D}$ > 0.20, ||Δc||₂ < 1.5 m, and both |Δh| and |ΔV|/V$_{23}$ were below thresholds OR all informative statistics were below the LoD gate.
 - **Matched Vegetation:** Similar logic to buildings but with different thresholds (e.g., |Δh| > 0.35 m, |ΔV|/V$_{23}$ > 0.18). Disagreements between height and volume cues were resolved by inspecting vertical occupancy histograms.
 - **Ground:** Evaluated on a 2 m raster. "Increased" if median elevation difference (Δz) > 0.15 m and |Δz| > 1.1 LoD$_{95}$ over a connected region > 25 m². "Decreased" if Δz < -0.15 m under same conditions. "Unchanged" otherwise.
 - **Mobile Objects:** Unmatched mobiles were "Added" or "Removed." Matched mobiles were "Unchanged" if IoU$_{3D}$ > 0.20 and ||Δc||₂ < 2.0 m; otherwise, they were labeled "Added" or "Removed" based on the epoch with larger unmatched occupancy.
- **Confidence Score:** A deterministic confidence score (0-1) was computed for each matched pair using LoD-normalized cues, with class-specific weights. This score was for ranking/inspection, not calibrated probability.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

Metrics were computed after aggregating predictions over the full test split. The evaluation included all annotated non-ground instances. Micro accuracy (ACC), per-class IoU, per-class F1, macro IoU (mIoU), and macro F1 (mF1) were reported. If a method yielded no valid prediction for an annotated instance, it was counted as a false negative.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents quantitative and qualitative results on the newly introduced LoDA benchmark and the public Urb3DCD-V2 benchmark, along with an ablation study and efficiency analysis.

#### 4.1. Performance on LoDA Benchmark

- The proposed method achieved an overall accuracy (ACC) of **95.0%**, a macro F1-score (mF1) of **90.8%**, and a macro IoU (mIoU) of **83.0%**.
- When compared to the strongest baseline methods across various representations (raster, point-based, voxel-based), the proposed approach demonstrated notable improvements. For instance, it exceeded the best baseline (EFS KPConv) by **8.7 mIoU points** and **4.4 mF1 points**.
- The most significant gains were observed in the detection of "Increased" and "Decreased" object states, with improvements of **23.1 and 15.3 IoU points**, respectively, compared to the EFS KPConv baseline. This indicates the effectiveness of the LoD-aware and object-level reasoning for subtle changes.
- Qualitative results (Figure 5) illustrate the method's ability to produce clear object-level change maps for various urban structures.

#### 4.2. Performance on Urb3DCD-V2 Benchmark

- The method was further evaluated on the public Urb3DCD-V2 benchmark using its official point-wise evaluation protocol, by projecting instance-level decisions to point labels.
- It achieved a mean accuracy (mAcc) of **96.81%** and a mean change IoU (mIoUch) of **89.52%**.
- These results surpassed the strongest reported baselines on Urb3DCD-V2 by **1.36 points in mAcc** and **3.18 points in mIoUch**, demonstrating the transferability and robustness of the approach even under a different evaluation paradigm.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- The ablation study (Table 4) demonstrated the individual contributions of different components of the proposed pipeline to the overall performance on LoDA.
- **Removing LoD gating** in change decisions resulted in the largest drop in mIoU and mF1, decreasing mIoU by 7.8 points (from 83.0% to 75.2%) and mF1 by 4.8 points (from 90.8% to 86.0%). This specifically impacted the ability to distinguish "Increased" and "Decreased" changes accurately, highlighting the importance of explicit observability modeling.
- Other components, such as using a **global LoD** instead of spatially varying LoD, **removing the pose covariance term** from LoD calculation, or using **L2 point-to-plane ICP** without robust loss, also led to performance degradations, albeit smaller than removing LoD gating entirely.
- The contributions of **geometry-only proxies, Hungarian assignment, cut-pursuit superpoints, planar merging for buildings**, and **adaptive-radius vegetation clustering** were also quantified, showing their positive impact on performance.
- Among the change cues, the **height cue (Δh)** and **volume cue (ΔV)** were particularly important, with their removal causing mIoU drops of 3.8 and 3.1 points, respectively. The **normal-direction cue (D_⊥)** also contributed positively.

#### 4.4. Efficiency and Scalability

- The pipeline processed an average of **1.60 million points** and **341 non-ground instances** per 120m × 120m block in approximately **60.5 seconds** on the evaluation hardware.
- Peak host memory usage averaged **12.9 GB**.
- Segmentation was identified as the most computationally intensive stage, accounting for **58.8%** of the average runtime (35.6 seconds). Object matching, in contrast, was efficient, requiring only 2.5 seconds (4.1%).
- The system exhibited consistent scaling, with runtime and memory usage increasing proportionally to the input point count across different blocks (e.g., from 1.12 million points/47.6s/9.8GB to 2.18 million points/75.8s/16.8GB).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of 3D change detection, particularly for urban environments and applications reliant on high-definition 3D maps. The core significance and potential impacts are:

1. **Advancement in Object-Level Change Detection:** The proposed LoD-aware pipeline shifts the paradigm from per-point or surface-wise discrepancy detection to structured, object-level change inference. This is crucial for practical map maintenance systems, which require actionable intelligence regarding additions, removals, increases, or decreases of specific urban objects (e.g., "a building has increased in height," rather than "these points have changed elevation"). This directly addresses a gap in existing methodologies that often rely on post-processing of low-level change maps.

2. **Robustness to Real-World Data Imperfections:** By explicitly modeling the "Level of Detection" (LoD) which incorporates local roughness, sampling density, and residual pose uncertainty, the method enhances robustness to common challenges in multi-temporal LiDAR data, such as registration errors and varying data quality. This leads to more reliable change detection by suppressing false positives in under-sampled or noisy regions and providing a principled basis for asserting changes. The ablation study strongly supports the critical role of LoD gating in achieving this robustness, particularly for subtle changes like growth or shrinkage.

3. **Introduction of a Novel Benchmark (LoDA):** The creation and release of the LoDA benchmark is a substantial contribution to the research community. LoDA provides a controlled, multimodal dataset specifically designed for object-level change detection, complete with fused bi-temporal maps, semantic and instance annotations, and five-class object-level change labels. This benchmark facilitates standardized evaluation and encourages further research into LoD-aware and instance-level reasoning, which were previously underdeveloped due to a lack of suitable datasets. Its annotation protocol, with independent verification and QA, helps reduce protocol bias.

4. **Improved Accuracy for Subtle Changes:** The method demonstrates superior performance, especially for distinguishing "Increased" and "Decreased" states, which are often challenging to detect reliably. This higher accuracy for nuanced changes contributes directly to more precise and comprehensive map updates.

5. **Direct Applicability to Autonomous Driving and Smart Cities:** High-definition 3D maps are fundamental for autonomous vehicles (for localization and perception) and various smart-city services (for urban planning, infrastructure monitoring). The ability to automatically and reliably detect object-level changes ensures that these critical digital assets remain synchronized with the physical world, enhancing safety, efficiency, and the utility of these applications. The structured output (e.g., "Building X increased by Y meters") can be directly integrated into map update workflows.

6. **Methodological Generalizability:** The strong performance on both the novel LoDA benchmark and the public Urb3DCD-V2 dataset, without per-dataset retuning, suggests the generalizability of the proposed LoD-aware, object-level framework. This indicates that the core principles of decoupling processing stages and incorporating explicit uncertainty modeling can be broadly applicable.
