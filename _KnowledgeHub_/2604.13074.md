# PersonaVLM: Long-Term Personalized Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> PersonaVLM introduces an agent framework designed for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to achieve long-term, dynamic personalization through a novel memory architecture and a two-stage collaborative process. This framework demonstrates improved personalized understanding and alignment, outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o in open-ended generation based on factual accuracy and personality alignment.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs and personalization methods struggle with static user traits, fixed context windows, and a "one-size-fits-all" approach to output alignment, failing to capture evolving user preferences over long interactions.
- Current personalization techniques often lack mechanisms for proactively managing or updating user-specific knowledge from dynamic multimodal interactions.
- There is a lack of high-quality training data and comprehensive benchmarks specifically designed for evaluating long-term, dynamic, and multimodal personalization in MLLMs.

## Method

- The **PersonaVLM** framework proposes a ==personalized memory architecture== that includes a dynamic user personality profile (==Big Five traits==) and a ==multi-type memory database== (core, procedural, semantic, and episodic memories).
- It employs a ==two-stage collaborative process== (Response and Update) which utilizes ==multi-step reasoning== and ==timeline-based retrieval== to generate and refine personalized responses.
- A dedicated training pipeline leverages a ==synthetic large-scale multimodal dataset== and a ==two-stage fine-tuning== approach (==Supervised Fine-Tuning== followed by ==Reinforcement Learning==) to teach memory management, multi-turn reasoning, and alignment.

## Results

- PersonaVLM improved the baseline by **22.4%** on Persona-MME (**128k context**) and **9.8%** on PERSONAMEM, demonstrating superior personalized understanding compared to open-source models and being competitive with or outperforming GPT-4o in various configurations.
- The framework achieved robust personalized alignment, leading the next-best model by **9.16%** on the Persona-MME alignment sub-task and showing a greater than **12%** gain over the baseline on the P-SOUPS benchmark.
- In open-ended generation, PersonaVLM achieved a **79% win rate** against GPT-4o, based on factual accuracy and personality alignment, as evaluated by an automated judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic memory architectures, integrating evolving personality profiles and multi-type multimodal memory databases, are crucial for effective long-term personalization in MLLMs.
> - A multi-stage agentic retrieval and reasoning process allows MLLMs to proactively manage and leverage long-term user context beyond fixed context windows, leading to more precise and personalized responses.
> - Continuous personality evolution mechanisms, dynamically updated after each interaction, are essential for adapting to subtle shifts in user traits and preferences over extended dialogues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly deployed as daily assistants, but their capacity to generate responses tailored to individual, evolving preferences and personality remains limited. The current research landscape in MLLM personalization broadly encompasses three streams: adaptation-based, augmentation-based, and alignment-based approaches.

Adaptation-based methods, such as those employing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) or learnable embeddings (e.g., MyVLM, Yo’LLaVA), encode user-specific knowledge directly into model parameters. While effective for recognizing specific user concepts (e.g., "the user's pet dog"), these methods often lack scalability for new users or dynamic changes, as they typically require fine-tuning for each new concept or user trait.

Augmentation-based approaches, including those in the multimodal domain (e.g., RAP), equip models with external databases to store and retrieve user-specific memories. These methods offer advantages in accommodating new user concepts without continual fine-tuning, thereby extending beyond fixed context windows for lifelong dialogues. However, existing augmentation methods are often constrained by manually predefined databases and typically lack mechanisms for proactively managing or updating knowledge from dynamic interactions. Furthermore, general-purpose memory architectures predominantly focus on text-only data, limiting their applicability to truly multimodal inputs and raising privacy concerns due to reliance on proprietary models.

Alignment-based personalization aims to refine MLLM outputs to match user-specific preferences and communication styles, moving beyond a universal "one-size-fits-all" standard. Techniques like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (e.g., ALIGNXPERT) or user-specific "probes" (e.g., Personality-Activation Search, PAS) attempt to align responses with predefined or inferred user values. A fundamental limitation of these approaches is their assumption of static user traits, which prevents adaptation to evolving personalities or preferences over long-term interactions. Additionally, some methods face scalability challenges due to per-user training requirements.

This work positions PersonaVLM as a unified agent framework designed to address these limitations by integrating dynamic, long-term personalization. It specifically tackles the shortcomings of static memory management, fixed output alignment, and the lack of comprehensive multimodal support. The paper identifies two foundational pillars for effective long-term personalization: (i) a personalized memory architecture for dynamic, user-centric multimodal data management, and (ii) capabilities for memory utilization and response alignment that leverage reasoning and retrieval to generate contextually and personally aligned outputs. By integrating these pillars, PersonaVLM aims to bridge the gap between general-purpose MLLMs and truly personalized, empathetic assistants capable of adapting to a user's evolving intent, preferences, and personality over extended interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance general Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) into truly personalized assistants. This involves enabling MLLMs to accurately infer user intent, dynamically align their behavior with individual preferences and personality, and persistently remember user-specific multimodal information across prolonged interactions.

This objective is motivated by several critical limitations observed in existing MLLMs and personalization strategies:

1. **Static and Limited Personalization:** Current MLLMs predominantly offer static, single-turn personalization. Approaches like input augmentation can recognize user-specific concepts but lack mechanisms to manage or update these memories, thus failing to capture evolving user preferences (e.g., a user shifting from Sprite to Coca-Cola). Similarly, existing alignment techniques assume static user traits, preventing adaptation to personality changes that may manifest subtly over many unrelated dialogues (e.g., an introverted user needing a different tone than an extroverted one).
2. **Fixed Context Windows:** Most MLLMs are optimized within fixed context windows, which inherently limits their capacity to retain and utilize long-term user history and context. This results in models failing to leverage past interactions to inform current responses.
3. **"One-Size-Fits-All" Paradigm:** While general MLLMs are effective for broad problem-solving, user expectations are shifting towards more personalized and empathetic experiences. The universal behavioral standards enforced by typical alignment methods (e.g., RLHF) do not accommodate the diverse communication styles and preferences of individual users.
4. **Scalability and Data Privacy Concerns:** Many prior personalization methods, particularly those involving per-user fine-tuning, face significant scalability challenges. Furthermore, reliance on proprietary models for advanced capabilities introduces data privacy concerns, limiting their applicability in sensitive domains.
5. **Scarcity of Training Data and Evaluation Benchmarks:** There is a notable lack of high-quality training data and comprehensive benchmarks specifically designed for evaluating long-term, dynamic, and multimodal personalization in MLLMs. Existing datasets are often static, single-turn, or text-centric, providing an incomplete picture of personalization capabilities.

To address these motivations, the research outlines specific objectives:
- To propose PersonaVLM, an innovative agent framework that integrates proactive remembering, multi-step reasoning, and response alignment for long-term MLLM personalization.
- To introduce a personalized memory architecture comprising a dynamic user personality profile (Big Five traits) and a multi-type memory database (core, procedural, semantic, episodic memories) to manage diverse user information.
- To develop a two-stage collaborative process (Response and Update stages) that enables a general MLLM to leverage this memory architecture for personalized interaction.
- To construct a large-scale, personalized, multimodal interactive dataset through a synthesis pipeline to facilitate effective training and ensure data privacy.
- To establish Persona-MME, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating MLLM personalization across multiple facets and modalities, designed to assess long-term capabilities.

By achieving these objectives, the work aims to overcome the current limitations of MLLMs, fostering the development of truly user-centric AI assistants with enhanced satisfaction and trust, and unlocking their potential value in fields such as recommendation, healthcare, and education.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PersonaVLM framework is designed for long-term personalized multimodal interactions, built upon a personalized memory architecture and operating through two collaborative stages: Response and Update.

#### 3.1. Personalized Memory Architecture

The architecture maintains a comprehensive, long-term user profile consisting of two primary components:
- **User Personality Profile (P):** This quantitatively represents the user's personality as a five-dimensional vector of scores (1 to 5) for the Big Five traits: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism (OCEAN). This profile is dynamically updated after each interaction via the Personality Evolving Mechanism (PEM).
- **Multi-Type Memory Database (M):** This timeline-based, agentic system supports CRUD operations and categorizes knowledge into four distinct types:
 - **Core Memory:** Stores fundamental user attributes, such as name, age, gender, preferences (Human block), and identity, roles, or interaction style requirements (Persona block). This memory is dynamically updated, retaining only its latest version.
 - **Semantic Memory:** Archives event-independent, abstract knowledge by extracting key entities, relationships, and multimodal concepts. It includes explicit directives, core facts, preferences/habits, and user-specific visual concepts (stored as textual descriptions linked to image crops). This memory is purely additive, with new entries appended without modification or deletion.
 - **Episodic Memory:** Organizes raw dialogues into atomic, time-stamped events. Each entry contains a concise summary, relevant keywords, and indices of the dialogue turns. This memory is also purely additive, preserving the original dialogue data.
 - **Procedural Memory:** Tracks user goals and identifies recurring behaviors or habits, storing long-term goals and routines. Similar to Core Memory, it retains only its latest version and is mutable.

The memory architecture is designed to be self-contained, explicitly personalized, and supportive of multimodal inputs.

#### 3.2. Response Stage

This stage aims to generate an aligned response by leveraging multi-step reasoning and timeline-based retrieval. Given a user's multimodal query (text, optional image, timestamp), recent dialogue context, and the current state of the personalized memory database, PersonaVLM performs the following:
1. The model is prompted with the user’s input, context, and a consolidated profile (core memory and personality).
2. It then outputs a reasoning process and an action.
3. If the model determines that current information is insufficient, it outputs retrieval conditions (time period, keywords) within a predefined template.
4. The agent executes retrieval by first isolating memories within the inferred time period and then performing a parallel search across semantic, episodic, and procedural memory types. For text-based memories, `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` sentence transformer encodes memories for similarity search using FAISS. For visual concepts, Grounding DINO extracts objects from the input image, and CLIP embeddings are used for similarity search with stored visual concepts.
5. Top-k relevant results from each memory type are collected and fed back to the model for further reasoning. This iterative process continues until a final response is generated.
This multi-turn agentic retrieval is intended to yield more precise and efficient results by allowing the model to determine not just what to retrieve but also if and from when.

#### 3.3. Update Stage

This stage executes automatically during idle periods after a response is generated, involving two main processes:
1. **Personality Evolving Mechanism (PEM):** The user’s long-term personality profile (p ∈ R^5) is updated. The PEM infers a temporary set of personality scores (p'm) from the latest user query. The long-term profile is then updated using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA): `p_m ← λ ⋅ p_{m-1} + (1 - λ) ⋅ p'_m`, where `λ` is a dynamic smoothing factor (cosine decay) that allows rapid adaptation initially and greater stability over time. The updated numerical vector is converted into a descriptive textual summary for future use.
2. **Proactive Memory Update:**
 - **Semantic Memory:** Updated after each turn by extracting key information (preferences, multimodal concepts, explicit memorization requests) with timestamps and keywords.
 - **Core and Procedural Memory:** Updated at the end of each session; the agent analyzes the entire session's dialogue to perform automated CRUD operations, ensuring these mutable memories reflect the user's most current state.
 - **Episodic Memory:** Constructed by segmenting dialogues into distinct topics, with each entry containing a summary, keywords, and specific dialogue turns.

#### 3.4. Training of PersonaVLM

PersonaVLM uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B as its backbone and is trained in two stages:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The model is fine-tuned on a synthetic dataset of 78k samples, providing foundational memory management and multi-turn reasoning skills. This dataset includes examples for personality inference, CRUD operations for all four memory types, and QA pairs with complete, multi-step reasoning trajectories.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** This stage employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an improved PPO algorithm, to enhance multi-turn reasoning. The model's output is strictly structured (`<think>`, `<retrieve>`, `<answer>`). Rewards are calculated based on accuracy, logical consistency (using Qwen3-30B-A3B as an LLM-as-a-Judge), and format adherence. Optimization strategies include a deduplication filter for retrieved memories and a dynamic top-k strategy during training to robustify retrieval.

#### 3.5. Dataset and Persona-MME Construction

To facilitate both training and evaluation, the authors developed:
- **Dataset Synthesis Pipeline:** Generates a large-scale multimodal interactive dataset (>30k interactions across 500 personas). It starts with PersonaHub personas, enriches them with personality traits, and simulates long-term, dynamic conversations (hundreds of turns, weeks/months) using Seed1.6-thinking, inducing probabilistic shifts in preferences and personality. Over 15% of dialogues are multimodal. The pipeline generates dialogue, intermediate reasoning, retrieval, and memorization steps, providing rich supervisory signals.
- **Persona-MME Benchmark:** A comprehensive benchmark comprising over 2,000 "in-situ" cases from 200 diverse personas. It is structured around seven core dimensions (Memory, Intent, Preference, Behavior, Relationship, Growth, Alignment) encompassing 14 fine-grained tasks. It offers 32k and 128k context-length configurations. Evaluation includes multiple-choice questions for memory and understanding, and an optional personality test for alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate the effectiveness of the PersonaVLM framework across personalized understanding, alignment, and open-ended generation, benchmarking it against various proprietary and open-source models.

#### 4.1. Personalized Understanding Evaluation (RQ1)

- **Overall Performance:** PersonaVLM demonstrates significant improvements on both Persona-MME and PERSONAMEM benchmarks. Under a 128k context, it improves the baseline by 22.4% on Persona-MME and 9.8% on PERSONAMEM.
- **Comparison to Open-Source Models:** PersonaVLM consistently outperforms strong open-source models of similar or larger scale. For instance, in the 128k setting, PersonaVLM shows improvements of 8.62% over InternVL3-8B and 14.39% over LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B on Persona-MME. It also outperforms the larger InternVL3-38B by 3.87% on Persona-MME (128k).
- **Comparison to Proprietary Models:** PersonaVLM achieves competitive results and demonstrates notable improvements over GPT-4o. On PERSONAMEM, PersonaVLM surpasses GPT-4o by 17.3% (32k context) and 2.0% (128k context). On Persona-MME (128k context), PersonaVLM achieved 71.05% accuracy, compared to GPT-4o's 69.23%.
- **Specific Strengths:** While PersonaVLM’s memory recall sometimes lags behind GPT-4o (especially with full context), it shows a significant advantage (over 10%) in "Growth Modeling" and "Behavioral Awareness" tasks.
- **Training Effectiveness:** The two-stage training process (SFT followed by RL) contributes to an average improvement of 5.35% on Persona-MME, indicating the benefits of progressive learning.
- **RAG Impact:** Simple Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can negatively impact short-context scenarios (up to 9.33% degradation on preference understanding tasks) but provides a substantial boost (4.53%) in long-context settings.

#### 4.2. Personalized Alignment Evaluation (RQ2)

- **Performance on Persona-MME Alignment Sub-task:** PersonaVLM consistently outperforms existing models, leading the next-best model by 9.16% and achieving significantly higher accuracy (89.16% at 32k, 92.22% at 128k) than language-centric models like Qwen3-30B-A3B (80.09% at 32k, 83.06% at 128k) and multimodal models like InternVL3-38B (64.60% at 32k, 63.01% at 128k).
- **Performance on P-SOUPS Benchmark:** PersonaVLM achieves an overall score of 49.60%, leading the next-best model by 2.46% and showing a greater than 12% gain over the baseline, demonstrating robust personality alignment capabilities.
- **Impact of PEM:** Ablation studies confirm that the Personality Evolving Mechanism (PEM) contributes to the overall effectiveness, showing a 4% improvement on P-SOUPS when included.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Evaluation (RQ3)

- **Open-Ended Generation:** An automated evaluation using Gemini-2.5-Pro as a judge on 200 Persona-MME questions revealed PersonaVLM's superior performance. It achieved a 79% win rate against GPT-4o, with only a 16% loss rate, based on factual accuracy and personality alignment.
- **Case Studies:** Qualitative case studies further illustrate PersonaVLM's ability to perform accurate visual recall, integrate contextual memory, and maintain long-term personality alignment, in contrast to other models that exhibited memory hallucinations or tonally misaligned responses.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Appendix E)

- **Memory Type Effectiveness:** Removing any single memory type degrades performance, with Episodic memory being the most critical component, causing a 12.41% (32k) and 5.19% (128k) performance drop on Persona-MME. Procedural memory significantly impacts Behavior and Relationship tasks.
- **Reasoning Capability:** Disabling multi-turn reasoning and retrieval during the response stage results in performance drops of 2.75% (32k) and 3.73% (128k) on Persona-MME.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis (Appendix F)

- PersonaVLM without reasoning capability significantly reduces token consumption by 93.7% and achieves a 4.8× speedup compared to the baseline. With reasoning, token consumption further decreases by 20.4%, but response time increases by 21.1% compared to the baseline, indicating a trade-off between advanced reasoning and latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PersonaVLM introduces an agent framework that represents an advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by enabling long-term, dynamic personalization. The work addresses critical limitations of prior static and single-turn personalization approaches, offering a new paradigm for developing user-centric AI assistants.

The proposed personalized memory architecture, encompassing a dynamic user personality profile and a multi-type memory database (core, semantic, episodic, and procedural), provides a comprehensive and flexible system for managing diverse and evolving user information across multiple modalities and over extended periods. This architectural design could serve as a foundational blueprint for future personalized AI systems, moving beyond simple context windows to a more nuanced understanding of individual users.

The demonstrated ability of PersonaVLM to remember evolving preferences, reason with retrieved knowledge, and align responses with a user's inferred personality traits holds potential for enhancing user experience with AI systems. This can lead to more empathetic, relevant, and trustworthy interactions, which are crucial for increasing user satisfaction and fostering adoption in various practical applications.

The establishment of Persona-MME is a significant contribution to the research community. This comprehensive and multi-faceted benchmark, designed to evaluate long-term, multimodal personalization across 14 fine-grained tasks, provides a rigorous standard for assessing MLLM capabilities. Its availability will facilitate more robust research, enabling systematic comparisons and accelerated development in the domain of personalized AI.

The practical implications of PersonaVLM extend to several domains:
- **Recommendation Systems:** Enabling more accurate and dynamically personalized recommendations by accounting for evolving user interests and behaviors.
- **Healthcare:** Offering tailored health advice and personalized patient interaction that adapts to individual medical histories and preferences.
- **Education:** Creating adaptive learning environments and personalized tutoring systems that adjust to a student's learning style, pace, and knowledge acquisition over time.
- **Customer Service and Virtual Assistants:** Developing more intelligent and context-aware AI agents that can provide relevant support by remembering past interactions and understanding user intent and personality.

Furthermore, the emphasis on a self-contained dataset and local operation capabilities addresses critical concerns regarding data privacy and security, which are essential for the widespread and ethical deployment of personalized AI technologies.

While PersonaVLM demonstrates significant progress, its limitations, such as the lack of support for video/audio person recognition and the absence of connections between related episodic memories, identify clear directions for future research. This work lays a foundation for extending personalized capabilities toward more immersive and holistic multimodal AI experiences.
