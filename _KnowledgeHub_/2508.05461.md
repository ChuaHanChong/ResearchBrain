# How and Why: Taming Flow Matching for Unsupervised Anomaly Detection and Localization

> [!summary] Summary
> The authors propose **WT-Flow**, an extension of time-reversed Flow Matching (**rFM**) for unsupervised image anomaly detection. They show that (a) standard FM with linear-interpolation paths is ==inherently non-invertible== and (b) Gaussian reversed paths in high dimensions degenerate to trivial vector fields. Fix: ==Worst-Transport displacement interpolation== gives a non-probabilistic evolution path that admits an anomaly score. Flow-matching-native; tested on MVTec-AD and VisA.

## Problem

- Flow Matching is natural for learning continuous data manifolds, but has not been successfully used for unsupervised anomaly detection at scale.
- Two hidden pathologies:
  1. Linear-interpolation FM paths are **non-invertible** — the anomaly score one would want (inverse-flow-to-prior likelihood) is ill-defined.
  2. Reversed Gaussian probability paths in high dimensions produce **trivial vector fields** (near-zero magnitude) that don't discriminate ID from OOD.
- Normalizing Flows, the classical likelihood-based alternative, require strict architectural invertibility that limits expressiveness and scalability in high-dimensional data regimes.

## Method

- ==Formalize time-reversed Flow Matching (rFM)== as vector-field regression along a predefined probability path that transforms data into standard Gaussian.
- ==Worst-Transport (WT) displacement interpolation==: replace Gaussian path with a non-probabilistic evolution path that maximizes transport cost to the prior — forces the vector field to be informative.
- **WT-Flow** = extremely simple extension of rFM with WT paths, trained on ID samples only (unsupervised, calibrated on held-out ID for threshold); anomaly score is the path integral of vector-field magnitude on the reversed trajectory.

## Results

- Competitive with diffusion-based and normalizing-flow-based anomaly detectors on **MVTec-AD** and **VisA** (standard industrial anomaly benchmarks).
- Ablations: naive rFM (Gaussian path) underperforms baseline; WT-displacement is load-bearing.
- Localization maps produced by pixel-wise vector-field magnitude; spatially interpretable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ==Flow Matching is not invertible under linear interpolation==; reversed-flow anomaly scores require a carefully-designed probability path (WT).
> - WT-Flow is the first FM-native unsupervised anomaly detector (code at github.com/lil-wayne-0319/fmad, no explicit license); being FM-native means no backbone rework for UWM or Cosmos Policy, so port cost is LOW.
> - Relative to `logpZO` (FAIL-Detect), this is still a **density / manifold-fit signal family** — potential H5 redundancy risk if used as second imag anchor. Candidate only if EigenScore's FM-score reformulation is intractable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Unsupervised Image Anomaly Detection (IAD) is a critical area in industrial computer vision and medical image analysis. A principal paradigm for IAD involves likelihood-based deep generative models (DGMs), which quantify data regularity via probability density estimation, characterizing anomalies as low-likelihood observations. Normalizing Flows (NFs) have been a key method in this area due to their ability to compute exact likelihoods and perform bidirectional invertible transformations. However, NFs are often constrained by strict architectural invertibility requirements, which can limit their expressiveness and scalability, particularly in high-dimensional data regimes.

Recent advancements in DGMs, such as Flow Matching (FM), Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs), Score Matching (SMs), and Diffusion Models (DMs), offer more flexible approaches by modeling data generation as a continuous-time dynamic process. These methods predict vector fields to satisfy probability flow equations, thus avoiding the direct optimization of likelihood-based objectives. While FM has shown promise due to its sampling efficiency and architectural flexibility, its direct application to IAD (which requires data-to-noise mapping) is computationally challenging due to the prohibitive costs of Jacobian-trace estimation. Furthermore, recent NF-based methods have implicitly moved towards "density proxy estimation," where anomalies are identified in a latent space rather than through exact likelihood computation in the data space. This paradigm, which permits unidirectional distribution transformations, eliminates the need for Jacobian-trace estimations, enhancing tractability for modern DGMs in anomaly detection.

This work addresses the limitations of applying time-parameterized Flow Matching (FM) to IAD by proposing a time-reversed Flow Matching (rFM) paradigm. It positions itself by identifying and attempting to overcome fundamental theoretical bottlenecks specific to rFM, particularly the non-invertibility and dynamic instabilities that arise when reversing the flow for anomaly detection purposes. By doing so, it aims to expand the design space of flow-based models beyond traditional NF-based methods and provide a more stable and efficient solution for IAD in high-dimensional settings. The paper also contributes to the broader understanding of high-dimensional Out-of-Distribution (OOD) problems and the geometry of data manifolds, connecting these theoretical insights to practical anomaly detection solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations of this research are outlined as follows:

1. **Address Scalability and Computational Challenges of Flow Matching in IAD:** While Flow Matching (FM) offers scalability and architectural flexibility compared to Normalizing Flows (NFs), its direct application to Image Anomaly Detection (IAD) remains computationally demanding. The standard FM objective is designed for generative modeling (noise-to-data), making its use for IAD (data-to-noise, to infer noise density for anomaly distinction) non-trivial. A straightforward reversal of the flow (rFM) is considered, but this leads to significant issues.

2. **Overcome Theoretical Bottlenecks in Reversed Flow Matching (rFM):** The paper identifies two fundamental theoretical challenges that hinder the effectiveness of rFM for IAD:
 - **Non-Lipschitz Singularity:** The reversed vector field exhibits a non-Lipschitz singularity at the initial temporal boundary (t=0). This singularity causes an explosive accumulation of estimation errors, degrading IAD performance. The paper formally proves this non-invertibility (Theorem 1) and analyzes the Lipschitz continuity of the reversed vector field within a truncated time interval (Theorem 2).
 - **Centripetal Potential Field (CPF):** In high-dimensional Gaussian manifolds, the concentration of measure induces structured irregularities. This leads to the emergence of a Centripetal Potential Field (CPF) that steers trajectories away from Optimal Transport (OT) paths (Observation 2, Proposition 1). This mean-field effect, arising from temporal averaging, causes samples to drift inward toward the origin before reaching the target Gaussian annulus.

3. **Develop a Robust and Efficient Anomaly Detection Mechanism:** Motivated by the identified challenges, the research aims to propose a novel methodology that mitigates the initial singularity and leverages the observed CPF for effective anomaly separation. The goal is to construct a framework that is trainable, stable, and provides superior IAD performance.

4. **Formalize Density Proxy Estimation for IAD:** The work aims to formalize density proxy estimation within the context of rFM. This approach bypasses the need for exact likelihood computation and expensive Jacobian-trace estimations, allowing for more flexible and tractable models. An anomaly score based on the $\ell_2$-norm of the latent samples is proposed (Eq. 2).

5. **Achieve High Performance and Efficiency for Real-Time Applications:** Beyond theoretical soundness, a practical objective is to achieve state-of-the-art performance on various IAD benchmarks while simultaneously enabling rapid inference. The ability to perform one-step inference is a key goal for industrial applications requiring low latency.

In summary, the core motivation is to make flow matching a viable and performant paradigm for unsupervised image anomaly detection by systematically identifying and addressing its inherent theoretical and practical limitations when applied in a time-reversed manner, particularly in high-dimensional latent spaces.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around adapting Flow Matching (FM) for image anomaly detection (IAD) by reversing the flow direction and introducing a mechanism called Worst Transport Flow Matching (WT-Flow) to address identified theoretical challenges.

**A. Preliminaries**
- **Density Proxy Estimation:** The objective is to learn a mapping $\phi_{\theta}: \mathbb{R}^D \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^D$ that pushes normal samples $x_{tr}$ towards a target distribution support. The anomaly score $A(x)$ is defined based on the $\ell_2$-norm of the latent output, utilizing a Gaussian PDF as the target density: $A(x) = 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp(-\frac{1}{2} \phi_{\theta}^2(x))$.
- **Flow Matching (FM):** FM models the temporal evolution of a probability distribution $P_t(X)$ via a marginal vector field $u_t(x)$. By parameterizing a model $v_{\theta}(x_t|x_1)$ and defining a conditional probability path $p_t(x|x_1)$, FM optimizes $L_{cfm}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, p^*(x_1), p(x_0)} \|v_{\theta}(\psi(x_0), t) - u_t(\psi_t(x_0)|x_1)\|$. Rectified Flow (RF), a variant of CFM, is used due to its ability to generate straighter trajectories and eliminate noise terms.

**B. Non-Invertibility in Reversed Flow Matching (rFM)**
- The standard FM is for noise-to-data generation. For IAD, the flow direction is reversed (data-to-noise), where $x_0 \sim p^*(x)$ (data) and $x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ (Gaussian target).
- The paper re-derives the conditional vector field $u_t(x|x_0)$ for this reversed process. Critically, the denominator $\sigma_t(x_0) = t$ in the derivation of $u_t(x_t|x_0) = \frac{x_1}{t} - x_0$ vanishes at $t=0$. This leads to a division-by-zero singularity, meaning the initial vector field $u_0(x|x_0)$ is undefined (Theorem 1). This singularity causes explosive estimation errors in early time steps.

**C. Lipschitzness of the Reversed Vector Field**
- To mitigate the initial singularity, a small truncation threshold $t_{min}=T$ is introduced such that $t \in [T, 1]$.
- The marginal vector field $u(x, t)$ is re-expressed in terms of the score function: $u(x, t) = -\frac{1}{1-t}x - \frac{t}{1-t}\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ (Lemma 2).
- Upper bounds for the partial derivative of $u(x,t)$ with respect to time $t$ (Lemma 3, $O(D^2/T^4)$) and its spatial gradient with respect to $x$ (Lemma 4, $\max\{\frac{1}{T}, \frac{(1-T)D}{T^3}\}$) are derived.
- These lemmas collectively support Theorem 2, which states that the marginal vector field $u$ is jointly $\xi$-Lipschitz continuous on $\mathbb{R}^D \times [T, 1]$ for a specific $\xi$. This analysis demonstrates that when $T$ is sufficiently small, estimating the real vector field becomes challenging due to poor regularity.

**D. Local Worst Transport Flow Matching (WT-Flow)**
The core solution to the issues above is WT-Flow, which amplifies the observed Centripetal Potential Field (CPF) and refines the data manifold.

- **Centripetal Potential Field (CPF):**
 - The marginal vector field $u(x,t)$ at $t=0$ approaches $-x$ (Proposition 1, Eq. 12). This implies an initial centripetal contraction towards the origin, with magnitude proportional to the initial sample distance $d = \|x_0\|^2$.
 - This behavior arises from the interaction between the low-dimensional data manifold (Assumption 2) and the measure concentration of high-dimensional Gaussian targets (Lemma 1, Gaussian Annulus Theorem).
 - Geometric intuition: In the rFM, each training sample is matched to different Gaussian targets across "matching" processes. The weighted average of these conditional vector fields pulls samples towards the origin (Fig. 2B, Eq. 13). This is termed a temporal mean-field effect, a duality to the spatial mean-field effect observed in other flow models.
- **Degenerate Potential Wells (Solution):**
 - The strategy is to amplify this self-limiting CPF to mitigate the initial singularity. This is achieved by applying a normalization layer (LayerNorm or BatchNorm) to the input features.
 - Normalization layers perform mean centering and variance scaling. Mean centering ensures source and target means coincide. Variance scaling increases the initial radial distance $d$, intensifying the centripetal potential $|V(d)|$ (Fig. 4(C,D)).
 - LayerNorm is shown to project inputs onto a Gaussian annulus with radius $\sqrt{D}$ (Lemma 5), creating a "geometric coincidence" where source and target supports align radially.
 - Normal samples, thus, experience stronger energy dissipation and remain confined within "degenerate potential wells," while off-manifold anomalous samples are not subjected to this self-limiting mechanism, enabling separation.
- **Local Worst Transport Map:**
 - In the LN-based case, samples are projected onto the Gaussian annulus (source and target supports coincide radially). Optimal Transport (OT) between identical supports would ideally be an identity mapping, or tangential mass rearrangement.
 - However, the amplified CPF drives samples inward, often in directions orthogonal or opposite to the ideal OT direction (Proposition 2). This geometric misalignment inhibits distribution relaxation and traps normal samples, creating a theoretically grounded mechanism for anomaly separation.
- **Model Architecture:** A mini-U-Net is used as the vector field model $v_{\theta}$. A pre-trained Wide-ResNet50 serves as the feature extractor, providing inputs from its third residual stage. A basic Euler ODE solver is used for inference.

**Algorithm 1 (WT-Flow):**
1. **Feature Extract:** $X'_0 = \text{encoder}(X_0)$.
2. **WT Mapping:** $X''_0 = (X'_0 - \mu)/\sigma$ (LayerNorm or BatchNorm).
3. **Matching:** Couple $(X''_0, X_1)$ where $X_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_D)$.
4. **Sampling:** $X_t = (1-t)X''_0 + tX_1$, with $t \sim U(0,1)$.
5. **Training:** $\hat{\theta} = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}[\|X_1 - X''_0 - v_{\theta}(X_t, t)\|^2]$.
6. **Sampling (Inference):** $\hat{X}_1 = \text{ODE}(X''_0, v_{\hat{\theta}})$.
7. **Anomaly Score:** score $= A(\hat{X}_1)$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical analyses and empirical evaluations to support its claims.

**A. Theoretical Findings:**
- **Initial Singularity of rFM:** The conditional vector field $u(x, t|x_0)$ of time-reversed Flow Matching (rFM) is found to possess an initial singularity at $t=0$, rendering $u(x, 0|x_0)$ undefined (Theorem 1). This is due to a division-by-zero issue in its mathematical derivation.
- **Lipschitzness and Estimation Errors:** The marginal vector field $u(x, t)$ of rFM, when analyzed for $t \in [T, 1]$ (with $T>0$), is shown to be jointly $\xi$-Lipschitz continuous (Theorem 2). However, the upper bound for the Lipschitz constant $\xi$ is inversely proportional to powers of $T$ (e.g., $O(D^2/T^4)$), indicating that for small $T$, the vector field becomes highly irregular, leading to explosive estimation errors.
- **Centripetal Potential Field (CPF):** The marginal vector field $u(x, t)$ of rFM is shown to point towards the origin at $t=0$, with its magnitude positively correlated with the sample's initial distance $d = \|x_0\|^2$ (Proposition 1). This "centripetal contraction" tendency is attributed to a temporal mean-field effect arising from repeated matching processes on the same sample towards a high-dimensional Gaussian annulus.
- **Local Worst Transport Map:** The WT-Flow, particularly with LayerNorm (LN), projects samples onto the Gaussian annulus (Lemma 5). In this configuration, the centripetal gradient becomes orthogonal to the tangent space of the data manifold. This results in the learned vector field yielding directions either orthogonal or opposite to the Optimal Transport (OT) direction (Proposition 2), leading to a "worst-case transport" for normal samples while creating an anomaly separation mechanism.

**B. Empirical Results:**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** WT-Flow achieved competitive performance across five IAD benchmarks (MVTec AD, VisA, MVTec LOCO, MPDD, MVTec 3D).
 - On MVTec AD, WT-Flow (single-scale) achieved 99.05% mAUC for detection and 97.95% for localization, outperforming other single-scale flow-based methods and demonstrating competitiveness with leading multi-scale approaches.
 - On VisA, WT-Flow surpassed MSFlow (multi-scale) by margins of 1.46% (detection) and 1.11% (localization), indicating strong generalization robustness.
 - On MVTec LOCO AD, WT-Flow achieved 87.7% mAUC for detection, surpassing MSFlow by 5.6% and other methods optimized for logical anomalies.
 - On MPDD and MVTec 3D, WT-Flow significantly improved upon vanilla rFM, achieving 94.2% and 85.1% image-level mAUC, respectively.
- **Efficiency:** WT-Flow demonstrated superior one-step inference capability. It achieved a per-image flow latency of only 6.7 ms, which is approximately 49 times faster than multi-step methods and 4.7 times faster than state-of-the-art normalizing flow models, while maintaining high performance.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **WT Map Effectiveness:** The incorporation of the WT map (BatchNorm or LayerNorm) consistently improved mAUC across all comparative experiments (Table IV). LN-based WT generally yielded superior performance.
 - **Model Capacity:** Scaling U-Net base channels (NC) from 128 to 768 led to substantial gains (21.7% detection, 6.9% localization), indicating that increased capacity helps mitigate early-stage non-Lipschitz dynamics.
 - **Flow Steps:** Counterintuitively, single-step inference for WT-Flow often outperformed multi-step inference (Table VII), validating the geometric rationale of CPF at $t=0$ where discriminability arises from early-phase energy dissipation.
 - **Geometric Design:** Using a Gaussian annulus as the target (as in rFM/WT-Flow) significantly outperformed "Single Point" (origin contraction) and "Uniform Sphere" targets (Table VIII), confirming the importance of the high-dimensional Gaussian geometry.
 - **Normalization Layer Placement:** Placing the WT module solely as a standard normalization component before the U-Net input (std) led to performance degradation compared to its integrated design, emphasizing the specific intentional design of WT (Table XI).
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations of anomaly maps (Fig. 7) demonstrated that WT-Flow effectively suppressed false-positive patterns compared to rFM and MSFlow, producing fine-grained and robust detection results on complex categories.
- **WT Mapping vs. Standard Norm Layer:** Analysis showed that WT (LayerNorm or BatchNorm) introduces optimization resistance (higher loss), yet consistently enhances generalization (Table X). This higher loss is an intentional outcome, preventing normal samples from reaching the Gaussian hypersphere by subjecting them to stronger constraints from the potential field, actively countering the original Flow Matching objective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact:

1. **Theoretical Advancement in Flow Matching:** The work provides a systematic theoretical investigation into the limitations of time-reversed Flow Matching (rFM) for anomaly detection. By formally identifying and proving the initial singularity (non-Lipschitz continuity) of the vector field and the emergence of a Centripetal Potential Field (CPF), it fills a critical theoretical gap in understanding the behavior of flow-based models in reverse processes, particularly in high-dimensional settings. This deeper theoretical understanding can guide future research in designing more robust continuous-time generative models.

2. **Novel Anomaly Separation Mechanism:** The proposed Local Worst Transport Flow Matching (WT-Flow) introduces a novel, theoretically grounded mechanism for anomaly separation. By strategically amplifying the intrinsic CPF using simple normalization layers (like LayerNorm), the method creates "degenerate potential wells" that selectively constrain normal samples within the vicinity of the data manifold. This approach enables a clear geometric distinction between normal and anomalous data without relying on explicit, expensive density estimation.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness in IAD:** Empirically, WT-Flow achieves state-of-the-art performance among single-scale flow-based methods and competitive results against leading multi-scale alternatives across diverse and challenging IAD benchmarks (MVTec AD, VisA, MVTec LOCO, MPDD, MVTec 3D). This demonstrates a significant advancement in the accuracy and generalization capability of flow-based models for industrial anomaly detection.

4. **Exceptional Inference Efficiency for Real-Time Applications:** One of the most impactful findings is WT-Flow's ability to achieve superior one-step inference, with a per-image latency of only 6.7 ms. This high efficiency, dramatically faster than multi-step methods and other normalizing flow models, positions WT-Flow as a highly suitable solution for real-time industrial inspection, medical imaging analysis, and other applications where both high accuracy and minimal latency are critical.

5. **Expansion of Research Design Space:** By formalizing and successfully implementing density proxy estimation with unidirectional distribution transformations, the work expands the design space for deep generative models in anomaly detection. It demonstrates that effective anomaly detection does not always require exact likelihood computation, opening avenues for integrating more flexible and scalable deep learning architectures.

6. **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The research effectively bridges theoretical insights into manifold geometry, probability flow dynamics, and the practical challenges of anomaly detection. By translating theoretical problems (like singularities and potential fields) into actionable architectural designs (WT mapping), it offers a concrete example of how fundamental understanding can lead to practical breakthroughs.

7. **Addressing OOD Paradoxes:** The work implicitly contributes to addressing challenges related to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection, particularly the "likelihood OOD paradox" in high dimensions. By explicitly managing the geometric relationships between data manifolds and high-dimensional Gaussian targets, it offers a principled way to differentiate in-distribution from out-of-distribution samples.

Overall, this research offers a substantial step forward in unsupervised image anomaly detection, providing a robust, efficient, and theoretically well-founded flow-based framework that addresses key limitations of prior approaches and holds considerable promise for real-world deployment.
