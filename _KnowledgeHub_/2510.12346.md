# PolygMap: A Perceptive Locomotion Framework for Humanoid Robot Stair Climbing

> [!summary] Summary
> The PolygMap framework enables humanoid robots to climb stairs in unknown environments by constructing a real-time polygonal semantic map, achieved through multi-sensor fusion of LiDAR, RGB-D data, and IMUs for robust state estimation and foothold generation. Experiments demonstrated real-time performance on embedded hardware, with successful stair climbing in diverse indoor and outdoor settings and footstep errors as low as 11.4-12.1 mm for the Double Step gait.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with stair climbing due to reliance on reactive control, leading to conservative gaits and high footstep uncertainty, insufficient for sustained navigation in complex, varied conditions.
- Stair environments pose perception challenges like small treads, sharp edges, low texture, and light absorption, which compromise depth measurements and stable plane detection, exacerbated by robot body vibrations and sensor noise.
- The coupling between robot odometry and environmental mapping leads to accumulating local drift over extended movements, degrading the spatio-temporal consistency of perceived step boundaries crucial for precise footstep planning.

## Method

- Developed **PolygMap**, a perception-based locomotion framework that builds a real-time polygonal semantic map of staircases, leveraging multi-sensor fusion (LiDAR, RGB-D camera, IMU) for accurate plane segmentation and visual odometry.
- Implemented a multi-sensor fusion-based state estimation module using a Kalman filter to integrate proprioceptive sensing with LIO, yielding precise, smooth, and low-drift robot odometry.
- Designed a foothold generation module that constructs polygonal maps via anisotropic diffusion filtering and RANSAC, followed by layered erosion and candidate selection to identify optimal, safe, and stable foothold regions for bipedal gait planning.

## Results

- The multi-sensor fusion method successfully generated accurate and smooth state estimates, significantly reducing high-frequency noise and cumulative drift compared to individual proprioceptive or LIO odometry.
- Gazebo simulations showed efficient climbing (average **3.4**-**3.7** seconds per step) and high accuracy, with a maximum footstep error of **12.4** mm, while maintaining real-time plane detection frequencies of 20-29 Hz.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated robust stair climbing in indoor and outdoor environments, with the Double Step gait achieving footstep errors of **11.4**-**12.1** mm and foot trajectory tracking errors within 40 mm, though the Single Step gait showed larger errors (up to **33.4** mm) and increased collision risk in complex outdoor scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing LiDAR-based odometry (LIO) with proprioceptive observations through a Kalman filter significantly improves both the global accuracy and smoothness of robot state estimation, effectively suppressing high-frequency noise and cumulative drift.
> - Explicitly generating polygonal semantic maps of stair treads and applying layered erosion robustly handles perception noise and environmental variations, enabling precise and safe foothold selection for humanoid robots.
> - Real-time whole-body motion planning can be achieved on embedded hardware (NVIDIA Orin NX), demonstrating the practical feasibility of tightly coupled perception-planning-control loops for complex locomotion tasks like stair climbing.
