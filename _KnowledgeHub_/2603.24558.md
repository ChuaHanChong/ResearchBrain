# LensWalk: Agentic Video Understanding by Planning How You See in Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The LensWalk framework introduces an agentic approach for video understanding, empowering a Large Language Model reasoner to actively plan and control visual observations from raw video, dynamically adjusting temporal scope and sampling density. This method yielded over 11.5% accuracy gains on the LVBench benchmark and 6.7% on VideoMME (long split) with the o3 model, while significantly reducing frame consumption by 14-32x compared to some retrieval-based agents.

## Problem

- Video understanding is challenged by the multi-layered, high-density, and temporally unfolding information, making uniform exhaustive analysis intractable for finite cognitive resources.
- Traditional and advanced Vision-Language Models often rely on static, pre-processed information or fixed context windows, leading to the omission of crucial sparse events or inclusion of redundant data.
- Existing retrieval-based and agentic video analysis frameworks offer limited flexibility, operating on pre-computed indexes or predetermined workflows, and lacking active control over raw video observation or dynamic resource allocation.

## Method

- LensWalk implements an iterative ==reason-plan-observe cycle==, where an LLM Reasoner dynamically plans and controls a VLM Observer's visual sampling from raw video content.
- A ==multi-granularity observation tool suite== (Scan Search, Segment Focus, Stitched Verify) enables the agent to specify temporal intervals and sampling densities for targeted evidence gathering.
- ==Timestamp Anchors== and a global ==Subject Memory Table== are integrated to provide grounding and maintain coherence of entities and events across multiple observation turns.

## Results

- The framework consistently improved VLM performance, achieving over **11.5%** and **6.7%** accuracy gains on LVBench and VideoMME (long split) respectively when using the o3 model.
- LensWalk surpassed prior agentic methods on the VideoMME-Long benchmark by over **4%** accuracy and demonstrated adaptive efficiency by consuming **14-32x** fewer frames per query.
- Ablation studies confirmed the complementarity of the observation tools and the positive impact of Timestamp Anchors and Subject Memory on reasoning coherence and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively scheduling visual observations, similar to human cognition, is critical for efficient, adaptive, and accurate video understanding, moving beyond passive processing of pre-computed data.
> - The framework's observation tools facilitate a range of emergent, human-like reasoning strategies, such as progressive zoom-in, strategic reflection, and integrative verification, largely overcoming degenerative action repetition.
> - A plug-and-play design allows LensWalk to enhance various underlying VLM architectures without fine-tuning, demonstrating generality and practical applicability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video understanding presents a challenge due to the multi-layered, high-density, and temporally unfolding nature of information within videos. Traditional methods for automated video analysis, even those employing advanced Vision-Language Models (VLMs), commonly rely on static, pre-processed information. This approach typically involves sampling a video once into a fixed context window, which can lead to the omission of crucial sparse events or the inclusion of redundant information. While some efforts utilize heuristic frame or token selection to manage context length, these methods generally operate on a fixed, one-time sampling strategy and cannot adapt their focus or reallocate computational resources as intermediate hypotheses evolve during reasoning.

More recent advancements have introduced retrieval-based and agentic frameworks that aim to expand information as reasoning progresses. Reactive retrieval pipelines enable models to fetch pre-processed artifacts such as ASR transcripts, OCR data, or clip-level captions. However, perception in these systems remains largely bound to a pre-determined workflow and a fixed menu of video views, offering limited flexibility in deciding observation granularity or revisiting raw video content to resolve ambiguities. Similarly, video agents that allow a Large Language Model (LLM) controller to query over pre-computed video indexes offer increased querying flexibility but still operate on static representations rather than actively scheduling fresh observations from the source video. This results in a loose coupling between observation and reasoning, often leading to suboptimally planned computational budgets.

The LensWalk framework positions itself within this landscape by addressing the inherent disconnect between reasoning and perception in existing video understanding paradigms. It proposes to empower an LLM reasoner with direct, active control over its visual observation strategy. This approach aims to move beyond static or reactively retrieved visual evidence by enabling the agent to dynamically plan *how* it observes the video, including specifying temporal scope and sampling density. By integrating observation planning directly into the reasoning loop, LensWalk seeks to enable more adaptive, efficient, and interpretable video analysis that mirrors human-like purposeful information seeking and adaptation of focus.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties associated with analyzing the dense, temporal information found in videos. The extensive volume of data in videos can overwhelm the finite cognitive resources of any analytical agent, rendering uniform, exhaustive analysis both intractable and inefficient. The authors draw an analogy to human cognition, where intelligence adapts through purposeful information seeking, shifting observation focus (e.g., from broad peripheral scans to sharp foveal focus), and intermittently reflecting and verifying insights, all guided by an evolving reasoning process that shapes both intent and observational priorities. The core contention is that this principle of actively scheduled observation under limited capacity is fundamental for reliable and accurate video analysis.

Building on this, the key objectives of the LensWalk framework are:

1. **To bridge the gap between reasoning and active perception:** Current video understanding methods are limited by their reliance on static, pre-processed visual information. LensWalk aims to overcome this by enabling a Large Language Model (LLM) reasoner to actively control its visual observation, thereby establishing a tighter coupling between reasoning and perception.
2. **To implement a dynamic reason-plan-observe loop:** The framework is designed to allow the agent to dynamically specify, at each step, the temporal scope and sampling density of the video it observes. This iterative process involves the agent reflecting on the current question and accumulated evidence, planning an observation action, executing that observation to gather evidence, and then updating its understanding.
3. **To facilitate progressive, on-demand evidence gathering:** By dynamically controlling observation, the agent can seek raw evidence from the video as its understanding evolves. This contrasts with static, once-off sampling methods and supports an evolving chain of thought by allowing the agent to perform broad scans for cues, focus on specific segments for fact extraction, and integrate evidence from multiple moments for holistic verification.
4. **To enhance performance without requiring model fine-tuning:** A practical objective is to deliver substantial, plug-and-play performance gains on multiple model architectures as an agentic framework, without necessitating any model-specific fine-tuning.
5. **To unlock more accurate, robust, and interpretable video reasoning:** The authors hypothesize that enabling an agent to control its observational strategy is critical for achieving improved accuracy, robustness to ambiguities, and providing more interpretable reasoning traces, as the agent's steps directly reflect its information-seeking process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LensWalk conceptualizes video understanding as an active observation process, where an agent intelligently decides *where* to look, *what* to observe, and *how densely* to sample the video timeline at each reasoning step. The framework is built around an iterative reason-plan-observe cycle, comprising an LLM-based Reasoner (M_r), a VLM-based Observer (M_o), and a suite of observation tools.

#### 3.1. Overview of the LensWalk Architecture

The core components and their interactions are as follows:
- **Reasoner (M_r):** An LLM that analyzes the user's query, video metadata, and accumulated evidence. Its function is to formulate the next observation plan by calling specific observation tools with controllable video scopes.
- **Observation Toolkit (O):** A suite of versatile tools designed to leverage a core VLM (the Observer) for extracting information directly from a specified visible video context.
- **Observer (M_o):** A VLM that processes the frames sampled according to the Reasoner's plan and extracts visual evidence based on the guiding sub-question.
- **Memory Mechanisms:** Timestamp Anchors and a global Subject Memory Table are integrated to ensure coherence and grounding across multiple turns of interaction.

#### 3.2. Reason-Scheduled Video Observation

At each step `t` of the iterative process, the Reasoner `M_r` performs the following:
1. **Input Integration:** It receives the user query `Q`, video metadata `V_info` (e.g., duration, camera perspective), and the accumulated history of prior actions and observations `L_{t-1}`. Critically, it also incorporates the global `Subject Memory Table` to maintain entity consistency.
2. **Plan Formulation:** Based on the integrated information, `M_r` generates a fully specified plan, `a_t`. This plan is a tool call structured as `(o_t, q_t, I_t, ρ_{o_t})`:
 - `o_t ∈ O`: The specific observation tool to invoke (e.g., Scan Search).
 - `q_t`: A guiding sub-question to direct the observation.
 - `I_t`: Scope and budget controls, such as temporal interval(s), frame count, or frames per second (FPS), which define *where* and *how densely* to look.
 - `ρ_{o_t}`: Tool-specific arguments, providing further customization for the chosen tool.
3. **Plan Execution:** The formulated plan `a_t` is executed. This involves sampling frames from the raw video according to `I_t` and `ρ_{o_t}`, and feeding these frames, along with `q_t`, to the Observer `M_o`.
4. **Evidence Acquisition:** The Observer `M_o` processes the visual context and returns evidence `e_t` as a new observation.
5. **History Update:** The plan-observation pair `{a_t, e_t}` is appended to the historical record `L_t`. The explicit inclusion of controllable scope parameters (`I_t`) within each action ensures that the history `L_t` is a structured document with precise temporal tags, enabling the agent to track its exploration and target under-explored video sections.

#### 3.3. Multi-Granularity Observation Tool Suite

The framework provides a compact yet expressive toolkit of observation tools, enabling diverse compositions of observation contexts:
- **Scan Search:** Designed for efficient, broad discovery across a specified temporal interval. It partitions the interval into slices (by number of slices `n_s` or duration `d_s`), sparsely samples frames from each slice, and queries the VLM slice-by-slice. This tool returns per-slice summaries or relevance judgments, indicating potential locations of target events. It is suitable for initial exploration or cue discovery.
- **Segment Focus:** Used for focused, fine-grained inspection over a single, continuous time interval. It applies a dense sampling strategy to answer a precise sub-question. This tool is intended for hypothesis verification, attribute reading, or disambiguating visual details that coarser observations might miss. It also allows for re-examining the same interval with increased sampling density.
- **Stitched Verify:** Created to integrate evidence from multiple, non-contiguous video segments. It takes a list of segments, each defined by start time, end time, and a specific sampling rate. Frames from these distinct intervals are consolidated into a single batch for the VLM, supporting asymmetric sampling where action-heavy segments can receive higher frame rates. This enables the agent to verify complex event chains or comparisons (e.g., before-and-after) within a unified visual context.
- **Finish Action:** A designated action to conclude the reasoning process and return the final answer.

#### 3.4. Evidence Grounding for Long-Horizon Coherence

To manage potential ambiguities and ensure consistency across multiple observation turns, LensWalk incorporates two lightweight grounding components:
- **Timestamp Anchors:** During each observation, detailed timestamp anchors (e.g., "at 01:15-01:40") are inserted directly within the tool's visible frames as text. This prompts the Observer `M_o` to ground its answers with explicit temporal references, providing the Reasoner with fine-grained cues for subsequent planning, re-observation, or pruning of irrelevant sections.
- **Subject Memory Table:** After each observation, an LLM updates a global `Subject Memory Table` (`Sub_t`). This table, maintained outside the main reasoning history `L_t`, tracks persistent entities (e.g., people, objects) along with their summarized attributes and observed timestamps. It is inserted into the context before each reasoning turn, allowing the Reasoner to use canonical identifiers for consistent entity reference and to strategically plan future observations based on a structured knowledge base, thereby avoiding redundant information in the context window.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LensWalk's empirical evaluation demonstrated its effectiveness across long-video understanding and video reasoning benchmarks.

**Performance Gains:**
- **Overall Improvement:** The framework consistently improved the performance of various underlying Vision-Language Models (VLMs), including strong Reasoner-Observer combinations. For example, using the o3 model as both Reasoner and Observer resulted in over 11.5% and 6.7% accuracy gains on the challenging LVBench and VideoMME (long split) benchmarks, respectively, without any model fine-tuning.
- **Superiority over Prior Agentic Methods:** LensWalk surpassed the accuracy of previous agentic methods. On the VideoMME-Long benchmark, it achieved over 4% higher accuracy than leading approaches such as Mr. Video and Deep Video Discovery, which typically rely on extensive pre-generated captions.
- **Reasoning-Focused Benchmarks:** On MMVU and Video-MMMU, benchmarks designed for higher-level video reasoning, LensWalk boosted the accuracy of the specialized o3 model by an additional 2% and 3%, respectively.
- **Complementary Pairing:** The framework enabled effective complementary pairing, where using o3 as the Reasoner controlling GPT-4.1 for observations outperformed either model operating in isolation on MMVU.
- **Open-Source Model Applicability:** LensWalk demonstrated generality by boosting weaker open-source Observers like Qwen2.5-VL-7B (a 5.9% increase when paired with o3 as Reasoner). The choice of Reasoner was critical; while an open-source Reasoner like Qwen3-235B-A22B improved Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 4.3%, its effectiveness diminished with stronger Observers, suggesting that the Reasoner's cognitive strength significantly influences the quality of observation plans.

**Efficiency Analysis:**
- **Reduced Preprocessing:** LensWalk avoided the substantial upfront computational cost associated with prior methods that rely on pre-generating captions or indexes for entire videos, much of which can be irrelevant.
- **Adaptive Cost Management:** The framework exhibited an adaptive ability to manage observational cost. It resolved simpler queries with fewer turns and minimal token usage while allocating more computational budget (more turns and frames) to genuinely uncertain or reasoning-intensive cases. This resulted in total token usage comparable to single-forward baselines, but with significantly lower peak token counts per turn, mitigating memory pressure and throughput bottlenecks.
- **Frame Usage:** LensWalk consumed substantially fewer frames per query (14–32x fewer) compared to some retrieval-based agents like MR. Video and VideoAgent, while maintaining or improving accuracy.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Tool Complementarity:** The observation tools (Scan Search, Segment Focus, Stitch Verify) proved highly complementary. Removing Scan Search led to the largest accuracy drop (4.6%), highlighting its importance for efficient cue discovery. The absence of Stitch Verify (3.2% drop) and Segment Focus (1.9% drop) also degraded performance, underscoring their roles in causal analysis and fine-grained fact extraction, respectively.
- **Reasoning Coherence Modules:** Both Timestamp Anchors and the Subject Memory Table contributed positively. Adding Timestamp Anchors improved accuracy to 69.7%, and incorporating the Subject Memory further raised it to 70.0% on the VideoMME (long split), indicating the importance of precise temporal alignment and consistent entity tracking.

**Analysis of Tool-Call Behavior:**
- LensWalk exhibited emergent, diverse, and human-like strategies in its tool-call behavior, largely avoiding the "Static Repetition" common in prior agents. The six identified patterns were:
 - **Direct Inquiry:** Single-step resolution for straightforward queries.
 - **Progressive Zoom-in:** Starting with a broad scan and then focusing on promising segments.
 - **Scope Partitioning:** Dividing the timeline into segments, analyzing them independently, and integrating insights.
 - **Strategic Reflection:** Reassessing failing approaches and launching new, broader scans.
 - **Integrative Verify:** Jointly inspecting multiple segments to test complex hypotheses.
 - **Static Repetition:** Largely avoided, indicating effective recovery mechanisms.
- **Key Behavioral Insights:** The agent demonstrated difficulty-aware cost allocation, intuitively spending more observational effort on challenging problems. Emergent reflective strategies (like Strategic Reflection and Integrative Verify) replaced inefficient looping behaviors, leading to more stable and accurate outcomes. The quality of the Observer VLM significantly influenced the Reasoner's strategy and efficiency; stronger Observers facilitated low-cost Direct Inquiries, while weaker ones could lead to more complex and expensive observation plans due to ambiguity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LensWalk framework represents a notable advancement in the field of video understanding by fundamentally shifting the paradigm from passive, pre-processed analysis to active, reasoning-scheduled observation.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Active Perception:** The work highlights the critical role of actively planning visual observations, an aspect often overlooked in VLM-based video understanding. By empowering an LLM reasoner with direct control over *how* and *where* it observes video, LensWalk addresses the limitation of static perception and better aligns artificial agents with human-like cognitive processes of purposeful information seeking.
- **Flexible and Generalizable Framework:** As a plug-and-play framework, LensWalk is adaptable to various Vision-Language Models without requiring specific fine-tuning. This broad applicability makes it a versatile tool for enhancing existing video understanding systems.
- **Demonstrated Performance and Efficiency:** The framework delivers substantial and consistent performance gains across multiple challenging long-video and reasoning benchmarks. Concurrently, it achieves this with improved efficiency by strategically allocating computational resources, avoiding extensive upfront preprocessing, and managing token usage adaptively.
- **Emergent Cognitive Behaviors:** The analysis of tool-call behaviors reveals emergent, human-like strategies such as progressive zoom-in, strategic reflection, and integrative verification. These sophisticated behaviors indicate a move towards more intelligent and interpretable agents that can adapt their observational approach based on evolving reasoning needs, largely overcoming the degenerative action repetition seen in some prior agentic systems.
- **Advancing Self-Directed Multimodal Agents:** LensWalk provides a concrete step towards the next generation of self-directed multimodal agents. By integrating learning *how to observe* with *how to think*, the framework suggests that scalable video intelligence lies not solely in larger static representations but in scalable visual cognition.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Robust and Interpretable AI:** The ability for an agent to dynamically plan its observations could lead to more robust video understanding systems that can handle ambiguity and complex queries more effectively. The explicit planning and execution of observation steps also contribute to greater interpretability of the agent's decision-making process.
- **Applications in Long-Form Content Analysis:** The improvements in handling long videos could have a significant impact on applications involving extensive video content, such as surveillance, educational videos, sports analysis, and movie understanding, where efficient and accurate extraction of information is crucial.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** LensWalk opens new avenues for research into active perception, meta-learning for observation strategies, and the development of more autonomous and cognitively aligned AI systems capable of complex interactions with dynamic visual environments. This approach could inspire further work on agents that not only process information but actively manage their information-gathering processes.
