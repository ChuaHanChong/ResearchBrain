# LensWalk: Agentic Video Understanding by Planning How You See in Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The LensWalk framework introduces an agentic approach for video understanding, empowering a Large Language Model reasoner to actively plan and control visual observations from raw video, dynamically adjusting temporal scope and sampling density. This method yielded over 11.5% accuracy gains on the LVBench benchmark and 6.7% on VideoMME (long split) with the o3 model, while significantly reducing frame consumption by 14-32x compared to some retrieval-based agents.

## Problem

- Video understanding is challenged by the multi-layered, high-density, and temporally unfolding information, making uniform exhaustive analysis intractable for finite cognitive resources.
- Traditional and advanced Vision-Language Models often rely on static, pre-processed information or fixed context windows, leading to the omission of crucial sparse events or inclusion of redundant data.
- Existing retrieval-based and agentic video analysis frameworks offer limited flexibility, operating on pre-computed indexes or predetermined workflows, and lacking active control over raw video observation or dynamic resource allocation.

## Method

- LensWalk implements an iterative ==reason-plan-observe cycle==, where an LLM Reasoner dynamically plans and controls a VLM Observer's visual sampling from raw video content.
- A ==multi-granularity observation tool suite== (Scan Search, Segment Focus, Stitched Verify) enables the agent to specify temporal intervals and sampling densities for targeted evidence gathering.
- ==Timestamp Anchors== and a global ==Subject Memory Table== are integrated to provide grounding and maintain coherence of entities and events across multiple observation turns.

## Results

- The framework consistently improved VLM performance, achieving over **11.5%** and **6.7%** accuracy gains on LVBench and VideoMME (long split) respectively when using the o3 model.
- LensWalk surpassed prior agentic methods on the VideoMME-Long benchmark by over **4%** accuracy and demonstrated adaptive efficiency by consuming **14-32x** fewer frames per query.
- Ablation studies confirmed the complementarity of the observation tools and the positive impact of Timestamp Anchors and Subject Memory on reasoning coherence and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively scheduling visual observations, similar to human cognition, is critical for efficient, adaptive, and accurate video understanding, moving beyond passive processing of pre-computed data.
> - The framework's observation tools facilitate a range of emergent, human-like reasoning strategies, such as progressive zoom-in, strategic reflection, and integrative verification, largely overcoming degenerative action repetition.
> - A plug-and-play design allows LensWalk to enhance various underlying VLM architectures without fine-tuning, demonstrating generality and practical applicability.
