# Averaging Weights Leads to Wider Optima and Better Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) is a simple optimization technique that improves the generalization performance of deep neural networks by finding wider optima. It consistently outperforms conventional SGD across various architectures and datasets, achieving ensemble-like accuracy with a single model.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks trained with conventional SGD often converge to specific local minima, which may not be the optimal points for generalization.
- Ensembling multiple models is effective for boosting accuracy but incurs significant computational cost during inference, limiting practical deployment.
- The concept of "flat" versus "sharp" minima and their impact on generalization remained a subject of debate and lacked a practical method to consistently find flatter optima.

## Method

- ==Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)== modifies the standard training procedure by averaging model weights during the final portion of training.
- During the SWA phase, training continues with SGD using either ==cyclical== or high constant learning rates, periodically capturing model weights.
- The final SWA model is the simple arithmetic average of the captured weights, with a post-averaging step to re-calculate ==Batch Normalization== statistics.

## Results

- SWA consistently improved test accuracy by **0.5% to 1.5%** on CIFAR datasets and **0.6% to 0.9%** on ImageNet, outperforming conventional SGD across various architectures.
- Empirical analysis shows that SWA solutions are significantly flatter and wider in the loss landscape compared to those found by standard SGD.
- SWA achieves generalization performance comparable to model ensembles like Fast Geometric Ensembling (FGE) with virtually no additional computational overhead during inference, using only a single model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SGD with a high learning rate explores a region of high-performing networks, and SWA finds the center of this region, leading to more robust solutions.
> - Averaging weights in SWA effectively approximates the generalization benefits of ensembling (like FGE) with the efficiency of a single model.
> - SWA solutions are demonstrably wider and flatter in the loss landscape, providing empirical evidence that wider optima generalize better.
