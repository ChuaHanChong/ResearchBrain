# PhysiFormer: Learning to Simulate Mechanics in World Space

> [!summary] Summary
> PHYSIFORMER introduces a diffusion model operating directly on 3D mesh vertex trajectories to simulate mechanical systems in world space, outperforming autoregressive models in long-term physical consistency and generalizing to complex, unseen object configurations. The method achieves over 5x faster inference for elastic body simulation compared to a traditional physics simulator while preserving physical plausibility.

## Problem

- Video-based world models operate in view-dependent pixel space, leading to ambiguity in inferring underlying physical laws and inconsistent physical behavior.
- Autoregressive models for future prediction suffer from error accumulation over longer horizons, causing instability, loss of object coherence, and unphysical outcomes.
- Traditional physical simulators are computationally expensive, algorithmically complex, and often lack generalizability due to domain-specific optimization.

## Method

- **PHYSIFORMER** is a ==diffusion model== with a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== backbone that directly generates entire 3D mesh vertex trajectories in a single pass, rather than autoregressively predicting next states.
- The model employs ==factorized attention== across spatial, object-level, and temporal dimensions to efficiently encode object-aware and permutation-invariant information.
- It is conditioned on initial vertex positions and velocities, along with material properties, using coordinate-conditioned ==Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE)== and learnable ==register tokens==.

## Results

- PHYSIFORMER consistently outperforms autoregressive baselines, demonstrating superior maintenance of object rigidity, better momentum preservation, and significantly lower trajectory error over long simulation horizons.
- It robustly generalizes to complex, unseen object geometries (e.g., real-world meshes like fish and teapots) and object counts exceeding training maximums (e.g., **15 objects** vs. trained maximum of **10**).
- The model's inference time for elastic-body simulation (**6.4s** for **25 denoising steps**) is over **5 times faster** than the Genesis physics simulator for comparable scenes, which averaged **20-36s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - One-shot, full-trajectory generation with diffusion models effectively mitigates the long-standing problem of error accumulation that plagues autoregressive approaches in physical simulation.
> - Directly modeling dynamics in 3D world coordinates on meshes, coupled with structured attention, enables robust generalization to novel object geometries, counts, and mixed-material scenarios.
> - The inherent probabilistic nature of diffusion models allows for capturing uncertainty in unspecified physical properties, generating diverse yet physically plausible future trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the fundamental problem of predicting the temporal evolution of physical systems, a core component of spatial intelligence and world modeling with implications for robotics, computer graphics, and engineering. The broader landscape can be categorized into several approaches:

- **Traditional Physical Simulation:** These methods, grounded in physical laws, achieve high fidelity in motion and material modeling, contact resolution, and time integration. However, they are often computationally expensive, algorithmically complex, and lack generalizability due to domain-specific optimization constraints.
- **Per-scene Optimized Physical Dynamics:** Recent advancements couple 3D scene representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) or 3D Gaussian splats with learned dynamics or inverse-physics optimization. These typically involve overfitting per scene to recover deformable object motion from videos, often requiring dense multi-view capture or tracking supervision. Their applicability is limited by strong simulator assumptions, such as simplified spring-mass models or contact.
- **Learning-based Physics Simulation:** Deep learning has facilitated data-driven approaches. Convolutional networks have been applied to regular grids, while Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) offer a framework for particle-based simulations, representing particle states as node features and interactions via message-passing. Transformer-based models, such as Transformer with Implicit Edges (TIE), have adapted this by replacing message-passing with attention. For mesh-based representations, models like MeshGraphNets, FIGNet, HopNet, and HCMT incorporate mesh-aware inductive biases, though they often require explicit connectivity, topology preprocessing, hierarchical structures, or learned shape representations. PHYSIFORMER distinguishes itself from these by directly modeling dynamics as diffusion over raw 3D vertex trajectories, without relying on specialized contact modeling or explicit connectivity assumptions, and supporting multiple objects and materials.
- **Autoregressive Prediction in Visual Feature Spaces:** Deterministic autoregressive (AR) models have been adopted for future prediction, sometimes operating on feature spaces derived from Vision Foundation Models (e.g., DINO-Foresight). While effective for short-term prediction, these models are susceptible to error accumulation over longer horizons and may struggle to maintain long-term physical consistency, such as rigid body shapes.
- **Diffusion Models for World Simulation:** Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) have advanced visual content generation, but analysis indicates they frequently violate Newtonian dynamics and do not inherently learn physical laws from videos alone. Some approaches inject explicit physics signals into VDMs, but these require objective-specific training and remain view-dependent in pixel space. Prior work exploring diffusion over 3D representations often focuses on static objects or single-object animation from videos. PHYSIFORMER diverges by operating directly in 3D world coordinates, using meshes as a representation, and applying a diffusion transformer to predict mechanical dynamics. This approach aims to provide a view-invariant, geometry-aware world model.

PHYSIFORMER positions itself by reformulating the problem of physical system evolution in the world space of 3D meshes, moving beyond view-dependent pixel spaces or abstract latent features. It leverages the generative capabilities of diffusion models to predict entire trajectories in a single pass, addressing the limitations of autoregressive models and offering a more direct, physically grounded representation than video-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the challenge of accurately predicting the future state of physical systems, which is a core requirement for developing intelligent agents and realistic simulations in fields such as robotics, computer graphics, and engineering.

The authors identify several key limitations in existing approaches:
- **Ambiguity of Video-based Models:** Current video-world models, while successful in generating visual content, operate in view-dependent pixel space. Video is considered an indirect and ambiguous representation of a physical system's state, as it conflates geometry and motion with extrinsic factors like viewpoint, lighting, and occlusion. This indirectness makes it difficult to infer underlying physical laws and ensure consistent physical behavior.
- **Limitations of Autoregressive Models:** Autoregressive (AR) models, commonly used for future prediction, forecast the next state based on the current one. While inspired by the Markovian nature of mechanics, they suffer from inherent error accumulation. Small errors at each step compound over time, leading to significant divergence from ground truth, loss of object coherence (e.g., deformation of rigid bodies), and unphysical outcomes over longer horizons. Furthermore, a mismatch between teacher-forced training and self-conditioned inference (exposure bias) contributes to instability.

Motivated by the ubiquity of 3D meshes in applications and the success of diffusion transformer architectures, the work reformulates the problem directly in the space of 3D models, akin to physics simulators like MuJoCo or PyBullet. The core question is whether a neural network can reliably predict mechanical dynamics using 3D meshes as a direct representation of system geometry.

The key objectives of the PHYSIFORMER project are fourfold:
1. **Unified Neural Model for Multi-Material Mechanics:** To introduce a single neural model capable of simulating mechanical systems encompassing both rigid and elastic objects, predicting their dynamics directly within a 3D trajectory space.
2. **Effective Diffusion Transformer for Dynamics:** To demonstrate that a general-purpose diffusion transformer, enhanced with factorised attention across time, space, and objects, can effectively model such complex mechanical dynamics.
3. **Probabilistic Uncertainty Capture:** To show that this generative modeling approach can inherently capture uncertainty in unprovided physical properties (e.g., mass, friction). This allows for the generation of diverse yet plausible future trajectories from initial conditions, a capability not typically found in deterministic simulators.
4. **Addressing Autoregressive Limitations:** To empirically demonstrate that autoregressive approaches, even with similar input formulations, are limited by cumulative error and necessitate ad-hoc design choices to compensate. In contrast, PHYSIFORMER's one-shot trajectory generation aims to bypass these issues.

The overarching aim is to develop a view-invariant, geometry-aware world model that can support applications in robotics, computer graphics, and physical design by providing robust and physically plausible 3D dynamics prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed PHYSIFORMER model tackles the problem of animating 3D scenes in a physically plausible manner. The scene is modeled as a triangular mesh with `N` vertices, represented by positions `X(t)` and instantaneous velocities `V(t)`. Given initial conditions `X(0)`, `V(0)`, and contextual information `y` (such as object material properties like rigid or elastic), the objective is to sample from a stochastic process `(X(t))_t>0` that aligns with the underlying physics. The work emphasizes a one-shot trajectory generation approach, predicting the entire sequence `X` over `T` discrete time steps, in contrast to autoregressive (AR) next-state prediction. This design aims to mitigate issues like exposure bias and error accumulation.

#### 3.1. PHYSIFORMER Architecture

PHYSIFORMER is implemented as a diffusion model leveraging a Just image Transformers (JiT)-style objective and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone.
- **Diffusion Process:** During training, a noised sample `z_τ` is created by mixing data `x` (the ground-truth trajectory) and Gaussian noise `ϵ` according to a parameter `τ`. The model, a neural network `x_θ`, is trained to predict the "clean" data `x` from `z_τ` by minimizing a `v-loss` that measures the difference between the predicted and true flow velocities. At inference, the model generates a sample by numerically integrating an ordinary differential equation (ODE) from `τ=0` (pure noise) to `τ=1` (clean data), using a Heun integrator with 50 steps.
- **Encoding:** Input `z ∈ R^(T × N × 3)` (noised vertex coordinates across time, vertices, and 3D dimensions) is projected into a `D`-dimensional hidden space using a linear layer, resulting in `T × N` `D`-dimensional tokens.
- **Conditioning:** The model is conditioned on:
 - **Initial State:** Initial vertex positions `X_0` and velocities `V_0` are separately embedded into the `D`-dimensional space and additively combined with the vertex tokens.
 - **Material Properties:** A two-layer MLP maps material types (rigid/elastic) to embeddings, which are then broadcast to the relevant vertex tokens.
- **Structured Attention:** To improve computational efficiency and implicitly encode object-level information without explicit object tokens, self-attention is factorized across three dimensions:
 - **Spatial Attention:** Applied independently within each of the `T` frames, treating `T` as a batch dimension.
 - **Object-Level Attention:** Tokens are grouped by object, allowing attention within each object at each time step. This requires padding objects to have an equal number of vertices.
 - **Temporal Attention:** Each vertex attends across time.
 This factorization reduces the attention cost from `O(T^2 N^2)` to `O(T N^2 + N T^2)`. The interplay between global and object-level spatial attention enables the model to be object-aware and permutation-invariant.
- **Positional Encoding:** Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE) are used to inject spatio-temporal position information. Temporal attention utilizes 1D RoPE over time indices, while spatial and object-level attention employ coordinate-conditioned RoPE, making attention dependent on relative 3D vertex offsets.
- **Register Tokens:** Sixteen learnable register tokens are prepended to the input, replicated, and consolidated across factorized attention operations to aggregate and share global context, further enhancing information flow.
- **DiT Backbone:** The model utilizes a DiT-L backbone, consisting of 24 blocks, each with 16 attention heads and a hidden dimension `D=1024`, incorporating RMSNorm, AdaLN conditioning, QK-normalization, and SwiGLU activations.

#### 3.2. Dataset Generation

A synthetic dataset was generated using the Genesis physics simulator. Each scene comprises a 49-frame trajectory (`∆t = 1/240s`) of `N` vertices. Data includes initial positions `X_0`, velocities `V_0`, ground-truth trajectory `X`, face connectivity `F`, and per-vertex material types `M` (rigid or elastic). Scene parameters such as object count, size, shape, materials, and initial conditions were randomized. Collision dynamics were set to be near-elastic with minimal friction and standard damping. Four datasets of increasing complexity were generated:
- `D1`: 10k rigid floor-start scenes (1–5 convex objects, 4–20 vertices/object).
- `D2`: 15k rigid floor-start scenes (1–5 objects from 25 convex/10 concave templates, 4–88 vertices/object).
- `D3`: 60k airborne-start rigid scenes (1–10 objects, including angular velocity).
- `D4`: 20k elastic scenes (1–5 objects, floor-start and airborne-start).
All training scenes featured a single material type per scene.

#### 3.3. Baselines

- **Autoregressive Model (Φ_AR):** A transformer-based AR framework designed for next-timestep mesh vertex velocity prediction. Variants explored different context window lengths (L=1, L=4) and included noise injection during training (`Φ_AR_ctx4_noised`) to mitigate the train-test domain gap.
- **Transformer with Implicit Edges (TIE):** An AR transformer model that implicitly defines edges between particles (vertices in this context) through attention mechanisms. TIE was adapted to the mesh trajectory dataset, treating each vertex as a particle and exploring different interaction radii (`r=0.4`, `r=1.0`).

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

Performance was assessed using:
- **Trajectory MSE Error (`L_MSE`):** The mean squared error between predicted and ground-truth vertex positions across all time steps and vertices.
- **Rigidity Preservation (`L_rigid`):** Measures the average deviation of each object's predicted shape from a best-fit rigid transformation of its initial shape, computed using the Kabsch algorithm. This assesses the model's ability to implicitly maintain object rigidity.
- **Momentum Drift Ratio (`R_mom`):** Quantifies the deviation of the system's total momentum from its initial momentum, normalized by the ground-truth momentum drift. A value closer to 1 indicates better momentum preservation. This metric uses finite differencing to approximate vertex velocities and assumes constant mass for computational feasibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PHYSIFORMER involved comparisons against autoregressive baselines and analysis of its generalization capabilities and efficiency relative to traditional physics simulators.

#### 4.1. Comparisons Against Baselines

Experiments comparing PHYSIFORMER-L-10k (trained on the 10k rigid-body dataset D1) against `Φ_AR` variants and TIE revealed notable differences:
- **Autoregressive Limitations:** All autoregressive (AR) baselines demonstrated significant limitations due to error accumulation. Over longer prediction horizons (49 frames), AR models exhibited severe mesh deformation, loss of object rigidity, and substantial trajectory divergence from ground truth. Stationary objects would not remain at rest, and objects could escape the simulated bounding box.
- **Impact of AR Enhancements:** For `Φ_AR` variants, a longer context window and the injection of noise during training provided some improvements in stability but did not resolve the fundamental issue of error accumulation. Similarly, for TIE, increasing the implicit interaction radius (`r`) offered marginal improvements.
- **PHYSIFORMER's Superiority:** PHYSIFORMER, by generating the entire trajectory in a single pass, consistently maintained object rigidity and produced physically plausible long-horizon motion, substantially outperforming AR baselines across all metrics. It exhibited lower mean per-vertex position error (MSE), better rigidity preservation, and more accurate momentum prediction.
- **Generative vs. Deterministic Metrics:** While PHYSIFORMER's generative nature sometimes resulted in a higher standard deviation in MSE (as plausible alternative trajectories could diverge from ground truth due to minor contact differences), its overall physical consistency, particularly in rigidity and momentum preservation, was superior. Ground truth-conditioned rollouts for AR models showed low errors, indicating that their in-distribution learning was effective but their self-conditioned rollout suffered from compounding errors.

#### 4.2. Generalisation to Unseen Geometries and Object Numbers

PHYSIFORMER demonstrated robust generalization capabilities beyond its training distribution:
- **Complex Geometries:** The model successfully generated physically plausible motion for complex, real-world meshes (e.g., fish, teapot, bunny) with significantly more vertices per object, despite being trained solely on simpler mesh primitives.
- **Increased Object Counts:** It generalized effectively to scenes with object counts exceeding the maximum seen during training (e.g., 15 objects vs. a trained maximum of 10), attributing this to the implicit encoding of object identity via object-level attention.
- **Mixed Materials:** PHYSIFORMER successfully handled mixed-material scenes (e.g., rigid and elastic objects coexisting) even though its training data consisted of scenes with uniform material types.

#### 4.3. PHYSIFORMER vs. Physics Simulators

PHYSIFORMER presents a learned alternative to traditional physics simulators with several advantages:
- **Reduced Input Requirements:** It generates plausible motion using only initial positions and velocities, without requiring a fully specified physical state (e.g., density, friction, precise material parameters) as input.
- **Fixed Inference Cost:** After training, PHYSIFORMER's inference cost is capped, depending on the number of denoising steps and model size, enabling efficient rollout. For elastic-body simulation, PHYSIFORMER's inference time (e.g., 6.4s for 25 denoising steps on a single H100 GPU) was more than 5 times faster than Genesis simulator for comparable scenes, which averaged 20-36s.
- **Robustness to Challenging Scenes:** It showed an ability to produce plausible samples even in challenging scenarios where traditional simulators might fail due to contact-resolution artifacts or objects leaving the simulation bounding box.

#### 4.4. Ablations

- **Noise Scale in Diffusion:** An ablation study on the diffusion noise scale (`ϵ ∼ N(0, I) × noise_scale`) indicated that `noise_scale = 0.1` yielded optimal performance for raw coordinate signals. Smaller scales hindered generalization, while larger scales introduced jitter, potentially due to the strong conditioning on the first-frame state and coordinate-derived RoPE.
- **Object-Level Attention vs. Object-ID Embedding:** The factorized per-object attention strategy performed comparably to a baseline using explicit learnable per-vertex object-ID embeddings but showed better rigidity preservation. Crucially, object-level attention offers superior extrapolation to unseen object counts, as it does not rely on a fixed set of learned object identities.
- **Mesh Tokenization:** Per-vertex tokenization significantly outperformed per-triangle tokenization. Triangle tokens, despite representing richer local primitives, led to redundant predictions and poorer object rigidity due to implicit vertex consistency issues during training.

#### 4.5. Limitations

The paper acknowledges current limitations:
- **Fixed Trajectory Length and Resolution:** The model is currently constrained by a fixed trajectory length (49 frames) and mesh resolution, determined by the training data.
- **Physical Artifacts:** Operating with a general diffusion objective without explicit collision or object-consistency constraints can lead to occasional spurious contacts, interpenetration, and rare orientation discontinuities.
- **Long-Horizon Rollout (Chunked):** While explored, chunked long-horizon inference (using the end of one generated chunk as the start of the next) showed increasing rigidity error and visible deformations over extended periods, indicating a need for more robust initial-state conditioning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PHYSIFORMER represents a development in the field of neural physical simulation, offering a robust approach to modeling complex mechanical dynamics. Its key significance lies in several areas:

- **Novelty in World-Space Dynamics Modeling:** By directly operating on 3D mesh vertex trajectories in world coordinates, PHYSIFORMER addresses a fundamental challenge of physical simulation. This bypasses the ambiguities and view-dependency inherent in pixel-space video models and provides a more direct, geometry-aware representation than many latent-space or particle-based neural simulators.
- **Overcoming Autoregressive Limitations:** The successful implementation of one-shot, full-trajectory generation is a notable contribution. It effectively mitigates the long-standing problem of error accumulation that plagues autoregressive models, enabling significantly improved long-term physical consistency, rigidity preservation, and momentum conservation. This allows for more stable and plausible simulations over extended durations.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The model demonstrates robust generalization to novel object geometries, object counts beyond training maxima, and mixed-material scenarios without requiring specialized contact models or hard-coded physical assumptions. This adaptability suggests a potential for broader applicability in diverse and complex physical environments.
- **Probabilistic Dynamics for Uncertainty:** The inherent probabilistic nature of the diffusion framework allows PHYSIFORMER to capture uncertainty in physical properties not explicitly provided as input. This enables the generation of diverse yet physically plausible future trajectories, a capability valuable for applications requiring exploration of possible outcomes under unknown or variable conditions.
- **Efficiency and Practicality:** While initial training may be extensive, the inference process of PHYSIFORMER offers a capped computational cost. This can make it more efficient than traditional physics simulators for complex scenes, particularly those involving deformable objects or intricate contact dynamics, once the model is trained. Its ability to handle challenging scenarios where traditional simulators may fail due to numerical instability further enhances its practical utility.

The potential impact of PHYSIFORMER is broad, reaching various domains:
- **Robotics:** Provides a robust and efficient simulation platform for training robotic agents, testing control policies, and enabling predictive capabilities for real-world interaction, especially for tasks involving complex object manipulation.
- **Computer Graphics and Gaming:** Facilitates the creation of more realistic and dynamic content, enabling artists and developers to generate complex animations with physically plausible motion for various objects and materials with greater efficiency.
- **Engineering and Design:** Offers a tool for rapid prototyping and analysis of mechanical systems, allowing engineers to simulate the behavior of designs under different conditions without the full computational overhead or specialized expertise often required by traditional simulation software.
- **General World Modeling:** Positions coordinate-space diffusion as a promising avenue for developing general-purpose world models that are view-invariant and deeply integrated with 3D geometry and mechanics.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as extending to longer trajectory horizons, incorporating spatial compression, and integrating physics-aware objectives for contact resolution, suggests a path towards addressing current limitations and further solidifying the framework's capabilities and impact.
