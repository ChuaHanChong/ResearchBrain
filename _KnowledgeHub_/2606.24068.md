# ObsGraph: Hierarchical Observation Representation for Embodied Reasoning and Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> ObsGraph introduces a hierarchical observation representation for embodied AI that enables robots to intelligently reason about known and missing information, and actively explore unfamiliar environments. This framework integrates scene representation, task-aware information retrieval, and adaptive multi-scale exploration, leading to improved performance in embodied question answering and goal-oriented navigation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Autonomous robots struggle with effective reasoning and exploration within complex, unfamiliar environments.
- Existing scene representations have trade-offs, such as being memory-intensive, lacking explicit abstraction, losing fine-grained detail, or offering limited guidance for gathering missing information.
- A unified framework is needed that preserves sufficient visual evidence, allows flexible information access, and explicitly guides future information gathering efficiently for diverse tasks.

## Method

- Proposed **ObsGraph**, an observation-centric ==hierarchical scene graph== with three layers: object, view, and room, to represent environmental information.
- Implemented a hierarchical information retrieval process using Large Language Models (LLMs) to selectively access task-relevant data at varying abstraction levels.
- Developed an adaptive exploration strategy that structures options (room exploration, view refinement, frontier exploration) based on identified information gaps, guided by a Vision-Language Model (VLM).

## Results

- Achieved **61.5%** LLM-Match on EM-EQA, surpassing baselines by robustly preserving visual evidence and structural context for reasoning.
- Demonstrated superior exploration capabilities on A-EQA with **54.5%** LLM Match and **45.0%** LLM SPL, efficiently guiding VLM-based exploration while querying fewer images.
- Showcased generalizability with **72.7%** Success Rate and **51.5%** SPL on GOAT-Bench, performing effectively across diverse embodied reasoning and navigation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-layered scene representation, combining fine-grained object crops, contextual co-visibility views, and semantic room types, significantly enhances embodied reasoning capabilities.
> - Unifying scene representation, information retrieval, and active exploration into an adaptive loop allows robots to efficiently identify knowledge gaps and gather necessary information.
> - Dynamically structuring exploration choices based on the abstraction level of retrieved information enables targeted data collection, improving both accuracy and efficiency compared to unfocused exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in embodied artificial intelligence: enabling autonomous robots to operate effectively in complex, unfamiliar environments. This capability, termed embodied reasoning and exploration, requires robots to actively gather information, integrate it into a coherent scene understanding, and then reason over these accumulated observations to identify and acquire missing information necessary for task completion.

Current approaches to scene representation for embodied agents generally fall into several categories, each with inherent trade-offs:
- **Dense 3D Representations (e.g., 3D maps, radiance fields):** These methods excel at capturing rich geometric and semantic information. However, they are often memory-intensive and lack explicit abstraction mechanisms for task-conditioned information access. This can lead to scalability issues as the robot explores and the environment model grows, making selective extraction of task-relevant regions computationally challenging.
- **Scene Graphs:** These representations offer a compact and structured format, well-suited for reasoning by organizing environments into objects, their properties, and relationships. While they support efficient search at different abstraction levels, existing scene graphs often involve premature abstraction, potentially losing fine-grained visual evidence that may become relevant for unforeseen tasks. Some methods are object-centric, summarizing environments in terms of object properties, while others employ hierarchical abstraction.
- **Image-Set Based Memory / Task-Agnostic Memory:** More recent approaches preserve rich visual information by reasoning directly over subsets of task-relevant images or maintaining a collection of informative keyframes. While effective for retaining detailed evidence, these methods can lack explicit structural organization or may not provide adequate support for reasoning about *what* additional information is needed and *where* to gather it efficiently, especially for long-horizon or sequential tasks where objectives may change.

This paper positions itself by addressing the limitations of these existing paradigms. It aims to bridge the gap between structured scene graphs (which can lose detail) and image-based reasoning (which can lack structure) while also explicitly supporting adaptive exploration strategies. The paper contributes to the development of more versatile and efficient embodied agents by proposing a unified framework that tightly integrates scene representation, information retrieval, and active exploration, thereby improving both the accuracy of task completion and the efficiency of information gathering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework that enables autonomous robots to perform embodied reasoning and exploration efficiently and reliably in complex and unknown environments. To achieve this, the authors identify several key properties that an effective scene representation must possess, and these form the core motivation for their proposed system:

1. **Preservation of Sufficient and Compact Evidence:** The representation must retain enough visual and contextual evidence to support robust reasoning across diverse tasks. Simultaneously, it must remain compact and scalable, avoiding the memory-intensive nature of dense 3D maps, particularly as the agent's knowledge of the environment expands through exploration. The motivation here is to overcome the challenge where rich visual detail is needed but storing all observations is impractical.

2. **Flexible and Contextual Information Access:** The representation should allow flexible access to information at appropriate levels of abstraction, depending on the task at hand. It must be structured to leverage contextual cues (e.g., object co-visibility, room-level semantics) to accurately distinguish task-relevant information from irrelevant details. This addresses the limitation of representations that either force reasoning over vast amounts of data (dense 3D maps) or abstract away critical details too early (some scene graphs), making it difficult to extract precise, task-relevant information.

3. **Guidance for Future Information Gathering:** Crucially, the representation should enable the agent to reason about the *type* and *level* of additional information that would be most effective for progressing toward task completion. This directly tackles a recognized gap in prior work, where scene representations primarily focus on storing *known* information but offer limited support for actively inferring *missing* information or guiding subsequent exploration based on identified knowledge gaps. Many existing systems do not provide an explicit mechanism to structure exploration options adaptively based on the current state of knowledge.

Motivated by these requirements, the authors propose ObsGraph, an observation-centric hierarchical scene graph. This structure is intended to unify the process of scene representation, task-aware information retrieval, and adaptive, multi-scale exploration within a single, coherent framework. The goal is to allow robots to effectively reason about what is known, identify what is missing, and actively gather the necessary information to complete varied tasks efficiently and reliably.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, ObsGraph, integrates scene representation, information retrieval, and exploration within a hierarchical observation-centric scene graph. The overall pipeline operates in a loop: at each planning step, newly acquired observations update the scene graph, followed by hierarchical retrieval to identify task-relevant information. A termination check determines if the task can be completed. If not, exploration options, structured by the retrieved information's abstraction level, are activated, and a VLM selects the most appropriate option to gather missing information.

#### 3.1. Observation-Centric Scene Graph

The core of ObsGraph is a hierarchical scene graph comprising three layers:

- **Object Layer:** This layer stores fine-grained visual details by retaining object-centric crop images for each detected object. Object nodes are derived from RGB-D images and camera poses using a detection process. A visibility mask `M` tracks which objects are present in which views, defining edges to the view layer.
- **View Layer:** This layer preserves object co-visibility as contextual information. To maintain a semantically rich and compact representation, a specific view selection method is employed:
 - **Agglomerative Clustering:** Objects are hierarchically clustered based on their co-visibility patterns using a similarity metric `Sim(C_i, C_j) = |V(C_i ∪ C_j)| / |V(C_i) ∪ V(C_j)|`. The "child-aware view set" `V(C)` associates a view with a cluster if it observes at least one object from each child cluster, aiming to capture potentially rare but informative co-visibilities.
 - **Representative View Assignment:** After clustering, the hierarchy is traversed in inverse merge order (prioritizing clusters with fewer candidate views). Each cluster `C` is assigned a representative view `v_C` from its view set `V(C)`. This `v_C` is selected as the view most dissimilar to previously chosen views based on CLIP similarity computed over objects in `C`, balancing visual diversity and redundancy reduction.
 - **Incremental Updates:** The object hierarchy and representative views are updated incrementally as new observations arrive, integrating new objects locally and refining lower-level clusters while maintaining stability at higher levels.
- **Room Layer:** This layer represents semantic room types. When a view is added to the view layer, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) predicts the observed room types and the room affiliation of each detected object. Room-object assignments are made robustly by accumulating VLM-predicted room-wise probabilities across views via Bayesian update. Room-view assignments combine direct VLM predictions with object-level cues (a view is associated with a room if it contains objects assigned to that room) to enhance robustness against VLM noise.

#### 3.2. Reasoning for Information Retrieval

The retrieval stage filters and focuses on task-relevant information by exploiting the scene graph's hierarchy with a bounded query budget.

- **Hierarchical Traversal:** The system queries a Large Language Model (LLM) at each layer, selecting up to `k` (e.g., 5) relevant nodes. The candidate space for the next layer is restricted to nodes structurally connected to the selected nodes. This enables multi-branch reasoning without repetitive LLM calls.
- **Room-level Retrieval:** The LLM receives room types and assigned object classes, selecting relevant rooms based on explicit room cues or object-level hints.
- **View-level Retrieval:** A two-stage strategy is used. First, the LLM identifies task-relevant object classes connected to candidate views. Second, the minimum number of views required to cover these classes is greedily selected (up to `k` views), balancing semantic relevance with view redundancy.
- **Object-level Retrieval:** The LLM selects specific object-centric crops when fine-grained observation of an object is deemed crucial for the task.

This process yields relevant room types, views, and object crops, forming an interface for downstream exploration and decision-making.

#### 3.3. Reasoning for Exploration

Exploration is activated when the retrieved information is deemed insufficient for task completion. The exploration option space is structured according to the abstraction level of the retrieved information, enabling adaptive scope control. VLM-compatible visual inputs are constructed for each candidate.

- **Geometric Support:** An occupancy map is maintained for navigable space and target computation, along with a room segmentation map (derived by propagating SAM3 predictions from views to a top-down map).
- **Exploration Options:**
 - **Room Exploration:** Activated when rooms are retrieved and the agent is within one. Unexplored regions within the room are clustered, and target positions for observing these regions are computed. VLM-compatible inputs visualize these targets on projected views and the occupancy map.
 - **View Refinement:** Activated for retrieved views that might be insufficient (limited scale, insufficient context, suboptimal angle). Three strategies are defined: "closer view," "wider context," and "different angle." The VLM selects a view, a task-relevant region, and a refinement strategy. A preset function computes the target position using a SAM3 mask for the region.
 - **Frontier Exploration:** This is the default option, always active, based on updating frontier regions and observing them. A prompt is structured to select a new frontier only if it offers significantly greater potential than previously selected ones, preventing oscillation.
- **VLM Role:** The VLM makes high-level exploration selections (e.g., "room exploration" or "view refinement"). Precise target coordinates are computed programmatically (e.g., from precomputed positions or mask-based functions), as VLMs lack accurate coordinate-level reasoning.

#### 3.4. Termination Check

A VLM is queried to assess the sufficiency of the retrieved information and decide whether to stop or continue exploration. This decision is decoupled from exploration option selection to prevent interference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated ObsGraph across three benchmarks: Episodic Memory Embodied Question Answering (EM-EQA), Active EQA (A-EQA), and GOAT-Bench, demonstrating its performance in embodied reasoning and exploration.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Representation (EM-EQA)

- **Performance:** ObsGraph achieved an LLM-Match score of 61.5%, outperforming all baselines, including 3D-Mem (57.2%), Multi-Frame (48.1%), Sparse Voxel Maps Captions (38.1%), ConceptGraph Captions (34.4%), and Blind (35.5%). This indicates that the proposed hierarchical scene representation, which preserves both rich visual evidence and structural scene information, supports more robust reasoning than methods lacking either.
- **Efficiency:** The method used an average of 3.3 frames per episode for VLM queries, a slight increase of 0.2 frames compared to 3D-Mem (3.1 frames), but with a 4.3 percentage point improvement in accuracy.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Examples illustrated how each layer contributes:
 - **Hierarchical Retrieval (Room-level):** Reduced lexical exact-match bias. For instance, when searching for a "bedroom window," the system first retrieved the "bedroom" at the room level, then used contextual cues (e.g., a bed) to correctly identify the target, avoiding incorrect matches with windows in other rooms.
 - **View-level Memory:** Preserved informative co-visibility patterns. Unlike 3D-Mem, which prioritizes minimum views, ObsGraph retained views capturing rare but crucial object co-visibilities, allowing successful answers to questions requiring broad contextual understanding (e.g., identifying a "blanket" on a bed versus a "pillow").
 - **Object-level Crops:** Provided fine-grained evidence for object-state reasoning. When a view was insufficient (e.g., a distant toilet making its seat state unclear), object-level crops provided the necessary detail to determine the state ("closed").

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Exploration (A-EQA)

- **Performance:** ObsGraph achieved an LLM Match of 54.5% and an LLM SPL of 45.0%, which were the highest among all baselines. This includes improvements over 3D-Mem† (49.6% LLM Match, 37.0% LLM SPL) and other VLM-guided or question-agnostic methods like Explore-EQA (46.9% LLM Match, 23.4% LLM SPL). This suggests that the representation not only aids in retrieving observed information but also effectively guides VLM-based exploration.
- **Exploration Efficiency:** While storing slightly more informative views (12.97 from 44.48 observations) compared to 3D-Mem (10.94 from 39.76 observations), ObsGraph retrieved fewer images on average for VLM queries (2.34 vs. 3.26), indicating a reduction in VLM query load while maintaining useful observations.
- **View Refinement:** Qualitative examples demonstrated the efficacy of view refinement strategies in guiding the agent to better observe target regions (e.g., "closer view" for detailed inspection, "different angle" to resolve occlusion, "wider context" for broader semantic information).

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Goal-Oriented Navigation (GOAT-Bench)

- **Performance:** The method achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 72.7% and a Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 51.5%. These scores were the highest among all compared baselines, including 3D-Mem (69.1% SR, 48.9% SPL), and other modular or end-to-end approaches. This indicates the generalizability of ObsGraph across different embodied reasoning settings, including open-ended question answering and goal-oriented navigation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Representation Ablation (EM-EQA subset):**
 - Removing hierarchy and retaining only the view layer (R1) resulted in a drop from 63.5% to 58.1% LLM Match.
 - Replacing the proposed view construction with 3D-Mem's strategy (R2) led to the largest drop to 54.2% LLM Match, highlighting the importance of the informative view selection.
 - Removing the instance layer (R3) resulted in a drop to 59.0% LLM Match.
 - These results confirm that each hierarchical layer and the specific view construction strategy contribute positively to performance.
- **Exploration Ablation (A-EQA 184 subset):**
 - Removing the room exploration option (E1) reduced LLM Match to 52.2% and LLM SPL to 43.7%.
 - Removing the view refinement option (E2) reduced LLM Match to 52.7% and LLM SPL to 42.5%.
 - Both room exploration and view refinement were shown to contribute to accuracy and efficiency, providing intermediate exploration scales and enabling fine-grained verification, preventing unnecessary broad exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ObsGraph framework presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of robot reasoning and exploration in complex and unfamiliar environments.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Unified Framework for Embodied Intelligence:** ObsGraph offers a unified system that tightly integrates three critical components: scene representation, information retrieval, and active exploration. This integration allows these processes to operate synergistically within a single, task-aware loop, moving beyond prior fragmented approaches.
- **Hierarchical and Observation-Centric Representation:** The proposed room–view–object hierarchy effectively addresses the long-standing trade-off between the compactness and structure of scene graphs and the rich detail of visual observations. By retaining visual evidence and organizing it contextually, ObsGraph enables flexible, multi-scale access to information, which is crucial for diverse and dynamic task requirements. The observation-centric nature ensures that task-relevant visual cues are preserved, mitigating the risk of premature abstraction.
- **Adaptive and Targeted Exploration:** A central innovation is the adaptive exploration strategy, where the exploration candidate space is dynamically structured by the outcomes of hierarchical information retrieval. This mechanism allows the robot to adjust its exploration scope—ranging from broad frontier exploration to room-level investigation and fine-grained view refinement—based on identified evidence gaps. This targeted information gathering is expected to lead to more efficient and effective task completion compared to less informed or purely reactive exploration strategies.
- **Improved Performance Across Benchmarks:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate superior performance in both accuracy and efficiency across multiple embodied reasoning and exploration benchmarks (EM-EQA, A-EQA, GOAT-Bench). This indicates the robustness and generalizability of ObsGraph in diverse task settings, from open-ended question answering to goal-oriented navigation.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By providing a structured way for robots to reason about known and missing information, and then actively seek out what is needed, ObsGraph moves robots closer to achieving true autonomy in complex, human-centric environments. This capability is essential for future applications where robots need to act as versatile assistants, requiring strong perception, reasoning, and interaction skills.

**Potential Impact:**

The principles demonstrated by ObsGraph could have a broad impact on the design of intelligent robotic systems. It could enable robots to:
- More effectively navigate and understand previously unseen environments.
- Perform complex, multi-stage tasks that require gathering specific information over time.
- Respond to novel questions or objectives by strategically exploring to find answers.
- Reduce the computational burden of reasoning by focusing on task-relevant information at the appropriate abstraction level.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges that its approach relies on VLM reasoning for interpreting observations and selecting exploration options, which introduces sensitivity to potential errors in perception or language understanding. Future work could focus on:
- Integrating more robust geometric reasoning to complement VLM outputs and enhance decision-making.
- Extending the framework to dynamic environments where objects or scenes change over time.
- Scaling the approach to longer-horizon tasks that demand continuous adaptation and long-term memory management.

Overall, ObsGraph represents a step forward in creating embodied AI agents that can intelligently perceive, reason about, and interact with complex real-world environments.
