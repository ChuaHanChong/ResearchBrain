# VJEPA: Variational Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures as Probabilistic World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Yongchao Huang from the University of Aberdeen introduced VJEPA (Variational Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures) and BJEPA (Bayesian JEPA), probabilistic extensions to deterministic JEPAs. These models learn robust, uncertainty-aware latent representations of dynamic systems by filtering out high-variance nuisance distractors, maintaining high signal R^2 (>0.84) even under significant noise and enabling principled planning without explicit observation-level likelihood modeling.

## Problem

- Deterministic Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) lack explicit probabilistic semantics, limiting their ability to handle uncertainty in stochastic environments and for uncertainty-aware planning.
- Existing self-supervised learning methods (generative models, contrastive learning) struggle with modeling high-entropy nuisance variability or require complex negative sampling, consuming model capacity for task-irrelevant details.
- Current world models often model observation likelihoods or couple representation learning with task-specific rewards, hindering general, reward-agnostic learning and explicit uncertainty quantification.

## Method

- VJEPA was developed as a probabilistic JEPA formulation that models predictive distributions over future latent states using a variational objective, explicitly avoiding observation-level likelihoods.
- BJEPA was introduced as an extension that factorizes the predictive belief into a learned dynamics 'likelihood expert' and a modular 'prior expert' for auxiliary information like goals or constraints.
- The framework formally establishes VJEPA as a latent space dynamical system where representations serve as sufficient information states for optimal control, providing theoretical guarantees against representation collapse and for nuisance invariance.

## Results

- In a controlled "Noisy TV" experiment with high-variance nuisance, VAE and Pixel-Autoregressive models showed catastrophic performance degradation, with signal R^2 dropping to ~0.50 and ~0.58 respectively.
- VJEPA and BJEPA maintained high signal R^2 (>0.84) even when the distractor's variance caused the signal-to-noise ratio to drop to -2.2 dB, empirically confirming their ability to filter nuisance.
- Qualitative results demonstrated that BJEPA and VJEPA reconstructions successfully abstracted away high-frequency noise to track the underlying true signal with high fidelity, contrasting with noisy generative model reconstructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VJEPA's objective maximizes a variational lower bound on mutual information between past and future representations, causing it to act as a Predictive Information Bottleneck and prioritize predictable, task-relevant information.
> - Unlike generative models that act like nonlinear PCA and encode high-variance nuisance, VJEPA functions as a nonlinear Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA), inherently filtering out nuisance variability by focusing on shared predictable information.
> - BJEPA's Product of Experts formulation enables modular injection of task-specific priors, facilitating zero-shot task transfer and robust planning through latent Bayesian filtering by fusing learned dynamics with explicit constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of self-supervised learning, world modeling, and reinforcement learning, aiming to bridge gaps in existing paradigms by introducing a principled probabilistic framework for Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs).

#### 1.1. Limitations of Existing Self-Supervised Learning Paradigms

Historically, self-supervised representation learning has been dominated by two main approaches:
- **Generative Modeling:** Techniques like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) or autoregressive models (e.g., PixelCNN, GPT-like architectures) learn by optimizing likelihoods over high-dimensional observations (e.g., pixels, tokens). A key limitation highlighted by the author is that these models are forced to reconstruct—and thus model—all variability, including high-entropy nuisance details irrelevant for downstream tasks. This "nuisance variability" can consume significant model capacity and lead to representations that are not optimally compressed or task-relevant.
- **Contrastive Learning:** Methods like SimCLR or MoCo learn by discriminating between positive and negative pairs of augmented data. While powerful, they require careful negative sampling strategies and can introduce representational biases, often struggling with potential collapse or the need for large batch sizes.

Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), championed by LeCun, offer a "third paradigm." Instead of reconstructing observations or contrasting samples, JEPAs learn by predicting latent representations of missing or future data. This bypasses the need for observation-level likelihoods, explicitly biasing learning towards predictable, task-relevant structure. Early instantiations like I-JEPA and V-JEPA have shown strong empirical performance. However, a critical limitation addressed by this paper is that existing JEPA formulations rely on **deterministic regression objectives**, which mask their probabilistic semantics and limit their applicability in stochastic control scenarios where uncertainty estimation is crucial.

#### 1.2. Bridging Gaps in World Models and Active Inference

The paper also extensively contextualizes VJEPA within the domain of **world models** and **latent dynamics** in reinforcement learning. Current model-based RL approaches like PlaNet, Dreamer, and MuZero learn latent dynamics to facilitate planning.
- **PlaNet and Dreamer** explicitly model observation likelihoods `p(x_t | z_t)`, meaning they still incur the cost of modeling nuisance variability in pixel space.
- **MuZero** learns latent dynamics without reconstructing observations but relies on value and policy heads for planning, coupling representation learning with task-specific signals (rewards).
- **Active Inference** offers a theoretical framework where perception and action are cast as variational inference. While conceptually aligned with learning predictive latent states, Active Inference typically requires explicit modeling of sensory likelihoods `p(x | z)`, preferences, and policies, making it a full agent architecture rather than a self-supervised representation learning algorithm.

Existing JEPA-based world models (JEPA-WMs) have demonstrated the power of planning directly in representation space without observation reconstruction or reward supervision. However, their deterministic nature prevents explicit uncertainty quantification and formal guarantees for control under stochasticity.

#### 1.3. Enhancing Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)

The paper directly extends the JEPA framework by addressing its deterministic nature. It aims to endow JEPA with explicit probabilistic semantics, making it suitable for uncertainty-aware planning and bridging it with classical probabilistic state-space models and Bayesian filtering. This places VJEPA as a novel contribution that combines the "likelihood-free" advantage of JEPA with the probabilistic rigor of variational models, without the strong coupling to reward or policy optimization seen in other world models. It is positioned as a **self-supervised model** for **general representation learning** that can then be applied to control tasks, rather than a task-specific RL agent.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the limitations of deterministic JEPA formulations, particularly their inability to handle uncertainty in stochastic environments and their unformalized probabilistic foundations. The paper explicitly sets out to answer several foundational questions about JEPA:

1. **Implicit Probabilistic Model:** To uncover the probabilistic objective that deterministic JEPA implicitly optimizes and generalize it to explicitly handle uncertainty over future latent states.
2. **Dynamics and Control:** To formalize JEPA as a latent dynamical system where the learned representation serves as a sufficient information state for optimal control, crucially, without requiring reconstruction of high-dimensional observations (like pixels).
3. **Bayesian Connection:** To establish how JEPA relates to Bayesian filtering and Predictive State Representations (PSRs), and leverage this connection to inject structural priors or constraints into latent planning.
4. **Nuisance Invariance:** To determine whether introducing temporal structure forces JEPA to adopt autoregressive observation likelihoods (and thus model nuisance variability), or if it can remain "likelihood-free" and filter out high-variance nuisance distractors.

In addressing these questions, the paper's primary contributions are:

- **Variational JEPA (VJEPA):** A probabilistic formulation that explicitly models the predictive distribution over future latent states via a variational objective.
- **Theoretical Foundations:** Establishing VJEPA as a predictive state-space model, proving its representations can serve as sufficient information states for optimal control without pixel reconstruction, and providing formal guarantees against representation collapse.
- **Bayesian JEPA (BJEPA):** An extension that factorizes the predictive belief into a learned dynamics expert and a modular prior expert, enabling zero-shot task transfer and constraint satisfaction through a Product of Experts mechanism.
- **Empirical Validation:** Demonstrating VJEPA and BJEPA's ability to filter out high-variance nuisance distractors in noisy environments, which commonly cause representation collapse in generative baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in extending the deterministic JEPA framework into a principled probabilistic model.

#### 3.1. Variational Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (VJEPA)

VJEPA generalizes deterministic JEPA by replacing point prediction with **distributional prediction** in representation space.
- **Architecture:** It retains JEPA's context `x_C` and target `x_T` partitioning. A context encoder `f_theta(x_C)` produces `Z_C`. A probabilistic predictor `p_phi(Z_T | Z_C, xi_T)` models the distribution over future latent states (e.g., a Gaussian with learned mean and covariance). A target encoder `f_theta'(x_T)` (whose parameters `theta'` are an Exponential Moving Average of `theta`) defines an amortized inference distribution `q_theta'(Z_T | x_T)`.
- **Variational Objective:** The training minimizes a regularized negative log-likelihood:
 `L_VJEPA = E_x [ E_{Z_T ~ q_theta'(.|x_T)} [-log p_phi(Z_T | Z_C, xi_T)] + beta KL(q_theta'(Z_T | x_T) || p(Z_T)) ]`
 The first term trains the probabilistic predictor to match the target encoder's distribution, while the second term (KL regularization against a fixed prior `p(Z_T)`) prevents collapse of the target encoder. Crucially, this objective avoids any observation-level likelihood modeling `p(x_T | Z_T)`.
- **Collapse Avoidance:** Theorem 1 formally proves that VJEPA's objective naturally discourages representational collapse, provided there's sufficient target diversity and the predictive model is expressive enough. This intrinsic property differentiates it from heuristic-based collapse avoidance in other self-supervised methods.

#### 3.2. VJEPA as a Latent Space Dynamical System

The paper highlights that when context and target are explicitly time-indexed (`Z_t = f_theta(x_{<=t})` and `Z_{t+Delta} = f_theta'(x_{t+Delta})`), VJEPA induces a **latent transition model** `p_phi(Z_{t+Delta} | Z_t, xi_{t+Delta}, u_{t:t+Delta-1})`. This allows VJEPA to be interpreted as a **probabilistic state-space model** in latent space. A key clarification is that VJEPA is **sequential but not autoregressive** at the observation level, meaning it models `p(Z_{t+1} | Z_t)` without the computational burden of `p(x_{t+1} | x_{<=t})`. This enables belief propagation and filtering-style updates directly in the latent space.

#### 3.3. VJEPA for Control and Planning

VJEPA's latent state `Z_t` is cast as a **predictive information state** for control.
- **Predictive Sufficiency:** Theorem 2 formally states that if `Z_t` renders future task variables conditionally independent of history, then an optimal policy can be derived solely from `Z_t`, negating the need for observation reconstruction.
- **Latent-Space Planning:** Planning involves optimizing action sequences `u` to minimize expected cumulative cost `E[sum c(Z_{t+k+1}, u_{t+k}) | Z_t, u_{t:t+H-1}]` under the VJEPA latent dynamics.
- **Stochastic Model Predictive Control (MPC):** Algorithm 2 outlines a sampling-based VJEPA-MPC, performing Monte Carlo rollouts of latent trajectories.
- **MAP Control Optimality:** Theorem 3 shows that for Gaussian predictive dynamics with action-independent covariance and quadratic costs, planning with the predictive mean (or MAP estimate) is equivalent to planning with the full distribution, providing theoretical justification for simpler planning approaches under certain conditions.

#### 3.4. Information-Theoretic Foundations

The paper rigorously analyzes VJEPA through information theory.
- **Mutual Information Maximization:** VJEPA's objective is shown to maximize a variational lower bound on the mutual information `I(Z_t; Z_{t+Delta})` between past and future representations.
- **Predictive Information Bottleneck (PIB):** VJEPA inherently acts as a PIB, aiming to capture information about the future while discarding irrelevant details from the past.
- **Nuisance Invariance (Proposition 1):** This is a core advantage. Unlike generative models (which act like nonlinear PCA, forced to encode high-variance nuisance to minimize reconstruction error), VJEPA acts like nonlinear Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA). It prioritizes latent dimensions that maximize *predictive mutual information*, making it invariant to nuisance variability that is uncorrelated with the future signal.

#### 3.5. Bayesian JEPA (BJEPA)

BJEPA extends VJEPA by explicitly factorizing the predictive distribution into a **Product of Experts (PoE)**:
`p(Z_T | Z_C, eta) \propto p_like(Z_T | Z_C) * p_prior(Z_T | eta)`
- **Likelihood Expert `p_like(Z_T | Z_C)`:** A learned dynamics model, similar to VJEPA's predictor, capturing "what is physically possible."
- **Prior Expert `p_prior(Z_T | eta)`:** A new component that injects auxiliary information `eta` (e.g., goals, safety constraints, physical laws) representing "what is desired/must happen." This can be a learned goal prior or an analytical energy-based prior.
- **Training and Inference:** The likelihood expert is trained primarily with a VJEPA-like objective, potentially regularized by a structural prior. During inference, `p_like` and `p_prior` are fused via PoE to constrain planning to the intersection of physically probable and task-compliant states.
- **Latent Bayesian Filtering:** BJEPA naturally implements a Bayesian filter in latent space, where `p_like` performs the prediction step and `p_prior` acts as a "measurement update" from task constraints.
- **Gradient-Based MPC (Algorithm 3):** This allows differentiable planning where the cost function is the learned energy prior.
- **Modularity and Transfer:** This factorization enables zero-shot task transfer (by swapping `p_prior`) and mitigates catastrophic forgetting in continual learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation for VJEPA and BJEPA is presented through a controlled "Noisy TV" linear system experiment, specifically designed to test the models' ability to filter nuisance variability.

#### 4.1. "Noisy TV" Environment Setup

- **System:** A linear-Gaussian system where observations (`x_t`, 20 dimensions) comprise a low-variance signal (`s_t`, 4 dimensions) and a high-variance, predictable distractor (`d_t`, 4 dimensions).
- **Dynamics:** The signal `s_t` undergoes stable rotation, while the distractor `d_t` is a "sticky" random walk. A scaling factor `sigma` controls the distractor's variance, allowing it to overshadow the signal (SNR drops to -2.2 dB at `sigma=8.0`).
- **Model Dimensions:** All models use a latent dimension of 4, forcing a hard trade-off: either model the signal or the nuisance.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is measured by the coefficient of determination (R^2) of a linear probe trained to recover the true signal (`s_t` or `s_{t+1}`) from the model's latent representation. Noise R^2 is also tracked to see if models encode distractors.

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison

The results clearly demonstrate the superiority of JEPA-based architectures in filtering nuisance:

- **Failure of Generative Models:** The Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and Pixel-Autoregressive (AR) models suffer catastrophic performance degradation as noise increases. At `sigma=8.0`, VAE's signal R^2 drops to `~0.50` while its noise R^2 remains high (`~0.62`), indicating it prioritizes the high-variance distractor. Similarly, AR's signal R^2 drops to `~0.58`.
- **Robustness of Joint-Embedding Architectures:** All JEPA, VJEPA, and BJEPA models maintain high signal R^2 (`>0.84`) even at `sigma=8.0`. This empirically confirms their ability to effectively filter out the high-variance nuisance distractors, focusing on the predictable signal.
- **Stability of Probabilistic Variants:** While deterministic JEPA sometimes achieved slightly higher peak accuracy, VJEPA and BJEPA demonstrated superior training stability across different random seeds, confirming the benefits of explicitly modeling uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Filtering

Visualizations of latent reconstructions at high noise levels dramatically illustrate the filtering effect. VAE and AR reconstructions (dashed lines) closely track the high-frequency, noisy observations, whereas BJEPA and VJEPA reconstructions (solid lines) successfully abstract away this noise to track the underlying true signal with high fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions that collectively advance the field of self-supervised learning, world modeling, and control.

#### 5.1. Unification with Probabilistic State-Space Models

A major impact of VJEPA is its rigorous theoretical unification of the JEPA paradigm with classical probabilistic state-space models and Bayesian filtering. It formally decouples sequential modeling from the problematic requirement of autoregressive observation likelihoods. By demonstrating that robust temporal dynamics (`p(Z_{t+1}|Z_t)`) can be learned without modeling pixel-level details (`p(x_{t+1}|x_{<=t})`), VJEPA offers a scalable, computationally efficient, and principled foundation for model-based control in high-dimensional and noisy environments. This addresses a long-standing challenge in developing probabilistic world models that are both expressive and practical.

#### 5.2. Nuisance Invariance: A Core Advantage

The "Noisy TV" experiment provides compelling empirical validation for the theoretical claim of "nuisance invariance." This property is critical:
- **"PCA vs. CCA" Distinction:** The paper effectively argues that generative models, by minimizing reconstruction error, implicitly act like nonlinear Principal Component Analysis (PCA), prioritizing features that explain maximum variance, even if that variance comes from task-irrelevant noise. In contrast, VJEPA, by maximizing predictive mutual information, acts like a nonlinear Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA), focusing on shared predictable information between past and future.
- **Semantic Noise Filter:** This makes VJEPA a powerful semantic noise filter, ensuring that learned representations focus exclusively on the causal dynamics and task-relevant information, even when buried under overwhelming noise. This efficiency is paramount for building robust autonomous agents.

#### 5.3. Applications Across Domains

The VJEPA and BJEPA frameworks have far-reaching implications across various domains:

- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** VJEPA can enable robots to learn task-specific physics engines in latent space from raw sensory data without explicit reward signals. The `Z_t` representations capture object geometry and dynamics, while the predictive model anticipates action consequences. BJEPA’s modularity allows for **zero-shot task transfer** by swapping task-specific prior experts (e.g., a goal image or a safety constraint) without retraining the core dynamics model, greatly improving adaptability and sample efficiency.
- **Game Playing and Strategic Reasoning:** VJEPA can function as a self-supervised latent simulator for complex game environments. Unlike reward-dependent models like MuZero, VJEPA learns reward-agnostic game mechanics. This facilitates planning via Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) or trajectory optimization in the latent space, fostering long-term strategic reasoning and transferability to new in-game tasks.
- **Language-as-Action Planning:** The framework offers a novel perspective on language generation, framing it as planning in semantic space. Language tokens become "actions" that drive the system from an initial semantic state (`Z_t`) to a target state (`Z_goal`). This could potentially address issues like hallucinations and improve long-horizon coherence in large language models by decoupling reasoning from surface-form statistics.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

The author acknowledges several limitations and outlines promising future directions:

- **Expressivity of Predictive Distribution:** The current implementation of `p_phi` as a unimodal Gaussian may struggle with multi-modal future dynamics (e.g., a "fork in the road" scenario). Future work should explore more expressive predictive heads, such as Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) or latent diffusion models, to capture complex aleatoric uncertainty.
- **Optimization Dynamics:** The balance between predictive loss and KL regularization terms (`beta` and `gamma`) is crucial and currently requires careful hyperparameter tuning. Developing adaptive mechanisms for balancing these terms could improve training stability and performance.

### 6. Conclusion

Yongchao Huang's "VJEPA: Variational Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures as Probabilistic World Models" introduces a foundational paradigm shift in self-supervised learning for dynamic systems. By formally endowing Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures with probabilistic semantics, the paper resolves key ambiguities regarding uncertainty estimation and its role in control.

VJEPA offers a principled framework that maximizes predictive mutual information without the computational and representational burden of autoregressive observation likelihoods. Its inherent "nuisance invariance" ensures that learned representations are highly compressed and task-relevant, filtering out high-entropy distractors that plague generative models. This is demonstrated empirically in a challenging "Noisy TV" experiment.

The further development of Bayesian JEPA (BJEPA) enables a modular approach to world modeling, where learned dynamics (Likelihood Expert) can be combined with structural or task-specific priors (Prior Expert) via a Product of Experts mechanism. This not only facilitates robust, uncertainty-aware planning through latent Bayesian filtering but also unlocks capabilities for zero-shot task transfer and continual learning by disentangling environmental physics from task objectives.

In sum, VJEPA and BJEPA represent a significant theoretical and practical advancement, positioning JEPA as a robust, scalable, and uncertainty-aware foundation for building autonomous machine intelligence capable of reasoning and planning in complex, high-dimensional, and noisy real-world environments.
