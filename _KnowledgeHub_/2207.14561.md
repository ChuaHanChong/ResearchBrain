# Cyclic Policy Distillation: Sample-Efficient Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning with Domain Randomization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nara Institute of Science and Technology developed Cyclic Policy Distillation (CPD) to enhance sample efficiency and stability in zero-shot sim-to-real reinforcement learning for robotic control. This method partitions randomized domains, cyclically learns local policies with knowledge transfer, and distills them into a global policy, enabling successful real-world transfer for tasks like ball dispersal.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) with Domain Randomization (DR) suffers from high sample inefficiency and unstable learning when tackling complex tasks with broad parameter randomization ranges.
- The 'reality gap' between simulation and the real world makes zero-shot sim-to-real transfer challenging without further real-world interaction.
- Existing methods for improving DR's efficiency either require real-world samples, struggle with curriculum design, or are inefficient due to independent learning within sub-domains.

## Method

- The proposed **Cyclic Policy Distillation** (CPD) method divides the randomized domain into multiple sub-domains with similar adjacent ranges.
- It employs a cyclic learning process for local policies, incorporating a monotonic policy improvement scheme that dynamically mixes policies from neighboring sub-domains to enhance knowledge transfer.
- After local policies converge, they are distilled into a single global policy for zero-shot deployment to the real world, avoiding unstable simultaneous distillation.

## Results

- CPD achieved up to 6 times higher sample efficiency and consistently outperformed baseline methods in total reward across various simulation tasks (Pendulum, Pusher, Swimmer, HalfCheetah).
- In a real-robot ball-dispersal task, CPD demonstrated effective zero-shot transfer, successfully knocking down 13/14 juggling balls and 11/14 bead balls, significantly surpassing SAC-DR and SAC baselines.
- The method proved robust to different domain partitioning patterns (plane, edge, grid) and exhibited reasonable learning costs compared to other advanced multi-domain RL techniques.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically optimizing the policy mixture rate (`m`) between current and neighboring sub-domain policies improves sample efficiency and total reward, outperforming static or no mixing.
> - A cyclic transition order between sub-domains, rather than random transitions, is crucial for effective knowledge transfer and learning efficiency.
> - Distilling *converged* local policies into a global policy is more stable and effective for zero-shot transfer compared to attempting distillation with incomplete or concurrently learning policies.
