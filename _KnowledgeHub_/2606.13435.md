# GIVE: Grounding Human Gestures in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University developed GIVE, a method to integrate human gestures into pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without architectural modifications. This approach, which uses dual visual-semantic guidance, achieved an 80.0% success rate across manipulation stages in real-world human-robot interaction tasks, demonstrating improved robustness and generalization.

## Problem

- Contemporary VLA models are predominantly text-driven, overlooking crucial non-verbal cues like gestures, which convey a significant portion of human intent (estimated at 65-70%).
- Relying solely on text for inferring human intent often leads to misinterpretations and execution failures when language instructions are ambiguous or underspecified.
- Existing gesture integration methods either lack comprehensive semantic understanding (vision-based) or precise spatial anchoring (text-based reasoning), hindering robust intent interpretation.

## Method

- **GIVE** (Gesture Intent via Visual-Semantic Enhancement) introduces a dual-path ==visual-semantic== guidance mechanism to model human intent and integrate it into pre-trained VLA models without architectural modifications.
- The Visual Gesture Prompting pathway augments raw visual observations by overlaying hand skeletons and index-finger pointing rays directly onto the 2D image plane for precise spatial grounding of manipulation targets.
- The Semantic Intent Parsing pathway utilizes a VLM to translate visual gesture cues into high-level, object-agnostic textual descriptions for task semantics and temporal state transitions, which are then appended to original text instructions.

## Results

- GIVE achieved an **86.7%** Identify Success Rate and an **80.0%** success rate across all subsequent stages (Grasp, React, Handover) in real-world human-robot interaction tasks, significantly outperforming the baseline's **46.7%** Identify SR and **6.7%** Grasp SR.
- An ablation study demonstrated that the visual overlay strategy for incorporating gesture cues (skeleton and fingertip rays) achieved the highest Identify SR of **70.0%**, superior to token-based injection methods.
- The system showed robustness across unseen spatial layouts and generalized to new human participants, maintaining consistent identification success rates due to its explicit hand pose estimation and visual guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human communication is inherently multimodal, and explicitly incorporating non-verbal cues like gestures is crucial for robust human-robot interaction beyond purely text-driven systems.
> - Precise spatial grounding of objects is effectively achieved through direct visual prompts (like overlaid skeletons and pointing rays), while higher-level task semantics and temporal intent understanding benefit from textual descriptions generated by VLMs.
> - Architecturally efficient integration of new modalities into pre-trained VLA models can be achieved by augmenting existing input channels (visual overlays, text concatenation) without modifying the core network, preserving learned representations and reducing computational overhead.
