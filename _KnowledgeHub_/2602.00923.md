# SanD-Planner: Sample-Efficient Diffusion Planner in B-Spline Space for Robust Local Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> UCL researchers developed SanD-Planner, a diffusion-based local navigation planner leveraging B-spline trajectory representation. It achieves robust local navigation and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with significantly reduced training data, performing comparably or better than prior methods that use far more demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning methods for robotic navigation demand vast amounts of expert demonstrations, leading to high data acquisition costs and computational overhead.
- Generating smooth, dynamically feasible trajectories while inherently ensuring safety in complex and cluttered environments remains a challenge for learning-based planners.
- Traditional local planners often suffer from myopic decision-making and are brittle to perceptual uncertainty or inconsistent behavior in multi-modal situations.

## Method

- **SanD-Planner** uses a conditional diffusion model to generate diverse local trajectories, parameterized by ==clamped cubic B-splines==, using a multi-modal context derived from depth images, goal, and previous velocity.
- The B-spline trajectory representation provides inherent ==C2-continuity== (smoothness), local support for robustness against perceptual noise, and parameter efficiency for extended planning horizons.
- A decoupled ==geometric critic== module explicitly evaluates and selects the safest and most efficient trajectory from the generated candidates based on an analytical cost function (ESDF, path length, goal proximity).

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on the InternNav Benchmark using only **0.25%** (500 trajectories) of the training data compared to prominent baselines like NavDP.
- Demonstrates remarkable sample efficiency, obtaining a **55.5%** Success Rate with only **50** expert trajectories and recovering **76%** of its peak performance with **250** trajectories.
- Successfully performs zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, navigating complex 2D and 3D real-world environments, including stair-climbing, without fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding structural geometric priors, such as B-spline trajectory parameterization, into learning-based planners dramatically improves sample efficiency, robustness, and trajectory quality.
> - Decoupling complex trajectory generation from explicit safety assessment with a geometric critic simplifies the learning problem and enhances reliability and interpretability.
> - Augmenting the conditioning signal of generative models with temporal context, like previous velocity, is crucial for achieving consistent and stable behaviors in multi-modal navigation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robust navigation in complex, cluttered, and dynamic environments remains a grand challenge for general-purpose mobile robotics. Historically, classic navigation frameworks relied on a modular pipeline involving sequential perception, SLAM, and planning. While effective in structured settings, this modularity introduces latency, compounds errors from sensor noise and drift, and struggles with dynamic scenes due to its reliance on precise mapping.

This has spurred a significant shift towards **end-to-end (E2E) navigation approaches**, which aim to map raw sensor observations directly to actions or trajectories. Within E2E learning, two main paradigms exist:
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)**: Offers a principled way to acquire policies without explicit labeled data but is notoriously sample-inefficient and requires complex reward engineering.
- **Imitation Learning (IL)**: Bypasses DRL's challenges by learning directly from expert demonstrations. Recent IL methods have shown impressive results, often by aggregating massive real-world datasets to achieve general navigation behaviors. This trend, however, highlights a significant bottleneck: performance improvements often correlate with the scale and diversity of the training data, leading to substantial computational overhead and longer training cycles (e.g., NavDP leverages datasets spanning hundreds of kilometers). The reliance on data scale makes IL challenging for domains where data acquisition is inherently difficult, such as marine robotics or disaster environments.

SanD-Planner positions itself as a **complementary and crucial direction** within this landscape. Instead of primarily scaling data, it explores how to achieve high performance in **"data desert" scenarios** with limited expert demonstrations. The core innovation lies in injecting **structural geometric priors** into the learning process. Specifically, it leverages:
- **B-spline representation**: This moves beyond discrete waypoints or globally coupled splines, providing inherent smoothness, local support for robustness against perceptual noise, and parameter efficiency. This aligns with a growing interest in using structured output representations in learning-based control to embed domain knowledge and improve performance.
- **Conditional Diffusion Models**: These generative models are increasingly popular in robotics for their ability to capture multi-modal action distributions, which is vital for navigation tasks where multiple valid paths might exist (e.g., bypassing an obstacle from left or right). SanD-Planner applies this to the B-spline control-point space.
- **Decoupled Safety Assessment**: By separating trajectory generation from an explicit, interpretable geometric critic for safety, the method reduces the learning burden on the policy, allowing it to focus purely on mimicking expert trajectory distributions. This contrasts with end-to-end approaches that might attempt to learn safety implicitly, which can be brittle under distribution shifts.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The paper contributes to the broader effort of bridging the simulation-to-real-world gap through techniques like domain randomization and demonstrates zero-shot transfer, a critical step for practical robot deployment.

In essence, SanD-Planner addresses the pressing need for **sample-efficient and robust learning-based navigation** by intelligently combining advanced generative models (diffusion policies) with domain-specific geometric priors (B-splines) and an explicit safety mechanism, challenging the "data-hungry" paradigm prevalent in much of current IL research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SanD-Planner research are driven by fundamental bottlenecks in generating reliable local plans for mobile robots, especially in highly cluttered and dynamic environments:

1. **Overcome Data Scarcity and Improve Sample Efficiency:** The most significant motivation is to tackle the challenge of requiring vast amounts of expert demonstrations for imitation learning (IL). Current state-of-the-art IL methods often rely on hundreds of thousands of trajectories, which are expensive and time-consuming to acquire, particularly in real-world scenarios or specialized domains (e.g., marine robotics). SanD-Planner aims to achieve high-performance local navigation with significantly fewer expert demonstrations (a "data desert" approach).

2. **Enhance Learning Efficiency and Reduce Computational Overhead:** Directly related to data scale, large datasets translate to extensive training times and substantial computational resources. The authors seek a method that can learn effectively from limited data, thereby reducing the computational budget and accelerating the development cycle for robotic systems.

3. **Ensure Smooth and Feasible Trajectory Generation:** For robust robot control and interaction with the physical world, generated trajectories must be smooth (C2-continuous in position, velocity, and acceleration) and dynamically feasible. Traditional IL methods often struggle to inherently guarantee these properties, sometimes leading to jerky or untrackable paths.

4. **Improve Robustness to Perceptual Uncertainty and Myopic Planning:** Mobile robots operate with noisy, partial, and often occluded sensor data, especially over longer ranges. This uncertainty can destabilize planning. Additionally, many local planners suffer from "myopia," only considering a short-term horizon, which can lead to suboptimal or unsafe paths in complex environments.

5. **Decouple Trajectory Generation from Safety Assessment:** Learning complex distributions of expert behaviors and simultaneously satisfying strict safety constraints within a single policy can be excessively difficult. The authors are motivated to simplify the learning objective by separating these concerns.

To address these motivations, SanD-Planner proposes several key design choices:

- **B-Spline Trajectory Parameterization:** This is central to achieving sample efficiency and robustness.
 - **Parameter Efficiency:** B-splines represent long-horizon trajectories with a compact, fixed set of control points, avoiding the linear scaling of output dimensions seen in waypoint-based methods and mitigating myopia.
 - **Inherent Smoothness:** Cubic B-splines are C2-continuous by construction, embedding smoothness as an inductive bias rather than a learned feature, simplifying the policy's task.
 - **Representation-Level Robustness:** The local support and convex hull properties of B-splines naturally isolate the impact of perceptual noise/occlusions in far-field regions, preventing them from destabilizing immediate actions, and provide a strict bound on prediction errors.
- **Conditional Diffusion Model:** To capture the multi-modal nature of feasible navigation paths and generate diverse yet plausible candidates.
- **Explicit Geometric Critic:** To evaluate safety and efficiency, making feasibility assessment interpretable and robust without relying on learned value functions that might fail under distribution shifts.
- **Temporal Consistency via Velocity Token:** To prevent oscillatory behavior and mode-switching in multi-modal environments.
- **Domain Randomization:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap effectively for zero-shot transfer.

By integrating these elements, SanD-Planner aims to provide a robust, sample-efficient, and practically deployable local planning solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SanD-Planner adopts a two-stage **generate-and-select pipeline** designed for sample efficiency and robustness in mapless local navigation.

#### 3.1. Perception and Condition Encoding

The robot perceives its environment through a short history of four depth images ($O_t$). It also receives a 3D relative goal ($g_t$) and a motion context term ($v_{prev_t}$) encoding the heading direction from the previously executed plan.
- **Depth Image Encoding:** Each depth frame is processed by a lightweight ResNet-18 backbone. Instead of a final classification layer, spatial feature maps ($F \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}$) from an intermediate layer are extracted to preserve fine-grained geometric details crucial for obstacle avoidance. These features are flattened into visual tokens, augmented with 2D positional embeddings and learnable temporal embeddings.
- **Goal and Velocity Encoding:** The 3D relative goal ($g_t$) and previous velocity ($v_{prev_t}$) are projected into the same latent dimension $D$ using dedicated Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), yielding goal and velocity tokens. A learnable null token is used if historical velocity is unavailable.
- **Multi-modal Context Fusion:** All tokens (visual, goal, velocity) are concatenated into a sequence and aggregated by a two-layer Transformer encoder, producing a unified multi-modal context $C_t$. This $C_t$ conditions the subsequent trajectory generation.

#### 3.2. Trajectory Parameterization via B-Spline Control Points

The core of SanD-Planner's efficiency and robustness lies in its trajectory representation:
- **Clamped Cubic B-Splines:** Each local trajectory $\tau_t$ is parameterized as a clamped cubic B-spline using a fixed set of $N=8$ control points ($Q_t = \{Q_{t,i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$) in the robot frame. The first control point $Q_{t,0}$ is anchored at the robot's origin (0,0,0).
- **Advantages over Alternatives:** The paper explicitly compares B-splines against discrete waypoints and interpolating cubic splines, highlighting:
 - **Parameter Efficiency:** B-splines provide a compact, fixed-dimensional output for an extended planning horizon (approx. 6m), avoiding myopia and simplifying the diffusion process.
 - **Inherent Smoothness (C2-continuity):** Smoothness is a structural property of B-splines, not something the policy needs to learn from data, simplifying the learning objective.
 - **Representation-Level Robustness:**
 - **Local Support:** Each B-spline segment depends only on a local subset of control points. This means noise or errors in far-field control points (due to perceptual uncertainty) do not propagate to and destabilize the immediate execution segment.
 - **Convex Hull Property:** Ensures that the trajectory always lies within the convex hull of its control points, preventing prediction errors from amplifying into large path deviations, a common issue with interpolating splines (Runge's phenomenon).

#### 3.3. Diffusion Policy for Local Trajectory Generation

- **Conditional DDPM:** A conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) is trained to model the distribution $p_\theta(Q_t | C_t)$ of expert control points. The expert control points ($\bar{Q}_t$) are obtained by fitting a B-spline to ground-truth expert trajectories.
- **Training:** The model is trained using the standard v-prediction loss, where a denoiser (a conditional 1D U-Net with cross-attention to $C_t$ and timestep embeddings) learns to predict the diffusion target.
- **Inference:** During planning, the diffusion policy starts from Gaussian noise and performs $S$ reverse denoising steps to sample $K$ candidate B-spline control-point sets. These are then mapped to continuous trajectories.
- **Temporal Consistency:** To prevent inconsistent plan switching (e.g., oscillating between left/right bypasses), the conditioning signal is augmented with the velocity token $v_{prev_t}$. This token represents the normalized heading direction of the previously selected plan, guiding the diffusion model to generate candidates consistent with the robot's ongoing motion. During training, $v_{prev_t}$ is derived from expert control points to stabilize learning.

#### 3.4. Safety-Aware Candidate Selection via the Critic Module

To ensure safety without burdening the generative policy, a generate-and-select approach is used with an explicit geometric critic:
- **Candidate Evaluation:** The diffusion policy samples $K$ candidate trajectories. Each candidate $\tau_t^{(k)}$ is discretized into $M$ waypoints.
- **Cost Function:** An analytical cost function $J(\tau)$ is minimized to select the optimal plan $\tau_t^*$. This function combines three terms:
 - **Discounted Safety Cost ($J_{esdf}$):** An ESDF (Euclidean Signed Distance Field) map is constructed from the current depth image. This term penalizes clearance violations, with a temporal discount factor ($\gamma$) emphasizing safety in the near-horizon segment.
 - **Path Efficiency Cost ($J_{len}$):** Penalizes the total arc length of the trajectory to encourage shorter paths.
 - **Goal Reaching Cost ($J_{goal}$):** Penalizes the terminal waypoint's distance to the local goal $g_t$.
- **Selection:** The trajectory with the lowest total cost $J$ is selected for execution. The initial heading velocity of this selected trajectory is then fed back as $v_{prev_{t+1}}$ for the next planning step.

#### 3.5. Training and Sim-to-Real Considerations

- **Dataset:** Training data consists of 500 navigation episodes collected in simulation across 10 diverse environments (Matterport3D, Gazebo with multi-level structures/stairs). Sub-trajectories are randomly sampled and fitted with B-splines to maximize data efficiency.
- **Domain Randomization:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap, observations are subjected to:
 - **Temporal Latency:** Mimicking communication delays by freezing or replicating frames.
 - **Sensor Noise:** Emulating Intel RealSense D435 characteristics, including distance-dependent axial noise, pixel dropouts, stereo-occlusion bands, and speckle noise. This encourages the policy to learn robust geometric cues.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The model is trained quickly (approx. 5 hours on an RTX4080) and operates at approximately 8Hz during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors convincingly demonstrate the efficacy and advantages of SanD-Planner, addressing the research questions posed.

1. **Quantitative Performance Comparison (Q1):**
 - SanD-Planner achieves **state-of-the-art (SoTA) or competitive performance** on the InternNav Benchmark, despite being trained on only 500 trajectories – approximately **0.25% of the demonstration scale** used by NavDP, a prominent baseline.
 - On the realistic **InternScenes** environment, SanD-Planner significantly **outperforms NavDP** with a 72.0% Success Rate (SR) and 63.7% Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), compared to NavDP's 65.7% SR and 60.7% SPL.
 - In **ClutteredEnv**, SanD-Planner achieves the **highest SR of 90.1%**, while maintaining a competitive SPL of 84.0% (slightly lower than NavDP's 87.7% SPL, which the authors attribute to more conservative B-spline paths and data limitations).
 - Crucially, SanD-Planner achieves this using **depth observations alone**, outperforming ViPlanner which uses both depth and semantics but shows brittleness to semantic distribution shifts (e.g., lower performance in ClutteredEnv where primitive obstacles lack clear semantic categories). This suggests depth provides sufficient geometric cues.

2. **Sample Efficiency and Data Scaling (Q2):**
 - The model exhibits **monotonic performance gains** with increasing numbers of expert trajectories, confirming its ability to leverage more data.
 - It demonstrates remarkable efficiency even in **data-scarce regimes**, achieving a non-trivial 55.5% SR with only 50 expert trajectories (10% of the dataset).
 - With 250 trajectories (50% data), it reaches 65.0% SR, recovering 76% of its peak performance, showing that fundamental safe behaviors are learned quickly.
 - The SPL metric shows continuous improvement up to the full dataset, indicating that larger datasets enable the planner to learn more efficient bypass strategies and minimize detours.

3. **Ablation Study on Trajectory Representation (Q3):**
 - The study systematically investigates discrete waypoints, interpolating cubic splines, and B-spline representations.
 - **B-splines consistently outperform** the alternatives in both SR and SPL across various environments.
 - **Discrete waypoints** suffer from myopic behavior and lower efficiency.
 - **Interpolating cubic splines** perform better than waypoints but are prone to propagating prediction errors across the entire path due to their global coupling, leading to instability (Runge's phenomenon).
 - **B-splines' local support** and convex hull property prove critical, isolating far-field uncertainty and ensuring near-field stability and better preservation of the global path shape.

4. **Ablation Study on Velocity Token for Temporal Consistency (Q4):**
 - The **velocity token ($v_{prev_t}$) is vital for planning consistency**. Removing it leads to a consistent performance drop.
 - Without this conditioning, the diffusion model generates more dispersed, high-variance candidates in geometrically symmetric situations (e.g., pillars), causing the critic to frequently switch between conflicting bypass modes. This results in oscillatory steering and collisions.
 - The $v_{prev_t}$ condition effectively regularizes the generative process, yielding concentrated and temporally consistent candidate sets, ensuring stable headings and safe navigation.

5. **Real-world Experiments (Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Validation) (Q5):**
 - SanD-Planner successfully demonstrates **zero-shot transferability** to a **Unitree Go2 quadruped robot** in diverse real-world environments without any fine-tuning or domain adaptation.
 - It navigates **2D and 3D scenes**, including narrow mazes, cluttered offices, outdoor environments with challenging lighting, and **stair-climbing tasks**.
 - The robot generates smooth, collision-avoiding trajectories, confirming the effectiveness and robustness of the method in realistic, unseen conditions, despite the inherent challenges of real-world depth sensing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SanD-Planner presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic navigation and learning-based control, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Breaking the Data Dependency Barrier in Imitation Learning:** The most profound impact is demonstrating that high-performance local navigation can be achieved with dramatically reduced expert data – a mere 0.25% of what state-of-the-art baselines require. This is critical for practical robotics where data collection is often expensive, time-consuming, or hazardous. It makes IL-based navigation accessible to domains previously hindered by data scarcity (e.g., marine robotics, disaster response, planetary exploration). This efficiency has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous systems.

2. **Validation of Structural Priors and Decoupled Design:** The work powerfully validates the philosophy of embedding strong geometric inductive biases (B-splines) into learning-based systems and decoupling complex generative modeling from strict safety verification.
 - **B-splines' Role:** The inherent smoothness, local support, and bounded error properties of B-splines are shown to be fundamental for robustness against perceptual uncertainty and for reducing the learning burden, offering a compelling alternative to "end-to-end everything" approaches. This could inspire further research into structured representations for other robotic control tasks.
 - **Generate-and-Select Paradigm:** The success of the explicit geometric critic for safety highlights its advantages in interpretability and robustness, especially when compared to learning implicit safety metrics which can be brittle under distribution shifts. This modularity could lead to more certifiable and trustworthy autonomous systems.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:**
 - The effectiveness of domain randomization and the architectural choices allow for **zero-shot sim-to-real transfer** to complex 2D and 3D real-world scenarios, a holy grail for embodied AI. This reduces the need for costly real-world fine-tuning and accelerates deployment.
 - The inclusion of a **temporal consistency token** is a subtle yet crucial innovation that addresses a common failure mode in multi-modal generative planning, preventing oscillatory behaviors that undermine real-world performance.

4. **Practical Applicability and Reproducibility:** The successful deployment on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot across diverse real-world scenes, including stair climbing, firmly establishes the practical applicability of SanD-Planner. The commitment to open-sourcing the dataset and pre-trained models further amplifies its potential impact by fostering reproducibility and enabling other researchers to build upon this work.

5. **Future Research Directions:** While already impactful, the paper also points to future avenues, such as integrating visual foundation models for more robust depth estimation (addressing current limitations with small/specular objects) and scaling to larger, more diverse datasets to fully unlock the model's potential for even broader generalization. This sets a clear roadmap for advancing learning-based navigation.

In summary, SanD-Planner represents a significant step forward in making learning-based robot navigation more practical, efficient, and robust, particularly in data-constrained yet geometrically complex environments. Its principled integration of structural priors with modern generative models offers a powerful paradigm for the next generation of autonomous robotic systems.
