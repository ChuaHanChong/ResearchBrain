# Diffusion Transformer Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Lab and collaborators developed the Diffusion Transformer Policy (DiT Policy), a generalist robot policy that directly denoises continuous action sequences using a scalable multi-modal diffusion transformer. This approach, leveraging large transformer architectures, achieved state-of-the-art generalization across diverse simulation and real-world robot manipulation tasks, including a 3.61 average sequence length on CALVIN and an 82.4% average success rate on LIBERO.

## Problem

- Existing generalist vision-language-action (VLA) models often rely on small, fixed action heads or action discretization, which limits their ability to effectively handle diverse continuous action spaces.
- Previous diffusion policies in robotics were typically designed for single-task learning or used smaller, simpler networks (e.g., MLPs) for denoising, restricting their scalability and direct conditioning on detailed multi-modal observations.
- Developing robot policies that can generalize across varied tasks, environments, and robot embodiments with minimal task-specific training data remains a core challenge in robot learning.

## Method

- The **DiT Policy** introduces an ==in-context conditional diffusion transformer== architecture, using a large causal transformer as the core backbone for denoising.
- It directly denoises continuous 7D robot action *chunks* (sequences of actions) rather than individual actions, conditioned on multimodal inputs like language instructions (CLIP) and visual observations (jointly optimized **DINOv2** features selected by a ==Q-Former==).
- The model is trained with a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (==DDPM==) objective to predict noise added to action chunks, allowing end-to-end optimization on large datasets like Open X-Embodiment.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the CALVIN benchmark, completing an average of 3.61 tasks in a row using only a single static RGB camera, and attained a **50.0%** success rate for 5 consecutive instructions.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art baselines like Octo and OpenVLA on the LIBERO benchmark with an **82.4%** average success rate and showed strong real-to-sim and novel camera view generalization on SimplerEnv and Maniskill2 (**65.8%** success).
- Demonstrated consistent improvements in 10-shot finetuning and exhibited zero-shot generalization capabilities on a real Franka arm, successfully grasping novel objects and executing complex multi-step tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging a large transformer directly for action sequence denoising enables a more scalable and robust modeling of complex, high-dimensional continuous action distributions compared to traditional small action heads.
> - The 'in-context conditional' design allows the diffusion transformer to directly perceive and condition on detailed image patches and language tokens, fostering a stronger perception-action coupling.
> - Denoising entire *action chunks* offers a more holistic and temporally consistent prediction of future robot behavior, contributing to improved performance in long-horizon tasks.
