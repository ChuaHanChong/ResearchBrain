# TableVQA-Bench: A Visual Question Answering Benchmark on Multiple Table Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> NAVER Cloud AI introduces TableVQA-Bench, a visual question answering benchmark for tables, constructed by integrating visual representations and question-answer pairs from existing datasets. Evaluation on the benchmark demonstrates that commercial MLLMs like GPT-4V achieve 54.5% accuracy, outperforming open-source models, yet pure LLMs processing text-formatted tables significantly surpass MLLMs on visual inputs by over 20 percentage points.

## Problem

- A lack of specialized MLLM benchmarks exists for evaluating visual table understanding, distinct from general VQA or chart question answering.
- Existing textual Table Question Answering (TableQA) datasets lack the visual components necessary for directly assessing MLLMs' visual processing capabilities.
- Current Table Structure Recognition (TSR) datasets provide table images and structural information but lack the corresponding question-answer pairs needed for a VQA task.

## Method

- Constructed **TableVQA-Bench** by extending existing ==TableQA== (WikiTableQuestion, TabFact) and ==TSR== (FinTabNet) datasets to include both visual tables and QA pairs.
- Developed a custom ==table rendering system== to generate diverse, ==synthetic table images== for textual datasets, and sourced images for others, mitigating data contamination risks.
- Leveraged ==Large Language Models (LLMs)== (GPT-4) to automatically generate high-quality question-answer pairs for image-only TSR datasets, followed by meticulous human review and filtering.
- Established a standardized evaluation framework, including ==accuracy== and a ==relieved-accuracy== metric, to assess commercial and open-source MLLMs, as well as LLM baselines and ==two-stage inference== methods.

## Results

- GPT-4V achieved the highest average accuracy among MLLMs at **54.5%** across all TableVQA-Bench domains, with Gemini-ProV following at **38.3%**, demonstrating commercial models' current advantage.
- LLMs processing text-formatted HTML tables (e.g., GPT-4 at **75.5%**) significantly outperformed their MLLM counterparts on visual tables (e.g., GPT-4V at **54.5%**) by a margin of over **20 percentage points**.
- Two-stage inference, where an MLLM extracts HTML content for an LLM to answer questions, improved MLLM performance (e.g., GPT-4V → GPT-4 reaching **60.7%**) but still did not match the performance of LLMs directly consuming textual tables.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Commercial MLLMs currently exhibit a considerable performance lead over open-source models in visual table understanding, highlighting a gap in current public domain capabilities.
> - There is a significant performance disparity between MLLMs processing visual tables and LLMs processing text-formatted tables, indicating a bottleneck in MLLMs' ability to accurately extract and reason over visual table information.
> - Higher vision query counts or increased input image resolution are crucial for MLLMs to accurately perceive and process intricate visual details within tables, thereby improving answer accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits Into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have opened new frontiers in AI, enabling models to process and understand information across different modalities, particularly text and images. While MLLMs like GPT-4V, Gemini-ProV, and various open-source models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in general visual question answering (VQA) and instruction following, a significant gap exists in their evaluation for highly structured visual data, specifically tables.

This paper directly addresses this gap by introducing **TableVQA-Bench**, a dedicated benchmark for visual question answering on tables. Prior to this work, evaluation of MLLMs on table understanding was either scattered across general VQA benchmarks (which might include a small, unrepresentative portion of tabular data) or indirectly assessed through separate tasks like Table Question Answering (TableQA) or Table Structure Recognition (TSR).

- **Relationship with existing MLLM benchmarks:** The paper acknowledges the existence of general MLLM benchmarks such as MMBench, SEED-Bench, and MathVista. While these benchmarks assess diverse abilities, including aspects of "image-text understanding" or "mathematical reasoning in visual contexts," they do not offer a specialized or comprehensive evaluation for the nuances of visual table understanding. Similarly, recent chart question-answering benchmarks focus on graphical representations rather than structured tabular data. TableVQA-Bench carves out a distinct niche by meticulously focusing on the specific challenges posed by tables in image format.

- **Bridging TableQA and TSR:** Historically, research into table understanding has largely diverged into two main streams:
 - **Textual Table Question Answering (TableQA):** Datasets like WikiTableQuestion (WTQ) and TabFact provide text-based tables (e.g., HTML, markdown) and associated QA pairs. These datasets are crucial for evaluating logical reasoning over structured data but lack the visual component.
 - **Table Structure Recognition (TSR):** Datasets like FinTabNet and PubTabNet focus on extracting structural and content information (e.g., converting an image of a table into HTML) but do not typically include QA pairs.
 This work ingeniously bridges these two fields by taking existing TableQA datasets and generating visual representations, and by taking TSR datasets (with images) and generating QA pairs, thus creating a true TableVQA dataset.

- **Relevance to Real-world Applications:** The authors highlight the practical importance of understanding visual tabular data, as a large proportion of tables in real-world documents (e.g., reports, web pages, scanned documents) exist in image form. This benchmark is thus directly relevant to advancing technologies for document intelligence, data extraction, and information retrieval from visually rich content, making it a critical step for deploying robust MLLMs in enterprise settings.

In essence, TableVQA-Bench fills a significant void, moving beyond general visual understanding to provide a rigorous, domain-specific evaluation for MLLMs on a prevalent and complex data format: tables in image form.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind TableVQA-Bench stems from the recognition that despite the impressive advancements in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), there is a significant lack of specialized benchmarks to thoroughly evaluate their capability in understanding and answering questions based on tables presented as images.

The **key objectives** of this paper are:

1. **Establish a comprehensive TableVQA benchmark:** The primary goal is to create "TableVQA-Bench," a robust dataset specifically designed for visual question answering on tables, to facilitate the development and evaluation of MLLMs in this critical domain.
2. **Integrate visual components into existing TableQA datasets:** A major objective is to source or generate high-quality table images for existing textual TableQA datasets (like WTQ and TabFact) that traditionally lack a visual modality. This involves methods such as applying stylesheets to HTML or using a dedicated rendering system.
3. **Generate question-answer pairs for existing TSR datasets:** For datasets like FinTabNet, which provide table images and their textual representations but no QA pairs, a crucial objective is to automatically generate relevant and accurate QA pairs, primarily using advanced Large Language Models (LLMs).
4. **Provide a standardized evaluation framework:** To enable fair and consistent comparison, the project aims to define a clear evaluation protocol and metrics (e.g., accuracy, relieved-accuracy) for TableVQA tasks.
5. **Conduct an extensive empirical analysis of MLLMs:** Evaluate the performance of various state-of-the-art commercial and open-source MLLMs on the newly created benchmark to identify their strengths, weaknesses, and areas for improvement in visual table understanding. This also includes comparing MLLMs with their LLM backbones and exploring multi-stage inference approaches.

The **motivation** for these objectives is rooted in several critical observations:

- **Prevalence of visual tables:** Tabular data is ubiquitous, and a large portion of it appears in image form (e.g., rendered HTML, scanned documents, financial reports), making visual table understanding a crucial capability for AI systems.
- **Gap in existing benchmarks:** Current MLLM benchmarks are either too general or focus on other specific visual data types (like charts), leaving the domain of visual table comprehension underserved. This absence hinders targeted research and development in this area.
- **Limitations of existing TableQA and TSR datasets:** Textual TableQA datasets cannot directly evaluate MLLMs' visual understanding, while TSR datasets, though providing visual tables, lack the crucial question-answering component. There is a clear need to combine these elements.
- **Advancements in LLMs and MLLMs:** The emergence of powerful LLMs (like GPT-4) and MLLMs presents an opportunity to create such a benchmark by leveraging their generation capabilities for data synthesis, while also providing a means to evaluate these very models.
- **Understanding MLLM capabilities:** By comparing MLLMs' performance on visual tables versus LLMs' performance on textual tables, the authors aim to gain insights into whether the visual processing component of MLLMs introduces additional challenges or limitations compared to purely text-based understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic approach to construct TableVQA-Bench and evaluate various models, leveraging existing datasets and state-of-the-art AI models for data generation.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction (TableVQA-Bench)

The TableVQA-Bench is constructed by extending pre-existing table-related datasets: WikiTableQuestion (WTQ), TabFact, and FinTabNet. The core idea is to furnish images where only text was available, and QA pairs where only images/structure were available. The final dataset comprises 1,500 QA pairs across 894 images, encompassing four domains: VWTQ, VWTQ-Syn, VTabFact, and FinTabNetQA.

- **Components of TableVQA:** Each sample in TableVQA-Bench includes three parts: {IMG, HTML, QA}.
- **Image Acquisition/Generation:**
 - **VWTQ (from WTQ):** WTQ provides HTML with content and style. Images were sourced by applying Wikipedia stylesheets to the original HTML and then capturing screenshots.
 - **VWTQ-Synthesized (VWTQ-Syn) and VTabFact (from TabFact):** To mitigate potential data contamination (as Wikipedia is a common source for MLLM pre-training) and to introduce style diversity, the authors developed a **table rendering system**. This system takes HTML (or pseudo-HTML converted from CSV for TabFact) and generates synthetic table images with various random styles (fonts, colors, borders, padding, margins, dimensions, JPEG quality).
 - **FinTabNetQA (from FinTabNet):** FinTabNet already provides table images extracted from S&P 500 annual reports. These images were used directly.
- **QA Pair Generation:**
 - **VWTQ and VTabFact:** These datasets already came with human-annotated QA pairs (though TabFact's "entailment/refutation" answers were converted to "True/False" for consistency and efficacy with MLLMs).
 - **FinTabNetQA:** Since FinTabNet lacked QA pairs, GPT-4 was utilized. The text-formatted HTML of tables was fed to GPT-4 to generate questions and answers. The authors addressed two key issues during this process:
 1. **First question bias:** GPT-4 often generated questions whose answers were in the first non-header cell. This was mitigated by generating multiple QA pairs per table and then random sampling.
 2. **Inconsistent scale units:** GPT-4 sometimes omitted scale units (e.g., "thousand," "million") in answers. This was rectified through meticulous human revision.
- **Filtering and Review:** Any tables with more than 50 rows were filtered out, and a meticulous human review was conducted on the generated data (especially QA pairs and rendered images) to ensure quality and naturalness.

#### 3.2. Table Rendering System

This custom framework operates in two phases:
1. **Style Generation:** Style tags are added to the original HTML using the Bootstrap framework to introduce diverse visual attributes randomly (e.g., table background/margin, cell background/padding, border width/style/color, text font/size/alignment/color).
2. **Image Generation:** The styled HTML is rendered in a web browser, and a screenshot is captured using the Puppeteer library. Parameters like image dimensions and JPEG quality are randomized to further enhance diversity. A human review process filters out unnatural-looking images.

#### 3.3. Data Statistics and Analysis

The authors provided a detailed statistical analysis of TableVQA-Bench, including distributions of question length, answer length, aspect ratio, number of rows, number of cells, and text token lengths (with and without special HTML tokens). This analysis provided insights into the characteristics of each sub-dataset and highlighted potential challenges (e.g., longer questions in machine-generated FinTabNetQA, bimodal answer lengths in VTabFact due to "True/False" answers, impact of aspect ratio on table layout). Critically, they analyzed the number of text tokens required versus the number of vision queries used by MLLMs, noting the disparity and its potential impact on efficiency.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol

- **Evaluation Metric:** Accuracy is the primary metric. For FinTabNetQA, a "relieved-accuracy" metric is also used, where scale units (e.g., "million," "billion") are intentionally excluded during evaluation, as MLLMs currently struggle with strict unit adherence.
- **Models Compared:**
 - **Commercial MLLMs:** GPT-4V and Gemini-ProV.
 - **Open-source MLLMs:** A diverse set including BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, CogVLM, mPLUG-Owl2, SPHINX, LLaVA-1.5, and Qwen-VL. Specific versions with higher vision query counts (e.g., '-1k' variants) were also included.
 - **Large Language Models (LLMs):** GPT-4, Gemini-Pro, GPT-3.5, Vicuna-7B, and Vicuna-13B were evaluated on *text-formatted* tables (HTML) to serve as a baseline for comparison with their MLLM counterparts.
 - **Two-stage Inference Methods:** GPT-4V → GPT-4 and Gemini-ProV → Gemini-Pro were explored. This involved using an MLLM to first extract the HTML content from an image and then feeding this HTML to a pure LLM for the QA task, aiming to understand the impact of separating visual parsing from language reasoning. TSR performance of MLLMs was also compared against a state-of-the-art TSR model using the TEDs metric.
- **Prompt Tuning:** Minor prompt tuning was performed for each model to acquire suitable answer formats. Rule-based parsing was used for answer extraction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation on TableVQA-Bench yielded several significant findings regarding the capabilities and limitations of current MLLMs in visual table understanding.

1. **Commercial MLLMs Outperform Open-Source Models:** Across all table domains (VWTQ, VWTQ-Syn, VTabFact, FinTabNetQA), **GPT-4V consistently achieved the highest accuracy** among both commercial and open-source MLLMs, with an average score of 54.5%. Gemini-ProV followed with 38.3%. This suggests a substantial performance gap between proprietary, highly-trained models and their open-source counterparts in this specific domain.
2. **Importance of Vision Queries/Resolution:** The study found a direct correlation between the number of vision queries an MLLM uses (or input image resolution) and its performance. MLLMs with higher vision query counts (e.g., SPHINX-MoE-1k vs. SPHINX-MoE, CogVLM-1k vs. CogVLM) generally exhibited enhanced performance. This was further substantiated by the **GPT-4V specific experiment**, where the 'high' resolution input option significantly outperformed the 'low' option, emphasizing that preserving original visual information and detail is a crucial factor for accurate TableVQA.
3. **Text Modality Outperforms Vision Modality:** A striking finding was that **LLMs processing text-formatted tables significantly outperformed MLLMs processing vision-formatted tables**. GPT-4 achieved 75.5% on average with HTML input, which is a 21 percentage point lead over GPT-4V's 54.5% with image input. Similarly, Gemini-Pro (66.1%) outperformed Gemini-ProV (38.3%) by 27.8 percentage points. This indicates that while MLLMs can process visual inputs, the conversion of visual information into an interpretable format for accurate reasoning remains a substantial challenge, often requiring higher computational costs for lower performance.
4. **Two-stage Inference Improves MLLM Performance but Still Lags LLMs:** Employing a two-stage approach (MLLM for Table Structure Reconstruction, then LLM for QA) led to notable performance enhancements for GPT (GPT-4V → GPT-4 at 60.7%) and Gemini (Gemini-ProV → Gemini-Pro at 48.6%). This suggests that improving the initial visual extraction step is vital. However, even with this improvement, the two-stage MLLM + LLM pipeline still did not match the performance of LLMs directly consuming text-formatted tables, underscoring the current limitations of MLLMs in efficiently extracting high-fidelity information from visual tables.
5. **MLLM Limitations in Table Structure Recognition (TSR):** Comparisons using the TEDs metric showed that MLLMs' TSR capabilities generally lag behind state-of-the-art specialized TSR models. This further supports the idea that the initial visual understanding and structured data extraction phase is a bottleneck for MLLMs in TableVQA.
6. **Qualitative Observations and Error Analysis:** Incorrect answers frequently stemmed from limitations in OCR capability, where words or numbers crucial for answering were not accurately perceived from the image. The benefits of higher vision query length and resolution were qualitatively observed to mitigate some of these OCR-related errors.
7. **Dataset-Specific Performance:** FinTabNetQA, characterized by its machine-generated QA pairs and financial domain, showed relatively lower inherent QA complexity (LLMs performed very well), but also highlighted the need for "relieved-accuracy" due to MLLMs' struggle with precise unit inclusion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TableVQA-Bench represents a significant contribution to the field of multi-modal AI, with substantial implications for research and real-world applications.

1. **Filling a Critical Evaluation Gap:** The primary significance is the creation of a much-needed, specialized benchmark for Visual Question Answering on tables. This fills a crucial void in MLLM evaluation, which previously lacked a dedicated and comprehensive dataset for this prevalent and challenging data format. It provides a standardized framework for rigorously assessing MLLMs' ability to understand structured visual information.
2. **Driving MLLM Advancement in Document Intelligence:** By highlighting the current limitations of MLLMs—particularly the performance gap between text and vision modalities, the importance of visual resolution, and the challenges in precise table structure recognition—TableVQA-Bench offers clear directions for future research. It will serve as a vital tool for researchers to develop more robust MLLM architectures, pre-training strategies, and fine-tuning techniques specifically tailored for document understanding tasks. This could lead to MLLMs that are significantly better at parsing and reasoning over visual documents like reports, invoices, and web pages.
3. **Enhancing Real-world Applications:** The benchmark directly addresses a critical need in various practical domains. Industries dealing with large volumes of documents containing tabular data (e.g., finance, legal, healthcare, business intelligence) stand to benefit immensely. Improved TableVQA capabilities can power more accurate automated data extraction, intelligent report analysis, and sophisticated conversational AI systems that can interact with visual documents.
4. **Providing Insights into MLLM Architecture:** The comparative analysis offers valuable insights into the design of MLLMs. The findings on vision query counts and resolution suggest that how visual information is encoded and fused with language models is paramount. The relatively lower performance of MLLMs compared to LLMs on textual input, despite higher computational cost, indicates that the visual processing pipeline in current MLLMs might be a bottleneck or could benefit from more sophisticated integration strategies beyond simple concatenation of vision and text tokens.
5. **Fostering Open Science:** By making the TableVQA-Bench dataset and evaluation codes publicly available, the authors actively contribute to open science. This encourages community engagement, allows for reproducible research, and accelerates progress in the field by providing a common ground for comparison and innovation.

In conclusion, TableVQA-Bench is more than just a dataset; it is a diagnostic tool that critically assesses the maturity of MLLMs in a practical, yet underexplored, domain. Its introduction marks a significant step towards developing truly intelligent multi-modal agents capable of comprehensively understanding the visual world, particularly its structured data.
