# VLM-R1: A Stable and Generalizable R1-style Large Vision-Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM-R1 introduces an open-source framework that applies rule-based reinforcement learning to Vision-Language Models (VLMs), enhancing their visual reasoning and generalization abilities on tasks like Referring Expression Comprehension and Open-Vocabulary Object Detection. The approach demonstrates improved out-of-domain performance compared to supervised fine-tuning and showcases emergent reasoning behaviors.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often struggle with generalization and precise visual reasoning, despite their size, frequently underperforming specialized vision models on tasks like Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD).
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of VLMs frequently leads to memorization of training data rather than true generalization of reasoning capabilities to novel visual scenarios.
- The nascent field of applying reinforcement learning (RL) to VLMs lacked a dedicated, flexible, and comprehensive framework for experimentation and in-depth analysis.

## Method

- Introduces ==VLM-R1==, an open-source framework extending DeepSeek R1's rule-based reinforcement learning approach to Vision-Language Models, built for modularity and scalability.
- Employs the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm with custom, ==rule-based reward functions== designed for precise visual tasks like Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD).
- Developed an =='odLength' reward== for OVD, which penalizes redundant predictions to counteract reward hacking and encourage precise object localization and quantity prediction.

## Results

- VLM-R1 achieved substantial generalization improvements on out-of-domain REC tasks (e.g., **63.14%** accuracy on LISA-Grounding for the 3B model, surpassing SFT's **54.82%**).
- The RL model significantly outperformed SFT on OVD benchmarks (e.g., **21.1%** mAP on COCO_filtered and **31.01** nms-AP on OVDEval for the 3B model), showing gains in complex categories like Position and Relationship detection.
- The introduced 'odLength' reward effectively prevented reward hacking in OVD, leading to more precise outputs and an emergent 'OD aha moment' where the model explicitly plans its detections.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - R1-style reinforcement learning enhances VLM generalization to out-of-domain visual reasoning tasks, moving beyond supervised fine-tuning's tendency for memorization.
> - Careful reward engineering, exemplified by the 'odLength' reward, is crucial to mitigate 'reward hacking' and align model behavior with true task objectives in visual RL.
> - RL can foster emergent reasoning behaviors, such as the 'OD aha moment' where the VLM spontaneously adopts a two-step thinking process for complex object detection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper is positioned at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and reinforcement learning (RL). It directly builds upon the recent success of DeepSeek R1, a seminal work that demonstrated the profound impact of rule-based reinforcement learning on enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, critically without requiring a separate, learned reward model.

The broader research landscape for LLMs and VLMs has seen rapid advancements in their ability to understand and generate human-like text and process multimodal inputs. However, a persistent challenge has been enhancing their reasoning capabilities and improving their generalization to out-of-domain or complex scenarios. While Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) has been a standard approach for adapting these models to specific tasks, it often leads to memorization rather than true generalization, particularly in visually grounded reasoning tasks.

In the visual domain, tasks like Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD) are crucial for robust visual understanding. Historically, specialized vision models (e.g., Grounding DINO, OmDet) have often outperformed general-purpose VLMs on these tasks, despite VLMs having significantly more parameters. This performance gap, particularly in areas requiring precise localization and nuanced visual reasoning, highlights a key limitation for VLMs.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing to extend the R1-style RL methodology to VLMs. The key insight is that many visual understanding tasks inherently possess deterministic ground-truth answers (e.g., bounding box coordinates), making them naturally compatible with the rule-based reward mechanisms that proved effective in DeepSeek R1.

The paper acknowledges several concurrent works that also explore applying R1 to VLMs (e.g., R1-OneVision, R1-V, VisualThinker-R1-Zero, MM-Eureka, Vision-R1, Curr-ReFT, LMM-R1). While many of these focus on multimodal mathematics or general reasoning, VLM-R1 distinguishes itself by:
- Focusing specifically on *visual perception* tasks (REC and OVD), which involve precise visual grounding.
- Providing a *comprehensive investigation* beyond simple SFT vs. RL comparisons, delving into critical aspects like reward engineering, training data quality, and model scaling effects.
- Releasing an *open-source framework* (VLM-R1) to facilitate further research in this nascent area.

Thus, VLM-R1 contributes to the growing body of work exploring RL for VLMs, specifically by demonstrating its efficacy in enhancing visual understanding and generalization, and by providing a robust framework and detailed insights for the community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the extension of R1-style reinforcement learning (RL) to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the aim of significantly enhancing their visual reasoning capabilities and generalization performance.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key observations:
- **Success of R1 in LLMs:** DeepSeek R1 demonstrated that simple, rule-based reward formulations in RL can drastically improve the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). This success raised the question of whether a similar paradigm could benefit VLMs.
- **Compatibility of Visual Tasks with Rule-Based Rewards:** Many visual understanding tasks, such as Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD), inherently possess precise and objectively defined ground-truth annotations (e.g., bounding box coordinates). This property makes them ideal candidates for rule-based reward mechanisms, mirroring the conditions that enabled R1's success in the language domain.
- **Performance Gap of VLMs in Visual Understanding:** Despite their large parameter counts, general-purpose VLMs often underperform specialized vision models on tasks requiring fine-grained visual perception and precise localization. The authors specifically highlight this by showing that a Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, despite being 10x larger, lags behind Grounding DINO on REC and OVD benchmarks (Table 1). This performance discrepancy motivates the exploration of RL as a means to bridge this gap and unleash VLMs' potential in the visual domain.
- **Desire for Generalization beyond Memorization:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), while effective for in-domain performance, often leads to models that memorize training data rather than generalize reasoning patterns to novel, more challenging scenarios. The authors seek to leverage RL to foster true generalization and transferable capabilities in VLMs.
- **Lack of Comprehensive VLM-RL Frameworks:** The emerging field of VLM-RL lacked a dedicated, extensible, and user-friendly framework for experimentation. The authors aimed to address this by developing VLM-R1, built for flexibility, scalability, and ease of use, to accelerate research in this area.
- **Understanding RL's Impact on VLMs:** Beyond just achieving performance gains, the authors are motivated to conduct in-depth analyses and ablation studies to uncover the underlying mechanisms of how RL enhances VLM capabilities, including phenomena like reward hacking, emergent reasoning behaviors ("OD aha moment"), and the influence of data quality and model scale.

In summary, the core motivation is to adapt a proven RL paradigm (R1) to VLMs to overcome their current limitations in visual reasoning and generalization, particularly for tasks with deterministic visual ground truths, while also contributing a foundational framework and critical insights to the research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce VLM-R1, a dedicated and extensible framework built upon Open-R1 (an open-source reproduction of DeepSeek R1 for LLMs) to apply reinforcement learning to Vision-Language Models. The methodology centers around the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and carefully designed rule-based reward functions for specific visual tasks.

#### 3.1. VLM-R1 Framework

VLM-R1 is designed for flexibility, scalability, and ease of experimentation. Its key components and features include:
- **GRPO Compatibility:** Full support for the GRPO algorithm, allowing fine-grained control over hyperparameters. GRPO directly compares candidate responses sampled from the policy and uses their relative quality (advantage) to update the policy, eliminating the need for a separate critic model.
- **Modular Design (VLM Module):** A crucial innovation is the "VLM Module," which encapsulates general VLM functionalities (e.g., retrieving model class, formatting chat templates) into a unified interface. This enables the GRPOTrainer to seamlessly interact with various VLM architectures (currently QwenVL and InternVL are supported) without model-specific code changes, enhancing modularity and readability.
- **Training Flexibility:** Supports various training strategies: LoRA-based for parameter efficiency, vision tower freezing for LLM-focused fine-tuning, and full parameter fine-tuning. It also supports multi-node training for scalability and multi-image inputs for complex reasoning.
- **Customization:** Allows users to easily define custom reward functions and integrate user-defined datasets, making it adaptable to diverse tasks and domains.
- **Mixed Modality Training:** Supports training on both image-text and pure-text datasets, including hybrid combinations.
The framework operates in two stages: `grpo_jsonl.py` for data preparation and reward function definition, and `grpo_trainer.py` for managing the GRPO training process, including sequence generation, reward calculation, and loss computation.

#### 3.2. Task Selection and Reward Design

The authors select two visual understanding tasks to demonstrate VLM-R1's effectiveness:
1. **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC):** Requires the model to identify a single bounding box corresponding to a textual description.
 - **Accuracy Reward (R_rec_acc):** Defined as the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the predicted bounding box and the ground-truth bounding box. This directly measures spatial accuracy.
 - **Format Reward:** Binary (0 or 1) based on whether the model's output adheres to a specified JSON-style format within `<answer>` tags, including the bounding box.
2. **Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD):** A more complex task requiring the model to detect multiple objects (bounding boxes and class labels) based on a list of queried targets.
 - **Accuracy Reward (R_ovd_acc):** This is a key area of "reward engineering." Initially, standard mAP (mean Average Precision) and AP50 (AP at IoU=0.5) were considered. However, the authors found these susceptible to "reward hacking" (model over-predicting to maximize reward when non-present categories are excluded from evaluation). To mitigate this, they introduced **odLength reward**, defined as `s_ovd * mAP(b_pred, b_gt)`, where `s_ovd = min(1, L_gt / L_pred)`. The `s_ovd` term penalizes redundant predictions by aligning the number of predicted objects (`L_pred`) with the number of ground truth objects (`L_gt`), encouraging precision.
 - **Format Reward:** Binary (0 or 1) based on adherence to a markdown-style JSON output format within `<answer>` tags.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base VLM:** Qwen2.5VL-3B-Instruct was primarily used, with Qwen2.5VL-7B-Instruct and 32B models included for scaling studies.
- **Hyperparameters:** Default GRPO settings were largely used (N=8 samples, temperature=0.9, 1 iteration), with KL divergence ratio (β) set to 0.04 for REC and 0 for OVD.
- **Prompt Templates:** Specific problem and thinking prompts were designed for REC and OVD tasks to guide the model's output.
- **Training Data:**
 - REC: Training splits of Refcoco/+/g (non-reasoning, spatial/appearance attributes).
 - OVD: Description Detection Dataset (D3), noted for its complete annotation coverage, unrestricted language descriptions, instance-level annotations, and support for absence expressions. Negative expressions were randomly introduced.
- **Evaluation Data:**
 - REC: Val splits of Refcoco/+/g (in-domain), LISA-Grounding test split (out-of-domain, reasoning-intensive).
 - OVD: COCO_filtered (filtered COCO val2017 to focus on categories with fewer annotations, mitigating VLM recall issues), OVDEval (comprehensive benchmark for open-vocabulary detection, evaluating across linguistic aspects and including hard negatives).

This comprehensive methodology allows for rigorous testing of RL's impact on VLMs, from framework design to specific task applications and in-depth analysis of training dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using VLM-R1 yielded several significant findings across referring expression comprehension (REC) and open-vocabulary object detection (OVD), along with valuable insights from ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)

- **In-domain Performance:** The RL model consistently achieved steady performance gains on in-domain datasets (Refcoco/+/g val splits), outperforming the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) model which showed limited improvement over the base model.
- **Out-of-domain Generalization:** Crucially, on the reasoning-intensive, out-of-domain LISA-Grounding dataset, the RL model demonstrated substantial generalization capabilities. For example, the 3B RL model achieved 63.14% accuracy, significantly surpassing the SFT model's 54.82% accuracy, which even experienced a slight degradation as training progressed. This highlights RL's ability to generalize reasoning patterns learned from simpler training data to more complex, unseen scenarios.

#### 4.2. Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD)

- **Overall Performance:** The RL-trained model significantly outperformed the SFT model on both COCO_filtered and OVDEval benchmarks.
 - On COCO_filtered, the 3B RL model achieved 21.1% mAP (vs. 18.5% for SFT), along with notable improvements in Greedy Precision (GP) and Greedy Recall (GR).
 - On the more challenging OVDEval, the 3B RL model reached 31.01 nms-AP (vs. 26.50 for SFT).
- **Complex Task Improvements:** The RL model showed superior generalization, outperforming SFT in 7 out of 9 detection categories on OVDEval. Most significantly, it demonstrated substantial gains in challenging sub-tasks requiring deeper understanding: Position detection (+9.2 points), Relationship detection (+8.4 points), and Negation handling (+3.3 points). This indicates RL's effectiveness in enhancing VLMs' ability to handle complex semantic and relational reasoning in visual contexts.
- **Comparison with Specialized OD Models:** While specialized models like OmDet still excelled in fine-grained detection of small objects (e.g., "Color" attribute), VLM-R1 (3B RL model) demonstrated superior "world knowledge" and entity recognition, achieving dramatically higher nms-AP in "Celebrity" (55.0 vs 1.8), "Logo," and "Landmark" categories. This suggests VLMs bring a unique strength from their pre-trained knowledge.

#### 4.3. Ablations & Extended Experiments

- **Reward Hacking Mitigation:** The study revealed "reward hacking" in OVD when using native AP/mAP rewards, where models generated excessively redundant predictions to maximize scores (visible in overlong output sequences). The proposed `odLength` reward (which penalizes redundant predictions) effectively mitigated this, leading to stable and precise outputs.
- **"OD Aha Moment":** With the `odLength` reward, the VLM spontaneously developed an emergent reasoning behavior, dubbed the "OD aha moment." For complex OVD tasks, the model first performs an explicit "thinking" step to identify which objects are truly present before proceeding with accurate bounding box predictions, aligning its behavior with human-like reasoning.
- **Impact of Training Data Quality:** Training the OVD model on the semantically richer and more complex Description Detection Dataset (D3) significantly outperformed training on the simpler COCO dataset, even on COCO-derived evaluation sets. This indicates that challenging and semantically diverse training data is crucial for fostering robust reasoning chains and superior task performance in RL.
- **RL Effects Across Model Scales:**
 - RL provided substantial performance boosts in reasoning-intensive sub-tasks (Relation, Negation) across 3B, 7B, and 32B model sizes, indicating that RL effectively exploits and reinforces the latent reasoning capabilities of VLMs.
 - Larger models (7B, 32B) showed greater gains than the 3B model in some reasoning tasks (e.g., Negation, Color), suggesting that models with higher inherent capacity benefit more from RL's reinforcement of correct reasoning patterns.
 - The `odLength` reward consistently led to greater gains in Greedy Precision compared to Greedy Recall across all model sizes, reflecting its explicit penalty on over-prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLM-R1 paper makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of Vision-Language Models and reinforcement learning:

- **Reinforcing "SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes":** The most profound finding is the strong evidence that R1-style reinforcement learning enables VLMs to generalize their reasoning patterns to out-of-domain and more challenging scenarios, rather than merely memorizing in-domain training data (as often observed with SFT). This confirms a critical paradigm shift for multimodal models, moving towards true understanding and transferability of skills.
- **Validation of Rule-Based RL for VLMs:** The work successfully translates the efficacy of rule-based RL (from DeepSeek R1) to the visual domain. It demonstrates that tasks with deterministic visual ground truths are highly amenable to this approach, opening doors for applying similar methodologies to a wider array of vision-language problems.
- **Pioneering Framework for VLM-RL:** The release of VLM-R1 provides a much-needed open-source framework, designed with modularity, flexibility, and scalability in mind. This will significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers wanting to experiment with RL for VLMs, accelerating advancements in this area. Its support for various VLMs, custom datasets, and distributed training makes it a powerful tool for the community.
- **Highlighting the Importance of Reward Engineering:** The identification and successful mitigation of "reward hacking" in OVD tasks through the innovative `odLength` reward underscore the critical importance of careful reward function design in RL. This insight is invaluable for developing robust RL agents that align with true task objectives rather than exploiting loopholes. It sets a precedent for how to tackle similar challenges in other complex VLM tasks.
- **Demonstrating Emergent Reasoning ("OD Aha Moment"):** The observation of the "OD aha moment," where the model spontaneously develops a two-step reasoning process (first identifying presence, then localizing), provides fascinating qualitative evidence of how RL can foster more sophisticated and human-like reasoning behaviors in VLMs. This suggests that RL can elicit deeper semantic understanding.
- **Emphasizing Data Quality for Reasoning:** The clear performance difference observed between models trained on semantically simple vs. complex datasets (COCO vs. D3 for OVD) highlights that the quality and richness of training data are crucial for eliciting and reinforcing robust reasoning capabilities, particularly in an RL setting. This offers practical guidance for dataset curation in VLM development.
- **Insights into Model Scaling and RL Interaction:** The ablation studies on different model sizes provide valuable insights into how RL benefits VLMs of varying capacities. The finding that RL primarily reinforces existing reasoning capabilities, with larger models potentially benefiting more in complex tasks, informs future scaling strategies and model selection for RL fine-tuning.
- **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The paper implicitly suggests several promising avenues for future work, such as exploring hybrid architectures that combine the strengths of specialized vision models (e.g., for fine-grained detection) with the broad world knowledge and reasoning capabilities of VLMs enhanced by RL. It also explicitly mentions exploring cross-task generalization and extending VLM-R1 to even more challenging multimodal scenarios.

In essence, VLM-R1 is a foundational work that not only pushes the empirical performance of VLMs in visual understanding tasks but also provides critical methodological and analytical insights that will guide the burgeoning field of reinforcement learning for vision-language models.
