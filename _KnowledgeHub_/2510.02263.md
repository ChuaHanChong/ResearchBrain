# RLAD: Training LLMs to Discover Abstractions for Solving Reasoning Problems

> [!summary] Summary
> The RLAD framework enables large language models to self-discover and leverage high-level reasoning abstractions, leading to substantial improvements in accuracy and compute efficiency on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks. This approach teaches models to propose and utilize concise procedural and factual knowledge to guide complex problem-solving.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often engage in verbose or degenerate exploration, leading to 'underthinking' and inconsistent logic switches during complex reasoning.
- Current reinforcement learning approaches for LLMs incentivize 'depth' (longer reasoning chains) over 'breadth' (diverse solution strategies), limiting exploration of alternative paths.
- LLMs struggle to consistently capture, reuse, or generalize fundamental procedural and factual knowledge, hindering truly algorithmic reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces =='reasoning abstractions'== as concise natural language descriptions of procedural and factual knowledge to guide LLM reasoning.
- Develops ==RLAD==, a novel ==two-player reinforcement learning framework== that jointly trains an =='abstraction generator'== and an =='abstraction-conditioned solution generator'==.
- Employs a modified reward function for the solution generator and curriculum training to ensure effective abstraction utilization and learning on challenging problems.

## Results

- RLAD consistently outperformed state-of-the-art RL methods (e.g., DAPO) on math reasoning benchmarks, showing an average **44%** improvement on AIME 2025.
- Abstractions proposed by the RLAD-trained generator improved a stronger solution model's (o4-mini) pass@1 accuracy from **80.38%** to **85.83%** without further fine-tuning.
- Solutions generated with different abstractions exhibited increased semantic diversity and significantly higher adherence to their respective abstraction's guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-discovered reasoning abstractions, even from weaker models, can significantly improve the accuracy of LLM solutions and generalize to guide stronger models.
> - Allocating test-time compute to generating diverse abstractions is more efficient for performance gains than solely increasing the number of solution samples per abstraction.
> - Training with abstractions enhances an LLM's general reasoning capabilities and generalization, even when abstractions are not provided during inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has seen remarkable progress in generating human-like text and performing various language tasks. However, enabling LLMs to perform complex, multi-step reasoning, particularly in domains like mathematics, remains a significant challenge. Current state-of-the-art approaches often rely on incentivizing "long chains of thought" (CoT) through reinforcement learning (RL) post-training. While these methods aim to uncover algorithmic behavior, they frequently result in verbose, often degenerate, exploration of the solution space, characterized by "underthinking" or frequent logic switches rather than consistent capture and reuse of procedural knowledge. This leads to models optimizing for "depth" (longer traces) rather than "breadth" (diverse strategies).

RLAD positions itself directly within this landscape by offering a novel approach to tackle the limitations of existing reasoning strategies. It builds upon several lines of work while introducing a distinct contribution:

- **Scaling Test-Time Compute and Exploration:** Previous research has explored scaling test-time compute in various ways, including parallel sampling (generating multiple reasoning rollouts and selecting the best) and iterative editing (sequentially refining a single trace). Some work combines these, while others use hand-designed scaffolds to integrate multi-step evaluations. RLAD introduces a *hybrid* approach where abstractions guide a more structured exploration, allowing for both breadth and depth in a controlled manner. It specifically addresses the "depth over breadth" issue by encouraging diverse strategies.
- **Using Prior Knowledge for LLM Reasoning:** Many studies focus on leveraging textual artifacts—such as examples, plans, or prompts—as reusable knowledge. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines retrieve relevant information from a static corpus. Other works use LLMs to learn or refine prompts, or employ synthetic demonstrations, scratchpads, and memory-augmented agents to encode problem-solving knowledge. RLAD distinguishes itself significantly here:
 - Unlike RAG, where knowledge is *retrieved* from an external, typically human-written, corpus, RLAD trains models to *discover and propose* these textual artifacts (abstractions) *on their own*.
 - While similar to prompt optimization in using textual guidance, RLAD focuses on input-dependent *generation* of concise procedural and factual knowledge, rather than just optimizing or refining prompts.
 - Abstractions in RLAD function as dynamically generated "prior knowledge" that is tailored to the specific problem, rather than being static or pre-defined. This "self-generated" aspect of knowledge is a crucial differentiator.
- **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The paper explicitly references state-of-the-art RL methods like DAPO (Discriminant-aware Preference Optimization) and techniques for improving RL efficiency (e.g., curriculum training, reward masking). RLAD integrates these advances into a novel *two-player RL training paradigm* that jointly optimizes an abstraction generator and a solution generator. This cooperative training setup is distinct from training a single model for solution generation or simply distilling knowledge from stronger models.

In essence, RLAD introduces "reasoning abstractions" as a new, *model-generated* form of meta-strategy or high-level guidance. It addresses the challenge of achieving broader, more algorithmic reasoning by enabling LLMs to conceptualize and leverage these abstractions, thereby offering an "orthogonal axis" for scaling reasoning capabilities beyond simply increasing chain-of-thought length or parallel solution sampling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RLAD stems from the observed limitations in how current LLMs approach complex reasoning problems, particularly in mathematical domains. Despite advances in training LLMs with reinforcement learning (RL) to generate long "chains of thought" (CoT), these models often fall short of truly "algorithmic" behavior. The paper identifies several key issues:

- **Ineffective Exploration:** LLMs frequently engage in "verbose and degenerate exploration" or "underthinking," where their reasoning traces become excessively long, drift into irrelevant paths, or switch logic inconsistently. This indicates a failure to consistently capture, reuse, or generalize fundamental procedural and factual knowledge.
- **Depth vs. Breadth:** Existing RL post-training often incentivizes "depth"—producing longer response lengths by building on the current line of reasoning. This can trap models in seemingly optimal but ultimately incorrect strategies, hindering their ability to explore diverse solution approaches or "breadth."
- **Lack of Procedural and Factual Knowledge Reuse:** Effective reasoning requires not just pattern matching but identifying and implementing core algorithmic procedures, relevant primitives, intermediate results, or shared strategies. LLMs struggle to realize and leverage these underlying structures systematically.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of the RLAD research are:

1. **Introduce Reasoning Abstractions:** Define "reasoning abstractions" as concise natural language descriptions of procedural and factual knowledge that can guide LLMs toward successful reasoning. These abstractions are intended to function like "hints" or "meta-strategies."
2. **Enable Abstraction Proposal:** Develop a mechanism for LLMs to become capable of *proposing* multiple, diverse abstractions given a problem. This moves beyond relying on pre-defined or externally retrieved knowledge.
3. **Facilitate Abstraction Utilization:** Train LLMs to effectively *utilize* the information provided by these self-generated abstractions to construct solutions. The goal is for abstractions to serve as high-level subgoals or priors, guiding the low-level solution-generating policy.
4. **Develop a Joint Training Paradigm (RLAD):** Implement a novel two-player RL framework that jointly trains an "abstraction generator" (which proposes abstractions) and an "abstraction-conditioned solution generator" (which solves problems using abstractions). This setup aims to enable structured exploration and decouple the learning signals for abstraction proposal and solution generation.
5. **Improve Generalization and Efficiency:** Demonstrate that RLAD improves generalization to harder problems and makes more efficient use of test-time compute. Specifically, investigate whether allocating compute to generating diverse abstractions is more beneficial than generating more solutions alone.

Ultimately, the motivation is to foster a more sophisticated form of reasoning in LLMs, allowing them to move beyond mere "pattern matching or memorization" towards identifying and implementing adaptable "algorithmic procedures" by leveraging high-level, self-discovered conceptual guidance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLAD (Reinforcement Learning with Abstraction Discovery) addresses the problem of enhancing LLM reasoning by introducing and explicitly training models to propose and utilize "reasoning abstractions." The core of the methodology is a novel two-player reinforcement learning paradigm involving an **abstraction generator** (𝜋_abs) and an **abstraction-conditioned solution generator** (𝜋_sol).

#### 3.1. Reasoning Abstractions: Definition and Generation

- **Concept:** Reasoning abstractions are defined as concise natural language descriptions of procedural and factual knowledge relevant to solving a problem. They are intended to summarize useful substructures or patterns within the solution space.
- **Warmstarting Abstraction Generator:** To initiate the training of 𝜋_abs, a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase is crucial. This involves:
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Stronger LLMs are used to generate a corpus of (problem, abstraction, solution) triples. Specifically, Qwen3 models generate diverse solution traces, and a stronger reasoning model (o4-mini) summarizes these traces to deduce useful patterns, forming the abstractions.
 - **Filtering:** Abstractions are carefully filtered to ensure they do not "leak" the final answer but only provide useful procedural or factual guidance. This is verified by checking if prompting a model with only the abstraction (no problem) yields zero accuracy.
- **Evaluating Abstractions (Pre-RLAD):** Before full RLAD training, the efficacy of these synthetically generated abstractions is validated. An abstraction `z` is considered "good" if conditioning `𝜋_sol` on `z` improves its accuracy compared to solving the problem without `z`. This pre-training step ensures that meaningful abstractions can indeed be created and leveraged.

#### 3.2. RLAD Training Paradigm: Joint Optimization

RLAD employs a cooperative two-player RL setup where `𝜋_abs` and `𝜋_sol` are trained iteratively:

- **Abstraction Generator (𝜋_abs(z|x)):** This LLM takes a problem `x` as input and proposes candidate abstractions `z`.
 - **Reward Function:** `𝜋_abs` is rewarded based on how much the proposed abstraction `z` *improves* the accuracy of the `𝜋_sol` when `𝜋_sol` is conditioned on `z`. Formally, `𝑟_𝜋_sol(x, z) := E[Acc_x(ỹ, y*)]`, where the expectation is over solutions `ỹ` generated by `𝜋_sol(·|x,z)`.
 - **RL Algorithm:** `𝜋_abs` is trained using "batched" offline RL via RFT (Reinforcement Fine-Tuning) due to the computational cost of online rollouts.
- **Abstraction-Conditioned Solution Generator (𝜋_sol(ỹ|x, z)):** This LLM generates a solution `ỹ` given the problem `x` and the abstraction `z` proposed by `𝜋_abs`.
 - **Reward Function:** `𝜋_sol` is rewarded with a 0/1 binary outcome for correctly solving the problem `x`.
 - **Modified Reward for 𝜋_sol:** A critical modification is applied to the reward system to prevent `𝜋_sol` from ignoring abstractions or `𝜋_abs` from becoming a full problem solver itself. `𝜋_sol` is trained on a mixture of prompts: some augmented with `z` and some without (`z=∅`).
 - For prompts *without* abstractions (`z=∅`), the reward for `𝜋_sol` is explicitly set to 0, though a KL penalty for regularization is retained. This forces `𝜋_sol` to learn to effectively *utilize* the abstraction when it *is* provided, rather than finding shortcuts or performing well independently.
 - For prompts *with* abstractions, the reward is `Acc_x(ỹ, y*)`.
 - **RL Algorithm:** `𝜋_sol` builds on the DAPO (Discriminant-aware Preference Optimization) recipe, incorporating token-level policy loss normalization and asymmetric clipping.
 - **Curriculum Training:** To improve learning on challenging problems, `𝜋_sol` training employs a two-stage curriculum. Problems are partitioned into "easy," "medium," and "hard" based on the base model's success rate on DeepScaleR benchmarks. Training progresses from easy to medium problems before evaluating on the held-out "hard" split.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Base Models:** The abstraction generator starts from SFT, while the solution generator directly uses Qwen3-1.7B, a model distilled from Qwen3-32B.
- **Hyperparameters:** Standard RL hyperparameters are used, including specific learning rates, clip ratios, entropy, and KL loss coefficients (detailed in Appendix A.2).
- **Evaluation:** Models are evaluated using pass@k and average accuracy on unseen problems from test distributions, specifically focusing on math reasoning benchmarks like AIME, DeepScaleR Hard, and AMC.

This comprehensive methodology allows RLAD to jointly learn how to discover high-level strategic guidance and then effectively leverage it to solve complex reasoning tasks, overcoming limitations of previous methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RLAD demonstrates significant improvements in reasoning capabilities across various benchmarks and provides crucial insights into the utility of reasoning abstractions.

- **Initial Validation of Abstractions:**
 - The paper first validates that "good" reasoning abstractions, generated by summarizing solution traces from stronger models (e.g., o4-mini), indeed improve solution generator accuracy. This improvement is contingent on the abstraction being sufficiently informative (not too short) and the solution generator having adequate instruction-following capabilities (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B or Qwen3-4B).
 - Similar gains were observed on the ARC-AGI benchmark (Table 1) and across 37 diverse non-math domains (including healthcare, legal reasoning, web security), with an average performance improvement of 30% from abstractions (Table 7).
 - Qualitative analysis (Appendix D.3) shows that these discovered abstractions correspond to useful techniques (e.g., "productive launchpoint"), lemmas/heuristic principles (e.g., "blind-follow trajectory"), and cautionary advice (e.g., "caution alert"), highlighting their interpretability and utility.

- **RLAD's Superior Performance on Math Reasoning Benchmarks:**
 - RLAD consistently *outperforms* both the base Qwen3-1.7B model and models fine-tuned with state-of-the-art RL (DAPO) on challenging math reasoning benchmarks: AIME 2025, DeepScaleR Hard, and AMC 2023 (Table 2).
 - For example, on AIME 2025, RLAD shows an average 44% improvement over DAPO.
 - Crucially, RLAD demonstrates performance gains not only when abstractions are provided during inference ("w/ abs (avg)" and "w/ abs (best)") but also, surprisingly, in the "w/o abs" setting (without abstractions during inference). This suggests that exposure to diverse abstractions during training enhances the model's general reasoning capabilities and fosters better generalization. Similar trends are noted for AIME 2024 and HMMT 2025 (Table 5).

- **Weak-to-Strong Generalization of Abstractions:**
 - The abstractions proposed by the RLAD-trained abstraction generator, even though trained on a weaker base model, can effectively guide a *stronger* solution generator (o4-mini).
 - Conditioning o4-mini on these abstractions improved its pass@k accuracy significantly (e.g., from 80.38% to 85.83% pass@1), demonstrating that abstractions can generalize in a "weak-to-strong" setting without further supervision or modification of the stronger model.

- **Optimal Compute Allocation at Test Time:**
 - The study investigates the tradeoff between generating more abstractions (`m`) and sampling more solutions per abstraction (`k`) for a fixed total compute budget (`m × k`).
 - Results in Figure 5 indicate that, especially as the total compute budget increases, allocating more compute toward *generating diverse abstractions* yields greater performance improvements than solely focusing on generating more solution refinements (i.e., increasing `k` for a single abstraction). This supports the hypothesis that abstractions help explore "breadth" of strategies when "depth" (local search) saturates.
 - Further evidence is provided by comparing pass@k under equal compute budgets: `n` abstractions * `n` solutions per abstraction consistently outperforms `n^2` solutions without abstractions (Table 3), reinforcing the value of abstraction conditioning.

- **Behavior of the Abstraction-Conditioned Solution Generator:**
 - **Abstraction Adherence:** An LLM-based judge confirms that solutions generated by `𝜋_sol` when conditioned on an abstraction exhibit significantly *higher adherence* to that specific abstraction's guidance compared to solutions generated without abstractions or with unrelated ones (Figure 6, right).
 - **Semantic Diversity:** Solutions generated by conditioning on *different* proposed abstractions are semantically *less similar* to each other than solutions generated without abstractions or with the same abstraction (Figure 6, left). This implies that abstractions successfully guide the model to explore a more diverse range of reasoning traces.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Experiments (Table 6) confirm the importance of key design choices in RLAD: curriculum training, including "no-abstraction" prompts in the training mixture, and applying reward masking to these no-abstraction prompts are all critical for achieving the strongest performance.

In summary, RLAD successfully introduces, trains, and validates the concept of reasoning abstractions as a powerful tool for improving LLM reasoning. The method not only boosts raw accuracy but also promotes diverse, structured exploration and offers a more compute-efficient strategy for scaling performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLAD framework represents a significant step forward in enabling large language models to perform more robust, efficient, and generalized reasoning. Its introduction of "reasoning abstractions" and the novel two-player RL training paradigm has several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Gap to Algorithmic Reasoning:** By training LLMs to discover and leverage concise, natural language descriptions of procedural and factual knowledge, RLAD moves models closer to identifying and implementing "algorithmic procedures." This is crucial for solving complex problems where simple pattern matching or memorization falls short. It offers a structured way to instill deeper understanding and problem-solving capabilities.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains across diverse and challenging math benchmarks (AIME, DeepScaleR Hard, AMC) are a testament to RLAD's effectiveness. The observed improvement even when no abstractions are provided at inference time ("w/o abs" setting) is particularly impactful. This suggests that the training process with abstractions instills a more fundamental and generalized reasoning ability, making the models more robust.

3. **A New Axis for Scaling Compute:** Existing strategies for scaling LLM reasoning often involve increasing chain-of-thought length (depth) or parallel sampling of solutions (breadth within one strategy). RLAD introduces an "orthogonal axis" for scaling: *increasing the diversity of high-level strategies* via abstractions. The finding that allocating compute to generating diverse abstractions is more beneficial than just sampling more solutions highlights a more strategic and efficient way to utilize computational resources at test time, especially as other scaling methods hit diminishing returns. This can lead to more cost-effective deployment of reasoning LLMs.

4. **Improved Interpretability and Controllability:** Abstractions are expressed in natural language, offering a degree of interpretability into the model's reasoning process. They provide insight into the meta-strategies the model considers. This could also pave the way for better human-AI collaboration, where humans can guide or refine the proposed abstractions, making the reasoning process more transparent and controllable.

5. **Broader Applicability:** While primarily evaluated on mathematical reasoning, the initial success in diverse domains like healthcare, legal reasoning, and web security suggests that the concept of reasoning abstractions is broadly applicable. This could have far-reaching impacts on various specialized AI applications requiring sophisticated reasoning, from scientific discovery to legal analysis and medical diagnostics.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** RLAD opens up several exciting avenues for future work. The challenge of training a single model to both propose and utilize abstractions (rather than two separate models) is a significant next step. Further investigation into *why* training with abstractions improves performance even without them at inference time could uncover fundamental mechanisms of generalization in LLMs. Moreover, exploring how to actively intervene and guide abstraction discovery during RL training could lead to even more efficient and targeted learning.

In conclusion, RLAD offers a paradigm shift in how LLMs can approach complex reasoning. By empowering models to self-discover and utilize high-level procedural and factual guidance, it pushes the boundaries of AI's problem-solving capabilities, promising more intelligent, adaptable, and interpretable AI systems.
