# Hi Robot: Open-Ended Instruction Following with Hierarchical Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Physical Intelligence, Stanford, and UC Berkeley developed "Hi Robot," a hierarchical system that enables robots to understand and execute open-ended natural language instructions, including multi-stage commands and real-time human feedback. The system, which leverages Vision-Language Models at both high and low levels, consistently outperforms baselines in tasks like table bussing and sandwich making, demonstrating improved adaptability and scalability through synthetic data generation.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to understand and react to natural, open-ended human language, including complex multi-stage instructions and real-time corrections.
- Existing robotic systems lack the higher-level reasoning ("System 2") to process intricate commands and dynamically adjust plans based on unforeseen challenges.
- Collecting diverse and sufficient human-robot interaction data for training complex language understanding is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Method

- A hierarchical inference architecture, inspired by System 1 and System 2 cognition, utilizes a high-level VLM for complex instruction interpretation and a low-level VLA model for atomic skill execution.
- The system incorporates real-time user feedback, allowing the high-level policy to immediately recompute the next appropriate atomic command and generate verbal responses grounded in visual context.
- A scalable synthetic data generation scheme uses a large VLM to synthesize plausible complex user prompts and corresponding robot responses, addressing the data bottleneck for training the high-level policy.

## Results

- Hi Robot significantly outperformed baseline methods (GPT-4o and a Flat VLA policy) in Instruction Accuracy and Task Progress across diverse tasks like table bussing and sandwich making.
- The system demonstrated strong situated reasoning, adapting to real-time human feedback (e.g., "that's not trash!") and dynamically adjusting its low-level commands mid-task.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of synthetic data, which improved average Instruction Accuracy by **46%** and Task Progress by **39%**, and validated the hierarchical structure over a flat policy with **34%** higher IA and **19%** higher TP.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical VLM architecture effectively bridges the gap between semantic understanding and physical execution, mirroring System 1 and System 2 cognition.
> - Synthetic data generation from a powerful VLM is a viable and critical method for scaling training data for robust open-ended human-robot interaction.
> - The primary bottleneck for complex robotic tasks often lies in high-level reasoning and human intent interpretation, rather than solely in low-level physical actuation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly moving towards "generalist robots" capable of performing a wide array of tasks in unstructured, human-centric environments. A critical bottleneck in achieving this vision is enabling robots to truly understand and react to natural human communication, which goes far beyond simple, pre-defined commands. This paper directly addresses this challenge by focusing on **open-ended instruction following with hierarchical vision-language-action (VLA) models**.

Historically, robotic instruction following has largely fallen into two categories:
- **Direct Instruction Following:** Methods like language-conditioned imitation learning (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, VLA models like $\pi_0$) have shown impressive capabilities in executing simple, atomic commands such as "pick up the cup." These often correspond to what the authors refer to as "System 1" cognitive processes, which are fast, automatic, and rely on pre-learned skills. While effective for basic tasks, they struggle with complex, multi-stage instructions, dynamic user feedback, or abstract goals.
- **High-Level Planning with LLMs/VLMs:** More recent approaches have utilized Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to reason over observations and break down complex, multi-stage tasks into simpler sub-goals. Projects like SayCan, or more advanced VLM-based methods, can process more intricate commands. However, these systems often operate with pre-defined robot skills, limiting their physical dexterity, or lack the robust ability to incorporate real-time, situated human interaction (corrections, constraints, clarifications) that is common in human-robot collaboration. They represent a step towards "System 2" deliberative reasoning but often lack deep grounding in real-time physical interaction.

"Hi Robot" strategically positions itself by bridging the gap between these two paradigms. It explicitly adopts Daniel Kahneman's "System 1" and "System 2" analogy, presenting a novel hierarchical architecture where:
- The **high-level policy (System 2)**, implemented as a VLM, is responsible for interpreting complex, open-ended natural language prompts, user feedback, and visual context to deduce the *most appropriate next atomic step*.
- The **low-level policy (System 1)**, implemented as a VLA model (specifically building on $\pi_0$ VLA), is responsible for executing that atomic step with physical actions.

This hierarchical decomposition allows "Hi Robot" to combine the semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities of large VLMs with the fine-grained physical dexterity of modern VLA policies. Unlike prior works that either focus solely on atomic commands or use LLMs/VLMs only for high-level skill selection, "Hi Robot" integrates VLMs at *both* levels. Furthermore, a critical innovation lies in its scalable **synthetic data generation scheme**, which uses a large VLM to synthesize plausible complex user prompts and robot responses for training the high-level policy. This addresses the significant data bottleneck for real-world interactive human-robot demonstrations, enabling the system to generalize to diverse and unseen linguistic variations and interactive scenarios.

By demonstrating capabilities like handling intricate multi-stage instructions, real-time situated corrections, and complex user constraints across various robotic platforms (single-arm, dual-arm, mobile bimanual manipulators), "Hi Robot" represents a significant step towards truly flexible and adaptive generalist robots that can seamlessly integrate into human environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to **advance robotic intelligence by enabling robots to interpret and act on diverse natural language commands, feedback, and corrections, thereby achieving human-like adaptability in open-ended, human-centric environments.**

The primary motivations driving this work are:

- **Bridging the Human-Robot Communication Gap:** Current robots struggle to understand the nuances of human language, which often involves complex prompts, implicit meanings, real-time corrections, and dynamic feedback. Achieving a more intuitive and flexible communication channel is essential for effective human-robot collaboration.
- **Enabling Flexible and Steerable Robots:** Instead of rigidly following pre-programmed sequences, robots need to adapt to novel situations, modify behaviors in real-time based on human input, and respond to unforeseen challenges. This flexibility is a hallmark of intelligence and is crucial for generalist robots operating in open-ended settings like homes or offices.
- **Addressing Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:** Real-world tasks are rarely simple "pick up X" commands. They involve multiple steps, conditional logic (e.g., "only take away dishes if they are done eating"), and unexpected interjections ("that's not trash!"). Existing robotic systems often lack the higher-level reasoning ("System 2") required to parse such complexity and dynamically adjust their plans.
- **Leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The recent advancements in VLMs have shown immense potential for grounding language in visual observations. The motivation is to fully exploit these capabilities, not just for basic command following, but for high-level semantic understanding and reasoning in robotic control.
- **Scalability of Data Acquisition:** Collecting sufficient human-robot interaction data for training complex language understanding is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. A key motivation is to develop a method for synthetically generating diverse interaction data to make the training process more scalable and robust.
- **Achieving Robust Generalization:** By enabling robots to process and engage with complex natural language interaction, the authors aim to unlock not only better instruction following but also the ability for users to guide a robot through entirely new tasks and correct its behavior in real time, moving beyond specific task training.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond robots that merely execute atomic commands, towards intelligent agents that can reason through tasks, seamlessly integrate human feedback, and operate with a higher degree of autonomy and adaptability, much like how humans adapt and learn.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Hi Robot" system proposes a **hierarchical inference architecture** for robotic control, fundamentally driven by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) at both high and low levels. This structure is designed to mimic the "System 1" (fast, reactive) and "System 2" (deliberative, reasoning) cognitive processes.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Policy Decomposition

- **High-Level Policy ($p_{hi}$ - System 2):** This policy is a VLM that takes in complex, open-ended user prompts ($l_t$), real-time user interjections, and current visual observations (images from multiple cameras, $I_t$). Its role is to process this rich multimodal input, reason about the overall task and current context, and then generate a *simplified, atomic language command* ($\hat{l}_t$) for the low-level policy to execute. It can also generate verbal responses ($u_t$) for the user. This policy runs at a lower frequency (e.g., every second or upon new user input) as it handles more deliberative reasoning.
- **Low-Level Policy ($p_{lo}$ - System 1):** This policy is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. It receives the atomic language command ($\hat{l}_t$) from the high-level policy, along with detailed current observations (images, robot configuration $q_t$). Its job is to directly output robotic actions ($A_t$, an action chunk) to physically perform the commanded skill. This policy runs at a high frequency to enable smooth and reactive physical control. The authors specifically build on the $\pi_0$ VLA model, which uses flow-matching to produce continuous action chunk distributions.

#### 3.2. Incorporating User Interaction

- The system is designed to be highly interactive. Users can provide textual or spoken commands/feedback at any point.
- Upon receiving an intervention, the high-level inference is immediately triggered to recompute the next appropriate atomic command ($\hat{l}_t$).
- The high-level policy can generate verbal utterances ($u_t$) for the robot (e.g., confirmations, clarifications, apologies), which are converted to speech for the user.
- Critically, the high-level policy's responses are *contextual*, meaning they are grounded in both the language prompt and the current visual observations, allowing it to correctly interpret situated feedback like "that's not trash."

#### 3.3. Data Collection and Training Strategy

A key innovation is the scalable approach to data generation:

1. **Robot Data Collection ($D_{demo}$):** Initial robot demonstrations are collected via teleoperation. These are raw trajectories with coarse overall task annotations (e.g., "make a sandwich").
2. **Human Annotation & Segmentation ($D_{labeled}$):** The raw demonstration episodes are segmented into *short, atomic skills* (e.g., "pick up one piece of lettuce"). This process yields tuples of (atomic skill label $\hat{l}_t$, visual observations $I_t$). This dataset primarily trains the low-level policy.
3. **Synthetic Data Generation ($D_{syn}$):** This is the crucial step for training the high-level policy. A powerful, pre-trained Vision-Language Model ($p_{gen}$) is used to generate synthetic, complex user prompts ($l_t$) and corresponding robot verbal responses ($u_t$).
 - **Prompting $p_{gen}$:** Given visual context ($I_t$) and an atomic skill label ($\hat{l}_t$) from $D_{labeled}$, $p_{gen}$ is prompted to imagine a plausible, complex user interaction that *would have led to that specific atomic skill*. Examples include generating multi-stage instructions, situated corrections, or implicit constraints.
 - **Contextual Grounding:** To ensure realism and coherence, $p_{gen}$ is conditioned on prior skill labels within an episode and guided to incorporate "world knowledge" (e.g., dietary constraints for sandwich making). Scenario and response categorization are used to ensure diversity (e.g., negative tasks, situated corrections, specific constraints).
4. **Policy Training:**
 - **High-Level Policy ($p_{hi}$):** Trained on the combined dataset $D_{syn} \cup D_{labeled}$ using cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction. This teaches it to map complex prompts and observations to appropriate atomic commands and verbal responses.
 - **Low-Level Policy ($p_{lo}$):** Trained on $D_{labeled} \cup D_{demo}$ using a flow-matching objective, as in the $\pi_0$ VLA framework. This teaches it to execute atomic commands into continuous robot actions.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture and Implementation

- Both high-level and low-level policies use **PaliGemma-3B VLM** as their base model, a relatively efficient open-source VLM.
- The low-level policy's specific implementation is $\pi_0$ VLA, which finetunes PaliGemma-3B with an "action expert" to output continuous actions via flow-matching.
- The system includes speech-to-text (Whisper) and text-to-speech (Cartetia API) for voice interaction.
- It is evaluated on three robot platforms: UR5e (single-arm), Bimanual ARX (dual-arm), and Mobile ARX (mobile bimanual manipulator), showcasing its broad applicability.

This modular, VLM-centric hierarchical design, coupled with an innovative synthetic data generation pipeline, enables "Hi Robot" to tackle the complexities of open-ended instruction following and real-time human-robot interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of "Hi Robot" focused on three complex, real-world task domains: **Table Bussing**, **Sandwich Making**, and **Grocery Shopping**, each posing unique physical and linguistic challenges. The system was tested across single-arm, dual-arm, and mobile bimanual robot platforms. Two key metrics were used: **Instruction Accuracy (IA)**, measuring the alignment of the high-level policy's command with human intent, and **Task Progress (TP)**, quantifying task completion.

The core results are summarized as follows:

1. **Superior Performance in Open-Ended Instruction Following:**
 - Hi Robot significantly outperformed baseline methods, including **GPT-4o high-level policy** and a **Flat VLA policy**, across all tasks in both Instruction Accuracy and Task Progress (Figure 5).
 - It demonstrated a remarkable ability to understand and execute complex prompts that involved multiple stages, novel task variations (e.g., "bus only yellowish things"), and dynamic constraints (e.g., "I'm allergic to pickles").
 - Qualitative analysis (Figure 6) showed that GPT-4o often failed to maintain context, misidentified objects, or issued nonsensical commands, highlighting its lack of physical grounding compared to Hi Robot. The Flat VLA, by design, could not react to complex, dynamic prompts.

2. **Strong Situated Reasoning and Adaptation to Feedback:**
 - A key strength of Hi Robot was its ability to incorporate real-time, situated human feedback (e.g., "that's not trash!", "leave it alone") mid-task and adjust its low-level commands accordingly.
 - Unlike GPT-4o, which often struggled to maintain a coherent internal state or prematurely switched tasks, Hi Robot seamlessly updated its plan based on visual observations and verbal interjections. The Flat VLA had no mechanism to react to such feedback.

3. **Effectiveness Across Diverse Tasks and Robot Platforms:**
 - Hi Robot proved its versatility by successfully handling a range of object types (fragile cheese, tall bottles) and respecting dynamic constraints on various robot configurations (single-arm, dual-arm, mobile bimanual).
 - This demonstrated the generalizability of its hierarchical approach and underlying VLA policies to different manipulation and navigation challenges.

4. **Validation of Low-Level Policy and Highlighting Reasoning as Bottleneck:**
 - An "Expert Human High-Level" oracle baseline, where a human manually provided ideal low-level commands, showed that the low-level $\pi_0$ VLA policy could execute tasks nearly flawlessly. This confirmed that the physical actuation capabilities were largely sufficient.
 - This result underscores that the primary bottleneck for complex robotic tasks is often the high-level reasoning and interpretation of human intent, a gap that Hi Robot effectively bridges with its VLM-based high-level policy.

**Ablation Studies:**

The paper also conducted crucial ablation studies to isolate the contributions of synthetic data and the hierarchical structure:

1. **Synthetic Data is Critical for Open-Ended Instruction Following (Figure 7):**
 - Comparing Hi Robot (trained with human + synthetic data) to a variant trained *without* synthetic data revealed a significant performance gap (46% average Instruction Accuracy gap, 39% average Task Progress gap).
 - Without synthetic data, the high-level policy struggled with novel user deviations, clarifications, and constraints (e.g., ignoring "this is not trash" or adding forbidden ingredients). This clearly demonstrates that the synthetic data significantly boosts the system's ability to handle diverse and compositional language prompts, which are difficult to capture solely through human-labeled demonstrations.

2. **Hierarchical Structure Outperforms a Flat Policy (Figure 8):**
 - Comparing Hi Robot to a "Flat VLA with synthetic data" (same data, but no separate high-level reasoning step) showed that the hierarchical approach achieved better performance (34% average Instruction Accuracy gap, 19% average Task Progress gap).
 - The flat model, despite being exposed to synthetic data, often reverted to default behaviors or failed to process partial instructions and mid-task updates coherently. This confirms that explicitly separating the high-level reasoning and low-level control, allowing the high-level policy to re-check prompts and adapt, is beneficial for multi-step task coherence and responsiveness to dynamic user inputs.

In summary, the results firmly establish "Hi Robot" as a robust and flexible system for open-ended instruction following, highlighting the power of a hierarchical VLM-based architecture, particularly when augmented with intelligently generated synthetic interaction data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Hi Robot" system represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI and robotics, with profound implications for the development of truly generalist and human-centric robots.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction

The most immediate impact of "Hi Robot" is its ability to facilitate more natural and intuitive human-robot interaction. By enabling robots to process complex, multi-stage instructions, interpret situated feedback, and respond verbally, the system moves beyond simplistic command-response interactions. This paves the way for a future where humans can guide robots with the same ease and flexibility as they would guide another human, fostering more seamless and productive collaboration in diverse environments.

#### 5.2. Concrete Instantiation of Cognitive Architectures

The paper provides a compelling, VLM-based instantiation of the "System 1" and "System 2" cognitive architecture (Kahneman, 2011) in robotics. This hierarchical decomposition offers a principled way to combine deliberate, semantic reasoning with fast, reactive physical execution. This architectural blueprint could serve as a valuable model for future embodied AI systems aiming to achieve a blend of high-level intelligence and low-level control.

#### 5.3. Scalable Data Paradigm

The novel synthetic data generation scheme is a critical contribution. Acquiring large, diverse datasets of human-robot interactions, especially those involving complex prompts and real-time corrections, is a major bottleneck for robot learning. By demonstrating that a powerful VLM can synthesize plausible interaction data, "Hi Robot" offers a scalable pathway to train robust high-level reasoning capabilities without relying solely on expensive and time-consuming human demonstrations. This could significantly accelerate progress in developing language-conditioned robotic systems.

#### 5.4. Towards Truly Generalist Robots

"Hi Robot" contributes directly to the vision of generalist robots capable of performing a wide range of tasks in open-world settings. Its ability to handle tasks like cleaning, sandwich making, and grocery shopping across diverse robot platforms (single-arm, dual-arm, mobile bimanual) showcases its versatility. This generalization is not just across tasks but also across the linguistic complexity of human instructions, which is paramount for robots operating outside controlled laboratory environments.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research

The authors themselves acknowledge several exciting avenues for future work, stemming directly from the insights gained:
- **Unified Model:** The potential to combine the high-level and low-level VLMs into a single model, with the "System 1" vs. "System 2" distinction emerging purely at inference time, could lead to more compact and efficient architectures.
- **Adaptive Inference and Interleaving:** More intricate interleaving of high-level and low-level processing, where the system dynamically decides when to engage in deliberative reasoning versus reactive execution, could lead to even more flexible and efficient behavior.
- **Improved Awareness Between Layers:** Allowing the high-level policy to be more aware of the low-level policy's capabilities and success rates could enable more robust self-correction and task replanning, addressing current limitations like error accumulation.
- **Long-Context Reasoning and OOD Recovery:** Addressing challenges like instructions requiring long-term memory or graceful recovery from out-of-distribution scenarios remains an open area for further research.

In conclusion, "Hi Robot" pushes the boundaries of robotic intelligence by enabling a more profound and adaptable understanding of human language. Its hierarchical architecture and innovative data synthesis method lay a strong foundation for developing the next generation of generalist robots that can truly live and work alongside humans in complex, dynamic real-world environments.
