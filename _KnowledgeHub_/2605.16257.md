# DexJoCo: A Benchmark and Toolkit for Task-Oriented Dexterous Manipulation on MuJoCo

> [!summary] Summary
> DexJoCo is introduced as a comprehensive benchmark and toolkit for task-oriented dexterous manipulation within the MuJoCo simulator, featuring 11 complex tasks designed to assess fine-grained coordination, tool-use, and bimanual actions. It includes a low-cost human demonstration system and evaluates modern imitation learning policies, revealing current limitations in robustness and task generalization.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous manipulation benchmarks often feature limited task diversity and fail to fully exploit the unique capabilities of multi-fingered hands.
- There is a lack of accessible and reliable systems for collecting high-quality human demonstrations for complex dexterous manipulation tasks.
- Current evaluation frameworks for dexterous manipulation lack standardization in language instructions and data formats, hindering systematic model training and comparison.

## Method

- **DexJoCo** provides 11 functionally grounded tasks that require fine-grained finger coordination, tool-use, bimanual actions, and long-horizon planning.
- A low-cost ==teleoperation system== is developed for human demonstration data collection, utilizing ==Rokoko Smartgloves== and ==HTC Vive trackers== with a ==GeoRT retargeting== algorithm.
- The benchmark offers a standardized evaluation pipeline with uniform data formats and language instructions for training and testing modern imitation learning policies.

## Results

- The DexJoCo benchmark proved challenging, with significant performance drops (e.g., DP-T from **50.4% to 20.0%** average success rate) for policies under full visual randomization.
- **π0.5** achieved the highest average success rates across tasks, while DP-C demonstrated superior performance in specific precision-oriented tasks like 'Unlock iPad' and 'Pinch Tongs'.
- Empirical analysis revealed common failure modes for modern policies, including difficulties with fine-grained interactions, precise insertion, maintaining temporal sequences, and a lack of true language generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current dexterous manipulation policies exhibit limited robustness to visual domain shifts, leading to significant performance degradation under varied visual conditions.
> - Policies struggle with fine-grained actions, precise insertion, and complex temporal sequences, indicating limitations in perception and control for intricate dexterous tasks.
> - Multi-task training of existing policies for dexterous manipulation often leads to performance degradation rather than knowledge transfer, and language grounding in VLA models shows a lack of true generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robot manipulation capabilities, particularly through learning from human demonstrations, has advanced in recent years, with a focus on manipulator-gripper platforms. These efforts have led to the creation of low-cost data collection pipelines and foundation models based on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures. However, achieving human-level manipulation necessitates dexterous hands capable of fine-grained, contact-rich interactions, a domain that presents distinct challenges.

Within this context, the broader research landscape for dexterous manipulation learning is characterized by several gaps that DexJoCo aims to address. Existing evaluation benchmarks for dexterous hands often exhibit limitations:
1. **Limited Applicability of Setups:** Many benchmarks utilize hand-only setups that, while expanding workspace, generate trajectories difficult to replicate in real-world scenarios involving a manipulator arm.
2. **Insufficient Task Diversity:** Current benchmarks frequently focus on in-hand manipulation or simple pick-and-place tasks. These tasks often do not fully exploit the unique capabilities of dexterous hands compared to simpler grippers, thereby restricting the advancement of general manipulation skills.
3. **Challenges in Data Collection:** There is a notable absence of reliable and user-friendly systems for collecting high-quality human demonstrations for dexterous hands. Generating complex dexterous behaviors through conventional motion planning or reinforcement learning often results in behaviors that deviate from natural human patterns.
4. **Lack of Standardization:** Existing dexterous manipulation benchmarks often lack standardized language instructions and unified data formats, complicating systematic training and evaluation of modern VLA models.

DexJoCo positions itself as a response to these limitations by providing a standardized, task-oriented benchmark and toolkit. It seeks to bridge the gap between general robot manipulation research, which often prioritizes gripper systems, and the more complex, high-degree-of-freedom challenges of dexterous hand manipulation. By introducing tasks that highlight fine-grained coordination, tool-use, bimanual actions, and long-horizon planning, DexJoCo contributes to establishing a foundation for systematically evaluating and advancing generalist dexterous robot learning, thereby pushing towards human-level manipulation capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce DexJoCo, a comprehensive benchmark and toolkit for task-oriented dexterous manipulation. This initiative is motivated by the identified shortcomings in existing dexterous manipulation benchmarks and the increasing need for standardized evaluation methodologies to drive progress in this complex field.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Functional Gaps in Existing Benchmarks:** Existing dexterous manipulation benchmarks are frequently limited in the functional diversity of their tasks. Many focus on basic in-hand manipulation or simple pick-and-place, which do not sufficiently demonstrate the unique advantages of dexterous hands over parallel grippers. DexJoCo aims to provide functionally grounded tasks that necessitate fine-grained finger coordination, complex object interactions, and intricate tool-use, thereby emphasizing the distinct capabilities of dexterous hands in scenarios reflecting real-world activities.
- **Facilitating Evaluation of Advanced Manipulation Capabilities:** The paper recognizes the need for benchmarks that assess a broader spectrum of manipulation skills beyond basic object interaction. This includes:
 - **Tool-use:** Tasks requiring robots to manipulate tools effectively.
 - **Bimanual Coordination:** Scenarios where two hands must work in concert, often with asymmetric roles.
 - **Long-horizon Execution:** Tasks involving sequential steps and temporal dependencies.
 - **Reasoning:** Tasks that require logical sequencing or decision-making beyond direct perception.
 The benchmark is designed to systematically evaluate policies across these dimensions.
- **Developing an Accessible Data Collection System:** A significant barrier to progress in dexterous manipulation is the difficulty and cost associated with collecting high-quality human demonstrations. The research is motivated by the need for a low-cost, user-friendly teleoperation system that can efficiently capture natural human hand motions and retarget them to robotic hands, thereby generating physically plausible and consistent manipulation trajectories.
- **Establishing Standardized Data Formats and Evaluation Pipelines:** The proliferation of diverse experimental setups and data formats hinders systematic comparison and training of modern robot learning models, particularly VLA models. DexJoCo seeks to introduce standardized language instructions and unified data formats to streamline policy training and evaluation, ensuring reproducibility and comparability across different research efforts.
- **Identifying Limitations and Guiding Future Research:** By benchmarking modern manipulation policies under diverse settings, including domain randomizations for robustness, the research aims to empirically identify current limitations and common failure modes in dexterous manipulation. This analysis is intended to provide critical insights and highlight key challenges, thereby guiding future research directions in dexterous hand robot learning.

In summary, the core motivation is to accelerate research in dexterous robot manipulation by providing a comprehensive, accessible, and standardized platform that accurately reflects the complexities and unique potential of multi-fingered robotic hands.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexJoCo is implemented as a benchmark and toolkit, structured to provide task environments, data collection tools, policy training interfaces, and evaluation utilities. The methodology encompasses several key components:

#### 3.1. Robot Setup and Observation State

- **Simulation Environment:** The system is built on the MuJoCo physics simulator, selected for its accurate and realistic physics modeling capabilities.
- **Robotic System:** The robot configuration comprises a Rethink Robotics mount, a Franka Panda manipulator, and an Allegro Hand. These components are specified as mature, precisely modeled, and widely adopted in the robotics community.
- **Observations:** The simulation provides rich perceptual observations, including:
 - Third-person and wrist-mounted RGB and RGB-D images.
 - Object poses of interactive entities in the scene.
 - Robot's motion states.
 - Current end-effector pose.
 - Joint angles of the Allegro Hand.
- **Action Space:**
 - Manipulator actions are defined by the target absolute end-effector pose in the world coordinate frame.
 - Hand actions are specified as target absolute joint angles.

#### 3.2. Human Demonstration Data Collection System

- **Hardware Design:** A low-cost and user-friendly teleoperation system was developed.
 - **Hand Motion Capture:** Rokoko Smartgloves are used to capture human hand poses, mitigating occlusion issues common with camera-based methods.
 - **Wrist/End-Effector Tracking:** Two HTC Vive Trackers and two HTC Base Stations track wrist motions to control the Franka end-effector pose.
 - **Integration:** A 3D-printed connector integrates the trackers and gloves. The total hardware cost is approximately $2,300 USD.
- **Teleoperation Algorithm:**
 - **Hand Motion Retargeting:** Due to anatomical differences between human and robotic hands, direct linear mapping is impractical. The GeoRT method, a lightweight self-supervised retargeting algorithm, is employed. It maps human fingertip keypoints (x_H) to Allegro Hand joint positions (q_R = f(x_H)) by minimizing a loss function:
 - L_dir: Preserves fingertip motion directions.
 - L_cover: Enlarges workspace coverage.
 - L_flat: Maintains uniform sensitivity.
 - L_pinch: Preserves pinch behaviors.
 - L_col: Avoids self-collisions.
 This model is trained using only fingertip workspaces recorded during data collection.
 - **Wrist Tracking:** The Vive tracker is physically aligned with the human wrist to match Franka end-effector motions. Initial wrist pose serves as a reference, and subsequent actions are recorded as relative pose changes (delta actions) for robot execution.

#### 3.3. Task Design in the Benchmark

- **Task Formulation:** Each task T is defined by a set of interactive objects O and a set of functional success constraints G. G includes:
 - g_seq: Temporal or sequential execution constraints.
 - g_pose: Target object pose conditions.
 - g_joint: Articulated joint-state requirements.
 - g_contact: Defined collision conditions.
 Task success requires simultaneous satisfaction of all task-dependent constraints.
- **Task Design Principles:** Tasks are constructed to cover diverse dexterous manipulation capabilities based on four core principles:
 - **Functional Interaction:** Tasks involve semantic, everyday human activities with explicit visual feedback for progress.
 - **Dexterity Dependency:** Tasks require fine-grained finger coordination and articulated object interaction, skills beyond parallel grippers.
 - **Long-Horizon Compositionality:** Tasks involve multi-stage execution with temporal dependencies.
 - **Bimanual Coordination:** A subset of tasks requires synchronized bimanual manipulation with asymmetric roles.
- **Task Categories:** Based on these principles, 11 tasks are organized into categories: tool-use, reasoning, bimanual coordination, and long-horizon tasks. Examples include "Water Plant," "Fold Glasses," "Hanoi," and "Microwave Cook."
- **Task Asset Construction:** Base scenes from RoboSuite and robot assets from MuJoCo Menagerie are used. Task-specific objects are curated from RoboCasa and PartNet-Mobility (SAPIEN), or generated with Hunyuan3D and manually assigned physical properties. Visual state changes are incorporated for realism (e.g., water appearing in watering can, iPad buttons highlighting).

#### 3.4. Domain Randomizations

- **Purpose:** To evaluate policy robustness over a broader data distribution and enhance sim-to-real transfer potential.
- **Types of Randomization:**
 - **Dynamics:** Object placement (x, y planar range), table height offset (U(0, 0.05)m), and task-specific object dynamics (mass multipliers, joint friction, stiffness multipliers).
 - **Visual:** Third-person camera poses (50 pre-sampled, occlusion-minimal poses on a spherical surface), scene illumination (light position, direction, diffuse color, headlight ambient/diffuse RGB values), and tabletop textures (sampled from a library).
- **Implementation:** Visual randomization is applied by replaying collected trajectories under different rendering settings, enabling scalable data augmentation without additional teleoperation.

#### 3.5. Imitation Learning Policy Evaluation

- **Baseline Models:** Four modern imitation learning policies are benchmarked:
 - ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers)
 - Diffusion Policy (DP-T: Transformer-based, DP-C: CNN-based)
 - π0.5 (a Vision-Language-Action model)
 - GR00T N1.5 (a Generalist Robot Learning model)
- **Training Regimes:** Policies are trained from scratch (ACT, DP-T, DP-C) using vision and proprioception, or fine-tuned (π0.5, GR00T N1.5) with additional language conditioning. For bimanual tasks, their default 32-dimensional action heads are insufficient, requiring random initialization of extra dimensions (partial pretrain-AH).
- **Action Chunking:** All baselines model action chunking as P(a_t:t+k-1) = π_θ(a_t:t+k-1 | s_t-h+1:t, l), predicting a future k-step action chunk given historical observations s and optional language instruction l.
- **Model Deployment:** An asynchronous inference mechanism, inspired by SmolVLA, is used. The next action chunk is generated while the current one executes, and overlapping chunks are temporally ensembled for smoothness. This setup reflects real-world deployment, emphasizing inference frequency and policy reactivity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of policies on the DexJoCo benchmark revealed several insights into the capabilities and limitations of current dexterous manipulation policies.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Challenge and Robustness

- **High Difficulty:** The DexJoCo benchmark proved to be highly challenging. Some policies exhibited zero success rates on difficult bimanual tasks such as "Unlock iPad," "Hanoi," and "Assembly" under certain conditions.
- **Limited Robustness to Visual Randomization:** Success rates across nearly all policies experienced a sharp decline under "rand-full" conditions (which include visual randomization of camera poses, illumination, and textures), compared to "rand-obj" (object placement and table height randomization only). This indicates limited robustness of current policies to visual domain shifts. For instance, the average success rate across all tasks dropped from 50.4% to 20.0% for DP-T and from 52.5% to 34.1% for π0.5 under full randomization.

#### 4.2. Policy Performance Comparisons

- **π0.5 Performance:** π0.5 achieved the highest average success rates under both "rand-obj" (52.5%) and "rand-full" (34.1%) conditions, suggesting benefits from its large-scale pre-training. It generally dominated single-arm tasks.
- **DP-T Competitiveness:** Despite being a smaller model trained from scratch (approximately 100M parameters), DP-T performed comparably to π0.5 on bimanual tasks. This observation suggests that for tasks requiring expanded action dimensions, the pre-training advantage of π0.5 might diminish when the additional action dimensions are trained from scratch.
- **DP-C for Precision:** DP-C, a CNN-based Diffusion Policy, substantially outperformed other policies on specific tasks requiring precise operations, such as "Unlock iPad" and "Pinch Tongs." This advantage is hypothesized to stem from its use of FiLM for observation injection, which may enhance fine-grained visual perception crucial for precise manipulation.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Failure Modes

Extensive empirical analysis identified common failure patterns:
- **Fine-grained Actions:** In tasks like "Unlock iPad," "Click Mouse," and "Photograph," policies often successfully interacted with the primary object (e.g., grasping the iPad, moving the mouse) but failed to execute precise fine-grained actions like clicking specific buttons. This suggests policies perceive the object but may overlook its interactive elements.
- **Insertion and Alignment:** Insertion steps, as observed in "Assembly" and "Hanoi," posed a high probability of failure, indicating difficulties with precise alignment and force control required for insertion.
- **Temporal Memory and Sequential Control:** In "Pinch Tongs," policies frequently grasped the tongs but failed to perform the subsequent squeezing and releasing actions, pointing to limitations in temporal memory or maintaining complex sequential behaviors.
- **Unintended Object Interaction:** In the "Microwave" task, policies often placed the hot dog inside the microwave but then inadvertently withdrew it along with the hand, indicating a lack of robust release mechanisms or spatial awareness during multi-step interactions.

#### 4.4. Further Evaluations

- **Multi-task Training Degradation:** When trained jointly on all tasks with the same number of steps as single-task training, DP-T experienced performance degradation across every task. While π0.5 saw some improvements on specific tasks, its average success rate also dropped, suggesting that current multi-task training approaches may not effectively transfer knowledge or handle task interference.
- **Robustness to Dynamics Randomization:** Under randomized dynamics parameters (joint friction, stiffness, object mass), π0.5 demonstrated stronger robustness with a higher average success rate compared to DP-T (46.5% vs. 41.6%). This finding supports the utility of the simulated benchmark in approximating real-world performance under varying physical conditions.
- **Importance of Pretrained Action Head:** Retaining pretrained weights for the action head (partial pretrain-AH) generally yielded higher success rates and a better average performance compared to fully random reinitialization of the action head.
- **Lack of Language Generalization:** A detailed analysis of π0.5 on the "Unlock iPad" task, using single-digit passwords (1-5) for training and evaluating on seen digits, arithmetic expressions, and English words, revealed a severe mode collapse. The policy exhibited a fixed action bias, consistently outputting certain digits regardless of the language instruction. Metrics like Normalized Mutual Information (0.018) and average JS divergence (0.026) confirmed a negligible relationship between language input and policy output, indicating a failure to achieve true language generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexJoCo benchmark and toolkit offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robot learning, particularly in dexterous manipulation:

- **Standardized Evaluation Framework:** DexJoCo addresses a critical need for a standardized, comprehensive benchmark for dexterous manipulation. By providing 11 functionally grounded tasks that evaluate unique capabilities like tool-use, bimanual coordination, long-horizon execution, and reasoning, it allows for systematic and comparable evaluation of diverse manipulation policies. This standardization is crucial for tracking progress and identifying limitations in a field often fragmented by disparate experimental setups.
- **Highlighting Unique Dexterous Capabilities:** The benchmark's task design principles explicitly aim to showcase the advantages of dexterous hands over simpler grippers. This focus can drive research towards developing policies that fully leverage the high degrees of freedom and fine-grained contact capabilities of multi-fingered hands, moving beyond tasks that could be accomplished by less complex manipulators.
- **Valuable Data Resource:** The collection of 1.1K human demonstration trajectories in a standardized simulation environment, coupled with support for domain randomization, provides a substantial and high-quality dataset. This dataset is a valuable resource for training imitation learning policies, especially given the scarcity of such data for complex dexterous hand systems. It can facilitate the development and testing of new learning algorithms.
- **Insights into Current Policy Limitations:** The extensive empirical analysis and detailed failure mode breakdown offer concrete insights into the challenges faced by modern manipulation policies. Identifying issues such as failures in fine-grained actions, insertion tasks, temporal memory, and lack of true language generalization provides clear directions for future research. This includes motivating the development of better perception models for interactive elements, more robust control for contact-rich tasks, and improved temporal reasoning.
- **Guidance for Future Research Directions:** The discussion section explicitly outlines several key areas for future investigation:
 - **Need for Dexterous Hand-Centric Foundation Models:** Current VLA models, primarily pre-trained on gripper-based data, exhibit action space mismatches. This highlights the need for embodiment-aware representations and dedicated hand-centric pre-training to improve expressivity and transfer.
 - **Importance of Multi-modal Policies:** Vision-only policies, even with proprioception, are shown to be insufficient for contact-rich manipulation. Incorporating tactile sensing and other modalities is identified as crucial for complete interaction modeling and achieving precision.
 - **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The observed performance drop under domain randomization underscores the importance of enhancing simulation fidelity across physical, visual, and sensing aspects. Future work on systematic sim-to-real alignment, beyond simple randomization, is critical for achieving consistent real-world performance.
- **Accessible Development Toolkit:** The provision of a low-cost and user-friendly teleoperation system, combined with robust retargeting, lowers the barrier to entry for researchers in dexterous manipulation. This accessibility can foster broader participation and innovation in data collection and policy development.

In summary, DexJoCo is positioned to accelerate the development of generalist dexterous robotic manipulation by offering a comprehensive evaluation platform, valuable data, and clear insights into the current state and future challenges of the field.
