# LAGO Policy: Latency-Aware Asynchronous Diffusion Policies with Goal-Directed Collision-Free Planning for Smooth Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> LAGO Policy presents a unified framework for deploying diffusion-based visuomotor policies on physical robots, enabling smooth, temporally consistent, and collision-free manipulation. It addresses the challenges of asynchronous inference latency and unforeseen obstacles, leading to enhanced task success rates and improved motion quality.

## Problem

- Asynchronous inference latency in diffusion policies causes perception-execution misalignment, resulting in inter-chunk discontinuities and jerky robot motions.
- Generative control policies, trained on demonstrations without explicit collision modeling, generate geometrically infeasible trajectories when encountering unforeseen obstacles.
- Existing local, short-horizon safety mechanisms often lead to suboptimal avoidance behaviors, frequent interventions, or state deviations from training distributions.

## Method

- A ==Latency-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance== (LA-CFG) module robustly handles asynchronous inference by decoupling future-action conditioning and employing delay randomization during training.
- A Goal-Prediction Head is integrated with Collision-Free Trajectory Generation, enabling proactive, long-range obstacle avoidance guided by task-relevant goals.
- Spatial-Temporal Trajectory Optimization refines discrete action sequences from either the policy or planner into smooth, continuous, and physically feasible trajectories.

## Results

- The LA-CFG variant achieved higher success rates and better inter-chunk consistency under future-action condition shifts, outperforming previous methods and a non-randomized CFG.
- LAGO Policy consistently demonstrated smoother execution in real-world tasks, achieving lower inter-chunk consistency (CON) on seven tasks and lower integrated squared jerk (ISJ) on all eight tasks compared to a Diffusion Policy backbone.
- In environments with unseen obstacles, LAGO Policy attained the highest success rates (**1.0** for Box and Pen, **0.8** for Cup and Pouring) by generating smooth, collision-free paths towards predicted goals, effectively avoiding reactive short-horizon failure modes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling future-action conditioning and using delay randomization in CFG significantly improves policy robustness to temporal shifts caused by varying inference latencies.
> - A goal-prediction head, attached to the diffusion policy's bottleneck representation, can effectively guide model-based, proactive collision-free planning.
> - Integrating data-driven diffusion policies with model-based trajectory optimization allows for both learned skill generalization and explicit guarantees for smoothness and collision avoidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of visuomotor imitation learning, specifically focusing on the deployment of generative control policies (GCPs) for robotic manipulation. Recent advancements in this area have seen the emergence of diffusion-based policies, which generate multi-step action sequences through iterative denoising. These policies have demonstrated performance across various manipulation tasks.

Despite these developments, practical deployment of diffusion-based GCPs on physical robotic manipulators encounters specific challenges. One significant issue is managing non-negligible inference latency. Policies are commonly deployed using asynchronous inference, where the next action chunk is generated in parallel with the execution of the current one. This approach can lead to a perception-execution misalignment, as actions are conditioned on past observations but executed when the robot and scene states may have changed. This misalignment often results in inter-chunk discontinuities and jerky robot motions, which can hinder the execution of fine manipulation tasks. Previous works, such as SAIL, VLASH, and training-time RTC, have attempted to mitigate this by incorporating future-action or future-state awareness. However, these methods sometimes exhibit brittleness to temporal shifts in conditioning signals or are less compatible with certain diffusion architectures.

A second challenge involves the generation of safe actions in the presence of unforeseen obstacles. Many GCPs are trained on demonstrations without explicitly modeling collision-free feasible sets, making them prone to generating geometrically infeasible trajectories when out-of-distribution obstacles are present, particularly during long-range motions. Existing inference-time safeguards, including collision-cost guidance, control barrier functions (VLSA), or reachability-based safety filters (RAIL), are typically local and short-horizon. These approaches can lead to suboptimal avoidance behaviors, frequent interventions, and may push the robot into states that deviate significantly from the training demonstrations, potentially causing safety-induced distribution shifts and compounding errors during closed-loop execution.

LAGO Policy positions itself as a unified framework designed to address both these issues. It integrates model-based trajectory optimization with diffusion-based GCPs, aiming to enhance smoothness, temporal consistency, and safety during the execution of learned manipulation policies. By tackling these practical deployment bottlenecks, the work contributes to making diffusion-based visuomotor policies more robust and reliable for real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of LAGO Policy is to enable smooth, temporally consistent, and safe execution of diffusion-based visuomotor policies when deployed on physical manipulators, particularly under conditions of asynchronous inference and in environments with potentially unseen obstacles. This overarching goal is motivated by two core challenges identified in the current state of visuomotor imitation learning with generative models:

1. **Ensuring continuous and smooth motion under asynchronous inference latency:**
 - **Problem:** Diffusion-based policies, when deployed on physical robots, often face non-negligible inference latency. To maintain continuous motion, policies are typically executed asynchronously, meaning a new action chunk is generated while the robot is executing the previous one. This asynchronous operation creates a mismatch: the new chunk is conditioned on observations from an earlier time, but executed later, potentially when the robot's state and the environment have changed. This misalignment can lead to "inter-chunk discontinuities" where consecutive action chunks do not seamlessly connect, resulting in jerky, unstable motions that degrade the quality of manipulation, especially for tasks requiring precision.
 - **Motivation:** Existing methods that use future-action conditioning to improve consistency often tightly couple these conditions with visual observations. This coupling makes the policy sensitive to temporal shifts in the future-action signal, which are common due to varying inference latencies. Consequently, these methods can become brittle and still produce discontinuities at chunk boundaries, necessitating a more robust mechanism for maintaining temporal consistency.

2. **Achieving safe action generation in the presence of unseen obstacles:**
 - **Problem:** Generative control policies are primarily trained by imitating expert demonstrations. These demonstrations often do not include varied obstacles or scenarios requiring explicit collision avoidance. As a result, when deployed in cluttered real-world environments with out-of-distribution obstacles, these policies can generate trajectories that lead to collisions or are geometrically infeasible. This issue is particularly critical for long-range goal-reaching motions where local corrections may be insufficient.
 - **Motivation:** Current inference-time safety mechanisms (e.g., local collision-cost guidance or safety filters) are typically short-horizon and local in nature. While they can prevent immediate collisions, they often yield suboptimal avoidance behaviors. Furthermore, these local corrections can push the robot's state away from the distribution observed during training, potentially leading to cascading errors and reduced task performance in closed-loop execution. The motivation is to develop a mechanism that enables coherent, global collision-free planning while maintaining adherence to the learned demonstration manifold.

To address these motivations, LAGO Policy aims to achieve:
- Smooth and consistent transitions between action chunks by making the policy robust to temporal shifts in conditioning.
- The ability to plan collision-free trajectories towards task-relevant goals, even in the presence of novel obstacles.
- Overall enhanced task success rates and improved motion quality in real-world manipulation scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LAGO Policy is proposed as a unified asynchronous action-generation framework that integrates model-based trajectory optimization with a diffusion-based generative control policy. The methodology comprises three main components: Latency-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), a Goal-Prediction Head for Diffusion Policy, and a combination of Collision-Free Trajectory Generation with Spatial-Temporal Trajectory Optimization.

**A. Preliminaries**
The foundation of LAGO Policy is a CNN-based Diffusion Policy, which models the conditional distribution of a future action sequence ($A_t$) given recent observations ($O_t$). Observations consist of visual inputs ($v_t$) and robot states ($x_t$), encoded into conditioning vectors ($C_t$). The policy utilizes a DDPM denoising formulation, with the reverse process parameterized by a noise-prediction network ($\epsilon_\theta$). During inference, DDIM sampling is used, and actions are executed in a receding-horizon manner, where only the first $T_a$ actions of a predicted $T_p$-step chunk are executed before replanning.

**B. Latency-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance (LA-CFG)**
This component addresses inter-chunk consistency under asynchronous inference and temporal shifts in conditioning.
- **Problem with existing CFG:** Conventional future-action-conditioned CFG methods assume temporal alignment between the future-action condition ($A_c^t$) and the target action chunk during training. However, inference latency can shift the effective $A_c^t$ at deployment, leading to misalignment and discontinuities. Previous approaches like SAIL concatenate $A_c^t$ directly with the observation history, making the policy sensitive to these shifts.
- **LAGO Policy's Approach:**
 1. **Decoupled Conditioning:** LAGO Policy treats $A_c^t$ as a separate conditioning variable and injects it through CFG during sampling. This decouples $A_c^t$ from the observation features, mitigating the adverse effects of shifted conditioning on the denoising process.
 2. **Delay Randomization:** To improve robustness to temporal shifts, a delay randomization scheme is introduced during training. An offset $\delta$ (sampled from a distribution $P_\delta$) is applied to shift $A_c^t$ relative to the target action chunk. This exposes the denoiser to varied temporal misalignments, making the policy more robust at deployment.
 3. **CFG Dropout:** The CFG dropout scheme is employed to train a single noise-prediction network capable of both conditional and unconditional denoising.

**C. Goal-Prediction Head for Diffusion Policy**
This module provides a task-relevant objective to guide collision-free planning.
- **Mechanism:** A goal-prediction head (a multi-layer perceptron, MLP) is attached to the bottleneck representation ($h_t$) of the denoising U-Net. This representation, derived from the lowest-resolution stage of the U-Net encoder, aggregates global context and coarse motion intent. Global average pooling (GAP) is applied to $h_t$ to produce a fixed-length feature for goal prediction.
- **Training:** The goal prediction head is trained jointly with the diffusion policy, minimizing a regression loss ($L_{goal}$) between the predicted goal ($\hat{g}_t$) and a ground-truth task-relevant goal ($g^*_t$) extracted from expert demonstrations. The overall loss is a weighted sum of the diffusion denoising loss ($L_{DP}$) and $L_{goal}$.

**D. Collision-Free Trajectory Generation**
This component enables proactive obstacle avoidance by planning motions towards the predicted goal.
- **Trigger:** Trajectory optimization is activated if a line-of-sight collision check between the current end-effector position ($x_{ee}^t$) and the predicted goal ($\hat{g}_t$) detects an obstacle, or if the distance ($d_t$) to the goal exceeds a predefined threshold ($d_{th}$).
- **Trajectory Parameterization:** The end-effector position trajectory is parameterized by a uniform B-spline curve $\Phi(t)$, with control points $Q = \{Q_i\}$ as decision variables.
- **Optimization Objective:** The optimization minimizes a weighted sum of three costs:
 1. **Smoothness Cost ($J_s$):** Penalizes high-order derivatives of the spline for smooth motion.
 2. **Collision Cost ($J_c$):** Enforces obstacle clearance. For each colliding segment of an initial direct trajectory, an A*-generated guiding path ($\Gamma$) is used. Control points on the colliding segment are associated with anchor-direction pairs ({$p_{ij}, v_{ij}$}), where $v_{ij}$ is a repulsive direction orthogonal to the spline and oriented towards $\Gamma$. A differentiable penalty function ($\phi$) based on signed distance and a safety margin is applied for violation of clearance ($s_f = r_{ee} + s_m$).
 3. **Feasibility Cost ($J_f$):** Penalizes violations of physical limits (e.g., joint limits, velocity limits).
- **Optimization:** The optimization is solved to obtain the optimized trajectory $\Phi^*(t)$.

**E. Spatial-Temporal Trajectory Optimization**
This module refines any discrete action sequence, whether from the diffusion policy or the collision-free planner, to ensure smooth and feasible execution.
- **Mechanism:** The discrete action sequence is treated as a set of action keypoints. A continuous-time action trajectory $A(t)$ is parameterized as a polynomial.
- **Optimization Objective:** The refinement minimizes a weighted sum of:
 1. **Control Effort ($J_E$):** Penalizes high-order derivatives of the action trajectory.
 2. **Time Regularization ($J_T$):** Regularizes the execution duration.
 3. **Feasibility Penalty ($J_P$):** Penalizes violations of action, velocity, and acceleration limits, transcribed as a differentiable penalty.
- **Solution:** The optimization is efficiently solved using MINCO, and the resulting optimized continuous trajectory is resampled for execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of LAGO Policy was evaluated through real-world experiments addressing three main questions regarding robustness to shifts, smoothness, and obstacle avoidance.

**A. Robustness to Future-Action Condition Shifts (Q1)**
Experiments on the Box Organizing task, which is sensitive to inter-chunk continuity, evaluated three variants under simulated future-action condition shifts (0–4 steps): SAIL, OURS-CFG (LAGO Policy's CFG without delay randomization), and OURS-LA-CFG (full LAGO Policy's CFG with delay randomization).
- **SAIL vs. OURS-CFG:** OURS-CFG exhibited higher success rates (SR) and a smaller increase in inter-chunk consistency (CON) compared to SAIL as the shift increased. This indicated that decoupling the future-action condition from observation features in CFG improved robustness.
- **OURS-CFG vs. OURS-LA-CFG:** OURS-LA-CFG maintained higher SR and showed a smaller change in CON than OURS-CFG as the shift increased. This result demonstrated that delay randomization during training significantly enhances robustness to temporally shifted future-action conditions, leading to more consistent inter-chunk execution at deployment.

**B. Smooth Execution in Real-World Manipulation (Q2)**
The motion quality of LAGO Policy (with its goal-directed planning module disabled) was compared against a Diffusion Policy (DP) backbone across eight real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** LAGO Policy achieved lower CON on seven tasks and lower integrated squared jerk (ISJ) on all eight tasks compared to Diffusion Policy. Lower CON indicates improved inter-chunk continuity, while lower ISJ signifies smoother execution trajectories.
- **Qualitative Observations:** LAGO Policy was observed to be more robust to action ambiguity in tasks like Screw Sorting, where similar observations might otherwise lead to hesitant, stop-and-go motions. In contact-rich tasks such as Towel Folding and Pouring, LAGO Policy improved gripper command continuity, leading to more stable contact maintenance and reduced object drops or spills.
- **Ablation Study:** Removing the spatial-temporal trajectory optimization module from LAGO Policy on the Box Organizing task resulted in an increase in CON to 0.041 and ISJ to 40.03, indicating that this module contributes to both inter-chunk continuity and trajectory smoothness.

**C. Goal-Directed Obstacle Avoidance and Task Success (Q3)**
Four tasks were modified to include unseen obstacles that were absent from training demonstrations. Three variants were compared: NO-AVOID (direct policy execution), LOCAL (local safety filter), and GOAL-DIRECTED (full LAGO Policy with goal prediction and planning).
- **NO-AVOID:** This baseline frequently collided with obstacles, resulting in low success rates across all four tasks, with a 0% success rate in Cup Transfer due to liquid spillage from minor contact.
- **LOCAL:** While reducing direct collisions, this method's short-horizon corrections often pushed the robot into out-of-distribution states. This led to unstable behaviors, such as abrupt gripper commands and jerky motions, which hindered task completion, for example, causing a teapot to tip over during Pouring.
- **GOAL-DIRECTED (LAGO Policy):** This method achieved the highest success rates (1.0 for Box and Pen, 0.8 for Cup and Pouring). It generated smooth, collision-free trajectories towards the predicted task goal, and then transferred control back to the diffusion policy for the final interaction. This approach avoided the failure modes associated with reactive, short-horizon corrections, leading to more robust task completion in cluttered environments.

**D. Goal-Directed Planning for Long-Range Motion**
Observations on tasks involving long-range motions, where demonstrations often contained piecewise, axis-aligned motions (due to keyboard teleoperation), revealed an additional benefit. Goal-directed planning facilitated the generation of smooth, direct trajectories towards the task-relevant goal. This resulted in a substantial reduction in travel distance and task completion time compared to directly executing the policy-learned piecewise motions, as demonstrated in Fig. 8.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LAGO Policy presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by directly addressing critical practical challenges associated with deploying diffusion-based visuomotor policies in real-world environments. The work's significance lies in its ability to enhance the reliability, safety, and efficiency of learned robotic skills, particularly under conditions that are common in physical robot operation.

The key aspects of its potential impact include:

- **Improved Reliability of Diffusion Policies:** By introducing latency-aware classifier-free guidance, LAGO Policy provides a robust solution for maintaining inter-chunk consistency and smooth motion under asynchronous inference. This directly tackles the problem of jerky and discontinuous movements, which can degrade the performance of fine manipulation tasks and reduce overall system reliability. Making policies robust to inference latency shifts is crucial for their practical deployment beyond controlled laboratory settings.
- **Enhanced Safety in Cluttered Environments:** The integration of a goal-prediction head with collision-free trajectory generation allows LAGO Policy to perform proactive, globally coherent obstacle avoidance. This approach is demonstrated to be more effective than local, short-horizon safety filters, which can lead to suboptimal behaviors and state distribution shifts. The ability to safely navigate unseen obstacles expands the applicability of learned policies to more complex and unstructured environments.
- **Bridging Data-Driven and Model-Based Approaches:** LAGO Policy effectively combines the strengths of data-driven imitation learning (through diffusion policies) with model-based trajectory optimization. This hybrid approach leverages the generalization capabilities of learned policies while incorporating explicit guarantees for smoothness and collision avoidance, which are often challenging for end-to-end learning methods alone.
- **Increased Task Success Rates:** The empirical results demonstrate that LAGO Policy achieves higher task success rates across a range of manipulation tasks, particularly in scenarios involving unseen obstacles or requiring precise, smooth motions. This indicates that the proposed framework can enable robots to reliably perform more challenging and complex manipulation tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Advancements:** The framework establishes a foundation for future research in several directions, as indicated by the authors. Extending current task-space collision handling to whole-body collision-aware motion planning and improving task-relevant goal prediction in more complex manipulation settings represent logical next steps. This suggests that LAGO Policy is not a standalone solution but a component in an evolving research trajectory for robust and intelligent robotic manipulation.

In summary, LAGO Policy's contributions could facilitate broader adoption of diffusion-based visuomotor policies in diverse applications, from industrial automation to service robotics, where smooth, safe, and reliable manipulation is paramount.
