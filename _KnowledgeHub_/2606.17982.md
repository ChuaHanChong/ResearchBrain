# LAGO Policy: Latency-Aware Asynchronous Diffusion Policies with Goal-Directed Collision-Free Planning for Smooth Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> LAGO Policy presents a unified framework for deploying diffusion-based visuomotor policies on physical robots, enabling smooth, temporally consistent, and collision-free manipulation. It addresses the challenges of asynchronous inference latency and unforeseen obstacles, leading to enhanced task success rates and improved motion quality.

## Problem

- Asynchronous inference latency in diffusion policies causes perception-execution misalignment, resulting in inter-chunk discontinuities and jerky robot motions.
- Generative control policies, trained on demonstrations without explicit collision modeling, generate geometrically infeasible trajectories when encountering unforeseen obstacles.
- Existing local, short-horizon safety mechanisms often lead to suboptimal avoidance behaviors, frequent interventions, or state deviations from training distributions.

## Method

- A ==Latency-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance== (LA-CFG) module robustly handles asynchronous inference by decoupling future-action conditioning and employing delay randomization during training.
- A Goal-Prediction Head is integrated with Collision-Free Trajectory Generation, enabling proactive, long-range obstacle avoidance guided by task-relevant goals.
- Spatial-Temporal Trajectory Optimization refines discrete action sequences from either the policy or planner into smooth, continuous, and physically feasible trajectories.

## Results

- The LA-CFG variant achieved higher success rates and better inter-chunk consistency under future-action condition shifts, outperforming previous methods and a non-randomized CFG.
- LAGO Policy consistently demonstrated smoother execution in real-world tasks, achieving lower inter-chunk consistency (CON) on seven tasks and lower integrated squared jerk (ISJ) on all eight tasks compared to a Diffusion Policy backbone.
- In environments with unseen obstacles, LAGO Policy attained the highest success rates (**1.0** for Box and Pen, **0.8** for Cup and Pouring) by generating smooth, collision-free paths towards predicted goals, effectively avoiding reactive short-horizon failure modes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling future-action conditioning and using delay randomization in CFG significantly improves policy robustness to temporal shifts caused by varying inference latencies.
> - A goal-prediction head, attached to the diffusion policy's bottleneck representation, can effectively guide model-based, proactive collision-free planning.
> - Integrating data-driven diffusion policies with model-based trajectory optimization allows for both learned skill generalization and explicit guarantees for smoothness and collision avoidance.
