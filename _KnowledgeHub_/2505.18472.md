# ManiFeel: Benchmarking and Understanding Visuotactile Manipulation Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiFeel introduces the first comprehensive simulation benchmark for visuotactile robotic manipulation, providing a standardized platform for learning and evaluating policies across diverse contact-rich tasks. The framework demonstrates that tactile sensing consistently enhances manipulation performance, with specific tactile modalities offering task-dependent benefits, and validates its findings through sim-to-real consistency.

## Problem

- Supervised visuomotor policies, while strong, struggle in scenarios with limited visual information or contact-rich interactions that require precise object property perception.
- The field of visuotactile policy learning lacks standardized, reproducible simulation benchmarks for rigorous evaluation and comparison, hindering systematic progress.
- Accurate simulation of high-fidelity tactile sensing is challenging, often forcing prior research into expensive real-world experiments without scalable, controlled evaluations.

## Method

- ManiFeel introduces a comprehensive simulation benchmark in IsaacGym with 13 diverse visuotactile manipulation tasks, including insertion, screwing, and exploration.
- It integrates high-fidelity simulated GelSight R1.5 tactile sensors (TacRGB for images, TacFF for force fields) quantitatively validated against real-world data, alongside visual and proprioceptive inputs.
- A modular evaluation pipeline enables systematic benchmarking of different sensing modalities, pre-trained tactile representation encoders (e.g., UniT, T3, AnyTouch), and policy architectures (Diffusion Policy, Flow Matching).

## Results

- Adding tactile sensing improved peg insertion success by 26 percentage points with TacFF and object search by approximately 17 percentage points with TacRGB in simulation.
- Sim-to-real validation demonstrated that performance trends observed in the benchmark consistently reproduced in physical environments, for example, gear assembly success increased by 14 percentage points with TacFF.
- Different policy architectures showed varying strengths, with Equivariant Diffusion Policy performing strongly in visually guided tasks and standard Diffusion Policy demonstrating robustness when visual cues were unreliable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile sensing consistently improves policy performance, particularly in contact-rich and visually challenging tasks, acting as a crucial complement to visual data.
> - The optimal tactile modality is task-dependent: Tactile Force-Field (TacFF) excels in force-sensitive and precise alignment tasks, while Tactile RGB (TacRGB) is more effective for texture/geometry perception under visual degradation.
> - Pre-trained tactile encoders generally outperform scratch-trained ones, but existing models lack universal generalization across diverse manipulation scenarios, indicating a need for more robust tactile representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has witnessed significant advancements through supervised visuomotor policy learning, where robots learn complex tasks by imitating human demonstrations using primarily visual inputs. Frameworks like Diffusion Policy and large-scale policies integrating vision-language models have demonstrated strong performance in tasks with rich visual cues. However, this paper identifies a critical limitation: vision-only policies struggle in scenarios with limited or degraded visual information, such as confined spaces, dim lighting, or tasks requiring precise perception of object properties and contact interactions. In these situations, tactile feedback becomes indispensable, offering complementary information about contact geometry, force distribution, and subtle interaction dynamics that vision cannot provide.

The integration of visual and tactile modalities has emerged as a promising direction. Recent studies have leveraged high-resolution vision-based tactile sensors (e.g., GelSight) and other tactile sensing modalities (e.g., tactile arrays, force-torque sensors) to enhance manipulation performance in contact-rich and visually challenging environments. Despite this progress, the broader research landscape for visuotactile policy learning significantly lags behind visual imitation learning in terms of standardized and reproducible simulation benchmarks. Visual imitation learning benefits from robust platforms supporting large-scale experimentation and statistically meaningful comparisons, which have driven rapid progress. In contrast, accurate simulation of high-fidelity tactile sensing is challenging, leading most prior visuotactile research to report results directly in real-world environments without the benefits of controlled, consistent, and scalable simulation-based evaluation.

ManiFeel directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to provide the visuotactile domain with a comprehensive, reproducible, and scalable simulation benchmark akin to those available for visual imitation learning. By establishing standardized tasks, evaluation metrics, and a modular framework, ManiFeel seeks to accelerate the systematic development, rigorous evaluation, and comparative analysis of robust visuotactile manipulation policies, thereby filling a crucial void in the robot learning ecosystem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ManiFeel stems from the recognized lack of a comprehensive and reliable simulation benchmark for supervised visuotactile policy learning. While supervised visuomotor policies excel with ample visual data, their limitations in visually-deprived or contact-intensive tasks highlight the necessity of tactile feedback. The rapid progress in visual imitation learning is attributed to high-quality, reproducible simulation benchmarks, a resource conspicuously absent in the visuotactile domain.

The key objectives of ManiFeel are:
1. **Introduce a Reproducible and Scalable Simulation Benchmark:** To provide a standardized platform for systematically studying supervised visuotactile policy learning, enabling large-scale and rigorous evaluation.
2. **Offer a Diverse Suite of Tasks:** To create a collection of contact-rich and visually challenging manipulation tasks (insertion, assembly, object exploration, sorting) that require tactile feedback for robust performance.
3. **Develop a Modular Evaluation Pipeline:** To design a framework that allows for systematic comparison and integration of different sensing modalities (vision-only, vision+TacRGB, vision+TacFF), tactile representations (ResNet18, UniT, T3, AnyTouch), and policy architectures (Diffusion Policy, Equivariant Diffusion Policy, Flow Matching).
4. **Conduct Extensive Empirical Analysis:** To perform comprehensive experiments to demonstrate how tactile sensing enhances policy performance, reveal task-dependent strengths of different tactile modalities, and identify key design principles and open challenges for robust visuotactile policy learning.
5. **Validate in Real-World Settings:** To confirm that the simulation benchmark provides a reliable and meaningful foundation for benchmarking and future policy development by reproducing key results in physical environments.
6. **Foster Reproducibility and Future Research:** To open-source the codebase, datasets, training logs, and pretrained checkpoints, thereby lowering the barrier to entry and accelerating progress toward generalizable visuotactile policy learning.

In essence, ManiFeel aims to provide the necessary infrastructure to systematically understand and advance visuotactile manipulation, moving beyond ad-hoc real-world experiments to enable principled research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ManiFeel introduces a multi-faceted methodology centered around a novel simulation benchmark, a diverse task suite, high-fidelity tactile sensing, and a modular policy evaluation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Task Suite and Dataset

The benchmark features a diverse suite of 13 manipulation tasks, implemented in the IsaacGym simulator, with 4 representative tasks replicated in real-world settings. These tasks are categorized into three groups:
- **Insertion Tasks:** Including peg, USB, power plug insertion, and gear assembly (simulated and real). These tasks demand precise alignment and constrained motion, often with partial visual occlusion of the contact interface.
- **Screwing Tasks:** Such as nut-bolt assembly and lightbulb installation (simulated and real). These involve rotational motion, torque-sensitive interactions, and often occluded threading interfaces.
- **Exploration Tasks:** Including peg reorientation (occluded), object search (fully occluded), and ball sorting (simulated and real, under normal and dim light). These tasks emphasize tactile interaction for object localization, reorientation, and identification when visual information is limited or ambiguous.

Human demonstration datasets are collected using a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse for supervised policy learning. The process emphasizes the operator's reliance on real-time tactile feedback, especially in challenging visual conditions, to ensure high-quality, contact-rich demonstrations.

#### 3.2. Observation Modalities

ManiFeel is built on IsaacGym with TacSL for high-fidelity simulation of GelSight R1.5 vision-based tactile sensors. It supports:
- **Visual Modality (I_vis):** Various camera configurations (front, side, wrist-mounted) and conditions (occluded, degraded light).
- **Tactile Modalities:**
 - **Tactile RGB images (TacRGB):** High-resolution images capturing surface deformation patterns, providing texture and geometry information.
 - **Tactile Force-Field (TacFF):** Spatial force-field data describing distributed normal and shear forces, encoding contact pressure and directional forces.
- **Proprioception (o_prop):** Robot end-effector position and orientation.

Crucially, the simulated tactile data (TacRGB and TacFF) are quantitatively validated against real GelSight R1.5 measurements using Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID) metrics. Low FID and KID scores (below 7 and 8x10^-2 respectively) confirm a strong statistical similarity between simulated and real tactile observations, validating ManiFeel's realism.

#### 3.3. Modular Pipeline Design

The core of ManiFeel's approach is its modular evaluation pipeline, designed to systematically analyze the impact of different components on visuotactile policy learning:
1. **Sensing Modalities:** Policies are benchmarked under three configurations: vision-only, vision + TacRGB, and vision + TacFF. This pairwise comparison strategy is chosen to isolate and clearly interpret the contribution of each tactile modality. A multimodal observation encoder architecture fuses visual, tactile, and proprioceptive inputs into a concatenated latent representation for the policy network.
2. **Tactile Representations (for TacRGB):** Four different tactile encoders are benchmarked to extract features from high-resolution tactile images:
 - ResNet18 (trained from scratch)
 - UniT (pre-trained, generalizable, lightweight)
 - T3 (pre-trained on large-scale datasets, vision transformer backbone)
 - AnyTouch (pre-trained on multi-sensor data, ViT-Large backbone)
3. **Policy Architectures:** Three representative policy types are evaluated for action generation:
 - Diffusion Policy (DP)
 - Equivariant Diffusion Policy (EquiDP)
 - Flow Matching (FM)

#### 3.4. Experimental Evaluation

Experiments are conducted across the diverse task suite, systematically varying sensing configurations, tactile encoders, and policy architectures. Success rates are the primary metric, averaged over numerous rollouts, multiple random seeds, and environment initializations to ensure statistical significance. Real-world experiments using a Franka Emika Panda manipulator with a GelSight R1.5 sensor are performed for representative tasks to validate simulation-to-real consistency. The codebase, datasets, and pretrained checkpoints are made open-source to promote reproducibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ManiFeel's extensive experiments yield several significant findings that clarify the role of tactile sensing in manipulation policy learning.

#### 4.1. A. Modality Benchmarking

- **Tactile sensing consistently enhances performance:** Across nine diverse simulation tasks, adding tactile sensing (either TacRGB or TacFF) generally improves policy success rates, particularly in contact-rich (e.g., insertion, screwing) and visually challenging (e.g., occluded exploration, dim light sorting) scenarios. This confirms the complementary nature of tactile feedback.
- **Task-dependent strengths of tactile modalities:**
 - **TacFF** (tactile force-field) provides the most benefit for tasks requiring precise contact alignment, geometric fitting, and force regulation (e.g., peg insertion success increased by 26 percentage points). This is due to its physically grounded information on distributed normal and shear forces.
 - **TacRGB** (tactile RGB images) is more effective for tasks relying on perceiving surface texture and geometry, especially under visual degradation (e.g., object search improved by ~17 percentage points, ball sorting under dim light by ~15 percentage points). Its high-resolution images capture detailed surface deformation.
- **Not always beneficial:** In some cases (e.g., gear assembly with TacRGB), adding tactile input can slightly degrade performance if the information is not task-relevant or introduces noise. This highlights the importance of appropriate modality selection.

#### 4.2. B. Policy Benchmarking

- **General benefits of tactile sensing across architectures:** All three benchmarked policy architectures (Diffusion Policy, Equivariant Diffusion Policy, Flow Matching) exhibit similar trends: TacFF benefits contact-rich tasks, while TacRGB benefits exploration tasks.
- **Architectural differences:**
 - **Equivariant Diffusion Policy (EquiDP)** performs strongly in visually guided insertion and screwing tasks, leveraging its equivariant encoder for efficient spatial symmetry capture from camera views.
 - **Standard Diffusion Policy (DP)** demonstrates robustness when visual cues are unreliable and shows effective fusion with high-fidelity tactile sensing for tactile-centric tasks.
 - **Flow Matching (FM)** generally performs worse than DP and EquiDP in most tasks.
- **Sensitivity to TacRGB:** DP can be more sensitive to variability introduced by TacRGB, showing performance drops in certain tasks where EquiDP and FM exhibit more robustness. This reinforces that tactile modality selection should align with task demands before considering architectural nuances.

#### 4.3. C. Tactile Representation Benchmarking

- **Pre-trained encoders generally outperform scratch-trained:** Pre-trained tactile encoders like UniT and T3 consistently achieve higher success rates than a ResNet18 trained from scratch, particularly in dynamic contact reasoning or texture-dependent recognition tasks (e.g., object search, ball sorting).
- **Inconsistency in generalization:** While UniT shows balanced performance, other pre-trained models like AnyTouch struggle with force-sensitive control tasks, suggesting that existing tactile encoders may still be specialized and lack universal generalization across diverse manipulation scenarios.
- **Need for robust tactile representations:** The performance variation highlights an ongoing challenge: developing tactile representation models that are as generalizable and transferable as visual encoders in policy learning.

#### 4.4. D. Real-world Evaluation

- **Sim-to-real consistency:** The overall performance trends observed in ManiFeel's simulation benchmark are consistently reproduced in real-world experiments across gear assembly, bulb installation, and ball sorting (normal and dim light). This validates ManiFeel's fidelity and its ability to capture representative and transferable manipulation behaviors.
- **Tactile benefits reaffirmed:** In the real world, TacFF significantly improves force-sensitive tasks (e.g., gear assembly success increased by 14 percentage points with TacFF), and TacRGB enhances object perception in visually degraded conditions (e.g., ball sorting under normal light success increased from 33% to 80% with TacRGB).
- **Quantitative discrepancies:** While trends align, absolute success rates show some differences due to inherent sim-to-real gaps (e.g., camera noise, tactile signal quality, unmodeled contact dynamics).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManiFeel represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a critical infrastructure gap. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Accelerating Visuotactile Research:** By providing the first comprehensive, reproducible, and scalable simulation benchmark for supervised visuotactile policy learning, ManiFeel establishes a standardized platform that can accelerate research in this complex domain. Similar to how benchmarks propelled visual imitation learning, ManiFeel is poised to foster systematic development, rigorous evaluation, and fair comparisons of novel visuotactile algorithms.
2. **Deeper Understanding of Multimodal Integration:** The modular design of ManiFeel allows researchers to systematically dissect the contributions of different sensing modalities, tactile representations, and policy architectures. This enables a deeper understanding of "what works where" in visuotactile learning, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to principled insights.
3. **Practical Design Principles:** The extensive empirical analysis provides concrete design principles for future visuotactile policies:
 - **Task-specific modality selection:** Prioritizing TacFF for force-sensitive tasks and TacRGB for texture/geometry-dependent tasks, rather than simply adding all available sensory data.
 - **Consideration of policy architecture:** Recognizing that architectures like Diffusion Policy and Equivariant Diffusion Policy offer different strengths depending on the task's reliance on visual symmetry or robustness to unreliable visual cues.
 - **Importance of tactile representation quality:** Highlighting the value of pre-trained tactile encoders for enhancing policy performance, while also pointing out their current limitations in universal generalization.
4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The robust sim-to-real validation, demonstrating consistent trends between simulated and real-world results, reinforces ManiFeel's reliability as a predictive benchmark. This is crucial for iterating quickly in simulation before expensive real-world deployment, even while acknowledging remaining quantitative discrepancies.
5. **Open Science and Reproducibility:** The open-sourcing of ManiFeel's codebase, datasets, training logs, and pretrained checkpoints is a substantial contribution to the research community. This lowers the barrier to entry for new researchers, encourages transparent experimental protocols, and promotes cumulative progress towards more generalizable and robust visuotactile robotic manipulation in diverse real-world scenarios.
6. **Highlighting Open Challenges:** ManiFeel also clearly identifies key open challenges, such as the need for more universal and generalizable tactile representations that can adapt to diverse contact dynamics and task objectives, and the development of adaptive, task-aware multimodal fusion architectures.

In conclusion, ManiFeel provides a foundational toolset that will undoubtedly stimulate significant advancements in visuotactile robot learning, enabling robots to perform more dexterous and robust manipulation in scenarios where vision alone falls short.
