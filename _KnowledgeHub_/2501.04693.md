# Beyond Sight: Finetuning Generalist Robot Policies with Heterogeneous Sensors via Language Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces FuSe, a finetuning method that enables pre-trained generalist robot policies to incorporate heterogeneous sensory inputs such as touch and audio through language grounding. The approach demonstrates improved task performance, novel multimodal prompting, and compositional reasoning in real-world robot manipulation, even with limited data for new sensor modalities.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art generalist robot policies (e.g., Octo, RT-2) primarily rely on vision, limiting their effectiveness in visually occluded or ambiguous environments.
- Integrating heterogeneous sensors like touch and audio into these policies is challenging due to the scarcity of large-scale, paired datasets with low-level robot actions.
- Robots currently lack comprehensive multi-sensory interaction capabilities crucial for robust manipulation in complex physical environments, unlike humans.

## Method

- FuSe finetunes existing pre-trained, vision-centric generalist robot policies (like Octo and PaliGemma VLA) by adding specialized encoders for tactile images and audio spectrograms.
- It introduces two auxiliary language grounding losses—a multimodal contrastive loss and a multimodal generative loss—to align new sensor modalities with the policy's existing semantic knowledge.
- The method uses a weighted sum of behavioral cloning loss for action prediction and the auxiliary language grounding losses to effectively learn from smaller, specialized multimodal datasets.

## Results

- FuSe achieved over 60% success rate across real-world robot manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming vision-only finetuning and scratch-trained baselines, particularly in visually occluded scenarios.
- The policy demonstrated novel multimodal prompting capabilities (e.g., grasping based on combined visual and tactile descriptions) and performed complex compositional cross-modal reasoning.
- The approach successfully integrated heterogeneous sensors into both Octo and PaliGemma VLA architectures, and an ablation study confirmed the necessity of both proposed auxiliary language grounding losses for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language provides an effective common ground for integrating and semantically understanding disparate heterogeneous sensor modalities within pre-trained generalist robot policies.
> - Auxiliary language grounding losses are crucial for effectively leveraging limited multimodal data, enabling robust cross-modal reasoning and preventing over-reliance on pre-trained visual modalities.
> - Finetuning powerful pre-trained generalist policies is a more data-efficient approach for multimodal integration compared to training new policies from scratch, capitalizing on vast existing knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has made remarkable progress with the advent of "generalist robot policies," such as Octo, RT-1, RT-2, and Open-VLA. These policies leverage massive datasets of robot trajectories (like Open X-Embodiment, OXE) and large transformer architectures to learn a wide range of tasks from visual and proprioceptive observations. They aim to generalize across various robots and tasks, significantly reducing the need for task-specific engineering.

However, a fundamental limitation of these state-of-the-art generalist policies is their predominant reliance on **vision** as the primary sensing modality. While vision provides rich information, real-world interaction is inherently multi-sensory. Humans and animals constantly integrate touch, sound, proprioception, and other senses to effectively navigate and manipulate objects, especially in situations where vision is occluded or ambiguous (e.g., reaching into a bag, identifying objects by feel).

This paper addresses a crucial gap: how to empower these visually-dominant generalist policies with **heterogeneous sensor modalities** like touch and audio, for which large-scale, pre-existing datasets are scarce. Current multimodal robotics research often faces challenges due to this data scarcity, particularly for datasets that include both diverse sensory inputs and corresponding low-level robot actions.

The work leverages two key trends in AI:
1. **Large-scale pre-training of generalist policies:** The paper builds upon existing powerful pre-trained models (Octo, PaliGemma VLA), acknowledging the immense knowledge they've absorbed.
2. **Language grounding:** Natural language has emerged as a powerful interface for bridging different modalities and providing high-level semantic understanding in AI systems. Prior work has shown language's utility in multimodal alignment, even with minimally overlapping data.

"FuSe" (Fusing heterogeneous Sensors) positions itself as a solution to finetune these pre-trained, vision-centric policies, allowing them to leverage the existing semantic knowledge while effectively incorporating new, scarce sensory data. It moves beyond purely visual grounding to encompass a richer, more human-like sensory experience for robots, enabling more robust and adaptable interaction in complex, partially observable environments. This pushes the frontier of generalist robotics towards true multi-sensory intelligence, where robots can reason jointly over various inputs to achieve tasks that would be impossible with vision alone.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable pre-trained, visually-dominant generalist robot policies to effectively incorporate and reason over heterogeneous sensor modalities, such as touch and audio, despite the scarcity of large datasets for these modalities.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Bridging the sensory gap in generalist policies:** State-of-the-art generalist policies, while powerful, are largely limited to vision and proprioception. This prevents them from performing effectively in scenarios where visual information is incomplete, occluded, or ambiguous. The authors aim to overcome this limitation.
2. **Mimicking human-like multi-sensory interaction:** Humans rely on a rich array of senses (vision, touch, hearing) for complex object manipulation. Equipping robots with similar multi-sensory capabilities is crucial for achieving truly general-purpose and robust interaction with the physical world. For example, knowing an object "feels squishy" or "sounds clinking" can be vital for task completion.
3. **Leveraging existing knowledge while expanding sensory input:** Rather than training new multimodal policies from scratch, which would require prohibitively large datasets, the motivation is to effectively *finetune* existing pre-trained generalist policies. This capitalizes on the vast amount of knowledge these policies have already acquired from large visual-proprioceptive datasets.
4. **Addressing data scarcity for heterogeneous modalities:** Collecting large, diverse datasets for touch and audio, especially those paired with robot actions, is significantly more challenging and expensive than collecting visual data. The research is motivated by the need for a method that can learn effectively from smaller, specialized datasets.
5. **Unlocking novel, complex interaction capabilities:** By integrating heterogeneous sensors, the authors aim to enable advanced robotic behaviors that include:
 - **Multimodal prompting:** Instructing the robot using a combination of sensory descriptors (e.g., "pick the object that looks yellow and feels crinkled").
 - **Compositional cross-modal prompting:** Reasoning across modalities to infer properties or goals (e.g., "pick the object that has the same colour as the button that plays piano").
 - **Zero-shot tactile/audio descriptions:** Enabling the robot to describe objects it interacts with based on non-visual senses (e.g., "The object I have grasped feels soft and squishy").
6. **Establishing natural language as a common grounding:** The authors are motivated to use natural language as a universal interface to connect diverse sensory inputs with the robot's action space and semantic understanding, thereby enabling more intuitive human-robot interaction and reasoning.

In essence, the paper seeks to push robot intelligence beyond solely "sight," allowing robots to "feel" and "hear" the world, thereby enhancing their robustness, versatility, and ability to perform intricate tasks in human environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is "FuSe," a novel finetuning recipe designed to imbue pre-trained generalist robot policies with an understanding of heterogeneous sensor modalities like touch and audio, primarily through language grounding. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Foundation: Pre-trained Generalist Policies**
FuSe starts with existing state-of-the-art generalist robot policies, specifically:
- **Octo:** A large transformer-based policy pre-trained on the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, which primarily uses visual and proprioceptive inputs.
- **PaliGemma VLA:** A 3B parameter Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model based on the PaliGemma VLM backbone, also pre-trained on OXE with visual modalities. This demonstrates the broad applicability of FuSe.

**B. Incorporating Heterogeneous Sensor Modalities**
To integrate new senses:
- **Tactile Encoder:** A pre-trained TVL encoder is used for tactile images (from DIGIT sensors). This encoder was pre-trained via pairwise contrastive learning across vision, language, and tactile modalities, providing a strong initial representation for tactile data even with limited finetuning data. The tactile images are processed by subtracting a "background" image to emphasize deformation.
- **Audio Encoder:** Raw audio waveforms are first converted into spectrograms, treating them as images. These spectrograms are then fed through a ResNet26 encoder. This is a common approach for processing audio for deep learning models.
- All tokenized sensor modalities (vision, touch, audio) are then fed into the transformer backbone of the generalist policy.

**C. Auxiliary Language Grounding Losses**
A crucial aspect of FuSe is the introduction of two auxiliary losses that leverage natural language to connect the semantic knowledge of pre-trained policies with the new heterogeneous modalities. This prevents the policy from over-relying on its pre-training modalities and effectively learns from smaller multimodal datasets.

1. **Multimodal Contrastive Loss (L_contrast):**
 - **Purpose:** To align diverse language instructions with multimodal observations, maximizing mutual information across modalities. This is inspired by CLIP-style learning.
 - **Mechanism:** An observation embedding is created by feeding all modalities through the transformer and combining them. A CLIP-style loss is then computed between this observation embedding and every possible language instruction, which results from combining different available modalities (e.g., "feels squishy," "looks red," "feels squishy and looks red"). These individual losses are averaged.

2. **Multimodal Generative Loss (L_gen):**
 - **Purpose:** To enable the policy to generate high-level semantic language descriptions directly from sensory observations, further grounding the modalities in language.
 - **Mechanism:** An additional generative head (a single transformer for all modality combinations) is added to the backbone model. For each modality combination (e.g., only touch, vision+audio), an observation embedding is passed through this head to predict the appropriate language instruction (e.g., "the object I have grasped feels soft and squishy"). A cross-entropy loss is computed against the ground-truth language instruction. For VLA models like PaliGemma, this loss can be directly integrated into the model's existing language generation capabilities.

**D. Overall Training Objective:**
The final loss function is a weighted sum of the behavioral cloning (BC) loss for action prediction and the two auxiliary language grounding losses:
L = L_BC + βL_gen + λL_contrast
where L_BC is the standard mean-square-error (MSE) imitation loss, and β and λ are hyperparameters (set to 1 in experiments) controlling the contribution of the generative and contrastive losses, respectively.

**E. Language Rephrasing for Data Augmentation:**
To expand the diversity of language instructions and enable free-form input at test time, the authors use ChatGPT to generate rephrased versions of the templated language annotations collected with the robot trajectories. This enhances the model's robustness to varied linguistic inputs.

**F. Data Collection and Robot Setup:**
A novel dataset of 26,866 real-world robot trajectories was collected using a WidowX 250 6-DoF robot arm. This dataset is unique in that it includes:
- Vision (wrist and 3rd-person RGB cameras)
- Touch (two DIGIT tactile sensors)
- Audio (microphone)
- Proprioception
- **Robot actions** (delta end-effector position commands)
- Templated language instructions

This dataset, crucial for physically grounded multimodal tasks, is open-sourced along with the code and models to support future research.

By combining pre-trained generalist policies with specialized encoders for new modalities and innovative language-grounding auxiliary losses, FuSe offers an effective method for robots to learn comprehensive multi-sensory understanding from relatively small datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in the paper demonstrate the significant benefits of the FuSe approach across various challenging real-world robot manipulation tasks.

**A. Superior Finetuning Performance:**
- **Baselines:** FuSe was compared against several baselines: Octo trained from scratch (Octo Scratch), Octo finetuned only with vision and action (Octo Vision FT), and a ResNet-based policy trained from scratch (ResNet Scratch).
- **Overall Performance:** FuSe consistently outperformed all baselines across tabletop grasping, shopping bag, and button pressing tasks (Figure 5). On average, FuSe achieved a success rate of over 60% compared to ~40% for Octo Vision FT and much lower for scratch-trained models.
- **Importance of Pre-training:** Training Octo from scratch on the multimodal dataset without the FuSe recipe yielded poor results, highlighting the necessity of leveraging pre-trained knowledge from large datasets.
- **Crucial for Partially Observable Environments:** FuSe showed particularly strong advantages in the "shopping bag" task, which involves visual occlusions requiring reliance on touch. Here, FuSe achieved significantly higher success rates (over 50%) compared to Octo Vision FT (~30%), underscoring the value of heterogeneous sensors in such conditions.

**B. Enhanced Multimodal Prompting Capabilities:**
- **Resolving Ambiguity:** FuSe successfully leveraged multimodal instructions to discriminate between objects that would be ambiguous if described by a single modality (Table I). For instance, in scenarios with two visually similar "round objects," FuSe could grasp the correct one when prompted to "grab the round object that *feels squishy*." Similarly, it could distinguish between tactually similar objects when visual cues were added. This demonstrates effective joint reasoning over vision and touch.

**C. Demonstrations of Compositional Reasoning:**
- **Simple Compositional Task:** FuSe outperformed all baselines in a task requiring the robot to "grab an object that has the same color as the button that plays piano" (Figure 6, left). This shows its ability to compose information from visual attributes of an object, auditory attributes of a button, and language.
- **Multi-step Compositional Task:** In a more complex task, FuSe successfully performed a two-step compositional task. First, it pressed an unseen button based on visual instruction. Then, using its generative head, it described the sound of that button, and finally, used that audio description to press a *different* button (from the training set) that produced the *same sound* (Figure 6, right). This highlights the policy's ability to connect subtasks and reason across modalities dynamically, outperforming a scratch-trained Octo model with auxiliary losses, which struggled with language grounding.

**D. Ablation Study:**
- An ablation study on the shopping bag task (Figure 7) confirmed the necessity of both proposed auxiliary losses: the multimodal contrastive loss and the multimodal generative loss. Removing either loss led to a noticeable drop in performance, especially on unseen test objects, proving their importance in fully exploiting heterogeneous feedback and grounding new modalities in language.

**E. Applicability to Different Generalist Policy Architectures:**
- **PaliGemma VLA Integration:** FuSe was successfully applied to finetune a 3B parameter Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model based on the PaliGemma VLM backbone (Figure 8). This VLA FuSe policy was competitive with, and even slightly outperformed, the Octo-based FuSe on the challenging shopping bag task.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The authors note that VLA FuSe is the first open-source VLA finetuned on heterogeneous (non-visual) sensory inputs, marking a significant contribution to the community.

**F. Dataset Contribution:**
- The authors collected and open-sourced a new, unique dataset of 27,000 robot trajectories featuring vision, touch, audio, proprioception, language instructions, *and robot actions*. This dataset addresses a major scarcity in the field and is critical for training physically grounded multimodal policies.

In summary, FuSe effectively enables generalist policies to learn and reason with heterogeneous sensor data, demonstrating improved task performance, novel multimodal prompting abilities, and compositional reasoning. The auxiliary language-grounding losses are critical for this success, and the approach is applicable across different pre-trained policy architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FuSe approach represents a significant step forward in the development of truly generalist and robust robotic systems, with several key significances and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Robots to "Sense Beyond Sight":** The most direct impact is empowering robots to interact with the world more comprehensively, moving beyond the limitations of vision-only perception. This is critical for tasks in visually ambiguous or occluded environments, making robots more capable in real-world scenarios like domestic assistance (e.g., retrieving objects from a cluttered drawer or bag) or industrial inspection where tactile feedback or sounds are crucial for identifying defects.

2. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Heterogeneous Sensors:** FuSe provides a practical solution to the long-standing challenge of data scarcity for non-visual modalities. By leveraging powerful pre-trained generalist policies and efficiently finetuning them with smaller, specialized multimodal datasets via language grounding, the approach bypasses the need for massive, expensive data collection efforts for every new sensor modality. This significantly lowers the barrier for integrating diverse sensors into robot learning pipelines.

3. **Unlocking Advanced Human-Robot Interaction and Reasoning:**
 - **Intuitive Prompting:** The ability to use multimodal language prompts ("grab the object that feels squishy and looks red") makes human-robot interaction more natural and precise, allowing users to leverage all facets of an object's properties.
 - **Complex Reasoning:** Compositional cross-modal prompting demonstrates an advanced form of reasoning, where robots can connect abstract concepts across different sensory domains and leverage them for complex, multi-step tasks. This is a stepping stone towards more intelligent and autonomous robots.
 - **Self-Description:** The capacity for robots to generate zero-shot descriptions of objects based on touch or sound ("the object feels corded") not only aids in internal understanding but also allows for richer communication with human operators or even other AI systems.

4. **Enhancing Robustness and Adaptability:** By incorporating redundant and complementary sensory information, robots become inherently more robust to sensor failures or partial observability. If vision is compromised, touch and audio can provide essential cues, leading to more reliable operation in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

5. **Accelerating Generalist Robot Development:** The demonstrated applicability of FuSe to different generalist policy architectures (Octo and PaliGemma VLA) indicates its versatility and potential to become a standard recipe for enhancing existing and future generalist policies. The open-sourcing of the code, models, and especially the novel multimodal action dataset, will undoubtedly foster further research and development in this critical area, allowing other researchers to build upon this foundation.

6. **Broader Implications for AI:** The success of language grounding in bridging disparate sensor modalities in a real-world, action-oriented context reinforces the power of large language models and multimodal learning paradigms. It suggests that language can indeed serve as a universal interface for integrating diverse forms of intelligence and perception in embodied AI.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge a limitation in training resources, which currently restricts the observation history to 0.4 seconds. Increasing training efficiency to allow for longer context lengths could further improve reasoning, especially for sparse signals like tactile data that unfold over time. This points to an exciting direction for future research, as a longer temporal context would likely enable even more sophisticated multi-sensory understanding and manipulation skills.

In conclusion, FuSe is a pivotal contribution that extends the capabilities of generalist robot policies, moving them closer to human-like multi-sensory intelligence. By ingeniously combining large-scale pre-training with language-grounded finetuning on scarce multimodal data, it lays the groundwork for a new generation of more versatile, robust, and intelligently interactive robots.
