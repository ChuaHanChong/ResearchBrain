# A Low-Resolution Image is Worth 1x1 Words: Enabling Fine Image Super-Resolution with Transformers and TaylorShift

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from DFKI and the University of Kaiserslautern-Landau developed TaylorIR, an approach for transformer-based image super-resolution that combines 1x1 pixel-wise patch embeddings with the efficient TaylorShift attention mechanism. This method enables fine-grained image enhancement while reducing memory consumption by up to 60% compared to existing state-of-the-art transformer models, achieving competitive or superior reconstruction quality.

## Problem

- Transformer-based image super-resolution models achieve high performance but suffer from high computational costs and substantial memory requirements due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention.
- Existing workarounds like restricted window sizes or larger patch sizes (e.g., 4x4) compromise the model's ability to capture fine details and long-range dependencies.
- The prohibitive memory and computational demands limit the practical application and deployment of advanced transformer-based SR models, especially on resource-constrained devices.

## Method

- Introduces ==TaylorIR==, a novel approach for transformer-based image super-resolution that enables pixel-level processing and reduces computational load.
- Utilizes ==1x1 pixel-wise patch embeddings== to treat each pixel as an independent entity, allowing for precise, individualized feature extraction across the entire image.
- Implements ==TaylorShift==, an efficient attention mechanism based on Taylor series expansion, which approximates full self-attention with ==linear computational complexity==, significantly reducing memory requirements.

## Results

- TaylorSwinIR achieves competitive or superior PSNR and SSIM scores compared to the original SwinIR across multiple scaling factors (x2, x3, x4) on benchmark datasets.
- The method reduces memory consumption by up to **60%** (e.g., from **11.7 GB** to **4.87 GB**) compared to standard SwinIR with similar performance, making it more practical for deployment.
- Qualitative comparisons indicate TaylorSwinIR produces sharper and more detailed results, particularly in areas with complex textures and fine structures, due to pixel-level processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating self-attention using Taylor series expansion (TaylorShift) reduces computational complexity from quadratic to linear, enabling processing of longer sequences efficiently.
> - Using 1 A pixel-wise (1x1) patch embedding allows for fine-grained feature extraction, which is crucial for recovering intricate image details in super-resolution.
> - Combining linear-complexity attention with pixel-wise embeddings enables the use of significantly larger contextual windows (e.g., 48x48) without prohibitive memory costs, improving the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image Super-Resolution (SR) is a computer vision task focused on reconstructing high-resolution (HR) images from low-resolution (LR) inputs. This process is relevant in fields such as security, medical imaging, and remote sensing, where detailed visual information is critical. Historically, early SR methods utilized convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which demonstrated efficacy in learning hierarchical image features. Recent advancements have seen transformer-based models surpass CNNs in SR performance. These models leverage self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies and complex contextual relationships within images, which often leads to enhanced restoration quality.

However, transformer-based SR architectures generally present two primary limitations. First, the computational cost of standard self-attention mechanisms scales quadratically with the sequence length (number of tokens). This quadratic complexity restricts the size of images that can be processed efficiently and often necessitates the use of limited attention windows (e.g., 8x8 tokens), thereby constraining global contextual reasoning. Second, many transformer-based SR models group pixels into larger patches (e.g., 4x4 or 8x8) during the embedding process. While this strategy reduces the sequence length and thus the computational load, it inherently averages out local variations within these patches, which can compromise the reconstruction of fine spatial details and pixel-level fidelity. The current work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to address these efficiency and fidelity trade-offs in transformer-based SR, aiming to enable fine-grained detail restoration while mitigating the computational burden associated with global attention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the performance and efficiency of transformer-based image super-resolution models by addressing their inherent scalability and pixel-level fidelity limitations. The authors aim to enable fine-grained image reconstruction with transformers while maintaining computational feasibility.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Overcoming Scalability Limitations:** A central motivation is to mitigate the quadratic computational cost associated with conventional self-attention mechanisms in transformers. This cost restricts the processing of large images and limits attention to small, localized windows, preventing models from capturing global context effectively. The authors seek a mechanism that allows for full token interaction (global context modeling) with a more favorable computational complexity.
- **Enhancing Pixel-Level Fidelity:** Current transformer-based SR models often employ coarse patch embeddings (e.g., 4x4 or 8x8 pixels grouped into a single token). This practice, while reducing sequence length for efficiency, averages out sub-patch details, leading to a loss of fine spatial information. The research aims to enable true pixel-wise reasoning by processing individual pixels as distinct tokens, thereby improving the restoration of high-frequency details, texture, and sharpness.
- **Developing an Efficient Attention Mechanism:** The core motivation for introducing TaylorShift is to provide a memory-efficient alternative to standard self-attention. This mechanism is intended to approximate full token-to-token attention using a Taylor series expansion, thereby achieving near-linear complexity in terms of sequence length, which is crucial for handling pixel-level embeddings over larger image regions.
- **Creating a Plug-and-Play Framework:** The authors intend to develop a modular framework, TaylorIR, that can be seamlessly integrated into existing transformer-based SR architectures. This plug-and-play design aims to facilitate easy adoption and improvement of various SR models without requiring significant architectural modifications.
- **Balancing Performance and Efficiency:** The overarching goal is to achieve state-of-the-art or improved super-resolution performance (quantified by metrics like PSNR and SSIM) while simultaneously reducing computational resource consumption, particularly memory usage (VRAM). This balance is critical for making advanced SR models more practical for deployment and scalable to higher resolutions.
- **Expanding Contextual Scope:** By enabling larger attention windows with manageable memory and runtime, the research aims to allow SR models to leverage a broader contextual understanding of the input image, which is hypothesized to contribute to improved reconstruction quality, especially for complex textures and structures.

These motivations collectively drive the development of TaylorIR, a framework designed to bridge the gap between fine-grained detail restoration and efficient transformer scaling in image super-resolution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a framework termed TaylorIR, which is designed as a drop-in component to enhance transformer-based super-resolution models. TaylorIR primarily consists of two core components: a pixel-wise patch embedding strategy and the TaylorShift attention mechanism.

#### 3.1. Pixel-Wise Patch Embedding

Conventional Vision Transformer (ViT)-based SR architectures typically reduce the input sequence length by partitioning images into larger patches (e.g., 4x4 or 8x8 pixels), with each patch forming a single token. This approach reduces computational cost but inherently averages out local variations within patches, which can hinder the recovery of fine details.

TaylorIR adopts a degenerate patch size of 1x1, meaning each individual pixel is embedded as an independent token. For an image of resolution HxW, this results in a sequence of N = H*W tokens. This pixel-wise embedding strategy aims to expose the full-resolution spatial information directly to the network, eliminating the implicit downsampling effect of larger patches. The authors state that this fine-grained representation naturally aligns with the TaylorShift operator, which is designed to maintain tractable attention over longer spatial sequences.

#### 3.2. TaylorShift Attention

TaylorShift is introduced as a replacement for standard self-attention, designed to provide memory-efficient global token interactions by approximating the softmax function through a low-order Taylor series expansion.

- **Direct TaylorShift:** This variant uses a second-order Taylor approximation of the exponential function within the softmax. Specifically, for an attention matrix A = QKᵀ, the normalized scores are approximated as T-SM(A)_ij = (1 + A_ij + (1/2)A_ij²) / Σ_k(1 + A_ik + (1/2)A_ik²). While it avoids exponentials, this direct formulation still requires constructing the N x N attention matrix A, leading to an asymptotic computational cost of O(N²d), similar to standard self-attention, where N is sequence length and d is feature dimension.

- **Efficient TaylorShift:** To address the quadratic complexity for long sequences, TaylorShift reorders the computation. Instead of explicitly forming the N x N attention matrix for the squared term, it performs normalization after mixing with the value matrix V. The key is to expand the (QKᵀ)^⊙2 V term using a tensor-like operator ⊠ such that (QKᵀ)^⊙2 V = (Q ⊠ Q) ((K ⊠ K)ᵀ V). This reordering allows the constant and linear parts of the attention computation to run in O(Nd²) and the squared part to run in O(Nd³). This formulation avoids the explicit construction of the N x N (QKᵀ)^⊙2 matrix, making it computationally preferable for large N, as Nd³ becomes significantly less than N²d under typical parameter settings.

#### 3.3. Integrating TaylorIR into SwinIR (TaylorSwinIR)

To evaluate TaylorIR's compatibility with hierarchical transformer backbones, the authors integrated it into the SwinIR architecture, resulting in TaylorSwinIR. SwinIR, by default, uses pixel embeddings but restricts its self-attention to non-overlapping windows of size 8x8 (64 tokens). The modification in TaylorSwinIR involves two aspects:

- **Expanded Window Size:** The attention window size is significantly increased from 8x8 to 48x48 (2304 tokens), enabling the model to consider a much broader local context.
- **TaylorShift Substitution:** The original windowed attention mechanism in SwinIR is replaced with the TaylorShift attention (A_TS). Depending on the window size (w²), TaylorShift can operate in either its direct form for shorter sequences or its efficient variant for longer ones.

This integration aims to broaden the effective receptive field while maintaining a near-linear asymptotic cost in terms of the number of tokens N within a window, thereby supporting global context modeling and finer detail recovery.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The models were trained on the DIV2K dataset, using 192x192 RGB sub-images. Evaluation was conducted on standard benchmark datasets: Set5, Set14, BSDS100, Manga109, and Urban100. The performance was assessed for upscaling factors of ×2, ×3, and ×4. Quantitative metrics reported were Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) on the luminance channel of the YCbCr color space. Comparisons were made against several state-of-the-art models, including RCAN, SAN, IGNN, HAN, NLSA, and the baseline SwinIR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate TaylorIR, specifically integrated into SwinIR as TaylorSwinIR, yielded several findings regarding its performance, efficiency, and contextual scope.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

TaylorSwinIR demonstrated consistent performance improvements over the baseline SwinIR model and other state-of-the-art methods across various benchmark datasets and scaling factors:

- **Overall Performance:** On average, TaylorSwinIR generally achieved higher PSNR and SSIM scores compared to SwinIR across most datasets, particularly at lower scaling factors (×2 and ×3).
- **Specific Gains at ×2:**
 - On the Urban100 dataset, TaylorSwinIR achieved a PSNR gain of +0.31 dB over SwinIR, suggesting improved reconstruction of fine, high-frequency structures.
 - On the Manga109 dataset, it registered a PSNR of 39.70 dB compared to SwinIR's 39.60 dB, indicating better detail preservation in dense line-art textures.
 - Across all ×2 benchmarks, PSNR improvements ranged from +0.02 to +0.31 dB, accompanied by consistent SSIM gains.
- **Performance at ×3 and ×4:** TaylorSwinIR maintained improvements at ×3 scaling. At ×4 magnification, TaylorSwinIR achieved performance parity with SwinIR on most datasets, suggesting that the efficiency-oriented TaylorShift mechanism did not compromise reconstruction fidelity at higher scales.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons (e.g., Figures 3, 4, 5) indicated that TaylorSwinIR produced image quality that was subjectively comparable to or superior to SwinIR. For instance, on the Manga109 image "MiraiSan" at ×3 scale, TaylorSwinIR generated sharper and more detailed results. These qualitative improvements were attributed to an expanded receptive field, which enabled the model to capture richer contextual information.

#### 4.3. Memory Efficiency (VRAM)

A significant finding concerned memory consumption. When both SwinIR (fine) and TaylorSwinIR were configured with the same large 48x48 attention window size for 2× SR, TaylorSwinIR consistently utilized substantially less VRAM:

- **VRAM Reduction:** TaylorSwinIR exhibited VRAM reductions ranging from approximately 37% to 85% across different datasets compared to SwinIR (fine). For example, on the Urban100 dataset, memory usage decreased from 78.5 GB to 49.1 GB. This reduction in memory footprint makes inference with large attention windows more practical under hardware constraints.

#### 4.4. Computational Complexity (Throughput)

Regarding computational complexity, measured in images per second (throughput):

- **Comparable Throughput at Large Windows:** At the matched large 48x48 attention window size, TaylorSwinIR achieved a throughput of 0.124 images/s, which was comparable to SwinIR (fine) at 0.123 images/s. This indicates that TaylorShift maintains similar runtime characteristics to standard attention when operating over large window sizes, while providing substantial memory savings.
- **Contextual Comparison:** For reference, the default SwinIR with small 8x8 windows achieved a significantly higher throughput of 6.12 images/s, due to its highly localized attention. The authors emphasize that TaylorShift's scaling advantages in mitigating quadratic sensitivity become more pronounced as sequence length (window area) increases, offering a more efficient path to large-context SR.

#### 4.5. Contextual Scope

Analysis of Local Attention Maps (LAM) (Figure 7) demonstrated that TaylorIR facilitates an extended contextual scope:

- **Increased Diffusion Index:** TaylorSwinIR consistently showed a higher diffusion index compared to the unmodified SwinIR. A higher diffusion index indicates a greater spread of information used for reconstruction across relevant areas within the image.
- **Broader Contextual Diffusion:** This broader diffusion of contextual information suggests that TaylorIR's integration enables the model to leverage a wider receptive field for reconstruction, contributing to improved image quality metrics.

In summary, the main findings indicate that TaylorIR, when applied to SwinIR, enhances super-resolution quality, substantially reduces memory consumption, and allows for broader contextual reasoning, making it a more efficient and capable framework for high-quality image restoration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TaylorIR framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts within the domain of image super-resolution and broader deep learning applications.

#### 5.1. Addressing Core Limitations of Transformer-based SR

TaylorIR directly addresses two fundamental limitations of existing transformer-based SR models: the quadratic computational complexity of self-attention and the loss of fine details due to coarse patch embeddings. By introducing TaylorShift attention with near-linear complexity and enabling 1x1 pixel-wise patch embeddings, the framework offers a solution that simultaneously improves both efficiency and fidelity. This allows for models to consider global context without the prohibitive computational costs typically associated with such approaches.

#### 5.2. Improved Performance and Efficiency Balance

The demonstrated improvements in PSNR and SSIM, particularly at lower scaling factors, indicate that TaylorIR enhances reconstruction quality. Crucially, these performance gains are achieved alongside significant memory consumption reductions (up to 60% observed VRAM savings). This balance between high perceptual fidelity and computational efficiency represents an advancement, as it enables the deployment of high-performing SR models in environments with constrained hardware resources.

#### 5.3. Enabling Broader Contextual Understanding

By allowing for substantially larger attention windows (e.g., 48x48) that were previously unfeasible with standard attention mechanisms, TaylorIR facilitates a broader contextual understanding during image reconstruction. The visualization of Local Attention Maps (LAM) confirms that TaylorSwinIR utilizes a wider range of information, contributing to the generation of more coherent and detailed super-resolved images. This expanded contextual scope is critical for accurately restoring complex textures and intricate structures.

#### 5.4. Modular and Flexible Design

The plug-and-play nature of TaylorIR is a notable aspect. Its design allows for seamless integration into existing attention-based architectures, such as SwinIR, without requiring extensive architectural modifications. This modularity could accelerate research and development in SR by providing a readily adaptable component for improving various transformer-based models.

#### 5.5. Potential for Real-Time and Resource-Constrained Applications

The reduced memory footprint and near-linear complexity offered by TaylorIR have practical implications for deploying high-quality SR systems. It makes advanced SR more feasible for real-time applications and integration into edge devices or other resource-constrained environments where memory and computational power are limited.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the substantial memory demands for extremely high-resolution images despite the improvements. Future work suggested includes exploring hybrid models that combine CNN and transformer strengths, and optimizing TaylorIR for edge devices through techniques like quantization and model compression. These directions indicate a recognition of ongoing challenges and pathways for further development. The paper also highlights the need to address the environmental footprint of high-performance computing, aligning with broader concerns about sustainable AI development.

In conclusion, TaylorIR represents a step towards more efficient and effective transformer-based image super-resolution, offering a framework that can enhance detail restoration while managing computational resources.
