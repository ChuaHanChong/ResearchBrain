# MindEye-OmniAssist: A Gaze-Driven LLM-Enhanced Assistive Robot System for Implicit Intention Recognition and Task Execution

> [!summary] Summary
> MindEye-OmniAssist, developed at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, presents a gaze-driven assistive robot system that leverages LLMs and VFMs to interpret complex, implicit user intentions from gaze patterns. The system successfully executes a wide range of previously undefined tasks, demonstrating a high success rate in intention recognition and action planning.

## Problem

- Traditional gaze-based control systems for assistive robots are limited to basic, predefined tasks and a restricted set of commands.
- Existing methods struggle with robustly recognizing complex, implicit user intentions in open-ended environments.
- Reliance on artificially constructed domain-specific knowledge or fixed object databases severely restricts robot versatility and generalizability.

## Method

- An integrated hardware system combines a head-mounted eye tracker, collaborative robotic arms, and multiple depth cameras for sensing and action.
- A gaze-based implicit intention recognition module utilizes ==YOLO-World (VFM)== for open-vocabulary object detection and a custom Transformer-based network to analyze gaze patterns over objects.
- DeepSeek-R1 (LLM) is used to infer high-level user intentions from sequences of gazed-upon objects and generate robot action plans, with a gaze-driven verbal confirmation mechanism for user feedback.

## Results

- The proposed intention recognition network achieved an average accuracy of **96.52%** (experiment-based) and **93.63%** (subject-based), outperforming Midas-Net and fixation-based methods.
- The MindEye-OmniAssist system demonstrated a **74.5%** overall success rate across 55 trials for diverse, undefined assistive tasks.
- The LLM successfully inferred user intentions in **94.5%** of trials and generated appropriate action plans in **96.2%** of recognized intentions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) enables open implicit intention recognition from gaze, extending beyond predefined tasks.
> - A multi-scale CNN and Transformer-based network, processing extracted gaze features, significantly improves implicit intention recognition accuracy compared to traditional fixation-based methods.
> - LLMs can effectively translate high-level, implicitly inferred user intentions into sequences of low-level robotic actions, providing adaptable task execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "MindEye-OmniAssist" addresses a critical gap in the field of assistive robotics and human-robot interaction (HRI). Historically, gaze-based control systems for assistive robots have been limited to basic, predefined tasks, such as simple grasping actions or executing a restricted set of pre-programmed commands. This limitation stems primarily from the system's inability to robustly recognize complex, implicit user intentions that are not explicitly coded or confined to specific scenarios. Existing methods often rely on artificially constructed domain-specific prior knowledge, finite state machines (FSMs), or fixed object databases, which severely restricts their versatility and generalizability.

The broader research landscape in robotics and AI is currently undergoing a paradigm shift with the emergence and rapid advancement of **foundation models**, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs). These models, trained on vast and diverse datasets, possess remarkable capabilities in understanding, reasoning, and generating information across various modalities. While LLMs excel in language understanding and reasoning, their direct application in physical, gaze-driven robotic systems is challenging due to their reliance on textual input.

This paper strategically positions itself at the intersection of these two trends: enhancing assistive robotics through advanced gaze-based HRI, and leveraging the power of foundation models to overcome the limitations of traditional approaches. By integrating LLMs and VFMs, MindEye-OmniAssist aims to move beyond simple object recognition and basic actions towards a more intelligent system capable of interpreting nuanced, implicit user intentions in open-ended environments. It seeks to bridge the gap between high-level human intent (derived from gaze patterns over objects) and low-level robotic action planning, thereby offering a more intuitive, flexible, and comprehensive assistive solution. This work contributes to the evolving field of embodied AI, where intelligent agents (robots) interact with the physical world, understand human commands (implicit or explicit), and execute complex tasks by synthesizing perception, reasoning, and action.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop an Open Implicit Intention Recognition Framework:** The core aim is to create a gaze-based framework powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) that can process user gaze input and infer intentions that are not restricted to predefined tasks or specific, limited scenarios. This moves beyond the "basic grasping" or "pre-programmed task" limitations of current systems.
2. **Implement a Gaze-Driven LLM-Enhanced Assistive Robot System (MindEye-OmniAssist):** This objective focuses on building a practical, integrated robotic system that utilizes the proposed intention recognition framework. The system should be capable of recognizing a user's implicit intentions through gaze and subsequently assisting them in completing a wider range of diverse tasks.
3. **Enhance Versatility and Effectiveness of Assistive Systems:** Ultimately, the motivation is to significantly improve the utility of assistive robots for individuals with impaired limb function by providing a more flexible, user-friendly, and capable HRI interface that can adapt to varying user needs and dynamic environments.

The motivation for this research stems from the significant challenges faced by individuals with limb impairments in performing daily tasks independently. Assistive robotic systems offer a promising solution, but their current capabilities are often too constrained. The human gaze is a naturally intentional and goal-directed cue, making gaze-based control an ideal interface for users with motor disabilities. However, the lack of sophisticated implicit intention recognition has historically bottlenecked the development of truly versatile assistive robots. This research is driven by the desire to unlock this potential by leveraging cutting-edge AI (foundation models) to enable robots to understand more complex human desires, thereby making assistive technology more impactful and broadly applicable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MindEye-OmniAssist system integrates specialized hardware with a sophisticated software architecture, combining computer vision, deep learning, and large language models to achieve its objectives.

**A. Hardware Architecture:**
The system comprises:
- **Pupil Invisible head-mounted eye tracker:** Captures scene images (1088 × 1080 at 30 Hz) and real-time 2D gaze points on the scene (at ~120 Hz). This is crucial for natural, non-intrusive gaze tracking.
- **Four collaborative robotic arms:** Offers flexibility with two main and two follower arms, operating in teleoperation or autonomous modes.
- **Three Intel RealSense D435 series cameras:** Provide RGB and depth images from different fields of view, essential for object localization and manipulation in 3D space.
- **Laptop:** Serves as the central processing unit, running Ubuntu and the Robotic Operating System (ROS) for inter-device communication and system control.

**B. Software Architecture:**
The software is modular, consisting of three main components:
1. **Gaze-based Implicit Intention Recognition Module:** Interprets gaze data and scene images to infer user intent.
2. **User Intention Confirmation Module:** Verifies the inferred intention with the user.
3. **Motion Planning Module:** Generates and executes robot actions based on confirmed intentions.

**C. Gaze-based Intention Recognition (Fig. 2):**
This is the core innovation, combining VFM and LLM:
- **Object Detection Powered by VFM:** An open-vocabulary object detector, **YOLO-World**, is used to identify and localize objects in the scene images. This VFM allows detection of objects not seen during training, crucial for "open" intention recognition.
- **Feature Extraction:** For each detected object, features related to gaze are extracted: the object's size (distance from center to vertex, $d_{1,o}$), the spatial relationship between the gaze point and object center ($d_{2,o}$), and their ratio ($ratio_o = d_{1,o} / d_{2,o}$). These features, along with raw gaze coordinates ($g_x, g_y$), are organized into fixed-length segments using a sliding window.
- **Intention Recognition Network (Fig. 3):** This custom deep learning network takes the extracted gaze features as input. It consists of:
 - **Multi-scale Convolutional Layers:** Extracts features from varying receptive fields.
 - **Positional Encoders:** Captures temporal relationships in the time-series gaze data.
 - **Transformer Encoder:** Models global dependencies among features, crucial for understanding sequences of gaze.
 - **Fully Connected Layers and Sigmoid Activation:** Merges features and outputs a binary classification for each object: whether the user intends to interact with it (ˆy > 0.5) or not.
- **Intention Inference Based on LLM:** Objects classified as "intended for interaction" form a sequence. This sequence is then fed into an LLM, specifically **DeepSeek-R1**, through a carefully crafted prompt. The LLM, leveraging its vast knowledge base, infers potential high-level user intentions (e.g., "brush one's teeth," "pour water into the bowl") from the combination and sequence of gazed-upon objects.

**D. Intention Confirmation (Fig. 4):**
To ensure accuracy and user safety, the system verbally announces the inferred intention ("Is your intention <intention>?"). The user confirms or rejects this intention using a gaze-driven interface:
- The scene image is divided into three regions: an upper "Reject" area (Area 1), a central "Preserved" area for normal observation (Area 2), and a lower "Agree" area (Area 3).
- Gazing at Area 3 confirms the intention, while gazing at Area 1 rejects it, prompting the system to re-evaluate or await new input.

**E. Motion Planning:**
Upon confirmation, the LLM (**DeepSeek-R1**) is again utilized. It receives the confirmed user intention and, using a set of predefined robotic operation APIs, generates a sequence of robotic arm actions (e.g., object localization, grasping, placing, moving, pouring) required to complete the task. Simple actions are pre-programmed, while more complex operations are learned via a behavior cloning algorithm.

**F. Experimental Setup:**
- **Intention Recognition Evaluation:**
 - A novel eye movement dataset was created with 8 volunteers, including varied objects and both intentional and unconscious gaze. Data included scene images, 2D gaze points, and manually annotated interaction intents.
 - Evaluated using five-fold cross-validation (experiment-based) and leave-one-subject-out (subject-based) on the new dataset.
 - Compared against Midas-Net and a fixation-based method, using accuracy as the metric.
- **System Evaluation:**
 - Five participants tested the full MindEye-OmniAssist system in a desktop environment with randomly placed objects.
 - Participants expressed intentions freely through gaze.
 - Success was measured for overall task completion, intention recognition, action planning, and action execution. Failures were recorded for analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness and superior performance of the MindEye-OmniAssist system, particularly in its open implicit intention recognition capabilities and overall task execution.

**A. Intention Recognition Results:**
The proposed intention recognition network exhibited high accuracy in classifying whether a user intended to interact with a specific object:
- **Experiment-based Performance Evaluation:** Achieved an average accuracy of **96.52% ± 1.69%**. This significantly outperformed Midas-Net (91.13% ± 2.44%) and a traditional fixation-based method (61.17% ± 2.93%). This highlights the robustness of the multi-scale CNN and Transformer-based approach in handling natural gaze patterns.
- **Subject-based Performance Evaluation:** Demonstrated strong generalization across different users, with an average testing accuracy of **93.63% ± 1.39%** for eight participants. Individual subject accuracies ranged from 91.48% to 95.95%, indicating consistent performance despite inter-subject variations in eye movement behavior. Again, this surpassed Midas-Net (88.18% ± 2.07%) and the fixation-based method (59.83% ± 0.69%), confirming its superior cross-subject recognition capabilities.

**B. System Evaluation Results (MindEye-OmniAssist):**
The full system was evaluated across various undefined tasks, showcasing its ability to handle complex scenarios:
- **Overall Success Rate:** The system achieved an impressive **41 out of 55 trials (approx. 74.5%)** across diverse tasks such as "Fetch an Object," "Put Something into Something," "Water the Plants," "Toggle the Switch," and "Pour Water." This demonstrates the system's practical viability for general assistive functions.
- **Intention Recognition Success Rate:** The LLM-enhanced intention inference achieved a high success rate of **52 out of 55 trials (approx. 94.5%)**, indicating that the framework effectively inferred the user's high-level goals from the sequence of interacted objects.
- **Action Planning Success Rate:** The LLM's ability to generate appropriate action sequences was also very high, succeeding in **50 out of 52 trials (approx. 96.2%)** where intention recognition was successful. This underscores the power of LLMs in translating high-level intent into actionable robotic steps.
- **Action Execution Success Rate:** The robot's physical execution of the planned actions was successful in **41 out of 50 trials (approx. 82%)** where planning was successful. While slightly lower than recognition and planning, this rate is still commendable for complex, undefined tasks in real-world scenarios, and suggests areas for future robotic control refinement.

The results collectively indicate that the MindEye-OmniAssist system successfully integrates gaze-driven input with foundation models to enable open implicit intention recognition and subsequent task execution for a variety of previously undefined assistive tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MindEye-OmniAssist system represents a significant advancement in assistive robotics and human-robot interaction, offering several key areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Breaking Free from Predefined Tasks:** The most critical impact is the system's ability to recognize user intentions beyond predefined or specific scenarios. By leveraging open-vocabulary object detection (VFM) and powerful reasoning capabilities of LLMs, the system can interpret a wide array of implicit intentions. This fundamentally shifts assistive robotics from a rigid, script-based paradigm to a more flexible, adaptive, and truly "assistive" one.
2. **Enhanced Versatility and Effectiveness:** For individuals with impaired limb function, this means an assistive robot that is not limited to picking up a specific cup but can understand intentions like "prepare a drink," "organize the table," or "water the plant," involving multiple objects and sequential actions. This significantly enhances the versatility and overall effectiveness of assistive systems, providing more comprehensive support in daily living activities.
3. **More User-Friendly Human-Robot Interaction:** Gaze-based control is inherently intuitive for many users with motor disabilities, as eye movements often remain functional. By integrating this natural input with intelligent intention inference and verbal confirmation, the system offers a highly user-friendly interface. The gaze-driven confirmation mechanism (agree/reject) adds a crucial layer of safety and allows for corrective feedback without requiring complex physical interaction.
4. **Pioneering Integration of Foundation Models:** This work demonstrates a concrete and successful application of cutting-edge AI (LLMs and VFMs) in a practical robotics context. It highlights how these powerful models can be effectively integrated into embodied systems to enhance perception, high-level reasoning, and action planning, overcoming the limitations of traditional, domain-specific AI models in robotics. The creation of a dedicated eye movement dataset for implicit intention recognition is also a valuable contribution to the research community, enabling future work in this niche.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The system's performance, particularly the identified areas for improvement in action execution accuracy and the desire for more effective methods for updating the basic action library and implementing error correction, provides clear pathways for future research. This includes developing more robust robotic manipulation skills, adaptive learning for new tasks, and sophisticated error recovery strategies, all of which are critical for deploying such systems in real-world, uncontrolled environments.

In conclusion, MindEye-OmniAssist lays a robust foundation for the next generation of assistive robots that are more intelligent, adaptive, and truly responsive to human needs. Its successful integration of gaze control with foundation models for open implicit intention recognition paves the way for a future where assistive technology can provide more comprehensive and personalized support, thereby significantly improving the quality of life for individuals with disabilities.
