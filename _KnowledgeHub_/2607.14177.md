# Adaptive Control of Motor-Position-Controlled Flexible Joint Robots with Uncertain Joint Stiffness

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at TU Wien, Berliner Hochschule f		u r Technik, KAIST, and DLR developed an adaptive control scheme for motor-position-controlled flexible joint robots with uncertain, nonlinear joint stiffness. The approach online estimates and adjusts the torque-deflection model, achieving improved trajectory tracking performance with lower normed tracking errors on a Variable Stiffness Actuator compared to non-adaptive baselines.

## Problem

- Model-based control of flexible joint robots typically requires accurate knowledge of joint compliance, but physical elastic elements are subject to variations from aging, temperature, and load-dependent hysteresis.
- Existing adaptive control methods for flexible-joint robots often assume linear torque-deflection relations or require a motor-torque interface, leaving a gap for motor-position-controlled systems with uncertain, nonlinear stiffness.
- Inaccurate stiffness models directly degrade control performance for motor-position-controlled flexible joint robots where the desired motor position is derived by inverting the torque-deflection model.

## Method

- A model-based ==adaptive control== scheme is proposed that online estimates and adjusts an uncertain, nonlinear ==torque-deflection model==, represented as a product of a ==regressor matrix== and a parameter vector.
- The control input (desired motor position) is derived through an ==implicit control law==, ensuring the estimated spring torque balances the desired link-side torque.
- A ==Lyapunov-based adaptation law== is developed for the parameter estimates, providing proofs for uniform global stability and convergence of tracking errors, along with robustness analysis against non-ideal motor position control errors.

## Results

- Experimental validation on a single-joint Variable Stiffness Actuator showed the adaptive controller consistently achieved the **lowest normed tracking error** across both high and low stiffness settings.
- The adaptation mechanism effectively compensated for varying nonlinear joint stiffness, leading to improved link-side trajectory tracking even when the VSA's stiffness preset was dynamically switched.
- While parameter estimates did not necessarily converge to their exact physical values, the controller effectively minimized tracking errors, indicating the adapted stiffness configuration provided an effective control gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The adaptive control framework extends classical methods to directly address uncertain, nonlinear joint stiffness in motor-position-controlled flexible joint robots, a common challenge in modern compliant robotics.
> - An implicit control law allows for effective online adaptation to complex nonlinear stiffness characteristics by balancing estimated spring torque with the desired link-side torque.
> - The system exhibits finite-gain L2 stability with respect to motor position control errors, guaranteeing convergence of link-side tracking errors even with non-ideal inner-loop motor control, which is crucial for practical implementation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model-based control of flexible joint robots typically relies on accurate knowledge of joint compliance. However, the mechanical properties of physical elastic elements are subject to variations due to factors such as material aging, temperature fluctuations, and load-dependent hysteresis. Explicitly modeling these effects can be complex and impractical, leading to inaccuracies in identified torque-deflection models over time or under changing operating conditions. This degradation in model fidelity can compromise the performance of control strategies for flexible joint robots.

Flexible joint robots equipped with servo motors, such as Variable Stiffness Actuators (VSAs), present a motor-position interface rather than a direct torque interface. For these systems, model-based link-side control often involves inverting the torque-deflection model to determine the desired motor position. Consequently, the precision of torque generation and overall control performance are directly influenced by the accuracy of the utilized stiffness model.

Adaptive control is a recognized method for addressing uncertain system parameters. Prior research has successfully applied adaptive controllers to compensate for parametric uncertainties in inertial parameters in rigid robots and, to some extent, in flexible-joint robots. However, these adaptive approaches for flexible joints often assume a linear torque-deflection relation and require access to a motor-torque interface. Neural adaptive controllers have also been proposed for motor-position-controlled flexible-joint robots, but these typically assume constant and known joint stiffness and an ideal motor-position controller.

This work addresses a specific gap in the existing literature by focusing on adaptive control for *motor-position-controlled flexible joint robots with uncertain, nonlinear joint stiffness*. It builds upon classical adaptive control methodologies but extends them to handle the complexities of nonlinear stiffness and the motor-position control interface. By proposing an adaptive control framework that adjusts an estimate of the uncertain, nonlinear torque-deflection map online, this research aims to improve model-based control performance in systems where precise stiffness models are difficult to maintain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a model-based adaptive control scheme for motor-position-controlled flexible joint robots that can achieve accurate tracking of desired link-side trajectories despite uncertainties in the nonlinear torque-deflection model. A central aim is to adapt an estimate of this uncertain model online.

The motivation for this work stems from several practical challenges associated with controlling flexible joint robots:
- **Uncertainty in Joint Stiffness:** The physical properties of elastic elements in robotic joints, such as springs or flexible transmissions, are not constant. They can change gradually over time due to wear, aging, and fatigue. Furthermore, their behavior can be influenced by operating conditions like temperature variations and exhibit phenomena like load-dependent hysteresis.
- **Difficulty in Accurate Modeling:** Developing an explicit, precise model that captures all these variations in torque-deflection characteristics is often impractical. This means that stiffness models derived from initial calibration measurements may not remain accurate throughout the robot's operational lifespan or across different tasks and environments.
- **Impact on Control Performance:** Inaccurate stiffness models directly degrade the performance of model-based control strategies. For motor-position-controlled flexible joint robots, which are increasingly common (e.g., Variable Stiffness Actuators (VSAs)), the desired motor position is derived by inverting the torque-deflection model from a desired torque input. Any error in the stiffness model therefore propagates to errors in the commanded motor positions, leading to suboptimal or inaccurate torque generation and reduced link-side tracking performance.
- **Limitations of Existing Adaptive Methods:** While adaptive control has addressed uncertainties in robot parameters, existing approaches for flexible joint robots typically assume linear stiffness characteristics or require a motor-torque interface. Other advanced methods, such as neural adaptive control, may assume known and constant stiffness. These limitations leave a need for a robust adaptive solution specifically for systems with *uncertain, nonlinear stiffness* and a *motor-position control interface*.

The paper seeks to address these issues by proposing an adaptive control framework capable of online estimation and adjustment of the nonlinear torque-deflection relationship, thereby enhancing link-side control accuracy and robustness in practical robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is an adaptive control approach for motor-position-controlled flexible joint robots with uncertain, nonlinear joint stiffness. It builds upon established Lyapunov-based design methodologies for adaptive robot control, adapting them to the specific challenges of uncertain stiffness in this robot class.

**A. System Dynamics and Control Interface**
The robotic system is modeled as an `n`-flexible joint robot. The dynamics are described by two coupled equations:
1. **Link-side dynamics:** `M(q)¨q + C(q, ˙q)˙q + g(q) = ψ(θ − q)`, where `q` is link position, `M` is link inertia, `C` is Coriolis and centrifugal matrix, `g` is gravity, and `ψ(θ − q)` is the elastic torque.
2. **Motor-side dynamics:** `B¨θ + ψ(θ − q) = τm`, where `θ` is motor position, `B` is motor inertia, and `τm` is applied motor torque.
The system is considered under motor position control, meaning an inner-loop controller ensures the motor position `θ` tracks a desired trajectory `θd`. The control input for the proposed outer-loop adaptive controller is this desired motor angle, `u = θd`. The link-side dynamics then simplify to `M¨q + C˙q + g = ψ(θd − q + eθ)`, where `eθ = θ − θd` is the motor-side tracking error.

**B. Problem Formulation**
The objective is to develop a model-based adaptive control scheme that enables tracking of a desired link-side trajectory `qd(t)` despite uncertainties in the nonlinear torque-deflection model `ψ(θ − q)`, by adapting an estimate of this model online.

**C. Adaptive Control Framework**

1. **Assumption on Motor-Position Controller:** For the initial derivation of the adaptive control law, an ideal motor position controller is assumed, implying that the motor position error `eθ` is negligible (`eθ = 0`). This idealization is later relaxed for robustness analysis.

2. **Regressor and Parameter Vector:**
 The core of the adaptation is the assumption that the uncertain torque-deflection relation `ψ(u − q)` can be represented as a product of a known, control-input-dependent regressor matrix `Y(u − q) ∈ R^(n×p)` and an uncertain, constant parameter vector `k ∈ R^p`. That is, `ψ(u − q) = Y(u − q)k`. The regressor matrix `Y` contains a finite number of basis functions of the deflection `(u − q)`. Only an estimate `ˆk` of `k` is available, leading to an estimated torque-deflection relation `ˆψ(u − q) = Y(u − q)ˆk`.

3. **Control Law:**
 The control objective is to inject a desired link-side torque `τd = M(q)¨qr + C(q, ˙q)˙qr + g(q) − Ks(t)s`, where `s = ˙eq + Kpeq` is a sliding variable. The control input `u` (desired motor position) is chosen such that the estimated spring torque balances this desired torque: `0 = Y(u − q)ˆk − τd`. This constitutes an *implicit control law*, as `u` must be solved for.
 - **Explicit Solutions for Special Cases:**
 - **Linear Regressor:** If `Y(u − q) = diag(u − q)` (linear stiffness), `u = q + (diag(ˆk))⁻¹τd`.
 - **Special Cubic Regressor:** For `φi(θi − qi) = k3,i(θi − qi)³ + k1,i(θi − qi)`, an analytical solution for `u_i` is obtainable using Cardano's formula, provided `ˆk3,i` and `ˆk1,i` remain non-zero.

4. **Adaptation Law:**
 A Lyapunov function candidate `S(s, ek, t) = 1/2 sᵀM(q)s + 1/2 ekᵀAek` is constructed, where `ek = k − ˆk` is the parameter estimation error and `A` is a diagonal, positive definite matrix. By analyzing the time derivative of `S`, an adaptation law for `ˆk` is derived as `˙ˆk = A⁻¹Yᵀs`. This law ensures that `˙S` becomes negative semi-definite, rendering the equilibrium `(s, ek) = 0` uniformly globally stable. Barbalat's Lemma is then applied to demonstrate global uniform convergence of the tracking errors `eq` and `˙eq` to zero as `t → ∞`.

**D. Robustness Analysis against Motor Position Control Errors**
The initial assumption of `eθ = 0` is relaxed. The motor position error `eθ` introduces an additional disturbance term `τe` into the closed-loop system dynamics, where `τe` is bounded by `kψ ||eθ||2`.
- **Finite-Gain L2 Stability:** By re-evaluating the Lyapunov function's time derivative, the closed-loop system is proven to be finite-gain L2 stable with respect to the input `eθ` and the output `s`.
- **Convergence with L2 Bounded Errors:** If `eθ` belongs to L2 (i.e., its energy is finite), then `s` and `ek` are shown to be uniformly bounded. Furthermore, `s` converges to zero as `t → ∞`, which implies convergence of the link-side tracking errors `eq` and `˙eq` to zero. This demonstrates robustness against non-ideal but sufficiently well-behaved inner-loop motor position control.

**E. Experimental Validation**
The approach is validated on a single-joint Variable Stiffness Actuator (VSA) rotating a 0.5 kg weight in a horizontal plane.
- **Setup:** The VSA allows for changing stiffness by varying the relative motor position `Δθ`. Two distinct stiffness presets (high and low) are used, with their nominal torque-deflection relations approximated by hyperbolic sine functions.
- **Regressor Choice:** A cubic function `ψcubic(δ) = k3δ³ + k1δ` (`Y(δ)k`) is chosen as the regressor for the adaptive controller, where `δ = u − q`. This choice is motivated by its ability to approximate the hyperbolic sine function over the operating range and allows for an explicit control law formulation.
- **Trajectory:** A periodic desired link-side trajectory `qd(t)` is used, and the VSA's stiffness setting is alternated between high and low every two periods.
- **Comparison:** The adaptive controller, initialized with parameters corresponding to the high stiffness preset, is compared against two baseline controllers: one using a constant high stiffness model and another using a constant low stiffness model, both without adaptation. Lower thresholds are applied to parameter estimates to prevent numerical issues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation on a VSA demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed adaptive control approach.

- **Tracking Performance:**
 - Initially, the adaptive controller (initialized with high stiffness parameters) showed performance comparable to the baseline controller utilizing the constant high stiffness model during the first two cycles.
 - Subsequent to this initial phase, the tracking of the desired link-side trajectory `q_d(t)` by the adaptive controller improved.
 - Beginning from the third cycle and continuing through later cycles, the adaptive controller consistently achieved the lowest normed tracking error (quantified by `√(∫sᵀs dt)` per cycle) for both high and low stiffness settings of the VSA. This indicates that the adaptation mechanism successfully compensated for the uncertain and varying stiffness characteristics.

- **Parameter Adaptation:**
 - During the initial high stiffness setting in the first cycle, both the cubic (`k₃`) and linear (`k₁`) parameter estimates of the torque-deflection model were observed to decrease.
 - In the second cycle, when the VSA's stiffness was switched to the low setting, the adaptation rate further decreased, and the cubic parameter estimate (`k₃`) reached its pre-defined lower threshold (approaching zero).
 - The linear parameter estimate (`k₁`) stabilized at approximately 0.5, which was higher than the linear contribution to the datasheet value for the low stiffness preset.
 - An interesting observation was that the parameter estimates did not necessarily increase in subsequent high stiffness cycles (e.g., third and fifth cycles) after the stiffness setting was switched from low to high. Despite this, the tracking performance of the adaptive controller remained consistent and superior.
 - The authors suggest that the estimated low, constant stiffness configuration, as adapted by the controller, might effectively act as a high gain on the desired link-side torque. This interpretation aligns with the experimental observation that the baseline-low controller (using a constant low stiffness model) generally performed better than the baseline-high controller.

In summary, the experimental results indicate that the adaptive control scheme is capable of adjusting online to variations in nonlinear joint stiffness, leading to improved trajectory tracking performance that surpassed non-adaptive controllers. While convergence of the parameter estimates to their exact physical values was not guaranteed or observed in all conditions, the controller effectively minimized tracking errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of flexible joint robot control, particularly for systems with motor-position-controlled actuators and uncertain, nonlinear joint stiffness.

**Theoretical Significance:**
- The work introduces an adaptive control framework that directly addresses the challenge of uncertain, nonlinear torque-deflection characteristics in flexible joint robots controlled via a motor-position interface. This is a notable extension beyond classical adaptive approaches that often assume linear stiffness or a torque-controlled interface.
- The reliance on an implicit control law and a control-input-dependent regressor matrix provides a robust mathematical framework for handling complex nonlinear stiffness behaviors.
- The formal analysis of robustness against errors induced by the inner-loop motor position controller is crucial for practical implementation, providing theoretical guarantees of finite-gain L2 stability and convergence of tracking errors under realistic conditions of non-ideal motor control.

**Practical Impact:**
- **Enhanced Control Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed adaptive approach can substantially improve link-side trajectory tracking performance, even when the physical stiffness properties of the joint change dynamically (as shown with the variable stiffness actuator). This indicates a higher level of control fidelity compared to controllers relying on static, potentially inaccurate stiffness models.
- **Robustness to System Variations:** The ability to adapt online to uncertain and changing joint stiffness is critical for the long-term reliability and performance of robots. This method can mitigate performance degradation caused by material aging, wear, temperature changes, or load-dependent hysteresis, which are common in real-world robotic systems.
- **Applicability to Emerging Robot Designs:** The approach is particularly promising for robotic joints that utilize low-cost elastic elements (e.g., 3D-printed springs) or variable stiffness mechanisms, whose torque-deflection characteristics may be less predictable or more prone to variation. Robots designed for physical interaction, where compliant joints are beneficial, can particularly benefit from this improved control.
- **Broader Generalizability:** The findings motivate the application of this adaptive control strategy to other classes of robots with joint flexibility, such as antagonistically driven VSAs or twisted string actuators, expanding the scope of robust control solutions in compliant robotics.

**Future Directions:**
The research identifies pathways for future work, including the development of adaptation laws that can ensure physically meaningful estimates of stiffness characteristics and the integration of torque prediction errors to further improve parameter estimation. These advancements could lead to even more robust and interpretable adaptive control systems for flexible joint robots.

Overall, this work advances the state of adaptive control for flexible joint robots by providing a theoretically sound and experimentally validated solution for a challenging and practically relevant control problem, with clear implications for the design and operation of next-generation compliant robotic systems.
