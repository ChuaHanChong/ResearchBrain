# Generative Multimodal Models are In-Context Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> Emu2, a 37-billion-parameter generative multimodal model developed by the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI), demonstrates robust in-context learning capabilities by predicting the next multimodal element in a sequence. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance in few-shot settings across a wide range of multimodal understanding and generation tasks, including visual question answering, referring expression comprehension, text-to-image generation, and subject-driven image editing.

## Problem

- Multimodal AI systems often require task-specific architectures and extensive supervised datasets for each new task, which is difficult to scale and adapt to diverse, novel challenges.
- Existing multimodal models lack human-like in-context learning abilities, struggling to adapt to new tasks with minimal demonstrations or simple instructions.
- Inefficiency in AI development due to the repeated need to design and train new models for each distinct multimodal task.

## Method

- Introduces ==Emu2==, a **37-billion-parameter** generative multimodal model from BAAI, trained with a ==unified autoregressive objective== to predict the next multimodal element.
- Employs a three-component architecture: a ==Visual Encoder== (EVA-02-CLIP-E-plus), a Multimodal Modeling component (LLaMA-33B Transformer decoder), and a ==Visual Decoder== (SDXL-based for images, adapted 3D U-Net for video).
- Undergoes extensive pretraining on large-scale, diverse ==multimodal sequences== (image-text, video-text, interleaved, grounded) followed by specialized instruction tuning to create Emu2-Chat and Emu2-Gen models.

## Results

- Emu2 (**37B** parameters) demonstrates superior few-shot in-context learning on VQAv2, VizWiz, and TextVQA, outperforming larger models like Flamingo-**80B** and IDEFICS-**80B**.
- Emu2-Chat achieves state-of-the-art performance on image and video question answering benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2, OKVQA, MSVD-QA) and sets new records on complex LMM benchmarks (SEED-Bench, MM-Vet, MMMU).
- Emu2-Gen reaches state-of-the-art zero-shot text-to-image generation on MS-COCO (highest CLIP-I and CLIP-T scores) and excels in subject fidelity on DreamBench for subject-driven generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling a generative multimodal model with a unified autoregressive objective (predicting the next multimodal element) significantly enhances in-context learning capabilities.
> - A single, versatile base model can adapt to a broad spectrum of multimodal tasks with few demonstrations or instructions, mirroring human cognitive flexibility.
> - Sophisticated emergent abilities like visual prompting and object-grounded generation appear through large-scale generative pretraining, and Emu2 achieves competitive results with fewer parameters than some larger models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multimodal AI systems capable of understanding and generating content across different modalities (e.g., text, images, video) is a rapidly evolving field. Traditionally, achieving proficiency in diverse multimodal tasks has involved designing task-specific architectures and collecting extensive supervised training datasets for each new task. This approach is inherently challenging to scale and adapt, particularly for long-tail or novel tasks.

This paper addresses a critical gap: the lack of human-like **in-context learning** capabilities in existing multimodal systems. Humans can readily adapt to new tasks with just a few demonstrations or simple instructions, a flexibility that current AI models largely struggle to imitate. The broader research landscape has seen significant breakthroughs in **Large Language Models (LLMs)**, which have demonstrated remarkable emergent in-context learning abilities as they scale up in size and are trained on vast amounts of text data. This work aims to transfer and extend this paradigm of "scaling leads to in-context learning" from the language domain to the multimodal domain.

Emu2 positions itself among a growing family of large multimodal models (LMMs) such as Flamingo, Kosmos-1/2, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, IDEFICS, Shikra, Qwen-VL, LLaVA, and CogVLM. While many of these models integrate vision encoders with LLMs to enable multimodal understanding, Emu2 distinguishes itself by focusing on a **unified generative autoregressive objective**: "predict-the-next-multimodal-element." This means it learns to generate both visual embeddings and textual tokens within a single sequence, making it inherently capable of both understanding and generation tasks in a flexible, open-ended manner. This contrasts with models primarily focused on understanding or those that rely on separate generation components integrated post-hoc.

The paper argues that by effectively scaling up a generative multimodal model and training it on large-scale interleaved multimodal sequences (text, image-text pairs, video-text, interleaved image-text-video, grounded data), similar in-context learning abilities can emerge. This includes complex, on-the-fly reasoning tasks like visual prompting (interpreting visual cues in context) and object-grounded generation, which are often challenging for models without such emergent capabilities. Emu2's architectural simplification compared to its predecessor (Emu) also highlights a trend towards more streamlined, efficient designs for large models. By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in few-shot settings and across a wide range of benchmarks, Emu2 contributes to the ongoing shift towards more general-purpose, adaptable foundation models in AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a **generative multimodal model** that exhibits strong **in-context learning (ICL)** capabilities, mirroring the adaptability seen in humans and the emergent properties of large language models.

The primary motivations driving this work stem from the limitations of previous multimodal AI systems:
- **Lack of Generalizability:** Traditional systems often require task-specific architectures and substantial supervised datasets, making it difficult to scale and adapt to the highly diverse and "long-tail" nature of real-world multimodal tasks.
- **Inefficiency in Adaptation:** The need to repeatedly design and train models for each new task is inefficient and hinders rapid deployment in dynamic environments.
- **Bridging the Human-AI Gap:** Humans excel at solving new tasks with minimal demonstrations or simple instructions. Current multimodal models lag in this "in-context" adaptability.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives for Emu2 are:
1. **Demonstrate Enhanced In-Context Learning through Scaling:** To show that by significantly scaling up a multimodal generative model to 37 billion parameters (Emu2) and training it with a unified autoregressive objective on large-scale multimodal sequences, its task-agnostic in-context learning abilities can be substantially enhanced.
2. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Few-Shot Performance:** To set new benchmarks on multiple multimodal understanding tasks when evaluated in few-shot settings, demonstrating its ability to learn from limited examples within the context window.
3. **Exhibit Emergent Reasoning Capabilities:** To observe and validate the emergence of more complex reasoning capabilities, such as visual prompting (interpreting visual cues dynamically) and object-grounded generation (generating content based on specific object locations/identities), which require on-the-fly inference.
4. **Establish a Versatile Foundation Model:** To develop Emu2 as a robust "base model and general-purpose interface" capable of handling a broad spectrum of multimodal tasks—from understanding (e.g., visual question answering) to generation (e.g., text-to-image, subject-driven generation)—by merely following specific task instructions.
5. **Facilitate Future Research:** To publicly release the code and models, thereby promoting further research and development in the field of general multimodal AI systems.

In essence, the paper aims to prove that a unified generative approach, combined with significant scaling, can unlock versatile and adaptable multimodal intelligence, moving beyond specialized models towards more generalized AI capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Emu2's methodology centers on a **generative multimodal model** trained with a **unified autoregressive objective** to predict the next multimodal element in a sequence. This approach allows it to handle diverse multimodal tasks through a single framework.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The Emu2 architecture comprises three main components:
- **Visual Encoder:** Initializes with a pre-trained EVA-02-CLIP-E-plus. Its role is to tokenize input images or video frames into continuous visual embeddings.
- **Multimodal Modeling:** This is the core generative component, initialized with LLaMA-33B. It functions as a Transformer decoder that processes interleaved sequences of visual embeddings and textual tokens autoregressively.
- **Visual Decoder:** Initializes with SDXL. Its purpose is to decode the visual embeddings generated by the Multimodal Modeling component back into high-resolution images or videos.
 - **Image Decoding:** The image decoder trains to autoencode visual embeddings into images, functioning as a "detokenizer." Unlike the previous Emu model, Emu2's visual decoding can be trained independently of the language model, making it more efficient.
 - **Video Decoding:** For video, a diffusion-based decoder adapted from a 2D U-Net to 3D (similar to) is used, initialized with Stable Diffusion 2.1.

A notable architectural simplification from the previous Emu model is the connection between the Visual Encoder and Multimodal Modeling. Emu2 uses mean pooling of 8x8 image patches followed by a linear projection, eliminating the need for an additional C-Former module used in Emu.

#### 3.2. Pretraining

The pretraining process for Emu2 involves training on large-scale multimodal sequences to learn the predict-the-next-element objective.
- **Data:** The training data is vast and diverse, including:
 - Image-text pairs: LAION-2B, CapsFusion-120M.
 - Video-text pairs: WebVid-10M.
 - Interleaved image-text data: Multimodal-C4 (MMC4).
 - Interleaved video-text data: YT-Storyboard-1B.
 - Grounded image-text pairs: GRIT-20M, CapsFusion-grounded-100M (curated by the authors). This data introduces spatial grounding information.
 - Language-only data: Pile, to maintain strong textual reasoning capabilities.
- **Training Strategy:**
 - Images are encoded into N=64 dimension-fixed visual embeddings and interleaved with text tokens.
 - **Stage 1:** Initial pretraining on image-text and video-text pair data, focusing on captioning loss on text tokens. Input resolution is 224x224, then 448x448.
 - **Stage 2:** The Visual Encoder is frozen. Only the linear projection layer and Multimodal Modeling are optimized using both text classification loss and image regression loss on visual embeddings. This stage incorporates additional interleaved, grounded, and language-only datasets. All images are resized to 448x448.

#### 3.3. Visual Decoding Training Setup

The Visual Decoder (SDXL-base U-Net) is trained on LAION-COCO and LAION-Aesthetics for image autoencoding. The Visual Encoder and VAE in SDXL are frozen during this training. The objective is to decode Emu2's N visual embeddings into 1024x1024 images. Classifier-free guidance is employed. For video decoding, a separate diffusion model is fine-tuned.

#### 3.4. Instruction Tuning

After base model pretraining, Emu2 undergoes instruction tuning to align it with specific task instructions, yielding specialized models:
- **Emu2-Chat (Instruction-Following Chat):**
 - **Training Data:** A mix of academic-task-oriented datasets (e.g., COCO Caption, VQAv2, RefCOCO) and multimodal chat data (e.g., LLaVA, VideoChat, ShareGPT).
 - **Objective:** Supervised by cross-entropy loss on the assistant's response. Special tokens like `[USER]` and `[ASSISTANT]` structure the conversational data, and different system messages are used for task categories.
 - **Visual Input Resolution:** Increased visual encoder tokenization to 16x16 (from 8x8 in pretraining) for finer spatial details.
- **Emu2-Gen (Controllable Visual Generation):**
 - **Training Data:** Leverages high-quality datasets including grounded image-text (CapsFusion-grounded-100M, GRIT), image editing (InstructPix2Pix), and various text-to-image datasets (CapsFusion, LAION-Aesthetics, etc.), including premium sources like Unsplash and outputs from advanced text-to-image systems (Midjourney-V5, DALL-E-3).
 - **Objective:** Uses the unified generative pretraining objective. Input sequences include text, object images, and object localization images (coordinates drawn on a black image), with regression loss applied to the final image's visual embeddings. The Visual Encoder is frozen during fine-tuning.

This comprehensive methodology, from large-scale pretraining with a unified objective to specialized instruction tuning, aims to create a highly versatile and performant multimodal foundation model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Emu2 demonstrates remarkable capabilities across a wide range of multimodal tasks, particularly highlighting its strong in-context learning abilities and versatility as a generative model.

#### 4.1. Pretrained Base Model (Few-Shot In-Context Learning)

- **Superior Few-Shot Performance:** Emu2 exhibits significant in-context learning abilities, with performance consistently improving as more examples are provided in context. Notably, on VQAv2, VizWiz, and TextVQA datasets, Emu2 (37B parameters) outperforms larger models like Flamingo-80B and IDEFICS-80B (80B parameters) across all few-shot settings. This indicates a more efficient scaling behavior.
- **Emergent Capabilities "In the Wild":** The model showcases advanced multimodal reasoning, such as:
 - Classifying and counting objects in specific formats with only a few demonstrations.
 - Following **visual prompts** (e.g., red circles laid on images) in context, a capability that struggled at smaller scales or in zero-shot settings, suggesting it emerges with scaling.

#### 4.2. Instruction-Following Chat (Emu2-Chat)

- **State-of-the-Art Visual Question Answering:** Emu2-Chat consistently achieves state-of-the-art results on standard image question-answering benchmarks like VQAv2, OKVQA, GQA, VizWiz, and TextVQA. Its performance on OKVQA, which requires external knowledge, is particularly strong, highlighting its ability to integrate real-world knowledge.
- **Strong Video Question Answering:** Despite not being specifically trained on video question-answering data, Emu2-Chat demonstrates competitive performance on MSVD-QA and MSRVTT-QA, surpassing InstructBLIP and even the larger Flamingo-80B. This suggests the generalization power of its unified multimodal pretraining.
- **Superior on LMM Benchmarks:** Emu2-Chat sets new records on recent, more challenging benchmarks designed for Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), including SEED-Bench, MM-Vet, TouchStone, and MMMU. These benchmarks assess complex reasoning and problem-solving abilities, confirming Emu2-Chat's advanced multimodal comprehension.
- **Exceptional Visual Grounding:** On referring expression comprehension benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg), Emu2-Chat achieves the best results among generalist models. Its most significant advantage is observed in RefCOCO+, which focuses purely on appearance-based descriptions, underscoring its powerful perceptual abilities in capturing intricate visual details.

#### 4.3. Controllable Visual Generation (Emu2-Gen)

- **Highly Effective Image Autoencoding:** Emu2's Visual Encoder and Visual Decoder form a strong autoencoder, producing significantly superior autoencoding results compared to prior methods like SEED and Emu, with a strong CLIP-I score of 0.907 on MS-COCO. This demonstrates the high fidelity of its internal visual representation.
- **Versatile Controllable Generation:** Qualitatively, Emu2-Gen adeptly performs various controllable visual generation tasks in a zero-shot setting, leveraging its in-context learning. Examples include re-contextualization, stylization, image modification, region-controllable generation (e.g., based on bounding boxes), and multi-entity composition.
- **State-of-the-Art Text-to-Image Generation:** Emu2-Gen achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot text-to-image generation performance on MS-COCO validation set, scoring highest in both CLIP-I (image similarity) and CLIP-T (text-following ability) metrics, outperforming both unimodal and other multimodal generative models.
- **Superior Subject-Driven Generation:** On the DreamBench dataset for subject-driven image editing, Emu2-Gen excels in subject fidelity (measured by DINO and CLIP-I scores) with only a single image input in a zero-shot setting. This highlights its capability to faithfully reconstruct and re-contextualize specific subjects.

In summary, Emu2, through its scaled-up generative architecture and unified training objective, successfully demonstrates strong in-context learning capabilities across both multimodal understanding and generation tasks, often setting new state-of-the-art records with a comparatively smaller model size.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Generative Multimodal Models are In-Context Learners" carries significant implications for the future of AI, particularly in the realm of multimodal intelligence.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift towards General Multimodal AI

The most significant contribution of this work is its demonstration that scaling up a *generative* multimodal model with a unified autoregressive objective (predicting the next multimodal element) unlocks powerful **in-context learning** abilities. This represents a crucial step away from the traditional paradigm of designing task-specific models and datasets for every new multimodal problem. Emu2 shows that a single, versatile base model can adapt to a broad spectrum of tasks with just a few demonstrations or instructions, mirroring human cognitive flexibility.

#### 5.2. Versatile Foundation Model for Diverse Applications

Emu2 serves as a true **foundation model** and a **general-purpose interface** for multimodal AI. Its ability to process and generate both text and visual elements (images, videos) within a unified framework makes it applicable to a vast array of tasks, including:
- **Enhanced Understanding:** Visual question answering, conversational AI with visual input, referring expression comprehension, and complex multimodal reasoning.
- **Controllable Generation:** High-quality text-to-image generation, subject-driven image editing, re-contextualization, stylization, and multi-entity composition. This opens doors for creative industries, design, and personalized content creation.

#### 5.3. Emergent Abilities and Efficiency in Scaling

The emergence of sophisticated capabilities like visual prompting and object-grounded generation in Emu2, particularly in few-shot settings, underscores the power of large-scale generative pretraining. These abilities often emerge rather than being explicitly programmed, pointing towards a deeper understanding of multimodal relationships within the model. Furthermore, achieving state-of-the-art results with a 37-billion-parameter model that outperforms some 80-billion-parameter counterparts suggests a more efficient scaling strategy or superior architectural and training choices.

#### 5.4. Advancement of Open Science

The public release of Emu2's code and models (via the baaivision/Emu GitHub repository) is a critical step towards fostering collaborative research and accelerating advancements in the field. This open-source approach allows other researchers to build upon Emu2, validate its findings, and explore new applications and improvements.

#### 5.5. Addressing Societal Implications

The authors responsibly acknowledge the potential broader societal impacts and limitations of such powerful models. They highlight benefits in areas like visual navigation, medical diagnostics, and accessibility for visually impaired individuals. However, they also transparently discuss concerns such as model hallucinations (generating incorrect or unreasonable predictions), biases inherited from training data, and potential misuse of generative capabilities for harmful content. This proactive stance on ethical AI deployment emphasizes the need for continuous enhancement in robustness, fairness, and responsible development practices.

In conclusion, Emu2 marks a significant milestone in the development of general multimodal AI. By demonstrating that scaling generative multimodal models can unlock robust in-context learning and versatile capabilities, this research paves the way for more adaptable, general-purpose AI systems that can seamlessly interact with and generate content across diverse modalities, pushing the boundaries of what multimodal AI can achieve.
