# QUAR-VLA: Vision-Language-Action Model for Quadruped Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Westlake and Zhejiang Universities introduce QUAR-VLA, a unified Vision-Language-Action framework for quadruped robots, along with the large-scale QUARD dataset and the QUART transformer model. The QUART model significantly outperforms baselines across diverse tasks, exhibits strong generalization to novel instructions and objects, and successfully transfers learned policies from simulation to real-world robots.

## Problem

- Traditional quadruped robot learning often treats language interaction (QUAR-LA) and visual perception (QUAR-VA) as separate problems, limiting their synergistic potential for autonomous reasoning and complex, multi-faceted tasks.
- A significant lack of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality datasets that integrate visual, linguistic, and action data specifically for quadruped robots hinders the training of powerful Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Developing effective VLA models for quadruped robots is challenging due to their complex, dynamic action space and the need for precise alignment between fine-grained instructions and visual observations to generate appropriate actions.

## Method

- Proposes **QUAR-VLA**, a novel ==Vision-Language-Action== paradigm for quadruped robots, which defines an 11-dimensional high-level action space (e.g., velocities, gait parameters, body height) discretized into 256 bins for generating executable commands.
- Introduces QUARD, the first large-scale, multi-task dataset for quadruped VLA, comprising 259K episodes (256K simulated, 3K real) covering seven diverse tasks across three difficulty levels, collected using WR-2 quadruped robots.
- Develops QUART, a decoder-only transformer model built on a pre-trained multi-modal large language model (MLLM like Fuyu-8B), which takes RGB images and natural language instructions to output discretized action tokens, and employs a co-training pipeline for robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- QUART significantly outperformed VLM baselines (CLIP, R3M, VC-1) across all difficulty levels of multi-task performance, achieving an average success rate of **0.66** in "Distinguish Letter" and uniquely succeeding in complex tasks like "Crawl under Bar" (**0.32** SR) where baselines failed (0 SR).
- Demonstrated strong generalization to unseen objects, and critically, to novel verbal instructions and complex customized commands (e.g., multi-step instructions, spatial reasoning), showcasing an emergent ability to harmonize vision, language, and action.
- Achieved successful sim-to-real transfer, with real-world performance on a "Go to" task improving from 3/20 success using only 3K real data to 13/20 success when augmented with 256K simulated data, validating the co-training approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified Vision-Language-Action framework, specifically a decoder-only transformer architecture like QUART, can implicitly learn strong interdependencies between different action dimensions, which is crucial for coordinated whole-body movements in complex quadruped tasks.
> - Leveraging pre-trained Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) within a VLA architecture enables impressive generalization capabilities for quadruped robots, allowing them to adapt to unseen objects, novel verbal instructions, and demonstrate emergent complex reasoning.
> - A co-training pipeline that combines large-scale simulated data with smaller, carefully collected real-world data effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap, providing a scalable method to train robust and adaptable policies for physical quadruped robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: quadruped robot learning, vision-language-action (VLA) models, and the development of large-scale robotic datasets.

**Quadruped Robot Learning:** Traditionally, quadruped robots have been studied for their exceptional traversability and agility in complex terrains. Research in this area often focuses on locomotion control, autonomous navigation, and sometimes manipulation. However, these tasks are typically addressed through specialized, single-modal policies: either Vision-Action (QUAR-VA), where visual cues guide actions, or Language-Action (QUAR-LA), where textual commands drive behavior. The paper highlights a critical limitation of these approaches: QUAR-VA often relies on coarse-grained visual goals, struggling with detailed, multi-step instructions, while QUAR-LA, lacking visual perception, cannot autonomously navigate or interact with its environment. This work directly addresses this deficiency by proposing a unified Vision-Language-Action paradigm.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** The broader field of robotics has seen a surge in VLA models, which aim to integrate visual perception, natural language instructions, and action generation to achieve more intelligent and versatile robot behavior. Notable examples include models leveraging CLIP for semantic understanding and large language models (LLMs) for generating manipulation policies. However, much of this work has focused on general mobile robots or fixed-base manipulators. Applying VLA to agile quadruped robots introduces unique challenges due to their complex kinematics, dynamic stability requirements, and the need for a precisely defined action space that balances flexibility and computational efficiency. This paper extends the VLA paradigm to the demanding domain of quadruped robots, which is a significant step towards more universally capable embodied agents.

**Robot Datasets:** A common bottleneck in robot learning, especially for data-hungry foundation models, is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality datasets. While datasets exist for various robotic tasks, quadruped-specific datasets often lack the quantity and task diversity required for training complex VLA models, particularly those combining vision, language, and rich action spaces. The paper addresses this by introducing QUARD, a novel large-scale, multi-task dataset that integrates visual, linguistic, and robot command data, filling a crucial gap for training sophisticated quadruped VLA policies.

In summary, this work provides a novel solution to integrate the strengths of vision-based autonomy and language-based control for quadruped robots, a challenging domain that has historically lagged in multimodal integration. By introducing both a new paradigm (QUAR-VLA), a comprehensive dataset (QUARD), and a dedicated model (QUART), the authors are pushing the boundaries of embodied AI for agile mobile robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of enabling quadruped robots to naturally interact with their environment and autonomously make decisions by seamlessly integrating visual information and natural language instructions to generate executable actions. This fundamentally aims to elevate the overall intelligence and versatility of quadruped robots.

This ambitious objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Separated Modalities:** Traditional quadruped robot learning often treats language interaction (QUAR-LA) and visual perception (QUAR-VA) as separate problems. This separation limits the synergistic potential between these information streams, making it challenging for robots to achieve seamless autonomous reasoning, decision-making, and action execution, especially for complex, multi-faceted tasks. The authors seek to bridge this gap by creating a unified framework.
2. **Addressing Fine-grained Instruction Alignment:** A significant challenge in integrating vision and language is ensuring that the robot can accurately interpret and act upon fine-grained instructions in harmony with its visual observations. This requires a model that can understand subtle nuances in commands and relate them to specific visual features and corresponding actions.
3. **Lack of Large-scale Multi-task Quadruped Datasets:** Training powerful VLA models, particularly those based on large transformers, requires vast amounts of diverse trajectory data. Such datasets for quadruped robots performing a wide array of tasks (perception, navigation, manipulation) and incorporating multimodal inputs (vision, language) are largely absent. Manual data collection for real robots is expensive and time-consuming, necessitating an efficient data generation strategy.
4. **Complexity of Quadruped Action Space:** Unlike simpler mobile robots or fixed manipulators, quadruped robots possess agile locomotion behaviors with a rich and complex action space. Defining this action space to balance movement flexibility, computational efficiency, and the generation of appropriate high-level commands (not too simplistic like 2D velocities, nor too low-level like direct joint commands) is a non-trivial challenge.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to unlock a new level of autonomy and interactive capability for quadruped robots, making them more adaptable and useful in diverse real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel framework called **QUAR-VLA (Vision-Language-Action tasks for QUAdruped Robots)**, a large-scale multi-task dataset named **QUARD (QUAdruped Robot Dataset)**, and a Vision-Language-Action model dubbed **QUART (QUAdruped Robotic Transformer)**.

#### 3.1. QUAR-VLA Problem Setup

The QUAR-VLA paradigm defines the task as training a conditional policy, QUART, to interpret RGB images (state `s`) and natural language instructions (`w`) to generate executable actions (`a`). The action space `A` consists of an 11-dimensional high-level command vector and a termination signal `t`. The 11 dimensions control various aspects of the robot's motion:
- `v_x, v_y, ω_z`: velocities along x, y axes, and angular velocity around z-axis.
- `θ_1, θ_2, θ_3`: gait pattern parameters.
- `f`: gait frequency.
- `h_z`: robot body height.
- `ϕ`: pitch angle.
- `s_y`: foot width.
- `h_f_z`: foot height.
These continuous action dimensions are discretized into 256 uniformly sized bins, following prior work, to simplify action search and improve algorithm stability.

#### 3.2. QUARD: Large-scale Quadruped Robot Dataset

To address the data scarcity, QUARD is collected, encompassing a diverse set of tasks:
- **Task Definition:** Seven tasks across three difficulty levels: easy (e.g., "Distinguish Letter"), medium (e.g., "Go to Object"), and hard (e.g., "Go through Tunnel," "Go to Object and avoid obstacle," "Crawl under Bar," "Unload Object into Receptacle"). These tasks are designed to build upon foundational skills (perception, basic navigation) to advanced whole-body manipulation.
- **Scale and Composition:** The dataset comprises 259K episodes, with the vast majority (256K) generated in simulation and a small but crucial portion (3K) collected from real robots.
- **System Setup:** The WR-2 quadruped robot (12 joints, 25cm height, 40cm body length) is used. It receives high-level commands, which are then passed to a pre-trained low-level command-tracking controller for joint-level execution.
- **Data Collection:**
 - **Simulation Data:** Collected in Nvidia's Isaac Gym for massive parallel trajectory generation. Path planning is done using A* and D* algorithms, and a PD controller converts paths into target velocities. This allows for diverse speeds and gaits.
 - **Real Data:** Manually collected via remote control in a laboratory setting.
- **Consistency Constraints:** Strict protocols ensure consistency between simulation and reality, including robot start positions, target placement, obstacle positioning, and task-specific termination criteria.
- **Diversity:** The dataset includes various common indoor furniture, outdoor facilities, basic shapes with four color variations, and mandates robots to perform tasks with varying gaits (e.g., trotting, pacing) and speeds to enhance generalization.

#### 3.3. QUART: Vision-Language-Action Model

QUART is designed as a decoder-only transformer model that leverages a pre-trained multi-modal large language model (MLLM), specifically Fuyu-8B (and also tested with LLaVa-7B).
- **Architecture:** It takes a single RGB image (`s`) from the robot's first-view camera and a natural language instruction (`w`) as input.
- **Processing:** Input images and language are converted into corresponding tokens via a tokenizer `τ(t|s, w)`. These tokens are then fed into the decoder-only transformer module.
- **Action Generation:** The transformer outputs discretized action tokens `p(a_d|t)`, which are then detokenized `Detokenize(a_d)` into continuous robot actions `a_c` (except for the discrete termination command). This approach allows for implicit learning of interdependencies between different action dimensions, crucial for coordinated whole-body movements.
- **Training:** The model is fine-tuned on the QUARD dataset using a standard categorical cross-entropy objective and causal masking, analogous to a behavior cloning loss.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A co-training pipeline is employed, effectively distilling knowledge from large simulation data for real-scene deployment, thereby addressing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Inference Speed:** QUART achieves an inference speed of 2Hz for generating high-level command control, which is sufficient given that low-level motor control handles higher frequencies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of QUART focuses on multi-task ability, generalization capabilities, and sim-to-real scaling, comparing its performance against prominent VLM baselines: CLIP, R3M, and VC-1. These baselines were augmented with language conditioning by concatenating language embeddings with image tokens for action output.

#### 4.1. Multi-task Performance

- **Superiority of QUART:** QUART significantly outperforms all baselines across all difficulty levels of seen tasks (Table 2 in paper). For instance, in "Distinguish Letter," QUART achieved 0.66 success rate (SR) compared to R3M's 0.58, CLIP's 0.44, and VC-1's 0.46.
- **Handling Complex Tasks:** In the most challenging tasks requiring complex mechanical movements like "Crawl under Bar" and "Unload Object into Receptacle," QUART achieves SRs of 0.32 and 0.12 respectively, while all baselines record 0 SR. This highlights QUART's ability to grasp coordinated relationships between multiple robot parts.
- **Baseline Limitations:**
 - R3M performs poorly on most tasks (except "Distinguish Letter") due to a lack of alignment with language semantics, hindering its ability to comprehend tasks beyond basic discrimination.
 - CLIP and VC-1 show satisfactory performance on fundamental perceptual tasks (e.g., "go to" commands) but struggle significantly with tasks requiring intricate physical execution. This suggests that while VLMs can understand abstract principles, direct application does not easily translate to complex physical tasks.
- **Decoder-only VLA Advantage:** The decoder-only VLA framework of QUART implicitly learns interdependencies between different action dimensions through sequential generation, leading to noticeable performance enhancements in complex mechanical movements.

#### 4.2. Generalization Capabilities

- **Unseen Objects:** Both QUART and VC-1 demonstrate good generalization to unseen objects (e.g., same category but different textures/colors, different shapes, or entirely novel objects) (Fig. 6, Table 4 in supplementary). This is attributed to the inherent advantages of their pre-trained VLM components.
- **Unseen Verbal Instructions:** QUART dramatically surpasses baselines when confronted with unseen linguistic directives (e.g., "navigate to target" instead of "go to the object," or "move under barrier" instead of "crawl under bar"). Baselines often fail, exhibiting repetitive motions or wrong commands. QUART, leveraging the language prowess inherited from its MLLM backbone, adeptly generalizes to novel instructions, achieving harmonization of vision, language, and action (Fig. 7, Table 5 in supplementary).
- **Complex Customized Skills:** QUART demonstrates an impressive ability to extend basic skills to accommodate complex customized commands involving commonsense, combinational instructions ("Go to the drawer first, then go to white cube"), and spatial understanding ("Go to the left corner of white cube") (Fig. 8, Figs. 1-3 in supplementary). This emergent capability showcases its advanced reasoning.

#### 4.3. Sim2Real Transfer

- **Scaling with Simulation Data:** Experiments show that the performance of QUART in real-world scenarios consistently improves with the incorporation of larger amounts of simulated data. For the "Go to" task in real-world settings, a model trained with only 3K real data achieved 3/20 success. This increased to 7/20 with 25.6K simulated data and further to 13/20 with 256K simulated data (Table 3).
- **Effectiveness of Co-training:** This demonstrates the effectiveness of the co-training approach, where broad simulation data provides a comprehensive training ground, and narrow real-world data ensures robustness and adaptability to authentic environments. The method successfully leverages simulation to enhance real-world applicability.

#### 4.4. Architectural and Training Scheme Validation (Supplementary)

- **VLM+Policy vs. VLA:** Further experiments confirmed that even with a decoder-only transformer as the policy head (Aligned VLM+P(Transformer)), the VLM+Policy approach still struggles with complex tasks like "crawl" and "unload," maintaining a similar trend to MLP-based policy heads (Table 1 in supplementary). This reinforces the distinct advantage of the full VLA (QUART) paradigm in integrating all modalities within a unified model.
- **MLLM Backbone Choice:** Testing with different MLLM backbones (Fuyu-8B vs. LLaVa-7B) showed comparable performance, indicating that the VLA paradigm itself is more critical than the specific MMLM foundation chosen.
- **Multi-task vs. Single-task:** Multi-task training yielded superior results across most tasks compared to training on single tasks (Table 2 in supplementary), underscoring the benefits of learning commonalities and interdependencies between tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of quadruped robotics and embodied AI, presenting several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering a New Paradigm for Quadruped Autonomy:** The introduction of **QUAR-VLA** as a unified Vision-Language-Action framework for quadruped robots is groundbreaking. It addresses the critical limitation of previous single-modal approaches by enabling seamless integration of perception, planning, and decision-making. This paradigm shift will allow quadruped robots to interact more naturally with humans and operate more autonomously in complex, instruction-driven environments.

2. **Creation of a Crucial Dataset:** The **QUARD** dataset is the first large-scale, multi-task dataset specifically designed for Vision-Language-Action tasks on quadruped robots. By providing diverse, multimodal data from both simulation and the real world, it fills a significant void in the research community. This dataset will be invaluable for training and benchmarking future foundation models for quadruped robots, accelerating progress in robot learning.

3. **Development of a High-Performance VLA Model:** **QUART** demonstrates performant robotic policies, significantly outperforming existing VLM baselines across a wide array of tasks. Its ability to excel in complex whole-body manipulation tasks, where baselines completely fail, showcases its advanced understanding and control capabilities.

4. **Demonstration of Emergent Generalization and Reasoning:** QUART exhibits remarkable generalization to novel objects and, more importantly, to unseen verbal instructions. Its capacity for understanding complex combinational commands and performing rudimentary spatial reasoning based on language is a strong indicator of emergent intelligence. This capability moves robots closer to human-like understanding and flexible task execution, reducing the need for explicit programming for every scenario.

5. **Validation of Sim-to-Real Transfer for Quadrupeds:** The successful demonstration of sim-to-real transfer, where large-scale simulated data significantly enhances real-world performance, is crucial. This approach offers a scalable solution to the challenges of real-world data collection, making it feasible to train sophisticated models for physical robots.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** QUAR-VLA could enable more intuitive and natural human-robot interaction, allowing users to give complex, descriptive commands rather than relying on predefined gestures or interfaces.
- **Broader Applications for Quadruped Robots:** With increased autonomy and task versatility, quadruped robots could be deployed in a wider range of applications, from search and rescue, inspection in hazardous environments, and logistics to assistive roles in homes or industries.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** This work lays a strong foundation for developing generalist agents for quadruped robots, analogous to how large language models have revolutionized NLP. It encourages further research into multimodal learning, large-scale data collection, and robust sim-to-real transfer in the context of dynamic, agile robots.

**Future Directions Identified by Authors:** The authors themselves acknowledge areas for further enhancement, including exploring automated data collection methods, developing more robust sim-to-real solutions beyond co-training (e.g., domain randomization), improving inference speed for higher-frequency control, and leveraging sub-optimal data through reinforcement learning. These directions underscore the continuous evolution of this field, with QUAR-VLA serving as a significant catalyst.
