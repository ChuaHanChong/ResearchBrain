# QUAR-VLA: Vision-Language-Action Model for Quadruped Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Westlake and Zhejiang Universities introduce QUAR-VLA, a unified Vision-Language-Action framework for quadruped robots, along with the large-scale QUARD dataset and the QUART transformer model. The QUART model significantly outperforms baselines across diverse tasks, exhibits strong generalization to novel instructions and objects, and successfully transfers learned policies from simulation to real-world robots.

## Problem

- Traditional quadruped robot learning often treats language interaction (QUAR-LA) and visual perception (QUAR-VA) as separate problems, limiting their synergistic potential for autonomous reasoning and complex, multi-faceted tasks.
- A significant lack of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality datasets that integrate visual, linguistic, and action data specifically for quadruped robots hinders the training of powerful Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Developing effective VLA models for quadruped robots is challenging due to their complex, dynamic action space and the need for precise alignment between fine-grained instructions and visual observations to generate appropriate actions.

## Method

- Proposes **QUAR-VLA**, a novel ==Vision-Language-Action== paradigm for quadruped robots, which defines an 11-dimensional high-level action space (e.g., velocities, gait parameters, body height) discretized into 256 bins for generating executable commands.
- Introduces QUARD, the first large-scale, multi-task dataset for quadruped VLA, comprising 259K episodes (256K simulated, 3K real) covering seven diverse tasks across three difficulty levels, collected using WR-2 quadruped robots.
- Develops QUART, a decoder-only transformer model built on a pre-trained multi-modal large language model (MLLM like Fuyu-8B), which takes RGB images and natural language instructions to output discretized action tokens, and employs a co-training pipeline for robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- QUART significantly outperformed VLM baselines (CLIP, R3M, VC-1) across all difficulty levels of multi-task performance, achieving an average success rate of **0.66** in "Distinguish Letter" and uniquely succeeding in complex tasks like "Crawl under Bar" (**0.32** SR) where baselines failed (0 SR).
- Demonstrated strong generalization to unseen objects, and critically, to novel verbal instructions and complex customized commands (e.g., multi-step instructions, spatial reasoning), showcasing an emergent ability to harmonize vision, language, and action.
- Achieved successful sim-to-real transfer, with real-world performance on a "Go to" task improving from 3/20 success using only 3K real data to 13/20 success when augmented with 256K simulated data, validating the co-training approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified Vision-Language-Action framework, specifically a decoder-only transformer architecture like QUART, can implicitly learn strong interdependencies between different action dimensions, which is crucial for coordinated whole-body movements in complex quadruped tasks.
> - Leveraging pre-trained Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) within a VLA architecture enables impressive generalization capabilities for quadruped robots, allowing them to adapt to unseen objects, novel verbal instructions, and demonstrate emergent complex reasoning.
> - A co-training pipeline that combines large-scale simulated data with smaller, carefully collected real-world data effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap, providing a scalable method to train robust and adaptable policies for physical quadruped robots.
