# BaseReward: A Strong Baseline for Multimodal Reward Model

> [!summary] Summary
> BaseReward establishes a systematic, empirically-backed guide for constructing high-performance multimodal reward models, demonstrating a practical and efficient architecture. It outperforms previous models, achieving an 11% improvement in accuracy on MM-RLHF-Reward Bench and a 14.2% higher overall accuracy on VL-Reward Bench, while providing faster inference.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle to consistently align with human preferences and values, necessitating robust reward models.
- A systematic, empirically-backed methodology and clear blueprint for constructing state-of-the-art Multimodal Reward Models (MRMs) was largely absent across academia and industry, leaving research strategies fragmented.
- Fundamental questions regarding optimal MRM architectures, training strategies, and data curation remained unaddressed.

## Method

- Conducted exhaustive ablation studies on critical ==Multimodal Reward Model== (MRM) components, including architectures, training paradigms, regularization, and data curation.
- Empirically derived a "recipe" for high-performance MRMs, identifying optimal reward head design (2-layer MLP with SiLU) and effective data mixing strategies.
- Developed **BaseReward**, an efficient MRM based on a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, optimized architecture, and a curated mix of multimodal and text-only preference data.

## Results

- BaseReward outperformed previous SOTA on MM-RLHF-Reward Bench by approximately **11%** in accuracy and **23.32%** in Acc+.
- It also improved upon the previous best on VL-Reward Bench by approximately **14.2%** in Overall Accuracy.
- Integrated into a reinforcement learning pipeline, BaseReward successfully enhanced MLLM performance across diverse perception, reasoning, and conversational tasks, outperforming computationally more expensive R1-Reward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simple Naive Reward Model (direct scoring) approaches, using a two-layer MLP with SiLU activation for the reward head, offer high efficiency and performance comparable to more complex generative models for many tasks.
> - High-quality text-only preference data surprisingly enhances multimodal reward model performance, particularly in safety and mathematics, without degrading multimodal capabilities.
> - Ensembling multiple reward models, even with simple averaging, consistently leads to substantial performance gains across benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "BaseReward: A Strong Baseline for Multimodal Reward Model" is situated at the critical intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and alignment techniques, a rapidly evolving and strategically important area in AI research.

**Advancement of MLLMs:** The field has witnessed explosive growth in MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL, Llama 3-V), enabling sophisticated understanding and generation across diverse modalities like text, images, and video. These models are pushing the boundaries of AI capabilities, demonstrating impressive performance in complex tasks that blend different data types.

**The Alignment Challenge:** Despite their power, a central and pressing challenge is ensuring that MLLMs consistently produce outputs that are helpful, harmless, and aligned with human values and preferences. Unaligned models can generate biased, factually incorrect, or even harmful content, undermining their utility and trustworthiness. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as a cornerstone technique for this alignment process, largely driven by the success of text-only Large Language Models (LLMs).

**The Gap in Multimodal Reward Models (MRMs):** While reward models (RMs) are well-established for text-only LLMs, the blueprint for constructing state-of-the-art Multimodal Reward Models (MRMs) remains significantly less clear. The paper explicitly identifies this as a major void in both academic and industrial research. Current state-of-the-art MLLMs (like Seed 1.5 VL, Keye-VL, Mimo-VL, GLM 4.1 V Thinking) employ a variety of distinct, often ad-hoc, reward modeling strategies. These diverse approaches, lacking a systematic and comprehensive evaluation, create a fragmented research landscape where fundamental questions about optimal architecture, training strategies, and data curation for MRMs remain unanswered.

**Filling the Void:** This work directly addresses this critical gap by providing a systematic, empirically-backed investigation into the crucial components of MRM development. By exhaustively analyzing different paradigms, architectural designs, training strategies, data sources, backbone models, and ensemble methods, the authors aim to distill best practices and establish a clear "recipe" for building high-performance MRMs. The introduction of "BaseReward" as a powerful and efficient baseline serves to standardize practices and offer a robust reference point for future research and development in MLLM alignment. This systematic approach is vital for moving beyond fragmented, heuristic-driven development towards a more principled and efficient advancement of aligned MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **provide a clear, empirically-backed "recipe" or systematic guide for constructing high-performance Multimodal Reward Models (MRMs)**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the MLLM Alignment Challenge:** The rapid advancement of MLLMs has made aligning their outputs with human preferences (ensuring they are helpful, harmless, and accurate) a critical and immediate challenge. Reward Models are recognized as a pivotal technology for achieving this alignment through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).
2. **Lack of Systematic Guidance in MRM Development:** Unlike text-only RMs, there is currently no systematic guide or comprehensive study detailing how to build state-of-the-art MRMs in either academia or industry. This lack of a standardized blueprint leads to disparate, often ad-hoc, reward modeling strategies across different MLLMs, making it difficult for researchers and practitioners to efficiently develop and improve MRMs.
3. **Unanswered Fundamental Questions:** The existing research landscape leaves many crucial questions about MRM development unanswered. These include:
 - Which reward model architecture delivers optimal performance?
 - What constitutes the most effective architectural design for reward heads (e.g., number of layers, activation functions)?
 - How do different data sources, including text-only preference data, influence multimodal performance?
 - What roles do the MLLM backbone architecture and its parameter scale play in determining effectiveness?
 - Which training strategies and regularization techniques are most beneficial?
 - Can ensemble methods further enhance MRM capabilities?
4. **Developing a Robust and Efficient Baseline:** Beyond providing theoretical insights, the authors are motivated to deliver a practical, top-tier MRM. This led to the development of BaseReward, designed to be powerful, efficient, and capable of setting a new state-of-the-art on major benchmarks.
5. **Validating Practical Utility:** A crucial motivation is to demonstrate the real-world efficacy of the proposed MRM. This involves integrating BaseReward into a reinforcement learning pipeline to show its ability to genuinely enhance an MLLM's performance across diverse tasks, moving beyond static benchmark evaluations.

In essence, the research aims to demystify MRM development, provide concrete, data-driven recommendations, and offer a powerful, open-source-friendly solution that can serve as a foundational tool for the next generation of aligned MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research is an **exhaustive and systematic experimental analysis (ablation studies)** across every crucial component of the Multimodal Reward Model (MRM) development pipeline. This comprehensive approach aims to empirically derive a "recipe" for building high-performance MRMs.

1. **Preliminary Setup:**
 - **Reward Model Formulation:** The standard pairwise comparison loss function (Equation 1) is used, where the reward model is optimized to assign higher scores to preferred responses ($y_w$) over less preferred ones ($y_l$) given a query ($x$).
 - **Default Training Data:** R1-Reward's supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dataset, comprising approximately 200,000 preference pairs from MM-RLHF, VL-Feedback, and RLHF-V, serves as the baseline for comparisons.
 - **Default Backbone:** The Qwen2.5-VL-7B model is selected as the default backbone for the investigations.
 - **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **Multimodal:** VL-Reward Bench (Overall Acc, Macro Avg Acc), Multimodal RewardBench (six key areas: general correctness, preference, knowledge, reasoning, safety, VQA), and MM-RLHF-Reward Bench (Traditional Acc, Acc+).
 - **Text-Only:** RMBench (Easy/Normal/Hard Acc across Chat, Safety, Code, Math) and Reward Bench (Chat, Chat Hard, Safety, Reasoning, Prior Sets).

2. **Systematic Experimental Investigations:** The authors conducted ablation studies on the following key components:

 - **Reward Modeling Paradigms:** Compared Naive Reward Model (direct scoring), Critic-Based Reward Model (generates critique then scores), and Generative Reward Model (GRM, generates a token/phrase indicating preference). All were evaluated under a unified dataset and training strategy.
 - **Reward Model Design (Architectural):** Investigated the impact of the reward head's structure, specifically the number of layers (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) and the choice of activation function (None, Tanh, SiLU).
 - **Training Regularization Strategies:** Examined the effect of two common regularization techniques: Zero-Coefficient Regularization (penalizing rewards from deviating from zero) and Length Normalization (normalizing reward by response length). The regularization weight (λ) for zero-coefficient regularization was varied from 0 to 0.1.
 - **Data Curation:** Trained separate reward models on over ten different multimodal and text-only preference datasets (e.g., MMIF, Omni-Align, RLAIF-V, MMPR v.12, R1-Reward, Ultra-All, SHP, Tulu-3, Olmo-2, Ultra-Hard, WildChat, swe-arena). The goal was to understand the influence of each dataset on multimodal and text-only performance and identify high-quality data sources.
 - **Optimizing Multimodal RMs for Pure-Text Tasks:** Compared MLLMs trained on mixed-media vs. text-only data for pure-text RM tasks. Also benchmarked against dedicated LLMs trained on the same text-only data to assess the inherent capabilities of MLLMs versus LLMs for text-centric reward modeling.
 - **Impact of Base Model Selection and Scale:** Evaluated different MLLM backbones (Intern-VL series, Qwen-VL series) and various parameter scales (from 1B to 72B) to understand their influence on reward modeling capabilities.
 - **Ensemble Strategies for Reward Models:** Explored combining multiple reward models. Strategies included validation-based weighting (based on accuracy or confidence on a validation set) and a validation-free simple averaging of reward scores. The benefit of diversifying the ensemble with a pure-text LLM was also investigated.

3. **BaseReward Construction:**
 - Based on the insights from the ablation studies, BaseReward was designed. It utilizes the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, employs an optimized two-layer MLP as the reward head with the SiLU activation function, and uses the standard pairwise comparison loss without auxiliary regularization.
 - The training data for BaseReward consisted of a curated mixture of seven high-quality multimodal and text-only preference datasets, totaling 2.8 million preference pairs.
 - A grid search was performed for the learning rate (3e-6 chosen), with a batch size of 128, trained on 64 Nvidia H100 GPUs. An additional Qwen2-VL-7B based model was also trained for ensemble purposes.

4. **Reinforcement Learning with BaseReward:**
 - To validate practical utility, BaseReward (single Qwen 2.5 VL model) was integrated into a Reinforcement Learning pipeline using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.
 - Qwen-2.5-VL 3B was fine-tuned, generating 8 rollouts per prompt.
 - Three reward schemes were compared: pure rule-based, BaseReward-based, and a hybrid rule-based + BaseReward scheme.
 - The MLLM trained with these schemes was evaluated on a comprehensive suite of downstream benchmarks targeting perception, reasoning, and conversational tasks (MMbench v1.1, MME-RealWorld-Lite, MMStar, Mathvista, V*, Llavawild, Wildvision).

This systematic and multi-faceted approach allowed the authors to provide a data-driven "recipe" and a robust baseline, backed by extensive empirical evidence, for the design and training of high-performance MRMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental analysis yielded several crucial insights and led to the development of a state-of-the-art MRM, BaseReward:

1. **Reward Modeling Approaches:**
 - **Naive-RM** (direct scoring) was found to be highly efficient (fast training and inference) and achieved comparable or even superior performance to Generative Reward Models (GRMs) in VQA, general, and hallucination tasks, especially after appropriate data supplementation. GRMs showed advantages in safety and coding tasks, likely due to intrinsic MLLM knowledge but incurred higher computational costs. Critic-based RMs' performance was heavily dependent on the quality of generated critiques. For its simplicity and efficiency, Naive-RM was selected as the focus for further optimization.
2. **Reward Head Design:**
 - A **two-layer MLP with a SiLU activation function** yielded the best reward modeling performance. A single linear layer was suboptimal, while increasing layers beyond two did not bring significant performance gains.
3. **Training Regularization Strategies:**
 - Both zero-coefficient regularization and length normalization did **not improve performance**; in fact, increasing the regularization weight (λ) generally led to performance degradation. Consequently, BaseReward was trained without any auxiliary regularization losses.
4. **Data Curation:**
 - **Careful data selection is critical:** Some datasets (e.g., MMIF, SHP) offered limited or no benefit.
 - **Dataset-specific strengths:** Different datasets enhanced specific capabilities (e.g., MMPR and RLAIF-V improved hallucination scores, R1-Reward was effective for reasoning). No single dataset significantly improved coding tasks, indicating the need for specialized data.
 - **Surprising efficacy of text-only data:** Incorporating high-quality text-only preference datasets (e.g., Ultra-Hard, Olmo-2) significantly **improved multimodal RM performance**, particularly in dimensions like safety and mathematics, without necessarily degrading multimodal capabilities. Text-only data was also deemed necessary to maintain strong text-only reward modeling capabilities.
5. **Optimizing Multimodal RMs for Pure-Text Tasks:**
 - An MLLM trained on a larger, mixed-media dataset did not show performance improvements over an MLLM trained solely on text-only data for pure-text reward modeling tasks.
 - Dedicated **LLM-based architectures (e.g., Qwen 2.5 8B, Qwen 3 8B) inherently outperformed MLLMs** of comparable scale for pure-text reward modeling.
 - This suggests a **modular approach** (training a dedicated pure-text RM and integrating it with a multimodal RM, dynamically selected based on input modality) is currently more effective than a single, universal MLLM-based RM for both domains.
6. **Impact of Base Model Selection and Scale:**
 - Performance varied significantly across different MLLM backbone families; Qwen-VL series generally excelled on multimodal benchmarks, while Intern-VL series performed better on text-centric tasks.
 - **Diminishing returns with scale:** Simply increasing the model scale (e.g., from 2B to 8B or 7B to 72B within the same family) yielded limited performance gains, suggesting that models under 10B parameters offer a highly effective and resource-efficient option, especially given computational constraints.
7. **Ensemble Strategies for Reward Models:**
 - Ensembling multiple reward models consistently led to **substantial performance gains** on both multimodal and text-only benchmarks.
 - A simple **averaging strategy** proved as effective as or superior to more complex validation-based weighting methods, while requiring less overhead.
 - Increasing **model diversity** within the ensemble (e.g., adding a pure-text LLM) further improved reward modeling capabilities, especially for text-only tasks.

**BaseReward Performance (State-of-the-Art):**

- BaseReward (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a 2-layer SiLU MLP head, trained on curated mixed data) achieved a **new state-of-the-art (SOTA)** on major benchmarks:
 - **MM-RLHF-Reward Bench:** Outperformed previous SOTA (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) by approximately 11% in accuracy and 23.32% in Acc+.
 - **VL-Reward Bench:** Improved upon the previous best by approximately 14.2% in Overall Accuracy.
 - **Multimodal Reward Bench:** Achieved the second-best result, with the slight gap attributed to the lack of coding-specific data in its training set.
- BaseReward, as a classical reward model, demonstrated **significantly faster inference speed** compared to generative or critic-based RMs (e.g., R1-Reward, MM-RLHF-Reward).

**Reinforcement Learning with BaseReward:**

- Integrating BaseReward into an RL pipeline for fine-tuning an MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL 3B) showed its practical utility.
- BaseReward-based rewards **outperformed R1-Reward** across all evaluation benchmarks, despite R1-Reward being computationally more expensive.
- A **hybrid rule-based + BaseReward strategy** proved optimal, achieving consistent and significant performance gains across diverse perception, reasoning, and conversational tasks. This approach effectively combined the precision of rule-based rewards for objective tasks with the nuanced understanding of BaseReward for subjective evaluations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "BaseReward" paper makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact on the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and AI alignment:

1. **Standardization and "Recipe" for MRM Development:** The most prominent contribution is providing the first comprehensive, empirically-backed "recipe" for constructing high-performance Multimodal Reward Models (MRMs). In a rapidly evolving field often characterized by ad-hoc solutions, this systematic guide offers invaluable clarity and direction for researchers and practitioners. It distills complex design choices into actionable recommendations, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for effective MRM development.
2. **Establishment of a Powerful, Efficient SOTA Baseline:** BaseReward sets new state-of-the-art performance on critical MRM benchmarks like MM-RLHF-Reward Bench and VL-Reward Bench, often outperforming both previous open-source and proprietary models. Crucially, it achieves this with a simple, optimized Naive-RM architecture, which offers significantly faster inference speeds compared to more complex generative or critic-based RMs. This combination of performance and efficiency makes BaseReward a highly practical and accessible baseline for the community.
3. **Enhanced MLLM Alignment:** By providing a superior and more reliable reward signal, BaseReward directly enables more effective Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for MLLMs. This translates to MLLMs that are better aligned with human preferences, generating responses that are more helpful, harmless, factual, and reliable across a diverse range of multimodal tasks, from perception and reasoning to complex conversations. This is critical for building trustworthy AI systems.
4. **Key Insights into MRM Design and Data Curation:**
 - The research empirically validates the effectiveness of a simple yet optimized reward head architecture (2-layer MLP with SiLU).
 - It highlights the surprising and crucial role of **high-quality text-only preference data in enhancing multimodal judgment**, particularly for safety and mathematical capabilities. This insight challenges conventional wisdom that multimodal tasks strictly require multimodal data for preference learning.
 - It demonstrates the **diminishing returns of merely scaling up MLLM backbones**, suggesting that resource-efficient models under 10B parameters can be highly effective.
 - The findings advocate for a **modular approach**—combining dedicated text-only RMs with multimodal RMs, rather than forcing a single MLLM-based RM to excel in both domains—to optimize performance across all modalities.
5. **Practical Validation in Real-World RL:** The integration of BaseReward into a reinforcement learning pipeline, and its demonstrated success in consistently improving MLLM performance across various tasks, confirms its practical utility beyond static benchmark scores. The effectiveness of the hybrid rule-based + BaseReward strategy offers a robust framework for designing real-world reward functions.
6. **Acceleration of Future Research:** The detailed ablation studies and the open nature of the "recipe" (implied by providing a guide to the community) will undoubtedly serve as a foundational reference. This will accelerate future research into MLLM alignment, allowing other researchers to build upon established best practices and focus on new challenges and innovations, rather than re-evaluating fundamental architectural and training choices.

In conclusion, "BaseReward" not only delivers a top-tier multimodal reward model but, more importantly, provides the community with a clear, empirically-backed guide for developing robust reward models, thereby significantly advancing the alignment and practical utility of the next generation of Multimodal Large Language Models.
