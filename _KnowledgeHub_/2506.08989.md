# SwS: Self-aware Weakness-driven Problem Synthesis in Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SwS framework enhances Large Language Model reasoning by enabling self-aware, weakness-driven problem synthesis for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). It generates targeted, high-quality training data, leading to substantial performance gains on mathematical benchmarks compared to baseline RL.

## Problem

- High scarcity and cost of acquiring high-quality, human-labeled, verifiable data for training Large Language Models (LLMs) via Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).
- Existing synthetic datasets are often suboptimal for RLVR due to a lack of rigorous verification for reference answers, which are crucial for generating robust training signals.
- Inefficient problem augmentation strategies that indiscriminately expand datasets, resulting in problems that are either too trivial (no learning signal) or too challenging (gradient vanishing), hindering effective RL training.

## Method

- The SwS framework employs a ==three-stage pipeline: self-aware weakness identification, targeted problem synthesis, and augmented RLVR training==.
- It identifies an LLM's weaknesses by analyzing consistently failed problems during a preliminary RL training phase, classifying problems with ==low and decreasing accuracy== as targets.
- New problems are generated by ==extracting and recombining concepts from failed problems==, with the augmentation budget dynamically allocated based on category-specific failure rates, and then rigorously filtered for quality, verifiable answers, and appropriate difficulty.

## Results

- SwS consistently yielded average absolute performance improvements of **+10.0%** for 7B models and **+7.7%** for 32B models across a range of mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH, AIME).
- The framework effectively mitigated identified weaknesses, showing significant gains of **+20%** in 'Intermediate Algebra' and **+5%** in 'Geometry' for previously failed problems.
- Ablation studies confirmed that 'Medium' and 'Hard' synthetic problems provided more sustained and significant performance gains compared to 'Simple' problems, highlighting the importance of appropriate challenge levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Consistently failed problems in initial RL training reveal specific model weaknesses that can be systematically leveraged to guide more efficient and focused learning.
> - Targeted problem synthesis, by extracting and recombining concepts from identified weaknesses, creates high-quality, difficulty-tailored training data that significantly enhances an LLM's reasoning abilities and generalization.
> - Rigorous filtering of synthetic problems based on solvability and appropriate difficulty (e.g., accuracy within 25%-75%) ensures that the generated data provides meaningful gradient signals and prevents issues like gradient vanishing.
