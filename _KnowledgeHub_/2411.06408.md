# Visuotactile-Based Learning for Insertion with Compliant Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tel-Aviv University and Rutgers University developed a visuotactile learning framework that allows compliant robotic hands to perform robust object insertion tasks without relying on proprioceptive feedback. The system, trained via a teacher-student distillation process in simulation, successfully transferred zero-shot to a real robot, achieving higher insertion success rates for various objects, including novel ones, and demonstrating resilience to real-world perturbations.

## Problem

- Compliant, underactuated robotic hands, while adaptable and cost-effective, lack precise proprioceptive feedback (e.g., joint angles, loads), making precise control for contact-rich tasks like insertion difficult.
- Robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise contact management often rely on rigid hands, which are complex, expensive, and less adaptable to variations in object geometry or grasp uncertainty.
- Developing robust manipulation skills with compliant hands is challenging due to their inherent flexibility and the difficulty in accurately modeling and observing their interaction dynamics.

## Method

- A two-phase simulation-based learning framework employs a PPO-trained teacher policy with privileged information to distill knowledge into a Transformer-based student policy using only visuotactile inputs.
- Multimodal perception integrates depth-only visual point clouds of the object and socket with high-resolution tactile images from AllSight sensors, processed to be robust to sim-to-real discrepancies.
- Extensive domain randomization and sim-to-real bridging techniques, such as background subtraction for tactile data and action delays, ensure zero-shot transferability of the learned policy to a real compliant robot.

## Results

- The visuotactile policy achieved the highest simulation success rates and demonstrated superior robustness against increasing visual noise, outperforming visual-only counterparts.
- In zero-shot real-world deployment, the visuotactile policy significantly improved insertion success rates across 11 diverse object-socket pairs, including 6 novel objects, reaching **95%** for a cylinder compared to **80%** for visual-only.
- The robotic system exhibited robust recovery capabilities from manual disturbances, socket pose variations, and temporary visual occlusions, validating its real-world effectiveness for precision insertion tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile sensing is crucial for robust precision manipulation with compliant hands, effectively compensating for the absence of proprioceptive feedback by providing essential local contact information.
> - Structured 3D visual representations, specifically segmented point clouds, prove more effective than raw depth images for capturing the geometric and spatial relationships necessary for complex insertion tasks.
> - A teacher-student distillation framework, paired with extensive domain randomization and sim-to-real bridging, enables efficient training and zero-shot deployment of policies for contact-rich tasks on compliant hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robotic manipulation, compliant robotics, multimodal perception, and learning-based control, addressing a critical challenge in modern robotics: enabling robots to perform complex, contact-rich tasks in unstructured environments with high precision and reliability.

Traditionally, much of the research in robot manipulation has focused on **rigid hands**. While these hands can achieve high precision and often possess excellent proprioceptive feedback (e.g., joint angles, loads), they are typically complex, expensive, fragile, and less adaptable to variations in object geometry or grasping uncertainty. The state of rigid hands is often deterministic, allowing for more straightforward control when combined with visual and tactile perception.

In contrast, **compliant hands**, which utilize underactuated mechanisms (tendons, springs), offer inherent advantages like greater adaptability to object shapes, stable grasps, and lower cost. However, their inherent compliance introduces significant challenges: the true state of the hand (finger positions, forces) is often unobservable, making precise control and interaction modeling difficult due to the lack of proprioception and accurate analytical models. Developing robust manipulation skills with compliant hands remains a key challenge.

To overcome these limitations, **multimodal perception** has emerged as a crucial area, especially the integration of visual and tactile information, often termed visuotactile sensing. Visual perception provides global context, aiding in object localization and rough pose estimation, while tactile perception offers local, detailed contact information essential for delicate manipulations and responding to subtle changes in forces or configurations. For rigid hands, visuotactile sensing often works in conjunction with proprioception to fully capture the hand-object state. This paper distinguishes itself by exploring visuotactile sensing for *compliant hands without relying on proprioceptive feedback*, which is a novel and significant departure from much of the existing literature.

The paper leverages **learning-based methods** to acquire manipulation policies, a trend gaining widespread popularity for its potential for generalization. Given the data-intensive nature of real-world reinforcement learning (RL), the authors employ a **simulation-to-real (sim-to-real) transfer** approach. This involves training a policy in a high-fidelity simulator (IsaacGym) and then deploying it on a real robot. While sim-to-real has shown success in various tasks, its application to compliant hands presents unique difficulties due to the complex and uncertain dynamics that are challenging to simulate accurately. The authors address this by incorporating extensive domain randomization and specific sim-to-real bridging techniques tailored for compliant hands and multimodal inputs.

The specific task chosen, **insertion**, serves as an excellent benchmark for evaluating contact management in robotic manipulation. It is a contact-rich task common in industrial assembly and dense packing, requiring precise positioning and delicate interaction, thus providing a strong validation for the robustness of the proposed system. By tackling insertion with compliant hands and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, this work pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with simpler, more adaptable hardware, contributing significantly to the field of generalizable robot manipulation in uncertain environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable robust and precise object insertion tasks using **underactuated compliant hands**, despite their inherent challenges such as lack of precise proprioception and increased uncertainty in hand-object interactions.

The key motivations driving this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Leveraging Compliant Hands:** Compliant hands offer advantages over rigid hands in terms of adaptability to object shapes, stability of grasp, and cost-effectiveness. However, their flexibility makes precise control for contact-rich tasks, like insertion, particularly challenging due to the inherent uncertainties and the inability to precisely observe the hand's internal state (e.g., finger joint angles or loads). The authors are motivated to harness the benefits of compliant hands while mitigating their control difficulties.
- **Addressing Proprioception Deficiency:** A central motivation is to overcome the lack of precise finger proprioception in compliant hands, which is typically available in rigid hands and crucial for coordinated manipulation. The authors propose that **additional sensing modalities**, specifically visuotactile perception, are essential to compensate for this deficiency and enable robust insertion capabilities.
- **Investigating Essential Sensing Requirements:** The paper explicitly aims to explore the essential sensing requirements for successful insertion tasks with compliant hands. This involves understanding how visual perception (global context, rough pose estimation) and tactile perception (local contact information, subtle changes) can be effectively integrated to manage uncertainties.
- **Developing a Robust Policy Learning Framework:** To achieve robust insertion, the authors are motivated to develop a simulation-based multi-modal policy learning framework. This framework leverages all-around tactile sensing and an extrinsic depth camera, and crucially, employs a transformer-based policy trained via a teacher-student distillation process to enable zero-shot transfer to real-world robotic systems.
- **Enabling Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Given the prohibitive data requirements for real-world reinforcement learning, a significant motivation is to train policies entirely in simulation and deploy them on real robots without further training. This necessitates robust sim-to-real transfer techniques, including extensive domain randomization and careful adaptation of sensory data processing.
- **Pioneering Visuotactile Sensing without Proprioception:** The authors explicitly state their motivation to pioneer the use of visuotactile sensing for compliant hand manipulation without reliance on proprioceptive feedback, which represents a novel direction in the field.
- **Integrating Object-Centric and Spatial-Aware Representations:** The work is motivated by the need for effective policy design that incorporates object-centric, spatial-aware, and relative-sensitive visuotactile representations. These attributes are considered crucial for focusing on task-relevant objects, reasoning in 3D space, and detecting subtle contact variations, thereby improving the ability to manage contact in manipulation.
- **Establishing a Benchmark:** By selecting insertion as a test case, the authors aim to establish a robust benchmark for evaluating the effectiveness of contact management in robotic manipulation, particularly for compliant systems in real-world scenarios requiring precise positioning.

In essence, the core objective is to make compliant hands more capable and versatile for precision interaction tasks by intelligently combining visual and tactile feedback, facilitated by advanced simulation-to-real learning techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper involves a sophisticated, simulation-based, two-phase policy learning framework designed for zero-shot transfer to a real-world compliant robotic system.

**A. Problem Formulation and Hardware Setup:**
The system consists of a robotic arm (Kinova Gen3) equipped with a three-finger OpenHand Model-O compliant hand. Each fingertip integrates an AllSight 3D round optical tactile sensor. An extrinsic RGB-D camera (Zed Mini Stereo Camera) provides visual input. The task is to insert a grasped object into a nearby socket. Key assumptions include unknown exact object and socket shapes/poses (except visibility), initial grasp uncertainty, and crucially, the *unavailability of hand proprioception* (finger joint angles or loads) in the compliant hand. The policy's goal is to learn to align and insert the object, taking the End-Effector (EE) pose, RGB-D image, and tactile images as input, and outputting a change in EE pose.

**B. Visuotactile Perception:**
Robust perception across simulation and real world is critical:
1. **Visual Perception:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap, only **depth data** from the RGB-D camera is used. Inspired by, segmented depth images and masks are generated for both the object and the socket. These 2D masks are then back-projected into 3D space to create segmented **point clouds** (`p_object` and `p_socket`). This dual representation captures geometry and enhances spatial reasoning for precise insertion.
2. **Tactile Perception:** High-resolution AllSight optical tactile sensors are simulated using **TACTO** within IsaacGym. The simulator is calibrated using real-world AllSight reference images to minimize the sim-to-real gap (Figure 3). During inference, a randomly picked reference image is subtracted from the observed tactile image (T = ˆT - T_ref). This background subtraction focuses on color gradients caused by contact, making the model agnostic to background variations.

**C. Visuotactile Insertion Policy Learning (Two-Phase Simulation Training):**
Direct real-world RL is prohibitively data-intensive. Thus, a teacher-student distillation process is employed in simulation.

1. **Teacher Policy Training:**
 - **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a state-of-the-art RL algorithm.
 - **Environment:** IsaacGym, leveraging 4,096 parallel environments for efficient training.
 - **Input:** The teacher policy uses **privileged information**, which is not available to the student or in the real world. This includes:
 - **Task-relative features:** Hand and arm poses, velocities, object and socket positions/orientations, and their relative errors (50-dimensional vector `e_task`).
 - **Physical properties:** Object mass, dimensions, and coefficient of friction (13-dimensional vector `e_phys`).
 - The complete privileged information is `e_t = [e_task, e_phys]`.
 - Additionally, the policy receives observation `o_t` (current EE pose `x_t` and last action `a_t-1`) to ensure robot-agnosticism.
 - **Network:** Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
 - **Reward Function:** A composite reward (Equation 1) adopted from with auxiliary terms: `r_d` (penalizes key-point misalignment), `r_e` (rewards engagement with socket), `r_o` (penalizes orientation misalignment), `r_a` (penalizes high actions), and `r_w` (penalizes action differences for smooth motion).

2. **Student Distillation:**
 - Once the teacher policy is trained, its knowledge is distilled into a student policy that relies solely on visuotactile information.
 - **Architecture:** A **Transformer-based architecture** is used to process the multimodal sensory input, capturing temporal dependencies through `k` past feature vectors.
 - **Input Processing for Student:**
 - **Tactile Encoding:** Stacked tactile images (`T_t`) are processed through convolutional layers to produce an `m`-dimensional representation (`f_tactile,t`).
 - **Visual Encoding:** `p_object,t` and `p_socket,t` are separately encoded using an initialized PointNet, then concatenated and passed through an MLP to generate a joint feature representation (`f_joint,t`) capturing spatial relationships.
 - **Observation Encoding:** `o_t` (EE pose, last action) is processed through a separate MLP to yield `f_obs,t`.
 - These encodings are concatenated to form `f_t = [f_tactile,t, f_joint,t, f_obs,t]`, which is then fed into the Transformer.
 - **Training Phases:**
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) Warm-up:** The student policy is initialized by minimizing the `ℓ2` distance between its predicted actions (`ˆa_t`) and the teacher's actions (`a_t`), given visuotactile inputs collected during teacher-guided simulations.
 - **DAgger Refinement:** After BC, DAgger is used. The student generates actions, and the teacher provides corrective feedback. Trajectories are collected either by the teacher (with probability `β`) or the student (with probability `1-β`), with `β` gradually decreasing over time.

**D. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:**
Extensive techniques are employed to ensure robustness and zero-shot transfer:
- **Domain Randomization:** Applied to perception inputs (noise, lighting, gray-scaling for tactile; camera pose/FOV noise, pixel flips, update delays, random masking, point dropout, jittering, rotations/scaling for point clouds), physical properties (friction, finger mass), and object attributes (dimensions, clearances, initial poses/orientations).
- **Training Techniques:**
 - Up to 10-step action delay to account for real-world latencies.
 - Random perturbations to socket pose (10% probability) to train recovery behaviors.
 - Random scaling of actions for varied EE motions.

**E. Real-World Policy Deployment:**
The trained student policy is deployed zero-shot on the physical robot (Figure 4).
- **Tactile Processing:** Raw real-world tactile images undergo gray-scaling, background subtraction, and ROI cropping to match simulation inputs.
- **Visual Processing:** Task-relevant objects are segmented using **TrackingSAM** at 10Hz, generating 2D masks. These are back-projected to 3D point clouds using depth data, then transformed into the robot's base frame.
The processed visuotactile information is fed to the Transformer policy to generate control signals for the robot arm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulation and real-world settings to evaluate the proposed visuotactile learning framework.

**A. Simulation Results:**
- **Training Efficiency:** The teacher policy, leveraging privileged information, achieved a maximum insertion success rate of 92.4% over approximately 10^8 episodic states. The student policy, trained through distillation, required about 0.7 × 10^7 episodic states, demonstrating the efficiency of the teacher-student approach.
- **Ablation Study (Figure 5):**
 - **EE pose only:** Showed very poor performance, highlighting the insufficiency of basic kinematic data for precise, contact-rich tasks.
 - **Tactile sensing only:** Provided a slight improvement over EE pose but was largely ineffective, indicating that local contact information alone cannot compensate for the lack of global context and proprioception.
 - **Visual-Depth & Visual-PCL:** Both visual-only policies achieved higher success rates. Visual-PCL (segmented point clouds) outperformed Visual-Depth, underscoring the benefit of explicitly structured 3D representations for capturing geometric and spatial information crucial for insertion.
 - **Visuotactile Policy:** The proposed visuotactile policy consistently achieved the highest success rate among the student policies, demonstrating the synergistic advantage of combining detailed structural information from point clouds with local contact feedback from tactile sensors.
- **Robustness to Visual Noise (Figure 6):** The visuotactile policy exhibited significantly greater robustness to increasing levels of Gaussian noise added to the point cloud data compared to the Visual-PCL policy. This finding emphasizes that tactile sensing is crucial for maintaining accuracy, especially in scenarios where visual perception is degraded or uncertain.
- **Contribution of Training Techniques:** Preliminary tests showed that RMA struggled to generalize across multiple object-socket pairs. Furthermore, asynchronous restarting and diversified data sampling were identified as critical for the visuotactile policy, leading to a 50% improvement in success rate, highlighting the importance of comprehensive domain randomization and training strategies.

**B. Real-World Results (Zero-Shot Transfer):**
- **Generalization to Novel Objects (Table I):** The student policies, trained entirely in simulation, were deployed on a real robot without further training. They were tested on 11 different object-socket pairs, including 6 novel objects not seen during training.
- **Performance Comparison:**
 - **Visual-PCL:** Achieved strong accuracy on both training and novel objects (e.g., 80% for Cylinder, 80% for Hexagon (S), 75% for Gum Box).
 - **Visuotactile Policy:** Significantly boosted insertion success rates across almost all objects compared to Visual-PCL. For instance, it achieved 95% for Cylinder (vs. 80%), 90% for Ellipsoid (S) (vs. 85%), 85% for Triangular prism (vs. 65%), and 90% for Gum Box (vs. 75%). This confirms the real-world benefit of integrating tactile information.
 - **Visuotactile w/o Diff. & Aug.:** A visuotactile policy trained without tactile image differences and augmentation performed poorly (e.g., 70% for Cylinder, 60% for Ellipsoid (S)), underscoring the critical role of these sim-to-real bridging techniques for tactile data.
- **Robustness to Disturbances (Figure 8):** The robot demonstrated the ability to recover from manual perturbations to the object, variations in socket pose, and temporary visual occlusions (as shown in supplementary videos). This highlights the robust insertion capabilities learned by the visuotactile policy.
- **Failure Modes:** Failures in insertion tasks were generally attributed to unstable grasps, leading to object slippage or drops, rather than a lack of policy guidance.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate the superior performance of the visuotactile policy, both in simulation and real-world zero-shot deployments, particularly in noisy conditions and when dealing with novel objects. The findings validate the crucial role of tactile sensing in augmenting visual perception for robust precision manipulation with compliant hands, especially when proprioception is unavailable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for applications involving contact-rich tasks with cost-effective compliant hardware. Its significance and potential impact can be distilled into several key areas:

- **Enabling Robust Manipulation with Compliant Hands:** The most direct impact is in making compliant, underactuated hands viable for high-precision, contact-rich tasks like insertion. Compliant hands are inherently more adaptable and cost-effective than rigid counterparts, but their lack of precise proprioception and inherent uncertainties have historically limited their application in demanding tasks. This work effectively addresses these limitations, broadening the practical utility of compliant robotic systems.
- **Pioneering Visuotactile Sensing without Proprioception:** This paper marks a novel contribution by demonstrating the successful integration of visuotactile sensing for compliant hands *without reliance on proprioceptive feedback*. This is a crucial shift, as many previous multimodal approaches for rigid hands assumed proprioceptive data. By showing that robust contact management is achievable with only external vision and finger-tip tactile sensors, the research opens new avenues for control paradigms for simpler, under-instrumented robotic hands.
- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer for Compliant Systems:** The proposed teacher-student learning framework, coupled with extensive domain randomization and specific sim-to-real bridging strategies (e.g., depth-only vision, tactile image background subtraction, action delays), provides a robust methodology for transferring policies trained in simulation to real-world compliant hardware. This is particularly impactful given the noted difficulty in accurately simulating complex, uncertain compliant dynamics. The zero-shot transfer capability significantly reduces the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection and training.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The work demonstrates strong generalization to novel object-socket pairs and impressive robustness against various real-world disturbances, including manual perturbations, socket pose variations, and temporary occlusions. This indicates that the learned visuotactile representations are highly effective, object-centric, spatial-aware, and sensitive to relative positioning, which are critical for adaptability in unstructured environments.
- **Methodological Contributions to Multimodal Learning:** The use of a Transformer-based architecture to process fused visuotactile inputs, combined with the detailed encoding of point cloud data and tactile images, offers a strong blueprint for future research in multimodal perception for robotic control. The ablation studies clearly highlight the synergistic benefits of combining visual and tactile information, especially in mitigating the effects of noise.
- **Potential for Industrial and Service Robotics:** The ability to perform robust insertion tasks with compliant hands has direct applications in manufacturing, assembly, and logistics, where fine manipulation and adaptation to varied part geometries are common. By reducing hardware complexity and cost while maintaining performance, this research can accelerate the adoption of robotic automation in a wider range of industries.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The provision of open-source simulation and deployment code is a valuable contribution to the robotics community. It allows other researchers to build upon this work, benchmark new approaches, and accelerate progress in compliant manipulation and visuotactile learning.

While the current work focuses on insertion, the underlying methodology supports adaptability to variations within this task by modifying simulation parameters and environmental conditions. Future work aims to extend this framework to even more complex manipulation tasks, such as dexterous object reorientation or multi-step assembly, where long-horizon planning and intricate object dynamics pose further challenges. The exploration of leveraging RGB data alongside depth and addressing limitations with reflective/transparent surfaces also represents promising future directions. Ultimately, this research takes a significant step towards enabling robots with compliant hands to interact with the physical world with greater dexterity, adaptability, and autonomy.
