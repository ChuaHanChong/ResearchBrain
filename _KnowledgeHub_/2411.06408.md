# Visuotactile-Based Learning for Insertion with Compliant Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tel-Aviv University and Rutgers University developed a visuotactile learning framework that allows compliant robotic hands to perform robust object insertion tasks without relying on proprioceptive feedback. The system, trained via a teacher-student distillation process in simulation, successfully transferred zero-shot to a real robot, achieving higher insertion success rates for various objects, including novel ones, and demonstrating resilience to real-world perturbations.

## Problem

- Compliant, underactuated robotic hands, while adaptable and cost-effective, lack precise proprioceptive feedback (e.g., joint angles, loads), making precise control for contact-rich tasks like insertion difficult.
- Robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise contact management often rely on rigid hands, which are complex, expensive, and less adaptable to variations in object geometry or grasp uncertainty.
- Developing robust manipulation skills with compliant hands is challenging due to their inherent flexibility and the difficulty in accurately modeling and observing their interaction dynamics.

## Method

- A two-phase simulation-based learning framework employs a PPO-trained teacher policy with privileged information to distill knowledge into a Transformer-based student policy using only visuotactile inputs.
- Multimodal perception integrates depth-only visual point clouds of the object and socket with high-resolution tactile images from AllSight sensors, processed to be robust to sim-to-real discrepancies.
- Extensive domain randomization and sim-to-real bridging techniques, such as background subtraction for tactile data and action delays, ensure zero-shot transferability of the learned policy to a real compliant robot.

## Results

- The visuotactile policy achieved the highest simulation success rates and demonstrated superior robustness against increasing visual noise, outperforming visual-only counterparts.
- In zero-shot real-world deployment, the visuotactile policy significantly improved insertion success rates across 11 diverse object-socket pairs, including 6 novel objects, reaching **95%** for a cylinder compared to **80%** for visual-only.
- The robotic system exhibited robust recovery capabilities from manual disturbances, socket pose variations, and temporary visual occlusions, validating its real-world effectiveness for precision insertion tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile sensing is crucial for robust precision manipulation with compliant hands, effectively compensating for the absence of proprioceptive feedback by providing essential local contact information.
> - Structured 3D visual representations, specifically segmented point clouds, prove more effective than raw depth images for capturing the geometric and spatial relationships necessary for complex insertion tasks.
> - A teacher-student distillation framework, paired with extensive domain randomization and sim-to-real bridging, enables efficient training and zero-shot deployment of policies for contact-rich tasks on compliant hardware.
