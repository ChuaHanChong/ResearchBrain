# Generating Physically Stable and Buildable Brick Structures from Text

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University developed BRICKGPT, a generative AI model that produces physically stable and buildable 3D brick structures directly from text descriptions. The method achieves 98.8% stability in generated designs, which can be successfully assembled by both human users and a dual-robot-arm system, outperforming existing text-to-3D baselines in physical realizability.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-3D generation methods primarily produce digital designs that often lack physical realizability.
- Generated 3D objects are typically difficult or impossible to assemble using standard physical components.
- Digital designs frequently result in physically unstable structures (e.g., floating parts, collapse) when translated to the real world.

## Method

- Constructed the **StableText2Brick** dataset, containing over 47,000 physically stable brick structures paired with detailed GPT-4o generated text captions.
- Fine-tuned an ==autoregressive== Large Language Model (**LLaMA-3**.2-1B-Instruct) for "next-brick prediction," treating 3D assembly as a sequential text generation task.
- Integrated physics-aware rejection sampling (brick-by-brick validity checks) and a physics-aware rollback mechanism during inference to ensure structural stability and buildability.

## Results

- BRICKGPT achieved 98.8% stability in generated structures, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art baselines which ranged from 25.2% to 75.2% stability.
- The generated brick designs were successfully assembled by a dual-robot-arm system and manually by human users, validating their real-world buildability.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both brick-by-brick rejection sampling and physics-aware rollback are critical, boosting stability from 12.8% (without either) to 98.8%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repurposing autoregressive LLMs for sequential "next-brick prediction" can effectively generate complex 3D physical structures from text prompts.
> - Explicitly embedding physics-aware constraints, such as real-time validity checks and iterative rollback, is essential for generating physically stable and buildable designs.
> - A large-scale dataset of physically valid and stable structures with diverse text descriptions is crucial for training generative models that bridge the gap between digital design and physical realization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: text-to-3D generation, autoregressive modeling, robotics, and physics-aware AI.

**Text-to-3D Generation:** Recent advancements in generative models and neural rendering have revolutionized 3D content creation from text prompts. Works like DreamFusion and those using diffusion models have enabled the synthesis of diverse 3D objects with high-fidelity geometry and appearance. However, a significant gap remains: these digital designs often lack physical realizability. They might be geometrically intricate but impossible to assemble with standard components or physically unstable (e.g., floating parts, collapse). This paper directly addresses this critical challenge, moving beyond purely digital representations to generate *physically buildable* 3D objects.

**Autoregressive 3D Modeling and Large Language Models (LLMs):** The paper leverages the power of autoregressive LLMs, which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in sequence modeling and natural language understanding. While LLMs have been increasingly applied to 3D generation (e.g., LLaMA-Mesh for generating 3D shapes in plain-text format), most existing methods focus on generating meshes, which are high-resolution but still abstract geometric representations. BRICKGPT innovatively repurposes LLMs for "next-brick prediction," treating the assembly of a physical structure as a sequential token generation task. This is a novel application that capitalizes on LLMs' reasoning capabilities for a practical, physically constrained problem.

**Brick Assembly and Design Generation:** The specific domain of interlocking toy bricks (like LEGO®) has been studied extensively, often focusing on converting existing 3D shapes into brick structures or generating designs for a single object category. These approaches typically involve optimization problems guided by heuristics, stability analysis for rearranging brick layouts, or learning-based techniques with limitations in complexity or generality. BRICKGPT distinguishes itself by generating brick structures directly from *freeform text prompts*, without requiring an intermediate 3D model, and critically, ensuring *physical stability and buildability* from the outset. This moves beyond prior work that assumes a valid brick structure exists or focuses solely on 2D mosaics.

**Physics-Aware Generation:** Incorporating physics into 3D generation is an emerging area. Previous methods typically involve either direct constraint enforcement (e.g., collision avoidance, contact requirements) during optimization or learned validation using physics simulators or data-driven assessment models. This paper marks a significant step as the first attempt to integrate physics-aware constraints, specifically structural stability, into text-based *brick assembly structure generation*. The proposed brick-by-brick rejection sampling and physics-aware rollback mechanisms are sophisticated strategies for managing these constraints during the generative process, balancing creativity with physical laws.

In summary, BRICKGPT makes a crucial contribution by bridging the gap between high-level textual descriptions and the low-level physical constraints of real-world object assembly. It pioneers a new paradigm for generative AI by focusing on physically realizable designs, thus pushing the boundaries of what text-to-3D models can achieve for practical applications in design, robotics, and manufacturing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind BRICKGPT is to address the significant challenge of generating 3D objects that are not only aesthetically pleasing and semantically aligned with text descriptions but are also **physically realizable and buildable** in the real world. The authors identify two primary shortcomings of existing text-to-3D generation methods:
1. **Assembly Difficulty:** Digital designs often cannot be easily assembled using standard physical components.
2. **Physical Instability:** Even if assembly is theoretically possible, the resulting structure may be physically unstable, leading to collapse, floating parts, or disconnections.

To tackle these issues, the research focuses on the domain of interlocking toy bricks (e.g., LEGO® blocks) due to their widespread use, standardized components, and suitability as a reproducible benchmark for physical construction. The specific objectives for BRICKGPT are to train a generative model that produces designs with the following characteristics:

- **Physically Stable:** The generated structures must possess strong structural integrity, be built on a baseplate, and avoid any floating or collapsing bricks. This implies adherence to fundamental physics laws governing static equilibrium.
- **Buildable:** The designs must be compatible with standard toy brick pieces and capable of being assembled sequentially, brick-by-brick, by both humans and automated robotic systems. This requires precise placement and interlocking connections.
- **Direct Generation from Freeform Text:** The model should take natural language text prompts as input and directly output brick assembly structures, bypassing the need for intermediate 3D mesh representations. This aims to simplify the design process for users.
- **Diverse and Aesthetically Pleasing:** Beyond stability and buildability, the generated structures should be varied, visually appealing, and closely align with the semantic intent of the input text prompts.

To achieve these objectives, the authors were motivated to:
- **Construct a Large-Scale, Physically Stable Dataset:** Recognizing the data-driven nature of modern generative models, they set out to create StableText2Brick, a novel dataset explicitly containing physically stable brick structures paired with detailed text captions.
- **Repurpose Autoregressive LLMs:** They aimed to adapt existing powerful LLMs, originally designed for next-token prediction in natural language, for the novel task of "next-brick prediction," formulating 3D design as a sequence generation problem.
- **Integrate Physics-Aware Constraints into Inference:** To overcome the inherent limitations of purely data-driven models in adhering to strict physical laws, they sought to develop an efficient mechanism (validity checks and physics-aware rollback) that actively prunes infeasible predictions during the autoregressive generation process.
- **Demonstrate Real-World Utility:** A key motivation was to validate the practical applicability of their generated designs through manual assembly by humans and automated assembly by robotic arms, showcasing the tangible impact of their physically constrained generative approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BRICKGPT tackles the challenge of text-to-physically-stable-brick generation through a novel, end-to-end autoregressive approach. The methodology can be broken down into three main components: dataset construction, model fine-tuning, and physics-aware inference.

#### 3.1. StableText2Brick Dataset Construction

To train a large-scale autoregressive model, a comprehensive dataset of stable brick structures with associated captions was essential. The StableText2Brick dataset, consisting of over 47,000 brick structures from 28,000+ unique 3D objects across 21 ShapeNetCore categories, was created through a multi-step process:

1. **Shape-to-Brick Conversion:** 3D meshes from ShapeNetCore were first voxelized and downsampled into a 20x20x20 grid. A "delete-and-rebuild" algorithm, similar to prior work, was then applied to generate initial brick layouts. This algorithm was enhanced to introduce randomness and generate multiple structural variations for each 3D object, increasing data diversity.
2. **Brick Representation:** Each brick structure is represented as a sequence of bricks $B = [b_1, \ldots, b_N]$, where each brick $b_i$ is defined by its dimensions $(h_i, w_i)$ and position $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$. Bricks are 1-unit tall, axis-aligned cuboids, and ordered in a raster-scan manner from bottom to top. This custom text format is concise and crucial for the LLM.
3. **Stability Score Calculation:** Physical stability was assessed using an existing stability analysis method. This method models all forces (gravity, vertical, shear, knob connections) acting on each brick and formulates a nonlinear program to find a static equilibrium with minimum internal stress. A per-brick stability score ($s_i \in$) is derived, where $s_i=0$ indicates structural failure. Only structures where all bricks had $s_i > 0$ were included in the dataset.
4. **Caption Generation:** For each stable brick structure, GPT-4o was prompted to generate five detailed geometric descriptions from 24 different rendered viewpoints. The prompts specifically instructed GPT-4o to omit color and texture information, focusing solely on geometry, and to provide captions with varying levels of detail.

#### 3.2. Model Fine-tuning

The core of BRICKGPT is an autoregressive LLM, specifically LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct, fine-tuned for next-brick prediction.

1. **Instruction Fine-tuning Dataset:** The StableText2Brick data was formatted into instructions: `(user) Create a LEGO model of {caption}. (assistant) {brick-structure}.` The custom brick representation (e.g., `h x w (x,y,z)`) was used to tokenize brick structures, making them compatible with the LLM's text-based input/output.
2. **Autoregressive Prediction:** The fine-tuned model learns to predict the next brick $b_i$ given the previously generated bricks $b_1, \ldots, b_{i-1}$ and the initial text prompt, formulating the design process as $p(b_1, \ldots, b_N | \theta) = \prod_{i=1}^N p(b_i | b_1, \ldots, b_{i-1}, \theta)$. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) was used for efficient fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. Integrating Physical Stability during Inference

Despite training on stable data, the generative model can still produce invalid or unstable structures. To ensure real-world realizability, BRICKGPT incorporates two physics-aware mechanisms during inference:

1. **Brick-by-Brick Rejection Sampling:** For each brick predicted by the LLM, an efficient validity check is performed. This check ensures:
 - The brick is well-formatted and exists in the predefined brick library.
 - The brick's position is within the world space (e.g., 20x20x20 grid).
 - The brick does not collide with any previously placed bricks.
 If a brick violates any of these rules, it is rejected, and the model resamples a new brick. This process is relatively fast and prevents the accumulation of basic structural errors.

2. **Physics-Aware Rollback:** After the model completes generating a full brick structure, a comprehensive stability analysis (using the method described in Section 4.1.3) is performed.
 - If the structure is stable, it is accepted.
 - If the structure is found to be unstable (i.e., at least one brick has a stability score of 0), the algorithm identifies the *first* unstable brick in the sequence. The design is then "rolled back" to the state *before* this unstable brick was generated. Generation then resumes from this stable partial structure. This iterative process continues until a fully stable structure is produced or a maximum number of rollbacks is exceeded. This mechanism efficiently prunes physically infeasible global configurations without over-constraining the model during incremental generation.

#### 3.4. Extensions: Brick Texturing and Coloring

BRICKGPT also includes methods to enhance the visual appearance of the generated structures:

1. **UV Texture Generation:** Given a brick structure and a text prompt describing appearance, occluded bricks are removed. The remaining visible bricks are merged into a mesh, a UV map is generated, and FlashTex, a text-based mesh texturing approach, is used to apply a detailed texture.
2. **Uniform Brick Color Assignment:** For standard toy bricks, the structure is converted to a voxel grid and then to a UV-unwrapped mesh. FlashTex is used to generate a texture based on a color prompt. The average color from the texture for each voxel is calculated, and then for each brick, the average voxel color is found and mapped to the closest color in a standard brick color library.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate BRICKGPT's superior performance in generating physically stable, buildable, and text-aligned brick structures, significantly outperforming existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results and Baseline Comparisons

The paper evaluates BRICKGPT against several baselines, including pre-trained LLaMA models (zero-shot and in-context learning) and state-of-the-art text-to-3D mesh generation methods (LLaMA-Mesh, LGM, XCube, Hunyuan3D-2) followed by a mesh-to-brick conversion. Key metrics include:
- **Validity:** Percentage of generated structures free from out-of-library bricks, out-of-bounds placement, or colliding bricks.
- **Stability:** Percentage of physically stable structures (among valid ones), along with mean and minimum brick stability scores.
- **Prompt Alignment:** CLIP score (between rendered image and text prompt) and DINOv2 similarity (between rendered generated structure and ground-truth structure for the same prompt).

BRICKGPT achieved remarkable results:
- **Validity:** 100% of structures generated by BRICKGPT were valid, significantly higher than 0% for pre-trained LLaMA (zero-shot) and 2.4% for in-context learning. While mesh-to-brick baselines could achieve 94.8-100% validity, their stability was much lower.
- **Stability:** BRICKGPT achieved 98.8% stability, vastly outperforming all baselines (e.g., LLaMA-Mesh: 50.8%, LGM: 25.2%, XCube: 75.2%, Hunyuan3D-2: 75.2%). Even when augmented with post-hoc stability analysis (generating multiple variations and picking the first stable one), baselines still fell short (e.g., Hunyuan3D-2 + stability: 88.4%). BRICKGPT also exhibited higher mean and minimum stability scores (0.996 and 0.915 respectively), indicating robust structural integrity across the entire design.
- **Prompt Alignment:** BRICKGPT maintained strong text-to-image (CLIP score: 0.324) and image-to-image (DINOv2 similarity: 0.880) alignment, comparable to or exceeding the best baselines, demonstrating that the physics constraints did not compromise semantic fidelity.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results and Ablation Study

- **High-Quality and Diverse Generation:** Figure 5 showcases a gallery of results, illustrating that BRICKGPT generates diverse, high-quality, and novel brick structures that closely adhere to text prompts, ranging from chairs and tables to cars and vessels. The black bricks indicate collisions in baseline outputs, visually emphasizing BRICKGPT's advantage.
- **Importance of Physics-Aware Mechanisms:** The ablation study (Table 1, Figure 6) clearly demonstrates the critical role of rejection sampling and physics-aware rollback. Without rejection sampling or rollback, validity plummeted to 37.2% and stability to 12.8%. Without rollback (but with rejection sampling), validity was 100%, but stability was only 24.0%. This confirms that both mechanisms are essential for achieving robust, physically realizable designs.
- **Novelty Analysis:** Figure 9 presents a novelty analysis, showing that generated structures are distinct from their closest neighbors in the training dataset (as measured by Chamfer distance), suggesting that BRICKGPT creates genuinely novel designs rather than merely memorizing examples.

#### 4.3. Extensions and Applications

- **Robotic Assembly:** The generated designs were successfully assembled by a dual-robot-arm system (Figure 10). The robots utilized manipulation policies and asynchronous multi-agent planners, demonstrating the practical utility of BRICKGPT for automated construction.
- **Manual Assembly:** The designs were also successfully hand-assembled by human users (Figure 11), confirming their buildability and physical stability for real-world interaction. The step-by-step output of the autoregressive model naturally serves as an intuitive assembly guide.
- **Brick Texture and Color Generation:** The method demonstrated the ability to apply diverse UV textures or assign uniform colors to individual bricks based on text prompts (Figure 7), adding an appearance dimension to the structural generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BRICKGPT represents a significant leap forward in text-to-3D generation by specifically addressing the critical, yet often overlooked, challenge of **physical realizability and buildability**. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

**1. Bridging the Digital-Physical Divide:** The most profound impact of this work is its ability to bridge the gap between abstract digital 3D models and tangible physical objects. By guaranteeing physical stability and buildability, BRICKGPT moves generative AI beyond virtual representations, enabling the direct creation of instructions for real-world construction. This is a crucial step towards "design for manufacturing" in generative AI.

**2. Democratizing Physical Design:** Complex physical designs, particularly those involving interlocking components, traditionally require specialized expertise in CAD software, structural engineering, and assembly planning. BRICKGPT allows users to generate sophisticated, buildable structures from simple text prompts, making design accessible to a broader audience, including hobbyists, educators, and non-expert designers.

**3. Advancing Robotics and Automated Manufacturing:** The demonstration of automated robotic assembly of the generated structures highlights a direct and powerful application in robotics. BRICKGPT can serve as a front-end for automated manufacturing pipelines, generating assembly instructions directly from high-level user intent. This could accelerate prototyping, reduce manual labor in modular construction, and open new avenues for human-robot collaboration in design and production.

**4. Educational and Creative Tools:** For educational purposes, BRICKGPT can facilitate learning about structural integrity, design principles, and engineering concepts through hands-on construction. Creatives and artists can leverage the tool to rapidly prototype physical sculptures or explore novel architectural forms with guaranteed stability.

**5. Foundational for Future Physics-Aware AI:** The novel integration of physics-aware rejection sampling and rollback into an autoregressive LLM provides a powerful template for future generative models dealing with physical constraints. This methodology can be extended to other modular assembly systems, material science applications, or even more complex dynamic physical interactions, pushing the frontier of AI that understands and respects the laws of the physical world.

**6. Rich Dataset and Open-Source Contribution:** The release of the StableText2Brick dataset, along with the code and models, is a substantial contribution to the research community. This large-scale, carefully curated dataset, explicitly linking text descriptions to physically stable brick structures, will undoubtedly accelerate future research in text-to-3D, robotics, and physics-aware generation.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** While groundbreaking, the paper acknowledges limitations such as the current restriction to a 20x20x20 grid and a fixed set of common brick types. Future work aims to scale up to larger datasets, higher resolutions, and a broader library of brick components (e.g., slopes, tiles), further enhancing the expressiveness and utility of BRICKGPT. Addressing these limitations will expand its applicability to an even wider range of complex and intricate designs.
