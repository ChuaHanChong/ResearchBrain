# Reuse your FLOPs: Scaling RL on Hard Problems by Conditioning on Very Off-Policy Prefixes

> [!summary] Summary
> The PrefixRL framework enables Large Language Models to learn effectively on challenging reasoning tasks by conditioning their reinforcement learning policy on prefixes from successful off-policy traces. It achieves a 2x speedup in training reward acquisition and over 45% increase in final performance on hard problems, while also revealing a unique "back-generalization" phenomenon.

## Problem

- Traditional RL methods for LLMs often stall on hard reasoning problems due to extremely sparse rewards and vanishing policy gradients, wasting significant computational resources.
- Existing approaches like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on off-policy data or importance-weighted RL suffer from issues such as memorization, entropy collapse, or high-variance gradient estimates.
- The inefficiency stems from the difficulty of finding correct reasoning traces during exploration when the probability of success (pass@k) is near zero.

## Method

- PrefixRL conditions the LLM's RL policy on prefixes of successful off-policy traces, which are generated via large-scale rejection sampling from the base model itself, creating a self-improvement loop.
- These prefixes are prepended to the original problems, effectively guiding the RL policy to higher-rewarding states and reframing hard exploration into more tractable completion problems.
- During training, gradients are masked on the off-policy prefixes, ensuring that the RL objective optimizes only the continuation of the trace after the prefix, preventing instability while leveraging the prefix for guidance.

## Results

- PrefixRL achieves the same training reward 2 times faster and increases the final reward by over **45%** on original no-prefix training problems compared to the strongest baselines.
- Performance gains transfer robustly to held-out benchmarks, with a **12%** improvement in pass@1 on AIME '25 and consistent improvements (over **10%** absolute) across other math reasoning benchmarks.
- The method preserves token-level entropy and reduces the fraction of 'all-negative batches' during training, leading to more stable learning dynamics and a higher gradient signal-to-noise ratio compared to standard and importance-weighted RL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning on off-policy prefixes offers a stable and effective way to reuse computational effort, transforming hard exploration problems into more manageable completion tasks without the instabilities of direct supervision or importance weighting.
> - The method is theoretically proven to be consistent with the standard RL objective and significantly more sample-efficient, achieving a smaller suboptimality gap with fewer samples compared to standard RL.
> - The research empirically discovered "back-generalization," a phenomenon where training solely on prefixed problems substantially improves performance on original, unprefixed problems, indicating a deeper strategic understanding by the LLM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks, such as math and coding: the inefficiency and eventual stalling of learning on "hard problems." In these scenarios, correct reasoning traces are exceedingly rare, leading to vanishing policy gradients and a significant waste of computational resources (FLOPs) on unproductive exploration.

**Current Landscape and Limitations:**
Traditional RL methods, often on-policy (e.g., REINFORCE, PPO), rely on sampling numerous traces from the current policy to derive updates. This paradigm breaks down when the "pass@k" (probability of finding a correct solution within k attempts) for hard problems approaches zero. To mitigate this, researchers have explored several avenues:

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or "mid-training" on off-policy data:** While seemingly intuitive to use past successful traces as supervision, this often leads to memorization, entropy collapse, and hampered exploration during subsequent RL (Chu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b).
- **Importance-weighted off-policy RL:** Directly using off-policy data in RL via importance weighting can be highly unstable due to high-variance gradient estimates, causing issues like reward collapse or memorization (Liu et al., 2025a; Yan et al., 2025).
- **Teacher-driven methods:** Approaches that use human- or oracle-provided traces (Lightman et al., 2023) are limited by the teacher's capacity and require extensive reward shaping or hyperparameter tuning. For truly hard problems, compatible "chains of thought" are scarce.
- **Conditioning on hints or subgoals:** Some works augment prompts with partial human solutions or strategic hints (Chen et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2025), but these often depend on external, human-engineered input.
- **Exploring new exploration objectives:** Intrinsic motivation, entropy regularization, or count-based signals (Gao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b) aim to improve exploration but still struggle with sparse rewards on truly hard problems.
- **Resetting to off-policy states:** The general idea of "resetting" an RL policy to states encountered in successful demonstrations exists in broader RL (Nair et al., 2018), but its specific application to LLMs, with a focus on compute efficiency and a self-improvement loop, has not been thoroughly explored.

**PrefixRL's Position:**
PrefixRL introduces a novel and elegant approach that sidesteps the pitfalls of existing methods. Instead of using off-policy traces as direct supervision targets or relying on unstable importance weighting, it leverages them as *conditioning contexts*. By appending prefixes of successful off-policy traces to the original problems, PrefixRL effectively "places the RL policy in higher-rewarding states." This re-frames the hard exploration problem into a more tractable completion problem, allowing standard on-policy RL to function effectively. The key innovation is to *condition* on off-policy data rather than imitate it, and to mask gradients on the prefix, thereby avoiding instabilities. Furthermore, the paper establishes a self-improvement loop by generating these off-policy traces via rejection sampling from the *base model itself*, significantly reducing reliance on external or human-curated data. This positions PrefixRL as a highly relevant and practical contribution to the field, particularly for scaling LLM capabilities on complex, sparsely rewarded tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PrefixRL stems from the observation that typical RL methods for LLMs "waste compute on hard problems" where learning stalls due to rare correct traces and vanishing policy gradients. This leads to a bottleneck in scaling LLMs to solve increasingly difficult reasoning tasks efficiently.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Enhance Compute Efficiency:** To develop an RL method that can effectively reuse "old sampling FLOPs" from prior inference or RL training, thereby accelerating learning on hard problems and making LLM training more resource-efficient.
2. **Overcome Learning Stalls on Hard Problems:** To address the phenomenon where LLM RL policies plateau and fail to improve on hard reasoning problems due to a lack of positive learning signals. PrefixRL aims to provide a robust mechanism to bootstrap and sustain learning in this challenging regime.
3. **Leverage Off-Policy Data Safely:** To find a robust way to incorporate successful off-policy traces without suffering from the instabilities typically associated with direct supervision (e.g., SFT leading to entropy collapse, memorization) or importance-weighted off-policy RL (e.g., high-variance gradients).
4. **Boost the Learning Signal:** By conditioning on off-policy prefixes, the method aims to place the RL policy in states that are more likely to lead to success, thus increasing the frequency of non-zero rewards and strengthening the learning signal.
5. **Provide Theoretical Guarantees:** To formally prove that the proposed PrefixRL objective is consistent with the standard RL objective and offers superior sample efficiency, providing a strong theoretical foundation for its empirical gains.
6. **Demonstrate Empirical Superiority:** To empirically show that PrefixRL outperforms strong baselines (like SFT on off-policy data followed by RL) in terms of compute efficiency and final performance on hard reasoning problems, and that these gains transfer to held-out benchmarks.
7. **Explore "Back-Generalization":** To identify and characterize the novel phenomenon of "back-generalization," where training on prefixed problems improves performance on the original, unprefixed problems, and to understand its underlying mechanisms.
8. **Ensure Flexibility and Self-Improvement:** To validate that PrefixRL works effectively in a self-improvement loop (generating off-policy data via rejection sampling from the base model) and is robust to different sources of off-policy data, including those from different model families.

In essence, the authors are motivated to make RL for LLM reasoning more practical, scalable, and self-sufficient, moving beyond the current limitations imposed by sparse rewards and computational waste.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PrefixRL introduces a novel framework for integrating off-policy data into LLM Reinforcement Learning. The core of its methodology revolves around conditioning the RL policy on prefixes of successful off-policy traces rather than directly supervising or importance-weighting them.

**1. Sourcing Off-Policy Traces (Self-Improvement Loop):**
- The primary source of off-policy traces (`D_off`) is through **large-scale rejection sampling** on the base LLM (`π_0`). For each hard problem, the model repeatedly samples responses until a correct trace (verified by an external checker) is found. This creates a self-improvement loop, making the method independent of external human labels or oracle models.
- The paper also demonstrates flexibility by testing scenarios where `D_off` is sourced from a different model family.

**2. Creating Prefixed Problems:**
- For each original hard problem `x` in the training set, a corresponding successful off-policy trace `y_x` is drawn from `D_off`.
- Prefixes of varying lengths (`y_x_1:h`) are extracted from `y_x`.
- These prefixes are then prepended to the original problem `x` to form "prefixed problems" (e.g., `concat(x, y_x_1:h)`). The lengths `h` are chosen such that conditioning on them yields a "reasonable accuracy" from the base LLM, typically revealing a high-level problem-solving strategy.
- The training dataset for PrefixRL (`D_pre`) comprises these prefixed problems alongside the original "no-prefix" problems.

**3. PrefixRL Training Objective:**
- The LLM policy (`π`) is trained using standard on-policy RL (specifically, REINFORCE in experiments) on both the prefixed problems from `D_pre` and the original no-prefix problems from `D`.
- A crucial aspect is that **gradients are masked on the off-policy prefix**. This means the RL objective only optimizes the *continuation* of the trace after the prefix, preventing the instability that arises from trying to directly supervise very off-policy tokens. The prefix merely guides the policy to a higher-rewarding starting state.
- The objective function is a sum of expected rewards over both prefixed and no-prefix problems.

**4. Theoretical Analysis:**
- **Objective Consistency (Theorem 3.2):** The paper formally proves that any policy maximizing the PrefixRL objective also maximizes the standard RL objective (on no-prefix problems), under the assumption that the off-policy traces are correct and realizable by some optimal policy within the model class. This ensures that optimizing the augmented objective doesn't introduce a bias towards suboptimal solutions for the original problem.
- **Sample Efficiency (Theorem 3.3 & Proposition 3.4):** Using a Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) variant for analysis, the authors demonstrate that PrefixRL is more sample-efficient. It achieves a smaller suboptimality gap with fewer samples compared to standard RL, effectively "reusing FLOPs" by outsourcing the initial exploration burden to the off-policy prefixes. Proposition 3.4 further shows a worst-case exponential separation in sample complexity between standard RL and PrefixRL.

**5. Empirical Evaluation:**
- **Models:** Llama-3.1-8B-instruct (distilled on OpenThoughtsV3) and Qwen3-4B-instruct are used as base LLMs.
- **Training Data:** 1,000 hard reasoning problems from DAPO and OMNI-MATH, characterized by a pass@512 of approximately zero under the base LLMs.
- **Baselines:**
 - Standard on-policy RL (REINFORCE).
 - SFT on off-policy data followed by standard RL (SFT+RL).
 - Importance-weighted off-policy RL (CISPO, LUFFY).
- **Metrics:** Compute efficiency (FLOPs to reach target reward), final reward (accuracy on no-prefix training problems), pass@k on held-out benchmarks (AIME '25, HMMT '25, IMO-AnswerBench), and various training dynamics (token-level entropy, frequency of all-negative batches, average response length, gradient norm and standard deviation).
- **Back-Generalization Studies:**
 - Controlled experiments to observe how training on prefixed problems affects performance on unprefixed ones, including tracking strategy keywords (Figure 5) and using an in-context learning setup with related/unrelated problems (Figure 6).
- **Robustness Checks:** Testing PrefixRL's effectiveness when off-policy prefixes are sourced from a different model family (e.g., Qwen prefixes for Llama RL and vice-versa).

This comprehensive methodology, combining theoretical grounding with rigorous empirical validation and detailed ablation studies, strongly supports the claims of PrefixRL's effectiveness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several compelling findings that underscore the efficacy and novelty of PrefixRL:

1. **Superior Compute Efficiency and Final Performance:**
 - On hard training problems, PrefixRL achieves the same training reward **2 times faster** than the strongest baseline (SFT on off-policy data followed by RL), even after meticulously accounting for the initial compute spent on rejection sampling to gather off-policy traces.
 - It also significantly **increases the final reward by over 45%** (a 3x relative gain) on the original no-prefix training problems for Llama-3.1-8B-instruct, and over 30% for Qwen-3-4B-instruct.
 - This demonstrates that PrefixRL effectively reallocates "wasted sampling FLOPs" from unproductive exploration in standard RL towards meaningful learning.

2. **Robust Transfer to Held-Out Benchmarks:**
 - The performance gains translate to held-out benchmarks: PrefixRL improves pass@1 on AIME '25 by 12% over the strongest baseline and shows consistent improvements (over 10% absolute) in pass@k across AIME '25, HMMT '25, and IMO-AnswerBench.
 - The gains are visible even at pass@1 and widen for larger `k`, indicating improvements in both the mean performance and the diversity/tail of the trace distribution, making the model more likely to find correct solutions.

3. **Theoretical Guarantees:**
 - **Objective Consistency:** The paper proves that maximizing the PrefixRL objective is equivalent to maximizing the standard RL objective, ensuring that the method does not introduce a bias towards suboptimal solutions for the original problem.
 - **Sample Efficiency:** PrefixRL is formally shown to be more sample-efficient than standard RL, reducing the suboptimality gap. A worst-case separation proof demonstrates that PrefixRL can achieve a solution with polynomially many samples, while standard RL may require exponentially many.

4. **Discovery of "Back-Generalization":**
 - A key empirical discovery is "back-generalization": training *only* on prefixed problems substantially boosts performance on the original, *untrained* no-prefix problems.
 - This generalization is unique because it alters the next-token distribution on states it was never explicitly trained on, suggesting that it arises from favorable function approximation properties of LLMs.
 - PrefixRL enables the model to discover and learn strategies *beyond* what is explicitly provided in the off-policy prefix. The model can amplify successful strategies and even reject suboptimal ones present in the prefix, demonstrating a deeper learning mechanism than mere imitation.
 - Back-generalization is most effective when the in-context problem (prefix) and the target problem (suffix) are "related," implying that shared internal representations are crucial for successful transfer. In such cases, PrefixRL can achieve stronger generalization than direct RL on either problem alone.

5. **Robustness to Off-Policy Data Source and Model Family:**
 - PrefixRL remains effective even when off-policy prefixes are sourced from a different model family (e.g., Qwen3-4B-instruct prefixes for Llama-3.1-8B-instruct training). This highlights the flexibility and practical applicability of the method in diverse deployment scenarios, potentially leveraging external or open-source successful traces. While transfer can be asymmetric (stronger from a more capable source model), it generally shows robustness.

6. **Improved Training Dynamics:**
 - **Entropy Preservation:** Unlike SFT on off-policy data, which leads to a dramatic drop in token-level entropy (harming exploration), PrefixRL preserves most of the token-level entropy during training, allowing for more robust exploration.
 - **Reduced "All-Negative Batches":** PrefixRL consistently reduces the fraction of training batches where all on-policy rollouts yield zero reward on no-prefix problems, indicating that the prefixes successfully guide the policy to states with non-zero advantages, breaking the stalling regime.
 - **Shorter, More Efficient Traces:** PrefixRL achieves better accuracy with fewer sampled tokens. This is because, with improved strategies, the model reaches decisive steps earlier, reducing "unproductive wandering" and overall token generation per trace.
 - **Higher Gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio:** PrefixRL policy gradients exhibit a higher expected norm and lower variance compared to standard RL and importance-weighted off-policy RL. This indicates a more stable and effective learning process, avoiding the noise and instability often seen with off-policy importance weighting.

In summary, PrefixRL empirically and theoretically demonstrates a significant leap in scaling RL for LLMs on hard reasoning problems, introducing a new generalization phenomenon that leverages the intrinsic capabilities of LLMs for self-improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PrefixRL framework presents a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models, with profound implications for the development of more capable, efficient, and autonomous AI systems.

1. **Breaking the RL Stalling Barrier:** The most immediate impact is the effective solution to the "RL stalling" problem on hard reasoning tasks. By allowing RL to receive consistent learning signals even for problems with near-zero initial success rates, PrefixRL unlocks a new pathway for LLMs to master complex reasoning abilities that were previously intractable with traditional RL methods. This is crucial for pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in domains like advanced mathematics, logical deduction, and complex code generation.

2. **Unlocking Compute Efficiency at Scale:** In an era where LLM training costs are astronomical, PrefixRL offers a practical and theoretically grounded method to "reuse FLOPs." By leveraging previously spent compute (in the form of off-policy traces), it significantly reduces the effective training cost and time required to achieve high performance. This efficiency is vital for both large-scale industry applications and for democratizing access to cutting-edge LLM capabilities. The self-improvement loop, deriving off-policy data from the base model itself, further enhances cost-effectiveness by reducing reliance on expensive external data sources or human feedback.

3. **Novel Generalization Paradigm: Back-Generalization:** The discovery and characterization of "back-generalization" is a significant scientific contribution. It reveals a unique capability of LLMs, where training on *partial* solutions can lead to improved performance on *full*, untrained problems. This phenomenon, which transcends simple imitation or "stitching" of solutions, suggests a deeper reshaping of the model's internal representations and strategic understanding. Understanding and harnessing back-generalization could inspire new theoretical and empirical research into how LLMs learn and generalize, potentially leading to more powerful and efficient learning algorithms beyond RL.

4. **Stable and Robust Learning:** PrefixRL's design bypasses the common instabilities associated with off-policy RL (e.g., high-variance gradients from importance sampling) and SFT-induced issues (e.g., entropy collapse). By using off-policy data purely for conditioning and masking gradients on prefixes, it ensures a more stable and robust training process. This reliability is critical for deploying RL at scale in production environments.

5. **Accelerated Self-Improvement for LLMs:** The ability to generate off-policy traces via rejection sampling from the base model creates a powerful self-improvement loop. This means LLMs can continually learn and refine their reasoning capabilities using their own successful attempts, reducing the need for constant external intervention. This aligns with the long-term vision of autonomous AI agents capable of continuous learning and adaptation.

6. **Broader Applicability and Design Space Expansion:** Demonstrated effectiveness across different model families (Llama and Qwen) and problem types (math reasoning) suggests PrefixRL's wide applicability. Furthermore, the concept of "conditioning" is highly flexible, opening a vast design space for future algorithms that strategically select, generate, and utilize prefixes to guide LLM exploration and learning. This could extend to other complex tasks where sparse rewards and exploration challenges persist.

In conclusion, PrefixRL offers a timely and impactful solution to a fundamental problem in LLM scaling. By making RL more efficient, stable, and self-improving, and by unveiling a novel generalization mechanism, it paves the way for LLMs to tackle increasingly difficult problems autonomously and effectively.
