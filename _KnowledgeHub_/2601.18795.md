# Reuse your FLOPs: Scaling RL on Hard Problems by Conditioning on Very Off-Policy Prefixes

> [!summary] Summary
> The PrefixRL framework enables Large Language Models to learn effectively on challenging reasoning tasks by conditioning their reinforcement learning policy on prefixes from successful off-policy traces. It achieves a 2x speedup in training reward acquisition and over 45% increase in final performance on hard problems, while also revealing a unique "back-generalization" phenomenon.

## Problem

- Traditional RL methods for LLMs often stall on hard reasoning problems due to extremely sparse rewards and vanishing policy gradients, wasting significant computational resources.
- Existing approaches like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on off-policy data or importance-weighted RL suffer from issues such as memorization, entropy collapse, or high-variance gradient estimates.
- The inefficiency stems from the difficulty of finding correct reasoning traces during exploration when the probability of success (pass@k) is near zero.

## Method

- PrefixRL conditions the LLM's RL policy on prefixes of successful off-policy traces, which are generated via large-scale rejection sampling from the base model itself, creating a self-improvement loop.
- These prefixes are prepended to the original problems, effectively guiding the RL policy to higher-rewarding states and reframing hard exploration into more tractable completion problems.
- During training, gradients are masked on the off-policy prefixes, ensuring that the RL objective optimizes only the continuation of the trace after the prefix, preventing instability while leveraging the prefix for guidance.

## Results

- PrefixRL achieves the same training reward 2 times faster and increases the final reward by over **45%** on original no-prefix training problems compared to the strongest baselines.
- Performance gains transfer robustly to held-out benchmarks, with a **12%** improvement in pass@1 on AIME '25 and consistent improvements (over **10%** absolute) across other math reasoning benchmarks.
- The method preserves token-level entropy and reduces the fraction of 'all-negative batches' during training, leading to more stable learning dynamics and a higher gradient signal-to-noise ratio compared to standard and importance-weighted RL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning on off-policy prefixes offers a stable and effective way to reuse computational effort, transforming hard exploration problems into more manageable completion tasks without the instabilities of direct supervision or importance weighting.
> - The method is theoretically proven to be consistent with the standard RL objective and significantly more sample-efficient, achieving a smaller suboptimality gap with fewer samples compared to standard RL.
> - The research empirically discovered "back-generalization," a phenomenon where training solely on prefixed problems substantially improves performance on original, unprefixed problems, indicating a deeper strategic understanding by the LLM.
