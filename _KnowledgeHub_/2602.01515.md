# RAPT: Model-Predictive Out-of-Distribution Detection and Failure Diagnosis for Sim-to-Real Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Queensland and CSIRO Robotics developed RAPT, a model-predictive framework for out-of-distribution detection and failure diagnosis in sim-to-real humanoid robots. It achieved an aggregate AUROC of 0.92 in simulation and 75% recall on physical hardware, while also providing automated root-cause analysis with 75% Top-1 accuracy for real-world failures.

## Problem

- Learned control policies, particularly from Deep Reinforcement Learning, often fail silently in real-world humanoid robots when encountering Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) states due to the 'Sim-to-Real gap', leading to potential hardware damage or safety risks.
- Existing anomaly detection methods for high-frequency robotic control are often computationally intensive, lack reliable calibration for low false-positive rates, and provide little to no interpretability or diagnostic information about failure causes.
- Generic time-series anomaly detection methods often fail to account for the complex kinematic constraints and rigid-body dynamics inherent in legged systems like humanoids, limiting their effectiveness in identifying physically inconsistent behaviors.

## Method

![[2602.01515-method.png]]
*The RAPT pipeline. (A) The neural architecture. (B) The hierarchical detection logic using three gates. (C) The post-hoc diagnostic process utilizing saliency heatmaps and multi-modal Large Language Models.*

- **RAPT**, a ==self-supervised recurrent probabilistic model==, learns the spatio-temporal structure of nominal robot proprioceptive data in simulation and detects anomalies as deviations (elevated ==reconstruction error==) from this learned distribution.
- A hybrid detection strategy combines RAPT's contextual ==Negative Log-Likelihood== (NLL) anomaly scoring with a deterministic ==range-based detector==, using hardware-calibrated thresholds to adapt to the Sim-to-Real gap while maintaining sensitivity.
- A two-stage diagnostic pipeline integrates ==temporal saliency== (via ==Integrated Gradients== with Backpropagation Through Time, weighted by NLL inverse variance) with a ==multi-modal LLM== for zero-shot semantic classification of root causes.

## Results

- RAPT achieved an aggregate AUROC of **0.92** across simulation tasks, significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., LSTM-VAE at **0.77**), and demonstrated an absolute improvement of approximately **+0.34** in Safety Score (TPR at 0.5% FPR).
- On a physical Unitree G1 humanoid, RAPT achieved **75%** recall for detecting real-world anomalies while maintaining zero false positives during nominal operation and a low inference latency of **1.63 ms**, well within real-time constraints.
- The automated diagnostic pipeline attained **75%** Top-1 accuracy (improving to **87.5%** with visual input) for zero-shot semantic root-cause classification of real-world failures, proving useful for debugging subtle Sim-to-Real discrepancies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconstruction likelihood, when properly calibrated and interpreted as a continuous signal, can effectively quantify the Sim-to-Real mismatch during robot deployment, moving beyond binary success/failure metrics to enable fine-grained monitoring.
> - Localized anomaly signals, identified by maximizing per-dimension anomaly scores (Max Mean) rather than averaging them, significantly improve detection performance, indicating that OOD events often manifest in specific, critical observation channels.
> - Combining spatio-temporal attribution maps from a probabilistic model with the reasoning capabilities of a multi-modal Large Language Model allows for effective zero-shot semantic diagnosis of robot failures, enhancing interpretability and debugging.
