# Vec-QMDP: Vectorized POMDP Planning on CPUs for Real-Time Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Vec-QMDP presents a CPU-native parallel planning framework for real-time autonomous driving, integrating multi-threading with SIMD vectorization and Data-Oriented Design. It achieves a 227x to 1073x speedup over state-of-the-art serial POMDP planners and delivers competitive driving performance on the nuPlan benchmark within 9-14ms planning cycles.

## Problem

- Real-time autonomous driving planning under uncertainty is computationally intractable with traditional POMDP solvers due to high dimensionality and long decision histories.
- Existing parallelization strategies (CPU multi-threading, hybrid CPU-GPU, GPU-native) face limitations like load imbalance, synchronization latency, and control-flow divergence in complex driving scenarios.
- Applying SIMD vectorization to online POMDP planning is difficult due to the need to evaluate multiple 'scenario trees,' complex non-linear dynamics, and dynamic spatial data structures for collision checking.

## Method

- **Vec-QMDP**, a CPU-native parallel planner, leverages the ==QMDP approximation== to decompose planning under uncertainty into independent, fully observable MDPs, suitable for parallel execution.
- Utilizes a ==hierarchical parallelism== scheme, combining coarse-grained multi-threading for distributing scenario minibatches and fine-grained ==SIMD vectorization== for parallel processing within each thread.
- Employs ==Data-Oriented Design (DOD)== to reorganize data structures (e.g., scenario trees, spatial STR-tree) into cache-efficient, contiguous arrays (Structure-of-Arrays), facilitating efficient SIMD operations and minimizing memory fragmentation.

## Results

- Vec-QMDP achieved a substantial speedup in tree construction throughput, ranging from **227x to 1073x**, compared to state-of-the-art serial POMDP planners on complex autonomous driving scenarios.
- The framework outperformed various baselines (learning-based, model-based, hybrid) on the nuPlan benchmark, achieving competitive driving performance within **9 ms** and peak performance within **14 ms** planning time.
- Ablation studies confirmed near-linear scaling with CPU core count (approximately **8x** speedup with **8 cores**) and showed that the load-balancing UCB improved throughput by up to **24%** in high-imbalance scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern CPU architectures, when strategically utilized with Data-Oriented Design and specialized parallelization, can efficiently perform real-time online POMDP planning for autonomous driving, providing an alternative to GPU-centric approaches.
> - A hierarchical parallelization approach, combining coarse-grained multi-threading with fine-grained SIMD vectorization, effectively scales to handle the computational demands of multi-scenario planning under uncertainty.
> - Novel vectorization strategies for complex components like multi-agent dynamics and dynamic collision checking, coupled with load-balancing techniques, are essential for maximizing SIMD throughput in irregular POMDP search.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Planning under uncertainty is a fundamental challenge in real-world robotics, particularly for autonomous driving in complex, interactive urban environments. Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) provide a principled mathematical framework for such problems, enabling robust decision-making when the system state is not fully known due to perception noise and the unpredictable behavior of other agents. However, the inherent "curse of dimensionality" and "curse of history" associated with POMDPs render exact solutions computationally intractable for real-time applications, especially on resource-constrained edge computing platforms found in autonomous vehicles.

The broader research landscape has explored various approaches to accelerate POMDP and related tree-search-based planning methods:

- **CPU Multi-threading for Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** Traditional parallel MCTS strategies (root, tree, or leaf parallelization) are effective when per-simulation costs are uniform. However, in robotics tasks like autonomous driving, simulation costs vary significantly across different tree paths due to diverse scenarios and agent interactions. This non-uniformity often leads to load imbalance and frequent synchronization overhead, resulting in sub-linear scaling with increasing CPU cores.
- **GPU Parallelization for Online POMDP Planning:** Graphics Processing Units (GPUs) offer high computational throughput. Researchers have leveraged GPUs in two main ways:
 - **Hybrid CPU-GPU solvers (e.g., HyP-DESPOT):** These offload computationally intensive Monte Carlo simulations to the GPU while retaining higher-level search logic on the CPU. While this can improve device-side simulation throughput, the overall planning time can be constrained by host-device communication latency and synchronization requirements between CPU and GPU tasks.
 - **GPU-native solvers (e.g., VOPP):** These aim to keep all data structures and belief tree search entirely on the GPU to avoid communication overhead. However, the irregular control flow often encountered in online POMDP planning, particularly in interactive environments with data-dependent branching (e.g., different driving scenarios requiring distinct actions like yielding or overtaking), leads to control-flow divergence on Single Instruction, Multiple Threads (SIMT) architectures. This forces sequential execution of divergent paths within a warp, reducing effective GPU utilization and throughput.
- **CPU Single Instruction, Multiple Data (SIMD) Parallelization:** Modern CPUs feature wide SIMD units capable of performing the same operation on multiple data elements simultaneously. Recent work, such as VAMP in sampling-based motion planning, has demonstrated the efficiency of SIMD, achieving significant speedups by adopting Data-Oriented Design (DOD) principles. However, extending SIMD vectorization to online POMDP planning presents additional challenges:
 - **Multiple Scenario Trees:** POMDPs with uncertainty in future states necessitate reasoning over many different "scenario trees" rather than a single rollout.
 - **Complex Dynamics:** Multi-agent interactions in urban environments involve non-linear trajectories and intricate simulation logic, making internal vectorization of dynamics functions difficult.
 - **Dynamic Collision Checking:** Spatial trees used for broad-phase collision detection vary across scenarios and time steps, preventing pre-compilation and complicating vectorization.

Vec-QMDP positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing the limitations of existing parallelization strategies for POMDP planning in real-time autonomous driving. It aims to overcome the synchronization latency of hybrid CPU-GPU approaches and the branch divergence issues of GPU-native solvers by developing a CPU-native framework. Furthermore, it tackles the inherent difficulties of applying SIMD to POMDPs by introducing novel techniques for vectorizing multi-scenario search, complex dynamics, and dynamic collision checking, leveraging the QMDP approximation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Vec-QMDP is to enable real-time planning under uncertainty for autonomous vehicles operating in complex, high-dimensional belief spaces, such as dense urban traffic. The authors highlight the computational intractability of existing POMDP solvers for real-time deployment on edge computing platforms commonly found in autonomous vehicles.

The key objectives and their underlying motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Objective 1: Achieve Real-Time Performance on Onboard CPUs:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing POMDP solvers are too slow for millisecond-level planning required for high-frequency, closed-loop control in autonomous driving. While GPUs offer high throughput, they introduce overheads (host-device synchronization latency in hybrid systems) or inefficiencies (branch divergence on SIMT architectures for varied scenarios in GPU-native systems). This necessitates a CPU-native solution that can leverage the CPU's architecture without these specific bottlenecks.
- **Objective 2: Develop a Scalable Parallel POMDP Planner:**
 - **Motivation:** The "curse of dimensionality" and "curse of history" in POMDPs lead to enormous computational demands. Traditional parallelization techniques often face limitations like sub-linear scaling due to load imbalance in MCTS or inefficient execution due to branch divergence on GPUs. The objective is to design a hierarchical parallelism scheme that effectively scales with available CPU resources.
- **Objective 3: Vectorize Complex POMDP Search Components:**
 - **Motivation:** Directly applying SIMD vectorization to POMDP planning is challenging. The need to evaluate multiple independent "scenario trees," the complex non-linear dynamics of multi-agent interactions, and the dynamic nature of spatial data structures for collision checking (which change across scenarios and time steps) make it difficult to achieve the contiguous memory access and uniform control flow required for efficient SIMD. The objective is to develop specific vectorization modes (global and local) to address these complexities.
- **Objective 4: Reformulate Data Structures for CPU Efficiency:**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) and pointer-based data structures often lead to fragmented memory access, poor cache utilization, and incompatibility with SIMD execution. The objective is to adopt Data-Oriented Design (DOD) principles to refactor these structures into cache-efficient, contiguous memory layouts (e.g., Structure-of-Arrays) to maximize SIMD throughput.
- **Objective 5: Validate Performance on Large-Scale Autonomous Driving Benchmarks:**
 - **Motivation:** Theoretical advantages and micro-benchmarks are insufficient to demonstrate practical utility in autonomous driving. The objective is to rigorously evaluate the proposed planner on a realistic and challenging benchmark (nuPlan) to demonstrate its competitive or superior driving performance and computational efficiency under strict real-time constraints, thereby establishing CPUs as a viable high-performance computing platform for such tasks.

In essence, the authors aim to bridge the gap between the theoretical robustness of POMDPs and the practical requirements of real-time autonomous driving by designing a highly optimized, CPU-native parallel planning framework that effectively exploits modern hardware architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vec-QMDP presents a CPU-native parallel planner that combines multi-threading with SIMD vectorization, built upon the QMDP approximation. Its methodology centers on Data-Oriented Design (DOD) and a hierarchical parallelism scheme to efficiently conduct belief tree search and trajectory optimization.

**A. Mathematical Foundation: QMDP Approximation**
The work grounds its parallelization strategy in the QMDP approximation. This approximation assumes that uncertainty in the future behavior of exogenous agents is resolved after the first planning step. By sampling *K* scenarios (initial state and deterministic future trajectories of agents) from the current belief, the problem decomposes into *K* independent, fully observable MDPs. This allows the value function *V<sub>ϕk</sub>(s')* for each scenario *ϕk* to be computed independently after the initial action branching, making it suitable for parallel execution.

**B. Hierarchical Parallelization Scheme**
Vec-QMDP employs a two-tiered parallelization approach:
1. **Coarse-grained Multi-threading:** The *K* sampled scenarios are divided into *M* minibatches, with each minibatch of *N* scenarios (where *K = M × N*) assigned to a separate CPU core (thread). This distributes the workload across multiple cores, taking advantage of CPU multi-core capabilities.
2. **Fine-grained SIMD Vectorization:** Within each CPU thread, SIMD instructions are used to process multiple scenario search components or multiple agents simultaneously in a single instruction.

**C. Data-Oriented Design (DOD) and Data Structures**
To enable efficient SIMD processing and optimize cache usage, Vec-QMDP refactors traditional pointer-based data structures:
- **Scenario Search Trees:** Instead of scattered, pointer-linked nodes, scenario trees are flattened into contiguous arrays. Topology is implicitly encoded (e.g., the *i*-th child of node *v* is at *|A| × v + i*). Per-node statistics (Q-values, visit counts, rewards, ego states) and flags are stored in a Structure-of-Arrays (SoA) layout, ensuring contiguous memory for each field across all nodes.
- **Spatial STR-Tree for Collision Checking:** The Sort-Tile-Recursive (STR)-tree, used for broad-phase collision filtering, is also stored as an array-based balanced hierarchy in contiguous buffers, pre-allocated with padding. Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) fields (e.g., min/max in Frenet frame) are organized in SoA form, allowing SIMD lanes to load and process multiple bounding boxes in parallel.
- **Exogenous Trajectories:** These are scenario-level inputs, stored once per scenario in a time-major layout, with an SoA organization over agents at each time step.

**D. Vectorized QMDP Belief Tree Search (Algorithm 1)**
The core search process involves a loop of Traverse, Expansion, Rollout, and Backup operations:
- **Traverse:** Selects a node for expansion. This step is modified with a load-balancing UCB (see E).
- **Expansion/Rollout:** Simulates the joint evolution of the ego-vehicle and exogenous agents.
 - **Global Vectorization:** Multiple expanded nodes, drawn from different scenario trees within a CPU thread's minibatch, are batched. SIMD vector registers gather corresponding ego states and metadata. A single step-synchronous kernel advances all SIMD lanes (representing different scenarios) in lockstep. Control flow is largely uniform, with masking used for infrequent divergences (e.g., lane changes). Rollouts continue until all SIMD lanes are masked (reaching horizon or terminal event).
 - **Dynamics Simulation:** Ego-vehicle executes macro-actions using closed-loop control (IDM for longitudinal, Stanley for lateral, MOBIL for lane changes). Exogenous agents follow sampled trajectories.
- **Reward Evaluation and Collision Checking (Local Vectorization):** This is accelerated by SIMD-parallelizing within a scenario node.
 - **Broad-Phase Filtering:** SIMD-accelerated STR-tree traversal. Child AABBs are batch-loaded into SIMD registers and intersected with the ego-vehicle's AABB. Leaf hits provide candidate agent indices.
 - **Narrow-Phase Refinement:** A SIMD Separating Axis Theorem (SAT) kernel evaluates multiple ego-agent pairs simultaneously, with each SIMD lane handling one pair. Masking and early exit optimize performance.
- **Backup:** Updates Q-values, visit counts, and the expansion-depth range.

**E. Load-Balancing UCB for Selection**
To mitigate load imbalance during global vectorization, particularly due to depth divergence (scenarios expanding to different depths), Vec-QMDP modifies the UCB selection process:
- Each node stores an expansion-depth range *D(s) = [d<sub>min</sub>(s), d<sub>max</sub>(s)]*.
- During selection, standard UCB is first run independently for each scenario to determine a tentative expanded node and its depth. A reference depth *d<sub>ref</sub>* is then determined by a majority vote over these depths.
- A modified UCB score is then used: *U(s, a) = UCB(s, a) − λ ⋅ |clamp(d<sub>ref</sub>, D(s')) − d<sub>ref</sub>|*. This penalizes selections that lead to sub-trees whose expansion-depth range deviates significantly from *d<sub>ref</sub>*, encouraging alignment of expansion depths across SIMD lanes and improving utilization.

**F. Vectorized Belief-Space Trajectory Optimization**
After belief tree search, Vec-QMDP refines driving trajectories:
- **Importance Sampling:** *K* scenarios are re-sampled using importance sampling, focusing on safety-critical interactions by shifting probability mass towards hazardous intentions for risk-relevant agents.
- **Cross-Scenario Evaluation:** The evaluation of candidate ego trajectories is organized as a parallel block-diagonal process. Each CPU thread handles a minibatch of *N* scenarios, restricting cross-evaluation within that minibatch (reducing *O(K^2)* to *O(N^2)* per thread).
- **Global Vectorization for Optimization:** Ego trajectories for the *N* scenarios in a minibatch are generated in parallel via SIMD-batched forward simulation. During evaluation, trajectory points across this SIMD batch are processed together. Reward computation, particularly collision checking, uses vectorized narrow-phase checks, comparing multiple ego trajectories to each candidate exogenous agent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Vec-QMDP was conducted on the nuPlan benchmark, a large-scale autonomous driving dataset, under strict real-time constraints.

**A. Computational Throughput Comparison**
- **Significant Speedup:** Vec-QMDP achieved a substantial speedup in tree construction throughput, ranging from **227× to 1073×** compared to Hi-Drive, a state-of-the-art serial POMDP planner.
- **Scalability with Scene Complexity:** The speedup was observed to increase proportionally with traffic density, indicating that Vec-QMDP's multi-tiered parallelization effectively overcomes computational bottlenecks in complex, dense urban scenarios. Vec-QMDP maintained robust throughput even as agent density increased, while Hi-Drive's performance declined sharply.
- **Per-Edge Processing Time:** Vec-QMDP demonstrated a stable per-edge processing time (mean 0.038 ms, std. 0.013 ms), supporting its robust search capacity. Compared to HyP-DESPOT, Vec-QMDP achieved 1683.49 edges/ms versus 10.88 edges/ms, representing a 154.73× average tree-construction-throughput speedup.

**B. Driving Performance Comparison on nuPlan**
- **Overall Driving Score:** Vec-QMDP outperformed learning-based (PLUTO, Diffusion Planner), model-based (Hi-Drive), and hybrid (PDM-Hybrid, PLUTO w/ refine, Diff. Planner w/ refine) baselines across all nuPlan scene difficulties (Val14, Test14-random, Test14-hard).
- **Real-Time Latency:**
 - Vec-QMDP (match) achieved performance parity with state-of-the-art methods within **9 ms** planning time.
 - Vec-QMDP (best) reached peak driving performance within **14 ms**, significantly faster than typical planning cycles (e.g., Diffusion Planner at 80 ms, Hi-Drive at 503 ms, HyP-DESPOT at 259 ms).
 - End-to-end latency (including prediction and perception) was 65 ms, which is lower than Diffusion Planner's 80 ms while yielding better driving performance.
- **Safety Metrics:** Vec-QMDP demonstrated improvements in Safety Score (SS) and Time-to-Collision Score (TTCS) over HyP-DESPOT on both Val14 and Test14-hard datasets, suggesting that the QMDP approximation, when implemented with Vec-QMDP's efficiency, can maintain or enhance safety performance.

**C. Ablation Study on Multi-threading**
- **Near-Linear Scaling:** A comparison between the full multi-threaded Vec-QMDP (8 cores) and a single-threaded vectorized variant (ST) showed a consistent speedup of approximately 8×. This indicates near-linear scaling with core count and confirms that the lock-free architecture effectively minimizes synchronization overheads.

**D. Ablation Study on Load-Balancing UCB**
- **Throughput Improvement:** The proposed load-balancing UCB (w/ LB) consistently improved throughput compared to a UCB-only variant (w/o LB). This improvement increased with the degree of load imbalance, reaching approximately **24%** gains in scenes with high depth divergence (imbalance > 90%).
- **Planning Quality:** The throughput improvement achieved by load-balancing UCB did not materially affect planning quality. Driving scores remained slightly higher with LB, and safety scores were comparable.

**E. Scalability Test on Planning Time**
- **Rapid Convergence:** Both driving score and constructed tree size stabilized within approximately **14 ms** of planning time. This suggests that Vec-QMDP rapidly identifies high-quality driving trajectories and effectively covers the necessary belief space well within real-time planning budgets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Vec-QMDP represents a notable advancement in the field of online planning under uncertainty, particularly for real-time robotic applications such as autonomous driving. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Real-Time POMDP Planning on CPUs:** The work fundamentally demonstrates that modern CPU architectures, when leveraged with appropriate data structures and parallelization strategies, can serve as a high-performance computing platform for complex online POMDP planning. This counters the conventional perception that such computationally intensive tasks necessitate GPUs, which often face their own architectural limitations (e.g., branch divergence, host-device latency) in complex, interactive, and data-dependent robotics scenarios.
- **Advancement in Autonomous Driving Capabilities:** By achieving millisecond-level planning latency and state-of-the-art driving performance on challenging benchmarks, Vec-QMDP directly addresses a critical bottleneck in autonomous vehicle perception-to-action loops. This allows for higher-frequency control, more agile responses to dynamic and uncertain traffic conditions, and the ability to reason over a significantly larger number of future scenarios, which is crucial for safety-critical decision-making (e.g., detecting and negotiating low-probability, high-risk interactions).
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Parallelization:** Vec-QMDP effectively navigates the drawbacks of prior parallel planning efforts. It avoids the host-device communication overhead inherent in hybrid CPU-GPU systems and mitigates the performance degradation caused by branch divergence in GPU-native SIMT architectures. Furthermore, it demonstrates how the challenges of vectorizing multi-scenario POMDPs (multiple scenario trees, complex dynamics, dynamic spatial data structures) can be systematically addressed through Data-Oriented Design and a dual vectorization strategy.
- **Practical Deployment Potential:** The CPU-native implementation, supporting AVX2 on x86 and NEON on ARM platforms, makes Vec-QMDP highly transferable to embedded automotive Systems-on-Chips (SoCs), such as NVIDIA Orin. This facilitates the deployment of sophisticated planning capabilities directly onto the vehicle's onboard computing systems, which is essential for real-world robotic applications.
- **Generalizability of Principles:** The core principles of Vec-QMDP—specifically, the hierarchical parallelization combining multi-threading and SIMD, Data-Oriented Design, and the dual modes of global and local vectorization for complex dynamics and collision checking—are not exclusive to QMDP or autonomous driving. These methodologies could be adapted and extended to other robotics domains that require robust, real-time planning under uncertainty in dynamic, multi-agent environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays the groundwork for future advancements. While Vec-QMDP utilizes the QMDP approximation, its efficient framework opens avenues for extending these vectorization principles to more general online POMDP solvers (e.g., DESPOT), potentially enabling richer belief-dependent reasoning while maintaining real-time performance. It also suggests possibilities for scaling to larger or higher-dimensional action spaces by integrating with techniques like learned macro-actions.

In summary, Vec-QMDP represents a significant contribution by demonstrating the viability and superior performance of CPU-native, highly parallelized POMDP planning, thus paving the way for more robust and responsive autonomous systems.
