# Energy-based Compositional Diffusion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Energy-based Compositional Diffusion Planning (ECD) introduces a framework addressing non-conservative score fields in diffusion planners for long-horizon robotic tasks by formulating a global energy function from local denoisers. It achieved improved success rates on various long-horizon maze and high-dimensional planning benchmarks, maintaining efficient inference times.

## Problem

- Robotic planning for long-horizon tasks using diffusion models is challenging when training data consists of only short, fragmented trajectories.
- Heuristic stitching methods, used to compose short trajectory chunks, often lead to inconsistencies and non-conservative score fields, impeding convergence and mode consistency.
- Prior methods that enforce consistency typically incur substantial computational overhead, creating a trade-off between performance and efficiency.

## Method

- **ECD** constructs a global, scalar-valued energy function by summing local '==bridge potentials==' derived from each chunk denoiser, ensuring a conservative correction field.
- It explicitly incorporates a 'boundary reaction term,' which propagates residual error from a chunk's interior back to its boundary conditions via a Jacobian-vector product.
- An efficient Markov-based score approximation is used to compute the boundary reaction term rapidly, maintaining linear time complexity in the planning horizon.

## Results

- ECD achieved success rates of **82%** on AntMaze Giant (vs. **72%** for CompDiffuser) and **49%** on HumanoidMaze Giant (vs. **42%** for CompDiffuser), showing consistent improvements on long-horizon tasks.
- It demonstrated improved performance in high-dimensional planning, reaching **51%** success on AntMaze Giant 15D (vs. **41%** for CompDiffuser) and achieving the highest mean success rates on AntSoccer environments.
- The method maintains inference runtime comparable to heuristic stitching (e.g., 8 seconds for PointMaze Giant), significantly outperforming other consistency-enforcing methods like CDGS.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Heuristic stitching in compositional diffusion planners can create a non-conservative score field by omitting the 'boundary reaction term,' leading to inconsistencies across trajectory segments.
> - Defining the global trajectory as the minimizer of a sum of local energy potentials inherently yields a conservative and globally consistent correction field.
> - Exploiting the local Markovian dependency structure of robotic tasks allows for an efficient approximation of the crucial boundary reaction term without resorting to computationally expensive exact Jacobian calculations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of robotic planning, specifically leveraging diffusion models for decision-making. Diffusion models have emerged as a robust paradigm for offline decision-making and robotic planning, offering capabilities such as handling multimodality, flexible conditioning on goals and constraints, and integration with guidance-style control.

A significant challenge in deploying diffusion planners, particularly in realistic settings, is addressing long-horizon tasks when training data primarily consists of short, fragmented trajectories. This scenario, common in real-robot datasets due to skill segmentation or task-agnostic play, creates a fundamental mismatch: models are trained on abundant local motion statistics but lack full episodes covering extensive task horizons. Consequently, the planner must generate long trajectories by composing short trajectory "chunks," a process referred to as compositional generation.

Prior approaches to compositional generation in diffusion planners have typically relied on heuristic stitching methods during the denoising process. These methods often employ mode-averaging or boundary-conditioned inpainting for local chunks, which are then combined using simple stitching rules or hierarchical refinement. Examples include CompDiffuser (CD), which uses autoregressive or interleaved chunk updates. While such methods can be efficient, they have been observed to exhibit inconsistencies, particularly when dealing with multimodal data, where the trajectory might inconsistently jump between different valid modes.

More recent work, such as Compositional Diffusion with Guided Search (CDGS), has attempted to address these inconsistencies by incorporating inference-time search, resampling, and scaling computational resources. While CDGS can improve success rates, its iterative nature introduces substantial computational overhead, making it significantly slower than heuristic stitching methods.

This paper identifies a theoretical limitation in existing heuristic stitching approaches: the aggregation of overlapping updates can lead to a non-conservative score field. A non-conservative field cannot be expressed as the gradient of a global log-density function, which violates the underlying mathematical assumptions of score-based reverse-diffusion. This theoretical gap results in practical issues such as slower convergence and failure to commit to a consistent trajectory mode. The present work aims to bridge the identified gap by proposing a method that combines the efficiency of heuristic stitching with enhanced consistency, thereby seeking to improve performance without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Identify and Formalize a Fundamental Failure Mode in Heuristic Stitching:** The authors aim to demonstrate that current compositional diffusion planners that rely on heuristic stitching of local predictions (e.g., CompDiffuser) operate on an incomplete mathematical foundation. Specifically, they contend that the resulting stitched update field is generally non-conservative. This means it cannot be represented as the gradient of any valid global trajectory log-density function, which is a requirement for standard score-based reverse diffusion. This omission stems from neglecting how a chunk's interior mean shifts in response to perturbations of its boundary conditions, effectively dropping a necessary chain-rule term—the "boundary reaction term." The empirical consequence of this is observed as slower convergence and a failure to settle into a single, consistent trajectory mode, especially in multimodal environments.

2. **Propose an Energy-based Formulation for Compositional Diffusion Planning:** To address the non-conservativity issue, the core objective is to introduce a new framework, the Energy-based Compositional Diffuser (ECD). Instead of heuristically composing local trajectory predictions, ECD proposes constructing a global, scalar-valued energy function. This global energy is defined as the sum of local "bridge potentials" derived from each chunk denoiser. By formulating the global trajectory as the minimizer of this energy, the correction field is derived as its negative gradient, ensuring conservativity by construction. This energy-based perspective provides a principled approach to mode-consistent denoising.

3. **Reveal and Incorporate the Crucial Boundary Reaction Term:** A key motivation is to explicitly identify and integrate the "boundary reaction term" that heuristic stitching methods omit. This term, which manifests as a Jacobian-vector product, propagates inconsistencies from a chunk's interior back to its boundary conditions. By incorporating this feedback mechanism, the system can ensure better alignment and consistency between overlapping trajectory segments, which is critical for long-horizon planning in multimodal environments.

4. **Develop an Efficient Inference Mechanism:** Recognizing that computing the exact Jacobian for the boundary reaction term can be computationally expensive (requiring differentiation through the neural network at each denoising step), another objective is to develop an efficient approximation. The aim is to introduce a Markov-based score approximation that can compute this reaction term rapidly. This approximation should maintain a time complexity linear in the planning horizon, making the proposed method practically viable and competitive with existing fast, albeit inconsistent, approaches.

5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance and Efficiency:** Ultimately, the motivation is to develop a compositional diffusion planner that achieves superior success rates on challenging long-horizon tasks compared to existing methods, while simultaneously maintaining inference speeds comparable to heuristic stitching methods. This addresses the existing trade-off in the literature between performance and computational cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Energy-based Compositional Diffuser (ECD) addresses the limitations of heuristic stitching in compositional diffusion planning by introducing a principled energy-based correction mechanism and an efficient approximation for its critical component.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The paper considers a standard goal-conditioned planning setting where the objective is to generate a feasible trajectory $x_{0:L}$ from a start state $x_s$ to a goal state $x_g$. Planning is formulated as conditional generative modeling, where $x_{1:L-1} \sim p_{\theta}(\cdot | x_0 = x_s, x_L = x_g)$. The core challenge is the "out-of-span long-horizon setting," where training data consists only of short trajectory fragments of length $l \ll L$, necessitating compositional generation to bridge the length mismatch.

#### 3.2. Critique of Heuristic Stitching (CompDiffuser Context)

The paper first analyzes existing compositional diffusion planners like CompDiffuser (CD), which combine a local conditional diffusion model with heuristic prediction stitching.
- **Local Chunk Denoiser:** CD divides the task horizon $L$ into $K+1$ overlapping chunks of length $l$ with stride $d$. For each chunk $k$, a local denoiser estimates a clean local prediction $\mu_k(t)$ for selected coordinates $P_k S_k x(t)$, conditioned on local boundary conditions $c_k(t) := O_k S_k x(t)$. The crucial point is that $\mu_k$ is differentiated with respect to the boundary condition.
- **Inference via Stitching:** During reverse diffusion, local denoisers generate noisy-mean predictions. In overlapping regions where indices $j \in C_k \cap C_{k+1}$ receive conflicting candidates, CD resolves these conflicts via heuristic rules (e.g., weighted averaging $\nu_j(t) = w_k \mu_{k,j}(t) + (1-w_k) \mu_{k+1,j}(t)$ or overwriting). The resulting stitched noisy-mean score estimate $score_{stitch}(x,t) = (\nu(t) - x(t)) / \sigma_t^2$ is then used for updating the global state.
- **Fundamental Flaw:** The paper argues that this heuristic composition is problematic because it only uses the local predictions themselves and does not account for how a chunk prediction changes with respect to its boundary conditions (i.e., it omits the chain-rule derivative, the "boundary reaction term"). This omission leads to a non-conservative score field, which cannot be integrated to form a valid log-density, thereby invalidating the standard score-based reverse-diffusion interpretation.

#### 3.3. Energy-based Compositional Diffuser (ECD)

ECD proposes an alternative approach:
- **Inverting the Paradigm:** Instead of stitching predictions, ECD constructs a global scalar-valued energy landscape from local chunk denoisers. The desired plan is defined as one of the energy minimizers. The resulting correction direction, being the negative gradient of a scalar field, is conservative by construction.

- **Properties of a Consistent Global Energy:** The paper outlines three properties for a robust global energy function:
 1. **Interior Update:** For fixed boundary conditions, the energy minimum for interior nodes should align with a feasible manifold (e.g., maintaining local kinematic validity).
 2. **Reaction to Boundary Conditions:** The energy must couple the interior to the boundary conditions, allowing high-energy interior states to exert a "reaction force" on boundaries, pushing them towards feasible modes.
 3. **Chunk Consensus:** Due to overlapping chunks, the global energy should be additive, ensuring that overlapping nodes simultaneously satisfy the constraints of all contributing chunks.

- **Local and Global Energy Formulation:**
 - **Local Energy:** For each chunk $k$, the local energy $E_k^{bridge}(x, t)$ is defined as the squared Mahalanobis distance between the current selected local state $P_k x_k$ and its conditional mean prediction $\mu_k(O_k x_k, t)$:
 $E_k^{bridge}(x, t) := \frac{1}{2\sigma_t^2} \|P_k x_k - \mu_k(O_k x_k, t)\|_{W_k}^2$.
 $W_k$ is a diagonal weighting matrix to handle overlap redundancy. The derivative of $\mu_k$ is with respect to the boundary condition $O_k x_k$, with other denoiser inputs treated as stop-gradient.
 - **Global Energy:** The global energy $E_t(x)$ is simply the sum of all local bridge energies: $E_t(x) = \sum_{k=0}^K E_k^{bridge}(x, t)$.

- **Energy-based Correction and Gradient Decomposition:** The induced bridge-model score/correction direction is $score(x, t) := -\nabla_x E_t(x)$. The gradient of a single chunk's energy reveals two key terms:
 $score_k(x, t) = -\frac{1}{\sigma_t^2} S_k^T P_k^T r_k(t) + \frac{1}{\sigma_t^2} S_k^T O_k^T J_{O,k}^T r_k(t)$.
 The first term is the **Interior Update**, pulling local coordinates toward the predicted mean. The second term is the **Boundary Reaction**, which propagates residual error from predicted coordinates back to the boundary variables via the Jacobian $J_{O,k}(t) := \frac{\partial \mu_k(O_k x_k, t)}{\partial O_k x_k}$. Summing these local scores across all chunks enforces **Chunk Consensus**. The conservativity of the resulting field is ensured because it is derived from a scalar potential.

- **Why Heuristic Stitching is Non-Conservative (Formalization):** Proposition 3.1 formally states that if a stitched mean depends non-trivially on a boundary condition, but that boundary condition itself is assigned without reciprocal dependence on the stitched coordinate, the Jacobian of the stitched score field is asymmetric, making it non-conservative. This is precisely what happens when the boundary reaction term (the Jacobian $J_{O,k}$) is ignored.

- **Efficient Approximation via Markovian Structure:**
 - **Challenge:** Computing the exact Jacobian $J_{O,k}(t)$ at each denoising step is computationally expensive.
 - **Solution:** Exploit the common locally Markovian dependency structure in robotic planning (where $x_i$ depends primarily on $x_{i-1}$ and $x_{i+1}$). This allows approximating the term $J_{O,k}^T r_k$ efficiently.
 - **Mechanism:** A local Markov surrogate energy $E_k^{ms}(y, c, t)$ is introduced. By defining the surrogate mean from its stationarity condition, implicit differentiation yields $\partial y / \partial c = -H_k(t)^{-1} G_k(t)$, where $H_k$ is the Hessian of $E_k^{ms}$ with respect to core coordinates and $G_k$ is the cross-term Hessian with respect to boundary conditions. The approximate reaction message is then computed by solving a sparse, block-tridiagonal linear system $H_k u_k = r_k$, where $H_k$ is block-tridiagonal due to the first-order Markov assumption. This reduces the complexity to $O(L)$, making inference efficient.
 - **Algorithm 1:** The overall DDIM inference loop with ECD correction involves: (1) running a local CD DDIM proposal for each chunk, (2) computing the bridge residual, (3) applying the interior update correction, (4) if using approximate ECD, solving the Markov system for the boundary reaction term, (5) applying the boundary reaction correction, and (6) updating the chunk state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate ECD's performance on the long-horizon trajectory stitching benchmark from OGBench, comparing it against heuristic stitching methods (CompDiffuser, GSC) and compute-scaled methods (CDGS), as well as various offline reinforcement learning baselines.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness Study

- **Trajectory Stitching on Maze Environments:** ECD consistently achieves high success rates on various PointMaze, AntMaze, and HumanoidMaze stitching tasks.
 - **PointMaze:** On PointMaze-stitching datasets, ECD matches baselines for Medium and Large mazes (100% success). For the more challenging Giant maze (longest horizon), ECD achieves an 84% success rate, outperforming CompDiffuser (77%) and CDGS (69%).
 - **AntMaze and HumanoidMaze:** ECD shows consistent improvement across all sizes (Medium, Large, Giant) in AntMaze and HumanoidMaze. For AntMaze Giant, ECD achieves 82% success, surpassing CD (72%) and CDGS (78%). Similarly, for HumanoidMaze Giant, ECD reaches 49% success compared to CD (42%) and CDGS (23%). These results suggest that the bridge plus boundary-reaction update promotes coherent boundary consistency, particularly crucial for longer planning horizons.

- **High-Dimensional Planning:** ECD demonstrates improved performance in high-dimensional trajectory stitching scenarios, where the state space includes joint positions in addition to x-y positions.
 - **AntMaze 15D:** On AntMaze Large and Giant with a 15-dimensional state space, ECD consistently outperforms CD. For AntMaze Giant 15D, ECD achieves 51% success versus CD's 41%.
 - **AntSoccer 17D:** In AntSoccer environments (Arena and Medium) with a 17-dimensional state (tracking ant and ball positions plus joint states), ECD achieves the highest mean success rates among compared methods (66% for Arena, 20% for Medium). This indicates the formulation's applicability to joint dynamics and multi-object planning.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Feasibility of Long-Horizon Plans (Replanning Budget):** An evaluation of replanning budgets shows that ECD consistently outperforms CD across all replan budgets on PointMaze Giant and AntMaze Giant. This indicates that ECD generates more feasible long-horizon plans, requiring fewer replans to achieve the goal, as its energy formulation better enforces boundary consistency and keeps local segments in-distribution after stitching.

- **Effectiveness of Exact vs. Approximate Boundary Reaction Term:** A comparison between the approximate Markov reaction term and the exact term (computed via automatic differentiation) shows that the exact computation yields slightly higher success rates (e.g., 92% vs. 91% on PointMaze Giant). However, the exact calculation significantly increases inference runtime (approximately 3x slower, e.g., 22s vs. 8s on PointMaze Giant). This suggests that the Markov approximation provides a favorable trade-off, retaining most of the benefits of the energy-derived correction while ensuring efficiency.

- **Approximation Fidelity:** The accuracy of the Markov approximation, measured by cosine similarity and norm ratio with the exact reaction term, improves significantly as reverse diffusion proceeds (i.e., in later denoising stages, $t \rightarrow 0$). This trend aligns with theoretical error bounds that predict approximation error scales with noise level.

- **Inference Runtime:** ECD maintains an inference runtime comparable to heuristic stitching methods like CompDiffuser. For PointMaze Giant, ECD runs in 8 seconds per episode (matching CD), while for AntMaze Giant, it takes 25 seconds per episode (compared to CD's 17 seconds). In contrast, CDGS, which performs multiple resampling rounds, is considerably slower (e.g., 46 seconds for 4x resampling and 168 seconds for 8x resampling on AntMaze Giant). This demonstrates that ECD achieves state-of-the-art success rates at a practical inference speed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Energy-based Compositional Diffusion Planning (ECD) presents several significant contributions to the field of robotic planning:

1. **Theoretical Foundation for Compositional Planning:** The work identifies and formally characterizes a fundamental theoretical flaw in existing heuristic stitching methods used in compositional diffusion planners. By demonstrating that such methods often induce a non-conservative score field, the paper highlights a key mathematical inconsistency. This formalization provides a deeper understanding of why these methods can exhibit mode drift and inconsistency in long-horizon planning.

2. **Principled Energy-based Framework:** ECD introduces a novel, principled energy-based framework for compositional diffusion planning. By formulating the global trajectory as the minimizer of a sum of local bridge potentials, the proposed method inherently defines a conservative correction field. This shift from heuristic stitching to an energy-derived gradient direction offers a robust and mathematically sound approach to ensuring global consistency.

3. **Revelation and Integration of the Boundary Reaction Term:** A crucial aspect of ECD is the explicit identification and incorporation of the "boundary reaction term." This term, which represents how interior chunk predictions respond to changes in boundary conditions, was previously omitted by heuristic methods. Its inclusion allows for effective propagation of internal inconsistencies back to chunk boundaries, promoting coherent and mode-consistent trajectories.

4. **Efficiency Through Markov Approximation:** The paper addresses the computational challenge of calculating the exact boundary reaction term by introducing an efficient Markov-based approximation. This approximation leverages the local Markovian structure of robotic planning tasks, enabling the computation of the reaction term via a single block-tridiagonal solve. This design maintains linear time complexity in the planning horizon, making ECD practically viable for real-world applications without incurring the significant computational overhead seen in other consistency-enforcing methods like CDGS.

5. **Enhanced Performance and Practicality:** Empirically, ECD achieves state-of-the-art success rates on various long-horizon trajectory stitching tasks within the OGBench benchmark, including low- and high-dimensional maze environments. Notably, it accomplishes this while maintaining inference speeds comparable to faster, but less consistent, heuristic methods. This addresses a critical trade-off in the existing literature, offering both improved performance and practical efficiency.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for robotic planning, particularly in scenarios where training data is fragmented. By enabling more reliable and consistent long-horizon planning from short, offline demonstrations, ECD could reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming full-task data collection. This could facilitate the deployment of robots in complex tasks such as autonomous navigation, manipulation, and intricate task execution in various environments.

However, the authors also acknowledge limitations and potential negative impacts. The effectiveness of the Markov approximation depends on the local Markovian structure of the task, which may not hold for all complex dynamics. The method's sensitivity to chunking parameters also requires careful consideration. Furthermore, as with any advanced planning method, its deployment in safety-critical systems would necessitate rigorous validation, monitoring, and oversight to ensure responsible and safe operation, as the experiments are currently confined to simulated environments. Future work could focus on learning better reaction approximations, adaptive chunking strategies, and real-world evaluation under disturbances.
