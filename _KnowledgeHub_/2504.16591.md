# JEPA for RL: Investigating Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Bielefeld researchers develop an adapted Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) framework for reinforcement learning from images, combining vision transformers with predictive modeling to learn low-dimensional state representations while addressing model collapse through gradient propagation and regularization techniques.

## Problem

- Learning directly from high-dimensional image inputs in reinforcement learning is computationally expensive and sample-inefficient
- Traditional JEPA architectures are prone to model collapse when applied to reinforcement learning tasks
- Raw image observations contain substantial task-irrelevant detail, requiring embeddings that abstract away noise while retaining information essential for solving the RL task

## Method

- Adapt ==JEPA architecture== using separate ==context and target networks== with ==vision transformers== as encoders
- Combine ==JEPA loss== with ==actor-critic gradient propagation== and ==regularization== to prevent ==model collapse==
- Use ==learnable classification tokens== to generate ==lower-dimensional representations==

## Results

- Successfully learned effective state representations from pixel observations in Cart Pole task
- Combined JEPA-RL approach achieved faster learning and better overall performance
- Proposed collapse prevention techniques enabled stable training with informative embeddings

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training with both JEPA and RL losses produces better performance than using either alone
> - Backpropagating actor-critic gradients through the encoder helps prevent model collapse
> - Adding regularization enables learning useful representations even without RL gradients

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a crucial challenge in reinforcement learning (RL): efficiently learning from high-dimensional sensory inputs, specifically images. It tackles this problem by leveraging the recent advancements in self-supervised learning (SSL), particularly Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs).

Here's how the work fits into the broader research landscape:

- **Reinforcement Learning from Images:** RL traditionally relies on direct state representations. However, in many real-world scenarios, the agent perceives the environment through high-dimensional inputs like images. Learning directly from images is computationally expensive and sample-inefficient. This paper directly addresses this issue by learning a lower-dimensional, task-relevant representation from images before applying RL.

- **Self-Supervised Learning for Representation Learning:** SSL has emerged as a powerful tool for learning useful representations from unlabeled data. JEPAs, pioneered by Yann LeCun, have shown promising results in learning image and video embeddings that are effective for downstream tasks. This paper builds on this progress by investigating the applicability of JEPAs to the specific problem of RL from images.

- **Bridging the Gap between SSL and RL:** A major trend in RL research involves integrating SSL techniques to improve sample efficiency and generalization. The authors are contributing to this trend by adapting a specific SSL architecture (JEPA) to RL. This contrasts with other approaches that might use contrastive learning (e.g., CURL) or autoencoders (e.g., world models) for representation learning in RL.

- **Addressing Model Collapse in SSL:** A well-known issue in SSL, especially with JEPAs, is model collapse, where the model learns a trivial solution that minimizes the loss but doesn't capture any meaningful information. The paper explicitly addresses this issue by discussing collapse scenarios and proposing methods to prevent them, such as propagating RL gradients and adding a regularization loss. This contributes to the understanding and stabilization of JEPAs in the context of RL.

- **Vision Transformers in RL:** The use of vision transformers (ViTs) as the encoder network aligns with the increasing adoption of transformers in computer vision and RL. ViTs have demonstrated excellent performance in image recognition and representation learning tasks, making them a suitable choice for extracting features from image inputs in RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this paper are:

- **Adapt JEPA for RL from images:** To successfully modify the JEPA architecture, originally designed for general image and video representation learning, to be effective in reinforcement learning environments where the agent receives image-based observations.

- **Learn task-relevant embeddings:** The goal is to train the JEPA model to extract low-dimensional embeddings from image inputs that capture the essential information required for the RL agent to learn and solve the task. These embeddings should abstract away irrelevant details in the image.

- **Address and prevent model collapse:** To thoroughly investigate the potential for model collapse within the JEPA architecture in the RL context and propose and evaluate methods to prevent this issue.

- **Demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach:** To experimentally validate the performance of the proposed JEPA-based RL framework on a benchmark task (Cart Pole) and compare different configurations to analyze the impact of various design choices.

The motivation behind this work stems from the need to improve the efficiency and scalability of RL in real-world scenarios where agents interact with complex visual environments. By learning effective image representations using SSL principles, the authors aim to reduce the sample complexity and computational burden associated with training RL agents directly from raw pixel data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors' methodology involves the following key steps:

- **JEPA Architecture Adaptation:** They adapt the original JEPA architecture by using separate context (x-encoder) and target (y-encoder) networks. The x-encoder processes a sequence of recent frames as input, while the y-encoder processes a slightly shifted sequence of frames.
- **Vision Transformer Encoder:** They employ a vision transformer (ViT) as the core encoder network for both the context and target encoders. Patch embeddings of the input images are fed into the transformer, along with positional encodings that capture both spatial and temporal information.
- **Target Encoder Weight Averaging:** To prevent collapse, the weights of the target encoder are updated as a running average of the context encoder's weights, preventing the target encoder from deviating too far from the context encoder.
- **Predictor Network:** A shallow, two-layer MLP is used as the predictor network, which takes the embedding from the context encoder and the action taken by the RL agent as input and predicts the embedding of the target.
- **Learning Objective:** The JEPA loss is defined as the Euclidean distance between the predictor's output and the target encoder's output in the embedding space. The authors prepend a learnable classification token to the input of the x-encoder, and only use the final embedding of this token as input to the predictor, resulting in a lower-dimensional representation.
- **Collapse Prevention Techniques:** Two methods are employed to prevent model collapse: 1) Backpropagation of actor and critic losses through the context encoder, providing an incentive to learn informative representations. 2) A regularization loss that encourages batch-wise variance in the embeddings.
- **Experimental Evaluation:** The framework is tested on the Cart Pole task using a PPO-style actor-critic algorithm. Different configurations are evaluated by varying the presence of the JEPA loss, the backpropagation of RL gradients, and the application of the regularization loss.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings of the paper are:

- **JEPA can successfully learn useful embeddings for RL from images:** The experimental results on the Cart Pole task demonstrate that the proposed JEPA-based RL framework can learn effective state representations from pixel observations.

- **Combining JEPA and RL losses yields the best performance:** Training the encoder with both the JEPA loss and the gradients from the actor and critic networks results in faster learning and better overall performance compared to training with only one of these losses.

- **Model collapse is a significant issue:** When the encoder is trained solely with the JEPA loss and without actor-critic gradient propagation, the model is prone to collapse, leading to a trivial solution and preventing the agent from learning.

- **RL gradient propagation and regularization can prevent collapse:** Backpropagating the actor and critic losses through the encoder and adding a regularization loss that encourages variance in the embeddings can effectively prevent model collapse.

- **JEPA can learn informative state representations without RL gradients:** While performance is better with RL gradients, the regularization loss enables JEPA to still learn a usable state representation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Advances RL from images:** The successful adaptation of JEPA for RL from images provides a promising approach for addressing the challenges of learning from high-dimensional sensory inputs. This could lead to more efficient and scalable RL algorithms for real-world applications.

- **Provides insights into SSL for RL:** The paper contributes to the understanding of how SSL techniques can be effectively integrated into RL frameworks. The investigation of model collapse and the proposed prevention methods offer valuable insights for designing and training SSL-based RL systems.

- **Offers a practical solution for representation learning:** The JEPA-based approach provides a practical method for learning task-relevant representations from images. The use of vision transformers and the proposed training techniques make it a potentially applicable solution for a wide range of RL tasks.

- **Encourages further research:** The paper highlights the potential of JEPAs for RL and encourages further work in this direction. Future research could explore the application of this framework to more complex RL tasks, investigate different encoder architectures, and develop more sophisticated collapse prevention techniques.

- **Potential for broader applications:** The learned representations could be transferable to other tasks or domains, even beyond reinforcement learning.

In conclusion, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of reinforcement learning by demonstrating the effectiveness of JEPAs for learning from images. The findings highlight the importance of addressing model collapse and provide practical solutions for training stable and informative representations. This work paves the way for further research and development of SSL-based RL algorithms for real-world applications.
