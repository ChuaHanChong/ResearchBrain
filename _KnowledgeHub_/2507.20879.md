# DriveAgent-R1: Advancing VLM-based Autonomous Driving with Active Perception and Hybrid Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> DRIVEAGENT-R1 introduces a 3-billion-parameter autonomous driving agent that integrates active perception with a hybrid thinking framework, allowing it to dynamically request visual information and switch between text-only and tool-augmented reasoning. The agent achieves performance comparable to larger closed-source models and human drivers on complex driving scenarios, demonstrating a +6.07% accuracy gain with its active perception toolkit and a 0.28m ADE for motion planning.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Model (VLM) based autonomous driving agents rely on passive perception, making them unable to resolve ambiguities when initial visual inputs are insufficient.
- Processing exhaustive multi-view camera data for all scenarios leads to high computational inefficiency and redundant visual information.
- Existing VLM driving systems lack adaptive reasoning mechanisms, which prevents them from dynamically adjusting their cognitive load based on scene complexity, similar to human drivers.

## Method

- Introduces an active perception framework equipped with a ==Vision Toolkit==, enabling the agent to dynamically request specific visual evidence like alternative camera views, region-of-interest inspections, depth maps, and 3D object detection.
- Implements a hybrid thinking mechanism that allows the agent to adaptively switch between efficient text-only reasoning for simple scenarios and robust, tool-augmented visual reasoning for complex situations.
- Deploys a multi-stage progressive training strategy, including domain-specific supervised fine-tuning and cascaded reinforcement learning, to cultivate both mode proficiency and adaptive mode selection while mitigating visual neglect in the base VLM.

## Results

- DriveAgent-R1, a **3B**-parameter model, achieved competitive first-frame joint accuracy (**51.34%**) and sequence average joint accuracy (**45.42%**) on the Drive-Internal_test set, comparable to GPT-5 and human drivers.
- The active perception toolkit provided a +**6.07%** accuracy gain on Drive-Internal_test, with DriveAgent-R1 outperforming passive multi-view baselines (+1.29 points) even when accessing the same information pool.
- The hybrid thinking framework successfully reduced inference latency to **6.74s** on a single H20 GPU, improving efficiency compared to passive multi-view processing (**8.50s**) while maintaining strong performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively requesting and integrating specific visual evidence during reasoning significantly enhances decision reliability and interpretability in VLM-based autonomous driving, especially in ambiguous scenarios.
> - An adaptive "hybrid thinking" approach, switching between efficient text-only reasoning and robust tool-augmented visual analysis, optimizes computational efficiency and system robustness by avoiding redundant processing.
> - Domain-specific pre-training and alignment are essential for general VLMs to mitigate "visual neglect" and ensure that their driving decisions are genuinely grounded in perceptual evidence rather than textual shortcuts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving has seen substantial advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in enabling end-to-end systems that integrate perception, reasoning, and planning. VLMs offer a framework for unifying these tasks, with potential benefits in generalization and understanding complex scenarios. Within this paradigm, a notable direction involves leveraging VLMs for high-level behavioral decision-making, distinguishing it from low-level continuous motion planning.

Previous research in VLM-based autonomous driving has largely operated under a "passive perception" paradigm. This typically involves models reasoning over either a fixed set of textual descriptions derived from visual inputs or processing comprehensive multi-view camera data. This passive approach presents two main challenges:
1. **Inability to resolve ambiguity**: When initial or default visual inputs are insufficient to make a confident decision, passive models cannot actively seek additional information.
2. **Computational inefficiency and redundancy**: Providing exhaustive multi-view data to cover all potential information needs leads to high computational overhead and requires the model to process redundant visual information, potentially distracting it from critical cues.

The evolution of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (M-CoT) reasoning offers a pathway to more dynamic reasoning. While early M-CoT methods relied on text-based reasoning from visual descriptions, "Tool-based M-CoT" allows models to actively invoke external tools to gather new visual evidence during the reasoning process, facilitating a "think-while-seeing" capability. However, integrating this tool-based active perception for high-level behavioral planning in autonomous driving has largely remained an underexplored area. Furthermore, indiscriminately applying tool-based active perception to all scenarios is computationally inefficient, suggesting a need for adaptive reasoning mechanisms.

DriveAgent-R1 addresses these gaps by introducing active perception capabilities and a hybrid thinking framework. It aims to develop an autonomous driving agent that can dynamically adapt its reasoning mode based on scene complexity, thereby enhancing decision interpretability, reliability, and computational efficiency, drawing parallels to human cognitive patterns in driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing DriveAgent-R1 are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the limitations of passive perception in VLM-based autonomous driving**: Existing VLM-based driving agents typically rely on pre-processed or fixed sets of visual inputs, which restricts their ability to resolve ambiguities when critical visual evidence is missing or unclear. This passive approach can lead to uncertain or unreliable decisions in complex, long-tail scenarios. The motivation is to enable the agent to proactively seek specific visual information when uncertainty arises, thereby grounding its decisions in verifiable perceptual evidence.

2. **Enhance interpretability and reliability of autonomous driving decisions**: By actively requesting and integrating specific visual evidence into its reasoning chain, the agent can provide a clearer and more traceable justification for its decisions. This increased transparency is intended to improve confidence in the system's behavior, particularly in safety-critical driving contexts.

3. **Improve computational efficiency by avoiding redundant visual processing**: Processing all available multi-view or high-resolution visual data constantly, even for simple scenarios, incurs significant computational costs and can introduce unnecessary information noise. The motivation is to design a system that can selectively acquire visual information only when needed, thus reducing inference latency and optimizing resource utilization.

4. **Introduce an adaptive "hybrid thinking" mechanism inspired by human cognition**: Human drivers intuitively switch between rapid, heuristic-based judgments for routine situations and deliberate, in-depth analysis (e.g., re-checking mirrors or traffic signals) for complex or uncertain scenarios. The paper seeks to emulate this adaptive cognitive pattern, allowing the agent to dynamically choose between efficient text-only reasoning and robust, tool-augmented visual reasoning based on the perceived complexity of the scene.

5. **Develop a deployable and parameter-efficient autonomous driving agent**: Many high-performing VLM systems are large and computationally intensive, hindering real-world deployment. A key motivation is to develop a model with a relatively small parameter count (3 billion parameters) that can still achieve competitive performance, making it more practical for integration into autonomous vehicles.

In summary, the core objectives are to overcome the passive nature of current VLM-based driving agents by introducing active, evidence-seeking perception and to optimize reasoning efficiency and robustness through an adaptive hybrid thinking framework, all within a deployment-friendly parameter budget.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DriveAgent-R1 integrates active perception and a hybrid thinking framework through a specialized architecture and a multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Hybrid Thinking Architecture

The agent's task is to generate an 8-second meta-action sequence, (a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4), at 2-second intervals, based on multimodal inputs (initial front-view image I_0 and textual context T_0, including speed and navigation). Each meta-action a_t consists of a velocity token (Accelerate, Keep Speed, Decelerate, Stop) and a trajectory token (Straight, Right Turn, Left Turn).

The hybrid-thinking mechanism involves selecting a reasoning path by generating either a `<think_text>` or `<think_tool>` token. Both paths follow a unified Chain-of-Thought (CoT) structure: description, reasoning, and prediction.

- **Text-based M-CoT (M_text)**: For routine scenarios, the agent generates `<think_text>`, relying on its internal knowledge and initial inputs (I_0, T_0) for text-only reasoning.
- **Tool-based M-CoT (M_tool)**: For complex or uncertain scenarios, the agent generates `<think_tool>` to engage in active perception. This involves an iterative process where the agent generates a textual thought (T_k) and potentially a tool call request (T_k) based on its current history (H_k-1). If a tool is called, it executes to obtain new visual information (I_k), which is then encoded and integrated into the history (H_k = H_k-1 ⊕ T_k ⊕ I_k). This process continues until an action sequence is generated or a maximum interaction limit (K) is reached.

#### 3.2. Vision Toolkit Design

The Vision Toolkit enables active perception, allowing the model to acquire specific visual information on demand:
- **Retrieve View**: Fetches images from any camera view, including historical frames from a 5-second memory pool, mimicking human blind spot checks.
- **RoI Inspection**: Acts as a "zoom-in" function, cropping and magnifying a specified Region of Interest from a higher-resolution image, providing fine-grained details.
- **Depth Estimation**: Generates a depth map for a specified view using Depth Anything V2, providing 3D spatial awareness.
- **3D Object Detection**: An open-vocabulary tool (based on Detect Anything 3D) that identifies and localizes objects in 3D space from arbitrary camera views, allowing dynamic specification of objects.

#### 3.3. Autonomous Driving Domain Alignment

To mitigate the tendency of general VLMs to rely on textual shortcuts and neglect visual inputs, an autonomous-driving domain alignment step is performed. A VQA (Visual Question Answering) dataset of 530K question-answer pairs is constructed from real traffic images, covering scene description, traffic entity recognition, target localization, and traffic commonsense. This dataset is used to fine-tune all parameters of Qwen2.5-VL-3B, resulting in DriveAlign-3B, a model with enhanced visual sensitivity to driving scenes. This model serves as the unified initialization for subsequent training stages.

#### 3.4. Progressive Training via SFT and Cascaded RL

The hybrid-thinking capability is cultivated through a three-stage progressive training strategy:

#### 3.4.1. Stage 1: Dual-Mode Supervised Fine-Tuning (DM-SFT)

- **Objective**: Endow the model with a foundational understanding of both thinking modes (M_text and M_tool) and their format/semantic boundaries.
- **Data Construction**: A three-stage automated pipeline generates a high-quality CoT dataset (4K samples):
 1. **Necessity Assessment**: Raw data is partitioned into tool-unnecessary (D_text) and tool-necessary (D_tool) sets. Qwen2.5-VL-3B identifies simple scenarios for D_text (high accuracy without tools). Qwen2.5-VL-72B identifies complex scenarios for D_tool (performance gain with tools).
 2. **Mode-Specific CoT Annotation**: Qwen2.5-VL-72B acts as a "teacher model" to generate CoT annotations for each mode, performing "inverse" reasoning from ground-truth meta-actions. A separate "judge model" (Doubao-Seed-1.6) validates and filters annotations, with low-scoring samples regenerated. Dynamic Tool Preference Guidance ensures balanced tool use in D_tool.
 3. **Rule-Based Data Cleaning**: Ensures fidelity and uniformity through format standardization, content correction, and tool usage control.
- **Training**: DriveAlign-3B is fine-tuned using this dataset.

#### 3.4.2. Stage 2: Forced Contrastive Mode Reinforcement Learning (FCM-RL)

- **Objective**: Deepen the agent's independent performance within each thinking mode.
- **Algorithm**: Adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) into Mode-Partitioned GRPO (MP-GRPO). For each input from an exploratory dataset (D_explore), MP-GRPO compels the agent to generate G/2 responses in M_text and G/2 responses in M_tool by forcing the mode token. All G responses are aggregated for reward normalization and advantage calculation, facilitating both intra- and inter-mode comparison.
- **Reward Design**: A composite outcome-driven reward function evaluates the entire reasoning trajectory: R = R_acc + R_fmt.
 - **Accuracy Reward (R_acc)**: Weighted Levenshtein distance comparing predicted and ground-truth meta-action sequences, with higher weight on speed and position-action weights to counteract data imbalance (up-weighting rarer, critical actions). Relaxed Speed Matching awards partial scores for safer predictions.
 - **Format Consistency Reward (R_fmt)**: Penalizes structural errors and incorrect mode usage (e.g., calling tools in M_text).

#### 3.4.3. Stage 3: Adaptive Mode Selection Reinforcement Learning (AMS-RL)

- **Objective**: Train the agent to intelligently select the optimal thinking mode.
- **Algorithm**: Uses the native GRPO algorithm, allowing the agent to generate the initial mode-selection token autonomously.
- **Reward Design**: Augments the reward function with a conditional tool-usage reward (R_tool) to encourage judicious tool invocation: R = R_acc + R_fmt + I(mode = M_tool) · R_tool.
 - **Tool Usage Reward (R_tool)**: Applied only to M_tool trajectories. It uses a contrastive mechanism: a tool-based trajectory is rewarded only if its R_acc surpasses the average accuracy of text-only trajectories within the same group (¯Acc_text) by a margin, minus a penalty for the number of tool calls (R_tool ∝ (R_acc - ¯Acc_text - margin) - N_i · C_tool). This discourages superfluous tool calls.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper evaluates DriveAgent-R1 through extensive experiments on proprietary (Drive-Internal) and public (nuScenes) datasets, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art VLMs and human drivers.

#### 4.1. Datasets and Metrics

- **Training Data**: Drive-Internal dataset (35K video clips focusing on long-tail/complex scenarios). 4K high-quality CoT data for SFT (2K D_text, 2K D_tool). 15K exploratory instances for RL (D_explore).
- **Test Data**: 1.5K instances from Drive-Internal as in-distribution (Drive-Internal_test). 2K instances from nuScenes validation set for cross-dataset testing (nuScenes_test). Meta-action labels were generated via a rule-first, VLM-corrected pipeline.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Accuracy (Acc)**: First-Frame and Sequence Average Joint Accuracy (composite of speed and trajectory). Relaxed Speed Matching gives partial scores for safer predictions.
 - **Mode Selection Accuracy (MSA)**: Measures alignment between the agent's chosen mode and the empirically optimal mode for each sample.

#### 4.2. Comparison with SOTA VLMs and Human Drivers

DriveAgent-R1 (3B parameters) was benchmarked against models including Qwen2.5-VL series (3B, 7B, 72B), Doubao-Seed-1.6, Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-4.1, and GPT-5, as well as human drivers.
- **Performance**: On Drive-Internal_test, DriveAgent-R1 achieved competitive performance with GPT-5 (51.34% vs. 56.30% First-Frame Joint Acc, 45.42% vs. 47.97% Sequence Avg. Joint Acc) and human drivers (49.59% First-Frame Joint Acc). On nuScenes_test, DriveAgent-R1 surpassed GPT-5 in sequence accuracy (47.10% vs. 45.14%) and showed performance approaching human-level (52.96% vs. 50.48% First-Frame Joint Acc).
- **Tool Effectiveness**: DriveAgent-R1 demonstrated the most effective tool use, achieving a +6.07% accuracy gain with tools on Drive-Internal_test. While larger closed models like GPT-4.1 and Gemini-2.5-Flash showed improvements with tool access (+1.5% to +4%), smaller Qwen2.5-VL models (3B, 7B) exhibited performance degradation, indicating that effective tool use requires specific training or base model capability.

#### 4.3. Performance on DriveBench

Compared to other VLM-based driving agents on DriveBench (DriveLM, Dolphins):
- DriveAgent-R1 achieved a Perception score of 34.07, notably higher than DriveLM's 16.85, indicating its active perception captures more critical visual details.
- It also obtained a high Behavior score of 43.69, suggesting effective translation of visual evidence into decision-making.

#### 4.4. Performance of Motion Planning

When extended with a lightweight MLP motion planning head to regress physical trajectories on nuScenes:
- DriveAgent-R1 achieved an average L2 Displacement Error (ADE) of 0.28m, outperforming baselines like DriveVLM-Dual (0.31m) while maintaining a comparable collision rate. This suggests its high-level reasoning translates into effective low-level control.

#### 4.5. Ablation on Domain Alignment

- **Capability Enhancement**: Domain alignment (DriveAlign-3B) significantly boosted performance on the driving-specific VQA benchmark (+11.7 points) and improved scores across eight general VLM benchmarks (+2.0 points), confirming successful domain knowledge injection while strengthening general capabilities.
- **Mitigation of Visual Neglect**: DriveAgent-R1, built on DriveAlign-3B, achieved higher accuracy (+1.86% Sequence Avg-Joint Acc). Crucially, its performance dropped more significantly when images were removed (-15.8% vs. -11.0% for a variant without alignment), indicating its gains were grounded in visual evidence rather than textual shortcuts.

#### 4.6. Ablation on Progressive Training Strategy

The three-stage progressive training strategy achieved consistent performance improvements:
- **Stable Gains**: Each stage—DM-SFT, FCM-RL, and AMS-RL—contributed to steady increases in both accuracy and Mode Selection Accuracy (MSA).
- **Cascaded RL Superiority**: The cascaded RL strategy (FCM-RL then AMS-RL) outperformed single-stage RL variants with the same total training epochs. FCM-RL sharpened execution proficiency in both modes, while AMS-RL honed selection intelligence, demonstrating the functional complementarity of the stages.

#### 4.7. Ablation on Active Versus Passive Perception

Compared to passive perception baselines (Passive-FV with front-view, Passive-SV with all surrounding views):
- **Active Selection Superiority**: DriveAgent-R1 (Retrieve View Only) outperformed Passive-FV (+1.69 points) and Passive-SV (+1.29 points), indicating that actively retrieving task-relevant information is more effective than passively processing fixed inputs, even from the same information pool.
- **Deepened Visual Reliance**: Agents with more active and capable perception exhibited a larger performance drop when visual inputs were removed, confirming the framework fosters genuine, vision-driven decision-making.

#### 4.8. Efficiency Analysis

Inference latency was measured on a single H20 GPU with vLLM:
- **Active Perception Efficiency**: DriveAgent-R1 in M_tool mode had lower latency (7.91s) compared to Passive-SV (8.50s), as it avoids processing redundant images.
- **Hybrid Thinking Balance**: In M_adaptive mode, DriveAgent-R1 further reduced both inference latency (from 7.91s to 6.74s) and average output tokens (from 314.45 to 265.57), balancing performance with computational cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DriveAgent-R1 represents an advancement in VLM-based autonomous driving by introducing active perception and a hybrid thinking framework. The work addresses the limitations of passive perception, which often struggles with ambiguity and computational inefficiency in complex driving scenarios.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:
1. **Enhanced Reliability and Interpretability**: By enabling the autonomous agent to proactively seek and integrate specific visual evidence through its Vision Toolkit, DriveAgent-R1 grounds its decisions in verifiable perception. This approach has the potential to increase the reliability of autonomous driving systems, particularly in long-tail and uncertain scenarios, and enhance the interpretability of its reasoning process by revealing *why* it made a certain decision based on actively acquired information.
2. **Improved Efficiency and Adaptability**: The hybrid thinking framework, which allows the agent to dynamically switch between efficient text-only reasoning for simple scenes and robust, tool-augmented visual analysis for complex ones, offers a more computationally efficient paradigm. This adaptive strategy, inspired by human cognitive patterns, avoids the overhead of indiscriminately processing all visual data, making the system more deployment-friendly by reducing inference latency.
3. **Competitive Performance with Deployment Feasibility**: Achieving performance competitive with large, closed-source models (e.g., GPT-5) and human drivers with only 3 billion parameters suggests a practical pathway toward scalable and intelligent autonomous driving systems. The demonstrated efficiency, coupled with competitive accuracy, positions DriveAgent-R1 as a potentially viable solution for real-world applications where computational resources are constrained.
4. **Mitigation of Visual Neglect**: The domain alignment strategy effectively addresses the "shortcut tendency" observed in general VLMs, ensuring that the agent's decisions are genuinely grounded in visual evidence rather than relying solely on low-dimensional textual cues. This fosters a more robust and trustworthy perception-action loop.

However, the paper also identifies a limitation: the agent's current over-reliance on new visual evidence from its tools, which can lead to misinterpretations in complex scenarios (e.g., mistaking a pedestrian signal for its own traffic light). This suggests that while active perception provides information, the agent's ability to critically evaluate and synthesize potentially conflicting visual cues needs further development.

Future work aims to strengthen the agent's foundational understanding of complex road topologies and traffic rules and to cultivate a more nuanced reasoning process for resolving informational conflicts. Addressing these limitations could further enhance the robustness and safety of active perception systems in autonomous driving.
