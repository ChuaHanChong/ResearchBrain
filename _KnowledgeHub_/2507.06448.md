# Perception-Aware Policy Optimization for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Alibaba Group developed the Perception-Aware Policy Optimized (PAPO) algorithm, which integrates implicit perception loss into Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to enhance multimodal reasoning in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). This method significantly reduced perception-related errors by 30.5% and achieved up to 19.1% relative performance gains on vision-dependent tasks.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often exhibit suboptimal performance in complex multimodal reasoning tasks due to inaccurate perception of visual inputs.
- A comprehensive error analysis revealed that 67% of errors in LMMs trained with standard RLVR pipelines originated from inadequate visual perception.
- Existing RLVR adaptations for LMMs primarily focused on modifying data, rollout strategies, or reward functions, rather than integrating perception incentives directly into the core optimization objective.

## Method

- Introduced ==Perception-Aware Policy== Optimized (**PAPO**), a novel policy gradient algorithm designed as a drop-in replacement for existing RLVR methods like GRPO and DAPO.
- Implemented an Implicit Perception Loss (KLprcp) using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to encourage the model to assign higher probability to responses when full visual input is present compared to corrupted input, implicitly incentivizing visually grounded responses.
- Incorporated a Double Entropy Loss for regularization to prevent 'KLprcp hacking' and enhance training stability, ensuring the model does not generate nonsensical tokens while optimizing for perception.

## Results

- PAPO consistently outperformed baseline GRPO and DAPO algorithms across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, showing average performance gains of **4.4%** to **17.5%**, and up to **19.1%** relative gains on vision-dependent tasks.
- A qualitative error analysis demonstrated a **30.5%** reduction in perception-related errors with PAPO compared to the GRPO baseline.
- PAPO exhibited improved training stability and faster convergence, successfully preventing model collapse observed in some baseline configurations (e.g., DAPO-**7B**) through the implemented double entropy regularization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary bottleneck in multimodal reasoning for LMMs is often inadequate perception of visual content, accounting for a significant majority of errors.
> - Modifying the core optimization objective to include perception-aware signals is a more effective and fundamental approach than auxiliary adjustments to data, rollouts, or reward functions.
> - Directly maximizing an unbounded perception-aware objective requires robust regularization, such as double entropy loss, to maintain training stability and prevent performance collapse.
