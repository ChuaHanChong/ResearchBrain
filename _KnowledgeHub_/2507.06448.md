# Perception-Aware Policy Optimization for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Alibaba Group developed the Perception-Aware Policy Optimized (PAPO) algorithm, which integrates implicit perception loss into Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to enhance multimodal reasoning in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). This method significantly reduced perception-related errors by 30.5% and achieved up to 19.1% relative performance gains on vision-dependent tasks.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often exhibit suboptimal performance in complex multimodal reasoning tasks due to inaccurate perception of visual inputs.
- A comprehensive error analysis revealed that 67% of errors in LMMs trained with standard RLVR pipelines originated from inadequate visual perception.
- Existing RLVR adaptations for LMMs primarily focused on modifying data, rollout strategies, or reward functions, rather than integrating perception incentives directly into the core optimization objective.

## Method

- Introduced ==Perception-Aware Policy== Optimized (**PAPO**), a novel policy gradient algorithm designed as a drop-in replacement for existing RLVR methods like GRPO and DAPO.
- Implemented an Implicit Perception Loss (KLprcp) using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to encourage the model to assign higher probability to responses when full visual input is present compared to corrupted input, implicitly incentivizing visually grounded responses.
- Incorporated a Double Entropy Loss for regularization to prevent 'KLprcp hacking' and enhance training stability, ensuring the model does not generate nonsensical tokens while optimizing for perception.

## Results

- PAPO consistently outperformed baseline GRPO and DAPO algorithms across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, showing average performance gains of **4.4%** to **17.5%**, and up to **19.1%** relative gains on vision-dependent tasks.
- A qualitative error analysis demonstrated a **30.5%** reduction in perception-related errors with PAPO compared to the GRPO baseline.
- PAPO exhibited improved training stability and faster convergence, successfully preventing model collapse observed in some baseline configurations (e.g., DAPO-**7B**) through the implemented double entropy regularization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary bottleneck in multimodal reasoning for LMMs is often inadequate perception of visual content, accounting for a significant majority of errors.
> - Modifying the core optimization objective to include perception-aware signals is a more effective and fundamental approach than auxiliary adjustments to data, rollouts, or reward functions.
> - Directly maximizing an unbounded perception-aware objective requires robust regularization, such as double entropy loss, to maintain training stability and prevent performance collapse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as an effective strategy for enhancing the chain-of-thought reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in purely textual domains. However, its application to Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for complex multimodal reasoning tasks, which demand both fine-grained perception and multi-step reasoning, has encountered limitations. LMMs often exhibit suboptimal performance in these contexts, a contrast to the reasoning prowess of LLMs in text.

A growing body of research has sought to adapt RLVR to LMMs to improve multimodal reasoning. These efforts have primarily focused on modifications to the data (e.g., distilling chain-of-thought data, sophisticated sample selection), rollout strategies (e.g., diversifying rollout space with data augmentation, selective sample replay), or reward design (e.g., incorporating grounding metrics like IoU, captioning-based rewards, multi-turn rewards). Such approaches typically maintain the core RLVR optimization objective largely unchanged from its textual domain origins. Another direction involves enhancing perception through tool-use, incentivizing LMMs to perform visual operations rather than directly improving their native perceptual abilities.

This paper positions itself by challenging the prevailing assumption that multimodal reasoning in RLVR can be effectively addressed solely through data-, rollout-, or reward-level modifications. It investigates a deeper and more efficient integration of perceptual incentives directly into the core optimization objectives. By proposing a novel policy gradient algorithm, Perception-Aware Policy Optimized (PAPO), this work aims to move beyond auxiliary adjustments and directly optimize for visually grounded responses, thus contributing to the fundamental design of RLVR for multimodal domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the multimodal reasoning capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by addressing a fundamental bottleneck: the inaccurate perception of visual inputs within existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks. The authors articulate their motivation through a series of observations and research questions:

**1. Identification of the Perception Bottleneck:**
The authors conducted a comprehensive error analysis on a multimodal reasoning model trained using a standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) pipeline. By manually examining 200 error instances across four diverse benchmarks (Geometry3K, MMK12, LogicVista, MathVerse), they discovered that 67% of errors originated from inadequate perception of visual content. This finding suggests that LMMs often struggle to accurately interpret visual inputs, such as spatial relationships or label associations, even when their logical or algebraic reasoning capabilities are proficient. This bottleneck is attributed to current RLVR objectives not explicitly incentivizing models to generate responses that are visually grounded.

**2. Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
Prior efforts to improve multimodal reasoning within RLVR have primarily focused on modifying the data, rollout procedures, or reward functions. While these approaches have shown some promise, they often:
 - Impose a rigid separation between perception and reasoning (e.g., requiring explicit captioning before reasoning).
 - Rely on additional large neural-based reward models, which introduce significant computational overhead and are limited by the reward model's capacity.
 The authors contend that these strategies do not achieve a deep integration of perception and reasoning, leading to suboptimal performance, particularly in fine-grained visual understanding.

**3. Formulating Core Research Questions:**
Based on their error analysis and the limitations of existing methods, the authors posed two fundamental research questions to guide their work:
 - Are there unique challenges in multimodal reasoning that do not arise in text-only settings and cannot be addressed solely through data- or reward-level modifications? The error analysis answers this affirmatively by highlighting the dominant perception errors.
 - If so, how can these challenges be addressed by designing a new RLVR optimization objective that is more deeply grounded in multimodal domains? This question directly motivates the development of PAPO.

**4. Proposed Solution Direction:**
The motivation converges on the idea that instead of merely augmenting data, refining rollouts, or designing external rewards, a more effective solution lies in integrating perceptual incentives directly into the *core optimization objective* of RLVR. The goal is to encourage the model to learn to perceive *while* learning to reason, without requiring additional data annotation, stronger teacher models, or expensive neural reward models. This approach aims for a more efficient and joint improvement of both perception and reasoning abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Perception-Aware Policy Optimized (PAPO)**, a novel policy gradient algorithm designed to enhance multimodal reasoning by encouraging models to generate visually grounded responses. PAPO is introduced as a direct drop-in replacement for existing RLVR algorithms like GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) and DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization).

**1. Task Formulation:**
The work operates within a typical RLVR setting. The training dataset `D` comprises visual inputs `I`, questions `q`, and short ground-truth answers `a`. A rule-based verifier assigns rewards based on whether a generated response `o` matches the ground truth. RL training is initiated directly from base LMMs without prior supervised fine-tuning.

**2. Implicit Perception Loss (KLprcp):**
The core of PAPO is the **Implicit Perception Loss (KLprcp)**, implemented as a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term.
- **Intuition:** A robust multimodal model should depend on informative visual context for its responses. Removing this context should significantly alter the model's output distribution.
- **Definition:** The loss maximizes `D_KL[π_θ(o|q,I) || π_θ(o|q,I_mask)]`. Here, `π_θ(o|q,I)` is the probability of generating response `o` given the question `q` and the original visual input `I`. `π_θ(o|q,I_mask)` is the probability of generating the same response `o` given `q` and a *corrupted* visual input `I_mask`.
- **Rationale:** This "reverse KL" loss encourages the model to assign a substantially higher probability to its generated response when full, informative visual input `I` is available compared to when visual information is degraded or removed (`I_mask`). This implicitly incentivizes the model to rely on visual context, thus generating visually grounded responses, without requiring explicit external supervision or annotations for perception. The KL divergence is approximated using `r_prcp_i(θ) - log r_prcp_i(θ) - 1`, where `r_prcp_i(θ) = π_θ(o_i|q,I) / π_θ(o_i|q,I_mask)`.

**3. Double Entropy Loss for Regularization:**
To address the potential for the unbounded KLprcp objective to lead to "hacking" and performance collapse, PAPO incorporates a **Double Entropy Loss** term.
- **Problem:** Maximizing an unbounded KL divergence can destabilize training, potentially causing the model to generate nonsensical tokens in an attempt to satisfy the loss. This behavior is characterized by rising rollout entropy in both the original and corrupted policies.
- **Solution:** The regularization adds terms `- η1 * H[π_θ]` and `- η2 * H[π_mask_θ]` to the objective, where `H` denotes the entropy computed from the log probabilities of the response tokens.
- **Rationale:** By encouraging both the original and masked policy entropies to remain low, this regularization prevents the model from generating overly chaotic or unpredictable responses, thereby enhancing training stability while preserving performance.

**4. Overall PAPO Objective:**
The complete PAPO objective combines the standard GRPO/DAPO learning objective with the Implicit Perception Loss and the Double Entropy Loss. For PAPO_G (based on GRPO), the objective is:
`J_PAPO_G(θ) = E[{o_i}_G_{i=1}∼π_θ_old(O|q,I)] [ (1/G) Σ_i^G (1/|o_i|) Σ_t^|o_i| min(r_i,t(θ) * Â_i,t, clip(r_i,t(θ), 1-ϵ_l, 1+ϵ_h) * Â_i,t) - βD_KL[π_θ || π_ref] + γD_KL[π_θ || π_mask_θ] - η1 H[π_θ] - η2 H[π_mask_θ] ]`
where `γ`, `η1`, `η2` are hyperparameters for loss weighting. PAPO_D, based on DAPO, similarly integrates these terms but removes the `D_KL[π_θ || π_ref]` term and employs dynamic sampling.

**5. Masking Strategies for I_mask:**
Two strategies are investigated for generating the corrupted visual input `I_mask`:
- **Random Masking:** Image patches are selected uniformly at random for masking. This method is computationally efficient.
- **Semantic-Aware Masking:** Leverages DINOv2, a self-supervised pre-trained vision encoder, to identify and prioritize masking patches with high semantic saliency (e.g., by aggregating patch-level self-attention scores).
Both methods employ patch-based masking rather than pixel-level noise, as patch-based masking is considered more effective at removing informative semantic content.

**6. Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and Qwen3-VL-2B-thinking are used as base LMMs.
- **Dataset:** Models are trained on ViRL39K, a multimodal reasoning QA dataset, for 2 epochs.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Eight benchmarks covering math, geometry, multi-discipline, logical reasoning, and counting are used. These are categorized into "General Multimodal Reasoning" and "Vision-Dependent Multimodal Reasoning" based on the degree of reliance on visual input. Exact match accuracy is the primary metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the efficacy and characteristics of the Perception-Aware Policy Optimized (PAPO) algorithm:

**1. Consistent Performance Improvements:**
PAPO consistently outperformed baseline GRPO and DAPO algorithms across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks. Average performance gains ranged from 4.4% to 17.5% when using identical training data, rollout spaces, and reward designs. This was observed across different model sizes (3B and 7B).

**2. Enhanced Performance on Vision-Dependent Tasks:**
The improvements yielded by PAPO were more substantial on tasks categorized as "vision-dependent," showing relative gains of 8.0% to 19.1%. This result aligns with the core objective of the Implicit Perception Loss, which aims to encourage visually grounded responses, particularly where visual cues are critical for problem-solving.

**3. Significant Reduction in Perception Errors:**
A qualitative error analysis, mirroring the initial motivation, revealed a 30.5% reduction in perception-related errors with PAPO compared to GRPO. This indicates that PAPO effectively addresses the identified bottleneck, leading to improved perceptual capabilities in the multimodal reasoning process. Further analysis showed that 41% of original perception errors were corrected, with others shifting to different error categories or remaining perception errors.

**4. Improved Training Dynamics and Stability:**
PAPO demonstrated faster convergence, with performance gains observed from early training steps. Notably, the DAPO-7B baseline experienced model collapse in later stages of training. In contrast, PAPO_D, which incorporates the proposed Double Entropy regularization, maintained stable training and continued to show improvements, highlighting the regularization's effectiveness in preventing such instabilities. Experiments also showed that while adding single entropy loss to DAPO delays collapse and improves performance, PAPO_D with Double Entropy Loss consistently outperforms and fully prevents collapse.

**5. Compatibility with Other Algorithmic Modifications:**
The study investigated PAPO's compatibility with other types of RLVR enhancements. When combined with "NoisyRollout" (a rollout diversification technique), PAPO delivered additional, complementary gains. This suggests that PAPO, by modifying the optimization objective, can integrate effectively with advancements in other aspects of the RLVR framework (data, rollout, reward).

**6. Robustness in Various Scenarios:**
- **Reference KL Removal:** PAPO showed robustness even when the reference KL penalty was removed from the GRPO objective, achieving significant overall improvements (11.2% for 3B, 4.0% for 7B) over the baseline in this configuration, with greater gains on vision-dependent tasks.
- **Low Vision-Dependency Tasks:** When tested on text-only tasks (MMLU-Pro) with dummy visual inputs (random noise), PAPO achieved competitive or stronger performance. This indicates the model’s ability to avoid indiscriminately attending to uninformative visual tokens, demonstrating robustness in scenarios with low visual dependency.

**7. Ablation Studies on Key Design Choices:**
- **Masking Strategy and Ratio:** Random masking empirically outperformed semantic-aware masking, hypothesized to be due to a better balance in obscuring informative content without removing entire salient regions. An optimal masking ratio of 0.6 to 0.8 yielded the best performance; complete image blackening was less effective and more prone to instability.
- **Implicit Perception Loss Weighting (γ):** Increasing the weighting coefficient (γ) for KLprcp generally improved performance up to a certain point (e.g., 0.02). However, excessively large γ values (e.g., 0.04) led to severe model collapse. Larger models and configurations without a reference KL penalty were found to be more sensitive to higher γ values.
- **KLprcp Hacking and Regularization:** "KLprcp Hacking," characterized by over-optimization and subsequent performance collapse, was identified. Collapsed models tended to generate irrelevant tokens. Early signs included a drastic drop in KLprcp, increased clipping ratio, and rising entropy in both policies. Model size, loss weighting, and extreme masking ratios were identified as influential factors. Among tested regularization strategies, Double Entropy Loss was found to be the most effective in preventing collapse and achieving the most significant performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Perception-Aware Policy Optimized (PAPO) represents a notable contribution to the field of multimodal reasoning in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), particularly within the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

**1. Novelty in Optimization Objective Design:**
PAPO is presented as the first work to explore a deeper integration of perception-aware supervision signals directly into the *core optimization objective* of RLVR algorithms. This departs from previous efforts that predominantly focused on modifying data, rollout strategies, or reward functions. By addressing the optimization objective itself, PAPO opens a new avenue for research in advancing multimodal RLVR.

**2. Direct Mitigation of a Critical Bottleneck:**
The research identifies and directly addresses the high incidence of perception errors (67%) as a major bottleneck in multimodal reasoning. PAPO's design, through the Implicit Perception Loss, specifically targets and significantly reduces these errors, leading to more accurate visual interpretation and improved overall reasoning performance. This directly tackles a fundamental weakness of current LMMs in complex visual tasks.

**3. Efficiency and Accessibility:**
A significant aspect of PAPO's design is its efficiency. It achieves its improvements without relying on additional data annotations, stronger teacher models, or computationally expensive neural reward models. This makes PAPO a "direct drop-in replacement" for existing RLVR algorithms like GRPO and DAPO, lowering the barrier to adoption and making it accessible for integration into current LMM training pipelines.

**4. Joint Learning of Perception and Reasoning:**
By encouraging the model to "learn to perceive while learning to reason," PAPO promotes a more integrated understanding where visual grounding is intrinsically linked to the reasoning process. This is a departure from approaches that treat perception and reasoning as separate, sequential stages or require explicit external mechanisms for visual grounding.

**5. Robustness and Generalizability:**
The demonstrated consistency of PAPO's improvements across a diverse set of eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including those with high vision dependency, indicates its robustness. Furthermore, its compatibility with other algorithmic modifications (e.g., NoisyRollout) suggests it can complement future advancements in RLVR from different perspectives, potentially leading to cumulative gains. Its robustness to low-vision-dependency inputs also points to its practical applicability in varied settings.

**6. Guidance for Future Research:**
The identification and detailed analysis of "KLprcp Hacking" and the importance of Double Entropy Loss for training stability provide valuable insights into the complexities of designing and stabilizing multimodal RLVR objectives. This work offers a foundation and a new perspective for future research, pointing towards deeper integration of perception and reasoning and inspiring investigations into more fine-grained, token-level application of perception-aware objectives.

In summary, PAPO represents a methodological advancement that directly targets a critical limitation in multimodal LMMs. Its efficient and integrated approach to fostering visually grounded reasoning has the potential to significantly impact the development of more capable and reliable AI systems for complex multimodal tasks.
