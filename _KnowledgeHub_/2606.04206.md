# DLO-Lab: Benchmarking Deformable Linear Object Manipulations with Differentiable Physics

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Massachusetts Amherst introduces DLO-Lab, a differentiable simulation platform for deformable linear objects (DLOs) featuring diverse material properties, multi-material coupling, and a benchmark suite of 10 manipulation tasks. Policies trained in DLO-Lab successfully transferred to real-world robots, achieving up to 58% success for closed-loop tasks and enabling effective VLA model fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing DLO simulators lacked a combination of features like diverse material properties (e.g., bending plasticity), robust coupling with other materials (e.g., rigid, soft), and differentiability for efficient policy optimization.
- Prior DLO manipulation research was often limited to either perception-focused methods requiring extensive data or task-specific manipulation approaches lacking generalization.
- Learning-based DLO manipulation faced challenges with underactuated dynamics, grasp sensitivity, and the difficulty of optimizing policies for long-horizon or topologically complex tasks.

## Method

- A differentiable DLO simulation engine, **DLO-Lab**, was developed based on Discrete Elastic Rods theory, incorporating diverse material properties like bending plasticity, and robust two-way coupling with rigid and soft bodies.
- A comprehensive benchmark suite of 10 DLO manipulation tasks was established, formulated as finite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with differentiable reward functions.
- A specialized DLO agent was designed to provide structural guidance through VLM-based grasp point proposal and dynamic task decomposition for complex, long-horizon manipulation problems.

## Results

- Sample-based trajectory optimization (CMA-ES) achieved the strongest overall performance across the DLO-Lab benchmark, outperforming model-free and first-order model-based reinforcement learning.
- The DLO agent's VLM-driven grasp proposal in "Candidate mode" and dynamic task decomposition enhanced performance, particularly for long-horizon, precision-critical tasks.
- Successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated for both open-loop policies and a closed-loop PPO policy, achieving approximately **58%** success for Wiring-ring on a real robot.
- DLO-Lab facilitated the generation of synthetic data to fine-tune a multi-task VLA model, which attained an average success rate of **60.0%** across four tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Differentiable simulation aids gradient-based learning, but DLO tasks often present "gradient inaccessibility" and "non-smooth landscapes" due to discontinuous contacts, challenging first-order optimizers.
> - Sample-based trajectory optimization (CMA-ES) demonstrated superior performance in DLO manipulation tasks, particularly those with sparse rewards or non-smooth dynamics, by sidestepping local gradient issues.
> - Integrating structural guidance via a specialized DLO agent, which offers VLM-based grasp proposals and dynamic task decomposition, is crucial for tackling complex and long-horizon DLO manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation of deformable objects, particularly deformable linear objects (DLOs) such as ropes, cables, and wires, represents a notable challenge in robotics. Prior research has primarily addressed DLO manipulation through two main avenues: perception-focused approaches (e.g., cable tracing) that often require extensive data, frequently synthetic, and manipulation-focused approaches (e.g., untangling, shape control) that tend to be task-specific and lack generalization.

A critical limitation across these prior efforts is the absence of a comprehensive simulation environment capable of accurately modeling the diverse behaviors of DLOs and their interactions with other objects, while also providing differentiability for efficient policy optimization. Existing DLO simulators support only a subset of desired features, such as lacking robust coupling with other materials (rigid, soft, fluids), neglecting bending plasticity or complex loop topologies, or failing to offer differentiability, which is crucial for gradient-based learning. Learning-based surrogate models, while differentiable, often sacrifice physical rigor, whereas physically grounded models frequently encounter integration difficulties or lack gradient information.

This work positions itself to bridge these identified gaps by introducing a differentiable simulator and an accompanying benchmark suite. The aim is to provide a unified platform that supports a broad spectrum of DLO material properties and interactions, enabling efficient policy learning and robust evaluation, thereby fostering the development of generalizable DLO manipulation skills.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance robotic manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) by addressing several limitations inherent in existing approaches. The authors identify three key areas for improvement:

1. **Develop a comprehensive and differentiable DLO simulator:** Prior work in DLO simulation often lacked a combination of features necessary for versatile robotic manipulation. These missing features include support for diverse material properties (e.g., inextensibility, elasticity, bending plasticity), robust physical coupling with other objects (e.g., rigid bodies, soft bodies, fluids), and differentiability for efficient policy optimization. The motivation is to create a simulator that provides high physical fidelity and enables gradient-based learning, which can accelerate the development of manipulation policies.

2. **Establish a standardized benchmark suite for DLO manipulation:** Existing manipulation tasks are often narrow and task-specific, relying on real-world demonstrations or handcrafted heuristics. This limits generalizability and scalability. The authors aim to introduce a benchmark suite of representative tasks that highlight the unique challenges of DLO manipulation, such as topological complexity and grasp sensitivity. This benchmark is intended to facilitate the exploration and evaluation of versatile manipulation skills across a variety of DLOs and tasks.

3. **Propose a specialized DLO agent to manage manipulation challenges:** DLO manipulation tasks, particularly those that are long-horizon or involve complex topological changes, present significant challenges for end-to-end policy learning due to underactuated dynamics and sensitivity to grasping points. The motivation is to integrate structural guidance into the manipulation process by developing an agent capable of proposing strategic grasping points and decomposing complex tasks into manageable subtasks. This agent is designed to maximize control authority and improve the feasibility of solving intricate DLO manipulation problems.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to provide a robust foundation for learning and evaluating DLO manipulation skills, ultimately aiming to enable policies that can transfer effectively from simulation to real-world robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves the development of a differentiable DLO simulation environment (DLO-Lab), a suite of benchmark tasks, and a specialized DLO agent, followed by experimental evaluation and real-world validation.

#### 3.1. Simulation Environment

DLO-Lab is a differentiable simulation engine implemented using the Taichi programming language, integrated into the Genesis platform. It models DLO behaviors and interactions with other entities.

##### 3.1.1. DLO Modeling

DLOs are represented as a centerline defined by vertices and orthonormal material frames, following the Discrete Elastic Rods (DER) theory. The internal interactions are modeled by potential energy components: stretching, bending, and twisting. A symplectic Euler solver handles time stepping.
- **Elasticity**: Stretching, bending, and twisting energies are calculated based on strain, curvature binormal, and twist strain, respectively, with associated stiffness moduli.
- **Inextensibility**: When desired, stretching energy is deactivated, and geometric projections are used to iteratively enforce length constraints on edges.
- **Bending Plasticity**: The simulator tracks rest curvature, updating it based on a yield threshold and creep rate when elastic curvature exceeds the threshold, allowing for permanent deformation.
- **Loop Topology**: For closed-loop DLOs, a holonomy angle correction mechanism is applied to distribute angular mismatch evenly across edges, ensuring a continuous material frame.
- **Self-collision and Friction**: A hybrid Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) approach is used. Non-penetration is enforced through geometric projections at closest points between colliding segments. Coulomb friction is applied at the velocity level based on normal impulse.

##### 3.1.2. Coupling Implementation

DLO-Lab supports two-way coupling with various materials:
- **Rigid Body Coupling**: Leverages Genesis's rigid solver. A soft-coupling scheme uses an exponential influence factor based on penetration depth between DLO segments and rigid body Signed Distance Fields (SDFs). Collisions are resolved with an impulse-based response incorporating restitution and friction. Grasping is stabilized by treating DLO vertices in contact with a gripper as kinematic.
- **Soft Body Coupling**: Achieved through a two-way scheme with the Material Point Method (MPM) solver in Genesis. Collisions between active MPM grid nodes and DLO geometry are detected. Repulsive impulses, symmetric to ensure momentum conservation, are calculated based on relative velocity, surface normal, and material properties, updating both grid node velocities and DLO vertices.

##### 3.1.3. Gradient Back-propagation

The simulator utilizes Taichi's automatic differentiation. To manage memory for long simulation horizons, gradient checkpointing is employed. The forward pass computes segments of the trajectory, caching state checkpoints in CPU memory and discarding intermediate GPU states. The backward pass reconstructs computational graphs for each segment by re-running the forward simulation.

#### 3.2. DLO-Lab Benchmark

A collection of 10 DLO manipulation tasks, formulated as finite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), is provided.
- **State Space**: Includes DLO vertex positions and velocities, rest configurations, and robot joint positions and velocities.
- **Observation Space**: Full DLO state (positions, velocities), manipulator end-effector pose and orientation, joint configurations, and center-of-mass of other objects.
- **Action Space**: End-effector pose control in Cartesian space, resolved by an inverse kinematics (IK) solver.
- **Tasks**: Examples include Coiling, Gathering, Lifting, Separation, Slingshot, Unknotting, Wiring-post, Wrapping (fixed-horizon), and Letter Art, Wiring-ring (long-horizon). Each task features a differentiable reward function.

#### 3.3. DLO Agent

A specialized agent provides structural guidance for DLO manipulation, particularly for long-horizon and topologically complex tasks.
- **Grasp Proposal**: A Vision-Language Model (VLM) is used to suggest optimal grasping points. Three modes were evaluated:
 - *Candidate mode*: VLM selects from pre-sampled points along the DLO.
 - *Coefficient mode*: VLM outputs a scalar (0-1) for a normalized position.
 - *Marker mode*: VLM provides pixel coordinates on a rendered image.
 Candidate mode was found to be more reliable in preliminary experiments.
- **Task Decomposition**: For long-horizon tasks, the VLM generates an initial sub-task plan, defining reward functions, horizon lengths, and success criteria. Sub-tasks are optimized sequentially. A closed-loop evaluation mechanism allows the agent to assess the completion of sub-tasks and dynamically update the plan for subsequent phases, adapting to intermediate deformations and execution errors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Algorithm Performance on DLO-Lab Tasks

The paper evaluates three categories of algorithms on the benchmark tasks: model-free reinforcement learning (MFRL), first-order model-based reinforcement learning (FO-MBRL), and trajectory optimization methods.

- **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning (MFRL)**: PPO and SAC generally exhibited lower performance and sample efficiency compared to trajectory optimization methods. This is attributed to the higher burden of learning generalized closed-loop policies through extensive exploration in complex DLO environments, especially under sample constraints.
- **First-Order Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (FO-MBRL)**: SHAC and SAPO leveraged the simulator's differentiability, resulting in better performance than MFRL on contact-rich tasks like Unknotting. However, they still lagged behind sample-based trajectory optimization, suggesting that learning closed-loop policies with noisy or misleading gradients in non-smooth DLO dynamics remains challenging.
- **Trajectory Optimization Methods**:
 - **CMA-ES (Sample-based)**: Achieved the strongest overall performance and reasonable sample efficiency. Its success is attributed to bypassing policy network optimization complexities and its immunity to local gradient information issues, which arise in tasks with non-smooth or discontinuous contact dynamics (e.g., Lifting, Slingshot, where rewards are sparse until contact is made). CMA-ES benefits from massive parallelization in simulation.
 - **Gradient Descent (GD)**: Demonstrated high efficacy in tasks with relatively smooth landscapes and consistent gradients (e.g., Coiling, Separation). However, it encountered significant issues with "gradient inaccessibility" in tasks requiring tool-use or indirect manipulation (e.g., Gathering), and with "non-smooth landscapes" due to discontinuous contact and self-collisions, often leading to local optima (e.g., Unknotting, Wiring-post). This highlights the utility of differentiable simulation when the underlying dynamics are suitable for first-order optimization.

#### 4.2. DLO Agent Efficacy

- **Grasp Proposal**: The "Candidate mode," where the VLM selects from a discrete set of pre-sampled points, consistently yielded superior reliability and higher episodic returns across tasks compared to "Coefficient mode" or "Marker mode." This suggests that constraining the VLM's decision space to physically valid discrete options improves robustness for grasp point selection.
- **Task Decomposition**: For long-horizon tasks, the dynamic plan update strategy (closed-loop) in the DLO agent proved crucial. In precision-critical, sequential tasks like Wiring-ring, it enabled adaptive re-planning and refinement of reward functions based on intermediate states, effectively correcting execution drift and leading to successful outcomes. For tasks with more spatially distinct sub-goals like Letter Art, a static decomposition plan was comparable in performance.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **System Identification**: The differentiable simulator facilitated calibration of physical parameters (e.g., stretching and bending stiffness) of real-world ropes. By minimizing the projection error between simulated and real-world rope segmentation masks, the system achieved accurate geometric alignment and dynamic response, confirming that the identified parameters effectively captured the DLOs' physical properties.
- **Open-loop Policy Deployment**: Policies trained in simulation via sample-based trajectory optimization (CMA-ES with trajectory robustification) were successfully transferred zero-shot to physical hardware (Marvin M6 bimanual manipulator, xArm) for tasks like Gathering and Wiring-post. The simulated trajectories were executed on physical setups with comparable outcomes, indicating the simulator's physical realism.
- **Closed-loop Policy Deployment**: A closed-loop policy trained in DLO-Lab using PPO for the Wiring-ring task was transferred zero-shot to an xArm robot. Using a simulated sensor model mirroring a real-world HSV tracker for observation (384-point 3D point cloud), the policy achieved a success rate of approximately 58% across 12 trials with various initial rope positions. This demonstrated that DLO-Lab supports the development and transfer of reactive closed-loop policies.
- **VLA Fine-tuning (Appendix C.5)**: DLO-Lab was utilized as a synthetic data engine to generate 800 demonstrations across four tasks. A single multi-task Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model (SmolVLA) fine-tuned on this dataset achieved an average success rate of 60.0%, which was competitive with per-task RL baselines, indicating the platform's potential for training generalizable manipulation policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces DLO-Lab, a comprehensive and differentiable simulation platform that significantly contributes to the field of robotic manipulation of deformable linear objects. Its significance stems from several aspects:

- **Unified Simulation Platform**: DLO-Lab integrates diverse material behaviors (inextensibility, elasticity, bending plasticity) and robust coupling capabilities with other objects (rigid, soft, fluids) within a single differentiable physics solver. This addresses a critical gap where prior simulators offered only subsets of these features, enabling more realistic and versatile DLO dynamics.
- **Efficient Policy Learning**: The differentiability of the simulator allows for efficient gradient-based policy optimization. Experimental results suggest that when informative gradients are available, gradient-based methods can achieve high sample efficiency, accelerating the learning process for certain tasks.
- **Comprehensive Benchmark**: The established benchmark suite provides a standardized set of challenging DLO manipulation tasks. These tasks mimic real-world applications, ranging from precise cable routing to topological unknotting, offering a robust testbed for evaluating and comparing different policy learning algorithms.
- **Enhanced Manipulation Capabilities**: The specialized DLO agent, with its grasp proposal and task decomposition functionalities, provides a hierarchical approach to complex, long-horizon DLO manipulation. This structural guidance helps overcome challenges posed by topological complexity and grasp sensitivity, making intricate tasks more tractable.
- **Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer of both open-loop and closed-loop policies from simulation to physical hardware is a key indicator of the simulator's physical fidelity. This capability is essential for practical applications, as it reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection and training.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: DLO-Lab serves as a strong foundation for future advancements. It opens avenues for developing hybrid optimization algorithms that combine the strengths of gradient-based and sampling-based methods, enhancing DLO agents for sparse-reward scenarios, and scaling synthetic data generation for broader generalization of visuomotor policies, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

The potential impact of DLO-Lab extends to various domains, including industrial manufacturing (e.g., cable harnessing, wire forming), healthcare (e.g., surgical suturing, catheter manipulation), and household robotics. By enabling robots to robustly manipulate deformable linear objects, this work contributes to the long-term goal of deploying autonomous robots in unstructured and complex environments.
