# NORA-1.5: A Vision-Language-Action Model Trained using World Model- and Action-based Preference Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> NORA-1.5, developed by researchers primarily from Nanyang Technological University, introduces a VLA model that integrates an autoregressive backbone with a flow-matching action expert, achieving state-of-the-art performance across embodied tasks. The work also presents a scalable post-training method utilizing Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with learned world model- and action-based preference rewards, which boosts real-world robot task performance by an average of 13% and yields smoother, more efficient trajectories.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, heavily reliant on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), exhibit biases and limited generalization, struggling to adapt to novel scenarios or recover from errors.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) in robotics is hindered by the high computational cost and difficulty of collecting extensive real-world data or building precise, embodiment-specific simulators for reward generation.
- Current VLA methods lack robust post-training mechanisms to refine policies beyond initial imitation, limiting their ability to explore solution spaces or improve performance effectively.

## Method

- Introduces **NORA-1.5**, a VLA architecture enhancing a 3B-parameter autoregressive VLA backbone (NORA) with a ==flow-matching==-based action expert, jointly trained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset.
- Develops a scalable post-training paradigm for VLA policies using ==Direct Preference Optimization== (==DPO==), adapted for robotics to refine policies based on learned preference rewards.
- Designs a robust reward modeling framework that combines an action-conditioned world model for goal-based evaluation (WM-guided reward) with a ground-truth action heuristic (GTA reward) to generate synthetic preference datasets for ==DPO==.

## Results

- NORA-1.5 achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming previous VLA models with a **95.0%** average success rate on LIBERO and substantial improvements across SimplerEnv and real-world tasks on the Galaxea A1 robot.
- DPO-based post-training consistently improves performance, showing a **13%** average gain in real-world tasks on a novel robot embodiment, reducing unintended distractor grasps by **4%** and increasing correct object grasping accuracy by **11%**.
- DPO-trained NORA-1.5 demonstrates smoother, more efficient real-robot gripper trajectories, requiring fewer action chunks (7.0 vs 9.7) for object grasping, with the combined `WM (subgoal) + GTA` reward model proving most effective and robust.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of a flow-matching action expert with an autoregressive VLA backbone not only accelerates inference but also consistently enhances overall policy accuracy, highlighting a synergistic architectural advantage.
> - Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), when applied to VLA models with synthetically generated preference datasets, offers an effective and scalable post-training method that improves policy performance, generalization, and robustness in real-world robotic tasks.
> - A hybrid reward model, combining goal-based assessments from an action-conditioned world model with ground-truth action heuristics, provides a robust and data-efficient mechanism for generating high-quality preference signals to refine VLA policies.
