# NORA-1.5: A Vision-Language-Action Model Trained using World Model- and Action-based Preference Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> NORA-1.5, developed by researchers primarily from Nanyang Technological University, introduces a VLA model that integrates an autoregressive backbone with a flow-matching action expert, achieving state-of-the-art performance across embodied tasks. The work also presents a scalable post-training method utilizing Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with learned world model- and action-based preference rewards, which boosts real-world robot task performance by an average of 13% and yields smoother, more efficient trajectories.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, heavily reliant on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), exhibit biases and limited generalization, struggling to adapt to novel scenarios or recover from errors.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) in robotics is hindered by the high computational cost and difficulty of collecting extensive real-world data or building precise, embodiment-specific simulators for reward generation.
- Current VLA methods lack robust post-training mechanisms to refine policies beyond initial imitation, limiting their ability to explore solution spaces or improve performance effectively.

## Method

- Introduces **NORA-1.5**, a VLA architecture enhancing a 3B-parameter autoregressive VLA backbone (NORA) with a ==flow-matching==-based action expert, jointly trained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset.
- Develops a scalable post-training paradigm for VLA policies using ==Direct Preference Optimization== (==DPO==), adapted for robotics to refine policies based on learned preference rewards.
- Designs a robust reward modeling framework that combines an action-conditioned world model for goal-based evaluation (WM-guided reward) with a ground-truth action heuristic (GTA reward) to generate synthetic preference datasets for ==DPO==.

## Results

- NORA-1.5 achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming previous VLA models with a **95.0%** average success rate on LIBERO and substantial improvements across SimplerEnv and real-world tasks on the Galaxea A1 robot.
- DPO-based post-training consistently improves performance, showing a **13%** average gain in real-world tasks on a novel robot embodiment, reducing unintended distractor grasps by **4%** and increasing correct object grasping accuracy by **11%**.
- DPO-trained NORA-1.5 demonstrates smoother, more efficient real-robot gripper trajectories, requiring fewer action chunks (7.0 vs 9.7) for object grasping, with the combined `WM (subgoal) + GTA` reward model proving most effective and robust.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of a flow-matching action expert with an autoregressive VLA backbone not only accelerates inference but also consistently enhances overall policy accuracy, highlighting a synergistic architectural advantage.
> - Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), when applied to VLA models with synthetically generated preference datasets, offers an effective and scalable post-training method that improves policy performance, generalization, and robustness in real-world robotic tasks.
> - A hybrid reward model, combining goal-based assessments from an action-conditioned world model with ground-truth action heuristics, provides a robust and data-efficient mechanism for generating high-quality preference signals to refine VLA policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied artificial intelligence, specifically in robotic control. VLA models aim to enable robots to understand natural language instructions, perceive their environment through vision, and execute physical actions to achieve goals. Recent years have seen significant progress in this area, with models like RT-1, OpenVLA, and NORA demonstrating promising capabilities on various embodied tasks, such as pick-and-place operations.

However, the paper identifies critical limitations in existing VLA approaches. Most current methods rely heavily on **large-scale imitation learning (IL)** from expert-collected cross-embodiment action trajectories, followed by **supervised fine-tuning (SFT)** on embodiment-specific data for downstream tasks. While effective to a degree, this SFT-based adaptation suffers from several drawbacks:
1. **Bias from Limited Data:** It inherits a strong bias from the often limited and manually curated expert demonstrations, restricting the model's ability to generalize beyond the quality and scope of the expert data.
2. **Lack of Generalization:** It struggles with tasks or environments that deviate significantly from the training distribution, leading to failures in novel scenarios or across different robot embodiments.
3. **Poor Exploration and Failure Recovery:** SFT does not intrinsically encourage exploration of the solution space or provide mechanisms for recovering from unexpected failures, as it primarily imitates observed behaviors.
4. **Computational Expense:** Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) methods, which could offer more robust learning, are computationally expensive, slow to train, and difficult to scale in robotics due to the need for extensive real-world rollouts or highly accurate, embodiment-specific simulators.

NORA-1.5 directly addresses these gaps by proposing a two-pronged approach that pushes beyond conventional IL and SFT. First, it introduces an improved architectural design for VLA models by integrating a **flow-matching-based action expert** with an autoregressive VLA backbone, aiming to enhance both performance and inference speed. Second, and more critically, it pioneers a **scalable post-training paradigm** using **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**, a technique popularized in large language models, adapted for VLA policies. This paradigm leverages lightweight, learned reward models (combining world model-based goal assessment and ground-truth action heuristics) to generate synthetic preference datasets, thereby reducing reliance on costly human annotations or extensive physical robot interactions for policy refinement.

By integrating LLM-inspired alignment techniques with advanced world modeling in robotics, this work positions itself at the forefront of developing more robust, generalizable, and adaptable embodied agents. It offers a practical pathway to overcome the "calibration bottleneck" of traditional supervised methods, enabling VLA models to learn from preferences rather than solely from direct demonstrations, and to refine their policies in a computationally efficient manner suitable for real-world deployment. This approach significantly moves the needle towards achieving "System-II level intelligence" in robotics, where models can not only execute instructions but also evaluate and improve their actions based on complex criteria.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the reliability, generalization capabilities, and adaptability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for a diverse range of embodied tasks, particularly when deployed across different robot embodiments or in complex real-world environments.

This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and leads to distinct sub-objectives:

- **Motivation to Overcome SFT Limitations:** Existing VLA models, heavily reliant on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) from manually curated expert demonstrations, suffer from inherent biases and limited generalization abilities. This restricts their capacity to perform robustly in unseen scenarios or to improve beyond the quality of the initial expert data.
 - **Objective:** To develop a scalable and data-efficient post-training method that moves beyond the constraints of SFT, enabling VLA policies to generalize better and adapt more effectively.

- **Motivation to Leverage Flow-Matching for Performance:** While flow-matching techniques have been explored in robotics for improving inference speed, their full impact on overall policy performance for action generation remained underexplored.
 - **Objective:** To rigorously investigate and demonstrate that coupling a flow-matching-based action expert with a pre-trained autoregressive VLA backbone (NORA) can lead to substantial performance gains, not just faster inference. This involves analyzing the architectural synergy between these components.

- **Motivation for Scalable Reward Modeling in Robotics:** Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) in robotics is hampered by the high cost of collecting real-world interaction data or the complexity of building accurate, task-specific simulators for reward signal generation.
 - **Objective:** To design lightweight yet effective reward models capable of generating robust preference signals without requiring extensive on-robot rollouts or complex hand-engineered reward functions. These reward models should leverage advanced techniques like action-conditioned world models and simple heuristics.

- **Motivation to Utilize Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** DPO has proven highly effective in aligning large language models with human preferences without the complexities of full RL, but its application to VLA models has been limited.
 - **Objective:** To adapt and apply DPO for post-training VLA policies in robotics, using synthetically generated preference datasets from the novel reward models. This aims to convert policy improvement into a compute-bound process, leveraging synthetic rollouts for efficient refinement.

- **Motivation for Cross-Embodiment and Real-World Robustness:** A key challenge for generalist robot policies is reliable performance on unseen robot embodiments and in unpredictable real-world conditions, especially with limited adaptation data.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate that the proposed NORA-1.5 model, combined with reward-guided DPO post-training, achieves state-of-the-art performance and enhanced robustness across diverse simulated benchmarks (SimplerEnv, LIBERO) and critical real-world robot experiments on novel embodiments (Galaxea A1).

In essence, the paper aims to lay a robust foundation for building more dependable and adaptable embodied agents by synergistically combining architectural innovations with a novel, scalable, and data-efficient post-training paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper introduces NORA-1.5, a novel Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, and a sophisticated reward-driven post-training pipeline using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). The approach is structured into two main training stages: initial VLA development and subsequent preference-based refinement.

#### 3.1. NORA-1.5 Architecture

NORA-1.5 is built upon the existing 3B-parameter autoregressive VLA model, **NORA**. NORA itself is derived from fine-tuning the Qwen-2.5-VL-3B vision-language model (VLM) backbone on the Open X-Embodiment dataset, endowing it with strong multi-modal reasoning and instruction-following capabilities.

To enhance NORA, the authors introduce a **flow-matching-based action expert (A)**. This expert is designed to circumvent the often slower autoregressive action decoding of the original NORA. It directly regresses continuous action sequences (a_t:t+N) for a given horizon N, conditioned on the rich visual and linguistic encodings (key-value pairs) extracted from NORA’s transformer layers. The action expert is parameterized as a stacked transformer network, architecturally similar to NORA, and is trained to predict the "velocity" vector (v = a_0 - a_t:t+N) by minimizing a flow-matching loss (L_FM). This architectural choice directly investigates the performance benefits of flow-matching beyond just inference speed.

#### 3.2. Action-Expert Training (Pre-Training Phase)

In the first major training stage, the newly initialized action expert parameters are jointly trained with the pre-trained NORA backbone. The optimization involves a combined loss function: a flow-matching loss (L_FM) on the action expert's output and a cross-entropy loss (L_CE) on the FAST+ tokenizer output tokens from NORA's original VLA decoder head. This joint training is performed on the Open X-Embodiment dataset, encouraging a synergistic relationship where the flow-matching expert leverages NORA's rich representations, and NORA benefits from informative gradients propagated through the expert, leading to more coherent multi-step trajectory planning.

#### 3.3. Reward Modeling for Post-Training VLAs

To enable scalable and calibrated post-training, the paper develops a robust reward modeling framework that generates preference datasets for DPO. This addresses the limitations of sparse RL rewards or pure imitation learning. The reward model combines two complementary components:

- **WM-guided Goal-based Reward (R_g):** This component quantifies how well a generated action sequence aligns with a specified goal. It leverages an action-conditioned world model (W), specifically a 1.3B-parameter V-JEPA2-AC. This world model is trained to predict the embedding of a future observation (o_t+N) given the current observation (o_t) and a candidate action sequence (a_t:t+N). The reward R_g is then defined as the negative L1 distance between the predicted future embedding and the embedding of a target goal image (o_g). The authors experiment with two variants: `WM (endgoal)` for the final goal state and `WM (subgoal)` for immediate subgoal states (the frame N steps into the future). This provides a dense, step-wise evaluation.

- **Action-based Reward (R_a or GTA):** This heuristic reward measures the negative L1 distance between a sampled action sequence (a_t:t+N) and the corresponding ground-truth expert action sequence (a*_t:t+N) from the training data. It serves to mitigate potential noise from the world model (W) and provides a stable reference, especially when WM predictions might be imperfect due to limited data.

- **Total Reward (R_tot):** The final reward is a linear combination of the goal-based and action-based rewards: R_tot = R_g + 0.5R_a. The action-based component is given a smaller weight to balance adherence to demonstrated paths with the flexibility of goal-driven exploration.

#### 3.4. Reward-guided Post-Training (DPO Phase)

Once the reward model is in place, preference datasets (D_goal and D_act) are constructed. This involves sampling multiple action rollouts from the VLA policy (NORA-1.5), evaluating them using the reward model, and forming (winner, loser) preference pairs (a_W, a_L) where a_W has a higher reward than a_L.

These preference pairs are then used to fine-tune the action expert (and optionally NORA's FAST+ output head) using the **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** objective. DPO directly optimizes the policy against the reference policy (the SFT model) by maximizing the likelihood of preferred actions over dispreferred ones, without requiring an explicit reward model to be trained as in traditional RL with human feedback (RLHF). This process refines the VLA policy to generate actions that are better aligned with the learned reward signals, promoting desired behaviors such as successful task completion, efficiency, and safety. The DPO post-training is applied to the SFT models, meaning after initial fine-tuning on embodiment-specific supervised data.

#### 3.5. Evaluation

The efficacy of NORA-1.5 and the DPO-based post-training is rigorously evaluated across:
- **Simulated Benchmarks:** LIBERO (testing generalization across spatial layouts, objects, goals, and long horizons) and SimplerEnv (focusing on simulation-to-reality gap, evaluated with visual matching and variant aggregation).
- **Real-world Cross-Embodiment Evaluation:** Using a Galaxea A1 robotic arm, an embodiment explicitly *absent* from the pre-training dataset. Evaluation tasks include seen tasks, unseen-object tasks with distractors, and unseen-instruction tasks with distractors, to assess generalization and robustness.
- **Metrics:** Binary success rates for simulations; success rate, partial success rate (for progress towards goal), and distractor rate (for unintended grasps) for real-world experiments.

This comprehensive methodology, combining architectural innovation with a novel reward-guided preference learning framework, represents a significant step towards more adaptable and robust VLA models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across architectural performance, the impact of DPO-based post-training, and reward model effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Performance of NORA-1.5 Architecture

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** NORA-1.5 consistently outperforms its predecessor NORA and several state-of-the-art VLA models (e.g., SpatialVLA, MolmoAct, π₀) across both simulated benchmarks (SimplerEnv, LIBERO) and real-world settings (Galaxea A1).
 - **SimplerEnv:** NORA-1.5 shows superior performance in visual matching evaluations, particularly on "pick coke can" and "move near" tasks, outperforming baseline zero-shot models by 4.6% and 10.7%, respectively. For fine-tuned variants, it maintains advantages of 6.8% and 0.8%. Overall, NORA-1.5's zero-shot and fine-tuned variants surpass next-best models by 6.4% and 4.2% in visual matching. In variant aggregation, its DPO-trained version is comparable to the best, MolmoAct, but demonstrates more robust performance across diverse tasks.
 - **LIBERO:** NORA-1.5 achieves a 94.5% average success rate, surpassing models like π₀ (94.2%) and NORA-Long (87.9%). The DPO-trained NORA-1.5 further improves this to 95.0%.
 - **NORA vs. NORA-1.5:** The integration of the flow-matching action expert into NORA, forming NORA-1.5, consistently yields performance gains. This contradicts prior assumptions that flow-matching primarily improves inference speed, demonstrating its capacity to enhance policy accuracy through architectural synergy. The flow-matching expert benefits from NORA's rich representations, while NORA benefits from informative gradients, leading to better trajectory planning.
- **Real-world Performance (Galaxea A1):** NORA-1.5-FAST outperforms strong baselines (π₀, NORA) by a substantial margin (13% to 46% average improvement) across nine diverse real-world tasks, particularly on tasks involving unseen distractors, highlighting its robustness.
- **Data Regime Dependency for Flow-Matching:** An interesting observation on the Galaxea A1 dataset is that flow-matching-based action generation sometimes performs *worse* than autoregressive decoding, especially with limited real-robot data (50K frames). This is attributed to the action expert’s need for more extensive flow-matching pre-training or larger datasets to adapt effectively, unlike the more robust VLM-based autoregressive backbone.

#### 4.2. Impact of DPO-based Post-training

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** Reward-driven DPO post-training consistently improves performance in both simulated and real-robot settings across various benchmarks.
 - **SimplerEnv:** DPO significantly boosts NORA-1.5's performance. The best combined reward (WM (endgoal) + GTA) achieves an overall 82.8% success rate in Visual Matching, an increase of 4.9% over the SFT baseline.
 - **LIBERO:** DPO also yields improvements, though smaller (e.g., 0.6% average gain), likely due to the already high performance of the SFT baseline model on these tasks, leaving less room for improvement. The most challenging task, LIBERO-Long, shows consistent gains of 1% to 1.7%.
 - **Galaxea A1 (Real-world):** DPO-trained NORA-1.5 shows a notable **13% average performance improvement**. Specifically, correct-object grasping accuracy increases by 11%, and unintended grasps of distractors decrease by 4%. The gains are even larger (15%-16%) for tasks with unseen objects or instructions, underscoring DPO's contribution to generalization and robustness.
- **Improved Trajectory Execution:** Visual analysis of real-robot gripper trajectories shows that DPO post-training leads to smoother, more consistent movements with fewer corrective strokes and more reliable target grasps. DPO-trained NORA-1.5 requires fewer action chunks (7.0 vs 9.7) to grasp objects, indicating improved efficiency and planning.

#### 4.3. Reward Model Ablations

- **Effectiveness of Combined Rewards:** The combination of WM-guided goal-based reward and ground-truth action (GTA) reward (WM + GTA) generally yields the best overall performance, especially for visual matching in SimplerEnv and for real-world tasks.
- **Subgoal vs. Endgoal Rewards:** `WM (subgoal)`-based reward generally performs better and is more stable than `WM (endgoal)`. Pure `WM (endgoal)` can sometimes degrade performance (e.g., "Move Near" in SimplerEnv) due to potential noisiness or failure to account for implicit task constraints (like obstacle avoidance). Immediate subgoals provide more reliable, localized guidance.
- **Limitations of Pure GTA:** While `GTA` (ground-truth action-based reward) can be effective in some simulated scenarios, it often offers limited benefits or even degrades performance in real-world settings. This supports the hypothesis that multiple valid trajectories exist for a task, and forcing the model to follow a single "gold" trajectory can introduce unnecessary noise and reduce flexibility in unseen real-world conditions.
- **Robustness to Visual Changes:** The `WM (subgoal) + GTA` combination demonstrates stable performance under variant aggregation in SimplerEnv, suggesting its robustness to changing visual conditions.

In summary, NORA-1.5 establishes a new benchmark for VLA models, leveraging a powerful hybrid architecture. Crucially, the paper demonstrates that a reward-guided DPO post-training paradigm, utilizing a combination of world-model-based and action-based rewards, is a highly effective and scalable strategy for enhancing VLA performance, generalization, and robustness in complex real-world robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "NORA-1.5: A Vision-Language-Action Model Trained using World Model- and Action-based Preference Rewards" carries significant implications for the future of embodied AI and robotics. Its contributions pave the way for more capable, reliable, and deployable robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Advancement in VLA Model Capabilities

NORA-1.5, with its novel integration of an autoregressive VLA backbone (NORA) and a flow-matching action expert, achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks. A particularly impactful finding is that the flow-matching expert not only improves inference speed (as previously suggested) but also *consistently enhances policy accuracy*. This architectural synergy suggests a powerful design principle for future VLA models, encouraging researchers to explore hybrid architectures that combine the strengths of different action generation paradigms. This pushes the boundaries of what VLA models can achieve in complex, multi-modal tasks.

#### 5.2. Scalable and Data-Efficient Post-Training Paradigm

Perhaps the most profound impact of this work lies in its introduction of a scalable and data-efficient post-training method for VLAs using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and learned reward models. This paradigm fundamentally addresses a major bottleneck in robotics: the prohibitive cost and complexity of collecting expert demonstrations or training traditional reinforcement learning agents. By:
- **Leveraging World Models:** Repurposing action-conditioned world models (like V-JEPA2-AC) as implicit reward estimators for goal alignment.
- **Synthesizing Preference Data:** Automatically generating (winner, loser) preference pairs from model-generated rollouts, evaluated by the reward models.
- **Applying DPO:** Using DPO to refine the VLA policy directly from these preferences.

This approach converts policy improvement into a **compute-bound process**, allowing for massive, automated refinement without relying on extensive physical robot time or expensive human annotation. This is a game-changer for scaling robotic learning to vast, heterogeneous datasets and accelerating development cycles.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Robustness and Generalization for Real-World Deployment

The consistent performance improvements observed from DPO post-training, especially on unseen tasks and objects in real-world robot experiments, highlight a significant leap in robustness and generalization. The ability of NORA-1.5 to:
- **Reduce Distractor Grasping:** The decrease in unintended grasps of distractors.
- **Improve Affordance and Reliability:** The increase in correct object grasping accuracy.
- **Generate Smoother Trajectories:** The observed smoother and more efficient gripper movements.

These advancements directly translate to more dependable embodied agents suitable for real-world deployment. Robots equipped with such VLA capabilities will be better able to operate in dynamic, unstructured environments, handle novel objects, and execute instructions reliably, even with limited exposure to specific scenarios during initial training.

#### 5.4. Unifying Reward Mechanisms and Harmonizing Data

The proposed reward framework, combining goal-based world model predictions with ground-truth action heuristics, offers a flexible and robust mechanism for evaluating actions. This approach can harmonize heterogeneous corpora (like Open X-Embodiment, which includes data from dozens of embodiments and task specifications) by providing a consistent rating function. This ability to convert diverse action-rich data into a massive preference dataset for DPO signifies a powerful tool for leveraging large-scale, multi-modal robotics datasets more effectively.

#### 5.5. A Foundation for Future Research

This paper provides a "sturdy foundation" for future research in several directions:
- **More Sophisticated Reward Models:** Further exploration into advanced world models, different heuristic components, or even human-in-the-loop preference collection could lead to even more nuanced and effective reward signals.
- **Adaptive DPO for Robotics:** Research into DPO variants specifically tailored for the continuous action spaces and high-dimensional observations inherent in robotics.
- **Long-Horizon and Complex Task Learning:** Applying and extending this paradigm to tackle increasingly complex, multi-step, and long-horizon tasks where sequential decision-making and planning are crucial.
- **Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The insights from using world models as reward proxies can further bridge the sim-to-real gap, by allowing policies to be refined in simulation and then robustly transferred to the physical world.

In conclusion, NORA-1.5, coupled with its innovative DPO-based post-training strategy, represents a significant stride towards building more intelligent, autonomous, and reliable general-purpose robots. By making policy refinement more scalable and effective, this work brings the promise of adaptable embodied AI closer to practical realization.
