# Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Memory for Long-Range Mapless Navigation via End-to-End Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich introduced Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs) within an attention-based deep reinforcement learning framework to overcome the spatial memorization limitations of standard recurrent neural networks in mapless navigation. The system achieved robust long-range navigation on a legged-wheel robot, demonstrating zero-shot transfer to diverse real-world environments and outperforming other RL navigation baselines by up to 105% in success rate.

## Problem

- Standard recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in end-to-end navigation struggle with spatial memorization and transforming ego-centric observations into a coherent world representation.
- This limitation prevents effective long-range mapless navigation as RNNs cannot reliably integrate observations from continuously changing robot perspectives.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for learned navigation policies without fine-tuning on real hardware remains a significant hurdle.

## Method

- Propose Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs) that modify standard RNNs (LSTM/GRU) by introducing a spatial transformation term to implicitly learn spatial alignment and integration of ego-centric observations.
- Develop an end-to-end attention-based network architecture integrating SRUs, a pretrained depth encoder, and self/==cross-attention== layers for efficient visual feature compression.
- Train the policy using PPO with sparse rewards, leveraging Deep Mutual Learning and Temporally Consistent Dropout to foster robust spatial-temporal memory and prevent premature convergence.

## Results

- Policies utilizing SRUs achieved an overall **23.5%** higher success rate in simulated long-range mapless navigation tasks compared to standard recurrent units (LSTM, GRU).
- The SRU-enhanced system significantly outperformed state-of-the-art RL navigation baselines, showing a **29.6%** relative improvement over EMHP and a **105.0%** relative improvement over GTRL in success rate.
- The developed policy successfully transferred zero-shot to a real-world Unitree B2W legged-wheel robot, navigating over 100+ meters through diverse, unstructured environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard RNNs (LSTM, GRU, S4, Mamba-SSM) excel at temporal memorization but exhibit significant limitations in accurately transforming and registering spatial information from dynamic ego-centric viewpoints.
> - Modifying recurrent units with a dedicated "spatial transformation term" effectively addresses this spatial memorization deficit, enabling more coherent environmental representation from sequential observations.
> - Robust sim-to-real transfer for end-to-end learned navigation policies is achievable through extensive synthetic pretraining of vision encoders and the integration of realistic depth-noise models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robot navigation, deep learning, and reinforcement learning, specifically addressing the challenges of mapless navigation for agile robotic platforms. Traditionally, robot navigation relies on a modular pipeline involving explicit mapping (e.g., SLAM) and path planning (e.g., A* or RRT). While robust, these classical methods often suffer from system delays, difficulties in modeling complex kinodynamic interactions, and limitations in dynamic or unknown environments.

End-to-end learning, particularly via reinforcement learning (RL) or imitation learning, has emerged as a promising alternative, aiming to directly map raw sensor inputs to actions. This paradigm can implicitly learn complex environmental interactions and kinodynamics, making it suitable for high-speed, agile robots like legged or wheeled-legged systems. However, a significant hurdle for end-to-end approaches is their implicit nature of environmental representation, especially regarding spatial mapping. Unlike classical methods that explicitly build and update maps, end-to-end learning typically uses recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or Transformers to fuse current and historical observations into a latent representation.

The paper identifies a critical gap in existing end-to-end learning architectures: while RNNs (like LSTM, GRU, and even newer State-Space Models such as S4 and Mamba-SSM) excel at capturing temporal dependencies (e.g., in language tasks), they inherently struggle with *spatial memorization* and *transformation* of ego-centric observations from continuously changing perspectives. This limitation prevents them from effectively building coherent spatial representations necessary for long-range navigation, where the robot needs to integrate observations over significant movements to understand its surroundings. Some prior works have attempted to mitigate this by incorporating explicit mapping pipelines even within learning frameworks or by stacking historical observations, but these have their own drawbacks (delays, heuristic context windows).

This work directly tackles this identified limitation. It proposes a novel modification to RNNs, the Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Unit (SRU), specifically designed to imbue them with implicit spatial transformation and memorization capabilities. By enhancing the RNN's ability to create a consistent spatial understanding from sequential, ego-centric visual inputs, the authors aim to bridge the performance gap with explicit mapping methods and surpass other learning-based approaches that rely on less effective memory mechanisms (e.g., simple stacking of observations). Furthermore, the paper contributes to the crucial area of sim-to-real transfer by leveraging large-scale synthetic pretraining and a depth-noise model, making the end-to-end learned policies deployable on real-world robots without fine-tuning. This work pushes the boundaries of end-to-end RL for navigation by improving its core memory mechanism, making it more effective for complex, long-horizon tasks, especially for agile robotic platforms operating in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to overcome a fundamental limitation observed in existing end-to-end learning approaches for robot navigation: the inability of standard recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to effectively perform *spatial memorization* and *transformation* of sequential, ego-centric observations. While RNNs are proficient at capturing temporal dependencies, they lack an inherent mechanism to align and integrate visual information received from continuously changing perspectives, a capability essential for building coherent spatial representations that support long-range planning.

The key objectives of this paper are:
1. **Identify and Characterize Spatial Mapping Limitations in RNNs:** To empirically demonstrate that widely used RNN architectures (LSTM, GRU, S4, Mamba-SSM), despite their temporal prowess, struggle with spatial registration and memorization of observations from varying viewpoints under dynamic ego-motion.
2. **Develop Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs):** To propose a novel, simple yet effective modification to existing RNN units (LSTM and GRU) that explicitly enhances their capability to implicitly learn spatial transformations and integrate observations from different perspectives, thereby improving spatial memorization.
3. **Design an Attention-Based Network Architecture for Long-Range Mapless Navigation:** To integrate the proposed SRUs into an attention-based neural network. This architecture aims to leverage attention mechanisms for efficient feature compression and emphasis of task-relevant spatial cues from raw, forward-facing stereo depth inputs, further bolstering the SRU's ability to maintain a robust spatial-temporal representation for long-range navigation.
4. **Achieve Robust End-to-End Reinforcement Learning:** To successfully train this complex, recurrent, and attention-based architecture using end-to-end reinforcement learning with sparse rewards, employing specific regularization techniques (Deep Mutual Learning and Temporally Consistent Dropout) to prevent premature convergence and ensure robust spatial-temporal memory formation.
5. **Enable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To address the sim-to-real gap, which is crucial for practical deployment. This involves leveraging large-scale synthetic pretraining of the image encoder and incorporating a parallelizable depth-noise model to augment synthetic data, thereby allowing the learned policy to transfer directly to diverse real-world environments without further fine-tuning.

In essence, the motivation is to advance end-to-end learning for mapless navigation by making its implicit memory mechanisms—specifically RNNs—more spatially aware and capable, ultimately leading to more robust, efficient, and deployable robotic navigation policies for complex, long-horizon tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on enhancing the spatial memorization capabilities of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for end-to-end, long-range mapless navigation via reinforcement learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Spatial Mapping Limitations in RNNs (Empirical Analysis)

The authors first demonstrate the inherent limitations of standard RNNs (LSTM, GRU, S4, Mamba-SSM) in spatial mapping. They devise an abstract task where a simulated robot observes landmarks from changing ego-centric perspectives while moving randomly. The RNN is evaluated on two criteria: (i) *temporal memorization* (recalling sequential binary labels associated with landmarks) and (ii) *spatial memorization* (transforming and registering all observed landmark coordinates into the final robot frame). Results (Figure 1 and 2) show that while RNNs excel at temporal tasks, they struggle significantly with accurate spatial transformations and registration, especially for earlier observations, under dynamic ego-motion. This highlights the need for specialized spatial memory.

#### 3.2. Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs)

To address the identified limitation, the paper proposes **Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs)**. These are modifications to standard LSTM and GRU architectures. The core idea is to introduce an additional "spatial transformation term" ($s_t$) that enables the recurrent unit to implicitly learn spatial transformations. This term is computed based on the current input ($x_t$) and then used in a multiplicative form within the gate mechanisms (specifically, the candidate hidden state $g_t$ for LSTM and $\tilde{h}_t$ for GRU). This multiplicative interaction, inspired by homogeneous transformations and recent work on "star operations," allows the network to align and integrate observations from varying perspectives effectively. A refined gating mechanism is also introduced for SRU-Ours to prevent saturation during recurrent training, further enhancing performance. Experiments on the abstract spatial-temporal task validate that SRUs significantly reduce spatial memory errors while maintaining temporal memorization.

#### 3.3. Attention-Based Recurrent Network Architecture for Navigation

The SRU is integrated into an end-to-end attention-based network architecture for mapless navigation (Figure 3):
- **Depth Encoder Pretraining and Simulated Perception Noise:** A convolutional neural network (CNN) backbone (RegNet + FPN) is used as the depth image encoder. This encoder is pretrained for self-reconstruction using a variational autoencoder (VAE) on a large-scale synthetic depth dataset (TartanAir). To bridge the sim-to-real gap, a parallelized depth-noise model (Figure 4, Appendix B) is incorporated during pretraining and RL. This model introduces realistic edge, filling, and rounding noise to synthetic depth images, making the learned features more robust to real-world sensor artifacts.
- **Attention Layers for Feature Compression:** The encoded high-dimensional visual features ($F_t$) are processed by two attention layers. First, a **self-attention** mechanism enriches each spatial feature with global context. Second, a **cross-attention** layer uses the robot's proprioceptive state (linear/angular velocities, projected gravity, previous action) and the current relative goal position as queries to compress the refined feature map into a 1D latent representation ($\hat{F}_t$). This selective attention (visualized in Figure 5) helps focus on task-relevant spatial cues, reducing information density for the recurrent memory.
- **Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Unit (SRU) Integration:** The compressed visual feature ($\hat{F}_t$), concatenated with the proprioceptive state and goal position, is fed into the SRU. The SRU, by leveraging ego-motion information implicitly encoded in the proprioceptive state, processes these inputs to generate a spatial-temporal representation of the environment.
- **MLP Head:** The final hidden state from the SRU is passed through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to output continuous velocity commands (actions) for the robot's locomotion controller.

#### 3.4. Learning Navigation with Sparse Rewards and Regularizations

The policy is trained end-to-end using an Asymmetric Actor-Critic setup with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in NVIDIA IsaacLab simulated environments (Figure 6).
- **Sparse Reward Function:** A time-based reward (adapted from He et al. 2024) is used, providing sparse feedback mainly at the end of an episode or with a small probability during an episode. This encourages robust exploration in complex environments without reliance on dense, potentially misleading intermediate rewards. Regularization and penalty terms (for smooth actions, joint accelerations, collisions, and excessive tilt) are also included.
- **Training Regularization:**
 - **Deep Mutual Learning (DML):** Two policies are trained simultaneously, mutually distilling knowledge from each other via KL-divergence loss. This prevents premature convergence to suboptimal solutions (e.g., relying only on temporal cues) and promotes the development of robust spatial-temporal features, especially critical given the slower learning curve for spatial memorization.
 - **Temporally Consistent Dropout (TC-Dropout):** A consistent dropout mask is applied across all time steps during both rollout and training, ensuring stable memory learning within the recurrent structure and enhancing generalization.
- **Sim-to-Real Considerations:** Noise is deliberately added to the actor's observations (proprioceptive state and depth inputs) during training to improve robustness to real-world sensor imperfections, while the critic receives clean observations for stable value estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs) and the overall attention-based architecture for long-range mapless navigation.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Recurrent Units

- **Improved Training Returns:** Policies equipped with SRU units consistently achieved higher average episode returns during RL training compared to standard LSTM and GRU units (Figure 8).
- **Significantly Higher Success Rates (SR):** SRU-based policies (SRU-GRU, SRU-LSTM, SRU-Ours) showed substantial improvements in navigation success rates across diverse simulated environments (Maze, Random Pillars, Stairs, Pits) compared to standard LSTMs and GRUs (Table 1). SRU-Ours achieved an overall 23.5% increase in SR.
- **Enhanced Spatial Memorization in Challenging Environments:** Notably, in stair-like environments, SRU units more than doubled the success rate compared to baselines, demonstrating their effectiveness in navigating complex 3D structures requiring precise spatial memorization. Qualitatively, SRU policies could successfully navigate mazes without getting trapped and avoid previously seen obstacles (pits) even when out of current sensor view, unlike LSTM policies (Figure 7).

#### 4.2. Comparison against RL-based Navigation Baselines

- **Outperformance over SOTA Baselines:** The SRU-based approach significantly outperformed two state-of-the-art RL baselines: Goal-guided Transformer-based RL (GTRL, which uses stacked historical observations) and RL with Explicit Mapping and Historical Path (EMHP, which uses an external mapping pipeline) (Table 2).
 - A 29.6% relative improvement in SR over EMHP.
 - A 105.0% relative improvement in SR over GTRL.
- **Superior Long-Range Capabilities:** Unlike EMHP, which is limited by a fixed-length explicit memory and struggles over longer distances (Figure 9a, 10), the SRU-based implicit memory maintains a high success rate even beyond training distances and with longer episode times (Figure 9b, 10).
- **Validation of SRU Memory:** Replacing GTRL's stacked observations with SRU memory (GTRL*) yielded a 73.6% relative improvement over original GTRL, underscoring SRU's advantage over heuristic history stacking.

#### 4.3. Importance of Spatial Attention Layers

- **Boost in Performance:** Ablation studies showed that the proposed two-stage spatial attention layers significantly enhanced navigation performance (Table 3, Figure 12). The method achieved a 56.2% relative SR improvement over a no-attention baseline and a 15.4% relative improvement over using a Goal-guided Transformer (GoT) attention mechanism.
- **Dynamic Feature Selection:** Visualizations confirmed that the attention mechanism naturally learned to focus on task-relevant spatial cues (e.g., obstacles, navigable space) based on the robot's state, dynamically shifting focus with changes in robot intent (Figure 5, 11).

#### 4.4. Training with Regularizations

- **Crucial for SRU Effectiveness:** Deep Mutual Learning (DML) significantly improved SRU-Ours performance by 24.3% (from 65.7% to 78.9%), indicating its role in enabling the network to leverage the full potential of SRUs by preventing premature convergence to suboptimal solutions that neglect spatial memorization (Table 4, Figure 13).
- **Enhanced Robustness:** Temporally Consistent Dropout (TC-Dropout) also contributed to maintaining high SR while improving robustness and generalization (Table 5).

#### 4.5. Large-Scale Pretraining for Sim-to-Real Transfer

- **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap:** Pretraining the depth encoder on large-scale synthetic data, augmented with a parallelizable depth-noise model, dramatically reduced the sim-to-real gap.
 - Latent space analysis (PCA) showed that the pretrained encoder's feature distribution encompassed real-world data better than an encoder trained only on RL images (Figure 15).
 - Depth reconstruction quality was significantly better for the pretrained encoder (Figure 14).
 - Mahalanobis distance (MD) between synthetic and real-world latent features was reduced from 1.15 (RL images) to 0.69 (large-scale pretraining with noise), confirming improved generalization (Figure 16).
- **Successful Zero-Shot Real-World Deployment:** The full system (pretrained encoder, attention-based architecture with SRU, trained via RL with regularizations) demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to a Unitree B2W legged-wheel robot in diverse real-world environments (office, terrace, forest). It navigated through dead ends, adapted to dynamic changes (Figure 17), avoided unseen obstacles, and handled various terrains over long distances (up to 100+ meters), showcasing robust generalization (Figure 18).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of end-to-end learning for robot navigation, particularly for complex, long-range mapless tasks. The core contribution, the **Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Unit (SRU)**, directly addresses a fundamental weakness of existing RNNs in implicitly learning spatial transformations and memorization. By designing RNNs that are inherently better at understanding spatial relationships from ego-centric, sequential observations, this work opens new avenues for developing more capable and robust intelligent agents.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Mapless Navigation Capabilities:** The SRU-enhanced policies demonstrate superior performance over conventional RNNs and competitive baselines, especially in challenging environments requiring extensive spatial memory and rerouting. This pushes the boundaries of how effectively robots can navigate unknown or dynamic environments without relying on explicit, computationally heavy mapping pipelines.
2. **Simplified and More Maintainable Systems:** By leveraging implicit spatial memory within an end-to-end recurrent neural network, the proposed approach offers a more streamlined and potentially less error-prone alternative to modular systems that involve separate perception, mapping, and planning modules. This reduces system complexity and overhead, both in training and real-world deployment.
3. **Improved Agility and Responsiveness:** End-to-end architectures can inherently process information with lower latency, which is critical for agile and high-speed robotic platforms like legged-wheel robots. The demonstrated ability to handle dynamic changes and avoid obstacles effectively suggests improved responsiveness in real-world scenarios.
4. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The methodology for bridging the sim-to-real gap, through large-scale pretraining of the vision encoder and a specialized depth-noise model, is highly significant. This enables zero-shot deployment of learned policies on real hardware in diverse and complex environments, greatly accelerating the development and application of autonomous robots.
5. **New Research Directions in Recurrent Architectures:** The identification of spatial memorization as a distinct challenge for RNNs, separate from temporal memorization, and the introduction of SRUs as a solution, could inspire further research into architecturally enhancing recurrent models for tasks involving complex spatial-temporal reasoning beyond navigation. This might extend to areas like 3D reconstruction, object manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
6. **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** The effective use of sparse rewards combined with advanced regularization techniques (Deep Mutual Learning, Temporally Consistent Dropout) highlights crucial strategies for training complex recurrent-attention networks in RL, ensuring robust learning and preventing suboptimal convergence in difficult tasks.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding global-scale navigation and the explainability of implicit memory, its focus on local, long-range mapless navigation and the effective zero-shot transfer represents a substantial step forward. This work lays foundational groundwork for the next generation of truly autonomous robots that can navigate intelligently and robustly in unstructured and dynamic real-world settings.
