# Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Memory for Long-Range Mapless Navigation via End-to-End Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich introduced Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs) within an attention-based deep reinforcement learning framework to overcome the spatial memorization limitations of standard recurrent neural networks in mapless navigation. The system achieved robust long-range navigation on a legged-wheel robot, demonstrating zero-shot transfer to diverse real-world environments and outperforming other RL navigation baselines by up to 105% in success rate.

## Problem

- Standard recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in end-to-end navigation struggle with spatial memorization and transforming ego-centric observations into a coherent world representation.
- This limitation prevents effective long-range mapless navigation as RNNs cannot reliably integrate observations from continuously changing robot perspectives.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for learned navigation policies without fine-tuning on real hardware remains a significant hurdle.

## Method

- Propose Spatially-Enhanced Recurrent Units (SRUs) that modify standard RNNs (LSTM/GRU) by introducing a spatial transformation term to implicitly learn spatial alignment and integration of ego-centric observations.
- Develop an end-to-end attention-based network architecture integrating SRUs, a pretrained depth encoder, and self/==cross-attention== layers for efficient visual feature compression.
- Train the policy using PPO with sparse rewards, leveraging Deep Mutual Learning and Temporally Consistent Dropout to foster robust spatial-temporal memory and prevent premature convergence.

## Results

- Policies utilizing SRUs achieved an overall **23.5%** higher success rate in simulated long-range mapless navigation tasks compared to standard recurrent units (LSTM, GRU).
- The SRU-enhanced system significantly outperformed state-of-the-art RL navigation baselines, showing a **29.6%** relative improvement over EMHP and a **105.0%** relative improvement over GTRL in success rate.
- The developed policy successfully transferred zero-shot to a real-world Unitree B2W legged-wheel robot, navigating over 100+ meters through diverse, unstructured environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard RNNs (LSTM, GRU, S4, Mamba-SSM) excel at temporal memorization but exhibit significant limitations in accurately transforming and registering spatial information from dynamic ego-centric viewpoints.
> - Modifying recurrent units with a dedicated "spatial transformation term" effectively addresses this spatial memorization deficit, enabling more coherent environmental representation from sequential observations.
> - Robust sim-to-real transfer for end-to-end learned navigation policies is achievable through extensive synthetic pretraining of vision encoders and the integration of realistic depth-noise models.
