# ForceVLA: Enhancing VLA Models with a Force-aware MoE for Contact-rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ForceVLA enhances Vision-Language-Action models for contact-rich robotic manipulation by integrating 6-axis force feedback through a Force-aware Mixture-of-Experts (FVLMoE) module. This approach improves average task success rates by 23.2% and demonstrates superior generalization across diverse physical interaction scenarios, including under visual occlusion.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with contact-rich manipulation tasks due to their reliance on visual information, leading to brittle behavior.
- Current VLA models often fail under visual occlusion or dynamic uncertainty, lacking fine-grained physical control and the ability to adapt to subtle interaction forces.
- There is a scarcity of comprehensive datasets that effectively capture synchronized vision, proprioception, and high-fidelity force-torque data for diverse contact-rich tasks.

## Method

- **ForceVLA**, an end-to-end multimodal robotic policy, integrates ==6-axis force feedback== into an established VLA framework by treating force sensing as a "first-class modality."
- A novel ==Force-aware Mixture-of-Experts (FVLMoE)== module dynamically fuses real-time ==force-torque readings== with ==visual-language embeddings==, enabling ==phase-aware action generation== and ==context-aware routing==.
- The research includes the creation of ==ForceVLA-Data==, a new dataset of 244 trajectories capturing synchronized vision, proprioception, and force-torque signals for five challenging contact-rich tasks.

## Results

- **ForceVLA** achieved an average task success rate of **60.5%** across five challenging contact-rich tasks, representing a **23.2%** improvement over the strong π₀-base model with force feedback (**37.3%**).
- The model demonstrated superior generalization, achieving **90%** success under partial visual occlusion and maintaining compliant control to achieve **20%** success in highly unstable socket insertion scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed the FVLMoE's late-fusion strategy and dynamic routing are essential, with the full ForceVLA achieving **80%** success in a key task, while simpler force integration methods reached **60%** and early fusion methods performed significantly worse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating force feedback strategically after the initial Vision-Language Model (VLM) encoding is crucial to preserve powerful pretrained VLM representations and significantly improve performance.
> - The FVLMoE's dynamic gating network enables expert specialization and phase-aware action generation, allowing the robot to adapt to subtle contact dynamics for precise, context-sensitive control.
> - Sophisticated multimodal fusion, such as the FVLMoE, yields substantial performance gains over simpler force integration methods like concatenation, demonstrating the importance of dynamic interaction between modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: robotic manipulation, embodied AI, and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Recent advancements in large-scale manipulation datasets and foundation models have significantly pushed the boundaries of general-purpose robotic control, enabling greater adaptability and generalization to novel tasks. VLMs, in particular, have shown remarkable capabilities in multimodal alignment, learning transferable representations from vast amounts of visual and linguistic data.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** The paper acknowledges that "OpenVLA" and "π₀" represent state-of-the-art VLA models. These models leverage VLM-based encoders to bridge perception and control, demonstrating strong performance in semantic grounding, language following, and zero-shot generalization. They primarily rely on visual and linguistic inputs to generate actions, often using flow-based or diffusion-based architectures for high-frequency action outputs and diverse behaviors.

**The Identified Gap: Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Despite their successes, existing VLA models, including the strong π₀ baseline, are highlighted as struggling with *contact-rich manipulation tasks*. These tasks, such as insertion, tool use, or assembly, demand fine-grained physical control and are fundamentally driven by interaction forces. Current VLA models, heavily reliant on vision, often fail under visual occlusion or dynamic uncertainty, leading to brittle and unreliable behavior. The human analogy of naturally integrating tactile and proprioceptive feedback underscores this limitation. Furthermore, the paper notes that force requirements are dynamic and phase-dependent (e.g., delicate grasping vs. controlled insertion), a subtlety that current methods often miss.

**ForceVLA's Contribution to the Landscape:** ForceVLA directly addresses this critical gap by integrating *force sensing* as a "first-class modality" into the VLA framework. While some prior works have integrated force or tactile sensing, they often involve static modality fusion or lack unified modeling frameworks capable of cross-task generalization in real-world contact-rich settings. ForceVLA distinguishes itself by:
- **Dynamic Force-aware Fusion:** Introducing a novel **FVLMoE (Force-aware Mixture-of-Experts)** module that dynamically integrates real-time 6-axis force feedback with visual-language embeddings. This is a significant departure from simpler concatenation-based approaches.
- **Phase-Aware Action Generation:** The MoE architecture allows for context-aware routing, enabling the robot to adapt to subtle contact dynamics and generate "phase-aligned, and force-aware action chunking."
- **Addressing MoE Limitations:** While MoE architectures have been used in multimodal learning and robotics for scalability and adaptability, they often overlook explicit force/tactile modeling and dynamic routing for contact tasks. ForceVLA specifically tailors MoE for this purpose.
- **Data Contribution:** The creation of **ForceVLA-Data**, a new dataset of synchronized vision, proprioception, and force-torque signals for contact-rich tasks, fills another critical void in the research landscape. Existing datasets often lack comprehensive force interactions.

In essence, ForceVLA pushes the frontier of VLA models towards *physical intelligence*, enabling robots to move beyond purely visual-semantic understanding to incorporate a deeper, reactive understanding of physical interaction forces. This moves robotics closer to human-like dexterity in complex, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks.** The authors identify a significant limitation in existing VLA models: their struggle with scenarios that demand precise physical interaction and fine-grained control involving external forces, especially when visual information is ambiguous or occluded.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Bridging the Gap between High-Level and Low-Level Modalities:** Current VLA models excel at leveraging high-level visual and linguistic representations for general-purpose manipulation. However, they largely neglect the crucial role of low-level physical sensing, particularly force feedback, which is essential for successful interaction with the physical world. The objective is to formally integrate this missing modality.
2. **Addressing Brittleness in Contact-Rich Tasks:** Tasks like insertion, tool use, and assembly inherently involve dynamic physical contact. Existing VLA models, relying predominantly on visual cues, are often brittle and fail in these contact-intensive situations, lacking the ability to adapt to subtle force changes or unexpected physical disturbances.
3. **Mimicking Human Dexterity:** Humans naturally integrate multimodal feedback—vision, language, and crucially, tactile and proprioceptive senses—to execute complex manipulation tasks. The goal is to imbue robots with a similar ability to perceive and adapt to physical interactions, moving beyond purely visual reasoning.
4. **Enabling Phase-Aware Action Generation:** The authors recognize that force requirements change dynamically throughout a task (e.g., approach, contact, insertion, release). Existing models lack mechanisms to perceive and adapt to these phase-dependent variations. A key objective is to enable the robot to generate "phase-aligned, and force-aware action chunking."
5. **Creating a Comprehensive Dataset:** A practical challenge in developing force-aware policies is the scarcity of datasets that comprehensively capture synchronized vision, proprioception, and high-fidelity force-torque data in diverse contact-rich scenarios. A vital objective is to contribute such a dataset to the community.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose ForceVLA, an end-to-end manipulation framework that treats external force sensing as a "first-class modality" and introduces a novel force-aware Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) fusion module, FVLMoE, to dynamically integrate force feedback during action decoding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ForceVLA is an end-to-end multimodal robotic policy built upon the state-of-the-art π₀ framework, which utilizes a conditional flow matching model for action generation. The core innovation lies in its sophisticated approach to integrating 6-axis force feedback into this established VLA architecture.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture (ForceVLA Pipeline)

The ForceVLA pipeline processes multiple input modalities:
- **Visual Inputs:** From multiple RGB cameras (base and wrist-mounted).
- **Language Instructions:** Textual descriptions of the task.
- **Proprioceptive State:** Robot's end-effector pose (TCP position and Euler orientation) and gripper width.
- **External Force-Torque Readings:** 6-axis force and moment at the end-effector.

These inputs are fed into a process that ultimately predicts an action trajectory (low-level executable action chunk) using an iterative denoising process. The visual and language inputs are initially processed by a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) based on SigLIP (specifically, PaliGemma) to create contextual embeddings.

#### 3.2. FVLMoE Architecture (Core Innovation)

The FVLMoE module is the central component responsible for integrating force information. Its design reflects careful consideration of *where* and *how* to best introduce the novel force modality without disrupting the powerful pretrained VLM representations.

- **Input Mapping for Multiple Modalities:**
 - **Strategic Force Integration:** Force features are *not* introduced prior to or concurrently with the VLM's initial fusion of visual and language modalities. Instead, raw 6-axis force-torque sensor data (`f_raw`) is transformed by a linear projection (`ϕ_F`) into a dedicated force token embedding (`E_F`) *after* the primary VLM has processed visual-linguistic inputs. This "late fusion" strategy is crucial, as validated by ablation studies.
 - **Concatenated Input:** The VLM's visual-linguistic features (`E_VL`) are concatenated with the force token (`E_F`) to form the sequential input `E_in` for the FVLMoE.

- **Multimodal Routing and Fusion Computation:**
 - **Shared Refinement:** `E_in` first passes through an encoder layer (multi-head self-attention and Feed-Forward Network, FFN). This shared processing allows for initial holistic interaction and contextual refinement among all force, visual, and language tokens.
 - **Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Layer:** The refined embeddings (`E_enc`) are then channeled into a sparse Mixture-of-Experts layer. This layer comprises four distinct expert networks, each implemented as an independent Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). A dynamic gating network (router) determines which single expert (top `k=1`) is most appropriate for each token in `E_enc`.
 - **Weighted Aggregation:** The output from the selected expert(s) is aggregated, commonly via a weighted sum, to produce the fused feature `E_fused`. A residual connection integrates this back with the input to the MoE layer.
 - **Output Projection:** A final linear projection layer matches the dimensionality of the action expert.

- **Injecting Fused Features into the Action Flow Head:**
 - The rich, fused multimodal features from FVLMoE (`E_FVLMoE`) serve as a critical guidance signal for the action generation process.
 - A specific sub-sequence `G_FVLMoE` (representing pertinent guidance for the `H_action`-length action plan) is extracted from `E_FVLMoE`.
 - This `G_FVLMoE` is combined via element-wise addition with features derived from the robot's current proprioceptive state and the noisy action trajectory being refined by the flow-based denoising model. This ensures that the FVLMoE's contact-aware understanding directly modulates and refines the predicted action sequence at each step.

#### 3.3. Datasets (ForceVLA-Data)

Recognizing the scarcity of suitable datasets, the authors collected a new dataset specifically for contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- **Hardware Setup:** A Flexiv Rizon 7-DOF robotic arm with a Dahuan adaptive gripper. Visual data was captured by two RGB-D cameras (static third-person and wrist-mounted egocentric).
- **Data Collection Method:** Human teleoperation using a Quest3 VR interface, ensuring diverse and successful interaction patterns. Object positions and orientations were varied to enhance diversity.
- **Tasks:** Five challenging contact-rich tasks: bottle pumping, plug insertion, USB drive insertion, whiteboard wiping, and cucumber peeling.
- **Scale:** 244 trajectories, totaling 140,000 synchronized timesteps of vision, proprioception, and force-torque signals.
- **Availability:** The dataset, collection code, and processing scripts are planned for public release.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a novel architectural component (FVLMoE) with a custom-curated dataset, allows ForceVLA to robustly address the challenge of contact-rich manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of ForceVLA in real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks demonstrates its significant improvements over baseline models.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Superior Average Success Rate:** ForceVLA achieved an average task success rate of **60.5%** across five challenging contact-rich tasks (Bottle Pumping, Plug Insertion, USB Drive Insertion, Whiteboard Wiping, Cucumber Peeling).
- **Significant Improvement over Baselines:** This represents a **23.2% improvement** over the strong π₀-base model with force feedback (π₀-base w/ F), which achieved 37.3%. The standard π₀-base model without force (π₀-base w/o F) achieved a 37.3% average success rate.
- **High Individual Task Success:** ForceVLA achieved up to **80% success** in tasks like plug insertion, and 100% in "Wipe Board-2" (completely erasing markings).
- **Cucumber Peeling Metrics:** ForceVLA outperformed baselines on fine-grained metrics for cucumber peeling, achieving an average peel length of **14.12 cm** (compared to 13.17 cm for π₀-base w/ F and 10.27 cm for π₀-base w/o F) and requiring a minimum of **7 strokes** to clean (compared to 10 and 14 strokes for baselines), indicating superior precision and efficiency.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Force Fusion via FVLMoE

- The results clearly show that simply adding raw force signals to the π₀-base model provides only a modest performance boost (from 37.3% to 40.2%).
- In contrast, ForceVLA's highly specialized FVLMoE module, designed for dynamic and context-aware force integration, led to a substantial jump to 60.5% success. This indicates that the *method of integration* is paramount, going beyond merely the *presence* of force data.

#### 4.3. Model Generalization

ForceVLA demonstrated superior generalization capabilities across five diverse experimental settings, which introduced increasing task variability and physical uncertainty:
- **Object Generalization (Object Gen. 1 & 2):** ForceVLA maintained high success rates (e.g., 80.00% in Object Gen. 1 for bottle pumping), showing robustness to variations in object geometry.
- **Height Generalization (Height Gen.):** ForceVLA effectively adapted interaction forces to variable depths (88.89% success), avoiding torque limit violations observed in other models.
- **Visual Occlusion:** Crucially, ForceVLA achieved **90.00% success** under partial visual occlusion, a scenario where vision-reliant models typically fail. This highlights its effective reliance on multimodal feedback (force) when visual cues are degraded.
- **Unstable Socket:** ForceVLA maintained compliant control and adjusted the plug's pose dynamically, achieving 20.00% success in this highly challenging scenario (compared to 10.00% for most baselines), indicating adaptability to dynamic physical conditions.
- **Overall Generalization Average:** ForceVLA achieved an average of **63.78%** across these generalization settings, significantly outperforming all baselines.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the architectural design choices for FVLMoE:
- **Late Fusion is Key:** Early fusion methods, where force data was injected prior to the VLM (e.g., "linear before VLM" or "MoE before VLM"), significantly degraded performance, with "MoE before VLM" resulting in a 0% success rate. This validates the design decision to introduce force features *after* the VLM's core encoding to preserve its pretrained representations.
- **FVLMoE's Sophistication:** Simple concatenation of force features after the VLM ("concatenate after VLM") achieved 60% success, confirming the utility of force. However, ForceVLA's FVLMoE achieved a markedly higher **80% success**, underscoring the benefit of its specialized routing and deeper multimodal interaction.

#### 4.5. Router Analysis (Appendix C) and Multi-task Evaluation (Appendix D)

- **Dynamic Expert Utilization:** Analysis of the MoE router (Figure 9) revealed distinct expert utilization patterns across tasks and phases, suggesting that the router learns to dynamically allocate computation based on task semantics and temporal structure. Some experts showed specialization for specific task phases, while one expert (Expert 0) appeared to function as a general-purpose expert.
- **Multi-task Learning:** ForceVLA demonstrated superior multi-task learning capabilities, achieving the highest average success rate of **67.5%** when trained jointly on multiple tasks. Baselines largely failed in this setting, highlighting ForceVLA's capacity for concurrent skill acquisition and robust instruction following.

#### 4.6. Visualization and Case Studies

Visualizations (Figure 7 and Appendix E) provided qualitative evidence of ForceVLA's adaptive behavior. It showed the robot re-orienting, re-grasping, or maintaining compliant control in response to contact feedback, correcting errors and achieving success in scenarios where baselines failed due to rigid, non-adaptive motions or excessive force. This confirms that FVLMoE enables deep fusion for precise, context-aware control.

In summary, the findings robustly support that ForceVLA, through its innovative FVLMoE module and late-stage force integration, effectively transforms VLA models to achieve significantly higher precision, stability, and generalization in contact-rich manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ForceVLA framework represents a substantial step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring physical intelligence and dexterity. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancing General-Purpose Robotic Manipulation:** By formally integrating force sensing as a first-class modality, ForceVLA addresses a critical limitation of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It enables robots to move beyond purely visual-semantic understanding to a deeper, more reactive comprehension of physical interactions. This is crucial for expanding the range of tasks generalist robots can reliably perform in unstructured environments.
2. **Enhancing Robustness and Adaptability:** The ability of ForceVLA to achieve high success rates under challenging conditions such as visual occlusion, object variations, and environmental instability is highly significant. This increased robustness allows robots to operate more reliably in real-world scenarios, where perfect visual conditions are rare and dynamic uncertainties are common. Its capacity for fine-grained force control makes it less brittle and more adaptive.
3. **Novel Multimodal Fusion Architecture (FVLMoE):** The introduction of the Force-aware Mixture-of-Experts (FVLMoE) module is a key methodological contribution. It provides an effective and principled way to dynamically fuse high-level visual and linguistic information with low-level, real-time force feedback. This dynamic, phase-aware expert routing mechanism is a blueprint for integrating heterogeneous sensor modalities in a context-sensitive manner, potentially influencing future multimodal AI architectures beyond robotics.
4. **Enabling True Physical Interaction and Dexterity:** The superior performance in tasks like plug insertion and cucumber peeling, which demand precise alignment, compliant motion, and sustained contact force, demonstrates ForceVLA's ability to imbue robots with a greater degree of physical dexterity. This moves robotics closer to mimicking human-like manipulative capabilities, which are inherently multimodal and force-sensitive.
5. **Addressing Data Scarcity with a Dedicated Dataset:** The creation and planned open-sourcing of the ForceVLA-Data dataset is a valuable contribution to the research community. By providing synchronized vision, proprioception, and force-torque signals across diverse contact-rich tasks, it facilitates further research and development in learning force-aware manipulation policies, potentially accelerating progress in this underexplored area.
6. **Paving the Way for Industrial and Service Robotics:** The capabilities demonstrated by ForceVLA have direct implications for industrial automation (e.g., precision assembly, quality inspection under contact) and service robotics (e.g., household chores, assisting in medical procedures). Tasks that were previously difficult or impossible for robots due to their lack of force intelligence can now become feasible, leading to greater autonomy and efficiency.
7. **Future Research Directions:** While acknowledging limitations such as reliance on estimated wrench values and the use of high-cost sensors, the paper explicitly outlines pathways for future research, including exploring higher-fidelity sensing and adapting to lower-cost platforms. This forward-looking perspective highlights the foundational nature of ForceVLA's contributions and its potential to spark further innovation in force-aware manipulation.

In conclusion, ForceVLA represents a significant advancement in robotic learning, integrating sophisticated AI models with fundamental physical interaction capabilities. It sets a new benchmark for physically intelligent robotic control, laying the groundwork for more dexterous, robust, and adaptive robotic systems capable of operating effectively in complex, contact-rich environments.
