# Learning 3D Persistent Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce a persistent embodied world model that integrates an explicit 3D memory into a video diffusion model for action-guided RGB-D video generation. This approach significantly improves spatial consistency in generated long-horizon trajectories and enhances performance in downstream tasks like model predictive control and policy learning in novel environments.

## Problem

- Existing video-based world models are "myopic," lacking a persistent memory of unseen parts of the environment, leading to inconsistencies when revisiting previously observed areas.
- The absence of long-term spatial consistency prevents embodied agents from making consistent, long-horizon plans in complex, partially observed 3D environments.
- Relying solely on 2D image representations for memory neglects critical 3D spatial priors, making it difficult to accurately capture and regenerate scene geometry and object permanence.

## Method

- Developed a "Persistent Embodied World Model" that integrates an explicit 3D memory, called DINO-Map, into an action-guided RGB-D video diffusion model (based on CogVideoX).
- Employs Plücker embeddings to represent agent actions as precise pixel-wise controls for the video diffusion model, ensuring accurate conditioning on camera pose transformations.
- Constructs the 3D DINO-Map by unprojecting DINO-v2 features from RGB-D observations into a volumetric grid and injects these features into the video diffusion model via dedicated cross-attention expert blocks.

## Results

- Achieved an FVD score of 91.885, outperforming the Navigation World Model (194.040) and Image Memory (124.038), while attaining the highest Scene Revisit Consistency (SRC) at 81.7%.
- Demonstrated improved performance in Model Predictive Control and policy learning in new environments, with the model achieving a SIM of 70.8% for trajectory ranking compared to NWM's 68.7%.
- Successfully synthesized long video trajectories (up to 112 frames) while preserving 3D consistency and showed robustness in generalizing to novel, unseen scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a dynamically updated 3D feature map directly into a video diffusion model effectively maintains long-term spatial consistency in ego-centric video generation.
> - Representing actions via Plücker embeddings provides a robust mechanism for fine-grained, pixel-level control, crucial for accurate action-guided video synthesis.
> - A two-stage training approach, fine-tuning the video backbone first then training memory-specific blocks, efficiently integrates 3D memory without degrading the generative model's base capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Learning 3D Persistent Embodied World Models" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: embodied AI, generative models (specifically video diffusion models), 3D scene understanding, and robotic planning.

**Embodied AI and World Models:** A core aspiration of embodied AI is to create intelligent agents that can perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world. A crucial component for such agents is a "world model," which enables them to simulate the effects of their actions and plan future behaviors. Existing research has explored world models based on video generation, allowing agents to anticipate visual observations. However, as the paper highlights, a major limitation of these models is their "myopic" nature. They typically operate on observed image chunks, lacking a persistent memory of unseen parts of the environment. This leads to inconsistencies when an agent revisits a previously observed area or needs to plan over long horizons in partially observed environments. The proposed work directly addresses this foundational challenge in embodied AI.

**Video Generation and Diffusion Models:** The recent explosion in the capabilities of large video generation models, often powered by diffusion architectures, has broadened the horizons of computer vision. These models can synthesize highly realistic and diverse video content. This paper leverages these advancements, specifically using a video diffusion model (CogVideoX) as its backbone, to predict future observations. However, it goes beyond mere video synthesis by integrating it with embodied action and 3D memory, transforming a powerful generative tool into a functional component of an intelligent agent.

**3D Scene Representation and Memory:** While video models excel at temporal consistency within a short window, maintaining spatial consistency across long, ego-centric trajectories (where an agent moves through a 3D environment) remains a significant challenge. Previous attempts have included conditioning on immediate predecessors, episodic memory in 2D latent spaces (like LoRA modules), or static 3D maps (like HD maps in autonomous driving). This paper distinguishes itself by explicitly incorporating a *dynamic and persistent 3D memory* into the world model. This approach recognizes that real-world scenes unfold in three dimensions and that a true "understanding" requires a 3D representation that evolves with the agent's experience. By grounding its generation in a 3D feature map, the model moves beyond 2D image-based reasoning to a more geometrically informed understanding of the environment.

In summary, this research advances the state of the art by tackling a critical bottleneck in embodied world models: the lack of persistent, 3D-aware memory. It bridges the gap between high-fidelity video generation and consistent long-horizon environmental simulation, paving the way for more robust and capable embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a **persistent embodied world model** that can faithfully simulate the effects of future actions on the world, even in complex, partially observed 3D environments, by maintaining long-term spatial consistency.

The primary motivations stemming from the limitations of existing approaches are:

1. **Overcoming Myopia in World Models:** Traditional video-based world models are often "myopic," meaning they only remember what is currently visible or was visible in the immediate past. This leads to an inability to maintain consistency over longer horizons or when parts of a scene move out of view and are later revisited. For example, a painting on a wall might disappear or be replaced by a different object once the agent turns away, as illustrated in Figure 1, undermining the model's utility for realistic simulation.
2. **Enabling Consistent Long-Horizon Planning:** The lack of persistent memory prevents embodied agents from making consistent, long-horizon plans. If the world model cannot reliably track the state of unseen parts of the environment, any long-term plan based on its simulations will inevitably be flawed due to contradictions and inconsistencies. This limits the application of world models in tasks like navigation, manipulation, and exploration where stable environmental representations are crucial.
3. **Integrating 3D Spatial Understanding:** Real-world environments are inherently three-dimensional. Relying solely on 2D image representations for memory neglects critical 3D spatial priors, making it difficult to accurately capture and regenerate scene geometry and object permanence. The motivation is to explicitly integrate 3D understanding into the world model to ensure geometric and semantic coherence.
4. **Enhancing Downstream Embodied Applications:** The ultimate goal of an improved world model is to benefit downstream embodied AI tasks. By providing a more consistent and reliable simulation of environment dynamics, the proposed model aims to significantly enhance capabilities in areas like model predictive control (planning), policy learning, and data generation for training agents, especially in novel or unseen environments.

In essence, the authors are motivated to build a world model that not only predicts future observations but also "remembers" the environment in a spatially coherent 3D manner, thereby enabling more intelligent and robust embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, named **Persistent Embodied World Model**, lies in integrating an explicit 3D memory mechanism into a video diffusion model for action-guided RGB-D video generation.

#### 3.1. Formulation: World Models with Memory and Action-Driven Generation

The model is formulated as an action-guided video generation system that takes current RGB-D observations ($O_t$), agent actions ($A_t$), and a 3D feature map memory ($M$) as input. It then predicts future observations ($O_{t+1}, \ldots, O_{t+H}$). The memory $M$ is incrementally updated with observed and generated content, crucial for consistency.

- **Action Representation:** Agent actions ($A_t$), such as navigation commands, are converted into relative camera pose transformations. To ensure precise pixel-wise control in the video model, these camera poses are represented using **Plücker embeddings**, which encode the ray origin and direction for each pixel as an image. This avoids direct conditioning on raw camera poses, which is less effective.
- **Action-Driven Video Generation Backbone:** The authors utilize **CogVideoX** as their backbone video diffusion model due to its pre-trained generation capabilities. They modify it to accept the Plücker camera embeddings by concatenating them with the video latent space channels, rather than using cross-attention, which they found to be more effective for precise pixel-wise control.

#### 3.2. Video Generation with 3D Map (Memory Mechanism)

The most novel aspect is the introduction and integration of a 3D memory.

- **3D Memory Construction (DINO-Map):** A volumetric memory representation is created by populating 3D grids with **DINO-v2** features.
 1. **Feature Extraction:** DINO-v2, a pre-trained image encoder, extracts robust 2D image features from each RGB observation.
 2. **Feature Unprojection:** Using the associated depth information, intrinsic, and extrinsic camera matrices, these 2D features are lifted and unprojected into a 3D grid in world coordinates.
 3. **Aggregation:** Features in the 3D grid are aggregated (e.g., via max-pooling) to form the **DINO-Map**. To preserve 3D spatial relationships, each grid is concatenated with 3D sinusoidal absolute position embeddings.
- **Depth Information Injection:** To enhance 3D spatial understanding, the model processes and generates **RGB-D videos** instead of just RGB. RGB and depth information are separately encoded by a 3D VAE and then concatenated. The input and output layers of the video model are extended to handle these additional depth channels.
- **Injecting DINO-Map into Video Diffusion:** A **cross-attention expert block (memory block)** is designed to inject the DINO-Map features into the video diffusion model. These memory blocks are inserted after each original transformer block in the CogVideoX architecture. The video hidden states are concatenated with camera embeddings before being passed to the memory block, allowing the model to correlate hidden states with specific 3D feature grids. Expert adaptive layernorms are used to align features from different spaces.

#### 3.3. Training and Application

- **Two-Stage Training:**
 1. **Stage 1 (Backbone Fine-tuning):** CogVideoX is fine-tuned on the dataset without the memory block. This adapts the pre-trained model to the specific domain, integrates action-conditioned controls, and enables RGB-D video generation.
 2. **Stage 2 (Memory Block Training):** Only the newly introduced memory blocks are trained, while the rest of the model parameters are frozen. This teaches the model to effectively leverage the 3D feature map information for consistent generation without degrading the original model's capabilities.
- **Applications in Embodied Domains:**
 - **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** The model serves as a dynamics model to simulate future observations for a sequence of sampled actions. A reward function is used to rank these generated trajectories, and the best action sequence is executed. This enables more precise action selection in partially observable environments.
 - **Policy Learning in New Environments:** The model can act as a grounded simulator. Given a few-shot images of a new environment, a DINO-Map is initialized. The model then simulates interaction trajectories, generating future observations based on policy actions and updating the map. This synthetic data can then be used to train or fine-tune robotic policies, even leveraging techniques like hindsight relabeling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates that the Persistent Embodied World Model significantly enhances both the visual quality and consistency of video generation, with direct benefits for downstream embodied AI applications.

#### 4.1. Video Generation Quality and Consistency

The model was evaluated against several baselines, including Navigation World Model (NWM) and methods with 2D memory representations, on a dataset collected in Habitat Simulation with HM3D scenes.

- **Quantitative Metrics:**
 - **Visual Quality (FVD, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DreamSim):** The proposed model (Ours) achieves a remarkably low FVD score of 91.885, significantly outperforming NWM (194.040) and Image Memory (124.038). It also shows superior PSNR (22.458), SSIM (0.759), and lower LPIPS (0.157) and DreamSim (0.086) scores, indicating higher visual fidelity and perceptual similarity to ground truth.
 - **Consistency (Scene Revisit Consistency - SRC):** The model achieved the highest SRC score of 81.7%, showcasing its ability to maintain consistent scene content when revisiting previously observed locations, far exceeding NWM (63.4%) and other memory-based baselines.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Ours (w/o depth):** Removing depth information led to a drop in all metrics (e.g., FVD to 114.421), highlighting the importance of processing RGB-D data for 3D-aware consistency.
 - **Ours (w/ 2D-Map):** Using a 2D feature map instead of a 3D volumetric map also resulted in degraded performance (e.g., FVD to 98.152), confirming the benefit of explicit 3D spatial representation in the memory.
- **Qualitative Results:** Figures 1, 3, and 4 visually demonstrate that the proposed model generates videos that accurately preserve previously observed 3D structures and content, unlike NWM which introduces conflicting elements or changes scene layouts.

#### 4.2. Planning with World Models

The persistent world model showed benefits in robotic navigation policies.

- **Ranking Trajectories:** When used to rank action trajectories sampled from a state-of-the-art diffusion policy (NoMaD), the proposed model consistently outperformed NoMaD alone and NWM across metrics like Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE), Relative Pose Error (RPE), and Similarity (SIM) of final poses. For instance, with N=16 trajectories, "Ours (×16)" achieved a SIM of 70.8%, better than "NWM (×16)" at 68.7%.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** Applied with MPC using discrete actions, the model achieved competitive results, reaching a SIM of 87.5% after 720 iterations, demonstrating its utility in online planning.
- **Policy Learning in New Environments:** The model was shown to boost policy learning in unseen environments. By leveraging a few-shot images to initialize the 3D memory, the model significantly enhanced policy learning performance compared to NWM, indicating its ability to generalize and support adaptation to novel scenes (Figure 6b).

#### 4.3. Additional Video Generation Results

- **Long Video Generation:** The model successfully synthesized very long video trajectories (e.g., 112 frames) while maintaining 3D consistency by incrementally updating its memory (Figure 5).
- **Simulation in Unseen Scenes:** The framework demonstrated robustness in generalizing to novel, unseen scenes, preserving object permanence and scene consistency despite a modest decline in visual fidelity compared to the training domain (Figure 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Learning 3D Persistent Embodied World Model" paper presents a significant advancement in embodied AI and generative modeling, with substantial potential impact across several domains:

1. **Enhanced Embodied AI Capabilities:** By resolving the "myopia" problem in existing world models, this work enables embodied agents to possess a much more consistent and complete understanding of their environment. This is crucial for agents operating in complex, dynamic, and partially observable real-world settings, moving them closer to human-like spatial reasoning and memory.
2. **Robust Long-Horizon Planning:** The ability to simulate future states with persistent 3D memory greatly improves an agent's capacity for long-horizon planning. Robots can now make decisions based on anticipated interactions that are geometrically and semantically coherent over extended periods, leading to more reliable navigation, manipulation, and exploration strategies. This directly addresses a critical bottleneck for deploying autonomous systems in complex environments.
3. **Improved Policy Learning and Data Generation:** The model's utility as a grounded simulator for policy learning in new environments is highly impactful. It allows agents to generate synthetic interaction data, adapt to novel scenes from minimal observations, and iteratively improve their policies without needing extensive real-world interaction, which is often costly and time-consuming. This could accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots.
4. **Advancement in Generative Models for 3D Consistency:** The integration of 3D memory into video diffusion models represents a novel approach to achieving temporal and spatial consistency in generated content. This goes beyond mere aesthetic realism, embedding functional geometric understanding into the generative process. This could inspire new architectures and training paradigms for future video generation models that prioritize structural coherence.
5. **New Research Directions:** The paper's identified limitations also point towards exciting future work. Explicitly modeling dynamic environments (e.g., moving objects, other agents) within the 3D map is a clear next step. Overcoming the reliance on depth data, perhaps through learned depth estimation, would also expand applicability to a wider range of datasets. These directions underscore the foundational nature of this work, opening new avenues for research in cognitive AI, robotics, and computer vision.

In conclusion, this research delivers a critical component for building more intelligent and adaptable embodied agents. By grounding simulations in a persistent, 3D-aware memory, it pushes the boundaries of how AI systems can perceive, plan, and interact with the physical world, paving the way for more sophisticated robotic and autonomous systems.
