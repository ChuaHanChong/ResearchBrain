# Anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted, Prediction-Powered Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed an anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) framework, extending PPI to sequential data analysis. This method provides confidence sequences that maintain statistical validity uniformly over time, leveraging machine learning predictions and prior knowledge to achieve enhanced statistical efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) methods provide confidence intervals with fixed-time validity, limiting their use in continuous monitoring or sequential decision-making.
- Many practical applications require statistical conclusions to remain valid at any point in time as new data arrives, necessitating 'anytime-valid' inference.
- Traditional PPI methods do not explicitly leverage prior knowledge about the quality or expected bias of machine learning predictions to further improve inference efficiency.

## Method

- The authors extended the fixed-time ==Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI)== framework to a sequential setting by employing ==asymptotic confidence sequences (AsympCS)== for the core PPI estimators.
- They integrated ==Bayes-assisted== principles into the AsympCS construction for the ==rectifier== component, allowing prior beliefs about prediction bias to refine interval widths without invalidating coverage.
- A ==Strassen-type strong coupling== was established for ==control-variate PPI estimators==, enabling the application of AsympCS theory and combining component confidence sequences via ==Minkowski sums==.

## Results

- In synthetic experiments, Bayes-assisted PPI++ consistently reduced confidence interval volume when predictions were unbiased, while all methods maintained the cumulative miscoverage rate below the nominal α level.
- Bayes-assisted methods with heavier-tailed priors demonstrated greater robustness to increasing prediction bias, showing a slower increase in interval volumes compared to Gaussian priors.
- Across diverse real-world datasets, the proposed anytime-valid PPI methods yielded smaller confidence regions than classical inference, with Bayes-assisted variants providing additional efficiency gains while preserving the cumulative miscoverage rate below α.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymptotic confidence sequences can be successfully adapted to Prediction-Powered Inference estimators, providing time-uniform statistical validity for sequential data analysis.
> - Incorporating Bayes-assisted components into confidence sequences can improve statistical efficiency (narrower intervals) when prior beliefs about prediction bias are accurate, without sacrificing frequentist validity.
> - Heavier-tailed prior distributions offer greater robustness to misspecification of prior beliefs about prediction bias, making Bayes-assisted methods more resilient to inaccurate prior assumptions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper builds upon the recently introduced framework of Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI). PPI is designed to enhance the statistical efficiency of confidence interval procedures by incorporating machine learning predictions from a black-box model, particularly in settings with a limited amount of labelled data and an abundance of unlabelled data. Existing PPI methods, as well as related approaches in semi-supervised inference, missing-data methods, survey sampling, and double machine learning, generally guarantee statistical validity only at a fixed, pre-determined sample size.

This research extends the PPI framework into the *sequential* setting, where data (both labelled and unlabelled) accumulates over time. This aligns with a growing interest in "anytime-valid" inference, a concept rooted in the theory of confidence sequences (CS) pioneered by Ville, Wald, Darling, and Robbins. Recent renewed interest in CS has been driven by applications in online learning, A/B testing, and real-time monitoring. The paper specifically leverages *asymptotic* confidence sequences (AsympCS), which approximate exact confidence sequences and extend standard Central Limit Theorem (CLT)-based confidence intervals to a time-uniform setting.

Furthermore, the work integrates "Bayes-assisted" principles, similar to a fixed-time Bayes-assisted PPI variant previously proposed by Cortinovis and Caron (2025). This aspect aims to further improve efficiency by accommodating prior knowledge about the quality of the machine learning predictions, specifically regarding the bias of the predictor. While other time-uniform PPI methods exist, such as prediction-powered e-values, this work distinguishes itself by applying to more general observational setups (not restricted to active learning), not requiring bounded outcomes, and explicitly incorporating prior knowledge to influence efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for valid statistical inference that combines the benefits of Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) with the robustness of *anytime validity* and the efficiency gains of *Bayes-assisted* approaches. The motivation stems from several limitations of existing PPI methods and the demands of modern data analysis environments:

1. **Fixed-Time Validity Limitation of Standard PPI:** Traditional PPI, while improving statistical efficiency over methods using labelled data alone, only guarantees the desired coverage probability for a confidence interval at a *fixed* sample size, determined in advance. This means that if an experimenter wishes to continuously monitor the parameter of interest as new data arrives, recalculating standard PPI confidence intervals at each step does not provide a time-uniform guarantee of coverage. Drawing conclusions at arbitrary times or repeatedly checking for significance can inflate the Type I error rate.
2. **Need for Sequential Inference:** Many practical applications, such as online learning, real-time monitoring, clinical trials, and sequential decision-making, require statistical conclusions to remain valid at *any point in time* as data streams in. This necessitates confidence procedures that provide coverage uniformly over time, regardless of when an experiment is stopped or a conclusion is drawn. This is precisely the domain of confidence sequences.
3. **Leveraging Prior Knowledge:** In many scenarios, there exists prior information or expert knowledge regarding the quality or expected bias of the machine learning predictions. For instance, a black-box model might be known to be generally accurate, implying a small expected discrepancy between predictions and true labels. Current standard PPI methods do not explicitly leverage such prior information to further refine the inference.
4. **Boosting Efficiency Without Sacrificing Validity:** The core motivation of PPI is to enhance the precision (reduce the width of confidence intervals) of statistical estimates by utilizing auxiliary information from unlabelled data and predictions. This work aims to extend this efficiency gain to the sequential setting and further boost it by incorporating prior beliefs, all while strictly preserving the time-uniform statistical validity guarantee. The Bayes-assisted component, by placing a prior on the "rectifier" (the term accounting for prediction error), can lead to tighter confidence sequences when predictions are consistent with prior beliefs, without invalidating the inference if beliefs are inaccurate.

In summary, the key objective is to address the gap in the PPI framework by providing a robust, sequential inference method that is valid at any time, adaptable to the quality of predictions, and capable of incorporating prior knowledge for improved efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology extends the fixed-time Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) framework to a sequential setting by employing *asymptotic confidence sequences (AsympCS)* and integrating *Bayes-assisted* techniques.

#### 3.1. Foundation in Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI)

The work starts with the general PPI framework for estimating a parameter $\theta^\star = \arg \min_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}} E[\ell_\theta(X, Y)]$. The core idea of PPI is to express $\theta^\star$ as the solution to $g_{\theta^\star} := E[\ell'_{\theta^\star}(X, Y)] = 0$. The quantity $g_\theta$ is then decomposed into two terms: $m_\theta := E[\ell'_\theta(X, f(X))]$ (measure of fit from predictions) and $\Delta_\theta := E[\ell'_\theta(X, Y) - \ell'_\theta(X, f(X))]$ (rectifier, accounting for prediction error), such that $g_\theta = m_\theta + \Delta_\theta$. PPI constructs a confidence interval for $g_\theta$ using both labelled data (for $\Delta_\theta$) and unlabelled data (for $m_\theta$), and then inverts this to obtain a confidence interval for $\theta^\star$.

#### 3.2. Anytime Validity via Asymptotic Confidence Sequences (AsympCS)

To achieve anytime validity, the paper replaces fixed-time confidence intervals with confidence sequences (CS). An AsympCS $(C_{\alpha,t})_{t \ge 1}$ is a sequence of intervals that provides a $(1-\alpha)$ coverage guarantee *for all $t \ge 1$ simultaneously*, meaning $\text{Pr}(\mu \in C_{\alpha,t} \text{ for all } t \ge 1) \ge 1-\alpha$. The construction of these AsympCS primarily relies on:
- **Ville's inequality:** This inequality provides a non-asymptotic bound for the probability that a nonnegative supermartingale exceeds a certain threshold.
- **Method of mixtures:** By mixing a family of martingales (indexed by the parameter of interest) with a prior distribution over that parameter, a new supermartingale can be constructed. Applying Ville's inequality to this mixed supermartingale yields a confidence sequence.
- **Strassen's strong coupling:** This theorem states that partial sums of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables can be almost surely approximated by a Brownian motion path. This allows the extension of exact CS for Gaussian data to more general i.i.d. settings, leading to AsympCS. This coupling is crucial for ensuring the asymptotic validity of the confidence sequences when the underlying data are not perfectly Gaussian.

#### 3.3. Bayes-Assisted AsympCS Construction

A key innovation is the *Bayes-assisted AsympCS*. For a parameter $\mu$, the width of a standard AsympCS depends on a tuning parameter $\rho$. The Bayes-assisted AsympCS incorporates a prior density $\pi$ on the standardized mean $\mu/\sigma$. When the observed data aligns with this prior, the interval narrows; when it conflicts, the interval widens. This allows for efficiency gains when prior beliefs about the parameter are accurate, without sacrificing the validity guarantee if the prior is misspecified. Specifically, for i.i.d. data $Y_t$ with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, the Bayes-assisted AsympCS $C_{BA_{\alpha,t}}( \bar{Y}_t, \hat{\sigma}_t; \pi)$ is constructed, whose width depends on the marginal density $\eta_t(\bar{Y}_t/\hat{\sigma}_t)$. A Gaussian prior $N(\cdot; \mu_0, \tau^2)$ is presented as an example, where $\mu_0$ represents the prior belief about the mean and $\tau^2$ its certainty.

#### 3.4. PPI Estimators as Control Variates and Their Coupling

The paper highlights that PPI estimators for $g_\theta$ can be framed as *control-variate estimators (CVEs)*.
- **Standard PPI (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a):** This uses $\hat{g}^\text{PP}_{\theta,n} = \hat{m}_{\theta,n} + \hat{\Delta}^\text{PP}_{\theta,n}$, where $\hat{m}_{\theta,n}$ is the sample mean of $\ell'_\theta(\tilde{X}_i, f(\tilde{X}_i))$ from unlabelled data, and $\hat{\Delta}^\text{PP}_{\theta,n}$ is the sample mean of $(\ell'_\theta(X_i, Y_i) - \ell'_\theta(X_i, f(X_i)))$ from labelled data. This is a CVE with a fixed control-variate parameter $\lambda=1$.
- **PPI++ (Angelopoulos et al., 2023b):** This extends standard PPI by adaptively estimating the optimal control-variate parameter $\lambda$ from data, resulting in $\hat{g}^\text{PP++}_{\theta,n}$. This can lead to greater variance reduction when $\lambda \ne 1$.

The authors establish a *Strassen-type strong coupling* for these CVEs, showing that $\hat{g}_{\theta,n}$ (or its components) can be approximated almost surely by sums of i.i.d. Gaussian random variables. This is a critical step that enables the application of AsympCS theory to PPI estimators.

#### 3.5. Combining AsympCS for Anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted PPI

The final anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted PPI confidence sequence for $\theta^\star$ is constructed by:
1. **Decomposition of $g_\theta$:** As $g_\theta = m_\theta + \Delta_\theta$, two separate AsympCS are constructed:
 - An AsympCS for $m_\theta$ (using unlabelled data and predictions), typically a standard (non-Bayes-assisted) AsympCS.
 - A Bayes-assisted AsympCS for $\Delta_\theta$ (using labelled data and predictions), where a prior $\pi$ (e.g., zero-centered Gaussian, Laplace, or Student-t) is placed on $\Delta_\theta/\sigma_\Delta$, reflecting an expectation of small prediction bias.
2. **Minkowski Sum:** These two confidence sequences are then combined using a Minkowski sum to obtain an overall AsympCS for $g_\theta$. The coverage level $\alpha$ for the overall CS is split between the two component CSs (e.g., $\delta$ for $m_\theta$ and $\alpha-\delta$ for $\Delta_\theta$).
3. **Inversion:** The AsympCS for $g_\theta$ is inverted to yield the final anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted PPI confidence sequence $C_{avpp_{\alpha,n}}$ for $\theta^\star$. For estimands like the mean (using squared loss), this results in a simple interval around the PPI point estimate.

The approach implicitly assumes that the number of unlabelled samples ($N_n$) is large enough to estimate $m_\theta$ with high precision, effectively isolating the impact of the Bayes correction on the $\Delta_\theta$ component.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) method is demonstrated through synthetic and real-world data experiments, focusing on scalar mean estimation. The key findings are related to interval volume (efficiency) and cumulative miscoverage rate (validity).

#### 4.1. Synthetic Data Experiments

1. **Noisy Predictions Study:**
 - **Efficiency:** When predictions are accurate (low noise, e.g., $\sigma_Y = 0.1$), both standard PPI and PPI++ significantly reduce the interval volume compared to classical inference (using only labelled data). As noise increases ($\sigma_Y = 0.8, 3.0$), standard PPI's efficiency decreases, eventually performing worse than classical methods. PPI++, however, adapts to varying noise levels by learning the optimal control-variate parameter ($\lambda^*$) and consistently performs similarly to or better than classical and standard PPI.
 - **Bayes-Assistance:** Bayes-assisted PPI++ further improves efficiency across all noise levels when the zero-mean prior for the rectifier ($\Delta_\theta$) is well-specified (i.e., predictions are unbiased, even if noisy). This results in even narrower confidence sequences than the unassisted PPI++ in such scenarios.
 - **Validity:** All methods, including classical, unassisted PPI, PPI++, and Bayes-assisted variants, maintain their cumulative miscoverage rate below the nominal $\alpha$ level (0.1) across all labelled sample sizes ($n$), confirming their anytime validity.

2. **Biased Predictions Study:**
 - **Efficiency and Bias:** This experiment highlights the role of Bayes-assistance in scenarios with potential prediction bias. Classical inference and unassisted PPI/PPI++ show constant interval volumes regardless of the prediction bias level ($\upsilon$). In contrast, Bayes-assisted methods (with Gaussian, Laplace, or Student-t priors centered at zero for $\Delta_\theta$) exhibit reduced interval volumes when the bias is small (i.e., $\upsilon \approx 0$), indicating efficiency gains from correctly specified prior beliefs. However, as the absolute bias ($|\upsilon|$) increases, the interval volumes of Bayes-assisted methods widen, reflecting the increasing discrepancy between the prior assumption (zero bias) and the true bias.
 - **Prior Robustness:** Among the Bayes-assisted methods, those employing heavier-tailed priors (Laplace and Student-t) demonstrate greater robustness to prior misspecification. Their interval volumes increase at a slower rate with increasing bias compared to the Gaussian prior, which is more sensitive to deviations from its assumed distribution.
 - **Validity:** All procedures maintained coverage, with smaller degrees of freedom (df) for the Student-t noise distribution leading to slightly higher but still acceptable miscoverage rates.

#### 4.2. Real Data Experiments

- **Efficiency:** Across three diverse real datasets (flights, forest, galaxies), PPI methods (both standard PPI and PPI++) consistently yielded smaller confidence regions compared to classical inference relying solely on labelled data. Bayes-assisted approaches provided an additional boost in efficiency, particularly for moderate labelled sample sizes, further narrowing the intervals.
- **Validity:** Crucially, all evaluated methods, including classical, unassisted PPI/PPI++, and Bayes-assisted variants, maintained the cumulative miscoverage rate below the specified $\alpha = 0.1$ threshold throughout the sequential data acquisition process. This confirms their practical anytime validity across varied real-world scenarios.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the proposed anytime-valid PPI framework successfully extends the efficiency benefits of fixed-time PPI to sequential settings while preserving statistical validity. The Bayes-assisted component offers additional efficiency gains when prior knowledge about prediction quality is reasonably accurate, with heavier-tailed priors providing robustness against misspecification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces an anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted extension to the Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) framework, addressing a notable limitation of existing PPI methods. This advancement carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Real-time and Sequential Decision-Making:** By providing confidence sequences that are valid uniformly over time, the method directly supports applications requiring continuous monitoring and adaptive decision-making. This is crucial for fields such as online learning, A/B testing, clinical trials, and real-time anomaly detection, where it is often impractical or costly to fix a sample size in advance. Researchers and practitioners can now monitor statistical conclusions as data arrives without compromising the validity guarantees, mitigating the risk of inflated Type I error rates associated with repeated peeking at fixed-time intervals.
2. **Increased Statistical Efficiency:** The work preserves and enhances the core benefit of PPI, which is to increase statistical efficiency by leveraging machine learning predictions from abundant unlabelled data. By integrating Bayes-assisted confidence sequences, the method can achieve even tighter intervals when there is accurate prior knowledge about the quality or bias of the predictions, offering more precise estimates for the target parameter.
3. **Robustness and Flexibility:** The Bayes-assisted component allows for the incorporation of prior beliefs about the machine learning model's performance (e.g., expecting low bias). Crucially, this is achieved without sacrificing the frequentist validity of the confidence sequences, meaning the coverage guarantee holds even if the prior is misspecified. This provides a robust and flexible tool for practitioners who have some domain expertise but wish to avoid relying on strong, untestable assumptions. The use of different prior distributions (Gaussian, Laplace, Student-t) also offers flexibility, with heavier-tailed priors demonstrating increased robustness to large prediction biases.
4. **Broader Applicability of Confidence Sequences:** This work further demonstrates the utility and versatility of asymptotic confidence sequences in complex settings. By adapting AsympCS to control-variate estimators and combining them via Minkowski sums, the paper expands the methodological toolkit for time-uniform inference.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several avenues for future work, including extensions to multivariate parameters and the development of non-asymptotic, nonparametric Bayes-assisted confidence sequences. These directions could further broaden the applicability and robustness of the proposed framework, potentially leading to even stronger theoretical guarantees under specific data assumptions (e.g., bounded outcomes).

In essence, this research provides a statistically rigorous and practically relevant framework for sequential inference that effectively combines modern machine learning capabilities with robust statistical guarantees, offering a valuable tool for data-driven decision-making in dynamic environments.
