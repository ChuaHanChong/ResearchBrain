# Anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted, Prediction-Powered Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed an anytime-valid, Bayes-assisted Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) framework, extending PPI to sequential data analysis. This method provides confidence sequences that maintain statistical validity uniformly over time, leveraging machine learning predictions and prior knowledge to achieve enhanced statistical efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) methods provide confidence intervals with fixed-time validity, limiting their use in continuous monitoring or sequential decision-making.
- Many practical applications require statistical conclusions to remain valid at any point in time as new data arrives, necessitating 'anytime-valid' inference.
- Traditional PPI methods do not explicitly leverage prior knowledge about the quality or expected bias of machine learning predictions to further improve inference efficiency.

## Method

- The authors extended the fixed-time ==Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI)== framework to a sequential setting by employing ==asymptotic confidence sequences (AsympCS)== for the core PPI estimators.
- They integrated ==Bayes-assisted== principles into the AsympCS construction for the ==rectifier== component, allowing prior beliefs about prediction bias to refine interval widths without invalidating coverage.
- A ==Strassen-type strong coupling== was established for ==control-variate PPI estimators==, enabling the application of AsympCS theory and combining component confidence sequences via ==Minkowski sums==.

## Results

- In synthetic experiments, Bayes-assisted PPI++ consistently reduced confidence interval volume when predictions were unbiased, while all methods maintained the cumulative miscoverage rate below the nominal α\alphaα level.
- Bayes-assisted methods with heavier-tailed priors demonstrated greater robustness to increasing prediction bias, showing a slower increase in interval volumes compared to Gaussian priors.
- Across diverse real-world datasets, the proposed anytime-valid PPI methods yielded smaller confidence regions than classical inference, with Bayes-assisted variants providing additional efficiency gains while preserving the cumulative miscoverage rate below α\alphaα.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymptotic confidence sequences can be successfully adapted to Prediction-Powered Inference estimators, providing time-uniform statistical validity for sequential data analysis.
> - Incorporating Bayes-assisted components into confidence sequences can improve statistical efficiency (narrower intervals) when prior beliefs about prediction bias are accurate, without sacrificing frequentist validity.
> - Heavier-tailed prior distributions offer greater robustness to misspecification of prior beliefs about prediction bias, making Bayes-assisted methods more resilient to inaccurate prior assumptions.
