# A Distributional Treatment of Real2Sim2Real for Object-Centric Agent Adaptation in Vision-Driven Deformable Linear Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> An integrated Real2Sim2Real framework combines likelihood-free inference for accurate physical parameter estimation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) with informed domain randomization to achieve object-centric adaptation in robotic manipulation. Policies trained with this approach exhibit distinct behavioral patterns, adapting to specific DLO properties in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Manipulating Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) is complex due to their high dimensionality, nonlinearity, and sensitivity to visual noise.
- The "reality gap" significantly impedes Sim2Real transfer for soft objects, requiring accurate and often difficult manual calibration of simulator parameters.
- Existing Domain Randomization (DR) approaches often use broad uniform priors, which are not tailored to specific object properties and limit effective policy adaptation.

## Method

- Proposes an end-to-end Real2Sim2Real framework that integrates real-world DLO parameter inference, simulation-based policy training, and zero-shot real-world deployment.
- ==Employs BayesSim==, enhanced with RKHS-net distributional embeddings, to infer multimodal posterior distributions of DLO physical parameters (length, Young's modulus) from observed real-world manipulation trajectories.
- Uses these inferred, object-specific posterior distributions to guide Domain Randomization during Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) training, fostering policies adapted to fine differences in DLO properties.

## Results

- BayesSim-RKHS generated distinct 2D Mixture of Gaussian posterior heatmaps, demonstrating its capacity to classify varied DLO softness, albeit with higher uncertainty in length estimation.
- Policies trained with object-specific inferred distributions exhibited distinct end-effector (EEF) trajectories and motion patterns, reflecting behavioral adaptation to the specific DLO's physical properties when deployed in the real world.
- Dynamic Time Warping analysis of EEF trajectories confirmed that policies show high similarity when evaluated on DLOs aligning with their training distribution, quantifying the object-centric adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Likelihood-Free Inference combined with distributional embeddings can effectively classify subtle variations in DLO stiffness, though it shows higher uncertainty in length estimation.
> - Using fine-grained posterior distributions from LFI for Domain Randomisation guides policy training towards object-specific properties, resulting in more targeted simulated environments.
> - Policies trained with this approach develop distinct behavioral adaptations and object-centric control strategies, as evidenced by analysis of end-effector trajectories, even when scalar rewards are similar across policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) represents a significant and challenging area within robotics research, encompassing applications such as lace tying, surgical suturing, and rope manipulation. DLOs present complexities due to their high dimensionality, nonlinearity, and the inherent noise associated with visual servoing. Effective DLO manipulation often requires policies that can adapt to the specific physical parameters of the object, even when its geometry is known.

A common approach to address these challenges involves training robotic policies in simulation and then deploying them in the real world, a paradigm known as Sim2Real. This method aims to leverage the scalability of simulation environments to generate robust policies through extensive training. However, a major hurdle in Sim2Real transfer, particularly for soft objects like DLOs, is the "reality gap"—discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics. Bridging this gap necessitates accurate calibration of simulator parameters to match real-world physical properties, a process often referred to as Real2Sim.

Likelihood-Free Inference (LFI) is a technique used to solve the inverse problem of probabilistically mapping real-world observations to simulator parameters that are most likely to account for those observations. This allows for the inference of multimodal distributions of system parameters, capturing uncertainty. Domain Randomisation (DR) complements Sim2Real by training policies across a variety of simulated environments with randomized parameters, intending to improve robustness to variations in the real world. LFI can enhance DR by providing more precise, object-specific parameter distributions for randomization, rather than relying on broad uniform priors.

Recent advancements in this field include the use of distributional embeddings, such as Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS) of low-dimensional keypoint trajectories, to provide robustness against visual data noise and permutation invariance in Real2Sim calibration. While model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise for deformable object manipulation policies in Sim2Real, the integration of Bayesian inference (via LFI) with the flexibility of model-free RL in an end-to-end Real2Sim2Real system has not been extensively explored for DLOs. This paper aims to contribute to this gap by proposing and evaluating such an integrated framework, focusing on how inferring fine differences in DLO physical properties can lead to object-centric agent adaptation in vision-driven manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate an integrated (end-to-end) Real2Sim2Real framework for the robust manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) using visual perception. This overarching goal is motivated by the inherent challenges of DLO manipulation, which demand precise physical interactions and adaptive control policies. The difficulty of manually tuning simulation parameters to accurately represent diverse real-world DLOs, coupled with the "reality gap" in Sim2Real transfer for soft objects, underscores the need for an automated and robust calibration and policy learning mechanism.

Specifically, the paper outlines three key objectives:

1. **To propose an end-to-end Real2Sim2Real framework for robust vision-based DLO manipulation.** This objective aims to integrate the Real2Sim calibration process with subsequent policy training and Sim2Real deployment into a seamless system. The motivation is to provide a comprehensive solution that addresses both the accurate modeling of real-world DLOs in simulation and the effective transfer of learned policies back to the real environment without requiring further fine-tuning.

2. **To examine the capacity of BayesSim, combined with distributional state embeddings, to finely classify the physical properties of a DLO drawn from a parametric set.** This objective focuses on the Real2Sim component. The authors are motivated by the need to accurately infer DLO physical parameters (e.g., length and Young's modulus) from dynamic manipulation trajectories using visual and proprioceptive data. They seek to determine if a prominent LFI method like BayesSim, when enhanced with robust distributional embeddings (RKHS-net), can differentiate between DLOs with subtle variations in their physical characteristics. The quality of this inference is critical for reducing the reality gap.

3. **To study the implications of the resulting inferred domain distributions for model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy learning in simulation and how this translates to real-world performance.** This objective explores the impact of using the fine-grained posterior distributions obtained from LFI as a basis for Domain Randomisation (DR) during RL policy training. The motivation is to ascertain whether object-specific DR, guided by these inferred distributions, leads to faster policy convergence, more consistently successful behaviors, and critically, improved object-centric adaptation and performance when policies are deployed zero-shot in the real world. This addresses the limitations of using broad uniform priors in DR and seeks to demonstrate the practical utility of a distribution-aware approach to Sim2Real.

In summary, the research is motivated by the desire to achieve highly adaptive and precise robotic manipulation of DLOs by overcoming the reality gap through a principled, data-driven approach that leverages probabilistic inference for simulator calibration and informed domain randomization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes an integrated (end-to-end) Real2Sim2Real framework for vision-based DLO manipulation, structured around three main phases: Real2Sim DLO parameter inference, policy training in simulation, and Sim2Real policy deployment.

**A. Overall Framework (Algorithm 1)**
The framework begins by assuming a uniform proposal prior `˜p(θ)` for DLO parameters, which initializes a reference prior `p_0`. An initial policy `π_β0` is trained in simulation using `θ` sampled from `p_0`. A single rollout of `π_β0` is then executed in the real environment to collect a real trajectory `x_r`. The core Real2Sim phase involves iterative LFI using BayesSim to refine the posterior `ˆp(θ | x_r)`. This refined posterior `ˆp` then serves as the domain randomization distribution for training the final policy `π_β1` in simulation. Finally, `π_β1` is deployed and evaluated in the real world in a zero-shot manner.

**B. Perception Module: Keypoint Detection from Segmentation Images**
The perception system processes RGB images to identify the DLO and the target.
1. **Object Segmentation:** YOLOv8.2 is fine-tuned to generate segmentation masks for the DLO and the target, which are used to create segmentation images. A dataset of 183 manually labeled images, augmented to 439, is used for training the YOLOv8.2 model over 64 epochs.
2. **Keypoint Extraction:** An unsupervised keypoint detection model, based on the transporter method, is trained on these segmentation images. This model extracts 4 keypoints for the DLO and calculates the pixel-space median of the target's segmentation mask as a 5th keypoint. The keypoint model is trained using 1500 simulated policy rollouts (with uniform parameter sampling) and a small number of real-world rollouts over 50 epochs.
3. **Distributional State Representation:** The extracted keypoint trajectories are then fed into an RKHS-net layer. This layer constructs a distributional state representation (kernel mean embedding) that is designed to be robust to visual noise and keypoint permutations. The RKHS-net uses 500 random Fourier features with cos-only components.

**C. Real2Sim with Likelihood-Free Inference (LFI)**
The Real2Sim component focuses on calibrating the physical parameters of the DLO in simulation to match its real-world behavior.
1. **Parameterization:** The DLO is parameterized by a vector `θ = ⟨l, E⟩`, where `l` is its length and `E` is its Young’s modulus. The simulated DLO parameters range from [1e3, 5e4] Pa for Young's modulus and mm for length.
2. **BayesSim-RKHS Implementation:** The BayesSim-RKHS variant is used. In each of 15 LFI iterations, `N=100` rollouts of the current policy `π_β0` are run in simulation with `θ` sampled from the current reference prior `p_i`. The resulting `(θ, x_s)` pairs are used to train a Mixture Density Neural Network (MDNN) `q_ϕ(θ | x)`, which models the conditional density as a mixture of Gaussians (4 components with full covariance).
3. **Posterior Inference:** Given a real-world trajectory `x_r`, the MDNN `q_ϕ` is used to estimate the posterior distribution `ˆp(θ | x = x_r)`. This posterior then becomes the updated reference prior `p_i+1` for the next iteration.
4. **Hardware DLOs:** Four custom DLOs are manufactured from silicone polymers with varying Shore hardness and lengths (Table I), representing the real-world objects for which parameters are inferred.

**D. Domain Randomisation (DR) and Policy Learning**
1. **Domain Randomisation:** The final inferred posterior `ˆp(θ | x = x_r)` is used as the sampler for `θ` during Domain Randomisation (DR) when training the task policy `π_β1`. This contrasts with baselines using a uniform distribution `U` or a fixed median parameterization `μ`. To manage computational intensity of DLO simulations, 12 concurrent vectorized environments are used, each with a different `θ` sampled from the current reference prior. Small randomness is also introduced in camera and target positions for Sim2Real robustness.
2. **Policy Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) is employed as the model-free on-policy RL algorithm. Default hyperparameters from Stable Baselines3 are maintained. Policies are trained for 120,000 steps with episodes lasting a maximum of 16 steps and a batch size of 16.
3. **Control and Observations:** The robot (Franka Emika Panda) end-effector (EEF) is position-controlled in 2D (`⟨dx, dz⟩` in the `[−0.06, 0.06]` m range). Observations consist of the EEF's 2D `⟨x, z⟩` position (proprioception) and the flattened concatenation of 5x2D visual keypoint positions (DLO and target).
4. **Reward Function:** A sparse reward function is used. The distance `d_t` between the DLO keypoint cluster and the target (Frobenius norm) is computed. If `d_t ≤ d_thresh` (1.5 pixels), the reward `r_t = 1.0 − (d_t / d_thresh)` is given. Episodes terminate if `r_t ≥ 0.75` or if the EEF leaves a predefined workspace (failure: `r_t = -1`).

**E. Simulation and Hardware Setup**
1. **Simulator:** IsaacGym, using the FleX physics engine for DLOs (tetrahedral grid, corotational FEM).
2. **Hardware:** Franka Emika Panda 7-DoF robot arm, RealSense D435i camera (side-view, 60 fps). A Cartesian impedance controller is used for real-world EEF control, empirically tuned to match simulation step dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments address the capacity of the inferred posteriors to describe different DLO parameterizations, their impact on domain randomization, and the resulting object-centric agent performance in real-world deployment.

**A. Different Physical Parameterisations in LFI & DR**
- **Posterior Inference Precision:** Figure 3 illustrates 2D MoG posterior heatmaps for four different DLOs. The BayesSim-RKHS method demonstrates the ability to classify varied softness (Young’s modulus) of the DLOs. However, it exhibits more difficulty in cleanly classifying varied dimensions (length), as indicated by the spread of MoG variance along the length axis and the distribution of component means. This suggests higher uncertainty in length estimation compared to stiffness.
- **Domain Randomisation Samples:** The tightness and spread of the inferred MoG posteriors directly influence the resulting domain samples used for DR. Domain samples tend to be more spread along dimensions where the posterior is less certain (e.g., length). For DLOs 1 and 3, which are both very soft (00-20 Shore hardness) but differ in length, their posteriors are notably similar, leading to similar spread in domain samples. For DLOs 0 and 2, which have distinct stiffness values, multiple alternative hypotheses (component means) contribute to the domain sample spread, reflecting the broader possibilities considered for these DLOs.

**B. Uncertainty over Parameter Estimation & DR**
The study observed a correlation between the certainty (sharpness) of a posterior along a specific parameter dimension and the clustering of domain samples along that dimension. Dimensions with higher uncertainty in the posterior resulted in a wider spread of domain samples. This is particularly evident when comparing the posteriors for DLO-1 and DLO-3, where their similar softness leads to a predominant spread of domain samples along the length dimension, reflecting the relative uncertainty in length estimation by the LFI model.

**C. Object-centric Agent Performance**
Six PPO policies were trained: four using the inferred MoG posteriors for specific DLOs (PPO-0, PPO-1, PPO-2, PPO-3), one using a uniform distribution (PPO-U), and one assuming a median parameterization (PPO-μ). These policies were evaluated across the four real-world DLOs and one simulated DLO (median parameters), with each evaluation repeated four times.

- **Behavioral Adaptation (Figure 5 & 6):** Analysis of the mean EEF trajectories (accumulated commanded actions) reveals distinct motion patterns, indicating behavioral adaptation of the agent to the physical parameterization of the specific DLO it is evaluated on.
 - **PPO-0 (200mm; A-40):** The policy trained on the short, stiff DLO-0 exhibits a tight "roaming pattern" (1) when tested on DLO-0, aiming to maximize reward close to the target. It also shows a similar initial trajectory (2) for the very soft DLOs 1 and 3.
 - **PPO-1 (200mm; 00-20):** The policy for the short, extra-soft DLO-1 displays a similar initial trajectory (3) for the softer DLOs (1, 2, 3) and attempts a "loop pattern" (4) in later steps for the softest DLOs (1 and 3). Its trajectory pattern for DLO-0 (5) appears cleaner than PPO-3, which is consistent with being trained on a shorter DLO.
 - **PPO-2 (270mm; 00-50):** This policy, trained on a longer, soft DLO, shows a similar initial trajectory (6) for DLOs 1, 2, and 3, but with greater variance. It exhibits similar reaching patterns in later steps for longer DLOs 2 and 3 (7), and for DLO-1 (8), with variations attributed to softness differences.
 - **PPO-3 (290mm; 00-20):** This policy, trained on the longest extra-soft DLO, consistently maintains a greater distance between the EEF and the table compared to other DR policies (9), an adaptation potentially to avoid DLO drag.
- **Quantitative Rewards:** Despite clear behavioral differences in trajectories, the averaged rewards and pixel-distance to the target across policies were relatively similar. This finding suggests that the adaptation occurs at a nuanced behavioral level that is not fully captured by the sparse reward function, highlighting the importance of trajectory-based analysis in dynamic DLO manipulation.
- **Trajectory Similarity (Figure 7):** A Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) heatmap of EEF trajectories quantifies similarities between policies. For instance, PPO-0 and PPO-1, both conditioned on short DLOs, show high similarity when evaluated on DLO-0. PPO-{1,2} show high similarity for the longer DLOs 2 and 3. On the simulated median DLO (DLO-μ), PPO-{1,3} trajectories are the most divergent, while PPO-{1,2} are the most similar, emphasizing the impact of DLO and domain distribution alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a proof of concept for an integrated, distributional Real2Sim2Real framework that contributes to the field of deformable linear object (DLO) manipulation. The primary significance lies in demonstrating that combining likelihood-free inference (LFI) for parameter estimation with informed domain randomization (DR) during model-free reinforcement learning (RL) can lead to object-centric agent adaptation in real-world settings.

The key impacts are:

1. **Enhanced Sim2Real Transfer for DLOs:** The framework offers a method to systematically bridge the "reality gap" for soft, deformable objects. By inferring specific physical parameters (length, Young's modulus) of real-world DLOs and using these inferred distributions for simulation-based training, the policies developed are more robust and adapted to the characteristics of actual DLOs, mitigating the challenges posed by manual parameter tuning and simulator inaccuracies.
2. **Object-Centric Agent Behavior:** The study demonstrates that policies trained with object-specific domain distributions exhibit distinct behavioral patterns, indicating adaptation to the underlying physical properties of the DLOs. This capacity for nuanced, object-centric control is crucial for complex DLO manipulation tasks that demand precise interactions. While scalar reward metrics may not always fully capture these adaptations, the analysis of EEF trajectories provides evidence of this refined control.
3. **Integration of Vision-based Inference and Model-Free RL:** The work successfully integrates vision-based LFI (BayesSim-RKHS) for system parameter estimation with model-free RL (PPO) in an end-to-end manner. This allows practitioners in soft robotics to condition RL policies on visually inferred system parameterizations, operating within a shared action and observation space for both inference and policy learning. This integration simplifies the pipeline for developing adaptive controllers for deformable objects.
4. **Application to Soft Robotics:** The methodology is directly applicable to broader challenges in soft robotics and soft body manipulation, where object deformability and varied material properties are central. The ability to automatically characterize and adapt to diverse deformable objects opens avenues for more versatile and intelligent robotic systems in domains such as manufacturing, assembly, and surgical robotics.

A recognized constraint is that while the framework reduces the reality gap in terms of visual realism, it does not fully guarantee physical accuracy (i.e., the inferred Young's modulus may not precisely match the true physical value), particularly if the LFI algorithm needs to account for higher-order or more complex parameterizations. Addressing this limitation in future work could further enhance the framework's robustness and accuracy. Overall, this research provides a valuable step towards developing highly adaptive and autonomous robotic systems capable of dexterously manipulating complex deformable objects in unstructured environments.
