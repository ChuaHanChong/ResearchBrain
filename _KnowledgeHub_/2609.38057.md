# EVO-WAM: Evolving World Action Models through Video-Action Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> EVO-WAM adapts world action models to unseen robot tasks by self-training on their own generated video-action rollouts, with no new demonstrations or environment execution. State prediction and anchored multi-frame context enable full autoregressive rollouts; a VLM selects task-completing prefixes and an inverse dynamics model checks video-action consistency. Cosmos3 rises from 26.9% to 68.0% on seven unseen RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and 20.0% to 76.7% on a real robot.

## Problem

- Prior world-model self-improvement methods generate experience with a separate world model or rely on task demonstrations, leaving a WAM's own video priors unexploited for unseen-task learning.
- Existing WAMs sample inconsistent outcomes: from identical initial conditions, generated videos may depict either success or failure, so task completion cannot be assumed from generation alone.
- Even visually successful WAM rollouts can pair with actions inconsistent with the depicted motion, and closed-loop WAMs need fresh observations, preventing complete rollouts without environment feedback.

## Method

- **State prediction plus anchored multi-frame context**: the WAM predicts end-of-chunk ==robot state== and conditions on the initial ==anchor frame== plus recent generated latents, enabling feedback-free autoregressive rollouts.
- **Two-stage video-action verification**: a VLM ==describe-then-judge== loop scans subgoals to fix task-completing prefix endpoints with ==2-of-3 voting==, and an ==inverse dynamics model== rejects prefixes whose action reconstruction error exceeds a threshold.
- **Iterative self-training**: verified prefixes accumulate across rounds, mix with the original base training data, and supervise fine-tuning of the WAM, which then generates the next round's candidate rollouts.

## Results

- On seven unseen RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, Cosmos3 climbs from **26.9%** to **68.0%** and DreamZero from **28.5%** to **46.4%**, beating pi0.5 at **16.9%** and Fast-WAM at **8.4%**.
- On three real Franka long-horizon composite tasks, Cosmos3 improves from **20.0%** to **76.7%** average, with air-fryer loading jumping from **10%** to **90%**, versus DreamZero **20.0%** and pi0.5 **6.7%**.
- Ablation: VLM-only verification reaches **43.7%** versus **68.0%** with the IDM added, simulator verification hits **72.7%**; new-scene success is **70.4%** versus **24.9%**, seen-task retention **84.8%** versus **85.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual success alone is a poor filter for action supervision; checking that the actions actually explain the generated video is what makes self-generated robot data useful.
> - A learned inverse dynamics model can approximate simulator-based execution verification closely, recovering most of its benefit while also working on real robots that lack task simulators.
> - Most self-training gain arrives in the first round and later rounds can regress, so verified self-distillation mainly harvests competence already latent in the base model's generations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Adapting a robot policy to a new task usually means collecting costly expert demonstrations. Consider a robot putting pink and blue toy ducks into their matching bowls while another duck acts as a distractor.

A world action model (WAM) is a robot model that predicts both what the scene will look like and the actions meant to produce it. Such a model can imagine this task being completed. An imagined success is not necessarily a real one, though. The imagined completion can look right while the goal is not actually met, or while the paired actions do not match the video.

EVO-WAM lets the model generate its own trajectories. It checks both the intended outcome and the actions, then learns from the accepted pieces. It does this without collecting new expert demonstrations or executing candidate actions during self-training. On three unseen, long-horizon composite tasks on a real robot, the reported Cosmos3 average success rises from 20.0% at the self-training initialization to 76.7% after self-training.

### 2. Imagine, then verify

The WAM predicts future video, actions, and an end robot state in chunks. To continue a rollout without new environment observations, the model feeds its own predicted state and generated visual context into the next chunk. The initial frame remains an anchor, and recent frames supply motion history.

Each candidate then passes through two kinds of checks:

- **Visual progress.** A vision-language model (VLM) checks progress through ordered subgoals and fixes the first accepted endpoint for each subgoal.
- **Action consistency.** An inverse dynamics model (IDM) infers the actions associated with the observed motion. It reconstructs actions from the generated video, and the system compares those reconstructions to the WAM's paired actions.
- **Endpoint voting.** Only after the consistency check passes does the VLM make two further judgments at each fixed endpoint. Each endpoint needs at least two accepts out of three.

A failed or missing endpoint, a failed consistency check, or a failed vote discards the candidate. The system does not search for a later endpoint. Accepted prefixes are accumulated and used for fine-tuning together with the original data. The updated WAM then generates the next round.

### 3. Does verification matter?

The paper tests this on seven RoboTwin 2.0 simulation tasks unseen during base-model training. Average success is the mean task success rate across the seven tasks. Each task uses 100 Clean and 100 Randomized trials.

The ablation variants share the same Cosmos3 initialization, the same Qwen3.8-Flash-Next task-completion VLM, and the same training-update budget. With visual-only filtering, average success reaches 43.7% at Round 4. Adding IDM consistency filtering raises it to 68.0%. A simulator-based action check, which tests whether the paired actions complete the task in simulation, reaches 72.7%.

The authors read this as evidence that visual completion alone is insufficient. They also see it as evidence that direct action execution can be stronger when a task simulator is available. The simulator check depends on a simulator of the target task and scene. The IDM approach is used for real-world tasks that have no such simulator.

Verification ablation. All variants start at 26.9%. At Round 4, success is 43.7% for VLM only, 68.0% for VLM + IDM with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, and 72.7% for VLM + Simulator. The smaller Qwen3.5-27B VLM with IDM reaches 65.7%.

### 4. What improves

In the full simulation results, Cosmos3 rises from 26.9% at the 30K-step self-training initialization to 68.0% after four rounds. The strongest listed baseline, Cosmos3 at 34K steps, scores 31.6%. DreamZero also improves, from 28.5% to 46.4%.

On a Franka robot, the three tasks are:

- stacking bowls;
- placing ducks in matching bowls amid a distractor;
- opening an air-fryer drawer before placing bread inside.

Each policy has ten trials per task. The 76.7% average is reached at Round 2. Both that policy and the Cosmos3 baseline were evaluated at 31K steps.

Gains are not guaranteed with every round. Cosmos3 dips in Round 3 before recovering, and the authors note that further self-training does not always improve performance.

### 5. Imagination needs a check

EVO-WAM turns a WAM's imagined trajectories into training experience for unseen tasks. An imagined future becomes useful experience when it depicts the goal and the paired actions also fit that depiction.
