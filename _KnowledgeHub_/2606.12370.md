# Breaking Entropy Bounds: Accelerating RL Training via MTP with Rejection Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Bebop, developed by Alibaba's Qwen Team, accelerates large language model reinforcement learning training by enhancing Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) efficiency. It addresses MTP acceptance rate degradation by identifying target model entropy as the dominant factor and introduces an end-to-end Total Variation loss and a pre-RL adaptation strategy, achieving up to 1.8x end-to-end training acceleration.

## Problem

- The inference rollout stage is a significant bottleneck in current Reinforcement Learning (RL) training pipelines for Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Direct application of Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) in RL training leads to significant degradation in acceptance rates, thereby limiting its potential for speedup.
- Prior solutions often attributed MTP degradation to distribution mismatch, leading to costly and inefficient online MTP training during the RL phase.

## Method

- Systematically analyzed MTP in RL settings, identifying target model policy entropy fluctuation as the dominant factor for acceptance rate degradation over distribution mismatch.
- Developed a novel end-to-end ==Total Variation (TV) loss== function to directly optimize multi-step rejection sampling acceptance, enhancing MTP stability and effectiveness.
- Proposed a pre-RL adaptation strategy where the MTP module is trained once during the SFT stage using e2e TV loss, eliminating the need for continuous, costly online MTP updates during RL training.

## Results

- Achieved up to **1.8x** end-to-end acceleration in asynchronous RL training of Qwen3.5, **3.6**, and **3.7** models, with rollout phases seeing up to **2.4x** speedup on agentic tasks.
- The end-to-end TV loss consistently improved rejection sampling acceptance rates by 3-**8%** on in-distribution tasks and up to **2.3%** on out-of-distribution tasks, compared to CE and KL baselines.
- MTP models trained with TV loss exhibited a more than **95%** reduction in the entropy-acceptance slope, effectively decoupling acceptance rates from the target model's entropy fluctuations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MTP acceptance rate degradation during RL training is primarily driven by the target model's policy entropy fluctuations, with the draft-target mismatch from policy updates being largely negligible under rejection sampling.
> - Conventional MTP training objectives like cross-entropy (CE) and KL divergence are suboptimal for rejection sampling, as they only indirectly improve Total Variation distance and maintain a strong negative correlation with policy entropy.
> - A single, lightweight pre-RL training of the MTP module with the proposed end-to-end TV loss and rejection sampling is sufficient to maintain high and stable acceptance rates throughout the entire RL process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become an integral component in the training and alignment of modern large language models (LLMs). However, a significant bottleneck in current RL training pipelines for LLMs is the computational expense and time consumed by the inference rollout stage. Multi-Token Prediction (MTP), a form of speculative decoding, has emerged as a strategy to accelerate LLM inference by predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously. While effective in standalone inference, the direct application of MTP in RL training environments often leads to a degradation in acceptance rates, thereby limiting its potential for speedup.

Prior research has attributed this degradation primarily to a distribution mismatch between the static MTP draft model and the dynamically evolving LLM policy model during RL training. Attempts to address this have included costly online MTP training during the RL phase, which often introduces significant memory and latency overhead with limited performance improvements.

This work, "Bebop," addresses this critical challenge by systematically studying MTP in LLM post-training within RL settings. It reframes the understanding of MTP acceptance rate degradation, identifying the fluctuation of the target model's entropy as a more dominant factor than previously emphasized distribution mismatch. By introducing a novel training objective (end-to-end Total Variation (TV) loss) and leveraging probabilistic rejection sampling, Bebop aims to stabilize MTP acceptance rates and achieve consistent acceleration throughout the RL training process, without requiring continuous online MTP updates. This approach offers a novel perspective and a more efficient solution to a recognized bottleneck in the rapidly developing field of LLM RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to accelerate the reinforcement learning training process for large language models by enhancing the efficiency and stability of Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) within RL pipelines. The authors identify the rollout stage as the main bottleneck in RL training, and MTP as a natural candidate for acceleration through speculative decoding.

However, the direct application of MTP in RL training has been observed to suffer from a significant decline in acceptance rates, leading to limited speedup. This degradation motivated a detailed investigation into its underlying causes. The authors pinpoint two main factors potentially affecting MTP acceptance rates during RL:
1. **Policy Entropy Fluctuation:** The policy model in RL often maintains high entropy to encourage exploration, or may even exhibit an increasing entropy curve. High entropy makes it inherently more challenging for the MTP draft model to accurately predict future tokens, thus degrading acceptance rates.
2. **Distribution Mismatch:** The continuous weight updates of the policy model during RL training create a growing mismatch between the evolving policy distribution and the typically static MTP draft module (which is often frozen during RL training).

A key motivation for this work was the authors' empirical decomposition and theoretical analysis, which revealed that the policy entropy is the dominant factor driving MTP acceptance rate degradation, while the mismatch introduced by policy updates is comparatively negligible under certain conditions (specifically, with rejection sampling). This insight challenges the conventional "mismatch-centric" view of MTP degradation in RL and guides the development of Bebop.

Furthermore, the research is motivated by the observed suboptimality of conventional MTP training objectives, such as cross-entropy (CE) or KL divergence, when used in conjunction with rejection sampling. These objectives only indirectly improve the distributional overlap that determines rejection sampling acceptance. This highlights the need for a more direct optimization approach that aligns with the mechanics of rejection sampling to achieve superior and more stable acceptance rates. The overarching goal is to develop a practical and robust method that allows MTP to maintain its acceleration benefits consistently throughout the entire RL training process, thereby reducing computational costs and speeding up LLM development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Bebop involves a systematic three-pronged approach to integrate Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) into large-scale RL pipelines effectively.

#### 3.1. Analysis of Entropy Constraints on MTP Acceptance

The authors begin by formulating the target model’s next-token distribution `p` and the draft head’s predicted distribution `q`. They define target entropy `H(p)` as a measure of prediction uncertainty.
- **Target-Only Sampling:** For target-only sampling (where the draft token `ˆy` is greedily selected as `arg max_y q(y)` and accepted with probability `p(ˆy)`), the acceptance rate `α_TO` is shown to be largely approximated as `max_y p(y)`. This is proven to be a monotonically decreasing function of `H(p)`, empirically exhibiting a negative linear relationship: `α_TO ≈ a_TO − b_TO · H(p)`. This linearity is robust even with imperfect drafts.
- **Rejection Sampling:** For rejection sampling (where a draft token `ˆy ∼ q(·)` is accepted with probability `min(1, p(ˆy)/q(ˆy))`), the expected single-step acceptance rate `α_RS` is `1 − d_TV(p, q)`, where `d_TV` is the Total Variation distance. Although `α_RS` is not directly bounded by `H(p)`, the authors demonstrate that under conventional CE/KL-trained draft models, `α_RS` also exhibits a negative linear relationship with `H(p)`. This is attributed to per-token mismatches accumulating across an effective support size that scales with `exp(H(p))`.
- **Decomposition Analysis:** A decomposition method (Equation 16) is introduced to separate the total change in acceptance length (`∆α_t`) during RL training into an entropy-driven component (`∆α_entropy = b · (H_t − H_0)`) and a draft-target mismatch component (`∆α_mismatch`). This analysis is crucial for understanding the dominant factor behind MTP degradation.

#### 3.2. Development of End-to-End TV Loss

Recognizing the limitations of CE/KL divergence for rejection sampling (which only indirectly optimize the TV distance), the authors propose a novel loss function.
- **TV Loss (L_TV):** This loss directly minimizes the Total Variation distance: `L_TV = d_TV(p, q) = 1 − Σ_v min(p(v), q(v))`. In this, `p` is treated as a constant, and gradients flow only through `q`. The gradient analysis (Equation 11) shows that `∂L_TV/∂z_j = −q_j[1[q_j ≤ p_j] − S]`, which is bounded (ensuring stability), proportional to `q_j` (providing natural tail suppression), and directly aligns with the rejection sampling mechanism by distinguishing between accepted and rejected tokens. This leads to a probability-proportional mismatch, making `d_TV` bounded independently of `H(p)`.
- **End-to-End Multi-Step TV Loss (L_e2e):** For multi-step MTP with `γ` steps, the expected acceptance length is `E[L] = Σ_(j=1 to γ) Π_(i=1 to j) α_i`. The `L_e2e` loss is defined as `1 − (1/γ) Σ_(j=1 to γ) Π_(i=1 to j) (1 − d_TV(p_i, q_i))`. This objective directly optimizes the normalized expected acceptance length, accounting for the multiplicative nature of multi-step verification and dynamically weighting earlier steps more heavily.

#### 3.3. MTP Adaptation Strategy for RL

Based on the decomposition analysis, the authors address the question of online MTP updates during RL.
- **Pre-RL Adaptation Sufficiency:** The decomposition analysis (Fig. 3) empirically demonstrates that under rejection sampling, the draft-target mismatch component (due to backbone weight updates) is negligible. The degradation is almost entirely entropy-driven when using CE loss, and near-zero with TV loss. This finding suggests that a one-time pre-RL adaptation of the MTP module with e2e TV loss, performed during the SFT stage, is sufficient to maintain high and stable acceptance rates throughout RL training. This eliminates the need for costly online MTP updates during RL, reducing memory and computational overhead.
- **Cross Training:** If online MTP co-training is desired (e.g., for target-only sampling where mismatch is non-negligible), the paper suggests joint training with separate learning rates and gradient norm normalization, ensuring MTP training does not interfere with backbone RL optimization.

#### 3.4. Experimental Validation

The methodology is empirically validated using Qwen3.5, 3.6, and 3.7 models across various tasks including mathematical reasoning, code generation (SWE-bench), and agentic tasks. Experiments compare different MTP loss objectives (CE, KL, Reverse KL, TV, e2e TV) and acceptance methods (Target-Only, Rejection Sampling) during both SFT and RL training. Key metrics include acceptance rate, accept length, inference throughput, and training latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings demonstrating the effectiveness of Bebop:

- **Entropy-Acceptance Relationship:** MTP acceptance rates are fundamentally constrained by the target model’s entropy during RL training. Both target-only and rejection sampling (when MTP is trained with CE/KL loss) exhibit a clear negative linear relationship with the rise of policy entropy (Figure 1a, Figure 8). This relationship is consistent across different model sizes, tasks, and training stages.
- **Rejection Sampling's Robustness to Entropy:** Probabilistic rejection sampling is shown to largely alleviate the disturbance introduced by entropy fluctuations in RL compared to greedy target-only sampling. While CE/KL-trained MTPs still show entropy dependence, rejection sampling itself is less sensitive to entropy shifts because its acceptance depends on the full distributional overlap rather than just the top-1 prediction.
- **Superiority of End-to-End TV Loss:**
 - **Acceptance Rate Improvement:** The proposed end-to-end TV loss (e2e TV loss) consistently improves rejection sampling acceptance rates by approximately 3–8% on in-distribution tasks (Math, Code, Agent, SWE) and up to 2.3% on out-of-distribution tasks (MT-Bench), compared to CE and KL baselines (Table 2). This translates to up to 97% acceptance rates on certain agentic tasks.
 - **Entropy Invariance:** TV-trained MTP models achieve substantially more stable acceptance rates across varying target entropy. The entropy-acceptance slope is reduced by over 95% (e.g., from approximately -1.68 with CE/KL to -0.06 with TV loss), effectively decoupling acceptance from target entropy (Figure 1a, Figure 8). This is attributed to the TV loss driving a probability-proportional mismatch, concentrating optimization on high-probability tokens.
 - **Distributional Characteristics:** TV loss produces sharper draft distributions with entropy closer to the target model's entropy, which leads to better distributional overlap for rejection sampling (Figure 1b, Figure 10, Figure 11).
- **Sufficiency of Pre-RL Adaptation:** A critical finding is that the draft-target mismatch introduced by RL policy updates is negligible under rejection sampling. The decomposition analysis (Figure 3) shows that MTP degradation with CE loss is almost entirely entropy-driven, while with TV loss, all degradation components are near zero. This implies that a lightweight, one-time pre-RL MTP training with e2e TV loss and rejection sampling is sufficient to maintain consistent acceptance rates throughout the entire RL process, eliminating the need for costly online MTP updates during RL (Figure 9a).
- **Significant Acceleration Gains:** The improved acceptance rates directly translate to substantial inference throughput gains. Bebop achieves up to 25% extra inference throughput, leading to up to 1.8x end-to-end acceleration in asynchronous RL training of Qwen3.5, Qwen3.6, and Qwen3.7 models. For specific agentic tasks, rollout phases show up to 2.4x speedup (Figure 7, Figure 9b).
- **Scalability and Generalization:** MTP acceptance rates consistently increase with model size (Table 3). The approach shows strong generalization, with models trained without specific agent-specific data still achieving approximately 70% acceptance on agent tasks.
- **Temperature and Length Effects:** Rejection sampling maintains relatively stable acceptance lengths across varying sampling temperatures, unlike target-only sampling which degrades sharply at higher temperatures (Figure 12a). Furthermore, rejection sampling maintains a stable advantage over target-only sampling across different generation lengths (Figure 12b).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the field of large language model development and reinforcement learning.

Firstly, the work provides a **fundamental re-evaluation of MTP performance in RL settings**. By identifying the target model's entropy fluctuation, rather than distribution mismatch from policy updates, as the primary driver of MTP acceptance rate degradation, it shifts the focus of future optimization efforts. This enhanced understanding is crucial for developing more effective and targeted acceleration strategies.

Secondly, Bebop introduces a **novel and highly effective MTP training objective: the end-to-end TV loss**. Directly optimizing the total variation distance for multi-step rejection sampling not only yields significant improvements in acceptance rates (up to 95% and 10% gains over baselines) but, critically, largely decouples the MTP acceptance rate from the target model's entropy. This makes MTP acceleration stable and predictable throughout the often-volatile RL training process.

Thirdly, the research offers a **highly practical and resource-efficient MTP adaptation strategy for RL**. By demonstrating that a single, lightweight pre-RL MTP training phase with TV loss and rejection sampling is sufficient to maintain high acceptance rates, Bebop eliminates the need for complex, memory-intensive, and computationally expensive online MTP updates during RL. This simplification significantly reduces the engineering overhead and computational cost associated with large-scale RL training for LLMs.

The **substantial acceleration gains** (up to 1.8x end-to-end acceleration and up to 2.4x rollout speedup on agentic tasks) are a direct result of these innovations. Such efficiency improvements are paramount for mitigating the immense computational costs of LLM training, especially as models and contexts grow larger and RL-based alignment becomes more sophisticated. This will enable faster iteration cycles, allow for more extensive experimentation with RL algorithms, and facilitate the development of more capable LLMs in areas like mathematical reasoning, code generation, and complex agentic behaviors.

Finally, the extensive empirical validation across multiple Qwen models and diverse tasks suggests the **broad generalizability and robustness** of the proposed methods. The insights regarding temperature dependence, generation length effects, and the inherent robustness of rejection sampling under policy updates further provide valuable guidance for practical MTP deployments. In summary, Bebop offers a significant advancement in LLM RL infrastructure, making the training process more efficient, stable, and accessible.
