# Saliency-Guided Representation with Consistency Policy Learning for Visual Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Chinese University of Hong Kong and Chinese Academy of Sciences developed SRCP, a framework enhancing zero-shot generalization for successor representation methods in visual unsupervised reinforcement learning by integrating saliency-guided representation learning and consistency policy learning. It demonstrated superior performance across 16 visual control tasks, achieving improvements of up to 33% in some domains over existing state-of-the-art approaches.

## Problem

- Successor Representation (SR) methods, effective in low-dimensional settings, perform poorly in high-dimensional visual environments for unsupervised reinforcement learning (URL).
- Existing visual URL approaches often learn suboptimal representations from visual inputs, focusing on dynamics-irrelevant areas and leading to inaccurate successor measure estimations.
- Developing expressive and controllable multi-modal skill-conditioned policies from visual inputs remains challenging, hindering zero-shot generalization in visual URL.

## Method

- Decoupling representation learning from the direct ==successor representation== (SR) objective by introducing a saliency-guided dynamics task to compel the encoder to capture dynamics-relevant features.
- Integrating a fast-sampling consistency policy with URL-specific classifier-free guidance to enhance the modeling of multi-modal skill-conditioned action distributions and improve their controllability.
- Utilizing an iterative training loop that includes saliency map generation from Q-function gradients to guide the representation learning process.

## Results

- Achieved superior zero-shot generalization across 16 continuous control tasks on the ExORL benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art methods with improvements up to **33%** in some domains.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both saliency-guided dynamics representation learning and consistency policy learning are crucial and work synergistically for improved generalization.
- The saliency-guided encoder exhibited significantly faster convergence and lower physical state prediction error, consistently focusing on dynamics-relevant visual regions, while the consistency policy demonstrated competitive skill modeling performance with lower computational overhead than diffusion policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Traditional joint optimization in visual SR often leads to representations that focus on dynamics-irrelevant visual regions, impairing the accuracy of successor measures.
> - Guiding representation learning with saliency maps, derived from Q-function gradients, effectively compels the encoder to extract dynamics-relevant features from visual inputs.
> - Consistency models, when combined with classifier-free guidance, can effectively learn multi-modal and controllable skill-conditioned policies, offering an efficient alternative to diffusion models for visual URL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenges of zero-shot unsupervised reinforcement learning (URL) in high-dimensional visual environments. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved success in domains like games and robotics, but traditional methods typically rely on task-specific reward functions, limiting their generalization to new tasks without additional training. URL aims to pretrain agents on reward-free data to acquire generalizable policies, offering a solution for agents to perform unseen tasks without further supervision (zero-shot generalization).

Within URL, two main paradigms exist: unsupervised skill discovery (USD) and successor representation (SR) methods. USD approaches focus on learning diverse skills by maximizing divergence between skill-specific and average state distributions. However, these methods often lack direct alignment between learned skills and task objectives, limiting their zero-shot generalization capabilities.

Successor Representation (SR) methods, introduced by Dayan (1993) and extended to successor features (SF) by Barreto et al. (2017), offer a framework for zero-shot generalization by decoupling reward learning from environment dynamics. This approach allows agents to infer near-optimal skills from limited task-specific data. While SR methods have demonstrated effectiveness in structured, low-dimensional state-based URL settings, their performance significantly degrades when applied to high-dimensional visual environments. This limitation is a crucial gap in the broader research landscape of generalist RL agents operating in real-world visual settings.

Furthermore, the paper connects to the growing use of generative models, particularly diffusion models, in RL for modeling complex action distributions. While diffusion models are expressive, their high inference latency can be a practical drawback. Consistency models have emerged as a lightweight alternative, maintaining generative capabilities with improved inference speed. This work integrates consistency models into the visual URL setting for skill-conditioned policy learning, which has been an underexplored area.

The paper identifies specific limitations of current SR methods in visual URL, specifically related to learning effective representations from visual inputs and modeling expressive and controllable skill-conditioned policies. By addressing these, the proposed framework aims to advance the state-of-the-art in zero-shot generalization for visual URL, contributing to the development of more generalist and adaptable RL agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the zero-shot generalization capability of Successor Representation (SR) methods in high-dimensional visual environments. This objective is motivated by two key limitations identified through empirical analysis of existing SR approaches in visual URL settings:

1. **Suboptimal Representation Learning:** Traditional SR objectives, when jointly optimizing an encoder for visual inputs and successor networks, often lead to the extraction of representations that focus on dynamics-irrelevant regions of the observation. This is depicted in Figure 2(b), which illustrates that attention maps of conventional SR methods (like HILP and FB) tend to highlight static or background elements rather than crucial, dynamics-related features. Such suboptimal representations result in inaccurate estimations of successor measures, which are fundamental for effective reward and value estimation in SR. The authors hypothesize that this tight coupling in training impairs the quality of successor measures, thereby limiting generalization.
 - **Empirical Evidence:** Section 4 presents an experiment comparing HILP-pixel (visual input) with HILP-state (state input) and HILP-SDE-pixel (visual input with saliency-guided encoder). While all methods exhibited robust basic feature learning (high reward-return correlations), HILP-pixel showed a weaker value-return trend and reduced task returns, indicating inferior value estimation due to suboptimal representations. This suggests that the issue primarily impacts successor measures rather than basic features. A theoretical analysis using bounds on generalization performance (Equation 1) further underpins that the quality of successor features is directly linked to generalization.

2. **Challenges in Skill-Conditioned Policy Learning and Controllability:** In visual URL, policies must be learned from latent representations derived from high-dimensional visual inputs. If these representations are suboptimal, it becomes difficult for SR policies to effectively model multi-modal skill-conditioned action distributions and ensure skill controllability. Traditional policy networks often struggle to balance expressive modeling with computational efficiency. While diffusion models offer expressive modeling, they typically incur high inference latency.
 - **Empirical Evidence:** The paper compares trajectories generated by HILP-state and HILP-pixel in the Walker domain (Figure 4). HILP-state, operating on compact state space, produced diverse and controllable trajectories under random and inferred walking skills. In contrast, HILP-pixel, relying on learned visual representations, exhibited limited diversity under random skills and unstable, short-range walking behaviors under the inferred walking skill. This observation indicates that learned visual representations in visual URL pose challenges for policies to express diverse behaviors and maintain controllability, which are critical for generalization.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes Saliency-Guided Representation with Consistency Policy Learning (SRCP). The framework’s specific objectives are:
- To decouple representation learning from the direct SR objective by introducing a saliency-guided dynamics task. This aims to compel the encoder to capture dynamics-relevant features, thus improving the accuracy of successor measures.
- To integrate a fast-sampling consistency policy with URL-specific classifier-free guidance and tailored training objectives. This component is designed to enhance the modeling of multi-modal skill-conditioned policies and improve their controllability.

Ultimately, the motivation is to enable SR methods to achieve robust zero-shot generalization in complex visual environments, paving the way for more adaptable and generalist reinforcement learning agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Saliency-Guided Representation with Consistency Policy Learning (SRCP), aims to improve zero-shot generalization in visual URL by enhancing representation learning and skill-conditioned policy modeling. SRCP operates through an iterative training loop involving five main components, as illustrated in Figure 5:

1. **Unsupervised Datasets:** The foundation of SRCP is a collection of diverse, task-agnostic trajectories obtained from reward-free interactions with the environment. These datasets serve as the primary source for pretraining.

2. **Saliency Map Generation:**
 - To guide the encoder towards dynamics-relevant features, SRCP generates saliency maps for input observations.
 - This is achieved by computing the gradient of the Q-function (derived from successor features, Q = ψ(f(o), a, z)ᵀz) with respect to the input observation (o).
 - The saliency map (oα) is then constructed by retaining only the top-ranked pixels based on the magnitude of these gradients, effectively masking out less informative regions. This process highlights areas of the visual input that are most influential for value estimation.

3. **Saliency Dynamic Representation Learning:**
 - SRCP decouples representation learning from the direct SR objective to avoid biased feature extraction.
 - A saliency-guided dynamics module is introduced to train the encoder `f`. This module consists of a forward dynamics model `D` and an inverse dynamics model `I`.
 - **Forward Dynamics:** `D` predicts the next-state representation `s'` from the current observation's representation `f(o)` and action `a`. The loss `L_D1` minimizes the squared error between predicted and actual `s'`.
 - **Inverse Dynamics:** `I` predicts the action `a` given the current `f(o)` and next `s'` representations. The loss `L_I1` minimizes the squared error between predicted and actual `a`.
 - **Saliency Guidance:** To explicitly focus the encoder on dynamics-relevant regions, the saliency-masked observation `oα` is used.
 - The forward dynamics model also predicts `s'` using `f(oα)` and `a`, with loss `L_D2`.
 - The inverse dynamics model also predicts `a` using `f(oα)` and `s'`, with loss `L_I2`.
 - The total representation loss `L_rep` is a weighted sum: `L_rep = L_D1 + L_I1 + β * (L_D2 + L_I2)`, where `β` is a weighting coefficient. By minimizing `L_rep`, the encoder is encouraged to learn features that capture essential dynamics from both raw and saliency-focused observations.

4. **Successor Measure Training:**
 - Once the encoder `f` is updated, its output `f(o)` is used to learn basic features `φ`. The paper employs a Hilbert space representation for `φ`, as in HILP.
 - The successor feature `ψ` is then trained to satisfy Bellman consistency, minimizing the loss `L_ψ = ||ψ(s, a, z) - φ(s') - γ * ψ_target(s', a', z)||²`. `ψ_target` is a target successor feature learner (EMA of `ψ`).

5. **Consistency Policy Learning:**
 - SRCP addresses the challenge of modeling multi-modal skill-conditioned action distributions and ensuring skill controllability in visual URL using a consistency policy.
 - **Policy Formulation:** The policy is formulated as a denoising process `g_θ`, which learns to recover a clean action `a0` from a noisy version `at`, conditioned on both the state `s` (derived from `f(o)`) and a skill vector `z`.
 - **Classifier-Free Guidance:** To balance behavioral diversity and controllability, a classifier-free guidance strategy is adopted. The final action `a` is computed as `a = g_θ(s, a_t, ∅) + ω * (g_θ(s, a_t, z) - g_θ(s, a_t, ∅))`. Here, `g_θ(s, a_t, z)` is the conditioned output, `g_θ(s, a_t, ∅)` is the unconditioned output (trained without `z`), and `ω` is a guidance weight. This mechanism allows the policy to generate diverse actions while being strongly influenced by the skill `z`.
 - **Training Objectives:** The consistency policy is trained with three objectives:
 - **Skill-Conditioned Value Objective (`L_πQ`):** Encourages the policy to maximize expected skill returns under the successor feature formulation: `L_πQ = E_{(s,z)~D} [-ψ(s, π(s, z), z)ᵀz]`.
 - **Skill-Conditioned Behavior Consistency Loss (`L_πbc1`):** Regularizes the policy by encouraging consistent outputs on Gaussian-noise perturbed dataset actions across different noise levels, `a_dt`.
 - **Unconditional Behavior Consistency Loss (`L_πbc2`):** Promotes expressive multi-modal action distribution modeling by training the unconditional branch `g_θ(s, a_t, ∅)` to produce consistent outputs on noise-perturbed actions sampled from a random skill policy, `a_rt`.
 - The final policy loss is `L_π = L_πQ + λ1 * L_πbc1 + λ2 * L_πbc2`, where `λ1` and `λ2` are weighting coefficients.

SRCP integrates these components, enabling the learning of effective dynamics-relevant representations and expressive, controllable skill-conditioned policies, which together enhance zero-shot generalization in visual URL. The framework is designed to be compatible with various SR methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the ExORL benchmark, comprising 16 visual continuous control tasks across 4 domains (Walker, Quadruped, Cheetah, Jaco) and 4 different pretraining datasets (RND, PROTO, APS, APT), yielded several findings regarding SRCP's performance and the contribution of its components.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Generalization Performance

- SRCP achieved superior or near-optimal performance across all 16 tasks (Table 1, Figure 9, Figure 10). It significantly outperformed existing state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization methods, including various Successor Feature (SF) methods (AE, CL, Lap, LRA-SR, FDM, HILP) and Forward-Backward (FB) representations.
- Specifically, SRCP demonstrated improvements of 13% in the Walker domain, 33% in Quadruped, and 11% in Cheetah over the current SOTA. It also achieved the best average results across all four diverse datasets (RND, PROTO, APS, APT), indicating robustness to data variations.

#### 4.2. Contribution of SRCP Components (Ablation Study)

- An ablation study (Table 2, Table 7) evaluated SRCP variants: `SRCP w/o SE` (without saliency-guided dynamics representation learning) and `SRCP w/o CP` (without consistency policy learning).
 - `SRCP w/o SE` outperformed the HILP baseline, suggesting that the consistency policy significantly enhances action diversity and skill controllability, benefiting generalization.
 - `SRCP w/o CP` also surpassed HILP, indicating the effectiveness of saliency-guided dynamics representation learning in improving generalization.
 - The full SRCP framework consistently achieved the best performance, demonstrating that both saliency-guided representation learning and consistency policy learning are critical and work synergistically in visual URL tasks.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Saliency-Guided Dynamics Encoder

- Comparing HILP with integrated contrastive representation learning methods (HILP-TACO, HILP-PTACO) against SRCP variants with the saliency-guided dynamics encoder (HILP-SE, SRCP) (Figure 6, Table 8) showed that generic representation learning methods yielded inconsistent performance gains.
- In contrast, HILP-SE (SRCP w/o CP) consistently outperformed baselines across domains, particularly with over 90% average improvement in Walker and Quadruped domains compared to HILP. This highlights the superior effectiveness of the proposed saliency-guided representation in extracting dynamics-relevant features.
- Further analysis (Figure 11, Figure 13) showed that directly supervising an encoder to predict physical states from pixels did not significantly improve performance, suggesting that reconstructing physical states is challenging and may not be the most effective strategy for SR. Instead, SRCP's saliency-guided encoder exhibited significantly faster convergence and lower prediction error in regressing physical states, indicating it learns more "physically effective" representations compared to HILP and FB encoders.
- Visualizations of attention heatmaps (Figure 12) confirmed that SRCP's encoder consistently focused on dynamics-relevant regions, unlike baseline methods that attended to task-irrelevant areas.

#### 4.4. Effect of Consistency Policy on Skill Modeling

- Trajectory visualizations in the Walker domain (Figure 7) demonstrated the impact of the consistency policy.
 - HILP showed limited trajectory diversity under random skills and unstable, short-distance walking.
 - HILP-CP (HILP with consistency policy) improved trajectory diversity and enabled longer walking, indicating enhanced skill expressiveness and controllability, though still unstable due to suboptimal representations.
 - SRCP achieved both multi-modal skills and consistent long-distance locomotion, confirming that combining the consistency policy with dynamics-relevant representations is crucial for expressive and controllable skill behaviors.
- A comparison with diffusion policies (Table 15) indicated that SRCP's consistency policy achieved competitive performance with significantly lower computational overhead (similar training time, single-step inference) compared to multi-step diffusion models, offering a superior performance-computation trade-off.

#### 4.5. Compatibility with Other SR Methods

- SRCP's generality was validated by integrating it with the Forward-Backward (FB) method, denoted as SRCP(FB) (Figure 8, Table 9).
- SRCP(FB) consistently outperformed the standalone FB method across all tested tasks and domains (e.g., up to 490% increase in Walker Walk performance), confirming that SRCP serves as a general and effective framework for enhancing the generalization capability of various SR approaches in visual URL.

#### 4.6. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

- Analysis of the guidance weight `ω` (Table 3, Table 10) for the consistency policy showed that `ω=0` (no guidance) led to a significant performance drop, while moderate values (e.g., `ω=3` or `ω=4`) improved generalization. Overly large `ω` values slightly degraded performance by over-constraining the policy.
- Analysis of the saliency guidance weight `β` (Table 4, Table 11) in representation learning indicated that increasing `β` improved generalization up to a point, after which excessive focus on salient regions could reduce the ability to capture broader dynamics cues.
- Overall, SRCP exhibited stable performance across a range of hyperparameters.

#### 4.7. Robustness in Complex Environments

- SRCP demonstrated strong generalization in visually complex and noisy environments (DMC-GB benchmark, Table 13), outperforming baselines (FB, HILP) under mild and severe color perturbations and natural video distractions.
- It also showed scalability and cross-domain applicability in the Point Mass Maze navigation task (Table 14), a goal-directed control problem, where it significantly outperformed baselines.
- SRCP was also robust to limited task-specific transitions during adaptation, maintaining stable performance with a modest number of transitions (Table 16).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, Saliency-Guided Representation with Consistency Policy Learning (SRCP), that addresses significant limitations of existing Successor Representation (SR) methods in visual Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning (URL). The work identifies and empirically validates two core issues: the learning of suboptimal visual representations that fail to capture dynamics-relevant features, and the consequent difficulty in modeling expressive and controllable skill-conditioned policies.

The primary significance of SRCP lies in its innovative approach to overcoming these challenges, leading to enhanced zero-shot generalization in complex visual environments. By decoupling representation learning from the direct SR objective and guiding it with saliency maps derived from value functions, SRCP enables the encoder to extract features that are directly relevant to environment dynamics. This approach yields more accurate successor measures, which are fundamental for effective task transfer. The introduction of a consistency policy, further augmented with URL-specific classifier-free guidance, provides a mechanism for learning multi-modal skill-conditioned action distributions that are both diverse and controllable, while maintaining computational efficiency compared to iterative diffusion models.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:

- **Advancement in Generalist RL Agents:** SRCP contributes to the development of more generalist RL agents capable of operating in diverse, real-world visual settings. The ability to generalize to unseen tasks without additional training is a crucial step towards autonomous systems that can adapt to novel situations with minimal human intervention.
- **Improved Efficiency and Robustness:** The framework's robustness to various data distributions, visual distractions, and task categories, coupled with its competitive computational cost, suggests its practical applicability in scenarios where data is limited or visual conditions are challenging.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By demonstrating the importance of dynamics-relevant representations and effective skill-conditioned policy learning in visual URL, SRCP provides a new direction for future research. Its compatibility with various SR methods also suggests it could serve as a foundational enhancement for existing and future SR-based algorithms.
- **Bridge Between Representation Learning and Policy Control:** The paper effectively bridges the gap between sophisticated visual representation learning and robust skill-conditioned policy control, showcasing how these two aspects are interdependent and how their synergistic optimization can yield substantial performance gains.

While the paper primarily focuses on offline visual URL, the demonstrated capabilities open avenues for extending SR methods to online settings, further broadening the applicability of SRCP to more dynamic and interactive learning scenarios. The findings provide a robust benchmark and methodology for future exploration in visual generalization for reinforcement learning.
