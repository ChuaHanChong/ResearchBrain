# SOUS VIDE: Cooking Visual Drone Navigation Policies in a Gaussian Splatting Vacuum

> [!summary] Summary
> The SOUS VIDE framework enables robust, end-to-end visual unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) navigation policies that transfer from simulation to the real world without further tuning. It combines a photorealistic Gaussian Splatting-based simulator, FiGS, with an adaptive visuomotor policy, SV-Net, achieving a 0.17-meter trajectory tracking error in baseline tests and maintaining performance under various disturbances.

## Problem

- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for learned visuomotor policies in autonomous UAV navigation remains a significant challenge, requiring extensive real-world data or complex simulations.
- Collecting real-world expert flight data for agile UAV maneuvers is impractical due to safety concerns and data volume requirements.
- Existing simulators often lack photorealism, speed, or necessitate laborious manual scene creation, hindering scalable data generation for policy training.

## Method

- The **SOUS** VIDE framework introduces the FiGS (Flying in Gaussian Splats) simulator, which reconstructs real-world scenes photorealistically from short video clips and renders images at high frame rates.
- It employs an ==MPC== (Model Predictive Control) expert within FiGS, combined with domain randomization, to generate large datasets of expert UAV trajectories and corresponding visual-inertial data.
- The SV-Net policy is a lightweight neural network designed for onboard execution, fusing images, optical flow, and IMU data, and incorporating a Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)-inspired history network for online adaptation to dynamics.

## Results

- SOUS VIDE achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, with the complete SV-Net demonstrating **100%** success and a 0.17m trajectory tracking error in baseline real-world flights.
- Policies showed high resilience to various real-world disturbances, maintaining performance with a **30%** mass increase, 40 m/s wind gusts, and human presence in the scene.
- SV-Net generalized effectively to novel scenarios, including multi-objective tasks and extended trajectories, and demonstrated a **93.3%** success rate in a visually complex, cluttered workshop environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gaussian Splatting provides an efficient and high-fidelity method for creating photorealistic simulation environments directly from video, accelerating data generation for visuomotor policy training.
> - Integrating an adaptive module, inspired by Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), into the policy significantly enhances real-world robustness by allowing online compensation for unmodeled drone dynamics and environmental disturbances.
> - Training policies to output low-level thrust and body rate commands enables them to fully exploit the UAV's agility, critical for navigating complex 3D environments, as opposed to higher-level velocity commands.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenge of developing robust, learned visuomotor policies for autonomous drone navigation, particularly focusing on the persistent sim-to-real gap. Learned visuomotor policies, which integrate perception and control, offer a streamlined alternative to traditional modular navigation stacks. However, training these policies for agile flight and collision avoidance typically necessitates extensive visual and state data, making direct acquisition from human pilot demonstrations in the real world impractical. Simulation-based training offers scalability and safety but historically struggles with transferring policies effectively to real-world scenarios due to discrepancies between simulated and physical environments.

Previous efforts have demonstrated highly agile, even superhuman, performance of learned policies in controlled settings or with meticulously crafted digital twins. These methods often require significant human curation or specific environmental conditions. For instance, some approaches utilize learned representations like Gaussian Splats (GSplats) for policy training. However, existing GSplat-based methods for drone policies have limitations; one requires initial real-world expert flight data collected via motion capture and uses a downward-facing camera, limiting spatial information for obstacle avoidance. Another treats GSplat reconstruction as a background, relying on visual markers and high-level velocity commands, thus delegating low-level control. This work distinguishes itself by leveraging GSplats to generate low-level drone navigation policies for unstructured environments without external assistance or real-world expert flight data.

The paper also contrasts with existing drone simulators, which typically focus on enhanced dynamics models or improved rendering assets. While these improve fidelity, they often cannot match the speed and visual quality of GSplat scene reconstructions for rapid data generation, and their scene construction can be laborious. Some solutions involve visual abstractions like depth maps or learned feature embeddings to reduce domain variance, but these may discard valuable raw pixel data. High-performance policies for drone racing have demonstrated impressive results but often combine real and simulated data, physics-based and learning models, and hand-engineered visual features. This work aims for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer from policies trained solely on video clips, with minimal real-world tuning.

Furthermore, the research incorporates concepts from Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), a technique originally for legged robots to adapt to runtime conditions. While RMA has been applied to drones for stable flight, it has not been specifically designed for visual navigation. This paper integrates a lightweight RMA implementation to enhance real-world robustness in visual navigation tasks.

Finally, while some research explores generalist collision avoidance policies for various scene domains, these often simplify drone dynamics by relying on Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) to constrain movement to planar velocities and yaw. This approach limits the drone's ability to exploit its full agility in complex 3D environments. In contrast, this work directly commands low-level thrust and body rates, mirroring the capabilities of expert human pilots and enabling more agile maneuvers in cluttered indoor spaces.

In summary, this research positions itself by introducing a comprehensive framework—SOUS VIDE—that addresses the sim-to-real gap for end-to-end visual drone navigation. It achieves zero-shot transfer by combining a novel, photorealistic, and efficient GSplat-based simulator (FiGS) with a scalable data synthesis approach and an adaptive, lightweight visuomotor policy architecture (SV-Net) that commands low-level controls in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, end-to-end visual drone navigation policy that demonstrates zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and reliable real-world performance, utilizing only onboard perception and computation. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, each motivated by current limitations in the field:

1. **Development of a Photorealistic and Efficient Simulator (FiGS):** A key objective was to create a simulator that could rapidly generate high-fidelity visual and inertial data. This was motivated by the limitations of existing simulators, which often require laborious manual scene creation, extensive calibration for digital twins, or compromise on either visual fidelity or simulation speed. The goal was to reconstruct real-world scenes quickly from simple video captures, thereby accelerating the data generation process for policy training.

2. **Scalable Visuomotor Policy Generation:** The research aimed to establish a scalable method for synthesizing large datasets of expert demonstrations. The motivation stems from the impracticality of collecting real-world human pilot demonstrations for complex, agile drone behaviors, which are essential for training robust learned policies. Simulation, if capable of bridging the sim-to-real gap, offers a viable alternative for generating the necessary volume of diverse data.

3. **Design of a Lightweight, Onboard Visuomotor Policy (SV-Net):** A specific objective was to design a neural network architecture capable of running efficiently on resource-constrained onboard drone hardware (e.g., Orin Nano). This policy needed to effectively fuse multiple sensor modalities, including color images, optical flow, and IMU data, to produce low-level thrust and body rate commands. This was motivated by the need for practical deployment where computational resources are limited, and by the desire to command fundamental drone movements rather than higher-level, abstracted velocity commands.

4. **Integration of Online Adaptation Mechanisms:** The research sought to incorporate a learned module within the policy that could adapt in real-time to variations in drone dynamics and environmental conditions. This was motivated by the inherent variability in real-world flight, such as changes in drone mass due to payload, battery drain, rotor downwash effects, and external wind gusts. Such adaptation is crucial for achieving robust performance in unstructured and dynamic environments without requiring post-training fine-tuning or explicit re-calibration.

5. **Demonstration of Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robustness:** A final objective was to rigorously evaluate the trained policies in hardware experiments to demonstrate their ability to transfer from simulation to the real world without any real-world demonstrations or fine-tuning (zero-shot transfer). Furthermore, the policies needed to exhibit robustness against a range of realistic disturbances, including variations in mass, wind gusts, ambient brightness, and changes in the scene's static and dynamic elements. This objective addresses the core challenge of the sim-to-real gap, aiming to prove that policies learned purely in simulation can perform reliably in complex real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SOUS VIDE framework for end-to-end visual drone navigation encompasses a novel simulator, a data generation approach, and a policy architecture.

**A. FiGS (Flying in Gaussian Splats) Simulator**
The FiGS simulator integrates a high-fidelity visual scene model with a simplified drone dynamics model.
- **Gaussian Splats (GSplats):** Real-world scenes are reconstructed using 3D Gaussian Splatting. This learned representation models geometry and appearance with millions of Gaussians, each defined by its position, covariance, color, and opacity. GSplat models are trained from short video recordings (2-3 minutes) captured by a handheld camera, using the open-source Nerfstudio tool. This process enables photorealistic, full-resolution image rendering from any virtual camera pose within the reconstructed region at speeds exceeding 100 frames per second (fps) on a standard GPU. Metric scale and alignment with a global scene frame are established by including an ArUco tag marker in the initial video recording.
- **Drone Dynamics Model:** The drone's motion is simulated using a 10-dimensional semi-kinematic state vector, x = [pW, vW, qBW]T, which represents its position, velocity, and orientation in quaternion form within the world frame. Control inputs, u = [fth, ωB]T, consist of normalized thrust and angular velocity (body rates), corresponding to an "Acro mode" control scheme. The dynamics equations are:
 - `˙pW = vW`
 - `˙vW = gzW − (kth/m_dr)fthzB`
 - `˙qBW = 1/2 W(ωB)qBW`
 where `g` is gravitational acceleration, `zW` and `zB` are the z-axis unit vectors for world and body frames, and `kth` and `m_dr` are the thrust coefficient and mass, respectively, forming the drone parameter vector θ. This formulation omits rotational acceleration equations, simplifying the model while aligning with common low-level flight controller inputs. Trajectories are forward-integrated using ACADOS. For each state in a simulated trajectory, the corresponding first-person-view (FPV) image is rendered from the GSplat model using a body-to-camera transform (TBC).

**B. MPC Expert and Data Synthesis**
The SOUS VIDE pipeline generates large datasets of expert demonstrations within FiGS.
- **Desired Trajectory:** Trajectories are defined by a sequence of hand-picked waypoints designed to guide the drone through challenging spaces. These waypoints are converted into a dynamically feasible spline using methods like, then sampled at a control frequency (νctl) to generate a desired state (Xd) and input (Ud) trajectory over Nd steps.
- **Domain Randomization (Algorithm 1):** To enhance robustness and account for real-world variations, demonstrations are generated using domain randomization. For each rollout, drone parameters θs (kth, m_dr) are sampled from a uniform distribution (θmin, θmax), and the initial drone state x_s0 is perturbed from the desired trajectory segment (x_di ± ∆x). An MPC expert, with privileged access to the ground truth state and the instantiated dynamics model f_d (encoding θs), solves an N-step receding horizon optimal control problem (2). This MPC guides the drone towards the desired trajectory segment while respecting control limits. The resulting state trajectories (Xs), input trajectories (Us), and corresponding photorealistic FPV images (Is) rendered from the GSplat are collected to form the demonstration dataset D, which contains 100k-300k image/state-action pairs.

**C. SV-Net Policy Architecture**
SV-Net is a lightweight neural network architecture designed for onboard execution at 20 Hz on an Orin Nano. It relies on images from an onboard camera and state estimates (height pz, velocity vW, orientation qBW) from an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) that fuses IMU, downward-facing time-of-flight, and optical flow sensor data. Notably, the policy does not observe lateral position (px, py).
- **Inputs:** Color images, estimated height, velocity, and orientation. Additionally, a history of observable data is computed: δt, δpW (inferred from vW), δvW, and δqBW.
- **Components (Fig. 3):**
 1. **Feature Extractor:** Uses a SqueezeNet architecture, re-trained on the synthetic data, as a vision encoder. Its output is combined with estimated height and orientation and processed by a small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to create a pose-aware visual feature vector.
 2. **History Network:** Inspired by Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), this network takes a sliding window of the computed history data (δt, δpW, δvW, δqBW). It processes this sequence to generate a latent vector that dynamically encodes the drone's evolving flight dynamics and real-time operating conditions (e.g., unmodeled forces, mass changes). Section V-A explains how an equivalent thrust coefficient/mass ratio (ˆc) can effectively compensate for various model inaccuracies and external forces when aligned with the body's z-axis.
 3. **Command Network:** This MLP integrates the output of the feature extractor, the latent vector from the history network (specifically, the second-to-last layer of its output), observable states (pz, vW, qBW), and an objective vector (Ok). The objective vector encodes trajectory-specific goals (e.g., changes in position, initial/final velocities/orientations, total trajectory time), allowing a single policy to execute multiple distinct trajectories. The command network then outputs the low-level thrust (fth) and body rate (ω) commands.
- **Training Stages:**
 1. The history network is initially trained to predict the drone parameters θs (mass and thrust coefficient) from the generated history data.
 2. Once trained, the history network is frozen. The remainder of SV-Net, including the SqueezeNet vision encoder, is then trained end-to-end to predict the expert's body rate commands (Us) given the observable states and images (Is). During hardware testing, using the second-to-last layer of the history network's output as the latent code for the command network yielded superior performance compared to using the explicit estimate of θ.

**D. Experimental Setup**
Experiments were conducted using a quadrotor drone featuring a PixRacer Pro for low-level body-rate control and an Orin Nano for SV-Net policy execution. The onboard sensing suite comprised an IMU, an ARK Flow sensor, and a monocular camera. An EKF fused IMU and ARK Flow data for state estimation. Motion capture markers were used for diagnostic purposes to provide ground truth trajectories for evaluation but were not utilized by the drone's policy. Performance was quantified using four metrics: Completion (categorized from full success to failure), Collision Rate (CR), Trajectory Tracking Error (TTE), and Proximity Percentile (PP).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated SOUS VIDE policies across three categories: policy architecture ablations, robustness to disturbances, and generalization to novel scenarios.

**A. Policy Architecture Ablations (Section VI-A)**
A 15-second trajectory involving a gate and a ladder was used to compare SV-Net against three ablated versions. All policies were trained on the same 180k observation-action pairs.
- **SV-Net (ours):** The complete architecture achieved a 100% success rate across five flights (5/5 ✔✔), with a collision rate (CR) of 0.00 c/m, a trajectory tracking error (TTE) of 0.17m, and a proximity percentile (PP) of 96.0%.
- **SV no RMA:** This variant, lacking the history network, showed significantly degraded performance, completing only 3 out of 5 flights successfully (3/5 ✔✔, 1/5 ∼, 1/5 ✘✘). Its TTE was 0.65m, and PP was 37.7%, demonstrating the crucial role of the RMA module for robust flight.
- **SV no pre-train:** A variant with an unlocked history network trained end-to-end without initial pre-training. This policy exhibited highly unstable ˆc estimates (mean -2.81, std 9.56) and performed poorly (1/5 ∼, 4/5 ✘), with a CR of 0.10 c/m, TTE of 0.39m, and PP of 51.4%. This indicated that pre-training the history network for parameter estimation is beneficial.
- **SV no latent:** This variant used the explicit estimate of θ from the RMA network instead of the second-to-last layer's latent code. Its ˆc estimates were less stable (mean 6.39, std 1.07) compared to SV-Net (mean 6.05, std 0.25). Performance was inferior to the full SV-Net (1/5 ✔✔, 2/5 ✔, 1/5 ∼, 1/5 ✘), with a CR of 0.05 c/m, TTE of 0.43m, and PP of 42.0%. The higher-dimensional latent code from the second-to-last layer was more effective.
These results confirmed the efficacy of the complete SV-Net architecture, particularly the pre-trained RMA module's contribution to robustness.

**B. Robustness Experiments (Section VI-B)**
Using the SV-Net from the ablation study as a baseline, five additional disturbance conditions were tested.
- **Dynamic (people moving):** SV-Net maintained baseline performance (5/5 ✔✔, 0.00 c/m CR, 0.19m TTE, 86.3% PP), indicating robustness to active human presence in the scene.
- **Payload (30% mass increase):** The policy adapted well to a 350g payload (30% weight increase), achieving baseline-comparable performance (5/5 ✔✔, 0.00 c/m CR, 0.20m TTE, 80.1% PP). The RMA module maintained a stable ˆc estimate.
- **Wind (40 m/s gust):** SV-Net successfully navigated strong wind gusts (5/5 ✔✔, 0.00 c/m CR, 0.20m TTE, 81.7% PP). The RMA module demonstrated an adaptive response, with a downward spike in ˆc correlating with the drone passing the leaf blower, compensating for the external force.
- **Lighting (60% reduction):** Under reduced ambient brightness, the policy showed some degradation, particularly struggling to distinguish dark objects at the end of the trajectory (4/5 ✔✔, 1/5 ∼, 0.00 c/m CR, 0.24m TTE, 64.2% PP). Performance failed consistently at less than 40% original lumens.
- **Static (objects removed/occluded):** This condition, where trained objects (gate, ladder, monitor) were removed or occluded, posed the greatest challenge. The policy exhibited minor collisions and under-flew some waypoints, yet reliably tracked the overall trajectory shape and recovered, reaching the final position in 4 out of 5 flights (4/5 ∼, 1/5 ✘✘, 0.06 c/m CR, 0.49m TTE, 25.3% PP). This suggests the policy retained essential scene information beyond visual abstractions.

**C. Skill-Testing Experiments (Section VI-C)**
Three novel scenarios evaluated the generalization capabilities of SV-Net.
- **Multi-Objective:** One SV-Net policy was trained to execute three distinct trajectories in the same scene, differentiated by objective inputs. It achieved a 100% collision-free success rate (15/15 ✔✔, 0.00 c/m CR, 0.20m TTE, 83.4% PP). However, there was observable degradation in one task, where the drone consistently under-flew and overshot its endpoint, suggesting limitations in the objective encoding.
- **Extended Trajectory:** A policy was deployed on a trajectory double the length and duration of previous tests. Performance was comparable to the baseline (23/30 ✔✔, 4/30 ✔, 3/30 ✘), with a CR of 0.02 c/m, TTE of 0.24m, and PP of 72.5% over 30 attempts.
- **Cluttered Environment:** In a highly cluttered workshop with obstacles as close as 1.0m, the policy achieved a 93.3% success rate (13/15 ✔✔, 1/15 ✔, 1/15 ✘✘) over a 20-second trajectory in a visually complex scene, demonstrating robustness in unstructured real-world settings. TTE and PP metrics were not available due to the lack of a motion capture system in this environment.

Overall, the findings indicate that SOUS VIDE policies, leveraging the FiGS simulator and the SV-Net architecture with its adaptive RMA module, are capable of robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across a range of disturbances and generalized scenarios, with graceful degradation under extreme visual changes. The RMA module proved instrumental in maintaining stable flight dynamics under varying conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of autonomous drone navigation by presenting SOUS VIDE, a comprehensive framework that addresses critical limitations in training and deploying learned visuomotor policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap with Zero-Shot Transfer:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration of robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for low-level drone navigation policies. This eliminates the need for real-world expert demonstrations or fine-tuning, which are typically time-consuming, expensive, and often unsafe. This capability accelerates the development and deployment cycle for autonomous drone applications in complex real-world environments.

- **Efficient and Photorealistic Simulation:** The FiGS simulator, based on Gaussian Splatting, offers a novel approach to generating high-fidelity visual-inertial data. By reconstructing scenes from simple video captures within hours and rendering photorealistic images at high frame rates (up to 130 fps), FiGS drastically reduces the effort and time traditionally associated with creating detailed simulation environments (weeks for handcrafted digital twins). This efficiency enables rapid iteration and large-scale data generation, which is crucial for training complex learned policies.

- **Robustness to Real-World Variability:** The trained SOUS VIDE policies exhibit resilience to a wide array of real-world disturbances, including significant mass variations (30%), strong wind gusts (40 m/s), changes in ambient brightness (60%), and dynamic or static scene modifications. This robustness is critical for practical applications where environmental conditions and drone characteristics are rarely constant.

- **Adaptive and Lightweight Policy Architecture:** SV-Net is a computationally efficient architecture designed for onboard drone execution. Its ability to fuse multiple sensor inputs (images, optical flow, IMU) and, crucially, adapt online to changing flight dynamics via an integrated RMA module, represents a notable advancement. This online adaptation helps to compensate for both modeled and unmodeled inaccuracies in drone dynamics, providing stability and performance that prior visuomotor approaches often lack.

- **Exploitation of Full Drone Agility:** By directly outputting low-level thrust and body rate commands, the policies exploit the full 3D agility of the drone. This contrasts with approaches that simplify drone dynamics or rely on higher-level, abstracted commands, thereby enabling more complex and agile maneuvers necessary for navigating cluttered or unstructured environments.

- **Scalable Data Generation:** The methodology of using an MPC expert with privileged information in conjunction with domain randomization within FiGS allows for the programmatic synthesis of large, diverse datasets. This scalability is essential for training data-hungry learned policies for sophisticated behaviors without reliance on prohibitive real-world data collection.

- **Foundation for Future Advancements:** This work lays a foundation for future research directions. Potential impacts include:
 - **Generalist Skills:** Enabling the training of policies across multiple environments in FiGS to achieve general collision avoidance and scene-agnostic navigation.
 - **Semantic Understanding:** Augmenting policies with the ability to understand and respond to high-level goals or natural language commands from human operators.
 - **Expanded Applications:** Broadening the scope of autonomous drone operations in fields such as inspection, logistics, environmental monitoring, and search and rescue, particularly in environments that are unstructured, dynamic, or inaccessible to human pilots.

In conclusion, the SOUS VIDE framework represents a significant step towards practical and deployable autonomous drone navigation. By effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap through innovative simulation, data generation, and policy design, it enhances the capabilities of learned visuomotor policies, making them more robust, efficient, and versatile for real-world applications.
