# EgoZero: Robot Learning from Smart Glasses

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoZero learns closed-loop Franka manipulation policies purely from egocentric human demonstrations captured with Project Aria smart glasses, using no robot data. It unifies human and robot states/actions as morphology-agnostic 3D point sets, derived via triangulation and hand-pose correction rather than depth sensors, achieving 70% zero-shot success across 7 tasks with only 20 minutes of data per task.

## Problem

- Prior human-to-robot imitation methods need extra wearables, robot data, multi-camera calibration, or online fine-tuning, limiting scalable in-the-wild deployment.
- Affordance-based approaches from human video produce only low-precision open-loop trajectories, failing on tasks needing nonlinear, precise motions.
- Vision pretraining on large multi-robot datasets yields morphology-robust representations but has not shown zero-shot transfer purely from human data.

## Method

- ==Unified action space construction== that fuses ==HaMeR== hand-mesh keypoints with Aria's egocentric hand pose via a chained ==SE(3) transformation== to yield accurate 3D end-effector and gripper-closure actions.
- ==Triangulation-based object state estimation== using Aria SLAM extrinsics and ==CoTracker3== point tracking, solved with ==RANSAC== plus ==Huber-loss reprojection minimization== and a soft depth-penalty term to counter tracker stickiness.
- ==Closed-loop Transformer policy== trained via ==behavior cloning== with ==action chunking==, history buffers, and randomized 3D rotation/translation augmentations on the unified point-based state-action space.

## Results

- EgoZero reaches an average **70%** zero-shot success rate across **7** manipulation tasks (e.g., **13/15** open oven, **11/15** pick bread, **9/15** sweep broom), all trained on only **100** human demonstrations per task.
- Baselines fail entirely or partially: image-based Baku policy scores **0/15** on every task, while affordance-based open-loop trajectories succeed only on simple tasks (**12/15** oven) and fail on nonlinear ones (**0/15** bread, **0/15** erase board).
- Ablations confirm necessity of design choices: removing 3D augmentations or replacing triangulated depth with monocular metric depth estimation collapses success rate to **0/15** on all **7** tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Point-based, morphology-agnostic state-action representations can bridge the human-robot embodiment gap without any teleoperated or robot-collected training data.
> - Closed-loop policies with 3D data augmentation are essential to generalize beyond a narrow training distribution when transferring from unconstrained, in-the-wild human demonstrations.
> - Monocular depth estimation remains unreliable for precise manipulation; camera-trajectory triangulation is a more robust way to recover 3D structure without dedicated depth sensors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the context of general-purpose robotics, acknowledging significant progress in deep learning for other domains (language, vision, speech, games) but highlighting a critical bottleneck in robotics: the scarcity of internet-scale, diverse datasets aligned with real-world deployment conditions. This "data bottleneck" prevents robot policies from matching basic human capabilities.

Existing approaches to robot learning from human demonstrations have various limitations that EgoZero aims to overcome:
- **Teleoperation:** While effective, it is difficult to scale due to hardware requirements and the inherent "robot-centric" nature of data collected directly from a robot.
- **Wearables and Motion Capture:** Systems like VR headsets, wrist trackers, or motion capture gloves offer detailed human motion data but can be bulky, tethered, susceptible to occlusion, or require extensive calibration, thus lacking "in-the-wild" scalability.
- **Multi-camera Setups:** These can provide accurate 3D information but are not suitable for "in-the-wild" data collection due to setup complexity.
- **Vision-based Learning (pretraining on multi-robot datasets):** Large datasets like Open X-Embodiment and models like RT-1/RT-2 and Pi-0/Pi-0.5 demonstrate strong generalization across robot morphologies. However, they typically rely on substantial robot-collected data or pretrain on diverse *robot* data, and have not yet shown zero-shot transfer purely from human data in a closed-loop manner.
- **Affordance-based Policies:** Methods like can learn from egocentric human videos without robot data but often rely on open-loop trajectories or low-precision outputs, struggling with complex, non-linear motions.
- **Human Video to Robot Data Translation:** Works like use image editing models to convert human videos into robot-centric training data, or use smart glasses alongside robot data, often requiring careful data renormalization due to visual domain shifts.

EgoZero directly addresses these limitations by proposing a "minimal system" that enables robots to learn *zero-shot manipulation skills* from *only egocentric in-the-wild human data*, captured using *only Project Aria smart glasses*, and critically, with *zero robot data*. This represents a significant shift from robot-centric to human-centric data collection, aiming to unlock the vast, untapped resource of everyday human interaction with the physical world. The core innovation lies in its ability to extract complete, robot-executable actions and morphology-agnostic state representations from this challenging data source, enabling closed-loop policy learning that generalizes broadly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central, ambitious objective of EgoZero is to answer the question: **"can robots learn zero-shot manipulation skills from only egocentric in-the-wild human data?"**

The motivation behind this objective is multifaceted:
- **Overcoming the Data Bottleneck in Robotics:** The authors contend that the primary impediment to general-purpose robotics is the lack of internet-scale, diverse, and real-world-aligned datasets. Human interaction with the physical world generates a massive, continuous stream of relevant data, which remains largely inaccessible to robots due to technical challenges in capture and representation.
- **Leveraging Untapped Human Expertise:** Humans possess unparalleled dexterity and common sense in manipulating objects in diverse environments. Tapping into this inherent capability through natural human demonstrations offers a potentially "renewable source of rich, real-world data" that is far more scalable and diverse than traditional robot data collection methods.
- **Minimizing Data Collection Overhead:** Existing methods for human-to-robot transfer often require cumbersome setups (additional wearables, multi-camera calibration, teleoperation). EgoZero aims to streamline this process by using only smart glasses, allowing humans to interact naturally without intervention, significantly improving data collection ergonomics and scalability.
- **Achieving Zero-Shot Generalization:** The ultimate goal is for robots to learn policies from human data that can immediately transfer to novel robot morphologies, spatial configurations, camera viewpoints, and object instances without any robot-specific fine-tuning or data. This necessitates a morphology-agnostic data representation and robust policy learning framework.
- **Paving the Way for Human-Centric Robotics:** By making human demonstrations the primary source of learning, EgoZero seeks to foster a paradigm where robots can naturally acquire skills from human experiences, leading to more adaptable and generalizable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoZero introduces a lightweight framework designed to bridge the human-robot morphology gap by defining unified, morphology-agnostic 3D state and action spaces derived purely from egocentric smart glasses data.

#### 3.1. Data Collection with Project Aria Smart Glasses

The system relies on **Project Aria smart glasses** for data capture. These glasses provide:
- **Sensors:** Fisheye RGB camera and two SLAM cameras.
- **Machine Perception Services (MPS):** Provides accurate online 6DoF hand poses, camera intrinsics, and camera extrinsics.
- **Recorded Data:** For each timestep, RGB images ($I_t$), 6DoF palm poses ($H_t$), and 6DoF camera extrinsics ($T_t$) are recorded.

#### 3.2. Human-Robot Domain Unification

The core of EgoZero's methodology is the transformation of raw human observation data into morphology-agnostic state ($\tilde{S}$) and action ($\tilde{A}$) spaces, both defined in an egocentric 3D point frame.

- **Unified Action Space ($\tilde{A}$):** This space consists of concatenated 3D end-effector egocentric coordinates and gripper closures.
 - **Challenge:** Project Aria's MPS provides only palm poses ($H_t$), not precise end-effector (fingertip) information.
 - **Solution:** The authors use **HaMeR**, a 21-keypoint egocentric hand model, to infer detailed hand kinematics. They combine HaMeR's local hand deformation (more reliable in hand frame) with Aria's egocentric palm pose to compute a corrected hand pose. This corrected pose is then projected into the first frame to derive consistent 3D thumb and index finger coordinates.
 - **Gripper Closure:** Grasp detection is performed by thresholding the Euclidean distance between the thumb and index coordinates. The final action is a vector of these coordinates and gripper closure.

- **Unified State Space ($\tilde{S}$):** This space comprises egocentric object point sets and robot end-effector actions.
 - **Challenge:** Aria glasses do not provide accurate direct depth measurements (e.g., from stereo triangulation due to narrow field-of-view overlap, or built-in depth sensors). Monocular metric depth models are found to be inconsistent and inaccurate even with grounding.
 - **Solution: Triangulation over Camera Trajectory:** The system leverages Aria's accurate SLAM extrinsics and **CoTracker3** (a point tracking model) to triangulate 2D points into 3D over the demonstration trajectory. This method relies on three assumptions: (1) the object is stationary pre-grasp, (2) sufficient camera movement exists, and (3) the environment is not stochastic.
 - **Procedure:**
 1. **2D Point Labeling:** Expert-labeled 2D points on the object are mapped onto the start frame using **Grounding DINO** and **DIFT**.
 2. **Point Tracking:** These 2D points are tracked across the demonstration with CoTracker3, yielding a trajectory of (camera pose $T_t$, 2D point $u_t$) pairs.
 3. **Triangulation Optimization:** An optimization problem is solved to find the 3D point $q^*$ in the first frame that minimizes pixel reprojection error across inlier frames, identified via epipolar geometric consistency and RANSAC. A "soft depth penalty" is added to prefer closer solutions, mitigating "stickiness" in CoTracker3 predictions.
 4. **Unified Object Representation:** All triangulated points are concatenated and ordered to represent the object state ($\tilde{s}$).

#### 3.3. Learning a Robot Policy on Human Data

- **Policy Architecture:** A **closed-loop Transformer policy** ($\pi_\theta$) is trained using behavior cloning on a dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\tilde{s}^{(i)}, \tilde{a}^{(i)})\}$ derived from human demonstrations.
- **Loss Function:** The policy minimizes the negative log likelihood, modeling predictions as the mean of a normal distribution.
- **Enhancements:** The policy is augmented with a history buffer and temporally aggregated action chunking to provide temporal context.
- **Crucial Augmentations:** **Random 3D rotations and translations** are injected into the object points and applied to states and actions during training. This is shown to be critical for zero-shot in-the-wild transfer by regularizing the policy to learn a more general solution across a larger 3D volume.
- **Data Filtering:** Stationary points are removed, and noisy training examples (from DIFT failures or points far from fingertips) are discarded.

#### 3.4. Policy Inference

- **Robot Deployment:** Policies are deployed on a Franka Panda gripper robot.
- **Egocentric View in Inference:** An iPhone is used to represent the stationary egocentric view, leveraging its accurate depth sensor for unprojection of 2D points into 3D.
- **Calibration:** A one-time calibration maps the iPhone-to-robot transform using an Aruco tag.
- **Action Execution:** The policy's 3D predictions are mapped to robot frame via inverse kinematics. Gripper predictions are binarized.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EgoZero demonstrates significant breakthroughs in zero-shot robot learning from in-the-wild human demonstrations.

- **Zero-Shot Success Rate:** EgoZero policies achieve an impressive **70% average zero-shot success rate** across 7 diverse manipulation tasks. This is achieved without any robot-collected training data, deployed on a Franka Panda robot in unseen real-world environments.
- **Data Efficiency:** Policies are trained with only **100 human demonstrations per task**, requiring approximately **20 minutes of data collection** per task. This highlights the robustness, transferability, and data efficiency gained from using unified 3D state-action representations.
- **Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **Morphological Generalization:** By design, the point-based representations unify human and robot morphologies, enabling direct transfer.
 - **Spatial Generalization (Object Pose):** Policies generalize to object configurations significantly outside the training volume, demonstrating robustness to varied object positions and relative layouts. The diversity in human demonstrations across different environments, tabletops, and demonstrator postures contributes to this.
 - **Semantic Generalization (Object Instances):** The use of language-conditioned grounding models like Grounding DINO allows the policy to generalize to entirely new object instances (e.g., a different model of oven) by simply prompting with the object category. This capability is enabled by compressing visual diversity into geometric abstractions.
 - **Camera Generalization:** EgoZero is completely camera-agnostic. Policies trained on fisheye Project Aria data successfully infer actions using an iPhone camera (pinhole lens) during deployment, overcoming significant visual distribution shifts.
 - **Human-Scale Generalization:** Data collected from multiple unique demonstrators in various environments and postures further enhances the diversity and generalizability of the learned policies.

- **Baseline Comparisons and Ablations:**
 - **"From Vision" (Baku variant):** This baseline, attempting to learn directly from image inputs, failed unequivocally (0% success rate across all tasks). This confirms the difficulty of learning closed-loop policies from human video with zero-shot robot transfer due to large visual distribution differences (fisheye lens, human vs. robot morphology).
 - **"From Affordances" ( variant):** This open-loop, affordance-based baseline showed limited success (e.g., 12/15 for "Open oven," 10/15 for "Fold towel," but 0/15 for "Pick bread" and "Erase board"). It struggled with tasks requiring complex, non-linear motions, demonstrating the necessity of EgoZero's closed-loop policy learning for precision and dexterity.
 - **Ablation: "EGOZERO - 3D augmentations":** Without crucial 3D augmentations (random rotations and translations), EgoZero also failed (0% success rate). This finding underscores that even with unified 3D state-action spaces, the inherent sparsity and limited volume of initial human demonstrations require data augmentation to learn robust and interpolative 3D-to-3D mappings for out-of-distribution generalization.
 - **Ablation: "EGOZERO - triangulated depth" (vs. monocular depth):** Replacing the novel triangulation method with monocular metric depth estimation also resulted in 0% success. Even with Aruco tag grounding, monocular depth models exhibited >5cm error, likely due to distortion from Aria's fisheye lens, proving them unsuitable for accurate 3D state extraction in this setting. This validates the necessity and efficacy of EgoZero's custom triangulation approach.

The results strongly suggest that EgoZero successfully addresses the core challenges of extracting usable, morphology-agnostic data from in-the-wild human demonstrations, translating it into effective, zero-shot robot policies, and demonstrating superior performance compared to adapted baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoZero represents a significant advancement in robot learning, particularly in the domain of human-to-robot skill transfer. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating for the first time **successful zero-shot transfer of closed-loop manipulation policies from purely in-the-wild egocentric human data, collected with minimal hardware (smart glasses), and requiring zero robot-specific training data.**

The potential impact of EgoZero is profound:
- **Scalable and Abundant Data Collection:** By making data collection ergonomic and "in-the-wild," EgoZero unlocks the vast, continuously generated reservoir of human interaction data. This has the potential to overcome the long-standing data bottleneck in robotics, providing an unprecedented scale and diversity of training experiences for robots.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** The minimal hardware requirements (smart glasses) and the elimination of robot data collection simplify the process of gathering training data. This could democratize access to robot learning, allowing non-experts to contribute demonstrations and accelerating research and development in robotics.
- **Enabling General-Purpose Robotics:** The morphology-agnostic 3D point representations allow policies to generalize across different robot embodiments, object instances, and environments. This is a crucial step toward building truly general-purpose robots that can adapt to novel tasks and unseen variations, akin to how large language models generalize across textual contexts.
- **Foundational Framework for Human-Centric Robotics:** EgoZero offers a foundational framework for a new theme in robotics that is inherently more "human-centric." By directly leveraging human experiences, robots can learn more natural, intuitive, and complex behaviors, potentially leading to more seamless human-robot collaboration.
- **Advancement in Perception and Representation:** The novel methods for extracting accurate 3D state and action representations from challenging egocentric, non-depth-sensing smart glasses data (e.g., triangulation with a soft depth penalty, combining HaMeR with Aria's poses) are significant technical contributions that can inspire future work in robust perception for robotics.

Despite its breakthroughs, the authors acknowledge limitations, which also point to future research directions:
- **Limitations of 3D Representations:** Reliance on correspondence models (DIFT) can introduce errors, which the policy cannot self-correct. Future work might explore learning from dense, unordered geometric information.
- **Limitations of Triangulation:** The current triangulation method assumes stationary objects and sufficient camera movement. Future hardware (stereo cameras, lidar) could remove these constraints for tracking dynamic objects.
- **Limitations of Hand Models:** Inaccuracies in current hand pose estimation models (HaMeR, Aria MPS) lead to 1-2cm errors in action labels, limiting precision for very delicate tasks. Continued research in hand estimation is crucial.

In conclusion, EgoZero presents a compelling vision for robot learning, demonstrating that real-world human data, collected unobtrusively, can serve as a powerful and scalable foundation for training robust, generalized robot policies. This work significantly pushes the frontier of robot learning towards a future where robots can readily learn from the abundance and diversity of human interaction with the world.
