# ObjectForesight: Predicting Future 3D Object Trajectories from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> ObjectForesight introduces a model for predicting future 3D 6-DoF trajectories of rigid objects from short human videos, leveraging a large-scale dataset of over 2 million object-centric 3D trajectories curated through an automated pipeline. The diffusion-based transformer model predicts multimodal, physically plausible object motions, outperforming baselines in 3D trajectory accuracy on EPIC-Kitchens and HOT3D-Clips datasets.

## Problem

- Computational systems lack the ability to intuitively anticipate how objects move and transform due to human interactions, a key human cognitive skill for planning and action.
- Existing methods for human-object interaction often focus on 2D predictions or abstract latent spaces, lacking explicit 3D grounding and object-level motion dynamics suitable for robotics.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, clean, physically grounded 3D interaction datasets for training models to understand object dynamics from human videos.

## Method

- A scalable, automated pipeline was developed to curate a dataset of over 2 million 3D 6-DoF object trajectories from unconstrained egocentric videos using advanced vision models (e.g., SAM2, TRELLIS, FoundationPose, SpaTrackerV2).
- ObjectForesight, a geometry-aware diffusion model, predicts future 6-DoF object poses by integrating monocular geometry, recent motion history, and local scene structure.
- The model uses a PointTransformerV3 encoder for scene understanding and a Diffusion Transformer with AdaLN-Zero conditioning for multimodal temporal trajectory prediction in a depth-normalized token space.

## Results

- The automated pipeline successfully curated over 2 million high-quality 3D object trajectories from EPIC-Kitchens, providing the first dataset of its scale and fidelity for learning object dynamics.
- ObjectForesight-DiT achieved superior performance in 3D trajectory forecasting, with an Average Displacement Error (ADE) of 0.016m and Average Rotation Error (ARE) of 2.30° on EPIC-Kitchens, outperforming Constant Velocity by 41% in ADE.
- Ablation studies confirmed that PointTransformerV3 as the scene encoder and scaling up the Diffusion Transformer consistently improved performance, demonstrating the impact of strong geometric and temporal modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An automated data curation pipeline can reliably extract high-quality, metrically grounded 3D object trajectories from in-the-wild egocentric videos at scale, addressing a major data bottleneck.
> - Directly modeling object motion in SE(3) space using a diffusion-based approach is more effective than inferring it from synthesized frames or relying on autoregressive methods, especially for capturing multimodal future possibilities.
> - Effective scene conditioning via a strong geometric backbone (like PointTransformerV3) combined with sufficient model capacity in the diffusion transformer significantly improves the accuracy and plausibility of 3D object trajectory predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the problem of endowing computational systems with the ability to anticipate future object motions and transformations resulting from human interactions. This capability is analogous to human intuitive understanding of how objects move and change in response to actions, which is fundamental for planning, prediction, and effective action in the physical world.

Existing approaches to understanding human-object interaction from videos have primarily focused on extracting 3D hand and object poses, reconstructing object geometry, or estimating dense motion over time. Recent advancements in segmentation (e.g., SAM, SAM2), mesh reconstruction (e.g., TRELLIS), and 3D pose estimation (e.g., FoundationPose, SpaTrackerV2) have enabled the automatic extraction of 3D object trajectories from unconstrained images and videos. This work leverages these developments to move beyond mere geometric estimation towards learning the dynamics of object movement over time.

Another line of related research involves predicting manipulation cues and affordances from human videos. These methods often operate in 2D or intermediate feature spaces, forecasting contact regions, future hand movements, or categorical actions. ObjectForesight distinguishes itself by aiming to learn a continuous model of 3D object dynamics, predicting how objects themselves move in 6-DoF (degrees of freedom) pose space, thereby grounding predictions in explicit geometry. This approach extends affordance learning by providing physically coherent, object-centric reasoning about future motion.

In the broader context of world models, prior efforts have largely concentrated on learning predictive representations at the pixel level through video generation or in latent spaces. While these approaches capture temporal dependencies, they often lack explicit 3D grounding and object-level motion prediction. ObjectForesight explicitly models object evolution in SE(3) space, operating on 6-DoF poses rather than pixels or abstract latent codes. This explicit 3D representation is intended to be more suitable for integration into robotic manipulation frameworks. While future prediction has been explored in domains such as self-driving, those contexts typically involve largely independent agents and external objects with different dynamics regimes. ObjectForesight focuses specifically on fine-grained, human-object interaction dynamics.

A key challenge identified in the field is the scarcity of large-scale, clean, and physically grounded 3D interaction datasets. Existing robotic datasets are often limited and scripted, while vast human video corpora lack aligned 3D information. This research addresses this data gap by developing a scalable pipeline to curate 3D object trajectories from existing egocentric video datasets, positioning it to facilitate large-scale learning of object dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to enable computational systems to predict plausible future object motions directly from passive visual observations, mirroring humans' intuitive understanding of object interactions. When observing a scene, humans can anticipate how objects might move or change through interaction, such as a cup being lifted or a knife slicing. This ability to mentally simulate object interactions is critical for intelligent behavior, planning, and effective action in the physical world.

The work aims to move beyond simply recognizing current states to inferring and predicting future configurations of objects. The focus is specifically on predicting 3D object dynamics, which involves learning how objects can move and interact in 3D space as a result of human actions. Unlike approaches that model explicit manipulation trajectories or low-level control policies, ObjectForesight seeks to model the *effects* of human actions—the diverse, physically coherent object motions that arise from everyday interactions.

To achieve this overarching goal, the research establishes three key objectives:

1. **Introduce and Formalize the Task of 3D Object Dynamics Prediction from Human Videos:** The paper aims to establish a standardized setting for learning how objects move in the real world based on observational data. This formalization is intended to enable models to leverage the vast quantities of in-the-wild egocentric video data to acquire physical interaction priors without requiring explicit action supervision.
2. **Propose a 3D Object-Centric Dynamics Model (ObjectForesight):** The objective is to develop a model that can predict future 6-DoF trajectories of rigid objects from short egocentric video sequences and monocular geometry. The model is designed to operate in an object-centric coordinate system to enhance generalization across varied objects, scenes, and manipulation styles, thereby capturing the underlying semantics of object affordances.
3. **Construct a Large-Scale Dataset of Object-Centric 3D Trajectories:** A significant challenge in this domain is the lack of suitable training data. The research aims to address this by developing a scalable data curation pipeline. This pipeline transforms passive human videos into structured 3D motion supervision, creating a dataset of over 2 million object-centric 3D trajectories by leveraging advances in segmentation, mesh reconstruction, and 3D pose estimation. This dataset is positioned as the first of its scale, fidelity, and semantic diversity for this task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ObjectForesight is designed to learn a forward dynamics model that predicts future 3D poses of rigid objects from passive human videos. The approach involves two main components: a data curation pipeline and a geometry-aware diffusion model for trajectory prediction.

#### 3.1. Data Curation: From Egocentric Video to 3D 6-DoF Object Trajectories

A scalable, automated pipeline was developed to convert in-the-wild egocentric videos into metrically grounded trajectories of hand-manipulated objects. The pipeline processes videos from EPIC-Kitchens and HOT3D-Clips, following these stages:

1. **Action Segment Prefiltering:** Initial action segments from EPIC-Kitchens are prefiltered to retain clips under 10 seconds with visible hands, ensuring relevant human-object interactions.
2. **Hand-Object Discovery with EgoHOS:** EgoHOS is used to segment hands and candidate manipulated objects frame-wise. Frames lacking hands or object hypotheses are removed. Temporal smoothing is applied to binary hand/object presence indicators to mitigate false segmentations and guide subsequent steps.
3. **Robust Object Masks with Temporal Consensus:** SAM2 is initialized using point prompts derived from EgoHOS masks. Positive prompts come from object mask interiors, while negative prompts are drawn from hand masks, other object masks, and object boundary regions. Temporal consensus prompts (mask intersections over a small window) are used to bias SAM2 towards stable shapes. Morphological operations and de-duplication are applied for temporal stability and unique object instance tracking. Diffusion-VAS is used to generate amodal masks by estimating the complete physical shape of objects, filling occluded regions.
4. **VLM Gating for Manipulation and View Quality:** A two-stage visual-language model (VLM) filter, using InternVL3, is applied. Video-level filtering discards static objects, retaining only those actively manipulated by hand. Frame-level filtering evaluates visibility (absence of blur, limited occlusion).
5. **Object 3D Reconstruction from Clean Views:** TRELLIS reconstructs a 3D object mesh from the selected clean views. This mesh serves as a geometric template for model-based pose estimation, not directly for training the dynamics model. Background clutter is masked out to enhance recovery.
6. **Model-Based 6-DoF Pose with Metric Depth and Amodal Masks:** SpaTrackerV2 provides metric depth and camera geometry. FoundationPose is adapted for 6-DoF pose estimation with three modifications:
 - **Metric Scale Estimation:** TRELLIS meshes lack scale, which is estimated by comparing masked depth points to mesh radii and refined via depth-silhouette alignment. Mesh diameter is locked after initial alignment for consistent error scoring.
 - **Multi-View Initialization:** Up to five clean views are used for FoundationPose initialization, refined through depth alignment and silhouette consistency, with the best-scoring view selected. Initial views with high alignment errors or inconsistencies are rejected.
 - **Bidirectional Tracking with Re-registration:** Tracking proceeds bidirectionally from the optimal seed. Complementary consistency terms (silhouette IoU, geometric residuals, motion deltas) monitor drift. Re-registration is triggered by compounded evidence from these metrics to correct drift under occlusions or rapid ego-motion.
7. **Trajectory Slicing and Final Quality Control:** A sliding window of length C+H (C observed, H predicted) is applied. Trajectories are kept if they remain within a single registration segment and maintain stable projection IoU. All poses are re-expressed in the anchor-frame camera coordinates to remove ego-motion.

This pipeline results in over 2 million short (2-3 second) clips, each containing a sequence of 3D object trajectories, providing metric, temporally coherent pose supervision.

#### 3.2. Predicting Future Trajectories in 3D

The forecasting model learns to generate future pose sequences conditioned on geometric and motion context, combining a geometry-aware encoder with a diffusion-based transformer.

1. **Overview:** Given C observed frames ($I_{1:C}$) and their object poses ($P_{1:C}$), along with an object mesh, the model predicts H future 6-DoF poses ($P_{future}$) relative to the anchor frame (first frame of the prediction horizon). The default setting uses C=3 and H=8.
2. **Scene and Context Encoding:**
 - **Point Cloud Generation:** The anchor-frame depth map is back-projected to form a 3D point cloud $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$. Subsampling and voxel-grid downsampling ensure a fixed-size input (N=4096 points).
 - **Object-Centric Dual-Coordinate Features:** Each point feature is augmented with its position in both anchor-camera coordinates and the object’s local reference frame, providing an explicit geometric prior.
 - **Context Vector ($ctx$):** For each conditioning frame, a 9D pose token $p_k$ is concatenated with a normalized bounding box $B_k$. These 13D descriptors are projected, then pooled via anchor-query cross-attention, producing a 64D vector summarizing recent motion.
 - **PointTransformerV3 (PTV3) Encoder:** The PTV3 encoder processes the point cloud, with its features conditioned by $ctx$ using Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM).
 - **Object-Aware Attentive Pooling:** Point features are pooled into a global scene embedding ($z_{geom} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$) using an attention head that combines content-based scores (from $ctx$) and distance-based scores (proximity to the object origin).
3. **Tokenization of Pose Sequences:**
 - 6-DoF poses $p_t = [x_t, y_t, z_t, r_{t,6D}]$ are reparameterized into depth-normalized tokens $y_t = [u_t, v_t, s_t, r_{t,6D}]$, where $u_t = x_t/z_t$, $v_t = y_t/z_t$, and $s_t = \log z_t$. This improves numerical stability.
 - Tokens are channel-wise standardized. Normalized context-frame poses are prepended as a prefix.
4. **Diffusion Transformer (DiT):**
 - A Diffusion Transformer is used as the temporal backbone. It processes the noised sequence of depth-normalized future pose tokens, conditioned on a diffusion-timestep embedding, the geometric embedding $z_{geom}$, and the normalized context tokens prefix.
 - **AdaLN-Zero Conditioning:** $z_{geom}$ is injected into the DiT via Adaptive Layer Normalization with zero initialization (AdaLN-Zero), modulating normalization parameters and gated residuals within each transformer block.
 - **Forward Diffusion Process and Cosine Schedule:** A standard diffusion framework with a cosine $\beta$-schedule is used, and diffusion timesteps are sampled uniformly.
 - **v-Parameterization and p2 Weighting:** The model predicts a v-parameterized target, stabilizing training. An SNR-weighted regression loss (p2 reweighting) and horizon-aware weighting are applied.
5. **Losses:** The total objective function combines several terms:
 - **v-prediction MSE ($L_v$):** Primary loss in normalized token space.
 - **Auxiliary Pose Loss ($L_{aux}$):** Translation ($L_2$) and geodesic rotation errors (d_geo) on the decoded SE(3) trajectory.
 - **Smoothness Losses ($L_{vel}, L_{acc}$):** L2 and d_geo losses on consecutive pose velocity and acceleration increments to encourage smooth trajectories.
 - **Depth-Floor Penalty ($L_{z\_min}$):** Discourages degenerate predictions with extremely small depth values.
6. **Diffusion Sampling:** At inference, deterministic DDIM with 50 denoising steps is used to sample from Gaussian noise, reconstructing the predicted future trajectory.

The choice of a diffusion model is motivated by its ability to capture the multimodal nature of future object motion (e.g., a mug can be picked up, slid, or rotated), encouraging temporally smooth and physically plausible trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to evaluate the curated dataset, the plausibility and consistency of predictions, and the model's generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics

- The data curation pipeline yielded over 2 million high-quality 3D object trajectories from EPIC-Kitchens, suitable for training multi-modal 3D dynamics models. This demonstrates the scalability and reliability of the automated pipeline (Fig. 4b).
- Motion statistics for the curated EPIC-Kitchens and HOT3D-Clips datasets were presented, showing average total translation of 0.07m and rotation of 30.8° for EPIC-Kitchens, and 0.34m and 118.6° for HOT3D, respectively (Fig. 4a).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- ObjectForesight produced smooth, physically plausible 6-DoF trajectories across various manipulation scenarios (Fig. 5). Examples included lifting a plate, rotating a kettle, and wiping with an eraser, indicating the model's ability to capture realistic interactions and maintain temporal coherence with observed context.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results

- **Overall Performance (Table 1):**
 - ObjectForesight-DiT demonstrated superior performance in 3D trajectory forecasting compared to baselines on both EPIC-Kitchens and HOT3D-Clips datasets across all six trajectory-level metrics (ADE, FDE, DES for translation; ARE, FRE, RES for rotation).
 - On EPIC-Kitchens, ObjectForesight-DiT achieved an average displacement error (ADE) of 0.016m and an average rotation error (ARE) of 2.30°, outperforming the Constant Velocity baseline by 41% in ADE and 45% in FDE.
 - The autoregressive variant (ObjectForesight-AR) generally underperformed the Constant Velocity baseline on most metrics, suggesting that the diffusion formulation is critical for capturing the multimodal nature of future object motion.
 - On HOT3D-Clips, ObjectForesight-DiT maintained its lead (ADE 0.021m, ARE 8.92°), with a larger performance gap relative to Constant Velocity, indicating its effectiveness on more complex object motions.
- **Comparison with Video Generation (Table 1):**
 - When compared against a state-of-the-art video generation model (Luma AI Ray3), ObjectForesight-DiT substantially outperformed it in terms of recovered 6-DoF pose accuracy (ADE 0.029m vs. 0.084m; ARE 7.29° vs. 12.86°). This suggests that directly modeling motion in SE(3) is more effective than inferring it from synthesized frames. This comparison was conducted on a subset due to computational costs.
- **Ablation Studies (Table 2):**
 - **Scene Encoders:** PointTransformerV3 (PTV3) achieved the best results across all metrics compared to other point cloud encoders (DGCNN, PointNet++, SparseConv, No-Encoder). This indicates that a well-chosen geometric backbone, specifically PTV3, provides meaningful gains through effective scene conditioning. SparseConv, in some cases, performed worse than having no encoder, highlighting the importance of the encoder's design.
 - **DiT Scaling:** Scaling up the Diffusion Transformer model (from 6 layers, 384 dimensions to 12 layers, 768 dimensions) consistently improved performance across all metrics. For instance, ARE decreased from 4.242° to 2.299°, and ADE decreased from 0.0193m to 0.0165m, confirming that increased model capacity is beneficial for this task.
- **Window-Size Ablation Studies (Appendix B):**
 - **Context Length (C):** An input context length of C=3 frames was found to be optimal. Shorter contexts (C=1, 2) provided insufficient historical information, while longer contexts (C=5, 10) led to performance degradation, likely due to accumulated noise or overfitting to past trajectories.
 - **Prediction Horizon (H):** Training with a longer horizon (e.g., H=8) sometimes improved short-term predictions (e.g., at H=4) compared to training specifically for the short horizon. However, excessively long training horizons (e.g., H=32) negatively impacted short-term accuracy, while models explicitly trained for longer horizons yielded superior performance for those extended durations, demonstrating a trade-off between short-term precision and long-term stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces and formalizes the task of forecasting future 3D object motion directly from passive human videos, framing it as an object-centric SE(3) trajectory prediction problem. This formulation is significant as it provides a standardized setting for learning how objects move in the real world based on readily available observational data.

A key contribution is the development of a large-scale dataset of object-centric 3D trajectories, curated through an automated pipeline leveraging existing vision models. This dataset, comprising over 2 million metrically grounded trajectories, addresses a significant data scarcity challenge in the field, eliminating the need for manual annotation or motion capture. Its existence provides a resource for training and evaluating models that understand physical interaction dynamics at an unprecedented scale.

ObjectForesight, the proposed model, integrates monocular geometry, recent motion history, and local scene structure within a diffusion-based transformer. Its ability to produce multimodal, physically consistent trajectory predictions is central to its significance. The diffusion formulation effectively captures the inherent uncertainty of future object motion, enabling diverse yet plausible outcomes rather than a single deterministic prediction. The quantitative results demonstrate that ObjectForesight outperforms autoregressive and video-generation baselines, achieving higher accuracy and geometric consistency for 6-DoF trajectories.

The work establishes a scalable framework for learning physically grounded, object-centric dynamics models directly from observation. This capability has several potential impacts:
- **Embodied Visual Reasoning:** ObjectForesight provides a foundational component for advanced embodied visual reasoning systems, enabling agents to predict and understand how objects will respond to interactions.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** The explicit 3D and object-centric nature of the predictions makes the model directly relevant for robotic planning and manipulation policies. Robots could use such models to anticipate object movements and plan their actions more effectively in complex, unconstrained environments.
- **Generalization and Scalability:** By learning from large-scale observational data and operating in object-centric coordinates, the model demonstrates potential for generalization across varied objects, scenes, and manipulation styles, a critical step towards more versatile AI systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines potential extensions, including adopting more expressive representations for non-rigid or articulated objects, exploring longer prediction horizons, and integrating with downstream planning or manipulation policies. These directions suggest that ObjectForesight lays groundwork for richer predictive models of physical interaction and advanced AI capabilities.
