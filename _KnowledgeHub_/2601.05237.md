# ObjectForesight: Predicting Future 3D Object Trajectories from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> ObjectForesight introduces a model for predicting future 3D 6-DoF trajectories of rigid objects from short human videos, leveraging a large-scale dataset of over 2 million object-centric 3D trajectories curated through an automated pipeline. The diffusion-based transformer model predicts multimodal, physically plausible object motions, outperforming baselines in 3D trajectory accuracy on EPIC-Kitchens and HOT3D-Clips datasets.

## Problem

- Computational systems lack the ability to intuitively anticipate how objects move and transform due to human interactions, a key human cognitive skill for planning and action.
- Existing methods for human-object interaction often focus on 2D predictions or abstract latent spaces, lacking explicit 3D grounding and object-level motion dynamics suitable for robotics.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, clean, physically grounded 3D interaction datasets for training models to understand object dynamics from human videos.

## Method

- A scalable, automated pipeline was developed to curate a dataset of over 2 million 3D 6-DoF object trajectories from unconstrained egocentric videos using advanced vision models (e.g., SAM2, TRELLIS, FoundationPose, SpaTrackerV2).
- ObjectForesight, a geometry-aware diffusion model, predicts future 6-DoF object poses by integrating monocular geometry, recent motion history, and local scene structure.
- The model uses a PointTransformerV3 encoder for scene understanding and a Diffusion Transformer with AdaLN-Zero conditioning for multimodal temporal trajectory prediction in a depth-normalized token space.

## Results

- The automated pipeline successfully curated over 2 million high-quality 3D object trajectories from EPIC-Kitchens, providing the first dataset of its scale and fidelity for learning object dynamics.
- ObjectForesight-DiT achieved superior performance in 3D trajectory forecasting, with an Average Displacement Error (ADE) of 0.016m and Average Rotation Error (ARE) of 2.30° on EPIC-Kitchens, outperforming Constant Velocity by 41% in ADE.
- Ablation studies confirmed that PointTransformerV3 as the scene encoder and scaling up the Diffusion Transformer consistently improved performance, demonstrating the impact of strong geometric and temporal modeling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An automated data curation pipeline can reliably extract high-quality, metrically grounded 3D object trajectories from in-the-wild egocentric videos at scale, addressing a major data bottleneck.
> - Directly modeling object motion in SE(3) space using a diffusion-based approach is more effective than inferring it from synthesized frames or relying on autoregressive methods, especially for capturing multimodal future possibilities.
> - Effective scene conditioning via a strong geometric backbone (like PointTransformerV3) combined with sufficient model capacity in the diffusion transformer significantly improves the accuracy and plausibility of 3D object trajectory predictions.
