# Mitigating the Safety Alignment Tax with Null-Space Constrained Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A new reinforcement learning framework, Null-Space constrained Policy Optimization (NSPO), is introduced by researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology to align Large Language Models for safety. This framework projects policy gradients into the null space of general task representations, effectively enhancing safety performance across multiple benchmarks while preserving core general capabilities with minimal degradation.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often suffer from a 'safety alignment tax,' where enhancing safety degrades their general capabilities.
- Existing safety alignment methods, like dual-objective optimization, do not explicitly resolve the underlying conflict between safety and general capability objectives at the gradient level.
- Safety-aligned models can become overly cautious, refusing even non-harmful queries, which reduces their overall utility.

## Method

- The proposed ==Null-Space== constrained Policy Optimization (**NSPO**) is an RL framework that geometrically projects safety policy gradients into a null space.
- This null space is constructed using representations derived from general task data (e.g., common sense, math, code) to ensure safety updates are orthogonal to directions responsible for general capabilities.
- The method constructs a projection matrix from the non-central covariance matrix of general task representations using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), then applies it to safety gradients to preserve core abilities.

## Results

- NSPO achieved strong safety performance, reducing Attack Success Rate (ASR) by up to **16.69%** on SORRY-Bench and consistently outperforming baseline methods across various safety benchmarks.
- The method preserved general capabilities, with performance degradation typically within **1%** on benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, and LiveCodeBench, matching or exceeding methods using mixed datasets.
- NSPO demonstrated data efficiency, achieving comparable performance using only **40%** of the PKU-SafeRLHF dataset, and introduced minimal computational and memory overhead during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Updating model parameters within the null space of general task representations allows for safety improvements without interfering with previously acquired core capabilities.
> - Removing the KL divergence penalty in the underlying GRPO algorithm, combined with null-space projection, allows for more direct safety optimization while implicitly regularizing updates.
> - Theoretical guarantees demonstrate that the projected gradient maintains numerical stability and constitutes a valid descent direction for the safety objective, ensuring effective and constrained optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved proficiency across various applications, including language comprehension, instruction following, code generation, and mathematical reasoning. Their increasing deployment in real-world contexts necessitates ensuring their behavior aligns with human values, societal norms, and ethical principles, a process commonly referred to as safety alignment.

However, a significant challenge in LLM safety alignment, particularly when utilizing Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques, is the "safety alignment tax." This phenomenon describes the degradation of a model's learned general abilities when its safety is enhanced. Previous research has observed a trade-off where models optimized for safety may become overly cautious, refusing even non-harmful queries, while models focused on general capabilities may compromise safety. This suggests an inherent conflict between optimizing for general capabilities and safety.

Existing approaches to address the alignment tax often frame safety alignment as a dual-objective optimization problem. These methods typically involve modeling separate objective functions for safety and general capability, then balancing their contributions within a combined objective function. Some strategies also incorporate large amounts of general task data into safety training sets to counteract capability forgetting. Despite these efforts, these methods generally do not explicitly address the underlying conflict between safety and general capability objectives at the gradient level during the training process. This gap in explicitly resolving the objective conflict is where the presented work positions itself, by proposing a method that geometrically constrains safety policy updates to preserve general abilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the observation that while safety alignment is crucial for the responsible deployment of LLMs, current methods often incur a "safety alignment tax." This tax manifests as a degradation of the model's general capabilities when safety is enhanced, leading to models that might be safe but less useful or overly cautious. Specifically, the authors identify that the safety gradient itself, when applied broadly, can weaken a model's previously acquired general knowledge and skills. This implicit conflict between safety and general capability objectives is not directly resolved by existing dual-objective optimization or data mixing strategies, which primarily balance contributions rather than disentangling the update directions.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a novel RL framework for LLM safety alignment that explicitly mitigates the safety alignment tax.** The core of this objective is to preserve the model's core general abilities while enhancing its safety.
2. **Introduce a geometric projection mechanism for safety policy gradients.** This involves projecting safety gradients into a null space defined by general task representations, thereby constraining safety updates to be orthogonal to the directions responsible for general capabilities.
3. **Provide theoretical guarantees for the proposed framework.** This includes demonstrating that the method preserves the model’s original core capabilities and ensures a valid descent direction for effective safety alignment.
4. **Achieve state-of-the-art safety performance.** The method aims to surpass existing safety alignment techniques across multiple benchmarks.
5. **Minimize performance degradation on general tasks.** This objective directly addresses the alignment tax, ensuring that improved safety does not come at a significant cost to capabilities like math, code, and instruction-following.
6. **Demonstrate data efficiency.** The research aims to achieve promising safety performance using a reduced amount of human-annotated safety data, thereby reducing the reliance on extensive mixed datasets often used in other alignment methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Null-Space constrained Policy Optimization (NSPO), is an RL framework designed to perform safety alignment while preserving the general capabilities of LLMs. It builds upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an RL algorithm that estimates advantages by normalizing rewards within groups of sampled responses, thereby eliminating the need for a separate critic model and maintaining reliable gradient signals even with sparse rewards. The overall objective function for GRPO includes a term for maximizing reward and a KL divergence penalty to a reference policy.

#### 3.1. Motivation for Decoupled Update from General Capabilities

The core idea behind NSPO is to ensure that safety-related parameter updates do not interfere with the parameters responsible for general capabilities. This is motivated by considering a linear transformation within an LLM (e.g., a weight matrix `W` for an FFN or MHA). If an update `∆` to `W` (where `W_aligned = W_base + ∆`) is in the null space of a representation `K` derived from general reasoning data (i.e., `∆K = 0`), then the mapping `V = W_base K` remains unchanged (`W_aligned K = W_base K = V`). This implies that updates in the null space of `K` preserve the model’s general reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.2. Policy Gradient Projection

To implement this, NSPO geometrically projects the safety policy gradients into the null space of general task representations.
1. **Construction of the Null Space:** Instead of directly using `K` (which can be large), the method uses the non-central covariance matrix `KK^T ∈ R^(dxd)`, where `d` is the representation dimension and `N` is the number of tokens. This is computationally efficient because `d << N`. A Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is applied to `KK^T` to obtain `U, Λ, U^T`. The eigenvectors in `U` corresponding to non-zero eigenvalues (or eigenvalues above a certain threshold, e.g., 5e-4) are identified. The remaining eigenvectors form `ˆU`, which spans the null space.
2. **Projection Matrix:** A projection matrix `P = ˆUˆU^T` is constructed.
3. **Gradient Projection:** The safety policy gradient, `∇_W J` (derived from the reward maximization term in GRPO, excluding the KL divergence), is projected into the null space using this matrix: `∇_W J_NSPO = (∇_W J)P`. This ensures that the updated parameters `(W - η∇_W J_NSPO)K = WK = V`, preserving general capabilities.
4. **Theoretical Guarantees:**
 - **Gradient Stability (Proposition 4.1):** NSPO ensures numerical stability by proving that the spectral norm of the projected gradient is bounded by the norm of the original gradient, meaning `||∇_W J_NSPO||_2 ≤ ||∇_W J||_2`. Orthogonal projection is a non-expansive mapping.
 - **Valid Descent Direction (Theorem 4.2):** It is proven that `∇_W J_NSPO` is a valid descent direction for the safety objective function `J`. The inner product `⟨∇_W J, ∇_W J_NSPO⟩` is non-negative, ensuring that descending along the projected gradient improves safety performance.
5. **Removal of KL Divergence:** Unlike standard GRPO, NSPO removes the KL divergence penalty `D_KL[π_θ || π_ref]`. The rationale is that the KL penalty can conflict with the alignment objective by pushing the policy towards a potentially unsafe reference model. The null-space projection implicitly acts as a regularization, preventing over-optimization while ensuring safety objective descent and capability preservation.
6. **Computational Complexity and Memory:** The method introduces minimal overhead. The SVD for `KK^T` is a one-time `O(d^3)` cost. During training, the projection step is `O(d^3)`, which is negligible compared to the LLM's forward/backward passes (`O(n^2 d + nd^2)` where `n` is sequence length and `n >> d`). GPU memory usage is `O(d^2)` due to an offloading mechanism.

#### 3.3. Experiment Setup

- **Base Models:** Llama3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (7B parameters each).
- **RL Framework:** Verl.
- **Safety Reward Model:** Llama-Guard-4-12B for evaluating safety rewards.
- **Hyperparameters:** Default GRPO hyperparameters (`β = 0.1, ϵ = 0.2`) for NSPO, with the eigenvector threshold for null-space construction set to `5e-4`.
- **Datasets:**
 - **Safety Training:** 40% subset of PKU-SafeRLHF (11K samples). Baselines used the full dataset.
 - **General Capability for K:** 1,000 randomly sampled instances from mixed datasets: Common sense (Dubois et al., 2023), Math (Cobbe et al., 2021), and Code (Xia et al., 2025).
- **Baselines:** DPO-H (helpfulness), DPO-S (safety), DPO-Mix (50/50 mix), SafeRLHF, PeCAN, MoCAN, W-DOOR, and BFPO.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **Safety (7 benchmarks):** AdvBench, PKU-SafeRLHF, HarmBench, JailbreakBench, SORRY-Bench (Harmful Query types); HarmfulQA, ALERT (Red-team Query types).
 - **General Capability (7 benchmarks):** MMLU, SuperGPQA (General Knowledge); AlpacaEval (Instruction Following); LiveCodeBench (Code Generation); GSM8K, MATH, OlympiadBench (Math & Reasoning).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Safety:** Attack Success Rate (ASR), evaluated by GPT-4 and benchmark-specific classification models (e.g., for SORRY-Bench).
 - **General Capability:** Accuracy, Win Rate, Pass@1.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to evaluate NSPO's effectiveness in safety alignment while preserving general capabilities, the impact of the projection mechanism, and the role of the projection matrix construction.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Q1)

- **Safety Alignment:** NSPO demonstrated strong safety performance across all tested benchmarks on both Llama3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. For instance, on Llama3, NSPO reduced ASR by up to 1.30% on AdvBench, 0.28% on PKU-Safe, 7.32% on HarmBench, 16.69% on SORRY-Bench, 1.77% on HarmfulQA, and 1.02% on ALERT compared to base models. It also showed performance gains over previous methods (e.g., up to 2.63% on HarmBench and 1.8% on JailBreakBench). On Qwen2.5, NSPO achieved ASRs as low as 0.29% on AdvBench and 0.50% on HarmBench, significantly outperforming other baselines.
- **Preservation of General Capabilities:** Despite training with only a safety-focused dataset (40% of PKU-SafeRLHF), models trained with NSPO maintained robust performance across general capability tasks. The performance degradation on benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, and LiveCodeBench was typically within 1%, with a few instances showing up to a 2.67% drop. This performance was comparable to or better than BFPO, which explicitly uses a mixed dataset of both safety and general capability data. In contrast, most other safety alignment methods exhibited more substantial degradation in general capabilities, particularly in mathematical reasoning and code generation.

#### 4.2. Visualization and Ablation Study (Q2)

- **Ablation Study (GRPO w/o KL, GRPO, NSPO):**
 - GRPO w/o KL achieved strong safety alignment but resulted in significant degradation of general capabilities.
 - Standard GRPO (with KL constraint) successfully mitigated capability loss but led to reduced safety performance.
 - NSPO achieved an balance by maintaining effective safety alignment while minimizing impact on general capabilities. This indicates that the null-space projection effectively constrains safety optimization directions without compromising general abilities.
- **Visualization of Parameters and Hidden Representations:**
 - **Parameter Shift:** NSPO exhibited a larger shift in model parameters compared to GRPO and GRPO w/o KL, suggesting exploration in different parameter spaces for optimization.
 - **Hidden Representation Shift:** For safety-related prompts, NSPO showed a larger hidden representation shift than GRPO and GRPO w/o KL. For general capability prompts, NSPO's hidden representation shift was roughly on par with GRPO and smaller than GRPO w/o KL. This supports the hypothesis that NSPO directs updates to enhance safety while preserving existing general knowledge.
 - **Token-level Gradient:** Visualization confirmed that after null-space projection, core tokens relevant to safety showed more significant contributions to model updates, providing interpretability for NSPO's mechanism.

#### 4.3. Impact of the Projection Matrix K (Q3)

- **Data Source for K:** Constructing the projection matrix `K` using a mixture of data from various domains (Common sense, Code, Math) led to higher utility scores while maintaining robust safety, outperforming `K` matrices constructed from single-domain data. This suggests that a diverse representation of general capabilities is beneficial for effective projection.
- **Sample Size of General Data:** Increasing the sample size of general data used to construct `K` (from 500 to 2,000 instances) generally improved utility but showed a decreasing trend in safety. Larger sample sizes result in smaller null spaces, which impose stronger constraints on the model's exploration space, making it harder to optimize for safety.
- **Eigenvector Threshold:** A moderate eigenvector threshold (e.g., `5e-4`) for selecting `ˆU` achieved the best balance between model safety and general capabilities. Smaller thresholds preserved general capabilities better but degraded safety, while larger thresholds improved safety at the expense of utility.

#### 4.4. Space and Computation Complexity

- **Data Efficiency:** NSPO demonstrated data efficiency, achieving comparable model performance when trained on only 40% of the PKU-SafeRLHF dataset compared to using the full 100%. Training logs indicated that rewards plateaued after 40% of the data, suggesting limited further improvement with more data.
- **Training Efficiency:** NSPO introduced a minimal increase in memory consumption and computational cost. On Llama3-8B-Instruct, NSPO's training time per step (84.824s) and memory (72.073GB) were slightly higher than GRPO w/o KL (81.156s, 68.746GB) but comparable to or better than standard GRPO (108.490s, 70.495GB), demonstrating its practical feasibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Null-Space constrained Policy Optimization (NSPO) offers a novel approach to addressing a fundamental challenge in the alignment of Large Language Models: the "safety alignment tax." The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to simultaneously enhance LLM safety while preserving their general capabilities, a trade-off that has hindered the widespread and responsible deployment of highly capable models.

The potential impact of NSPO is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced LLM Utility and Safety:** By explicitly decoupling safety-related updates from general capabilities, NSPO enables the creation of LLMs that are both safer and more performant across a wide range of tasks. This is crucial for applications where both reliability and versatility are required, such as in education, customer service, or scientific research.
- **Theoretical Foundation for Constrained Optimization:** The theoretical proofs demonstrating gradient stability and a valid descent direction provide a robust foundation for NSPO. This geometric approach to constrained optimization offers a distinct paradigm compared to regularization or dual-objective methods, potentially inspiring new research directions in addressing complex multi-objective problems in machine learning.
- **Data Efficiency and Reduced Resource Burden:** NSPO's demonstrated data efficiency, requiring only a fraction of human-annotated safety data to achieve strong performance, can significantly reduce the cost and resources associated with LLM alignment. This makes advanced alignment techniques more accessible to researchers and organizations with limited datasets or computational budgets.
- **Mitigation of Over-Censorship:** By maintaining general capabilities, NSPO helps prevent the "overly cautious" behavior observed in some safety-aligned models, where benign queries are refused. This ensures that models remain helpful and accurate without compromising safety.
- **Interpretability and Control:** The concept of projecting gradients into a null space defined by specific capabilities offers a degree of interpretability regarding how different objectives influence model parameters. This could lead to more controllable and predictable model behaviors during alignment.
- **Broader Applicability:** The underlying principle of null-space projection could be generalized to other multi-objective optimization problems in LLMs or other deep learning domains where preserving certain characteristics while optimizing for others is critical.
