# Mitigating the Safety Alignment Tax with Null-Space Constrained Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A new reinforcement learning framework, Null-Space constrained Policy Optimization (NSPO), is introduced by researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology to align Large Language Models for safety. This framework projects policy gradients into the null space of general task representations, effectively enhancing safety performance across multiple benchmarks while preserving core general capabilities with minimal degradation.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often suffer from a 'safety alignment tax,' where enhancing safety degrades their general capabilities.
- Existing safety alignment methods, like dual-objective optimization, do not explicitly resolve the underlying conflict between safety and general capability objectives at the gradient level.
- Safety-aligned models can become overly cautious, refusing even non-harmful queries, which reduces their overall utility.

## Method

- The proposed ==Null-Space== constrained Policy Optimization (**NSPO**) is an RL framework that geometrically projects safety policy gradients into a null space.
- This null space is constructed using representations derived from general task data (e.g., common sense, math, code) to ensure safety updates are orthogonal to directions responsible for general capabilities.
- The method constructs a projection matrix from the non-central covariance matrix of general task representations using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), then applies it to safety gradients to preserve core abilities.

## Results

- NSPO achieved strong safety performance, reducing Attack Success Rate (ASR) by up to **16.69%** on SORRY-Bench and consistently outperforming baseline methods across various safety benchmarks.
- The method preserved general capabilities, with performance degradation typically within **1%** on benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, and LiveCodeBench, matching or exceeding methods using mixed datasets.
- NSPO demonstrated data efficiency, achieving comparable performance using only **40%** of the PKU-SafeRLHF dataset, and introduced minimal computational and memory overhead during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Updating model parameters within the null space of general task representations allows for safety improvements without interfering with previously acquired core capabilities.
> - Removing the KL divergence penalty in the underlying GRPO algorithm, combined with null-space projection, allows for more direct safety optimization while implicitly regularizing updates.
> - Theoretical guarantees demonstrate that the projected gradient maintains numerical stability and constitutes a valid descent direction for the safety objective, ensuring effective and constrained optimization.
