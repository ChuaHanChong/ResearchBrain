# AR-VRM: Imitating Human Motions for Visual Robot Manipulation with Analogical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> AR-VRM pretrains a keypoint-predicting VLM on Ego4D human hand keypoints, then fine-tunes on robot data by retrieving similar human demos and learning a learnable analogical-reasoning map between hand keypoints and robot arm components. Achieves state-of-the-art on CALVIN, especially under 10% data and unseen scenes/language.

## Problem

- Existing VRM approaches pretrain on web vision-language data unrelated to manipulation, giving weak guidance for robot action learning.
- Prior human-video pretraining methods (contrastive learning, pixel-level future-frame prediction) learn implicitly, absorbing irrelevant background and pixel noise.
- Robot trajectory datasets with paired language, vision, and action are costly to collect, limiting generalization under data-scarce fine-tuning.

## Method

- ==Keypoint VLM pretraining==: extracts 3D hand keypoints via ==InterHand== from Ego4D videos and trains a transformer to predict future keypoints from language and image tokens via ==next-token MSE loss==.
- ==Retrieval-based fine-tuning==: retrieves top-J human action videos most similar to the robot sample by ==cosine similarity== of language and visual embeddings across timesteps.
- **Analogical Reasoning** module: learns a matrix mapping human hand keypoint features to robot arm component features, fused with robot state features to compute an auxiliary state-prediction loss.

## Results

- On CALVIN ABCD to D full-data setting, average success rate improves from **84.2%** to **85.4%** over GR-1 baseline.
- Under unseen-scene ABC to D generalization, average success rate jumps from **61.2%** to **65.9%**, a **4.7-point** gain over prior SOTA.
- In **10%** data few-shot setting, 5-task success rate rises from **40.0%** to **45.6%**; real-robot articulated manipulation and object transportation also beat RT-1, MT-R3M, GR-1 baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit structured signals (keypoints) from cross-domain human video transfer more cleanly than implicit pixel or contrastive representations for action learning.
> - Freezing the pretrained keypoint encoder/head while fine-tuning the VLM backbone balances knowledge retention against overfitting to scarce robot data.
> - Retrieval-augmented analogical mapping between human and robot embodiments boosts generalization most exactly where robot data is scarcest (few-shot, unseen scenes/language).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Visual Robot Manipulation (VRM) aims to enable robots to execute tasks based on natural language instructions and visual observations. A central challenge in VRM is the scarcity of multi-modal robot data, which includes paired images, language instructions, and robot action states. Collecting such data is costly and time-consuming, limiting the performance and generalization ability of robot learning models.

Previous approaches to address this data deficiency have largely focused on vision-language pre-training (VLP) using large-scale datasets. These methods can be broadly categorized:
1. **Utilization of general web data:** Some studies pre-train models on internet-scale web data comprising images and texts. However, this data often lacks direct relevance to robot manipulation tasks (e.g., visual question-answering about animals), thus offering limited knowledge transfer.
2. **Implicit learning from human action videos:** Other approaches leverage human action video datasets, such as Ego4D, which are more pertinent to manipulation tasks. These methods typically involve implicit learning mechanisms, such as contrastive learning in feature space or pixel-level future frame prediction using generative models. While these methods acquire some human action knowledge, they often introduce irrelevant background information or pixel-level noise, which can hinder the model's ability to focus on the essential action patterns and limit generalization, particularly in data-scarce robotic scenarios.

This research positions itself by identifying a gap in existing methods: the lack of explicit extraction and utilization of human action knowledge. The proposed AR-VRM framework aims to overcome the limitations of implicit learning by explicitly imitating human motions through hand keypoints. This approach hypothesizes that focusing on the kinematic details of human hands, which are central to manipulation, can provide a more direct and efficient way to transfer human dexterity to robots, thereby enhancing generalization and performance, especially when robot-specific training data is limited. The work attempts to bridge the morphological gap between human hands and robotic end-effectors by introducing an analogical reasoning mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and generalization ability of Visual Robot Manipulation (VRM) systems, particularly under conditions of limited robot-specific training data. This overarching goal is motivated by the inherent challenges of data collection in robotics and the limitations of current pre-training paradigms.

The detailed key objectives and motivations are:

1. **To compensate for the deficiency of robot data:** Robot data, comprising synchronized visual observations, natural language instructions, and precise robot states/actions, is expensive and labor-intensive to acquire. This scarcity restricts the complexity of tasks robots can learn and their ability to generalize to novel situations. The motivation is to leverage abundant, readily available large-scale human action video datasets as a complementary data source.

2. **To move beyond implicit learning for human action knowledge transfer:** Existing methods that use human action videos for pre-training often learn implicitly. This involves either learning general visual representations through contrastive tasks or predicting future frames at a pixel level. The motivation behind AR-VRM is that such implicit methods, while transferring some knowledge, also tend to capture extraneous visual information (e.g., background clutter, irrelevant objects) and pixel-level noise, diluting the focus on critical manipulation cues. This can lead to inefficient learning and poor generalization in downstream robotic tasks.

3. **To explicitly extract and utilize human action knowledge:** The paper posits that the essence of manipulation lies in the specific movements and interactions of hands with objects. Therefore, a key objective is to explicitly extract human action knowledge, specifically in terms of hand keypoints, from large-scale human video datasets. This is motivated by the hypothesis that focusing directly on these kinematic keypoints allows the model to learn the core motion patterns relevant to manipulation, unburdened by irrelevant visual context.

4. **To establish a functional correlation between human hand kinematics and robot arm components:** A significant challenge in transferring human-learned actions to robots is the morphological difference between a human hand and a robotic end-effector. The objective is to develop a mechanism, termed "Analogical Reasoning," to map the learned human hand keypoint actions to the operational components of a robotic arm. This is motivated by the belief that despite physical differences, human and robot manipulation share fundamental geometric and functional correlations (e.g., gripping actions, approaching objects). Learning this explicit correspondence aims to guide the robot to imitate human actions effectively and interpret human motion patterns in a robot-centric manner.

5. **To enhance generalization across diverse scenarios:** By explicitly learning core action knowledge and establishing robust human-robot mappings, the research aims to improve the robot's generalization ability. This includes generalizing to unseen environments (scenes), novel language instructions, and scenarios with limited robot training data (few-shot learning), which are critical for deploying robots in varied real-world applications. The motivation is to create more adaptable and robust robotic manipulation policies.

In summary, the motivation stems from the limitations of data availability and the inefficiency of current implicit learning methods in VRM. The objectives are to introduce an explicit, keypoint-centric learning paradigm from human demonstrations and an analogical reasoning mechanism to bridge the human-robot morphological gap, thereby fostering more generalized and data-efficient robot manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Visual Robot Manipulation (VRM) task is formulated as a sequence prediction problem where a robot model maps a language instruction and a history of visual observations and robot states to a future robot action, which is defined as the variation of state parameters. The scarcity of robot-specific multi-modal data motivates the proposed **AR-VRM** framework, which comprises two main stages: Keypoint Vision-Language Model (VLM) Pretraining and Robot Fine-tuning with Analogical Reasoning.

#### 3.1. Keypoint Vision-Language Model (VLM) Pretraining

This stage focuses on explicitly acquiring human action knowledge from large-scale human videos, expressed through hand keypoints.

- **Human Action Data:** The Ego4D dataset, which consists of egocentric human-object interaction videos with natural language annotations, is utilized. An offline hand pose estimation model, InterHand, is applied to each video frame to extract 3D hand keypoints ($k_t \in \mathbb{R}^K$, where K is the number of keypoints). This transforms the original human video dataset into a processed dataset comprising language descriptions, visual frames, and corresponding 3D hand keypoints.
- **Multi-modal Encoders:**
 - **Language Encoder:** A pre-trained CLIP text encoder extracts language embeddings, followed by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for projection into a fixed dimension $d$.
 - **Image Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) pre-trained with MAE is used as the image encoder. It outputs a global CLS token and local patch tokens. A Perceiver Resampler reduces the number of patch tokens, and both global and local representations are projected to dimension $d$ via MLPs.
 - **Keypoint Encoder:** HandFormer is employed as the keypoint encoder, with its output also projected to dimension $d$ by an MLP.
- **VLM Architecture and Training:** The aligned tokens from the three modalities (language, visual, and keypoint) are concatenated sequentially and fed into Transformer layers. The model is trained using a next token prediction objective. Specifically, at each timestep $t$, the Transformer predicts the keypoint token for $t$, which is then projected back to a keypoint vector by a KeypointHead (an MLP). The pretraining loss is the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted keypoints and the ground truth keypoints. During this stage, the parameters of the CLIP text encoder and the pre-trained image encoder are kept frozen.

#### 3.2. Robot Fine-tuning with Analogical Reasoning

This stage adapts the pre-trained Keypoint VLM to robot manipulation tasks using limited robot data, incorporating an Analogical Reasoning (AR) module.

- **Robot Data Encoding:** Similar to pretraining, language and visual inputs are encoded. Robot states, comprising the 6D pose of the end-effector ($s_e$) and a binary gripper state ($s_g$), are encoded by separate MLPs and then concatenated and projected to dimension $d$.
- **Robot State Prediction:** The pre-trained VLM's Transformer layers process the sequence of language, visual, and robot state tokens. It predicts the future robot state token, which is then projected to robot states by a StateHead (an MLP). The loss is an MSE between predicted and ground truth robot states.
- **Analogical Reasoning (AR) Module:**
 - **Human Action Retrieval:** For a given robot data sample, relevant human action videos are retrieved from the Ego4D database. Retrieval is based on the cosine similarity between the robot data's language and visual features and those of the human videos. This ensures that retrieved human demonstrations are contextually similar in both task and visual observation.
 - **Analogical Map:** A learnable analogical map matrix $m \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ is introduced, where $S$ is the number of robot arm components and $K$ is the number of human hand keypoints. This matrix represents the influence of each human hand keypoint on each robot arm component.
 - **Imitated Robot State Features:** The features from the last layer of the keypoint head (for retrieved human data) and the robot state head (for the current robot data) are used as representations for human keypoint nodes and robot state nodes, respectively. The imitated robot state features are computed as a weighted sum: $(1-\alpha) \cdot m \cdot f_{k_j} + \alpha \cdot f_s$, where $f_{k_j}$ are the human keypoint features from a retrieved video, $f_s$ are the current robot state features, and $\alpha$ is a learnable parameter controlling the influence of keypoint features.
 - **AR Loss:** A new linear layer processes the imitated robot state features to predict new robot states, and an MSE loss is computed against the ground truth robot states.
- **Overall Fine-tuning Loss:** The total fine-tuning loss is a weighted sum of the robot state prediction loss and the analogical reasoning loss.
- **Parameter Management during Fine-tuning:** During fine-tuning, the parameters of the keypoint encoder and keypoint head are fixed. The Transformer layers of the VLM are fine-tuned. This design aims to preserve the knowledge of encoding and predicting keypoints learned during pre-training, prevent overfitting to the limited robot data, and allow the VLM to adapt to robotic kinematics while benefiting from the explicit human action guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the AR-VRM framework across various experimental settings on the CALVIN benchmark and real-world robot experiments.

#### 4.1. Performance on CALVIN Benchmark

- **Full Dataset Multitask Learning (ABCD→D):** When trained on the full dataset (4 scenes) and evaluated on a specific scene, AR-VRM achieved an average success rate of 85.4%, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) method, GR-1, by +1.2%. This indicates improved performance even with ample robot data.
- **Unseen Scene Generalization (ABC→D):** In this challenging setting, where the model was trained on three scenes and evaluated on an entirely unseen fourth scene, AR-VRM demonstrated substantial improvement. It achieved an average success rate of 65.9%, a notable increase of +4.7% over GR-1 (61.2%). This highlights the strong generalization capability of AR-VRM across different environments.
- **Data-Efficient Few-Shot Learning (10% training data):** Under a scenario with only 10% of the robot training data, AR-VRM significantly outperformed baselines. The average success rate for completing tasks rose from 40.0% (GR-1) to 45.6%, and the average length of successfully completed tasks increased to 2.28. This finding underscores the effectiveness of explicitly imitating human actions under data scarcity.
- **Unseen Instruction Language Generalization:** When evaluated on language instructions not encountered during training, AR-VRM consistently achieved SOTA performance. It improved the average success rate from 43.3% (GR-1) to 46.6%, suggesting that the integration of relevant human action demonstrations aids the robot in generalizing to new verbal commands.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

- **Object Transportation:**
 - **Seen Objects:** AR-VRM maintained high success rates when transporting trained objects in disturbed scenes (with distractors or altered backgrounds), outperforming baselines.
 - **Unseen Instances:** The method showed minimal performance drop when handling new instances of previously seen object categories, demonstrating strong in-category generalization.
 - **Unseen Categories:** AR-VRM exhibited superior performance compared to baselines (RT-1, MT-R3M, GR-1) when generalizing to entirely untrained object categories (e.g., tomato, yellow peach).
- **Articulated Manipulation:** AR-VRM surpassed baselines in articulated manipulation tasks, though it exhibited some failure modes, such as incomplete drawer closure or missed handle engagement during opening tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Proposed Modules:** Ablation studies confirmed the individual contribution of each component:
 - Training directly with robot data (without pretraining/guidance) resulted in limited performance (60.0% Avg. Rate).
 - Adding keypoint VLM pretraining alone significantly improved performance (81.3% Avg. Rate).
 - Introducing human action video retrieval during fine-tuning (without analogical mapping) further boosted the rate to 84.3%.
 - The complete AR-VRM framework, including keypoint VLM pretraining, retrieval, and analogical reasoning, achieved the best performance (85.4% Avg. Rate), validating the cumulative effect of the proposed modules.
- **Design Choices for Robot Fine-tuning:** Analysis of fine-tuning parameter strategies showed that freezing the keypoint encoder and keypoint head while fine-tuning the VLM parameters yielded the optimal performance (85.4% Avg. Rate). Freezing the VLM parameters or making keypoint parameters trainable resulted in performance drops, indicating the importance of VLM adaptation and stable keypoint feature encoding.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **Prediction Visualization:** An example of "grasp the blue block in drawer" demonstrated AR-VRM's ability to retrieve functionally similar human actions (e.g., "picks a receipt from the drawer"). By imitating the human action process of approaching and grasping, the robot successfully completed the task.
- **Analogical Map Visualization:** The learned analogical map between human hand keypoints and robot arm components revealed interpretable correlations. The robot's grasper (end-effector) showed strong relationships with human fingertips, which are critical for grasping. The root segment of the robotic arm correlated with the human palm, indicating its role in overall movement direction. This suggests that the model learned a functionally meaningful mapping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AR-VRM framework introduces a novel approach to Visual Robot Manipulation by explicitly leveraging human action knowledge through hand keypoints and analogical reasoning. This work addresses several critical limitations in the current VRM landscape, leading to significant potential impact.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity:** A primary challenge in VRM is the cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale, high-quality robot demonstration data. AR-VRM provides a method to effectively utilize abundant human action video datasets (like Ego4D) as a substitute for or complement to robot data. This is particularly significant for few-shot learning scenarios, where the method demonstrates substantial performance gains, indicating a pathway to making robot learning more accessible and less resource-intensive.

2. **Enhanced Generalization:** By focusing on explicit human hand keypoints, the model learns fundamental manipulation kinematics rather than superficial visual cues. This explicit knowledge transfer, combined with the analogical reasoning mechanism, leads to improved generalization abilities. The demonstrated superior performance in unseen scenes, with unseen language instructions, and for unseen object instances/categories, suggests that AR-VRM contributes to developing more robust and adaptable robotic policies for diverse real-world environments.

3. **Novel Knowledge Transfer Mechanism:** The introduction of a keypoint Vision-Language Model pre-training scheme and an Analogical Reasoning module represents a novel approach to human-to-robot skill transfer. Unlike implicit methods that might capture noise or irrelevant visual information, AR-VRM's explicit imitation of hand keypoints allows for a more focused and efficient learning of manipulative actions. The analogical map provides an interpretable way to bridge the morphological gap between human hands and robotic end-effectors, establishing functional correspondences that guide robot movements.

4. **Improved Robustness and Adaptability:** The ability to retrieve and imitate relevant human actions based on task instructions and visual observations allows the robot to draw from a wider range of experiences. This can enhance the robot's capacity to handle variations in tasks, objects, and environments, leading to more resilient manipulation skills.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Robot Deployment:** By reducing the reliance on costly robot-specific data, AR-VRM could significantly accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots in various domains, including manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and hazardous environments.
- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework's ability to interpret human-like demonstrations and generalize from them could lead to more intuitive methods for teaching robots new tasks, potentially enabling non-expert users to program robots more easily.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** This research contributes to the broader field of embodied AI by exploring how rich human behavioral data can be effectively distilled and transferred to embodied agents, moving towards more generally capable and intelligent robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The explicit keypoint-based learning and analogical reasoning paradigms open new avenues for research into more sophisticated human-robot skill transfer mechanisms, potentially incorporating more complex human sensory and motor information.

In conclusion, AR-VRM presents a scientifically grounded method for advancing visual robot manipulation, particularly in data-constrained settings, by intelligently leveraging human expertise.
