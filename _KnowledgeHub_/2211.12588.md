# Program of Thoughts Prompting: Disentangling Computation from Reasoning for Numerical Reasoning Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Program of Thoughts (PoT) prompting enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance their numerical reasoning capabilities by generating executable programs (e.g., Python code) for computation, which are then processed by an external interpreter. This method consistently improves performance on mathematical word problems and financial question-answering tasks, yielding gains of approximately 8% on MWP datasets and 15% on financial datasets in few-shot settings compared to Chain-of-Thought.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are prone to arithmetic errors and struggle with complex mathematical expressions when performing calculations directly within their natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT).
- CoT prompting is inefficient and error-prone for numerical problems involving repetitive calculations or complex iterations.
- The inherent computational weaknesses of LLMs limit their reliability and accuracy in quantitative reasoning tasks, even when their high-level reasoning is conceptually sound.

## Method

- The method prompts Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate ==formal programs (e.g., Python code)== that encapsulate the reasoning steps for numerical problems.
- An ==external program interpreter== (e.g., Python 3.8 with SymPy) executes the generated code to perform all calculations and obtain the final answer.
- This approach allows LLMs to leverage their strengths in language understanding while ==offloading precise computation to a deterministic system==.

## Results

- PoT consistently outperformed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, showing an average accuracy gain of approximately **8%** on Math Word Problem (MWP) datasets and **15%** on financial datasets in few-shot settings.
- Zero-shot PoT often surpassed the performance of few-shot CoT, highlighting the method's strong generalization capabilities without needing task-specific examples.
- When combined with Self-Consistency (PoT+SC), the method achieved the best-known results on all evaluated MWP datasets (pre-GPT-4), and `gpt-3.5-turbo` emerged as a top-performing LLM backend for PoT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling reasoning (handled by the LLM) from precise computation (delegated to an external interpreter) is critical for achieving robust numerical problem-solving with LLMs.
> - Prompting LLMs to generate executable code, rather than natural language rationales, enables them to leverage external, exact computational tools effectively.
> - Using semantically meaningful variable names and encouraging multi-step program generation significantly aids LLMs in structuring their reasoning for complex numerical problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper significantly contributes to the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their application to complex reasoning tasks, particularly numerical reasoning. It directly builds upon and seeks to address limitations of the groundbreaking "Chain-of-Thought (CoT)" prompting paradigm, which revolutionized how LLMs approach multi-step problems.

Before CoT, LLMs often struggled with complex reasoning, providing direct answers that lacked intermediate steps or logical coherence. CoT (Wei et al., 2022; Kojima et al., 2022) demonstrated that by prompting LLMs to generate intermediate "thoughts" or "rationales" in natural language, their reasoning capabilities could be significantly unleashed, leading to state-of-the-art performance across various reasoning benchmarks, including mathematical word problems.

However, the authors identify a critical weakness in CoT when applied to numerical reasoning: LLMs, despite their impressive linguistic capabilities, are inherently poor at precise arithmetic calculation, solving complex equations, or efficiently handling iterative processes. They are essentially "fuzzy calculators" prone to errors, especially with large numbers or intricate mathematical expressions. This inherent limitation means that while CoT helps LLMs *reason* about the steps, the *computation* within those steps remains error-prone.

"Program of Thoughts" (PoT) emerges as a direct response to this challenge. It positions itself as an evolution of CoT, specifically designed to "disentangle computation from reasoning." Instead of relying on the LLM to perform calculations within its natural language chain of thought, PoT instructs the LLM to generate formal programs (e.g., Python code) that describe the reasoning steps. These programs can then be executed by an external, precise program interpreter. This approach marries the LLM's strength in language understanding and high-level reasoning with the interpreter's exactness in symbolic computation.

This work fits into a broader trend of augmenting LLMs with external tools or knowledge sources to overcome their inherent limitations. This includes:
- **Tool Use / Augmented LLMs:** PoT is a prime example of an LLM leveraging an external tool (a Python interpreter) for enhanced capabilities. This aligns with approaches like ReAct (Yao et al., 2022), which integrates reasoning and acting (using external tools like search engines). The authors explicitly mention this connection, generalizing Python programs to broader API calls.
- **Neural-Symbolic Integration:** PoT represents a powerful form of neural-symbolic integration, where the neural network (LLM) handles the symbolic manipulation (generating code) and high-level reasoning, while the symbolic system (interpreter) handles precise computation. This hybrid approach is increasingly seen as crucial for robust AI systems.
- **Program Synthesis:** The work draws on the field of program synthesis, where models generate executable code from natural language specifications. However, PoT's emphasis is less on synthesizing a complete, optimal program and more on generating a "thoughtful program" that mirrors the human reasoning process, using semantically meaningful variable names and step-by-step logic. The paper explicitly differentiates PoT from direct equation generation, arguing that breaking down the problem into a multi-step program is more effective for LLMs.
- **Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks:** The paper thoroughly evaluates PoT on established mathematical word problem (MWP) and financial QA datasets, contributing to the ongoing effort to benchmark and advance AI capabilities in quantitative reasoning.
- **Concurrent and Follow-up Work:** The paper acknowledges contemporary work like PaL (Gao et al., 2022), which also explores hybrid text/code reasoning, and notes PoT's competitive or superior performance. It also sets the stage for subsequent advancements like self-critic (Gou et al., 2023), self-eval (Xie et al., 2023), and plan-and-solve (Wang et al., 2023a), which build upon PoT by adding further layers of self-correction and planning.

In essence, PoT represents a significant step forward in making LLMs more reliable and capable for numerical reasoning by strategically offloading their computational weaknesses to dedicated, precise tools, thereby enhancing the overall reasoning pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "Program of Thoughts" (PoT) research is to **significantly improve the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) on complex numerical reasoning tasks** by a novel prompting strategy. More specifically, the core objective is to **disentangle the computational aspects of problem-solving from the reasoning and language understanding capabilities of LLMs.**

This objective is driven by a clear set of motivations, stemming from the observed limitations of current state-of-the-art prompting methods, particularly Chain-of-Thought (CoT):

1. **Inaccuracy in Arithmetic Calculation:** The foremost motivation is the inherent proneness of LLMs to arithmetic errors. While CoT allows LLMs to outline the steps of a calculation, the models themselves are not robust calculators. They frequently make mistakes, especially when dealing with large numbers, decimal precision, or a sequence of operations. This leads to incorrect final answers even if the reasoning chain is conceptually sound.

2. **Inability to Solve Complex Mathematical Expressions:** LLMs struggle with more advanced mathematical operations beyond basic arithmetic. They cannot reliably solve polynomial equations, differential equations, or symbolic manipulations that are common in various numerical reasoning problems. CoT, relying on the LLM's internal "computation," hits a hard wall when faced with such complexities.

3. **Inefficiency in Expressing Iterations:** Many numerical problems involve repetitive calculations or iterations. Expressing these directly within a natural language CoT can be cumbersome, inefficient, and highly susceptible to error, as the LLM has to "simulate" each step. For example, performing 50 additions sequentially via natural language tokens is highly impractical and error-prone for an LLM.

The authors propose PoT as a direct solution to these motivations. By delegating the exact computation to an external, precise program interpreter (like Python), the LLM is freed from its computational burden. Its role then shifts to expressing the *reasoning process* in a structured, executable programming language, thereby leveraging its strengths in language understanding and logical structuring, while offloading its weaknesses to a system designed for precision. This decoupling is hypothesized to yield more accurate and robust solutions for numerical reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Program of Thoughts" (PoT) prompting method proposes a novel way for Large Language Models (LLMs) to solve numerical reasoning tasks by **generating executable code snippets** rather than natural language rationales. This generated code is then executed by an external interpreter to obtain the final answer, effectively disentangling computation from reasoning.

**Core Approach:**
1. **Program Generation by LLM:** Instead of producing a text-based "Chain of Thought" (CoT) describing steps like "first, calculate A, then add B to it...", PoT prompts the LLM (primarily Codex, a code-focused LLM) to generate a Python program. This program encapsulates the reasoning process using variables with semantic meanings and standard programming constructs (e.g., loops, functions, algebraic solvers).
2. **External Computation:** The generated Python program is then fed into an external Python interpreter (Python 3.8 with the SymPy library). This interpreter precisely executes the code, performing all calculations, solving equations, and handling iterations with perfect accuracy.
3. **Answer Extraction:** The final numerical result derived from the program's execution is considered the answer to the problem.

**Prompting Strategies:**

- **Few-Shot PoT:** Similar to few-shot CoT, this involves providing the LLM with a few examples (demonstrations) in the prompt. Each demonstration consists of a question and its corresponding "program of thoughts" (Python code) that solves it. The LLM then imitates this pattern to generate a program for a new, unseen question. The paper used 4-8 shots, selected from the training set and tuned on a small validation set.
- **Zero-Shot PoT:** This setting involves no explicit examples. The prompt only provides a general instruction for the LLM to generate a Python program to solve the given problem. A key challenge here is preventing the LLM from falling back to generating natural language comments (starting with '#') instead of executable code. To address this, the authors apply a small negative bias to the logits of the '#' token, discouraging its generation and encouraging the model to produce more pure Python code.

**Advanced Integration (PoT as an Intermediate Step):**
For certain complex problems (e.g., in AQuA dataset) that might require both numerical computation and subsequent common-sense reasoning or formatting, PoT can be used as an intermediate step. The numerical result from the executed PoT program is then fed back into the LLM, which can then apply a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) over this result and the original question to derive the final answer (e.g., translating "2.05 hours" into "2 hours and 3 minutes" for a multiple-choice option).

**Datasets and Input Handling:**
The method was evaluated on a diverse set of eight numerical reasoning datasets:
- **Math Word Problem (MWP) Datasets:** GSM8K, AQuA, SVAMP, TabMWP, MultiArith.
- **Financial-QA Datasets:** FinQA, ConvFinQA, TATQA.
These datasets feature heterogeneous input formats, including plain text questions, tables, and conversational histories. To standardize inputs for the LLM, tables were linearized into text strings (columns separated by '|', rows by '\n'), and conversational turns were concatenated.

**Implementation Details:**
- **LLM Backend:** The primary model used was OpenAI Codex (`code-davinci-002`). Ablation studies also explored `gpt-3.5-turbo`, `text-davinci-002`, and open-source models like CodeGen, CodeT5+, and Xgen.
- **Interpreter:** Python 3.8 was used, leveraging the `SymPy` library for symbolic mathematics (e.g., solving algebraic equations).
- **Decoding Strategy:** Both greedy decoding and Self-Consistency (SC) decoding (sampling 40 completions with temperature 0.4 and taking a majority vote) were used to assess performance and upper bounds.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Exact Match (EM) scores:** For datasets like GSM8K, SVAMP, and MultiArith, with numerical answers rounded to specific precision.
- **Official Scripts:** For TabMWP, ConvFinQA, and TATQA.
- **Relaxed Evaluation for CoT on FinQA:** Using `math.isclose` for comparison due to LLMs' precision issues.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
The performance of PoT was compared against:
- Direct answer generation (LLM outputting only the answer).
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (both with and without an external calculator).
- Other large models (GPT-3, PaLM, LaMDA).
- Published state-of-the-art results for each dataset (excluding GPT-4 results).

**Ablation Studies:**
To understand the contribution of different components, the authors conducted several ablation studies:
- **Backend Model Comparison:** To assess PoT's performance across different LLMs.
- **Exemplar Sensitivity:** To analyze the impact of the number and specific choice of few-shot examples.
- **Comparison with PaL:** A contemporary program-aided language model.
- **Semantic Binding and Multi-Step Reasoning:** To confirm the importance of using meaningful variable names and breaking down problems into multiple steps (vs. direct equation generation).
- **Breakdown Analysis:** Categorizing errors by problem type (e.g., linear equations, iterative) to identify where PoT offers the most significant gains.
- **Error Analysis:** Classifying errors into "value grounding errors" (incorrect variable assignment) and "logic generation errors" (incorrect computation logic).

This comprehensive methodology allows for a thorough evaluation of PoT's effectiveness, its robustness across different settings and datasets, and an understanding of its core strengths compared to existing methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Program of Thoughts (PoT) prompting consistently and significantly outperforms Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting across a wide range of numerical reasoning tasks, both in few-shot and zero-shot settings.

**Overall Performance Gains:**
- **Few-Shot Setting:** PoT with greedy decoding showed an average performance gain of approximately 8% on Math Word Problem (MWP) datasets (e.g., GSM8K, AQuA, TabMWP saw gains of over 8%) and a more substantial ~15% gain on financial datasets (FinQA, ConvFinQA). The larger improvement on financial datasets is attributed to LLMs' particular struggles with large numbers and precision in these domains, where PoT's reliance on an external interpreter shines.
- **Zero-Shot Setting:** PoT’s performance advantage over zero-shot CoT was even more pronounced, averaging around 12% across MWP datasets. Notably, zero-shot PoT sometimes even surpassed the performance of few-shot CoT, highlighting its strong generalization capabilities without task-specific exemplars.

**Impact of Self-Consistency (SC) Decoding:**
- When combined with self-consistency (PoT+SC), the method achieves even stronger results, outperforming CoT+SC by an average of 10% across all datasets. PoT+SC achieved the best-known results on all evaluated MWP datasets and near best-known results on financial datasets (excluding those obtained by GPT-4). This indicates that PoT's generated programs, even when slightly varied by temperature, lead to a higher consensus on the correct answer, suggesting a more robust underlying reasoning process.

**Comparison with Baselines:**
- **CoT vs. CoT+calc:** The study found that simply adding an external calculator to correct computations in CoT (CoT+calc) offered only mild improvements over standard CoT. This suggests that LLMs' issues are not just about incorrect arithmetic but also about the difficulty in *expressing* calculations in a way that can be easily parsed and corrected, validating PoT's approach of generating structured, executable programs from the outset.
- **GPT-4 Performance:** The paper includes preliminary results using GPT-4, where PoT-GPT4 achieved extremely high scores (e.g., 97.2% on GSM8K vs. 92.0% for CoT-GPT4), further validating the approach and indicating its scalability with more advanced LLMs.

**Ablation Study Insights:**

- **Backend Model Performance:**
 - `gpt-3.5-turbo` emerged as the top performer, surprisingly outscoring `code-davinci-002` (the Codex model). This suggests that `gpt-3.5-turbo`'s strong instruction following and general language capabilities translate well to generating executable code.
 - `text-davinci-002` (GPT-3) performed worse than Codex, implying that text-based instruction tuning might undermine models' code generation abilities.
 - Open-source models like CodeGen, CodeT5+, and XGen were significantly behind, highlighting the performance gap between proprietary and open-source LLMs for code generation tasks.

- **Sensitivity to Exemplars:**
 - Increasing the number of few-shot exemplars generally improved performance, especially for more diverse datasets like GSM8K.
 - Performance variance was higher with fewer exemplars (e.g., 7% variance with K=2 shots), becoming more stable as the number of examples increased, reinforcing the general importance of quality exemplars in few-shot learning.

- **Comparison with PaL:** PoT was generally found to be superior to the contemporary PaL (Program-aided Language Models) method, achieving approximately 6% higher accuracy on datasets like SVAMP and ASDIV.

- **Importance of Semantic Binding and Multi-Step Reasoning:**
 - Ablating "semantic binding" (i.e., using generic variable names like `a, b, c` instead of meaningful ones like `total_eggs`) negatively impacted performance, especially on more complex questions. This confirms that meaningful variable names aid the LLM in grounding its reasoning and understanding.
 - Prompting the LLM to directly generate a single, complex mathematical equation (removing "multi-step reasoning") significantly hurt performance. This reinforces that breaking down the problem into smaller, logical programming steps, akin to human thought, is crucial for eliciting better reasoning from LLMs.

- **Breakdown Analysis (AQuA):**
 - PoT demonstrated the largest performance gains on problem categories requiring more complex arithmetic or symbolic skills, such as "linear/polynomial equations," "iterative" problems, and "symbolic" questions.
 - For simpler "arithmetic," "probability," and "geometric" questions, PoT and CoT performed similarly, indicating that PoT's benefits are most pronounced when the computational complexity or iterative nature of the problem exceeds LLMs' inherent calculation capabilities.

- **Error Analysis:**
 - The majority of errors (47%) were classified as "value grounding errors," where the model failed to correctly extract and assign numerical values to variables from the problem text, despite correct computation logic.
 - "Logic generation errors" (33%), where the model failed to generate the correct computational process, also contributed significantly. This suggests that while PoT addresses the computation precision, challenges remain in accurate information extraction and perfect logical formulation within the program itself.

In summary, the results conclusively show that PoT is a superior prompting strategy for numerical reasoning, effectively bridging the gap between LLMs' linguistic strengths and their computational weaknesses by intelligently offloading precise calculations to external interpreters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Program of Thoughts" (PoT) prompting method presents a significant advancement in the field of large language models and their application to complex reasoning, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Accuracy and Robustness in Numerical Reasoning:** PoT fundamentally addresses a long-standing weakness of LLMs: their unreliability in precise numerical computation. By delegating computation to an external, deterministic interpreter, PoT significantly boosts accuracy on mathematical word problems and financial question-answering tasks. This makes LLMs far more trustworthy for applications requiring numerical precision, such as financial analysis, scientific calculations, and engineering.

2. **A Novel Paradigm for Neural-Symbolic AI:** PoT represents a powerful and practical instantiation of neural-symbolic AI. It effectively combines the strengths of neural networks (LLMs excel at language understanding, high-level reasoning, and generating structured representations) with the precision and deterministic nature of symbolic systems (program interpreters). This hybrid approach offers a blueprint for building more capable and reliable AI systems that leverage the best of both worlds.

3. **Extending the "Tool-Use" Capabilities of LLMs:** PoT is a specific yet highly impactful example of LLMs acting as intelligent agents that can "teach themselves to use tools." It demonstrates that LLMs are not merely passive text generators but can be prompted to interact with external computational environments. This opens broad avenues for LLMs to interface with various external APIs, databases, search engines, or specialized solvers, pushing them beyond purely textual reasoning to solve real-world problems that require grounding in external systems.

4. **Improved Generalization through Disentanglement:** The significant performance gains observed in zero-shot settings suggest that PoT fosters better generalization. By focusing the LLM on generating the logical structure of a solution (the "reasoning") rather than also performing the "computation," the model may learn more abstract problem-solving patterns that transfer more easily to unseen problems without needing extensive task-specific examples.

5. **Inspiration for Future Research Directions:**
 - **Beyond Numerical Tasks:** The success of PoT in numerical reasoning strongly suggests its applicability to other symbolic reasoning tasks where precise, step-by-step logic or external knowledge is crucial. This could include logical puzzles, constraint satisfaction problems, formal verification, or even specialized domain-specific reasoning in legal or medical contexts.
 - **Smarter Tool Orchestration:** While PoT uses a Python interpreter, future work can explore more sophisticated ways for LLMs to select and orchestrate a wider array of specialized tools (e.g., statistical software, CAD tools, physics simulators) based on the problem context.
 - **Self-Correction and Verification:** The paper notes follow-up work on self-critic and self-eval approaches, which build upon PoT by allowing LLMs to generate and then verify their own code or reasoning paths, further increasing robustness. This highlights PoT as a foundational contribution for more advanced reasoning architectures.
 - **Addressing Current Limitations:** The identified "value grounding errors" point to future research on improving LLMs' ability to accurately extract numerical and relational information from complex text/tables and map them to variables in the generated program. Addressing the diversity of questions in datasets like AQuA also remains an open challenge.
 - **Safety and Control:** The practical limitation of executing generated code (e.g., blocking `os.rmdir`) necessitates future research into secure and sandboxed execution environments for LLM-generated programs, especially as tool-use becomes more widespread.

In conclusion, "Program of Thoughts" is more than just an incremental improvement; it proposes a new conceptual framework for augmenting LLMs. By strategically offloading computation, it not only achieves remarkable performance gains on numerical reasoning tasks but also sets a precedent for how LLMs can become more capable, reliable, and versatile problem-solvers by seamlessly integrating with the powerful, precise world of symbolic computation and external tools. This work paves the way for a new generation of hybrid AI systems with enhanced reasoning capabilities across diverse domains.
