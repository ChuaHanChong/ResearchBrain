# Refining Few-Step Text-to-Multiview Diffusion via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Wuhan University, Sun Yat-sen University, Tencent, and Xiaomi introduce MVC-ZigAL, a reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance few-step Text-to-Multiview (T2MV) diffusion models. This framework significantly improves both per-view fidelity and cross-view consistency, achieving superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines on benchmarks like MATE-3D while maintaining fast generation speeds.

## Problem

- Few-step Text-to-Multiview (T2MV) diffusion models, while fast, suffer from compromised per-view fidelity and a lack of cross-view consistency.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning methods for diffusion models primarily optimize for single-image quality, neglecting the critical cross-view coordination required for T2MV.
- The constrained policy space and tightly clustered reward values in few-step generation lead to weak learning signals, limiting the effectiveness of standard RL finetuning.

## Method

- **MVC-ZigAL** introduces a ==multiview-aware== Markov Decision Process (MDP) and a joint-view reward function to model the collective quality and consistency of all generated views.
- A novel advantage learning strategy, MV-ZigAL, utilizes self-reflection-based zigzag multiview sampling (ZMV-Sampling) to generate stronger reference trajectories and amplify learning signals.
- A multiview-constrained policy optimization framework balances per-view fidelity and cross-view consistency through a Lagrangian dual formulation, integrating adaptive primal-dual updates and a self-paced constraint threshold curriculum for stable training.

## Results

- MVC-ZigAL consistently outperformed adapted T2MV policy optimization baselines (MV-PG, MV-DPO, MV-RDL) across all HyperScore metrics and PickScore, demonstrating superior T2MV generation quality.
- The framework significantly enhanced geometric consistency and detail, effectively mitigating issues like the 'multi-face' problem, and achieved quality comparable to **50**-step models at just 4 or 8 inference steps.
- On the out-of-distribution MATE-3D benchmark, MVC-ZigAL surpassed state-of-the-art T2MV diffusion baselines and other methods, achieving higher scores across HyperScore subcategories, ImageReward, HPSv2, and PickScore with fewer function evaluations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating the T2MV denoising process as a multiview-aware MDP with a joint-view reward function is crucial for optimizing multiview consistency.
> - Leveraging 'self-reflection' through zigzag sampling to create advantage signals from refined trajectories effectively addresses the issue of weak learning signals in few-step generation regimes.
> - A constrained optimization approach, rather than simple weighted sum of rewards, more effectively balances the often conflicting objectives of individual view quality and overall multiview geometric consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements, particularly with Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models that synthesize high-fidelity images from text prompts. Building upon these, Text-to-Multiview (T2MV) diffusion models extend this capability to generate multiple consistent views of a single scene from a textual description. This progress is crucial for applications requiring 3D content generation, virtual reality, and comprehensive scene understanding.

A significant challenge in T2MV generation is achieving real-time performance. Few-step backbone models, such as Latent Consistency Models (LCMs), enable faster generation by reducing the number of denoising steps required. However, directly applying these accelerated models to T2MV tasks often compromises the quality of the generated output, specifically impacting per-view fidelity (image quality, text alignment) and, critically, cross-view consistency (geometric coherence across different viewpoints).

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning has emerged as a method to align diffusion models with human preferences or specific reward functions. While RL finetuning has demonstrated success in optimizing single-image quality for T2I tasks, directly transferring these methods to the few-step T2MV setting presents difficulties. Existing RL approaches for T2I primarily focus on optimizing rewards for individual images, thereby neglecting the inherent need for cross-view coordination essential in T2MV. Furthermore, the few-step nature of these models can lead to samples with consistently low quality and tightly clustered reward values, resulting in weak learning signals that limit the effectiveness of standard RL finetuning.

This research aims to address these identified gaps by developing a tailored RL finetuning framework designed specifically for few-step T2MV diffusion models. The work seeks to enhance both the fidelity of individual views and the consistency across multiple views, while operating within the constraints of real-time generation. It positions itself as a development that bridges the gap between fast T2MV generation and high-quality, consistent output, moving beyond single-image optimization to address the multi-faceted challenges of multiview synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the performance limitations observed in few-step Text-to-Multiview (T2MV) diffusion models. While these models facilitate real-time content generation by significantly reducing the number of inference steps, this acceleration typically results in a compromise of crucial generative qualities. Specifically, existing few-step T2MV models often exhibit reduced per-view image fidelity (e.g., lower image quality, less precise text alignment) and a lack of cross-view consistency, where different generated views of the same object or scene may appear geometrically inconsistent or uncoordinated.

Current reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning methods, which have shown promise in improving Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models, are not directly applicable or sufficiently effective for few-step T2MV tasks for two main reasons:
1. **Neglect of Cross-View Coordination:** Traditional RL finetuning for diffusion models is designed for single-image generation, meaning their reward functions and optimization objectives do not inherently account for the complex interdependencies and consistency requirements across multiple views of a single scene. Applying them directly would likely improve individual view quality at the expense of overall multiview coherence.
2. **Weak Learning Signals in Few-Step Regimes:** In few-step generation, the policy space is constrained, and the initial quality of generated samples might be uniformly low, leading to reward values that are tightly clustered. This lack of significant variation in rewards provides weak gradients, making it challenging for RL algorithms to effectively learn and refine the model's policy.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
1. **Formulate a Multiview-Aware Markov Decision Process (MDP):** The first objective is to redefine the T2MV denoising process within an MDP framework that explicitly models the joint generation of all views. This involves developing a joint-view reward function capable of evaluating the collective quality and consistency of the entire set of generated multiview images.
2. **Develop a Novel Advantage Learning Strategy:** To overcome the issue of weak learning signals in few-step T2MV, the research aims to introduce a new advantage learning approach. This strategy seeks to leverage "self-refinement" sampling schemes to generate stronger reference samples, allowing the model to learn from the relative improvements (advantages) rather than relying solely on absolute reward values, thereby yielding more informative policy updates.
3. **Propose a Multiview-Constrained Policy Optimization Framework:** The objective is to design an optimization framework that can simultaneously enhance both per-view fidelity and cross-view consistency. This involves balancing single-view rewards (which focus on individual image quality) with joint-view constraints (which enforce multiview coherence) through a unified optimization mechanism, such as a Lagrangian dual formulation.
4. **Integrate Adaptive Optimization Mechanisms:** To ensure stable and effective training, the framework aims to incorporate adaptive strategies for primal-dual updates and a self-paced curriculum for the constraint threshold. These mechanisms are designed to dynamically adjust learning parameters based on the policy's evolving performance, harmonizing exploration with the enforcement of constraints.

Collectively, these objectives aim to provide a robust and balanced RL finetuning framework capable of significantly improving the quality of few-step T2MV diffusion models, making real-time multiview generation both fast and high-quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes MVC-ZigAL, a tailored reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning framework designed to enhance few-step Text-to-Multiview (T2MV) diffusion models. The methodology encompasses a novel MDP formulation, an advantage learning strategy, and a multiview-constrained policy optimization framework with adaptive mechanisms.

#### 3.1. Multiview-Aware MDP Formulation

To overcome the limitations of T2I RL methods in T2MV tasks, the denoising process is reformulated as a multiview-aware Markov Decision Process (MDP). This formulation jointly models the generation of all views, treating a set of `V` state-action pairs concurrently, each conditioned on its corresponding camera embedding `e_v`. A critical component is the introduction of an arbitrary joint-view reward function, `R_mv`, which evaluates the collective quality of all generated views `{x_v^0}`. The RL objective is to maximize the expected `R_mv` over sets of multiview images.

For comparative evaluation, three representative T2I RL finetuning paradigms—policy gradient, direct preference optimization (DPO), and reward difference learning—are adapted to this multiview-aware MDP:
- **Multiview-aware policy gradient (MV-PG):** Accumulates log-likelihood gradients over all `V` views using the `R_mv`.
- **Multiview-aware direct preference optimization (MV-DPO):** Aggregates log-likelihood ratios across all views in preferred and non-preferred trajectories.
- **Multiview-aware reward difference learning (MV-RDL):** Minimizes the squared error between log-likelihood ratio differences and `R_mv` differences across views for trajectory pairs.

The joint-view reward function `R_mv` is implemented using the overall quality score from HyperScore, a multi-dimensional quality evaluator for text-to-3D generation.

#### 3.2. Learning from Self-Reflected Advantages for Few-Step T2MV Diffusion Models

To address weak learning signals in few-step T2MV finetuning, a novel advantage learning strategy, MV-ZigAL, is introduced, leveraging a self-reflection-based technique called zigzag multiview sampling (ZMV-Sampling).

- **ZMV-Sampling:** This technique refines T2MV generation at test time by reinforcing viewpoint conditioning during denoising. It involves a three-step inversion-denoising pass ("zigzag" pass) at selected sampling steps. This pass alternates between high-guidance denoising steps and a low-guidance inversion step. The inversion step reintroduces noise under low guidance, enabling "self-reflection" by suppressing misaligned features and reinforcing condition-aligned ones during subsequent re-denoising.
- **First-step zigzag schedule:** The zigzag pass is found to be most effective when restricted to the very first sampling step (`t=T`). This is because early steps establish global structures, and applying zigzag here enhances the foundational geometric layout without disrupting later detail refinement.
- **MV-ZigAL Objective:** MV-ZigAL uses the reward advantages of self-refined trajectories (from ZMV-Sampling) over standard trajectories as learning signals. The zigzag advantage function `A_mv` is defined as the difference in `R_mv` between a ZMV-Sampling trajectory and a standard sampling trajectory for the same prompt. The MV-ZigAL objective minimizes the squared error between the log-likelihood ratio gap of each trajectory pair and its corresponding `A_mv`. This approach amplifies learning signals by focusing on relative improvements, which are more informative than absolute reward values in low-quality, tightly clustered reward regimes.

#### 3.3. Multiview-Constrained Policy Optimization

To achieve a balance between per-view image fidelity and cross-view consistency, a multiview-constrained policy optimization framework is proposed.

- **Multiview-constrained MDP formulation:** The MDP is redefined to maximize the sum of single-view rewards `R(x_v^0, c)` for each view, subject to a lower bound constraint (`τ`) on the expected joint-view reward `E[R_mv]`. This explicitly balances individual image quality with multiview consistency.
- **Lagrangian dual optimization:** The constrained optimization problem is solved using a Lagrangian dual approach. A Lagrange multiplier `λ ≥ 0` is introduced to incorporate the joint-view constraint into the objective.
- **Multiview-constrained reward function:** A unified multiview-constrained reward function `R_mvc(x_v^0, c)` is defined, amalgamating single-view and joint-view rewards, weighted by `λ`. This allows the constrained optimization problem to be treated as a standard RL problem with modified rewards, facilitating the application of the advantage learning strategy.

#### 3.4. Adaptive Primal-Dual Updates under a Self-Paced Constraint Curriculum

The final MVC-ZigAL framework integrates the multiview-constrained objective with advantage learning and incorporates adaptive mechanisms for stable training.

- **Multiview-constrained advantage learning:** The zigzag advantage function is reformulated to `A_mvc`, using the `R_mvc` rewards. The final MVC-ZigAL objective then minimizes the squared error between the log-likelihood ratio gap and `A_mvc`.
- **Primal-dual updates with adaptive step size:** During training, policy parameters `θ` and the Lagrange multiplier `λ` are concurrently optimized. `λ` is updated based on the deviation of the batch-averaged joint-view reward (`¯R_mv`) from the threshold `τ`. An adaptive step size `α±` is used: a larger step (`α+`) for constraint violation to reinforce promptly, and a smaller step (`α-`) for satisfaction to allow stable adjustments.
- **Self-paced constraint thresholding:** The constraint threshold `τ` is dynamically adapted using an exponential moving average (EMA) of `¯R_mv`. This curriculum allows `τ` to evolve with the policy's performance, encouraging exploration in early stages and progressively tightening the constraint as the policy improves, preventing prolonged constraint breaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MVC-ZigAL demonstrated its capabilities in enhancing few-step Text-to-Multiview (T2MV) diffusion models across various quantitative and qualitative metrics.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **Comparison with T2MV Policy Optimization Baselines:** MVC-ZigAL consistently outperformed adapted T2MV policy optimization baselines (MV-PG, MV-DPO, MV-RDL) across all HyperScore metrics (Alignment, Geometry, Texture, Overall) and showed a slight advantage in PickScore. This indicates its superior effectiveness in improving T2MV generation quality. For instance, in HyperScore Overall, MV-ZigAL achieved 9.17 compared to MV-RDL's 9.03, MV-PG's 8.39, and MV-DPO's 8.00.
- **Effectiveness of Advantage Learning:** Analysis of the reward gap between standard sampling and ZMV-Sampling during MVC-ZigAL finetuning revealed that an initial advantage of ZMV-Sampling progressively narrowed and converged. This convergence indicates that the base model successfully internalized the benefits of ZMV-Sampling, validating the advantage learning strategy.
- **Multiview-Constrained Policy Optimization:** When compared against alternative RL paradigms (ZigAL, which uses only single-view rewards; and WS-ZigAL, which uses a weighted sum of rewards), MVC-ZigAL demonstrated a more effective balance. While ZigAL and WS-ZigAL often showed a trade-off between per-view (PickScore/HPSv2) and joint-view (HyperScore) rewards, MVC-ZigAL consistently achieved higher joint-view rewards as per-view rewards improved, affirming its ability to harmonize both objectives.
- **Out-of-Distribution Evaluation (MATE-3D Benchmark):** On unseen prompts from the MATE-3D benchmark, MVC-ZigAL significantly outperformed state-of-the-art T2MV diffusion baselines (SPAD, MV-Adapter) and other methods listed in the MATE-3D benchmark. Operating at significantly lower numbers of function evaluations (NFE = 4 or 8), MVC-ZigAL achieved higher scores across HyperScore subcategories, ImageReward, HPSv2, and PickScore. For example, at 8 NFE, MVC-ZigAL achieved a HyperScore Overall of 7.04 compared to the MV-Adapter baseline's 6.67.
- **Scalability to Large-Scale Finetuning:** Experiments with large-scale RL finetuning on the MATE-3D prompt set showed that MVC-ZigAL scales effectively, yielding further performance enhancements across all metrics, including a substantial improvement in ImageReward from -0.846 (baseline) to 0.865.
- **Extension to Hyper-Step Models:** The framework's versatility was demonstrated by its application to hyper-step T2MV diffusion models (MV-Adapter with SDXL backbone), where it also consistently improved all evaluated reward metrics during finetuning.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Enhanced Generation Quality:** Visual comparisons demonstrated that the MVC-ZigAL finetuned model produced multiview images with noticeably enhanced geometric consistency and finer details compared to the few-step MV-Adapter baseline (LCM-SDXL). The quality was observed to closely match that of the computationally intensive 50-step SDXL baseline, but with significantly fewer inference steps.
- **Reduced "Multi-face" Problem:** The multiview-constrained optimization effectively mitigated issues such as the "multi-face" problem, where single-view optimization might lead to inconsistent outputs across views, instead ensuring cohesive multiview generation.
- **Generalization to Diverse Prompts:** Qualitative results for unseen and training prompts, including a challenging 4-step setting, further illustrated the robustness and effectiveness of MVC-ZigAL in enhancing generative performance across various text inputs and inference conditions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Effect of Zigzag Pass in Advantage Learning:** Comparing MVC-ZigAL with MVC-ZigPG (which uses standard policy gradients but retains the zigzag pass for sampling), MVC-ZigAL showed superior performance across all reward metrics. This validated the effectiveness of the proposed advantage learning strategy in leveraging self-refined trajectories.
- **First-Step Zigzag vs. Full-Step Zigzag:** The first-step zigzag schedule in ZMV-Sampling outperformed the full-step variant in HyperScore while maintaining comparable single-view metrics (PickScore, HPSv2), indicating its efficiency and optimal design choice.
- **Weighted Sum vs. Constrained Optimization:** WS-ZigAL, which optimized a weighted sum of rewards, consistently underperformed MVC-ZigAL in HyperScore, particularly across varying weight settings. This highlighted that the efficacy of weighted sum approaches is sensitive to manual weight tuning, whereas the constrained framework provided a more balanced and effective optimization without such tuning.
- **Fixed vs. Adaptive Thresholds:** Variants using fixed constraint thresholds either failed to enforce the joint-view constraint consistently (too lenient) or hindered single-view reward improvements (too stringent). In contrast, the adaptive thresholding mechanism in MVC-ZigAL enabled steady improvements in both single-view and joint-view rewards, balancing exploration and constraint enforcement without manual adjustment.
- **Fixed vs. Adaptive Step Sizes:** Using fixed step sizes for Lagrange multiplier updates led to either slow reactions to constraint violations (small step size) or oscillations, destabilizing training (large step size). The adaptive step size strategy in MVC-ZigAL facilitated responsive yet stable constraint enforcement, contributing to consistent progress in both reward types.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents MVC-ZigAL, a novel RL finetuning framework that addresses critical limitations in few-step Text-to-Multiview (T2MV) diffusion models. The work demonstrates a methodological advance in achieving a balance between computational efficiency (few-step generation) and output quality (per-view fidelity and cross-view consistency).

The significance of MVC-ZigAL lies in several aspects:
- **Bridging the Quality-Efficiency Gap:** By effectively finetuning few-step T2MV models, MVC-ZigAL helps to bridge the existing gap between real-time generation capabilities and the demand for high-quality, consistent multiview outputs. This is particularly relevant for applications where rapid content generation is paramount but quality cannot be significantly compromised.
- **Novel RL Paradigm for Multiview Generation:** The introduction of a multiview-aware MDP formulation, coupled with an advantage learning strategy based on self-reflected refinements and a multiview-constrained policy optimization framework, constitutes a novel approach to RL finetuning for complex generative tasks. This paradigm moves beyond single-image optimization to explicitly consider and enforce multi-object or multi-view relationships.
- **Enhanced Generative Performance:** The quantitative and qualitative results consistently show that MVC-ZigAL significantly outperforms existing baselines and state-of-the-art methods across various metrics, even in out-of-distribution scenarios and with fewer inference steps. This indicates a robust improvement in the underlying generative capabilities of T2MV models.
- **Robust and Adaptive Optimization:** The integration of adaptive primal-dual updates and a self-paced constraint threshold curriculum enhances the stability and effectiveness of the finetuning process. These adaptive mechanisms reduce the need for extensive manual hyperparameter tuning, making the framework more practical for researchers and developers.

The potential impact of this work is substantial across several domains:
- **3D Content Creation:** The ability to generate high-quality, consistent multiview images from text prompts with fewer steps can accelerate the creation of 3D assets for various industries, including gaming, virtual reality, augmented reality, and industrial design. It streamlines the process of converting textual descriptions into coherent visual data that can be used for 3D reconstruction.
- **Computer Graphics and Animation:** Improved T2MV generation can facilitate the rapid prototyping of scenes, characters, and objects, enabling artists and designers to iterate more quickly and efficiently.
- **E-commerce and Product Visualization:** Businesses could leverage this technology to generate comprehensive 360-degree views of products from simple text descriptions, enhancing online shopping experiences.
- **Research in Generative AI:** The novel RL framework and insights, particularly regarding advantage learning and constrained optimization for multi-component generation, could inspire future research in other generative tasks requiring complex coherence, such as video generation, 3D reconstruction, or large-scale scene synthesis.

In conclusion, MVC-ZigAL represents a step forward in making high-quality, real-time T2MV generation a more attainable reality, with implications for various creative and industrial applications.
