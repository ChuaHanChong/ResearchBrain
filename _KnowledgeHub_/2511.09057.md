# PAN: A World Model for General, Interactable, and Long-Horizon World Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PAN, a world model from MBZUAI's Institute of Foundation Models, employs a Generative Latent Prediction (GLP) architecture to enable open-domain, action-conditioned simulation with coherent, long-term dynamics. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source models, demonstrating 70.3% accuracy in agent simulation and improving planning success by 26.7% in open-ended tasks when integrated with an external VLM.

## Problem

- Current high-fidelity video generation models lack causal control, interactivity, and long-horizon consistency, operating in an open-loop manner without explicit state or action representations.
- Existing world models are typically confined to restricted domains (e.g., game environments) and struggle to generalize across diverse scenarios, provide deep interactive control, or unify different capabilities.
- Purely latent-space predictive models are susceptible to representational collapse, where their learned latent transitions do not reliably correspond to observable and physically grounded world dynamics.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Generative Latent Prediction (GLP)== architecture, unifying a vision encoder, an autoregressive world model backbone (based on a large language model), and a video diffusion decoder.
- Utilizes a generative supervision objective (==flow matching loss==) that explicitly anchors latent space predictions to observable data, ensuring that learned world dynamics are perceptually grounded and interpretable.
- Integrates a Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-based LLM as the latent dynamics backbone for general knowledge and long-horizon reasoning, alongside an enhanced ==Causal Shift-Window Denoising Process Model (Causal Swin-DPM)== for high-fidelity, temporally coherent video generation.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-source world models and is competitive with leading commercial models across action simulation fidelity, long-horizon forecast, and simulative reasoning benchmarks.
- Demonstrated superior action simulation fidelity with **70.3%** accuracy on agent simulation and **47.0%** on environment simulation, along with leading long-horizon forecasting (**53.6%** transition smoothness, **64.1%** simulation consistency).
- Improved planning performance when integrated with an external VLM agent, leading to a **26.7%** increase in task success for Open-Ended Planning and a **23.4%** increase for Structured Planning compared to a VLM-only baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit generative supervision through observable data reconstruction is essential for preventing representational collapse in world models, ensuring that abstract latent dynamics correspond to real-world changes.
> - Leveraging large language models as the core for latent world dynamics enables the integration of vast real-world knowledge and facilitates open-domain, natural language-conditioned interaction and long-horizon reasoning.
> - A hierarchical generative approach, combining an LLM for global causal dynamics with a video diffusion model for local perceptual detail, effectively bridges abstract causal reasoning and high-fidelity visual simulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent agents capable of understanding, predicting, and interacting with their environment is a central goal of artificial intelligence. World models are a crucial component in this pursuit, enabling an agent to form an internal simulation of how the world evolves, thus supporting reasoning, planning, and strategic action. PAN addresses critical limitations in the current landscape of both video generation models and existing world models.

**Video Generation Models:** Recent advancements in generative AI, particularly diffusion models, have led to "stunning visual fidelity" in video generation (e.g., Sora, DeepMind Veo). However, the paper points out that these models typically operate in an "open-loop" or "prompt-to-full-video" manner. They generate complete video sequences from fixed prompts without "causal control, interactivity, or long-horizon consistency." This means they lack the explicit notions of state, action, and object-level representations necessary for an agent to reason about consequences or evaluate decision alternatives. PAN aims to leverage these high-fidelity generation capabilities but integrate them into a causally controllable, interactive framework.

**Existing World Models:** While efforts in world modeling have advanced, they often suffer from significant limitations:
- **Domain Restriction:** Many existing models focus on "restricted domains" such as physical simulators, game environments (e.g., Genie 2, Matrix-Game), or 3D scene dynamics (e.g., World Labs). They struggle to generalize across diverse environments and interaction formats.
- **Limited Depth and Controllability:** Even within their domains, these models may offer limited depth of understanding or controllable interaction.
- **Lack of Unification:** The paper notes a "fragmented landscape" where models achieve either visual realism without sustained dynamics, or limited controllable interaction without open-domain generality.

**PAN's Contribution to the Landscape:**
PAN positions itself as a crucial step towards unifying these disparate strengths. It explicitly aims for a "general, interactable, and long-horizon world model" that can perform "open-domain, action-conditioned simulation with coherent, long-term dynamics." Its core innovation, the Generative Latent Prediction (GLP) architecture, is presented as a principled foundation to achieve this unification. This framework distinguishes PAN from prior approaches like the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which the authors argue are prone to representational collapse due to their latent-space matching objectives. By explicitly anchoring latent dynamics to observable data through generative supervision, PAN seeks to overcome the "indefinability problem" in latent spaces and ensure that learned transitions correspond to "realizable world dynamics."

In essence, PAN seeks to build a world model that is not just a predictor but a *simulator*, capable of creating "visually grounded worlds that evolve coherently under natural language control," bridging the gap between high-fidelity perception and abstract causal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to develop a **general-purpose world model** that empowers intelligent agents with the fundamental capacity to **imagine, predict, and reason about how the world evolves in response to its actions, and consequently to plan and strategize.** This capability is presented as a cornerstone of general intelligence.

More specifically, the key objectives and motivations driving the development of PAN are:

- **Achieve Generalization and Open-Domain Capability:** To create a world model that is not confined to specific, restricted domains (like physics engines or game environments) but can simulate diverse scenarios and environments, supporting "open-domain" interactions.
- **Enable Interactability and Causal Control:** To move beyond open-loop video generation by allowing agents to exert causal control over the simulated world through "natural language actions," facilitating real-time feedback and adaptive behavior. This is crucial for purposeful reasoning and planning.
- **Ensure Long-Horizon Consistency and Coherence:** To overcome the challenge of error accumulation and temporal drift in extended simulations, enabling the model to maintain visual fidelity and dynamic plausibility over "long-horizon" rollouts.
- **Unify Latent Imagination with Perceptual Reality:** To bridge the gap between abstract internal reasoning (latent state evolution) and concrete sensory experience (perceptually detailed video generation). This is operationalized through the Generative Latent Prediction (GLP) architecture, which seeks to establish a coherent link between "latent space reasoning (imagination) and realizable world dynamics (reality)."
- **Support Simulative Reasoning and Planning:** To provide a robust internal simulator that allows an agent to perform "hypothetical thinking, explore counterfactual outcomes, and act with foresight," ultimately enhancing decision-making and goal-directed behavior.
- **Address Limitations of Prior Art:** To explicitly overcome the shortcomings of:
 - **Video generation models:** Their lack of causal control and explicit state/action representations.
 - **Existing world models:** Their domain specificity, limited depth, and difficulty in generalizing.
 - **Latent-space only predictive models (e.g., JEPA):** Their susceptibility to representational collapse and ungrounded latent transitions.
- **Ground Knowledge in Real-World Context:** To leverage the vast "text-based knowledge" of large language models (LLMs) to enrich the understanding and prediction of perceptual environments, mitigating information sparsity from raw sensory data.

By achieving these objectives, PAN aims to build a foundational AI system that can serve as a versatile "sandbox for simulative reasoning," paving the way for more intelligent and autonomous agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PAN employs a novel **Generative Latent Prediction (GLP) architecture** (Xing et al., 2025) which unifies latent dynamic modeling and perceptual grounding through a hierarchical generative process. This architecture comprises three core components: a vision encoder, an autoregressive world model backbone, and a video diffusion decoder.

#### 3.1. The GLP Architecture Overview

The GLP framework formalizes a world model as a generative system that predicts future world states in a latent space and supervises these predictions by anchoring them to observable data.
- **Encoder (h):** Maps an observation (`o_t`) to a compact latent representation (`ˆs_t`).
- **Predictive Module (f):** Models latent world dynamics, evolving `ˆs_t` to `ˆs_t+1` conditioned on action (`a_t`).
- **Decoder (g):** Reconstructs observable outcomes (`ˆo_t+1`) from `ˆs_t+1`, anchoring the simulation back to the sensory domain.

This generative process (`p_PAN(o_t+1 | o_t, a_t) = Σ p_h(ˆs_t | o_t) p_f(ˆs_t+1 | ˆs_t, a_t) p_g(o_t+1 | ˆs_t+1)`) ensures both latent consistency and perceptual realism.

#### 3.2. Generative Supervision and Learning Objective

PAN is trained using a **generative supervision objective** (`L_PAN = E disc(ˆo_t+1, o_t+1)`) that minimizes the discrepancy between the predicted observation and the ground-truth. This contrasts with latent-space matching objectives (like JEPA), which are prone to collapse. By reconstructing observable outcomes, PAN ensures latent transitions correspond to "realizable sensory change," making learned dynamics interpretable and grounded. The discrepancy function is instantiated as a **flow matching loss**, known for its strong supervision signals and high generation quality.

#### 3.3. Design Choices and Parameterization

- **Vision Encoder (h):** Utilizes the vision tower of **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct**, a Vision Transformer (ViT). It encodes video frames into spatially structured continuous embeddings, capable of representing short-term motion through 3D patch partitioning.
- **Autoregressive World Model Backbone (f):** Based on the language model of **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct**. This LLM-based backbone predicts the next latent world state (`ˆs_t+1`) conditioned on the full history of latent states and natural language actions. Leveraging LLM pre-training grounds perceptual encodings in massive real-world context and knowledge, enabling interactive, long-horizon reasoning in a unified multimodal latent space. Input conditioning follows a multi-turn conversational format.
- **Video Diffusion Decoder (g):** Adapted from **Wan2.1-T2V-14B**, a large-scale diffusion transformer, and enhanced with a **Causal Shift-Window Denoising Process Model (Causal Swin-DPM)**. This component generates perceptually detailed and temporally coherent video chunks, ensuring local visual consistency. It conditions on both the latent world state from the backbone and the natural language action.
 - **Causal Swin-DPM:** Addresses local inconsistency and rapid quality degradation in long-horizon simulation. It uses a sliding temporal window holding two video chunks at different noise levels and a chunk-wise causal attention mask. This design reduces error propagation and handles future uncertainty by operating on partially noised representations, emphasizing high-level semantic consistency.
 - **Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Padding:** Utilizes Wan2.1-VAE for video compression, with random padding during training to provide proper temporal context for the sliding window.
 - **Conditioning Frame and Noise Augmentation:** Applies noise to the conditioning frame (last frame of the previously dequeued chunk) to mitigate error accumulation over long-horizon generation.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

Training is conducted in two stages:
1. **Module-Wise Training:** The video diffusion decoder is adapted into the Causal Swin-DPM architecture. The Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct components are already extensively pre-trained. Uses BFloat16, AdamW, cosine decay, gradient clipping, Data Parallelism (DP) with Fully Sharded Data Parallelism (FSDP), activation checkpointing, FlashAttention-3, and FlexAttention. Trained for 5 epochs on 960 NVIDIA H200 GPUs.
2. **Joint Training:** All modules are integrated and trained end-to-end under the GLP generative objective (flow-matching loss). The VLM is frozen, while query embeddings and the video diffusion decoder are optimized. Sequence parallelism (SP) with the Ulysses method is used for the VLM to manage large sequence lengths. Training uses BFloat16, AdamW, cosine decay, and early stopping after 1 epoch based on validation convergence.

#### 3.5. Model Inference

PAN performs autoregressive multi-step simulation. Initial observation (`o_1`) is encoded to `ˆs_1`. For each step, future states are predicted sequentially, and corresponding video observations are reconstructed. To improve consistency, predicted states are augmented with encoder outputs of their reconstructed observations. The world model backbone predicts the next state based on this enhanced history and the current action. The decoder then synthesizes the next observation, conditioned on the predicted latent state and the previous observation. Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is used, and inference is accelerated via Sequence Parallel (SP) and SageAttention2++ for 8-bit/4-bit quantization on Hopper GPUs.

#### 3.6. Training Data

A large-scale dataset of aligned video and text is constructed to capture diverse experiences.
- **Video Segmentation:** Dynamic shot-boundary detection segments raw long-form videos.
- **Data Filtering Pipeline:** Combines rule-based filters (motion patterns, color uniformity), pre-trained detectors (aesthetic quality, obstructive text), and a custom VLM-based filter (lecture videos, screen recordings, low quality, heavily edited, residual scene cuts) to ensure high-quality, relevant data.
- **Dense Video Captioning:** A VLM is prompted to generate dense, temporally grounded descriptions that focus on "evolving dynamics" and causal structures, which are critical for learning interactive world models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PAN's performance is rigorously evaluated across three dimensions: Action Simulation Fidelity, Long-horizon Forecast, and Simulative Reasoning and Planning, comparing against both open-source (WAN 2.1/2.2, Cosmos 1/2, V-JEPA 2) and closed-source (KLING, MiniMax, Gen-3) baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

PAN achieves **state-of-the-art performance among open-source models and is competitive with the best-performing closed-source commercial models**, demonstrating its effectiveness as a general-purpose world model.

#### 4.2. Action Simulation Fidelity

- PAN recorded the **highest overall fidelity among open-source world models, with 70.3% accuracy on agent simulation and 47.0% on environment simulation, resulting in an overall score of 58.6%**.
- This significantly surpasses all open-source baselines and most commercial ones, indicating PAN's superior capacity to precisely simulate action-driven world evolution, a capability where general video generation models often struggle despite their visual prowess.

#### 4.3. Long-horizon Forecast

- PAN achieved **53.6% on Transition Smoothness and 64.1% on Simulation Consistency**, substantially outperforming all baselines, including commercial models.
- These results demonstrate PAN's ability to **maintain visual clarity and temporal coherence throughout extended rollouts**, effectively mitigating motion amplification and temporal drift that often plague other models in long-horizon predictions.

#### 4.4. Simulative Reasoning and Planning

- **Step-Wise Simulation:** PAN achieved the **highest score (56.1%) among all open-source world model baselines**, proving its effectiveness in capturing fine-grained causal dependencies between actions and immediate outcomes in robotic manipulation scenarios. This capability is foundational for more complex reasoning.
- **Open-Ended and Structured Simulation and Planning:** When integrated with an external VLM agent (OpenAI-o3), PAN consistently and substantially improved planning performance. It led to a **26.7% increase in task success for Open-Ended Planning and a 23.4% increase for Structured Planning** relative to the VLM-only baseline. This highlights that PAN's reliable simulated rollouts provide sufficient and accurate dynamics to guide iterative decision-making, demonstrating its utility as a functional internal simulator for goal-directed planning. In contrast, other baselines showed inconsistent performance, suggesting that realistic appearance alone is insufficient without reliable causal grounding.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

The paper provides qualitative examples demonstrating PAN's capabilities:
- **Interactive Long World Simulation:** Ability to sustain long-horizon, closed-loop state evolution under real-time interventions, maintaining consistent latent states and preserving object identities/spatial relations.
- **Diverse Action and World Simulation:** Demonstrated generalization across drastically different worlds and adherence to diverse action instructions, disentangling action semantics from appearance.
- **Rare Case Simulation:** Capacity to synthesize physically coherent tail events and counterfactuals on demand, which is valuable for robustness testing and safety-focused augmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PAN represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of general artificial intelligence, with profound implications and potential impact across multiple domains:

- **Foundation for General AI Agents:** By providing a "general, interactable, and long-horizon world model," PAN delivers a core capability for future AI agents. The ability to internally simulate the world, reason about actions, and plan with foresight is considered essential for achieving general intelligence, allowing agents to understand, adapt, and operate in complex, dynamic, and open-ended environments.
- **Validation of the GLP Paradigm:** The work successfully instantiates and validates the Generative Latent Prediction (GLP) architecture, a principled framework proposed by the same research group. This provides a robust and theoretically grounded approach for building future world models, addressing key challenges like representational collapse in purely latent-space objectives. The GLP framework offers a blueprint for unifying abstract causal dynamics with high-fidelity perceptual generation.
- **Bridging Key AI Capabilities:** PAN effectively unifies the strengths of large language models (for abstract reasoning, natural language understanding, and general knowledge grounding) with advanced video diffusion models (for high-fidelity, temporally coherent visual generation). This integration, particularly using an LLM as the latent dynamics backbone, is a crucial step toward creating multimodal AI systems that can reason both conceptually and perceptually.
- **Enabling Robust Simulative Reasoning and Planning:** The demonstrated improvements in simulative reasoning and planning tasks underscore PAN's utility as an internal "thought experiment" engine. This capacity is invaluable for model-based reinforcement learning, where agents can explore actions, predict outcomes, and optimize policies in a simulated environment before interacting with the real world, potentially reducing real-world trial-and-error costs and safety risks.
- **Impact on Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** For robotics and autonomous driving, PAN could enable more robust planning, predictive control, and anomaly detection. Robots could simulate diverse manipulation scenarios, and autonomous vehicles could forecast complex traffic evolutions and evaluate evasive actions in virtual environments. The ability to simulate "rare case" and "counterfactual" scenarios is particularly impactful for safety-critical applications.
- **Advancing Synthetic Data Generation:** PAN's capability to generate diverse, action-conditioned, and causally coherent long-horizon videos opens new avenues for synthetic data generation. This could address data scarcity issues in various AI training pipelines, providing high-quality, labeled data for tasks like object detection, action recognition, and policy learning.
- **Enhancing Human-AI Interaction and Creativity:** By allowing users to control complex simulations via natural language, PAN could empower novel interactive tools for content creation, scientific exploration, and educational platforms. Imagine designing complex scenarios or testing hypotheses by simply describing actions and observing their simulated outcomes.

In conclusion, PAN represents a significant step towards the realization of truly general-purpose world models. Its robust performance across multiple challenging benchmarks and its principled architectural design suggest a promising path for future research in causal reasoning, embodied AI, and the development of intelligent agents capable of sophisticated interaction and foresight. Future work, as hinted by the authors, will likely explore scaling to broader modalities and enhancing its real-time decision-making capabilities, further solidifying its role as a foundational technology in AI.
