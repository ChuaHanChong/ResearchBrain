# The Duke Humanoid: Design and Control For Energy Efficient Bipedal Locomotion Using Passive Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Duke University developed an open-source humanoid robot, the Duke Humanoid, and a reinforcement learning control policy that leverages passive dynamics to achieve energy-efficient bipedal locomotion. This approach reduced the robot's Cost of Transport by 31% in real-world walking tasks compared to a fully active baseline policy, while maintaining robustness.

## Problem

- A scarcity of open-source, high-performance humanoid hardware limits fundamental research into hardware-control co-optimization and customizability.
- Many existing humanoids consume high energy due to continuous active joint actuation, leading to practical limitations like short battery life.
- Integrating passive dynamics for energy efficiency into modern end-to-end deep reinforcement learning frameworks for bipedal locomotion is largely unexplored.

## Method

- Developed an open-source humanoid platform, "==The Duke Humanoid== v1.0," a child-sized (1m, 30kg) bipedal robot with 10 DoFs, designed for dynamic locomotion and customizability.
- Implemented an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for bipedal locomotion, trained in parallel simulation environments (Isaac Gym).
- Introduced a "passive policy" that modulates individual joint activeness during locomotion, incentivizing the agent to leverage passive dynamics for energy savings.

## Results

- The Duke Humanoid successfully performed zero-shot real-world forward walking on a carpeted surface at **0.3** m/s using policies trained solely in simulation.
- The passive policy achieved up to a **50%** reduction in Cost of Transport (CoT) in simulation for low-speed walking and a **31%** reduction in CoT during real-world experiments at **0.3** m/s.
- The passive walking policy maintained comparable robustness to external disturbances (push recovery) when compared to the baseline active policy, demonstrating that energy efficiency did not compromise stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An open-source hardware platform like the Duke Humanoid can provide the robotics community with an accessible, high-performance tool for research and rapid prototyping.
> - Reinforcement learning can be effectively designed to incorporate and exploit passive dynamics, leading to substantial energy efficiency gains in bipedal locomotion.
> - Modulating joint activeness within an RL framework allows for a flexible balance between active control and passive, energy-saving movements, without significantly compromising robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen progress in enabling dynamic motions such as walking, running, and complex maneuvers. However, the broader research landscape faces several challenges that this work aims to address.

**Current Landscape Challenges:**
- **Limited Open-Source Hardware:** Many commercially available humanoids (e.g., Boston Dynamics Atlas, Agility Digit, Unitree G1) are closed systems. This restricts researchers' access to low-level sensor and actuator interfaces, limiting customizability and fundamental investigations into hardware-control co-optimization. While some efforts provide open-source software (e.g., Berkeley Humanoid, HECTOR), open-source hardware capable of dynamic locomotion is less common, with existing platforms sometimes limited by the use of high-reduction-ratio servo motors that impede dynamic capabilities (e.g., Poppy Project, Nimbro-Op2X, iCub).
- **Energy Inefficiency in Humanoids:** Many humanoids rely on continuous active joint actuation, which leads to high energy consumption. This results in practical limitations such as frequent battery charging and increased operational costs. In contrast, biological systems like humans leverage passive dynamics, such as pendulum-like leg swings, to achieve energy-efficient gaits with minimal muscular effort.
- **Integration of Passive Dynamics in Learning-Based Control:** While passive dynamics has been extensively studied in traditional model-based control (e.g., passive dynamic walkers like Mike, various straight-knee designs), its integration into modern end-to-end deep reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks for bipedal locomotion remains relatively unexplored. Existing passive dynamic walkers are often specifically tuned for particular mass distributions, which can limit customization or payload capabilities.
- **Control Strategy Approaches:**
 - **Model-based control** (e.g., Zero-Moment Point, Model Predictive Control, Whole-Body Control) offers stability and precision but requires extensive, robot-specific modeling and assumptions, making transferability challenging.
 - **Learning-based control** (e.g., imitation learning, reinforcement learning) has gained prominence for its ability to operate with less prior knowledge. Reinforcement learning, particularly with massively parallel simulations (e.g., Isaac Gym), offers flexibility and rapid convergence for developing control policies.

**Contribution of This Work to the Landscape:**
This paper addresses these gaps by introducing an open-source humanoid hardware platform, the "Duke Humanoid," specifically designed for dynamic locomotion and customization. This platform aims to fill the void of accessible, high-performance open-source hardware. Concurrently, the research explores a novel control strategy that integrates passive dynamics into an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework. This approach seeks to develop energy-efficient locomotion controllers by modulating joint activeness, thereby bridging the gap between bio-inspired passive walking principles and contemporary learning-based control paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research presented in this paper is driven by two primary objectives, each motivated by specific challenges and opportunities within the field of humanoid robotics.

**Objective 1: Design and Present an Open-Source Humanoid Platform for Dynamic Locomotion Research.**
- **Motivation:** The availability of high-performance humanoid robots for research is often limited by proprietary designs and closed systems. Many commercial humanoids offer limited customizability and access to low-level sensor and actuator interfaces, hindering fundamental research that requires hardware modifications or deep interaction with the robot's control system. While some open-source efforts exist, they often compromise on dynamic motion capabilities due to actuator choices or design constraints. There is a demand within the research community for a transparent, customizable, and capable open-source hardware platform that can facilitate investigations into low-level hardware interactions and enable co-optimization of hardware and control systems. The Duke Humanoid is intended to fulfill this need by providing such a platform.

**Objective 2: Enhance Energy Efficiency in Bipedal Locomotion by Leveraging Passive Dynamics within an End-to-End Reinforcement Learning Framework.**
- **Motivation:** A significant challenge in humanoid robotics is the high energy consumption associated with locomotion. Most humanoids rely on continuous active actuation of their joints, which leads to substantial power draw, frequent battery recharges, and increased operational costs. In contrast, biological systems, such as humans, achieve highly efficient walking by effectively utilizing passive dynamics, where leg swing motions, for example, approximate pendulum-like movements requiring minimal active effort. While traditional robotics has explored passive dynamic walking, these designs are often highly specialized and may not easily accommodate variations or payloads. The potential for integrating these energy-saving principles into modern, flexible learning-based control systems remains largely unexplored. This objective is motivated by the fundamental question of how robots can achieve energy-efficient, human-like walking, aiming to reduce energy expenditure by explicitly encouraging the robot to leverage its passive dynamics through a reinforcement learning approach. The intent is to develop controllers that can intelligently switch between active and passive control modes to optimize energy use without sacrificing dynamic capabilities or robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves a two-pronged approach: the development of a novel open-source humanoid hardware platform and the design of reinforcement learning control policies, including one specifically optimized for energy efficiency through passive dynamics.

**A. Hardware Design (The Duke Humanoid v1.0)**
- **Physical Characteristics:** The Duke Humanoid is a child-sized (1 meter tall, 30 kg) bipedal robot with 10 degrees of freedom (DoFs), 5 per leg. Its dimensions are designed to facilitate interaction with human-centric environments and to balance functionality, affordability, and customizability.
- **Mechanical Design:**
 - **Leg Structure:** Each leg comprises three hip joints (Hip Rotation (HR), Hip Flexion/Extension (HFE), Hip Abduction/Adduction (HAA)), one knee joint (Knee Flexion/Extension (KFE)), and one ankle joint (plantar flexion/dorsiflexion).
 - **Physiological Mimicry:** The design incorporates human-like proportions, with a tibia-to-femur ratio of 0.825 (comparable to the human average of 0.78). A nearly symmetric weight distribution and frontal plane hip alignment facilitate static straight-knee standing, a characteristic known to promote passive walking efficiency.
 - **Actuation System:** KFE and ankle joints are actuated via parallel linkages. This design relocates the motors higher in the leg, reducing the inertia of the lower leg segments and consequently the torque required for actuation. The ankle joint, having only one DoF (plantar flexion/dorsiflexion), features a linear contact surface on the foot to compensate for the absence of inversion/eversion DoFs.
 - **Actuators:** All joints utilize brushless DC motors with planetary gearboxes from Motorevo. Varying reduction ratios are employed across joints to meet specific torque and velocity requirements (e.g., HR, HAA, KFE: 18:1; HFE: 20:1; Ankles: 10:1). This uniform motor selection enhances interchangeability. Control is managed via EtherCAT communication at 2000 Hz.
 - **Manufacturing and Modularity:** The upper body features off-the-shelf aluminum extrusions, while legs and body plates are custom-machined from aluminum 6061, supporting a modular and easily modifiable design. Space is reserved for onboard power and computing.
- **Sensors:** The robot is equipped with a MicroStrain 3DM-CV7-AHRS IMU for orientation and angular velocity data, and four load cells (two at the toes, two at the heels) for contact sensing. A Teensy 4.0 microcontroller handles sensor data acquisition.
- **Co-design Philosophy:** The mechanical design and actuator selection were iteratively refined through simulations in Isaac Gym, where preliminary humanoid models were used to train walking policies. The resulting joint torque and velocity data informed actuator choices.

**B. Reinforcement Learning for Bipedal Locomotion (Baseline Policy)**
- **Task Definition:** The objective is to train the robot to track desired linear and angular velocities.
- **Observation Space:** The RL agent observes the robot's base angular velocity, base gravity vector, joint positions and velocities, target body velocity commands, a periodic phase signal, and a desired foot contact pattern. The critic network also receives privileged information, including linear velocity and actual foot contact.
- **Network Architecture:** An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is used, with both actor and critic networks comprising three fully connected layers with ELU activation functions.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs target joint position offsets (`a_q`), which are scaled and added to default joint positions (`q_0`) to generate target joint positions (`q*`) at 50 Hz. These target positions are then smoothed with a low-pass filter and converted to torques via a position-based PD controller operating at 200 Hz in simulation and 2000 Hz on the physical robot.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function incentivizes velocity tracking, maintaining base height and orientation, regularization of joint accelerations and torques, adherence to joint limits, and proper gait characteristics such as foot air time, contact patterns, and foot placement.
- **Training:** Training was conducted in 4096 parallel environments using Isaac Gym on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU for 30 minutes.

**C. Policy for Energy-Efficient Locomotion (Passive Policy)**
- **Concept:** To encourage energy efficiency, a mechanism for modulating joint activeness was introduced within the RL framework.
- **Torque Modulation:** A per-joint torque activation parameter, `α ∈`, scales the output of the position-based PD controller. When `α = 0`, the joint becomes passive, its movement governed by passive dynamics. The modified torque command is `τ = α (k_p(q* - q) + k_d(q_dot* - q_dot))`.
- **Actor Output and Activation:** The new actor network outputs `[a_q, a_α]`. `a_α` is passed through a sigmoid function to ensure `α ∈`. A constant `α_0` is added to define a minimum activeness, decaying linearly from 0.5 to 0 during training to prevent policies from getting stuck at low `α` values.
- **Passive Action Reward:** A specific reward term, `r_a_α = ||1 - a_α||_1`, is added to the total reward, scaled by 0.005, to explicitly incentivize the agent to reduce joint activeness.

**D. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**
- **Dynamics Parameter Tuning:** Critical joint dynamics parameters (damping, friction, armature) were identified and tuned in simulation. This involved control frequency sweeps (1.5-3 Hz) to minimize discrepancies between simulated and real-world joint positions, velocities, and torques.
- **Domain Randomization:** To enhance the robustness of the trained policies for zero-shot real-world deployment, various parameters were randomized during simulation training. These included additive changes to base center of mass (COM) and joint offsets/positions/velocities, scaling changes to base mass, link mass/inertia, and motor strength, and randomization of observation delay, angular velocity, gravity vector, and friction parameters. This randomization strategy addresses issues such as motor backlash, sensor noise, and inherent dynamics mismatches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded results demonstrating both the functional capabilities of the Duke Humanoid platform and the energy efficiency benefits of the proposed passive control policy.

**A. Locomotion on the Real Hardware (Baseline Policy)**
- **Zero-Shot Deployment:** The reinforcement learning policy, trained solely in simulation, was successfully deployed on the physical Duke Humanoid hardware without any fine-tuning. This demonstrates effective sim-to-real transfer.
- **Qualitative Performance:** The robot successfully performed forward walking tasks on a flat carpeted surface at a command velocity of 0.3 m/s. Visual evidence, including chronophotographs, confirmed stable locomotion.
- **Quantitative Sim-to-Real Comparison:** A comparison of HFE (Hip Flexion/Extension) and KFE (Knee Flexion/Extension) joint dynamics between simulation and real-world execution revealed that target joint positions, actual joint positions, and joint velocities exhibited reasonable agreement. Maximum differences were 0.1 rad for HFE target position and 0.15 rad for KFE target position. Actual joint positions showed maximum errors of 0.1 rad for HFE and 0.2 rad for KFE, primarily significant during half of the gait cycle. These bounded differences indicate a comparable behavior between the simulated and physical systems.

**B. Passive Policy for Energy-Efficient Locomotion**
- **Distinct Behavior:** The passive policy exhibited visually distinct behavior compared to the baseline. At zero velocity, the baseline policy actively stepped in place, whereas the passive policy remained motionless, indicating a reduction in energy consumption for maintaining static poses.
- **Energy Efficiency (Simulation):** The Cost of Transport (CoT), a metric for energy efficiency, was evaluated.
 - **Walking:** At low walking speeds (0.1 m/s to 0.5 m/s), the passive policy demonstrated up to a 50% reduction in CoT compared to the baseline policy, with the highest reduction observed at 0.1 m/s.
 - **Running:** For running speeds, the passive policy also outperformed the baseline, achieving approximately a 10% reduction in CoT.
 - **Gait Characteristics:** The passive policy resulted in a reduced range of motion for both HFE and KFE joints at low velocities, suggesting less active involvement. Base height and foot heights were comparable to the baseline, implying similar gait patterns. A slightly larger base orientation error (averaging 0.01 rad) in the passive walking policy suggested a forward tilt, potentially to leverage passive dynamics.
- **Robustness (Simulation):** Push recovery tests were conducted by applying random horizontal forces (up to 300 N) to the robot's base.
 - **Walking:** The passive walking policy achieved comparable success rates in push recovery to the baseline walking policy.
 - **Running:** The passive running policy showed slightly reduced success rates at lower push magnitudes but performed slightly better at higher magnitudes compared to the baseline.
 - These results indicate that the integration of passive actions did not significantly compromise the policy's robustness against external disturbances.

**C. Passive Locomotion in the Real World**
- **Zero-Shot Deployment:** The passive control policy was successfully deployed zero-shot on the physical Duke Humanoid. Similar to the baseline policy, real-world joint positions and velocities for HFE and KFE showed reasonable agreement with simulated trajectories.
- **Real-World Energy Efficiency:** In real-world trials at a command forward velocity of 0.3 m/s, the baseline policy achieved an average CoT of 1.13 ± 0.1. The passive policy improved the CoT to 0.77 ± 0.1, representing a 31% reduction in energy consumption. This real-world validation confirms the energy efficiency benefits observed in simulation.
- **Joint Actuation Patterns:** Analysis of joint activation patterns during passive walking (Fig. 9) revealed periodic activation in hip joints. Crucially, the KFE joint demonstrated deactivation during the leg swing phase, consistent with the utilization of passive dynamics for pendulum-like motion. The ankle joint, however, did not exhibit a significant periodic activation pattern, which the authors suggest might be due to higher joint friction affecting passive rotation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics through both hardware and control innovations, with several potential impacts.

- **Advancement of Open-Source Hardware Platforms:** The introduction of the Duke Humanoid v1.0 as an open-source platform addresses a recognized gap in the robotics research community. By providing accessible and customizable hardware and software, it lowers the barrier to entry for researchers to conduct fundamental studies. This transparency enables detailed investigation of low-level hardware interactions, facilitates rapid prototyping and modification, and promotes co-optimization of hardware and control systems, which can accelerate advancements in dynamic locomotion and other humanoid capabilities.
- **Novel Approach to Energy-Efficient Locomotion:** The work demonstrates a method for achieving energy-efficient bipedal locomotion by directly integrating passive dynamics into an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework. This approach moves beyond traditional model-based passive walkers and shows that learning-based controllers can effectively leverage passive mechanical properties. The observed 31% reduction in Cost of Transport in real-world experiments represents a tangible improvement in energy efficiency, addressing a critical limitation for the practical deployment of humanoids.
- **Bridging Traditional and Learning-Based Control:** By explicitly modulating joint activeness within an RL framework, the research illustrates a strategy for combining principles from traditional robotics (passive dynamics) with modern data-driven learning techniques. This hybrid approach has the potential to yield controllers that are not only robust and performant but also bio-inspired and energetically frugal, opening new avenues for designing intelligent control systems.
- **Practical Implications for Humanoid Deployment:** Enhanced energy efficiency directly translates to longer operational durations, reduced battery dependency, and lower maintenance costs for humanoid robots. This could make humanoids more viable for a wider range of applications in real-world unstructured environments, including exploration, logistics, and assistance tasks, where extended autonomy is crucial.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The developed platform and control methodology establish a foundation for subsequent research. The paper explicitly outlines future directions, such as adding arms for whole-body control and mobile manipulation studies, and implementing onboard power for untethered, more dynamic motions like running and jumping. This suggests the Duke Humanoid will serve as a fertile ground for exploring complex robotic behaviors and pushing the boundaries of humanoid capabilities.
