# The Duke Humanoid: Design and Control For Energy Efficient Bipedal Locomotion Using Passive Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Duke University developed an open-source humanoid robot, the Duke Humanoid, and a reinforcement learning control policy that leverages passive dynamics to achieve energy-efficient bipedal locomotion. This approach reduced the robot's Cost of Transport by 31% in real-world walking tasks compared to a fully active baseline policy, while maintaining robustness.

## Problem

- A scarcity of open-source, high-performance humanoid hardware limits fundamental research into hardware-control co-optimization and customizability.
- Many existing humanoids consume high energy due to continuous active joint actuation, leading to practical limitations like short battery life.
- Integrating passive dynamics for energy efficiency into modern end-to-end deep reinforcement learning frameworks for bipedal locomotion is largely unexplored.

## Method

- Developed an open-source humanoid platform, "==The Duke Humanoid== v1.0," a child-sized (1m, 30kg) bipedal robot with 10 DoFs, designed for dynamic locomotion and customizability.
- Implemented an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for bipedal locomotion, trained in parallel simulation environments (Isaac Gym).
- Introduced a "passive policy" that modulates individual joint activeness during locomotion, incentivizing the agent to leverage passive dynamics for energy savings.

## Results

- The Duke Humanoid successfully performed zero-shot real-world forward walking on a carpeted surface at **0.3** m/s using policies trained solely in simulation.
- The passive policy achieved up to a **50%** reduction in Cost of Transport (CoT) in simulation for low-speed walking and a **31%** reduction in CoT during real-world experiments at **0.3** m/s.
- The passive walking policy maintained comparable robustness to external disturbances (push recovery) when compared to the baseline active policy, demonstrating that energy efficiency did not compromise stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An open-source hardware platform like the Duke Humanoid can provide the robotics community with an accessible, high-performance tool for research and rapid prototyping.
> - Reinforcement learning can be effectively designed to incorporate and exploit passive dynamics, leading to substantial energy efficiency gains in bipedal locomotion.
> - Modulating joint activeness within an RL framework allows for a flexible balance between active control and passive, energy-saving movements, without significantly compromising robustness.
