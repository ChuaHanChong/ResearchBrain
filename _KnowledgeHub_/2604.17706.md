# OmniVLA-RL: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Spatial Understanding and Online RL

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniVLA-RL introduces a vision-language-action (VLA) model for robotic manipulation, integrating enhanced spatial understanding, deep multimodal fusion, and a stable online reinforcement learning method. It achieved an average success rate of 97.6% on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrated superior convergence stability and sample efficiency on LIBERO-Plus compared to prior RL approaches.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack precise 3D spatial perception, hindering accurate object grasping and manipulation.
- Current multimodal fusion strategies in VLA models typically modify only peripheral components, limiting deep integration of linguistic, visual, and spatial information.
- Online reinforcement learning (RL) methods like PPO and GRPO exhibit instability and inefficiency when applied to VLA models, complicating policy refinement.

## Method

- **OmniVLA-RL** uses a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture with dedicated ==Spatial==, ==Reasoning==, and ==Action Experts== for deep and bidirectional multimodal fusion, addressing spatial understanding.
- **Flow-GSPO**, a principled online reinforcement learning method, reformulates deterministic ==Flow Matching== into a ==Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)== process for stable policy exploration and refinement.
- A ==three-stage progressive training paradigm== is employed, including ==multimodal spatial perception pre-training==, ==action generation pre-training==, and ==online reinforcement learning== with Flow-GSPO.

## Results

- OmniVLA-RL achieved an average success rate of **97.6%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods across all four task suites.
- On the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, Flow-GSPO demonstrated a **70%** success rate within the initial **50 training steps**, indicating faster convergence and higher stability compared to PPO and GRPO.
- Ablation studies confirmed the Spatial Expert's criticality, with its removal causing an **8.3%** performance drop, and highlighted Flow-GSPO's contribution, providing a **39.1%** absolute success rate increase over supervised fine-tuning alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a specialized Spatial Expert within a MoT backbone enhances granular spatial understanding and enables robust scene perception, crucial for precise robotic tasks.
> - Reformulating Flow Matching from a deterministic ODE to a stochastic SDE allows for effective integration with reinforcement learning, enabling exploration and policy optimization.
> - Action block-level optimization within Flow-GSPO improves RL stability and sample efficiency for continuous robotic trajectories by capturing sequence-level dependencies better than token-based approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper operates within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. VLA models aim to enable robots to interpret human language instructions and execute corresponding actions within complex visual environments.

Existing VLA models, often built upon Vision-Language Models (VLMs), generally exhibit robust scene understanding and instruction parsing capabilities. However, the authors identify several limitations in the current landscape:
- **Spatial Perception Deficiencies:** Traditional VLMs frequently struggle with precise spatial understanding, such as accurately determining the 3D positions, dimensions, and orientations of objects. This limitation directly impacts the precision required for robotic manipulation tasks like grasping, object handling, and obstacle avoidance.
- **Suboptimal Multimodal Fusion:** Many existing VLA methods, categorized as either early or late fusion approaches, incorporate spatial features only at the encoder module before the VLM or at the action generation head after the VLM. These methods typically do not alter the core structure of the VLM itself, which the authors argue prevents the efficient and deep integration of linguistic, visual, spatial, and robotic action information within the large model's Transformer layers. Examples of such approaches mentioned include Evo-0, SpatialVLA (early fusion), and FALCON (late fusion).
- **Reinforcement Learning Instability:** The integration of reinforcement learning (RL) into VLA models, intended to enhance generalization and reduce data dependency from imitation learning, has faced challenges. Methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) are described as computationally complex, requiring a value model of similar dimensions to the action model, leading to cumbersome training. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is noted for design flaws in its token importance ratio, contributing to instability and a high tendency to collapse during training.

This research aims to address these identified gaps by proposing a novel architecture that integrates spatial understanding more deeply within the VLA model and introduces a more robust online reinforcement learning methodology. The development of OmniVLA-RL represents an effort to advance the capabilities of embodied AI by improving the precision, integration, and training stability of VLA systems. It builds upon previous advancements in generative models, such as flow matching, and reinforcement learning techniques, adapting them for the specific requirements of robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of OmniVLA-RL are multi-faceted, stemming from the identified limitations in contemporary VLA models for embodied AI:

1. **Enhance Spatial Perception for Robotic Manipulation:** A core motivation is to overcome the imprecise spatial understanding often observed in existing VLA models. Robotic manipulation tasks inherently demand accurate 3D perception of targets and the environment to perform tasks such as precise object grasping, intricate manipulation, and collision avoidance. The authors highlight that general VLMs, despite their broader scene understanding, frequently lack the ability to output exact 3D positions and dimensions, which is critical for physical robot interactions. Therefore, a key objective is to effectively integrate and leverage spatial information to improve the precision of VLA models.

2. **Improve Multimodal Fusion within the Core Model Architecture:** The paper identifies that current early and late fusion strategies for incorporating spatial features into VLA pipelines are suboptimal. By merely adding spatial information at the periphery of the VLM (either before the encoder or after the action head), these methods do not allow for a deep, synergistic interaction between linguistic instructions, visual semantics, 3D spatial features, and robotic actions within the central large model component. A significant objective is to develop an architecture that facilitates the flow and interaction of these diverse feature types directly within the Transformer layers, thereby enabling a more efficient and integrated representation.

3. **Develop a Stable and Efficient Online Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The authors are motivated to address the challenges associated with applying reinforcement learning to VLA models. Existing RL methods like PPO are noted for their computational complexity and the need for a separate value model, making VLA model training cumbersome. GRPO, while simplifying some aspects, is criticized for its instability and tendency to collapse due to issues with its token importance ratio. The objective is to introduce an RL method that enhances action precision and training robustness for VLA models, is compatible with action sequence generation, and offers improved stability compared to existing approaches. This includes adapting flow matching for stochastic exploration within the RL framework.

4. **Generate High-Precision Spatial Action Trajectories:** Ultimately, the overarching goal is to enable VLA models to directly generate high-precision spatial action trajectories. This involves transforming multimodal inputs (visual observations, language instructions, and detailed spatial information) into precise robotic control signals that are accurately grounded in the physical environment.

In summary, OmniVLA-RL is motivated by the need for VLA models that possess superior spatial intelligence, integrate multimodal information more effectively, and can be fine-tuned robustly and efficiently using reinforcement learning to achieve higher success rates in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OmniVLA-RL is structured around two primary components: a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model and an online reinforcement learning (RL) module. Its development follows a three-stage progressive training paradigm.

#### 3.1. VLA Model Architecture: Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)

The VLA model utilizes a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone, which integrates a novel tri-expert mechanism to process and interact with different modalities.

- **Reasoning Expert:** This expert is designed for robust instruction following and scene understanding. It is initialized with a pre-trained VLM (PaLiGemma). It employs SigLIP as a vision encoder to extract high-level semantic features (z_sem) from multi-view observations. These visual embeddings are concatenated with linguistic tokens (z_lang) and processed by a decoder-only Transformer to model the conditional distribution p(z_lang | z_sem), enabling cross-modal alignment and providing a global semantic context.
- **Spatial Expert:** This component focuses on extracting fine-grained 3D structural information from multi-view observations, which is crucial for manipulation tasks often neglected by high-level VLMs. It uses VGGT to extract granular features, which are then integrated into the Transformer backbone to form spatial representations (z_spatial). To aid optimization during training, a lightweight Transformer decoder, serving as a spatial auxiliary head, is appended to the final hidden state. This head is supervised by spatial-centric pretext tasks (e.g., reconstruction losses for point clouds, camera parameters, and surface normals) but is decoupled during inference.
- **Action Expert:** The Action Expert is responsible for generating precise robotic control commands. It takes the fused spatial-semantic representations and linguistic supervision as input. An action chunking strategy is adopted, where action sequences are projected into the Transformer's latent space. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) is employed to model the action distribution, p(a | z_spatial, z_sem, z_lang), ensuring both action precision and computational efficiency.

#### 3.2. Block-wise Causal Attention

To facilitate the integration of heterogeneous multimodal representations within a unified Transformer architecture, OmniVLA-RL introduces a Block-wise Causal Attention mechanism.
- **Prefix Tokens:** Tokens from the Reasoning and Spatial Experts form an "omni-visible prefix." This allows for bidirectional cross-modal attention between spatial patches and semantic contexts, guided by task prompts. This mechanism aims to establish a physically grounded environmental representation before action generation.
- **Action Suffix Tokens:** Action chunks constitute a causal suffix. They can attend to the complete prefix information but are restricted to autoregressive causality internally (i.e., only to preceding action tokens within the suffix). Critically, prefix modules are prevented from attending to the latent noise within subsequent action blocks, safeguarding scene understanding from stochastic noise during the diffusion sampling process.

#### 3.3. Flow-GSPO: Online Reinforcement Learning Module

Flow-GSPO is a novel online reinforcement learning method designed to fine-tune the VLA model.

- **Stochastic Flow Matching:** The deterministic Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) process of flow matching, used for action generation, is reformulated into a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) via the Fokker-Planck equation. This transformation introduces random noise, rendering the action generation process differentiable and suitable for stochastic exploration required in reinforcement learning. The Euler-Maruyama method is used to discretize the SDE, leading to an update formula where action transitions are modeled as isotropic Gaussian distributions.
- **GSPO on Stochastic Flow Matching:** This method applies Group Segmented Policy Optimization (GSPO) at the action-block level, addressing limitations of token-based optimization (e.g., single-step bias and disruption of action continuity). For each state, the agent samples a group (G) of action sequences. The likelihood of each action sequence is calculated as a product of Gaussian probabilities over its denoising steps. An action block-level importance ratio is computed based on the ratio of new and old policy likelihoods for the entire action block. The advantage of each action block is estimated by normalizing task rewards (cumulative reward over the action block sequence). The final optimization objective for Flow-GSPO includes a clipped advantage term and a KL divergence penalty between the old and new policies to enhance training stability. A detailed gradient analysis is provided, demonstrating how the model parameters are updated.

#### 3.4. Three-stage Progressive Training Paradigm

OmniVLA-RL is trained in three progressive stages:

1. **Multimodal Spatial Perception Pre-training (Stage I):** The VLM (PaLiGemma) is initialized with pre-trained weights, while the Spatial Expert is stochastically initialized. Both the Reasoning and Spatial Experts are jointly optimized on large-scale 3D datasets, with the Action Expert frozen. Supervision is provided by spatial-centric reconstruction losses (L_Spatial) for point clouds, camera parameters, and surface normals. This stage establishes stable spatial-semantic representations.
2. **Action Generation Pre-training (Stage II):** Building on Stage I, the Action Expert is unfrozen, and the Spatial Head is deactivated. Training occurs on the DROID dataset, with the primary objective being the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) loss, which bridges scene understanding with policy synthesis.
3. **Online Reinforcement Learning with Flow-GSPO (Stage III):** The full model, initialized from the Stage II checkpoint, is fine-tuned. During this stage, all parameters are unfrozen. The agent generates G candidate action blocks via stochastic flow matching, executes them in the environment, and observes task rewards (binary task-completion and continuous gripper-alignment). The policy is updated using the Flow-GSPO objective with specified hyperparameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of OmniVLA-RL was evaluated through extensive experiments on two robotic manipulation benchmarks: LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus.

#### 4.1. LIBERO Benchmark Results

OmniVLA-RL demonstrated strong performance across all four task suites of the LIBERO benchmark (LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long), achieving an average success rate of 97.6%.
- **Superiority in Complex Reasoning:** The model surpassed the π_0.5 baseline by 0.4% in LIBERO-Spatial and 0.5% in LIBERO-Goal tasks, indicating effective modulation of spatial perception and goal-directed reasoning.
- **Robustness in Long-Horizon Tasks:** OmniVLA-RL achieved a 93.5% success rate in LIBERO-Long, which was 1.1% higher than the second-best method. This suggests effectiveness in tasks sensitive to accumulating errors over longer durations.
- **Overall Performance Gains:** Compared to the π_0 baseline, OmniVLA-RL provided a 21.1% absolute improvement in average success rate, which the authors attribute to the representational capabilities of its three-expert architecture and supervised fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. LIBERO-Plus Benchmark Results

Evaluation on the more challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, designed for compositional long-horizon tasks, focused on training dynamics and multi-task scalability.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** Flow-GSPO achieved a 70% success rate within the first 50 training steps, demonstrating faster convergence than both PPO and GRPO baselines. This efficiency is attributed to its action block-level importance ratio.
- **Superior Convergence Stability:** Flow-GSPO exhibited a consistent, monotonic improvement trend throughout training, in contrast to PPO, which experienced performance fluctuations and regressions (e.g., around step 80). This stability is linked to the segmented policy optimization and the action-block KL divergence term.
- **High Performance Ceiling:** In the latter stages of training (post 100 steps), Flow-GSPO maintained a success rate above 80%, outperforming GRPO by approximately 14.6% and PPO by 1.6% (as indicated in ablation studies). This suggests that the Stochastic Flow Matching approach contributes to both faster policy discovery and a higher maximum achievable performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark investigated the contribution of core components.
- **Superiority of Flow-GSPO:** Compared to the OmniVLA-RL trained with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) only, the integration of Flow-GSPO resulted in a 39.1% absolute increase in success rate (from 41.2% to 80.3%). Under identical training conditions, Flow-GSPO outperformed PPO (78.7%) and GRPO (65.7%), providing a 1.6% and 14.6% higher success rate respectively. This suggests that action block-level optimization is more suitable for continuous robotic trajectories than token-based policy gradient methods.
- **Critical Impact of the Spatial Expert:** Removing the Spatial Expert led to an 8.3% performance drop (from 41.2% to 32.9%), which was the most significant degradation among all architectural ablations. This result indicates that the high-resolution spatial features provided by the Spatial Expert are necessary for precise object localization and handling complex occlusions in manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of OmniVLA-RL represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Core Limitations in VLA Models:** The work directly addresses identified fundamental challenges in existing VLA models, specifically imprecise spatial perception, suboptimal multimodal fusion, and instability in reinforcement learning. By proposing solutions to these issues, OmniVLA-RL contributes to the development of more capable and reliable robotic agents.
- **Establishing a Unified Architecture for Multimodal Integration:** The Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture, with its specialized Spatial, Reasoning, and Action Experts, offers a method for deeply integrating linguistic, visual, and spatial information within a unified framework. The Block-wise Causal Attention mechanism further refines this integration, enabling robust scene understanding alongside coherent action generation, which has implications for how multimodal data can be processed in other complex AI systems.
- **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Action Spaces:** The introduction of Flow-GSPO, which reformulates flow matching as a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) process and integrates it with Group Segmented Policy Optimization at the action-block level, presents a more stable and sample-efficient online reinforcement learning approach. This method's demonstrated superior convergence speed and higher performance ceiling over traditional RL algorithms like PPO and GRPO can accelerate policy discovery and refinement for continuous control tasks in robotics.
- **Enhancing Robotic Manipulation Capabilities:** The superior performance demonstrated on the LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus benchmarks indicates that OmniVLA-RL can enable robots to execute human language instructions with greater precision and success in complex visual environments. The high success rates, particularly in spatial reasoning and long-horizon tasks, suggest the potential for more advanced and versatile robotic systems.
- **Emphasizing the Importance of Spatial Grounding:** The ablation study's finding that the Spatial Expert is critical for performance underscores the necessity of dedicated architectural components for fine-grained geometric understanding. This highlights that while general VLMs are powerful, explicit spatial grounding remains essential for high-precision physical interaction tasks, influencing future architectural designs in embodied AI.
- **Future Trajectories for Embodied AI:** The limitations acknowledged by the authors, such as the sim-to-real gap and the lack of a dedicated world model for long-horizon planning, point towards future research directions. The framework established by OmniVLA-RL provides a foundation upon which subsequent work can build, including deployment on physical platforms, integration of generative world models for imagination-based planning, and expansion to more unstructured, open-world manipulation scenarios. This work contributes to the broader goal of developing generalizable and high-performance robotic agents capable of mastering diverse operational scenarios.
