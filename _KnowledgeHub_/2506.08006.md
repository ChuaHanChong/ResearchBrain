# Dreamland: Controllable World Creation with Simulator and Generative Models

> [!summary] Summary
> UCLA researchers develop Dreamland, a hybrid framework that combines physics-based simulators with large-scale generative models for controllable world creation through a novel Layered World Abstraction (LWA) that decomposes scenes into traffic participants, map layouts, and background elements, achieving 52.3% better image quality (FID) and 17.9% improved depth alignment compared to SimGen while demonstrating zero-shot generalization across simulators and enabling precise object-level control for safety-critical scenario generation in autonomous driving, with their D3Sim dataset containing ~60,000 paired simulated and real-world driving conditions and downstream validation showing 3.9% accuracy improvements for embodied agents trained on Dreamland-generated synthetic data, alongside extensions to 4K video generation up to 121 frames that preserve both photorealistic quality and fine-grained simulator control through minimal adaptation of pre-trained foundation models.

## Problem

- Large-scale generative models lack fine-grained, element-wise controllability needed for precise scenario creation in embodied AI training
- Physics-based simulators provide accurate control but generate unrealistic visuals that create domain gaps for real-world deployment
- Existing hybrid approaches either sacrifice simulator fidelity or compromise controllability, failing to effectively bridge simulation and generative modeling

## Method

- Introduce Layered World Abstraction (LWA) as intermediate representation that decomposes scenes into traffic participants, map layout, and background layers
- Implement three-stage pipeline: simulation generation, LWA-**Sim2Real** refinement via instructional editing, and mixed-condition generation using pre-trained models
- Create **D3Sim** dataset with ~60,000 paired simulated and real-world driving scenarios for training and evaluation

## Results

- Achieves 52.3% lower FID and 17.9% better si-RMSE compared to SimGen baseline across image quality and controllability metrics
- Demonstrates zero-shot generalization to other simulators like MetaUrban and extends naturally to video generation with 4K resolution and 121-frame sequences
- Downstream validation shows 3.9% absolute accuracy improvement for InternVL2-8B vision-language model on real-world VQA tasks when trained with Dreamland synthetic data

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stage-2 LWA-Sim2Real refinement is critical for bridging domain gaps, with ablation studies showing consistent improvements in both fidelity and controllability
> - Minimal adaptation of pre-trained generative models (fine-tuning only linear projection layers) preserves world knowledge while enabling structural control
> - The framework demonstrates excellent scalability, with performance gains when upgrading from SDXL to SD3 to Flux foundation models

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative modeling has witnessed monumental progress with the advent of large-scale pre-trained models capable of synthesizing highly diverse and realistic content across various modalities, including text, images, and videos. These "foundation models" are increasingly seen as holding the "world knowledge" necessary to create dynamic, interactive environments, thus presenting immense potential for providing synthetic data to train embodied AI agents, reducing reliance on expensive human supervision or physical environments.

However, a critical challenge remains: while these generative models excel at photorealism, they often lack the fine-grained, element-wise controllability essential for practical applications such as autonomous driving. Training autonomous vehicles, for instance, demands precise control over specific scene layouts, object configurations, and dynamic behaviors (e.g., simulating a specific lane change maneuver in dense traffic). Generic generative models typically struggle to offer this object-level control, limiting their utility in scenarios where precise interactions are paramount.

To address this limitation, a hybrid approach has emerged, combining the strengths of physics-based simulators and data-driven generative models. Simulators provide accurate physical and spatial information (e.g., vehicle dynamics, traffic rules), while generative models overlay this structural information with photorealistic visual content. Existing hybrid methods, however, grapple with striking the right balance. Some approaches prioritize simulator fidelity by directly rendering scenes without significant "hallucination" (e.g., Lucidsim, SimWorld), which necessitates extremely high-fidelity simulators and compromises generalizability and scalability. Conversely, others fully rely on generative models for re-rendering without stringent constraints (e.g., SimGen), leading to a sacrifice of fine-grained simulator control.

Dreamland positions itself as a direct evolution and improvement upon these hybrid methodologies. By introducing a novel "Layered World Abstraction (LWA)" as an intermediate representation, Dreamland aims to synergistically combine the precise controllability of simulators with the rich visual detail and creative freedom of large-scale generative models. Unlike prior work that either sacrifices fidelity or control, Dreamland's LWA design bridges the two domains effectively, aligning the structured output of a simulator with the input requirements of advanced generative models. This allows it to leverage existing and future pre-trained models "off-the-shelf" with minimal adaptation, thereby minimizing development costs and maximizing scalability. The work explicitly notes its superior performance over previous state-of-the-art methods like SimGen, underscoring its advancement in the field of controllable world creation for embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Dreamland stems from the evolving needs of embodied AI research, particularly for agents operating in complex real-world environments like autonomous driving. While large-scale generative models have made significant strides in synthesizing realistic visual content, their inherent lack of fine-grained, object-level controllability poses a significant hurdle for practical agent training. Training embodied AI agents requires not just diverse visual data, but also data where specific elements (e.g., individual vehicles, pedestrians, traffic signs) can be manipulated, and their interactions controlled precisely. This is crucial for simulating safety-critical scenarios, specific maneuvers, or rare events that are either impossible, dangerous, or cost-prohibitive to collect in the real world.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the Dreamland project are:

- **To develop a hybrid world generation framework:** The core aim is to combine the deterministic and physically grounded control offered by physics-based simulators with the photorealistic and diverse content generation capabilities of advanced, large-scale generative models.
- **To achieve granular element-wise controllability:** A fundamental goal is to enable precise control over individual objects and scene layouts within the generated world, allowing users to define specific traffic scenarios, object movements, and environmental conditions.
- **To ensure photorealistic and diverse visual output:** The generated scenes must be visually convincing and high-fidelity to enhance the realism of synthetic training data, crucial for effective Sim2Real transfer for embodied agents.
- **To minimize adaptation costs for pre-trained models:** The framework is designed to seamlessly integrate with off-the-shelf and future large-scale generative models (e.g., Text-to-Image/Video diffusion models) with minimal fine-tuning, preserving their extensive world knowledge and capabilities.
- **To bridge the domain gap effectively:** Introduce an intermediate representation, the Layered World Abstraction (LWA), to align the structured output of simulators with the input requirements of generative models, thereby mitigating the visual domain gap between simulation and reality.
- **To construct a dedicated dataset:** Develop a large-scale dataset, D3Sim, specifically designed to facilitate the training and evaluation of such hybrid generation pipelines, providing paired simulated and real-world conditions.
- **To demonstrate tangible benefits for embodied agent training:** Validate that the controllable, high-fidelity synthetic data generated by Dreamland can significantly enhance the learning and real-world adaptation of downstream embodied AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dreamland's methodology revolves around a novel **Layered World Abstraction (LWA)** and a three-stage pipeline designed to synergize physics-based simulators and large-scale generative models.

#### 3.1. Layered World Abstraction (LWA)

LWA serves as the crucial intermediate representation, aligning the simulator's output with the generative model's input in pixel space, enabling fine-grained control. It decomposes a scene into multiple "world layers," each corresponding to different classes of objects or regions. The paper defines LWA as $W = \bigcup_{i=0}^N (L_i \odot V_i)$, where $L_i$ represents the $i$-th world layer (e.g., depth, segmentation maps for specific objects) and $V_i$ is its visibility mask. Each layer encodes both pixel-level and object-level geometry and semantics through multiple modalities of condition. For autonomous driving scenes, LWA is composed of three primary layers:
- $L_d$: Traffic participants (dynamic objects like vehicles, pedestrians).
- $L_l$: Map layout (static elements like roads, crosswalks, fences).
- $L_b$: Background (static objects like buildings, vegetation, sky).

#### 3.2. Three-Stage Pipeline

1. **Stage-1 Simulation:**
 - A physics-based simulator (e.g., MetaDrive) constructs an initial scene based on user-provided "scene records" or agent trajectories.
 - This stage generates the "Simulator Layered World Abstraction" (Sim-LWA). Sim-LWA provides accurate, physically grounded information for layers requiring precise control ($L_d$, $L_l$). However, it often lacks the rich detail and diversity of the real world, particularly for background elements ($L_b$).

2. **Stage-2 LWA-Sim2Real:**
 - This is a critical refinement stage that bridges the domain gap between Sim-LWA and real-world distributions.
 - The traffic participants ($L_d$) and map layout ($L_l$) layers derived from the simulator are preserved to maintain precise control.
 - The background layer ($L_b$), which exhibits the most significant domain disparity from real-world visual distributions, is made editable.
 - An "instructional editing model" (e.g., ACE++) refines $L_b$ according to user text instructions, transferring it to a real-world distribution while preserving the structural grounding from the simulator.
 - This process yields the "Real-world Layered World Abstraction" (Real-LWA), which is now aligned with real-world visual distributions.

3. **Stage-3 Mixed-Condition Generation:**
 - A large-scale pre-trained conditional generative model (e.g., Flux Depth) takes the refined Real-LWA as input to render a realistic and aesthetically pleasing scene.
 - Because Real-LWA is already aligned with real-world distributions, minimal adaptation (e.g., fine-tuning only a small linear projection layer $\xi$ to incorporate LWA modalities) is required for the generative model, thereby preserving its vast pre-trained world knowledge.
 - The LWA conditions are encoded, concatenated, and projected into the latent space of the generative model, providing structural guidance to the noise latent.

#### 3.3. Training Scheme and Implementation Details

The Dreamland pipeline is trained in two sequential steps:
1. The instructional editing model for Stage-2 (LWA-Sim2Real) is trained to learn the Sim2Real refinement of LWA. This uses a reconstruction loss comparing the refined $L_b$ to a real-world $L_b$ target.
2. The conditional generative model for Stage-3 (Mixed-Condition Generation) is fine-tuned, specifically its linear projection layer $\xi$, to effectively incorporate structural control from the Real-LWA. This minimizes overfitting and preserves the generative model's world knowledge.

#### 3.4. Data Curation: D3Sim Dataset

A significant contribution is the creation of **D3Sim (Diverse Driving Scenario in Real World and Simulation)**, a large-scale dataset specifically designed for training and evaluating hybrid generation pipelines.
- **Construction:** It's built upon the nuPlan dataset (real-world driving scenarios) and MetaDrive/ScenarioNet (simulation). Digital twins of real-world scenarios are created in MetaDrive.
- **Alignment:** Crucially, sensor data (e.g., depth, segmentation, instance maps) from the simulator (Sim Conditions) are pixel-wise aligned with "Real Conditions" obtained by running state-of-the-art perception models (DepthAnythingV2, SegFormer, GroundingDino+SAM2) on real nuPlan images. Text descriptions are generated via LLMs (Llama 3).
- **Training Dataset:** Contains ~1,800 digital twin scenarios with ~60,000 paired Sim and Real conditions, split into the three LWA layers.
- **Validation Dataset:** ~600 scenarios with ~16,000 samples, derived from real-world scenarios but modified to ensure unambiguous object placements for accurate controllability evaluation. Diverse text prompts are also generated.
- **D3Sim Video Dataset:** An extension for video generation, containing ~20,000 video clips with paired Sim and Real video conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Dreamland demonstrates significant advancements in controllable world creation, showcasing superior performance across quantitative and qualitative metrics, as well as in downstream applications.

- **Superior Image Quality and Controllability:**
 - **Quantitative:** Dreamland vastly outperforms the previous state-of-the-art hybrid method, SimGen, achieving a 52.3% lower FID (Fréchet Inception Distance, indicating better image quality) and 17.9% stronger si-RMSE (scale-invariant Root Mean Squared Error for depth alignment) in Table 1.
 - **Scalability:** The pipeline exhibits excellent scalability. By plugging in progressively stronger pre-trained models for Stage-3 (e.g., from SDXL to SD3 to Flux), Dreamland consistently yields marked gains in both image quality and controllability, confirming its ability to leverage future advancements in generative models.
 - **World-Knowledge Preservation:** A key finding is that fine-tuning Dreamland on Flux (a powerful generative model) results in visual fidelity comparable to the "frozen" Flux model, while simultaneously improving adherence to simulator conditions. This highlights Dreamland's effectiveness in preserving the vast world knowledge embedded in foundation models.

- **Qualitative Results and Versatility:**
 - **Diverse Scene Rendering:** Dreamland can re-render simulated driving scenes into diverse, high-quality images (up to 1024x576 resolution) that closely align with the simulator's spatial layout but vary significantly in appearance (weather, location, time of day, style) based on user-provided text prompts (Figure 4, Figure D, Figure E).
 - **Flexible Simulator Editing:** The LWA design enables dynamic scene editing. Users can define "preserved" and "editable" regions based on instructions, allowing for precise manipulation of specific objects or background elements while maintaining consistency with the simulator's original layout (e.g., removing a truck and adding a speedy car, Figure 6a, Figure F).
 - **Safety-Critical Scene Generation:** Dreamland can effectively generate safety-critical or rare driving scenarios that are difficult or dangerous to collect in the real world (Figure 6b), showcasing its practical utility for autonomous driving development.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalization:** The pipeline demonstrates zero-shot capability on other simulators (e.g., MetaUrban), highlighting its generalizability across different simulation platforms (Figure 6c).

- **Extension to Video Re-Rendering (Dreamland-Video):**
 - The simple and low-adaptation-cost design of Dreamland naturally extends to video generation. Dreamland-Video successfully re-renders simulator reference videos into realistic and diverse video frames while maintaining strong condition-following capabilities and preserving pre-trained world knowledge (Table 2, Figure 7). It can produce videos up to 4K resolution and 121 frames long.

- **Ablation Study on Stage-2 (LWA-Sim2Real):**
 - The ablation study strongly validates the necessity and effectiveness of the Stage-2 instructional editing model (Real-LWA refinement). Using Real-LWA as input for Stage-3 consistently leads to better FID scores (improved fidelity to real driving scenes) and significantly better controllability (higher mIoU and lower si-RMSE in preserved regions) compared to directly using Sim-LWA (Table 3, Table B). Without this editing, the Stage-2 model struggles to produce accurate object boundaries and maintain fine-grained simulator control.

- **Impact on Embodied Agent Training:**
 - Dreamland's generated synthetic data serves as an effective data augmentation strategy. When InternVL2-8B (a Vision-Language Model) was fine-tuned using Dreamland's re-renderings, it showed a significant improvement of 3.9% in absolute accuracy on a real-world visual question answering (VQA) test set (Table 4). This directly demonstrates Dreamland's potential to enhance the real-world adaptation and learning of downstream embodied agents by providing enriched visual observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Dreamland represents a substantial step forward in the quest for realistic and controllable synthetic data generation, with profound implications for embodied AI and computer vision research.

- **Resolving a Core Dilemma:** The most significant contribution of Dreamland is its effective resolution of the long-standing trade-off between the granular control offered by physics-based simulators and the photorealistic diversity provided by data-driven generative models. By introducing the Layered World Abstraction (LWA), Dreamland creates a robust bridge that allows both attributes to coexist and synergize, enabling truly controllable and configurable world creation.

- **Accelerating Embodied AI Development:** The ability to generate high-quality, diverse, and controllable synthetic data is paramount for training robust and generalizable embodied AI agents, particularly for applications like autonomous driving, robotics, and virtual assistants. Dreamland's framework facilitates the creation of complex and safety-critical scenarios that are otherwise difficult, dangerous, or cost-prohibitive to collect in the real world. This will significantly accelerate the development, testing, and deployment of safer and more capable AI systems.

- **Enhanced Sim2Real Transfer:** By producing synthetic data that is not only realistic but also precisely aligned with physical and semantic properties from simulations, Dreamland directly improves the efficacy of Sim2Real transfer. The demonstrated improvement in VQA performance for an embodied agent highlights how Dreamland's output can bridge the domain gap, allowing models trained in simulation to perform better in real-world settings.

- **Scalability and Future-Proofing:** Dreamland's design emphasizes minimal adaptation costs for integrating with existing and future large-scale pre-trained generative models. This "off-the-shelf" compatibility ensures that the framework remains relevant and benefits from continuous advancements in foundation models, making it a sustainable solution for long-term research and development.

- **D3Sim Dataset as a Catalyst:** The introduction of the D3Sim dataset, a large-scale collection of precisely aligned simulated and real-world driving scenarios, is a valuable resource for the research community. It provides a standardized benchmark and training ground for future hybrid generation pipelines, fostering further innovation in this critical area.

- **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated primarily for autonomous driving, the general principles of Dreamland's LWA and hybrid generation pipeline could be extended to other domains requiring controllable visual world creation, such as virtual reality content generation, game development, and architectural visualization.

Despite its current limitation of increased inference time due to the additional editing model and the cost of high-quality paired data annotation, Dreamland's foundational contributions—particularly its novel LWA and its validated effectiveness in improving embodied agent learning—lay a crucial groundwork for a new generation of AI systems that can seamlessly operate between simulated and real environments.
