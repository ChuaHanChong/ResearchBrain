# $\mathcal{P}^3$: Toward Versatile Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The P3 framework introduces a system for versatile embodied agents to operate in dynamic real-world environments by integrating active omni-task perception, feedback-agnostic tool integration, and dynamic multi-task planning. It achieves robust performance across various real-world tasks, including proactive task generation and adaptive execution, demonstrated with physical robots and smart home devices.

## Problem

- Embodied agents typically rely on passive environmental perception, missing dynamic changes caused by external factors and leading to incomplete observations.
- Existing agents are limited by rigid tool compatibility, often requiring bidirectional communication, which hinders integration with many real-world, unidirectional IoT devices.
- Multi-task planning in current systems is often simplistic (e.g., FIFO), failing to account for task dependencies, priorities, or dynamic adaptations in complex environments.

## Method

- A Perception Module continuously observes the environment via video streams, generating scene graphs, captions, and actively proposing new tasks using Vision-Language Models (VLMs), decoupling perception from specific tool feedback.
- A "Tool Plug-ins" architecture enables the integration of diverse devices (navigation, manipulation, IoT, web agents) without requiring direct bidirectional feedback, with their environmental impact observed by the Perception Module.
- An LLM-based Task Planner dynamically schedules passive, active, and scheduled tasks by evaluating priorities and dependencies, allowing for adaptive execution, interruptions, and resumptions.

## Results

- The Active Perception Capability Evaluation showed that VLMs like QwenVL-max achieved **77.09%** accuracy in identifying active tasks from video snapshots while minimizing false positives.
- Real-world deployment on humanoid robots demonstrated robust performance across 11 diverse tasks, successfully integrating navigation, manipulation, IoT, and web agents.
- The framework effectively managed competing tasks, handling interruptions and dynamically adjusting execution plans, showcasing human-like prioritization and adaptability in complex scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active, continuous environmental perception, decoupled from specific tool feedback, is crucial for real-world adaptability and enables agents to proactively identify and respond to dynamic changes.
> - Decoupling tool feedback from the core agent's perception allows for seamless integration of a wide array of devices, including simple IoT components, significantly expanding agent versatility and deployment possibilities.
> - An LLM-based dynamic task scheduler can effectively manage competing tasks, priorities, and dependencies, facilitating human-like adaptive behavior in complex, open-ended environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "Embodied Agents" is a frontier in artificial intelligence, aiming to create intelligent systems that can perceive, reason, and act within physical or virtual environments in real time. This field represents a convergence of robotics, computer vision, natural language processing, and machine learning, seeking to bridge the gap between abstract AI algorithms and tangible interaction with the world. P3 positions itself firmly within this landscape, specifically addressing critical limitations that hinder the deployment of embodied agents in complex, dynamic real-world scenarios.

Existing research in embodied intelligence has made significant strides, leading to frameworks like ROS (Robot Operating System) for modularity, RoboOS and RAI for hierarchical reasoning and skill libraries, and generalist agents such as Being-0 and Gemini Robotics that integrate vision, language, and action across various tasks. Many systems, particularly in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) and Vision-Language Action (VLA), excel at executing passive tasks—following explicit human instructions in structured settings.

However, P3 identifies several crucial gaps in these prior works:
1. **Passive and Incomplete Environmental Perception**: Most systems rely on feedback *from* tool agents (e.g., a gripper confirming a grasp) to infer environmental changes. This passive approach means agents often miss dynamic alterations caused by external factors (humans, other agents, unexpected events). This leads to incomplete observations, error accumulation, and a lack of adaptability to real-time dynamics, such as a sudden furniture rearrangement during a cleaning task. P3 directly challenges this by introducing *active perception*.
2. **Limited Tool Compatibility and Feedback Dependence**: Previous embodied agents often require bidirectional communication and feedback mechanisms from their toolkits. This restricts them to predefined hardware and skill sets and renders them incompatible with many real-world edge devices (e.g., IoT components, basic mechanical devices) that operate with unidirectional communication due to design, security, or resource constraints. P3 aims for a "plug-any-tool" capability by decoupling perception from tool feedback.
3. **Rigid Multi-task Planning**: Current task planning methods frequently employ simplistic approaches like first-in-first-out (FIFO) scheduling. This fails to account for task dependencies, user preferences, or dynamic priorities (e.g., an urgent fire alarm vs. routine room tidying). Such rigidity severely limits adaptability in multi-task, open-ended environments. P3 proposes a novel dynamic scheduler to address this complexity.
4. **Focus on Passive vs. Active Tasks**: While instruction-following (passive tasks) is well-explored, autonomous task generation based on environmental perception (active tasks) like emergency response or proactive cleaning has received less attention, often constrained by limited scene scopes or text-based inputs. P3 integrates both passive and active tasks.

By introducing a unified framework with active omni-task perception, feedback-agnostic tool integration, and dynamic multi-task planning, P3 directly contributes to advancing the state of the art in embodied AI. It moves beyond "benchmarks" that often simplify real-world complexities, striving for practical deployment in truly dynamic and unstructured environments. This work pushes towards more generalized, flexible, and robust embodied agents capable of more human-like reasoning and proactive interaction with their surroundings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the P3 framework is to develop **versatile embodied agents** that can seamlessly operate in complex, dynamic real-world environments. This overarching goal is motivated by three critical challenges identified in current embodied AI systems:

1. **Dynamic Environment Perception**: Existing embodied agents suffer from passive environmental perception, primarily relying on feedback from their own actions or tools to infer changes. This leads to a critical inability to detect real-time environmental dynamics caused by external factors (e.g., human actions, other agents), resulting in incomplete observations and a lack of adaptive decision-making. The motivation here is to enable agents to be truly "aware" of their surroundings, constantly observing and understanding changes beyond their direct interactions.
 - **Objective**: Introduce a unified perception module that actively and continuously perceives relevant task information from the environment, decoupling perception from specific tool agents and eliminating reliance on tool feedback. This module should detect dynamic changes, identify tasks autonomously, and update an internal representation of the scene.

2. **Open-ended Tool Usage and Compatibility**: A significant practical hurdle for current embodied agents is their dependence on predefined toolkits that often require bidirectional communication. This severely limits their compatibility with a vast array of real-world devices, particularly IoT components or basic mechanical devices that are designed for unidirectional communication or lack complex feedback protocols. This restriction hinders wide-scale practical deployment.
 - **Objective**: Enable agents to "plug and utilize any tool" without the prerequisite of feedback mechanisms. This involves designing an architecture that can seamlessly integrate diverse devices, from advanced robotic manipulators to simple IoT sensors and web agents, by effectively managing context and command issuance.

3. **Complex Multi-task Planning**: As agents become more capable and interact with richer environments, they inevitably face multiple competing tasks—some user-initiated, some self-identified, and some scheduled. Current planning methods are often simplistic (e.g., FIFO), failing to manage task dependencies, prioritize urgent actions, or adapt to interruptions and resumptions in dynamic settings. This limits their effectiveness in real-world scenarios where unforeseen events are common.
 - **Objective**: Develop a dynamic multi-task planning module that can jointly process various types of tasks (passive, active, scheduled), evaluate their priorities and dependencies, and dynamically adjust task execution order to ensure efficient and adaptive behavior in complex, resource-constrained environments.

In essence, P3 is motivated by the desire to bridge the significant gap between controlled benchmark environments and the unpredictability of practical deployments. By tackling these three core challenges—perception, tool interaction, and planning—the authors aim to deliver a new generation of highly transferable and general-purpose embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The P3 framework is designed as a unified system that integrates real-time perception, dynamic scheduling, and flexible tool interaction. It consists of three core, interconnected modules: the Perception Module, Tool Plug-ins, and the Task Planner.

**Overall Framework (Figure 2):**
P3 accepts passive tasks from user instructions, actively perceives tasks from the environment via its Perception Module, and manages scheduled tasks stored in memory. The Perception Module feeds into the Task Planner, which then dispatches commands to various Tool Agents. Crucially, the Perception Module also observes the impact of Tool Agents on the environment, creating a closed loop without requiring direct feedback *from* the tools.

#### 3.1. Perception Module

The Perception Module is the cornerstone of P3's active environmental awareness. It operates as a centralized, always-on service, decoupled from specific tool agents, to continuously observe and interpret the environment.
- **Input**: A continuous video stream from the agent's sensors.
- **Processing Pipeline (Figure 3)**:
 1. **Scene Graph Generation**: For each new frame, a scene graph generator updates a "Scene Graph" stored in "Spatial Memory," capturing spatial relationships between objects.
 2. **Visual Memory and Context Engineering**: Frames are sent to "Visual Memory," which uses a gradual frame-level down-sampling strategy to manage storage and processing overhead (short-term for high detail, long-term for compressed historical context). A Vision-Language Model (VLM) is employed, alongside carefully crafted system prompts (context engineering), to parse the visual input.
 3. **Key Functions**:
 - **Scene Captioning**: For frames exceeding their time limit in visual memory, VLMs generate captions summarizing key events. These are recorded in "Event History" within the overall Memory, ensuring maximum informational retention.
 - **Task Proposal**: Based on continuous observations and current robot state, the VLM actively proposes "active tasks" (e.g., "pick up the yellow paper on the ground"). These are combined with situational context to localize the task and are then stored in the "pending" split of "Task Memory" for future scheduling.
- **Benefit**: This architecture allows the agent to proactively detect environmental changes (including those caused by its own actions, humans, or other agents), propose new tasks, and maintain a comprehensive understanding of its surroundings, enhancing responsiveness and enabling interaction with feedback-lacking tools.

#### 3.2. Tool Plug-ins

P3's "Plug-any-tool" capability stems from its ability to integrate diverse tools without requiring conventional bidirectional feedback.
- **Essential Agents**: Navigation agents (NaviAgent, leveraging VLN models) and manipulation agents (ManiAgent, leveraging VLA models like the Moka pipeline for grasping) are treated as foundational tools for direct robot control.
- **Open-ended Integration**: The framework extends to a wide variety of real-world tools, including:
 - **IoT Devices (IoTAgent)**: Examples include turning off lights or adjusting air conditioning (e.g., Xiaomi Smart Home devices).
 - **Web Agents (WebAgent)**: Used for information retrieval (e.g., weather forecasts) to inform decision-making.
 - **Custom Tools**: Users can register any controllable device by specifying its function and call interface.
- **Feedback Decoupling**: The key innovation is that these tools do not need to provide feedback directly to the agent. Their impact on the environment is instead observed by the Perception Module, which then updates the agent's understanding.
- **Efficiency and Safety**: Context engineering (dynamic scene summarization, relevance filtering) maintains concise, high-value memory for LLMs. Command validation is applied to manipulation commands for safety, and hierarchical memory buffers with on-the-fly summarization manage context window limitations for the LLM-based planner, preserving low latency.

#### 3.3. Task Planner

The Task Planner is an LLM-based module responsible for dynamic, intelligent scheduling of all types of tasks.
- **Input Tasks**:
 - **Passive Tasks**: Triggered by user instructions or queries (e.g., "open the window").
 - **Active Tasks**: Proposed by the Perception Module and stored in Task Memory (e.g., "tidy up the room").
 - **Scheduled Tasks**: Registered by the user and stored in Task Memory, triggered by a timer (e.g., "turn on the air-conditioner at 5 p.m.").
- **Planning Process**:
 1. When a task is triggered, all instantly executable tasks (not pending a timer) are sent to an LLM.
 2. The LLM evaluates tasks for **priority** and **dependencies**. It retrieves relevant "Event History" from memory using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to make informed judgments.
 3. Tasks with higher priority and immediate availability are then dispatched to the appropriate Tool Agents for execution.
- **Adaptability**: This module allows for adaptive execution, handling interruptions (e.g., a higher-priority task arises) and resumptions (returning to a suspended task), thereby overcoming the limitations of fixed FIFO scheduling.

By combining these modules, P3 establishes a robust, versatile architecture where a robot can continuously sense its surroundings, integrate a wide array of tools without complex feedback loops, and dynamically manage a diverse set of tasks with human-like prioritization and adaptability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper evaluates the P3 framework through two main experimental settings: an Active Perception Capability Evaluation using a custom benchmark and extensive Real-world Deployment Evaluations.

#### 4.1. Active Perception Capability Evaluation

This evaluation assesses the Perception Module's ability to observe and propose tasks autonomously.
- **Benchmark**: The "Active Task Perception Benchmark" was created, comprising 272 first-person video snapshots (from public datasets and original videos). It focuses on three common household task categories: debris cleaning, item organization, and safety checks, along with "negative samples" (tidy rooms where no task should be proposed) to prevent arbitrary task guessing.
- **Methodology**: Several off-the-shelf VLMs (e.g., QwenVL-max, GPT-o4-mini, Cosmos-Reason1-7B, RoboBrain) were evaluated in a zero-shot setting. Performance was measured by category accuracy (using ChatGPT-3.5-Turbo as an LLM judge) and the average number of proposals on negative samples.
- **Key Finding**:
 - **QwenVL-max** achieved the best balance, with an overall accuracy of 77.09%. It demonstrated high accuracy in identifying positive tasks while minimizing unnecessary proposals in tidy scenes. This indicates its suitability for P3's Perception Module.
 - Other models showed trade-offs: Cosmos-Reason1-7B and RoboBrain performed strongly in proposing positive samples but tended to generate excessive proposals for negative (tidy) scenes. Conversely, Qwen2.5VL-7B and MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B produced fewer proposals overall, potentially leading to missed active actions.
- **Implication**: The results confirm that current VLMs, particularly QwenVL-max, are capable of meeting the requirements for active task perception within the P3 framework, with flexibility for users to tune proposal preferences via different VLMs or prompting strategies.

#### 4.2. Real-world Deployment Evaluation

This evaluation validates the integrated P3 framework on physical robots in realistic environments.
- **Experimental Setup**: The framework was deployed on humanoid dual-arm robots (Realman and AgiBot). It integrated a high-precision grasping solution (Moka pipeline) for manipulation, a SLAM-based chassis (Woosh) for navigation, and Xiaomi Smart Home devices for IoT interaction. Experiments were conducted in a research laboratory and a typical office.
- **Task Categories**: 11 real-world tasks were designed, categorized into:
 - **Independent Tasks (6 tasks)**: Focused on assessing tool integration (VLN, VLA, IoT, Web) and could be executed without external interference.
 - **Competing Tasks (5 tasks)**: Involved executing multiple tasks with limited resources, requiring complex task planning, prioritization, and dependency handling.

- **Findings for Independent Tasks (Table 2, first 6 rows)**:
 - P3 demonstrated **robust performance and versatility** in integrating diverse tools.
 - High success rates were observed for IoT-related tasks (e.g., Task 2, turning off a humidifier), showing proficiency in smart device interaction.
 - Active perception was successful, with Task 1 (picking up scrap paper) being autonomously executed.
 - Complex multi-step tasks combining navigation, manipulation, and web ordering (e.g., Task 6, "Move a cardboard box and place an order for delivery") were carried out effectively.
 - The framework's ability to flexibly adjust to complex environments (e.g., minor adjustments during navigation in Task 1, Figure 8) was evident.

- **Findings for Competing Tasks (Table 2, last 5 rows)**:
 - P3 exhibited **strong competing-task management**, mirroring human-like reasoning in task prioritization and adaptation.
 - Tasks 7 and 8 showed high success rates, benefiting from simpler environments.
 - Tasks 9, 10, and 11, which involved active tasks introduced mid-execution, interruptions, and real-time plan adjustments (e.g., picking up a knocked-over cup, Task 11), presented greater challenges and resulted in slight performance drops (e.g., Task 11 with 35.71% and 42.86% success for different LLM planners) but generally demonstrated the dynamic scheduler's effectiveness.
 - Figure 4 and Figure 8 (Task 2 & 3) visually illustrate P3's ability to handle task interruptions and resumptions (e.g., suspending a low-priority task to address an urgent one, then returning to the original task) and robustness to external disturbances (e.g., re-attempting a grab after human interference).

- **Acknowledged Challenges**: The authors candidly point out that imperfect performance of LLMs in the perception and planning modules, as well as unstable robot hardware (e.g., network issues, overheating), contributed to some failures. These indicate avenues for future improvement, suggesting that enhanced underlying AI models and more robust hardware could further boost P3's capabilities.

Overall, the experimental results strongly support P3's core contributions: enabling omni-task perception, seamless tool integration, and efficient multi-task planning in complex, dynamic, and unpredictable real-world scenarios, thereby bridging the gap between theoretical benchmarks and practical deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The P3 framework represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI, offering solutions to long-standing challenges that have hindered the widespread deployment of intelligent agents in real-world settings. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized through its core innovations:

1. **Bridging the Reality Gap**: Perhaps the most profound impact of P3 is its explicit focus on bridging the gap between controlled research benchmarks and the complexities of practical deployment. By addressing issues like dynamic environmental changes, diverse tool compatibility, and complex multi-task planning, P3 moves embodied agents closer to being truly useful in unstructured, unpredictable environments. This shift is crucial for realizing the full potential of robotics and AI in daily life.

2. **Enhanced Autonomy and Adaptability**: The unified Perception Module fundamentally enhances agent autonomy. By actively detecting environmental changes and autonomously proposing tasks, P3 allows agents to be proactive rather than solely reactive. This means robots can identify needs (e.g., a messy room, a knocked-over object, a safety hazard) and act upon them without explicit human instruction, mimicking a higher level of intelligence and self-sufficiency. This active perception, combined with dynamic scheduling, makes agents significantly more adaptable to unforeseen circumstances and interruptions.

3. **Ubiquitous Tool Integration**: The "plug-any-tool" capability is a game-changer for practical robotics. By eliminating the requirement for bidirectional feedback, P3 vastly expands the range of devices an embodied agent can interact with. This includes not only sophisticated robotic manipulators and navigation systems but also simpler IoT devices, web services, and custom-built tools that might lack complex communication protocols. This flexibility democratizes the development and deployment of embodied agents, making them compatible with existing infrastructure and reducing the need for specialized, costly hardware.

4. **Sophisticated Multi-task Management**: Real-world environments are inherently multi-tasking. P3's dynamic scheduler, which considers task priorities, dependencies, and supports interruptions and resumptions, moves beyond simplistic planning methods. This capability is essential for agents operating in shared spaces with humans, where urgent events must supersede routine tasks, and plans must constantly evolve. This aspect enhances the agent's efficiency, reliability, and human-robot collaboration potential.

5. **Foundation for Scalable Data Collection**: The paper briefly mentions that P3 "provides a foundation for scalable embodied data collection." This is a critical understated impact. Active perception and robust tool integration mean that agents can operate for extended periods in diverse environments, autonomously gathering a rich variety of interaction data. This data can then be used to further train and refine underlying AI models (LLMs, VLMs, policies), creating a virtuous cycle of improvement for embodied AI research.

6. **Potential Applications**: The advancements offered by P3 open doors to numerous applications:
 - **Smart Homes**: More intelligent personal assistants that can proactively manage household tasks, respond to emergencies, and interact with a wider range of smart devices.
 - **Industrial Automation**: Robots in factories or warehouses that can adapt to dynamic layouts, unexpected events, and coordinate multiple tasks more efficiently.
 - **Assisted Living**: Robotic aids for the elderly or disabled that can perceive needs, retrieve items, manage appliances, and respond to safety concerns without constant supervision.
 - **Emergency Response**: Agents that can monitor environments, detect hazards, prioritize actions, and use available tools (e.g., smart sprinklers, communication systems) to mitigate risks.

In conclusion, P3 pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by addressing fundamental limitations in perception, tool interaction, and task planning. Its focus on versatility, adaptability, and real-world deployment sets a new standard, paving the way for more capable, autonomous, and widely applicable intelligent agents in our physical world.
