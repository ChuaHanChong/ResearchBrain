# Masking Teacher and Reinforcing Student for Distilling Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new distillation framework called Masters facilitates the creation of compact Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by progressively masking a large teacher and employing offline reinforcement learning with dual rewards. This approach allows smaller student models (e.g., 2B-8B parameters) to achieve performance competitive with or exceeding significantly larger VLMs from various institutions across multiple multimodal benchmarks, enhancing their deployability on resource-constrained devices.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are too computationally intensive for deployment on mobile or edge devices due to their immense size.
- Traditional knowledge distillation methods struggle to effectively transfer knowledge from large teachers to much smaller student models due to a significant "size gap," leading to unstable learning.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) based distillation is computationally expensive, and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data can lack diversity, limiting efficient knowledge transfer.

## Method

- Employs magnitude-based ==teacher masking== to progressively simplify the teacher model's complexity, making it more digestible for the student.
- Implements a mask-progressive distillation strategy that gradually increases the teacher's capacity, offering a curriculum-like learning environment.
- Integrates an offline reinforcement learning framework with dual rewards: one for factual accuracy (LLM-as-a-Judge) and another for distillation quality (logit-level similarity).

## Results

- Compact Masters-applied VLMs (e.g., **2B**, **3B**, **4B**, **7B**, **8B** parameters) consistently outperform previous compact models and, in some cases, partially surpass or match larger open and closed-source VLMs across multiple benchmarks.
- The mask-progressive distillation and dual-reward reinforcement learning components cumulatively contribute to substantial performance gains, with InternVL3.5-**8B** improving from **75.4%** to **77.1%** average performance.
- Gradual teacher-size scaling during distillation, starting with an intermediate teacher, leads to smoother convergence and stronger generalization, achieving up to **80.0%** average performance for InternVL3.5-**8B**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Progressively adapting the teacher's complexity through masking provides a stable and effective curriculum for students to absorb knowledge despite large capacity differences.
> - Integrating offline reinforcement learning with dual rewards (accuracy and distillation) provides efficient and targeted feedback crucial for refining student model responses.
> - Gradually increasing the teacher model's size during the distillation process leads to smoother convergence and improved generalization in the student models.
