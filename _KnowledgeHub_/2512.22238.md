# Masking Teacher and Reinforcing Student for Distilling Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new distillation framework called Masters facilitates the creation of compact Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by progressively masking a large teacher and employing offline reinforcement learning with dual rewards. This approach allows smaller student models (e.g., 2B-8B parameters) to achieve performance competitive with or exceeding significantly larger VLMs from various institutions across multiple multimodal benchmarks, enhancing their deployability on resource-constrained devices.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are too computationally intensive for deployment on mobile or edge devices due to their immense size.
- Traditional knowledge distillation methods struggle to effectively transfer knowledge from large teachers to much smaller student models due to a significant "size gap," leading to unstable learning.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) based distillation is computationally expensive, and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data can lack diversity, limiting efficient knowledge transfer.

## Method

- Employs magnitude-based ==teacher masking== to progressively simplify the teacher model's complexity, making it more digestible for the student.
- Implements a mask-progressive distillation strategy that gradually increases the teacher's capacity, offering a curriculum-like learning environment.
- Integrates an offline reinforcement learning framework with dual rewards: one for factual accuracy (LLM-as-a-Judge) and another for distillation quality (logit-level similarity).

## Results

- Compact Masters-applied VLMs (e.g., **2B**, **3B**, **4B**, **7B**, **8B** parameters) consistently outperform previous compact models and, in some cases, partially surpass or match larger open and closed-source VLMs across multiple benchmarks.
- The mask-progressive distillation and dual-reward reinforcement learning components cumulatively contribute to substantial performance gains, with InternVL3.5-**8B** improving from **75.4%** to **77.1%** average performance.
- Gradual teacher-size scaling during distillation, starting with an intermediate teacher, leads to smoother convergence and stronger generalization, achieving up to **80.0%** average performance for InternVL3.5-**8B**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Progressively adapting the teacher's complexity through masking provides a stable and effective curriculum for students to absorb knowledge despite large capacity differences.
> - Integrating offline reinforcement learning with dual rewards (accuracy and distillation) provides efficient and targeted feedback crucial for refining student model responses.
> - Gradually increasing the teacher model's size during the distillation process leads to smoother convergence and improved generalization in the student models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed extraordinary progress, with large-scale models like GPT-4o, Gemini, and Claude demonstrating remarkable multimodal understanding and reasoning capabilities across a wide array of tasks, from visual captioning to complex question answering. However, these state-of-the-art VLMs come with a significant drawback: their enormous parameter counts (e.g., tens to hundreds of billions) and massive computational requirements make them impractical for deployment on resource-constrained platforms such as mobile phones or edge devices. This scalability challenge creates a critical gap between powerful research models and real-world deployable AI.

Knowledge distillation has emerged as a widely adopted technique to address this challenge. It involves transferring the "knowledge" from a large, high-performing "teacher" model to a smaller, more efficient "student" model. While effective, conventional distillation methods face significant hurdles, primarily due to the substantial "size gap" or "capacity gap" between the teacher and student. The student often struggles to reproduce the teacher’s complex, high-dimensional representations, leading to unstable learning and degraded performance.

Existing research in VLM distillation has explored various avenues, including modifying training objectives, dynamic intermediate-layer distillation, vision-attention distillation, multi-step distillation, and reinforcement learning (RL)-based approaches. However, many of these approaches indirectly tackle the size gap or introduce additional complexities. Masters distinguishes itself by directly confronting the fundamental issue of the large parameter gap. By progressively adjusting the teacher's complexity through masking and employing an efficient offline reinforcement learning framework with dual rewards, this work provides a novel and robust solution to stabilize and enhance knowledge transfer. It carves out a scalable pathway for developing efficient VLMs that retain high performance, thus bridging the gap between theoretical VLM advancements and practical, on-device deployment needs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient knowledge distillation framework that can effectively transfer knowledge from large, powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to smaller, more compact student models, making them suitable for deployment on mobile or edge devices.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing the "Size Gap" Challenge:** Large-scale VLMs, despite their remarkable performance, are computationally intensive and resource-hungry, rendering them impractical for widespread real-world deployment on constrained devices. The core challenge in distilling these models is the significant capacity mismatch between a massive teacher and a much smaller student, which often leads to unstable learning and performance degradation in the student.
- **Enhancing Stability and Smoothness of Knowledge Transfer:** Traditional distillation methods struggle with the abrupt complexity of a full-sized teacher. The authors aim to introduce a mechanism that allows the student to absorb knowledge in a more gradual and stable manner, preventing the "struggle" often observed in students trying to mimic a much more complex teacher.
- **Improving Knowledge Transfer Efficiency:** Current RL-based distillation paradigms, particularly those employing "think–answer" strategies, are computationally expensive and time-consuming, limiting the scale and diversity of training data. The researchers are motivated to develop a more efficient RL approach that leverages pre-generated responses to reduce computational overhead without sacrificing performance.
- **Overcoming SFT Data Limitations:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) datasets, often generated by large closed-source models or human-filtered, tend to have limited answer diversity and can be "over-rich" for small student models, leading to representational mismatch. The objective is to provide a richer yet capacity-aligned guidance signal to the student.
- **Achieving Competitive Performance with Compact Models:** Ultimately, the goal is to enable compact VLMs to achieve performance levels comparable to, or even surpassing, much larger and more complex models, thereby fulfilling the demand for intelligent yet deployable AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, "Masters" (Masking teacher and reinforcing student), integrates several innovative components into a unified distillation paradigm:

#### 3.1. Magnitude-based Teacher Masking

Masters first addresses the parameter gap by masking non-dominant weights of the large teacher model. This strategy is inspired by network pruning techniques where weights with smaller magnitudes are deemed less critical and are set to zero (Equation 1). The masking is applied layer-wise to prevent excessive pruning in specific layers, ensuring the masked teacher remains functional and representative (Equation 2). This initial masking step simplifies the teacher's representational space, making it more manageable for the smaller student to learn from.

#### 3.2. Mask-Progressive Distillation

Instead of using a fixed masked teacher, Masters employs a mask-progressive strategy. The teacher's full capacity is gradually restored throughout the training process by progressively decreasing the masking ratio (Equation 3). This creates a curriculum-like learning environment: the student first learns from a simplified teacher, capturing coarse-grained patterns, and then progressively refines its representations as the teacher's complexity increases. The distillation objective at each iteration uses Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) to align the student's logit-softmax output with the masked teacher's (Equation 4), promoting smooth and stable knowledge transfer.

#### 3.3. Multiple Responses for Enriched Learning

To overcome the limitations of conventional SFT datasets (e.g., single answers, over-rich labels from large models), Masters generates multiple diverse responses for each question. These responses come from both the masked teacher and the student itself. This dual-source approach ensures that the student receives adaptive guidance signals that evolve with its learning capacity, maintaining alignment between teacher guidance and the student's evolving representational capacity (Equation 5). This enriches the learning experience and prevents overfitting to narrow linguistic styles.

#### 3.4. Offline Reinforcement Learning with Dual Rewards

To further refine knowledge transfer and address undesirable responses (e.g., factual errors, overly complex language), Masters integrates an offline RL stage with two complementary rewards:

- **Offline RL Setup:** Unlike computationally expensive online RL approaches that require repeated "think–answer" generations, Masters pre-generates multiple responses from both the teacher and student for all training questions. This offline setup drastically reduces training time and computational costs, enabling large-scale, diverse training.
- **Accuracy Reward ($\mathcal{R}_{\text{acc}}$):** This reward measures the factual correctness and semantic fidelity of the generated responses. It employs an LLM-as-a-Judge (Equation 6), which provides robust and generalized judgments across diverse VQA scenarios, overcoming the limitations of domain-specific rule-based parsers. This ensures the student generates accurate outputs.
- **Distillation Reward ($\mathcal{R}_{\text{distill}}$):** This reward quantifies the ease of knowledge transfer by measuring the logit-level similarity (JSD) between the teacher and student for the generated responses. To enhance sensitivity and stabilize optimization, a reverse min-max normalization is applied to the divergence values (Equation 7), ensuring that responses with smaller divergence (better alignment) receive higher rewards. This guides the student to generate responses that are not only accurate but also effectively learnable.

#### 3.5. Unified Objective

The final objective combines the GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization) framework (Equations 9, 10, 11) with the distillation objective, leveraging the dual rewards to refine the student's policy. This unified approach allows the student to effectively absorb and refine knowledge from the large teacher in a stable and efficient manner (Equation 8).

In essence, Masters creates a progressive, curriculum-driven learning environment where the teacher's complexity is adaptively matched to the student's capacity, while offline RL provides targeted feedback on both correctness and transferability, leading to highly efficient and capable compact VLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted across diverse VLM benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of the Masters framework.

- **Superior Performance of Compact VLMs:** Masters consistently enables compact student VLMs (e.g., 2B, 3B, 4B, 7B, 8B parameters) to significantly outperform existing compact VLMs. As shown in Figure 1 and Tables 3 and 4, Masters-applied models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Masters, Qwen3-VL-8B-Masters, InternVL3-8B-Masters, and InternVL3.5-8B-Masters achieve average performance percentages that are not only substantially higher than their original compact counterparts but also partially surpass or match many larger open-source and even closed-source VLMs (e.g., some Claude, Gemini, GPT models) across a wide range of benchmarks like AI2D, ChartQA, MathVista, MMMU, MM-Vet, and RealWorldQA.

- **Effectiveness of Mask-Progressive Distillation:** Table 1 clearly shows a step-wise improvement. Starting from a naive distillation baseline with a large teacher, applying mask-progressive distillation ("Mask-Progressive") yields a noticeable performance boost. This confirms that adaptively matching the teacher's capacity to the student's learning stage stabilizes optimization and leads to richer representations.

- **Impact of Reinforcement Learning with Dual Rewards:** Further integrating RL feedback with accuracy and distillation rewards ("Reward Feedback") leads to another significant cumulative performance gain (Table 1). This highlights the synergistic contribution of each component, demonstrating that evaluating correctness and transferability is crucial for refining student responses. For example, InternVL3.5-8B's average performance increases from 75.4% (original) to 75.8% (+Large Teacher), then to 76.3% (+Mask-Progressive), and finally to 77.1% (+Reward Feedback).

- **Advantage of Gradual Teacher-Size Scaling:** Table 2 reveals that gradually increasing the teacher sizes during distillation (e.g., from an intermediate-sized "Mid Teacher" to a "Large Teacher") yields smoother convergence and stronger generalization. For instance, distilling InternVL3.5-8B with a +Mid Teacher approach first, then applying mask-progressive distillation and reward feedback, results in an 80.0% average performance, which is higher than the 77.1% achieved by direct distillation from a large teacher alone. This finding suggests a scalable and effective pathway for model compression.

- **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Contributions:**
 - **Number of Responses:** Figure 5 shows that performance steadily improves with more pre-generated responses, converging around eight responses, which was chosen for training.
 - **Optimal Masking Ratio:** Table 5(a) indicates that an optimal maximum masking ratio (r_max) exists for different teacher models (e.g., 0.2 for most, 0.4 for Qwen3-VL-32B and InternVL3.5-8B/14B), suggesting that the degree of initial teacher simplification is important.
 - **Teacher Composition:** Table 5(b) confirms that a progressive teacher scaling (e.g., 14B + 38B + 78B) leads to the best performance for InternVL3 series, reinforcing the findings in Table 2.
 - **Source of Training Samples:** Table 5(c) demonstrates the importance of balancing teacher-generated and student-generated responses, with a 1:1 ratio proving most effective for adaptability and semantic richness.
 - **Decomposing Components:** Table 5(d) explicitly shows that each element (Mid Teacher, Multi-Response, Reward Feedback, Mask-Progressive) contributes synergistically to the overall performance, and removing any component leads to degradation.
 - **Reward Design:** Table 5(e) highlights the critical role of both accuracy and distillation rewards. Removing the accuracy reward or the normalization for the distillation reward significantly reduces performance, underscoring their necessity for identifying high-quality responses.
 - **Application to Other Frameworks:** Table 5(f) demonstrates Masters' versatility by showing that applying it to other recent distillation frameworks (DistiLLM, LLaVA-KD, VLsI, RIL) consistently mitigates performance degradation caused by large parameter gaps, simplifying multi-step pipelines into a single, more efficient training process.

- **Inference Efficiency:** The discussion highlights that Masters achieves its strong performance without sacrificing inference speed, differentiating it from "think–answer" paradigms that incur high latency (Figure 6).

In summary, Masters not only achieves state-of-the-art performance for compact VLMs but also provides valuable insights into the mechanics of effective knowledge distillation, particularly regarding adaptive teacher complexity and efficient reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Masters framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of deploying powerful AI on resource-constrained devices. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling On-Device AI Intelligence:** The core contribution of Masters is its ability to produce compact VLMs that are not only efficient but also highly capable, often matching or surpassing the performance of much larger models. This directly facilitates the deployment of advanced multimodal AI on edge devices and mobile platforms, unlocking new possibilities for applications in areas like real-time augmented reality, smart assistants, mobile diagnostics, and embedded systems where low latency and limited computational resources are paramount.
- **A Scalable Pathway for Model Compression:** The finding that gradually increasing teacher sizes during distillation leads to smoother convergence and stronger generalization offers a scalable and robust methodology for compressing large models. This "mask-progressive" approach provides a more stable learning trajectory for student models, reducing the instability commonly encountered when distilling from a massive teacher to a tiny student. This paradigm shift can influence future research in efficient AI, suggesting a curriculum-based approach to knowledge transfer.
- **Efficient and Effective Knowledge Transfer:** By pioneering an offline reinforcement learning approach with dual rewards (accuracy and distillation), Masters addresses the computational inefficiency of traditional online RL methods. This makes large-scale RL-driven distillation practical, allowing for richer training signals and more diverse responses without incurring prohibitive computational costs. This methodology can inspire similar efficiency-focused innovations in other knowledge distillation tasks.
- **Bridging the Gap Between Research and Application:** Masters helps to bridge the gap between the theoretical capabilities of large, frontier-class VLMs and their practical applicability in real-world scenarios. It offers a clear, unified, and scalable framework that simplifies complex multi-step distillation pipelines into a single, effective training process, making the development of deployable VLMs more accessible and efficient.
- **Insights into Distillation Dynamics:** The detailed ablation studies provide valuable insights into the individual contributions of different components—teacher masking, progressive scheduling, multiple response generation, and dual rewards. This empirical validation helps the research community understand "what matters" in multimodal large language model distillation, guiding future research toward more optimized and effective strategies.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions:**

While highly impactful, the paper acknowledges certain limitations:
- **Offline Training:** Masters' reliance on offline training, while efficient, inherently limits its adaptability to real-time feedback and continuous online data sampling. The authors note that fully online training remains computationally infeasible with current technology.
- **Computational Infrastructure:** Despite its efficiency gains, the training of Masters still requires significant computational resources (e.g., NVIDIA A100 GPUs) for pre-generating responses and the RL stage. Advances in efficient multi-node training and inference libraries (like vLLM) are crucial for future online distillation.

Future work suggested by the authors includes exploring data-driven masking techniques to adaptively determine which teacher weights to mask, and incorporating RL-based replay buffers to better handle hard examples, which could further enhance the model's robustness and learning efficiency.

In conclusion, Masters represents a significant step towards creating intelligent, efficient, and deployable VLMs, addressing a critical bottleneck in the widespread adoption of advanced multimodal AI. Its methodological innovations and strong empirical results are poised to inspire further research and development in the pursuit of truly ubiquitous AI.
