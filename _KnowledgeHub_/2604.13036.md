# Lyra 2.0: Explorable Generative 3D Worlds

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed Lyra 2.0, an interactive system that generates persistent, explorable 3D worlds from a single input image using an autoregressive loop with anti-forgetting and anti-drifting mechanisms. The system produces long, 3D-consistent videos converted into high-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splatting and mesh representations, demonstrating superior performance in video and 3D scene quality compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Scaling generative video models to expansive, complex 3D environments while maintaining global 3D consistency over long camera trajectories.
- Combating "spatial forgetting" (inconsistent structures upon revisiting regions) and "temporal drifting" (accumulation of synthesis errors) in autoregressive generation.
- Ensuring robust 3D reconstruction from generatively produced videos, which often contain subtle multi-view inconsistencies problematic for traditional methods.

## Method

- **Lyra 2.0** introduces an ==autoregressive retrieve–generate–update loop== that uses an ==incremental 3D cache== and ==geometry-aware retrieval== to inject spatial memory via ==canonical coordinate maps==.
- A ==self-augmentation training scheme== to mitigate the training-inference discrepancy, preventing temporal drift by teaching robustness to imperfect historical contexts.
- A tailored ==3D reconstruction pipeline== that fine-tunes a feed-forward 3D foundation model (**DAv3**) on generated videos to handle subtle multi-view inconsistencies.

## Results

- Outperformed baselines across video generation metrics (**SSIM**, **LPIPS**, **FID**, subjective quality, style consistency) and 3D scene generation metrics (**LPIPS-G**, **FID**, subjective quality) on multiple datasets.
- The fine-tuned DAv3 reconstruction model consistently yielded superior 3D results, confirming the benefit of adapting the reconstruction pipeline to generative artifacts.
- A distilled model achieved comparable visual quality with a **13x speedup** in generation time (**4** vs. **35** denoising steps), significantly enhancing interactive exploration capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Storing per-frame geometry independently in a 3D cache and using learned MLP aggregation for dense 3D correspondences from canonical coordinate maps prevents error accumulation and handles noisy depth estimates.
> - A self-augmentation training strategy teaches the model to recover high-quality outputs from imperfect historical contexts, directly counteracting autoregressive error accumulation and temporal drift.
> - Fine-tuning the 3D reconstruction model on generatively produced data significantly improves its robustness to minor inconsistencies, yielding clean and coherent explicit 3D assets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene creation has seen advancements with the emergence of generative models, particularly video diffusion models. These models, trained on extensive internet data, are capable of synthesizing visually coherent videos with local 3D consistency. A paradigm termed "generative reconstruction" has emerged, where camera-controlled videos are generated from a single input image and subsequently converted into explicit 3D representations using feed-forward reconstruction techniques. This approach aims to circumvent the labor-intensive processes associated with real-world 3D data acquisition, offering a scalable method for creating diverse and potentially imaginary 3D environments.

However, a significant challenge in this landscape is scaling generative reconstruction to expansive and complex environments, such as navigating through multiple rooms or along extensive city streets. Existing autoregressive video generation models exhibit limitations in maintaining 3D consistency over long camera trajectories that involve substantial viewpoint changes or revisits to previously observed areas. These limitations primarily manifest as two forms of degradation: "spatial forgetting" and "temporal drifting." Spatial forgetting occurs when previously generated regions fall outside the model's finite temporal context, leading to inconsistent or hallucinated structures upon revisiting these areas. Temporal drifting, conversely, results from the accumulation of small synthesis errors across autoregressive steps, progressively distorting scene appearance and geometry. This is exacerbated when camera exploration continuously introduces new regions, reducing visual overlap with earlier frames and thus diminishing geometric and textural constraints.

Prior efforts to address spatial forgetting have explored incorporating historical memory into the generation process. Some methods employ cumulative 3D representations, conditioning future frames on rendered views of reconstructed geometry. However, this tightly coupled design can amplify errors, as generative artifacts degrade the 3D geometry, which then provides flawed conditioning for subsequent frames. Other approaches integrate history frames directly into the context window via camera pose embeddings, yet these methods often struggle with inferring long-range geometric correspondences under significant viewpoint variations. To mitigate temporal drifting, strategies typically involve extending the temporal context length to anchor on past frames. However, in scenarios of scene exploration, early frames often move out of the field of view, rendering long-context anchoring less effective for suppressing drift in newly observed areas.

Lyra 2.0 positions itself within this context by proposing a framework that specifically addresses both spatial forgetting and temporal drifting. It aims to decouple geometric tracking from pixel synthesis, using an explicit 3D proxy solely for information routing rather than for appearance generation, and introduces a self-augmentation training scheme to mitigate the underlying training-inference discrepancy that contributes to temporal drift. Furthermore, it incorporates mechanisms to facilitate robust 3D reconstruction from the generated video sequences, which often contain minor multi-view inconsistencies problematic for traditional reconstruction methods. This work seeks to advance the capabilities of generative reconstruction towards creating persistent, explorable 3D worlds at a larger scale.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of generating persistent and explorable 3D worlds at scale from a single input image. This involves producing long, camera-controlled videos that maintain global 3D consistency throughout the sequence, which can then be reliably converted into high-quality, explicit 3D representations.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current generative reconstruction approaches:

1. **Scaling to Large, Complex Environments:** Existing video diffusion models, while demonstrating high visual fidelity and local 3D consistency, encounter significant degradation when tasked with generating content for large-scale environments. The goal is to enable scene exploration across extensive areas, such as multiple rooms or long street segments.
2. **Addressing Spatial Forgetting:** In autoregressive generation for scene exploration, previously observed regions inevitably move beyond the model's active temporal context window. When these regions are revisited, current models tend to hallucinate structures, resulting in a loss of global layout consistency and geometric integrity. A key objective is to enable the model to recall and appropriately utilize geometrically relevant historical observations, regardless of their temporal distance, to ensure consistent synthesis upon revisits.
3. **Mitigating Temporal Drifting:** Autoregressive generation is susceptible to error accumulation, where minor synthesis artifacts (e.g., color shifts, blurring, structural distortions) compound over successive frames. This degradation is amplified in exploration scenarios due to continuous introduction of novel content and reduced visual overlap with early history frames. The motivation is to develop mechanisms that prevent or correct these accumulating errors, thereby maintaining visual and geometric quality over extended sequences.
4. **Enabling Robust 3D Reconstruction:** Videos produced by diffusion models, even with improved consistency, often contain subtle multi-view inconsistencies. These inconsistencies can pose challenges for traditional 3D reconstruction models, leading to artifacts such as "floaters" or noise in the reconstructed 3D assets. A specific objective is to develop or adapt a 3D reconstruction pipeline that is robust to these generative artifacts, ensuring reliable recovery of clean and coherent 3D scenes (e.g., 3D Gaussians and surface meshes).
5. **Facilitating Interactive Exploration and Downstream Applications:** The ultimate motivation is to provide users with an interactive system that allows for arbitrary long-horizon camera trajectory definition and progressive environment reconstruction. The generated 3D assets should be immediately deployable in various applications, including embodied AI simulation, virtual reality, and other interactive viewers, without extensive post-processing.

By addressing these challenges, Lyra 2.0 aims to significantly advance the practicality and scope of generative 3D world creation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Lyra 2.0 operates as an interactive system for large-scale 3D scene exploration, built upon an autoregressive retrieve–generate–update loop. This loop involves three primary stages at each iteration: retrieving relevant history frames, generating new video segments, and updating the memory. The core of the methodology lies in its "anti-forgetting" and "anti-drifting" mechanisms, complemented by a tailored 3D reconstruction pipeline.

#### 3.1. Generative Backbone

The system is built upon DiT-based latent video diffusion models, specifically the Wan 2.1 VAE, which performs an 8x spatial and 4x temporal downsampling. Generation occurs in this latent space using flow matching. Camera control is integrated through two strategies: depth-based warping, which warps the most recent frame to the target viewpoint using estimated depth, and Plücker ray injection, which adds 6D ray coordinates per pixel as an additional conditioning signal to the DiT’s hidden dimensions. Context compression is managed by FramePack, which uses variable patchification kernels to maintain a long temporal horizon within a fixed token budget, including an initial anchor frame for reference.

#### 3.2. Anti-Forgetting for 3D-Persistent Video Generation

This mechanism addresses the problem of recalling geometrically relevant historical observations for long-range spatial consistency.
- **3D Cache Construction:** A 3D cache incrementally stores per-frame geometry information. For each generated frame, its full-resolution depth map, camera parameters, and a downsampled point cloud (obtained by unprojecting the depth map into world coordinates with a subsampling factor of 𝑑=8) are stored. A critical aspect is that the geometry of each frame is maintained independently, avoiding the accumulation of errors that would occur if fused into a single global point cloud from potentially imperfect depth estimations.
- **Geometry-Aware Retrieval:** To select the most informative history frames (𝑁𝑠=5) for a given target viewpoint, a visibility score 𝜑 is computed. This involves projecting each downsampled point cloud onto the target image plane and counting visible points, considering occlusion by minimum projected depth. During inference, a greedy approach selects frames that maximize coverage of not-yet-covered target pixels. This allows recalling relevant observations even if they are hundreds of frames apart.
- **Injecting Spatial Memory:** The retrieved history frames are injected into the video model as spatial slots, encoded independently by the VAE. These spatial slots, along with temporal FramePack slots and generation tokens, are processed by the DiT self-attention.
 - **Dense 3D Correspondence:** To provide precise geometric grounding, canonical coordinate maps (𝑢, 𝑣, normalized frame index) for each retrieved frame are forward-warped to the target viewpoint using full-resolution depth. These 4-channel maps (including warped depth) are encoded via positional encoding and aggregated through an MLP, with the resulting embeddings added to the tokens at the self-attention layer of every transformer block (specifically to queries and keys). This process provides geometric correspondence without introducing appearance artifacts that might arise from warping RGB images.

#### 3.3. Anti-Drifting for Long-Horizon Video Generation

This mechanism aims to counteract the accumulation of errors over long autoregressive sequences by mitigating the observation bias between training and inference.
- **Self-Augmentation Training:** During training, with a probability 𝑝_aug_ (set to 0.7), the history latent (z_hist_0) is corrupted by adding noise (z_hist_t). The video model then performs a one-step denoising to produce an approximate reconstruction (˜z_hist_0). This ˜z_hist_0 is used as the conditioning context for the current chunk. The model's loss, however, still supervises denoising towards the clean ground-truth current chunk latent (z_cur_0). This teaches the model to recover high-quality outputs even when conditioned on imperfect historical contexts, directly counteracting the effects of drift encountered during autoregressive inference. This strategy requires minimal overhead, involving only one additional DiT forward pass.

#### 3.4. 3D Reconstruction

The generated videos are converted into explicit 3D representations.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** Depth Anything v3 (DAv3), a feed-forward 3D foundation model, is used to predict per-pixel 3DGS attributes. The authors modify the Gaussian DPT head in DAv3 to downsample the feature map by a factor of 𝑘×𝑘 (𝑘=2), reducing the number of Gaussians and yielding a more compact representation suitable for real-time rendering.
- **Fine-tuning DAv3:** The DAv3 model is fine-tuned on sequences generated by Lyra 2.0. This process improves its robustness to the minor geometric inconsistencies and artifacts commonly present in generatively produced data, bridging the domain gap and enabling the recovery of clean, coherent 3D structures.
- **Surface Mesh Extraction:** A hierarchical sparse grid approach based on OpenVDB is used to extract surface meshes from the 3DGS. Median depth from the Gaussian reconstruction is rasterized, and oriented point clouds are used to construct a signed distance function on the sparse grid. Surfaces are extracted via marching cubes, stitched across hierarchy levels, and decimated for efficiency.

#### 3.5. Distilled Model for Accelerated Inference

A distilled version of the model is trained using Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD). This student model generates videos in 4 denoising steps, significantly faster than the full model's 35 steps, and eliminates the need for separate classifier-free guidance passes. The self-augmentation strategy is retained during distillation to maintain robustness against autoregressive error accumulation. This distillation reduces per-step generation time by approximately 13x.

#### 3.6. Training Details

The model is trained on DL3DV, a dataset of 10K long video clips, with camera poses estimated by ViPE and per-frame depth by DAv3. Video captions are generated using Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. Training involves both image-to-video mode (30% probability, generating 𝐿=80 frames from an initial frame) and autoregressive chunk-based training (70% probability, generating 𝐿 frames conditioned on a history window).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Lyra 2.0 encompasses both long video generation and subsequent 3D scene reconstruction, demonstrating its efficacy across various quantitative metrics and qualitative comparisons.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Long Video Generation

- **Quantitative Performance:** Lyra 2.0 achieved superior performance across most metrics on both the in-domain DL3DV-Evaluation dataset and the out-of-domain Tanks and Temples dataset (Table 1). It recorded the best SSIM, LPIPS, FID, Subjective Quality Score, and Style Consistency Score. While its Camera Controllability and Reprojection Error were competitive, they were slightly lower than GEN3C, which prioritizes rigid geometric constraints at the expense of generation quality. The results validate that Lyra 2.0's anti-forgetting and anti-drifting mechanisms enable high visual fidelity and accurate camera control simultaneously, preventing quality degradation and ensuring long-range consistency.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - GEN3C achieved strong camera control and low reprojection error but exhibited lower subjective quality and SSIM due to rigid geometric conditioning.
 - CaM and SPMem, while strong in quality metrics due to multi-view history memory, showed substantially lower Camera Controllability, indicating implicit camera conditioning limitations. SPMem's global point cloud conditioning also led to more pronounced drifting.
 - VMem exhibited the weakest scores across nearly all metrics, struggling to maintain coherence.
 - Yume-1.5 and HY-WorldPlay lacked explicit camera trajectory conditioning and suffered from severe temporal drifting.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visual comparisons (Figure 3) demonstrated Lyra 2.0's ability to maintain realistic structures and appearances at long horizons (e.g., frame 800+), in contrast to baselines which showed severe degradation, geometric distortions, or content drifting.
- **Distilled Model (Ours DMD):** The DMD-distilled model, operating in 4 denoising steps, achieved comparable or slightly better per-frame quality (LPIPS, FID) compared to the full model (35 steps), despite a moderate decrease in camera controllability. This indicates significant acceleration potential for interactive applications.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on 3D Scene Generation

- **Quantitative Performance:** Lyra 2.0 demonstrated superior results across all 3D scene generation metrics on both datasets (Table 2). Both "Ours + DAv3" (using pretrained DAv3) and "Ours Full" (fine-tuned DAv3) variants substantially outperformed all baselines in LPIPS-G, FID, and Subjective Quality.
 - The "Ours Full" model consistently outperformed "Ours + DAv3," confirming the benefit of fine-tuning the reconstruction model on generated scenes for improved robustness to generative artifacts.
 - Lyra 2.0 achieved substantially lower LPIPS-P, indicating that its video model produces inherently more 3D-consistent outputs, allowing for more faithful 3D reconstruction and re-rendering from novel viewpoints.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Renderings from 3DGS scenes (Figure 4) showed that Lyra 2.0 produces realistic 3D scenes with high fidelity, while baselines exhibited artifacts and floaters. Further comparisons (Figure 5) with Lyra and FantasyWorld highlighted Lyra 2.0's capability to generate scenes of significantly greater spatial extent and complexity, enabled by its interactive exploration framework.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

A comprehensive ablation study (Table 3, Figure 6) confirmed the contribution of each key component:
- **"w/ Global Point Cloud":** Replacing the per-frame 3D cache and correspondence-based conditioning with a fused global point cloud significantly degraded Camera Controllability and Style Consistency, supporting the approach of avoiding accumulated depth errors.
- **"w/ Explicit Corr. Fusion":** Using explicit depth-reasoning-based fusion for correspondences, instead of learned MLP aggregation, reduced Camera Controllability, suggesting that learned aggregation handles noisy depth estimates more effectively.
- **"w/o FramePack":** Removing FramePack temporal slots led to significant reductions in Style Consistency and increases in Reprojection Error, indicating proneness to drifting without temporal grounding.
- **"w/o Self-Augmentation":** Removing the self-augmentation training strategy resulted in substantial degradation of long-range consistency (Style Consistency and Camera Controllability), despite a slight improvement in per-frame subjective quality. This demonstrated the model's brittleness to imperfect conditioning during inference without this mechanism.

#### 4.4. Applications

Lyra 2.0's practical applicability was demonstrated through:
- **Interactive GUI:** An interface allows users to define camera trajectories within the 3D cache for real-time scene generation and exploration (Figure 7).
- **In-the-Wild Scene Generation:** The method was shown to generate large-scale explorable 3D scenes from diverse single input images, including indoor and outdoor environments, and support flexible camera trajectories (Figure 8).
- **Embodied AI Simulation:** Generated 3DGS representations and meshes were successfully exported to NVIDIA Isaac Sim, enabling physically grounded robot navigation and interaction within the generated environments (Figure 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Lyra 2.0 represents an advancement in the field of generative 3D content creation by offering a framework for scalable and persistent 3D world generation from a single image. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Scalable 3D Environment Creation:** The system enables the creation of large-scale, explorable 3D environments without the labor-intensive requirements of traditional real-world 3D data acquisition. This capability could democratize 3D content generation for various applications.
2. **Mitigation of Core Generative Limitations:** By effectively addressing "spatial forgetting" and "temporal drifting," Lyra 2.0 enhances the long-horizon 3D consistency of generated videos. The decoupling of geometric tracking from appearance synthesis and the self-augmentation training strategy are central to this improvement, leading to more robust and higher-quality generative outputs over extended trajectories and revisits.
3. **High-Fidelity and Consistent 3D Outputs:** The framework produces 3D-consistent videos that can be reliably lifted into high-quality 3D Gaussian Splatting representations and surface meshes. The fine-tuning of the DAv3 reconstruction model on generated data is crucial for robustness to inherent generative artifacts, ensuring usable 3D assets.
4. **Interactive Exploration:** The provision of an interactive GUI allows users to define arbitrary camera trajectories and progressively expand environments in real-time. This user-guided exploration capacity offers a more dynamic and creative approach to world building compared to static or predefined scene generation methods.
5. **Direct Utility for Downstream Applications:** The generated 3D assets are immediately exportable and deployable in physics engines and interactive viewers. Demonstrated integration with NVIDIA Isaac Sim for embodied AI simulation highlights its potential to provide diverse and scalable virtual training grounds for robots, reducing reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data acquisition or manual environment design. This could accelerate research and development in robotics and AI.
6. **Accelerated Inference for Practical Use:** The development of a distilled model significantly reduces inference time while maintaining comparable visual quality. This acceleration is critical for enabling interactive real-time exploration and iterative design workflows, making the system more practical for a wider range of users and applications.

Despite these advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the current focus on static environments and potential photometric inconsistencies inherited from training data that can lead to artifacts. Addressing dynamic scenes and improving photometric stability represent areas for future research.

In summary, Lyra 2.0 contributes to the advancement of generative AI by enabling the practical creation of large-scale, consistent, and explorable 3D worlds, with direct implications for sectors requiring virtual environments and sophisticated 3D content.
