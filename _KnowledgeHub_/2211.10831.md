# Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures Focus on Slow Features

> [!summary] Summary
> Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) demonstrate efficacy in learning world models by predicting latent representations of future states from image and action sequences. While proficient at ignoring dynamic visual noise, JEPA methods are shown to struggle with static background features, which they can trivially learn, consequently neglecting dynamic objects important for decision-making.

## Problem

- Traditional world models, trained with pixel-level reconstruction objectives, often suffer from 'object-vanishing,' failing to inherently prioritize important dynamic objects over static background elements.
- These models can inefficiently allocate learning capacity to irrelevant details, potentially hindering the learning of salient features crucial for decision-making in reinforcement learning tasks.
- There is a need to evaluate reconstruction-free world model learning approaches, like Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), in environments with various visual distractors to understand their strengths and limitations.

## Method

- Implement and test Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) using ==VICReg== and SimCLR objectives to learn world models from offline image and action sequences without pixel-level reconstruction.
- Compare JEPA-based methods against a reconstruction-based generative architecture and an inverse dynamics modeling approach in a simplified moving dot environment with varying types and temporal characteristics of visual noise.
- Evaluate the quality of learned representations by training linear probes to recover known state properties, and provide theoretical analysis for observed failure modes.

## Results

- JEPA methods achieved low RMSE for dot location prediction in clean environments and with changing uniform/structured noise, outperforming reconstruction when hyperparameter tuning was not consistently applied.
- With fixed uniform or structured noise, JEPA methods largely failed, showing high RMSE values close to the random baseline, empirically confirming their bias towards static features.
- Inverse Dynamics Modeling demonstrated robust performance across all noise settings (changing and fixed), consistently focusing on action-relevant dynamic features, while reconstruction maintained efficacy with fixed noise where JEPA failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - JEPA methods (VICReg, SimCLR) effectively disregard dynamic visual noise that changes at each timestep, performing comparably or better than reconstruction-based methods in such scenarios.
> - A critical limitation of current JEPA formulations is their tendency to trivially minimize loss functions by focusing solely on static or 'slow' features (unchanging background noise), thereby neglecting dynamic, salient objects.
> - Unlike JEPA, reconstruction-based models compel learning representations of all pixel information (both static and dynamic), while Inverse Dynamics Modeling inherently focuses on features relevant to agent actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly reinforcement learning and self-supervised learning, heavily relies on the development of effective "world models" for pixel-based environments. These models aim to learn latent representations of observations and predict future states or outcomes.

Traditionally, the most common approach to learning world models involves generative architectures that are trained using pixel-level reconstruction objectives. Methods such as PlaNet and Dreamer often fall into this category, focusing on accurately reconstructing the visual input. However, reconstruction objectives have been identified with a limitation known as "object-vanishing," where the model does not inherently distinguish between important dynamic objects and static background elements. Consequently, the model might invest capacity in reconstructing irrelevant details, potentially hindering the learning of salient features for decision-making.

In response to these limitations, a new paradigm called Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) has been proposed. JEPA offers a reconstruction-free alternative by focusing on learning representations where predictions of future states align with independently encoded future states, without requiring a pixel-level reconstruction of the input. This approach draws inspiration from the success of joint-embedding methods in self-supervised learning for static images, such as VICReg, SimCLR, MoCo, and BYOL, which learn robust representations by contrasting or aligning different views of the same data. The application of these principles to sequential data, particularly video and action sequences for world model learning, represents a significant area of current research.

This paper specifically contributes to this landscape by analyzing the performance characteristics of JEPA when applied to learning world models from offline image and action sequences. It critically examines how JEPA-based methods, utilizing VICReg and SimCLR objectives, behave in the presence of various types of visual distractors, particularly distinguishing between dynamic and static noise. This analysis helps to delineate the specific strengths and weaknesses of JEPA in a controlled environment, providing insights into its applicability and potential failure modes in more complex real-world scenarios. It also contrasts JEPA's behavior with that of traditional reconstruction-based models and inverse dynamics modeling, thereby positioning JEPA within the spectrum of world model learning techniques. The work extends existing discussions on the tendency of contrastive losses to focus on easy or "slow" features, applying this concept specifically to predictive learning in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to analyze the performance of Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) when trained with specific self-supervised learning objectives, namely VICReg and SimCLR, for learning a world model from offline state-action sequences. The analysis specifically focuses on pixel-based environments where rewards are not accessible during pre-training.

A significant motivation stems from the limitations of conventional world model learning approaches that rely on pixel-level reconstruction objectives. These generative models, while widely used, are observed to suffer from "object-vanishing." This phenomenon refers to the model's inherent difficulty in prioritizing important, dynamic objects within a scene over static background information when the learning signal is derived from reconstructing the entire image. This can lead to inefficient representation learning where critical information about agent-environment interaction might be overlooked.

JEPA, as a reconstruction-free alternative, is hypothesized to overcome some of these limitations by focusing directly on predictive consistency in a latent space rather than pixel-level fidelity. The authors aim to empirically evaluate this hypothesis in a controlled setting.

The paper outlines several specific objectives:
1. **Implementation and Testing of JEPA:** To implement JEPA using VICReg and SimCLR objectives for learning from image and action sequences.
2. **Comparative Analysis:** To compare the performance of JEPA-based methods against a reconstruction-based generative architecture and an inverse dynamics modeling approach. A supervised baseline and a random baseline are also included to establish performance bounds.
3. **Robustness to Distractors:** To investigate how these methods perform in the presence of different types of visual noise (distractors) in the environment. This includes varying the nature of the noise (uniform vs. structured) and, critically, its temporal characteristics (changing vs. fixed across timesteps).
4. **Probing Learned Representations:** To evaluate the quality of the learned representations by training a linear probe to recover a known property of the environment (the dot's location) from the latent states.
5. **Theoretical Explanation of Failure Modes:** To provide a theoretical explanation for potential limitations observed in JEPA-based methods, particularly concerning their behavior with static or "slow" features in the input. This involves demonstrating how the loss functions of VICReg and SimCLR can be trivially minimized by focusing on unchanging noise, thereby ignoring dynamic elements.

Ultimately, the motivation is to understand if and under what conditions JEPA offers a superior or complementary approach to existing methods for learning robust and relevant world models, especially for downstream tasks in reinforcement learning where identifying salient, dynamic features is crucial.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research focuses on learning an unsupervised world model from a fixed set of offline state-action sequences within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework. The goal is to learn an encoder ($g_\phi$) that maps observations (images $o_t$) to D-dimensional representations ($s_t$) and a forward model ($f_\theta$) that predicts the next representation ($\tilde{s}_{t+1}$) given the current representation and action ($s_t, a_t$).

**Training Protocol:**
- **Offline Setting:** All methods are trained on pre-recorded sequences of observations and actions. No online interaction or access to reward functions is allowed during pre-training.
- **Auto-regressive Prediction:** During both training and evaluation, the forward model is applied auto-regressively. An initial observation $o_1$ is encoded to $\tilde{s}_1 = g_\phi(o_1)$, and subsequent representations are predicted as $\tilde{s}_t = f_\theta(\tilde{s}_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$ for a sequence of length $T+1$.
- **Probing for Evaluation:** After pre-training, the encoder and predictor weights are frozen. A simple linear layer, referred to as a "prober" ($q(\tilde{s}_t)$), is trained to recover specific known properties of the states, such as the dot's location. The probing error (RMSE) is used as the primary evaluation metric.

**Compared Methods:**

1. **VICReg (JEPA-based):**
 - **Architecture:** Consists of an encoder and a predictor.
 - **Objective:** Adapts the VICReg loss for predictive learning. The total loss is a weighted sum of three components:
 - **Prediction Loss ($L_{prediction}$):** Measures the L2 distance between the forward model's output $\tilde{s}_t$ and the actual encoder's output for the next observation $s_t$.
 - **Variance Loss ($L_{variance}$):** Encourages the variance of each dimension in the representation batch to be above a threshold, preventing dimensional collapse.
 - **Covariance Loss ($L_{covariance}$):** Encourages the off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix of the representations to be close to zero, promoting decorrelation among dimensions.
 - Variance and covariance losses are applied to encoder outputs ($S_t$) at each time step.

2. **SimCLR (JEPA-based):**
 - **Architecture:** Similar to VICReg JEPA, with an encoder and a predictor.
 - **Objective:** Utilizes the InfoNCE loss, characteristic of SimCLR. The forward model output ($\tilde{s}_t$) and the encoder output ($S_t$) for the same time step are treated as positive pairs. Negative pairs are other samples within the batch. The InfoNCE loss aims to maximize agreement between positive pairs while minimizing agreement with negative pairs.

3. **Reconstruction:**
 - **Architecture:** Employs an encoder, a predictor, and a decoder ($d_\xi$).
 - **Objective:** The encoder and predictor are trained to enable the decoder to reconstruct the original observation. The loss is the L2 distance between the decoded representation ($\tilde{o}_t = d_\xi(\tilde{s}_t)$) and the actual observation ($o_t$).

4. **Inverse Dynamics Modeling (IDM):**
 - **Architecture:** Adds a linear layer (inverse dynamics model $h_\xi$) to predict the action $a_t$ given two consecutive encoder outputs ($s_t, s_{t+1}$). The forward model predicts the next encoder output.
 - **Objective:** Combines a prediction loss (L2 distance between predicted and actual next encoder output) with an inverse dynamics loss (L2 distance between predicted and actual action $a_t$).

5. **Supervised:**
 - **Objective:** All components (encoder, predictor, and prober) are trained end-to-end by propagating the error directly from the probing function (dot location prediction). This serves as a lower bound on the probing error.

6. **Random:**
 - **Objective:** The encoder and predictor weights are initialized randomly and fixed. The prober is then trained. This serves as an upper bound on the probing error.

**Dataset and Environment:**
- **Moving Dot Environment:** A simplified 2D environment featuring a single dot moving within a square. Actions specify delta coordinates.
- **Image Generation:** Dots are rendered and subjected to Gaussian blur. Image resolution is 28x28. Episode length is 17 timesteps.
- **Distractor Noise:** Introduced to the background to test robustness:
 - **Types:** Uniform noise (pixels sampled uniformly) and Structured noise (overlaid CIFAR-10 images).
 - **Temporal Settings:**
 - **Changing Noise:** Background noise is re-sampled at every timestep.
 - **Fixed Noise:** Background noise remains constant throughout a single video sequence but changes between different sequences.
 - **Noise Brightness Coefficient ($\alpha$):** Controls the intensity of the noise relative to the dot.
- **Dataset Sizes:** 1 million sequences for pre-training, 300,000 for prober training, and 10,000 for evaluation.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Hyperparameters for all methods were individually tuned for each noise level and type for the main results. An additional experiment showed performance without such re-tuning.

**Theoretical Analysis of Spurious Correlation:**
The paper provides a theoretical argument demonstrating how JEPA methods (VICReg and SimCLR) can trivially minimize their loss functions when presented with fixed background noise that constitutes the "slowest feature."
- For VICReg, if the encoder learns to output a static representation (e.g., normal noise) that reflects the fixed background, the prediction loss becomes zero (as $S_t = S_{t+1}$). Variance and covariance losses can also be satisfied with sufficiently large, decorrelated noise.
- For SimCLR, similar reasoning applies; if the static noise is consistently encoded, positive pairs are perfectly aligned, and the uniform distribution on the unit sphere can be achieved, minimizing InfoNCE. This leads to the model ignoring the dynamic, salient object (the moving dot).

**Additional Experiments:**
A "3-dots dataset" was used to further evaluate the feature learning capabilities. This dataset includes three types of dots on separate color channels: an action-controlled dot, an uncontrollably moving dot with unknown actions, and a stationary dot (fixed within an episode but changing between episodes). The goal was to see if models could learn to capture the controlled and stationary dots while ignoring the random one.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the moving dot dataset and the 3-dots dataset revealed distinct performance characteristics for the compared world model learning approaches.

**Performance on Moving Dot Dataset (Figure 3):**

1. **No Distractors (α=0):** All tested methods (VICReg, SimCLR, IDM, Reconstruction, Supervised) performed well, achieving low RMSE values in predicting the dot's location. This indicates their basic capability to learn salient features in clean environments. The 'Center' baseline (always predicting the center) and 'Random' baseline confirmed the effectiveness of the learning approaches.

2. **Changing Noise (Uniform and Structured):**
 - **JEPA Methods (VICReg and SimCLR):** These methods performed on par with or better than the reconstruction-based approach across various levels of changing uniform and structured noise. They demonstrated a capability to effectively ignore noise that varied at each timestep.
 - **Reconstruction:** Performed well for changing noise, similar to JEPA methods.
 - **Robustness to Tuning (Figure 5):** When hyperparameters were *not* re-tuned for different noise levels (fixed to optimal for no-noise setting), JEPA methods showed stronger robustness. Their performance remained relatively stable even with increased changing noise, whereas reconstruction-based methods experienced a more pronounced degradation.

3. **Fixed Noise (Uniform and Structured):**
 - **JEPA Methods (VICReg and SimCLR):** In stark contrast to their performance with changing noise, JEPA methods largely *failed* in the presence of fixed noise. Their RMSE values increased significantly, approaching or exceeding the 'Random' baseline, even with hyperparameter tuning. This empirical observation supports the theoretical explanation that JEPA objectives can be trivially minimized by focusing on static background noise (slow features) and consequently ignore the dynamic moving dot.
 - **Reconstruction:** This method performed effectively with fixed noise, particularly for noise levels up to α=1.5. This indicates that reconstruction objectives compel the model to learn representations that account for all pixel information, including static elements, preventing it from solely focusing on the unchanging background at the expense of dynamic objects.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Modeling (IDM):** IDM exhibited robust performance across all noise settings, including both changing and fixed noise. This suggests that the objective of predicting actions inherently forces the model to focus on features relevant to the agent's interaction and movement, which are typically dynamic.

**Performance on 3-Dots Dataset (Table 1 - Appendix A.6.2):**

This experiment aimed to distinguish how different methods handle multiple features with varying dynamics. The task was to predict the locations of an action-controlled dot, a randomly moving dot, and a stationary dot.

- **JEPA Methods (VICReg and SimCLR):** Consistent with the "slow features" hypothesis, these methods primarily focused on the stationary dot, achieving low RMSE for its location. However, they largely failed to capture the locations of the action-controlled and randomly moving dots, demonstrating a strong bias towards the slowest-changing feature.
- **Inverse Dynamics Modeling (IDM):** As expected, IDM excelled at capturing the action-controlled dot (RMSE 0.035), as its objective directly relates to predicting actions from state transitions. However, it largely ignored the stationary and randomly moving dots, as they are not directly relevant to action prediction.
- **Reconstruction:** This approach demonstrated a more balanced capability, successfully capturing both the action-controlled dot (RMSE 0.021) and the stationary dot (RMSE 0.026). It also provided a more reasonable estimate for the random dot compared to JEPA methods, likely due to its mandate to reconstruct all visual information.
- **Supervised Baseline:** Achieved the lowest errors across all dot types (e.g., 0.010 for action-controlled, 0.005 for stationary), confirming its role as a lower bound.

In summary, JEPA methods proved effective at disregarding changing, unpredictable noise but exhibited a critical vulnerability when confronted with static, persistent background features, leading them to ignore dynamic objects. Reconstruction, while potentially less focused, demonstrated greater robustness to static noise by learning representations that encompassed both static and dynamic elements. IDM focused precisely on features related to agent actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a detailed analytical and empirical investigation into the behavior of Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) within the context of learning world models from visual observations. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Validation of Reconstruction-Free World Models:** The paper contributes to the ongoing shift away from pixel-level reconstruction objectives in world model learning. It empirically demonstrates that JEPA-based methods (VICReg and SimCLR) can learn effective forward models capable of ignoring unpredictable, dynamic noise, sometimes outperforming reconstruction-based approaches in such scenarios. This suggests JEPA as a viable alternative for representation learning when pixel-perfect reconstruction is not the primary goal, aligning with hypotheses about its ability to focus on abstract, salient features.

2. **Identification of a Critical Limitation in JEPA:** The most significant finding is the identification and theoretical explanation of a specific failure mode for current JEPA formulations. The paper rigorously shows that when "slow features"—static or unchanging visual elements—are present in the environment, JEPA methods tend to converge to a trivial solution by focusing solely on these predictable, but often irrelevant, features. This leads to a neglect of dynamic and potentially crucial aspects of the environment (e.g., a moving object). This "spurious correlation" issue, while observed in other contrastive learning contexts, is critically highlighted here for predictive world models.

3. **Implications for Real-World Applications:** The identified limitation has direct implications for applying JEPA in complex, real-world environments. For instance, in domains like autonomous driving, a JEPA model might focus on static background elements (e.g., cloud patterns, buildings) that are easily predictable, rather than critical dynamic elements like other vehicles or pedestrians. This finding serves as a cautionary insight for researchers developing self-supervised world models for decision-making tasks.

4. **Guidance for Future Research and Model Development:**
 - **Input Modality:** The paper suggests that using alternative input modalities, such as image differences or optical flow, could mitigate the "slow features" problem by inherently filtering out static elements. However, it also acknowledges that these modalities might discard useful background information and may still contain fixed noise.
 - **Architectural Enhancements:** The authors propose that introducing hierarchical architectures (e.g., Hierarchical JEPA, as outlined in the original JEPA framework) could be a path toward learning both fast and slow features simultaneously.
 - **Objective Function Modifications:** The research calls for the development of new objective functions or additional constraints within JEPA that prevent representations from becoming constant over time in response to static features. Such modifications could encourage the model to capture a broader range of dynamics without being distracted by persistent background information.

5. **Comparative Understanding of World Model Approaches:** By comparing JEPA with reconstruction-based models and Inverse Dynamics Modeling (IDM), the study provides a more nuanced understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of different unsupervised world model learning paradigms. Reconstruction excels with fixed noise but can be less robust to changing noise without tuning; IDM learns highly agent-centric representations; and JEPA is effective at filtering changing noise but susceptible to static features. This comparative analysis helps researchers select appropriate methods or design hybrid approaches based on the characteristics of their target environment and learning objectives.

In conclusion, this paper significantly advances the understanding of JEPA by providing a critical analysis of its behavior, particularly its interaction with different types of environmental dynamics. It not only validates JEPA's potential but also outlines a fundamental challenge, thereby guiding the direction for robust and broadly applicable reconstruction-free world model learning.
