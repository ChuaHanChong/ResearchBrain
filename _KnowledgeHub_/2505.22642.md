# FastTD3: Simple, Fast, and Capable Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> FastTD3, developed by researchers at UC Berkeley including Pieter Abbeel, is an optimized off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm based on TD3. It achieves state-of-the-art wall-clock training times for complex robotic tasks, solving many HumanoidBench tasks in under 3 hours on a single A100 GPU, and demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer to a physical humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) for complex robotic systems, such as humanoids, faces prohibitively long training times and high algorithmic complexity, hindering rapid iteration during reward design.
- On-policy RL algorithms (e.g., PPO) are fast with parallel simulation but sample-inefficient, while off-policy RL algorithms (e.g., SAC, TD3) are sample-efficient but often slower in wall-clock time and more complex.
- Many state-of-the-art RL algorithms fail to solve challenging benchmark tasks, like those in HumanoidBench, even after extended training durations (e.g., 48 hours).

## Method

- **FastTD3** builds upon the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic ==Policy Gradient== (TD3) algorithm, integrating massively parallel environments, unusually large batch sizes (32,768), and a distributional critic (C51) for accelerated, stable learning.
- The approach emphasizes optimized implementation through PyTorch features like mixed-precision training and `torch.compile` for significant speedups, coupled with careful hyperparameter tuning, rather than introducing new algorithmic complexities.
- The system avoids complex architectural additions such as residual paths or layer normalization, leveraging data diversity from parallel simulation and large batches to maintain stability.

## Results

- FastTD3 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines (PPO, SAC, SimbaV2, TDMPC2, DreamerV3) in wall-clock time across HumanoidBench, IsaacLab, and MuJoCo Playground, solving a range of HumanoidBench tasks in under 3 hours on a single A100 GPU.
- FastTD3 successfully transferred a policy trained in MuJoCo Playground to a real-world Booster T1 humanoid robot, marking a documented instance of off-policy RL policy deployment on a full-size humanoid.
- Ablation studies confirmed that massively parallel environments, large batch sizes (32,768), and a distributional critic are key components contributing to the algorithm's speed and performance, while complex architectural stabilizers were found to be unnecessary and sometimes detrimental to speed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art wall-clock performance for complex robotic control is achieved by efficiently scaling well-understood off-policy RL components combined with implementation optimizations like mixed-precision training and `torch.compile` (up to 70% combined speedup), rather than relying on novel complex algorithms.
> - Massively parallel environments and large-batch training stabilize and accelerate off-policy learning, reducing the need for architectural complexities like residual paths or layer normalization.
> - Rapid training times enable faster iteration on reward functions, revealing that different RL algorithms may require specific reward tunings for deployable policies, as demonstrated by the distinct human-like gaits achieved with custom rewards for FastTD3 vs. PPO on humanoid locomotion tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has undeniably driven substantial advancements in robotics, enabling robots to learn complex behaviors and adapt to various environments. However, a persistent challenge in the field, particularly for complex robotic systems like humanoids, has been the **prohibitively long training times and the inherent complexity of state-of-the-art RL algorithms**. This bottleneck hinders rapid iteration during reward design, which is crucial for developing deployable policies in robotics.

Historically, **on-policy RL algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** (Schulman et al., 2017) have been a go-to choice for training robotic policies in simulation, primarily due to their ability to learn fast with massively parallel simulation (Heess et al., 2017; Hwangbo et al., 2019). However, PPO suffers from being sample-inefficient, making it less suitable for scenarios requiring fine-tuning with real-world data or initialization with demonstrations.

In contrast, **off-policy RL algorithms** (e.g., SAC, TD3) are generally more sample-efficient as they can reuse past experiences. However, they often come with increased algorithmic complexity and tend to have longer wall-clock training times, making their widespread adoption for deployable robotics policies challenging. Recent research has attempted to improve the sample efficiency of off-policy methods (D’Oro et al., 2023; Nauman et al., 2024b; Hansen et al., 2024), but often at the cost of even greater algorithmic complexity.

This paper addresses this crucial gap by focusing on making **off-policy RL both fast and practical** for complex robotic control, specifically humanoids. It builds upon the insights from **Parallel Q-Learning (PQL)** (Li et al., 2023b), which demonstrated the potential of scaling off-policy RL through massive parallel simulation, large batch sizes, and a distributional critic. While PQL itself achieved impressive results, its asynchronous parallel processes introduced significant implementation complexity, limiting its widespread adoption.

FastTD3's contribution lies in **distilling these successful scaling principles into a remarkably simple and easy-to-use off-policy algorithm based on TD3**, without resorting to complex asynchronous processes. It positions itself not as a novel algorithmic breakthrough but as a highly optimized, practical implementation that leverages existing ideas to achieve state-of-the-art wall-clock training times on challenging humanoid benchmarks. By providing an open-source implementation, it aims to accelerate broader RL research in robotics, making these powerful techniques more accessible to practitioners. This work represents a significant step towards democratizing high-performance RL for real-world robotics applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of FastTD3 are:

- **To significantly reduce the wall-clock training time for Reinforcement Learning algorithms in complex robotic tasks, particularly for humanoid robots.** The authors highlight that state-of-the-art RL algorithms often fail to solve many tasks in benchmarks like HumanoidBench even after 48 hours of training, which is a major bottleneck for practical robotics.
- **To develop an RL algorithm that is not only "capable" of solving challenging tasks but also "fast" and "simple" to implement and use.** This addresses the common issue where advanced RL algorithms become overly complex, hindering their adoption and iteration speed in real-world applications.
- **To enable rapid iteration in robotics reward design.** The iterative nature of designing effective reward functions for complex robotic behaviors demands algorithms that can learn and demonstrate results quickly. FastTD3 aims to shorten this feedback loop.
- **To bridge the gap between sample-efficient off-policy RL and the wall-clock speed of on-policy methods.** The goal is to provide the benefits of off-policy learning (e.g., suitability for fine-tuning with real-world data, initialization with demonstrations) without sacrificing the speed needed for practical deployment.
- **To provide a lightweight and easy-to-use open-source implementation.** This objective is crucial for accelerating future RL research in robotics by offering a reproducible and extendable codebase that lowers the barrier to entry for high-performance RL.
- **To demonstrate the effectiveness of combining known successful components** (parallel simulation, large batches, distributional critic, tuned hyperparameters) within a simple framework (TD3) to achieve surprisingly strong performance.

In essence, the motivation is to make advanced RL for humanoids more accessible, efficient, and practical for real-world deployment and rapid development cycles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FastTD3 is built upon a "remarkably simple recipe" by taking an existing off-policy algorithm, **Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3)** (Fujimoto et al., 2018), and incorporating several key optimizations and design choices. The approach does not introduce fundamentally new algorithms or complex architectural innovations but rather optimizes the training pipeline and leverages known effective components at scale.

The core methodological components include:

- **Off-Policy TD3 Foundation:** FastTD3 uses TD3 as its base, benefiting from its stability improvements over DDPG, such as clipped double Q-learning and delayed policy updates.
- **Parallel Simulation:** Inspired by successful scaling in other RL works, FastTD3 employs massively parallel environments. This significantly accelerates data collection, increasing diversity in the data distribution, which is hypothesized to be particularly effective for deterministic policy gradient algorithms like TD3. For HumanoidBench, it uses `SubprocVecEnv` (CPU-based), while for IsaacLab and MuJoCo Playground, it leverages their native GPU-based parallelization.
- **Large-Batch Updates:** An unusually large batch size of 32,768 is used for training. This is posited to provide a more stable learning signal for the critic, especially when combined with massively parallel environments, by ensuring high data diversity in each gradient update.
- **Distributional Critic (C51):** The C51 algorithm (Bellemare et al., 2017) is incorporated, where the critic learns a distribution over returns rather than just their expected value. This is found to be helpful in most cases, similar to PQL.
- **Carefully Tuned Hyperparameters:** Extensive hyperparameter tuning is a critical aspect, including exploration noise schedules (large $\sigma_{max}=0.4$), and the specific architecture of the neural networks.
- **Architectural Choices:**
 - MLPs with a descending hidden layer configuration (1024, 512, 256 for critic; 512, 256, 128 for actor) are used.
 - Crucially, FastTD3 avoids complex architectural stabilizers like residual paths or layer normalization, which are often used in other state-of-the-art RL methods (e.g., BRO, Simba). The authors hypothesize that the data diversity from parallel simulation and large batches reduces the "effective off-policyness," mitigating instabilities without these additions.
- **Clipped Double Q-learning (CDQ):** While some recent works suggest averaging Q-values, FastTD3 finds that CDQ (using the minimum of two Q-values) performs better in the absence of layer normalization, suggesting its continued importance as a tunable hyperparameter.
- **Update-to-data (UTD) Ratio:** FastTD3 operates at relatively low UTDs (2, 4, 8 updates per 128 to 4096 environment steps). This contrasts with approaches that increase UTD dramatically and require additional techniques to maintain stability, suggesting that the FastTD3 recipe is robust to higher UTDs without such modifications.
- **Replay Buffer Design:** The replay buffer size is set dynamically as `N × num_envs` to decouple its effect from the number of parallel environments, ensuring full trajectory storage. The entire buffer is stored on the GPU for efficiency in non-vision domains.

**Implementation Details:**
The codebase is built on **PyTorch** (Paszke et al., 2019) for flexibility and simplicity. It leverages:
- **Mixed-precision training with AMP and bfloat16:** Achieves up to 40% speedup.
- **`torch.compile`:** Provides up to 35% speedup.
- **Combined optimizations:** Up to 70% speedup.
- **Environment wrappers:** Standardized API for HumanoidBench, IsaacLab, and MuJoCo Playground.
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** Support for privileged states for the critic in IsaacLab and MuJoCo Playground.

Overall, the methodology emphasizes efficient scaling of a robust off-policy algorithm through carefully chosen components and optimized implementation, rather than complex theoretical novelty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FastTD3 demonstrates significant improvements in training speed and capability across a range of challenging humanoid and dexterous control tasks.

- **Overall Performance (Wall-Clock Time):** As summarized in Figure 3, FastTD3 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including PPO, SAC, SimbaV2, TDMPC2, and DreamerV3, in terms of wall-clock time across all three benchmark suites: HumanoidBench (39 tasks), IsaacLab (6 tasks), and MuJoCo Playground (4 tasks).
 - FastTD3 solves a range of HumanoidBench tasks in **under 3 hours on a single A100 GPU**.
 - It trains humanoid locomotion policies faster than PPO in IsaacLab and MuJoCo Playground, especially on rough terrain with domain randomization.

- **Comparison to Baselines (Individual Tasks):** Figure 4 and Appendix A provide detailed learning curves. FastTD3 often reaches high returns much faster than other methods, sometimes solving tasks that others fail to complete within the given training duration (e.g., 48 hours for HumanoidBench baselines).

- **Effect of Design Choices (Ablation Studies - Figure 5 & 6):**
 - **Parallel Environments:** Massively parallel environments (e.g., 128 environments) significantly accelerate training, confirming the hypothesis that diversity in data helps TD3's exploitation capabilities.
 - **Large-Batch Training:** Increasing batch size (e.g., from 2K to 32K) generally reduces overall training time, indicating improved training efficiency despite higher per-update wall-clock time.
 - **Distributional RL:** Using a distributional critic (C51) is consistently beneficial in most cases, though it introduces a few additional hyperparameters.
 - **Clipped Double Q-learning (CDQ):** CDQ remains a critical design choice and performs better than averaging Q-values when layer normalization is absent, contrary to some prior findings.
 - **Architecture (Model Size):** Larger models (e.g., 1024-512-256 for critic) improve both time and sample efficiency. Residual paths and layer normalization, surprisingly, tend to slow down training without significant gains, suggesting that the data diversity from parallel simulation and large batches naturally stabilizes the training.
 - **Exploration Noise Schedules:** A large `sigma_max` (0.4) is found to be helpful, while mixed noise (different scales per environment) provides no significant gains.
 - **Update-to-data (UTD) Ratio:** FastTD3 scales favorably with higher UTDs in terms of sample efficiency, but at the cost of increased wall-time. This suggests robustness to UTD variations without needing special techniques.
 - **Replay Buffer Size:** A larger `N` (multiplier for `num_envs`) generally improves performance, though at the cost of increased GPU memory.

- **Reward Function Sensitivity (Figure 7):** A crucial qualitative finding is that different RL algorithms may require different reward functions. FastTD3 trained with a PPO-tuned reward function resulted in an "undeployable gait" with abrupt arm movements. However, by tuning the reward function specifically for FastTD3 (adding stronger penalty terms), a smoother, more visually appealing gait was achieved. Conversely, PPO struggled with the FastTD3-tuned reward. This highlights that standard episode return metrics may not always capture the practical utility of learned policies and that faster iteration allows for more effective reward shaping.

- **FastSAC Experiments (Figure 8):** Applying the FastTD3 recipe to SAC (resulting in "FastSAC") also yielded significantly faster training than vanilla SAC. However, FastSAC tended to be unstable, possibly due to the challenges of maximizing action entropy in high-dimensional spaces.

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Figure 1):** FastTD3 successfully transferred a policy trained in MuJoCo Playground to a real-world **Booster T1 humanoid robot**. This is claimed to be "the first documented instance of a successful deployment of an off-policy RL policy on a full-size humanoid robot in the real world," demonstrating the practical deployability and robustness of policies learned with FastTD3.

In summary, the main findings confirm that a "simple, fast, and capable" off-policy RL agent can be achieved by optimizing existing components and training at scale, leading to breakthrough training speeds and successful real-world deployment for humanoids.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "FastTD3: Simple, Fast, and Capable Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Control" carries significant implications for the field of reinforcement learning and robotics:

- **Accelerating RL Research in Robotics:** By drastically reducing training times, FastTD3 enables researchers and practitioners to iterate on ideas much faster. This rapid experimentation cycle is crucial for developing and refining RL algorithms, reward functions, and simulation environments for complex robotic systems. The provided open-source implementation further amplifies this impact by making the recipe easily accessible and reproducible.
- **Empowering Rapid Iteration in Reward Design:** One of the long-standing challenges in robotics RL is the manual and iterative process of designing effective reward functions. FastTD3's short training times mean that engineers can quickly test different reward formulations, observe their impact on learned behaviors, and refine them. This makes RL-based robot development more agile and efficient, potentially leading to more robust and desired behaviors. The finding that different RL algorithms might require different reward functions (Figure 7) underscores the importance of fast iteration.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Off-Policy RL:** The successful sim-to-real transfer to a full-size humanoid robot (Booster T1) is a monumental achievement. While sim-to-real has been demonstrated with on-policy methods, achieving this with an off-policy algorithm like TD3 opens new avenues. Off-policy methods are inherently more sample-efficient and better suited for fine-tuning with limited real-world data or for leveraging demonstrations, which are crucial for overcoming the remaining sim-to-real challenges and for deploying robots in diverse, unstructured environments.
- **Democratizing High-Performance Robotics RL:** By demonstrating that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved with a "simple" recipe built on well-understood components (TD3, parallelization, large batches, distributional critic), FastTD3 makes advanced robotics RL more accessible. It shows that complex algorithmic novelty is not always required for substantial practical gains, encouraging broader adoption and application of these techniques.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper explicitly states that FastTD3 is orthogonal to many recent advances in RL. This means that future improvements in areas like robust Q-learning, advanced normalization techniques, or model-based components could be readily integrated into FastTD3, potentially leading to even greater performance and stability.
- **Applications in Demo-Driven and Iterative Inverse RL:** As an off-policy algorithm, FastTD3 is well-suited for learning from human demonstrations, which can significantly bootstrap the learning process. Furthermore, its rapid iteration capabilities make it a strong candidate for iterative inverse RL setups, especially those leveraging large language models for automated reward generation, addressing a critical bottleneck in autonomous system development.

In essence, FastTD3 doesn't just push the performance envelope; it makes high-performance RL for complex robots more practical, more usable, and more amenable to real-world deployment and rapid development cycles, thereby significantly contributing to the advancement of intelligent robotics.
