# Learning compositional models of robot skills for task and motion planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT CSAIL developed an integrated framework that leverages Gaussian Processes and active learning to efficiently acquire probabilistic models of robot manipulation skills. The system then embeds these models into a Task and Motion Planning framework, allowing a PR2 robot to autonomously solve complex, multi-step tasks by composing learned skills with conventional actions.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to efficiently learn and compositionally use sensorimotor skills to solve complex, long-horizon manipulation problems, requiring robust generalization across diverse situations.
- Acquiring new robot skills on physical robots is often prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, necessitating highly sample-efficient learning methods.
- Traditional planning methods lack probabilistic models of skill preconditions and effects, limiting robust decision-making in uncertain, dynamic environments.

## Method

- Developed a framework utilizing ==Gaussian Processes== with an ==active learning== strategy (==Straddle algorithm==) to efficiently learn probabilistic, continuous-parameter models for robot skill success criteria.
- Integrated these learned skill models into the **PDDLStream** Task and Motion Planning framework as 'streams,' enabling the generation of valid continuous parameters for planning.
- Introduced risk-aware, adaptive, and diversity-aware sampling methods (**ADAPTIVESAMPLER**, **DIVERSESAMPLER**) to efficiently provide the TAMP planner with diverse and high-probability successful skill parameters, enhanced by task-level kernel learning.

## Results

- In simulation, GP learners with active learning demonstrated superior success rates and F1 scores in classifying skill parameters compared to neural network and random forest baselines, particularly with limited training data.
- Adaptive and diversity-aware samplers reduced sample acquisition time by orders of magnitude compared to uniform rejection sampling, while generating notably more diverse sets of valid continuous parameters for planning.
- Real-world experiments on a PR2 robot confirmed active learning's efficacy in accelerating skill acquisition and showed the integrated system successfully solving multi-step tasks, including obstacle relocation and inter-arm coordination, by composing learned and pre-existing actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gaussian Processes, particularly with a multi-layer perceptron kernel and active learning, offer a sample-efficient approach to model the probabilistic success boundaries of dynamic robot manipulation skills.
> - Integrating learned skill models as 'streams' within a hybrid planning framework like PDDLStream effectively bridges low-level sensorimotor learning with high-level symbolic task planning.
> - Adaptive and diversity-aware sampling, especially when the diversity metric is learned at the task level, significantly enhances TAMP efficiency by providing more relevant and varied continuous parameter options.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a challenge in robotics concerning the efficient acquisition and compositional use of robot skills for solving complex, long-horizon manipulation problems. The broader research landscape for this work encompasses several interconnected fields:

- **Robot Learning (Sensorimotor Skills):** A significant portion of robot learning research focuses on acquiring individual closed-loop sensorimotor skills, such as pouring, scooping, or object manipulation. This paper builds upon this foundation by providing a framework for integrating such learned skills into larger task sequences.
- **Task and Motion Planning (TAMP):** TAMP problems involve robots carrying out multi-object manipulation tasks in complex environments, requiring both discrete choices (which actions, on which objects, in what order) and continuous parameter selection (grasps, placements, trajectories). Existing TAMP approaches often combine symbolic AI planning with constrained sampling or optimization for continuous parameters. This work integrates learned skill models directly into TAMP frameworks.
- **Model Learning for Planning:** Traditional planning requires explicit models of actions, including their preconditions and effects. Learning these models for complex, dynamic manipulation skills is challenging. This paper contributes by developing methods for learning probabilistic, continuous-parameter models of skills that are suitable for TAMP.
- **Gaussian Processes (GPs) and Active Learning:** GPs are widely used for probabilistic modeling of functions and estimating uncertainty. Active learning strategies, particularly those focused on level-set estimation, aim to efficiently gather data to characterize function boundaries. This paper applies and extends these methods to learn the success criteria (preconditions and effects) of robot skills from limited, costly real-world interactions.
- **Sampling for Continuous Spaces in Planning:** TAMP often involves sampling continuous parameters. The efficiency of planning is influenced by the quality and diversity of these samples. This work introduces adaptive and diversity-aware sampling strategies to generate appropriate continuous parameters for learned skills within a planning context.

The paper differentiates itself from much of the existing robot learning work, which primarily focuses on acquiring individual closed-loop skills, by addressing the subsequent challenge of how to combine and execute these skills in novel ways to achieve high-level goals. It extends previous work on planning-model learning by providing a probabilistic characterization of skill preconditions and effects, and by developing sophisticated sampling methods for deployment in TAMP.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to augment the basic capabilities of a robot by enabling it to learn and compose sensorimotor primitives to solve complex, long-horizon manipulation problems. This overarching objective is driven by several specific motivations and challenges:

1. **Generalization and Compositionality:** For robots to be useful in unstructured environments (e.g., homes), they require a foundation of basic capabilities (e.g., locomotion, simple manipulation) and the ability to build upon these by acquiring specialized skills (e.g., pouring, scooping). A critical requirement is that these acquired skills must be used compositionally, allowing novel combinations with existing skills to generalize across a wide variety of situations and purposes. This flexibility is essential for robots to adapt to diverse problems rather than being limited to predefined sequences.
2. **Efficient Skill Acquisition:** Learning new robot skills, especially on physical robots, is often expensive and time-consuming due to the cost of trial executions. Therefore, the learning process must be highly sample-efficient, requiring relatively few training examples to acquire the necessary models. This efficiency is paramount for practical deployment.
3. **Probabilistic Characterization of Skill Effectiveness:** To plan effectively with primitive actions, robots need models that describe not only what an action achieves but also the conditions under which it succeeds and with what confidence. Learning models with quantified uncertainty allows the TAMP planner to make robust decisions, favoring skill executions with a high probability of success, especially when considering sequential tasks where failures can propagate.
4. **Comprehensive Characterization of Control Parameters:** Instead of identifying a single optimal control parameter value for a skill, the system needs to characterize a comprehensive set of successful control parameter values. This is because downstream constraints (e.g., robot kinematics, collision avoidance) might render a seemingly optimal value infeasible in a broader planning context. A diverse set of feasible parameters provides the planner with more options to find a complete solution.
5. **Integration with Task and Motion Planning (TAMP):** The learned skill models must be in a form directly usable by TAMP planners. This involves formally describing the intended effects and preconditions (conditions for successful initiation) of a new skill, allowing it to be incorporated and combined with existing skills to achieve high-level goals. The goal is to bridge the gap between low-level sensorimotor learning and high-level symbolic planning.
6. **Addressing Hybrid Discrete-Continuous Problems:** TAMP problems are inherently hybrid, requiring both discrete choices (e.g., action sequences) and continuous parameter selection (e.g., object poses, gripper configurations, skill parameters). The learned models and associated sampling strategies must effectively handle these continuous dimensions within a discrete planning framework.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to develop an integrated system where robots can efficiently learn new manipulation skills, characterize their success conditions probabilistically, and leverage these models within a TAMP framework to solve complex, multi-step tasks in dynamic, uncertain environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology focuses on learning compositional models of robot skills by estimating constraints on their parameters using Gaussian Processes (GPs) and integrating these models into a Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) framework, specifically PDDLStream.

#### 3.1. Skill Modeling and Constraint Estimation

The core problem is to learn a constraint $\chi_O(s, \omega, s')$ for each skill $O$, where $s$ is the initial state, $\omega$ is a skill parameter value, and $s'$ is the resulting state. This constraint indicates whether executing $O(\omega)$ from $s$ successfully leads to $s'$. The paper refines this by defining $\chi_O$ generically on fixed context parameters $\alpha$ (e.g., object dimensions) and chosen control parameters $\theta$ (e.g., pouring waypoints). The constraint is expressed as an inequality $\chi(\theta, \alpha) = [g(\theta, \alpha) > 0]$, where $g$ is a real-valued scoring function (e.g., proportion of liquid successfully poured minus a target). The objective is to characterize the conditional super-level set $A_\alpha \equiv \{\theta \in B | g(\theta, \alpha) > 0\}$, representing successful control parameters for a given context.

#### 3.2. Active Learning for Constraint Estimation

To efficiently characterize $A_\alpha$ with high confidence, especially with limited, expensive real-world trials, the method uses Gaussian Processes (GPs) for function approximation and active learning for sample selection.
- **Gaussian Processes:** GPs model the score function $g(\theta, \alpha)$, providing both a mean prediction $\mu(\theta, \alpha)$ and a variance estimate $\sigma^2(\theta, \alpha)$ for any input. The multi-layer perceptron (MLP) kernel is identified as particularly effective due to the potentially discontinuous nature of manipulation skill scores.
- **Straddle Algorithm:** The active learning process employs the straddle algorithm (Bryan et al., 2006) to select informative query points $\theta$ for evaluation. The acquisition function $\psi_{\mu,\sigma}(\theta, \alpha) = -|\mu(\theta, \alpha)| + 1.96\sigma(\theta, \alpha)$ is maximized. This function prioritizes sampling points near the zero boundary of $g$ or regions with high uncertainty, aiming to refine the boundary of the super-level set efficiently.
- **Sample Efficiency:** The active learning approach is motivated by the need for sample-efficient learning, particularly on physical robots where each trial is costly in terms of time and resources.

#### 3.3. Planning Integration with PDDLStream

The learned skill models are integrated into the PDDLStream planning framework, which extends PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language) with "streams" – declarative specifications of conditional samplers.
- **Hybrid Planning:** PDDLStream addresses hybrid discrete-continuous TAMP problems by combining symbolic planning with black-box samplers for continuous parameters.
- **Skill Description:** For each learned skill, a PDDLStream description includes:
 - **Constraint on Parameter Values:** The learned $\chi(\theta, \alpha)$ is used to define a `GoodPour` or `GoodScoop` predicate in the PDDLStream domain.
 - **Sampler (Stream):** A stream is defined (e.g., `sample-pour-path`) that, given context parameters $\alpha$, generates a sequence of assignments to control parameters $\theta$ that satisfy the learned constraint with high probability.
- **Modular Separation:** The approach modularly separates primitive sampling operations (e.g., generating object paths for pouring based on learned models) from traditional model-based algorithms (e.g., robot motion planning for following an object path), leveraging the strengths of both.

#### 3.4. Risk-Aware and Diversity-Aware Sampling for Planning

During planning, it is crucial to sample a diverse set of $\theta$ values that are likely to satisfy the learned constraint and other downstream planning constraints (e.g., kinematic reachability, collision avoidance).
- **Risk-Aware Sampling:** Given a context $\alpha$, the system samples $\theta$ values from a high-probability super-level set $\hat{A}_\alpha = \{\theta | \varphi_{\mu,\sigma}(\theta, \alpha) > \beta^*\}$, where $\varphi_{\mu,\sigma}(\theta, \alpha) = \mu(\theta, \alpha)/\sigma(\theta, \alpha)$ and $\beta^*$ is a threshold chosen using a concentration bound to ensure that samples satisfy $g(\theta, \alpha) > 0$ with high probability ($1-\delta$). A relaxed threshold $\beta$ is used in practice, based on the most confident parameter.
- **Efficient Adaptive Sampling (ADAPTIVESAMPLER):** To overcome the inefficiency of uniform rejection sampling in high-dimensional spaces with small feasible regions, an adaptive sampler is developed. This sampler maintains a buffer of feasible samples and iteratively draws new samples from a mixture of truncated Gaussian distributions centered around these known good samples, re-weighting them by importance weights to encourage exploration.
- **Diversity-Aware Sampling (DIVERSESAMPLER):** To prevent the planner from repeatedly trying similar samples that fail due to downstream constraints, a diversity-aware sampler is introduced. It selects new samples that are maximally diverse from previously tried samples, quantifying diversity using the determinant of a Gram matrix (related to Determinantal Point Processes). This encourages the exploration of the feasible region.
- **Task-Level Kernel Learning:** The kernel parameters of the diversity measure are adapted online. If a sampled $\theta$ fails to lead to a solution, the inverse length scale of the feature that contributed most to its "diversity" is decreased. This mechanism allows the system to learn which dimensions are most important for diversity in a given task distribution, thus molding the diversity metric to the demands of the planning problems.

#### 3.5. System Architecture

The integrated system comprises:
- **Perception:** Uses RGB-D camera data, Faster R-CNN for object detection, and point cloud registration for coarse object localization and pose estimation.
- **Learning:** GPs with active learning for estimating skill constraints.
- **Planning:** PDDLStream (specifically the FOCUSED algorithm) combining learned skill models with pre-existing motion planning primitives (e.g., RRT-Connect, Inverse Kinematics).
- **Execution:** Low-level position control to follow planned joint trajectories, with real-time scoring of outcomes for data collection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed methodology through extensive experiments in both simulated (Kitchen2D, Kitchen3D) and real-world (KitchenPR2) environments, demonstrating its effectiveness in skill learning, sampling, and integrated task and motion planning.

#### 4.1. Skill Learning Performance (Simulation - Kitchen3D)

- **Comparison of Learners:** A Gaussian Process (GP) using a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) kernel was compared against neural network (classifier/regressor) and random forest (classifier/regressor) baselines.
 - **Success Rate:** GP achieved the best average success rate for the most confident control parameter, outperforming neural networks and random forests, a result attributed to its uncertainty awareness.
 - **F1 Score:** Regressors generally outperformed classifiers, with the GP showing competitive F1 scores on held-out test data. This suggested that real-valued score functions are more appropriate for learning, especially near decision boundaries.
 - **Kernel Selection:** The MLP kernel for the GP slightly outperformed squared exponential (RBF) and Matérn kernels, potentially due to the discontinuous nature of the pouring and scooping scoring functions.
- **Active Learning Impact:**
 - **Success Rate & F1 Score:** GPs trained with active learning (GP-LSE, GP-LSE2) generally outperformed GPs without active learning (GP). GP-LSE2 (sampling context, actively optimizing control parameters) often yielded the best success rates and F1 scores, especially with limited training examples (e.g., 100 trials). This highlights the benefit of methodically exploring high-variance regions over random sampling.
 - **Real-world Relevance:** For small datasets (e.g., 100 examples), simulating real-world constraints, active learning improved performance, indicating its potential for reducing real-robot trials.

#### 4.2. Adaptive and Diverse Sampling Performance (Simulation - Kitchen3D & Kitchen2D)

- **Efficiency:** The adaptive sampler (ADAPTIVE) significantly reduced the time to gather 50 samples ($T_{50}$) compared to uniform rejection sampling (REJECTION) for both pouring and scooping tasks in Kitchen3D. Similar improvements were observed in Kitchen2D.
- **Diversity:** The diversity-aware sampler (DIVERSE) generated samples with notably higher diversity (measured by $D(S)$) compared to ADAPTIVE and REJECTION. While DIVERSE sometimes required slightly more samples to find 5 positive ones, this trade-off was considered acceptable for providing a wider range of options to the planner.
- **False Positive Rate:** All sampling methods maintained a low false positive rate, indicating that they effectively sampled from the high-probability super-level set.

#### 4.3. Learning Kernels for Diverse Sampling (Simulation - Kitchen3D & Kitchen2D)

- **Planning Time Reduction:** Task-level kernel learning (DIVERSE-LK) generally led to lower planning runtimes and higher success rates within time limits, especially for complex tasks (WASH, UNCLOG in Kitchen3D; Task I, II, III in Kitchen2D). This indicates that adapting the diversity metric to task-specific requirements improves the efficiency of the overall planning process by providing more useful samples to the planner.
- **Stability:** DIVERSE-LK and DIVERSE-GK (fixed kernel) showed more stable performance compared to ADAPTIVE in terms of success rate.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments (KitchenPR2)

- **Perception System:** The system successfully used Faster R-CNN for object detection and point cloud registration for pose estimation, allowing the robot to recognize and localize objects.
- **Data Collection & Training:** The robot successfully autonomously collected training data for pouring and scooping using a planning pipeline that incorporated skill setup, execution, scoring, and scene reset. This demonstrated the feasibility of self-supervised data acquisition.
- **Active Learning Efficacy:** Real-world experiments confirmed that active learning enabled the GP learner to more quickly classify successful pours and scoops, as evidenced by improved F1 scores on test objects.
- **Most Confident Prediction:** The active learner consistently outperformed non-active learners in real-world tests for both pouring and scooping, showing higher percentages of valid (admitting a full robot plan) and successful (in the super-level set) control parameters, and a higher average filled capacity.
- **Integrated System for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The integrated system, combining learned pouring models with 7 pre-existing robot operations (move, pick, place, fill, push, stir), successfully solved challenging multi-step manipulation problems on a PR2 robot.
 - **Obstacle Avoidance and Relocation:** The robot demonstrated the ability to plan to relocate obstructing objects (e.g., a green block) to enable picking the target cup, followed by successful pouring.
 - **Inter-arm Coordination / Workspace Extension:** The robot successfully planned to push a bowl into reach of its pouring arm when direct reach was initially impossible, illustrating adaptive behavior in response to environmental constraints.
 - **Compositionality:** These demonstrations highlighted the system's ability to compose learned skills (pouring, scooping) with conventional actions (picking, placing, pushing) to achieve complex goals in novel arrangements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an integrated framework for learning and deploying dynamic manipulation skills within a task and motion planning system, demonstrating several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Generalization and Compositional Robotics:** The work advances the capability of robots to generalize and compose skills in novel combinations. By learning probabilistic models of skill preconditions and effects, robots can adapt acquired skills to diverse, previously unseen scenarios, moving beyond predefined fixed behaviors. This is a crucial step towards more versatile and autonomous robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured human environments.
- **Efficient Skill Acquisition for Real-World Robots:** The application of Gaussian Processes with active learning significantly reduces the number of real-robot trials required to learn robust skill models. Given the high cost (time, resources, potential damage) of real-world robot experimentation, this sample efficiency is critical for the practical deployment and widespread adoption of robot learning. The automated data collection pipeline further streamlines this process.
- **Robustness through Uncertainty-Aware Planning:** Learning probabilistic models that quantify uncertainty allows the TAMP planner to make risk-aware decisions. This enhances the robustness of multi-step plans by enabling the selection of control parameters that are confidently predicted to succeed, thereby reducing unexpected failures in complex tasks.
- **Improved Planning Efficiency via Advanced Sampling:** The novel adaptive and diversity-aware sampling methods contribute to the efficiency of TAMP algorithms. By generating high-probability, diverse sets of continuous action parameters, the planner can explore the feasible space more effectively, quickly identifying solutions that satisfy both learned skill constraints and other kinematic or collision-avoidance requirements. Task-level kernel learning further refines this by adapting the diversity metric to specific task distributions.
- **Seamless Integration of Learning and Planning:** The PDDLStream framework provides a principled way to integrate learned generative models (streams) for dynamic skills with symbolic task planning and traditional motion planning primitives. This modularity allows the system to leverage the strengths of both data-driven learning and model-based reasoning, bridging a long-standing gap in robotics research.
- **Foundation for More Complex Robotic Autonomy:** The demonstrated ability to solve complex, long-horizon manipulation problems (e.g., making coffee, obstacle relocation for pouring, pushing objects into reach) serves as a proof-of-concept for intelligent autonomous systems. This work lays a foundation for future robots that can learn new capabilities online, adapt to changing environments, and tackle more intricate real-world tasks with minimal human intervention.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines significant avenues for future work, including incorporating learned predictions into action costs for risk-aware planning, investigating sim-to-real transfer challenges for active learning, and extending to belief-space planning for stochastic and partially observable environments. These directions highlight the broader impact of this work in shaping future research in intelligent robot control and learning.
