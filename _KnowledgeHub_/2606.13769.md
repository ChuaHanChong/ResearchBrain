# $μ_0$: A Scalable 3D Interaction-Trace World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new scalable 3D interaction-trace world model, μ0, learns to predict future motions of semantic interaction points from diverse video data, significantly reducing reliance on action-labeled robot datasets. The model demonstrates superior multi-sample trace prediction quality and enables robot policies to achieve up to an 18.4 percentage-point higher success rate in real-world manipulation tasks than VLM-only baselines.

## Problem

- Robot learning struggles with the scarcity and embodiment-specificity of action-labeled robot data, despite an abundance of general video data.
- Prior pixel-space world models dedicate excessive capacity to visual appearance, neglecting metric geometry and contact structures essential for manipulation tasks.
- Existing motion-centric methods suffer from issues like under-sampling task-critical regions, conflating object and camera motion, and using coarse, episode-level language descriptions.

## Method

- Development of **μ0**, a query-conditioned 3D trace-space world model designed to predict future 3D trajectories of semantic interaction points.
- Introduction of ==TraceExtract==, an automated data pipeline that generates high-quality, event-captioned 3D trace supervision from uncurated human and robot videos.
- μ0 integrates a multi-modal conditioning backbone for vision and language, a permutation-equivariant trace expert predicting B-spline control points, and a flow matching objective with semantic rigidity constraints.

## Results

- μ0 demonstrated improved multi-sample 3D trace prediction, achieving the best Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE) across all time horizons while being **2.9** times faster than the next-best 2D baseline.
- When used as a motion prior in simulated robot tasks (RoboCasa365), μ0 with an action expert achieved a **30.25%** average success rate, surpassing action-labeled π0 by **5.0** percentage points.
- In real-world UR3 manipulation, μ0 + action expert reached a **91.7%** average success rate, an **18.4** percentage-point improvement over a VLM + action expert baseline and outperforming previous action-labeled VLA methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing physical interactions as 3D traces of semantic keypoints offers a compact, metric, and embodiment-agnostic format for transferable robot learning.
> - Automating the extraction of globally consistent 3D traces and aligning them with event-centric language captions from diverse video sources effectively addresses data scarcity.
> - Pretraining a world model on video-only 3D interaction traces creates a powerful motion prior that significantly enhances the performance of robot policies with minimal task-specific action supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot learning faces a challenge where abundant video data detailing physical behaviors contrasts with the scarcity of action-labeled robot data. Action-labeled data is expensive to collect, specific to particular hardware, and often incompatible across different robot embodiments. World models offer a strategy to circumvent this limitation by learning general dynamics from observation-rich video data, with the intent of later adapting these learned dynamics to specific robot platforms.

Within this context, existing world model approaches exhibit distinct characteristics and limitations. Pixel-space video models, while scalable and capable of providing broad visual priors, allocate significant model capacity to dense appearance and background reconstruction. This often results in a reduced focus on metric geometry, contact structures, and occlusion patterns, which are crucial for effective manipulation tasks. Conversely, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which directly predict actions, are constrained by the scarcity and embodiment-specificity of the action-labeled robot demonstrations required for their training.

Recent advancements have explored motion-centric methods, including 2D optical flows, 3D flows, and object trajectory prediction. However, these methods typically exhibit several limitations: they may under-sample small but task-critical regions (e.g., tool tips or contact patches), conflate object motion with camera motion by operating in local or 2D image-space coordinates, and often pair lengthy demonstrations with coarse, episode-level captions rather than granular, event-level intent. For instance, TraceGen, a prior work, predicts 3D traces on a fixed grid, uses episode-level captions, and requires depth-conditioned input, reflecting these aforementioned limitations.

The present work, μ0, positions itself in an intermediate space between pixel-level and direct action modeling. It focuses on predicting 3D traces of semantic interaction points, such as specific object parts, tools, hands, and contact regions. This approach aims to provide a compact and embodiment-agnostic representation of motion, thereby addressing the scalability challenges and the limitations of previous world modeling and motion-centric techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable world model that can enable robot learning without extensive reliance on embodiment-specific, action-labeled data. This model aims to forecast how actions instigate physical changes within an environment.

More specifically, the key motivations and objectives include:
- **Scalability for Robot Learning:** To overcome the fundamental data paradox in robot learning, where large volumes of visual data exist, but precise, action-labeled data for robots is scarce, costly, and specific to individual hardware.
- **Compact and Embodiment-Agnostic Representation:** To introduce a world model that predicts future motion in a compact and reusable 3D trace space. This representation is intended to describe *what* needs to move, rather than *how* a specific robot should move, thus making it transferable across different robot morphologies and control interfaces.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing World Models:**
 - To improve upon pixel-space video models by avoiding the expenditure of computational capacity on dense appearance reconstruction, which often lacks the metric geometric and contact information vital for manipulation tasks.
 - To provide an alternative to direct action prediction models (VLA models) that are bottlenecked by the difficulty of acquiring large-scale, action-labeled robot demonstration datasets.
- **Enhancing Motion-Centric Priors:** To advance beyond existing motion-centric approaches by:
 - **Semantic Measurement Target:** Selecting salient and task-critical interaction points for motion measurement, rather than relying on dense or uniform grids that can be inefficient or miss important details.
 - **Global 3D Consistency:** Ensuring that keypoint identities and 3D positions are consistently tracked across long video sequences, effectively separating object motion from camera motion by operating in a globally aligned 3D coordinate system.
 - **Event-Level Language Alignment:** Associating local motion segments with descriptive language captions at an event level, thereby providing fine-grained intent that is crucial for understanding and replicating complex manipulation skills.
- **Developing a Scalable Data Pipeline:** To introduce TraceExtract, a data engine designed to automatically extract high-quality, event-captioned 3D trace supervision from diverse, heterogeneous video sources (human and robot), facilitating the large-scale training of the proposed world model.
- **Enabling Transferable Robot Policies:** To demonstrate that the pretrained, frozen μ0 world model can serve as an effective motion prior for downstream robot policies, allowing action experts to be trained with minimal robot-specific action supervision while leveraging the comprehensive motion understanding acquired during video-only pretraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper integrates two primary systems: TraceExtract, a data engine for generating supervision, and μ0, a query-conditioned 3D trace-space world model.

#### 3.1. TraceExtract: A Scalable Cross-Embodiment Data Pipeline

TraceExtract is designed to convert uncurated human and robot manipulation videos into structured, event-captioned 3D trace supervision. Its pipeline comprises three stages:

- **Semantic Keypoint Sampling:** This stage addresses the issue of sparse or uninformative keypoint selection in prior methods.
 1. DINOv2 patch features are extracted from representative frames of a video chunk.
 2. These features are clustered into entity-level groups (e.g., specific object parts).
 3. Entity identities are propagated temporally across the video clip using bipartite matching.
 4. A fixed keypoint budget is allocated proportionally to each entity's visible patch coverage, with minimum allocations for small but salient entities. Spatially diverse keypoints are selected via farthest-point sampling within each entity mask.
 5. A movement filter marks static or background-dominated tracks, ensuring that only actively moving points contribute to the interaction signal. The DINO cluster identity is stored with each keypoint for subsequent use in rigidity loss.

- **3D Trace Construction:** This stage ensures globally consistent 3D tracking of selected keypoints across long videos.
 1. **Global–local reconstruction:** A hybrid VGGT scheme is employed. Sparse anchor frames establish a shared global coordinate frame and intrinsics. The full video is divided into chunks, each processed for local depth and extrinsics. Each chunk's local poses are then rigidly aligned to the global anchor frames, preventing error accumulation.
 2. **Progressive 3D Tracking Across Chunks:** To maintain keypoint identity and continuity, a 3D point tracker (TAPIP3D) processes the first chunk. For subsequent chunks, active keypoint groups are propagated using their last known 3D world positions as queries, ensuring robustness to camera motion due to global coordinate operation.
 3. **Reference-Frame Traces:** The globally aligned 3D tracks are reprojected into a per-chunk reference camera. This representation removes egocentric camera motion and maintains image alignment for the visual backbone. Trace speed is normalized using arc-length reparameterization.

- **Event-Centric Captioning:** This stage provides language labels aligned with specific motion events.
 1. **Motion-Centric Chunking:** A scalar motion signal, derived from averaged per-frame accelerations of valid moving keypoints, is smoothed. Prominent peaks in this signal define "action anchors," and chunk boundaries are placed at low-acceleration valleys between these anchors. This results in short, motion-centric events aligned with subgoals.
 2. **Hierarchical VLM Captioning:** For each motion-centric chunk, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) generates structured captions based on the start, midpoint, and end frames, optionally incorporating motion masks or episode-level task descriptions. A text-only Large Language Model (LLM) then merges adjacent captions over sliding windows to create both fine-grained event descriptions and coarser task summaries.

The output of TraceExtract is a set of tuples containing an observation image, an event or merged task caption, a query keypoint set, and corresponding past and future 3D traces in the reference camera.

**4.2. μ0: Query-Conditioned Trace World Model** μ0 learns to predict the future motion of interaction-centric keypoints in 3D, conditioned on observation, language instruction, and optional keypoint history. It addresses three core challenges: semantic-metric fusion, query equivariance, and multi-modal dynamics.

- **Multi-Modal Conditioning Backbone:**
 1. **Semantic Reuse:** A pretrained SmolVLM2-2.2B prefix encodes RGB observations and language instructions. A separate Trace Expert then cross-attends to the VLM's key-value cache, separating semantic memory from motion computation.
 2. **Depth Pathway:** Optional metric depth maps are routed through a dedicated trainable patch-embedding stem (cloned from the RGB stem), then share deeper SigLIP layers with RGB tokens. This allows the model to leverage geometric cues without disrupting pretrained RGB statistics.

- **Permutation-Equivariant Trace Expert:**
 1. **Exchangeable Queries:** The model processes each keypoint as an independent and exchangeable query, ensuring that predictions are invariant to the order of keypoints.
 2. **Spline Targets:** For each query, the future trajectory is represented as cubic B-spline control points, relative to the current 3D anchor. This parameterization promotes compactness, smoothness, and easier denoising compared to direct waypoint regression.
 3. **Query Tokenization:** Each keypoint's history and noisy future controls are tokenized. These tokens combine segment embeddings (history vs. future), Fourier embeddings for current pixel location (passed through an MLP), and localized DINO features. This grounds each token in its visual entity.

- **Flow Matching with Semantic Structure:** To handle uncertainty and avoid averaging plausible futures, the trace expert is trained as a conditional flow model over B-spline control points.
 1. **Conditional Denoising:** The model (vθ) predicts a velocity field to transport noisy control points to clean target control points. This is conditioned on VLM context, per-query token conditions, and flow-time modulation (adaLN-Zero).
 2. **Structural Constraints:**
 - **Validity Prediction:** A dedicated head predicts per-step trajectory validity (e.g., due to occlusion or track loss) via a sigmoid cross-entropy loss (Ldone).
 - **Semantic Rigidity:** An auxiliary rigidity loss (Lrig) encourages keypoints within the same DINO cluster to maintain their local geometric configuration, penalizing non-rigid deformations during denoising.
 3. **Joint Objective:** The overall training loss combines the flow loss, validity prediction loss, and rigidity loss: L = Lflow
- λdoneLdone
- λrigLrig. Inference involves a multi-step Euler denoising loop.

- **Trace-Conditioned Action Expert:** For robot execution, the pretrained μ0 (VLM backbone + trace expert) is frozen and serves as a motion prior.
 1. **Embodiment Transfer:** Only a lightweight action expert is trained on target embodiment data.
 2. **Policy Interface:** The action expert reads intermediate hidden states (motion descriptors ztrace) from a single partial-denoising step of μ0. These features are injected into VLM features via a gated cross-attention module. The action expert then predicts continuous action chunks using an action denoiser, conditioned on gripper-camera images, robot proprioception, and language tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of μ0 focused on its trace prediction quality and its effectiveness as a motion prior for robot action generation in both simulated and real-world environments.

**5.1. μ0 Trace Prediction Quality** μ0 was evaluated against various 2D and 3D baselines using metrics such as Average Displacement Error (ADE), Final Displacement Error (FDE), Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), and Fréchet Distance (FD).
- **Performance:** μ0 consistently demonstrated improved multi-sample trace prediction quality. In 2D, it achieved the best Top-5 ADE, FDE, and DTW across all tested time horizons, indicating more accurate goal-directed futures even when Top-1 performance was competitive with VLM baselines. In 3D, μ0 obtained the best results on every reported ADE, FDE, DTW, and FD metric across all horizons.
- **Efficiency:** μ0 exhibited a prediction latency of 0.29 seconds, making it approximately 2.9 times faster than the next-fastest 2D baseline.
- **Qualitative Assessment:** Visual comparisons indicated that μ0 produced coherent and goal-directed traces, avoiding the noisy, overly dense, or spatially misaligned predictions observed in several prior methods.

**5.2. Action Generation with Pretrained μ 0** The pretrained μ0 model was evaluated for its ability to serve as a motion prior for action generation in downstream tasks.

- **Simulated Environment (RoboCasa365):**
 - μ0, when paired with an action expert, achieved an average success rate of 30.25% across 8 representative tasks. This performance surpassed π0 by 5.0 percentage points, despite μ0 's pretraining relying solely on video data without action labels.
 - Compared to TraceGen, a previous video-only trace baseline, μ0 improved the average success rate by 7.25 percentage points.
 - While π0.5 achieved a higher average success rate (42%), it benefited from large-scale action-labeled pretraining, making it not a directly comparable data-matched benchmark.

- **Real-World Environment (UR3 Robot):**
 - In real-world experiments involving three UR3 manipulation tasks (Pick into Sink, Pour Almonds, Unfold Towel), μ0
- action expert achieved the highest average success rate of 91.7%.
 - This result represented an 18.4 percentage-point improvement over a VLM + action expert baseline (which used the same policy architecture but omitted the trace expert), demonstrating the utility of frozen trace features.
 - μ0
- action expert also surpassed action-labeled VLA baselines π0 and π0.5 by 20.0 and 11.7 percentage points, respectively.
 - The method improved upon the previous video-only baseline, TraceGen, by 10.0 percentage points, attributed to stronger TraceExtract supervision and architecture.

#### 4.1. Scaling Analysis

The study included an analysis of μ0 's scalability with model capacity and training data volume.
- **Model Scaling:** Trace prediction quality consistently improved with larger model capacities (from 342M to 2.59B parameters), indicating that the objective remains capacity-limited at the current scale.
- **Data Scaling:** Increasing the pretraining dataset size from 5% to 100% of the TraceExtract dataset led to improved trace prediction quality, particularly for longer time horizons.
- **Action-Head Scaling:** The performance gap between μ0
- action expert and a variant without trace features widened as the action head's capacity decreased. This suggests that the structured motion information provided by trace-space pretraining is particularly beneficial for policies with limited capacity.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the contributions of key architectural and optimization choices:
- **B-spline parameterization:** Direct regression of raw trace steps resulted in lower performance compared to B-spline parameterization.
- **DINOv2 features:** Their removal led to a slight degradation in trace prediction quality.
- **Rigidity loss:** Disabling this loss also resulted in a slight performance decrease.
- **Input Robustness:** The model exhibited robustness to the omission of metric depth or historical traces, maintaining competitive or slightly degraded performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper carries several implications for the field of robot learning and artificial intelligence:

- **Scalable Robot Learning Paradigm:** By demonstrating that effective robot policies can be achieved through video-only pretraining, μ0 offers a path towards more scalable robot learning. This reduces the dependency on expensive, hardware-specific, and scarce action-labeled data, which has historically been a major bottleneck.
- **Transferable Motion Representation:** The introduction of 3D interaction traces as a compact, metric, and embodiment-agnostic representation is significant. This representation can serve as a reusable motion prior, enabling the transfer of learned dynamics across diverse robot platforms and tasks without requiring extensive adaptation for each new embodiment.
- **Efficient Pretraining:** The performance achieved by μ0
- action expert, competitive with or exceeding models extensively pretrained with action supervision, suggests a more data-efficient pretraining paradigm. This could accelerate the development and deployment of generalist robots.
- **Novel World Model Interface:** μ0 's approach to predicting explicit 3D trajectories for semantically selected interaction points provides an alternative to traditional pixel-space world models (which can be visually dense but geometrically imprecise) and direct action prediction models (which are embodiment-specific). This intermediate representation is designed to be directly actionable and interpretable.
- **Automated Data Curation:** TraceExtract, the accompanying data engine, represents a substantial contribution to automating the creation of high-quality supervision data. Its ability to extract event-captioned 3D trace tuples from heterogeneous video sources addresses a critical challenge in scaling up robot learning datasets.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens avenues for future research into incorporating more complex physical phenomena, such as forces, tactile feedback, and diverse contact modes, into the trace representation. Furthermore, it sets the stage for broader validation of the approach on more complex robot embodiments (e.g., mobile manipulators, dexterous hands) and longer-horizon, multi-stage tasks.

In summary, μ0 establishes 3D interaction traces as a viable and effective representation for scalable, cross-embodiment robot manipulation, contributing to the development of more generalist and adaptable robotic systems.
