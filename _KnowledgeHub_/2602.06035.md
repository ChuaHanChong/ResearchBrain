# InterPrior: Scaling Generative Control for Physics-Based Human-Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> InterPrior introduces a three-stage framework for generative control of physics-based human-object interactions, demonstrating how to scale policies to complex loco-manipulation tasks while maintaining robustness and physical realism. The approach combines large-scale imitation learning with reinforcement learning finetuning, achieving superior performance in long-horizon tasks and zero-shot generalization to novel objects and interaction styles.

## Problem

- Existing physics-based character animation methods for human-object interaction (HOI) struggle to scale to diverse tasks and dynamic environments, often leading to brittle behaviors.
- Current generative controllers either suffer from unstable optimization and mode collapse or lack the robustness and generalization needed for out-of-distribution scenarios and failure recovery.
- Developing a unified policy capable of generating expressive, diverse, and physically coherent whole-body HOI from sparse, high-level goals remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A three-stage learning paradigm is proposed: (I) a robust full-reference ==imitation expert== (InterMimic+) trained with expanded reference scope and reference-free hand rewards, (II) variational distillation into a generative policy (**InterPrior**) using multi-modal goal conditioning and latent space regularization, and (III) post-training via reinforcement learning to enhance robustness and learn recovery behaviors.
- The InterPrior model utilizes a Transformer encoder for a Prior network and MLPs for Encoder/Decoder, incorporating stochastic mask sampling for goals, latent bounding, and temporal consistency regularization.
- RL finetuning is applied as a local optimizer with an 'in-betweening' task formulation and a distributed training schedule to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, enabling the policy to go beyond reference data for skills like regrasping or getting up.

## Results

- InterPrior achieves a significant increase in success rates for long-horizon multi-goal chaining (from **45.1%** to **68.8%**) and under random initialization stress tests (from **41.1%** to **88.6%**) compared to prior methods like MaskedMimic.
- The framework demonstrates zero-shot generalization to novel objects and interaction styles from unseen datasets (BEHAVE, HODome), effectively completing tasks and recovering from dataset imperfections, showcasing a strong reusable prior.
- InterPrior successfully transfers control to different humanoid embodiments (SMPL and Unitree G1 robot) and across physics engines (IsaacGym to MuJoCo), indicating its versatility and potential for real-world robotic deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid approach combining large-scale imitation distillation for naturalness and RL finetuning for robustness is crucial for scaling generative control in complex physics-based human-object interaction.
> - Enhancements to the expert policy, such as expanded reference scope with randomized perturbations and reference-free hand rewards, are essential for improving fundamental physical coherence and stability in grasping.
> - Regularization techniques in the latent space, like latent bounding and temporal consistency, significantly stabilize skill learning and prevent the generation of unnatural behaviors, especially for long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "InterPrior" addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of physics-based character animation, human-object interaction (HOI), and generative control for embodied AI. The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in generating realistic human movements, but scaling these to complex, physics-based interactions with diverse objects, especially for humanoid robots, remains a formidable hurdle.

Historically, physics-based character animation has relied on two main paradigms:
1. **Motion imitation policies:** Methods like DeepMimic or InterMimic learn to track detailed reference motions. While achieving high fidelity, they are often brittle, failing when actual environments or interactions deviate from the exact reference. They also typically require explicit, dense planning, which is difficult for whole-body loco-manipulation in complex scenarios.
2. **Generative controllers:** Approaches utilizing adversarial imitation or variational autoencoders (VAEs) aim to model a distribution of feasible behaviors. Adversarial methods can expand motion coverage but are often plagued by unstable optimization, discriminator mode collapse, and the need for handcrafted task objectives. VAE-based approaches, especially those distilling from reference imitation policies like MaskedMimic, can absorb large-scale data and generate diverse motions by sampling from a latent space. However, these methods can be brittle when the reference data's coverage doesn't adequately span the vast configuration space of loco-manipulation, leading to failures in out-of-distribution scenarios.

InterPrior positions itself as a synergistic solution, directly addressing the limitations of existing methods by combining the strengths of large-scale imitation distillation and reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning. It aims to bridge the gap between high-fidelity, data-driven priors and the robustness and generalization capabilities needed for real-world (or high-fidelity simulated) humanoid control.

The paper contributes to several sub-areas:
- **Scalable HOI Priors:** It recognizes the need for models that can generalize across tasks, remain robust to imperfect data, and synthesize physically realistic HOIs, moving beyond skill-specific or hand-centric solutions.
- **Hybrid RL and Imitation Learning:** By using distillation for a strong, natural initialization and RL as a local optimizer, InterPrior leverages a powerful hybrid paradigm that is gaining traction in robotics for tackling complex control problems. This allows it to learn from existing data while also adapting to novel situations and recovering from errors.
- **Generative Control for Loco-Manipulation:** It pushes the envelope for generative control, enabling policies to sample diverse, physically plausible loco-manipulation behaviors conditioned on sparse, high-level goals, rather than just mimicking fully specified trajectories.
- **Addressing Data Imperfections:** The focus on robustness and failure recovery is crucial, as real-world HOI datasets often contain physical inconsistencies or sparse coverage that can impede learning.

In essence, InterPrior offers a principled and scalable solution for generating agile whole-body human-object interactions that are both natural (like human motion) and robust (like a well-trained robot).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental motivation behind InterPrior stems from observing how humans interact with objects: they rarely plan whole-body movements at a low, explicit level. Instead, high-level intentions and affordances guide their actions, while coordinated balance, contact, and manipulation emerge naturally from underlying physical and motor priors. The challenge for humanoids and simulated characters is to replicate this intuitive, adaptable behavior.

The paper identifies the difficulty in scaling such motor priors. Current methods either struggle with the vast configuration space of human-object interactions (especially complex loco-manipulation tasks), leading to brittleness, or fail to produce physically coherent whole-body coordination and generalization across diverse contexts.

To address these challenges, InterPrior sets out to achieve scalability along four crucial axes:
1. **Task Coverage:** The objective is to develop a single, unified policy capable of supporting multiple goal formulations. This includes sparse targets, trajectory goals, contact goals, and their various compositions, moving beyond task-specific controllers.
2. **Skill Coverage:** The framework aims to scale to large and diverse Human-Object Interaction (HOI) datasets, enabling a rich repertoire of interactions that go beyond simple grasping, encompassing complex affordance-rich behaviors.
3. **Motion Coverage:** The goal is to generate expressive and diverse trajectories, rather than merely reconstructing or mimicking existing demonstrations. This implies the ability to synthesize novel, yet physically plausible, motions.
4. **Dynamics Coverage:** The policy must maintain task success and stability under varied physical properties and perturbations, making it robust to real-world dynamics and unexpected disturbances.

The core insight motivating InterPrior's approach is that a combination of large-scale imitation distillation and reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning is essential for achieving these objectives. Distillation alone provides a strong, natural starting point derived from extensive human demonstration data. However, it is inherently limited by the data distribution and can be brittle when faced with out-of-distribution states or goals. RL finetuning, when applied as a local optimizer that remains anchored to the pretrained model, can significantly improve robustness, enable exploration of unseen yet plausible configurations, and facilitate recovery from failure states, thereby transforming data reconstruction into a truly generalizable and robust policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InterPrior proposes a novel three-stage paradigm for learning a scalable, generative controller for physics-based human-object interaction: (I) Full-Reference Imitation Expert Training (InterMimic+), (II) Variational Distillation (InterPrior), and (III) Post-Training Beyond Reference via Reinforcement Learning (InterPrior). Each stage is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with consistent state, action, and goal representations.

**Policy States and Actions:**
The policy input `x_t` at time `t` comprises aggregated human kinematics (positions, orientations, velocities), object kinematics, and their interaction/contact states (signed distances to object surfaces, binary contacts). All continuous quantities are normalized in a human root-centric and local heading frame for invariance. Actions `a_t` are low-level joint position targets, converted to torques via PD control in the physics simulator (IsaacGym). Goals `G_t` are future desired human-object configurations, provided as masked residual encodings `˜y_t+k` (log-map for rotations, subtraction for Euclidean quantities) with binary masks `m_t+k` indicating visible components. Goals include short-horizon previews (set of offsets `K`) and a long-horizon snapshot (`L`).

**Stage I: InterMimic+: Full-Reference Imitation Expert**
This stage trains a "teacher" policy (`π_E`) for large-scale human-object co-tracking, building upon InterMimic.
- **Input:** `π_E` receives `x_t` and *dense, unmasked* future references `y_t`.
- **Training:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to maximize a composite reward `r = r_track × r_energy`, promoting alignment with reference and physically plausible behavior.
- **Improvements over original InterMimic:**
 - **Expanded Reference Scope:** To overcome brittleness under perturbation, episodes are initialized with random human-object pose variations. Sparse impulses are applied during rollouts, and object shapes and physical properties (mass density, CoM offsets, inertia, friction) are randomized. A termination penalty `r_ter` is introduced to discourage failure states.
 - **Reference-Free Hand Reward (`r_h`):** A novel reward term guides the hand to target and wrap around the *actual* object based on the current simulation state, rather than rigidly following reference trajectories. This helps maintain precision in grasping even when the simulation deviates from the reference. The full reward becomes `r_t = (r_track × r_energy × r_h) + r_ter`.

**Stage II: InterPrior: Variational Distillation**
The goal is to distill the powerful `π_E` into a variational policy (`π`) that operates with *sparse* goal cues while maintaining naturalness and diversity.
- **Model Architecture:** `π` consists of a Prior network (`p_ψ`), an Encoder (`q_ϕ`), and a Decoder (`f_θ`).
 - `p_ψ` (Transformer encoder): Takes recent history and sparse goals (`G_t`) to predict a latent skill distribution `N(μ_p, Σ_p)`.
 - `q_ϕ` (MLP): During training, it also takes full future reference `y_t:t+H` to form a residual posterior `N(μ_p + μ_q, Σ_q)`.
 - `f_θ` (MLP): Maps `x_t` and sampled latent `z_t` to action `a_t`. It also has an auxiliary head for masked goal reconstruction.
- **Key Designs:**
 - **Multi-modal Conditioning:** Goals are constructed via stochastic mask sampling, exposing the policy to diverse partial and sparse human, object, and contact features (body-wise, independent sampling, temporal consistency).
 - **Latent Bounding:** After sampling, `z_t` is projected onto a unit hypersphere (`z_t ← z_t / ||z_t||`). This stabilizes skill learning by limiting extreme latent draws while preserving directional variability.
 - **Online Distillation and Regularization:** A DAgger-like framework is used, gradually increasing student-driven rollouts. The policy is optimized with `L_total = L_ELBO + λ_scale L_scale + λ_tc L_tc`.
 - `L_ELBO` combines imitation loss (expert actions), goal reconstruction loss (completing masked goals), and KL regularization (posterior vs. prior).
 - `L_scale`: Regularizes prior mean `μ_p` to maintain unit magnitude.
 - `L_tc`: Promotes temporal consistency of the latent prior using 2-Wasserstein distance.

**Stage III: InterPrior: Post-Training Beyond Reference**
This stage finetunes the distilled policy (`π`) using RL to enhance robustness, handle unseen configurations, and learn recovery behaviors, addressing the brittleness of distillation alone.
- **RL as Local Optimizer:** RL finetuning acts as a local optimizer, expanding the policy's scope while remaining anchored to the naturalness encoded by the pretrained model.
- **In-Betweening for Finetuning:** The task is formulated as tracking from a randomly sampled initial configuration towards a single-frame goal (snapshot goal) from the dataset.
- **Finetuning Reward (`r_PT`):** `r_PT_t = (r_energy × r_h) + r_goal + r_ter`. `r_goal` is a sparse success signal activated when the masked feature distance to the target `˜y_t+L` falls below a threshold, avoiding dense distance-based rewards that might lead to unnatural motions.
- **Learning New Skills:**
 - **In-distribution extensions (e.g., regrasping):** Emerges naturally from goal-conditioned in-betweening with diverse initializations and perturbed states.
 - **Out-of-distribution skills (e.g., getting up):** Learned explicitly by appending a learnable token and adding auxiliary rewards for upright posture and center-of-mass elevation.
- **Prior Preservation:** Catastrophic forgetting is mitigated via a distributed training schedule. A subset of environments continues to optimize the original distillation objective, preserving pretrained knowledge, while others perform RL finetuning. Gradients are aggregated synchronously.
- **Inference-time Mask Prompt Engineering:** Lightweight, rule-based modifications to goal specifications `G_t` (e.g., simplifying distant goals, handling contradictory targets) are applied during inference to further enhance robustness without altering the trained policy.
- **Embodiment Flexibility:** The framework is validated on both the SMPL humanoid and the Unitree G1 robot, showcasing its potential for sim-to-sim and real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InterPrior's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated through quantitative and qualitative experiments across various human-object interaction tasks, comparing it against strong baselines and performing detailed ablation studies.

**Quantitative Results:**

1. **Full-Reference Tracking (InterMimic+ vs. InterMimic):**
 - InterPrior's underlying expert (InterMimic+) shows superior **Success Rate (SR)** on challenging thin-geometry interactions and under initialization noise compared to the original InterMimic.
 - While InterMimic achieves slightly lower **Human Position Error (E_h)** by strictly tracking the reference, InterMimic+ allows for minor, intentional deviations to correct hand-object alignment, leading to more stable grasps and higher task completion rates despite a marginally higher `E_h`. This highlights a strategic trade-off for overall task success.

2. **Sparse Goal Following (InterPrior vs. MaskedMimic & Ablations):**
 - InterPrior consistently outperforms MaskedMimic and its own pre-RL finetuning variants across all goal-conditioned tasks: snapshot, trajectory, contact, long-horizon multi-goal chaining, and random initialization.
 - The most significant gains are observed in **long-horizon multi-goal chaining** and **random-initialization stress tests**, where InterPrior shows dramatic increases in SR (e.g., Chain SR from 45.1% to 68.8%; Rand Init SR from 41.1% to 88.6%). This validates the efficacy of RL finetuning in improving robustness and generalization to out-of-distribution states.
 - **Ablation Study:**
 - **InterMimic+ Expert:** Replacing the original InterMimic expert with InterMimic+ (with expanded reference scope and reference-free rewards) yields initial improvements across tasks.
 - **Latent Shaping Loss:** Introduces modest gains on in-distribution tasks but shows clear benefits for long-horizon behaviors and under random initialization, confirming its role in mitigating latent space drift.
 - **Bounded Latent & Observations:** This step provides substantial improvements in SR and error metrics, particularly for contact goals and stress tests, demonstrating its crucial role in stabilizing skill learning by preventing unnatural behaviors from out-of-distribution latents.
 - **RL Finetuning:** This is the most impactful component, dramatically boosting robustness and recovery capabilities, especially in scenarios requiring generalization beyond training data.
 - **Trajectory Following Preservation:** Despite finetuning primarily on snapshot goals, trajectory following performance is well-preserved due to concurrent distillation loss (protecting trajectory-conditioned inputs) and implicit benefits from improved snapshot goal following (redefining snapshot goals when deviations occur).

**Qualitative Results:**

1. **Full-Reference Tracking (Figure 3):** InterMimic+ successfully grasps thin objects under perturbations, demonstrating its ability to adapt and correct hand-object alignment, unlike InterMimic which rigidly follows reference and often fails.
2. **Long-Horizon Tasks (Figures 1, 4):** InterPrior sustains minute-long whole-body interactions with multiple objects, exhibiting smooth transitions between diverse skills (approach, grasp, lift, reposition). Its self-correction capabilities prevent error compounding.
3. **Novel Objects and Interactions (Figures 5, 7):** The model demonstrates zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and interaction styles from datasets like BEHAVE and HODome, effectively completing tasks guided by sparse snapshot goals and recovering from dataset imperfections (e.g., contact artifacts). This showcases InterPrior's strong reusable prior capabilities.
4. **Sim-to-Sim Transfer (Figure 6):** InterPrior shows successful transfer from IsaacGym to MuJoCo for the G1 humanoid, maintaining coherent long-horizon interactions conditioned on object goals, suggesting real-world deployment potential.
5. **Diverse Behaviors (Figure B in Supplementary):** Given the same goal, InterPrior can generate multiple valid and distinct interaction trajectories, indicating a meaningful and expressive latent space.
6. **Integration with Kinematic HOI Generators (Figure C in Supplementary):** InterPrior effectively follows targets generated by kinematic HOI models (InterDiff), adaptively completing tasks even with sparse inputs, showing its flexibility.

**Failure Cases:**
Despite significant improvements, InterPrior exhibits limitations:
- Difficulty with extremely thin or elongated objects not present in training data.
- Partial goal completion in multi-goal chaining, particularly when canonicalization introduces large alignment discrepancies (the policy may prioritize balance over precise goal achievement).
- Occasional artifacts like shallow interpenetrations, foot skating, or object drops over very long rollouts.
- The current hand and contact representations are not optimized for fine-grained finger dexterity or in-hand manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InterPrior represents a significant advancement in the field of physics-based generative control for human-object interactions, offering a scalable and robust framework with broad implications across several domains.

**Significance:**

1. **Bridging the Gap:** The work successfully bridges the gap between high-fidelity imitation learning (which provides natural, human-like behaviors) and the robustness/generalization capabilities typically associated with reinforcement learning. This hybrid approach is a powerful paradigm for complex embodied AI tasks.
2. **Scalable Generative Control:** InterPrior provides a principled, three-stage recipe that scales a generative motion controller across four critical axes: task coverage, skill coverage, motion coverage, and dynamics coverage. This means it can handle diverse goals, a wide range of interaction skills, generate varied yet plausible motions, and maintain performance under physical perturbations.
3. **Enhanced Robustness and Competence:** By leveraging RL finetuning, InterPrior dramatically improves robustness to unseen configurations and initializations. It enables crucial behaviors like failure recovery (e.g., regrasping, getting up from falls), smooth transitions between skills, and stability under external perturbations, which are vital for real-world application.
4. **Reusable Prior for Novel Interactions:** The ability to generalize zero-shot to novel objects and interaction styles (as demonstrated with BEHAVE and HODome datasets), and even recover from data imperfections, establishes InterPrior as a powerful reusable prior for HOI. This makes it easier to adapt to new environments and learn additional skills incrementally.
5. **Embodiment Flexibility and Real-World Potential:** Demonstrating successful control on different humanoid embodiments (SMPL and Unitree G1) and showing sim-to-sim transfer (IsaacGym to MuJoCo) highlights its versatility and strong potential for deployment on physical humanoid robots and for real-time interactive control.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Robotics:** InterPrior offers a practical and powerful framework for developing general-purpose humanoid loco-manipulation controllers. This could accelerate progress in assistive robotics, teleoperation, and human-robot collaboration, allowing humanoids to perform complex tasks in unstructured environments with high-level human guidance.
- **Computer Graphics and Animation:** The ability to generate physically plausible, diverse, and robust human-object interactions from sparse goals can revolutionize character animation for games, virtual reality, and film. It allows animators to specify high-level intent, with the system handling the intricate low-level physical details, leading to more realistic and dynamic virtual worlds.
- **Embodied AI Research:** InterPrior's methodology provides a blueprint for building "behavior foundation models" for embodied agents. By pretraining on vast interaction data and then selectively finetuning, future agents could acquire a broad repertoire of skills that are both natural and adaptable.
- **Interactive Systems:** The demonstrated interactive steering control capability opens doors for intuitive control interfaces where users guide complex humanoid behaviors through high-level commands, offloading the burden of micromanagement.

**Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations and suggest promising avenues for future work, including integrating perception (e.g., vision-language models for more semantic goals), incorporating richer affordance understanding, enhancing fine-grained finger dexterity for complex in-hand manipulation, expanding dataset diversity and quality, and exploring ways to simplify or unify the current three-stage training scheme.

**Societal and Ethical Considerations:**
The paper responsibly touches upon the societal and ethical implications of developing highly capable humanoid controllers. While offering immense benefits, such technologies also raise concerns regarding their deployment in unsafe settings or for applications that could conflict with societal norms (e.g., surveillance). The authors encourage careful consideration of safety mechanisms, usage policies, and ethical guidelines, emphasizing the importance of responsible development and deployment of advanced humanoid capabilities.

In conclusion, InterPrior represents a robust and scalable solution that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in physics-based human-object interaction, paving the way for more capable and adaptable humanoid agents in both virtual and physical worlds.
