# Micro-Macro Retrieval: Reducing Long-Form Hallucination in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Micro–Macro Retrieval (M2^22R), enhances factual consistency in large language models by integrating external evidence retrieval with dynamic, fine-grained internal reuse of key facts from a model-managed repository. This method significantly improves accuracy and reduces hallucination in long-form generation by ensuring critical evidence is positioned near the generated output.

## Problem

- Large Language Models frequently generate factually incorrect content, a problem intensified in complex, long-form generation tasks.
- LLMs often fail to effectively utilize crucial evidence embedded within lengthy retrieved contexts or forget intermediate reasoning steps over long generation chains, a phenomenon termed "Lost in Lengthy Contexts."
- Current Retrieval-Augmented Language Models (RALMs) do not explicitly ensure key factual evidence is positioned in close proximity to the generated output, diminishing factual accuracy in extended responses.

## Method

- A ==retrieve-while-generate== framework, Micro–Macro Retrieval (M2^22R), integrates coarse-grained external retrieval (macro retrieval) with fine-grained internal retrieval (micro retrieval) from a model-constructed key information repository.
- During reasoning, the LLM identifies and saves "answer-aligned" facts into an internal repository; during generation, it queries this repository on-demand to insert key information directly before corresponding output tokens.
- Reinforcement learning, guided by curriculum learning and rule-based reward functions, trains the LLM to stably acquire skills for effective information retrieval, key information saving, and precise grounding of generated text.

## Results

- M2^22R demonstrated superior answer correctness on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle, for instance, achieving **44.11%** Exact Match and **65.98%** LLM-as-a-Judge on HotpotQA with Qwen-**7B**-Instruct.
- The framework effectively reduced hallucination and maintained stable accuracy in long-context, multi-question scenarios (e.g., HotpotQA-2Q, 3Q), where other baselines showed rapid decline.
- An ablation study confirmed the critical role of the retrieve-while-generate mechanism of micro retrieval, showing that one-shot grounding of all key information led to weaker factual consistency.
- M2^22R incurs a low inference overhead, increasing latency by less than **10%** on average compared to baselines, while the key-information repository storage is negligible (50-150 tokens).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ensuring explicit proximity between critical factual evidence and the specific output tokens it grounds is vital for mitigating hallucination in long-form generation.
> - A retrieve-while-generate paradigm with two distinct retrieval levels—coarse-grained external and fine-grained internal from a model-managed repository—effectively addresses the "Lost in Lengthy Contexts" issue.
> - Curriculum learning is necessary for stable and effective training of the LLM to acquire complex skills for multi-level retrieval, key information storage, and precise factual grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities across a range of tasks, including complex reasoning and generation. Despite this progress, LLMs are known to be susceptible to factual hallucination, which is the generation of incorrect but plausible content. This issue is particularly pronounced in long-form generation tasks, where the integration of extensive retrieved contexts and lengthy reasoning chains can amplify factual errors.

Existing research efforts to mitigate hallucination primarily fall into categories such as hallucination detection, approaches that stimulate the LLM's internal knowledge, and Retrieval-Augmented Language Models (RALMs). RALMs integrate external knowledge sources to complement the parametric memory of LLMs, aiming to enhance factual accuracy and reduce reliance on potentially outdated or incomplete internal knowledge. While RALMs have shown effectiveness in reducing hallucination, they encounter specific challenges in long-form generation.

A significant challenge, termed "Lost in Lengthy Contexts," arises from the difficulty LLMs face in utilizing key evidence that is obscured within long retrieved passages or forgotten over extended reasoning chains. Empirical studies have indicated a correlation between the factual accuracy of LLM outputs and the proximity of key information to the generated tokens. That is, information located closer to the output tends to be more faithfully incorporated. However, current RALM frameworks, despite employing multi-turn retrieval to inject external evidence into the reasoning process, do not possess explicit mechanisms to ensure this critical proximity. External knowledge is introduced, but its strategic placement relative to the final answer is not guaranteed, allowing key facts to become diluted or overlooked.

This work addresses this gap by introducing a framework that explicitly manages the positioning of critical evidence. It distinguishes itself from prior multi-turn retrieval frameworks like ReAct and Self-RAG by operating over an internal, model-generated key-information repository, rather than solely external documents. Furthermore, it explicitly enforces evidence proximity to the generated output tokens, a constraint not present in these earlier methods, thereby aiming to counteract the "long-context drift" that contributes to hallucination. By focusing on both the retrieval of coarse-grained external evidence and the strategic reuse of fine-grained internal facts, the research positions itself as an advancement in ensuring factual consistency and reducing hallucination in complex, knowledge-intensive generation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reduce factual hallucination in Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically within the context of long-form generation tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several more specific objectives and motivations.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

- **Mitigate Hallucination in Long-Form Generation:** To develop a robust method capable of significantly reducing the incidence of factually incorrect outputs generated by LLMs, especially when producing extensive text.
- **Address the "Lost in Lengthy Contexts" Problem:** To tackle the dual challenge where crucial factual evidence becomes difficult for LLMs to utilize effectively due to its entanglement within verbose retrieved documents, and where intermediate reasoning results are forgotten over long reasoning chains.
- **Ensure Key-Information-to-Output Proximity:** To create a mechanism that guarantees the relevant factual evidence is positioned in close proximity to the specific parts of the generated output that it is intended to ground. This objective is directly motivated by empirical observations indicating that closer proximity improves factual accuracy.
- **Integrate Multi-Level Retrieval:** To design a novel retrieve-while-generate framework that effectively combines coarse-grained external retrieval (macro retrieval) with fine-grained internal retrieval (micro retrieval) from a model-constructed knowledge repository.
- **Stable Acquisition of Retrieval and Grounding Skills:** To develop a reinforcement learning strategy, supported by curriculum learning and customized reward functions, that enables LLMs to stably acquire and refine the abilities required for both retrieving information and grounding their generations in that information.

**Motivation:**
- **Prevalence and Exacerbation of Hallucination:** Despite their impressive capabilities, LLMs frequently produce factually incorrect content. This problem is exacerbated in long-form generation tasks, where the complexity of integrating information from multiple sources and maintaining coherence over extended outputs increases the likelihood of factual errors.
- **Limitations of Existing RALMs:** While Retrieval-Augmented Language Models (RALMs) have improved factual grounding by incorporating external knowledge, they have not fully resolved the hallucination issue in lengthy outputs. A critical deficiency is their inability to ensure that key evidence, once retrieved, remains salient and accessible to the model precisely when it is needed for specific parts of the answer.
- **Empirical Evidence for Proximity:** Recent studies, along with theoretical considerations from a positional encoding perspective (e.g., Rotary Position Embeddings), highlight that the effective utilization of information by LLMs is highly dependent on its position relative to the generated output. Information "lost in the middle" of a long context is often overlooked, underscoring the necessity for a mechanism that actively brings critical facts closer to the point of generation.
- **Need for Internal Knowledge Management:** Current multi-turn retrieval systems often focus solely on external documents. There is a recognized need for models to not only retrieve external facts but also to strategically manage and reuse internally generated or identified key information throughout a multi-step reasoning process, preventing the forgetting of earlier intermediate results.
- **Enhancing Trustworthiness and Utility:** By addressing these limitations, the research aims to enhance the factual consistency and reliability of LLM outputs, thereby increasing their trustworthiness and practical utility in applications requiring high factual accuracy, such as scientific summarization, complex question answering, and report generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes Micro–Macro Retrieval (M$^2$R), a retrieve-while-generate framework designed to enhance factual consistency in long-form generation. The methodology integrates a two-level retrieval process within a reinforcement learning (RL) training paradigm, guided by curriculum learning and custom rewards.

**Framework Architecture: Micro–Macro Retrieval (M$^2$R)**
M$^2$R operates in two distinct but interconnected phases:
1. **Macro Retrieval (Reasoning Phase):** During this initial phase, the LLM engages in multi-turn reasoning and retrieves coarse-grained evidence from external knowledge sources. This process is initiated through `<macro_tool_call>` tags. Crucially, as the reasoning progresses and the model identifies "answer-aligned" evidence (i.e., facts directly relevant to the final answer), it explicitly saves this information into a structured internal "key information repository" using a `<key_info_save>` tag. The detection and storage of this key information are performed directly by the model.
2. **Micro Retrieval (Answer Phase):** Once the model transitions to the answer generation phase, it leverages a novel micro retrieval mechanism. This mechanism allows the model to query the previously built key information repository using `<micro_tool_call>` tags. The extracted essential results are then returned within `<micro_response>` tags and directly used to ground the final output. This on-demand retrieval ensures that key information is inserted immediately before the corresponding output tokens, explicitly enforcing proximity between evidence and generation.

**Reinforcement Learning with M$^2$R**
The framework employs a curriculum learning–based reinforcement learning strategy to train the LLM.
- **RL Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is utilized. GRPO estimates a baseline from a group of rollouts, thereby not requiring an explicit value critic like PPO.
- **Staged Policy Execution:** The M$^2$R policy is formulated as a sequential composition of the thinking and answering phases: `π_answer(· | x, M; R_micro) ◦ π_think(· | x; R_macro)`. This means the model first executes the `<think>` phase, collecting macro retrieval results (`R_macro`) and building the key information repository (`M`), then proceeds to the `<answer>` phase, utilizing micro retrieval results (`R_micro`) from `M`.
- **Rollout Structure:** Rollouts explicitly include retrieval calls. In the `<think>` phase, the policy calls macro retrieval tools and uses `<key_info_save>` to store facts. In the `<answer>` phase, it calls micro retrieval tools and conditions decoding on the returned values to keep key information proximal to output tokens.
- **Retrieval Result Masking:** To ensure accurate credit assignment, the policy loss calculation explicitly masks out tokens corresponding to retrieval results (which are injected by the environment). Gradients are computed only for policy-generated tokens, such as reasoning text and retrieval queries.
- **GRPO Optimization Scope:** GRPO optimizes the LLM's behavior regarding *when* and *how* to invoke retrieval, compose tool calls, save key information, and integrate retrieved data into the answer. The underlying retrieval module itself is not modified by GRPO, rendering M$^2$R compatible with various external retrieval systems.

**Training Template**
A structured prompt template guides the model's interaction, specifying the use of `<macro_tool_call>`, `<key_info_save>`, `<micro_tool_call>`, and `<micro_response>` tags to delineate reasoning, information storage, and answer generation steps. The final answer is required to be directly grounded on micro retrieval results, with key values enclosed in `\boxed{}`.

**Reward Modeling**
A rule-based reward function optimizes the policy:
- **Format Reward:** Penalizes deviations from the specified tag usage and `\boxed{}` enclosure.
- **Answer Reward:** A composite score based on F1-scores, including:
 - **Final Answer Correctness (s_final):** Measures agreement between the model's boxed output and ground truth.
 - **Key Information Correctness (s_key):** Assesses alignment of stored key information in the repository with ground truth.
 - **Consistency Score (s_cons):** Evaluates alignment between the stored key information and the final output.
 - The total answer reward `r_ans = s_final + α s_key + β s_cons` (with `α=1/3`, `β=1/10`) contributes to the overall reward `r`.

**Curriculum Learning for Training Stability**
To address the complexity of simultaneously training macro retrieval, key information saving, and micro retrieval, a two-stage curriculum learning approach is adopted:
1. **Stage 1:** Focuses on training the model to perform macro retrieval and correctly save key information into the repository.
2. **Stage 2:** Introduces micro retrieval and fine-grained answer grounding, enabling the model to effectively utilize the stored key information for final answer generation.
This staged training is designed to simplify the learning process, improve stability, and ensure progressive skill acquisition.

**Implementation Details**
M$^2$R was implemented on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models. Training was conducted on the MuSiQue training split. The retrieval environment utilized the FlashRAG toolkit, with E5-base-v2 as the dense retriever and a December 2018 Wikipedia snapshot as the knowledge base. For each query, the top-5 passages were retrieved.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of M$^2$R involved extensive tests on multi-hop question answering benchmarks, comparing its performance against a range of strong baselines.

**RQ 1: Answer Correctness**
M$^2$R consistently demonstrated superior performance in answer correctness across all evaluated multi-hop QA benchmarks: HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle. It surpassed strong baselines, including Naive RAG, Iter-RetGen, IRCoT, COFT, SURE, and notably, ReSearch, which is a reinforcement learning-based framework for multi-turn search. For instance, on HotpotQA, M$^2$R-Qwen-7B-Instruct achieved an Exact Match (EM) of 44.11% and an LLM-as-a-Judge (LJ) score of 65.98%, outperforming ReSearch's 43.52% EM and 63.62% LJ. Similar gains were observed across other datasets and with the Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model. These results indicate that the explicit mechanism of re-accessing and grounding answers with key information through micro retrieval contributes to enhanced factual accuracy in the final output.

**RQ 2: Hallucination Reduction in Long-Context Scenarios**
To evaluate M$^2$R's robustness in challenging long-context scenarios, experiments were conducted on HotpotQA with concatenated questions (HotpotQA-2Q and HotpotQA-3Q), requiring the model to answer multiple questions jointly. In these settings, which amplify reasoning depth and contextual redundancy, M$^2$R consistently outperformed all baselines. While methods like Naive RAG and ReSearch experienced a rapid decline in accuracy (indicating increased hallucination) as context length increased, M$^2$R maintained more stable accuracy and significantly lower hallucination rates. This resilience is attributed to its retrieve-while-generate paradigm, which continuously re-anchors key evidence near the outputs via micro retrieval, proving particularly effective in high-redundancy, long-context situations. Additional experiments on 3Q and 5Q settings across all benchmarks further confirmed M$^2$R's largest gains in these extended reasoning chains.

**RQ 3: Ablation Study – Importance of Retrieve-while-Generate Design**
An ablation study was conducted to ascertain the contribution of the retrieve-while-generate mechanism inherent in micro retrieval. A variant of M$^2$R, "One-shot Grounding," was tested, where all saved key information was provided to the model at once in the answer phase, without on-demand micro retrieval during generation. Results showed that one-shot grounding led to weaker factual consistency compared to the full M$^2$R framework. This suggests that injecting all evidence simultaneously can lead to dilution by redundant reasoning tokens. Conversely, M$^2$R's ability to selectively fetch and insert relevant key information precisely where needed during generation is critical for stable performance and hallucination mitigation in long-form tasks.

**RQ 4: Reward Dynamics during Reinforcement Learning**
Analysis of reward curves during reinforcement learning training showed differences in adaptation speed between model sizes. The Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model exhibited a sharper initial increase in reward, indicating faster adaptation to the retrieve-while-generate paradigm. However, the Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model's reward growth gradually caught up, with both models eventually converging to similar levels. This suggests that while larger models might adapt more quickly in early stages, the long-term learning dynamics between different model scales tend to align.

**RQ 5: Case Study**
A case study exemplified M$^2$R's operational flow. It demonstrated how the model, in its `<think>` phase, decomposes a question, uses `<macro_tool_call>` for external search, and then explicitly preserves answer-aligned facts (e.g., "finalPayableAmount": "180.0", "RoomNumber": "301") via `<key_info_save>`. In the subsequent `<answer>` phase, the model retrieves these stored values using `<micro_tool_call>` and incorporates them as boxed text directly into the final output. This illustrates M$^2$R's capacity to manage essential evidence and ground predictions by placing supporting evidence close to the generated answer.

**RQ 6: Inference Cost and Efficiency**
The analysis of inference-time overhead revealed that M$^2$R introduces a minimal increase in computational cost. The majority of model invocations stem from the `<think>` (macro retrieval) phase, a cost shared by most multi-turn RAG frameworks. M$^2$R adds approximately 1-2 micro-retrieval calls per query, translating to a relative increase of 20-30%. Micro retrieval itself is a lightweight, rule-based lookup within a small, local repository. End-to-end latency benchmarks showed that M$^2$R increases inference time by less than 10% on average compared to ReSearch (e.g., 4.7s vs. 4.3s for HotpotQA), while delivering notable improvements in accuracy. The storage cost for the key-information repository was also found to be negligible, typically between 50-150 tokens. Latency scaling experiments demonstrated approximately linear growth with reasoning complexity in multi-question settings.

**Additional Findings (Appendix E.4): Importance of Curriculum Learning**
A supplementary ablation study highlighted the critical role of curriculum learning. Direct joint optimization of macro and micro retrieval resulted in significantly poorer performance, even trailing behind a Naive RAG baseline. This indicated that attempting to train all components simultaneously led to unstable rollouts and optimization difficulties. In contrast, the two-stage curriculum learning approach, by simplifying the learning task and enabling progressive skill acquisition, dramatically stabilized training and led to superior end-to-end performance, validating its necessity for M$^2$R's effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Micro–Macro Retrieval (M$^2$R) as a contribution to the field of large language models, particularly in addressing the persistent challenge of factual hallucination in long-form generation.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Directly Addresses "Lost in Lengthy Contexts":** M$^2$R offers a principled solution to the "Lost in Lengthy Contexts" problem, a recognized limitation in existing Retrieval-Augmented Language Models (RALMs). By explicitly managing the retention and proximity of key evidence, it overcomes issues where crucial facts are obscured or forgotten during extended reasoning and generation processes.
- **Novel Retrieve-While-Generate Mechanism:** The introduction of a novel retrieve-while-generate mechanism during the answer phase, which operates on model-generated internal knowledge, represents an advancement in how LLMs can dynamically ground their outputs. This differs from prior methods that primarily retrieve from external sources, enabling a more integrated and controlled use of relevant facts.
- **Empirical and Theoretical Validation of Proximity:** The work provides empirical evidence and theoretical justification (via positional encoding analysis) for the importance of key-information proximity to output tokens. M$^2$R operationalizes this insight by ensuring that supporting evidence is positioned immediately prior to the generated tokens it grounds, thereby enhancing factual reliability.
- **Robust Performance in Challenging Scenarios:** M$^2$R consistently demonstrates substantial improvements in factual consistency and hallucination reduction, especially in challenging long-context and multi-question scenarios where the complexity typically exacerbates LLM errors. This indicates an increased robustness of the model's reasoning and generation capabilities.
- **Stable Training Paradigm:** The development of a curriculum learning–based reinforcement learning strategy with customized rule-based rewards provides a stable and effective method for training complex multi-level retrieval and grounding skills. This addresses the inherent difficulty in optimizing such integrated systems directly.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced LLM Trustworthiness:** The reduction in hallucination, particularly in long-form generation, can significantly improve the trustworthiness and reliability of LLMs for real-world applications. This is critical for domains where factual accuracy is paramount, such as scientific research, legal analysis, medical documentation, and journalistic reporting.
- **Advancements in RAG Paradigms:** M$^2$R introduces a new dimension to Retrieval-Augmented Generation by focusing on the strategic *placement* and *reuse* of knowledge, beyond just the initial retrieval. This could inspire further research into dynamic evidence management and grounding mechanisms in RAG systems.
- **Broader Applicability for Knowledge-Intensive Tasks:** By offering a more reliable method for handling complex, knowledge-intensive queries, M$^2$R could broaden the applicability of LLMs in tasks requiring detailed reasoning, synthesis of multiple facts, and generation of extensive, accurate responses.
- **Foundation for Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies several avenues for future work, including the integration of learned reward models, dynamic optimization of evidence-output proximity, and extension to richer tool use, diverse external sources, and multimodal capabilities. This suggests M$^2$R could serve as a foundational framework for further innovations in LLM grounding and hallucination mitigation.
- **Insights into LLM Mechanisms:** The findings regarding the "lost in the middle" phenomenon and the effectiveness of proximity also offer further insights into how LLMs process and utilize information, potentially informing future architectural designs or training methodologies.
