# When Vision Overrides Language: Evaluating and Mitigating Counterfactual Failures in VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNC Chapel Hill identify and address "counterfactual failures" in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, where visual cues override language instructions. They introduce LIBERO-CF, a benchmark to evaluate this issue, and propose Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG), an inference-time method that improves faithful instruction following by up to 15.5% in grounding rate and 8.5% in success rate in simulation, demonstrating consistent real-world improvements.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often fail to faithfully follow natural language instructions when tasks deviate from common visually-driven behaviors, leading to "counterfactual failures."
- This limitation stems from vision-biased robotic datasets and modality imbalance within VLA architectures, causing language's influence on action selection to be marginalized.
- A systematic evaluation benchmark and a general mitigation strategy for these counterfactual failures in robotic manipulation tasks were previously underexplored.

## Method

- The authors developed **LIBERO-CF**, the first ==counterfactual benchmark== for VLAs, which extends the LIBERO dataset with alternative feasible language instructions to systematically diagnose vision shortcuts.
- They proposed **CAG** (==Counterfactual Action Guidance==), an inference-time ==dual-branch scheme== that enhances language conditioning by linearly combining outputs from a conditional VLA policy and a language-unconditioned Vision-Action (VA) policy.
- CAG can be implemented either through a ==training-free strategy== or by training a separate VA prior, offering a plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic solution without requiring model retraining or architectural modifications.

## Results

- Evaluated VLAs, such as **OpenVLA-OFT**, demonstrated severe counterfactual failures, achieving only a **0.4%** success rate on counterfactual tasks on LIBERO-CF, while performing at **78.6%** on original training tasks in the same settings.
- In simulation, **CAG** (VA variant) consistently improved language grounding and success rates on counterfactual tasks (e.g., for π_0.5, grounding increased from **30.8%** to **46.3%** and success from **13.2%** to **21.7%**).
- Real-world experiments validated CAG's effectiveness, improving average success rates across various language grounding dimensions (e.g., **13.3%** for object recognition, **36.7%** for goal targeting) and enhancing zero-shot generalization to unseen objects without degrading in-domain performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art VLAs exhibit severe "vision shortcuts," relying predominantly on visual cues, with language serving more as a secondary conditioning signal than a primary driver of action.
> - Training-task objects in a scene act as potent visual attractors, causing VLAs to disproportionately center actions around them, leading to counterfactual failures on novel or under-observed instructions.
> - Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG) effectively re-balances the influence of vision and language, enabling VLAs to follow instructions more faithfully and generalize better to novel objects and tasks, even in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a rapidly evolving frontier in robotics, aiming to create generalist robotic agents capable of understanding and executing complex natural language instructions within diverse physical environments. This field has seen impressive advancements, largely driven by the integration of powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the availability of large-scale robotic datasets, such as the Open-X-Embodiment and LIBERO benchmarks. These developments promise to equip robots with rich semantic reasoning capabilities, allowing them to interpret human commands and perform manipulation tasks with unprecedented versatility.

However, despite these successes, the paper identifies and addresses a critical, yet underexplored, failure mode in VLAs: "counterfactual failures" or "vision shortcuts." This phenomenon occurs when VLAs, faced with instructions that are visually plausible but deviate from frequently observed training tasks, prioritize visual cues from the scene over the given language instruction. Instead of faithfully executing the novel command, the robot defaults to well-learned, scene-specific behaviors or targets objects heavily featured during training. This poses a significant challenge to the reliability and trustworthiness of VLAs, as it undermines their ability to follow user intent, even in familiar settings.

Existing research has touched upon related issues such as linguistic robustness and modality imbalance. Works like LIBERO-PLUS have investigated robustness to linguistic rephrasing within the same task distribution, while others like CAST and CounterfactualVLA have explored data augmentation techniques to mitigate counterfactual biases, primarily in navigation and driving domains. However, a systematic study, a dedicated benchmark, and a generalized solution for counterfactual failures in the context of robotic manipulation VLAs have been largely absent.

This paper fills this crucial gap by:
1. **Systematically diagnosing the problem:** Providing empirical evidence and a theoretical framework (Bayesian perspective) for why vision shortcuts occur due to modality imbalance in datasets and architectural biases.
2. **Introducing a novel benchmark:** Developing LIBERO-CF, the first counterfactual benchmark specifically designed to evaluate language following in VLAs under controlled counterfactual settings. This allows for rigorous assessment of how well models distinguish visually plausible yet linguistically distinct instructions.
3. **Proposing a universal solution:** Presenting Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG), an inference-time technique that aims to explicitly re-balance the influence of vision and language, thereby enhancing language conditioning without requiring architectural modifications or additional data collection.

By addressing the pervasive issue of counterfactual failures, this research contributes significantly to improving the safety, usability, and generalizability of VLAs, pushing the boundaries towards more intelligent and reliable embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a critical observation: despite their impressive capabilities, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models frequently fail to faithfully follow natural language instructions, particularly in situations where the instruction deviates from the most common or "well-learned" behaviors associated with a given visual scene. This failure mode, termed "counterfactual failure," manifests as VLAs disregarding explicit language commands and instead defaulting to visually-driven "shortcuts" acquired during training. Such behavior severely limits the reliability and trustworthiness of VLAs for real-world deployment, as it means robots cannot consistently act according to user intent.

The authors attribute this limitation to two main factors:
1. **Vision Bias in Robotic Datasets:** Robotic manipulation datasets are often task-specific and visually dominant. Under a fixed scene, demonstrations are typically collected for only a small subset of possible tasks. This encourages VLAs to learn strong associations between visual observations and specific actions, relying on visual cues rather than fully grounding language.
2. **Modality Imbalance in Architectures:** In many VLA architectures, visual tokens vastly outnumber language tokens. While VLAs often leverage powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the alignment between vision, language, and action modalities can be insufficient, leading to language's influence being marginalized in action selection.

Given this prevalent and underexplored problem, the paper sets forth two key objectives:

1. **Systematically Evaluate Language Following Capabilities and Counterfactual Failures:** The first objective is to rigorously quantify the extent of counterfactual failures in state-of-the-art VLAs. This involves developing a specialized evaluation methodology that can identify when a VLA ignores instructions and falls back on vision shortcuts. This systematic study aims to highlight the severity and ubiquity of this problem across different VLA architectures and task types.
2. **Develop a General and Effective Mitigation Strategy:** The second objective is to propose a practical solution, Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG), that can enhance language conditioning in VLAs and mitigate counterfactual failures. This solution is designed to be universal, compatible with diverse VLA architectures, and applicable without requiring modifications to existing model weights or extensive re-training, thereby offering a plug-and-play approach for improving VLA robustness.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to provide both a clearer understanding of a critical limitation in current VLAs and a practical method to address it, ultimately paving the way for more reliable and truly language-guided robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research methodology is structured around two main components: a novel benchmark for evaluating counterfactual failures and a proposed guidance mechanism for mitigating them.

**A. LIBERO-CF Benchmark Design**
To systematically study language-following capabilities and diagnose counterfactual failures, the authors introduce **LIBERO-CF**, the first counterfactual benchmark for VLAs. This benchmark extends the widely used LIBERO robotic manipulation dataset by assigning *alternative feasible language instructions* under existing LIBERO scene layouts. These counterfactual instructions represent tasks that are visually plausible but were under-observed or entirely unseen during the original VLA finetuning.

LIBERO-CF comprises four distinct suites:
- **CF-Spatial (15 tasks):** Tests spatial language grounding by instructing the robot to interact with objects that were previously only background elements.
- **CF-Object (10 tasks):** Evaluates object-centric instructions targeting different instances of objects in the scene.
- **CF-Long (10 tasks):** Focuses on long-horizon tasks, requiring multi-step instructions involving new target objects or sequences.
- **CF-OOD (15 tasks):** Assesses generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios by including entirely unseen objects not present during VLA finetuning.

The evaluation metrics include:
- **Grounding Rate:** Measures whether the gripper makes contact with the target object specified in the instruction, reflecting faithful language following.
- **Success Rate:** A stricter metric, measuring whether the robot successfully completes the entire task.
Additionally, the evaluation distinguishes between "Faithful" executions (following the instructed task) and "Biased" executions (defaulting to the original training task).

To validate the presence of vision shortcuts, the authors conduct several analyses:
- **Ablation of Input Modalities:** Evaluating VLA performance under standard (vision + language), vision-only, and language-only inputs.
- **Grasping Distributions:** Visualizing grasp positions under different instructions to observe tendencies towards training-task objects.
- **Impact of Training-task Objects:** Creating a "CF-Focused" variant by physically removing training-task objects from the scene to assess their biasing effect.

**B. Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG)**
To mitigate the identified counterfactual failures, the paper proposes **Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG)**, an inference-time dual-branch scheme inspired by Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) from generative models.

The core idea of CAG is to enhance the influence of language conditioning by linearly combining the outputs of two policies:
1. **Conditional Policy (π_cond):** A standard VLA policy that predicts actions given both visual observations (`o`) and a language instruction (`l`), i.e., `π(a | o, l)`.
2. **Unconditional Policy (π_uncond):** A language-unconditioned Vision-Action (VA) policy that predicts actions based solely on visual observations, i.e., `π(a | o, ∅)`.

The CAG policy is defined as:
`π_CAG(a | o, l) = π_uncond(a | o, ∅) + ω * [π_cond(a | o, l) - π_uncond(a | o, ∅)]`
where `ω` is a guidance scale that controls the strength of language conditioning. Intuitively, this amplifies the "language-induced shift" in action preference, effectively reweighting the action distribution to favor actions that are more consistent with the language instruction. From a Bayesian perspective, this corresponds to sharpening the language likelihood `P(l | a, o)` in the posterior action distribution `P(a | o, l) ∝ P(a | o) · P(l | a, o)^ω`.

CAG offers two implementation options for obtaining the unconditional policy (`π_uncond`):
1. **Training-Free Strategy (TF):** This approach directly uses a standard trained VLA model to approximate both policies. The language-conditioned VLA serves as `π_cond`, and an approximate `π_uncond` is derived by simply dropping the language input at inference time.
2. **Training a Vision-Action Prior (VA):** For stronger and more stable performance, a separate Vision-Action (VA) model is explicitly trained on the LIBERO dataset, *without* any language input. This VA model provides a cleaner, vision-only prior, which is then paired with the language-conditioned VLA within the CAG framework.

Crucially, CAG is designed to be plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic, and requires no modifications to existing model architectures, pre-trained weights, or additional demonstrations (beyond possibly training a dedicated VA model), making it a highly practical solution.

The methodology encompasses extensive simulation experiments on the LIBERO-CF benchmark using state-of-the-art VLAs (OpenVLA-OFT, π_0, π_0.5, X-VLA), as well as real-world experiments on a Franka Research 3 robot arm, to validate its effectiveness across various language grounding dimensions and task complexities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings regarding counterfactual failures in VLAs and the effectiveness of the proposed Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG).

**A. Validation of Vision Shortcuts and Prevalence of Counterfactual Failures:**
- **Dominance of Vision:** Ablation studies reveal that all evaluated VLAs (OpenVLA-OFT, π_0, π_0.5) primarily rely on visual cues. They maintain high performance even with vision-only inputs but collapse to near-zero success rates when only language is provided. This highlights that language acts more as a secondary conditioning signal rather than a primary driver of action.
- **Biased Grasping Distributions:** Visualizations of grasping positions show that under counterfactual or even empty instructions, VLAs disproportionately center their actions around objects associated with well-learned training tasks, rather than the objects specified in the new instructions.
- **Impact of Training-Task Objects:** Removing the training-task objects from the scene (CF-Focused suite) drastically improves the success rates on counterfactual instructions (average grounding increase of 25.4%, success increase of 20.0%). This provides strong evidence that these objects act as potent visual attractors, causing vision shortcuts.
- **Severity on LIBERO-CF:** All state-of-the-art VLAs exhibit severe counterfactual failures on the LIBERO-CF benchmark. For instance, OpenVLA-OFT achieves only a 4.7% grounding rate and 0.4% success rate on counterfactual tasks, while still performing highly (83.6% grounding, 78.6% success) on the original training tasks in the same counterfactual settings. This disparity underscores a profound inability to follow novel instructions when visually biased.

**B. Effectiveness of Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG) in Simulation:**
- **Consistent Improvements:** Both the Training-Free (TF) and Vision-Action (VA) variants of CAG consistently improve "Faithful" grounding and success rates on counterfactual tasks across all evaluated VLAs and LIBERO-CF suites. Simultaneously, they reduce "Biased" execution towards original training tasks.
- **Quantitative Gains:** For π_0.5, CAG (VA variant) improves the average grounding rate from 30.8% to 46.3% and average success rate from 13.2% to 21.7%. These improvements are coupled with significant reductions in biased execution (e.g., 13.4% reduction in biased grounding, 24.7% reduction in biased success for π_0.5).
- **Unlocking Generalization:** CAG, particularly with a VA prior, helps unlock the generalization capabilities of VLAs. For example, π_0.5 with VA achieves 36.4% grounding and 10.3% success on CF-OOD tasks, which involve entirely unseen objects.
- **VA vs. TF:** The VA strategy generally yields stronger and more stable improvements than the TF strategy, indicating that an explicitly trained vision-only prior provides a more reliable counterfactual correction.

**C. Effectiveness of CAG in Real-World Experiments:**
- **Across Diverse Scenarios:** Real-world evaluations using π_0.5 and CAG (VA variant) demonstrate consistent improvements across various dimensions of language grounding:
 - **Object Recognition:** CAG improves average success rates by 13.3% in both Coke/Sprite/Fanta and Tape/Mustard/Pringles scenes, overcoming visual shortcuts even for simple object differentiation.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** CAG mitigates failures in spatially differentiated tasks (e.g., Middle/Left/Right cups, Table/Plate/Bowl corn cans), improving grounding by 16.6% and task success by 13.3%.
 - **Goal Targeting:** CAG significantly improves goal execution, increasing task success by 36.7% for tasks like Stack/Plate/Basket, where π_0.5 often failed to release objects at the correct goal.
 - **OOD Generalization:** For unseen objects like Cube and Basketball, CAG reduces counterfactual failures and further unlocks zero-shot generalization capabilities.
 - **Long-Horizon Reasoning (Appendix):** CAG enhances performance in multi-step tasks like Move and Pour and Apple and Banana, preventing the robot from reverting to memorized sequences or ignoring instructions about task order and cardinality.
- **Preservation of In-Domain Performance:** Importantly, CAG achieves these improvements on under-observed tasks without degrading performance on the well-learned original training tasks.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Training Strategies for CAG:** Explicitly training a separate VA model (R4/R5) is more effective than using language dropout during VLA training (R2) or simply using a standard VLA as both conditional and unconditional policies (R3). Dropout-based training was found to degrade instruction conditioning for VLAs.
- **Guidance Scale (ω):** Increasing the guidance scale effectively strengthens language conditioning and improves grounding. However, excessively large scales can lead to "over-guidance," degrading overall task success. Optimal `ω` values vary between VLAs (e.g., `ω=1.5` for π_0.5, `ω=3.0` for OpenVLA-OFT).

In summary, the research clearly validates the existence and severity of vision shortcuts and counterfactual failures in modern VLAs. It then provides CAG as a robust, universal, and practical inference-time solution that significantly enhances language conditioning, leading to more faithful instruction following and improved task success in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant importance for the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, addressing a fundamental limitation in the current generation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The findings and proposed solution have several profound implications:

1. **Enhancing Reliability and Trustworthiness of VLAs:** Counterfactual failures, where robots prioritize ingrained visual cues over explicit language instructions, pose a major roadblock to the reliable and safe deployment of VLAs in real-world environments. By systematically diagnosing this issue and demonstrating its prevalence across various state-of-the-art models, the paper highlights a critical area for improvement. The proposed Counterfactual Action Guidance (CAG) effectively mitigates these failures, enabling VLAs to follow user intent more faithfully. This direct improvement in instruction following builds greater trust in autonomous systems.

2. **A Standardized Benchmark for Language Grounding:** The introduction of LIBERO-CF is a major contribution. Prior to this work, a dedicated and systematic benchmark for evaluating how well VLAs handle counterfactual instructions was lacking. LIBERO-CF provides a robust, standardized tool for the community to rigorously assess language-following capabilities, fostering targeted research and development in this crucial area. This benchmark will likely become a foundational resource for future studies aiming to build more robust and language-faithful robotic policies.

3. **Universal, Plug-and-Play Mitigation Strategy:** CAG's design as an inference-time, dual-branch scheme is a key advantage. It is model-agnostic, compatible with diverse VLA architectures, and, crucially, does not require modifications to existing model architectures or costly re-training of pre-trained weights. This "plug-and-play" nature significantly lowers the barrier to adoption, allowing current and future VLA developers to readily integrate CAG into their pipelines to enhance language conditioning. This flexibility makes CAG a highly practical and impactful solution for a wide range of applications.

4. **Unlocking Full Generalization Capabilities:** The research demonstrates that vision shortcuts actively suppress the inherent generalization capabilities of VLAs, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. By reducing reliance on these shortcuts, CAG allows VLAs to better leverage the rich semantic priors learned during large-scale pretraining, thereby unlocking and improving zero-shot generalization to novel objects and tasks. This means robots can become more adaptable and perform tasks involving items or situations they haven't explicitly encountered during finetuning.

5. **Advancing the Science of Embodied AI:** The systematic analysis of modality imbalance from a Bayesian perspective offers deeper theoretical insights into why VLAs exhibit such behaviors. This understanding is crucial for designing future VLA architectures and training strategies that inherently promote a more balanced integration of vision and language. The work contributes to the broader goal of creating truly intelligent embodied agents that can reason effectively across multiple modalities.

6. **Fostering Open Research:** The inclusion of a project website (vla-va.github.io) strongly suggests that the authors intend to make their benchmark, code, or trained models publicly available. This commitment to open science further amplifies the potential impact, enabling researchers globally to reproduce results, build upon the findings, and accelerate progress in addressing counterfactual failures.

In conclusion, this paper not only sheds light on a pervasive and critical flaw in modern VLAs but also provides a concrete, accessible solution. By making robots more faithful to human instructions and enhancing their generalization abilities, this research represents a significant step forward in developing robust, reliable, and truly general-purpose robotic agents for a myriad of real-world applications.
