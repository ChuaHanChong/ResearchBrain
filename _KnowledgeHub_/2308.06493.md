# EgoPoser: Robust Real-Time Egocentric Pose Estimation from Sparse and Intermittent Observations Everywhere

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoPoser, developed by ETH Zürich, introduces a real-time full-body pose estimation system that uses sparse and intermittent head and hand tracking data from Mixed Reality headsets. The system demonstrates robust performance across diverse environments and individual body shapes through its global motion decomposition and realistic field-of-view modeling, operating at over 600 frames per second.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric pose estimation methods often overfit to origin-centric indoor motion-capture environments, limiting generalization to large, diverse real-world scenes.
- Current approaches typically assume continuous availability of hand tracking data, but in real-world Mixed Reality (MR), hands frequently move outside the headset's field of view, leading to intermittent observations.
- Prior methods largely disregard natural body shape variations, assuming a mean body shape, which can cause visual artifacts like avatar floating or ground penetration.

## Method

- ==Realistic FoV Modeling== simulates hand tracking failures based on the headset’s actual field of view during training, teaching the network to handle continuous tracking gaps.
- Global Motion Decomposition uses temporal and spatial normalization to create a position-invariant input representation, enabling robust full-body pose prediction independent of global position.
- Calibration-free shape estimation implicitly optimizes body shape parameters through positional error, allowing for personalized avatar representations without manual calibration.

## Results

- EgoPoser achieved significantly lower Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE) on the HPS dataset compared to state-of-the-art methods, while maintaining an inference speed of over 600 fps.
- The method demonstrated stable pose estimation accuracy with positional offsets up to 50 meters from the origin, overcoming the performance degradation seen in previous learning-based approaches.
- Calibration-free shape estimation reduced Mean Vertex Error to **4.08** cm and significantly mitigated visual artifacts such as ground penetration and foot floating, providing more accurate and personalized avatar representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Realistic simulation of Field of View (FoV) constraints during training is crucial for robustly handling intermittent hand tracking in MR applications.
> - Decomposing global motion into position-invariant relative trajectories, while retaining global vertical translation, enables effective generalization to large-scale and diverse environments.
> - Implicitly learning body shape parameters during pose estimation allows for accurate, personalized avatars that adapt to individual body dimensions without explicit calibration.
