# EgoPoser: Robust Real-Time Egocentric Pose Estimation from Sparse and Intermittent Observations Everywhere

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoPoser, developed by ETH Zürich, introduces a real-time full-body pose estimation system that uses sparse and intermittent head and hand tracking data from Mixed Reality headsets. The system demonstrates robust performance across diverse environments and individual body shapes through its global motion decomposition and realistic field-of-view modeling, operating at over 600 frames per second.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric pose estimation methods often overfit to origin-centric indoor motion-capture environments, limiting generalization to large, diverse real-world scenes.
- Current approaches typically assume continuous availability of hand tracking data, but in real-world Mixed Reality (MR), hands frequently move outside the headset's field of view, leading to intermittent observations.
- Prior methods largely disregard natural body shape variations, assuming a mean body shape, which can cause visual artifacts like avatar floating or ground penetration.

## Method

- ==Realistic FoV Modeling== simulates hand tracking failures based on the headset’s actual field of view during training, teaching the network to handle continuous tracking gaps.
- Global Motion Decomposition uses temporal and spatial normalization to create a position-invariant input representation, enabling robust full-body pose prediction independent of global position.
- Calibration-free shape estimation implicitly optimizes body shape parameters through positional error, allowing for personalized avatar representations without manual calibration.

## Results

- EgoPoser achieved significantly lower Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE) on the HPS dataset compared to state-of-the-art methods, while maintaining an inference speed of over 600 fps.
- The method demonstrated stable pose estimation accuracy with positional offsets up to 50 meters from the origin, overcoming the performance degradation seen in previous learning-based approaches.
- Calibration-free shape estimation reduced Mean Vertex Error to **4.08** cm and significantly mitigated visual artifacts such as ground penetration and foot floating, providing more accurate and personalized avatar representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Realistic simulation of Field of View (FoV) constraints during training is crucial for robustly handling intermittent hand tracking in MR applications.
> - Decomposing global motion into position-invariant relative trajectories, while retaining global vertical translation, enables effective generalization to large-scale and diverse environments.
> - Implicitly learning body shape parameters during pose estimation allows for accurate, personalized avatars that adapt to individual body dimensions without explicit calibration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Mixed Reality (MR) systems, such as Microsoft HoloLens, Meta Quest, and Apple Vision Pro, has increased the demand for realistic and immersive virtual avatar representations. These MR systems primarily rely on egocentric tracking cues derived from head-worn devices, specifically the head pose and the positions and orientations of the user’s hands when they are within the headset’s field of view (FoV). This sparse and intermittent input limits the ability of current systems to generate full-body avatars, often restricting them to upper-body representations.

Within this context, full-body egocentric pose estimation from head and hand poses alone has emerged as an active area of research. Previous methods, such as AvatarPoser, AGRoL, and AvatarJLM, have attempted to address this challenge by leveraging large motion-capture (MoCap) datasets to learn realistic body poses and animations. However, these methods typically operate under assumptions that do not fully align with real-world MR scenarios. They often assume continuous availability of hand tracking data, which is frequently interrupted as hands move outside the headset's FoV. Furthermore, many existing approaches use global pose representations as network input, leading to overfitting to the specific, often origin-centric, indoor MoCap environments where datasets were recorded. This limits their generalization ability to diverse, large-scale, or unseen environments and positions. Additionally, a common simplification is the use of a mean body shape, which neglects natural variations in user body dimensions and can result in visual artifacts such as floating or ground penetration.

The research also relates to human pose estimation from inertial sensors, which typically use distributed sensors across the body and struggle with accurate global position tracking due to only observing relative changes. In contrast, EgoPoser focuses on inferring full-body pose, including lower body motions, from the global movements of just the head and hands, without additional body-worn sensors. Furthermore, efforts to address pose estimation under FoV constraints have explored custom sensor configurations or software solutions like FLAG, which randomly masks hands in training data. EgoPoser aims to improve upon these by realistically modeling FoV constraints based on actual spatial relationships and temporal continuity.

EgoPoser positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome these identified limitations, providing a robust, real-time, headset-centered solution for full-body pose estimation that accounts for intermittent observations, generalizes across diverse environments, and adapts to individual body shapes. This work aims to establish a more practical and generalizable baseline for avatar representation in MR applications, moving beyond the reliance on idealized motion-capture conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, systematic approach for robust, real-time full-body pose estimation exclusively from the sparse and intermittent tracking information available on contemporary Mixed Reality (MR) head-mounted devices (HMDs). This overarching goal is motivated by several critical limitations identified in existing egocentric pose estimation methods when applied to real-world MR scenarios:

1. **Over-reliance on Indoor Motion-Capture Environments and Global Input Representations:** Existing methods frequently utilize global pose in world space as network input. While effective within the confined, origin-centric motion-capture spaces where datasets like AMASS are typically recorded, this approach leads to models overfitting to specific environments and positions. This overfitting results in significantly degraded prediction performance even with minor positional offsets, and severely limits generalization to large-scale, real-world environments where users may move far from a defined origin. The motivation here is to enable full-body pose estimation that is robust to global position changes and can operate "everywhere."

2. **Assumption of Continuous Hand Pose Availability:** Current methods often assume that hand pose data is continuously available. However, inside-out tracking systems on HMDs provide only intermittent information about hands, as they frequently move outside the headset cameras' field of view (FoV). This discontinuity in input signals poses a significant challenge for maintaining accurate and smooth full-body pose estimates. The motivation is to develop a method that can robustly model body pose and maintain temporal coherence even when hand tracking is sparse and intermittent due to FoV limitations.

3. **Disregard for Body Shape Variations:** Most prior approaches assume a mean body shape, ignoring the natural variations in body dimensions across different users. This simplification prevents the model from accurately representing individual users, potentially leading to visual artifacts such as floating avatars, ground penetration, or self-penetration, which diminish the fidelity and immersion of the MR experience. The motivation is to enable personalized avatar representations that adapt to diverse body shapes without requiring cumbersome calibration steps.

To address these limitations, EgoPoser outlines four specific contributions as its key objectives:

1. **Robust Modeling of Intermittent Hand Tracking:** To develop a system that accurately estimates full-body pose from intermittent hand position and orientation tracking, specifically when hands are within the headset’s FoV, by realistically simulating and handling tracking gaps.
2. **Novel Global Motion Decomposition:** To rethink input representations for headset-based ego-pose estimation by introducing a global motion decomposition method that predicts full-body pose independently of global positions, ensuring robustness to environmental scale and user location.
3. **Enhanced Temporal Context through Efficient SlowFast Module:** To improve pose estimation accuracy by capturing longer motion time series, while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time applications, through the design of an efficient SlowFast module.
4. **Generalization Across Various Body Shapes:** To enable EgoPoser to generalize across different users and body shapes, providing personalized and accurate avatar representations that reduce common motion artifacts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoPoser is designed as a headset-centered estimation method for full-body poses from the sparse and intermittent tracking cues provided by inside-out tracking systems. The method aims to map 3-point pose input (head and two hands: positions *p* and orientations *Θ*) to the user's full-body pose, specifically the positions of all *J* full-body joints. The output representation is based on the SMPL-H skeleton model, providing global root orientation (*θ*<sub>global</sub>), local joint rotations (*θ*<sub>local</sub>), and 16 shape parameters (*β*). The architecture includes Realistic FoV Modeling, Global Motion Decomposition, a SlowFast Feature Fusion module, a Transformer Encoder, and a human motion decoder, followed by joint optimization using the SMPL body model and forward kinematics.

#### 3.1. Realistic Field of View (FoV) Modeling

Unlike prior work that models tracking failures through random frame drops, EgoPoser realistically simulates hand tracking failures based on the headset’s cameras’ field of view. Given the head's pose (determining the viewing angle) and the relative position of the hands, the method simulates whether hands are inside or outside the FoV for headsets with varying horizontal (α<sub>h</sub>) and vertical (α<sub>v</sub>) FoVs. For joints estimated to be outside the FoV, their corresponding input features are set to 0 during training, allowing the network to learn to handle continuous tracking gaps realistically.

#### 3.2. Global Motion Decomposition

To address the overfitting issues of global pose inputs to origin-centric datasets, EgoPoser introduces a position-invariant global motion decomposition strategy. This strategy combines the advantages of both global and local representations through two key operations:
- **Temporal Normalization (TN):** This involves subtracting the translation of each joint at the first frame of the temporal window from its corresponding positions over the entire window. This process extracts the relative global trajectory of each joint within the considered time series.
- **Spatial Normalization (SN):** Instead of simply converting to a head-relative frame (which can be sensitive to head rotation), SN normalizes only the horizontal translations relative to the head’s projected ground location. The global vertical translation is retained as a crucial feature, encoding motion priors related to vertical body movements (e.g., jumping, crouching).
The transformed positions, along with 6D representations of rotations and calculated linear/angular velocities of the head and hands, form a total of 59 input features for the network.

#### 3.3. SlowFast Feature Fusion

To incorporate a longer temporal context without significantly increasing computational costs (which scale quadratically with sequence length in Transformers), EgoPoser employs a SlowFast feature fusion module, inspired by video recognition networks. Given an input window of τ (80) past frames, the module concatenates:
- **FAST Path:** Linear embeddings for the most recent τ/2 (40) frames.
- **SLOW Path:** Linear embeddings for τ/2 (40) frames sampled with a stride of 2 over the complete τ (80) frame window.
This design reduces the effective input sequence length by a factor of 2 while still capturing information from a longer temporal context and maintaining high temporal resolution for recent movements.

#### 3.4. Shape-aware Pose Optimization

EgoPoser addresses the limitation of assuming a mean body shape by supporting personalized body representations:
- **Solution 1: Data Augmentation + T-pose Calibration:** During training, ground truth shape parameters are incorporated. For inference, body height and arm length are measured, and their ratio to mean shape values is used as a scaling factor to adjust the entire skeleton. This is a calibration-dependent approach.
- **Solution 2: Joint Pose and Shape Estimation (Calibration-Free):** This method estimates the user's shape directly from the tracking input. Instead of direct supervision of shape parameters *β*, the network implicitly optimizes *β* through the positional error of joints generated by a shape-aware differentiable SMPL body model. This model takes estimated joint rotations and *β* as input. An L1 regularization term is applied to *β* to encourage sparsity. To ensure temporal consistency, the median shape parameters from an initial sequence of frames are applied.

#### 3.5. Loss Functions

The total loss function (L<sub>total</sub>) is a weighted sum of several components:
- **L1 Global Orientation Loss (L<sub>ori</sub>):** For the root orientation.
- **L1 Local Rotational Loss (L<sub>rot</sub>):** For local joint rotations.
- **L1 Positional Loss (L<sub>pos</sub>):** Calculated using forward kinematics (FK) to compare predicted joint positions with ground truth.
- **L1 Regularization of β (∥β∥<sub>1</sub>):** To encourage sparsity in shape parameters.
The weights λ<sub>ori</sub>, λ<sub>rot</sub>, λ<sub>pos</sub>, and λ<sub>β</sub> are set to 0.05, 1, 1, and 0.01, respectively.

#### 3.6. Datasets and Training Details

EgoPoser was trained and tested using subsets of the AMASS dataset (CMU, BMLrub, HDM05), with a 90/10% split for training/testing. For "in the wild" evaluation, the HPS dataset was used. The Adam solver was utilized with a batch size of 256. The input window comprised 80 frames (τ=80), resulting in 40 frames after SlowFast fusion. The learning rate was initialized at 1 × 10<sup>−4</sup> and decayed by 0.5 every 2 × 10<sup>4</sup> iterations. Training was conducted on one NVIDIA GeForce GTX 3090 GPU.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Metrics

Performance was assessed using Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE [cm]) and Mean Per Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE [cm/s]) for accuracy and smoothness. Shape-aware pose estimation was evaluated using Mean Vertex Error (MVE [cm]), mean errors for predicted height and bone lengths (cm), ground penetration (GP [cm]), and foot floating (FF [cm]).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of EgoPoser demonstrated its performance across various challenging scenarios, comparing it against state-of-the-art methods and analyzing the effectiveness of its individual components.

#### 4.1. Pose Estimation Robustness in Large Scenes (HPS Dataset)

On the HPS dataset, which features motion captured in large scenes, EgoPoser achieved significantly lower MPJPE and MPJVE compared to AvatarPoser, AGRoL, and AvatarJLM. For instance, across various HPS sequences, EgoPoser consistently showed MPJPE values ranging from 6.90 cm to 11.05 cm and MPJVE values from 35.60 cm/s to 49.39 cm/s, which were notably lower than the competitors. Even when state-of-the-art methods were "Improved" by adapting local representations, EgoPoser still maintained a substantial performance lead. Furthermore, EgoPoser achieved this with a smaller model size and faster inference speed (over 600 fps) compared to the other methods, as indicated by computational complexity comparisons.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Distance from Origin (AMASS with Positional Offsets)

When evaluating robustness to location variations on AMASS subsets augmented with positional offsets (0, 2, 5, 10, and 50 meters), EgoPoser demonstrated stable performance. Unlike prior learning-based methods (AvatarPoser, AGRoL, AvatarJLM) that use global input representations and exhibited significant performance degradation with increasing offsets, EgoPoser maintained consistent accuracy. Methods utilizing local representations, such as CoolMoves and FinalIK, also maintained stable performance but with generally higher error rates than EgoPoser. This finding supports the efficacy of EgoPoser's global motion decomposition in achieving position-invariant prediction.

#### 4.3. Outside-the-FoV Pose Estimation (AMASS with FoV Simulation)

Evaluation of scenarios where hands intermittently leave the HMD's field of view (simulated FoVs of 180°, 120°, 90°) revealed the following:
- A default model trained with full hand visibility showed a considerable increase in MPJPE as the FoV narrowed (e.g., from 24.75 cm at 180° to 41.24 cm at 90°).
- Models trained with random hand masking (RM) improved performance, but EgoPoser's realistic FoV modeling strategy, which accounts for actual spatial hand-to-head relationships and temporal continuity, achieved the best results. EgoPoser yielded MPJPE values of 5.31 cm, 6.07 cm, and 6.60 cm for 180°, 120°, and 90° FoVs respectively, and also exhibited improved smoothness (MPJVE). This indicates that realistic FoV modeling is critical for handling intermittent tracking.

#### 4.4. Shape-aware Pose Estimation (AMASS Dataset)

Comparing different strategies for handling body shape variations:
- Using a mean body shape resulted in a higher MPJPE of 6.36 cm, a Mean Vertex Error (MVE) of 6.74 cm, and significant errors in height (7.67 cm) and arm length (7.42 cm), leading to ground penetration (3.87 cm) and foot floating (5.38 cm) artifacts.
- The first solution (Data Augmentation + T-pose Calibration) reduced MPJPE to 5.26 cm, MVE to 4.69 cm, and considerably decreased body dimension errors (height: 1.36 cm, arm: 1.24 cm) and artifacts.
- The second solution (Calibration-Free Shape Estimation) showed further improvements in MPJPE (4.79 cm) and MVE (4.08 cm), while delivering comparable outcomes for body size metrics and motion artifacts. This demonstrates the effectiveness of EgoPoser’s calibration-free shape prediction.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Global Motion Decomposition:** The combination of temporal and spatial normalization, as proposed in EgoPoser, achieved the best performance (MPJPE of 4.14 cm, MPJVE of 25.95 cm/s). The studies showed that retaining vertical position information was beneficial, and that combining both temporal and spatial normalization surpassed individual normalization strategies.
- **SlowFast Design:** The SlowFast module demonstrated its ability to capture longer temporal context without increasing computational cost or model size. It achieved comparable or superior accuracy (e.g., MPJPE of 4.14 cm) and superior smoothness (MPJVE of 25.95 cm/s) compared to using shorter input sequences or longer sequences without SlowFast, which would have increased FLOPs.

#### 4.6. Demos on Commodity Mixed Reality Devices

Visual demonstrations on real data collected from participants using Meta Quest 2 headsets, with diverse body shapes, indicated EgoPoser's robustness and practical applicability in real-world MR environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoPoser presents a systematic and comprehensive approach to addressing several fundamental challenges in real-time, egocentric full-body pose estimation for Mixed Reality (MR) applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Robust Full-Body Avatars in Real-World MR:** By overcoming limitations such as over-reliance on motion-capture spaces, the assumption of continuous tracking, and disregard for individual body shapes, EgoPoser facilitates the creation of robust and realistic full-body avatar representations in arbitrary, large-scale real-world settings. This moves beyond the constraints of traditional "outside-in" motion capture systems, which are not suitable for widespread MR adoption. This capability is crucial for enhancing user immersion and social presence in virtual and mixed environments.

2. **Addressing Real-World Tracking Imperfections:** The method's novel realistic Field of View (FoV) modeling directly confronts the practical reality of intermittent hand tracking in HMDs. By learning to smoothly estimate poses even when hands are out of view, EgoPoser improves the reliability and coherence of avatar animation, a critical factor for a seamless user experience.

3. **Position-Invariant Pose Estimation:** The proposed global motion decomposition strategy allows EgoPoser to generalize effectively across diverse spatial contexts. This means that users can perform motions far from an initial origin point, or in entirely new environments, without significant degradation in pose estimation accuracy. This is a crucial step towards truly mobile and scalable MR experiences.

4. **Personalized and Accurate Representations:** EgoPoser's calibration-free shape estimation method enables avatars to accurately reflect individual user body shapes. This reduces visually jarring artifacts like ground penetration, self-penetration, or floating, leading to more believable and personalized virtual embodiment. This enhances the sense of self-presence and authenticity for users.

5. **Real-Time Performance and Efficiency:** Achieving high inference speeds (over 600 fps) with a relatively compact model, as demonstrated by its computational efficiency, makes EgoPoser a practical solution for integration into contemporary MR hardware, which often has computational constraints. This real-time capability is essential for interactive applications.

6. **Establishing a New Baseline:** EgoPoser establishes a robust baseline for future research in egocentric pose estimation. Its identified issues with global input representations, and the solutions provided for intermittent tracking and shape variability, offer valuable insights and methodologies for the broader research community.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements introduced by EgoPoser have the potential to significantly impact the development and adoption of MR technologies. It can lead to:
- **More Immersive Social VR/AR Experiences:** By enabling accurate, full-body representations, users can feel more present and connected in shared virtual spaces.
- **Enhanced Training and Simulation:** Realistic full-body tracking can improve the fidelity of training simulations, particularly for tasks requiring precise body movements.
- **New Forms of Human-Computer Interaction:** More granular and robust understanding of full-body pose can unlock novel interaction paradigms in MR.
- **Democratization of Full-Body Tracking:** By relying solely on commodity HMDs, EgoPoser makes full-body tracking accessible to a wider user base without the need for expensive external equipment.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges some limitations. The assumption that the user is moving on the same floor for vertical global position encoding necessitates redefinition of the origin system when changing floors. The current reliance on a straightforward Transformer backbone suggests that more elaborate models could be explored. Additionally, the method does not currently incorporate post-processing steps, which could potentially further refine estimation accuracy or enhance physical plausibility. These areas represent avenues for future research and development.
