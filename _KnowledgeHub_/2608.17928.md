# A Theoretical Framework for Parallel Lifelong MAPF Using Group Decentralized Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> GD-RHCR extends Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution for Lifelong MAPF by first proving RHCR near-optimal via a discounted Locally Interdependent Multi-Agent MDP framework, then partitioning agents into connected-component groups planned in parallel with lazy replanning and a fast fallback solver, cutting plan time 24.9x while nearly matching RHCR throughput and beating collapsed RHCR/PIBT by up to 57.7%.

## Problem

- RHCR gives high-quality L-MAPF throughput but its one-shot solver cost explodes exponentially with agent count, forcing partial plans or timeouts.
- No theoretically principled way exists to parallelize RHCR across agents without silently sacrificing its near-optimal throughput guarantees.
- Naive fully decentralized planning with local visibility fails badly, taking roughly twice as many steps as centralized planning in simple counterexamples.

## Method

- **Group Decentralized RHCR (GD-RHCR)** partitions agents into connected components via a ==visibility-radius graph==, planning each group independently and in parallel.
- A ==lazy evaluation== scheme dynamically sets per-group replan windows, only recomputing when agents disagree on cached plans rather than every fixed interval.
- ==Soft constraints== penalize collisions against stale out-of-group plans in the lower-level ==SIPP/PBS== search, plus a secondary ==PIBT== solver kicks in above a group-size threshold.

## Results

- Theorem 6.1 shows GD-RHCR's suboptimality bound matches RHCR's up to constants, with exponent min(floor(V/2)+1, H) revealing a space-time duality.
- On warehouse-10-20-10-2-1, GD-RHCR stays within about **2%** of RHCR throughput while cutting average plan time by **24.9x** before RHCR collapses.
- At k=800 agents on the same map, GD-RHCR improves throughput **57.7%** over collapsed RHCR/PIBT, with smaller **12.6%** and **9.66%** gains on two other maps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A discounted MDP lens can retroactively justify heuristic multi-agent planning tricks, revealing RHCR's replan window bound is independent of window length.
> - Space-based agent grouping and time-based horizon truncation are mathematically dual mechanisms for trading computation against near-optimality.
> - Treating solver fallback as a designed graceful-degradation path, not a failure mode, lets throughput scale further into large agent counts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding (L-MAPF) problem, a variant of Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) where agents continuously move between assigned destinations. MAPF is a well-studied area with broad applications in areas such as warehouse logistics, airport logistics, UAV traffic management, and video games. While one-shot MAPF focuses on navigating agents from single start to end points, L-MAPF deals with dynamic, continuous task assignment, requiring high throughput of goal achievements over time.

Existing approaches to L-MAPF typically fall into two categories: high-performing but computationally expensive methods, and fast but potentially suboptimal or greedy methods. The Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution (RHCR) framework is widely recognized for achieving high throughput in L-MAPF. However, its primary drawback is its substantial computational cost, which often limits its practical applicability, especially with increasing numbers of agents. RHCR relies on repeatedly calling one-shot MAPF solvers (which are NP-hard in the general case) within a short time window, leading to exponential cost increases even with suboptimal one-shot solvers like Priority Based Search (PBS). This computational burden often necessitates early termination or fallback mechanisms to faster, simpler algorithms like Priority Inheritance with Backtracking (PIBT). PIBT, while fast and scalable, is known to be greedy and can suffer from deadlocks or suboptimal performance.

The broader research landscape includes efforts to speed up MAPF and L-MAPF through various means, including approximation algorithms, learning-based methods, and grouping strategies. While some works have explored grouping agents to reduce computational complexity in MAPF, these often lack theoretical guarantees regarding performance preservation or focus on completeness rather than coordination groups. Developing theoretically principled methods for parallelizing RHCR without sacrificing its performance has remained an open challenge.

This work integrates a novel theoretical model, the Locally Interdependent Multi-Agent MDP (LI-MDP), into the L-MAPF domain. The LI-MDP framework, introduced by DeWeese and Qu (2024, 2025), models multi-agent systems with dynamic local dependencies and provides a theoretical basis for group decentralized planning. By applying LI-MDP theory, this paper aims to provide a formal underpinning for RHCR's effectiveness and to derive a new, parallelized L-MAPF solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop theoretically principled parallelized methods for the RHCR framework in Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding (L-MAPF). This overarching goal is driven by several specific aims and motivations:

1. **Establish a Theoretical Foundation for RHCR:** A significant motivation is the lack of a formal theoretical framework explaining RHCR's effectiveness and near-optimality in the L-MAPF setting. The authors aim to model L-MAPF as a discounted Markov Decision Process (MDP) and, by leveraging recent advancements in Locally Interdependent Multi-Agent MDP (LI-MDP) theory, to theoretically prove the near-optimality of the standard RHCR framework. This objective seeks to provide a formal justification for RHCR's strong empirical performance and its common practices, such as the use of a replan window.

2. **Mitigate RHCR's Computational Cost:** Despite its high-quality solutions, RHCR's computational expense severely limits its applicability to scenarios with even a modest number of agents. It frequently requires significant computational resources, leading to long simulation times or reliance on fallback mechanisms. The authors are motivated to reduce this computational burden, enabling the use of RHCR-level solution quality for a larger number of agents and in time-sensitive applications.

3. **Develop a Parallelized, High-Throughput RHCR Variant:** The motivation to reduce computational cost naturally leads to exploring parallelization. However, simply parallelizing RHCR without a theoretical understanding risks sacrificing its performance. A key objective is to design a parallelized version of RHCR that retains or exceeds its high throughput capabilities while significantly reducing the per-plan computational cost. This involves identifying a "correct way" to ignore agent interactions to enable parallelization without compromising solution quality.

4. **Leverage Group Decentralization for Scalability:** The LI-MDP framework offers insights into optimal policy structures, particularly the concept of group decentralization. This motivates the development of a Group Decentralized RHCR (GD-RHCR) framework. The objective is to utilize a spatial partitioning scheme, where agents are grouped based on their proximity and transitive connectivity, to allow for independent parallel planning within these groups. This is intended to address the limitations of naive decentralization, which can lead to performance degradation.

5. **Provide Theoretical Guarantees for GD-RHCR:** Beyond empirical demonstration, a core objective is to theoretically prove that the proposed GD-RHCR framework also achieves near-optimal guarantees, similar to (and quantifiable in relation to) the vanilla RHCR. This aims to establish a formal duality between the time-based restrictions of RHCR and the space-based partitioning of GD-RHCR, validating the theoretical soundness of the proposed parallelization.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for scalable, high-quality L-MAPF solutions. It addresses the computational bottleneck of RHCR by developing a theoretically grounded parallelization strategy, drawing upon novel LI-MDP theory to ensure performance preservation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach, combining theoretical analysis with algorithmic development and empirical validation.

### 4. Theoretical Foundation: L-MAPF as a Discounted LI-MDP

The core of the theoretical methodology involves modeling the Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding (L-MAPF) problem as a discounted Multi-Agent Markov Decision Process (MDP) that fits within the framework of a Locally Interdependent Multi-Agent MDP (LI-MDP).
- **State Space:** For each agent, the state includes its position, previous direction (to infer edge collisions), and current goal. The joint state space is the Cartesian product of individual agent states.
- **Action Space:** Standard grid movements (up, down, left, right, stay) for each agent.
- **Transition Function:** Deterministic transitions based on agent actions, with agents remaining in place if their desired location is blocked by an obstacle or another agent (vertex or edge collision). Goals are updated upon arrival.
- **Reward Function:** A sum of positive rewards for agents reaching their goals and negative penalties for agents involved in vertex or edge collisions. Collision penalties are assumed to be significantly larger than goal rewards.
- **Discounted Value Function:** The metric of evaluation is the sum of discounted rewards over an infinite horizon, suitable for the lifelong setting.

This modeling approach allows the authors to leverage properties and theorems from the LI-MDP literature, which specifically deals with multi-agent systems where interactions are local and dynamic. The paper notes a minor discrepancy regarding edge collisions in the standard LI-MDP definition but asserts that critical lemmas, such as the Dependence Time Lemma, still hold.

### 5. Near-Optimality Proof for RHCR

Using the LI-MDP formulation, the authors formally analyze the performance of the traditional RHCR framework.
- **Assumptions:** It is assumed that the underlying one-shot solver used by RHCR is $\epsilon$-near optimal for a finite horizon.
- **Bounding Suboptimality:** The analysis involves bounding the difference between the optimal infinite-horizon discounted value function and the value function achieved by RHCR. This bound quantifies the suboptimality of RHCR.
- **Key Result (Theorem 4.1):** The proof demonstrates that RHCR's suboptimality decreases exponentially with the increase in the planning horizon (`H`). Crucially, the theoretical bound is shown to be independent of the replan window (`α`), providing a theoretical justification for using a smaller `α` to reduce computational cost without degrading the worst-case optimality guarantee beyond a certain point.

### 6. Algorithmic Development: Group Decentralized RHCR (GD-RHCR)

Motivated by the insights from LI-MDP theory, particularly its support for group decentralized policies, the authors propose a new framework: Group Decentralized RHCR (GD-RHCR). The algorithm is designed with four main components:
- **Group Decentralization:** At each timestep, agents are partitioned into groups. This is achieved by defining a graph where agents are connected if they are within a specified visibility radius (`V`) based on a shortest path distance metric. Groups are then identified as the connected components of this graph using a union-find data structure.
- **Lazy Evaluation:** Instead of replanning for all agents simultaneously at fixed intervals (as in vanilla RHCR), GD-RHCR dynamically decides which groups require replanning. A group's plan is recomputed only if it is the initial timestep, if agents within the group no longer agree on a shared plan (e.g., due to a new agent joining), or if the existing plan is exhausted. This aims to stagger computations and extend the lifespan of existing plans.
- **Soft Constraints:** To account for potential interactions between agents in different, independently planned groups, soft constraints are introduced. When planning for a specific group, the previously computed plans of agents outside that group are incorporated into the lower-level solver as costs (`c_soft`) for potential collisions. This provides a mechanism for approximate coordination across group boundaries.
- **Secondary Solver (Fallback):** To handle highly congested scenarios or large groups that would otherwise incur excessive computation time, a fast, secondary solver (e.g., PIBT) is used. If a group's size exceeds a predefined threshold (`K_threshold`), GD-RHCR immediately switches to this faster solver for that group, preventing timeouts and facilitating continuous operation. This makes the fallback an integral part of the strategy rather than just a failure recovery mechanism.

### 7. Theoretical Guarantee for GD-RHCR

The methodology includes proving a theoretical guarantee for GD-RHCR under simplified conditions (no secondary solver, zero soft constraint cost).
- **Key Result (Theorem 6.1):** It is shown that GD-RHCR also achieves near-optimal guarantees. The suboptimality bound for GD-RHCR is similar to that of RHCR, but its exponential decay factor depends on `min(⌊V/2⌋ + 1, H)`. This establishes a theoretical duality: the performance is limited by either the temporal horizon (`H`) or the spatial visibility (`V`), with the latter directly linked to the "Dependence Time Lemma" which states that agents in different groups cannot collide within `⌊V/2⌋` timesteps.

### 8. Empirical Validation

The proposed GD-RHCR algorithm is empirically evaluated and compared against RHCR (with PIBT fallback) and PIBT across a diverse set of simulated environments:
- **Environments:** Multi-Agent Pickup Delivery (MAPD) settings on four different maps (warehouse, sortation) and random navigation L-MAPF settings on six different maps (random, room variations, empty).
- **Metrics:** Throughput (goals achieved per unit time) and average plan runtime. For runtime, both per-plan cost (assuming perfect parallelism) and total wall time (accounting for imperfect parallelism and evaluation frequency) are reported.
- **Parameters:** RHCR uses PBS as the solver with PIBT fallback, `α=5`, `H=20`. GD-RHCR uses PBS as the primary solver, PIBT as the secondary solver, `c_soft=1`, `H=20`, `V=2`, and a dynamic `K_threshold`.
- **Ablation Studies:** Simulations are performed to analyze the impact of different `V`, `c_soft`, and `K_threshold` values on GD-RHCR's performance.
- **Group Statistics:** Analysis of the average number of groups, mean group size, and mean maximum group size for GD-RHCR across simulations.

This comprehensive approach allows for both a rigorous theoretical understanding and a practical evaluation of the proposed method.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical and empirical findings regarding the RHCR framework and the proposed Group Decentralized RHCR (GD-RHCR).

### 10. Theoretical Findings

1. **Near-Optimality of RHCR:** The paper provides a theoretical proof demonstrating the near-optimality of the Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution (RHCR) framework when the L-MAPF problem is formulated as a discounted Markov Decision Process (MDP). Specifically, the difference between the optimal discounted value and the value achieved by RHCR is bounded by $\frac{2\gamma^H}{(1-\gamma)^2} + \frac{\epsilon}{1-\gamma}$, where $\gamma$ is the discount factor, $H$ is the planning horizon, and $\epsilon$ is the suboptimality of the underlying one-shot solver. This result indicates that RHCR's suboptimality decreases exponentially with an increased planning horizon. An important implication is that this bound is independent of the replan window ($\alpha$), providing a theoretical basis for adjusting $\alpha$ to manage computational cost without necessarily degrading the worst-case optimality guarantee beyond that related to $H$.

2. **Near-Optimality of GD-RHCR:** The proposed GD-RHCR framework is also shown to satisfy a similar near-optimality guarantee under simplified conditions (zero soft constraints and no secondary solver). The suboptimality bound for GD-RHCR is given by $\frac{6\gamma^{\min(\lfloor V/2 \rfloor+1, H)}}{(1-\gamma)^2} + \frac{\epsilon}{1-\gamma}$. This establishes a theoretical duality between the time-based restrictions (planning horizon $H$) and the space-based partitioning (visibility radius $V$) in group decentralized planning. The exponent $\min(\lfloor V/2 \rfloor+1, H)$ indicates that the performance is limited by the tighter of the two restrictions. This result provides a formal justification for GD-RHCR's group decentralized parallelization scheme and lazy evaluation, asserting that it can maintain performance comparable to RHCR.

### 11. Empirical Findings

1. **Reduced Computational Cost with Maintained Throughput:**
 - GD-RHCR significantly reduces the average planning time per step compared to RHCR. For example, on the `warehouse-10-20-10-2-1` map, GD-RHCR achieved a 24.9x improvement in plan time while maintaining throughput within approximately 2% of RHCR before RHCR's collapse.
 - This reduction is attributed to two factors: the parallel execution of planning for independent groups and the lazy evaluation scheme, which staggers replanning across different groups, reducing the peak computational load at any given timestep.
 - Wall times, which account for imperfect parallelism and evaluation frequency, also demonstrated consistent improvements, often achieving at least a 2x reduction in computation time compared to RHCR.

2. **Extended Applicability to Higher Agent Counts:**
 - GD-RHCR demonstrated an ability to operate effectively with higher agent counts where vanilla RHCR typically collapses due to excessive computation time and falls back to a simpler, less effective solver (like PIBT).
 - The framework's use of a `K_threshold` to immediately switch to a faster secondary solver for large, congested groups (rather than waiting for a primary solver timeout) allows for a more graceful degradation of performance. This "fallback as a feature" approach prevents exponential slowdowns.
 - On the `warehouse-10-20-10-2-1` map, GD-RHCR showed a +57.7% improvement in throughput over collapsed RHCR/PIBT at 800 agents. Improvements of 12.6% and 9.66% were observed on `warehouse-10-20-10-2-2` and `sortation-1`, respectively, at 1000 agents.

3. **Impact of Map Topology:**
 - The effectiveness of GD-RHCR is influenced by the map's topology. Maps with dense obstacles or complex shortest paths (e.g., `warehouse-10-20-10-2-1`) tend to induce smaller, more isolated groups of agents. This promotes greater parallelism and allows GD-RHCR to leverage its group-based planning more effectively, leading to significant advantages.
 - Conversely, on small or less obstacle-dense maps (e.g., `random-32-32-20`, `empty-48-48`, `sortation-2`), agents are more likely to form large, interconnected groups. In such scenarios, the benefits of parallelization diminish as groups become large, leading to either planning for a large group centrally or falling back to the secondary solver, reducing the throughput advantage over PIBT.
 - For maps that are not biconnected, such as `random-32-32-20` and `room-32-32-var1`, reliance on PIBT (either by RHCR fallback or GD-RHCR's secondary solver) can lead to deadlocking and reduced throughput.

4. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - Smaller visibility radii (`V`) with fixed `K_threshold` generally led to earlier reliance on the secondary solver, resulting in performance degradation if the `K_threshold` was hit too soon.
 - Increasing the soft constraint cost (`c_soft`) led to a faster degradation in computation time, which subsequently reduced throughput as groups experienced timeouts.
 - The throughput was observed to be relatively insensitive to increases in the `K_threshold` for the tested maps, suggesting that GD-RHCR effectively routes smaller groups while the PIBT fallback manages larger congestion points.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of multi-agent systems and robotics, particularly in the domain of path planning, with both theoretical and practical implications.

### 13. Theoretical Significance

1. **Formal Justification for RHCR:** The work provides the first known theoretical framework that rigorously proves the near-optimality of the widely used Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution (RHCR) in a discounted MDP formulation of Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding (L-MAPF). This theoretical underpinning formalizes why RHCR delivers high-quality solutions, moving beyond purely empirical observations. The finding that the suboptimality bound is independent of the replan window ($\alpha$) offers a deeper understanding of RHCR's mechanics.
2. **Duality of Temporal and Spatial Restrictions:** By extending the theoretical analysis to Group Decentralized RHCR (GD-RHCR), the paper establishes a theoretical duality between RHCR's time-based restrictions (planning horizon $H$) and GD-RHCR's space-based partitioning (visibility radius $V$). This framework, derived from the Locally Interdependent Multi-Agent MDP (LI-MDP) theory, provides a principled approach to decentralizing complex multi-agent planning problems while maintaining strong performance guarantees.
3. **Foundation for Future Theoretical Work:** The application of LI-MDP to MAPF problems opens new avenues for theoretical analysis in multi-agent coordination. It offers a structured way to analyze the impact of local interactions and communication constraints on global system performance, potentially informing the design of other decentralized multi-agent algorithms.

### 14. Practical Significance and Potential Impact

1. **Scalable High-Quality L-MAPF:** GD-RHCR offers a practical solution to the long-standing challenge of scaling high-quality L-MAPF methods like RHCR to larger numbers of agents. By significantly reducing the computational cost (e.g., 24.9x faster planning time in some scenarios) while largely preserving or exceeding RHCR's throughput, GD-RHCR makes advanced L-MAPF applicable in real-world scenarios that were previously computationally prohibitive.
2. **Enhanced Throughput for Large-Scale Operations:** The ability of GD-RHCR to maintain higher throughput for agent counts where vanilla RHCR collapses (e.g., up to 57.7% improvement in peak throughput) has direct impact on applications such as automated warehouses, drone traffic management, and logistical operations, where continuous, efficient movement of many agents is critical.
3. **Robustness through Graceful Degradation:** GD-RHCR's integrated secondary solver mechanism, which leverages a faster solver like PIBT for highly congested or large groups, ensures a more graceful degradation of performance rather than hard timeouts and failures. This inherent robustness is valuable in dynamic and unpredictable environments.
4. **Flexible and Modular Planning Framework:** The group decentralized structure provides a modular framework where different, potentially heterogeneous, one-shot MAPF solvers can be assigned to different groups based on their characteristics (e.g., group size, congestion level, criticality). This flexibility could lead to more optimized and adaptive planning systems, allowing system designers to tailor planning strategies to specific sub-problems.
5. **New Research Directions:** The work lays the groundwork for several future research directions. These include integrating group decentralization with learning-based MAPF methods, exploring dynamic and adaptive grouping strategies, and further investigating heterogeneous solver assignments across groups to optimize trade-offs between solution quality and computational efficiency. The insights into map topology's influence on grouping further suggest research into topology-aware planning strategies.

In essence, this research not only advances the fundamental understanding of complex multi-agent pathfinding but also provides a powerful and practical algorithmic framework that can enable more efficient and scalable autonomous systems in real-world applications.
