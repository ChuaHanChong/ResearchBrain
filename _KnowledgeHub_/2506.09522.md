# Revisit What You See: Disclose Language Prior in Vision Tokens for LVLM Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> ReVisiT, from Yonsei University, proposes a decoding strategy that leverages intrinsic object-level semantics within Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) vision tokens to reduce visual hallucinations. The method enhances visual grounding and general VQA performance across models, achieving this with negligible computational overhead compared to greedy decoding.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently suffer from "visual hallucination," generating text descriptions of objects or attributes not present in the input image.
- Existing methods to mitigate hallucinations often incur significant computational overhead, requiring multiple forward passes or complex interventions during decoding.
- The intrinsic semantic information within LVLM "vision tokens"—visual embeddings projected into the text embedding space—is often latent and under-leveraged by conventional decoding processes.

## Method

- ReVisiT employs a ==training-free, model-agnostic decoding strategy== that dynamically refines output logits at each generation step.
- It constructs an ==adaptive, context-aware vocabulary subset== and projects relevant vision tokens from specific decoder layers over this subset.
- The method combines the vanilla output probability distribution with the selected vision token's projected distribution (chosen by minimizing ==Jensen-Shannon Divergence==) to guide text generation, amplifying visually grounded tokens.

## Results

- ReVisiT significantly reduced hallucination rates across benchmarks (e.g., HallusionBench qAcc improved from **11.55%** to **20.22%** for LLaVA-1.5-7B) and consistently improved general VQA performance (e.g., VQAv2 accuracy for LLaVA-1.5-7B from **69.47%** to **70.40%**).
- The method maintains inference efficiency comparable to greedy decoding, with only a marginal runtime increase (**+1.9%** for LLaVA-1.5-7B), substantially outperforming other intra-alignment techniques.
- It demonstrated broad applicability and scalability, improving performance across various LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL) and model sizes (**7B to 32B**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Even at hallucinated steps, ground-truth visual information often remains present among the LVLM's high-probability output candidates.
> - Vision tokens intrinsically encode meaningful object-level semantics, but their interpretability becomes explicit only when projected onto a semantically coherent, constrained vocabulary subset.
> - The presence of vision tokens significantly contributes to visual grounding, with their removal leading to a reversal in probability mass distribution between ground-truth and hallucinated objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in language understanding and generation. Following this development, Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have emerged, integrating visual perception with language processing to address multimodal tasks. LVLMs typically represent visual inputs as "vision tokens" embedded within the LLM decoder's text embedding space, which are then processed alongside text tokens. This approach facilitates multimodal system construction by treating vision tokens as auxiliary contexts.

However, a notable challenge in LVLMs is the frequent occurrence of "hallucinations," where the model generates textual descriptions or answers that are inconsistent with or unsupported by the visual input. This issue suggests that the unique characteristics and role of vision tokens as carriers of visual information are not always adequately captured or utilized during the decoding process. Existing research efforts to mitigate hallucinations in LVLMs can be broadly categorized based on the stage of intervention:
- **Pre-alignment methods** involve modifying model architectures and training pipelines to enhance cross-modal alignment before inference, such as refining vision encoders or cross-modal projection modules.
- **Post-alignment approaches** operate on the generated sequence, using auxiliary revisors or verification pipelines to correct errors after decoding.
- **Intra-alignment methods** intervene during the decoding process itself. These include perturbing inputs to suppress language-prior-driven hallucinations, recalibrating attention to visual tokens, or introducing auxiliary visual-sensitive distributions to modify decoding rules.

While these intra-alignment methods implicitly influence decoding through hidden states or attention scores, the direct textual semantics encoded within individual vision tokens and their explicit influence on the decoding process have remained under-explored. The current work positions itself within the intra-alignment paradigm by focusing on explicitly interpreting and leveraging these latent visual semantics during text generation. It intersects with research on interpreting internal representations by projecting them into the vocabulary space, which has typically been used for diagnostic or reweighting purposes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate how vision information contributes to the LVLM decoding process and to develop a method that effectively utilizes this information to improve visual grounding and reduce hallucinations. The authors identify a gap in understanding how vision tokens, despite being the sole carriers of visual information, influence text generation.

The motivation for this work is built upon two key empirical analyses conducted on vision tokens:

1. **Evidence of visual grounding in output distributions:** Through analysis of LVLM output distributions, particularly at steps where hallucinations occur, the authors found that ground-truth (GT) objects often remain among the model's high-probability candidates. For example, in 63.2% of hallucinated steps, at least one GT object appeared within the top-50 candidate predictions, increasing to 95.8% within the top-500 candidates. This suggests that LVLMs possess correct visual information even when their top-1 prediction is a hallucination. Further comparative analysis between ground-truth and hallucinated object probabilities revealed that the presence of vision tokens increases the probability assigned to ground-truth objects on average. However, at specific hallucinated steps, language priors could still dominate, leading to the selection of incorrect tokens despite the presence of visual grounding signals. These findings indicate that while visual grounding signals exist, they are not always effectively leveraged by conventional unconstrained decoding.

2. **Object-level semantics in vision tokens:** To pinpoint the source of these visual grounding signals, the authors investigated the interpretability of vision token representations by projecting them through the model’s language-modeling head into the text token distribution. Initially, when projected over the full vocabulary, vision token semantics appeared diffuse, with only 2.03% of projections surfacing a GT synonym at top-1. However, when the projection was constrained to semantically coherent vocabulary subsets (e.g., MS-COCO object synonyms), the alignment with visual semantics significantly improved, with at-least-one recall@k rising to 40.44% at top-1. This indicated that vision tokens intrinsically encode rich object-level semantics, but these signals typically remain latent or obscured under conventional unconstrained projections and require appropriate semantic constraints to become explicit and interpretable.

Based on these observations, the core motivation is to develop a decoding strategy that can dynamically "reveal" and integrate the intrinsic object-level semantics from vision tokens into the text generation process, thereby enhancing visual grounding and mitigating hallucinations without requiring model retraining or architectural modifications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (ReVisiT)

The authors propose ReVisiT (Referencing Vision Tokens), a training-free decoding method designed to guide text generation in LVLMs by leveraging the intrinsic visual semantics in vision tokens. ReVisiT operates in three main steps at each decoding timestep:

#### 3.1. Preliminary Concepts

The LVLM processes visual inputs `v` and textual inputs `x` to autoregressively generate output text tokens `y`. The model's language modeling head `ϕ` maps the final hidden state `h_L_T+t-1` to a probability distribution `p_θ(h_L_T+t-1, V)` over the entire vocabulary `V`.

#### 3.2. Context-Aware Vocabulary Subset Construction

At each decoding step `t`, ReVisiT dynamically constrains the full vocabulary `V` to a subset `V_t_cons`. This subset is designed to capture the plausible semantic space of the current output distribution, reducing semantic distraction and focusing the integration of visual information. The subset `V_t_cons` is defined as:
`V_t_cons = {w ∈ V : p_θ(w | h_L_T+t-1, V) ≥ α · max_w' p_θ(w' | h_L_T+t-1, V)}`
where `α ∈ (0, 1)` is a hyperparameter controlling the tightness of the selected candidate set. A smaller `α` yields a broader subset, while a larger `α` results in a more restrictive subset.

#### 3.3. Vision Token Selection with Projection

After constructing `V_t_cons`, ReVisiT selects the most relevant vision token. Vision token hidden states `h_j_i` (where `i` is the vision token index and `j` is the decoder layer) are projected through the language modeling head to obtain a distribution `p_θ(h_j_i, V_t_cons)` over the constrained vocabulary subset. This projection reveals the textual likelihood of tokens within `V_t_cons` conditioned on the individual vision token.
To identify the vision token most relevant to the current decoding context, ReVisiT compares each vision token's projected distribution `p_θ(h_j_i, V_t_cons)` against the vanilla output distribution `p_θ(h_L_T+t-1, V_t_cons)` by computing the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD):
`(i*, j*) = arg min_i,j JSD(p_θ(h_L_T+t-1, V_t_cons) ∥ p_θ(h_j_i, V_t_cons))`
The pair `(i*, j*)` corresponds to the selected vision token and its optimal decoder layer. For efficiency, vision token projections over the full vocabulary are precomputed once, and then sliced and masked to adapt to `V_t_cons` at each step.

#### 3.4. Logit Refinement with Projected Token Distribution

Finally, ReVisiT refines the vanilla output distribution using the selected vision token's projected distribution as a visual grounding signal. This refinement is performed via log-space addition, which can be interpreted as a KL-regularized, Product-of-Experts (PoE)-style combination:
`l_ReVisiT(y_t) = log p_θ(y_t | h_L_T+t-1, V_t_cons) + log p_θ(y_t | h_j*i*, V_t_cons)`
The final distribution `p_fin(y_t)` is obtained by applying a softmax over `l_ReVisiT(y_t)` for tokens within `V_t_cons`, with probabilities for tokens outside the subset set to zero. The next token `y_t` is then sampled from `p_fin`. This mechanism dynamically integrates explicit visual grounding signals without additional inference passes or architectural modifications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ReVisiT was evaluated across five benchmarks using various state-of-the-art LVLMs, including LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL3.

#### 4.1. Empirical Analysis Outcomes

The initial empirical analyses confirmed that vision tokens encode object-level semantics that become explicit under semantically coherent vocabulary constraints. When projected over a constrained subset, vision tokens showed significantly higher recall of ground-truth objects (e.g., 40.44% at top-1 recall compared to 2.03% with full vocabulary). This foundational insight motivated the design of ReVisiT.

#### 4.2. Hallucination Benchmarks

- **HallusionBench:** ReVisiT achieved the best scores on four out of five metrics (qAcc, fAcc, aAcc, HaAcc) for LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and InternVL3-8B. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, ReVisiT improved qAcc over greedy decoding by approximately 75%, and by 28% on InternVL3-8B. This suggests improved visual grounding, particularly in scenarios where image and context provide conflicting cues.
- **CHAIR:** This benchmark measures object hallucination rates in image captioning. ReVisiT demonstrated competitive or superior performance across LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and InternVL3-8B, consistently ranking within the top-2 for sentence-level (CHAIR_S), instance-level (CHAIR_I) hallucination, and object-level F1 score. This indicates ReVisiT's ability to reduce hallucinations while maintaining comprehensive object coverage. Across eight different LVLM models (7B to 32B), ReVisiT consistently improved F1 scores and reduced CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I compared to greedy decoding.
- **POPE:** Evaluating object presence through binary questions, ReVisiT achieved the best Accuracy and F1 on LLaVA-1.5-7B and InternVL3-8B, and ranked second on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. These results indicate robust gains in discriminative grounding across various datasets and query types.

#### 4.3. General-Purpose Benchmarks

- **VQAv2:** ReVisiT improved accuracy over greedy decoding on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieving the highest accuracy among all decoding methods for these models. It maintained competitive performance across all three models, indicating robustness without causing VQA failures.
- **MMMU:** On this multi-discipline multimodal question answering benchmark, ReVisiT achieved the highest accuracy for all three evaluated LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B). This demonstrates its generalization capability across diverse LVLMs and complex reasoning tasks.

#### 4.4. Inference Speed

ReVisiT maintained inference efficiency comparable to vanilla greedy decoding, with an increase in runtime of only 0.6% to 2.0% across the three LVLMs. This contrasts with other intra-alignment methods that require additional forward passes and can incur up to 2x overhead or 14-30% overhead for methods like DoLa.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

The ablation study, conducted on Qwen2.5-VL-7B under the CHAIR benchmark, yielded several findings:
- **Vision-token selection criterion:** The `min-JSD` selection mechanism was effective, significantly outperforming `max-JSD` and `random` selection.
- **Vocabulary constraint:** The adaptive vocabulary subset constraint was crucial. Projecting over the full vocabulary resulted in degenerate behavior, leading to poor performance (F1 = 1.52) due to diffuse and off-target vision-token projections.
- **Layer scope and threshold α:** ReVisiT showed modest sensitivity to the `α` threshold. Using all even-numbered layers (`all`) performed marginally better than only the last layer (`last`) for Qwen2.5-VL. Optimal `α` values varied slightly by task (e.g., `10^-5` for CHAIR, `10^-2` for VQAv2), indicating that an overly loose or tight candidate set can be detrimental.
- **Selection metrics and fusion strategies:** JSD was found to be the most effective selection metric among those tested (KL divergence, cosine similarity). The Product-of-Experts (PoE) fusion method, particularly with a weighting of 1.0, generally outperformed interpolation-based fusion methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research makes contributions to the field of large vision-language models by addressing the challenge of visual hallucination and enhancing visual grounding.

**Contributions:**
- **Empirical evidence of latent visual semantics:** The study provides quantitative and qualitative evidence that vision tokens intrinsically encode interpretable object-level semantics that become explicit when projected over semantically coherent vocabulary subsets. This insight contributes to the understanding of how vision information is represented within LVLMs.
- **Novel training-free decoding method:** ReVisiT introduces a novel decoding strategy that dynamically references the most relevant vision token through a context-aware constrained divergence minimization. This method refines next-token logits without requiring any additional training, architectural modifications, external modules, or multi-pass inference.
- **Demonstrated effectiveness and efficiency:** Extensive experiments across six model sizes (7B to 32B) and three architectures demonstrate ReVisiT's consistent ability to reduce hallucinations and yield performance gains on five diverse benchmarks (HallusionBench, CHAIR, POPE, VQAv2, MMMU). Furthermore, it achieves these improvements with negligible computational overhead, maintaining inference speed comparable to greedy decoding.

**Potential Impact:**
The findings and the ReVisiT method have several potential impacts:
- **Improved LVLM reliability:** By reducing object hallucinations and improving visual grounding, ReVisiT can enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of LVLM outputs in various applications, from image captioning to visual question answering and multimodal reasoning.
- **Enhanced interpretability:** The method's approach of explicitly surfacing textual semantics from individual vision tokens offers a more interpretable way to understand how visual information influences text generation, potentially aiding in further model diagnostics and development.
- **Broad applicability:** Being training-free and model-agnostic, ReVisiT can be readily integrated into existing and future LVLMs, providing a generalizable solution for improving visual alignment across different architectures and tasks. Its efficiency makes it practical for real-world deployments where inference latency is a critical factor.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations. ReVisiT's reliance on the semantics encoded in vision tokens could lead to overemphasis on visually salient cues, potentially at the expense of implicit, commonsense, or non-visual contextual information. While adaptive interpolation schemes might offer a more balanced approach, this remains an area for future exploration. The method also exclusively relies on the model's internal representations, limiting its ability to correct factual errors or hallucinations stemming from pretraining data or requiring external knowledge. However, its modular nature suggests compatibility with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines. Finally, the empirical analysis was restricted to open-source LVLMs in the 7–32B parameter range due to computational constraints, thus direct transferability to larger or proprietary models is not claimed.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The work utilizes publicly available benchmarks and LVLM checkpoints, adhering to their original licensing and usage conditions. As ReVisiT operates on existing LVLMs, it inherits any biases or failure modes present in those underlying models and their training corpora. While it aims to improve visual grounding, it does not introduce new training data or objectives for mitigating social biases. The authors note that deployments of LVLMs enhanced with ReVisiT, especially in high-stakes applications, should include appropriate human oversight and domain-specific safeguards.
