# TimeSearch-R: Adaptive Temporal Search for Long-Form Video Understanding via Self-Verification Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TimeSearch-R, developed by researchers at ByteDance and Peking University, introduces an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for adaptive temporal search in long-form video understanding. The system incorporates a Completeness Self-Verification mechanism and achieves new state-of-the-art performance, including a 4.1% accuracy improvement on LongVideoBench.

## Problem

- Large Video-Language Models (LVLMs) and multimodal AI often rely on static frame sampling, which is inefficient and lacks the adaptability of human visual systems for long-form video content.
- Existing interactive video agents depend on manually designed, heuristic-based temporal search strategies, leading to suboptimal and inflexible workflows.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to interactive temporal search in videos presents challenges like insufficient temporal exploration and inconsistent logical reasoning when rewards are based solely on final outcomes.

## Method

- **TimeSearch-R** reformulates ==temporal search== as an interleaved text-video thinking process, enabling the model to dynamically generate search instructions and retrieve relevant clips during reasoning.
- The framework employs Group Relative Policy Optimization with Completeness Self-Verification (GRPO-CSV), a novel RL algorithm that provides intermediate supervision to ensure sufficient video exploration and consistent logical reasoning.
- A two-stage data filtering pipeline is designed to curate high-quality datasets for both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, addressing linguistic biases and noisy samples.

## Results

- TimeSearch-R achieves a temporal F1 score of 8.1 on Haystack-LVBench, more than tripling the previous best, and a visual similarity F1 of 69.2, surpassing existing methods.
- The model obtains **52.1%** QA accuracy on Haystack-LVBench and **53.5%** on Haystack-Ego4D, outperforming advanced API models like GPT-4o.
- It establishes new state-of-the-art performance on long-form video understanding benchmarks, with **66.6%** accuracy on VideoMME and **60.1%** on LongVideoBench, a **4.1%** improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is essential for developing initial search capabilities, while Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training significantly enhances reasoning consistency and overall understanding.
> - The Completeness Self-Verification (CSV) mechanism is crucial for mitigating issues of insufficient temporal exploration and inconsistent reasoning in RL, providing stability to the training process.
> - Meticulous two-stage data filtering is critical for effective RL training, as it curates challenging datasets that necessitate genuine visual reasoning over linguistic shortcuts and noisy samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in artificial intelligence: **long-form video understanding**. Modern video content, ranging from user-generated footage to professional productions, often spans minutes to hours, presenting an immense data volume (tens of thousands of frames). Effectively processing and extracting relevant information from such videos is computationally expensive and difficult for current models.

Existing Large Video-Language Models (LVLMs) and multimodal AI typically rely on **static frame sampling** (e.g., uniform sampling or heuristic-based strategies). This approach is fundamentally limited because it lacks the adaptability of human visual systems, which perform adaptive temporal search, iteratively refining focus based on intermediate findings. Hand-crafted search strategies, as seen in works like VideoAgent (Wang et al., 2024) and T* (Ye et al., 2025), attempt to bridge this gap by using Large Language Models (LLMs) to orchestrate external tools for frame retrieval. While these interactive agents offer improvements, they are constrained by manually designed workflows, which often lead to suboptimal search strategies and limited end-to-end optimization.

TimeSearch-R introduces a significant paradigm shift by advocating for **end-to-end learning of optimal temporal search strategies** directly from data. It reformulates temporal search as "interleaved text-video thinking," extending the "Thinking with Images" concept (Su et al., 2025) to the dynamic video domain. This approach contrasts sharply with prior methods by integrating video clip searching seamlessly into the reasoning process rather than treating it as a separate, tool-based operation.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself within the evolving landscape of **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for multimodal reasoning**. While RL has been successfully applied to textual search (Jin et al., 2025) and spatial search in images (Zheng et al., 2025) using algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), its application to *interactive temporal search in long videos* is largely unexplored. Existing video reasoning models leveraging RL, such as Video-R1 (Feng et al., 2025), primarily focus on text-only reasoning based on pre-sampled frames, without dynamic visual interaction. TimeSearch-R directly tackles the unique challenges of applying RL to *dynamic video exploration*, aiming to overcome issues like insufficient temporal exploration and inconsistent logical reasoning that arise when rewards are solely based on final outcomes. By doing so, it pushes the boundaries of how AI can intelligently navigate and comprehend vast amounts of visual information in temporal sequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an advanced framework, TimeSearch-R, that enables Large Video-Language Models (LVLMs) to perform **adaptive temporal search for long-form video understanding** by learning optimal search strategies directly from data.

This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and challenges:

- **Bridging the Human-Model Gap:** Humans naturally employ adaptive temporal search, dynamically focusing on relevant segments of a video based on evolving understanding. Current LVLMs, however, largely rely on static, pre-defined frame sampling, which is inefficient and rigid. The motivation is to imbue models with a more human-like, interactive video exploration capability.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Hand-Crafted Strategies:** Existing interactive video agents (e.g., VideoAgent, T*) depend on manually designed workflows for temporal search. These heuristic-based approaches are inherently suboptimal and lack the flexibility to adapt to diverse video content and complex reasoning tasks. The paper aims to replace these static strategies with an end-to-end learned approach.
- **Addressing RL Challenges in Video Reasoning:** While Reinforcement Learning (RL) holds promise for learning complex behaviors, applying it to interactive temporal search in videos presents unique difficulties. Standard RL, with its outcome-only reward structure, can lead to:
 - **Insufficient Temporal Exploration:** Models might arrive at correct answers through partial evidence or linguistic bias without thoroughly exploring the video, missing critical visual information.
 - **Inconsistent Logical Reasoning:** The model's intermediate thinking processes might become disconnected from its final answers, leading to plausible but unfounded reasoning.
- **Curating High-Quality Training Data:** Existing long-video datasets often contain many samples solvable through linguistic shortcuts (without actual visual reasoning) or noisy samples that are unsolvable even with extensive search. This "data quality" issue hinders effective learning of temporal search and reasoning, motivating the need for a specialized dataset construction pipeline.

To address these motivations, TimeSearch-R sets out to:
- Reformulate temporal search as an **interleaved text-video thinking process**, where the model actively searches for relevant clips as part of its reasoning.
- Introduce **GRPO with Completeness Self-Verification (GRPO-CSV)**, a novel RL algorithm designed to provide supervision for intermediate search decisions, ensuring sufficient video exploration and consistent logical reasoning.
- Develop a **two-stage data filtering pipeline** to create a high-quality, challenging dataset that forces models to engage in genuine temporal search and reasoning.
- Demonstrate the superiority of its approach on both dedicated **temporal search benchmarks** and broader **long-form video understanding tasks**.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TimeSearch-R tackles adaptive temporal search by reformulating it as an **interleaved text-video thinking process** and employing a novel Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithm for end-to-end optimization.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation: Interleaved Text-Video Thinking

The core idea is to enable the model to dynamically interact with the video. Given a video $V$ and a question $Q$, an initial preview $\tilde{V}$ is uniformly sampled. At each step $k$:
1. The policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ generates a textual reasoning $T_k$.
2. If $T_k$ contains a search instruction, a **video environment** executes it. The instruction specifies temporal boundaries ($t_s, t_e$) and a textual query ($q_k$).
3. The environment retrieves a clip $V_k \subseteq V$ containing $F$ frames, which is appended to the Chain of Thought (CoT).
4. This interaction continues until a final answer $A$ is generated or a reasoning budget is reached.
This process effectively decomposes into "Temporal Search" (generating $C$) and "Answer Prediction" (generating $A$ from $C$).

#### 3.2. Dynamic Video Frames with Search Function

To implement the frame retrieval, the model outputs temporal boundaries and a query. The `search` function then identifies relevant frames:
- It uses a small Vision-Language Model (VLM), such as SigLIP, to calculate similarity between frames within the specified temporal clip and relevance to the textual query.
- The most informative $F$ frames are sampled using **Determinantal Point Process (DPP)**. DPP is chosen for its ability to balance **query relevance** and **diversity**, penalizing redundancy among selected frames.
- Each selected frame is augmented with an explicit absolute timestamp (e.g., "12.3s") before its image token sequence to maintain temporal grounding, especially for sparse and non-uniform sampling.

#### 3.3. GRPO with Completeness Self-Verification (GRPO-CSV)

To overcome the limitations of standard RL (insufficient exploration and inconsistent reasoning), the authors introduce GRPO-CSV, which complements outcome-based rewards with supervision over intermediate search steps.

- **Completeness Self-Verification (CSV) Mechanism:**
 - During the GRPO rollout, the policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ generates a text-video interleaved CoT $C$ and a final answer $A$.
 - For CSV, the video clips from $C$ form a *dynamic frame set* $V_c$.
 - The *same policy model* is then required to re-answer the question $Q$ using *only* $V_c$ (yielding $A_c$), *without* performing any further temporal searching.
 - The expectation is that $A_c$ should be consistent with the original answer $A$, indicating sufficient visual evidence.

- **Reward Components:** The total reward $R$ is a sum of three components:
 - **Completeness Reward ($R_c$):** $R_c = \mathbb{1}[\text{Acc}(A, A^*) > 0.5] \cdot \text{Acc}(A_c, A^*)$. This reward is only activated if the *original* answer $A$ is correct, ensuring that CSV rewards are applied to promising trajectories. It promotes acquiring sufficient visual evidence and consistency between the reasoning process and the final answer.
 - **Format Reward ($R_{fmt}$):** A binary reward (1 or 0) for adhering to the predefined thinking and tool-call patterns (`<think>...</think><tool_call>...</tool_call>` or `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`). This enforces structural integrity of the reasoning trajectory.
 - **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** A binary reward (1 or 0) for the correctness of the final answer $A$. For multiple-choice, it's an exact match; for open-ended, an LLM-as-a-Judge approach (Zheng et al., 2023) assesses semantic agreement.

#### 3.4. Model Training

A two-stage training scheme is employed:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This serves as a "cold start" to guide the model in adopting the correct reasoning format and generating initial search queries. GPT-4o is used to generate the text-video interleaved reasoning processes. Crucially, temporal search results are *masked* during SFT to force the model to learn meaningful temporal windows and textual queries independently. The objective is standard cross-entropy loss over reasoning tokens.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Building on the SFT cold start, GRPO-CSV is applied. The model, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, uses an AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-6, a KL penalty coefficient $\beta = 0.005$, and a batch size of 4 with 8 rollouts per prompt. Training limits each search to 8 frames and up to 8 search steps in total.

#### 3.5. Dataset Construction

A **two-stage data filtering pipeline** is designed to curate high-quality datasets for both SFT and RL, specifically addressing linguistic bias and noisy samples:
1. **Stage 1: Visual Dependency Filtering:** Samples solvable by Qwen2.5-VL using only 4 uniformly sampled frames are filtered out. This encourages the model to rely on visual information beyond simple linguistic shortcuts.
2. **Stage 2: Search Usefulness Filtering:** Samples that remain unsolvable even with multiple temporal searches and numerous video frames (indicating noise or fundamental unanswerability) are discarded.
The dataset is further enriched by incorporating samples from Haystack-Ego4D, VideoMarathon (Panda-70M), and CinePile to enhance diversity across egocentric and exocentric views, motion patterns, and narrative structures. Manual annotation is performed for VideoMarathon samples to ensure accuracy and prevent erroneous ground truths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TimeSearch-R demonstrates significant improvements across both temporal search and long-form video understanding benchmarks, establishing new state-of-the-art results.

#### 4.1. Temporal Search Performance

Evaluated on Haystack-LVBench and Haystack-Ego4D, TimeSearch-R showcases its superior temporal exploration capabilities:
- **Haystack-LVBench:** With a budget of ~9 keyframes, TimeSearch-R achieves a temporal F1 score of **8.1**, which is more than triple the previous best (T* at 2.5). In visual similarity, it reaches an F1 score of **69.2**, surpassing VideoAgent (64.7) and even outperforming methods with larger keyframe budgets.
- **Question-Answering (QA) Accuracy:** TimeSearch-R achieves **52.1% accuracy on Haystack-LVBench** and **53.5% on Haystack-Ego4D**, consistently outperforming advanced API models like GPT-4o. These results validate the effectiveness of end-to-end learned search strategies over human-designed workflows.

#### 4.2. Long-Form Video Understanding Performance

On benchmarks like VideoMME, MLVU, and LongVideoBench, TimeSearch-R establishes new SOTA:
- **VideoMME:** TimeSearch-R achieves an overall accuracy of **66.6%**, representing a 1.5% improvement over the base model Qwen2.5-VL. Notably, the gains are more pronounced as video duration increases (e.g., +1.4% on long videos vs. +0.5% on short videos), confirming the value of temporal search for longer content.
- **MLVU:** The model achieves **71.5%** accuracy, a 1.3% improvement over the base model.
- **LongVideoBench:** TimeSearch-R reaches **60.1% accuracy**, demonstrating a substantial 4.1% improvement over Qwen2.5-VL and a 2.0% improvement over the advanced video reasoning model Video-R1.
- Compared to other video search agents like VideoAgent and T*, TimeSearch-R shows significant performance leads (e.g., +10.6% and +2.5% on VideoMME respectively), emphasizing the benefit of end-to-end optimization.
- A crucial finding: Directly applying temporal search to Qwen2.5-VL via CoT prompting *without* RL training actually *degrades* performance, underscoring the necessity of the proposed GRPO-CSV post-training.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Scheme Impact:**
 - **SFT is essential for search capability:** Without SFT, the model cannot perform search well (temporal F1 0.0). SFT dramatically improves temporal F1 to 7.8 and search completeness to 60.5%.
 - **RL enhances reasoning:** While RL provides modest improvements to temporal similarity and completeness, its primary benefit is boosting overall understanding. It improves reasoning consistency by 2.6% and raises QA accuracy from 59.2% (SFT) to 66.6%.
- **GRPO-CSV Component Analysis:**
 - **GRPO alone is insufficient:** Without Completeness Self-Verification (CSV), GRPO training leads to reduced completeness (from 60.5% to 57.2%) and temporal F1 (from 7.8 to 7.4), demonstrating that outcome-only rewards are inadequate for comprehensive temporal exploration.
 - **CSV ensures training stability:** Removing CSV causes training instability and collapse, leading the model to cease search calls.
 - **GRPO-CSV with accuracy reward yields best QA:** While completeness reward alone boosts completeness and consistency, combining it with accuracy reward results in the best overall QA performance.
- **Data Composition Analysis:**
 - **Data filtering is critical:** Without the two-stage data filtering, RL training substantially degrades performance compared to the baseline, as linguistic biases create zero advantage in GRPO (all rollouts are trivially correct), hindering learning.
 - **Domain diversity is beneficial:** After filtering, training solely on egocentric data recovers baseline performance. Incorporating exocentric data significantly improves general QA accuracy.
 - **Generalization of learned patterns:** RL training, even with general QA tasks, markedly boosts the model's temporal and action reasoning capabilities (e.g., +7.4% temporal, +5.7% action), indicating that TimeSearch-R learns fundamental cognitive patterns that generalize well.

#### 4.4. Case Studies

The paper highlights emergent search patterns from the end-to-end RL training, mimicking human cognition:
- **Hypothesis-driven search:** The model forms hypotheses from limited context and performs targeted searches to find supporting evidence.
- **Confirmation or elimination:** Multi-faceted search strategies are used to gather additional evidence, confirm hypotheses, or eliminate uncertainties when initial evidence is insufficient.
- **Sequential search:** For tasks requiring understanding sequential relationships, the model performs segment-by-segment analysis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TimeSearch-R represents a significant leap forward in long-form video understanding, carrying several implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Video Understanding:** The work pioneers an **end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for adaptive temporal search**, fundamentally shifting the paradigm from static frame sampling and hand-crafted heuristics to dynamic, interactive, and learned search strategies. This approach more closely mirrors human cognitive processes for video analysis, making video understanding systems more intelligent and efficient.

2. **Enhanced Model Capabilities and State-of-the-Art Performance:** By achieving new state-of-the-art results across multiple challenging temporal search and long-form video understanding benchmarks (e.g., a 4.1% improvement on LongVideoBench over a strong baseline), TimeSearch-R demonstrates the practical effectiveness and superiority of learned search policies. Its ability to extract greater gains on longer videos highlights its relevance for real-world applications with extensive video content.

3. **Improved Interpretability and Explainability:** The framework's interleaved text-video reasoning traces provide transparent insights into the model's decision-making process. The introduction of **completeness** and **consistency** as quantitative metrics for assessing the quality of searched frames and reasoning steps makes temporal search decisions auditable and interpretable. This is crucial for building trust and reliability in AI systems that operate on complex, high-stakes visual data.

4. **Scalable Weakly-Supervised Process Rewards:** The novel **Completeness Self-Verification (CSV)** mechanism offers a powerful approach to providing supervision over intermediate search steps *without requiring explicit, fine-grained frame-level annotations*. This weakly-supervised, outcome-based process reward system addresses a major bottleneck in training complex interactive AI systems, making the method more scalable and applicable to diverse domains where manual annotation is prohibitively expensive.

5. **Robustness Against Data Biases:** The meticulously designed two-stage data filtering pipeline directly tackles pervasive issues of linguistic bias and noisy samples in existing datasets. By ensuring that models learn genuine visual reasoning rather than relying on shortcuts, TimeSearch-R contributes to more robust and visually grounded video understanding systems. The demonstrated generalization of learned patterns (e.g., improved temporal and action reasoning) further indicates the fundamental nature of the cognitive skills acquired.

6. **Broader Applications:** The principles and methodology introduced by TimeSearch-R could have wide-ranging impacts beyond academic benchmarks. Industries reliant on analyzing vast amounts of long-form video, such as surveillance, media production, education (lecture analysis), sports analytics, and even medical imaging (e.g., analyzing long surgical videos), could benefit from more adaptive, efficient, and interpretable video understanding capabilities.

In conclusion, TimeSearch-R makes meaningful progress towards developing more intelligent, adaptive, and explainable AI systems for long-form video understanding, paving the way for future research into dynamic and interactive multimodal reasoning.
