# STARE-VLA: Progressive Stage-Aware Reinforcement for Fine-Tuning Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> StARe-VLA introduces Stage-Aware Reinforcement (StARe), a rule-based module that segments long-horizon manipulation trajectories into semantic stages (e.g. Reach, Grasp, Transport, Place) via end-effector geometry, then feeds stage-wise costs and potential-shaped rewards into offline and online VLA fine-tuning, reaching 98.0% on SimplerEnv and 96.4% on ManiSkill3, beating prior state of the art by 5.4 and 25.9 points.

## Problem

- Trajectory-level preference methods like TPO assign a single scalar to a whole rollout, making credit assignment across long, causally chained action stages highly ambiguous.
- Binary success/failure preference labels ignore relative quality among partially successful trajectories, discarding information about which specific stage went wrong.
- PPO fine-tuning on sparse terminal rewards gives insufficient guidance for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks, causing stagnation on precision-critical stages like stacking or upright placement.

## Method

- **StARe**: a stage separator that detects semantic stage transitions from end-effector translation/orientation thresholds (e.g. contact, lift height, target distance) and a stage calculator that computes per-stage Euclidean cost and potential-based dense intra-stage rewards.
- ==Stage-Aware TPO (StA-TPO)==: extends trajectory preference optimization by subtracting a stage cost penalty from the log-likelihood ratio and only comparing stage pairs whose preceding stage succeeded, yielding hierarchical stage-level preference gradients.
- ==Stage-Aware PPO (StA-PPO)== plus the ==Imitation to Preference to Interaction (IPI)== pipeline: replaces PPO's sparse reward with potential-shaped dense stage rewards online, then chains SFT, StA-TPO, and StA-PPO serially into one fine-tuning framework.

## Results

- IPI reaches **98.0%** average success on SimplerEnv-WidowX and **96.4%** on ManiSkill3-Franka, outperforming the strongest RL baselines RL4VLA (**92.5%**) and pi-RL by **5.4** and **25.9** points respectively.
- SFT-to-StA-PPO alone already lifts ManiSkill3 OpenVLA-7B success from **70.5%** (RL4VLA) to **93.4%**, a **22.9-point** gain, with the largest gains on contact-rich LiftPegUpright (**32.0%** to **89.7%**).
- Stage-toggle ablation shows disabling StARe at early stages (Reach, Grasp) costs **2-11** points, while disabling it at final precision stages (Place, Upright) drops success by over **20%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward density should track the causal, ordered structure of a task: dense stage-aligned signals matter most at the final precision-critical stage, not uniformly across all stages.
> - Treating action trajectories like flexible-order language sentences is a mismatch; enforcing semantic and causal stage continuity in the optimization objective yields large, disproportionate gains on precision-critical substeps.
> - Offline preference alignment and online interaction fine-tuning need not be disjoint; unifying them under a shared stage decomposition (imitation then preference then interaction) compounds gains rather than being redundant.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "StARe-VLA" is positioned at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, reinforcement learning (RL), and robotic manipulation, a rapidly evolving field fueled by advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs).

**Current State of VLA Models and Fine-tuning:**
The paper acknowledges the emergence of large-scale VLA models (e.g., RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, Pi0) as powerful generalist policies for robotic manipulation. These models unify image, language, and action modalities, allowing robots to interpret complex instructions and generate executable actions. Pre-trained on massive multimodal datasets, they offer strong priors for adaptation to diverse downstream tasks through fine-tuning.

Existing fine-tuning techniques, such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), have been largely adopted from LLM and VLM domains. However, the paper critically identifies a fundamental mismatch: these methods often treat long-horizon action trajectories as undifferentiated linguistic sequences. This approach leads to several problems:
1. **Coarse Credit Assignment:** Optimizing over an entire trajectory makes it difficult to pinpoint which specific actions or sub-sequences were responsible for success or failure.
2. **Unstable Training:** The large optimization space and sparse rewards inherent in long-horizon robotic tasks contribute to unstable and inefficient training.
3. **Ignoring Action Semantics:** Unlike language, where meaning is flexible, action trajectories are composed of *causally chained, semantically meaningful stages* that vary in difficulty (e.g., Reach → Grasp → Transport → Place). Current methods fail to exploit this inherent structure.

**Relation to Prior Work:**

- **Long-horizon Robotic Manipulation in RL:** The paper situates itself within a long line of research addressing the challenges of long-horizon tasks in RL, such as sparse rewards and credit assignment. Previous efforts have explored hierarchical planning (Plan-Seq-Learn), continuous action chunks (AC3), demonstration augmentation (DEMO3), LLM-generated sub-goals (RoboHorizon), and skill libraries (ARCH, SARM). StARe-VLA builds on this inspiration of decomposing tasks, but takes a "further step" by explicitly defining and leveraging "semantically meaningful stages" within trajectories, offering *denser feedback* and enabling *progressive optimization* at a finer granularity than typical sub-goal approaches.

- **RL Fine-tuning for LLMs:** The authors draw parallels with RL fine-tuning for LLMs (RLHF, DPO, RLAIF, RAFT, GRPO). They note that even Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) that perform multi-step reasoning (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) face similar challenges of sparse rewards and difficult credit assignment, reinforcing the motivation for stage-aware approaches.

- **RL Fine-tuning for VLAs:** The paper extensively reviews recent RL fine-tuning methods specifically for VLA models (e.g., GRAPE's TPO, ConRFT, ReinboT, RL4VLA, VLA-RL, SimpleVLA-RL). It critiques these approaches for predominantly operating at the *trajectory level*, thus inheriting the issues of sparse rewards, coarse credit assignment, and difficult exploration. StARe-VLA directly contrasts with these by proposing a *stage-aware* reinforcement framework that decomposes trajectories and assigns stage-level rewards, aiming for denser, more interpretable feedback and progressive optimization.

In essence, StARe-VLA addresses a critical missing piece in the VLA fine-tuning landscape: the explicit modeling and exploitation of the inherent multi-stage structure of robot action trajectories. By providing a more granular and semantically aligned reward signal, this work aims to significantly enhance the stability, sample efficiency, and overall performance of VLA models in complex robotic manipulation tasks, moving beyond the "monolithic trajectory-level optimization" paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind StARe-VLA stems from a fundamental observation about robotic action trajectories: unlike flexible linguistic sequences, actions in robotic manipulation progress through a series of *causally chained, semantically distinct stages*, each potentially varying in learning difficulty. For example, a "pick-and-place" task inherently involves "Reach," "Grasp," "Transport," and "Place" in a strict order, where "Grasp" and "Place" might be more challenging due to precision requirements.

Existing fine-tuning methods for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, largely borrowed from large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), typically treat these long-horizon actions as undifferentiated sequential data. This **trajectory-level optimization** approach leads to two significant problems:
1. **Coarse Credit Assignment:** When an entire trajectory is judged as a success or failure, it is difficult for the model to ascertain which specific actions or sub-sequences contributed positively or negatively. This ambiguity hinders efficient learning and precise error correction.
2. **Unstable Training:** The vast optimization space of long-horizon tasks, coupled with often sparse terminal rewards, results in unstable training and poor sample efficiency.

Therefore, the **key objectives** of this research are:
- To design a novel module, **Stage-Aware Reinforcement (StARe)**, that can robustly decompose long-horizon action trajectories into semantically meaningful, progressive stages.
- To enable the provision of **dense, interpretable, and stage-aligned reinforcement signals** at each stage, moving beyond sparse, trajectory-level feedback.
- To integrate StARe into established reinforcement learning paradigms, specifically **Trajectory-wise Preference Optimization (TPO)** and **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, to yield more effective stage-aware fine-tuning algorithms: **Stage-Aware TPO (StA-TPO)** and **Stage-Aware PPO (StA-PPO)**.
- To propose a comprehensive, serial fine-tuning pipeline, **Imitation → Preference → Interaction (IPI)**, that systematically leverages supervised learning, offline stage-wise preferences, and online stage-aware interactions to achieve robust and highly accurate VLA model performance.
- Ultimately, the overarching goal is to significantly improve the action accuracy, stability, and sample efficiency of VLA models in complex robotic manipulation tasks by explicitly accounting for the multi-stage nature of robot behaviors, thereby enabling more precise credit assignment and progressive learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the proposed **Stage-Aware Reinforcement (StARe)** module and its integration into a three-step fine-tuning pipeline called **Imitation → Preference → Interaction (IPI)**.

#### 3.1. Stage-Aware Reinforcement (StARe)

StARe is a plug-in module designed to decompose long-horizon action trajectories into semantically meaningful stages and provide dense, stage-aligned feedback. It comprises two main components:

- **Stage Separator:** This component identifies stage transitions within an action trajectory `τ` based on task-specific rules and geometric constraints. Instead of arbitrary temporal cuts, it detects "semantically meaningful manipulation events." For instance, in a pick-and-place task, stages like Reach, Grasp, Transport, and Place are identified by thresholds on the end-effector's translation and orientation (e.g., contact with object, object lifted, object within goal distance). The entry condition of stage *k* coincides with the terminal condition of stage *k-1*, ensuring causal progression. This process groups timesteps with the same stage label into segments `τ^(k)`.

- **Stage Calculator:** For each identified stage segment `τ^(k)`, this component computes:
 - **Stage-wise Costs (`ℓ_k(τ^(k))`):** These quantify how well a stage was executed. For the Reach stage, it's defined as the mean Euclidean distance between the end-effector and the target object over the stage duration. A lower cost signifies better execution. These costs allow for granular evaluation of partial successes and failures within a trajectory. Detailed cost functions are provided for other stages (Grasp, Transport, Place, Push/Pull, Lift, Upright, Goal), typically based on positional or orientational deviations from the desired state.
 - **Intra-stage Dense Rewards (`r'_t`):** To provide continuous guidance, StARe employs potential-based reward shaping. For an active stage *k*, a per-timestep potential `Φ_k(s_t)` is defined. For Reach, this potential is a sigmoid function of `(1 - normalized_distance_to_object)`, increasing as the end-effector approaches the target. The shaped reward `r'_t` augments the sparse environment reward `r_t` as `r'_t = r_t + γ(Φ_k(s_t+1) - Φ_k(s_t))`. This transformation converts sparse terminal rewards into dense, progressive signals.

#### 3.2. From StARe to Stage-Aware Fine-tuning Algorithms

- **Stage-Aware Trajectory-Wise Preference Optimization (StA-TPO):** This offline fine-tuning method extends standard TPO by incorporating StARe. Instead of evaluating preferences on entire trajectories, StA-TPO computes preferences at the stage level. The original `q(τ)` (log-likelihood ratio) is modified to `ˆq(τ^(k)) = q(τ^(k)) - λℓ_k(τ^(k))`, where `λ` is a penalty weight and `ℓ_k` is the stage cost. This modification allows StA-TPO to propagate precise gradient signals to specific action stages. It enables credit assignment not only between successful and failed *trajectories* but also among varying degrees of *stage-level success*, thus providing finer-grained supervision. Preference pairs `(τ^(k)+, τ^(k)-)` are only considered if the preceding stage `τ^(k-1)` was successfully completed, ensuring progressive consistency.

- **Stage-Aware Proximal Policy Optimization (StA-PPO):** For online fine-tuning, StARe is integrated into PPO. During online rollouts, the stage separator determines stage transitions in real-time. At each timestep within an active stage, the stage calculator produces the shaped reward `r'_t`. This dense, stage-aligned feedback replaces the sparse `r_t` in the standard PPO objective, `L_PPO`, turning it into `L_StA-PPO`. This significantly accelerates policy learning and stabilizes intra-stage updates, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards.

#### 3.3. The Imitation → Preference → Interaction (IPI) Pipeline

To sufficiently fine-tune pre-trained VLA models, the authors propose a serial, three-step pipeline:

1. **Imitation (SFT):** The VLA model is first initialized and warmed up through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using expert demonstrations. This provides a strong behavioral prior.
2. **Preference (StA-TPO):** The SFT-initialized policy is then further optimized using offline stage-aware preferences via StA-TPO. This refines the policy by learning from stage-level comparisons of successful and failed behaviors.
3. **Interaction (StA-PPO):** Finally, the policy is refined for robustness and performance through online exploration in environments, guided by the dense, stage-aware rewards provided by StA-PPO.

The IPI framework offers a comprehensive approach that systematically integrates supervised learning, offline preference alignment, and online reinforcement learning, leveraging the multi-stage structure of robotic trajectories for more precise credit assignment and robust learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of StARe-VLA, particularly through its full Imitation → Preference → Interaction (IPI) pipeline, demonstrates substantial advancements in fine-tuning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **SimplerEnv:** IPI achieved an impressive 98.0% average success rate on the four canonical single-object tasks in the SimplerEnv-WidowX benchmark. This represents a significant improvement of +5.4 percentage points over the prior state-of-the-art RL fine-tuning method, RL4VLA (92.5%).
- **ManiSkill3:** On the more challenging ManiSkill3-Franka tasks (including stacking and contact-rich manipulations), IPI achieved a 96.4% average success rate. This is a dramatic improvement of +25.9 percentage points over RL4VLA (70.5%), nearly solving these complex benchmark tasks.
- **Backbone Agnostic Improvements:** The benefits of StARe-VLA were consistent across two widely used VLA backbones, OpenVLA-7B and Pi0.5_base, showing that the stage-aware reinforcement design is a general enhancement rather than being tied to a specific model architecture. For Pi0.5-based models, IPI also showed strong performance (91.5% avg success), outperforming `π_RL` (79.6%) by +11.9 points.

#### 4.2. Benefits of Stage-Aware PPO (StA-PPO)

- **Accelerated Convergence and Higher Asymptotic Performance:** Learning curves (Figure 4) clearly illustrate that StA-PPO consistently accelerates convergence and achieves higher asymptotic success rates compared to standard PPO (which itself improves over SFT).
- **Crucial for Complex Tasks:** The most significant performance gains from StA-PPO were observed in challenging tasks requiring high precision or complex contact interactions, such as "LiftPegUpright" and "StackGreenOnYellow." For simpler, short-horizon tasks (e.g., basic pick-and-place or push/pull), StA-PPO still offered faster convergence and reduced variance, even if final success rates eventually matched those of PPO. This highlights that stage-aware guidance is particularly vital where strict geometric accuracy or multi-stage coordination is paramount.

#### 4.3. Benefits of Stage-Aware TPO (StA-TPO)

- **Improved Conditional Stage Success:** By analyzing conditional stage success rates (P(stage_k | stage_k-1)), the paper shows that StA-TPO provides clear advantages over standard TPO. The largest improvements were concentrated in critical stages such as "grasp," "place," and "upright" (Figure 5). These stages, often requiring precise geometric alignment, are decisive for overall task success, explaining why StA-TPO significantly boosts final outcomes for tasks like stacking and peg lifting. StA-TPO's ability to provide credit assignment among varying degrees of stage-level success proves more effective than binary trajectory-level preferences.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Stage-Aware Signals

- **Criticality of Final Stages:** A stage toggle ablation (Figure 6) demonstrated the importance of StARe signals at different manipulation phases. Disabling StARe in early stages (e.g., Reach or Grasp) led to moderate performance drops, suggesting that later corrective actions could partially mitigate early errors. However, removing StARe at final, precision-critical phases (e.g., "Place" in stacking or "Upright" in peg lifting) resulted in the largest degradation, with success rates dropping by over 20%. This decisively confirms that StARe's guidance is most valuable in stages where geometric accuracy, stability, and fine-grained control directly determine task completion.

#### 4.5. Efficacy of the IPI Pipeline

- The seamless integration of supervised fine-tuning, offline stage-aware preference learning, and online stage-aware interaction within the IPI pipeline proves to be highly effective. The sequential application of these methods culminates in the strongest overall performance, validating the comprehensive and progressive nature of the proposed framework.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the central hypothesis: explicitly modeling the multi-stage structure of robot trajectories and providing dense, stage-aligned reinforcement signals is a critical missing ingredient for effective VLA fine-tuning. StARe-VLA delivers state-of-the-art performance across diverse manipulation tasks and VLA backbones, driven by its enhanced credit assignment and stable optimization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The StARe-VLA framework introduces a stage-centric perspective to the fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, marking a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Addressing a Fundamental Limitation in VLA Fine-Tuning

The most critical contribution of this work is the explicit recognition and systematic exploitation of the inherent multi-stage structure of robotic action trajectories. By moving beyond monolithic, trajectory-level optimization, StARe-VLA tackles the persistent problems of coarse credit assignment and unstable training that plague existing VLA fine-tuning methods adapted from language models. This paradigm shift provides a more natural and effective way for VLA models to learn complex, long-horizon tasks.

#### 5.2. Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance and Robustness

The empirical results are compelling, demonstrating substantial gains across various benchmarks (SimplerEnv and ManiSkill3) and VLA backbones (OpenVLA-7B and Pi0.5). Achieving success rates of 98.0% and 96.4% on these benchmarks, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods by significant margins, underscores the effectiveness and robustness of the StARe-VLA approach. This level of performance brings VLA models closer to real-world deployment for a broader range of manipulation tasks.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Training Stability

By providing dense, stage-aligned reinforcement signals, StARe-VLA (particularly StA-PPO) accelerates convergence and reduces variance during online training. This improved sample efficiency is crucial in robotics, where real-world data collection can be costly and time-consuming. The stable optimization enabled by stage-aware signals is also key to reliably fine-tuning large, complex VLA models.

#### 5.4. Improved Interpretability and Diagnosability

The decomposition of trajectories into semantically meaningful stages, coupled with stage-wise costs and potentials, offers a higher degree of interpretability. Researchers and engineers can now diagnose precisely which stage of a task a robot is struggling with (e.g., "Grasp" vs. "Place"), rather than only knowing that the overall task failed. This fine-grained feedback facilitates targeted policy improvement and debugging.

#### 5.5. Generalizability Across Tasks and Backbones

The consistent performance gains across diverse tasks (pick-and-place, stacking, pushing, pulling, reorientation) and different VLA model backbones suggest that the StARe-VLA framework is broadly applicable. This indicates a generalizable solution for enhancing robotic capabilities, rather than a task-specific hack.

#### 5.6. Comprehensive and Coherent Fine-tuning Pipeline (IPI)

The Imitation → Preference → Interaction (IPI) pipeline provides a structured and theoretically sound approach to VLA model fine-tuning. By integrating supervised learning, offline stage-wise preference optimization, and online stage-aware reinforcement learning, IPI leverages different data modalities and learning signals synergistically, leading to robust and highly performant policies.

#### 5.7. Future Directions in Robotic Learning

The stage-centric perspective introduced by StARe-VLA "opens up promising directions for scalable, interpretable, and robust robotic learning." This could inspire future research into more sophisticated stage detection methods, adaptive stage definitions, and hierarchical learning architectures that leverage the semantic structure of robotic tasks even more deeply.

**Potential Societal Impact:**
The improved accuracy and robustness in robotic manipulation brought about by StARe-VLA could accelerate the adoption of general-purpose robots in various sectors, including manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and even domestic environments. Robots capable of reliably performing complex, long-horizon tasks, especially those requiring high precision or intricate interactions, can boost productivity, automate hazardous tasks, and assist in daily living. However, as noted in the Broader Impacts section, ensuring that these policies align with "human-preferred, semantically correct behaviors" is crucial to mitigate potential risks associated with biased preferences or misuse in unsafe settings. The enhanced interpretability of StARe could contribute to developing safer and more trustworthy autonomous systems.
