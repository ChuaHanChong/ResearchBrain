# Pointing-VLA: Typed Spatial Grounding Interfaces for Vision-Language-Action Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Pointing-VLA replaces text-coordinate output in vision-language-action models with typed hidden-state readouts: geometry-specific heads on a frozen Embodied-R1 backbone emit normalized points, object-functional grounding heatmaps, and visual trajectories. A deterministic contract binds OFG to PICK and Pointing to PLACE. It averages 72.9% on Bridge/WidowX, decodes 6.68-6.90x faster than text, and lifts real-robot success from 52.7% to 80.7%.

## Problem

- Prior VLA models serialize geometry as autoregressive text coordinates, spending tokens spelling numbers and requiring string parsing that can fail before any valid coordinate appears.
- A syntactically parseable text coordinate can still land outside the target region, so parsing success is an extra execution dependency rather than a grounding guarantee.
- Opaque action tokens hide spatial rationale from auditors, and collapsing points, dense affordances, and trajectories into one output distribution causes negative interference.

## Method

- ==Grounding Adapter== projects shared multimodal hidden states, and expert-specific learned queries with cross-attention produce grounded tokens feeding separate **Pointing**, **OFG**, and **VTG** decoders.
- OFG head ==FiLM==-modulates the visual feature map by the grounded summary before a convolutional heatmap decoder; its normalized peak becomes the functional contact for grasping.
- ==Stage-wise training== (geometry warm-up, ==LoRA== specialization, task continuation) plus ==GRPO==-style group-normalized refinement on the continuous point head with in-box reward and distance shaping.

## Results

- Sets **72.9%** average on four Bridge/WidowX tasks with collision-enabled CuRobo, beating MemoryVLA at **71.9%** and the strongest alternative interface composition by **14.6 points**.
- OFG lifts full Part-Affordance-2K accuracy from **40.9%** to **57.3%** over Embodied-R1 text generation; typed heads decode **6.68-6.90x** faster, and NORA-1.5 transfer cuts controller time over **20x**.
- Real PiPER deployment with pi-0.5 rises **52.7%** to **80.7%** over **150 trials**; pre-lift grasp failures drop **47 to 16**, tray-arrival failures **13 to 4**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Match the output geometry to the task: sparse points, dense part-level heatmaps, and temporal waypoints are distinct targets that degrade when serialized into one channel.
> - Deterministic stage-to-geometry contracts beat inference-time selectors; the OFG-PICK/Pointing-PLACE assignment gained 14.6 points over the next-best composition with no extra machinery.
> - Typed readouts are a portable add-on: they transfer to frozen backbones and other action policies, but post-grasp transfer failures remain, since guidance is open-loop.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly leveraging large vision-language-action (VLA) models to enable robots to understand observations, language instructions, and execute corresponding behaviors. This involves adapting large pre-trained models, such as those used in embodied multimodal models, Robotics Transformers (e.g., RT-1, RT-2), and open robot policies (e.g., Octo, OpenVLA), to diverse robot action spaces. These advancements have primarily focused on improving policy learning, action parameterization, and inference throughput, addressing the question of how perception and language should translate into actions.

However, a recurring challenge within this landscape is the interface through which VLA models communicate spatial information required for manipulation tasks. Current approaches often rely on autoregressive text coordinates or opaque action tokens. Autoregressive text coordinates, while convenient for prompting, introduce a serialization bottleneck, forcing the model to generate numbers as text, requiring subsequent parsing, and potentially discarding rich part-level spatial evidence. Opaque action tokens, conversely, hide the spatial rationale, making inspection by a robot's execution stack or human auditors difficult. These methods can lead to brittle interfaces, where generation failures (e.g., unparseable text) or semantic inaccuracies (e.g., parseable but incorrect coordinates) compromise task execution.

Prior work has recognized the importance of spatial structure for manipulation. Neuroscience offers concepts like "vision-for-action" and "population-readout" views, suggesting that spatial variables should be decoded from distributed internal activity rather than serialized. In robot learning, this is reflected in systems like Transporter Networks that preserve spatial feature maps, CLIPort that combines semantic and spatial pathways, and PerAct that discretizes 3D voxel actions. More recent grounding-centric VLA efforts, such as RoboPoint, FSD, and Embodied-R1, have extended these ideas to embodied reasoning, formalizing embodied pointing across various grounding types like referring-point, relational-region, object-functional grounding (OFG), and visual trajectory generation (VTG). Affordance datasets like AGD20K further underscore that actionable regions are often specific object parts.

Pointing-VLA positions itself as addressing a *complementary interface question* within this landscape: specifically, what *spatial representation* should be explicitly exposed by a VLA model *before* final execution? Instead of focusing on generating actions or improving backbone capacity, it focuses on the structured output of spatial intent in a robot-consumable format. It builds upon existing VLA backbones, particularly Embodied-R1, to provide a more direct, efficient, and inspectable spatial grounding interface.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and validate a robust, efficient, and inspectable spatial grounding interface for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) manipulation models, addressing the limitations of current text-coordinate generation and opaque action token approaches.

The motivation stems from several observed bottlenecks and failures in existing VLA model outputs:
1. **Brittle Spatial Grounding Interfaces:** Many manipulation subtasks necessitate an explicit spatial commitment (e.g., a precise contact point, a functional region, or a short visual path). Current VLA models often expose this spatial grounding through either:
 - **Autoregressive text coordinates:** This method forces the model to encode geometry as a sequence of numbers in text, which then requires parsing back into pixels for execution. This process is token-intensive, slows down downstream execution, introduces an additional dependency for parsing, and can discard dense part-level spatial evidence generated by the multimodal reasoning process. As illustrated in Figure 1, text generation can fail to produce a parseable coordinate, or generate a syntactically valid coordinate that is spatially incorrect, making parsing an unreliable guarantee of correct grounding.
 - **Opaque action tokens:** While avoiding parsing, these tokens abstract away the underlying spatial rationale. This opacity limits the ability of robot execution stacks or human auditors to inspect or correct the model's spatial intent.

2. **Mismatch Between Spatial Targets and Output Formats:** Referring points, functional regions (affordances), and visual trajectories are distinct types of spatial targets, each with specific geometric properties (sparse, dense part-level, temporally structured). The research posits that collapsing these varied targets into a single output distribution or serializing all of them as text can lead to negative interference during learning and execution, making downstream processes slower and less stable.

3. **Inspiration from Neuroscience:** The work draws motivation from the "vision-for-action" and "population-readout" views in neuroscience, which suggest that spatial variables should be decoded directly from distributed internal neural activity rather than being serialized through the system that represents them. This principle implies that robot-facing systems should directly consume geometry-specific representations.

To address these motivations, Pointing-VLA sets forth the following key objectives:
- To formulate embodied grounding as *typed interface prediction*, replacing serialized text coordinates with geometry-specific, robot-facing readouts.
- To introduce separate, lightweight hidden-state readouts for specific spatial targets:
 - **Pointing:** For sparse target points.
 - **Object-Functional Grounding (OFG):** For dense affordance heatmaps representing functional contact regions.
 - **Visual Trajectory Generation (VTG):** For short visual waypoint traces.
- To establish *explicit execution contracts* that bind each structured execution slot (e.g., PICK, PLACE) to the specific geometry it requires, thereby aligning embodied reasoning with downstream robot execution. For instance, a primary objective is to implement a fixed source-conditioned OFG-PICK/Pointing-PLACE contract.
- To validate the proposed interface through comprehensive evaluations, including:
 - Assessing native spatial grounding performance across different types of spatial targets.
 - Evaluating cross-dataset and cross-backbone transfer capabilities.
 - Measuring runtime efficiency compared to text decoding.
 - Demonstrating effectiveness in practical robot deployment scenarios, both in simulation (Bridge/WidowX) and on a real robot.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aim to establish typed spatial readouts as an efficient, inspectable, and effective interface between VLM reasoning and robot execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Pointing-VLA operationalizes the concept of typed interface prediction by directly decoding geometry-specific robot targets from shared multimodal hidden states, leveraging an existing embodied vision-language backbone.

#### 3.1. Hidden-State Spatial Readout Architecture (Figure 2)

The system builds upon an Embodied-R1 backbone, utilizing its shared multimodal states. This backbone, along with its base weights, is primarily frozen. A compact **Grounding Adapter** projects the multimodal sequence into a grounding space. This adapter uses learned queries specific to each spatial expert (point, OFG, VTG), but within a shared adapter body.

The core of the methodology lies in its three specialized **Geometry Heads (Decoders)**, each designed to output spatial information in its native geometric format:

- **Pointing Head:** This decoder uses a learned pointer query (`q_pt`) to select relevant grounded evidence. It directly outputs normalized `(ˆx, ˆy)` coordinates in `^2`, which are then scaled to pixel coordinates `(ˆu, ˆv)`. This eliminates the need for text generation or parsing. The prediction is evaluated using point-in-bbox (PIB) or point-in-mask (PIM).

- **Object-Functional Grounding (OFG) Head:** Unlike Pointing, OFG preserves spatial extent. It modulates the visual tower's 2D feature map (`F_vis`) using FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) conditioned on a global grounded summary (`g_ofg`). A convolutional heatmap decoder then generates `ˆH_ofg`, representing heatmap logits. The peak of this heatmap (`(i*, j*)`) identifies the functional contact point (`ˆp_ofg`). This head is crucial for tasks requiring understanding of object parts for interaction.

- **Visual Trajectory Generation (VTG) Head:** This head handles temporally structured spatial outputs. It employs learned waypoint queries (`q_τt`) with temporal encodings that attend directly to the full multimodal sequence. It outputs `T=8` normalized image-space waypoints (`ˆτt`), describing a visual trace rather than low-level robot actions. VTG is evaluated using RMSE, Average Displacement Error (ADE), and Final Displacement Error (FDE).

#### 3.2. Spatial Interfaces and Deployment Contracts

The system employs explicit deployment contracts to assign specific spatial readouts to execution stages:

- **Structured PICK/PLACE Contracts:** For pick-place tasks, a fixed, deterministic contract is used:
 - **PICK:** Source-conditioned OFG (Object-Functional Grounding) is used to emit the functional contact point for grasping. It constructs queries from the instruction and source description (`c_src`).
 - **PLACE:** Pointing is used to emit a compact target point in the destination region. It constructs queries from the instruction and goal description (`c_goal`).
 - VTG is reserved for annotated waypoint requests, not directly used in the primary pick-place contract.

- **Image-to-Robot Frame Conversion:** An external wrapper is responsible for converting the image-space outputs from the learned heads to robot-frame targets. This involves using calibrated depth (`D`), camera intrinsics (`K`), and a camera-to-robot transform (`T_r←c`) to project the 2D image coordinates into 3D robot space. The existing robot executor then consumes these 3D coordinates.

#### 3.3. Training Objective and Strategy

The training objective is a weighted spatial objective (`L`) combining terms for each head:
- `L_pt`: SmoothL1 loss for point coordinates.
- `L_ofg`: Mean-squared error (for Gaussian keypoint targets) or class-balanced binary cross-entropy (for dense affordance masks) for heatmap predictions.
- `L_vtg`: SmoothL1 loss for trajectory waypoints.
- Language modeling and legacy reconstruction terms from the base backbone are inactive, focusing optimization on the adapters, heads, and LoRA specialization.

A **stage-wise training strategy** (Figure 3) is employed:
1. **Geometry Warm-up:** The Grounding Adapter, eight learned queries, and geometry heads are trained while the VLA backbone base is frozen.
2. **LoRA Specialization:** Only low-rank backbone updates (LoRA) are trained to further specialize the backbone.
3. **Task Continuation:** A task-required subset of modules (Grounding Adapter + Geometry Head) is trained with task-specific data.

For **source-conditioned OFG specialization**, specific training teaches OFG to preserve functional contact while disambiguating the source instance. This involves updating the OFG LoRA, shared Grounding Adapter body, OFG learned query, and heatmap decoder, using dense source-mask reconstruction combined with source-over-distractor ranking and distractor suppression.

Additionally, a GRPO-style **group-normalized refinement** is applied to the Pointing model. This directly refines the continuous spatial head by sampling around the predicted point, assigning rewards based on target box inclusion, and optimizing with a policy gradient-like objective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Pointing-VLA is structured around four key questions, covering geometry preservation, phase specialization, transferability/efficiency, and real-robot deployment.

#### 4.1. Q1: Geometry Preservation (Table 1)

- **Sparse Point Grounding:** Pointing-VLA, using its Pointing head, achieves 64.3% accuracy on VABench-P, matching the performance of Embodied-R1 on sparse referring-point grounding tasks.
- **Dense Functional Grounding:** The OFG head significantly improves part-level functional grounding accuracy on Part-Affordance-2K, raising it from 40.9% (Embodied-R1) to 57.3%. This 16.4 percentage point gain demonstrates the benefit of geometry specialization for tasks requiring spatial extent rather than just a sparse point.
- **Sequential Geometry:** For ordered spatial outputs, the VTG readout on 300 VABench-V examples achieves 0.1042 RMSE, 0.1368 ADE, and 0.1493 FDE in normalized image coordinates, indicating its ability to predict visual waypoint sequences.

#### 4.2. Q2: Phase Specialization and Fixed-Contract Ablations (Table 2)

- **Mechanism Analysis (Table 2a):** Ablations on VABench-P (PIB accuracy) show that an optimal configuration uses 8 query tokens and a LoRA rank of 32, yielding 50.3% and 53.3% respectively. GRPO-style refinement further boosts PIB from 61.3% to 64.3%, indicating the impact of specific spatial-readout design choices.
- **Fixed-Contract Validation (Table 2b):** The proposed fixed OFG-PICK/Pointing-PLACE contract achieves 72.9% average success across four Bridge/WidowX manipulation tasks (70/96 episodes) under collision-enabled CuRobo execution. This performance is 14.6 percentage points higher than the strongest alternative composition. Notably, it achieves 100% success on the "Stack cube" task and 91.7% on "Eggplant basket." The source-conditioned OFG expert successfully selects the instructed source in all 48 online Stack cases, completing 43/48 episodes, validating its role in disambiguating source instances while providing functional contact.

#### 4.3. Q3: External Diagnostics and Cross-Backbone Transfer (Table 3, Table 4)

- **Cross-Dataset Geometry (Table 3):** Performance varies with the type of spatial interface and dataset. Pointing shows stronger performance on RefCOCO (PIM 40.7%, PIB 56.1%) and RoboAff (PIM 11.5%). OFG demonstrates superiority on AGD20K (PIM 64.4%) and Part-Affordance-2K (PIM 57.3% vs. 40.9% for Embodied-R1 text generation). This crossover supports the hypothesis that expert-specific output geometries are more effective than a single serialized coordinate interface.
- **Interface-Level Runtime (Table 4, top):** The geometric readouts are significantly faster. The OFG head achieves a 6.90× speedup (0.434 s/sample) and the Pointing head a 6.68× speedup (0.448 s/sample) compared to Embodied-R1's autoregressive text decoder (2.993 s/sample) on a shared external suite. This highlights efficiency gains from avoiding coordinate serialization.
- **NORA-1.5 Transfer (Table 4, bottom):** The transferred OFG/contact readout, when integrated with a frozen NORA-1.5 backbone, preserves 100% success for horizontal pose tasks, improves laid-vertical success from 89.0% to 95.0%, and reduces recorded controller time by over 20×.

#### 4.4. Q4: Deployment Studies (Table 5, Table 6)

- **Bridge/WidowX Simulation (Table 5):** Pointing-VLA + CuRobo achieves an average success rate of 72.9% across four Bridge/WidowX tasks (Spoon on towel, Carrot on plate, Stack cube, Eggplant basket), outperforming Embodied-R1 + CuRobo (52.1%) and other baselines. This performance is achieved with active collision checking in every rollout, indicating reliable task completion and motion planning.
- **Real-Robot Deployment (Table 6):** When integrated as spatial guidance for a π_0.5 action policy on an AgileX PiPER robot, Pointing-VLA raises autonomous real-robot success from 52.7% to 80.7% across three visual contexts (no distractor, yellow cylinder distractor, red cylinder distractor), an improvement of 28 percentage points. Failure analysis reveals that these gains concentrate at the intended control boundaries: pre-lift grasp failures decreased from 47 to 16, and tray-arrival failures from 13 to 4 out of 150 trials. Figure 4 illustrates successful rollouts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Pointing-VLA represents a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) manipulation by re-conceptualizing the interface between high-level embodied reasoning and low-level robot execution. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency:** By replacing autoregressive text-coordinate generation with direct, typed hidden-state spatial readouts, Pointing-VLA achieves substantial speedups in inference time (6.68–6.90× faster). This efficiency gain is critical for real-time robot operation, where latency can severely impact performance and responsiveness. The reduction in recorded controller time by more than 20× in NORA-1.5 transfer scenarios further underscores this impact on practical deployment.

2. **Improved Inspectability and Reliability:** The output of Pointing-VLA is explicitly geometry-specific (normalized points, functional heatmaps, visual trajectories), rather than opaque action tokens or serialized text. This inherent transparency allows for better inspection by robot control stacks and human operators, enabling clearer diagnostics and debugging. The use of explicit execution contracts that deterministically align spatial outputs with specific execution stages (e.g., OFG for PICK, Pointing for PLACE) further enhances reliability by providing unambiguous spatial targets to downstream planners and executors.

3. **Increased Robot Task Success:** The methodology demonstrates a notable improvement in robot task success across various benchmarks. Achieving state-of-the-art performance (72.9% average success) on the Bridge/WidowX benchmark under collision-enabled CuRobo execution signifies robust performance in complex simulated environments. More critically, the 28 percentage point increase in autonomous real-robot success (from 52.7% to 80.7%) on an AgileX PiPER, with a reduction in critical failure stages like grasping and tray arrival, validates its practical utility and impact on real-world robot capabilities.

4. **Geometry-Appropriate Grounding:** The research establishes that different spatial grounding tasks (sparse pointing, dense affordances, temporal trajectories) are best served by distinct geometric representations. The complementary strengths of the Pointing and OFG heads across various datasets (e.g., Pointing for RefCOCO, OFG for AGD20K and Part-Affordance-2K) indicate a more nuanced and effective approach to spatial grounding compared to a monolithic output. This principle could guide future designs of multimodal robot interfaces.

5. **Robustness and Transferability:** Pointing-VLA demonstrates robust performance under collision-aware motion planning and shows promising cross-dataset and cross-backbone transfer capabilities (e.g., to NORA-1.5). This suggests that the typed spatial readout approach is not overly specialized to a particular VLA backbone or dataset, indicating potential for broader applicability in robot learning.

6. **Foundation for Future Work:** The established typed spatial readout serves as an inspectable interface that can be leveraged for further advancements. The paper itself identifies future directions, such as integrating visual correction, supporting broader structured task decompositions, and facilitating cross-robot transfer, as natural extensions of this work. This approach could simplify the integration of VLM reasoning into diverse robotic systems and enable more sophisticated, geometry-aware manipulation skills.

In summary, Pointing-VLA contributes a critical methodological shift in how VLA models interface with robot execution, offering a more efficient, reliable, and inspectable pathway for spatial grounding. This can lead to more capable and trustworthy autonomous robotic systems.
