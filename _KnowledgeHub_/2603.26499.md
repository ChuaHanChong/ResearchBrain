# AIRA_2: Overcoming Bottlenecks in AI Research Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at FAIR at Meta and collaborators developed AIRA
        2^{2}2, an AI research agent designed to overcome structural bottlenecks in
        autonomous machine learning research. It achieved a mean Percentile Rank of 76.0%
        on MLE-bench-30 over 72 hours, demonstrating sustained performance improvements
        by enhancing compute throughput, evaluation reliability, and agent operational
        capabilities.

## Problem

- Current AI research agents suffer from low compute throughput due to synchronous, single-GPU execution, limiting candidate solution exploration and evaluation.
- A generalization gap exists between validation and test metrics, leading to unreliable search signals and performance degradation over extended search horizons.
- Fixed, single-turn LLM operators restrict agents' ability to perform complex reasoning, iterative debugging, or dynamic task adaptation, setting a ceiling on search performance.

## Method

- An ==asynchronous multi-GPU worker pool==, employing ==steady-state evolutionary search==, enables parallel execution of tasks to maximize GPU utilization and experimental throughput.
- A ==Hidden Consistent Evaluation== (HCE) protocol uses fixed, disjoint data splits (D_train, D_search, D_val) and ==externalized evaluation== to provide a stable, reliable reward signal, preventing metric gaming.
- Static LLM operators are replaced with dynamic ==ReAct agents==, allowing ==multi-step reasoning==, interactive debugging of code exceptions, and adaptive decision-making within a sandboxed environment.

## Results

- AIRA 2 achieved a new state-of-the-art mean Percentile Rank of **76.0%** on MLE-bench-30 at 72 hours, improving from **71.8%** at 24 hours and demonstrating continuous performance gains with additional compute.
- The Hidden Consistent Evaluation (HCE) protocol accounted for a **13.0** Percentile Rank point improvement at 24 hours and **18.4** points at 72 hours by completely eliminating performance degradation observed in prior evaluation setups.
- Asynchronous multi-GPU execution demonstrated superior long-term performance, with an 8-GPU setup showing a **7.5** Percentile Rank point advantage over single-GPU at 144 cumulative GPU-hours, despite initial overheads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parallel computation, when combined with an evolutionary search framework, leads to superior long-term performance by enabling broader exploration and avoiding local optima, effectively transforming throughput into higher asymptotic performance.
> - Performance degradation observed in prior AI agents is primarily due to inconsistent and noisy evaluation procedures, not classical data memorization or overfitting, which HCE successfully mitigates to enable continuous improvement.
> - Dynamic ReAct agents, offering multi-turn interaction and self-correction capabilities, serve as an efficiency multiplier for search, accelerating problem-solving compared to static operators by allowing iterative refinement within a single mutation attempt.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of AI research agents is situated within the broader context of applying Large Language Models (LLMs) to automate complex, multi-step workflows. Initially, automated machine learning (AutoML) focused on fixed search spaces and simple heuristics. However, recent advancements in LLMs have enabled the creation of autonomous agents capable of performing long-horizon research, mirroring human scientific inquiry. These agents are increasingly applied to diverse domains such as software engineering, mathematics, chemistry, and material science, where environments provide rapid, verifiable feedback.

Automating the scientific process, particularly in AI research itself (AI4AI), presents distinct challenges. Unlike coding, scientific research involves navigating vast, partially observable design spaces, dealing with noisy evaluation signals, and designing proxy tasks for performance estimation. Existing state-of-the-art systems, such as MARS, MLEvolve, PiEvolve, FM-Agent 2.0, and ML-Master 2.0, leverage inference-time scaling and evolutionary search to tackle benchmarks like Kaggle competitions. These systems conceptualize research as a search process over a graph of candidate solutions, employing iterative refinement through tree or graph search, multi-agent collaboration, advanced context management, or external knowledge integration.

However, prior work, specifically AIRA-dojo (Toledo et al., 2025), systematically identified three structural bottlenecks that have limited the scaling and performance of these agents: (1) computational throughput limitations due to synchronous execution, (2) a generalization gap where validation-based selection leads to performance degradation over extended search horizons, and (3) a ceiling on search performance imposed by the limited capability of fixed, single-turn LLM operators. This current work, AIRA$^2$, directly builds upon these identified limitations by proposing an architecture specifically designed to mitigate each of them. It aims to advance the field by enabling more robust, scalable, and generalizable AI research agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the identified structural performance bottlenecks in AI research agents, thereby enabling sustained performance improvements over extended search horizons and increased computational resources. The motivation is rooted in the practical limitations observed in existing AI research agents when performing complex, open-ended scientific tasks.

Specifically, the work is motivated by three distinct bottlenecks:

1. **Compute Throughput Bottleneck (Synchronous Single-GPU Execution)**: Existing agent architectures often rely on synchronous execution, where the reasoning loop idles while awaiting feedback from computationally intensive experiments. In environments like MLE-bench, where model training and evaluation can span hours, this serialization severely limits the number of candidate solutions (samples) that can be generated and evaluated per unit of time. This "sample-bound" nature prevents effective exploration of the vast combinatorial space of machine learning solutions, rendering advanced search methods theoretically powerful but practically intractable without parallelization. The motivation is to unblock this limitation and increase the effective exploration capacity of agents.

2. **Generalization Gap Bottleneck (Validation-Test Divergence/Overfitting)**: The effectiveness of any search process depends on the fidelity of its reward signal. In competitive settings like Kaggle, agents often optimize validation metrics that diverge from the true objective (held-out test performance). This gap arises from two main issues: (a) agents "gaming" self-reported metrics, and (b) the reuse of validation sets for both optimization and final selection, leading to overfitting over prolonged search horizons. Furthermore, the search signal is frequently corrupted by execution noise, such as implementation bugs that spuriously inflate metrics or stochasticity in data splitting. The motivation is to provide a reliable, stable evaluation signal that faithfully represents underlying performance, preventing agents from converging on solutions that exploit evaluation artifacts rather than genuine predictive structures.

3. **Static Operator Limitation Bottleneck (Fixed, Single-Turn LLM Operators)**: Research agents decompose tasks into a search policy and a set of atomic operators. In current systems, these operators are often pre-defined (e.g., a fixed prompt for EDA, another for hyperparameter tuning). This design is inherently brittle and inflexible; agents can only perform actions anticipated by their designers. As research tasks become more complex, requiring multi-file codebases, shared artifacts, and iterative debugging, a static operator pipeline cannot adapt to unpredictable dependencies. This limits agents to shallow, single-turn reasoning. The motivation is to enable agents to dynamically scope their actions and engage in iterative, multi-step reasoning, thereby expanding the range of solvable tasks and improving search efficiency.

By addressing these structural issues, the authors aim to build an AI research agent that can achieve sustained, reliable, and high-performance exploration in complex machine learning engineering tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AIRA$^2$ addresses the identified bottlenecks through a two-tier architecture comprising a Global Orchestrator and an Asynchronous Worker Pool of ReAct agents.

**Evolutionary Search Orchestration:**
The **Global Orchestrator** manages a population of candidate solutions, including their fitness scores and metadata. It employs an asynchronous, steady-state evolutionary search process:
- **Selection**: When a worker becomes available, the orchestrator samples one or two parent solutions using temperature-scaled rank-based selection. This method assigns selection probabilities based on ranks (1 for best, N for worst) rather than absolute fitness scores, making it robust to variations in metric scales across tasks. A temperature parameter (T) controls the exploration-exploitation trade-off, with a setting of T=0.2 used in experiments to balance diversity and greediness.
- **Mutation/Crossover**: The orchestrator randomly chooses between mutation (refining a single parent) and crossover (combining two parents) with a crossover probability ($p_c$) of 15%. Both operations are executed by ReAct agents, which receive the parent solution(s) and metadata as context.

**Scaling Compute: Asynchronous Multi-GPU Execution:**
To overcome the compute throughput bottleneck, AIRA$^2$ leverages the asynchronous nature of steady-state evolution for parallel orchestration:
- **Decoupled Execution**: Unlike generational evolution that waits for all workers, steady-state evolution allows the orchestrator to dispatch tasks to any available worker without synchronization barriers. This is particularly effective for highly variable task durations (e.g., ReAct trajectories).
- **Static Resource Allocation**: Each worker is assigned a dedicated NVIDIA H200 GPU (141GB VRAM), 12 logical CPU cores, and 120GB of system RAM, ensuring ample resources and preventing contention. This 1:1 worker-to-GPU mapping facilitates predictable performance scaling.
- **Remote Execution & Containerization**: Code execution is decoupled from agent logic using a remote tool execution system. Workers run code within ephemeral Apptainer containers, utilizing the Superimage environment (Toledo et al., 2025) pre-installed with ML and data science packages. Fakeroot mode grants agents perceived root privileges for installing system-level dependencies (apt, pip), ensuring flexibility, reproducibility, and isolation from orchestrator or other workers.
- **Stateful Interaction**: Unlike some prior systems, AIRA$^2$'s Bash command and Jupyter kernel execution tools are stateful, allowing agents to maintain context across multiple turns, which supports interactive debugging. Tool outputs include execution duration, enabling agents to monitor their own efficiency.
- **Data Management & Limits**: A central, in-memory program database stores solutions and artifacts, with large artifacts offloaded to hard disk. Subagents communicate exclusively via this database. Evaluation runs in the foreground with a dedicated GPU. A global hard time limit governs the search, with individual code executions capped at 9 hours.

**Closing the Generalization Gap: Hidden Consistent Evaluation (HCE) Protocol:**
To provide a reliable reward signal and prevent overfitting, AIRA$^2$ implements HCE:
- **Data Partitioning**: Before search, available labels are split once into three disjoint sets (80%/10%/10%):
 - **D_train**: Visible to the agent for model training.
 - **D_search**: Used by the orchestrator for fitness computation; labels are hidden from agents.
 - **D_val**: Used only for final solution selection after search terminates; hidden from both agents and the search process.
 These fixed splits are reused across all seeds, ensuring consistency. The use of D_search and D_val sets aside 20% of training data, making comparisons with baselines that use full training data conservative.
- **Externalized Evaluation**: Agents never self-report metrics. When a worker returns a solution, the orchestrator evaluates it on D_search in a separate, identical container. Agents observe only the resulting score, not the labels, thus preventing metric gaming. Evaluations also run on D_val and D_test, but D_test scores are neither forwarded to agents nor the orchestrator during search.
- **Decoupled Selection**: Since D_val is never used during search, the final solution selection is insulated from the optimization dynamics that could lead to overfitting. This separation, combined with consistent evaluation, aims to reduce the validation-test gap.

**Beyond Static Operators: ReAct Agents:**
To overcome the limitations of fixed, single-turn operators, AIRA$^2$ replaces them with ReAct agents (Yao et al., 2022):
- **Multi-step Reasoning Trajectories**: Each mutation or crossover operation is executed by a ReAct agent, which performs iterative reasoning (Reason$_t$), actions (Act$_t$, e.g., Python or Bash commands in a sandboxed environment), and observations (Obs$_t$, execution outputs). No additional guidance is provided within the trajectory. The final action is a "submit" tool to return the solution to the orchestrator.
- **Dynamic Scoping**: ReAct agents dynamically determine necessary actions at runtime. This includes performing exploratory data analysis, running small development experiments, inspecting logs, or prioritizing hyperparameter tuning based on the task context. This eliminates the need for brittle, human-scoped "scope engineering."
- **Interactive Debugging**: If code execution results in an exception, the agent observes the traceback within the same trajectory, formulates a hypothesis for a fix, and re-executes the code. This enables iterative error resolution without abandoning the mutation attempt, a capability static debug operators lack.

The combination of these architectural components aims to provide a system that is computationally efficient, reliably evaluable, and capable of complex, adaptive reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates AIRA$^2$ on MLE-bench-30, a curated subset of 30 Kaggle competitions, using Percentile Rank as the primary metric, which provides a continuous measure of performance against a leaderboard.

**Overall Performance:**
- **Early Search (3h)**: AIRA$^2$ achieved a mean Percentile Rank of 59.9%. This result positions it comparably to top single-GPU baseline agents at an equivalent 24 GPU-hour budget, demonstrating rapid initial progress despite using 8 GPUs for broader exploration.
- **Standard Evaluation (24h)**: At the 24-hour mark, AIRA$^2$ achieved a mean Percentile Rank of 71.8%. This surpassed the previous state-of-the-art of 69.9% (MARS+ (Chen et al., 2026)). This was achieved even with 20% of the training data reserved for the Hidden Consistent Evaluation (HCE) protocol, indicating that the reliable search signal contributed to superior performance.
- **Long-Horizon Search (72h)**: AIRA$^2$ demonstrated sustained improvement over extended time horizons, reaching 76.0% Percentile Rank at 72 hours. This represents a 4.2 percentage point gain over its 24-hour performance and contrasts with prior systems where extended runtime often led to performance degradation.

**Ablation Studies:**
The studies systematically removed components to assess their individual contributions:

1. **Resolving the Compute Bottleneck:**
 - **Parallel Compute vs. Efficiency (Figure 3a)**: Comparing AIRA$^2$ with 1 GPU versus 8 GPUs, normalized by total GPU-hours, revealed an initial performance lag for the 8-GPU setup due to population initialization. However, the 8-GPU setup subsequently achieved significantly higher long-term performance, with the gap widening to 7.5 Percentile Rank points at 144 GPU-hours. This indicates that parallel exploration facilitates a more robust traversal of the search space, avoiding local optima, and increases the effective branching factor by allowing simultaneous diverse mutations.
 - **Parallelism without Evolution (Figure 3b)**: A "Best-of-K" baseline, using 8 GPUs in an embarrassingly parallel fashion (agents generate solutions from scratch without evolutionary lineage), showed rapid initial scaling but quickly plateaued at the same performance level as the single-GPU evolutionary agent (around the 9-hour mark). This suggests that parallelism *without shared state or evolutionary mechanisms* is sample-inefficient, merely achieving results faster rather than better. Evolutionary search is crucial for effectively utilizing distributed compute to reach higher asymptotic performance.

2. **Closing the Generalization Gap (HCE):**
 - **Reproducing Degradation (Figure 4a)**: Replicating evaluation setups of prior work (self-reported metrics with dynamic validation splits) confirmed performance peaks early and then degrades. The authors hypothesized this was due to evaluation noise rather than classical overfitting.
 - **Eliminating Degradation with HCE (Figure 4a)**: Implementing the HCE protocol, with a fixed, hidden D_search for optimization and a held-out D_val for final selection, entirely eliminated performance degradation. AIRA$^2$ showed monotonic improvement, and HCE contributed a substantial 13.0 Percentile Rank points at 24 hours and 18.4 points at 72 hours. Without HCE, performance stagnated between 24h and 72h.
 - **Diagnosing the Remaining Gap**: The marginal difference in test performance between selecting on D_search (seen during optimization) versus the unseen D_val was small, and test performance monotonically improved even with D_search selection. This suggested that prior reported "overfitting" was primarily driven by evaluation inconsistency and noise, not true data memorization, once the evaluation metric was stabilized.

3. **Overcoming the Static Operator Limitation (ReAct Agents):**
 - **ReAct as Efficiency Multiplier**: At 3 hours, AIRA$^2$ with ReAct agents outperformed its variant using static, single-turn LLM operators by 5.5 Percentile Rank points (Table 1, Figure 4b). This highlights ReAct's ability for self-correction and iterative refinement within a single mutation, leading to more effective early search space traversal.
 - **Gap Narrows with Compute**: The performance gap between ReAct and static operators reduced to 3.2 points at 24 hours and 2.3 points at 72 hours. This suggests that while static operators are less efficient, given sufficient time, the evolutionary loop can compensate for their lack of internal agency by externalizing context across independent attempts. The study hypothesizes ReAct's full benefit might be more pronounced in tasks requiring broader, dynamic tool use beyond standard Python/Bash.

**Case Studies: Eureka Moments:**
Qualitative analysis of specific high-complexity tasks provided insights:
- **champs-scalar-coupling**: AIRA$^2$ achieved a "eureka" moment where it initially introduced an auxiliary prediction task that led to a validation score drop. However, by inspecting execution logs, the agent correctly identified underfitting (training too short) rather than a flawed idea. It then scaled the model and extended training, leading to a dramatic performance boost and medal-winning results, surpassing all other agents.
- **Other Tasks**: AIRA$^2$ uniquely broke the medal ceiling on tasks like `billion-word-imputation` (achieving 100% PR by decomposing it into two learned sub-tasks with RoBERTa models) and `imet-2020-fgvc7` (91% PR through sophisticated ensembling and hyperparameter tuning), where other methods failed to make significant progress.

Overall, the findings indicate that AIRA$^2$'s integrated architecture successfully addresses the identified bottlenecks, leading to state-of-the-art performance, sustained improvement over time, and advanced reasoning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on AIRA$^2$ carries significant implications for the development of autonomous AI research agents and the broader scientific discovery process.

**Advancing the State-of-the-Art:** AIRA$^2$ demonstrates a new state-of-the-art performance on the MLE-bench-30 benchmark, achieving 71.8% mean Percentile Rank at 24 hours and 76.0% at 72 hours. This numerical improvement, particularly the sustained gains over longer horizons, indicates a more robust and effective automation of aspects of the machine learning research process.

**Overcoming Fundamental Bottlenecks:** The research provides empirical evidence that the three structural bottlenecks—compute throughput, generalization gap, and static operator limitations—can be systematically addressed. This is significant because these issues were previously identified as major barriers to scaling AI research agents. By providing concrete architectural solutions, AIRA$^2$ offers a framework for designing more resilient and capable agents.

**Enabling Sustained and Reliable Improvement:** A key finding is that AIRA$^2$'s performance improves monotonically with additional compute, contrasting with prior systems that experienced performance degradation over extended runtimes. This indicates that the HCE protocol, in particular, delivers a more trustworthy evaluation signal, allowing agents to genuinely generalize rather than overfit to evaluation noise. This reliability is crucial for long-term scientific exploration where continuous progress is desired.

**Architectural and Design Insights:** The detailed ablation studies are impactful as they disentangle the contributions of individual components. They confirm that no single "silver bullet" solution exists; rather, the synergistic interplay of asynchronous multi-GPU execution, robust Hidden Consistent Evaluation, and dynamic ReAct agents is necessary for achieving superior performance under practical constraints. Specifically:
- Parallelism without evolutionary mechanisms is shown to be merely faster, not better, emphasizing the need for intelligent search strategies to leverage distributed compute.
- Evaluation consistency is paramount for long-horizon search, suggesting that previously reported "overfitting" was largely an artifact of evaluation noise.
- ReAct agents enhance efficiency and expand the problem-solving scope, though the full extent of their benefit may be realized in more complex, dynamic environments requiring broader tool use.

**Qualitative Advancement in Agent Reasoning:** The case studies illustrate that AIRA$^2$ exhibits more sophisticated reasoning capabilities than previous systems. The agent's ability to diagnose underfitting, strategize based on execution logs, and combine different approaches effectively (e.g., through crossover) represents a step towards more human-like scientific intuition and problem-solving. This moves agents beyond simple script execution towards more nuanced, interactive discovery.

**Implications for Future AI Research:** By solving fundamental engineering challenges, AIRA$^2$ contributes to the vision of autonomous systems capable of genuine, open-ended scientific discovery. The methodology and insights could be applied to develop agents for generating novel knowledge in various scientific domains beyond standard benchmarks. While limitations like potential data contamination during LLM pre-training are acknowledged, the work lays a foundation for future evaluations on "closed" or private benchmarks to further isolate intrinsic research capabilities.
