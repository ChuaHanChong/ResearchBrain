# seq-JEPA: Autoregressive Predictive Learning of Invariant-Equivariant World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> From the Université de Montréal and Mila - Quebec AI Institute, `seq-JEPA` introduces an autoregressive predictive learning framework for world models, processing sequences of action-observation pairs to simultaneously learn invariant and equivariant visual representations. This approach disentangles these properties through architectural inductive biases, demonstrating improved performance on both classification and transformation prediction tasks across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning methods often struggle with a fundamental trade-off, either excelling at learning invariant representations (for classification) or equivariant representations (for fine-grained tasks), but rarely both simultaneously.
- The prevalent 'two-view paradigm' in self-supervised learning inherently limits the flexibility of learned representations, often forcing models to discard or preserve transformation-specific information.
- Many current equivariant self-supervised methods rely on explicit equivariance loss terms, dual predictors, or complex contrastive strategies, which can make them less elegant or generalizable.

## Method

- `seq-JEPA` integrates sequential processing and ==action-conditioned predictive learning== into ==Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)== to learn distinct invariant and equivariant representations.
- A ==transformer-based sequence aggregator== integrates information from multiple observations and their corresponding relative transformations (actions), generating an invariant aggregate representation.
- A predictor head, conditioned on the upcoming action, forecasts the latent representation of the next observation, compelling the online encoder to learn equivariant features without additional loss terms.

## Results

- `seq-JEPA` effectively resolves the invariance-equivariance trade-off, matching or surpassing leading equivariant baselines in transformation prediction (e.g., R² of **0.71** on 3DIEBench rotation) while significantly outperforming them in classification (**86.14%** vs. **80.40%**).
- Qualitative UMAP analysis shows encoder representations exhibiting clear equivariance (smooth gradients across transformations) and aggregate representations forming compact, invariant class clusters, confirming architectural disentanglement.
- The framework excels at biologically inspired tasks like Predictive Learning Across Saccades (PLS) with **83.44%** accuracy on STL-10 and demonstrates robust path integration, with performance scaling positively with longer training and inference sequence lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architectural inductive biases, specifically action conditioning and sequence aggregation, can naturally disentangle invariant and equivariant properties within a single self-supervised model.
> - Biologically inspired sequential processing of action-observation pairs provides a powerful and generalizable learning signal, allowing models to learn from dynamic interactions rather than static image pairs.
> - Non-generative world models predicting latent representations conditioned on actions can effectively learn the dynamics of transformations and support complex tasks like path integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of self-supervised learning (SSL), specifically addressing a critical challenge in visual representation learning: the fundamental trade-off between **invariance** and **equivariance**.

**Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** In recent years, SSL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning rich visual representations from unlabeled data, often bridging or even surpassing the performance of supervised methods on various downstream tasks. Most dominant SSL methods, such as SimCLR, BYOL, and VICReg, primarily focus on learning *invariant* representations. They achieve this by generating two augmented "views" of an image and forcing their latent representations to be similar, thereby making the model insensitive to transformations like cropping, color jitter, or rotation. These invariant representations are highly effective for high-level tasks such as image classification, where the identity of an object needs to be recognized regardless of its specific appearance or pose.

**Equivariance:** In contrast, *equivariant* representations are designed to explicitly preserve information about the transformations applied to an input. If an input undergoes a transformation, its equivariant representation should undergo a corresponding, predictable transformation in the latent space. This property is crucial for fine-grained tasks that require understanding geometric or stylistic variations, such as pose estimation, 3D reconstruction, object tracking, or even aligning with neural responses in biological vision systems.

**The Invariance-Equivariance Trade-off:** A growing body of theoretical and empirical work has highlighted a fundamental tension: models optimized for strong invariance often sacrifice equivariance, and vice versa. Representations that are robust to variations (invariant) tend to "forget" the specific details of those variations (making them poor for equivariance-demanding tasks). This trade-off limits the flexibility and adaptability of learned representations for diverse downstream applications.

**Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) and World Models:** The proposed `seq-JEPA` framework is rooted in the concept of non-generative world models and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), a paradigm championed by Yann LeCun. Unlike traditional generative models that reconstruct pixel-level details, JEPAs predict the effects of actions or transformations directly in a compact latent space. This approach bypasses the computational cost and representational collapse issues often associated with pixel-level reconstruction while still allowing the model to learn a predictive "world model." Recent JEPAs like I-JEPA (predicting masked regions) and IWM (conditioning on augmentation parameters) have shown promise in learning robust representations.

**`seq-JEPA`'s Position:** `seq-JEPA` extends the JEPA paradigm by introducing **sequential processing** as a core architectural inductive bias. Instead of the typical "two-view" SSL paradigm, it mimics biological vision by processing short sequences of observations, each associated with a relative transformation or "action." Crucially, it aims to resolve the invariance-equivariance trade-off *architecturally*, by having different parts of the model (encoder outputs vs. transformer aggregate) naturally learn distinct representational properties, without relying on explicit dual loss terms or separate predictors for invariance and equivariance. This differentiates it from many existing equivariant SSL methods that augment invariant objectives with explicit equivariance terms or dedicated predictors. By integrating sequence modeling (transformers) with action-conditioned prediction, `seq-JEPA` represents a significant step towards more versatile and biologically plausible self-supervised learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-supervised learning framework capable of simultaneously learning robust representations that excel at *both* invariance-demanding tasks (like image classification) and equivariance-demanding tasks (like transformation prediction). This aims to resolve the fundamental trade-off observed in previous SSL methods.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations and inspirations:

- **Addressing the Invariance-Equivariance Trade-off:** Current SSL methods often create a performance dilemma, where optimizing for one representational property compromises the other. The authors seek a unified approach that can reconcile these competing goals within a single framework.
- **Biological Inspiration:** Humans and animals do not perceive the world through static, isolated images. Instead, they actively interact with their environment, generating sequences of actions (e.g., eye movements, head turns, grasping) and observations (sequential views). This active, sequential exploration is crucial for building robust visual representations, particularly for understanding 3D objects and their properties. `seq-JEPA` is inspired by this natural mechanism.
- **Architectural Simplicity:** The authors aim to achieve the dual learning of invariant and equivariant representations through architectural inductive biases rather than relying on complex, explicit dual loss terms or specialized equivariance predictors. This seeks to simplify the training process and potentially improve generalization.
- **Enhancing World Modeling:** By processing sequences of action-observation pairs, the framework is designed to inherently build a more dynamic and predictive "world model" in its latent space. This world model should be capable of understanding not just what objects are (invariance), but also how they change and interact under different actions (equivariance).
- **Support for Sequential Tasks:** A secondary motivation is to enable better performance on downstream tasks that inherently involve sequences of observations, such as path integration (tracking cumulative changes over time) and predictive learning across simulated eye movements (saccades), which are crucial for embodied AI.

In summary, the core goal is to enable a single self-supervised model to capture both the "what" (invariant object identity) and the "how" (equivariant transformation details) of visual perception, mimicking more closely how biological systems learn about their world through active exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `seq-JEPA` framework is a novel self-supervised world modeling approach that integrates joint-embedding predictive architectures with architectural inductive biases for sequential processing.

#### 3.1. Invariant and Equivariant Representations (Definitions)

The paper first formalizes invariance and equivariance. An encoder `f` is equivariant to a transformation `t` if `f(t(x)) = u_t(f(x))`, where `u_t` is a corresponding transformation in the latent space. If `u_t` is an identity function, `f` is invariant. The authors also define equivariance with respect to relative transformations (actions `a`), where `f(x_2) = u_a(f(x_1))` if `a` maps `x_1` to `x_2`.

#### 3.2. Architecture (Figure 1)

`seq-JEPA` processes a short sequence of `M+1` views (`x_1..., x_{M+1}`) generated from an input image via transformations, and `M` corresponding relative transformations or "actions" (`a_1..., a_M`), where `a_i` maps `x_i` to `x_{i+1}`.

- **Backbone Encoder (f):** A standard vision backbone (e.g., ResNet-18) encodes each view `x_i` into a latent representation `z_i`.
- **Action Encoder:** Each action `a_i` (representing relative transformation parameters) is mapped to a learnable embedding (default 128-d linear projector).
- **Sequence Aggregator (g):** A transformer encoder acts as a "learned working memory." It receives a sequence of `(z_i, a_i)` pairs: `(z_1, a_1), (z_2, a_2)..., (z_{M-1}, a_{M-1}), z_M`. Note that `z_M` is the final observation in the input sequence, but its *corresponding action* `a_M` is used separately later. The transformer employs a learnable `[AGG]` token (similar to `[CLS]` in ViT) to produce a single, aggregate representation `z_AGG` summarizing the entire sequence context.
- **Predictor Head (h):** An MLP predictor receives the `z_AGG` (aggregate context) concatenated with the embedding of the *upcoming* action `a_M` (the transformation from `x_M` to `x_{M+1}`). Its task is to predict `ˆz_{M+1}`, the latent representation of the next observation `x_{M+1}`.
- **Target Encoder:** An exponential moving average (EMA) copy of the backbone encoder `f` generates the ground truth `z_{M+1}`. A stop-gradient operator is applied to `z_{M+1}` to prevent representational collapse, a common technique in JEPAs and other SSL methods.
- **Loss Function:** The training objective is to maximize the cosine similarity between the predicted `ˆz_{M+1}` and the ground-truth `sg(z_{M+1})`, using a 1 minus cosine similarity loss: `L_seq-JEPA = 1 - (ˆz_{M+1} / ||ˆz_{M+1}||_2) ⋅ (sg(z_{M+1}) / ||sg(z_{M+1})||_2)`. Importantly, no additional loss terms or equivariance-specific predictors are used.

#### 3.3. Emergent Dual Representations

The core hypothesis is that this architectural design naturally disentangles invariant and equivariant properties:
- The individual encoded views (`z_i`) become **equivariant** because the predictor needs them to represent transformation-specific information in order to accurately predict the next state given an action. The action conditioning in the predictor is key here.
- The aggregated representation (`z_AGG`) becomes **invariant** because it summarizes multiple views of the same underlying "object" across different transformations, effectively filtering out transformation-specific variability to capture semantic content.

#### 3.4. Action and Observation Sets

The framework is evaluated across three diverse settings to demonstrate generalization:

- **3D Invariant Equivariant Benchmark (3DIEBench):** Synthetic dataset of 3D object renderings with controlled variations in rotation, floor hue, and lighting. Actions correspond to relative changes in these factors.
- **Hand-Crafted Augmentations:** Common image augmentations (crop, color jitter, blur) applied to CIFAR100 and Tiny ImageNet. Actions are relative parameters of these augmentations.
- **Predictive Learning Across Saccades (PLS):** Sequences of image patches are extracted from full images, simulating foveated vision and eye movements (saccades). Actions correspond to relative 2D positions of patch centers. This setting uses biologically inspired mechanisms:
 - **Saliency-Based Fixation Sampling:** Patches are sampled probabilistically from high-saliency regions (using DeepGaze IIE).
 - **Inhibition of Return (IoR):** Previously sampled areas are suppressed to encourage exploration and reduce redundancy.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Backbone:** ResNet-18 for all models.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of invariant SSL methods (SimCLR, BYOL, VICReg, VICRegTraj) and equivariant SSL methods (SEN, EquiMod, SIE, ContextSSL). Custom ablations (Conditional BYOL, Conv-JEPA) are also included.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Equivariance:** R-squared (R^2) score of a linear regressor predicting relative transformation parameters (e.g., rotation quaternion, augmentation parameters, 2D position) from frozen *encoder* representations (`z_i`). Retrieval metrics (MRR, Hit@1, Hit@5) are also used to assess predictor quality.
 - **Invariance:** Top-1 classification accuracy of a linear probe trained on frozen *aggregate* representations (`z_AGG` for seq-JEPA) or *encoder* representations for baselines.
 - **Path Integration:** R^2 of a regressor predicting the *cumulative* transformation from the first to the last observation, given `z_AGG` and `a_M`.
- **Training:** Models trained from scratch with AdamW (for transformers) or Adam (for ConvNets) for up to 2000 epochs, with batch size 512.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide compelling evidence for `seq-JEPA`'s ability to simultaneously learn invariant and equivariant representations, resolving the long-standing trade-off.

- **Resolving the Invariance-Equivariance Trade-off (3DIEBench):**
 - On 3DIEBench (Table 1, Figure 3), `seq-JEPA` achieved a classification accuracy of 87.41% (with Mtr=3, Mval=5) while also obtaining a relative rotation R^2 of 0.71. This performance is superior to both invariant methods (e.g., BYOL: 82.90% Acc, 0.12 R^2) and equivariant methods (e.g., ContextSSL: 80.40% Acc, 0.74 R^2; SIE: 77.49% Acc, 0.58 R^2), which typically show a strong trade-off.
 - Ablating action conditioning in `seq-JEPA` led to a sharp drop in equivariance (R^2 of 0.29) while maintaining high classification accuracy (86.05%), confirming the distinct roles of action conditioning and sequential aggregation in the architectural disentanglement.
- **Qualitative Evidence of Disentanglement (3DIEBench):**
 - Nearest neighbor retrievals (Figure 4) showed that `seq-JEPA` (and SIE) consistently preserved the rotation of the query image, unlike invariant baselines.
 - UMAP projections (Figure 5) visually confirmed the architectural disentanglement: encoder representations (`z_i`) exhibited smooth gradients according to rotation angle (equivariance), while aggregate representations (`z_AGG`) formed tighter, well-separated clusters based on class labels (invariance).
- **Generalization to Hand-Crafted Augmentations:**
 - On CIFAR100 and Tiny ImageNet (Table 2), `seq-JEPA` consistently achieved higher equivariance (R^2 for crop, color jitter, blur parameters) than all baselines, including specialized equivariant methods, while remaining competitive in classification accuracy.
- **Predictive Learning Across Saccades (PLS) and Path Integration:**
 - In the biologically inspired PLS setting on STL-10 (Table 3), `seq-JEPA` achieved 83.44% top-1 accuracy (with Mval=6, 84.12%), comparable to SimCLR trained on full images with strong augmentations (85.23%), despite learning from small, partial patches.
 - Action conditioning was crucial for both classification and positional equivariance in PLS.
 - The framework successfully performed visual path integration (predicting cumulative saccade positions) and angular path integration (predicting cumulative 3D rotations) (Figure 6), demonstrating its ability to integrate information over sequences. Ablating action information severely degraded path integration performance, while ablating visual input had a minimal effect, highlighting the dominance of action cues for this task.
- **Action Conditioning Ablations:**
 - Ablation studies (Table 4) showed that action conditioning in the *predictor* was more critical for achieving equivariance than in the sequence transformer, although both contributed. The dimensionality of action embeddings had a plateau effect, suggesting that even smaller dimensions (e.g., 64-d) were sufficient to capture transformation structure.
- **Scaling Properties with Sequence Lengths:**
 - Longer *training* sequence lengths generally improved equivariance across all tasks (Figure 7, left panel).
 - For tasks like 3DIEBench and PLS (where sequential observations are inherently beneficial), classification accuracy also improved with longer *training* sequences (Figure 7, middle panel).
 - Crucially, all datasets benefited from longer *inference* sequence lengths, indicating that richer aggregate representations lead to stronger performance (Figure 7, right panel). This demonstrates the model's scalability with increased context.
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - `seq-JEPA`'s training time is competitive with other SSL baselines, with a moderate increase for longer sequences. Inference costs can be offset by using low-resolution foveated patches, making it comparable to single-view, full-resolution pipelines (Table 5, 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The `seq-JEPA` framework represents a significant advancement in self-supervised learning for visual representations, offering a compelling solution to the fundamental trade-off between invariance and equivariance. Its impact can be multifaceted:

- **Bridging the Gap in Visual AI:** By effectively learning both invariant and equivariant representations within a single, architecturally coherent framework, `seq-JEPA` can bridge the divide between AI systems focused on high-level semantic understanding (classification) and those requiring fine-grained geometric reasoning (pose estimation, manipulation). This versatility is crucial for developing more general-purpose intelligent agents.
- **Biologically Plausible AI:** Inspired by how biological organisms learn through active interaction and sequential observation, `seq-JEPA` offers a more biologically plausible approach to representation learning. This could lead to AI models that are not only more robust and efficient but also more interpretable in terms of their learning mechanisms. The integration of saliency and inhibition of return in PLS further strengthens this connection.
- **Enhancing Embodied AI and Robotics:** The framework's demonstrated ability to learn from sequences of partial observations (like saccades) and perform path integration is highly relevant for embodied AI agents and robotics. Such agents often operate with limited fields of view, sequential sensory inputs, and a need to track their state and surroundings over time. `seq-JEPA` could enable more efficient and robust perception in these dynamic, resource-constrained environments.
- **Advancing World Modeling:** As a non-generative world model, `seq-JEPA` contributes to the development of latent-space predictive models that can reason about actions and their outcomes without costly pixel-level reconstruction. This has implications for planning, model-based reinforcement learning, and understanding causality in complex environments.
- **Architectural Innovation:** The proposal that architectural inductive biases (sequential transformer aggregation and action conditioning) alone can naturally disentangle invariant and equivariant representations, without recourse to explicit dual losses, is an elegant and impactful contribution. It simplifies the model design and could inspire similar architectural solutions for other complex multi-objective learning problems.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** The observed scalability of `seq-JEPA` with sequence length during both training and inference, especially when coupled with low-resolution foveated inputs, suggests a path towards efficient representation learning for large-scale datasets and real-time applications.
- **Future Research Directions:** The authors identify several exciting future perspectives, including applying `seq-JEPA` to autoregressive latent planning in control tasks, facilitating multi-modal fusion across language, audio, or proprioceptive inputs for comprehensive world modeling, and scaling to larger video and multi-modal datasets like Ego4D. Addressing the challenge of group-agnostic or learned transformation models (without explicit parameter access) is also highlighted as an important open problem.

In conclusion, `seq-JEPA` offers a sophisticated and biologically inspired framework that pushes the boundaries of self-supervised learning by effectively addressing a critical representational challenge. Its robust performance, architectural elegance, and clear path to diverse applications position it as a foundational step towards more holistic and intelligent visual perception systems.
