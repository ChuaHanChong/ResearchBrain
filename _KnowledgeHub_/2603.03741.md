# HALO: Learning Human-Robot Collaboration via Heterogeneous-Agent Lyapunov Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Heterogeneous-Agent Lyapunov Policy Optimization (HALO) provides a framework to stabilize decentralized learning in human-robot collaboration by ensuring the monotonic contraction of the rationality gap between individual and team objectives. This approach yields higher task success rates (e.g., 87.2% in Orientation-sensitive pushing) and significantly reduces the gradient conflict rate to 4.2% compared to existing methods, demonstrating robust adaptation in both simulation and real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Traditional human-robot collaboration (HRC) methods often model humans as static, leading to robots that overfit specific interaction patterns and fail to generalize to diverse human behaviors.
- Heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for HRC introduces a "rationality gap" where individual policy updates diverge from the shared team objective, hindering optimal cooperation.
- Decentralized learning in MARL suffers from inherent dynamical instabilities, such as rotational dynamics and limit cycles in policy-parameter space, preventing stable convergence to cooperative optima.

## Method

- **HALO** introduces a novel Lyapunov-based approach that directly certifies and stabilizes policy-parameter learning dynamics in heterogeneous MARL, establishing a formal stability certificate in parameter space.
- The framework quantifies coordination disagreement as a "rationality gap" and enforces its monotonic contraction by performing an optimal quadratic projection of decentralized optimization dynamics.
- It efficiently computes necessary second-order derivatives as Hessian-vector products via double back-propagation, ensuring scalability without materializing the full Hessian matrix.

## Results

- HALO achieved superior average success rates (e.g., **87.2%** in OSP tasks) and demonstrated faster convergence in simulations compared to state-of-the-art baselines like HAPPO and HATRPO.
- The framework significantly enhanced stability metrics, reducing the rationality gap to **0.09** (vs. **4.89** for HAPPO), increasing gradient alignment to **0.91**, and cutting the gradient conflict rate to **4.2%** at steady state.
- Real-world experiments with a humanoid robot validated HALO's robust sim-to-real transfer, enabling stable load handling (e.g., **1.22** cm/s post-halt drift, **2.2** deg/s object tilt) and adaptive responses to unscripted human behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lyapunov stability principles can be applied directly to policy-parameter space in heterogeneous MARL to stabilize decentralized learning dynamics, ensuring monotonic contraction of the rationality gap.
> - Geometrically rectifying the policy update vector field through an optimal quadratic projection can suppress rotational instabilities and non-conservative forces, leading to higher gradient alignment.
> - The synergistic effect of Lyapunov-based projection, adaptive scheduling, and alignment rectification is crucial for refining policy trajectories and minimizing coordination dissonance in HRC.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human-robot collaboration (HRC) represents a significant challenge within embodied artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the ability of robots to coordinate effectively with diverse and adaptive human partners in real-world environments. Historically, HRC approaches have often treated the human as a static or perturbing element of the environment, framing it as a single-agent task for the robot. This traditional paradigm, relying on predefined scripts, simulators with fixed human inputs, or log-replay mechanisms, tends to produce robots that overfit to specific interaction patterns. Such overfitting leads to a lack of generalization and performance degradation when confronted with out-of-distribution (OOD) human behaviors or novel contexts.

To overcome these limitations, the field has increasingly explored multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for HRC. MARL enables robots to co-adapt with learning-capable partners, facilitating the emergence of adaptive strategies and the handling of a wider spectrum of interaction manifolds, including complex edge cases. This shift from static scripting to adaptive learning is seen as a computational imperative for robust HRC.

However, the application of MARL in heterogeneous HRC introduces specific challenges. A primary issue is the "rationality gap" (RG), which arises because, while agents may share a common team-level objective, their heterogeneous nature necessitates individual policy updates that can diverge from the optimal cooperative direction. Unlike some MARL methods that rely on parameter-sharing (which is infeasible in heterogeneous HRC), this structural mismatch in decoupled architectures exacerbates the RG.

Furthermore, decentralized learning in MARL is susceptible to inherent dynamical instabilities. The optimization process in MARL can be characterized as a non-conservative vector field with a non-symmetric Jacobian. This can lead to rotational dynamics, limit cycles, and unstable oscillations, hindering convergence to cooperative optima and restricting effective exploration. Prior work in differentiable games has attempted to mitigate these instabilities through methods like symplectic gradient adjustment, consensus optimization, extra-gradient methods, and opponent-shaping approaches. However, these techniques often require assumptions such as low-dimensional game settings, centralized access to Jacobians, or fully modeled opponents, which are typically not met in heterogeneous, partially observed, embodied HRC environments. Consequently, existing HRC architectures frequently lack a robust mechanism to neutralize these non-conservative forces and ensure learning stability.

This research addresses this gap by proposing a stability kernel for MARL-based HRC. It introduces a Lyapunov-based approach to directly certify and stabilize policy-parameter learning dynamics in heterogeneous MARL, aiming to ensure monotonic contraction of the rationality gap and robust generalization in complex HRC scenarios. This work distinguishes itself by applying Lyapunov principles not to state/trajectory constraints (as in safe RL), but to the policy-parameter space to stabilize decentralized policy learning, thereby bridging the theoretical gap between MARL-based interaction paradigms and learning-stability kernels for HRC.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the generalization and resilience of robots in human-robot collaboration (HRC) by enabling them to effectively manage the complex and diverse behaviors of human partners and varying interaction contexts.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations observed in traditional HRC paradigms:

- **Inadequate Handling of Human Variability**: Conventional HRC systems often model humans as static, predictable, or merely perturbative environmental components. They rely on predefined scripts, fixed inputs in simulators, or imitation learning from recorded data. This approach inherently restricts robot-human coordination to a finite set of interaction patterns.
- **Susceptibility to Overfitting and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Failures**: Because traditional methods learn from limited, pre-scripted, or logged human behaviors, robots tend to overfit to these specific interaction traces. This leads to a significant performance degradation and potential collapse when encountering human behaviors or environmental conditions that deviate from the training data (i.e., OOD scenarios). Such brittleness hinders the practical deployment of collaborative robots in dynamic and unstructured human environments.
- **The Need for Co-Adaptation**: To move beyond the limitations of static human models, the research advocates for a shift to heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). This paradigm allows for robots to co-adapt with learning-capable human "proxies," enabling the exploration of an effectively infinite manifold of interaction possibilities. The motivation here is to foster the emergence of adaptive strategies that are intractable through manual scripting, thereby capturing complex edge cases crucial for generalization.
- **Addressing Stability Issues in Heterogeneous MARL**: While MARL offers a path to greater adaptivity, heterogeneous learning introduces significant stability challenges:
 - **Rationality Gap (RG)**: In heterogeneous MARL, agents operate with individual policy updates based on local perspectives, even when pursuing a shared team objective. This creates a "rationality gap" where individual gradients diverge from the true cooperative joint optimization direction, leading to misaligned learning.
 - **Dynamical Instabilities in Decentralized Learning**: The underlying dynamics of decentralized MARL updates are non-conservative, characterized by non-symmetric Jacobians. This can induce rotational forces, limit cycles, and oscillatory behaviors in policy-parameter space, preventing convergence to stable cooperative optima. Existing stabilization techniques for differentiable games are often unsuitable for the complexity of embodied, partially observed, and heterogeneous HRC settings.

To address these motivations and challenges, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Stable Learning Kernel for Heterogeneous MARL**: Propose Heterogeneous-Agent Lyapunov Policy Optimization (HALO), a novel framework that enforces stable policy-parameter updates by establishing a formal stability certificate in parameter space.
2. **Ensure Monotonic Contraction of the Rationality Gap**: Design HALO to quantify coordination disagreement as a Lyapunov potential and employ an optimal quadratic projection to rectify decentralized optimization dynamics, thereby guaranteeing the monotonic contraction of the rationality gap (RG).
3. **Provide Theoretical Guarantees**: Establish theoretical assurances, through nonlinear stability analysis, that HALO can stabilize decentralized learning and drive the multi-agent system towards a state of rationality agreement.
4. **Demonstrate Generalization and Robustness**: Empirically validate HALO's efficacy across diverse HRC tasks in both high-fidelity simulations and real-world humanoid robot experiments, showing its ability to improve generalization and robustness, especially in challenging collaborative scenarios and against OOD human behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Heterogeneous-Agent Lyapunov Policy Optimization (HALO) framework is designed to stabilize decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) in human-robot collaboration (HRC) by addressing the rationality gap and inherent dynamical instabilities in policy-parameter space.

**Problem Formulation:**
The HRC task is modeled as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), $M = \langle S, A, P, R, \gamma, N, O, Z \rangle$. Unlike setups with parameter sharing, heterogeneous agents utilize independent policies $\pi_{\theta_i}(a_{i,t}|o_{i,t})$ based on local observations $o_{i,t} = Z(s_t, i)$. Agents share a global reward $r_t = R(s_t, a_t)$, with the objective of maximizing the total return $J(\theta) = E_{a_t \sim \pi_\theta, s_t \sim P}[\sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t R(s_t, a_t)]$.

**Core Concepts:**

1. **Vector Field Misalignment and Rationality Gap**:
 - **Independent Rationality Field ($u_{ind}(\theta)$)**: This field represents the concatenated gradients of individual actor objectives, $J_i(\theta_i)$, computed as if other agents' policies were stationary. It aims for local greedy progress.
 $u_{ind}(\theta) \triangleq [\nabla_{\theta_1} J_1^\top, \ldots, \nabla_{\theta_N} J_N^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^D$.
 - **Team Rationality Field ($u_{team}(\theta)$)**: This field represents the true ascent direction of the global team reward function $J(\theta)$, reflecting the overall cooperative objective.
 $u_{team}(\theta) \triangleq \nabla_\theta J(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^D$.
 - **Rationality Gap ($V(\theta)$)**: Defined as a Lyapunov candidate function, it quantifies the discrepancy between decentralized updates and the team-optimal direction:
 $V(\theta) \triangleq \frac{1}{2} \|u_{ind}(\theta) - u_{team}(\theta)\|^2_2$.
 - **Structural Pathology**: The $u_{ind}$ field is generally non-conservative, with a non-symmetric Jacobian. This leads to solenoidal components that drive limit cycles and oscillations in policy-parameter space.

2. **Stability-Aware Control Objective**:
 The core objective of HALO is to design an update direction $d$ for the joint parameter vector $\theta$ such that it ensures a Lyapunov dissipation constraint: $\langle \nabla_\theta V, d \rangle \le -\sigma V(\theta)$ for some $\sigma > 0$. This ensures the asymptotic contraction of the rationality gap $V(\theta)$, thereby stabilizing decentralized learning.

**HALO Framework Details:**

1. **Structural Stability and Analytic Projection**:
 - HALO seeks an optimal update direction $d^*$ by formulating a constrained quadratic program. This program minimizes the Euclidean distance between the desired update $d$ and the independent rationality field $u_{ind}$, subject to the Lyapunov stability certificate:
 $\min_{d \in \mathbb{R}^D} \frac{1}{2} \|d - u_{ind}(\theta_k)\|^2_2$
 s.t. $\langle \nabla_\theta V(\theta_k), d \rangle \le -\sigma V(\theta_k)$.
 - This formulation performs a minimum-norm projection of $u_{ind}$ onto the "stability half-space" $H_{stable} = \{d \in \mathbb{R}^D | \nabla V^\top d \le -\sigma V\}$.
 - The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions allow for an exact analytic solution. By defining $h \triangleq \nabla_\theta V(\theta_k)$ (the stability normal vector), the optimal update direction $d^*$ is derived as:
 $d^* = u_{ind} - \max\left(0, \frac{\langle h, u_{ind} \rangle + \sigma V}{\|h\|_2^2 + \epsilon}\right) h$, where $\epsilon$ is a damping constant.

2. **Scalability via Hessian-Vector Product (HVP)**:
 - Calculating $h = \nabla_\theta V$ explicitly involves second-order derivatives (Jacobians of vector fields). Direct computation of the Hessian matrix can be computationally expensive ($O(D^2)$).
 - HALO leverages double back-propagation to compute $h$ efficiently as a Hessian-vector product. Specifically, $h = (H_{ind} - H_{team})^\top (u_{ind} - u_{team})$, where $H_{ind}$ and $H_{team}$ are the Jacobians of the independent and team rationality fields, respectively. By retaining the computational graph during the forward pass, the backward pass on $V$ yields this product without materializing the full Hessian.

**Theoretical Analysis:**

- **Monotonic Descent of the Rationality Gap**: Under regularity and smoothness assumptions, HALO is proven to ensure that the rationality gap $V(\theta)$ is monotonically non-increasing. If the learning rate $\eta$ satisfies a stability bound, $V(\theta_{k+1}) - V(\theta_k) \le -\eta\sigma V(\theta_k) + \frac{L\eta^2}{2} \|d^*_k\|^2_2$.
- **Asymptotic Convergence to Equilibrium**: Under appropriate learning rate conditions (Robbins-Monro conditions), HALO drives the multi-agent system towards a state where the disagreement energy $V(\theta)$ converges to zero, implying that $ \lim_{k\to\infty} \|u_{ind}(\theta_k) - u_{team}(\theta_k)\|_2 = 0$. This means decentralized preferences align with global team ascent directions.

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Tasks**: Three continuous-space coordination tasks for HRC were chosen: Orientation-sensitive pushing (OSP), Spatially-confined transport (SCT), and Super-long object handling (SLH). These tasks require precise coordination and demonstrate physical coupling.
- **Simulation Environment**: Training was conducted in Isaac Lab, a massively parallelized simulation infrastructure.
- **Physical Experiments**: Real-world validation was performed using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot collaborating with a human partner, utilizing a motion-capture (MoCap) system for human state sensing.
- **Baselines**: HALO was compared against state-of-the-art heterogeneous MARL methods, including HAPPO and HATRPO (sequential trust-region paradigms), and PCGrad (a method integrating gradient surgery). A "Robot-Script" baseline (IPPO with human modeled as stochastic load) was used for real-world comparisons.
- **Metrics**: Performance was evaluated using success rate (SR), gradient alignment (cos($\phi$)), rationality gap ($V(\theta)$), and gradient conflict rate (GCR) as primary stability indicators. Real-world experiments additionally used metrics like time-to-destination (T), object drop rate (DR), tilt rate (Ang), post-halt drift (v\_d), and proactive waiting (Wait).
- **Control Architecture**: A tri-level hierarchical control framework was employed:
 - **Top-level (N/A freq.)**: Global mission planner (A\* or VLM) for object's center of mass (CoM) path generation.
 - **Mid-level (2 Hz)**: Tactical MARL policy (HALO) for motion command generation.
 - **Bottom-level (50 Hz)**: Whole-Body Controller (WBC) for body balancing and command tracking.
- **Observation and Command Space**: A 210-dimensional observation vector included look-ahead waypoints, self-proprioception, partner observation, object geometry, contact feedback, temporal features, and environment awareness. The 11-dimensional command space specified locomotion (target velocities, yaw), postural setpoints (CoM height, torso pitch), and wrist modulation (3D XYZ offsets).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HALO was conducted through extensive simulations and real-world humanoid robot experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in stabilizing heterogeneous human-robot collaboration.

**Performance Benchmarks in Simulation:**

- **Superior Success Rate**: HALO consistently achieved higher average success rates across all three categories of continuous-space coordination tasks (OSP, SCT, SLH). For instance, in the OSP tasks, HALO achieved an average SR of 87.2%, surpassing HATRPO (81.6%) and HAPPO (78.0%). Similar improvements were observed in SCT (HALO 87.0% vs. HATRPO 81.6%) and SLH (HALO 83.7% vs. HATRPO 79.3%).
- **Faster Convergence**: HALO demonstrated faster convergence in cumulative reward, reaching its performance plateau at approximately 1.3 billion steps, comparable to PCGrad and HATRPO but significantly faster than HAPPO.
- **Enhanced Stability Metrics**: At the 2 billion-step steady state, HALO exhibited superior global optimization metrics:
 - **Rationality Gap ($V(\theta)$)**: HALO achieved a significantly lower rationality gap of 0.09, compared to HAPPO (4.89), HATRPO (1.53), and PCGrad (0.20). This indicates successful contraction of the disagreement between individual and team rationality.
 - **Gradient Alignment (cos($\phi$))**: HALO showed the highest gradient alignment of 0.91, signifying better alignment of decentralized gradients with the cooperative objective, compared to other baselines (e.g., PCGrad 0.84, HAPPO 0.67).
 - **Gradient Conflict Rate (GCR)**: HALO drastically reduced the GCR to 4.2%, indicating that it effectively filtered out conflicting gradient directions, whereas HAPPO had a GCR of 72.5%.
- **Computational Efficiency**: Despite involving second-order derivatives, HALO's use of Hessian-vector products via double back-propagation resulted in negligible memory overhead (< 21.1%). The increased computation time for HVP was offset by other factors, leading to a 13.1% reduction in total wall-clock training time compared to HAPPO in high-throughput settings.

**Mechanism Analysis of HALO:**

- **Geometric Rectification of the Vector Field**: The results confirmed HALO's ability to ensure a monotonic descent of $V(\theta)$, which is a direct validation of the Lyapunov stability certificate. This stabilization was evidenced by the low steady-state rationality gap and its consistent decay over training steps.
- **Stabilization via Alignment**: By projecting the independent rationality field $u_{ind}$ onto the stability half-space, HALO effectively suppressed rotational instabilities and non-conservative forces, leading to high gradient alignment and low GCR.

**Ablation Study:**

An ablation study on the SLH-extreme (pivoting) task provided insights into the contributions of HALO's components:

- **Importance of Hard Projection**: A "Soft-V penalty" variant (incorporating $V(\theta)$ as a Lagrangian penalty) yielded only marginal improvements in SR (76.5%) compared to the HAPPO baseline (74.9%). In contrast, the "Static projection" variant (implementing the hard analytic projection without adaptive scheduling or alignment) showed a more substantial improvement (79.5% SR) and significantly reduced the rationality gap (0.85). This confirms that a direct geometric projection onto the stability half-space is crucial for rectifying update directions, whereas soft regularization primarily modulates gradient magnitudes.
- **Synergy of Full HALO**: The full HALO framework, which includes the Lyapunov projection, adaptive scheduling ($\eta$), and alignment rectification (cos $\phi$), achieved the highest success rate (82.3%) and lowest rationality gap (0.09). This underscores the synergistic effect of these components in refining the policy trajectory and suppressing coordination dissonance.

**Real-World Human-Robot Collaboration:**

Real-world experiments with a Unitree G1 robot demonstrated HALO's effectiveness and sim-to-real transferability under partner non-stationarity, comparing it against PCGrad and a Robot-Script baseline.

- **Micro-view Resilience Analysis**:
 - **Vertical Synchronization**: HALO enabled the G1 robot to autonomously maintain a horizontal load plane even when the human partner's height varied unexpectedly.
 - **Movement Synchronization**: During unscripted 20-second obstructions by the human, HALO maintained stability through behaviors like stationary stepping and subsequent velocity re-synchronization, demonstrating robust adaptation.
- **Quantitative Real-World Performance**:
 - **OSP and SCT Tasks**: HALO exhibited superior coordination resilience, significantly reducing the time-to-destination (e.g., 76.2 seconds for SCT) and minimizing object tilt rates (e.g., 2.2 degrees/second for SCT), outperforming PCGrad and the Robot-Script baseline.
 - **SLH Task (Stability Under Halting)**: HALO showed exceptional stability during unscripted human halts. Unlike the Robot-Script baseline (which often led to 100% object drop rate), HALO proactively dissipated residual momentum, resulting in a minimal post-halt drift of 1.22 cm/s. This performance highlights HALO's ability to internalize team-level synergy and maintain fluid interaction even under unexpected perturbations. The Robot-Script baseline frequently failed, with high object drop rates (60% in SCT, 100% in SLH) and inability to adapt to halting, underscoring the limitations of non-adaptive, scripted approaches.

In summary, the results indicate that HALO effectively addresses the inherent structural instabilities in decentralized HRC by enforcing Lyapunov-based stability in the policy-parameter space. This leads to superior performance, faster convergence, and enhanced robustness in both simulated and real-world collaborative scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of human-robot collaboration (HRC) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), addressing fundamental challenges that hinder the practical deployment of collaborative robots.

**Theoretical Significance:**

- **Novel Stability Kernel for MARL**: The introduction of HALO provides a novel, theoretically grounded mechanism to stabilize decentralized MARL. By defining the "rationality gap" as a Lyapunov potential and enforcing its monotonic contraction through an optimal quadratic projection, the framework offers a formal stability certificate in the policy-parameter space. This extends Lyapunov methods beyond traditional safe RL (which focuses on state/trajectory constraints) to directly stabilize the learning dynamics of policies.
- **Geometric Rectification of Learning Dynamics**: The approach demonstrates how to geometrically rectify decentralized gradients, transforming a potentially oscillatory, non-conservative learning process into a dissipative dynamical system. This mechanism helps to neutralize the rotational forces and non-symmetric Jacobians that commonly cause instability and divergence in heterogeneous MARL.
- **Unified Paradigm for Interaction Manifolds**: By framing HRC as a MARL problem over an "effectively infinite interaction space," the work offers a more comprehensive and adaptive paradigm compared to limited, scripted human models. This paradigm is crucial for exploring the vast diversity of human behaviors and contexts.

**Practical Impact and Potential Applications:**

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: HALO significantly improves the generalization capabilities and robustness of collaborative robots. By preventing overfitting to specific interaction traces and enabling co-adaptation with diverse human partners, robots can perform reliably even when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) human behaviors or unexpected events, such as unscripted halts or changes in partner movement patterns. This is critical for real-world reliability where human behavior is inherently stochastic and unpredictable.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability in HRC**: The certified stability in parameter space directly translates to superior trajectory coordination and resilience in safety-critical, unstructured environments. This is demonstrated by the robot's ability to maintain stable load handling during partner height variations, adapt to prolonged obstructions, and proactively dissipate momentum during unexpected halts, reducing object drop rates and improving overall operational safety.
- **Scalable Deployment of Collaborative Robots**: The framework provides a scalable foundation for deploying collaborative robots in complex settings, including industrial workflows, logistics operations, and assistive environments. These settings often involve heterogeneous users, dynamic intent patterns, and rare interaction modes, where current methods struggle due to their brittleness.
- **Foundation for Bridging Rationality Gaps**: HALO offers a pathway to bridge the inherent gap between individual, decentralized rationality and global collaborative synergy, a long-standing challenge in multi-agent systems.

**Societal Implications:**

- **Positive Impacts**: The deployment of more capable and reliable collaborative robots can lead to several positive societal impacts. These include reducing physical workload for human workers, expanding human operational capacity in various industries, and improving safety in labor-intensive or hazardous environments.
- **Ethical Considerations**: The paper acknowledges important ethical and deployment considerations. These primarily revolve around ensuring the **safety** of human-robot interactions, maintaining **transparency** in robot decision-making, clearly defining **operational responsibility** in mixed-autonomy systems, and addressing potential long-term issues such as **labor displacement** and the **transition of the workforce** in society. These considerations highlight the need for careful societal planning and policy development alongside technological advancements.

In conclusion, HALO contributes a robust, theoretically-backed solution to a critical problem in HRC, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptive, and reliable collaborative robots in diverse real-world applications.
