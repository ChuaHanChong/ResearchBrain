# EmbodMocap: In-the-Wild 4D Human-Scene Reconstruction for Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodMocap provides a portable framework for metrically accurate 4D human-scene reconstruction using two iPhones, addressing limitations of cost and environment. This system yields high-fidelity data, improving human-scene alignment to approximately 5cm accuracy and enabling robust training for complex embodied AI tasks like robot control.

## Problem

- Existing 4D human-scene data capture systems are costly, complex, and confined to controlled studio environments, limiting data diversity and scalability for embodied AI research.
- Automated extraction of 3D motion and scene geometry from current video sources often suffers from occlusions, self-occlusions, and inherent depth ambiguities, leading to inaccurate reconstructions.
- A lack of metrically accurate, unified representations where dynamic human motion is seamlessly anchored within static scene geometry impedes realistic human-scene interaction modeling.

## Method

- A portable framework utilizing two synchronized RGB-D iPhones captures dynamic human motion and static scene geometry in diverse, "in-the-wild" environments.
- A multi-stage pipeline reconstructs a metrically accurate scene, processes dual-view human sequences, and calibrates all data into a unified, gravity-aligned world coordinate frame.
- Joint optimization leverages point tracking, Chamfer distance, and bundle adjustment for robust camera-scene alignment, followed by ==World-Space SMPLify== for precise human pose and shape reconstruction.

## Results

- The dual-view EmbodMocap system achieved approximately 5cm accuracy in aligning human trajectories to scene coordinates, outperforming single-view methods that resulted in over 30cm error.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of all optimization components, with removing L_track increasing Reprojection Error from **9.3** to **44.2**, highlighting its critical role in view stitching and resolving depth ambiguity.
- EmbodMocap data successfully enabled training embodied agents for complex human-object interaction skills, achieving a **66.0%** success rate for challenging 'Support' tasks where monocular data yielded only **20.6%**, and facilitated sim-to-real transfer for physical humanoid robot control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-view RGB-D setup effectively addresses depth ambiguity and occlusion, enabling significantly more accurate human-scene alignment (approx. 5cm) compared to single-view or monocular methods.
> - The comprehensive optimization pipeline, combining various loss functions (tracking, Chamfer distance, reprojection, 3D keypoints, smoothness), is crucial for achieving high-fidelity 4D human and scene reconstructions.
> - Data captured with the EmbodMocap framework provides sufficient quality and fidelity to improve performance in downstream embodied AI tasks, including training robust monocular reconstruction models and complex physics-based robot control policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in EmbodMocap addresses a recognized challenge within the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (Embodied AI). Embodied AI endeavors to create agents capable of perceiving, understanding, and interacting within real-world environments. A foundational requirement for advancing this field is the availability of high-quality datasets that capture both human motion and the surrounding 3D scene. Such data is essential for modeling realistic human-scene interactions, simulating behaviors, and training robotic systems to operate effectively in complex environments.

Historically, acquiring precise 3D motion and scene geometry data has been difficult. Publicly available internet videos are often inadequate due to issues like occlusion and inherent depth ambiguity. Existing high-quality capture systems typically fall into several categories, each with limitations:
- **Multi-view camera rigs (e.g., PROX, RICH, EgoBody):** These setups provide high fidelity but are expensive, complex, and generally restricted to controlled studio environments.
- **Wearable motion capture suits (e.g., EMDB, SLOPER4D, Nymeria):** While offering more flexibility in terms of environment, these systems can be costly, require extensive manual alignment and post-processing to synchronize motion with 3D scenes, and the wearable devices themselves can affect the natural appearance of subjects in RGB images.
- **LiDAR scanners:** Used for scene geometry, but typically integrated with other systems for motion capture.

These constraints have impeded the large-scale acquisition of scene-aware data, consequently limiting the ability of Embodied AI models to learn from natural human behavior in a wide array of indoor and outdoor settings.

The current research landscape is actively moving towards joint reconstruction of humans and 3D scenes from monocular or multi-view input, with works like HSFM, HAMSt3R, JOSH, and Human3R demonstrating advances in this area. Additionally, training humanoid robots from video data (e.g., VideoMimic, ASAP, HDMI) has emerged as a promising paradigm to overcome the limitations of marker-based methods. However, video-driven approaches often struggle with capturing complex skills or accurate scene geometries due to occlusions and depth ambiguities when relying solely on monocular inputs or estimations.

EmbodMocap aims to bridge this gap by offering a portable and affordable solution that leverages readily available consumer devices (two iPhones) to capture metrically accurate 4D human and scene data. By jointly calibrating dual RGB-D sequences, the system seeks to overcome the limitations of existing methods regarding cost, environmental restrictions, and data quality (especially depth ambiguity and occlusion), thereby providing a scalable foundation for advanced Embodied AI research without compromising the natural appearance of the subjects. This positions EmbodMocap as a contribution to making high-quality human-scene interaction data more accessible for a broader range of research and application scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EmbodMocap is to address the limitations of existing 4D human-scene data capture systems, which impede the progress of Embodied Artificial Intelligence. The core problems identified are:

- **Cost and Complexity:** Traditional high-quality capture systems rely on expensive studio setups, multi-view camera rigs, wearable motion suits, or specialized LiDAR scanners. These systems are costly to acquire and operate, demanding significant technical expertise and resources.
- **Environmental Restrictions:** Due to their complexity and specialized hardware, existing systems are largely confined to controlled studio environments. This restricts the collection of human motion data to artificial settings, failing to capture the rich, long-term contextual information inherent in human behaviors within diverse, natural real-world environments.
- **Lack of Scalability:** The high cost and environmental limitations prevent large-scale data collection. This scarcity of diverse, scene-conditioned human motion data hinders the training of robust embodied agents for perception, understanding, and acting in varied real-world scenarios.
- **Data Quality Issues:** Automated extraction of precise 3D motion and scene geometry from internet videos is often compromised by occlusions, self-occlusions, and depth ambiguities, leading to inconsistent or inaccurate reconstructions. Wearable devices can also alter the natural appearance of subjects.
- **Discrepancy between Human Motion and Scene Geometry:** Many datasets either focus solely on human motion or provide static scene models, lacking a seamless, unified metric-scale representation where human motion is accurately anchored within the scene geometry. This "scene-awareness" is crucial for physically grounded learning.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of the EmbodMocap research are:

1. **Develop a Portable and Affordable Capture Framework:** To design a data collection pipeline using minimal, readily available, and low-cost consumer devices (specifically, two moving iPhones equipped with RGB-D capabilities) that can be deployed "in-the-wild" across diverse environments.
2. **Achieve Simultaneous 4D Human-Scene Reconstruction:** To reconstruct both dynamic human motion and static scene geometry concurrently and precisely.
3. **Ensure Unified Metric World Coordinate Frame:** A central objective is to jointly calibrate and optimize dual RGB-D inputs to produce reconstructions that are consistently aligned within a single, metrically accurate, gravity-aligned world coordinate frame. This ensures spatial consistency between human motion and scene geometry.
4. **Mitigate Depth Ambiguity and Occlusion:** Leverage a dual-view setting to improve depth estimation, resolve ambiguities, and better handle occlusions compared to single-view or monocular approaches.
5. **Create a High-Quality, Multi-modal Dataset:** To generate a new collection of scene-aware human motion data that is suitable for scalable training of Embodied AI models.
6. **Validate Utility Across Embodied AI Tasks:** To demonstrate the reliability and versatility of the captured data and the system through its application to three representative embodied AI tasks:
 - Monocular human-scene reconstruction.
 - Physics-based character animation and human-object interaction skill training.
 - Real-world humanoid robot motion control via sim-to-real transfer.

By achieving these objectives, EmbodMocap aims to lower the barrier for high-quality data acquisition in Embodied AI, fostering advancements in understanding and generating realistic human behavior in complex, interactive environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EmbodMocap is designed as a portable and affordable framework for capturing metrically accurate 4D human motion and scene geometry using two iPhones. The capture and processing system operates through four sequential stages, progressively reconstructing and aligning the scene, cameras, and human motion within a unified world coordinate frame.

**Stage I: Scene Reconstruction**
The initial stage focuses on establishing a metrically accurate, Z-up scene mesh that serves as the reference world coordinate system.
1. **Data Capture:** A single iPhone is used to record an RGB-D video of the scene, accompanied by synchronized Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data.
2. **Camera Parameter Estimation:** The recorded data is processed by the SpectacularAI SDK (SAI). SAI automatically identifies keyframes based on accumulated camera translation and estimates corresponding camera parameters (intrinsics K\_s, rotation R\_s,n, and translation T\_s,n) in a Z-up world coordinate system with metric scale. These trajectories define a consistent world frame for subsequent stages.
3. **Depth Refinement and Mesh Generation:** iPhone LiDAR depth maps are refined using PromptDA. These refined depths are then unprojected into 3D space. The resulting point clouds are integrated through Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) fusion to create a dense and metrically accurate global mesh (M\_g). Depth maps are truncated based on the iPhone's sensor range (3.5m for indoors, 5m for outdoors).
4. **Mesh Post-processing:** Lightweight post-processing steps, including outlier removal and small-component filtering, are applied to clean the generated mesh.
5. **Sparse Structure Database:** SIFT features are extracted from the same SAI keyframes, and COLMAP is executed with fixed camera parameters to construct a sparse structure database. This database preserves the metric scale and functions as a reference for registering dual-view sequences later.

**Stage II: Sequence Processing**
Following scene reconstruction, this stage involves capturing and preparing dual-view human motion sequences within the established environment.
1. **Dual-View Capture:** Two synchronized iPhones record RGB-D videos of a performer moving within the reconstructed scene. Each device provides an independent camera coordinate system.
2. **Per-frame Camera Calibration:** SAI is used independently for each iPhone view (v1, v2) to obtain per-frame calibrated camera intrinsics (K\_v) and extrinsics (R\_v,t, T\_v,t) in the native coordinate system of each view.
3. **Human Information Extraction:** Several off-the-shelf models are utilized to extract human-related data:
 - YOLO for person detection and proposal pruning.
 - ViTPose for 2D human keypoints with confidence scores.
 - SAM2 for person segmentation masks.
 - PromptDA for refining dual-view depths.
 - VIMO for camera-space SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) parameters.
4. **Temporal Synchronization:** A laser pointer cue is employed for frame-level synchronization between the two camera streams. By identifying the frame index where the laser dot disappears, both videos and their associated data (images, depths, parameters) are temporally aligned and segmented.

**Stage III: Sequence Calibration**
This stage focuses on aligning all camera coordinate systems into the unified world frame defined in Stage I.
1. **Initial Transformation from COLMAP:** Each dual-view sequence is registered to the sparse COLMAP model (from Stage I) using known intrinsics and SIFT features extracted from background-only regions (human removed). This process yields initial COLMAP camera poses (R̂\_v,t, T̂\_v,t) in the scene's metric, gravity-aligned world coordinates. An offset transformation (s\_off, R\_off, T\_off) is then computed using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to align the SAI camera trajectories (T\_v,t) with their COLMAP counterparts (T̂\_v,t). The rotation R\_off is constrained to z-axis rotation to maintain gravity alignment.
2. **Calibration via Multiple Constraints (Joint Optimization):** The initial alignment is further refined through a joint optimization process that minimizes a composite loss function (L\_calib). This optimization refines the per-view global offsets (R\_off\_v, T\_off\_v, constrained to z-axis rotations for R\_off\_v). L\_calib combines three types of constraints:
 - **Point Tracking Loss (L\_track):** VGGT tracking selects keyframes and provides dual-view pixel tracking results within human mask regions. Back-projected 3D points from each view (Q\_1,t, Q\_2,t) are then compared, with the loss minimizing the distance between corresponding points across views, weighted by tracking confidence. This explicitly stitches the two views together.
 - **Chamfer Distance (d\_Chamfer):** This term aligns local point clouds (P\_v), reconstructed from the scene with humans masked out, with the global scene mesh (P\_g) from Stage I. This ensures spatial consistency between the reconstructed scene and the global reference.
 - **Bundle Adjustment Loss (L\_ba,v):** This loss enforces reprojection consistency for persistent matches obtained from COLMAP image registration, further refining camera poses.
 - The optimization is performed using the Adam optimizer with gradient clipping.

**Stage IV: Motion Optimization**
The final stage refines the human reconstruction results to achieve accurate and temporally consistent body motions within the now unified world frame, with camera poses and scene geometry fixed.
1. **3D Keypoint Triangulation:** Dual-view 2D human keypoints (y\_v,t,j) obtained in Stage II are triangulated into world-space 3D keypoints (Y\_t,j). This is achieved by minimizing the weighted reprojection error across all views using a weighted least squares optimization, solved via SVD. These triangulated 3D keypoints serve as robust geometric constraints.
2. **World-Space SMPLify:** Starting with initial SMPL shape (β\_0) and body pose (θ\_b,0\_t) from VIMO in Stage II, the World Frame SMPLify process jointly optimizes the global shape (β), per-frame pose (θ\_t = {θ\_g\_t, θ\_b\_t}), and root translation (γ\_t). This optimization minimizes a composite loss (L\_SMPLify = L\_3D + L\_smooth + L\_prior + L\_reproj). A two-stage optimization is employed: first, fitting body shape and translation, then refining all parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents an evaluation of the EmbodMocap pipeline through ablation studies, comparisons with existing methods, and demonstrations across three distinct embodied AI downstream tasks.

**Evaluation of Pipeline Effectiveness:**

1. **Ablation Study on Loss Functions (Table 2):** An ablation study was conducted on five core loss functions within the data optimization pipeline: tracking loss (L\_track), Chamfer distance (L\_chamfer), reprojection loss (L\_reproj), smoothness loss (L\_smooth), and 3D keypoint loss (L\_kp3d). Performance was measured using Intersection over Union (IoU) for mask overlap, Reprojection Error (Reproj), Depth Error (MSE between rendered and sensor depths), and Jitter (temporal foot skating).
 - **Impact of L\_track:** Removing L\_track resulted in significant degradation across all metrics (IoU dropped from 73.0% to 54.3%, Reproj increased from 9.3 to 44.2, Depth error increased from 0.078 to 2.372), indicating its critical role in stitching the two views and improving overall reconstruction.
 - **Impact of L\_kp3d:** Removing L\_kp3d also led to a substantial performance drop (IoU from 73.0% to 59.3%, Reproj from 9.3 to 20.4, Depth error from 0.078 to 0.609), highlighting its importance in providing 3D joint positions and resolving depth ambiguity.
 - The complete set of loss functions yielded the best performance, validating the contribution of each component to the optimization process.

2. **Comparison on Capture Methods (Table 3):** EmbodMocap was directly compared against a monocular model (GVHMR) and single-view optimization in an optical motion capture studio with Vicon ground truth.
 - The dual-view EmbodMocap method consistently outperformed both the monocular model and single-view optimization across various chunk lengths (100, 500, 1000 frames) for Weighted-Mean Per Joint Position Error (W-MPJPE), World-Aligned Mean Per Joint Position Error (WA-MPJPE), and Root Translation Error (RTE). The advantage of the dual-view system became more pronounced with increasing chunk length.
 - This superiority is attributed to dual-view's ability to address occlusion and self-occlusion more effectively, and its capacity to resolve depth ambiguity in camera localization, enabling a more accurate (approximately 5cm) alignment of human trajectories to the scene coordinate system, compared to over 30cm for single-view methods.

**Downstream Tasks:**

1. **Monocular Human & Scene Reconstruction (Table 4, Fig. 4):**
 - The collected EmbodMocap dataset was used to fine-tune two feedforward models: π\_3 for SLAM and VIMO for metric-scale human motion reconstruction.
 - Results on the EMDB benchmark showed that fine-tuning VIMO with EmbodMocap data improved its accuracy. Further fine-tuning both π\_3 and VIMO together yielded additional improvements in WA-MPJPE (from 83.56 to 82.21), W-MPJPE (from 229.04 to 220.65), and RTE (from 1.78 to 1.71) compared to the original models. This demonstrates the dataset's quality for training monocular human-scene reconstruction pipelines.

2. **Physics-based Character Animation:**
 - **Human-Object Interaction Skill Training (Table 5, Fig. 5):**
 - Embodied agents were trained with goal-conditioned reinforcement learning for various human-object interaction skills (Follow, Climb, Sit, Lie, Prone, Support). The EmbodMocap data was compared to optically captured data and monocularly estimated motions.
 - For skills with lower inherent difficulty (Follow, Climb, Sit), EmbodMocap data achieved success rates comparable to optical mocap, and superior to monocular estimations. Increasing the quantity of EmbodMocap data generally led to improvements in success rate, contact error, and motion diversity (APD).
 - For more challenging skills (Lie, Prone, Support), policies trained with EmbodMocap data demonstrated a significant advantage over those trained with monocular estimated motions. For example, the Support skill showed a 66.0% success rate with EmbodMocap data compared to 20.6% with monocular data. This highlights EmbodMocap's utility for complex, high-precision interaction tasks.
 - **Scene-aware Motion Tracking (Table 6, Fig. 6):**
 - A scene-aware motion tracking framework, extending MimicKit by incorporating height maps, was trained using EmbodMocap data across four diverse 3D scenes.
 - The policies achieved high success rates (ranging from 87.2% to 96.7%) and maintained long episode lengths (average near 10s), indicating the simulation-readiness and fidelity of the collected data. Qualitative results showed accurate motion tracking and adaptation to imperfections in the reference data.

3. **Real-world Humanoid Robot Control (Fig. 7):**
 - Human motions (locomotion and cartwheels) captured with EmbodMocap were used for real-to-sim reconstruction. The generated motions were then used to train a single tracking policy via sim-to-real reinforcement learning with domain randomization (BeyondMimic).
 - The trained policy was deployed on a physical High Torque Hi humanoid robot. The robot successfully replicated the human motions depicted in the videos, demonstrating that the EmbodMocap pipeline produces data of sufficient quality and accuracy for advanced humanoid robot control.

In summary, the results indicate that EmbodMocap effectively mitigates depth ambiguity and occlusion, providing high-fidelity 4D human-scene reconstructions. This quality is validated through quantitative comparisons and its successful application across perception, animation, and robotics tasks in embodied AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EmbodMocap presents a significant contribution to the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence by addressing a fundamental bottleneck: the scalable acquisition of high-quality, scene-conditioned human motion data in diverse, real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Democratization of 4D Human-Scene Data Capture:** By proposing a system that utilizes readily available, portable, and low-cost consumer devices (two iPhones), EmbodMocap substantially lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners. This accessibility allows for data collection outside of specialized, expensive studios, making high-fidelity data more attainable for a broader research community.

2. **Enhanced Data Quality in Uncontrolled Environments:** The dual-view RGB-D approach, combined with sophisticated calibration and optimization techniques, demonstrates a notable ability to mitigate common challenges like depth ambiguity, occlusion, and self-occlusion. This results in metrically accurate and scene-consistent 4D human and scene reconstructions, a critical improvement over single-view or monocular methods in uncontrolled settings. The direct comparison against optical motion capture ground truth validates this improved accuracy.

3. **Scalable and Diverse Data Generation:** The portability of EmbodMocap enables the collection of large-scale datasets across a wider variety of indoor and outdoor environments. This diversity in scene contexts and human behaviors is crucial for training robust and generalizable embodied agents, moving beyond the limited scope of studio-captured data.

4. **Advancement of Embodied AI Applications:** The research demonstrates the direct utility and versatility of the generated data across key embodied AI tasks:
 - **Monocular Human-Scene Reconstruction:** The data can effectively fine-tune feedforward models, improving their ability to jointly recover human poses and scene geometry in a world-aligned metric space. This is vital for developing more robust perception systems for embodied agents.
 - **Physics-based Character Animation:** The high-fidelity motion data enables the training of more physically realistic and scalable human-object interaction skills. Its superiority over monocular estimations for complex interactions highlights its value for generating authentic character behaviors in virtual environments. Scene-aware motion tracking capabilities also contribute to more intelligent and adaptive simulated agents.
 - **Real-world Humanoid Robot Control:** The successful sim-to-real transfer of policies trained on EmbodMocap data to a physical humanoid robot demonstrates that the system produces data of sufficient quality for learning complex motor skills in robotics. This opens new avenues for training humanoid robots to interact naturally and effectively in human-centric environments.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** EmbodMocap provides a robust pipeline and dataset that can serve as a foundation for further research in various areas, including:
 - Developing more sophisticated human-robot interaction systems.
 - Creating more realistic virtual reality and augmented reality experiences.
 - Advancing fundamental research in human perception, action understanding, and generative models for human behavior.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the iPhone LiDAR range and challenges with highly dynamic scenes or extreme lighting, the core contribution of a portable, affordable, and high-quality 4D human-scene reconstruction system significantly lowers a critical barrier in embodied AI. This work is positioned to accelerate innovation by making richer, more ecologically valid data accessible, thereby fostering the development of more intelligent and capable embodied agents.
