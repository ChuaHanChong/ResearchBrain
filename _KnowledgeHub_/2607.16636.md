# PhyAgentOS: A Self-Evolving Operating System for Embodied Agents with Decoupled Cognitive Planning and Physical Execution

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyAgentOS is an operating-system layer beneath VLA models, world models and agentic planners. It materializes the cognition-physics boundary as Markdown/YAML files, schedules whole sessions, and uses a SessionVerifier to issue evidence-grounded success/failure/replan verdicts consolidated into persistent memory. Without retraining any policy, it lifts RoboCasa365 success by up to 9.2 points and drives 19+ robot embodiments through one protocol.

## Problem

- VLA models, world models and agentic planners each solve one subproblem, but none is responsible for semantically verifying whether the intended physical outcome actually occurred.
- Composing them fails structurally: incompatible abstraction levels force ad hoc converters, failures become harder to attribute, and knowledge gained in one session is discarded.
- ROS supplies publish-subscribe middleware only, leaving task-level scheduling, cross-session memory, semantic acceptance and layered safety as application-specific code re-implemented per robot.

## Method

- **State-as-a-File** protocol materializes every cross-layer datum in human-readable Markdown with embedded YAML, giving an ==append-only audit trail== and removing code dependencies between Agent and Runtime layers.
- **Session-Centered Runtime** makes a session the scheduling unit; **WatchdogSupervisor** claims pending sessions, checks adapter compatibility and monitors heartbeats, while **SessionVerifier** maps evidence bundles to success/failure/replan verdicts.
- **Epistemic Memory** runs an ==execute-verify-diagnose-revise-reverify-consolidate loop== splitting verified successes into KNOWLEDGE.md and corrected failures into LESSONS.md, plus a layered safety stack of preflight, action bridges and **SafetyGuard**.

## Results

- On Optimus-67 Minecraft, success reaches **99%** Wood, **96%** Stone, **52%** Iron and **30%** RedStone; StarDojo scores **22.0%** overall versus **18.0-20.3%** for SPIKE variants.
- StarDojo crafting rises to **50.0%** against **23.8%**; on Don't Starve, mean survival doubles from **1.02** to **2.10** days with **30%** Day-3 survival versus **0%**.
- Policy-agnostic gains without weight updates: LIBERO **+0.4** to **+1.3** points, CALVIN five-step chain success **38.9%** to **45.6%**, RoboCasa365 **+7.2** to **+9.2** points across three backends.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Execution termination is not task completion; a separate evidence-grounded verifier catches silent failures that controller return codes and world-model agreement both report as success.
> - Measurable self-improvement is achievable without gradient updates: verified verdicts consolidated into retrievable lessons and skills give cumulative adaptation across sessions and embodiments.
> - Files as an inter-process boundary buy auditability and language-agnostic decoupling, but polling costs latency and unbounded memory growth needs principled compression.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) currently leverages several distinct paradigms, including vision-language-action (VLA) models, world models, and agentic planners. Each of these approaches contributes to specific aspects of physical intelligence. VLA models excel at mapping perceptual inputs to motor commands, demonstrating end-to-end generalization. World models focus on learning predictive dynamics for look-ahead planning. Agentic systems are designed for task decomposition, tool use, and multi-step inference.

However, the paper identifies a structural limitation in current embodied AI architectures: the straightforward composition of these individual components often results in a fragmented system. Key issues include a lack of common execution abstraction, an absence of shared state representation, insufficient semantic verification of task outcomes, and limited persistence of experience across diverse robotic embodiments. Traditional robot middleware, such as the Robot Operating System (ROS), provides communication infrastructure but does not inherently address high-level task orchestration, semantic outcome interpretation, or persistent memory. Consequently, current systems often conflate execution termination with actual task completion, leading to systemic diagnostic challenges and a lack of self-correction capabilities.

PhyAgentOS positions itself as a foundational operating system layer that operates beneath and integrates these existing paradigms. It aims to bridge the "verification gap" and provide system-level services for scheduling, semantic verification, memory management, benchmarking, and safety. This approach seeks to overcome the limitations of monolithic VLA models (opacity, poor diagnostic feedback), weakly coupled world models (brittle sim-to-real transfer, weak task-language coupling), and tightly coupled agentic systems (ambiguous failure attribution, re-implementation for new hardware). By providing a unified runtime foundation, PhyAgentOS addresses the architectural deficiencies that prevent current embodied AI systems from achieving reliable, verifiable, and self-evolving physical intelligence across heterogeneous platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PhyAgentOS is to address fundamental architectural deficiencies in contemporary embodied AI systems, which hinder their reliability, verifiability, and ability to learn from experience. The paper highlights a critical "verification gap," where the successful completion of a motor command is often erroneously equated with the achievement of a high-level task objective. This conflation leads to systems signaling "success" even when the intended environmental outcome has not been realized, resulting in what the authors term "systematic self-deception."

Specifically, the work is motivated by three structural failures inherent in simply composing existing VLA models, world models, and agentic planners:
1. **Incompatible Levels of Abstraction**: These components operate at disparate abstraction levels (e.g., pixel arrays and joint trajectories for VLAs, latent states for world models, symbolic tool calls for agents), necessitating ad-hoc converters and preventing a unified understanding of state.
2. **Compounded Opacity**: When failures occur in multi-step plans involving multiple components, identifying the root cause becomes significantly challenging due to the lack of shared diagnostic feedback and explicit interfaces.
3. **Lack of Persistent Memory**: Knowledge gained during one execution session (e.g., a successful grasping strategy or a safe navigation route) is typically discarded, preventing cumulative learning and continuous improvement without costly neural model retraining.

In response to these motivations, PhyAgentOS outlines several key objectives:
- **Decouple Cognitive Planning from Physical Execution**: To clearly separate the "what to do" (Agent layer) from the "how to do it" (Runtime layer), preventing direct code dependencies and allowing independent evolution of each plane.
- **Establish a Common Execution Abstraction and Shared State Representation**: To create a unified, inspectable, and versionable record of execution by representing the cognition-physics boundary as a file system, using a "State-as-a-File" protocol. This protocol materializes cross-layer state in human-readable Markdown documents with embedded YAML.
- **Provide Semantic Verification of Task Outcomes**: To move beyond mere execution termination signals and implement a "Session-Centered Runtime." Here, a session is the unit of scheduling, validation, and acceptance. The `SessionVerifier` assesses task completion based on an evidence bundle against defined acceptance criteria, issuing explicit verdicts of success, failure, or replan.
- **Enable Persistent Experience and Self-Evolution**: To facilitate a closed trial-and-error learning loop. Verified outcomes are consolidated into an "Epistemic Memory" that stores reusable knowledge (`KNOWLEDGE.md`) and corrective lessons (`LESSONS.md`), allowing the system to adapt and improve across sessions, tasks, and embodiments without requiring neural model retraining.
- **Deliver System-Level Services**: To integrate scheduling, verification, memory, benchmarking, and safety as first-class services, elevating the functionality beyond traditional middleware.
- **Implement a Layered Safety Architecture**: To constrain both policy-driven and agent-driven execution through a defense-in-depth approach, including compatibility preflight, action bridges, a `SafetyGuard`, heartbeat monitoring, and target-local constraints.
- **Integrate Benchmarking as an Internal Mechanism**: To ensure that evaluation processes reuse the same session, runtime, and verification paths as deployment, making benchmark results directly traceable and instrumental for self-evolution.

By pursuing these objectives, PhyAgentOS aims to provide a robust, transparent, and adaptable foundation for building reliable and intelligent embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhyAgentOS is designed as a runtime foundation that sits beneath existing embodied AI paradigms, providing system-level services. Its core methodology revolves around a strict decoupling of cognitive planning from physical execution, mediated by a standardized, human-readable file-based protocol.

**Overall Architecture:**
The system is structured into two main reasoning planes:
1. **Agent Layer (Cognitive Planning)**: Responsible for determining "what should be done." This layer interprets user requests, builds task context, decomposes goals, selects appropriate execution methods (`SkillRuntime`), and identifies target platforms. Its output is a structured "session contract." The `Goal Planner` translates user intent into objectives, the `Goal Graph` and `Session Compiler` break objectives into subtasks, and the `SkillRuntime-Target Selector` binds subtasks to execution methods and targets. A `ContextBuilder` integrates relevant environment state, prior experiences, and capabilities. A `ToolRegistry` constrains the Agent's interactions to structured, authorized operations.
2. **Runtime Layer (Physical Execution)**: Responsible for determining "how the task is carried out." This layer claims, validates, supervises, and executes the session contract on the physical target. It comprises a `WatchdogSupervisor`, `SessionRunner`, `SkillRuntime`, and `Target` interface.

**Protocol Layer: State-as-a-File:**
A central design principle is the "State-as-a-File" protocol, which treats the cognition-physics boundary as a file system. All cross-process data are materialized in human-readable Markdown files with embedded YAML blocks. This ensures auditability, version control, and language-agnostic interaction. The five primary protocol documents are:
- `SESSIONS.md`: The transactional core, containing session ID, task objective, chosen `SkillRuntime` and target, preconditions, acceptance criteria, lifecycle state, and execution results.
- `SKILLRUNTIME.md`: Defines reusable execution methods, their observation requirements, action forms, orchestration modes, parameters, and adapter needs.
- `TARGETS.md`: Describes available execution objects, including their type, capabilities, observation modalities, action semantics, and operational constraints.
- `ENVIRONMENT.md`: Stores a structured environment snapshot, derived from raw sensor data through a perception pipeline, enabling the Agent to reason about entities and relations.
- `LESSONS.md` and `KNOWLEDGE.md`: Constitute the Epistemic Memory, storing verified failure causes and corrective actions (`LESSONS.md`), and validated successful patterns with applicability conditions (`KNOWLEDGE.md`).
Complementary YAML files (`sensors.yaml`, `perception.yaml`, `runtime_contract.yaml`, `safety.yaml`) manage deploy-time parameters.

**Dual Execution Flows:**
PhyAgentOS supports two variants of `SkillRuntime` to accommodate different decision loops:
1. **Policy-driven execution (`PolicySkillRuntime`)**: The Agent compiles a session, but a continuous control policy (e.g., VLA model) is responsible for generating actions. The runtime manages the observation-inference-action loop. `PolicyAdapter` normalizes observations for the model, and `ActionBridge` components transform model outputs into target-compatible commands while enforcing constraints. `TargetAdapter` encodes actions into the target-native interface.
2. **Agent-directed tool execution (`BuiltinSkillRuntime`)**: The Agent participates in an online observe-decide-invoke-tool loop via a controlled `TargetSessionHandle`. This is suitable for discrete game interactions or scripted procedures. Access to target operations is filtered by the session's `TargetToolManifest` during preflight.

**Core Mechanisms:**
- **Cognitive State Space and Dynamic Coordination**: The protocol files form a unified state space for aligning task intent, runtime status, environment state, and historical experience across distributed components. This allows for selective replanning and dynamic coordination across multiple embodiments.
- **SessionVerifier**: After execution termination, the `SessionVerifier` evaluates the session's evidence bundle (`G`, `S0`, `ST`, `τ`, `H`) against its semantic objective. It returns a verdict of `success`, `failure`, or `replan`, thereby closing the loop between intention and accomplishment. Every judgment is appended to the session's records.
- **Closed-loop Trial-and-Error and Experience Consolidation (Epistemic Memory)**: This mechanism enables self-evolution through a six-stage process: Execute, Verify, Diagnose (for failures), Revise, Re-verify, and Consolidate (into `LESSONS.md` or `KNOWLEDGE.md`). This process ensures that only verified outcomes contribute to long-term adaptation.
- **Benchmarking as an Instrument for Self-Evolution**: Benchmarking uses the identical runtime, verification, and memory mechanisms as deployment. An `Availability Gate` validates configurations, and a `Session Compiler` generates reproducible session sets. This ensures traceability of metrics and enables iterative improvement.
- **Layered Safety Mechanisms (Defense-in-Depth)**:
 1. **Compatibility preflight**: The `WatchdogSupervisor` checks if `SkillRuntime`, policy, adapters, and target form a valid, authorized execution chain, rejecting incompatible configurations.
 2. **Action bridges**: Provide deterministic transformations (e.g., coordinate conversion, clamping) between abstract actions and target-specific commands, preventing unsafe values.
 3. **SafetyGuard**: Examines every action chunk for admissibility against target-configured constraints (e.g., workspace bounds, velocity limits), intervening and recording violations.
 4. **Heartbeat monitoring**: The `WatchdogSupervisor` monitors heartbeats from runtime components to detect stalls or disconnections, triggering controlled termination.
 5. **Target-local constraints**: Hardware or simulator-native safeguards (e.g., joint limits, emergency stops) act as the final, authoritative layer of protection.

**Progressive Validation Path:**
The methodology includes a progressive validation approach, starting with game environments to isolate cognitive planning, moving to simulation to introduce physical dynamics, and finally to real robots to incorporate hardware constraints and noise. The cognitive layer and protocol remain constant across these tiers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of PhyAgentOS follows a progressive path, evaluating cognitive capabilities in game environments, physical dynamics in simulations, and hardware constraints on real robots.

#### 4.1. Game-Tier Cognitive Verification

This tier evaluates the cognitive layer in isolation, where physical execution is assumed to be frictionless.
- **Optimus-67 (Minecraft)**: PhyAgentOS demonstrated near-saturated performance on simpler tasks (Wood 99%, Stone 96% success rates). On complex tasks like RedStone, it achieved a 30% success rate, exceeding all reported baselines, attributed to the `SessionVerifier` detecting partial constructions and `Epistemic Memory` reusing crafting recipes. Performance on Iron (52%), Gold (6%), Diamond (19%), and Armor (15%) was competitive or showed relative advantages on RedStone tasks, indicating effective long-horizon planning and memory retention.
- **StarDojo Lite-100 (Stardew Valley)**: Using a text-only model, PhyAgentOS achieved an overall success rate of 22.0%, surpassing the SPIKE baseline (18.0%). Significant gains were observed in Crafting tasks (50.0% vs. 23.8% for the strongest baseline), primarily due to validated recipes accumulating in `KNOWLEDGE.md` and the `SessionVerifier` accepting partially completed sequences.
- **DST-Dojo (Don't Starve)**: Pilot results showed PhyAgentOS doubling mean survival days from 1.02 to 2.10 and achieving a 30% Day 3 survival rate (compared to 0% for baseline). A reduction in darkness-related deaths and an increase in starvation deaths over longer lifespans suggested that `LESSONS.md` was partially effective in preventing recurring fatal errors.
- **Cross-Environment Cognitive Analysis**: The results across these environments converge to demonstrate that the cognitive layer effectively performs memory retention, multi-step planning, and self-evolution through the `SessionVerifier`-driven retry loop and consolidation of failures into persistent memory, leading to measurable improvements in subsequent sessions.

#### 4.2. Simulation-Tier Physical Regression

This tier introduces rigid-body dynamics and control challenges in simulation, evaluating the system's ability to recover from execution errors. Performance was measured for "First" (policy's initial attempt) and "Final" (after PhyAgentOS intervention/retry).
- **LIBERO Manipulation Benchmark**: Consistent, though modest, improvements were observed across all four evaluated policy backends (OpenVLA, $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, X-VLA). Overall success rates increased by +1.0 to +1.3 percentage points, indicating the system's ability to recover residual execution failures, even for policies already operating at high first-attempt success rates.
- **CALVIN Long-Horizon Manipulation Benchmark**: PhyAgentOS showed significant improvements in full-chain completion rates for long-horizon, five-subtask sequences. For example, $\pi_0$ improved from 38.9% to 45.6% (a +6.7 percentage point gain) and $\pi_{0.5}$ from 85.3% to 89.4% (a +4.1 percentage point gain) on 5/5 success. This indicated that many CALVIN failures are recoverable mid-sequence execution errors.
- **RoboCasa365 Household Manipulation Benchmark**: PhyAgentOS achieved substantial absolute gains in overall success rates, increasing by +9.2, +7.2, and +8.4 percentage points for $\pi_{0.5}$, RLDX-1, and WorldDreamer, respectively. A significant number of episodes were "rescued." The gains were observed in both atomic skills and composite activities, suggesting versatility in addressing different types of policy failures.
- **Cross-Benchmark Analysis**: Across all simulation benchmarks, PhyAgentOS consistently improved final performance for every evaluated policy without modifying policy weights or task goals. This supports the premise that many failures are recoverable execution errors, which semantic verification and controlled recovery can address, establishing PhyAgentOS as a model-agnostic operating layer for embodied control.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Deployment and Safety Validation

This tier tests the system's safety mechanisms and adapter chains under real-world hardware noise and latency.
- **Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: The system was deployed on a diverse fleet of real robots, including industrial arms (Franka Research 3), desktop manipulators (AgileX PIPER, Dobot Nova 2), and mobile/humanoid platforms (Unitree Go2, Unitree G1, Zerith). The same Agent protocol and `SESSIONS.md` format drove all robots, with only `TargetAdapter` and `PolicyAdapter` swaps required, validating the architecture's cross-embodiment portability.
- **Safety Validation**:
 - **Preflight rejection**: Successfully rejected deliberately incompatible adapter configurations, preventing unsafe operations before reaching motor controllers.
 - **SafetyGuard effectiveness**: Intercepted and clamped actions that violated workspace bounds or velocity limits, recording each intervention.
 - **Emergency stop latency**: Measured the swift halting of motors upon violation detection, confirming the efficacy of the layered safety architecture.

Overall, the experimental results demonstrate that PhyAgentOS effectively integrates cognitive planning with reliable physical execution across various domains and embodiments. It provides a robust framework for semantic verification, self-evolution, and safe operation in embodied AI systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhyAgentOS represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied AI by addressing fundamental architectural gaps that limit the reliability, verifiability, and adaptability of current systems. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key innovations:

- **Architectural Unification**: By introducing an operating system layer that integrates disparate paradigms—VLA models, world models, and agentic systems—PhyAgentOS offers a unified framework. This addresses the prevalent issue of fragmented interfaces and incompatible abstraction levels that hinder the development of cohesive embodied intelligence.
- **Semantic Verification as a Core Service**: The `SessionVerifier` mechanism, which distinguishes execution termination from semantic task completion, is a critical advancement. This directly combats the "systematic self-deception" often observed in embodied agents, leading to more truthful assessment of task outcomes and a more robust foundation for learning.
- **Self-Evolution without Neural Retraining**: The `Epistemic Memory` and closed-loop trial-and-error process allow the system to accumulate verified knowledge and lessons. This mechanism enables continuous adaptation and improvement across sessions and embodiments without the computational expense and data requirements of constant neural model retraining, which could accelerate the development cycle and broaden accessibility.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness**: The layered safety architecture—encompassing compatibility preflight, action bridges, `SafetyGuard`, heartbeat monitoring, and target-local constraints—provides defense-in-depth against various failure modes. This systematic approach to safety is crucial for the deployment of autonomous agents in real-world, safety-critical environments.
- **Transparency and Auditability**: The "State-as-a-File" protocol ensures that all cross-layer state transitions are recorded in human-readable and version-controlled documents. This inherent auditability enhances debugging capabilities, promotes trust in autonomous systems, and facilitates scientific reproducibility.
- **Cross-Embodiment Portability**: The standardized protocol and adapter chain architecture enable cognitive strategies to be reused across heterogeneous embodiments, from games and simulations to diverse real robots. This significantly reduces the effort required to port algorithms and facilitates generalist robot development.
- **Integrated Benchmarking for Iterative Improvement**: Treating benchmarking as an internal orchestration mechanism that uses the same execution path as deployment ensures that evaluation metrics are directly traceable and reflect real-world performance. This integration makes benchmarking an active instrument for guiding self-evolution rather than a passive measurement tool.
- **Foundation for Long-Duration Autonomy**: The progressive validation path, especially the use of cognitive stress-testing in complex game environments like DST-Dojo, provides a systematic method to develop and validate agents capable of sustained, intelligent operation under multi-dimensional pressures, a crucial step towards long-duration real-robot autonomy.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges several limitations. The current polling-based file protocol introduces latency, suggesting a future transition to event-driven mechanisms while preserving atomic-write semantics. `Epistemic Memory` lacks principled compression bounds for long-horizon operation and automated skill-quality assurance is semi-manual. Real-robot evaluation coverage, while diverse in platform support, has prioritized safety validation over large-scale task-completion statistics.

Future work includes fully implementing the `Goal Graph` and `Session Compiler` for advanced task decomposition and multi-robot coordination, deeper integration of world models for predictive verification, and scaling cross-embodiment transfer to a wider fleet with quantitative measures of adapter code efficiency. Developing theoretical foundations for memory compression and skill quality assurance, alongside leveraging DST-Dojo for real-robot readiness, are also identified as key future directions.

In summary, PhyAgentOS provides a systematic and extensible runtime foundation that addresses core architectural challenges in embodied AI, offering a significant step towards developing more reliable, adaptable, and intelligent physical agents.
