# WorldCam: Interactive Autoregressive 3D Gaming Worlds with Camera Pose as a Unifying Geometric Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed WorldCam, a model for interactive 3D gaming worlds that utilizes camera pose as a unified geometric representation to enhance action control and long-term 3D consistency. It achieved lower relative pose errors, higher visual quality, and improved spatial coherence compared to existing models, while also introducing a large-scale, open-licensed gameplay dataset.

## Problem

- Existing interactive gaming world models often lack precise action control and consistent 3D world representation over extended durations.
- User actions are typically treated as abstract signals, neglecting their fundamental geometric relationship to the 3D environment and camera movement.
- A scarcity of large-scale, high-fidelity video datasets depicting authentic human gameplay hinders the development and reproducibility of robust interactive world models.

## Method

- WorldCam integrates camera pose as a singular geometric representation within a progressive autoregressive video diffusion transformer.
- It translates user actions into precise 6-DoF relative camera poses using Lie algebra, injecting these into the generative model via a dedicated camera embedder.
- A pose-anchored long-term memory system retrieves past generated latents based on global camera pose similarity to maintain 3D consistency during extended interactions.

## Results

- Achieved a 16.3% improvement in camera extrinsics Relative Pose Error (RPE_camera = 0.086) for action controllability compared to the best baseline (0.100).
- Demonstrated 8.1% higher average VBench++ score (0.844) for long-horizon visual quality compared to the next best model (0.781), and showcased improved 3D consistency, achieving the highest PSNR (16.69) and best performance across other 3D consistency metrics.
- Introduced WorldCam-50h, a 3,000-minute open-licensed human gameplay dataset annotated with camera trajectories and textual descriptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing user actions as Lie algebra twist vectors enables more geometrically precise camera control than linear approximations, crucial for complex coupled movements.
> - Global camera poses serve as effective spatial indices for memory retrieval, allowing for consistent revisiting of locations and preventing visual degradation over long-horizon generations.
> - A combination of progressive noise scheduling, attention sink, and short-term memory mechanisms significantly stabilizes autoregressive video generation, reducing error drift and maintaining visual fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the advancing field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the creation of interactive 3D gaming world models. Recent developments in Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have enhanced the realism and scalability of video generation, paving the way for models that can simulate playable environments.

Prior research has made progress in generating visually plausible interactive gaming worlds. However, existing approaches encounter challenges in two primary areas: precise action control and consistent 3D world representation over extended durations. Many earlier models tend to interpret user actions as abstract conditioning signals, which overlooks the inherent geometric relationship between actions and the 3D environment. In gaming, user inputs (e.g., keyboard and mouse actions) fundamentally translate into relative camera movements that accumulate into a global camera pose within the 3D world. This geometric coupling is critical for accurately reflecting user intent and maintaining spatial coherence. Consequently, a disconnect arises where models struggle to accurately align generated visuals with intended camera motion, leading to inconsistent 3D geometry.

While some camera-controlled video generation methods exist, they typically focus on short video sequences (e.g., 16 frames) and are not designed to handle action-driven control or long-horizon inference, which are essential for interactive world modeling. Another significant barrier identified in the landscape is the scarcity of large-scale, high-fidelity video datasets that capture authentic human gameplay dynamics. Existing datasets often rely on simplified geometries (e.g., Minecraft) or are derived from closed-licensed games, which limits accessibility and reproducibility for the broader research community.

WorldCam addresses these limitations by establishing camera pose as a unifying geometric representation. This approach is intended to jointly ground immediate action control and long-term 3D consistency, offering a more principled framework for generating interactive gaming worlds compared to methods that treat actions as abstract signals or only partially utilize geometric information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a foundational model for interactive gaming worlds, named WorldCam, that concurrently achieves precise action control, sustained long-horizon inference, and consistent 3D world modeling.

The motivation for this work stems from an identified gap in existing interactive gaming world models. These models, despite their progress in generating plausible visual outputs, often exhibit shortcomings in two critical aspects: precise action control and long-horizon 3D consistency. These are considered prerequisites for a functional gaming engine. The authors hypothesize that this limitation arises because prior approaches frequently overlook the fundamental geometric coupling between user actions and the 3D environment. In a 3D game, user actions do not act as abstract control signals but instead induce relative camera motions that accumulate into a global camera pose. This global camera pose, in turn, dictates how the underlying 3D world is projected into 2D observations. Therefore, the accuracy of action control and the maintenance of 3D consistency are inherently interdependent and mediated by the camera pose.

To address this, the research sets forth several specific objectives:
1. **Precise Action Control:** To define a physics-based, continuous action space where user inputs, including complex coupled keyboard and mouse actions, are represented in the Lie algebra se(3). This representation is used to derive precise 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) relative camera poses, intended to ensure accurate alignment between user actions and the generative model's output. These derived poses are then injected into the generative model via a dedicated camera embedder.
2. **Long-Horizon 3D Consistency:** To leverage global camera poses as spatial indices. This enables the retrieval of relevant past observations from a memory pool, thereby facilitating geometrically consistent revisiting of locations during extended navigation scenarios. This approach is designed to maintain spatial coherence and prevent visual degradation over long periods of interaction.
3. **Unified Geometric Representation:** To establish camera pose as a singular geometric representation that simultaneously grounds both immediate action control and long-term 3D consistency, thereby providing a more integrated and coherent framework for interactive world generation.
4. **Dataset Provision:** To introduce a large-scale, high-fidelity dataset, WorldCam-50h, comprising 3,000 minutes of authentic human gameplay. This dataset is annotated with camera trajectories and textual descriptions, aiming to overcome the data scarcity issue that hinders research reproducibility and the development of robust interactive gaming world models. The inclusion of open-licensed game data in the dataset is intended to support reproducible research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldCam is built upon a progressive autoregressive video diffusion transformer architecture, specifically leveraging a pretrained video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, Wan-2.1-T2V. The methodology focuses on integrating camera pose as a unifying geometric representation throughout the generation process.

#### 3.1. Action-to-Camera Mapping

To achieve precise action control, WorldCam redefines the action space using Lie algebra. Instead of directly injecting raw user inputs or relying on linear approximations, user actions (e.g., keyboard presses and mouse movements) are translated into a 6-DoF twist vector `A_i = [v_i; ω_i] ∈ R^6` at each time step. Here, `v_i` represents linear velocity and `ω_i` represents angular velocity. The corresponding relative camera pose `∆P_i ∈ SE(3)` is then derived using the matrix exponential map `∆P_i = exp(Â_i)`, where `Â_i ∈ se(3)` is the 4x4 matrix representation of the twist. This Lie algebra-based formulation aims to integrate linear and angular velocities jointly on the SE(3) manifold, which is designed to yield geometrically precise camera trajectories, especially for complex coupled motions such as screw motion, unlike decoupled linear approximations.

#### 3.2. Camera-Controlled Video Generation

The derived relative camera poses are accumulated into global camera poses aligned with the first frame of the current generation window. For explicit view-dependent geometric conditioning, these camera poses are converted into Plücker embeddings `p̂ ∈ R^(f×6r)`. A lightweight camera embedding module, consisting of two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), processes these Plücker embeddings. The resulting camera embeddings are subsequently added to the intermediate features `d` of the DiT after each self-attention layer, i.e., `d ← d + c_ϕ(p̂)`. This process conditions the generative model on the precise camera motion.

#### 3.3. Pose-Anchored Long-Term Memory

To ensure 3D consistency over long horizons, particularly when revisiting locations, WorldCam utilizes the camera pose as a geometric anchor for memory retrieval.

- **Global Pose Accumulation:** The relative camera motions, precisely derived from the action-to-camera mapping, are reliably accumulated to compute the global camera pose `P_global_j` for each frame `j` via pose composition: `P_global_j = P_global_(j-1) ◦ ∆P_j`.
- **Pose-Indexed Memory Retrieval:** A long-term memory pool `M` stores previously generated latents `z_j` along with their associated global camera poses `P_global_j`. A hierarchical retrieval strategy is employed:
 1. First, `K` candidates (`M_trans`) are selected whose camera positions (`t_j`) are closest to the current camera position (`t_i`).
 2. From these `M_trans` candidates, `L` entries (`M_rot`) are further selected based on the highest alignment of their viewing directions (`R_j`) with the current orientation (`R_i`), measured by the trace of the relative rotation matrix `tr(R_j^T R_i)`.
- **Long-Term Memory Conditioning:** The retrieved long-term memory latents (`z_j`) and their associated camera poses provide geometric context. These latents are concatenated with the current input latent sequence. Their camera poses are realigned to the first frame of the current denoising window, embedded via the camera embedding module, and injected into the DiT's intermediate features. This mechanism establishes geometric correspondences between current and past latents, thereby reinforcing spatial consistency.

#### 3.4. Progressive Autoregressive Inference

WorldCam incorporates several strategies for stable long-horizon autoregressive video generation.
- **Progressive Noise Scheduling:** A per-frame noise schedule is adopted, assigning monotonically increasing noise levels to latent frames within each denoising window. This setup aims to provide a reliable low-noise anchor for early frames while keeping future frames at higher noise levels, allowing for correction. This design is intended to enable efficient cross-frame conditioning and stable rollout, and to improve robustness to corrupted frames during training.
- **Attention Sink:** An attention sink mechanism, inspired by StreamingLLM, is integrated to stabilize attention. It retains initial global frames as attention anchors, which is intended to help preserve frame fidelity, scene style, and UI consistency over extended generations, mitigating visual saturation.
- **Short-Term Memory:** Recently generated latents are used as short-term memory. This is intended to reduce error drift during autoregressive generation. The number of short-term memory latents is set to match the number of generated latents for balance between stability and computational efficiency.

#### 3.5. WorldCam-50h Dataset

To address data limitations, the authors introduce WorldCam-50h, a large-scale dataset of human gameplay videos totaling 3,000 minutes.
- **Data Collection:** Gameplay was recorded from Counter-Strike (closed-licensed) and open-licensed games like Xonotic and Unvanquished. The focus was on single-player exploration of static environments. Participants performed diverse behaviors, including navigation, combined keyboard-mouse inputs, rapid camera movements, and revisiting locations.
- **Annotation:** Each video chunk is annotated with detailed textual descriptions generated by Qwen2.5-VL-7B, focusing on global layout, visual theme, and environmental conditions. Pseudo ground-truth camera poses (intrinsics and extrinsics) are extracted using ViPE, with additional filtering to remove erroneous estimates.
- **Preprocessing:** Collected videos (avg. 8 minutes) are segmented into 1-minute clips. Camera parameters are extracted. These clips are further divided into 64-frame segments (32 for short-term memory, 32 for generation). Long-term memory frames are selected from the 1-minute clip based on pose similarity. VAE latents are precomputed and cached for training efficiency.

#### 3.6. Training Stages

Training is conducted in three stages:
1. Camera-controlled video generation with short-term memory (10k iterations).
2. Progressive autoregressive training with short-term memory (10k iterations).
3. Progressive autoregressive training with both short- and long-term memory (10k iterations), specifically to enforce 3D consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WorldCam focused on three key aspects: action controllability, long-horizon visual quality, and 3D consistency, comparing it against state-of-the-art interactive gaming world models and camera-controlled video generation methods.

#### 4.1. Action Controllability

WorldCam demonstrated improved action controllability.
- In quantitative comparisons against interactive gaming world models (Yume, Matrix-Game 2.0, GameCraft) over 200-frame generations, WorldCam achieved the lowest Relative Pose Errors (RPE) across translation (RPE_trans = 0.080), rotation (RPE_rot = 0.696°), and camera extrinsics (RPE_camera = 0.086). This represents a 16.3% improvement in RPE_camera compared to the next best baseline, GameCraft (0.100).
- In short-horizon comparisons (16-frame generation) with camera-controlled models (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl), WorldCam also exhibited the lowest errors, with RPE_camera at 0.030, which is a 36.1% improvement over CameraCtrl (0.083).
- An ablation study on the action-to-camera mapping confirmed that the Lie algebra-based approximation (WorldCam (Lie)) yielded better results across all RPE metrics compared to a linear approximation (WorldCam (Linear)), validating its precision in modeling complex camera motions.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Visual Quality

WorldCam achieved higher visual quality over extended durations.
- It scored the highest VBench++ average score (0.844) among interactive gaming world models, surpassing GameCraft (0.781) by 8.1%. This includes improvements in Aesthetic Quality, Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, and Imaging Quality.
- Ablation studies indicated that increasing the number of short-term memory latents and incorporating an attention sink mechanism contributed to reducing long-horizon error drift and improving the overall visual quality score.

#### 4.3. 3D Consistency

The model demonstrated improved 3D spatial consistency.
- WorldCam achieved the best performance across all 3D consistency metrics, including PSNR (16.69), LPIPS (0.3277), MEt3R (0.0342), and DINO Similarity (0.8884).
- The Sharpness score (656) for WorldCam was close to that of real videos (577), suggesting that its higher PSNR was not due to blurrier outputs, unlike some baseline models (e.g., Yume with Sharpness 95).
- Ablation studies showed that increasing the number of long-term memory latents improved PSNR and SSIM, thus enhancing 3D consistency. The camera pose-based memory retrieval strategy significantly outperformed random and temporal retrieval methods in all 3D consistency metrics.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples illustrated WorldCam's capabilities:
- Accurate following of user actions, even with challenging coupled keyboard and mouse inputs.
- Maintenance of high visual quality and consistent 3D geometry over long horizons, generating new scenes without significant error drift.
- Preservation of consistent 3D geometry when previously visited locations are revisited.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluation

A user study involving 30 participants further supported the quantitative findings. WorldCam received higher average ratings (on a scale of 1 to 5) across action controllability (4.31), visual quality (4.44), and 3D consistency (4.36), demonstrating relative improvements of 14.0%, 29.8%, and 29.8% respectively over the second-best baselines.

#### 4.6. WorldCam-50h Dataset

The introduction of WorldCam-50h, a large-scale, open-licensed dataset with diverse human gameplay and detailed annotations (camera poses, text descriptions), is a direct outcome intended to address a critical resource gap in the field.

#### 4.7. Inference Efficiency (Discussed as a Limitation)

While not the primary focus, inference time comparison showed WorldCam achieving a comparable time per chunk to Matrix-Game 2.0 and the lowest time per step among compared approaches, despite not utilizing distillation techniques. This suggests potential for future real-time application with further optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WorldCam research introduces a framework that directly addresses fundamental challenges in generating interactive 3D gaming worlds, particularly regarding precise action control and long-horizon 3D consistency. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Interactive World Models:** By establishing camera pose as a unifying geometric representation, the work offers a principled solution to the long-standing problem of harmonizing immediate action control with enduring 3D spatial consistency. This integration aims to create more coherent and responsive interactive virtual environments than previous approaches that treated actions as abstract signals or lacked robust long-term memory.
- **Enhanced User Experience:** The demonstrated improvements in action controllability mean that user inputs are more accurately reflected in the generated environment, leading to a more intuitive and immersive interactive experience. The enhanced 3D consistency ensures that the virtual world remains spatially coherent, even during extended exploration or when revisiting locations, which is crucial for immersion and usability in gaming and simulation.
- **Foundation for Future Generative AI:** The methodology, particularly the Lie algebra-based action-to-camera mapping and the pose-anchored long-term memory retrieval, provides architectural components that could serve as a robust foundation for future generative AI models. This approach could be generalized to other domains requiring precise physical control and consistent long-term generation, such as robotics simulation, virtual reality content creation, or digital twin environments.
- **Data Availability and Reproducibility:** The introduction of WorldCam-50h, a large-scale dataset incorporating open-licensed game data and comprehensive annotations, is a significant contribution to the research community. This dataset addresses a critical bottleneck in training robust interactive world models and promotes reproducible research by providing an accessible and standardized resource for further development and benchmarking.
- **Pathway to Real-Time Interaction:** Although the primary focus was not on inference efficiency, the reported low time per step, even without distillation, indicates a promising trajectory towards real-time interactive generation. Future work integrating distillation and acceleration techniques could potentially enable highly responsive, real-time interactive world creation, expanding the practical applications of such generative models.
- **Bridging Geometry and Generative Models:** The core contribution lies in effectively bridging the gap between low-level user actions and high-level 3D scene generation through a unified geometric representation. This geometric grounding provides a more physically consistent and controllable output, which is a key step towards building truly intelligent and interactive virtual worlds.
