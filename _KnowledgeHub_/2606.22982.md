# Distilling Collaborative Dynamics into Latent Space for Implicit Coordination in Decentralized Multi-Agent Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLS-DP framework enables decentralized multi-agent manipulation by distilling privileged collaborative dynamics into a latent space during centralized training. This allows individual agents to infer coordination cues from local RGB observations and shared task instructions for action generation, achieving an average success rate of 38% on the RoboFactory benchmark, outperforming centralized baselines.

## Problem

- Centralized multi-arm robotic manipulation policies struggle with scalability, synchronization complexity, and reliance on shared global observations, limiting real-world deployment.
- Decentralized multi-agent systems face significant coordination failures under partial observability, as agents with local views lack information about teammates' intentions, leading to issues like collisions or deadlocks.
- Existing learning-based multi-agent approaches often assume near-full state observability or shared global views, restricting their applicability in realistic environments with occlusions and communication constraints.

## Method

- A CLS-DP (Collaborative Latent Space-conditioned ==Diffusion Policy==) framework is introduced, employing a dual-module architecture (contextualizer and action-expert) under a centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm.
- During centralized training, a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) contextualizer distills privileged future joint trajectories of all agents into a coordination-aware latent variable, inferred from local observations and task instructions.
- For decentralized execution, each agent independently samples this collaborative latent from its trained contextualizer's prior network using only local RGB observations, then generates coordinated actions with a diffusion policy without inter-agent communication or global state access.

## Results

- CLS-DP achieved an average success rate of **38%** across six RoboFactory multi-arm tasks, significantly outperforming the best centralized baseline (Global GauDP, **20%**) and a decentralized ablation without the collaborative latent (**9%**).
- It was the only method to achieve a non-trivial success rate (**20%**) on the challenging Three Robots Stack Cube task, where all other baselines recorded **0%** success.
- The framework demonstrated superior parameter efficiency across 2-agent, 3-agent, and 4-agent configurations, with qualitative analysis showing CLS-DP agents attended to teammates' kinematics, unlike egocentric baselines, confirming effective implicit coordination.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit coordination in decentralized multi-agent systems can be effectively achieved by distilling complex multi-agent dynamics into a learnable, observation-conditioned latent space during training.
> - By sampling a collaborative latent variable from a prior network at inference, individual agents can anticipate and adapt to teammates' behaviors based solely on their local observations, even under partial observability.
> - The proposed architecture maintains a per-agent computational cost independent of team size, demonstrating a scalable approach to multi-agent control without relying on explicit communication channels during deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressing from single-arm operations to complex, multi-arm collaborations that require precise spatiotemporal coordination. Within this context, policy learning, particularly using generative models like diffusion-based policies, has shown capability in modeling high-dimensional action distributions for complex tasks. However, many multi-arm approaches primarily rely on centralized execution, where a single policy coordinates all agents by aggregating observations and actions. This centralized paradigm encounters challenges related to scalability, synchronization complexity, vulnerability to single-agent failures, and a dependence on communication and shared observations, which are often impractical in real-world deployments.

To address these limitations, a shift towards decentralized execution has occurred, framing multi-arm manipulation as a coordination problem among independent agents. In this decentralized paradigm, each robot operates using an independent policy conditioned solely on local observations. However, decentralization introduces its own set of challenges, particularly the need for consistent coordination under partial observability. In such scenarios, asymmetric and incomplete observations can obscure global state information, leading to coordination failures like collisions, object drops, or deadlocks due to minor temporal or spatial discrepancies. Many existing decentralized approaches mitigate these issues by relying on assumptions such as full state access or shared global views, which restrict their applicability in realistic environments characterized by partial observability and the absence of explicit communication.

Existing decentralized diffusion policies have explored various strategies. Some, like MIMIC-D, adopt a centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) framework where coordination emerges implicitly from joint supervision without explicitly modeling other agents' internal states. Others, such as MADIFF, incorporate cross-agent attention mechanisms for explicit prediction of teammates' future states, which enhances awareness but can reintroduce scalability issues. LatentToM uses a shared embedding space derived from third-person global-view camera images, relying on consistent global observations during both training and inference and primarily capturing current scene state rather than prospective collaborative dynamics. These methods often assume near-full state observability, including access to other agents' positions, which is not always available in RGB-based manipulation where state inference, coordination, and uncertainty from occlusions must be jointly addressed.

This work, CLS-DP, contributes to this landscape by proposing a decentralized multi-agent framework that seeks to overcome the limitations of both centralized and existing decentralized approaches. It aims to enable implicit coordination under partial observability without requiring shared global views, explicit state information, or inter-agent communication at inference time. By distilling privileged multi-agent dynamics into a latent space during centralized training, and then allowing each agent to infer this collaborative latent from local RGB observations for decentralized execution, the paper addresses a critical gap in enabling scalable and robust multi-agent manipulation in realistic, constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a decentralized multi-agent framework, termed CLS-DP (Collaborative Latent Space–conditioned Diffusion Policy), that facilitates implicit coordination among multiple robotic arms. This framework is specifically designed to operate under conditions of partial observability, without reliance on shared global observations, explicit state information, or direct inter-agent communication during deployment.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current multi-arm manipulation systems:

- **Scalability Limitations of Centralized Approaches:** Many existing multi-arm systems employ centralized policies, where a single controller aggregates all agents' observations and dictates their actions. While effective for small teams, this approach suffers from a rapid growth of the joint observation-action space as the number of agents increases. This leads to heightened computational complexity, increased synchronization demands, and reduced robustness, as a failure in any part of the system can compromise the entire operation. Furthermore, centralized policies often assume ubiquitous communication and shared global observations, which are not always guaranteed or feasible in real-world deployment scenarios.
- **Coordination Challenges in Decentralized Execution:** Shifting to decentralized execution, where each agent acts independently based on its local observations, alleviates some scalability issues. However, it introduces the critical problem of maintaining consistent coordination under partial observability. Agents with asymmetric and incomplete local views often lack essential information about their teammates' intentions or global task progress. This lack of awareness can lead to minor temporal or spatial discrepancies that result in coordination failures, such as collisions, object drops, or deadlocks, thereby hindering task completion. Existing decentralized methods often circumvent this by making strong assumptions, such as full state access or the availability of shared global views, which limit their applicability to more realistic, constrained settings.
- **Need for Robust and Expressive Policy Learning:** Recent advances in generative policy learning, particularly diffusion-based policies, have demonstrated strong capabilities in modeling complex, high-dimensional, and multimodal action distributions for single-agent manipulation tasks. The motivation is to extend these benefits to multi-agent scenarios while specifically addressing the coordination challenges in a decentralized setting.
- **Bridging the Gap between Training and Deployment:** The centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm offers a promising avenue by leveraging rich, privileged information during training that may not be available during deployment. The motivation is to effectively distill this privileged multi-agent dynamic information into a representation that individual agents can leverage from their local observations during decentralized execution, thereby enabling coordination implicitly rather than through explicit communication or shared global states.

In summary, the key objective is to enable robust, scalable, and implicitly coordinated multi-agent manipulation by developing a framework where individual agents can infer and act upon collaborative dynamics solely from their local sensory inputs and task instructions, without external coordination signals or complete global state knowledge at inference time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLS-DP formulates multi-agent manipulation as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process (Dec-POMDP) involving `N` agents, where each agent `i` executes a policy conditioned on its local observation (`o_i_t`) and proprioceptive history (`s_i_t`), given a shared task instruction (`l`). Critical constraints include the absence of shared global RGB observations, explicit global state information, or inter-agent communication during inference.

The CLS-DP framework employs a dual-module architecture under a centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm, comprising a contextualizer and an action-expert.

**Centralized Training:** This phase occurs in two distinct stages:

- **Stage 1: Contextualizer Training:**
 - **Objective:** To distill privileged multi-agent dynamics into a coordination-aware latent variable (`z_i_t`) that can be inferred by each agent from its local observation and task instruction.
 - **Mechanism:** A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) is utilized.
 - **Prior Network (`p_theta_i(z_i_t | o_i_t, l)`):** This network encodes the agent's current local RGB observation (`o_i_t`) and the shared task instruction (`l`). It uses frozen pre-trained SigLIP encoders for visual and text embeddings, which are then fused via Transformer layers. The output defines the parameters (`mu_i_rho`, `sigma_i_rho`) of a Gaussian distribution for the observation-conditioned prior. The prior is conditioned only on the current local observation to prevent it from becoming overly informative, ensuring `z_i_t` learns collaborative dynamics from the posterior.
 - **Posterior Network (`q_psi_i(z_i_t | s^1:N_t+1:t+H)`):** This network leverages *privileged* future joint trajectories of *all* agents (`s^1:N_t+1:t+H`). A multi-agent kinematics encoder (`E_psi_i`) and decoder (`D_psi_i`), implemented with Transformer layers, are used to capture cross-agent interactions. The encoder predicts residual parameters (`mu_i_E`, `sigma_i_E`) which, when added to the prior's parameters, define a residual posterior.
 - **Latent Sampling:** `z_i_t` is sampled from this posterior using the reparameterization trick.
 - **Reconstruction:** The multi-agent kinematics decoder (`D_psi_i`) reconstructs the future joint trajectories of all agents from only the agent's own current state (`s_i_t`) and the sampled `z_i_t`. This step verifies that `z_i_t` sufficiently encodes information about prospective multi-agent dynamics beyond the agent's individual state.
 - **Loss Function (`L_CT`):** This loss combines a KL divergence term and a reconstruction term. The KL divergence regularizes the residual posterior towards the observation-conditioned prior, effectively distilling privileged collaborative dynamics into the prior. The reconstruction term ensures that `z_i_t` and `s_i_t` are sufficient to reconstruct the privileged future joint trajectories of all agents. Crucially, the kinematics encoder and decoder (`E_psi_i`, `D_psi_i`) are discarded at deployment.

- **Stage 2: Action-Expert Training:**
 - **Objective:** To train a decentralized diffusion policy for each agent that generates coordinated action sequences conditioned on the collaborative latent variable.
 - **Mechanism:** A diffusion-based policy.
 - **Conditioning:** The collaborative latent `z_i_t` is sampled from the *frozen* prior network (`p_theta_i(z_i_t | o_i_t, l)`) of the contextualizer. Gradients are not backpropagated into the contextualizer.
 - **Input Integration:** `z_i_t` is injected into the diffusion policy's U-Net architecture via cross-attention blocks within the downsampling and bottleneck stages. The agent's local observation history (`O_i_t`) and proprioceptive history (`S_i_t`) are incorporated using FiLM layers.
 - **Loss Function (`L_AE`):** A standard diffusion policy denoising objective is used, minimizing the difference between the predicted noise and the actual noise injected into the action sequence.
 - **Action Generation:** The trained action-expert denoises actions from a noisy action sequence, conditioned on (`O_i_t`, `S_i_t`, `z_i_t`), to produce a coordinated action sequence (`A_i_t`).

**Decentralized Execution:** At inference time, each agent independently executes CLS-DP as a two-stage pipeline:
1. **Latent Inference:** Agent `i` samples a collaborative latent variable `z_i_t` from its trained contextualizer's prior network `p_theta_i(z_i_t | o_i_t, l)`.
2. **Action Generation:** The agent's action-expert then performs the K-step reverse denoising process, conditioned on its local observation history (`O_i_t`), proprioceptive history (`S_i_t`), and the inferred latent `z_i_t`, to generate its action sequence `A_i_t`.

This design ensures that the per-agent computational cost remains constant irrespective of the team size, enabling scalability. The framework operates without needing shared global observations, explicit state information, or inter-agent communication at deployment.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted on the RoboFactory benchmark, which includes six multi-arm tasks requiring 2 to 4 agents and various forms of coordination (tight synchronization, role-asymmetric, sequential dependency). Baselines include centralized and decentralized diffusion-policy-based and autoregressive behavior cloning methods. All methods use standardized vision encoders (ResNet-18) and lightweight MLPs for proprioceptive states to ensure fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CLS-DP on the RoboFactory benchmark involved comparing its performance against various centralized and decentralized baselines across six multi-agent manipulation tasks.

**Task Success Rate (Table II):**
- **Overall Performance:** CLS-DP achieved an average success rate of 38% across all tasks. This performance exceeded the best centralized baseline, Global GauDP (20%), and significantly outperformed a decentralized ablation of CLS-DP without the collaborative latent (Ours w/o CLS, 9%).
- **Performance on Specific Tasks:**
 - CLS-DP demonstrated substantial gains in tasks like Place Food (43%), Two Robots Stack Cube (39%), and Camera Alignment (55%).
 - It was the only method to achieve a non-trivial success rate on the complex Three Robots Stack Cube task (20%), where all other baselines recorded 0%.
 - In the Take Photo task, CLS-DP achieved an 8% success rate, ranking third. While methods using explicit 3D point clouds (e.g., DP3 (XYZ+RGB) at 11%) performed slightly better, CLS-DP achieved this using only 2D local observations, suggesting its implicit latent-level coordination is competitive even when fine-grained spatial precision might be a bottleneck.
- **Baseline Performance:**
 - **Centralized Baselines:** Global GauDP was the strongest centralized baseline (20% average), benefiting from reconstructing a shared 3D Gaussian field. However, its performance decreased sharply with more agents (e.g., 72% on Lift Barrier down to 3% on Take Photo). Other centralized methods like DP (5%), LargeDP (18%), and 2D/3D Dense Policies (2-14%) generally showed lower success rates, particularly on tasks involving more agents or sequential dependencies. LargeDP's performance indicated that simply increasing model size was insufficient for effective multi-agent coordination.
 - **Decentralized Baselines:** Local GauDP (5%) and Ours w/o CLS (9%) exhibited significant performance degradation, highlighting the importance of the collaborative latent for implicit coordination in decentralized settings. The performance gap between CLS-DP and Ours w/o CLS provided quantitative evidence for the contribution of the collaborative latent.

**Parameter Efficiency and Scalability (Table III):**
- CLS-DP demonstrated the highest parameter efficiency (measured as average success rate per parameter count) across all 2-agent, 3-agent, and 4-agent configurations, by a substantial margin.
- LargeDP, despite its increased model capacity, showed lower efficiency than even the vanilla DP in the 2-agent setting, corroborating that model size alone does not guarantee better coordination or efficiency.
- Comparison between GauDP and its policy-only variant (GauDP-G) indicated the perceptual overhead introduced by GauDP's global scene reconstruction module at deployment. In contrast, the minimal difference in efficiency between CLS-DP (which discards training-only modules at deployment) and CLS-DP+K (a conservative variant including training modules) suggested that CLS-DP's performance gains originate from its coordination mechanism rather than increased perceptual overhead.

**Cross-Modal Grounding Analysis (Fig. 4, Table IV):**
- Analysis of cross-attention weights in the contextualizer revealed that both text instructions and image observations contributed to grounding the collaborative latent across all tasks.
- Agents consistently, albeit slightly, relied more on textual context than image observations for task-level coordination. This weighting adapted to task demands.
 - Tasks requiring explicit subgoals or sequential decomposition (e.g., Place Food, Two/Three Robots Stack Cube, Lift Barrier) showed higher text attention (0.578, 0.545, 0.522, 0.537 respectively).
 - Tasks requiring more visual feedback for alignment (Camera Alignment, Take Photo) showed near-parity attention between text and image (0.510, 0.509 respectively). This confirmed the contextualizer's ability to adaptively modulate cross-modal attention based on task-specific coordination requirements.

**Qualitative Analysis (Fig. 5):**
- Integrated Gradients attribution maps for the Take Photo task showed that CLS-DP agents, at all timesteps, assigned high attribution not only to their own joints and gripper but also to those of their teammates. This indicated sensitivity to both self and teammate kinematics, with temporally coherent attribution patterns suggesting that the collaborative latent effectively captured the progression of teammates' dynamics without explicit communication.
- In contrast, a baseline diffusion policy without the collaborative latent produced sparse and egocentric attribution maps, focusing primarily on its own joints and gripper. This egocentric behavior led to coordination failures, such as a misaligned and dropped camera, and a missed object grab. This suggested that the collaborative latent assists both inter-agent coordination and individual task progression.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents CLS-DP, a framework that addresses several fundamental challenges in multi-agent robotic manipulation, offering notable significance and potential impact:

- **Enabling Implicit Coordination under Realistic Constraints:** CLS-DP provides a method for multi-agent systems to achieve precise spatiotemporal coordination without relying on shared global observations, explicit state information, or inter-agent communication at deployment. This is a crucial advancement for real-world applications where such resources are often limited or unavailable due to sensor occlusions, network latency, or security concerns. By enabling agents to infer collaborative dynamics implicitly from their local RGB observations and shared task instructions, CLS-DP moves closer to the practical deployment of multi-robot systems in complex, unstructured environments.

- **Improved Scalability and Parameter Efficiency:** The framework's decentralized execution, coupled with a per-agent computational cost independent of team size, directly addresses the scalability limitations inherent in many centralized multi-agent policies. The empirical results demonstrate that CLS-DP achieves higher parameter efficiency compared to baselines, including those that simply scale model size. This indicates that the performance gains are derived from the sophisticated coordination mechanism rather than an increase in model capacity or perceptual overhead, making CLS-DP more suitable for larger robot teams.

- **Robustness to Partial Observability:** By learning to distill privileged multi-agent dynamics into a latent space during centralized training, CLS-DP equips individual agents with the capacity to anticipate and react to teammates' behaviors even under partial observability. This mechanism of implicitly inferring coordination cues from local information enhances the system's robustness against incomplete or asymmetric sensory inputs, a common characteristic of real-world scenarios.

- **Advancement in Learning-Based Robotics:** The use of a CVAE to distill prospective multi-agent dynamics into an observation-conditioned prior, combined with conditioning a diffusion policy on this latent, represents a novel architectural and learning approach for multi-agent control. It extends the expressive power of diffusion policies to solve complex coordination problems in a decentralized manner, contributing to the broader field of learning-based robotic control.

- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated ability of CLS-DP to succeed in complex manipulation tasks requiring tight synchronization, role-asymmetric coordination, and strict sequential dependencies on a challenging benchmark like RoboFactory suggests its potential applicability across various domains. This includes industrial assembly lines, medical surgery, domestic service robotics, and logistics, where coordinated manipulation by multiple robots could lead to increased efficiency, safety, and automation.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines clear pathways for future research, such as integrating safety constraints like collision avoidance for real-world deployment and exploring the applicability of CLS-DP to a wider range of multi-agent environments beyond manipulation tasks. This indicates a foundational contribution that can serve as a basis for further innovations in multi-robot systems.

In summary, CLS-DP offers a principled and effective solution for decentralized multi-agent coordination under realistic constraints, demonstrating superior performance and efficiency. Its ability to bridge the gap between privileged training information and constrained deployment conditions marks a significant step towards more autonomous and capable multi-robot systems.
