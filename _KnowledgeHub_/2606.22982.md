# Distilling Collaborative Dynamics into Latent Space for Implicit Coordination in Decentralized Multi-Agent Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLS-DP framework enables decentralized multi-agent manipulation by distilling privileged collaborative dynamics into a latent space during centralized training. This allows individual agents to infer coordination cues from local RGB observations and shared task instructions for action generation, achieving an average success rate of 38% on the RoboFactory benchmark, outperforming centralized baselines.

## Problem

- Centralized multi-arm robotic manipulation policies struggle with scalability, synchronization complexity, and reliance on shared global observations, limiting real-world deployment.
- Decentralized multi-agent systems face significant coordination failures under partial observability, as agents with local views lack information about teammates' intentions, leading to issues like collisions or deadlocks.
- Existing learning-based multi-agent approaches often assume near-full state observability or shared global views, restricting their applicability in realistic environments with occlusions and communication constraints.

## Method

- A CLS-DP (Collaborative Latent Space-conditioned ==Diffusion Policy==) framework is introduced, employing a dual-module architecture (contextualizer and action-expert) under a centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm.
- During centralized training, a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) contextualizer distills privileged future joint trajectories of all agents into a coordination-aware latent variable, inferred from local observations and task instructions.
- For decentralized execution, each agent independently samples this collaborative latent from its trained contextualizer's prior network using only local RGB observations, then generates coordinated actions with a diffusion policy without inter-agent communication or global state access.

## Results

- CLS-DP achieved an average success rate of **38%** across six RoboFactory multi-arm tasks, significantly outperforming the best centralized baseline (Global GauDP, **20%**) and a decentralized ablation without the collaborative latent (**9%**).
- It was the only method to achieve a non-trivial success rate (**20%**) on the challenging Three Robots Stack Cube task, where all other baselines recorded **0%** success.
- The framework demonstrated superior parameter efficiency across 2-agent, 3-agent, and 4-agent configurations, with qualitative analysis showing CLS-DP agents attended to teammates' kinematics, unlike egocentric baselines, confirming effective implicit coordination.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit coordination in decentralized multi-agent systems can be effectively achieved by distilling complex multi-agent dynamics into a learnable, observation-conditioned latent space during training.
> - By sampling a collaborative latent variable from a prior network at inference, individual agents can anticipate and adapt to teammates' behaviors based solely on their local observations, even under partial observability.
> - The proposed architecture maintains a per-agent computational cost independent of team size, demonstrating a scalable approach to multi-agent control without relying on explicit communication channels during deployment.
