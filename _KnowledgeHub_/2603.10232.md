# Hierarchical Task Model Predictive Control for Sequential Mobile Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Technical University of Munich and University of Toronto developed a Hierarchical Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) framework for mobile manipulators to execute sequential tasks. The system completes tasks 2.3 times faster than single-task architectures and achieves a 42% improvement in hierarchical tracking performance on a 9-DoF mobile manipulator.

## Problem

- Efficiently executing sequences of geographically distributed manipulation tasks for mobile manipulators remains challenging due to sequential, single-task execution.
- Current control approaches do not fully leverage robot kinematic redundancy for concurrent task execution, leading to reduced efficiency and reactivity.
- Real-time application of hierarchical task control to high-degrees-of-freedom, nonlinear mobile manipulators is computationally intensive, hindering online deployment.

## Method

- A Hierarchical-Task ==Model Predictive Control== (HTMPC) framework integrates nonlinear hierarchical task control directly into an MPC loop for mobile manipulators.
- The controller uses lexicographic optimization to solve a sequence of Single-Task MPC problems, enforcing a time-ordered task hierarchy.
- Reformulated lexicographic optimality constraints and added a control effort cost to improve numerical stability, handle singularities, and enhance approximation of the feasible set.

## Results

- HTMPC improved hierarchical trajectory tracking performance by **42%** over Hierarchical Task Inverse Difference Kinematic Control (HTIDKC).
- A mobile manipulator using the HTMPC-based architecture completed sequential delivery tasks **2.3** times faster than a baseline single-task architecture.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated HTMPC's superior reactivity to task changes and better handling of robot singularities compared to HTIDKC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling lexicographic optimality constraints across task dimensions and prediction steps leads to improved tracking performance by better approximating the nonlinear feasible set.
> - A tunable tolerance in relaxed constraints allows for a controlled trade-off, enabling improved performance for lower-priority tasks with minimal impact on higher-priority tasks.
> - Embedding hierarchical task control within an MPC framework leverages its predictive capabilities for superior reactivity to task changes and robust handling of robot singularities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of mobile manipulation aims to enable robots to perform complex service roles by combining the mobility of a base with the dexterity of a robotic arm. A significant challenge in this area involves executing sequences of manipulation tasks that are geographically distributed within a complex environment, often in response to high-level human instructions. Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have improved the capability of task planners to decompose human verbal commands into a sequence of sub-tasks. However, a gap remains in the decision-making algorithms that can efficiently and reactively bridge these high-level task plans with low-level robot motion control.

Existing control architectures for sequential mobile manipulation typically involve cascaded modules operating at different frequencies. Many approaches treat each task independently, leading to sequential execution where the robot may need to pause between tasks. This "single-task" paradigm often results in reduced efficiency and reactivity, as it does not fully leverage the robot's kinematic redundancy (e.g., performing an end-effector task while simultaneously repositioning the base for a subsequent task).

Methods that do attempt to leverage redundancy often adopt a planning-oriented approach, such as whole-body planners, which prioritize optimality but may incur high computational costs for high-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) robots. This can limit their reactivity to real-world changes or disturbances. In the control literature, the problem of executing ordered tasks by leveraging robot redundancy is known as hierarchical task control. Traditional hierarchical control methods, often based on Inverse Difference Kinematic Control (IDKC) or Hierarchical Quadratic Programming (HQP), primarily address linear systems and may struggle with the nonlinear kinematics inherent to mobile manipulators or maintaining real-time performance on high-DoF platforms in dynamic environments. While some Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based hierarchical methods have been proposed, they have often been demonstrated in simulation, on low-DoF robots, or solve the core hierarchical problem outside the MPC framework.

This work positions itself to address the need for an online, reactive, and efficient control framework for sequential mobile manipulation tasks. It aims to integrate nonlinear hierarchical task control directly within an MPC framework, enabling high-DoF mobile manipulators to leverage their redundancy for concurrent task execution while maintaining reactivity to environmental changes, an area where previous methods have presented limitations. The paper focuses on a common scenario where human instructions are translated into a sequence of alternating base and end-effector waypoints, although the proposed method is described as applicable to general trajectory tracking tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a decision-making algorithm that can seamlessly interface with high-level task planners to efficiently execute sequences of tasks for mobile manipulators. This overarching goal is driven by the vision of mobile manipulators serving more complex roles in everyday human environments, which necessitates enhanced performance and reactivity.

Specific objectives and motivations include:

- **Improving Performance and Reactivity:** The authors aim to enhance the robot's ability to complete sequential tasks efficiently and reactively. Current single-task control architectures often lead to robots executing tasks one after another, potentially requiring complete stops between them for safety. This reduces overall efficiency and responsiveness to dynamic environments.
- **Leveraging Robot Redundancy:** A key motivation is to enable mobile manipulators to effectively utilize their kinematic redundancy. This means coordinating the arm and base to achieve multiple objectives concurrently, such as performing an end-effector (EE) task while the mobile base moves towards its next goal. This contrasts with planning-oriented redundancy resolution approaches that, while optimal, may lack reactivity.
- **Solving Nonlinear Hierarchical Task Control Online:** The work is motivated by the challenge of applying hierarchical task control to high-DoF mobile manipulators in real-time. Traditional methods for hierarchical control, particularly those dealing with strict hierarchies, often face computational burdens when extended to nonlinear problems or when applied online to high-dimensional robots, limiting their practical deployment as reactive controllers.
- **Interfacing with High-Level Planners:** With task planners becoming more proficient at translating human instructions, there is a clear need for a control layer that can robustly and efficiently execute the generated task sequences. The proposed framework aims to provide this interface, allowing robots to interpret task sequences as hierarchical trajectory tracking problems.
- **Addressing Task Changes, Singularity, and Reference Variations:** Mobile manipulators operating in real-world environments frequently encounter situations such as changes in task specifications, robot singularities (configurations where certain movements become difficult or impossible), and variations in desired reference trajectories. The research aims to develop a controller that can maintain performance and reactivity under these challenging conditions.
- **Demonstrating Real-World Applicability:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the practical viability of the proposed approach through real-world experiments on a high-DoF mobile manipulator, validating its efficiency and reactivity in tangible scenarios.

In summary, the motivation stems from the limitations of existing methods in providing efficient, reactive, and robust control for sequential, redundant mobile manipulation tasks in dynamic, human-centric environments, aiming to bridge the gap between high-level task planning and low-level robot execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a Hierarchical-Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) framework designed to address sequential mobile manipulation tasks. The overall architecture (Fig. 2) consists of cascaded modules: a Task Planner, Motion Planner (comprising EE and Base Task-space Planners), the HTMPC Controller, and a Joint Controller. The HTMPC controller is responsible for taking a time-ordered sequence of task-space trajectory tracking tasks (e.g., alternating base and EE waypoints) and generating joint-space trajectories that coordinate the robot’s full body toward these multiple tasks, operating at a frequency of 10-100 Hz.

**A. System and Task Model:**
The mobile manipulator is modeled as a 9 DoF system, composed of a holonomic mobile base and a 6 DoF robotic arm. The robot's state `x = [q^T v^T]^T` includes generalized coordinates `q` (for both base and arm) and generalized velocities `v`. Control inputs `u` are chosen as accelerations (`u = dv/dt`). The kinematic model is given by `dx/dt = A(q)x + Bu`.
Tasks `T` are formulated as trajectory tracking problems where the robot needs to follow a desired reference signal `r(t)`. The tracking error `e` for a point `P` on the robot (determined by forward kinematics `f(q)`) relative to the reference `r` is defined as `dist(P, r) = ||f(q) - r||`.

**B. Hierarchical-Task MPC (HTMPC) Formulation:**
At each control time step `t`, the HTMPC controller solves a lexicographic optimization problem over a prediction horizon `N` steps into the future (with discretization time `Δt = 0.1s`, `N = 10`). The problem is formulated as:
`lex min [J_1, J_2..., J_L]`
subject to:
- Robot motion model (`x_k+1 = A(q_k)x_k + Bu_k`).
- Collision avoidance (`sd(x_k) >= δ_safe`, where `sd` is signed distance from robot spheres to obstacles).
- State and control input constraints (`x_k ∈ X, u_k ∈ U`).
- Initial state (`x_0 = x_o`).

The objective functions `J_l` represent the accumulated tracking error for the `l`-th task in the sequence. Each `J_l` is a sum of squared tracking errors over the prediction horizon, including stage and terminal costs: `J = 1/2 * Σ(k=0 to N-1) ||e_k||^2_Q_k + ||e_N||^2_P`. The crucial aspect is that these cost functions `J_l` are arranged in a vector according to their desired hierarchy, enforcing a time-ordered sequence where optimality of a higher-priority task is not compromised by lower-priority tasks.

**C. Solving HTMPC:**
The HTMPC problem (7) is solved using a sequential approach based on Algorithm 1 (Lexicographic Optimization), adapted for the online control context (Algorithm 2). This involves solving a sequence of Single-Task MPC (STMPC) problems. For each iteration `l`, the STMPC problem optimizes the current task `T_l`:
`min J_l + J_eff`
subject to the standard MPC constraints and additional "lexicographic optimality constraints" that enforce the optimality achieved for preceding higher-priority tasks `T_1..., T_l-1`.

Two modifications were introduced to the standard lexicographic optimization formulation:
1. **Control Effort Cost (`J_eff`):** An additional cost term `J_eff = 1/2 * (||x||^2_S + ||u||^2_R + ||Δu||^2_W)` is added to the objective function of each STMPC problem. This term encourages smooth motions, improves numerical stability, and provides regularization, especially in cases of singularity.
2. **Reformulated Lexicographic Optimality Constraints:** Instead of strict equality (`f_i(x) = f*_i(x)`) as in Algorithm 1, the authors propose two formulations for the constraints (`h_i(x, u; x*_i, u*_i) <= 0`) that preserve the optimality of preceding tasks:
 - **Baseline (11):** `J_i(x, u) <= J_i(x*_i, u*_i)`. This inequality reflects that `x*_i, u*_i` now minimize `J_i + J_eff`, not just `J_i`.
 - **Proposed (12):** Decoupled constraints `|e_kp| <= |e_k i*p|` for each prediction step `k` and task-space dimension `p`. This directly limits the tracking error of preceding tasks based on their previously achieved optimal values, serving as an inner approximation of the feasible set.

**D. Implementation and Relaxation:**
The STMPC, a Nonlinear Program (NLP), is solved using the Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) approach. Each SQP iteration solves a Quadratic Program (QP) using the Gurobi solver. The initial guess for SQP for each task `l` is either provided externally (for `l=1`) or taken from the solution of the previous iteration (`l-1`). A maximum of `MAX_ITER = 1` SQP iteration is performed, allowing a quick update direction towards the local minimum.
To handle real-world challenges like disturbances and state estimation errors that might lead to infeasibility, hard constraints (motion model, collision avoidance, state/control limits) are softened using relaxed log barrier functions.
For the lexicographic optimality constraints (9c), an alternative relaxation method is applied during the SQP line search. Instead of strictly enforcing `h_i <= 0`, a tolerance `δ_i > 0` is allowed (`h_i <= δ_i`). This enables a controlled compromise of higher-priority task accuracy to improve subsequent tasks, providing a tunable parameter for control accuracy trade-offs.

The HTMPC controller operates at an average compute time of 63ms, allowing a control frequency of 10Hz. Experiments were conducted on a 9 DoF mobile manipulator (Ridgeback mobile base + UR10 arm), with CasADi framework used for implementation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings derived from experimental evaluations on a 9 DoF mobile manipulator, addressing the performance of the HTMPC controller, its comparison with state-of-the-art methods, and the efficiency of its proposed architectural integration.

**A. Lexicographic Optimality Constraints Evaluation:**
The two proposed formulations for lexicographic optimality constraints were tested on random square wave base tracking tasks while maintaining an EE task (Table I, Fig. 5).
- The **proposed decoupled constraint formulation (12)**, which directly limits tracking error per dimension per time step, demonstrated improved tracking performance for both the primary (EE) and secondary (base) tasks compared to the baseline formulation (11). This improvement is attributed to better approximation of the nonlinear feasible set, enabling larger and more effective step sizes during the SQP line search.
- The introduction of **tolerance (δ)** in the relaxed constraints (13) showed that increasing this tolerance could improve the performance of the secondary task, albeit with a transient, non-negligible increase in the standard deviation of the primary EE task's error. This allows for a quantifiable trade-off between task priorities.

**B. HTMPC vs. HTIDKC Comparison:**
HTMPC was compared against Hierarchical Task Inverse Difference Kinematic Control (HTIDKC) and a variation, HTMPC Waypoint (HTMPC_WPT), using 25 randomly generated square wave tests designed to include task changes, robot singularities, and reference variations (Fig. 3, 4, 6, 7).
- **Hierarchical Tracking Performance:** HTMPC achieved a 42% improvement in hierarchical trajectory tracking performance (measured in terms of mean normalized base error in Part 2 of the square wave task) compared to HTIDKC (Fig. 6). While both controllers were tuned for similar performance in the higher-priority tasks and the initial part of the base tracking, HTIDKC struggled to adapt to the second part of the base tracking task, where the target became easily reachable.
- **Reactivity to Task Changes:** HTMPC demonstrated superior reactivity. Its predictive nature, allowing it to foresee the entire square wave trajectory, enabled faster convergence and lower tracking error in Part 2 of the base task compared to both HTIDKC and HTMPC_WPT (which received the trajectory as separate waypoints) (Fig. 4). HTIDKC, optimized for Part 1, did not generalize well to Part 2 without re-tuning, suggesting a need for parameter scheduling in such an approach.
- **Singularity Handling:** HTMPC exhibited better performance in scenarios where the robot arm approached singular configurations. As shown in the radial heat map (Fig. 7), HTMPC experienced the least performance decline when the base peak target, valley target, and EE target were collinear, forcing aggressive arm maneuvers. This is attributed to HTMPC's better regularization of joint velocity and acceleration, coupled with improved constraint handling via prediction and line search.

**C. Hierarchical-Task vs. Baseline Control Architectures:**
The proposed HTMPC-based hierarchical-task control architecture (HT-Arch) was compared against a typical single-task architecture (ST-Arch), which utilized an arm-base decoupled controller.
- **Efficiency:** For a sequence of delivery tasks to three people over four rounds, the HT-Arch executed the tasks **2.3 times faster** than the ST-Arch (Fig. 9). This significant improvement is a direct result of HTMPC's ability to leverage robot redundancy, allowing the mobile base to move towards the next delivery target while the end-effector is still engaged in the current task. In contrast, the ST-Arch required sequential execution.
- **Optimality in Path Planning:** HT-Arch demonstrated optimal base positioning for EE tasks at varying heights (Fig. 8). Unlike ST-Arch, which relied on the motion planner to adjust intermediate base waypoints based on heuristics, HTMPC in HT-Arch could automatically adapt its base path, positioning the robot such that the EE waypoint was just reachable.
- **Reactivity to External Events:** The HT-Arch demonstrated robust reactivity to unexpected events. For instance, when the robot had to hold its EE in place due to an unknown timing of a person picking up an object, HTMPC allowed the base to continue moving, stopping only immediately before compromising the EE task. The architecture also successfully reacted to an unexpected shift in the current task, showcasing fast replanning and efficient hierarchical control.

All reported results were obtained from real-world experiments on a 9 DoF mobile manipulator, validating the practical applicability of the framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of mobile manipulation control, particularly for tasks involving sequences and the need for reactive behavior in dynamic environments. The development of the Hierarchical Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) framework has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Autonomy in Complex Environments:** The ability of HTMPC to seamlessly interface with high-level task planners and efficiently execute sequential tasks with improved reactivity is crucial for deploying mobile manipulators in more complex, unstructured service roles. This includes applications in logistics, healthcare, personal assistance, and disaster response, where robots need to perform multiple, varied tasks over a broader operational area.
- **Improved Efficiency and Throughput:** The demonstrated 2.3 times faster execution of sequential delivery tasks compared to conventional single-task architectures highlights a significant gain in operational efficiency. By leveraging kinematic redundancy to perform tasks concurrently (e.g., base repositioning while arm is active), robots can achieve higher throughput, making them more economically viable and practical for industrial and service applications.
- **Increased Reactivity and Robustness:** The HTMPC's capacity to react to unforeseen task changes, dynamic obstacles, and variations in reference trajectories, even when encountering robot singularities, enhances the robot's robustness in real-world scenarios. This is vital for safe and reliable operation alongside humans, where environmental conditions are often unpredictable. The method's ability to handle singularity configurations better than state-of-the-art inverse kinematic methods is a particular strength.
- **Advancement in Hierarchical Control for Nonlinear Systems:** Integrating nonlinear lexicographic optimization directly within an MPC framework for high-DoF robots is a methodological contribution. By reformulating and relaxing lexicographic optimality constraints, the authors have made online solution feasible, pushing the boundaries of real-time hierarchical control for complex robotic systems.
- **Generalizability of the Framework:** While demonstrated with alternating base and EE waypoints, the proposed method is applicable to general trajectory tracking tasks. This broad applicability suggests the framework could be adapted to a wide range of mobile manipulation scenarios, requiring only appropriate task definitions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for further investigation into alternative regularization techniques and lexicographic constraint formulations, potentially leading to even more robust and efficient control algorithms. It also sets a benchmark for performance against which future online hierarchical controllers can be evaluated.
- **Bridging the Gap between Planning and Control:** By providing a robust and reactive control layer that can effectively interpret and execute task sequences from high-level planners (especially those potentially informed by LLMs), this research contributes to the overarching goal of more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems.

In conclusion, this work contributes to making mobile manipulators more capable, efficient, and adaptable to real-world operational demands, thereby accelerating their integration into various service and industrial sectors.
