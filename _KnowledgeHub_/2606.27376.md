# Ask, Solve, Generate: Self-Evolving Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation via Self-Consistency Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Ask, Solve, Generate is a self-evolving post-training framework for unified multimodal models: three LoRA roles (Proposer, Solver, Generator) on a frozen backbone learn from unlabeled images alone. Rewards come from prompt-perturbed self-consistency, Solver Token Entropy, plus QA-fidelity and cycle-consistent-captioning checks on generated images. One fixed recipe lifts eight understanding metrics and raises GenEval by three points on diffusion, rectified-flow and autoregressive backbones.

## Problem

- Post-training of unified models still leans on curated VQA labels, preference annotations, or task-trained reward and judge models, making improvement pipelines costly and brittle.
- Prior self-improvement recipes each leave a dimension open: one backbone family, one task direction, or a method-specific signal, so none reports one unchanged recipe across generation paradigms.
- Removing labels causes reward degeneracy, since sample-level self-consistency collapses to unanimous agreement even under low confidence, while understanding and generation objectives stay weakly coupled.

## Method

- Three ==LoRA== role adapters on a frozen backbone: **Proposer** asks visual questions, **Solver** answers under seven prompt perturbations, **Generator** synthesizes images; ==GRPO== trains the Proposer, ==REINFORCE== the Solver, both ==KL-regularized==.
- **Solver Token Entropy** takes maximum next-token entropy over the first five answer tokens, ==rank-normalized== within a ==128-sample rolling window==, yielding a continuous ==difficulty score== that drives the Proposer curriculum.
- Generator reward is ==multi-scale internal evaluation==: ==QA fidelity== weighted 0.65 against Solver answers on the real image, ==cycle-consistent captioning== 0.20, diversity 0.10, minus a yes/no ==contradiction penalty==.

## Results

- Understanding improves on all eight reported metrics per backbone: MMMU **50.6 to 52.8** on BLIP3o-8B, **55.3 to 58.8** on BAGEL, **48.6 to 51.6** on VARGPT-v1.1, MME cognition up to **+14.9**.
- GenEval gains a uniform **+3 points** on all three backbones, concentrated in compositional subcategories: Two Objects **85 to 93** and Counting **63 to 71 percent** on BLIP3o-8B.
- Ablations: removing STE costs **1.8 MMMU**, removing QA fidelity drops GenEval from **87 to 84**; joint training beats understanding-only (**83 GenEval**) and generation-only (**50.6 MMMU**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token-level uncertainty rescues curriculum signals when output-level agreement saturates, so measure difficulty inside the prediction distribution, not only across sampled answers.
> - Coupling comprehension and generation through a shared internal evaluator is asymmetric: better understanding sharpens generation rewards, but generation updates do not train the understanding path.
> - Low-rank adapters beat full fine-tuning under noisy self-derived rewards, and label-free gains stay bounded by the internal evaluator's own quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large multimodal models (LMMs) that integrate both visual understanding and image generation capabilities has advanced significantly. These unified models aim to leverage shared representations to foster synergistic improvements between tasks, where stronger comprehension can lead to more reliable generation assessment and generated visual contexts can expose new perceptual scenarios.

However, a predominant challenge in the post-training of these LMMs is their continued reliance on curated supervision. This often involves expensive and labor-intensive resources such as human annotations for visual question answering (VQA), preference labels for image generation quality, or external reward models specifically trained for various tasks. This dependency can make post-training pipelines costly, difficult to scale, and potentially brittle to distribution shifts.

Prior research has explored various self-improvement methodologies to mitigate this dependency, including role-based self-play, dual likelihood rewards, internal gap exploitation, and reinforcement-based optimization. While these methods have demonstrated progress in reducing reliance on external labels, they often present limitations. These limitations commonly manifest as validation being restricted to a single model family, a specific task direction (e.g., understanding only or generation primarily), or relying on method-specific signals such as proxy tasks, reconstruction, dual likelihood, self-generated data, or rule-based rewards. A notable gap in the existing literature is the absence of a universally applicable, fully self-supervised recipe for jointly improving both understanding and generation (U+G) that consistently yields gains across diverse underlying generation architectures, specifically diffusion-based, rectified-flow, and autoregressive paradigms, using only unlabeled images.

This work positions itself to address this identified gap by proposing a generalizable, fully self-supervised post-training framework. It aims to demonstrate that a single, unified algorithmic approach, utilizing only unlabeled images, can autonomously enhance both visual understanding and image generation capabilities across a range of fundamentally different multimodal model architectures, without requiring human annotations, preference labels, or external task-trained reward models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether a unified large multimodal model (LMM) can autonomously enhance both its visual understanding and image generation capabilities using solely unlabeled images. This overarching question is driven by the motivation to overcome the limitations of existing post-training methodologies for LMMs, which frequently depend on costly and brittle curated supervision, such as human annotations, preference labels, or external reward models.

The authors identify two specific technical challenges that arise when attempting to remove external labels entirely from the self-improvement process:

1. **Reward Degeneracy:** In self-supervised settings, sample-level self-consistency can prematurely collapse. This means that even when a model's internal confidence is low or its reasoning is flawed, it might consistently produce the same incorrect or uninformative answer across multiple internal queries. Such a collapse leads to zero-entropy agreement, thereby generating weak or uninformative curriculum signals for learning. This hinders the model's ability to identify and learn from its own competence frontier—questions that are challenging but solvable.

2. **Weak Cross-Task Coupling:** Visual understanding and image generation tasks are often optimized with separate objectives during model development. This decoupling limits the direct benefits that improvements in one capability might confer upon the other. Specifically, stronger visual comprehension, if not explicitly linked, may not reliably translate into more effective assessment of generated images, thus diminishing the internal training signals for improving generation quality. Conversely, generated visual contexts might not effectively expose new perceptual cases to improve understanding without explicit mechanisms.

To address these motivations and challenges, the research sets forth several objectives:

- To propose a self-evolving training framework that eliminates the need for human annotations, curated supervision, or task-trained external reward/judge models for unified LMM post-training.
- To introduce a novel mechanism, Solver Token Entropy (STE), to provide a continuous difficulty measure derived from token-level prediction uncertainty. This aims to resolve the degenerate-signal limitation of sample-level self-consistency and enable effective curriculum learning from a "cold start."
- To design a multi-scale internal evaluation scheme for image generation, combining QA fidelity scoring with cycle-consistent captioning. This scheme is intended to utilize the Solver as an internal evaluator, ensuring that improvements in visual understanding directly sharpen generation-side rewards, thereby fostering a solver-mediated coupling between the two tasks.
- To demonstrate the backbone-portable generality of the proposed algorithm by applying a single, fixed self-evolving recipe, using only backbone-native wrappers, to three distinct unified backbone architectures: diffusion-based, rectified-flow, and autoregressive models. This objective aims to provide empirical evidence that such an unsupervised approach can yield consistent gains across fundamentally different generation paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a self-evolving training framework that operates on a frozen backbone unified multimodal model, utilizing only unlabeled images for post-training. The core approach involves decomposing the LMM into three specialized, lightweight roles, each implemented as a LoRA adapter, which are trained collaboratively.

**Framework Architecture and Roles:**
The unified model's parameters ($\theta$) are frozen, and three LoRA adapters ($\phi_p, \phi_s, \phi_g$) are trained to instantiate the following roles:

- **Proposer ($\pi_{\phi_p}$):** Generates visual questions (q) given an image (I), aiming to probe the Solver's understanding.
- **Solver ($\pi_{\phi_s}$):** Answers questions (a) about an image (I, q), and also serves as an internal evaluator.
- **Generator ($\pi_{\phi_g}$):** Synthesizes images ($\hat{I}$) from text prompts (t).

**Training Data and Constraints:**
Training is conducted exclusively on an unlabeled image set ($D$). No human annotations, captions, QA pairs, or preference labels are used. The framework strictly adheres to three constraints: (C1) no curated supervision, (C2) no task-trained external reward/judge model, and (C3) controlled policy drift using KL regularization against the frozen reference policies.

**Optimization Objective:**
The training optimizes a joint objective function that combines rewards for both visual understanding ($R_p, R_s$) and image generation ($R_g$), weighted by hyperparameters ($\lambda_u, \lambda_g$). Training alternates between understanding steps (updating Proposer and Solver) and generation steps (updating Generator).

**Self-Consistent Understanding Loop:**
This loop focuses on improving the Proposer and Solver through self-derived consistency signals.

1. **Question Generation and Prompt Perturbation:** For a given image $I$, the Proposer samples $K$ candidate questions $q_k$. For each question, the Solver is queried $N$ times under fixed, semantically equivalent prompt framings ($\rho_i(q_k)$) to obtain $N$ answers $a_{k,i}$.
2. **Sample-Level Self-Consistency Entropy (H_sc):** The diversity of answers from these $N$ framings is measured by Shannon entropy. Low $H_{sc}$ indicates robust reasoning, while high $H_{sc}$ suggests unstable comprehension. Answers are normalized (lowercased, punctuation stripped, truncated) before computing consistency to focus on semantic agreement.
3. **Solver Token Entropy (STE):** To address reward degeneracy where $H_{sc}$ can be low even when the Solver is uncertain, STE is introduced. It measures token-level uncertainty by computing the maximum entropy over the Solver's next-token distributions for the first $K_{ste}=5$ answer tokens ($\hat{H}$). This $\hat{H}$ is then converted into a continuous difficulty score ($d_{ste}$) by taking its percentile rank within a rolling window of recent $\hat{H}$ values. This provides a fine-grained signal of difficulty, even when sample-level answers collapse.
4. **Proposer and Solver Optimization:**
 - **Proposer ($R_p$):** Trained to generate questions that lie at the Solver's competence frontier—neither trivial (high confidence, low STE) nor unsolvable (low consistency, high STE). The reward combines a weighted sum of $d_{ste}$ and a bounded $r_{sc}$ (favoring medium $H_{sc}$ values, guided by an EMA target). The Proposer is updated using KL-regularized policy gradients (GRPO).
 - **Solver ($R_s$):** Trained to produce consistent answers across framings. It receives rewards for agreement with the modal answer ($a^*$) and penalties for non-canonical or excessively verbose outputs. The Solver is updated using REINFORCE over answer tokens, also with KL regularization.

**Generation Assessment via Internal Evaluation Loop:**
This loop updates the Generator, with the Solver acting as an internal evaluator, creating a "solver-mediated coupling."

1. **Prompt Generation and Candidate Images:** A source image $I$ is captioned by the Solver to obtain a text prompt $t$. The Generator then synthesizes $L$ candidate images $\hat{I}_{\ell}$ from $t$.
2. **QA Fidelity Scoring ($S_{fid}$):** The Proposer generates $M$ diagnostic questions about the original image $I$. The Solver provides reference answers ($a^{ref}_j$) for these questions on $I$. These same questions are then posed to the Solver regarding each generated image $\hat{I}_{\ell}$ to obtain answers $\hat{a}_j$. $S_{fid}$ measures the match between $\hat{a}_j$ and $a^{ref}_j$ using a soft matching function (exact, substring, numeric agreement, and Solver majority fraction).
3. **Cycle Consistent Captioning ($S_{cyc}$):** To assess global fidelity, each generated image $\hat{I}_{\ell}$ is captioned by the Solver to produce $\hat{t}$. $S_{cyc}$ measures the semantic similarity between the original prompt $t$ and the generated caption $\hat{t}$ (using frozen text embeddings $f(\cdot)$) and vision-language similarity between $\hat{I}_{\ell}$ and $t$ (using fixed vision-language similarity $\text{sim}_{vl}(\cdot,\cdot)$).
4. **Generator Optimization ($R_g$):** The Generator reward combines weighted $S_{fid}$ and $S_{cyc}$, a diversity term ($S_{div}$), and a penalty for explicit contradictions ($S_{ctr}$). Updates are routed through the Generator's native parameterization: token-policy updates for autoregressive models or reward-weighted denoising for diffusion/flow models. Low-quality prompt cards are filtered using a spec-quality and minimum-QA gates before any generator update.

**Backbone Portability:**
The framework is designed to be backbone-agnostic. The same role decomposition, reward logic, and training schedule are applied across different LMM paradigms: BLIP3o (diffusion-based), BAGEL (rectified-flow), and VARGPT-v1.1 (autoregressive). Only the backbone's native chat wrapper and generation interface are adapted.

**Experimental Setup:**
Training is performed for approximately 10,000 steps on a pool of 10,000 unlabeled images sampled from COCO, SA-1B, TextVQA, GQA, and LAION-COCO datasets. LoRA rank 16 is used, with a 3:2 understanding-to-generation schedule. Performance is evaluated on 7 understanding benchmarks (MMMU, MMBench, TextVQA, SEED-Bench, RealWorldQA, MM-Vet, MME Perception/Cognition) and the GenEval benchmark for image generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study reports consistent improvements across all evaluated unified multimodal models and metrics when the proposed self-evolving framework is applied.

**Visual Understanding Improvements:**
- The framework consistently improved all eight reported understanding metrics over the respective base unified models.
- **MMMU:** Significant absolute gains were observed. For BLIP3o-8B, MMMU increased from 50.6% to 52.8% (+2.2%). For BAGEL, it improved from 55.3% to 58.8% (+3.5%). VARGPT-v1.1 saw an increase from 48.6% to 51.6% (+3.0%).
- **Reasoning-heavy Metrics:** The largest relative gains were found on metrics assessing higher-order reasoning, such as MMMU and MME Cognition (up to +14.9 points), particularly where the base models had lower initial scores.
- **Perceptual Metrics:** Even on perceptual tasks closer to the models' performance ceilings (e.g., MMBench, TextVQA), consistent improvements were noted (e.g., BLIP3o-8B MMBench from 83.5% to 86.1%, TextVQA from 83.1% to 85.2%). This indicates that the self-evolving signals refine reasoning capabilities without degrading foundational recognition abilities.
- **Backbone Portability:** The consistent gains across BLIP3o-8B (diffusion), BAGEL (rectified-flow), and VARGPT-v1.1 (autoregressive) demonstrate the portability of the single self-evolving algorithm, using only backbone-native interfaces.

**Image Generation Improvements:**
- A uniform absolute gain of +3 percentage points on the GenEval overall score was achieved across all three backbones.
 - BLIP3o-8B: 84% to 87%.
 - BAGEL: 82% to 85%.
 - VARGPT-v1.1: 53% to 56%.
- **Compositional Subcategories:** The improvements in image generation were largely concentrated on more challenging compositional subcategories. For instance, on BLIP3o-8B, "Two Objects" improved from 85% to 93% (+8%) and "Counting" from 63% to 71% (+8%).
- **Near-Saturated Scores:** Categories where base models already performed well (e.g., "Single Object" at ≥96%) remained stable, indicating the framework targets areas of weakness without regressing on strengths.
- **Backbone-Specific Adaptation:** The generation loop adapted to each backbone's current capabilities, providing targeted improvements without altering the core reward design or training schedule. For VARGPT-v1.1, while gains were observed (e.g., Counting +8%, Two Objects +6%), certain categories like "Position" and "Color Attribution" remained relatively low, reflecting inherent architectural limitations not fully overcome by semantic-level supervision.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Understanding Signals:** Both Solver Token Entropy (STE) and self-consistency were found to be complementary and essential. Removing STE reduced MMMU by 1.8 points and MMBench by 1.6 points. Removing self-consistency reduced MMMU by 1.3 points and SEED-Bench by 1.1 points. This suggests each signal captures distinct failure modes.
- **Generation Rewards:** QA fidelity scoring contributed more significantly to generation gains than cycle-consistent captioning (removing QA fidelity caused a 3% GenEval drop, while removing cycle consistency caused a 2% drop), but both components were necessary for optimal joint performance.
- **Loop Coupling:** Joint training, where understanding and generation loops alternate, significantly outperformed single-loop variants (understanding-only or generation-only) on both tasks. This supports the concept of solver-mediated asymmetric coupling, where improved understanding in the Solver enhances its discriminative capability for generation evaluation. Training only on self-generated images also underperformed the full framework.
- **Parameter Update Strategy:** LoRA adapter updates proved to be more stable and effective for the internal reward signals compared to QLoRA or full fine-tuning. Full fine-tuning notably led to a degradation in performance below the base model on some metrics, indicating potential instability with purely internal rewards.

**Training Dynamics and Qualitative Results:**
- Training dynamics showed STE-driven exploration followed by stabilization of self-consistency and monotonic increases in generation rewards across all backbones.
- Qualitative examples demonstrated that evolved checkpoints corrected errors in object, action, and spatial understanding, and produced generated images with improved color, count, and compositional fidelity compared to baseline models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the autonomous improvement of unified large multimodal models, primarily through its ability to entirely eliminate the reliance on curated supervised data for post-training. The implications of this work are broad, affecting both the research and application landscapes of LMMs.

**Reduced Dependency on Curated Data:** The most immediate and substantial impact is the demonstration that unified LMMs can autonomously enhance both visual understanding and image generation using *only unlabeled images*. This removes the significant bottlenecks and costs associated with acquiring and maintaining human annotations, preference labels, or developing specialized external reward/judge models. This facilitates more scalable, flexible, and accessible model development, particularly for resource-constrained settings or rapidly evolving domains where extensive manual labeling is impractical.

**Enhanced Generalizability and Portability:** The study introduces a single, backbone-portable self-evolving algorithm that consistently yields performance gains across diverse LMM architectures, including diffusion-based (BLIP3o), rectified-flow (BAGEL), and autoregressive (VARGPT-v1.1) paradigms. This level of cross-architectural generality for a self-supervised U+G recipe is a novel contribution, suggesting a robust and adaptable methodology that is not tied to specific model designs. This can accelerate the development and post-training of new LMM architectures without requiring re-engineering of the self-improvement mechanisms.

**Technical Innovations in Self-Supervision:**
- The introduction of **Solver Token Entropy (STE)** effectively addresses the challenge of reward degeneracy in self-supervised learning. By providing a continuous, token-level uncertainty signal, STE allows the model to identify and learn from its competence frontier more effectively, even when sample-level self-consistency appears saturated. This is crucial for guiding the Proposer to generate appropriately challenging questions and for enabling a meaningful curriculum.
- The **multi-scale internal evaluation scheme** for image generation, combining QA fidelity scoring and cycle-consistent captioning, represents a practical solution for leveraging understanding improvements to sharpen generation rewards. By positioning the Solver as an internal evaluator, the framework establishes a solver-mediated coupling where enhanced visual comprehension directly translates into more discriminative assessment of generated content, creating a positive feedback loop.

**Robustness and Efficiency:** The LoRA-based approach, which freezes the underlying backbone model and trains only lightweight adapters, contributes to both robustness and efficiency. It preserves the base capabilities of powerful pre-trained models while enabling targeted improvements, and it minimizes the computational overhead and parameter footprint compared to full model fine-tuning, which was shown to be less stable under the internal reward signals.

**Catalyst for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research. It motivates further exploration into developing even stronger internal evaluators, scaling the approach to much larger unlabeled image and video pools, and extending the self-evolving paradigm to other modalities or tasks, such as video generation, 3D scene understanding, or multimodal editing. The public release of code and models further fosters open science and reproducibility, allowing the community to build upon these foundations.

In summary, this research demonstrates a viable and effective path towards truly autonomous LMM improvement, significantly reducing external supervision requirements while maintaining generalizability across diverse architectures. This has the potential to democratize access to advanced LMM capabilities and accelerate innovation in multimodal AI.
