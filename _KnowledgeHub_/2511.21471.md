# SpatialBench: Benchmarking Multimodal Large Language Models for Spatial Cognition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced SpatialBench, a cognitively grounded, hierarchical evaluation framework and a large-scale dataset for benchmarking Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) spatial cognition. The study found MLLMs excel at basic spatial perception but exhibit significant limitations in higher-level symbolic reasoning, causality, and planning, revealing a substantial performance gap when compared to human intelligence which achieved a near-perfect 96.40% overall score.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks for spatial abilities are fragmented, task-oriented, and rely on simplified datasets, failing to capture the hierarchical and interdependent nature of spatial cognition.
- Prior evaluations provide only a partial view of MLLMs' spatial intelligence, making it difficult to systematically diagnose deficiencies or analyze cognitive processes comprehensively.
- A lack of robust, diagnostically powerful methods for evaluating MLLM spatial cognition hinders the development of AI systems capable of effective interaction with the physical world.

## Method

- A ==five-level hierarchical framework== for spatial cognition was established, decomposing spatial intelligence into observation, topology and relation, symbolic reasoning, causality, and planning.
- A new, large-scale, and fine-grained benchmark dataset named ==SpatialBench== was constructed, comprising **1,347** question-answer pairs across **15** tasks derived from real-world, egocentric videos with LiDAR ground truth.
- A novel, ==capability-oriented metric== was introduced, which assigns adaptive weights to each cognitive level to provide a holistic and complexity-aware assessment of overall spatial reasoning ability.

## Results

- MLLMs generally performed well on Level 1 (Observation) and Level 2 (Topology and Relation) tasks but exhibited a sharp performance drop for Levels 3 (Symbolic Reasoning), 4 (Causality), and 5 (Planning).
- Proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-pro consistently secured top positions, demonstrating up to **15.6%** higher average accuracy on higher-level tasks compared to open-source models.
- One-shot evaluation revealed varied generalization capabilities across models, with some weaker models showing substantial performance improvements, indicating the potential of in-context learning to boost higher-level cognitive capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs demonstrate strong performance on lower-level spatial cognition tasks (observation, topology & relation) but show a significant decline in higher-level tasks (symbolic reasoning, causality, planning).
> - Leading proprietary MLLMs consistently achieved substantially higher overall scores than open-source counterparts, particularly in complex cognitive tasks.
> - Human participants achieved a near-perfect overall score of 96.40%, significantly outperforming all MLLMs and highlighting their superior goal-directed reasoning and robust egocentric perspective taking.
