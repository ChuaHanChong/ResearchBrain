# EgoGuide: Egocentric Guidance for Efficient Robot-Free Demonstration Collection and Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoGuide presents a robot-free data collection system offering real-time visual-geometric guidance to human demonstrators and introduces a Gated Egocentric Residual Policy (GERP). This approach boosts data efficiency by promoting diversity and yields more robust robot manipulation policies through adaptive integration of head-camera context, demonstrating improved success rates with fewer training demonstrations.

## Problem

- Robot-free demonstration systems like UMI often suffer from low data efficiency due to redundant demonstrations and lack of feedback to human operators.
- The primary reliance on local wrist-view cameras in UMI leads to limited observability, making tasks difficult during occlusions or when global scene context is crucial.
- Learning effective policies from viewpoint-varying human egocentric head camera data is challenging, as head movements are often unintentional and complicate robot deployment with fixed cameras.

## Method

- **EgoGuide** provides online data curation through a multi-modal hardware setup (wrist and head cameras, spatial tracking) and real-time ==visual-geometric novelty guidance== to human demonstrators, encouraging diverse data collection.
- The system supports partial demonstrations, allowing collection to start from intermediate task states, which significantly increases the diversity of object arrangements and later-stage task configurations.
- The Gated Egocentric Residual Policy (GERP) employs a two-stage learning process: first, a wrist-only base policy, then an independent egocentric residual branch that predicts a corrective action and a gate to blend it with the base policy, leveraging contextual information without controlling head motion during robot deployment.

## Results

- EgoGuide-guided data collection achieved comparable policy success rates with approximately **50%** fewer demonstrations (e.g., 200 EgoGuide demos vs. over 400 unguided demos on "Pepper Sorting") and resulted in datasets covering a larger feature space.
- The Gated Egocentric Residual Policy (GERP) consistently improved task success rates and progress scores, outperforming "Wrist Only" and "Wrist+Ego Direct" baselines, achieving up to **80%** success and **87.5%** task progress on "Pepper Sorting" with 400 demonstrations.
- GERP's gating mechanism adaptively increased the influence of the egocentric branch when the target object was occluded or outside the wrist camera's field of view, and the policy demonstrated greater robustness to variations in fixed egocentric camera placements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time visual and geometric novelty guidance during data collection is effective in reducing redundancy and enhancing the quality and diversity of datasets, leading to more efficient policy learning.
> - Integrating egocentric head-camera observations as a gated residual input allows policies to leverage broader scene context when local information is ambiguous (e.g., during occlusion) while maintaining stable local control.
> - Training a robust wrist-only base policy before introducing a gated egocentric residual branch, with a curriculum-based blending, provides a robust mechanism for combining precise local manipulation with global contextual awareness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The scaling of robot learning through real-world demonstrations is a fundamental challenge in robotics. Traditional teleoperation systems, while effective, are often costly and resource-intensive. The Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) paradigm has emerged as an alternative, offering a robot-free, portable, and low-cost method for collecting manipulation data in various environments. This has led to several extensions, including FastUMI for improved portability and throughput, and systems like exUMI, TacUMI, ViTaMIn, and FreeTacMan that integrate tactile or force sensing for contact-rich tasks. Other works such as MV-UMI and UMI-3D have focused on enhancing visual or spatial observability. More recently, DexUMI, HoMMI, and HuMI have explored extensions to dexterous or whole-body manipulation.

Within this landscape, egocentric vision has also gained attention for UMI systems. Previous efforts such as Vision in Action (ViA), ActiveUMI, and EgoMI have explored using additional egocentric cameras to learn active robot head policies from human demonstrations, aiming to mimic human attention or provide broader contextual views.

Beyond data collection, the quality of demonstration data is critical for imitation learning. Methods like DAgger, SafeDAgger, and HG-DAgger involve interactive collection where a learned policy rolls out and queries expert corrections in novel states. Offline studies, including RoboMimic, RoboTurk, and RoboNet, have further emphasized the importance of demonstration quality and diversity. Recent techniques such as DemInf, Demo-SCORE, and CUPID focus on post-collection filtering or utility estimation to curate datasets.

EgoGuide positions itself within this research landscape by addressing two specific limitations of current UMI-style pipelines: suboptimal data efficiency due to redundancy and lack of global scene context, and the challenges of learning from viewpoint-varying egocentric observations. This work aims to enhance the utility of UMI by providing online guidance *during* the robot-free data collection process, rather than solely relying on post-collection filtering or on-policy robot rollouts. Furthermore, it introduces a novel approach to integrate egocentric observations as a robust, gated residual input for policy learning, diverging from methods that learn active head control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations in scaling robot learning using real-world demonstrations, particularly with Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) systems. While UMI offers a promising path for scalable data collection by circumventing the need for physical robots during data acquisition, its current implementations exhibit lower data efficiency compared to teleoperation systems, sometimes requiring significantly more demonstration episodes for comparable policy success. The authors define "data efficiency" as the human collection effort required to train a policy to a target success rate.

The paper identifies two critical bottlenecks contributing to UMI's data efficiency challenges:

1. **Redundant Demonstrations and Lack of Global Scene Context:** UMI data is typically collected using a human-held gripper surrogate, and these demonstrations are later transferred to a robot with potentially different kinematics, coordinate systems, and execution constraints. For a policy to generalize effectively across these differences, it requires broad coverage of data variations rather than an abundance of repetitive, successful trajectories. However, current UMI setups provide minimal feedback to demonstrators regarding which states are already well-represented in the dataset and which remain under-explored. This lack of guidance can lead to the collection of redundant demonstrations, diminishing the marginal utility of each additional episode.

2. **Limited Observability from Local Wrist-View Cameras:** Most UMI systems primarily rely on a single camera mounted near the wrist or gripper. While this viewpoint is suitable for precise manipulation, its observations can be overly local. This limitation becomes problematic in scenarios involving occlusions, object disappearance from the camera's field of view, or during long-horizon tasks where global scene context is necessary to understand overall task progress or object arrangements. The local nature of the wrist camera can lead to ambiguous or incomplete observations, making it difficult for policies to infer the full task state.

To address these identified bottlenecks, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Improve Data Efficiency of UMI Systems:** The overarching goal is to reduce the human effort required to collect effective training data for robot manipulation policies, making UMI a more practical and scalable solution for real-world applications.
- **Implement Collection-Time Coverage Guidance:** Develop a system that provides demonstrators with real-time feedback on the novelty and diversity of the current visual-geometric state, encouraging them to explore under-represented initial conditions, object arrangements, viewpoints, and workspace configurations.
- **Integrate Complementary Egocentric Observations:** Incorporate head/egocentric camera data to provide broader scene context, thereby mitigating the observability issues encountered with solely wrist-view observations, especially in situations of occlusion or when global context is beneficial.
- **Develop a Robust Policy for Viewpoint-Varying Egocentric Data:** Create a learning algorithm that can effectively utilize egocentric observations, even when the human head movement is often unintentional and the egocentric view is viewpoint-varying, without requiring the policy to learn or actively control head motion during robot deployment. This aims for a robust policy that can generalize to a fixed egocentric camera setup.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EgoGuide system addresses the identified bottlenecks through a combination of novel hardware integration, an online data curation pipeline, and a dedicated policy learning algorithm.

#### 3.1. EgoGuide System

The system is designed as a closed-loop robot-free collection system, encompassing both hardware and online data curation.

##### 3.1.1. EgoGuide-UMI Hardware

The hardware extends an open-source UMI design (exUMI) with multi-modal sensing and spatial tracking capabilities.
- **Handheld Gripper Interface:** A hand-held gripper features a rotary sensor to measure gripper opening width (`g`) and an industrial board-level fisheye camera for the wrist image (`I_W`). These are connected to an on-device Raspberry Pi controller.
- **Egocentric Sensing and Spatial Tracking:** A Meta Quest headset is used to capture the head-camera image (`I_H`) via its Passthrough API and provides the head pose (`T_H`). A Quest controller attached to the gripper provides the wrist pose (`T_W`). The AR passthrough view allows the demonstrator to see global context.
- **Synchronized Wireless Streaming:** All sensor modalities are synchronized over UDP. The headset streams `I_H`, `T_H`, `T_W`, and recording state to the Raspberry Pi at 72 Hz. The Raspberry Pi captures `I_W` and `g` at 20 Hz, aligns them to the closest headset timestamp, and sends the combined packet to a workstation. The workstation stores episodes, computes online coverage scores, and returns AR guidance. The system achieves cross-modality synchronization within 20 ms and end-to-end workstation latency within 100 ms. The collected observation is `o = {I_W, I_H, T_W, T_H, g}`.

##### 3.1.2. Online Data Curation

EgoGuide leverages the live data stream to improve data quality throughout the collection process.
- **Online Data Coverage Guidance:** Before each episode, EgoGuide quantifies how much the current collection state contributes to expanding the dataset's coverage. This "data coverage score" is computed from three complementary signals:
 - **Wrist Images (`I_W`):** Reflect diversity in hand-object appearance and contact configurations. Visual features are extracted (using DINOv2 and CLIP encoders) and compared to a view-specific feature memory via average cosine similarity to k-nearest neighbors.
 - **Wrist Poses (`T_W`):** Capture geometric and action-side diversity. A normalized translation-rotation distance is computed against a pose memory.
 - **Head/Egocentric Images (`I_H`):** Provide object arrangement, workspace layout, and contextual cues from a global viewpoint, complementing the local wrist camera. Visual features (DINOv2 and CLIP) are compared to a view-specific feature memory.
 All similarity scores are converted into novelty percentiles (0-100) relative to the current memories. Three such guidance values—wrist-view novelty, egocentric-context novelty, and wrist-pose novelty—are displayed in the AR interface. This feedback encourages the demonstrator to adjust initial hand pose, object arrangement, viewpoint, or workspace layout to generate more diverse data. The estimator runs at 2 Hz.
- **Partial Demonstration:** EgoGuide allows starting new recordings from the middle of a task. This expands data coverage beyond initial conditions by capturing underrepresented intermediate states or subtasks, enhancing flexibility and later-stage diversity.
- **Static Data Filtering:** After an episode is recorded, EgoGuide applies deterministic quality checks. Episodes are rejected if they: (1) miss any required modality, (2) are too short, (3) contain implausible wrist or head motion (detected via velocity thresholds), or (4) have severely blurred images (measured by Laplacian variance) or abnormal brightness. This step removes sensor failures and physically inconsistent trajectories, discarding approximately 2%–5% of collected episodes. Accepted episodes are downsampled and added to memory.

#### 3.2. Algorithm: Gated Egocentric Residual Policy (GERP)

GERP addresses the challenge of learning from viewpoint-varying egocentric observations by treating them as a gated alternative action cue rather than a direct replacement for wrist-view control or an active-perception output.

- **Stage 1: Wrist-Only Base Policy (π_b):** A standard wrist-view diffusion policy is initially trained. This policy (`π_b`) predicts a nominal action chunk (`A_b`) in a wrist-relative action space, conditioned on the wrist image (`I_W`), the current wrist pose (`T_W` in the world frame), and a task instruction (`ℓ`). It is trained using a flow-matching objective towards the ground-truth demonstration action chunk (`A*`), establishing a stable local manipulation prior.

- **Stage 2: Independent Egocentric Residual Policy (π_r):** With the base policy frozen, an independent residual action expert is introduced.
 - The egocentric branch receives the head/egocentric image (`I_H`) and the wrist pose expressed *relative to the head frame* (`T_H<-W = (T_H)^-1 T_W`). This alignment ensures the pose input is consistent with the egocentric image.
 - This branch predicts a complete action candidate (`A_r`) in the same wrist-relative action space as `A_b`, along with a scalar gating value (`α`).
 - The final action (`A_hat`) is formed by a gated blending: `A_hat = (1 - α)A_b + αA_r`.
 - **Training Objective:** The egocentric branch is trained with a flow-matching objective for `A_r` towards `A*`. Additionally, a composed-action loss (`λ_act * ||(1-α)A_b + αA_r - A*||^2`) is used to train the gate `α` to blend `A_r` with `A_b`. A curriculum schedule is employed for `λ_act`, starting with only the residual branch loss and gradually increasing the weighting of the composed-action loss. This design allows the egocentric context to modify the action when local observations are ambiguous while preserving stable wrist-view control in ordinary states. Crucially, GERP does not model head camera motion as an action output, making it compatible with deployment using a fixed, calibrated egocentric camera.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EgoGuide and GERP was conducted on real-world manipulation tasks using a Flexiv Rizon 4 robot, assessing improvements in data collection utility and policy robustness.

#### 4.1. Comparison on Online Data Curation

Experiments were conducted comparing unguided data collection with EgoGuide-guided collection, using a wrist-only policy (`π_0.5`) for evaluation.

- **Performance and Efficiency:** EgoGuide demonstrably improved policy performance and reduced the quantity of required data. For example, on the "Pepper Sorting" task, using 200 demonstrations collected with EgoGuide raised the success rate from 10% to 50% compared to unguided collection. EgoGuide also achieved comparable success rates with approximately 50% fewer demonstrations (e.g., reaching 50% success on Pepper Sorting with 200 EgoGuide demos vs. over 400 unguided demos). These gains were also observed in long-horizon tasks. While real-time guidance added an average of 4.3 seconds per sample, the overall time-to-success scaling curve remained more efficient than unguided collection. Mixing EgoGuide data into a regular dataset consistently improved performance, indicating higher data quality.
- **Dataset Distribution:** Visualization of camera image features (e.g., CLIP wrist-image features) indicated that EgoGuide-collected datasets covered a larger region of the feature space, suggesting greater diversity. Quantitatively, EgoGuide improved feature variance by 3%–5% across different camera views and feature models (CLIP and DINOv2).
- **Partial Demonstration:** The option to start data collection from intermediate task states significantly increased spatial coverage and prevented trajectories from converging to similar later-stage configurations, thereby enhancing data diversity.

#### 4.2. Egocentric Residual Policy Evaluation

GERP was compared against a "Wrist Only" baseline (default UMI setting) and a "Wrist+Ego Direct" baseline (direct input of both camera views to the policy). All policies were evaluated on a robot with a fixed egocentric camera.

- **Improved Robustness:** GERP consistently improved performance (both success rate and task progress score) over the "Wrist Only" baseline. For instance, on "Pepper Sorting," GERP achieved an 80% success rate and 87.5% task progress score with 400 demonstrations, outperforming "Wrist Only" (75% SR / 77.5% TPS). Similar improvements were observed across "Pick Cube," "Garlic Storage," and "Rubik’s Cube."
- **Advantages over Direct Integration:** The "Wrist+Ego Direct" approach sometimes led to degraded performance. This was attributed to the moving egocentric view mismatching policy pretraining and potentially introducing distractions during training. In contrast, GERP utilized egocentric observations as a gated residual cue, leveraging their contextual information without directly integrating them into the primary control loop in a potentially disruptive manner.
- **Gating Behavior:** Analysis of the gating value during policy execution showed that it increased when the target object was occluded or absent from the wrist camera's field of view, indicating that the egocentric branch became more influential when local observations were ambiguous.
- **Robustness to Camera Placement:** GERP exhibited greater stability and robustness to variations in fixed egocentric camera placements compared to the "Wrist+Ego Direct" baseline, maintaining higher success and progress scores across different camera configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EgoGuide system and the Gated Egocentric Residual Policy (GERP) represent an advancement in the field of robot learning from human demonstrations, particularly for robot-free data collection paradigms like UMI.

The primary significance of EgoGuide lies in its ability to address critical bottlenecks in data efficiency and quality that currently limit the scalability of UMI-style data collection. By providing **online, visual-geometric data coverage guidance** to human demonstrators, EgoGuide reduces the collection of redundant data and encourages the exploration of novel and diverse states. This directly translates to **reduced human collection effort** for training effective robot policies, making robot-free data collection more competitive with, and in some cases more efficient than, traditional teleoperation systems. The support for **partial demonstrations** further enhances dataset diversity by capturing crucial intermediate and late-stage task variations, which are often underrepresented in full-trajectory collections.

The introduction of the **Gated Egocentric Residual Policy (GERP)** is significant for improving policy robustness. By effectively integrating context from a head/egocentric camera as a gated residual input, GERP enables policies to perform robustly even under challenging conditions such as **visual occlusion or limited local observability**. This approach is notable for leveraging egocentric context without requiring the policy to learn or actively control head movements, simplifying robot deployment to a fixed egocentric camera setup. The robustness to varying fixed egocentric camera placements further enhances its practical applicability.

The combined contributions of EgoGuide and GERP have the potential for substantial impact:

- **Accelerated Robot Learning:** By making real-world demonstration data collection more efficient and the resulting datasets more informative, EgoGuide can accelerate the training of robust robot manipulation policies across a wider range of tasks and environments.
- **Enhanced Practicality of Robot-Free Collection:** The system mitigates key limitations of UMI, making it a more practical and scalable solution for collecting high-quality, diverse datasets, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for robot learning research and development.
- **Improved Policy Generalization:** The emphasis on data diversity and the incorporation of global context are expected to lead to policies that generalize better to unseen scenarios and exhibit greater resilience to real-world variations.
- **Applicability to Crowdsourcing:** The lightweight, real-time feedback and deterministic quality checks integrated into EgoGuide make it particularly suitable for crowdsourced data collection efforts. This enables non-expert users to contribute high-quality demonstrations more consistently, standardizing the collection protocol without requiring constant expert supervision.

In conclusion, this work contributes a comprehensive system for improving both the collection and utilization of robot-free demonstration data, offering a pathway toward more efficient and robust robot learning from human examples.
