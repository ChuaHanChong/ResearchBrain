# Visually Grounded Self-Reflection for Vision-Language Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VRRL, a reinforcement learning framework, enhances large vision-language models' ability to perform visually grounded self-reflection, allowing them to iteratively correct errors and significantly improve accuracy on out-of-distribution visual reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Large vision-language models (LVLMs) lack robust visually grounded self-reflection, struggling to effectively utilize visual feedback for error correction.
- A "modality gap" prevents LVLMs from adequately attending to relevant visual tokens or translating visual evidence into concrete, grounded corrections.
- Current LVLMs show poor generalization to complex or out-of-distribution (OOD) visual inputs, limiting their practical applicability.

## Method

- A two-stage training pipeline, **VRRL**, combines ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== for basic reflection structure with ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== for error recovery and generalization.
- The RL stage employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a composite reward function that includes a novel ==reflection reward== for iterative progress.
- Two new mechanisms are introduced: ==Random Turn Masking (RTM)== to optimize later refinement steps and ==Buffered Roll-In== to explicitly train models on recovering from diverse past failure states.

## Results

- VRRL consistently outperformed standard RL baselines and existing reflection methods, achieving **3-10%** absolute gains on most visual grounding OOD tasks and an average of **9.8%** improvement on spatial navigation OOD tasks.
- VRRL-trained models exhibited adaptive reflection, refining predictions over multiple iterations and achieving higher accuracy in later turns, especially for out-of-distribution examples, unlike single-turn baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effect of VRRL's components (Reflection Reward, RTM, and Buffered Roll-In), with the full model consistently yielding the highest OOD robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit reinforcement learning with multi-turn visual feedback is more effective than supervised fine-tuning for developing robust visually grounded self-reflection and out-of-distribution generalization.
> - The combination of Random Turn Masking and Buffered Roll-In is crucial, synergistically preventing single-turn collapse and enhancing the model's ability to recover from intermediate errors.
> - A well-designed reflection reward, providing partial credit for progress toward the target, is vital for incentivizing iterative improvements and effective generalization across diverse visual queries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research is situated within the field of large vision-language models (LVLMs), specifically addressing their capabilities in complex multimodal reasoning tasks that integrate textual and visual information. A recognized strength of textual reasoning models is their ability to generate chains of thought (CoT) and employ self-reflection—revisiting and correcting earlier decisions. However, the application of robust self-reflection in LVLMs remains an area with identified challenges.

Existing LVLMs often exhibit limitations in visually grounded self-reflection. Previous work suggests that a "modality gap" exists, where LVLMs may not adequately attend to relevant visual tokens or effectively translate visual evidence into concrete, grounded corrections. This issue is particularly pronounced when processing complex or out-of-distribution (OOD) images, which deviate significantly from the data distributions seen during pre-training. Consequently, current LVLMs often require additional post-training to develop effective visual self-reflection.

The broader landscape includes research on enhancing LVLM reasoning through various approaches:
- **Textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reflection:** Much prior work has focused on improving self-reflection through textual CoT reasoning. Models like VL-Rethinker augment training with selective sampling and forced rethinking, primarily relying on text-based reasoning. However, these methods can over-rely on textual information and may not effectively utilize direct visual feedback for corrections.
- **Thinking with Images and External Tools:** Another line of research involves augmenting VLMs with external tools (e.g., OCR, depth analysis, zooming, image segmentation) that provide new visual information across multiple turns. These approaches equip VLMs with the ability to make and integrate tool outputs into their reasoning process. However, their primary focus tends to be on incentivizing correct tool calls rather than developing the VLM’s intrinsic reflective capability to validate candidate answers based on tool outputs or intermediate visual feedback.

This work contributes to the landscape by specifically targeting the development of *visually grounded* self-reflection in LVLMs. It aims to bridge the identified gap by creating a training framework that enables models to iteratively refine predictions based on direct visual feedback from an environment, thereby improving robustness and generalization, especially for OOD scenarios. It differentiates itself from methods primarily focused on textual CoT or general tool-use by emphasizing the translation of visual feedback into grounded corrective behaviors through a reinforcement learning paradigm.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel reinforcement learning (RL) training framework, termed VRRL (Visual Reflection RL), designed to instill and enhance visually grounded self-reflection in large vision-language models (LVLMs). This framework aims to enable LVLMs to effectively revisit previous decisions, integrate visual feedback, and subsequently correct errors, particularly when confronted with out-of-distribution (OOD) visual inputs.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several identified limitations and challenges within existing LVLMs:

- **Under-developed Self-Reflection in LVLMs:** While large language models (LLMs) can use Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for self-reflection and error correction, this capability is less developed in their multimodal counterparts. Existing LVLMs often struggle to reason about the correctness of their candidate answers or intermediate solution steps with sufficient robustness.
- **Modality Gap and Lack of Visual Grounding:** A significant bottleneck is the "modality gap." LVLMs frequently fail to properly attend to relevant visual tokens during reflection, which impedes their ability to translate abstract textual feedback or internal reasoning into concrete, visually grounded corrections. This can lead to inaccurate or unrefined predictions, even if the model performs some form of textual self-correction.
- **Poor Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Images:** The challenge of visually grounded self-reflection is exacerbated for complex or OOD images. Models pre-trained on broad datasets may perform adequately on in-distribution data but degrade substantially when encountering visual inputs that differ significantly from their training distribution. This lack of OOD generalization limits the practical applicability of LVLMs in diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Need for Explicit Post-Training:** Current approaches, including zero-shot prompting or even methods that emphasize textual CoT reflection, have shown limited success in reliably eliciting effective visually grounded self-correction. This indicates a necessity for explicit post-training methodologies tailored to teach LVLMs how to use visual feedback for iterative refinement.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Accuracy:** By enabling LVLMs to perform visually grounded self-reflection, the researchers aim to improve the overall robustness of these models. This involves not only correcting errors but also determining when a prediction is sufficiently accurate to terminate the reasoning process. This iterative refinement process, guided by visual feedback, is posited to lead to higher accuracy, particularly in challenging visual reasoning tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to move beyond superficial or text-only self-reflection in LVLMs to a deeper, visually integrated form of self-correction that directly leverages environmental visual feedback to achieve more accurate and robust performance across a range of visual tasks, especially under distribution shift.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research proposes a two-stage training pipeline, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL), to instill and refine visually grounded self-reflection capabilities in LVLMs. This pipeline, termed VRRL, incorporates two novel mechanisms within the RL stage.

#### 3.1. Training Framework Overview

The training process consists of two sequential stages:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
 - **Objective:** To teach the model the fundamental structure of multi-turn visual feedback and basic error-correction behaviors.
 - **Dataset:** An offline dataset, `D_SFT`, is constructed. This dataset includes trajectories with both immediate correct answers (single-turn) and iterative corrections (multi-turn).
 - **Single-turn trajectories:** Aim to familiarize the model with the visual reasoning task.
 - **Multi-turn trajectories:** Teach the model the expected format for reflection, which involves processing visual feedback from the environment. The initial action (`a_0`) in multi-turn trajectories is intentionally designed to be erroneous, compelling the model to learn correction in subsequent turns.
 - **Loss Function:** Standard auto-regressive cross-entropy loss is optimized over the assistant's turns (`a_1, ..., a_T`), excluding `a_0`.

- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) with VRRL**
 - **Objective:** To explicitly train the model to recover from errors and generalize reflective capabilities to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.
 - **Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed.
 - **Reward Function:** A composite reward `R` is defined for a trajectory, integrating three components:
 1. **Format Reward (`r_fmt`):** A binary reward (1 or 0) for adhering to a valid tool-call output format. Invalid formats result in a total trajectory reward of 0.
 2. **Outcome Reward (`R_answer`):** A binary reward (1.0 or 0) for the correctness of the final answer based on a task-specific metric.
 3. **Reflection Reward (`R_refl`):** An improvement-based reward designed to encourage iterative convergence towards the target. It provides partial credit for improvements made during multi-turn inference, even if the final answer is incorrect. For visual grounding, it is based on the Euclidean distance to the target; for spatial navigation, it leverages a progress rate.
 - The final reward `R` is computed as `max(R_answer, R_refl)` if `r_fmt = 1`, otherwise `0`.

 - **Novel Mechanisms within RL:** VRRL introduces two complementary strategies:
 1. **Random Turn Masking (RTM):**
 - During on-policy rollout generation for a trajectory `τ` of length `T`, a random starting index `k` (from 1 to `T`) is sampled.
 - Policy-gradient updates are computed only for the suffix of the rollout, from turn `k` to `T`. Gradients for earlier turns (prefix `1` to `k-1`) are masked.
 - **Purpose:** This strategy trains the model to optimize returns from arbitrary intermediate states, including potentially erroneous ones, without inadvertently learning to *make* the initial mistakes. It implicitly prioritizes optimization of later refinement steps.
 2. **Buffered Roll-In:**
 - A replay buffer `B` stores previously generated prefixes of trajectories that terminated with an incorrect final answer (excluding the termination action). This captures diverse "mistake prefixes."
 - During RL training, a proportion of training batches (`1-ρ`) are constructed by sampling these stored prefixes from `B`. The policy then generates suffix completions for these prefixes.
 - **Purpose:** This mechanism directly exposes the model to a variety of past failure states, allowing it to explicitly learn to recover from them. As the policy improves, the buffer naturally accumulates more challenging failure modes, creating a self-paced curriculum.
 - **Combined Objective:** The total optimization objective is a weighted sum: `J_Total(θ) = ρJ_RTM(θ) + (1 − ρ)J_Buff(θ)`, where `ρ` is the mixing probability between RTM-applied on-policy rollouts and buffered roll-ins.

#### 3.2. Task Setups

VRRL is evaluated on two visual feedback environments:

- **Visual Grounding:**
 - **Task:** Localizing specific visual elements (e.g., coordinates of values) within images of tables or charts based on a natural language instruction.
 - **Environment Feedback:** After each coordinate proposal, the environment returns a 200x200 pixel crop centered at the proposed location, with a red marker indicating the prediction.
 - **Data:** Training and in-distribution (ID) tests use synthetic small arXiv-style tables. OOD evaluations are performed on: Large Tables (increased size), Cell Query (querying inner cells instead of headers), Bar Chart (transfer to bar chart images), and Scatter Plot (localizing labeled points).
 - **Reward:** Final answer correct if within Euclidean distance threshold (`δ_tol`=40px) or within bounding box for bar charts. Reflection reward uses a distance-based potential function.

- **Spatial Navigation (FrozenLake):**
 - **Task:** Predicting the shortest, valid path from a start to a goal on a grid-based map, avoiding holes and obstacles.
 - **Environment Feedback:** After each path proposal, the environment draws a segmented red line representing the predicted path on the map image.
 - **Data:** Training on smaller 3-5 grid maps; OOD evaluation on larger 6x6 and 7x7 maps.
 - **Warm-up:** Models are initially warm-started with direct-answer training on in-distribution examples without visual feedback.
 - **Reward:** Exact match (EM) for final path correctness. Reflection reward is based on the progress rate, measuring the ratio of consecutive correct steps.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presented several findings regarding the performance of LVLMs with and without the proposed VRRL framework across visual grounding and spatial navigation tasks.

#### 4.1. Limitations of Existing Approaches

- **Zero-shot prompting and Textual CoT:** Off-the-shelf LVLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) demonstrated low accuracy on precise spatial localization tasks in a zero-shot setting. Prompting these models to engage in multi-turn reflection provided negligible benefit, and in some cases, degraded performance. This was attributed to zero-shot models often repeating previous predictions without meaningful correction. Similarly, VL-Rethinker models, which are trained for textual CoT reflection, underperformed on OOD tasks, indicating that textual CoT alone does not reliably translate to visually grounded self-correction.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** SFT methods, including both Single-SFT (training on perfect single-turn trajectories) and Multi-SFT (training on a mixture of perfect and synthetic recovery trajectories), primarily taught in-distribution knowledge. While they could instill the *format* of reflection, they exhibited brittleness and limited generalization on OOD tasks, failing to develop robust error-correcting behaviors. Reflection Tuning, an SFT-based approach designed for visual feedback reflection, also showed limited OOD generalization compared to RL methods.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **RL for OOD Generalization:** The application of standard GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) substantially improved OOD performance over SFT-only methods. Even Single-SFT → GRPO, which is a single-turn RL approach, demonstrated notable gains on certain OOD tasks (e.g., Large Table accuracy for 3B and 7B models).
- **Multi-turn RL Gains:** Significant performance gains were observed when combining RL with a multi-turn reflection formulation (Multi-SFT → GRPO). This approach led to 3-25% absolute gains over its single-turn counterpart on various visual grounding OOD tasks for the 3B model (e.g., 78.6% on Large Table, 13.5% on Cell Query, 30.7% on Bar Chart, and 37.2% on Scatter Plot). This indicates that training models to leverage multi-turn interaction with visual feedback through RL is beneficial.

#### 4.3. Performance of VRRL

- **Superior OOD Generalization:** VRRL consistently outperformed standard RL baselines (Multi-SFT → GRPO) and existing reflection-oriented fine-tuning methods on OOD tasks.
 - For visual grounding, VRRL achieved 3-10% absolute gains on most OOD tasks while maintaining near-perfect in-distribution accuracy for both 3B and 7B model scales. The OOD average accuracy for the 7B model reached 78.4%, compared to 73.2% for Multi-SFT → GRPO.
 - For spatial navigation, VRRL improved multi-turn SFT by 13.3% on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 11.4% on Qwen3-VL-4B on OOD tasks, outperforming the second-best baselines by an average of 9.8% across two models.
- **Effective and Adaptive Reflection:** Analysis of reflection behavior showed that VRRL's performance gains stemmed from improved multi-turn correction. Unlike single-turn baselines, VRRL models refined their predictions over iterations. The models demonstrated adaptive reflection, terminating earlier on in-distribution examples but continuing to refine predictions for OOD tasks, achieving higher accuracy in later turns. This suggested that VRRL induced a more robust and visually grounded error correction capability.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to determine the individual and combined impact of VRRL's components (Random Turn Masking, Buffered Roll-In, and Reflection Reward).

- **Individual Component Impact:**
 - Adding Random Turn Masking (RTM) alone to Multi-SFT → GRPO yielded mixed results, sometimes slightly improving or decreasing performance on specific OOD tasks.
 - Adding Buffered Roll-In alone could cause the model to converge to primarily single-turn behavior, losing some multi-turn reflection capability. However, it still improved average single-turn performance compared to Single-SFT → GRPO by exposing the model to diverse intermediate states.
- **Complementary Nature of RTM and Buffered Roll-In:** The combination of RTM and Buffered Roll-In was found to be crucial. This combination resolved the single-turn collapse issue, restoring and enhancing multi-turn behavior, and improving OOD performance across various settings.
- **Importance of Reflection Reward:** The reflection reward was critical for achieving strong overall performance in both visual grounding and spatial navigation. It provided a dense shaping signal that incentivized models to iteratively move toward better states, even when the final answer remained incorrect. This was particularly beneficial for generalizing to unseen query types or environments.
- **Full VRRL Model:** The full VRRL model, integrating Reflection Reward, RTM, and Buffered Roll-In, consistently achieved the highest OOD robustness, demonstrating the synergistic effect of these components.

In summary, the findings indicate that explicit RL training is more effective than SFT for improving OOD generalization in visually grounded self-reflection, and VRRL's specific mechanisms of RTM, Buffered Roll-In, and a targeted reflection reward significantly enhance this capability.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of large vision-language models (LVLMs) and has potential implications for the development of more robust and adaptable AI systems.

- **Advancing Visually Grounded Self-Reflection:** The work establishes that multi-turn reflection, explicitly trained to leverage visual feedback, is an effective mechanism for improving the robustness and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization of LVLMs. This contrasts with previous findings that showed limitations in self-reflection elicited through prompting or purely textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. It highlights the importance of grounding corrective behaviors in visual evidence.
- **Novel Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The introduction of VRRL, an RL training framework incorporating Random Turn Masking (RTM) and Buffered Roll-In, provides a methodological advancement for training LVLMs. These components specifically address the challenge of learning from and recovering from diverse intermediate errors, which is crucial for iterative refinement processes in dynamic environments. RTM encourages optimization of later-stage corrections, while Buffered Roll-In ensures exposure to a variety of failure states for recovery practice.
- **Improved LVLM Robustness and Generalization:** By demonstrating substantial improvements in OOD accuracy across tasks like visual grounding and spatial navigation, the research addresses a known limitation of current LVLMs: their brittleness when confronted with inputs outside their training distribution. The ability of VRRL to generalize across variations in table size, query type, and visual domain suggests a move towards more general-purpose visual reasoning capabilities. This enhanced robustness is valuable for deploying LVLMs in real-world applications where visual inputs are inherently diverse and unpredictable.
- **Insights into Reward Design for Multimodal RL:** The effectiveness of the proposed reflection reward, which provides partial credit for progress, underscores the importance of carefully designed reward functions in multimodal reinforcement learning. Such shaping rewards can guide models toward desired iterative behaviors (like self-correction) more effectively than sparse binary rewards, particularly in complex tasks where immediate success is rare.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work positions multi-turn self-reflection as a key direction for future research in LVLMs. It suggests that developing models that can "think with images" and critically evaluate their own visual outputs is essential for higher-level cognitive visual reasoning. This could inspire further exploration into how VLMs can leverage environmental interactions, beyond static image analysis, to achieve more intelligent and autonomous behaviors in applications such as robotics, interactive data visualization, and complex human-computer interfaces.
- **Practical Applicability:** The tasks evaluated, such as visual grounding in charts and tables and spatial navigation, are relevant to practical applications where precise visual interpretation and iterative problem-solving are required. Improved performance on these tasks suggests potential for more reliable automated data analysis, assistive technologies, or interactive agents.
