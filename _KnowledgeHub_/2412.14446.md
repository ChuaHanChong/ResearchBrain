# VLM-AD: End-to-End Autonomous Driving through Vision-Language Model Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM-AD introduces a framework that supervises end-to-end autonomous driving models using vision-language model-generated reasoning during training, enhancing their ability to handle complex scenarios. This approach reduced the average L2 planning error for UniAD from 1.03m to 0.88m and its collision rate from 0.31% to 0.19%, while also improving closed-loop driving scores in CARLA.

## Problem

- End-to-end autonomous driving models often lack commonsense reasoning, struggling with 'long-tail' events that require advanced contextual understanding.
- Traditional E2E models rely on pattern mimicry from observed data, which limits their adaptability to novel or challenging situations.
- Directly integrating large vision-language models for real-time inference in autonomous vehicles faces high computational costs and challenges in translating high-level language outputs into precise control signals.

## Method

- **VLM-AD** employs a ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== as an auxiliary teacher during the training phase only, generating reasoning-based text and structured action annotations for existing driving datasets.
- The system captures VLM insights by projecting future ego trajectories onto single front-view images and posing both freeform reasoning questions (Q1) and structured action classification questions (Q2).
- These VLM-generated annotations are integrated into arbitrary E2E models via specialized ==auxiliary heads==, distilling knowledge through ==alignment and classification losses== without VLM inference at runtime.

## Results

- VLM-AD consistently improved L2 planning accuracy and reduced collision rates across state-of-the-art E2E baselines (UniAD, VAD, SparseDrive) on the nuScenes dataset; for UniAD, L2 error decreased by **14.6%** and collision rate by **38.7%**.
- In closed-loop evaluations on the CARLA Town05 benchmark, VLM-AD (integrated with VAD-Base) achieved the best performance, improving the Driving Score on long routes from **30.31** to **35.25** and Route Completion from **75.20%** to **84.14%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the reasoning feature (Q1-3) provided the most substantial contribution to reducing L2 planning error, validating the efficacy of deep reasoning supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-level commonsense reasoning can be effectively distilled from large VLMs into end-to-end autonomous driving models by using the VLM as a 'teacher' during training, circumventing runtime computational burdens.
> - Both freeform text annotations and structured action labels generated by VLMs provide valuable supervisory signals that enhance an E2E model's feature representations and driving performance.
> - Explicit reasoning features, derived from VLM-generated explanations, are a more significant contributor to reducing planning errors than structured action predictions alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving (AD) is rapidly evolving, with a significant push towards end-to-end (E2E) models that integrate perception, prediction, and planning into a single, unified framework. Recent E2E approaches like UniAD and VAD have shown impressive results by directly mapping sensor data to planned ego trajectories. However, a critical limitation of these traditional E2E models is their reliance on mimicking observed driving patterns in data, often optimized solely for trajectory supervision (sequences of points). This "pattern mimicry" approach struggles with "long-tail" events—rare but critical challenging scenarios that require advanced commonsense reasoning, a capability human drivers possess.

Concurrently, the emergence of large foundation models, such as large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), has revolutionized various AI domains due to their extensive world knowledge and reasoning capabilities. Researchers have begun exploring their integration into AD systems to overcome the reasoning gap. Initial attempts have directly incorporated LLMs or VLMs into the driving pipeline, leveraging their ability to provide action recommendations or scene understanding. However, these direct integration methods often face significant hurdles:
1. **Computational Cost:** They require extensive fine-tuning for domain-specific tasks and incur high inference-time overhead, making them impractical for real-time, resource-constrained autonomous vehicles.
2. **Output Translation:** Translating high-level language outputs (e.g., "turn left") into precise numerical control signals or trajectories remains a non-trivial challenge.

VLM-AD positions itself precisely at this intersection, aiming to harness the reasoning power of VLMs without inheriting their practical deployment drawbacks. Instead of direct integration, VLM-AD employs VLMs as "teachers" during the *training phase only*. This paradigm shifts the role of foundation models from active decision-makers in the vehicle to knowledge distillers, enriching the training data with high-level reasoning. This approach builds upon the concept of multi-task learning, where auxiliary tasks are used to inject additional supervisory signals and encourage the model to learn richer, more robust feature representations. Unlike previous works that use existing labels (like bounding boxes or trajectories) for contrastive learning with VLMs (e.g., VLP), VLM-AD generates *novel reasoning information* that is explicitly absent in standard driving datasets. By focusing on distilling knowledge rather than performing inference, VLM-AD offers a practical pathway to incorporating sophisticated reasoning into E2E AD systems, distinguishing it from both traditional E2E models and direct VLM integration methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of VLM-AD is to enhance the performance and robustness of end-to-end autonomous driving models by endowing them with human-like commonsense reasoning capabilities, particularly for handling challenging and diverse real-world scenarios.

The key motivations driving this research are:
- **Bridging the Reasoning Gap:** Existing E2E AD models, while proficient in pattern recognition from large datasets, fundamentally lack the underlying reasoning processes that human drivers employ. This deficiency manifests as degraded performance in complex, long-tail events that demand contextual understanding and adaptive decision-making. The goal is to move beyond mere imitation of driving patterns to capturing the *rationale* behind those patterns.
- **Overcoming Data Annotation Limitations:** Manually annotating detailed reasoning information for driving datasets is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and susceptible to inconsistencies and subjectivity. This makes scaling up reasoning-rich datasets nearly impossible, hindering the training of more intelligent AD systems.
- **Addressing Practical Constraints of Foundation Models:** While LLMs and VLMs possess powerful reasoning abilities, their direct integration into AD pipelines for real-time inference is often impractical due to high computational demands, latency, and the complexity of translating symbolic language commands into precise control signals.
- **Improving Feature Representations:** The core hypothesis is that by providing explicit reasoning supervision, even if auxiliary, the E2E model will learn richer, more semantically meaningful feature representations that implicitly capture the "why" behind driving actions, leading to better generalization and planning.
- **Enhancing Interpretability:** Incorporating structured action labels provides an additional benefit of making the model's decision-making more interpretable, which is crucial for safety-critical systems like autonomous vehicles.

In essence, VLM-AD seeks to leverage the advanced reasoning of VLMs as a scalable, cost-effective "teacher" during training to instill a deeper understanding of driving scenarios into E2E models, ultimately leading to safer and more reliable autonomous driving without burdening the inference pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLM-AD proposes a novel two-component framework that integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as auxiliary teachers into arbitrary end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving pipelines. The method consists of a VLM-based annotation component and specially designed auxiliary heads for knowledge distillation during training.

#### 3.1. VLM Text Annotation

This component leverages a VLM to automatically generate rich, reasoning-based text annotations for existing driving datasets. The choice of VLM for this task is GPT-4o, known for its high performance and extensive world knowledge.

- **Visual Input:** To provide the VLM with adequate context while managing complexity, the ego vehicle's *future trajectory* is projected onto a *single front-view image*. This design choice addresses challenges observed with multi-view images (similar output quality but higher complexity) and sequences of frames (VLMs struggle with temporal continuity and ego identity). The projected trajectory explicitly communicates temporal movement information to the VLM.
- **Freeform Reasoning Annotation (Q1):** Open-ended questions are posed to the VLM to elicit unstructured, high-dimensional language information. A detailed context (C1) is provided, instructing the VLM to focus on the camera image and surrounding context for reasoning, while using the red line to represent the future trajectory. The questions (Q1-1, Q1-2, Q1-3) prompt for:
 - Current actions of the ego vehicle (A_c).
 - Predicted future actions (A_f).
 - Reasoning behind these actions (A_r).
 These responses generate rich, freeform text annotations capturing the VLM's understanding of the driving scenario and its rationale.
- **Structured Action Annotation (Q2):** To obtain more explicit, interpretable action labels, a second set of questions (Q2-1, Q2-2, Q2-3) is designed with predefined, structured action sets:
 - Control actions: {go straight, move slowly, stop, reverse}.
 - Turn actions: {turn left, turn right, turn around, none}.
 - Lane actions: {change lane to the left, change lane to the right, merge into the left lane, merge into the right lane, none}.
 This produces single, structured action annotations (A_control, A_turn, A_lane), which are valuable for supervising interpretable action predictions.

#### 3.2. Auxiliary Heads

These heads are designed to integrate the VLM-generated annotations as supervisory signals into any arbitrary E2E model, typically following its planning module and utilizing the model's learned ego feature (f_ego).

- **Annotation Encoding:**
 - Freeform text annotations (A1) are converted into fixed-size feature vectors (y1) using an off-the-shelf language model, specifically CLIP-ViT-B/32.
 - Structured action annotations (A2) are encoded as one-hot labels (y2).
- **Text Feature Alignment Head (for Q1):** This head distills knowledge from the freeform reasoning annotations.
 - Three learnable text queries (q_c, q_f, q_r) corresponding to current action, future action, and reasoning are initialized.
 - Each query interacts with the ego feature (f_ego) via a Multi-Head Cross-Attention (MHCA) block, where the query acts as the attention query, and f_ego serves as key and value.
 - The updated queries are concatenated with f_ego and passed through an MLP layer to produce output features (f̂1).
 - A knowledge distillation-inspired cross-entropy loss (L_align) is used, aligning the temperature-normalized distributions of the output features (f̂1) with the VLM-generated text features (y1). This allows for a flexible alignment, capturing feature similarity rather than strict identity.
- **Structured Action Classification Head (for Q2):** This head supervises the model with explicit action labels.
 - Three learnable action queries (q_control, q_turn, q_lane) are initialized.
 - Similar to the text alignment head, each query interacts with f_ego via an MHCA block.
 - Updated queries are concatenated with f_ego, passed through an MLP, and then a Softmax layer to generate action predictions (f̂2).
 - Standard cross-entropy loss (L_action) is applied to classify the predicted actions against the one-hot encoded VLM labels (y2).

#### 3.3. Auxiliary Loss

The overall training loss is a weighted sum of the planning loss (from the base E2E model) and the two auxiliary losses: L = λ1 * L_align + λ2 * L_action. This joint training encourages the E2E model to learn richer feature representations that are aligned with the VLM's reasoning and action decisions, without requiring the VLM during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VLM-AD demonstrates significant improvements across various evaluations, highlighting its effectiveness in enhancing end-to-end autonomous driving performance.

#### 4.1. Open-Loop Planning Results (nuScenes Dataset)

The method was integrated with three state-of-the-art E2E baselines: UniAD, VAD, and SparseDrive, and compared against VLP (Vision Language Planning). The key findings include:

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** VLM-AD consistently outperforms all baselines in L2 planning accuracy (lower is better) and collision rate (lower is better).
 - When applied to **UniAD**, VLM-AD significantly reduces the average L2 planning error and average collision rate. For instance, the original UniAD had an average L2 error of 1.03m and a 0.31% average collision rate. With VLM-AD (using both Q1 and Q2 annotations), these improved to 0.88m and 0.19% respectively.
 - Similar improvements were observed for **VAD-Base** (average L2 error from 0.62m to 0.48m) and **VAD-Tiny** (average L2 error from 0.64m to 0.55m).
 - For **SparseDrive-B**, VLM-AD also reduced the average L2 planning error from 0.58m to 0.52m, while maintaining the model's already low collision rates due to its inherent collision-aware design.
- **Superiority over VLP:** VLM-AD also surpasses VLP, a related method that uses VLM features for contrastive learning, in both L2 planning error and collision rate across different base models.
- **Impact of Reasoning Information:** The experiments show that using freeform reasoning annotations (Q1) alone yields better results than structured action labels (Q2) alone, validating the value of richer, unstructured reasoning information for enhancing driving performance. The combination of both Q1 and Q2 often provides the best overall results.

#### 4.2. Closed-Loop Planning Results (CARLA Town05 Benchmark)

To evaluate real-world interactive driving, VLM-AD was integrated into the VAD framework and tested in the CARLA simulator.

- **Enhanced Driving Performance:** VLM-AD achieved the best performance across all closed-loop metrics, including Driving Score (DS) and Route Completion (RC).
 - On "Town05 Short" routes, VAD-Base achieved DS of 64.29 and RC of 87.26; with VLM-AD, these improved to 67.78 DS and 88.56 RC.
 - On "Town05 Long" routes, the gains were even more significant: VAD-Base had 30.31 DS and 75.20 RC, while VLM-AD boosted these to 35.25 DS and 84.14 RC.
- **Robustness in Long-Horizon Tasks:** These results demonstrate VLM-AD's effectiveness and robustness in realistic interactive scenarios, indicating its ability to reason and act reliably over longer driving horizons.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the contributions of individual sub-questions within Q1 (current action, future action, reasoning) showed that each component positively contributes to overall performance. Notably, the *reasoning feature* (Q1-3) was identified as the most significant contributor to reducing the L2 planning error, underscoring its crucial role in enhancing driving intelligence. Further analysis on loss functions and model designs confirmed the robustness of the chosen approach. The evaluation of VLM annotation quality through questionnaires also confirmed high consistency and accuracy, despite occasional imperfections, which the two independent annotation questions help mitigate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLM-AD represents a significant advancement in end-to-end autonomous driving research, offering a practical and effective method to infuse higher-level reasoning into data-driven models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Human-AI Reasoning Gap:** The core contribution is enabling E2E AD models to learn beyond mere pattern imitation. By distilling rich, human-like commonsense reasoning from VLMs, VLM-AD helps models develop a deeper understanding of driving scenarios, addressing the long-standing challenge of handling complex, long-tail events where purely data-driven approaches falter. This moves AD systems closer to human-level intelligence in decision-making.
- **Practicality for Real-World Deployment:** A key strength of VLM-AD is its "teacher-student" paradigm, where the VLM acts as a supervisor *only during training*. This eliminates the need for large, computationally expensive VLMs during real-time inference, making the method highly practical and deployable in resource-constrained autonomous vehicles without incurring additional latency or computational overhead. This is a crucial differentiator from direct VLM integration approaches.
- **Scalable and Cost-Effective Annotation:** VLM-AD provides a scalable solution to the labor-intensive and costly problem of manual annotation for reasoning information. By leveraging powerful VLMs like GPT-4o, the generation of detailed textual explanations and structured action labels becomes automated and efficient, enabling the creation of rich datasets that were previously infeasible to obtain.
- **Enhanced Model Robustness and Performance:** The consistent and significant improvements demonstrated in both open-loop (planning accuracy, collision rate on nuScenes) and closed-loop (route completion, driving score on CARLA) evaluations highlight the method's ability to create more robust and performant AD systems. This enhanced robustness is critical for safety-critical applications.
- **Improved Interpretability:** The structured action labels generated by the VLM and learned by the auxiliary heads provide a degree of interpretability to the E2E model's decision-making. Being able to predict human-understandable actions (e.g., "turn left," "stop") in conjunction with trajectory planning adds a layer of transparency that is vital for validation, debugging, and regulatory compliance in autonomous driving.
- **Generalizable and Plug-and-Play Framework:** VLM-AD is designed as a plug-and-play module, compatible with various existing E2E AD frameworks (demonstrated with UniAD, VAD, SparseDrive). This generalizability means it can readily augment and improve a wide range of current and future E2E systems without requiring fundamental architectural redesigns, facilitating its adoption across the research community and industry.

**Potential Future Impact:**
The principles introduced by VLM-AD could be extended beyond planning to other crucial modules of autonomous driving, such as perception and prediction, leading to a more holistic enhancement of the entire AD pipeline. Further research could explore more diverse and challenging driving datasets, and develop a broader range of VLM-driven questions to extract even more nuanced knowledge. Ultimately, VLM-AD pushes the frontier of making autonomous vehicles not just capable of *performing* driving tasks, but of *understanding* and *reasoning* about them, paving the way for safer, more reliable, and ultimately, more widely accepted self-driving technology.
