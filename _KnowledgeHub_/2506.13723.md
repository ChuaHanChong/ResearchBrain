# OTFusion: Bridging Vision-only and Vision-Language Models via Optimal Transport for Transductive Zero-Shot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> OTFusion unifies Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-only Foundation Models (VFMs) using Optimal Transport to enhance Transductive Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL). The training-free framework achieves an average accuracy improvement of nearly 10% over the CLIP baseline across 11 datasets, often surpassing training-based methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at semantic alignment but struggle with fine-grained visual distinctions and domain shifts in Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL).
- Vision-only Foundation Models (VFMs) provide rich, discriminative visual features but inherently lack semantic grounding for direct ZSL.
- Existing methods to combine these models are often computationally expensive, require extensive training, or lack broad generalizability across different foundation models.

## Method

- OTFusion proposes a ==training-free framework== that models both VLM and VFM outputs as probability distributions over class labels.
- A ==Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)== is fitted to VFM features to generate a visual prior, while VLM predictions provide a semantic prior.
- ==Optimal Transport== is employed to fuse these visual and semantic priors, creating a unified probabilistic representation through an ==alternating optimization scheme== with mutual reinforcement.

## Results

- The framework achieves an average accuracy improvement of nearly **10%** over the CLIP baseline across **11** diverse benchmark datasets.
- OTFusion frequently outperforms state-of-the-art training-based ZSL methods by over **10%** on average, demonstrating high efficiency without additional training.
- The method exhibits fast convergence (typically within **5 iterations**) and robust performance across a range of hyperparameters, particularly the entropy regularization strength.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs and VFMs offer complementary strengths: VLMs provide semantic structure, while VFMs offer fine-grained visual discrimination.
> - Optimal Transport effectively bridges these diverse foundation models by operating in the probability space, allowing for training-free and model-agnostic integration.
> - A joint optimization that mutually reinforces GMM parameter updates with the evolving Optimal Transport plan ensures that visual clusters are not only coherent but also semantically aligned.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Foundation models, such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP and Vision-only Foundation Models (VFMs) like DINO, have demonstrated considerable capabilities in zero-shot classification across various domains, including natural images, medical pathology, and remote sensing. These models leverage large-scale pre-training to learn generalizable representations, enabling direct application to unseen categories without task-specific supervision.

Research in this area generally follows two main directions: the development of novel foundation models and the enhancement of existing models' zero-shot transfer abilities. The latter category includes methods such as prompt engineering, label propagation, and distribution alignment. While these approaches have shown performance improvements, they typically focus on optimizing or adapting a single foundation model.

This paper addresses a perceived gap in the existing literature by exploring the complementary strengths of *multiple* foundation models. The authors observe that different types of foundation models possess distinct characteristics and limitations. For instance, VLMs often over-rely on class-level textual priors, potentially overlooking fine-grained visual details, whereas VFMs provide rich visual features but lack inherent semantic alignment with textual labels. Furthermore, the performance of various VLMs can differ substantially across datasets due to their specific pre-training regimens.

The integration of multiple models is a recognized strategy in machine learning to enhance robustness and performance. This work situates itself within this context by proposing a framework that systematically combines the outputs of diverse foundation models for zero-shot classification. The chosen approach, Optimal Transport (OT), is a mathematical framework for comparing and aligning probability distributions, which has found increasing application in machine learning tasks such as domain adaptation, cross-modal alignment, and multi-label classification. This paper extends the application of OT to the multi-model ensemble problem in zero-shot classification, offering a training-free and self-adaptive mechanism for model integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this work stems from two primary observations regarding the current state and limitations of foundation models in zero-shot classification:

1. **Complementary Strengths and Weaknesses of VLMs and VFMs:**
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP, are designed for cross-modal alignment between visual and textual information. This capability makes them suitable for zero-shot classification by directly linking image features to class names. However, their reliance on large-scale, often noisy, image-text pairs can lead to an over-reliance on class-level textual priors. This can hinder their ability to capture subtle, fine-grained visual cues essential for distinguishing visually similar categories, as demonstrated by their clustering accuracy on certain datasets (e.g., StanfordCars, Flower102, Pets). Their performance may also degrade in domain-specific scenarios (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing) where the pre-training data distribution significantly differs from the target domain.
 - **Vision-only Foundation Models (VFMs)**, such as DINO, focus purely on visual pre-training, learning generic and transferable representations from unlabeled image collections. They are noted for producing discriminative visual features that capture fine-grained details, which are crucial for many classification tasks. However, VFMs inherently lack direct semantic alignment with textual category labels, making them less straightforward to apply in zero-shot classification tasks that require semantic understanding.
 The authors identify that these two types of models offer distinct yet valuable information: VLMs provide semantic alignment, while VFMs offer rich visual discrimination. This suggests that combining them could leverage their respective strengths while mitigating individual weaknesses.

2. **Performance Variability Across Different VLMs and Datasets:**
 - The paper observes that the zero-shot classification performance of different VLMs varies considerably across various datasets. This variability is attributed to differences in their pre-training settings, architectures, or the specific data used for training. Relying on a single VLM, therefore, might lead to suboptimal performance on certain tasks or domains. This motivates an approach that can dynamically account for these performance discrepancies.

These observations lead to the central research question guiding this work: "Rather than relying solely on a single foundation model, can we enhance zero-shot classification by integrating multiple foundation models to fully exploit their complementary strengths?"

To address this question, the key objective is to propose a training-free ensemble framework, named SOTA (Self-adaptive Optimal TrAnsport), capable of integrating the outputs of multiple diverse foundation models (both VFMs and VLMs). The framework aims to learn a self-adaptive transport plan that automatically balances the contributions of individual models and effectively leverages their complementary information, without requiring manual weighting or access to ground-truth labels for adaptation. The authors also aim for a prior-free and black-box compatible solution, which means it should operate without needing to fine-tune the foundation models or requiring access to their internal weights.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SOTA (Self-adaptive Optimal TrAnsport), is a training-free ensemble framework for zero-shot classification that integrates multiple foundation models using optimal transport.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting

The work addresses zero-shot classification, where the goal is to categorize unlabeled images (`D_u = {x_i}^N_{i=1}`) using only a set of category names or descriptions as side information. Two inference settings are considered:
- **Transductive inference:** `D_u` constitutes the entire test set, and the model can leverage its global distributional structure for predictions.
- **Inductive inference:** `D_u` is treated as training data to learn a classifier that generalizes to unseen test instances from the same distribution.
The method assumes access to multiple foundation models, including both VFMs and VLMs.

#### 3.2. Optimal Transport (OT) Preliminary

OT provides a framework to compare and align probability distributions `p` and `q` by finding a transport plan `T` that maps mass from `p` to `q` with minimum cost. The cost is defined by a matrix `C` representing dissimilarities between source and target elements. The regularized variant of OT, which includes an entropy regularization term `ϵH(T)`, is used to ensure smoother and more computationally tractable solutions. The Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm efficiently solves this regularized problem.

In SOTA, `p` represents the distribution over `D_u` in the feature space, and `q` corresponds to the semantic distribution over candidate classes. The cost matrix `C` encodes pairwise semantic dissimilarities between samples and class labels.

#### 3.3. Deriving Probability Distributions

SOTA first converts the outputs of each foundation model into a categorical probability distribution `P ∈ R^(N×K)`, where `N` is the number of samples and `K` is the number of classes.

#### 3.3.1. Deriving Probability Distributions from VFMs

For VFMs, which lack direct semantic alignment, visual features `v_i = f_v(x_i)` are extracted for each image `x_i`. To obtain class probabilities, a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with parameters `Θ = {π_k, μ_k, Σ_k}^K_{k=1}` is fitted to these visual features. The posterior probability `p(y=k|v_i)` is then calculated using the GMM, forming the matrix `P ∈ R^(N×K)`. If `V_1` VFMs are used, `V_1` such distributions `{P_v}^V_1_{v=1}` are obtained. In the inductive setting, the learned GMM parameters can induce a classifier.

#### 3.3.2. Deriving Probability Distributions from VLMs

For VLMs, an image encoder `f_I` extracts visual features `v_i` and a text encoder `f_T` extracts text embeddings `t_j` for each class `s_j`. The similarity between image features and text embeddings is computed (e.g., via cosine similarity), followed by softmax normalization over classes to yield a probability distribution `P̂_ij` for each image. This forms the matrix `P̂ ∈ R^(N×K)`. If `V_2` VLMs are used, `V_2` distributions `{P̂_v}^V_2_{v=1}` are obtained.

#### 3.4. Self-adaptive Optimal Transport

The core of SOTA lies in integrating these derived probability distributions. A naive approach would be a weighted OT problem, `min_T ⟨T, Σ λ_v C_v + Σ μ_v Ĉ_v⟩`, where `λ_v` and `μ_v` are fixed weights. However, determining these weights in a zero-shot setting without ground truth labels is problematic.

SOTA addresses this by replacing the linear Frobenius inner product `⟨T, P⟩` with its squared form `⟨T, P⟩^2` in the objective function. The objective becomes:
`max_{T∈Π(p,q)} Σ_{v=1}^{V_1} ⟨T, P_v⟩^2 + Σ_{v=1}^{V_2} ⟨T, P̂_v⟩^2 + ϵH(T)`

This quadratic formulation adaptively adjusts the contribution of each model during optimization. Models whose probability distributions `P_v` or `P̂_v` are more "aligned" with the current transport plan `T` (resulting in a higher `⟨T, P⟩^2`) will implicitly receive greater influence in subsequent updates.

#### 3.5. Joint Optimization Framework (Extension)

To overcome the potential poor alignment between VFM-derived visual distributions (learned without semantic guidance) and VLM-derived semantic distributions, SOTA proposes a joint optimization framework. This framework simultaneously learns the GMM parameters `Θ` and the transport plan `T`:
`max_{T∈Π(p,q),Θ} Σ_{v=1}^{V_1} ⟨T, P_v(Θ)⟩^2 + Σ_{v=1}^{V_2} ⟨T, P̂_v⟩^2 + ϵH(T)`
Here, `P_v(Θ)` explicitly denotes the dependence of the VFM-induced posterior matrix on the GMM parameters. This coupling allows `T` to be shaped by both visual and semantic assignments while also guiding `Θ` updates, fostering visual coherence and semantic consistency.

#### 3.6. Optimization

The joint optimization problem (Eq. 8) is nonlinear due to the quadratic terms. SOTA employs an iterative Minorization-Maximization (MM) scheme for optimization. At each iteration `k`, a surrogate function `G^(k)(T, Θ)` is maximized, which is derived using the first-order Taylor expansion of the quadratic terms.

The optimization alternates between two steps:
1. **Update T given Θ^(k):** Adaptive weights `λ_v^(k)` and `μ_v^(k)` are computed based on the previous iteration's transport plan `T^(k)` and probability distributions `P_v^(k)`. A classical entropic-regularized optimal transport problem is then solved for `T^(k+1)` using the Sinkhorn algorithm, which involves computing a kernel `K = exp(S^(k)/ϵ)` and iteratively scaling to satisfy marginal constraints.
2. **Update GMM parameters Θ given T^(k+1):** The updated transport plan `T^(k+1)` is treated as soft assignments of samples to classes. An M-step update (similar to the Expectation-Maximization algorithm) is performed to update the GMM parameters (means `μ_c`, covariances `Σ_c`, and mixture weights `π_c`). Subsequently, the posterior matrices `P_v(Θ^(k+1))` are recomputed (E-step).

This iterative process, guaranteed to converge to a stationary point, is outlined in Algorithm 1. In the transductive setting, the final `T` serves as the prediction. In the inductive setting, the learned GMM parameters and adaptive coefficients are used to predict on new test data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of SOTA was evaluated across 26 benchmarks spanning natural images, medical pathology, and remote sensing domains under both transductive and inductive inference settings.

#### 4.1. Transductive Setting

- **Overall Performance and Effectiveness of Transductive Methods:**
 - Transductive approaches, including SOTA, consistently showed significant improvements in prediction accuracy compared to baseline CLIP models (Table 1). This is attributed to the utilization of visual feature distributions for label correction.
 - SOTA demonstrated higher performance gains, particularly when integrated with stronger visual encoders like DINOv3. For instance, `CLIP-1+DINOv3` achieved an average accuracy of 77.4%, outperforming the best competitor (GTA-CLIP) by approximately 2.9% and the baseline `CLIP-1` by 12.2% on natural image datasets.
 - On remote sensing datasets, SOTA achieved an average accuracy of 81.5%, surpassing the best individual model (GeoRSCLIP) by 17.0% and the best TransCLIP variant (T-GeoRSCLIP) by 5.3% (Table 2).
 - Similarly, on medical pathology datasets, SOTA yielded an average accuracy of 83.9%, outperforming the best individual model (MUSK) by 17.6% and the best TransCLIP variant (T-MUSK) by 7.9% (Table 3).
- **Complementarity Across Different VLMs:** The experiments confirmed that different VLMs have varied performance across datasets. SOTA, by integrating multiple foundation models, consistently surpassed individual models, demonstrating the benefit of leveraging their complementary strengths for zero-shot generalization. For example, `CLIP-1` (ViT-B/16) generally outperformed `CLIP-2` (RN50), and SOTA effectively combined their outputs.

#### 4.2. Inductive Setting

- SOTA achieved comparable or superior performance on most datasets when compared to state-of-the-art test-time adaptation (TTA) methods (Table 4). For instance, `CLIP-1+DINO(v2+v3)` achieved an average accuracy of 77.6%, which is higher than `COSMIC*` (73.3%) and competitive with other strong TTA methods.
- The framework allows for sample-wise inference at test time with only a few GMM parameters and adaptive coefficients, making it computationally efficient compared to TTA methods like DMN and COSMIC, which require maintaining cached sequences during testing, leading to higher storage consumption.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of VFM Introduction:** Introducing VFMs significantly improved performance compared to using only VLMs (Only-`P_v`), highlighting the crucial role of high-quality visual features in rectifying VLM predictions and addressing the modality gap. Clustering accuracy analysis (Figure 6) further supported that VFMs (e.g., DINOv3, CONCH visual branch) often produce more discriminative visual representations than VLMs, which SOTA effectively leverages.
- **Importance of Self-adaptive Optimal Transport:** The self-adaptive weighting scheme in SOTA consistently yielded better average performance than a fixed-weight strategy (Non Self-adaptive). This improvement was particularly noticeable when there was a significant performance disparity between the contributing models (e.g., CLIP vs. MUSK on medical datasets, Table 5), confirming the adaptive mechanism's role in balancing model contributions.
- **Importance of Joint-learning:** The joint optimization of the transport plan `T` and GMM parameters `Θ` (SOTA) outperformed the decoupled learning approach (Disjoint-learning). This suggests that the mutual reinforcement between these components, where `T` guides GMM updates and refined `Θ` reshapes `T`, leads to better alignment and more robust fusion.

#### 4.4. Convergence Analysis

The MM algorithm employed for SOTA's optimization demonstrated rapid convergence, typically reaching a stable state within five iterations and full convergence within ten iterations across natural image datasets (Figure 5). This, combined with the reliance on basic matrix operations, suggests computational efficiency.

#### 4.5. Computational Efficiency Analysis

A comparison with other methods (Table 6) indicated that SOTA achieves competitive efficiency while delivering stronger accuracy. It offers a balance between effectiveness and efficiency, particularly against graph-based approaches (ECALP, TransCLIP) which have higher overhead, and faster but less accurate methods (ADAPT).

#### 4.6. Model Subset Analysis

Experiments on remote sensing and medical datasets with different model subsets (Table 7) showed that SOTA consistently improved performance even when integrating models with weaker cross-domain performance (e.g., CLIP) with domain-specific models. This indicates that SOTA does not rely on a single dominant model but can cooperatively leverage diverse model outputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SOTA framework presents a contribution to zero-shot classification with several significant implications:

- **Novelty in Multi-Model Integration:** This work introduces a systematic investigation into the complementary strengths of diverse foundation models (VLMs and VFMs) for zero-shot classification. By proposing a self-adaptive optimal transport framework, it establishes a novel direction for enhancing zero-shot transfer ability through the intelligent integration of multiple, heterogeneous models. This approach extends beyond merely ensembling predictions, actively aligning underlying distributions.

- **Practicality and Accessibility:** SOTA is a training-free and prior-free framework. It does not require fine-tuning of the foundation models or access to ground-truth labels for adaptation, making it suitable for real-world scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable. Importantly, its design allows it to enhance the zero-shot performance of "black-box" models, including those accessible only via APIs, as it operates solely on their output probability distributions and visual features. This broadens the applicability of advanced foundation models.

- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The extensive evaluation across diverse domains—natural images, medical pathology, and remote sensing—validates SOTA's generalizability and robustness. The consistent and substantial accuracy improvements over individual models demonstrate its capacity to effectively leverage complementary information in varied data distributions and domain-specific challenges.

- **Efficiency:** The iterative Minorization-Maximization (MM) optimization algorithm for SOTA is computationally efficient, converging rapidly within a few iterations. This contributes to the practicality of deploying the framework for inference without significant computational overhead, especially when compared to some graph-based label propagation methods.

- **Addressing Foundation Model Limitations:** By specifically targeting the observed limitations of VLMs (over-reliance on textual priors, struggle with fine-grained cues) and VFMs (lack of semantic alignment), SOTA offers a method to synthesize their strengths. The joint optimization framework further refines this by ensuring visual coherence and semantic consistency, leading to more reliable predictions.

- **Future Research Directions:** The authors identify future work directions, including developing more sophisticated ensemble learning algorithms to exploit latent semantic complementarities among VLMs and exploring advanced multi-view clustering strategies for VFMs. These directions could further enhance the capabilities of multi-model zero-shot classification and build upon the foundational work presented by SOTA.

In summary, SOTA provides an effective, efficient, and versatile method for improving zero-shot classification performance by integrating multiple foundation models, addressing key limitations of single-model approaches and opening new avenues for research in model fusion.
