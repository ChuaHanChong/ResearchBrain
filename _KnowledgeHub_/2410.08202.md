# Mono-InternVL: Pushing the Boundaries of Monolithic Multimodal Large Language Models with Endogenous Visual Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Mono-InternVL introduces a monolithic multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that achieves competitive performance against leading modular MLLMs. The model demonstrates substantial improvements in deployment efficiency and effectively addresses catastrophic forgetting through its unique architecture and training strategy.

## Problem

- Monolithic MLLMs have historically underperformed modular counterparts due to visual processing constraints and complex training.
- Existing methods for integrating visual capabilities into pre-trained LLMs often lead to catastrophic forgetting of learned linguistic knowledge.
- Modular MLLMs, while performant, incur deployment inefficiencies like higher first-token latency and increased memory usage due to their two-component architecture.

## Method

- Integrates visual encoding and language decoding into a single LLM using a ==multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE)== structure with modality-specific FFNs and static routing.
- Employs an ==Endogenous Visual Pre-training (EViP)== strategy, a three-stage progressive learning process that utilizes ==delta tuning== to optimize only visual parameters, preserving the LLM's core language abilities.
- Incorporates a ==visual tokenizer== for direct image patchification, supporting high-resolution inputs and integrating a "thumbnail" for global context within the monolithic architecture.

## Results

- Mono-InternVL-2B surpasses all existing monolithic MLLMs across **16** benchmarks and achieves comparable or superior multimodal performance to leading modular MLLMs, including its counterpart InternVL-1.5-2B.
- The model reduces first token latency by up to **67%** and increases overall throughput by up to **31%** compared to modular MLLMs, validating its architectural efficiency.
- NLP capabilities are retained, with Mono-InternVL-2B performing similarly to its base LLM, demonstrating effective mitigation of catastrophic forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Delta tuning, which freezes the pre-trained LLM parameters while optimizing visual experts, is critical for integrating visual knowledge without catastrophic forgetting.
> - The multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) architecture allows for efficient, modality-specific processing and capacity expansion within a unified model.
> - Progressive endogenous visual pre-training effectively guides the model to learn from low-level concepts to high-level semantics, leading to robust multimodal capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen explosive growth, driven by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs). This paper addresses a critical bifurcation within this landscape: the distinction between "modular" and "monolithic" MLLMs.

**Modular MLLMs** currently dominate the research landscape, represented by models like GPT-4V, Gemini, BLIP, LLaVA, and the group's own InternVL. These models typically operate by coupling a separate, pre-trained visual encoder (such as CLIP-ViT) with an LLM via an intermediate connector. While highly successful in achieving superior multimodal performance, this architecture presents several challenges:
- **Visual Processing Limitations:** The visual encoder might constrain the LLM's full visual reasoning potential.
- **Deployment Inefficiencies:** Separate components can lead to higher memory footprint and increased latency, particularly for the first token generation.
- **Complexity:** Balancing the capacities and integration of distinct LLMs and Large Vision Models (LVMs) adds architectural complexity.

In response to these issues, an alternative paradigm, **Monolithic MLLMs**, has emerged, with examples including Fuyu-8B, EVE-7B, SOLO, Chameleon, and Emu3. Monolithic MLLMs aim for a more integrated design, embedding visual perception directly within the LLM. This approach promises:
- **Design Simplicity:** A unified architecture reduces overall complexity.
- **Deployment Efficiency:** Eliminating a separate visual encoder can lead to faster inference and lower resource requirements.

However, existing monolithic MLLMs have historically struggled to match the performance of their modular counterparts. This performance gap is primarily due to fundamental challenges in their training:
- **Unstable Optimization:** Training from scratch can be prohibitively expensive and unstable.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Continuously pre-training a pre-existing LLM with visual data often leads to "catastrophic forgetting," where the model's pre-trained language capabilities are undermined in the process of learning visual knowledge. This issue arises because vision and language modeling often share architecture, leading to conflicts in optimization.

Mono-InternVL directly tackles these core limitations of monolithic MLLMs. It seeks to bridge the performance gap with modular models while preserving the inherent advantages of a monolithic design. The paper strategically leverages the concept of **delta tuning**, where only a subset of parameters is updated, a technique previously explored to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in other contexts. Furthermore, the integration of a **multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** structure builds upon existing research in efficient, modality-aware model architectures (e.g., VLMo, BEiT-3, MoMa), but innovatively applies it to embed *visual experts* directly into the LLM, a unique contribution within the monolithic MLLM space. By focusing on stable visual knowledge acquisition and preserving linguistic capabilities, Mono-InternVL positions itself as a critical advancement for the monolithic MLLM paradigm, attempting to redefine its potential and practical applicability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the Mono-InternVL project is to **significantly advance the capabilities of monolithic Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to achieve performance comparable to, or even exceeding, state-of-the-art modular MLLMs, while retaining the inherent advantages of a unified, single-model architecture in terms of simplicity and deployment efficiency.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific challenges within the MLLM landscape:

- **Overcoming Performance Discrepancy:** Modular MLLMs currently hold a significant performance lead over monolithic ones. The primary motivation is to close this gap, demonstrating that a monolithic design does not necessitate a compromise on multimodal understanding and reasoning capabilities.
- **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting:** A critical challenge in continuously pre-training LLMs for multimodal tasks is the tendency to catastrophically forget the rich linguistic knowledge acquired during their initial language-only pre-training. The authors are motivated to develop a training strategy that stably integrates visual knowledge without undermining the foundational language abilities of the LLM.
- **Ensuring Stable Optimization:** Existing pre-training approaches for monolithic MLLMs either require immense computational resources (training from scratch) or suffer from optimization instability when adapting pre-trained LLMs. The motivation here is to devise a more robust and stable pre-training paradigm.
- **Enhancing Deployment Efficiency:** Monolithic MLLMs inherently offer advantages in design simplicity and deployment efficiency due to their unified structure. The authors aim to capitalize on these benefits, specifically targeting a reduction in first-token latency and an increase in overall inference throughput, which are crucial for real-time applications and resource-constrained environments.
- **Maximizing Visual Scalability:** The project seeks to develop an architecture and pre-training strategy that allows the MLLM to progressively and effectively learn from vast amounts of visual data, from noisy low-level concepts to high-level semantics, thereby maximizing its visual perception and understanding.
- **Novel Architectural Paradigm:** By embedding visual experts directly into a pre-trained LLM using a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) mechanism, the authors aim to introduce a novel and effective architectural blueprint for monolithic MLLMs, distinguishing it from prior attempts that might rely on simpler linear projections or external visual tokenizers without deep architectural integration.

In essence, the motivation is to prove that monolithic MLLMs can be both high-performing and efficient, thereby "pushing the boundaries" of this architectural paradigm and opening new avenues for future MLLM development and deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Mono-InternVL revolves around a novel monolithic architecture coupled with an innovative three-stage Endogenous Visual Pre-training (EViP) strategy, designed to address the challenges of unstable optimization and catastrophic forgetting.

#### 3.1. The Monolithic Architecture

Mono-InternVL integrates visual encoding and language decoding into a single Large Language Model (LLM) through a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) structure. The foundational LLM used is **InternLM2-1.8B**.

- **Visual and Textual Embeddings:**
 - **Visual Tokenizer:** Unlike modular MLLMs that rely on external visual encoders, Mono-InternVL employs a lightweight module to directly patchify input images. An `MLP(PatchEmbed(I) + PE)` is used, where `PatchEmbed` takes an image `I` and generates patches with a stride of 28x28 pixels. Learnable positional embeddings (PE) are added, similar to InternVL-1.5. A "thumbnail" is also incorporated to provide global visual context. An MLP layer projects these visual patches into the LLM's `d`-dimensional embedding space. This design allows processing high-resolution images (up to 8 million pixels, or 10,240 patches).
 - **Textual Tokenizer:** Standard textual tokenizer from the base LLM (InternLM2-1.8B).
 - The multimodal input `x_m` is formed by concatenating the visual and textual embeddings.

- **Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) Structure:**
 - The core idea is to embed a new visual parameter space into the pre-trained LLM. This is achieved by transforming the standard Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) within each LLM layer into an MMoE structure.
 - **Static Routing:** Visual tokens are routed to a dedicated `FFNv` (visual expert), while textual tokens are processed by `FFNt` (textual expert). This "static routing" ensures modality-specific processing.
 - **Initialization:** Crucially, `FFNv` is initialized from the pre-trained `FFNt` to leverage existing learned representations.
 - **Advantages:** This design enables specific optimization for visual tasks (`FFNv`) while preserving the pre-trained language knowledge by potentially freezing `FFNt`. It also enhances the model's capacity for vision-and-language modeling with negligible additional inference cost, as only one expert is activated per token type.

#### 3.2. Endogenous Visual Pre-training (EViP)

EViP is a novel, progressive learning strategy designed to maximize the benefits of Mono-InternVL's visual experts while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. It adheres to a **delta tuning** principle, where most of the pre-trained LLM parameters (`θ`) are frozen, and only the newly introduced visual parameters (`θ_v`, including patch embedding and visual experts) are optimized.

- **Objective:** `arg min_Δθ L(F_llm(x_m; θ, θ_v), ŷ)`, where `L` is the auto-regressive loss.
- **Progressive Learning Stages:**
 - **S1.1: Concept Learning:**
 - **Goal:** Learn fundamental visual concepts (objects, shapes).
 - **Data:** 922 million noisy image-text pairs (Laion-2B, Coyo-700M).
 - **Task:** Generative learning with a simple prompt: "provide a one-sentence caption for the image".
 - **Parameters Optimized:** Patch embedding and visual experts (`FFNv`).
 - **Parameters Frozen:** Entire LLM (including textual experts `FFNt` and multi-head attentions).
 - **Image Patches:** Max 1,280 patches for efficiency.
 - **S1.2: Semantic Learning:**
 - **Goal:** Capture higher-level visual semantics, such as relationships and world knowledge.
 - **Data:** 258 million synthetically generated, high-quality captions (produced by InternVL2-8B), which are cleaner and more descriptive than raw noisy captions.
 - **Task & Optimization:** Same as S1.1.
 - **Image Patches:** Max 1,792 patches.
 - **S1.3: Alignment Learning:**
 - **Goal:** Align learned visual knowledge with the requirements of downstream tasks.
 - **Data:** 143 million task-specific samples from InternVL-1.5 pre-training data, including image captioning, object detection, and Optical Character Recognition (OCR) data.
 - **Task:** Generative learning using task-specific prompts from InternVL-1.5.
 - **Parameters Optimized:** Patch embedding, visual experts (`FFNv`), *and multi-head attention layers*. (The base LLM's `FFNt` remains frozen). This stage selectively unfreezes MHA to achieve better vision-language alignment.
 - **Image Patches:** Max 3,328 patches.

#### 3.3. Instruction Tuning (S2)

After EViP, the model undergoes supervised instruction tuning to accommodate diverse multimodal tasks and instructions.
- **Data:** Around 5 million bilingual instructions covering VQA, multimodal dialogue, mathematics, knowledge, etc. (detailed in Appendix A.1).
- **Optimization:** The *entire model* (all parameters, including `FFNt` and `FFNv`) is optimized in this stage.
- **Image Patches:** Max 6,400 patches to handle high-resolution inputs for fine-grained tasks.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- Mono-InternVL is built upon **InternLM2-1.8B**.
- The visual experts (`FFNv`) account for 1.2 billion parameters.
- A dynamic high-resolution strategy, similar to InternVL-1.5, is used for input images.
- Training involves approximately 16 days for EViP and 1 day for instruction tuning on 256 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

This comprehensive approach combines a novel architectural design with a structured, progressive training strategy to effectively integrate visual and linguistic capabilities within a single, efficient model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results extensively validate the effectiveness and efficiency of Mono-InternVL, particularly in pushing the performance boundaries of monolithic MLLMs.

- **Superiority over Existing Monolithic MLLMs:** Mono-InternVL-2B (with 1.8 billion activated parameters) consistently and significantly outperforms all existing monolithic MLLMs across 16 multimodal benchmarks.
 - For instance, it achieves **+80 points over Emu3 on OCRBench** and an average gain of **+2.5% over Emu3**.
 - Compared to EVE-7B (HD), Mono-InternVL demonstrates up to **15.4% average gains on VQA tasks**, despite EVE-7B using a larger 7 billion parameter model and high-quality pre-training data.
 - This confirms Mono-InternVL's robust visual perception, text recognition, and reasoning capabilities, especially in complex visual scenarios.

- **Comparability with Leading Modular MLLMs:** A groundbreaking finding is that Mono-InternVL-2B achieves **comparable multimodal performance** to leading modular MLLMs, including its modular counterpart, InternVL-1.5-2B (2.2 billion parameters). It even surpasses InternVL-1.5-2B by an average of +0.8% on the 9 MLLM benchmarks.
 - While it shows some inferiority on specific high-resolution benchmarks like InfoVQA (e.g., -12.4% vs. InternVL-1.5), this is attributed to relatively shallower model depth limiting visual encoding ability.
 - This result is a significant step towards demonstrating that monolithic MLLMs can indeed compete with modular architectures, a claim previously difficult to support.

- **Preservation of NLP Capabilities:** Mono-InternVL effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting. On four common NLP benchmarks (MMLU, CMMLU, AGIEval, MATH), Mono-InternVL-2B retains performance similar to its base LLM, InternLM2-Chat (1.8B). In contrast, other monolithic MLLMs like EVE-7B, despite having more parameters, exhibit inferior NLP performance, underscoring the success of Mono-InternVL's delta tuning and MoE approach in preserving language knowledge.

- **Efficiency Gains in Inference:** Mono-InternVL demonstrates superior deployment efficiency compared to InternVL-1.5.
 - It achieves a remarkable **reduction in first token latency (TTFT) by up to 67%**.
 - Overall throughput (TPS) is correspondingly **increased by up to 31%**.
 - These results validate the architectural advantage of eliminating a separate visual encoder, making Mono-InternVL more suitable for latency-sensitive applications.

- **Effectiveness of Endogenous Visual Pre-training (EViP):**
 - Ablation studies confirm the benefits of the progressive learning strategy. Performance consistently improves across the concept, semantic, and alignment learning stages (S1.1, S1.2, S1.3) as the pre-training data size and quality increase.
 - S1.2 (semantic learning) alone already outperforms existing modular MLLMs like MM1 on COCO Captions (+13.8 CIDEr), indicating its effectiveness in capturing fundamental multimodal relationships.
 - S1.3 (alignment learning) is particularly crucial for VQA and MME, providing significant gains by incorporating task-related knowledge and enabling selective optimization of multi-head attention layers.

- **Impact of Delta Tuning and MoE:**
 - Ablation studies show that delta tuning (freezing the LLM while optimizing visual experts) is critical. Full tuning of the LLM yields the worst performance, and even full tuning with visual experts is less effective than delta tuning. This confirms that preserving pre-trained language knowledge is paramount and delta tuning with visual experts is an effective strategy.
 - Unfreezing attention layers in the alignment learning stage consistently improves performance, highlighting the importance of optimizing cross-modal attention for better vision-language alignment.

- **Qualitative Insights from Attention Maps:** Visualizations of attention maps reveal an interesting dynamic:
 - Shallow layers primarily exhibit locality in visual encoding, similar to Convolutional Neural Networks, with minimal interaction between modalities.
 - As layers deepen, inter-modality attention weights increase, indicating a gradual fusion of visual and textual information, providing valuable insights into how monolithic MLLMs process multimodal input.

In summary, Mono-InternVL achieves state-of-the-art performance among monolithic MLLMs, closing the gap with leading modular MLLMs, while offering substantial efficiency benefits and effectively addressing the catastrophic forgetting problem through its innovative architecture and pre-training strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mono-InternVL represents a significant leap forward for the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in validating the potential of the monolithic architecture. Its contributions have several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

- **Redefining the Monolithic Paradigm:** For the first time, Mono-InternVL demonstrates that monolithic MLLMs can achieve performance levels comparable to, and in some aspects even superior to, state-of-the-art modular MLLMs. This fundamentally shifts the perception of monolithic designs, proving they are not inherently inferior in capability but rather require sophisticated architectural and training strategies. This breakthrough opens up monolithic MLLMs as a highly competitive and viable path for future research and development.

- **Enhanced Deployment Efficiency:** The substantial reduction in first-token latency (up to 67%) and increased throughput (up to 31%) makes Mono-InternVL significantly more practical for real-world applications. This efficiency is critical for:
 - **Real-time Interaction:** Faster responses in conversational AI, virtual assistants, and interactive systems.
 - **Resource-Constrained Environments:** Deployment on edge devices, mobile platforms, or in scenarios with limited computational resources, where the overhead of separate encoders in modular systems is prohibitive.
 - **Cost Reduction:** Lower inference costs for large-scale deployments due to improved throughput.

- **Robust Solution to Catastrophic Forgetting:** The novel MMoE architecture combined with the delta tuning principle and progressive EViP strategy offers a powerful mechanism to integrate visual knowledge into pre-trained LLMs without compromising their extensive language abilities. This addresses a long-standing challenge in continuous pre-training, enabling more stable and effective adaptation of powerful LLMs to multimodal tasks.

- **Innovative Architectural Design:** The seamless integration of visual experts via a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) mechanism directly into the LLM provides a novel and effective blueprint. This approach could inspire new architectural explorations, moving beyond simple connector layers to more deeply embedded, modality-aware components within unified models.

- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **Scalability:** The demonstrated data scalability of EViP suggests that further performance gains are likely with even larger and more diverse multimodal datasets.
 - **Architectural Insights:** The qualitative analysis of attention maps, revealing modality locality in shallow layers and gradual fusion in deeper layers, offers crucial insights for designing more optimized and intrinsically multimodal transformer blocks.
 - **High-Resolution Challenges:** Identifying specific areas where Mono-InternVL lags (e.g., high-resolution InfoVQA) also points to clear directions for future work, such as developing deeper models or more advanced visual encoding strategies within the monolithic framework.

- **Advancing Open Science:** The commitment to releasing the code and model (https://github.com/OpenGVLab/Mono-InternVL) is a valuable contribution to the AI community. It enables researchers worldwide to replicate, build upon, and further innovate on the Mono-InternVL foundation, accelerating the overall progress in multimodal AI.

In conclusion, Mono-InternVL not only pushes the boundaries of monolithic MLLMs but also challenges the prevailing notion that modular designs are inherently superior. By demonstrating both competitive performance and significant efficiency, it provides a compelling case for a unified architectural future in multimodal AI, with profound implications for research, development, and practical deployment.
