# SONG: A Photorealistic 3D Gaussian Simulation Platform for Benchmarking Social Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> SONG introduces a photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting simulation platform and benchmark, SONG-Bench, for vision-based social navigation, leveraging semantics-driven human dynamics. Evaluations on SONG-Bench revealed that existing methods perform poorly in high-fidelity social environments, while fine-tuning on SONG's data improved real-world robot navigation.

## Problem

- Existing 3D navigation simulators lacked photorealistic visual fidelity and realistic, dynamic human avatars, causing a "photometric domain gap" that hinders sim-to-real transfer.
- Pedestrian behaviors in prior platforms often relied on simplistic rules or repetitive motion capture, failing to capture complex human intent and natural movement.
- Evaluation protocols for vision-based social navigation were insufficient in complexity and lacked multi-dimensional metrics to rigorously assess policy performance.

## Method

- **SONG** employs ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== for rendering photorealistic static scenes and dynamic human avatars, addressing visual fidelity limitations.
- Semantics-driven pedestrian behaviors are synthesized by leveraging ==Large Language Models (LLMs)== for high-level intent generation and generative models for natural, continuous full-body motions.
- The platform integrates with ==Isaac Lab== for robot embodiment and physics, providing a comprehensive benchmark (**SONG-Bench**) with stratified difficulty and multi-dimensional evaluation metrics.

## Results

- Zero-shot evaluation on SONG-Bench revealed that leading vision-based navigation policies achieved success rates below **22%** even in "Easy" settings, struggling with basic safety (over **75%** failure rate due to collisions).
- Policies trained on more real-world data, like NoMaD, consistently outperformed others, highlighting that data distribution is more impactful than model scale for social navigation.
- Fine-tuning a policy (NavDP) with SONG's data led to improved real-world navigation success rates on a physical robot (e.g., from **10%** to **20%** in a single-pedestrian scenario), demonstrating the platform's sim-to-real fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art vision-based social navigation policies perform poorly on high-fidelity, dynamic human environments, underscoring that the problem is far from solved.
> - Existing models demonstrate a foundational deficit in basic safety (collision avoidance and recovery) before progressing to nuanced social compliance.
> - The distribution and realism of training data, particularly real-world trajectories, are more crucial for robust policy performance and real-world transfer than the sheer scale of the model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robot navigation has been extensively studied, encompassing goal-directed paradigms, vision-language navigation (VLN), and autonomous exploration. While these areas have advanced robots' abilities to reason about spatial layouts and achieve specific goals, many approaches have not sufficiently addressed the complexities of operating in environments densely populated with dynamic humans. The field of social navigation specifically focuses on enabling robots to interact with humans in a socially compliant manner, which includes respecting personal space, avoiding disruptive maneuvers, and maintaining predictable behavior.

Historically, social navigation research began with simplified 2D environments, often relying on strong assumptions about pedestrian states. However, the field has progressed toward vision-based settings, where a robot must derive socially compliant behaviors directly from onboard visual observations. This shift necessitates simulation platforms that can accurately replicate the visual fidelity and dynamic human behaviors of the real world.

Existing simulation platforms for navigation can be broadly categorized, each with limitations regarding vision-based social navigation:
- **2D Simulators**: These platforms, such as those described in, abstract environments to top-down geometry, where navigation policies do not process visual input. They are suitable for benchmarking modular navigation pipelines that rely on abstracted pedestrian states but are incompatible with vision-based learning.
- **3D Platforms for Static Navigation**: Platforms like iGibson and Habitat offer rich visual observations but typically feature static environments without dynamic human avatars. This limits their utility for social navigation tasks, often reducing them to semantic goal finding.
- **3D Platforms with Pedestrians**: A limited number of 3D platforms, including Habitat 3.0, Social-HM3D, and HabiCrowd, incorporate pedestrian agents. However, these platforms often fall short in several aspects:
 - **Visual Fidelity**: Many rely on mesh-based rendering, which produces synthetic appearances and a noticeable "photometric domain gap" compared to real-world camera observations, hindering sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Pedestrian Behavior**: Pedestrians often follow hand-crafted rules or randomized paths toward arbitrary waypoints, lacking semantic intent or plausible long-horizon activity sequences.
 - **Avatar Motion**: Motions are frequently assembled from looping motion capture (mocap) clips, resulting in stiff, repetitive, and unnatural gaits.

The research presented in this paper, SONG, addresses these identified gaps by developing a high-fidelity simulation platform that combines photorealistic rendering, semantics-driven pedestrian dynamics, and natural full-body motion synthesis. The aim is to provide a more faithful and rigorous testbed to advance vision-based social navigation research, particularly by bridging the existing sim-to-real gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce SONG (SOcial Navigation platform), a high-fidelity simulation environment powered by 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), along with a comprehensive benchmark called SONG-Bench, for evaluating vision-based social navigation policies.

The motivation for developing SONG stems from several limitations observed in existing simulation platforms, which hinder progress in developing robust vision-based social navigation agents:
- **Lack of Visual Fidelity for Vision-Based Agents**: Current simulation platforms frequently struggle to replicate the photorealistic appearance of real-world environments and dynamic human agents. This discrepancy, referred to as the "photometric domain gap," can lead to policies trained in simulation performing poorly when transferred to physical robots. Vision-based navigation policies require visually consistent and high-quality observations to learn effectively.
- **Unrealistic Pedestrian Behaviors**: Many simulators animate pedestrians using simplistic rule-based models, randomized movements, or repetitive motion capture loops. These approaches fail to capture the complexity, semantic intent, and natural fluidity of human movement in social spaces. Robots trained with such simplified human models may not generalize well to real-world interactions where human behaviors are dynamic and context-dependent.
- **Inadequate Benchmarking Protocols**: Existing benchmarks often lack the necessary complexity, diversity, and multi-dimensional metrics required for a thorough evaluation of social navigation policies. There is a need for a protocol that stratifies difficulty, ensures meaningful encounters with pedestrians, and assesses performance across effectiveness, safety, and social compliance.
- **Bottleneck for End-to-End Vision-Based Methods**: While end-to-end vision-based navigation, which directly maps raw visual inputs to control outputs, mirrors human-like navigation, its progress is significantly constrained by the high cost of real-world data collection and the substantial sim-to-real gap in available simulators. A high-fidelity simulation platform is essential to overcome this bottleneck by providing a scalable and realistic training and testing environment.

To address these motivations, SONG was designed with specific objectives:
- **Photorealistic Rendering**: To utilize 3D Gaussian Splatting for representing both static scenes and dynamic human avatars, ensuring visual fidelity that closely mirrors real-world observations.
- **Semantics-Driven Social Dynamics**: To generate pedestrian behaviors that are plausible and intent-aware. This involves leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level semantic intent generation and generative models like Kimodo for synthesizing continuous, natural full-body motions, moving beyond rigid mocap loops.
- **Rigorous Evaluation Framework**: To curate SONG-Bench, a set of evaluation episodes with tiered complexity and a multi-dimensional metric suite encompassing effectiveness, safety, and social compliance, providing a robust tool for benchmarking state-of-the-art navigation policies.
- **Extensibility**: To provide versatile interfaces for users to customize scenes, pedestrian profiles, and navigation tasks, fostering further research and development.

Ultimately, the goal is to provide a faithful and rigorous testbed that can facilitate the development of the next generation of vision-based social navigation agents capable of operating safely and compliantly in human-populated environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SONG operates through a three-stage pipeline designed to provide a high-fidelity, extensible platform for vision-based social navigation.

#### 3.1. Gaussian Asset Preparation

The initial stage focuses on preparing the fundamental visual assets:
- **Static Scenes**: The platform incorporates 1,000 high-quality 3DGS scenes, adopted from the SAGE-3D style. Each scene includes a 3DGS representation for photorealistic rendering, a lightweight geometry proxy for simulator interactions (e.g., collision detection in Isaac Lab), and a top-down semantic/traversability map used during episode construction. All these representations are registered within a shared metric frame, facilitating seamless integration of dynamic elements.
- **Animatable Avatars**: A pool of 500 diverse human Gaussian avatars is curated. This pool is sourced from two methods:
 - 195 subjects from DNA-Rendering, which are capture-based and provide high-quality geometry and appearance, requiring approximately six hours of optimization per identity.
 - 305 image-conditioned reconstructions via LHM, generated from diverse full-body human snapshots, taking about two minutes per identity. This method prioritizes appearance diversity while maintaining compatibility with the Gaussian renderer.
 All avatars are stored with metadata, aligned to the scene ground, and cached for efficient use.

#### 3.2. Social Episode Synthesis

This stage populates the static environments with dynamic, socially plausible human behaviors:
- **Semantic Intent Generation**: A Large Language Model (LLM), Gemini 3.1 Flash, is utilized to generate high-level pedestrian intents. Given a scene's semantic map, object labels, and traversability information, Gemini outputs structured intents specifying plausible activities (e.g., navigating from reception to a workstation) and their semantic start and goal locations. These raw intents are then filtered based on traversability and occupancy constraints to ensure feasibility.
- **Multi-avatar Trajectory Planning**: The refined semantic intents are converted into pedestrian root trajectories using a Recast/DetourCrowd-based planner. This planner inherently supports multi-agent collision avoidance among pedestrians, generating joint trajectories that include position and heading for each avatar over time. Infeasible or overly crowded configurations can trigger replanning or removal of trajectories.
- **Motion and Avatar Binding**: While root trajectories define *where* pedestrians move, Kimodo synthesizes the full-body motion (how the body moves). Kimodo generates full-body motion sequences from the planned root trajectories, conditioned on route, heading, duration, and activity descriptors. Post-processing steps include aligning the generated AMASS-format root motion with the planned trajectory, temporal resampling, smoothing to reduce jitter, and applying foot-ground corrections to mitigate floating or sliding artifacts. Finally, identities are sampled from the avatar pool, and their Gaussian avatars are bound to these natural, synthesized motions, creating animated avatar-Gaussian caches for each episode.

#### 3.3. Closed-Loop Agent Runtime

This stage integrates the dynamic Gaussian scenes with a robot simulation environment to enable closed-loop interaction and evaluation:
- **Isaac Lab Integration**: SONG is integrated with Isaac Lab, which provides robot embodiment, physics simulation, and advanced collision handling. At each simulator step, Isaac Lab queries the SONG runtime for observations and state updates.
- **Observation Generation**: The SONG runtime evaluates the social episode at the current timestamp, composes the static scene Gaussians with the posed avatar Gaussians, and renders synchronized RGB-D observations and state signals from the robot's viewpoint. The on-demand rendering from the queried pose allows for versatile use in training, closed-loop rollouts, and evaluation.
- **Collision Handling**: Collision detection is implemented via two paths:
 - Static scene collisions are resolved by Isaac Lab using imported scene geometry or collision proxies.
 - Dynamic avatar collisions are detected directly on the Gaussian avatars. The runtime counts the number of avatar Gaussian centers falling inside the robot's collision volume. A collision is flagged if this count exceeds a robot-specific threshold (η_col_ = 5). This approach distinguishes static from dynamic contacts while providing a unified interface for collision queries.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol (SONG-Bench)

SONG-Bench establishes a rigorous framework for evaluating vision-based social navigation:
- **Task Definition**: The core task is vision-based point-goal social navigation, requiring an agent to reach a destination while avoiding collisions with static obstacles and dynamic pedestrians, and maintaining social compliance. Pedestrians follow prescribed, non-reactive trajectories.
- **Episode Generation and Difficulty Grading**: 500 diverse social navigation episodes are generated across 500 scenes. Start and goal positions are sampled to ensure pedestrian encounters, using four modes: Co-directional, Counter-directional, Encounter, and Random. Episodes are stratified into Easy, Medium, and Hard (200, 200, and 100 episodes, respectively) based on a composite difficulty score derived from path length, significant turns, human avatar encounters (within 3m), and corridor width.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Performance is assessed across three dimensions:
 - **Effectiveness**: Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Success weighted by Time (STL), Average Time (AvgT), and Time-out (TO).
 - **Safety**: Collision Rate with Static Obstacles (CR_obs) and Collision Rate with Human Avatars (CR_hum).
 - **Social Compliance**: Personal Space Compliance (PSC, ratio of steps with nearest-avatar distance ≥ 0.5m), Minimum Human Avatar Distance (MinD), and Jerk (JK, RMS of temporal curvature rate) for trajectory smoothness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research involved two primary experimental components: zero-shot benchmarking of existing navigation policies on SONG-Bench and fine-tuning a policy for real-world transfer.

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Benchmarking

A systematic zero-shot evaluation was conducted on leading pre-trained visual navigation methods, including NavDP, iPlanner, SocialNav, and NoMaD. Human teleoperated trajectories were used to establish an empirical upper bound for performance. Key findings emerged from this evaluation:
- **Vision-based Social Navigation is Far from Solved**: All tested baselines demonstrated a significant performance drop on SONG-Bench. Success rates (SR) remained below 22% even in "Easy" scenarios and approached zero in "Hard" settings. This indicates that current state-of-the-art methods struggle to navigate effectively in high-fidelity environments with complex human dynamics, highlighting a substantial performance gap compared to human teleoperation (e.g., 95.5% SR in Easy). The photorealistic visual cues and nuanced human behaviors introduced by SONG pose challenges that existing architectures have not yet overcome.
- **Safety Deficit Precedes Social Etiquette**: The results revealed that current models require fundamental improvements in basic safety before addressing complex social rules. All four baselines exhibited failure rates exceeding 75% in "Easy" scenarios due primarily to collisions with either human avatars or static obstacles. An ablation study conducted in avatar-free environments confirmed that high failure rates persisted even without dynamic human agents, indicating an inherent difficulty in mapping photorealistic observations to safe geometric spaces for obstacle avoidance. Additionally, current models largely lacked effective collision recovery mechanisms; agents typically became stuck after a collision rather than replanning or recovering. This suggests a limitation stemming from reliance on perfect expert demonstrations, which do not typically include "error-and-recovery" transitions.
- **Real-World Data Distribution Matters More Than Model Scale**: Among the imitation learning baselines (NoMaD, NavDP, SocialNav), NoMaD consistently achieved superior performance across all difficulty levels despite its architecture not being inherently more complex. The authors attribute this to NoMaD's training data distribution, which includes over 100 hours of real-world trajectories, compared to NavDP's reliance on simulation and SocialNav's combined approach. This suggests that SONG's photorealistic 3DGS rendering and LLM-driven pedestrian behaviors generate observations that more accurately mirror real-world distributions than conventional simulators. Notably, when evaluated in human-free environments, the performance gap between these methods largely diminished, indicating that the observed ranking in social scenarios stemmed from differences in their capacity to handle dynamic human agents rather than just scene geometry understanding. This finding positions dynamic human interaction as a critical bottleneck and validates SONG as a discriminative testbed for evaluating this capability.

#### 4.2. Fine-tuning and Real-World Transfer

To validate the physical-world fidelity of SONG's data, a real-world transfer experiment was conducted:
- **Fine-tuning NavDP**: The NavDP foundation model was fine-tuned using a curriculum data collection strategy. This data consisted of approximately 2,000 trajectories, combining human teleoperated data with high-quality trajectories generated by a fine-tuned Height model replayed within SONG.
- **Real-World Setup**: A Unitree Go2 quadruped robot equipped with an Intel Realsense D435i camera was used for real-world trials. The robot was assigned a goal 7 meters ahead, with state estimation from its onboard odometry. Two social settings were tested, each with 20 trials: a single pedestrian walking toward the robot, and two pedestrians approaching in a staggered formation.
- **Transfer Results**: Fine-tuning NavDP on the data curated from SONG-Bench resulted in an improved success rate in both real-world scenarios. The success rate increased from 10% to 20% in the single pedestrian scenario and from 5% to 10% in the two pedestrian scenario. While the absolute performance remained limited, these improvements indicate that the high-quality multi-modal data from SONG effectively contributes to better navigation in physical environments, thereby validating the platform's cross-domain fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SONG and SONG-Bench represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied AI, particularly for social navigation research. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancing Vision-Based Social Navigation**: SONG provides a high-fidelity simulation environment that addresses critical limitations of previous platforms, specifically concerning visual realism and complex human dynamics. By offering a testbed that closely mirrors real-world conditions, it directly supports the development and evaluation of vision-based navigation policies, which are crucial for robots operating in human-centric spaces.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: The use of 3D Gaussian Splatting for both static scenes and dynamic avatars, combined with semantics-driven pedestrian behaviors synthesized by LLMs and generative motion models, significantly enhances the photorealism and behavioral realism of the simulation. This improved fidelity is expected to reduce the sim-to-real gap, enabling policies trained in SONG to generalize more effectively to physical robots. The real-world transfer experiments with the Unitree Go2 robot provide initial evidence for this capability.
- **Standardized and Rigorous Benchmarking**: SONG-Bench offers a comprehensive and systematically organized benchmark. Its curated episodes with stratified difficulty levels and multi-dimensional evaluation metrics (effectiveness, safety, social compliance) provide a robust framework for comparing navigation policies. This standardization enables researchers to rigorously assess and quantify progress in social navigation, fostering transparent and reproducible research.
- **Diagnostic Insights for Future Research**: The systematic evaluation of representative baselines yielded critical insights into the current state of vision-based social navigation. Findings such as the persistent performance gap, the priority of safety over social etiquette, and the importance of real-world data distributions over model scale, provide clear diagnostic guidance. These insights can inform and prioritize future research directions, helping to focus efforts on fundamental challenges like robust obstacle avoidance, collision recovery, and learning from diverse, realistic human interaction data.
- **Enabling Data-Driven Development**: The platform includes tools for scene authoring, pedestrian scripting, and autonomous robot data collection. This ecosystem facilitates the generation of high-quality, multi-modal data at scale, which is essential for training data-hungry learning-based navigation policies.
- **Potential for Broader Applications**: The principles and techniques demonstrated in SONG, particularly the integration of 3DGS for dynamic environments and LLMs for behavioral synthesis, could extend beyond social navigation to other embodied AI tasks requiring high fidelity and realistic human-robot interaction.

In conclusion, SONG offers a foundational platform that addresses key bottlenecks in vision-based social navigation. By providing a more faithful and rigorous simulation environment, it is poised to significantly facilitate the development of more robust, safe, and socially compliant autonomous agents for deployment in diverse real-world settings.
