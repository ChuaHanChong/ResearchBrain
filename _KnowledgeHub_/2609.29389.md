# Robo-Harness K1: Harnessing Robot-Use Agents via Perception Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> Robo-Harness K1 turns perception into callable tools for a robot-use agent, letting a frozen vision-language model query calibrated depth, grounded regions with persistent identity anchors, and projected grasp hypotheses before choosing generic motions, with no depth encoder or action head trained. Gemini 3.7 Flash with K1 reaches 77.8% on matched LIBERO-PRO cases, beating an RGB-only Astra harness at 61.1%, and lifts Astra itself to 88.9%.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action models bolt learned action heads onto VLMs, which degrades the inherited vision-language representations and demands large demonstration coverage to generalize at all.
- Benchmarks beyond memorization show these action policies replay memorized trajectories and collapse once objects, layouts, or language instructions change.
- RGB-only robot-use agents must infer metric geometry from pixels, while adding depth as an input modality requires invasive architectural change and extra alignment training.

## Method

- ==Perception-as-tools harness== with four families: region grounding via promptable segmentation, calibrated depth back-projection and plane fitting, point-tracking anchors that persist across frames, and ==diffusion==-based grasp candidate proposal.
- ==Agent-harness loop== formalized as an ==MDP== whose tool space splits into reasoning calls that only return evidence receipts and action calls that advance state under bounded per-axis motion limits.
- Tool-call traces become supervision: a compact student is fine-tuned with language-side ==low-rank adaptation== and frozen vision encoder to predict one executable call per decision by next-token loss.

## Results

- K1 with Gemini averages **77.2%** on the benchmark suite (Object **86.7%**, Spatial **81.7%**, Goal **63.3%**), against **43.8%** for the strongest code-as-policy baseline and **12.8%** for the best action policy.
- Student trained on only 107 teacher episodes reaches **51.2%** in-domain and **44.2%** under position disturbance versus **30.2%** for the open action-token baseline, plus **13.9%** on held-out tasks where every action baseline scores **0.0%**.
- Zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer gives **90.0%**, **88.8%** and **86.2%** on three arms for shared tasks; on hard randomized bimanual conditions K1 holds **28.0%** while trained references fall from **47.6%** to **19.2%** and from **64.0%** to **25.2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The missing piece between a capable VLM and a policy is accessible metric evidence, not a new action head; tool-mediated perception avoids representation damage entirely.
> - Supervising tool calls matches the model's native next-token objective, so compact students acquire reusable spatial reasoning from roughly a hundred episodes rather than thousands.
> - Separating semantic grounding from metric measurement buys robustness to visual randomization, but precise contact and two-arm handover coordination remain unsolved by better perception alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot is asked to locate the black bowl between a plate and ramekin, then put the bowl on the plate. A vision-language model (VLM) can recognize a bowl and understand an instruction. A robot also needs to know where the bowl's surface is in 3D, how it relates to the target, and whether a movement worked. RGB-only control leaves those measurements uncertain. Robo-Harness K1 gives the VLM perception tools. The model can request calibrated spatial evidence through its existing image-and-language interface, then choose its own target, motion, and recovery strategy.

BOWL ONTO PLATE: locate the black bowl between the plate and ramekin, then place it on the plate. The project page identifies this as a successful Gemini 3.7 Flash + K1 demonstration, with current-view perception overlays appearing alongside function calls.

### 2. Measure before moving

K1 turns the model into a robot-use agent (RUA): a VLM that directly calls tools to observe and control a robot. The model sees RGB views, the instruction, robot state, and prior context, while depth and camera calibration stay behind the harness. The agent can ground a visible region, query a metric surface location or geometry, track an anchor across camera motion, and inspect proposed grasp poses. K1 overlays this evidence on existing RGB views, and the model does not receive raw point clouds or dense depth arrays.

The model then chooses generic motions, observes execution feedback, and can remeasure. The tools supply evidence. They do not supply hidden object poses, guaranteed grasps, or task-specific pick/place solutions. This design exposes calibrated geometry without retraining a depth encoder, and target choice and recovery remain the model's responsibility.

Perception as tools. The rows show candidate-region contours with projection into another existing camera view, a depth-consistency check rather than proof of identity. They also show measured surface points and geometry, an anchor tracked as the wrist camera moves and then marked lost, and three grasp hypotheses, which are not executed grasps. No scene geometry is synthesized.

### 3. What the tests establish

The clearest comparison uses the same 18 LIBERO-PRO task–variant–initial-state cases for each system, with six cases per category. Accuracy counts episodes that meet the simulator's native success criterion. Gemini 3.7 Flash with K1 succeeds on 14 of 18 cases (77.8%). GPT-6 Astra with its RGB-only interface succeeds on 11 of 18 (61.1%), and Astra with K1 succeeds on 16 of 18 (88.9%). This compares complete interfaces. It is not a controlled estimate of perception tools alone, because K1 also changes memory, motion tools, and completion feedback.

The second comparison asks whether a compact student can learn the interaction from tool-call traces. Qwen3.5-9B learns tool calls through supervised fine-tuning on 107 successful Gemini episodes. On new initial states of familiar conditions, Qwen trained to call K1 tools reaches 44.2%. The RGB-only text-action Qwen RUA, trained on the same episodes, reaches 4.7%. This supports the value of the K1 interaction package, but it does not isolate depth as the cause. Perception, memory, and control interfaces differ, and the paper does not ablate each tool family separately.

Task accuracy across training epochs. The three panels cover in-domain cases (A), new initial states (B), and held-out task conditions (C). At epoch 5, Qwen K1 RUA scores 51.2%, 44.2%, and 13.9% across A/B/C, and all action baselines score 0% in C. K1's B result peaks at 46.5% at epoch 3. All methods share the 107 teacher episodes but differ in inputs/tools and action representation.

K1 shows that a VLM's semantic understanding can become more actionable when the model can request calibrated spatial evidence and check execution. The main boundary is scope. The student's held-out-task accuracy is modest, and the paper explicitly says its results are simulation-only.
