# ProAct: A Benchmark and Multimodal Framework for Structure-Aware Proactive Response

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST and Tencent introduced ProAct-75, a vision-based benchmark featuring explicit task graphs for proactive robot response, and ProAct-Helper, a multimodal framework. The framework integrates visual perception with graph-constrained planning to enable structure-aware action selection, demonstrating improved task and step detection (e.g., 17.09% higher task F1) and enhanced collaborative efficiency with more parallel actions.

## Problem

- Existing robotic systems typically operate as passive agents, requiring explicit instructions and limiting autonomous proactive behavior in dynamic environments.
- A scarcity of specialized benchmarks hindered the development of proactive agents, particularly those requiring vision-based input and explicit task graphs for structured decision-making.
- Current video understanding datasets lacked consistent temporal granularity, unified annotation schemes, and crucial task graphs necessary for complex planning and workflow optimization in human-robot collaboration.

## Method

- Developed **ProAct**-75, a new vision-based benchmark comprising 75 tasks with detailed annotations and explicit Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) defining step dependencies and parallel execution possibilities.
- Introduced ProAct-Helper, a multimodal framework built on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), designed to integrate visual perception with structured task knowledge for proactive decision-making.
- ProAct-Helper incorporates a Hierarchical Binding Module (HBM) to enforce cross-level semantic consistency across trigger, task, and step detections, and an entropy-driven heuristic search algorithm to select optimal proactive actions by prioritizing parallelizable threads.

## Results

- ProAct-Helper (7B) achieved a **17.09%** gain in task F1 and an **11.72%** gain in step F1 over the best closed-source MLLM baseline (Gemini-**2.5**-Pro) on the ProAct-75 benchmark.
- The framework demonstrated superior collaborative efficiency with **0.361** Saved Steps (SS) and a **19.41%** Parallel Actions (PA) rate, significantly outperforming baselines.
- ProAct-Helper exhibited strong robustness to cross-view shifts, retaining approximately **73%** of its Task F1 performance on 'Other View' videos, compared to significantly lower retention rates for baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit task graphs with AND/OR dependencies are essential for sophisticated planning, enabling agents to identify parallel execution opportunities and optimize workflows beyond sequential actions.
> - Enforcing hierarchical semantic consistency across trigger, task, and step detection using a binding module significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of perception, especially for fine-grained and long-tail categories.
> - An entropy-driven heuristic search, leveraging task graph structures, effectively guides proactive action selection by prioritizing parallelizable threads, leading to more stable and efficient human-robot collaboration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent agents capable of proactive behavior is an area of ongoing research in artificial intelligence and robotics. Traditional robotic systems typically function as passive agents, responding to explicit instructions and thereby imposing cognitive load on human collaborators or limiting autonomous operation. In contrast, proactive agents are designed to align with higher-level objectives, such as assistance or safety, by continuously monitoring their environment and autonomously determining when and how to intervene.

However, the advancement of proactive agents has been impeded by a lack of specialized resources. Existing research on proactive robotic systems often focuses on inferring human goals or intentions, with the assumption that robot intentions should align with human intentions. Recent studies suggest that optimal robot behavior may involve independent decisions based on scene understanding and task structure, especially in unattended scenarios or when human intentions are suboptimal.

Current video understanding benchmarks, while advancing activity recognition, exhibit limitations when applied to proactive response systems. These limitations include inconsistent temporal granularities, diverse annotation schemes, and a notable absence of explicit task graphs. Task graphs, which encode temporal precedence and parallel execution possibilities, are considered essential for effective planning and for enabling robots to execute tasks concurrently rather than strictly sequentially. Without such structured representations, agents are often compelled to treat all steps as conservative, sequential actions, thus missing opportunities for workflow optimization. Furthermore, much of the prior work on proactive response has been text-based, overlooking the critical role of visual input for real-time perception in physical environments. While large-scale egocentric datasets exist, proactive systems benefit from holistic scene context and structured task representations derived from exocentric views.

This work addresses these identified gaps by introducing a new benchmark, ProAct-75, and a corresponding multimodal framework, ProAct-Helper. The benchmark aims to provide the structured data necessary for training and evaluating proactive agents, particularly focusing on vision-based input and explicit task dependencies. The framework is designed to leverage these resources for perception and structure-aware action selection, thereby contributing to the development of more autonomous and efficient human-robot collaboration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to facilitate the development of proactive robot agents capable of observing an environment, determining when intervention is necessary, and selecting appropriate actions. This overarching goal is pursued through two key objectives:

1. **To address the scarcity of specialized resources for training and evaluating proactive agents.**
 - **Motivation:** The existing landscape of benchmarks and datasets is insufficient for developing proactive agents that require continuous environmental monitoring and structured decision-making. Current datasets often lack the consistent temporal granularity, unified annotation schemes, and, critically, explicit task graphs that represent step dependencies and parallel execution possibilities. This absence hinders the training of agents that can perform complex planning, anticipate needs, and optimize workflows in collaborative or autonomous settings. Furthermore, while activity understanding has progressed, there is a need for benchmarks that support vision-based proactive responses in scenarios such as assistance, maintenance, and safety monitoring, moving beyond purely passive or text-based reactive behaviors.
 - **How Addressed:** The researchers introduce **ProAct-75**, a new benchmark dataset. ProAct-75 is designed to provide comprehensive, vision-based data with detailed annotations, including step-level temporal spans, natural language context cues, and human-rated task priority scores. A central feature of ProAct-75 is the provision of explicit task graphs for each of its 75 tasks. These graphs are formulated as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) with AND/OR dependencies, specifically encoding step prerequisites and opportunities for parallel execution. This structural grounding is intended to enable agents to make more informed and efficient decisions. The benchmark supports the evaluation of five distinct proactive response tasks: trigger detection, task detection, step detection, future action prediction, and proactive action selection.

2. **To propose and evaluate a multimodal framework that effectively integrates visual perception with structured task knowledge for proactive decision-making.**
 - **Motivation:** Proactive agents require not only robust visual perception to understand the current state of an environment but also sophisticated reasoning capabilities guided by structured knowledge about task procedures. Existing multimodal models often struggle with the hierarchical nature and long-tail distributions of real-world activities, leading to inconsistencies in predictions across different levels of abstraction (e.g., trigger vs. task vs. step). There is also a need for planning mechanisms that can leverage task graph structures to enable efficient parallel execution, allowing robots to assist humans without strictly mirroring their sequential actions, thereby reducing workflow disruptions and improving overall efficiency.
 - **How Addressed:** The researchers propose **ProAct-Helper**, a reference baseline framework built upon a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM). ProAct-Helper is designed to process multimodal inputs (video keyframes and task graphs) and generate a suite of structured predictions for proactive response. To enhance the accuracy and consistency of perception tasks, the framework incorporates a **Hierarchical Binding Module (HBM)**. This module enforces cross-level semantic consistency between trigger, task, and step detections by maximizing agreement between hierarchical representations through contrastive binding losses. For action selection, ProAct-Helper employs an **entropy-driven heuristic search** algorithm. This algorithm operates on the provided task graphs to identify the next best proactive action. It prioritizes actions on parallelizable threads to enable concurrent progress, aiming to minimize "thread mixing entropy," which quantifies coordination overhead and frequent switching, thereby promoting stable parallel execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research encompasses two main components: the development of the ProAct-75 benchmark and the design of the ProAct-Helper multimodal framework.

#### 3.1. ProAct-75 Benchmark Development

**Data Composition and Annotation:**
ProAct-75 is a vision-based benchmark comprising 75 tasks across three interaction scenarios: assistance, maintenance, and safety monitoring. It aggregates 5,383 exocentric videos (totaling 91,581 step segments) from existing public datasets—Ego-Exo4D, COIN, and UCF-Crime—and complements these with 495 self-collected clips to ensure broad coverage of underrepresented tasks.

A unified annotation protocol was established to ensure consistent atomic action-level temporal granularity across all videos. Each video segment is annotated with:
- Task-level and step-level temporal spans (aligned timestamps and frame indices).
- Natural language context cues, including scenario and trigger descriptions.
- Human-rated task priority scores, intended for cost-sensitive evaluation.

Annotation quality control involved three trained annotators producing initial annotations over 1.5 months, followed by two rounds of expert review (half a month) to resolve inconsistencies and refine boundaries through deliberation.

**Task Graph Construction:**
For each task, an explicit Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) is constructed to model procedural dependencies. The graphs define two types of nodes:
- **Executable steps (V_e):** Atomic actions a robot or human can perform.
- **Non-executable structural nodes (V_n):** Such as start/terminate nodes and mid-level node pairs, which abstract over categories or sequences of actions.

Directed edges (u, v) indicate that `u` must be executed before `v`. Nodes are further characterized by a type (AND/OR) via a function `ϕ(v)`:
- **AND node:** Can only be executed if all its predecessors (`Pred(v)`) have been executed.
- **OR node:** Can be executed if at least one of its predecessors (`Pred(v)`) has been executed.

Task graphs were built incrementally:
1. GPT-4o proposed local dependencies and activation conditions for a given step inventory.
2. Automatic validation ensured DAG validity (acyclicity, reachability, absence of dead ends/isolated nodes) and basic physical feasibility.
3. Gemini-3-Pro grouped atomic steps into mid-level phases.
4. All graphs underwent manual review for final corrections.

**Proactive Response Formulation:**
The benchmark supports the evaluation of proactive response across five interconnected tasks:
1. **Trigger Detection (y_trig):** A binary decision (0 or 1) indicating if intervention is needed.
2. **Task Detection (y_task):** Identification of the high-level task category.
3. **Step Detection (y_step):** Localization of the current ongoing step within the task.
4. **Future Action Prediction (a_t+1:t+n):** Prediction of `n`-step future actions as a candidate set.
5. **Proactive Action Selection (a*_t+1):** Selection of the robot's next graph-feasible action, or "WAIT."

These tasks form a hierarchical structure where trigger detection gates subsequent predictions, and task/step detection localizes the state for action planning.

#### 3.2. ProAct-Helper Framework

ProAct-Helper is a multimodal framework built on an MLLM, designed to integrate visual state perception with task-graph-constrained planning.

**Framework Architecture and Training:**
The framework takes multimodal input `X_t` (video keyframes and task graph) at time `t` and produces structured predictions `Y_t`. It is built upon MLLMs such as Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct (3B/7B versions) and fine-tuned using LoRA.
The overall training objective `L` combines:
- **Autoregressive Cross-Entropy Loss (L_CE):** Applied over supervised tokens for standard sequence generation.
- **Binary Classification Loss (L_trig):** Applied at the trigger token position to prevent signal dilution.
- **Hierarchical Binding Loss (L_bind):** Introduced by the Hierarchical Binding Module (HBM).

**Hierarchical Binding Module (HBM):**
HBM is designed to enhance cross-level semantic consistency and mitigate issues with long-tail distributions in the trigger-task-step hierarchy. It extracts token hidden states for trigger, task, and step fields. By mean pooling these states, `H_l` representations are obtained for each level `l`.
HBM introduces two cross-level contrastive constraints (binding losses) to maximize mutual information between parent and child level representations while strengthening class separability:
- `L_trig2task`: For trigger-to-task alignment.
- `L_task2step`: For task-to-step alignment.
The binding loss `L(p, c)` is a symmetric InfoNCE-style loss that compares a positive parent-child pair with mismatched negative pairs within a mini-batch, using cosine similarity and a temperature parameter.

**Proactive Action Selection:**
This module leverages the annotated task graphs to select the next feasible action, prioritizing parallelizable threads.
1. **Thread Definition:** Parallel threads are defined based on the reachability structure of the task graph. For a mid-level start node `u` with multiple successors that later merge, each successor and its downstream nodes are assigned to a distinct thread. Nodes not covered by such regions are assigned to a primary thread.
2. **Entropy-Driven Heuristic Search:** For each candidate action `a` in the set of legal actions `A_legal_t`, the framework estimates its induced thread mixing entropy `H_mix`.
 - `n_alpha_k` calculates per-thread step counts for human and robot.
 - `p_k` calculates the mixing ratio (human participation proportion) for thread `k`.
 - `H_k(p_k)` is the binary entropy for thread `k`.
 - `H_mix` is a length-weighted sum of `H_k(p_k)` across all threads, where `w_k` weights threads by total executions.
3. **Action Selection:** The robot selects action `a*_t+1` that minimizes `H_mix`, favoring actions on threads different from the human's current thread to reduce coordination overhead while enabling parallel progress. A one-step lookahead strategy is employed.
4. **Implementation Details:** Keyframes are extracted based on appearance changes, and a 5-frame sliding window with stride 3 serves as input. For action selection, the candidate actions are filtered by the intersection of `A_legal_t` (graph-feasible actions) and `A_pred_t` (predicted actions). A deadlock-prevention safeguard is applied in full-trajectory simulations, relaxing feasibility for the observed next human action if it is otherwise blocked.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the ProAct-75 benchmark demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed ProAct-Helper framework in structure-aware proactive response.

**Overall Performance (Table 1):**
ProAct-Helper, particularly the 7B parameter version, generally outperformed both open-source and closed-source Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) baselines across most evaluation metrics.
- **Perception Tasks (Trigger, Task, Step Detection, Future Action Prediction):**
 - For trigger detection, ProAct-Helper (7B) achieved a mF1 of 76.56% and Acc of 80.08%, which were competitive or superior to baselines.
 - For task detection, ProAct-Helper (7B) achieved a mF1 of 34.79% and F1 of 69.20%. This represented a substantial improvement in task F1 (17.09% gain) and mF1 over the best closed-source baseline (Gemini-2.5-Pro).
 - For step detection, ProAct-Helper (7B) achieved a mF1 of 11.56% and F1 of 33.13%, demonstrating an 11.72% gain in step F1 over Gemini-2.5-Pro.
 - The Hierarchical Binding Module (HBM) contributed to these gains, with an average boost of 2.71% in task mF1 and 1.63% in step mF1 across both backbone models compared to the plain ProAct-Helper variant. This indicates improved performance in handling long-tail categories.
 - For future action prediction, ProAct-Helper (7B) achieved the lowest Edit Distance (ED) of 3.90, indicating more accurate and coherent prediction of future action sequences compared to baselines (e.g., GPT-4o with 4.64 ED).
- **Proactive Action Selection:**
 - ProAct-Helper (7B) demonstrated superior collaboration efficiency, achieving 0.361 Saved Steps (SS) and a Parallel Actions (PA) rate of 19.41%. These figures represent significant improvements over the best closed-source model (Gemini-2.5-Pro, with 0.120 SS and 3.83% PA), indicating a stronger capability for task parallelization. The contribution of HBM to improved perception accuracy was noted to enable more effective action selection.

**Ablation Study (Table 2):**
An ablation study conducted on a stratified mini-set explored the impact of the HBM’s binding losses.
- Introducing the Trigger-Task alignment loss (L_trig2task) resulted in improvements of 2.82% in Task mAcc and 2.56% in Step mAcc.
- The Task-Step dependency loss (L_task2step) yielded larger gains: 3.48% in Task mAcc and 2.70% in Step mAcc.
- The full HBM model achieved the best performance, with L_task2step consistently being more effective, suggesting that grounding tasks in execution steps provides stronger supervision for fine-grained understanding.

**Analysis of Text-only Proactive Action Selection (Table 3):**
To isolate the performance of the action selection strategy from perception errors, full-trajectory simulations were conducted using ground-truth labels.
- ProAct-Helper achieved 9.868 Saved Steps, matching a greedy baseline and outperforming closed-source LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o at 5.872 SS).
- ProAct-Helper also achieved the lowest Robot thread Entropy (ER) at 0.654 and the highest Parallel Actions (PA) rate at 33.95%. This suggests that its entropy-driven heuristic search effectively promotes stable parallel execution strategies, while closed-source LLMs tended to either wait more or exhibit less stable parallel threads.

**Failure Case Analysis (Figure 5):**
- **Hallucination Rates (Figure 5a):** ProAct-Helper exhibited low trigger and near-zero step hallucination rates, indicating strong vocabulary alignment. However, it showed some "Future" hallucination, suggesting challenges in controlling action sequence generation. Among closed-source models, GPT-4o had the lowest overall hallucination.
- **Parallel Action Execution (Figure 5b):** ProAct-Helper demonstrated the highest parallel action ratio, surpassing all closed-source models. This indicates a preference for parallel thread execution. Most closed-source models performed below a "Greedy" baseline in parallel actions, reflecting insufficient preference for parallel execution, which could hinder efficient collaboration. Analysis of waiting behaviors showed varying forced wait ratios among models, indicating differences in constraint adherence.

**Cross-View Generalization (Table 4, Appendix B.2):**
In out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluations, models trained on "Best View" videos were tested on both "Best View" and "Other View" (non-best views).
- ProAct-Helper maintained substantially more reliable task and step recognition and future action prediction under viewpoint shifts compared to baselines. For instance, with the 7B backbone, ProAct-Helper retained approximately 73% of its Task F1 performance on "Other View," whereas a baseline like Ego-Exo4D dropped to 15%. Similar trends were observed for Step F1, where ProAct-Helper preserved around 55% of its performance.
- Superior robustness on tail categories was also observed in macro-averaged metrics. This suggests the approach's stability and transferability in non-optimal viewing conditions.

**Inference Latency (Table 5, Appendix B.3):**
ProAct-Helper (7B) demonstrated a substantial reduction in end-to-end inference latency compared to general-purpose open-source MLLMs, particularly in the perception stack. Its planning latency remained low (0.08s), which is beneficial for real-time interactive deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of intelligent agents and human-robot interaction in several ways, with potential implications for the development of more autonomous and collaborative robotic systems.

**Advancement of Proactive Agents:** The work moves beyond reactive, instruction-following agents by focusing on agents that can autonomously monitor environments, interpret ongoing activities, and proactively intervene based on structured task knowledge. This shift supports the development of robots that can anticipate needs, optimize workflows, and enhance safety in complex, dynamic settings.

**Introduction of ProAct-75 Benchmark:**
- **Standardized Evaluation:** ProAct-75 provides a standardized and comprehensive benchmark for training and evaluating vision-based proactive agents across diverse and practical scenarios, including assistance, maintenance, and safety monitoring.
- **Structural Grounding:** The inclusion of explicit task graphs with AND/OR dependencies and parallel execution possibilities is a critical contribution. This structural grounding enables the development and evaluation of more sophisticated planning algorithms that can account for procedural constraints and opportunities for concurrent task execution, leading to more efficient human-robot collaboration.
- **Data Consistency:** The unified annotation protocol and atomic action-level granularity across aggregated and self-collected videos provide a robust foundation for learning fine-grained procedural understanding.

**Development of ProAct-Helper Framework:**
- **Multimodal Integration:** The ProAct-Helper framework demonstrates an effective method for integrating Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with structured task representations. This integration is crucial for visual perception and context-aware reasoning in real-world environments.
- **Enhanced Perception:** The Hierarchical Binding Module (HBM) improves the accuracy and cross-level consistency of perception tasks (trigger, task, and step detection), particularly addressing challenges posed by long-tail data distributions. This enhances the robot's ability to accurately understand the current state of a task.
- **Efficient Collaboration:** The entropy-driven heuristic search for action selection, which prioritizes parallelizable tasks, offers a mechanism for robots to engage in more efficient and less disruptive collaboration. By taking initiative on independent threads, robots can reduce human cognitive load, accelerate task completion, and move beyond simply mirroring human actions.

**Demonstrated Performance and Robustness:**
- The empirical results show that ProAct-Helper significantly outperforms strong closed-source models in both perception tasks and collaborative efficiency metrics (Saved Steps, Parallel Actions). This validates the proposed methodology and highlights the benefits of combining structured task knowledge with advanced MLLMs.
- The demonstrated cross-view generalization capability suggests that the framework has potential for deployment in real-world scenarios where environmental conditions and camera perspectives may vary.

**Future Research Directions:**
- The identified failure cases, such as hallucination in future action prediction and occasional graph inconsistencies, suggest clear avenues for future research. This includes developing stricter graph-constrained decoding mechanisms for MLLMs and integrating more sophisticated planning algorithms that can handle dynamic human behavior, environmental uncertainties, and more complex forms of human-robot coordination.
- The benchmark's trigger descriptions also offer additional post-training data for refining proactive responses, indicating opportunities for continued model improvement.

In summary, this research provides both a foundational dataset and a strong reference framework, pushing the capabilities of proactive agents towards more intelligent, autonomous, and seamlessly collaborative interactions with humans in diverse practical applications.
