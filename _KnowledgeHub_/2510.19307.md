# Unified Reinforcement and Imitation Learning for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Unified Reinforcement and Imitation Learning (RIL) framework enhances lightweight Vision-Language Models (VLMs) through an efficient training algorithm, allowing them to match or surpass the performance of larger state-of-the-art models. This enables practical deployment in resource-constrained environments while maintaining original inference speeds.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models are too large and computationally demanding for efficient deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.
- Traditional methods for improving VLM performance, such as scaling model size or adding complex architectural components, exacerbate problems with inference latency and memory footprint.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for VLMs often rely on conventional answer parsing for rewards, limiting their applicability to open-ended visual question answering.

## Method

- A ==Unified Reinforcement and Imitation Learning (RIL)== framework is proposed, combining ==Dr.GRPO== (a reinforcement learning algorithm) with an adversarial imitation learning component inspired by GAIL.
- The framework employs a ==dual reward system==: a similarity reward from an LLM-based discriminator assessing how closely student responses mimic teacher responses, and an answer reward from an ==LLM-as-a-Judge== for factual correctness.
- RIL leverages responses from multiple large teacher VLMs to provide diverse exemplars and a more robust discriminator, while avoiding explicit 'think' phases to preserve the student VLM's original inference speed.

## Results

- RIL-trained student VLMs consistently outperform RL-only baselines (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B saw approximately **8.2%** improvement on MathVista and **12.3%** on ChartQA over Dr.GRPO).
- RIL-applied models achieve competitive performance against leading open-source models and in several cases surpass closed-source models like GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro, and Claude-3.5 Sonnet on benchmarks like MathVista.
- The framework effectively trains lightweight VLMs suitable for edge deployment, with RIL-trained models maintaining their original inference speeds without additional architectural complexity or verbose reasoning steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining reinforcement learning with adversarial imitation learning through a dual reward system is crucial for transferring sophisticated generation capabilities to smaller VLMs, significantly outperforming RL-only approaches.
> - Utilizing multiple diverse large teacher VLMs provides a richer source of knowledge, leading to substantially better student VLM performance than training with a single teacher.
> - An LLM-as-a-Judge offers a flexible and robust mechanism for evaluating factual correctness in open-ended visual question answering, overcoming limitations of conventional parsing methods for reward computation.
