# FluidNexus: 3D Fluid Reconstruction and Prediction from a Single Video

> [!summary] Summary
> FluidNexus reconstructs 3D fluid appearance and velocity and predicts future fluid states from a single video input by integrating generative models for multi-view video synthesis with differentiable physics simulation. The framework achieves higher visual quality in novel-view synthesis and future prediction and demonstrates superior physical consistency in re-simulation compared to existing multi-view baselines.

## Problem

- Existing 3D fluid reconstruction and prediction methods primarily rely on multi-view video inputs, which are often impractical to obtain in real-world scenarios.
- Monocular dynamic 3D reconstruction methods typically focus on appearance and geometry without explicit physics modeling, limiting their capability for future prediction or interaction simulation.
- Reconstructing intricate 3D fluid structure and dynamics from a single 2D video is an ill-posed problem due to inherent information sparsity.

## Method

- **FluidNexus** uses a novel-view ==video synthesizer== to generate multiple temporally consistent novel-view videos from a single input video, leveraging frame-wise diffusion and generative video refinement.
- A ==visual-physical particle fluid representation== integrates ==Position-Based Fluid== (PBF) simulation with ==3D Gaussian Splatting==, utilizing distinct physical and visual particles to model fluid dynamics and appearance.
- A generative reconstruction and prediction process sequentially optimizes particle dynamics and appearance using visual and physics losses, further enhanced by generative refinement for future predictions and interaction simulations.

## Results

- **FluidNexus** consistently achieved higher **PSNR**, **SSIM**, and lower **LPIPS** scores in novel view synthesis and future prediction compared to state-of-the-art baselines on three fluid datasets.
- The method demonstrated superior physical correctness during re-simulation, achieving significantly **lower velocity field divergence (∇·V)** compared to all baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the multi-view video synthesizer (especially generative video refinement) and physics constraints (simulation and incompressibility losses) are critical for robust 3D reconstruction and physically accurate predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successfully bridging generative models (video diffusion) with differentiable physics simulation can effectively overcome the information sparsity of single-view inputs for complex dynamic phenomena like fluids.
> - Synthesizing consistent multi-view videos from a single source provides sufficient 'virtual' observational data to enable robust 3D fluid reconstruction.
> - Representing fluids with separate but coupled physical and visual particles within a differentiable simulation framework allows for accurate physical dynamics while preserving realistic appearance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in FluidNexus addresses challenges in video-based fluid analysis, a field that has seen advancements in both controlled laboratory settings and, more recently, through computational methods. Traditionally, fluid flow analysis has relied on specialized measurement equipment like laser scanners and structural light, or passive techniques such as particle imaging velocimetry (PIV). Recent computational approaches have integrated neural rendering with physics priors for fluid reconstruction from videos. However, a significant limitation of these existing methods, including those based on tomography or differentiable rendering (e.g., GlobTrans, Physics-Informed Neural Fluid, HyFluid), is their reliance on multi-view video inputs. Obtaining multiple synchronized camera views is often impractical in real-world scenarios, thereby restricting the applicability of these techniques.

Dynamic 3D reconstruction methods, such as those employing dynamic Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) or 3D Gaussians, have advanced the reconstruction of complex dynamic scenes. Some recent techniques even enable reconstruction from monocular videos, often by leveraging monocular depth estimation. However, these methods typically focus on appearance and geometry reconstruction without explicitly incorporating physics modeling, which limits their capability for future prediction or interaction simulation.

Physics-based dynamic scene modeling, encompassing traditional Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) and real-time approximations like stable fluids or position-based dynamics, excels at physical accuracy but often faces difficulties in synthesizing realistic appearances and fine details from observations. Learning-based approaches have emerged to enhance simulations with learned fluid details and dynamics. Concurrently, physics-integrated representations combined with differentiable rendering have been developed to model motion and interaction of various materials. These, too, commonly require multi-view inputs or pre-reconstructed 3D scenes.

The rapid development of video generation models, particularly video diffusion models (e.g., Sora), has demonstrated the ability to simulate complex physical events, including fluid dynamics, albeit in 2D and with limited direct controllability. FluidNexus integrates these advancements in video generation with physics simulation to specifically address the problem of 3D fluid reconstruction and prediction from a *single* video, a scenario where previous methods have been largely insufficient due to their multi-view requirements or lack of physics integration for future state prediction. By bridging generative models with differentiable physics simulation, FluidNexus aims to overcome the information sparsity of single-view input and enable physically plausible prediction and interaction simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reconstruct 3D fluid appearance and velocity and predict future fluid states from a single video input. This capability is motivated by the desire to enable fluid dynamics analysis in practical scenarios where multi-view measurement equipment or complex computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations are not feasible. Potential applications span industrial monitoring, visual special effects, and scientific visualization.

The authors identify this problem setup as novel and challenging due to its ill-posed nature. A single video provides limited information about a fluid's intricate 3D structure and dynamics, meaning numerous 3D fluid states could correspond to the observed 2D frames. To address this, FluidNexus aims to generate multiple synchronized novel-view videos from the single input, which then serve as references for 3D reconstruction.

Specifically, the work aims to tackle three key challenges:

1. **Single-view video to multi-view video synthesis:** The goal is to synthesize realistic videos of the same fluid scene from novel viewpoints using only a single-view video input. This constitutes a conditional video-to-video translation problem with substantial viewpoint changes, a task that existing video generation methods have not fully addressed for complex fluid dynamics.
2. **Multi-view video to 4D reconstruction:** With potentially inconsistent generated multi-view videos, the objective is to reconstruct a spatiotemporally consistent and physically plausible 4D (3D + time) fluid flow motion. This requires mechanisms to reconcile discrepancies introduced during synthesis and enforce physical realism.
3. **Reconstruction to prediction:** Building upon the reconstructed 4D fluid flow data, the aim is to integrate physical models to predict future fluid motion. This is particularly challenging due to difficulties in extracting inherent physical priors from the reconstructed data and effectively applying these constraints for accurate future prediction in the absence of ground truth video references. Furthermore, extending this to simulate interactions with external forces or objects presents additional complexity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FluidNexus proposes a framework that integrates generative models with physics simulation. The system is composed of two main components: a novel-view video synthesizer and a visual-physical particle fluid representation.

#### 3.1. Novel-view Video Synthesizer

Given a single input video, this component generates multiple temporally consistent novel-view videos to act as references for 3D reconstruction. This process involves two stages:

1. **Frame-wise Novel View Synthesis:** A camera view-conditioned image diffusion model is employed to synthesize individual frames from novel viewpoints. For each frame in the input video, the diffusion model generates a corresponding frame from a target viewpoint, conditioned on the input frame and the target camera's transform matrix. This stage produces a set of "rough" novel-view videos, where each frame is spatially consistent with the input but lacks temporal coherence. The authors use a fine-tuned Zero123 model for this.
2. **Generative Video Refinement:** To ensure temporal coherence, a generative video refinement approach is applied. This technique extends SDEdit to video diffusion, where an intermediate perturbed video is created by adding noise to the "rough" frames. A video diffusion model then performs denoising steps, conditioned on the perturbed video, to generate a temporally coherent and realistic video sequence. The strength of this refinement, controlled by a parameter $\lambda_{SDEdit}$, balances content preservation from the rough frames and temporal consistency learned by the video diffusion model. The authors use a fine-tuned CogVideoX-5b model for this.
3. **Long Video Generation:** To handle video lengths exceeding the typical time window of video diffusion models, a recursive approach is used. An unconditional video refiner generates the first segment, and a conditioned refiner, leveraging a few fixed previous frames, progressively extends the sequence, maintaining consistency across segments.

#### 3.2. Visual-Physical Particle Fluid Representation

This component addresses the physical plausibility aspect by integrating Position-Based Fluid (PBF) simulation with 3D Gaussian Splatting for differentiable rendering. The fluid is represented by two types of particles:

1. **Physical Particles:** These particles represent the fluid's velocity and density fields in 3D space. They are defined by their positions and associated velocities. The velocity at any 3D point is computed through kernel-weighted interpolation of these particle velocities, and density is derived via kernel-weighted summation of particle contributions. The velocity field is implicitly represented by the evolving positions of these particles.
2. **Visual Particles:** These particles represent the fluid's appearance and are characterized by attributes such as position, color, scale, opacity, and orientation. They are passively advected by the velocity field determined by the physical particles and rendered using 3D Gaussian Splatting. The distinction between physical and visual particles accommodates differences in their optimal spatial distributions for dynamics and appearance.

The core idea is to bridge differentiable physics and differentiable rendering:

- **Physical Constraints from Simulation:** PBF simulation is used to generate physically plausible particle positions ($p_{sim_t}$) which inform a physics loss. The physics loss consists of two terms: a simulation loss ($L_{sim}$) that encourages consistency with the PBF simulation guess, and an incompressibility loss ($L_{incomp}$). The incompressibility loss enforces fluid density conservation around both physical and visual particles at current and future timesteps, maintaining a minimal distance between visual particles. External forces like drag are also incorporated.
- **Initialization:** The simulation is initialized by running stabilization steps with a source region to establish initial physical and visual particle states.

#### 3.3. Generative Reconstruction and Prediction

**Reconstruction:**
The reconstruction process is formulated as a sequential optimization problem over time. For each timestep, given multi-view videos and camera poses, the overall loss function combines $L_{physics}$, a visual loss ($L_{visual}$), and a regularization term ($L_{reg}$). The visual loss measures discrepancies between rendered images from the visual particles and the generated reference video frames. The regularization term encourages temporal consistency of visual particle attributes (color, scale, opacity, orientation).

The optimization is decomposed into two stages to manage complexity:
1. **Dynamics Optimization:** Physical particle positions ($p_t$) are optimized by minimizing $L_{physics}$ and $L_{visual}$, while appearance attributes are fixed. This updates the underlying velocity field.
2. **Appearance Optimization:** With $p_t$ fixed (and thus visual particle positions $x_t$ determined by advection), the visual particle attributes ($c_t, s_t, o_t, r_t$) are optimized by minimizing $L_{visual}$ and $L_{reg}$.

**Prediction (Generative Fluid Simulation):**
For predicting future states beyond the observed video ($t > T$), a generative simulation approach is used to overcome the limitations of pure physics simulation, which might accumulate inaccuracies from reconstruction.
1. **Rough Simulation:** Initial physical and visual particle states are simulated using the learned dynamics to produce rough future particle positions ($p_{pred_t}, x_{pred_t}$).
2. **Rough Video Rendering:** These rough particle positions are rendered from multiple novel viewpoints to create "rough" future multi-view videos.
3. **Video Refinement:** The generative video refinement model is applied to these rough videos, generating realistic and temporally coherent reference prediction videos. This step leverages the generative priors learned from fluid videos to add detail and realism that pure simulation might miss.
4. **Reconstruction Application:** The reconstruction algorithm (as described above) is then applied to these refined prediction videos to solve for detailed particle dynamics and appearance in the future timesteps.
Interaction simulations (e.g., wind or object interaction) are incorporated by applying external forces or simplified contact constraints to the physical particles within the PBF simulator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FluidNexus was evaluated on the existing ScalarFlow dataset and two newly introduced datasets: FluidNexus-Smoke (featuring turbulent smoke with textured backgrounds) and FluidNexus-Ball (including fluid-solid interactions with a shiny ball). The method was compared against state-of-the-art 3D fluid reconstruction approaches: Physics-Informed Neural Fluid (PINF), HyFluid, and the 4D dynamic reconstruction model SpacetimeGaussians (STG). Evaluation metrics included PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS for visual quality, and divergence of the velocity field (∇·V) for physical correctness during re-simulation.

#### 4.1. Comparison to Baselines

- **Novel View Synthesis:** FluidNexus demonstrated superior performance in synthesizing novel views across all datasets. Quantitatively, FluidNexus consistently achieved higher PSNR and SSIM, and lower LPIPS scores compared to baselines (Tables 1, 2, 3). Qualitatively, PINF and HyFluid, designed for multi-view input, struggled with single-view input to produce coherent novel views, often failing to disambiguate fluid thickness. STG also showed limitations. FluidNexus, leveraging its novel-view video synthesizer, produced plausible and consistent novel views even with complex backgrounds and object interactions (Figures 3, 4).
- **Future Prediction:** In future prediction tasks, FluidNexus outperformed baselines, especially from novel viewpoints. PINF and HyFluid often produced predictions that mimicked the last observed frame due to inadequate 3D velocity fields, leading to physically inaccurate advection. While these methods showed some performance in the input view, FluidNexus maintained higher visual quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all views (Tables 1, 2, 3, Figures 5, 6). The generative refinement component was critical for maintaining realistic appearance in predicted frames.
- **Re-simulation:** FluidNexus demonstrated higher physical correctness and appearance consistency during re-simulation. By reconstructing explicit fluid particle positions and velocity fields under physical constraints, FluidNexus achieved significantly lower divergence of the velocity field (∇·V) compared to all baselines (Tables 1, 2, 3). This indicates a more physically accurate underlying fluid model. Other methods exhibited poorer performance in reproducing fluid dynamics from the reconstructed initial state.

#### 4.2. Interaction Simulation

FluidNexus's PBF-based differentiable simulator, combined with generative models, enabled physically plausible interaction simulations. The explicit particle-based representation allowed direct application of forces (e.g., wind) or object interaction constraints (e.g., a ball). In qualitative comparisons with SpacetimeGaussians (STG), FluidNexus produced more realistic outcomes for wind-fluid and object-fluid interactions (Figures 7, 8), whereas NeRF-based methods like PINF and HyFluid were less stable for such simulations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on the FluidNexus-Smoke dataset investigated the contribution of core components:

- **Video Generation Components:**
 - **"w/o NVS" (without Novel-view Video Synthesizer):** Performance degraded drastically. The absence of multi-view generative priors led to noisy floaters and distorted background textures due to insufficient reconstruction references from different viewpoints (Table 4, Figure 9).
 - **"w/o GVR" (without Generative Video Refinement):** Results showed noticeable jittering artifacts in synthesized videos.
 - **"w/o LVG" (without Long Video Generation):** Exhibited abrupt transitions at segment boundaries in long videos.
 These indicate that all components of the video synthesizer are crucial for producing stable, coherent, and realistic multi-view videos for reconstruction.
- **Physics Constraints:**
 - **"w/o L_physics" (without Physics Loss):** Re-simulation results degraded significantly. Without physical constraints, image loss dominated, leading to physically inaccurate velocity fields and numerous artifacts during re-simulation.
 - **"w/o L_incomp" (without Incompressibility Loss):** Visual dynamics appeared unnatural with mild jittering.
 - **"w/o L_sim" (without Simulation Supervision):** Visual results also appeared unnatural with abrupt transitions.
 These ablations confirmed that all aspects of the physics constraints are necessary to reconstruct velocity fields that are physically accurate and lead to plausible fluid dynamics during re-simulation and prediction (Table 4, Figure 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FluidNexus framework represents an advancement in 3D fluid analysis by enabling reconstruction and prediction from a single video input, a task previously limited by the requirement for multi-view data. This capability has implications for broader applicability in real-world scenarios where complex multi-camera setups or extensive CFD simulations are impractical.

The key contribution lies in effectively bridging generative models (specifically video diffusion models) with differentiable physics simulation. The novel-view video synthesizer addresses the ill-posed nature of single-view input by generating multi-view references, while the physics-integrated particle representation ensures physical plausibility in the reconstructed dynamics and predictions. This dual approach facilitates the extraction of 3D fluid velocity and appearance over time and enables the prediction of future states and the simulation of interactions.

The introduction of two new real-world fluid datasets (FluidNexus-Smoke and FluidNexus-Ball), featuring textured backgrounds, turbulent flow, and solid obstacles, provides more challenging benchmarks for evaluating fluid reconstruction and prediction models. These datasets can support future research in this domain.

Potential impacts include:
- **Increased Accessibility:** Lowering the barrier for 3D fluid analysis, making it more accessible with standard single-camera video recordings.
- **Enhanced Applications:** Facilitating applications in industrial monitoring, where capturing multi-view data might be difficult, and improving realism in visual effects by enabling physics-aware fluid simulation from observed footage.
- **Scientific Visualization:** Offering tools for understanding complex fluid dynamics from observations without extensive lab equipment.
- **Future Research Direction:** The work highlights the potential of integrating generative models with physical simulations for tasks that are traditionally data-intensive or computationally demanding, potentially fostering further research into single-view dynamic scene analysis and physics-informed generative modeling.

While the current reconstruction results on challenging datasets exhibit some blurriness and lack of fine details, and the novel-view video synthesizer's generalization is limited by its training data, the framework provides a foundation for future improvements, such as collecting larger-scale multi-view fluid video datasets.
