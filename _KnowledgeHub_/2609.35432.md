# Self-Evolving Coding Agents: From Digital Programs to Physical-World Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Physical Coding represents robot task state and execution as executable programs. Code as World records objects, relations, constraints and progress predicates, and Code as Policy handles planning, tool calls, verification and recovery. The HexaAnything harness calls perception, planning and VLA/WAM tools under typed verifier verdicts. Its verified traces then train HexaModel, which lifts RoboCasa365 Composite-Unseen success over native XR-1 from 34.3% to 39.5%.

## Problem

- VLA and WAM policies hide subgoals, completion conditions and recovery inside action chunks and a stop signal, so errors like closing an oven early cannot be detected or repaired.
- Code-as-policy systems trust perception outputs without checking them, let errors compound along primitive chains, and pay for test-time fixes again in every episode instead of keeping them.
- Embodied harnesses store what they learn in memory, skill libraries or critics while the reasoning model stays frozen, and they often verify against privileged simulator state that real robots lack.

## Method

- ==Code as World==: a persistent ==world program== built from images, depth, proprioception and tool outputs, recording objects, relations, constraints and ==progress predicates==, each tagged with the observation it came from.
- ==Code as Policy== inside the **HexaAnything** Harness: an editable workflow that calls VLA/WAM and other tools, gates actions on world predicates, and gets typed verdicts (pass, fail, insufficient evidence, blocked, safety stop) from an ==independent verifier==.
- ==Self-evolution loop==: verified Harness traces and VQA data fine-tune **HexaModel** from Qwen3.8-27B, while a programming agent revises tool code against a fixed evaluator, with ==regression tests and rollback== for each revision.

## Results

- On RoboCasa365 with a fixed XR-1 action tool, HexaAnything raises overall success from **56.6%** to **61.1%**. Codex driven by the same model reaches **59.5%** and gains nothing on Composite-Unseen.
- HexaModel v0.1 beats its Qwen3.8-27B base on every split, reaching **39.5%** on Composite-Unseen versus **37.3%**. Case studies show gains of up to **31 points**, e.g. LoadKebabSandwich rising from **17%** to **48%**.
- Tool revision raises RoboDojo Fold cloth, Pour vase and Press by number from **0/40/0%** to **80/100/100%**. On a real AgileX dual-arm robot, **5 of 7** tasks succeed in all **three** trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instruction masking costs a LIBERO VLA only 3.9 points on average, so high benchmark success can come from mapping scenes to trajectories rather than following language.
> - Wrapping an unchanged VLA in a verifier-gated workflow helps most on long-horizon composite tasks, where explicit progress predicates catch early termination that the policy's stop signal misses.
> - Self-improvement is not monotonic: some tool revisions temporarily cut success to zero, and authors warn that model and verifier must not co-adapt toward a narrow or self-generated evaluator.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A kitchen task asks a robot to put both a kebab and bread into an oven, then close the door. In one run, a baseline policy puts the bread inside but closes the door with the kebab still on the plate. The other agent checks whether both ingredients are inside before it allows the door to close. This is one illustrative run, not an average result. It shows the problem: a direct-action policy can finish moving while leaving the task undone.

The authors call their approach **Physical Coding**. It gives a robot two linked, executable records: one for what matters in the world, and one for what to do about it. The authors argue that making state, procedure, and feedback inspectable lets an agent check progress and preserve experience for revision.

### 2. Make the hidden state visible

**Code as World** is an executable record of task-relevant objects, relations, constraints, observations, and progress predicates. **Code as Policy** is an executable workflow for decomposing the task, selecting and calling tools, checking outcomes, recovering, and replanning. The two parts depend on each other. State without a procedure cannot act, and a procedure without state cannot reliably check what remains.

The loop runs in order:

1. Observations and tool outcomes update the world record.
1. The policy uses its predicates to choose and execute the next action.
1. After each tool call, the agent checks fresh evidence.
1. The system then continues, observes again, recovers, or stops.

When the evidence is insufficient, the agent should avoid guessing. In simulation, the benchmark evaluator assigns success, not the agent's "finished" message.

The closest comparison is [[2603.22435|CaP-X: A Framework for Benchmarking and Improving Coding Agents for Robot Manipulation]]. CaP-X finds that success increases with human-designed high-level abstractions. It also reports that multi-turn execution feedback and visual differencing can improve low-level coding-agent performance. Physical Coding's claimed distinction has three parts. It makes both the world state and the action workflow explicit, checks progress against evidence, and turns verified execution traces into reusable records and training data. The paper reports no head-to-head comparison with CaP-X, and the authors describe their evidence as preliminary.

### 3. Test the workflow, then the trace

On RoboCasa365, success rate means the fraction of task trials that the benchmark evaluator judges successful. Native and Harness conditions share a maximum environment-step budget.

The authors tested one split, **Composite-Unseen**, with XR-1 as the same underlying action model and GPT-5.6-Sol as planner. Success rose from 34.3% for native XR-1 to 38.3% for HexaAnything. The gain over other coding agents is smaller. Against Codex driven by the same GPT model, HexaAnything's overall advantage is only 1.6 percentage points. Codex does better on Atomic-Seen, and HexaAnything's advantage is on composite tasks.

A skeptic might suspect the gain comes from a larger action budget. On PortionHotDogs, doubling the native VLA action budget produced 21 successes in 100 trials, versus 22 under the standard native budget. The coding-agent condition completed 37. This argues against action budget alone explaining that result. It does not identify which part of the combined Harness caused the gain: state tracking, verification, interruption, or recovery.

The authors also tested a first data-to-model update. HexaModel v0.1 is Qwen3.8-27B fine-tuned on a mixture of Harness-returned and general-domain data. When both the base and fine-tuned models run inside the same Harness, Composite-Unseen success improves from 37.3% to 39.5%. This supports improvement after this recipe. It does not isolate the Harness data as the sole cause.

The work also extends to simulated physics experiments in PhyBench and to a dual-arm robot. On the robot, five of seven tabletop tasks succeeded in all three trials.

### 4. Make experience reusable

The established contribution is an agent that checks conditions before acting and keeps a verified record of what happened. It raised success modestly on one benchmark with one action model, and it gave an early model update. The boundary is that the paper does not show general physical-world robustness, and full co-evolution remains future work.
