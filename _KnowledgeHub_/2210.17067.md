# Unified Optimal Transport Framework for Universal Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Optimal Transport framework addresses Universal Domain Adaptation by automatically detecting common classes and discovering distinct private categories in the target domain, outperforming existing methods on standard benchmarks, as evidenced by a new H3-score. This approach eliminates the need for manual thresholds and enables fine-grained recognition of unknown classes.

## Problem

- Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) is challenging as both source and target domains can possess private classes, with no prior knowledge of label set overlap.
- Existing UniDA methods often rely on manually specified thresholds to distinguish common samples from target-private ones, making them difficult to generalize across varying scenarios.
- Current approaches typically group all target-private samples into a single 'unknown' category, failing to discover and differentiate distinct classes within these private samples.

## Method

- Proposes **UniOT**, a unified ==Optimal Transport (OT)== framework using ==Unbalanced OT== for common class detection and standard OT for private class discovery.
- Introduces an adaptive, threshold-free mechanism for common sample detection based on ==UOT assignment confidence scores== and an ==adaptive filling strategy== for robust statistics.
- Employs standard OT for ==intra-domain representation learning==, fostering global discrimination and local consistency among target features to enable the discovery and differentiation of private classes.

## Results

- UniOT achieved state-of-the-art H-scores across four UniDA benchmarks: Office (**91.13%** average), Office-Home (**76.57%** average), VisDA, and DomainNet.
- The method demonstrated superior performance on the new H3-score, designed to evaluate private class discovery, with an average of **82.24%** on Office and **70.84%** on Office-Home datasets.
- Qualitative t-SNE visualizations confirmed UniOT's ability to separate and cluster distinct target-private categories, unlike prior methods that group them as a single unknown.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unbalanced Optimal Transport's inherent ability to model partial mappings naturally distinguishes common samples from private ones without requiring hard thresholds.
> - Standard Optimal Transport can effectively regularize feature spaces within the target domain, enabling the discovery of intrinsic clusters for private categories.
> - A unified framework combining inter-domain partial alignment for common classes and intra-domain representation learning for private classes via Optimal Transport offers a comprehensive solution for Universal Domain Adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the broader field of **Domain Adaptation (DA)**, a subfield of machine learning that aims to transfer knowledge from a source domain (with abundant labeled data) to a target domain (with scarce or no labeled data), where the data distributions differ.

Traditionally, **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** assumes a "closed-set" scenario, meaning both source and target domains share identical label sets. However, real-world applications often deviate from this strong assumption. This led to the emergence of more relaxed settings:
- **Partial Domain Adaptation (PDA)**: The target label set is a subset of the source label set, implying that the source domain might contain classes not present in the target.
- **Open Set Domain Adaptation (OSDA)**: The target domain may contain "private" (unknown) classes not present in the source, while all source classes are assumed to be present in the target.

The paper focuses on **Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA)**, a more challenging and realistic setting that generalizes PDA and OSDA. In UniDA, *both* source and target domains can possess private classes, and critically, there is no prior knowledge about the overlap between their label sets (i.e., which classes are common, or how many unique classes exist in the target). This makes UniDA particularly complex, as models must simultaneously identify common classes for alignment and recognize novel classes in the target domain without explicit supervision.

Existing UniDA methods largely suffer from two main limitations:
1. **Reliance on Manual Thresholds:** Many methods require hand-tuned or manually specified threshold values to distinguish common samples from target-private ones. This makes them difficult to generalize to diverse real-world scenarios where the ratio of common to private classes can vary significantly.
2. **Treating Private Classes as a Single "Unknown" Category:** Most methods group all target-private samples into a single "unknown" or "out-of-distribution" class, failing to discover and differentiate distinct categories within these private samples. This overlooks valuable intrinsic structure within the target domain.

This paper proposes a novel solution based on **Optimal Transport (OT)**. OT is a powerful mathematical framework for comparing probability distributions by finding the most efficient way to transform one distribution into another. While OT has been extensively applied in various DA settings (e.g., DeepJDOT, JUMBOT, TS-POT), these prior OT-based methods often assume closed-set conditions or rely on some form of label-set prior, or focus on sample-to-sample mapping. This paper distinguishes itself by being the first to propose a *unified OT framework* specifically designed to address the unique challenges of UniDA, particularly by enabling the *automatic discovery and recognition of private categories* in the target domain. By leveraging both Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) for partial alignment and standard OT for robust representation learning, the authors aim to overcome the aforementioned limitations, thus pushing the boundaries of practical domain adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome the critical limitations of existing Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) methods and propose a more robust and adaptable framework. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Eliminate the need for manually specified or hand-tuned threshold values** for detecting common samples in the target domain. Existing methods' reliance on such thresholds makes them impractical for real-world UniDA scenarios where the ratios of common to private classes are diverse and unknown. The motivation is to create a method that can automatically adapt to varying class compositions.
2. **Enable the recognition and differentiation of distinct categories among target-private samples**, rather than treating them all as a single "unknown" class. Current approaches neglect the internal structure of target-private data, losing potentially valuable discriminative information. The authors argue that a truly "universal" solution should be able to discover these intrinsic private categories.
3. **Develop a unified framework** that can simultaneously handle both the common class detection problem (distinguishing known from unknown) and the private class discovery problem (clustering the unknown into meaningful sub-categories) within UniDA.
4. **Introduce a more comprehensive evaluation metric**, the H3-score, that accounts for both classification accuracy on common classes and the quality of clustering for discovered private classes, thereby providing a fairer assessment of UniDA methods with private class discovery capabilities.

The core motivation stems from the recognition that real-world domain adaptation scenarios are complex and often involve unknown category gaps. Current UniDA methods, despite their advancements, fall short in truly generalizing due to their rigidity (thresholds) and incompleteness (neglecting private class structure). By leveraging the inherent capabilities of Optimal Transport, which can effectively model distribution transportation and encourage robust data assignments, the authors aim to build a more adaptive, autonomous, and comprehensive UniDA solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **UniOT**, a unified optimal transport framework designed to address the two core challenges in UniDA: common class detection and private class discovery. The framework leverages both Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) and standard Optimal Transport (OT).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Optimal Transport (OT) and Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT)

The paper first reviews the basics of OT and UOT.
- **Optimal Transport (OT)**: Given two probability distributions (represented by simplex vectors α and β) and a similarity matrix M, OT seeks to find an optimal coupling matrix Q* that maximizes the total "transportation" (Tr(Q^T M)) while satisfying strict marginal constraints (Q sums to α along rows and β along columns). An entropic regularization term (εH(Q)) is added for efficient computation via Sinkhorn's Algorithm. OT ensures that all "mass" from one distribution is transported to another.
- **Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT)**: Unlike classical OT, UOT relaxes the strict marginal constraints by incorporating Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalties. This allows for partial displacement, meaning that not all mass from one distribution needs to be mapped to the other. This property is crucial for handling situations where some samples in one domain might not have a corresponding match in the other (e.g., private classes).

#### 3.2. Inter-domain Partial Alignment for Common Class Detection (CCD)

To identify common classes, UniOT employs UOT to establish a partial mapping between **target samples** and **source prototypes**.
- **UOT Formulation**: The optimal assignment matrix Q_st is computed using UOT, considering the cosine similarity (S_st) between target features (Z_t) and learned source prototypes (C_s). The source prototypes are learned via a prototype-based classifier on the labeled source data. Initial marginals are set uniformly.
- **Adaptive Common Sample Detection**: The key innovation here is an adaptive, threshold-free mechanism. The normalized UOT assignment matrix (Q_st) is used to derive confidence scores:
 - `w_t_i`: Maximum value in each row of Q_st for target sample `i`. Higher score implies closer proximity to a source prototype.
 - `w_s_j`: Sum of each column of Q_st for source prototype `j`. Higher score implies more frequent assignment from target samples.
 - A target sample `x_t_i` is confidently detected as a common sample if its `w_t_i` is above the mean target score *and* its pseudo-labeled source prototype `w_s_y_hat_t_i` is above the mean source prototype score. This adaptive criterion allows for automatic detection without predefined thresholds.
- **Adaptive Filling for Unbalanced Proportions**: To ensure robust statistics for UOT, a memory queue (FIFO) stores previous target features. An "adaptive filling" mechanism is introduced to balance the proportions of "positive" (high similarity) and "negative" (low similarity) samples in a mini-batch. If one category (e.g., negatives) is disproportionately low, synthetic "fake private" features are generated by mixing target features with their farthest source prototypes to fill the gap. Conversely, for positive filling, confident common features are reused. This ensures that the statistics-based detection (Eq. 7) is reliable even with skewed class distributions.
- **Adaptive Update for Marginal Probability Vector (β)**: The marginal probability vector for source prototypes (β) is dynamically updated using a moving average of the column sums of Q_st from the previous iteration. This allows β to adapt to the true distribution of common classes in the source, preventing private source classes from being equally weighted in the alignment process.
- **Loss Function**: A standard cross-entropy loss (L_CCD) is applied only to the confidently detected common target samples, guiding their alignment with corresponding source prototypes.

#### 3.3. Intra-domain Representation Learning for Private Class Discovery (PCD)

To discover and differentiate target-private classes, UniOT uses standard OT for clustering target features around a set of learnable target prototypes (C_t).
- **OT Formulation**: A full mapping (OT) is used to assign target features (concatenated with their nearest neighbor features from the memory queue, Z_t_bar) to K pre-defined (but learnable) target prototypes (C_t). The uniform marginal constraints on both sides ensure balanced assignments across prototypes and samples. The number of target prototypes (K) is set empirically based on dataset size, acknowledging that the *true* number of private classes is unknown, but OT inherently balances assignments across prototypes.
- **Global Discrimination Loss (L_global)**: This loss encourages target features to form discriminative clusters around the target prototypes. It's a cross-entropy-like loss where the "pseudo-labels" are derived from the soft assignments of the OT coupling matrix (Q_tt).
- **Local Consistency Loss (L_local)**: Inspired by self-supervised learning, this loss promotes local consistency. It involves a swapped prediction mechanism between an anchor target feature and its nearest neighbor feature (retrieved from the memory queue). This encourages similar samples to have similar assignments, strengthening the local structure of the learned representation.
- **Combined PCD Loss (L_PCD)**: L_PCD is the average of L_global and L_local, encouraging both overall cluster separation and local neighborhood coherence within the target domain.

#### 3.4. Unified Framework and Overall Objective

UniOT integrates the above components along with a standard cross-entropy classification loss on source samples (L_cls). The overall objective function is:
`L_overall = L_cls + λ(L_CCD + L_PCD)`
where λ is a weighting factor.

**4.5. Inference**: During testing, only the feature extractor and source prototype classifier are used. Target samples are classified as common if their confidence score (derived from the UOT-based partial alignment, as in Eq. 5) is above a dynamic threshold (mean confidence for all samples). Otherwise, they are marked as "unknown." For H3-score evaluation, K-Means is then applied to these "unknown" samples to measure clustering quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments on four standard domain adaptation benchmarks: Office, Office-Home, VisDA, and DomainNet, demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of UniOT.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

- **H-score**: A widely used metric in UniDA, representing the harmonic mean of accuracy on common classes (a_C) and accuracy on the single "unknown" private class (a_C_bar).
- **H3-score (New Metric)**: Introduced by the authors to specifically evaluate the ability to discover and recognize private categories. It is the harmonic mean of accuracy on common classes (a_C), accuracy on the single "unknown" class (a_C_bar), and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) for target-private clusters. NMI is obtained by applying K-Means to the target-private samples, using the ground truth number of private classes for evaluation. This metric provides a more comprehensive assessment of UniDA methods that aim for fine-grained private class discovery.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Arts

- **H-score Performance**: UniOT consistently achieved the best performance across all four datasets (Office, Office-Home, VisDA, DomainNet), surpassing a wide range of state-of-the-art UniDA methods (e.g., UAN, CMU, DANCE, DCC, TNT) and traditional DA methods (e.g., DANN, RTN). For instance, on the Office dataset, UniOT achieved an average H-score of 91.13%, significantly outperforming CMU (73.14%) and DCC (80.16%). Similarly, on Office-Home, UniOT reached 76.57% average H-score, compared to TNT's 74.70% and DCC's 70.18%.
- **H3-score Performance**: UniOT demonstrated even more pronounced advantages in terms of H3-score, which directly reflects its capability for private class discovery. On Office, UniOT achieved an average H3-score of 82.24%, considerably higher than DANCE (73.67%) and DCC (75.17%). On Office-Home, UniOT scored 70.84% on average, significantly outperforming competitors (e.g., DANCE at 40.97% and DCC at 61.66%). This validates that UniOT not only performs well on common classes but also excels at learning discriminative representations for target-private samples, enabling their discovery.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- The effectiveness of the proposed components (L_CCD, L_global, L_local) was rigorously evaluated.
- The combination of L_global and L_local (L_PCD) significantly contributed to performance, underscoring the importance of target representation learning. L_global (global discrimination) was found to contribute more than L_local (local consistency), though both were crucial.
- The adaptive filling mechanism for L_CCD was shown to be effective, as performance dropped when it was removed (denoted as L_†_CCD). This confirms its role in handling unbalanced class proportions.

#### 4.4. Robustness in Realistic UniDA

- UniOT demonstrated superior robustness to varying class compositions. Experiments with different numbers of source-private and target-private categories (while fixing common classes) on the Office A2W task showed that UniOT maintained high performance and was less sensitive to these variations compared to UAN, DCC, and DANCE. This highlights its adaptability to realistic UniDA scenarios with diverse ratios of common classes.

#### 4.5. Feature Visualization (t-SNE)

- t-SNE visualizations of learned target features on Rw2Pr of Office-Home qualitatively confirmed UniOT's ability. Unlike UAN, which treated all private samples as a single cluster, UniOT effectively separated and clustered different target-private categories. The visualizations demonstrated that UniOT learns a better, more discriminative target representation with clear global discrimination and local consistency among samples, even for unknown classes.

#### 4.6. Hyper-parameter Sensitivity

- UniOT was shown to be robust to the choice of several key hyper-parameters, including the rough boundary γ for adaptive filling, the moving average factor μ for updating the marginal probability vector, and the overall loss weight λ. This indicates the stability and practical applicability of the method.

#### 4.7. Effect of Local Neighbor for Representation Learning

- The authors also compared their local consistency approach with augmentation-based self-supervised learning methods (SimSiam, SwAV). Their findings suggest that directly combining these self-supervised methods with domain adaptation did not yield significant improvements. This indicates that learning from local neighbors (as implemented in UniOT's L_local) is more effective for UniDA, particularly under the presence of source supervision, distinguishing their approach from general self-supervised representation learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniOT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Universal Domain Adaptation, addressing long-standing limitations and offering a more practical and comprehensive solution for real-world scenarios.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Unified Framework with Optimal Transport**: UniOT is the first method to jointly address both common class detection and private class discovery within a single, unified Optimal Transport framework. This synergistic approach effectively tackles the dual challenges of UniDA, leading to superior performance across various benchmarks.
2. **Adaptive Common Class Detection without Thresholds**: By leveraging the statistical properties of the UOT assignment matrix and introducing an adaptive filling strategy, UniOT eliminates the need for manual or hand-tuned threshold values. This is a crucial step towards truly "universal" adaptation, as it allows the method to automatically adjust to diverse and unknown class compositions in the target domain, making it highly practical for deployment.
3. **Fine-Grained Private Class Discovery**: Unlike previous methods that treat all target-private samples as a single "unknown" blob, UniOT's OT-based target representation learning explicitly encourages the discovery and recognition of distinct categories within the private set. This capability extracts more meaningful structure from the unlabeled target domain, enhancing the utility of the adapted model. The t-SNE visualizations provide compelling qualitative evidence for this.
4. **Novel Evaluation Metric (H3-score)**: The introduction of the H3-score is a significant contribution to the UniDA research community. By incorporating Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) for private class clusters, it provides a more holistic and fair evaluation metric that acknowledges the importance of both common class accuracy and the discriminative quality of learned private representations. This will encourage future research to focus on this crucial aspect of UniDA.
5. **Robustness and Stability**: Extensive experiments demonstrate UniOT's robustness to varying ratios of common and private classes, as well as its insensitivity to a range of hyper-parameter settings. This reinforces its practical applicability in diverse real-world conditions where domain characteristics might be unknown or unpredictable.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Broader Real-World Applications**: By overcoming the limitations of manual thresholds and enabling private class discovery, UniOT makes Universal Domain Adaptation more viable for complex real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, medical imaging, and industrial inspection, where data distributions can shift unexpectedly and novel object categories may emerge without prior knowledge.
- **Enhanced Model Interpretability and Utility**: The ability to differentiate between unknown categories provides richer insights into the target domain data, which can be invaluable for human operators or downstream tasks. Instead of just flagging "unknown," the model can potentially suggest "unknown type A," "unknown type B," etc., which can guide further data labeling or analysis.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: UniOT's unified OT framework and its novel approach to private class discovery lay a strong foundation for future research in generalized domain adaptation. It opens new avenues for exploring more sophisticated OT variants, memory management strategies, and self-supervised learning techniques tailored for unknown label spaces. The H3-score will also serve as a new standard for evaluating such advancements.

In conclusion, UniOT represents a substantial leap forward in universal domain adaptation, moving closer to truly autonomous knowledge transfer in complex, open-world environments.
