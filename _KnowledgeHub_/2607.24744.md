# Data Pyramid for Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and collaborators introduce the "data pyramid," a comprehensive taxonomy that categorizes and analyzes diverse data sources for embodied AI based on scalability and robot alignment. The work provides a systematic framework for understanding how different data types contribute to and are utilized by embodied foundation models, highlighting trends towards heterogeneous data mixtures and identifying key challenges in data collection and integration.

## Problem

- Embodied AI faces a fundamental challenge in applying multimodal foundation models, as agents require data explicitly coupling observations with physical states and actions, unlike readily available internet data.
- The existing embodied data ecosystem is fragmented, comprising diverse sources (real-robot, simulation, human egocentric videos) whose roles, trade-offs, and optimal integration strategies remain unsystematized.
- A lack of comprehensive, data-centric organizational frameworks for comparing and utilizing heterogeneous embodied data sources, beyond model-specific views, hinders the development of generalist robot policies.

## Method

- Introduces a five-layer **"data pyramid"** taxonomy to systematically organize and characterize embodied AI data sources, ordered by ==scalability== and ==robot alignment==.
- Analyzes each data layer (==real-robot==, ==UMI-style==, ==egocentric/exocentric==, ==simulation==, ==general vision-language==) based on its definition, collection paradigms, scale, and trade-offs in ==quality==, ==diversity==, ==reusability==, and ==physical fidelity==.
- Conducts an in-depth review of how embodied foundation models utilize diverse data sources, examining data recipes, ==action-space alignment==, ==geometric alignment== strategies, and mapping data to specific model capabilities.

## Results

- The paper establishes the "data pyramid" taxonomy, categorizing embodied data into **five** distinct layers and detailing their characteristics, advantages, and limitations.
- Analysis of embodied foundation models reveals a consistent trend of increasing data scale and a clear progression towards pretraining on increasingly diverse and heterogeneous data mixtures from various pyramid layers.
- **Six** key open challenges for embodied data are identified, including the scarcity of tactile and failure data, the need for scalable collection pipelines, and the development of principled data recipes for optimal integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An inherent tension exists between data scalability (ease of collection) and robot alignment (direct relevance to physical robots), forming the core organizing principle of the data pyramid.
> - Embodied foundation models exhibit a clear trend towards utilizing heterogeneous data mixtures across multiple pyramid layers, acknowledging the complementary strengths of diverse data types to build broader capabilities.
> - Effective integration of diverse data requires specific strategies for action-space and geometric alignment to reconcile differences in control dimensionality, semantics, and coordinate frames across various datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in multimodal foundation models, particularly in vision and language, have been driven by their ability to leverage vast amounts of internet-scale data. However, the application of this paradigm to embodied AI faces a fundamental challenge: embodied agents require data that explicitly couples observations with physical states and actions. Such data, unlike readily available internet text and images, is complex, expensive to collect, and often tied to specific robotic embodiments. The existing embodied data ecosystem is characterized by a fragmented landscape of diverse data sources, including real-robot demonstrations, simulation data, human egocentric videos, and general vision-language data. The roles, trade-offs, and optimal integration strategies for these heterogeneous data sources remain largely unsystematized.

This paper addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive, data-centric review and organizational framework for the embodied AI data landscape. While some recent embodied foundation models (e.g., Motus, GR00T) have introduced hierarchical views of training data, these formulations are often tailored to specific model architectures and offer limited general analysis of how different data categories vary in scalability, embodiment dependence, physical fidelity, and transferability. Existing surveys tend to focus on model architectures (e.g., Vision-Language-Action models, World-Action models) rather than a holistic, data-centric perspective. This work positions itself as a foundational resource that systematically categorizes, compares, and analyzes the entire spectrum of data types relevant to embodied foundation models, aiming to provide a unified understanding that can guide future data collection, model design, and training efforts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this work is to systematically address the question of "What data should be used to train embodied foundation models with such capabilities?" To achieve this, the authors frame the problem around two core questions:

1. "How can heterogeneous embodied data sources be collected, organized, compared, and integrated despite their differences in scalability, robot alignment, physical fidelity, and transferability?"
2. "How can these heterogeneous data sources be effectively selected, combined, and utilized by embodied foundation models?"

The motivation for this detailed investigation stems from several factors:

- **Complexity of Embodied Data:** Unlike general visual and linguistic data, embodied data requires an understanding of physical states, dynamics, actions, and their environmental consequences. This fundamentally alters the nature of supervision needed for pretraining embodied foundation models.
- **Fragmentation of Data Sources:** The embodied AI community has explored various complementary data sources, each offering different forms of semantic, temporal, physical, and action-related supervision. However, a clear, unified understanding of their individual contributions, interrelationships, and optimal integration is lacking.
- **Need for Systematic Organization:** Existing data views are often model-specific or focus on subproblems, neglecting a comprehensive, category-level organization of the embodied data ecosystem. A systematic framework is required to analyze how data categories differ in collection scalability, embodiment dependence, physical fidelity, transferability, and downstream utility.
- **Guiding Future Development:** By analyzing the data landscape, the authors aim to identify key challenges and future directions in embodied data, particularly concerning the scarcity of certain valuable data types (e.g., tactile, failure/recovery data), the need for more scalable collection pipelines, and the effective integration of diverse data sources for next-generation embodied systems. The ultimate motivation is to pave the way for designing more general, robust, reusable, and physically grounded embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is a comprehensive literature review and synthesis, structured around a novel, multi-layered **"data pyramid" taxonomy**. This framework serves as an analytical tool to systematically organize and characterize the diverse data sources for embodied AI.

#### 3.1. Development of the Embodied Data Pyramid Taxonomy

The authors introduce a five-layer data pyramid, ordered from apex to base, guided by two primary design principles and four complementary dimensions:

- **Primary Organizing Principles:**
 - **Scalability:** Measures the efficiency of data expansion, considering factors like hardware dependence, human labor, environment reset, safety supervision, and marginal generation cost.
 - **Robot Alignment:** Assesses how directly observations, representations, and supervision signals support learning and execution on physical robots. The paper highlights the inherent tension between these two principles, as highly robot-aligned data often sacrifices scalability.
- **Complementary Category-Level Dimensions:**
 - **Quality:** Validity, consistency, informativeness, and task relevance of the collected experience.
 - **Diversity:** Coverage across tasks, objects, scenes, viewpoints, instructions, embodiments, sensing configurations, behaviors, and outcomes.
 - **Reusability:** Ease with which data can be transferred across tasks, environments, embodiments, sensing systems, and model families.
 - **Physical Fidelity:** Faithfulness in capturing real interaction dynamics and feedback, including contact, friction, compliance, sensing noise, actuation delay, and object motion.

Based on these principles, the five layers of the pyramid are:

1. **Real-Robot Data:** At the apex, offering direct physical grounding.
2. **UMI-style Data (Universal Manipulation Interface):** Next, relaxing direct robot involvement but retaining end-effector supervision.
3. **Egocentric and Exocentric Data:** Capturing human interaction in real physics with everyday diversity.
4. **Simulation Data:** Providing executable actions and privileged labels but approximating physics.
5. **General Vision-Language Data:** At the base, offering web-scale semantic and reasoning coverage.

For each of these five categories, the paper provides a detailed characterization, including:
- An overview of its definition, evolution, and typical use cases.
- Description of prevalent embodiments and sensing modalities.
- Analysis of data collection paradigms (e.g., scripted, teleoperation, human-in-the-loop for real robots; UMI-specific pipelines; various generation methods for simulation; annotation strategies for general data).
- Discussion of historical scale and diversity trends.
- Summary of advantages and limitations, accompanied by specific takeaways.

#### 3.2. Data-Centric Analysis of Embodied Foundation Models

The second part of the methodology involves an in-depth analysis of how embodied foundation models currently utilize these diverse data sources. This includes:

- **Review of Data Recipes:** Examination of data compositions used in representative models (e.g., RT-1, GR00T, Octo, various VLA and WAM architectures), highlighting the shift towards heterogeneous mixtures and increasing data scale.
- **Analysis of Data Accommodation Strategies:** Investigation into how models reconcile differences in heterogeneous datasets, specifically focusing on:
 - **Action-space alignment:** Strategies like embodiment-specific projection, fixed-dimensional zero-padded action interfaces, and semantic action slots to handle varying control dimensionality and semantics.
 - **Geometric alignment:** Different coordinate frames used for end-effector actions (robot-centric, camera-centric, wrist-centric) to ensure consistent spatial representations.
- **Mapping Data to Capabilities:** Relating the composition and alignment of training data to the specific capabilities supported by three major model families:
 - **Embodied Brain Models (VLMs):** Focus on perception, spatial/temporal reasoning, and high-level planning.
 - **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Emphasize mapping observations and instructions to executable behaviors.
 - **World Action Models (WAMs):** Prioritize modeling environment evolution and action consequences.

#### 3.3. Identification of Challenges and Future Directions

Finally, based on the comprehensive review and analysis, the authors identify and discuss six key open challenges and corresponding future research directions related to embodied data. These are categorized into "what to collect," "how to collect it," and "how to use it," providing a structured roadmap for the community.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's primary findings and results are presented through its structured taxonomy and the insights derived from analyzing the current state of embodied AI data and its application in foundation models.

#### 4.1. The Embodied Data Pyramid Taxonomy

The core finding is the introduction and detailed characterization of the "data pyramid" for embodied manipulation. This taxonomy categorizes data sources based on their **scalability** and **robot alignment**, supplemented by **quality, diversity, reusability, and physical fidelity**.

- **Real-Robot Data (Apex):** Offers the highest physical fidelity and direct action executability on target hardware, making it the most direct form of supervision. However, it is the least scalable due to high costs, safety concerns, and labor-intensive collection (teleoperation, scripted methods, human-in-the-loop enhancements). Despite increasing scale (millions of trajectories, thousands of hours), diversity across embodiments and tasks remains a challenge.
- **UMI-style Data:** Provides an intermediate layer, enabling more scalable, robot-free collection of real-world demonstrations. It offers explicit end-effector action structure but requires retargeting to specific robot embodiments, and its reliability is dependent on accurate pose tracking and robust calibration. It balances physical grounding with improved collection efficiency.
- **Egocentric and Exocentric Data:** Occupies a crucial middle ground, offering scalable collection of natural human interactions in diverse environments. It excels in physical fidelity and diversity of human behaviors, but lacks direct robot action signals, requiring complex human-to-robot alignment and motion reconstruction. Wearable sensors are increasing the richness of this data (e.g., hand pose, gaze, tactile).
- **Simulation Data:** Represents the most scalable and cost-efficient robot-aligned layer. It allows for repeatable, parallel data generation with privileged labels (e.g., object poses, contacts, success signals). However, its utility is constrained by the "sim-to-real gap," arising from observation mismatch (rendering, materials) and interaction mismatch (kinematics, dynamics approximations).
- **General Data (Base):** Supplies web-scale semantic, perceptual, and reasoning priors. It is maximally scalable and diverse in terms of visual and linguistic content, but provides weak grounding in robot actions, contacts, and physical outcomes. Its primary role is to build high-level perception and reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.2. Evolution of Data Utilization in Embodied Foundation Models

The analysis reveals several trends in how embodied foundation models utilize data:

- **Growing Scale:** All data categories have experienced substantial growth in scale over time, with total pretraining data reaching tens to hundreds of thousands of hours for recent models.
- **Shift to Heterogeneous Mixtures:** There is a clear progression from models trained predominantly on single-source robot data to those employing increasingly diverse mixtures across multiple pyramid layers. This suggests a recognition of the complementary strengths of different data types.
- **Emergence of Egocentric Data:** Egocentric human data has become a significant component in pretraining recipes for many recent models, sometimes even serving as the primary or exclusive source, due to its scalability and rich human-object interaction coverage.
- **Relaxed Quality Requirements:** Some research indicates a potential shift towards tolerating lower-quality or noisy trajectories, suggesting that clear prompt conditioning might enable learning from a broader spectrum of data.
- **Action-Space and Geometric Alignment Strategies:** The paper details how models address heterogeneity in action spaces through:
 - **Cross-embodiment structural alignment:** Employing embodiment-specific projectors, fixed-dimensional zero-padded action interfaces, or semantic action slots for a shared policy backbone.
 - **Geometric alignment:** Using robot-centric, camera-centric, or wrist-centric coordinate frames to standardize end-effector action representations across datasets.

#### 4.3. Role of Data in Different Embodied Foundation Model Architectures

- **Embodied Brain Models (VLMs):** Primarily use **action-free data** (general multimodal, egocentric videos) to develop broad semantic understanding, visual commonsense, spatial-temporal reasoning, and task comprehension. **Action-labeled data** are used indirectly, converted into transferable supervision like affordance locations, grasp poses, and task waypoints to ground perception and reasoning in physical relevance.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Heavily rely on **action-labeled data** (real-robot, UMI, simulation, retargeted egocentric) for direct policy learning and action generation. Trends include using continuous action heads (diffusion, flow matching) for fine-grained control. **Action-free data** contributes through action-proxy learning (latent actions, geometric motion representations) and hierarchical supervision (task decomposition, subgoal prediction).
- **World Action Models (WAMs):** Focus on learning world dynamics and action effects. **Action-labeled data** supervise action-conditioned future prediction, aligning world evolution with executable robot control. **Action-free data** (general video, egocentric/exocentric videos) establish basic world priors, teaching object permanence, scene geometry, and typical temporal structures through observation-only future prediction.

#### 4.4. Identified Challenges and Future Directions

The report concludes with six key challenges:
1. **Tactile Data:** Insufficient large-scale tactile datasets for contact-rich robot learning.
2. **Failure and Recovery Data:** Scarcity of data on failures, near-failures, and recovery trajectories to improve policy robustness.
3. **Scalable Data-Collection Pipelines:** Need for less manual teleoperation, broader coverage, and adaptive collection strategies across all pyramid layers.
4. **Cross-Embodiment State-Action Alignment:** Ensuring consistent geometric semantics and coordinate conventions for states and actions across heterogeneous robot platforms.
5. **Egocentric Priors for Dexterous Manipulation:** Optimally leveraging human egocentric data despite the visual, kinematic, and physical gaps between human and robot hands.
6. **Principled Data Recipes:** Determining effective data compositions, sampling ratios, and stage-wise allocation strategies for combining diverse sources.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work offers several significant contributions and has the potential to impact the field of embodied AI in multiple ways:

- **Unified Conceptual Framework:** The introduction of the "data pyramid" provides a coherent and structured conceptual framework for understanding the complex and diverse data landscape in embodied AI. This framework enables researchers to systematically categorize, compare, and discuss different data sources, fostering clarity and common understanding in a rapidly evolving field.
- **Informed Data Strategy:** By meticulously characterizing each layer of the pyramid regarding its scalability, robot alignment, quality, diversity, reusability, and physical fidelity, the paper equips researchers and practitioners with insights for making informed decisions about which data sources to prioritize for specific research objectives, model architectures, and deployment scenarios.
- **Guidance for Resource Allocation:** The analysis of collection costs, advantages, and limitations for each data type can guide strategic investments in data acquisition infrastructure and methodologies. This includes highlighting the need for more scalable collection pipelines, especially for real-robot and tactile data, and more efficient ways to leverage abundant human-centric data.
- **Enhanced Model Development:** The detailed examination of how different data types are used by various embodied foundation models (embodied brains, VLAs, WAMs) provides a data-centric perspective on model design. This can lead to more principled approaches for pretraining, fine-tuning, and integrating heterogeneous data, potentially enabling the development of more capable, generalist robot policies.
- **Identification of Critical Research Gaps:** By explicitly outlining open challenges such as the scarcity of tactile and failure data, the complexities of cross-embodiment alignment, and the need for principled data recipes, the paper acts as a roadmap for future research. Addressing these challenges is crucial for advancing robust, adaptable, and physically grounded embodied agents.
- **Acceleration of Generalist Robot Learning:** Ultimately, by systematizing the data landscape and identifying pathways for improved data collection and utilization, this work lays a foundational groundwork for creating more general-purpose embodied AI systems. These systems could exhibit enhanced perception, reasoning, and physical interaction capabilities across diverse real-world tasks and environments, moving beyond task-specific solutions to truly generalist robots.
