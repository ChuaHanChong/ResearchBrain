# Gaussian Splatting Visual MPC for Granular Media Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Gaussian Splatting Visual MPC represents a granular pile as a set of 3D Gaussian splats reconstructed from multi-view RGBD, learns a graph-neural-network dynamics model that predicts each splat's translation and rotation under a pusher action, and plans pushes by gradient-based model-predictive control against a density-field cost. It beats particle, field, and NeRF-based baselines in simulation and transfers zero-shot to real nuts and beans.

## Problem

- Granular piles have intricate particle physics, high-dimensional partially observable state, and self-similar grains that defeat visual tracking and data association in a pile.
- Prior particle-based dynamics models scale poorly as particle count grows, making accurate physics prediction computationally expensive and impractical inside a planning loop.
- Expressive pixel-space latent dynamics models lack inductive bias and can underperform linear models; most granular baselines act in 2D and train only in simulation.

## Method

- ==Gaussian splatting perception module== lifts multi-view RGBD to a point cloud, downsamples by ==farthest point sampling==, and prunes background and high-transparency splats to form the state vector.
- ==Graph neural network dynamics model== builds edges between splats within a distance threshold, runs iterative ==message passing==, and decodes per-Gaussian displacement and rotation, trained with a ==Chamfer loss== over position and quaternion.
- ==Gradient-based visual MPC== samples action sequences, rolls out the learned model, and minimizes a ==density-field occupancy cost== between predicted and target splats, re-planning closed-loop after each executed push.

## Results

- In simulation the method leads all baselines on success rate: **0.89** collection, **0.79** splitting, **0.75** redistributing, versus the best baseline's **0.89**, **0.74**, and **0.67**.
- State error, measured as Chamfer distance, drops sharply against baselines: **0.0027**, **0.0041**, and **0.0081** across the three tasks, roughly an order of magnitude below the strongest competitor.
- Zero-shot real-robot transfer gives **0.85** collection success on pistachios, almonds, and peanuts, **0.65** on rolling coffee beans; ablations show more views and more message-passing steps both help.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A rendering representation can double as a control state: splats give a compact, 3D, view-consistent state vector where raw pixels or latent codes lack structure.
> - Graph-structured dynamics over reconstructed primitives supplies the inductive bias that lets a model generalize to unseen particle counts and unseen object shapes.
> - Reconstruction fidelity bounds controllability: very small grains like rice or salt break the representation, and greedy short-horizon planning caps performance on complex targets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing the challenges associated with handling granular materials. Recent advancements in learned 3D representations have enabled progress in manipulating rigid-body objects through methods like point clouds, voxel-based representations, and implicit neural representations such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs). However, manipulating granular materials like beans, nuts, and rice remains a complex problem due to their intricate particle interactions, high-dimensional and partially observable states, the inability to visually track individual particles within a pile, and the computational demands of accurate dynamics prediction.

Existing approaches to granular material manipulation often rely on deep latent dynamics models, which frequently exhibit limited generalization due to a lack of inductive biases. Physics-inspired methods, such such as particle-based models, offer stronger inductive biases but suffer from scalability issues, becoming computationally expensive and impractical as the number of particles increases. Furthermore, many previous granular manipulation systems either represent materials in 2D space, restricting their applicability to 3D tasks, or struggle with generalization across varying material types and conditions.

This research aims to bridge this gap by proposing Gaussian Splatting, a neural rendering technique, as a novel 3D visual representation for granular media. By leveraging Gaussian Splatting to capture the continuous 3D state of granular piles, combined with a Graph Neural Network (GNN) for dynamics modeling and Model-Predictive Control (MPC) for action planning, the authors propose a method that seeks to overcome the limitations of prior work. This approach offers a compact yet descriptive 3D representation that can encapsulate the 'bulk' properties of granular materials, providing stronger inductive biases than purely latent models without incurring the prohibitive computational cost of explicitly simulating every particle. It extends the utility of neural rendering techniques from scene synthesis to dynamic manipulation of complex, deformable materials.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robotic systems to efficiently and precisely manipulate granular materials using visual feedback, thereby solving complex manipulation tasks such as collecting, splitting, and redistributing granular piles into desired patterns. This overarching goal is driven by several identified challenges inherent to granular material manipulation:

1. **Intricate Physics and Unknown Geometry**: The complex interactions between numerous individual particles and the unknown geometries of these particles make it difficult to model their collective behavior accurately. Traditional physics-based models are computationally intensive and often unsuitable for real-time robotic control.
2. **High-Dimensional and Partially Observable State**: Accurately tracking and accounting for all particles in a granular pile results in an extremely high-dimensional state space. Moreover, the visual similarity of individual particles and self-occlusion within a pile make visual identification and tracking of discrete particles nearly impossible, leading to significant partial observability.
3. **Computational Expense of Dynamics Prediction**: Simulating the dynamics of granular materials, whether through physics engines or detailed particle-based models, is computationally demanding, hindering real-time planning and control.
4. **Limitations of Existing Deep Latent Dynamics Models**: While deep learning models can learn dynamics directly from pixels, they often lack specific inductive biases necessary to robustly generalize across different granular configurations or materials. This can lead to underperformance compared to even simpler linear dynamics models in some scenarios.
5. **Constrained 2D Representations in Prior Work**: Many existing data-driven approaches for granular manipulation operate in 2D, which limits their applicability to scenarios requiring full 3D manipulation.

To address these motivations, the authors propose exploring Gaussian Splatting as a suitable 3D representation. Gaussian Splatting, originally developed for image rendering and reconstruction, represents a 3D scene as a collection of individually parameterized Gaussians. This representation offers several advantages:
- It provides a smooth and continuous 3D encoding of the scene, which is potentially well-suited for modeling the bulk behavior of granular materials without needing to track individual particles.
- It offers a discrete set of parameterized entities (Gaussians) that can serve as a state vector for a dynamics model, effectively reducing the dimensionality compared to raw pixel data or explicit particle simulations.
- It can be learned from multi-view images, making it compatible with visual robotic setups.

The specific objectives derived from this motivation include:
- To investigate the effectiveness of Gaussian Splatting as a 3D representation for downstream model-predictive control of granular media.
- To develop and train a visual dynamics model that operates directly on Gaussian Splatting representations.
- To integrate this learned dynamics model within an MPC framework to enable complex and precise granular material manipulation.
- To empirically evaluate the proposed approach in both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating its capability to solve diverse planning tasks.
- To establish the generalization capabilities of the method, including its ability to perform zero-shot transfer to new environments and varying object shapes.
- To quantitatively demonstrate improved dynamics prediction and manipulation performance compared to established baseline methods in granular material manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates Gaussian Splatting for visual state representation with a Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based dynamics model and Model-Predictive Control (MPC) for granular media manipulation.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The problem is formulated as a trajectory optimization task. Given multi-view RGBD observations of the current granular material state ($O_0$) and a target state ($O_{target}$), the objective is to find a sequence of actions ($u_{0:T-1}$) over a planning horizon $T$ that minimizes a cost function $c(Z_T, Z_{target})$. Here, $Z_t$ represents the Gaussian Splatting state at time $t$, derived from observation $O_t$ via a perception module $h(\cdot)$, and $Z_{t+1} = f(Z_t, u_t)$ is the evolution of the state predicted by the learned dynamics model $f(\cdot)$. Each action $u_t \in \mathbb{R}^4$ specifies the starting position and pushing direction of the end-effector. A dataset $D_{RGBD} = \{(O_t, u_t, O_{t+1})\}$ is collected in a simulator to train the dynamics model.

**B. Representation of Granular Materials:**
The method employs Gaussian Splatting to represent the visual state of granular materials.
- **Gaussian Representation**: A 3D scene is represented as a collection of 3D Gaussians $G = \{n_i\}$, where each Gaussian $n_i$ is parameterized by its position $g_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, orientation $R_i \in SO(3)$, scale $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, opacity $\sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^+$, and color $c_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
- **Initialization**: Instead of the typical structure-from-motion, RGBD images are directly used to form an initial point cloud. This point cloud is then downsampled using farthest point sampling to manage computational load and facilitate downstream training.
- **Filtering**: Gaussians representing the background or possessing high transparency are removed, ensuring that $Z_t$ specifically captures the granular material.
- **Dataset Conversion**: The raw RGBD dataset $D_{RGBD}$ is transformed into $D_{GS} = \{(Z_t, u_t, Z_{t+1})\}$ by performing Gaussian Splatting reconstruction for each image frame.

**C. Learning a Visual Dynamics Model:**
A dynamics model $f(\cdot)$ is learned over these Gaussian Splatting representations using a Graph Neural Network (GNN).
- **Graph Construction**: The Gaussian Splatting representation $Z_t$ is converted into a graph $G_t = (V_t, E_t)$. Nodes $V_t = \{v_i^t\}$ correspond to individual Gaussians, with node features composed of $(c_i^t, \sigma_i^t, R_i^t, g_i^t, s_i^t)$. Edges $E_t$ are created between Gaussians if the $L_2$ distance between their respective positions is below a predefined threshold $\omega$.
- **GNN Architecture**: The GNN comprises three main components:
 - **Node Encoder ($f_{enc}$)**: Maps initial node features $v_i^t$ and the action $u_t$ to an initial node representation $\bar{v}_i^t$.
 - **Message-Passing Encoder ($f_{msg}$)**: Performs $\Gamma$ iterative message-passing steps. In each step, a node's representation is updated based on its previous state and the aggregated mean of its neighbors' representations.
 - **Decoder ($f_{dec}$)**: Transforms the final node features after $\Gamma$ message-passing steps ($q_i^\Gamma$) into predicted displacement $\Delta g_i^t$ and rotation $\Delta r_i^t$ for each Gaussian.
- **Gaussian Update**: The positions and orientations of the Gaussians are updated according to these predicted displacements and rotations: $\hat{g}_i^{t+1} = g_i^t + \Delta g_i^t$ and $\hat{r}_i^{t+1} = \Delta r_i \cdot r_i^t$ (where $r$ is the quaternion representation of $R$). This yields the predicted next state $\hat{Z}_{t+1}$.
- **Loss Function**: The dynamics model is trained using a Chamfer distance metric $L_{dyna}$ between the predicted Gaussian set $\hat{Z}_{t+1}$ and the ground-truth Gaussian set $Z_{t+1}$. The individual Gaussian distance $L_{gaussian}(n, \hat{n})$ is defined as the squared Euclidean distance between their positions plus a weighted term for the angular difference between their quaternion representations: $||g - \hat{g}||^2 + \lambda(1 - |r \cdot \hat{r}|)$.

**D. Planning:**
Model Predictive Control (MPC) is used for planning actions.
- **Cost Function**: A density field $d(x)$ is defined to represent the occupancy of granular material at any 3D position $x$, calculated as a sum of contributions from all Gaussians. The planning cost function $c(Z_t, Z_{target})$ measures the squared difference between the density fields of the current state $Z_t$ and the target state $Z_{target}$ over a predefined set of query points $P$: $c(Z_t, Z_{target}) = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{x \in P} |d_t(x) - d_{target}(x)|^2$.
- **MPC Loop (Algorithm 1)**: The planning algorithm is gradient-based. It takes the current observation, target, planning horizon, and dynamics model as input. It initializes $K$ action sequences, then iteratively optimizes each sequence by computing the task loss based on dynamics rollouts and its gradient. The sequence yielding the minimum cost is selected.
- **Closed-Loop Execution**: Only the first action of the optimized sequence is executed by the robot. The process then repeats in a closed-loop fashion, re-running the planning algorithm with updated observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed Gaussian Splatting Visual MPC approach was evaluated through experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, compared against several baselines on tasks including Collecting, Splitting, and Redistributing granular materials.

**Implementation Details:**
The framework was implemented using PyTorch and PyTorch-Geometric. The simulation environment was based on PyBullet, adapted from the Ravens framework, utilizing 50 cubic blocks as granular material. The real-world setup involved a Franka Panda manipulator with a planar pusher and four Intel RealSense cameras. Zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world was performed by resizing input images and aligning pusher dimensions. Real-world experiments used coffee beans, peanuts, pistachios, and almonds. Evaluation metrics included success rate (all materials in target region) and state error (Chamfer distance in simulation).

**Reconstruction and Dynamics Prediction Results:**
Qualitative assessments of the dynamics model rollouts (Figure 4) indicated that the approach effectively captured the state of the granular material. The movement of the material was predicted accurately over several steps in both simulated and real-world scenarios, suggesting the Gaussian Splatting representation and GNN dynamics model could effectively model granular behavior.

**Manipulation Results (Quantitative):**

- **Simulation Experiments (Tables I and II):**
 - The proposed approach consistently demonstrated superior performance over all baseline methods (NeRF-dy, Dyn-Res, NFD, DVF) in terms of both manipulation success rate and state error across all three tasks (Collecting, Splitting, Redistributing).
 - For **Collecting**, the method achieved a 0.89 success rate and 0.0027 state error, outperforming the next best, NFD (0.89 success, 0.0073 state error).
 - For **Splitting**, it achieved a 0.79 success rate and 0.0041 state error, significantly better than Dyn-Res (0.72 success, 0.0533 state error) and NFD (0.74 success, 0.0310 state error).
 - For **Redistributing**, the performance gap was particularly pronounced, with a 0.75 success rate and 0.0081 state error, whereas Dyn-Res achieved 0.67 success (0.0901 state error) and NFD 0.46 success (0.0660 state error).
 - The improvements over NeRF-dy were attributed to the explicit incorporation of particle clustering and physics-based priors inherent in the Gaussian Splat representation, in contrast to NeRF-dy's reliance on less structured NeRF reconstructions for dynamics learning. NFD, while performing well on simpler tasks, exhibited limitations with more complex target patterns.

- **Real-World Experiments (Table III):**
 - The approach maintained high manipulation performance in real-world settings through zero-shot transfer for most tested materials.
 - For **Collection**, success rates were 0.85 for pistachios, almonds, and peanuts, and 0.65 for coffee beans.
 - For **Splitting**, success rates were 0.80 for pistachios, 0.75 for almonds, 0.85 for peanuts, and 0.60 for coffee beans.
 - Qualitative results (Figure 5) showed successful manipulation of various granular materials despite differences in scale and physical properties.

**Generalization Studies (Ablations):**

- **Number of Views (Figure 6):** Increasing the number of input viewpoints generally improved manipulation performance. The proposed method achieved higher performance than NeRF-dy even with fewer views, suggesting its representation is more robust or efficient.
- **Generalization to Different Numbers of Particles (Figure 7):** Despite being trained with a fixed number of 50 particles, the method demonstrated robust generalization to varying particle counts in the workspace. This generalization capability was attributed to the inductive biases of the Graph Neural Network, which processes the Gaussians as a graph, making it adaptable to changes in graph size. In contrast, baselines relying on latent vectors or raw visual observations struggled with different particle distributions.
- **Message Passing in Dynamics (Figure 8):** Ablation studies confirmed the crucial role of message passing within the GNN dynamics model. An increased number of message-passing steps correlated with improved manipulation performance, indicating that inter-Gaussian communication is vital for accurately capturing complex granular material dynamics and enabling precise long-term predictions necessary for effective manipulation.

**Limitations:**
The authors identified several limitations:
- **Manipulation Efficiency**: The inherent complexity of granular dynamics necessitated limiting the robot arm's movement speed to ensure stable and controllable manipulation.
- **Particle Size Limitation**: The framework is less effective for manipulating very small particles (e.g., salt, sugar, rice) because Gaussian Splatting struggles to maintain reconstruction precision at such tiny scales.
- **Shortsighted Planning**: The current MPC, while effective for optimizing immediate trajectories, focuses on short-term decision-making. More complex tasks or configurations might benefit from longer planning horizons to identify truly optimal multi-step actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing and validating Gaussian Splatting as a novel and effective 3D visual representation for controlling granular materials.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **address long-standing challenges** in granular material manipulation. By representing the amorphous and dynamic nature of granular piles as a collection of smooth 3D Gaussians, the method provides a compact yet expressive state representation. This approach effectively circumvents the difficulties associated with modeling intricate particle-level physics, high-dimensional state spaces, partial observability, and the computational burden of traditional physics-based simulations or less-structured neural representations.

The demonstrated **superior performance** in both simulation and real-world tasks (Collecting, Splitting, Redistributing) against established baselines highlights the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework. The robust generalization capabilities, particularly to varying numbers of particles and zero-shot transfer to new environments, are critical for real-world applicability, where material quantities and compositions can vary. The integration of a GNN for dynamics learning, leveraging the graph structure of Gaussian splats, provides the necessary inductive biases for this generalization.

The work's **potential impact** is multi-faceted:

- **Advancement in Robotics**: It expands the frontier of robotic manipulation to non-rigid and deformable materials, which are prevalent in many industrial, agricultural, and domestic settings (e.g., food handling, construction, pharmaceutical manufacturing). By enabling robots to reliably handle such challenging materials, the research contributes to the development of more versatile and autonomous robotic systems.
- **Novel Application of 3D Visual Representations**: It demonstrates a novel and effective application of Gaussian Splatting beyond its typical use cases in scene reconstruction and view synthesis. This showcases the broader utility of advanced neural rendering techniques as foundational representations for understanding and interacting with dynamic 3D environments.
- **Improved Model Learning for Dynamic Systems**: The success of the GNN-based dynamics model operating on Gaussian splats provides a compelling example of how structured 3D representations, combined with appropriate neural network architectures, can yield more accurate and generalizable learned dynamics models for complex physical systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The framework provides a strong foundation for future research. Extending this approach to other non-rigid materials (e.g., fabrics, liquids, gels) or to more complex manipulation primitives beyond pushing could unlock further capabilities for robotic systems in dynamic and unstructured environments. Addressing the identified limitations, such as optimizing manipulation efficiency and handling extremely fine particles, would also be a valuable direction for subsequent work.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the state-of-the-art in granular material manipulation by offering a robust, generalized, and visually-driven control strategy, thereby paving the way for more capable and adaptive robotic manipulation systems.
