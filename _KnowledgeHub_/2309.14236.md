# MoDem-V2: Visuo-Motor World Models for Real-World Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MoDem-V2 is a demonstration-augmented visual model-based RL system that learns contact-rich manipulation directly on real hardware from raw pixels, proprioception, and sparse rewards. It adds three safety ingredients to MoDem: sampling actions centered on a behavior-cloned policy, gradually handing agency from that policy to latent MPC planning, and actor-critic ensembles for uncertainty-aware value estimation. It learns pushing, picking, and in-hand reorientation within about two hours of real interaction.

## Problem

- Visual model-based RL is sample-inefficient and reward-starved, so prior demo-bootstrapped methods explore aggressively, confining them to simulation or heavily engineered setups.
- MoDem samples across the full action space from the start, where its world model and value function are untrained, producing torque and force violations that fault real hardware immediately.
- Hard velocity, acceleration, and torque limits or torque reward penalties are retrospective and task-specific, failing to stop unsafe actions at the onset of online exploration.

## Method

- ==Policy centering==: candidate actions are sampled from the behavior-cloned policy rather than the whole action space, keeping ==world-model== and critic evaluation near demonstration-supported regions.
- ==Agency transfer==: a probability parameter alpha rises linearly from 0 to 1 over a fixed step budget, shifting execution from BC actions to short-horizon ==latent MPC== as model coverage grows.
- ==Actor-critic ensembles==: five independently trained critics score trajectories, and the plan objective mixes ensemble mean with a negatively weighted standard deviation to penalize epistemic uncertainty.

## Results

- Across four simulated tasks, **MoDem-V2** matched or beat MoDem's sample efficiency while cutting safety violations sharply, and outperformed DAPG despite DAPG using privileged state and dense rewards.
- On real hardware MoDem beat torque limits within the first **two** exploration episodes of inclined pushing; MoDem-V2 exceeded its ten-demo BC policy within about **two hours** or less, roughly **8 minutes** for pushing tasks.
- Ablations show centering-plus-schedule gives the safety gains and the ensemble gives efficiency; torque reward penalties swept over **three orders of magnitude** left the safety profile near baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward penalties correct unsafe behavior after the fact; constraining the action distribution before the model is trustworthy is what prevents faults at exploration onset.
> - Safe and efficient exploration trade off differently across ingredients, so combining a conservative action schedule with an uncertainty-aware value ensemble recovers both properties.
> - Real deployment exposes failure modes that simulation studies suppress, including hardware faults, reset mechanisms, and visual success detection needed for sparse reward.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning, particularly for manipulation tasks, is highly active, with significant efforts focused on bridging the gap between simulated environments and the complexities of the real world. This paper addresses a critical challenge within this landscape: enabling vision-based, model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) to operate safely and effectively on physical robots.

**Traditional Approaches and Their Limitations:**
1. **Simulation-to-Reality (Sim-to-Real) Transfer:** A common paradigm where policies are trained in simulation and then deployed on real robots. While successful for many tasks, it often struggles with *contact-rich manipulation* due to the difficulty of creating and calibrating accurate physics simulations (e.g., friction, deformation) that precisely mirror the real world. Discrepancies between simulation and reality ("reality gap") can lead to poor performance or unsafe behavior.
2. **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning (MFRL):** Can learn directly in the real world, but is notoriously *sample-inefficient*, requiring vast amounts of interaction data, which is time-consuming, expensive, and often unsafe for physical robots.
3. **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):** Aims to improve sample efficiency by learning a dynamics model of the environment. When combined with visual world models, MBRL can eliminate the need for explicit state estimation and specialized sensor instrumentation. However, visual MBRL has historically faced its own set of challenges:
 - **Sample Inefficiency:** While better than model-free, still requires significant data.
 - **Sparse/Weakly Shaped Rewards:** Difficult to obtain dense, well-shaped reward signals from raw visual inputs in complex real-world tasks.
 - **Aggressive Exploration:** A major drawback, especially for real-world applications. Existing MBRL methods (like the original MoDem) often explore aggressively to compensate for weak reward supervision or to discover optimal behaviors, leading to actions that can be unsafe, damage hardware, or require frequent human intervention.

**The Niche of MoDem-V2:**
MoDem-V2 carves out a crucial niche by directly addressing the *safety and exploration challenges* of visual MBRL when applied to *real-world, contact-rich robot manipulation*. The original MoDem (V1) demonstrated strong sample efficiency in simulation but failed to transfer to real robots due to its aggressive exploration leading to safety faults. This paper identifies this as a "root cause" limiting the applicability of otherwise promising MBRL algorithms.

By focusing on "conservative exploration" and "uncertainty-aware planning," MoDem-V2 aims to bridge the gap between highly efficient simulated learning and robust, safe real-world deployment. It combines the benefits of demonstration-augmented learning (for initial guidance and sample efficiency) with intelligent exploration strategies tailored for physical systems. This work represents a significant step towards practical, autonomous robot learning that can operate in uninstrumented real-world environments, a long-standing goal in robotics and AI. It moves beyond purely sim-to-real or purely model-free real-world RL by proposing a safer, more viable approach to real-world model-based learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic system capable of safely and efficiently learning complex, contact-rich manipulation skills directly in uninstrumented real-world environments, utilizing only raw visual feedback and sparse reward signals.

The core motivations behind this objective stem from critical limitations observed in existing robot learning paradigms:

1. **Addressing Real-World Safety and Efficiency:** Robotic systems operating in the real world cannot afford inefficient learning (which is costly) or unsafe behavior (which can be catastrophic for hardware and surrounding environments). Vision-based learning, while desirable for its generality (eliminating the need for explicit state estimation or specialized instrumentation), exacerbates exploration challenges in contact-rich, high-dimensional search spaces due to sparse visual rewards.
2. **Overcoming Simulation-to-Reality Gaps:** While sim-to-real transfer is an option, creating and calibrating accurate physics simulations for contact-rich tasks is immensely challenging and time-consuming. This paper seeks an alternative by enabling learning directly from real-world interaction.
3. **Mitigating Aggressive Exploration in Visual MBRL:** The paper highlights that current state-of-the-art visual Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) algorithms, specifically the predecessor MoDem (V1), suffer from excessively aggressive exploration. While effective in simulation, this aggressiveness translates into unsafe behaviors on physical robots, frequently violating hardware limits (velocity, acceleration, torque) and leading to safety faults that halt training or damage equipment. This makes them impractical for real-world deployment.
4. **Enabling Learning from Sparse Rewards:** Collecting dense, finely-shaped reward functions for real-world tasks is labor-intensive and often requires extensive instrumentation. The goal is to enable learning with only sparse task completion signals, which are much easier to obtain visually.
5. **Leveraging Demonstrations for Sample Efficiency:** Expert demonstrations are a powerful tool to bootstrap learning and improve sample efficiency. The objective is to integrate demonstrations effectively into a visual MBRL framework while ensuring safe online exploration.

In essence, the motivation is to isolate the "root causes" of safety limitations in demonstration-augmented visual MBRL, which are identified as unsafe exploration and over-optimistic evaluation of actions in unexperienced regions. By addressing these, MoDem-V2 aims to provide a robust, practical solution for robotic manipulation that bridges the gap between simulation-level efficiency and real-world safety and reliability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MoDem-V2 is an enhancement of the MoDem (Model-Based Reinforcement Learning with Demonstrations) algorithm, specifically designed to enable safe and efficient learning of contact-rich manipulation skills directly on real-world robots. The methodology centers on addressing MoDem's tendency for aggressive, unsafe exploration in physical environments while retaining its sample efficiency.

**Foundation: MoDem (Model-Based RL with Demonstrations)**
MoDem itself is built upon a three-stage framework:
1. **Pretraining:** A state embedding (`hθ`) and a policy (`πθ`) are pretrained using Behavior Cloning (BC) on a small set of expert demonstrations.
2. **Seeding:** The BC policy, with injected Gaussian noise for initial exploration, is used to collect a small initial dataset for learning the world model.
3. **Online Interaction & Iterative Optimization:** The world model (comprising a state embedding, latent dynamics, reward predictor, and terminal value function) is iteratively refined using data from demonstrations, seeding, and ongoing online interaction. Action selection is performed via Model Predictive Control (MPC) in the learned latent space using the world model and value function.

**Limitations of Original MoDem in Real-World:**
The authors identified that MoDem, when applied directly to real robots, frequently exerted excessive forces and torques, leading to hardware faults. This aggressive exploration stems from two main issues:
- **Poor Initial Value Estimates:** At the start of online interaction, the world model and value function have only seen data close to the BC policy. When MoDem samples actions broadly across the entire action space, it cannot reliably estimate the value of actions far from its experience, leading to potentially dangerous choices.
- **Over-optimism:** The value function can be over-optimistic in unexplored regions, encouraging the agent to take risky actions.

**MoDem-V2: Three Key Adaptations for Safety and Efficiency**
To overcome these limitations, MoDem-V2 integrates three novel components into MoDem's planning procedure:

1. **Policy-Centered Actions (Conservative Exploration):**
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of sampling candidate action sequences for MPC from a broad distribution across the entire action space, MoDem-V2 initially centers its exploration around actions generated by the learned Behavioral Cloning (BC) policy.
 - **Rationale:** This conservative strategy ensures that at the onset of online learning, the agent's actions remain close to the known safe and effective trajectories provided by demonstrations. This reduces the likelihood of the world model and value function being queried in completely unseen, potentially hazardous regions of the state-action space, allowing for more reliable action quality discrimination.

2. **Gradual Agency Transfer from BC Actions to MPC (Agency Handover):**
 - **Mechanism:** A hyperparameter `α` is introduced into the planning algorithm. Initially, `α` is set to 0, meaning the agent primarily executes actions directly sampled from the BC policy. Over a fixed number of online interaction steps, `α` linearly increases to 1.0, gradually shifting the agent's action selection towards short-horizon planning using its learned world model and value function (MPC).
 - **Rationale:** At the beginning of online interaction, the learned world model and value function are still immature. Relying solely on them for multi-step planning can quickly lead the robot into unexplored and unrecoverable states. Gradual agency transfer allows the world model to gain sufficient experience and improve its predictive accuracy in a safe, guided manner before it takes full control of action generation, providing a controlled increase in exploration.

3. **Actor-Critic Ensembles for Uncertainty-Aware Planning:**
 - **Mechanism:** MoDem-V2 employs an ensemble of multiple independently trained actor-critic networks. Each actor is trained to maximize its corresponding critic. During planning, the ensemble provides multiple estimates of the terminal value (`Qθ`) for candidate trajectories. These multiple estimates are then used to estimate the *epistemic uncertainty* (uncertainty due to limited data) of a trajectory. The final value used for action selection combines the mean and standard deviation of these ensemble predictions, using weights `w1` (positive, for mean) and `w2` (negative, for standard deviation) to explicitly penalize uncertain (and thus potentially risky) actions.
 - **Rationale:** Ensembles address two issues:
 - **Overestimation Bias:** Individual critics can suffer from overestimation bias. By evaluating a critic with actions produced by other policies in the ensemble, this bias can be mitigated.
 - **Safe Exploration:** By explicitly modeling and penalizing uncertainty, the agent is encouraged to choose actions in regions where its model is more confident, thus promoting safer exploration and avoiding overly optimistic evaluations of unknown territories.

**Overall Approach:**
MoDem-V2 integrates these three components into a coherent planning and learning loop. The combination ensures that exploration is initially constrained by known safe behaviors (demonstrations), gradually expands as the world model becomes more competent, and is constantly guided by an awareness of the model's uncertainty, leading to a significantly improved safety profile without sacrificing the core sample efficiency benefits of MBRL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MoDem-V2, conducted across four contact-rich manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world, yielded several significant findings:

1. **Drastically Improved Safety Profile in Simulation:**
 - **Safety Violations:** The most critical finding is that MoDem-V2 exhibits significantly fewer safety violations (defined as exceeding manufacturer-specified torque limits or excessive contact force) compared to the original MoDem and other baselines. While MoDem's safety violations sharply increased at the onset of online interaction, MoDem-V2 maintained a consistently lower number of violations throughout training.
 - **Ineffectiveness of Torque Penalization:** Simple additive reward penalization for high torques was found to be ineffective. It did not prevent the initial spike in safety violations and could even stagnate learning in harder tasks, highlighting the necessity of MoDem-V2's architectural changes.

2. **Maintained or Improved Sample Efficiency:**
 - **Comparison with Baselines:** MoDem-V2 and the original MoDem both significantly outperformed other demonstration-augmented reinforcement learning baselines like DAPG (which even had access to privileged state information and dense rewards) and FERM in terms of sample efficiency.
 - **Early Training Performance:** While MoDem eventually caught up in success rate on some tasks, MoDem-V2 showed superior performance in the early stages of training across all tasks, avoiding the performance drop associated with MoDem's aggressive initial exploration.

3. **Successful Real-World Deployment and Learning:**
 - **MoDem Failure:** The original MoDem was deemed unsafe for direct real-world application. Attempts to run it resulted in frequent hardware safety faults (e.g., excessive force/torque on the inclined pushing task) within the first few exploration episodes, making its evaluation infeasible without significant, constant human intervention.
 - **MoDem-V2 Success:** In stark contrast, MoDem-V2 successfully enabled a physical robot (Franka Panda arm with Robotiq or D'Manus grippers) to learn all four complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks (planar pushing, inclined pushing, bin picking, in-hand reorientation) directly in the real world with *minimal human intervention*.
 - **Rapid Skill Acquisition:** MoDem-V2 agents were able to significantly surpass the performance of their initial Behavioral Cloning (BC) policies in a short amount of real-world interaction time, typically within two hours or less of online training data.

4. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Contributions:**
 - Individual ablations of MoDem-V2's three enhancements (Policy Centering, Agency Transfer, Actor-Critic Ensembles) revealed their specific benefits.
 - "Schedule" (representing the gradual agency transfer and policy centering) was crucial for improving safety.
 - "Ensemble" (actor-critic ensembles) generally led to better sample efficiency.
 - MoDem-V2, by combining these ingredients, effectively achieved the best of both worlds: high sample efficiency along with a robust safety profile.

In summary, the key result is the demonstration that MoDem-V2 is the first successful system for demonstration-augmented visual MBRL trained directly in the real world, effectively overcoming the critical barrier of unsafe exploration that hindered its predecessors, while retaining excellent sample efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MoDem-V2 represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for real-world applications. Its impact can be multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Safe and Practical Real-World Robot Learning:** The most immediate and profound impact is making advanced visual Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) algorithms practical and safe for deployment on physical robots. By specifically addressing the aggressive exploration issue, MoDem-V2 unlocks the potential of sample-efficient, vision-based learning for complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks that were previously too risky or impractical for direct real-world training. This is a crucial step towards truly autonomous robot learning beyond simulated environments.

2. **Reducing Development Costs and Time:**
 - **Less Reliance on Physics Simulations:** For contact-rich tasks, MoDem-V2 mitigates the need for meticulously crafted and calibrated physics simulations, which are notoriously difficult and time-consuming to create accurately. Learning directly in the real world bypasses the "reality gap" challenge.
 - **Lower Data Requirements:** The high sample efficiency (requiring only a few hours of interaction data and a small number of demonstrations) significantly reduces the time and expense associated with data collection compared to model-free approaches.
 - **Minimal Environment Instrumentation:** The reliance on raw visual feedback and sparse rewards removes the need for expensive and complex environment instrumentation or explicit state estimation pipelines, further lowering deployment barriers.

3. **Increased Robot Autonomy and Capabilities:** The ability to learn intricate manipulation skills like in-hand reorientation from vision and sparse rewards empowers robots with greater autonomy. This could expand the range of tasks robots can perform in unstructured or semi-structured environments, from logistics and manufacturing to service robotics and assistive technologies.

4. **Advancements in Safe Reinforcement Learning:** MoDem-V2 provides concrete, effective algorithmic solutions for safe exploration in complex, partially observable environments. The techniques of policy-centered exploration, gradual agency transfer, and uncertainty-aware planning with actor-critic ensembles are generalizable principles that can influence future research in safe RL for other safety-critical domains. This moves the field beyond theoretical discussions of safety to practical, deployable implementations.

5. **Lowering the Barrier to Entry for Robot RL:** By open-sourcing their implementation and discussing practical considerations, the authors directly contribute to making real-world RL more accessible for other researchers and practitioners. This can accelerate innovation and collaboration within the robotics community, fostering new applications and further methodological improvements.

6. **Paving the Way for More Complex Tasks:** The success across a diverse suite of manipulation tasks, including challenging ones like in-hand reorientation with a dexterous hand, suggests that MoDem-V2 (and its future iterations) could be a foundation for learning even more sophisticated and adaptive behaviors required for general-purpose robotic agents.

In essence, MoDem-V2 represents a practical breakthrough, moving real-world robot manipulation closer to a future where robots can learn new skills quickly, safely, and autonomously, directly from interaction with their physical environment.
