# Robust Adaptive Safe Robotic Grasping with Tactile Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> An integrated framework combines tactile sensing with Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) to provide formal safety guarantees for robust adaptive robotic grasping, preventing object damage and slippage. The Disturbance Observer CBF (DOBCBF) scheme achieved the least conservatism in experiments, reducing it by an average of 83.5% compared to purely robust methods while maintaining strict adherence to safety bounds.

## Problem

- Existing robotic grasping methods often focus on kinematic aspects or stability, neglecting contact dynamics and lacking formal safety guarantees against object damage or slippage.
- Current force-based grasping techniques do not rigorously ensure forces remain within safe minimum and maximum bounds or robustly achieve force closure, especially with model uncertainty and external disturbances.
- The absence of formal safety guarantees limits the applicability of robotic grasping in sensitive real-world scenarios involving fragile, valuable, or hazardous objects.

## Method

- An integrated framework combines ==tactile sensing==, a fingertip contact force controller, and ==CBF-based safety filters== (Standard CBF, RaCBF, RCBF, **DOBCBF**) designed to operate within a ROS environment.
- Safety constraints are formally defined for ==contact force regulation== (within minimum/maximum bounds) and ==force closure== (to prevent slippage) using a ==Kelvin-Voigt contact model== and a soft finger friction cone model.
- Techniques are developed to estimate contact points, forces, and torques from ==electromagnetic tactile sensors==, providing real-time feedback for the CBF-based controllers.

## Results

- Numerical simulations showed standard CBF failed under parametric uncertainty, leading to safety violations, while RaCBF, RCBF, and DOBCBF successfully maintained safe contact forces and prevented slippage.
- Hardware experiments on a Shadow Hand with diverse objects, including fragile lab glassware, confirmed that RaCBF, RCBF, and particularly DOBCBF achieved safe grasping without violations, whereas standard CBF consistently failed.
- **DOBCBF** demonstrated the least conservative performance in both simulations and hardware, showing an average **83.5%** reduction in conservatism compared to prior purely robust CBF methods, while effectively estimating and compensating for disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CBF-based safety filters can effectively provide formal safety guarantees for robotic grasping by modifying control inputs to prevent contact force violations and slippage.
> - Adaptive and disturbance-observing CBF schemes (RaCBF and DOBCBF) significantly reduce conservatism in safe grasping, enabling more efficient operation closer to safety boundaries than standard or purely robust CBFs.
> - Integrating real-time, tactile sensor-based estimation of contact points, forces, and torques is crucial for responsive and practical CBF-based safe grasping in dynamic, uncertain environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping has extensively explored methods to enable robots to interact with objects for various applications, including industrial assembly, grocery packing, and household tasks. Historically, much of this research has focused on the kinematic aspects of grasping, such as determining appropriate finger postures, as noted in references and. However, this kinematic focus often overlooks the crucial role of contact dynamics, which limits the dexterity and safety of robotic grasps, particularly in scenarios requiring prevention of slippage or object damage.

In response to these limitations, a segment of the research community has shifted towards force-based grasping techniques. Examples include adaptive force control frameworks, robust force controllers for secure grasping, impedance-based controllers incorporating task-based strategies, and tactile sensor-based force regulation. Concurrently, the modeling of contact dynamics, including internal and friction forces, has been utilized to achieve stable finger gaits and robust manipulation under dynamic changes. Online adaptive control strategies have also been developed to account for disturbances and model errors in dexterous manipulation.

Despite advancements in stable, compliant, and robust force-based grasping, a significant gap remains concerning formal safety guarantees. Many existing force-based methods primarily aim for stability without explicitly addressing safety considerations such as preventing excessive force that could damage an object or ensuring sufficient force to prevent slippage. The necessity of such safety guarantees is highlighted by applications in laboratory automation and space robotics, where object integrity and task reliability are paramount. While some works have explored safe grasping through impact force measurements, safety-optimized strategies with visual data, and transformer-based predictions for safe grasping forces, a comprehensive framework that integrates tactile sensing with formal safety guarantees for force-based grasping control remains largely unexplored.

This paper positions itself to address this gap by leveraging Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), a method for formally ensuring safety guarantees through reachability analysis and set invariance techniques. CBF-based controllers enforce the forward invariance of a defined safe set and can be implemented efficiently via quadratic programming, making them suitable for real-time robotic applications. While prior work, such as, introduced robust CBFs for safe grasping to handle external disturbances and avoid slippage, this paper aims to extend this by presenting an integrated framework that explicitly incorporates tactile sensing and explores a broader range of robust and adaptive CBF schemes to improve performance in terms of conservatism and adaptation to uncertainty. The work contrasts itself with by incorporating adaptation to parametric uncertainty and estimating external disturbances, thereby aiming for reduced conservatism and more precise force regulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the observation that while robotic grasping has made progress in dexterous manipulation, it often falls short in providing formal safety guarantees for force interactions. Human hands are capable of adapting to various objects and tasks through smooth and safe force interactions. Robotic counterparts, however, frequently overlook contact dynamics or lack mechanisms to rigorously prevent object damage or slippage. This limitation is particularly critical in applications involving fragile, valuable, or hazardous objects (e.g., lab glassware, medical instruments, space equipment) where safety regulations and object integrity are non-negotiable. Current force-based grasping methods primarily focus on stability, often neglecting the explicit regulation of forces within safe minimum and maximum bounds or robustly ensuring force closure in the presence of uncertainties. The absence of formal safety guarantees restricts the broader applicability of robotic grasping in sensitive real-world scenarios.

To address these challenges, the paper establishes several key objectives:

1. **Develop an Integrated Framework for Safe Grasping with Formal Guarantees:** The primary objective is to propose a comprehensive framework that integrates robust force control with formal safety guarantees. This framework is intended to maintain maximum and minimum safe grasping forces and ensure safe force closure, particularly in the presence of model uncertainty and external disturbances.
2. **Design and Enforce Contact Force and Force Closure Constraints:** A central objective is to formally define safety constraints related to contact force regulation (to prevent excessive force or insufficient force) and force closure (to avoid slippage). These constraints are to be enforced through various CBF-based safety filters.
3. **Implement and Evaluate Multiple CBF-based Safety Filters:** The work aims to demonstrate the scalability and comparative performance of the framework by integrating and evaluating several safety filter schemes: a standard Control Barrier Function (CBF), Robust Adaptive CBF (RaCBF), Robust CBF (RCBF), and Disturbance Observer CBF (DOBCBF). This comparative analysis is intended to identify the most effective approach for ensuring safety with minimal conservatism.
4. **Develop Techniques for Tactile Sensor-based Contact Estimation:** To enable effective force control and informed safety filtering, an objective is to develop and implement sensor processing techniques. These techniques aim to accurately estimate contact points, contact forces, and contact torques from electromagnetic tactile sensors embedded on the fingertips of a dexterous robotic hand.
5. **Empirically Validate the Framework in Real-World Scenarios:** A critical objective is to experimentally validate the proposed framework using a real robotic setup. This includes grasping multiple objects, specifically highlighting the ability to safely grasp fragile items like laboratory glassware, to demonstrate its practical effectiveness and robustness in scenarios requiring strict force regulation and slippage prevention.
6. **Quantitatively Compare Safety Filter Performance:** The paper aims to provide detailed comparisons of each safety filter's performance with respect to safety violation rates and the level of conservatism exhibited, both in simulation and hardware experiments. This objective seeks to identify which filter provides superior safety guarantees while minimizing unnecessary restrictions on control actions.

Through these objectives, the research seeks to advance robotic grasping by providing a method that is not only robust and adaptive but also formally guaranteed to be safe, thereby broadening its applicability to complex and sensitive tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for robust adaptive safe robotic grasping is an integrated system comprising three main components: safety filters, a fingertip contact force controller, and a contact information estimation module utilizing tactile sensors. The overall architecture is designed to operate within a Robot Operating System (ROS) environment.

**A. Contact Dynamics Modeling**
The framework models contact dynamics using a second-order Kelvin-Voigt model, which describes contact forces based on penetration depth (`p`) and its velocity (`ṗ`). This model captures both stiffness (`k`) and damping (`b`) properties. Two state-space representations are used:
- System (1): Represents contact force dynamics with parametric uncertainty, where unknown parameters `θ*` (e.g., stiffness and damping) affect the system.
- System (2): Represents contact force dynamics with an unknown additive bounded external disturbance `d`.
Additionally, a soft finger friction cone model is employed to define force closure, approximating it linearly for practical implementation.

**B. Safety-Critical Grasping Constraints**
Two primary safety constraints are defined:
1. **Contact Force Constraint:** To prevent both excessive force that could damage an object and insufficient force leading to slippage, the contact force (`f_c`) is constrained within a specified minimum (`f_min`) and maximum (`f_max`) range. This defines two safe sets, `H_f_min` and `H_f_max`, whose intersection forms `H_f_c`.
2. **Force Closure Constraint:** To prevent slippage, a linearized Coulomb friction cone model is used. This model specifies a relationship between tangential forces, normal force, and torsional friction, defining a safe set `H_s`.
The overall safe set for grasping, `H`, is the intersection of `H_f_c` and `H_s`.

**C. Safety Filters for Safe Grasping**
The core of the safety mechanism lies in CBF-based safety filters. These filters modify a nominal control input (`u_nominal`) to a safe control input (`u_safe`) by solving a quadratic program that minimizes the deviation from `u_nominal` while satisfying the safety constraints.
- **Standard CBF (Baseline):** A standard CBF is used as a baseline, enforcing safety for system (2) without explicitly accounting for disturbances. It serves to highlight the limitations when model uncertainties are present.
- **Robust Adaptive CBF (RaCBF):** For systems with parametric uncertainty (System 1), RaCBF incorporates a tightened safe set that considers the maximum possible estimation error of the unknown parameters (`θ*`). An adaptation law updates the parameter estimate (`θ̂`) dynamically, making the control less conservative than a purely robust approach by adapting to the uncertainty.
- **Robust CBF (RCBF):** For systems with bounded external disturbances (System 2), RCBF accounts for the worst-case effect of these disturbances by including a maximum disturbance bound (`w_0`) in its safety conditions. This ensures safety despite unknown but bounded disturbances.
- **Disturbance Observer CBF (DOBCBF):** This approach integrates a Nonlinear Disturbance Observer (NDOB) with CBFs. The NDOB estimates the external disturbance `d` in real-time. This estimate (`d̂`) is then directly incorporated into the safety conditions, allowing for a less conservative control action compared to assuming a worst-case disturbance bound. The NDOB uses problem-specific gain functions `P(x)` and `L_d(x)`.

**D. Fingertip Contact Force Controller**
To track the safe control inputs generated by the safety filters, a joint position-based force controller is implemented for each robotic finger. This controller uses a proportional gain (`K_p`) to drive the error between desired and current contact force (`f̃`). The desired joint reference trajectory (`q_ref`) is calculated using a PID-like control law, taking into account error torque (`τ̃`) derived from the contact force error and the Jacobian of the finger.

**E. Estimation of Contact Points, Forces, and Torques**
The Shadow Hand, equipped with seventeen electromagnetic tactile sensors per finger, provides three-axis magnetic values that reflect fingertip deformations.
- **Force and Torque Estimation:** A linear regression model is established to map these tactile sensor deformations to contact forces. This model is derived from experimental data where the hand presses against an external 6-axis force/torque sensor. The contact torque (`τ_c`) is then approximated as the cross product of the estimated contact point and contact force.
- **Contact Point Estimation:** A novel estimator determines the contact point (`p_cop`) on the fingertip. It first identifies the sensor with the highest magnitude (`s*`) and selects a set (`S_o`) including `s*` and its surrounding `k` sensors. The `p_cop` is then calculated as a weighted combination of the positions of these selected sensors, with weights (`γ`) normalized according to their sensor values. The contact force (`f_c`) is approximated by averaging the values of `S_o`.

**F. Experimental Setup and Validation**
- **Simulation:** Numerical simulations are conducted in MATLAB for a two-finger grasping scenario. The Kelvin-Voigt model is used as the "real" system, and parametric uncertainties (in stiffness and damping) are introduced to evaluate the robustness of the safety filters.
- **Hardware Experiments:** The framework is implemented in C++ on a robotic setup consisting of a Shadow Hand controlled by an ASUS laptop (high-level controller at 125Hz) and an Intel NUC (low-level tracking controller at 1kHz). Objects of varying fragility and material (wooden cube, plastic cylinder, aluminum bearing housing, fragile lab glassware) are used. The friction coefficients for each object are empirically tuned. Safety constraints include normal force regulation (`h_1`) and vertical friction cone planes (`h_2`, `h_3`). A linear function `α(h(x))` is used for the extended class K∞ function in CBFs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed safe grasping framework across both numerical simulations and physical hardware experiments.

**A. Numerical Simulation Results**
The simulations focused on a two-finger grasping scenario, introducing parametric model uncertainties in stiffness and damping to challenge the safety filters.
- **Standard CBF Performance:** The standard CBF, lacking robust mechanisms, demonstrated sensitivity to model parameter discrepancies. It resulted in safety violations, manifesting as initial application of excessive force (violating minimum force regulation) followed by slippage due to insufficient grasping force. This also caused the friction cone constraint (`h_1`) to be violated, confirming slippage.
- **Robust CBF (RCBF) and Robust Adaptive CBF (RaCBF) Performance:** Both RCBF and RaCBF exhibited robustness to the induced model uncertainty. They successfully maintained contact forces within safe limits and prevented slippage, ensuring the friction cone constraint was satisfied.
- **Disturbance Observer CBF (DOBCBF) Performance:** The DOBCBF demonstrated the least conservative behavior among all filters. By effectively estimating the external disturbances (representing model uncertainties) and incorporating these estimates into its safety conditions, it was able to operate closer to the safety boundaries without violating them. This indicated its superior ability to balance safety guarantees with efficiency.
- **Overall Simulation Conclusion:** The simulations clearly illustrated that robust and adaptive CBF variants are essential for safe grasping in the presence of uncertainties, with DOBCBF offering a distinct advantage in terms of conservatism.

**B. Hardware Experiment Results**
The framework was validated on a Shadow Robot platform, grasping diverse objects including a wooden cube, a plastic cylinder, an aluminum bearing housing, and fragile lab glassware.
- **Standard CBF Performance:** Consistent with simulations, the standard CBF failed in all hardware experiments due to model uncertainty. It typically led to an initial excessive force application, potentially damaging objects, followed by slippage as grasping force became insufficient. Safety conditions, particularly `h_3`, were observed to become negative, indicating safety violations.
- **RCBF Performance:** The RCBF consistently demonstrated significant robustness. It successfully maintained force regulation and force closure constraints across all objects, preventing both damage and slippage, by accounting for maximum disturbance bounds.
- **RaCBF Performance:** The RaCBF also maintained safety across all experiments. It exhibited slightly less conservative behavior compared to the RCBF, attributed to its ability to adapt to parametric uncertainties rather than relying solely on worst-case bounds.
- **DOBCBF Performance:** The DOBCBF showed the least conservative performance in the hardware experiments. By actively estimating and attenuating disturbances, it allowed the system to operate more efficiently while strictly adhering to safety constraints. This was reflected in its higher minimum safety condition values in Table I, indicating greater proximity to the safety boundary without violation.
- **Conservatism Comparison:** Quantitatively, the paper reported an average reduction in conservatism of 83.5% when leveraging RaCBF and DOBCBF compared to prior work that solely used RCBF. For fragile objects, where the feasible safe force region is inherently very small, the performance of the robust filters converged, showing similar levels of conservatism due to the tight constraints.
- **Contact Estimation Performance:** The developed techniques for estimating contact points, forces, and torques from tactile sensors were demonstrated to be effective, as illustrated in Fig. 3, providing the necessary feedback for the CBF-based controllers.
- **Observed Challenges:** Despite positive results, the authors noted challenges, including remaining uncertainty in friction coefficient estimation (even after iterative tuning) and approximations in the contact frame on the grasped object. These uncertainties were mitigated by the robustness of RaCBFs, RCBFs, and DOBCBFs.

**C. Overall Conclusion of Findings**
The empirical validation confirmed that the proposed integrated framework, particularly when employing DOBCBF, successfully achieved safe grasping for various objects, including fragile ones, without safety violations. DOBCBF was identified as providing the best balance of safety guarantees and minimal conservatism, allowing for more agile and efficient operations while upholding strict safety limits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Robust Adaptive Safe Robotic Grasping with Tactile Sensing" contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a critical challenge: the provision of formal safety guarantees in grasping, especially in the presence of real-world uncertainties. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Robotic Safety:** The primary significance lies in introducing an integrated framework for robotic grasping that incorporates formal safety guarantees based on Control Barrier Functions (CBFs). This moves beyond traditional stable grasping methods by rigorously ensuring that force interactions remain within predefined safe limits, preventing both object damage and slippage. This is a crucial step towards deploying robots in sensitive environments.

2. **Novel Integration of Tactile Sensing and Formal Guarantees:** The paper presents the first attempt to integrate tactile sensing with formal safety guarantees in a real-world robotic grasping setup. The development of techniques to estimate contact points, forces, and torques from tactile sensors directly feeds into the CBF-based safety filters, creating a closed-loop system that is responsive to real-time contact dynamics. This sensory integration is vital for practical and adaptive safe grasping.

3. **Reduced Conservatism in Safe Control:** A key impact is the demonstration that adaptive and disturbance observer-based CBFs (RaCBF and DOBCBF) significantly reduce conservatism compared to purely robust CBF approaches. By adapting to parametric uncertainties or estimating external disturbances, the proposed framework allows robots to operate closer to safety boundaries without violating them. This reduction in conservatism translates to more efficient, agile, and less restricted grasping actions, which is desirable for practical applications.

4. **Enabling Grasping of Fragile and Critical Objects:** The framework's successful validation with fragile objects, such as lab glassware, highlights its potential to enable robotic manipulation in applications where object integrity is paramount. This includes areas like laboratory automation (handling chemicals or delicate samples), medical robotics (manipulating surgical instruments or tissue), and advanced manufacturing (handling delicate components), where precise force control and absolute safety are non-negotiable.

5. **Robustness to Real-World Uncertainties:** The framework's robustness to model uncertainty and external disturbances, validated in both simulation and hardware, makes it more applicable to real-world scenarios. In practical settings, exact models of objects and environments are rarely available, and unexpected disturbances are common. The ability of RaCBF, RCBF, and especially DOBCBF to manage these uncertainties enhances the reliability and trustworthiness of robotic grasping systems.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified challenges, such as uncertainty in friction coefficient estimation and contact frame approximation, provide clear directions for future research. The work sets a foundation for integrating advanced estimation techniques (e.g., friction coefficient estimation, in-hand object pose estimation) into the safety-critical framework, which could further enhance performance and reduce remaining uncertainties.

In summary, this research advances the state of robotic grasping by providing a formally guaranteed, robust, and adaptive solution that can operate safely in complex and sensitive environments. Its successful real-world demonstration with tactile sensing and various CBF schemes paves the way for broader and more reliable deployment of robotic manipulators in a diverse array of human-centric and industrial applications.
