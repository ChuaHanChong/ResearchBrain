# Anticipation-VLA: Solving Long-Horizon Embodied Tasks via Anticipation-based Subgoal Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Anticipation-VLA is a hierarchical framework that enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to reliably perform long-horizon embodied tasks by adaptively generating multimodal subgoals. This approach leverages an "Anticipation Model" to dynamically adjust planning granularity and consistently outperforms existing baselines in both simulated and real-world robotic tasks, showing up to a 107% improvement in unseen real-world configurations.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models experience compounding errors over long task sequences, leading to policy failures.
- Current task decomposition methods rely on fixed-granularity subtask planning, which is either too fine-grained (increasing complexity) or too sparse (insufficient guidance).
- This lack of adaptive planning limits the scalability and robustness of VLA models for complex, long-horizon real-world scenarios.

## Method

- Introduces **Anticipation-VLA**, a hierarchical framework for long-horizon tasks that integrates an "==Anticipation Model==" for high-level planning with a low-level VLA policy.
- Develops the Anticipation Model to recursively and adaptively generate ==multimodal subgoals== (textual instructions and corresponding visual images) based on current observations and task progress.
- Implements dynamic subgoal management, re-planning every K timesteps by using an ==Optimal Value Function== to evaluate progress and adapt subgoal granularity (refine, pop, or reset).

## Results

- Anticipation-VLA achieved an **80.8%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and doubled the success rate (**4.2%**) on the VLABench "Hammer Nail & Hang Picture" task compared to baselines.
- In real-world experiments, the framework consistently outperformed baselines (e.g., π0.5), showing a **107%** improvement in unseen task configurations and being the only model to achieve non-zero success in the unseen "Spell Words" task.
- Ablation studies confirmed that adaptive planning, visual subgoals, and textual predictions are all essential and complementary components for robust real-world manipulation, particularly validating the importance of adaptive granularity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive and recursive multimodal subgoal generation is crucial for mitigating compounding errors and achieving robust execution in long-horizon embodied tasks.
> - A Unified Multimodal Model (UMM) can effectively implement both the anticipation model for subgoal generation and the optimal value model for evaluating task progress.
> - Reformulating the optimal value model as a classification task for progress assessment simplifies learning from sparse, trajectory-level rewards and enhances practical applicability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in embodied artificial intelligence, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and visual inputs to perform physical tasks. These models often leverage large-scale robot data pretraining to achieve generalization across various tasks, objects, and environments.

However, a significant challenge for existing VLA models lies in their application to long-horizon tasks. Such tasks typically involve a sequence of interdependent steps, and errors can compound over time, leading to policy failures. Previous approaches to address this issue include skill retrieval, VLM-based planning, and subgoal image prediction. A common limitation among these methods is their reliance on fixed-granularity subtask planning. This can result in either overly fine-grained subgoals, which increase complexity and computational overhead, or excessively sparse subgoals, which fail to provide adequate guidance for policy execution. This lack of adaptability to varying task complexities and execution states can hinder the scalability and robustness of VLA models in real-world long-horizon scenarios.

This work contributes to the field by proposing an adaptive and recursive subgoal generation mechanism designed to overcome the limitations of fixed-granularity planning. By integrating an "Anticipation Model" into a hierarchical VLA framework, the authors aim to improve the reliability and generalization of robot execution for complex, long-horizon tasks. This approach seeks to refine subgoals dynamically as a task progresses, thereby mitigating compounding errors and enhancing overall system robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to reliably perform long-horizon embodied tasks by addressing the limitations of existing subgoal generation strategies. The core motivation stems from two main issues observed in current VLA models:

First, existing VLA models demonstrate compounding errors when confronted with extended task sequences. As a robot executes a series of actions based on initial instructions, minor deviations or inaccuracies at early stages can accumulate, leading to significant failures later in the task. This makes direct end-to-end learning for long-horizon tasks challenging and often unreliable.

Second, prior methods for task decomposition, such as VLM-based planning or future image prediction, typically rely on generating subtasks or subgoals with a fixed granularity. This fixed granularity presents a dilemma:
- **Overly fine-grained subgoals:** If subgoals are too detailed, they can introduce unnecessary complexity, increasing the planning overhead and potentially making the overall task more susceptible to minor execution errors. The robot might get bogged down in achieving trivial steps.
- **Too sparse subgoal generation:** Conversely, if subgoals are too abstract or infrequent, they may fail to provide sufficient guidance for the low-level VLA policy. The policy might struggle to bridge large gaps between subgoals, leading to a lack of progress or incorrect intermediate actions.

The authors motivate their approach by arguing that robust long-horizon task execution requires an adaptive planning mechanism that can adjust the granularity of subgoals dynamically in response to the current observation and task progress. This adaptive adjustment is intended to ensure that subgoals are "actionable" – neither too broad nor too narrow – at any given stage, thereby simplifying the low-level control problem and improving execution reliability.

To achieve this, the paper introduces the concept of an "Anticipation Model" which adaptively and recursively generates future subgoals. The key objectives of this model are:
- To decompose high-level instructions into intermediate, achievable goals.
- To enable recursive generation, where each generated subgoal can be further decomposed if needed.
- To continuously update subgoals based on current observations and task progress, providing a principled approach for task decomposition.

By integrating this anticipation model into a hierarchical framework named "Anticipation-VLA," the research aims to provide a robust solution for long-horizon tasks, addressing the limitations of fixed-granularity planning and mitigating compounding errors in embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Anticipation-VLA framework integrates high-level planning and low-level control hierarchically for long-horizon embodied tasks. It is built upon the formalization of Goal-Conditioned Markov Decision Processes (GMDP) and introduces an "Anticipation Model" for adaptive subgoal generation.

#### 3.1. Goal-Conditioned Markov Decision Process (GMDP)

The problem is formalized as a GMDP, defined by (S, A, G, P, r, T). The state space S represents visual observations, and action space A comprises continuous control inputs. The goal space G is a Cartesian product of language space L and state space S, meaning a goal `g` is an observation-instruction pair `(ℓ_g, s_g)`. The reward function `r` measures improvement towards `g`, and the objective is to find an optimal goal-conditioned policy `π` that maximizes expected cumulative reward.

#### 3.2. Anticipation Model

The Anticipation Model (G) functions as a high-level planner, taking the current state `s` and active goal `g` as input to output an immediate subgoal `g'`. Its recursive nature allows `g'` to become the new active goal for further decomposition, enabling subgoal generation at various granularities. Optimality is guided by the Bellman Optimality Equation, ensuring that the maximum reward from the initial state `s_0` to the final goal `g` is decomposable into rewards from `s_0` to the waypoint `g'` and from `g'` to `g`.

#### 3.3. Anticipation-VLA Architecture

The Anticipation-VLA system comprises three core components:
1. **Anticipation Model (G):** Recursively generates intermediate subgoals.
2. **Optimal Value Function (V*):** Estimates optimal expected cumulative reward to guide subgoal generation.
3. **Goal-Conditioned VLA Model (π):** Executes low-level actions based on the current observation and immediate subgoal.

#### 3.4. Dynamic Subgoal Management

A key innovation is the adaptive subgoal management, which uses `V*` to dynamically trigger re-planning every K timesteps. The system maintains a subgoal stack and evaluates three conditions:
- **Goal Achievement:** If `V*(s, g)` is close to `V*(g, g)`, the current subgoal is popped.
- **Insufficient Progress:** If value improvement is negligible and the stack is not full, the Anticipation Model generates and pushes a refined subgoal. If the stack is full, the system backtracks to the initial state, pushing the final goal, and resetting the robot.
- **Sufficient Progress:** If substantial progress is made, the current subgoal remains active.

#### 3.5. Practical Implementation

##### 3.5.1. UMM-based Anticipation Model and Value Model

The anticipation model is implemented using Bagel, a Unified Multimodal Model (UMM) that handles both text and vision, allowing it to function as both the anticipation and optimal value model.
- **Anticipation Model Decomposition:** To mitigate hallucination, subgoal generation is a two-stage process: a language-based policy `l_θ` predicts a textual subgoal instruction `ℓ_g'`, then a dynamics model `P_θ` predicts the corresponding visual subgoal image `s_g'`.
- **Self-Discriminative Regularization:** An inverse dynamics model `P⁻¹_θ` infers the instruction `ℓ'_inv` from `s` to `s_g'`. If `ℓ'_inv` is not semantically equivalent to `ℓ_g'`, the subgoal is discarded and regenerated.
- **Optimal Value Model Reformulation:** Instead of predicting exact value, `V_θ` performs a classification task: goal achieved, progress stagnated, or progress improved. This simplifies learning from sparse, trajectory-level rewards.

##### 3.5.2. Data Preparation for Anticipation Model

Four hierarchical datasets are constructed for training:
- **Dynamics Dataset:** `(s, ℓ_g', s_g')` tuples for predicting next visual state.
- **Policy Dataset:** `(s, g_parent, ℓ_g')` tuples for predicting subgoal instruction.
- **Inverse Dynamics Dataset:** `(s, s_g', ℓ_g')` tuples for inferring instruction from state transitions.
- **Optimal Value Dataset:** `(s_1, s_2, g_h, y)` tuples, where `y` is a progress label (Insufficient, Sufficient, Achieved) based on temporal and geometric proximity to the goal.

##### 3.5.3. Training Recipe for Anticipation Model

A weighted sum of four loss functions is optimized over these datasets:
- `L_policy(θ)`: Cross-entropy loss for `l_θ`.
- `L_dyna(θ)`: Mean-squared-error loss for `P_θ` (flow matching).
- `L_inverse(θ)`: Cross-entropy loss for `P⁻¹_θ`.
- `L_value(θ)`: Cross-entropy loss for `V_θ`.

##### 3.5.4. Goal-conditioned VLA Model

The low-level policy is implemented by finetuning π0.5, a flow matching-based VLA model. It conditions on multi-view observations, the subgoal image `s_g`, robot configuration `q`, and subgoal instruction `ℓ_g`. To enhance robustness against potentially ambiguous or noisy generated subgoals, random masking of goal image tokens is applied during training. The overall action distribution is decomposed as `π_θ(a|s_o, g) = π_θ(a|s_o, g_t) * G_θ(g_t|g)`.

##### 3.5.5. Data Preparation and Training for VLA Model

A hierarchical dataset `D_policy = {(s, a, g_h)}` is used, reusing annotated subgoals from the anticipation dataset. The VLA model is finetuned using a flow matching loss, with random masking of goal inputs to improve robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Anticipation-VLA was conducted across simulated and real-world robotic tasks, comparing its performance against several VLA baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulated Experiments

Experiments were performed on two benchmarks:
- **LIBERO:** A lifelong learning benchmark for language-guided manipulation, tested across four task suites (Goal, Spatial, Object, Long). Models were trained in a one-shot setting with 40 trajectories.
- **VLABench:** A large-scale VLA benchmark for long-horizon reasoning, evaluated on the "Hammer Nail & Hang Picture" task, trained on 100 trajectories.

Anticipation-VLA demonstrated superior performance on both benchmarks.
- On **Libero**, it achieved the highest average success rate (80.8%) across all task suites, outperforming baselines like π0.5 (76.8%) and π0.5+VLM (76.0%). The most significant improvement was observed on the challenging Libero-Long suite.
- On **VLABench**, Anticipation-VLA recorded a higher process reward (56.3) and a success rate of 4.2%, which was at least double that of baselines like π0.5 (42.7% process reward, 2.1% success rate) and π0.5+VLM (47.9% process reward, 2.1% success rate). This indicated improved adherence to intermediate steps and robustness in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Two long-horizon tasks were designed for the Arx-X5 mobile manipulator: "Rearrange Objects" (image-based goal) and "Spell Words" (language-based goal). Models were tested on 40 rollouts per task (20 seen, 20 unseen configurations).
- Anticipation-VLA consistently outperformed baselines (π0.5 and π0.5+VLM) in both tasks and configurations.
- The improvement was more pronounced in unseen scenarios (+107%) compared to seen configurations (+60%).
- Notably, Anticipation-VLA was the only model to achieve a non-zero success rate in the unseen "Spell Words" task, indicating strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on real-world tasks assessed the contribution of individual components:
- **w/o subgoal image:** Removing predicted future frames from policy input.
- **w/o subgoal text:** Excluding generated textual subgoals.
- **w/o recursive:** Replacing adaptive recursive planning with fixed-level generation.
Results indicated that the full Anticipation-VLA consistently outperformed all ablated variants. This suggests that adaptive planning, visual subgoals (for physical guidance), and textual predictions (for semantic grounding) are complementary and essential for robust real-world manipulation. The "w/o recursive" variant showed a notable performance drop in later stages, validating the importance of adaptive planning granularity.

#### 4.4. Generalization Evaluation

The model's robustness was tested under object generalization (novel object instances) and background generalization (altered visual scenes/illumination).
- In "Rearrange Objects," Anticipation-VLA showed negligible performance degradation in generalization settings (scores of 0.58 for objects, 0.64 for backgrounds) compared to the standard unseen benchmark (0.59).
- In the "Spell Words" task, Anticipation-VLA was the only policy to achieve non-zero success rates under generalization, whereas baselines failed completely. This highlights the model's ability to handle significant distribution shifts.

#### 4.5. Anticipation Evaluation

The quality of generated subgoals was evaluated in terms of textual subtask prediction accuracy (l_θ) and visual subgoal generation quality (P_θ) on held-out samples.
- The anticipation model demonstrated high textual subtask prediction accuracy across benchmarks (e.g., 98.9% on Spell Words, 84.4% on Libero).
- Visual subgoal generation showed strong generalization to real-world tasks (e.g., SSIM of 0.93 for Rearrange Objects, 0.92 for Spell Words).
- Performance on simulated tasks was relatively lower for visual subgoal generation (e.g., SSIM of 0.76 for VLABench), attributed to smaller datasets, complexity of generating intricate unseen images, and lack of UMM pretraining on simulated data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Anticipation-VLA, a hierarchical framework designed to enhance the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in executing long-horizon embodied tasks. The work addresses a significant limitation in current VLA models, namely their struggle with compounding errors and reliance on fixed-granularity subtask planning when dealing with complex, multi-step operations.

The primary significance of Anticipation-VLA lies in its proposed Anticipation Model, which enables adaptive and recursive subgoal generation. This mechanism allows the robot to dynamically adjust the granularity of its planning based on the current execution state and task progress. By continuously refining multimodal subgoals (both textual and visual), the system aims to simplify the control problem for the low-level VLA policy, providing more actionable intermediate objectives. This adaptive approach has been demonstrated to reduce compounding errors, which are a common cause of failure in long-horizon robotic tasks.

The empirical evaluations, conducted in both simulated environments (Libero, VLABench) and real-world scenarios (Rearrange Objects, Spell Words), indicate that Anticipation-VLA consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art VLA baselines. The improvements observed across various metrics, including success rates and stage-wise scores, particularly in challenging long-horizon and unseen configurations, suggest an enhanced level of robustness and generalizability. The ability of Anticipation-VLA to perform in scenarios involving novel object instances or altered backgrounds suggests that the framework fosters a more resilient policy capable of handling environmental perturbations and novelties.

The development of the UMM-based Anticipation Model and Value Model, which jointly handle understanding and generation of multimodal content, represents an integrated approach to high-level planning. The reformulation of the optimal value model into a classification task for progress evaluation also contributes to practical applicability by circumventing the need for dense, step-wise rewards, which are difficult to obtain in real-world settings. The self-discriminative regularization used during subgoal generation aims to reduce issues like hallucination, promoting the generation of more consistent and plausible intermediate goals.

Potential impacts of this work include:
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** The ability to execute long-horizon tasks more reliably could lead to more autonomous and capable robots in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to domestic assistance.
- **Improved Generalization:** The demonstrated robustness to unseen objects and environments could facilitate the deployment of VLA models in dynamic and less constrained real-world settings.
- **Advancement in Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning:** The principled integration of a recursive anticipation model with a low-level policy provides a concrete example of effective hierarchical planning for complex sequential decision-making.

Despite its advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, including the continued requirement for some annotated subgoal demonstrations for fine-tuning and the computational cost associated with visual subgoal generation, which can cause occasional inference pauses. Future work could focus on reducing data dependency for planning (e.g., through improved pretraining for one-shot or zero-shot planning) and enhancing inference efficiency through model compression or acceleration techniques. These directions could further broaden the applicability and practical utility of anticipation-based VLA systems.
