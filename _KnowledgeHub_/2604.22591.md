# RedVLA: Physical Red Teaming for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called RedVLA was developed by researchers at Peking University to proactively uncover physical safety vulnerabilities in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models before their real-world deployment. This system effectively elicits diverse unsafe physical behaviors from various VLA models, achieving attack success rates between 64.9% and 95.5%, and facilitates a lightweight safety guard capable of reducing unsafe actions by 59.5%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face risks of unpredictable and irreversible physical harm when deployed in real-world environments.
- Existing VLA safety research primarily focuses on adversarial visual or linguistic inputs and semantic vulnerabilities, neglecting the proactive detection of physical risks stemming from embodied agent-environment interaction.
- Current red teaming methods, common for LLMs and VLMs, do not adequately address physical risks or model their causality within an embodied robotic context.

## Method

- Proposed **RedVLA**, a ==two-stage physical red teaming== framework comprising ==Risk Scenario Synthesis== and ==Trajectory-Driven Risk Amplification==, to systematically uncover unsafe behaviors.
- ==Risk Scenario Synthesis== constructs initial risk scenes by identifying critical interaction regions from benign trajectories and injecting relevant physical risk factors (objects) into the environment.
- ==Trajectory-Driven Risk Amplification== employs ==gradient-free optimization==, guided by end-effector trajectories, to efficiently refine risk factor placement for stable elicitation of unsafe behaviors across heterogeneous VLA models.
- Developed **SimpleVLA-Guard**, a lightweight safety guard trained on RedVLA data, designed to detect and intervene in unsafe VLA behaviors in real-time using ==internal model representations==.

## Results

- RedVLA achieved Attack Success Rates (ASR) ranging from **64.9%** to **95.5%** across six VLA models, successfully eliciting diverse unsafe behaviors across state-level (>**95%** ASR), cumulative-level (**88.9%** ASR), and conditional-level (**66.1%** ASR) scenarios.
- Analysis confirmed that most unsafe behaviors (over **90%**) occurred while models were actively attempting or succeeding at the task, rather than due to model collapse, indicating deeper safety deficiencies.
- SimpleVLA-Guard reduced the ASR of unsafe behaviors by **59.5%** (from **90.9%** to **31.4%** on seen tasks) with a modest **4.08%** decrease in benign task success rate, demonstrating effective real-time risk mitigation.
- Sim-to-real validation on a physical robot confirmed that the identified physical safety vulnerabilities manifest in real-world deployments, achieving an ASR above **80%** for critical scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models demonstrate substantial vulnerability to physical safety risks, with unsafe actions frequently occurring during active task execution rather than from trivial model failures.
> - A direct correlation exists between stronger VLA model capabilities (higher task success rates) and increased physical safety vulnerability, suggesting a complex performance-safety trade-off.
> - Physical safety vulnerabilities uncovered by RedVLA are primarily driven by environmental risk factors, distinguishing them from input perturbation attacks and highlighting a distinct class of safety challenges.
