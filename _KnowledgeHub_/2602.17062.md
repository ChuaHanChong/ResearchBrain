# Retaining Suboptimal Actions to Follow Shifting Optima in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Successive Sub-value Q-learning (S2Q) introduces a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework that learns and leverages multiple sub-value functions to track and adapt to dynamic shifts in optimal policies. It demonstrated faster convergence and higher asymptotic win rates on challenging StarCraft II and Google Research Football benchmarks, outperforming existing baselines like QMIX and WQMIX.

## Problem

- Existing value decomposition methods in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) struggle to adapt to dynamically shifting optimal policies during training, leading to premature convergence to suboptimal solutions.
- Conventional ϵ-greedy exploration in large joint action spaces is often inefficient and fails to adequately explore alternative high-value actions, especially when the true optimum changes.
- Monotonicity constraints in methods like QMIX limit the expressiveness of the joint action-value function, hindering effective minimization of the temporal-difference (TD) loss and representation of complex inter-agent interactions.

## Method

- **S2Q** learns a sequence of K+1 ==sub-value functions==, where the main function (Q0subQ^{sub}_0Q0sub) targets the global optimum, and subsequent functions (QksubQ^{sub}_kQksub) are trained to capture the k-th suboptimal action by iteratively suppressing previously identified optimal actions from their learning objectives.
- A Softmax-based behavior policy is employed during training, which samples a sub-value function index based on the estimated Q-values of the identified optimal and suboptimal actions, facilitating persistent and directed exploration.
- A centralized encoder-decoder architecture is used during training to allow agents to coordinate on the chosen sub-value function index for consistent execution, without requiring communication during decentralized execution.

## Results

- S2Q consistently achieved superior performance with faster convergence and higher asymptotic win rates across SMAC-Hard+ and Google Research Football benchmarks compared to baselines.
- Trajectory analysis demonstrated S2Q's ability to dynamically adapt to shifting optimal policies, for example, transitioning from 'move' to more aggressive 'hit' strategies in SMAC-Hard+ 6h vs 8z.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of coordinated sub-value selection and Softmax-based execution, with optimal performance achieved with 2 sub-networks (K=**2K**=**2K**=2) and a moderate Softmax temperature (T=0.1T=0.1T=**0.1**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly retaining and leveraging information about alternative high-value (suboptimal) actions is crucial for adapting to shifting optimal policies in non-stationary MARL environments.
> - Directed exploration guided by the relative values of multiple optimal and suboptimal actions is more efficient than naive ϵ-greedy, enabling faster discovery of global optima and better policy adjustment.
> - Coordinated selection of the appropriate sub-value function for execution is critical for effective learning and adaptation, highlighting the importance of synchronized decision-making among agents during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is a framework for addressing sequential decision-making problems involving multiple interacting agents. It has applications in fields such as robotic control, autonomous driving, traffic management, and strategic games. A prominent paradigm within MARL is Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE), where agents leverage centralized information during training but act independently during execution.

Value decomposition methods, such as Value-Decomposition Networks (VDN) and QMIX, are a core approach within CTDE for cooperative MARL. These methods decompose a joint action-value function into individual agent utilities, which allows for scalable learning. QMIX, a representative CTDE method, enforces the Individual-Global-Max (IGM) condition through a monotonicity constraint. This constraint ensures that maximizing an individual agent's utility aligns with maximizing the global objective. While this design facilitates competitive performance, it also restricts the expressiveness of the joint action-value function, which can limit its ability to represent complex inter-agent interactions and hinder effective minimization of the temporal-difference (TD) loss.

Previous research efforts have attempted to address the limitations imposed by the monotonicity constraint and improve the representational capacity of value decomposition methods. Examples include Weighted QMIX (WQMIX), which uses an unconstrained target network and weighted updates; methods that incorporate auxiliary information or reformulate the value function for better decomposition (e.g., PAC, RiskQ, DOP, FOP); and approaches that extend exploration strategies from single-agent RL to the multi-agent domain. Communication-based MARL methods also aim to improve coordination under partial observability by enabling agents to exchange messages.

Despite these advancements, a persistent challenge remains: existing methods often struggle to adapt effectively when the optimal joint action or underlying value function shifts dynamically during training. This limitation is partly attributed to the representational rigidity of some architectures and the reliance on $\epsilon$-greedy exploration in large joint action spaces, which may lead to premature convergence to suboptimal policies by not adequately exploring alternative high-value actions.

This work contributes to the MARL landscape by specifically targeting the problem of dynamic optimality shifts. It proposes a novel framework that explicitly retains and leverages information about alternative high-value actions, aiming to enhance adaptability and exploration beyond what current single-optimal-action-focused methods can achieve. By focusing on retaining suboptimal actions, the paper positions itself as an advancement in robust and adaptive MARL, particularly for non-stationary environments or those with complex, evolving optimal policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework that can effectively adapt to dynamic shifts in the optimal policy during training, thereby avoiding premature convergence to suboptimal solutions.

The motivation for this objective stems from a identified limitation in existing value decomposition algorithms, particularly those operating under the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm. Current methods, even advanced ones like Weighted QMIX (WQMIX), predominantly focus on identifying and exploiting a single optimal joint action. While this approach can achieve strong performance in many scenarios, it proves insufficient when the underlying value landscape changes. When the true optimal action shifts—a common occurrence during the exploration phase of training as value estimates are updated—methods that exclusively track a single optimum may become "stuck" in a previously optimal but now suboptimal policy.

The authors illustrate this critical issue using a simplified matrix game. In this game, a QMIX agent fails to represent the true global value due to its monotonicity constraint, performing poorly. WQMIX, designed to mitigate some representational limitations of QMIX by using an unconstrained target Q-function and weighted updates, initially learns the correct optimal action. However, when the payoff matrix is subtly altered to shift the true optimum to a different joint action, WQMIX fails to adapt, continuing to converge to the previous (now suboptimal) solution. This empirical observation highlights that simply enhancing the expressiveness or state-awareness of a single Q-function does not guarantee adaptability to a dynamically changing optimum. The core problem, as identified by the authors, is the failure of these conventional methods to explicitly track or retain information about alternative high-value (suboptimal) actions. Once such information is discarded, the learning agent lacks the necessary data to pivot quickly when one of these alternative actions becomes the new optimum.

Therefore, the key motivation is to enable MARL agents to:
1. **Retain information on suboptimal actions:** Explicitly learn and maintain representations for multiple high-value actions beyond just the single current optimum.
2. **Facilitate persistent exploration:** Move beyond naive $\epsilon$-greedy exploration, which can be inefficient in large joint action spaces and fail to adequately explore promising suboptimal modes.
3. **Rapidly adapt to shifting optima:** Leverage the retained suboptimal action information to quickly adjust the policy when the value function changes, preventing convergence to suboptimal states.
4. **Improve overall performance and convergence:** By addressing the adaptability challenge, achieve more robust learning, faster convergence, and higher asymptotic returns in complex and dynamic MARL environments.

The proposed method, Successive Sub-value Q-learning (S2Q), is specifically designed to address these motivations by learning multiple sub-value functions that collectively capture a richer understanding of the value landscape.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Successive Sub-value Q-learning (S2Q), is designed to enable Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) agents to track and adapt to shifting optimal actions by learning and leveraging multiple sub-value functions.

**Core Idea: Successive Sub-value Learning**
S2Q builds upon the principles of value decomposition under the CTDE paradigm, similar to WQMIX. However, it introduces a sequence of *K+1* sub-value functions, denoted $Q^{sub}_k$ for $k = 0, \dots, K$.
1. **Main Value Function:** $Q^{sub}_0$ is defined as $Q_{tot}$, aiming to capture the true global optimal action.
2. **Suboptimal Value Functions:** Each subsequent $Q^{sub}_k$ (for $k \ge 1$) is designed to capture the $k$-th suboptimal action. This is achieved by iteratively suppressing actions identified as optimal by previous sub-value functions in its learning objective.

All $Q^{sub}_k$ share the same mixing network architecture as QMIX and satisfy the Individual-Global-Max (IGM) condition. For each $Q^{sub}_k$, the greedy joint action $a^*_{k,t}$ is identified by maximizing individual utilities: $a^i_{k,t} = \text{arg max } Q^i_k(\tau^i_t, \cdot)$.

**Learning Objective with Suppression**
The optimization of each $Q^{sub}_k$ is based on the WQMIX TD-learning objective, but with a crucial modification for suppressing previously identified optimal actions. An unrestricted auxiliary value function, $Q^*$, is also trained using Equation 1 (standard WQMIX loss). The successive loss for $Q^{sub}_k$ is defined as:
$L_{successive}(\theta) = \sum_{k=0}^{K} E_{(s_t, \tau_t, a_t) \sim B} \left[ w_k(s_t, a_t) \left( Q^{sub}_{\theta,k}(s_t, \tau_t, a_t) - \left( y_t - \alpha I(a_t \in A_{k-1,t}) \cdot \text{max}(Q^*_{target}(s_t, \tau_t, a_t), C) \right) \right)^2 \right]$
where:
- $y_t = r_t + \gamma Q^*_{target}(s_{t+1}, \tau_{t+1}, a^*_{0,t+1})$ is the TD target from $Q^*$.
- $I(\cdot)$ is an indicator function.
- $A_{k-1,t} = \{a^*_{0,t}, a^*_{1,t}, \dots, a^*_{k-1,t}\}$ is the set of optimal actions identified by previous sub-value functions (for $k=0$, $A_{-1,t} = \emptyset$).
- $\alpha$ is a suppression factor controlling the strength of value reduction for actions in $A_{k-1,t}$.
- $C$ is a positive constant to handle potentially negative $Q^*_{target}$ values.
- $w_k(s_t, a_t)$ is a WQMIX-inspired weighting function (detailed in Appendix C.3), prioritizing underestimated actions. For $k=0$, it prioritizes actions whose $Q^*$ value is greater than or equal to the maximum $Q^*$ value among all identified suboptimal actions. For $k \ge 1$, it acts like WQMIX but compares against the suppressed target.

**Theoretical Guarantee:** Theorem 4.1 mathematically guarantees that with bounded rewards and a sufficiently large suppression factor $\alpha$, each $Q^{sub}_k$ will indeed learn to identify the $k$-th suboptimal joint action of $Q^*$.

**Softmax-based Behavior Policy for Exploration**
To move beyond $\epsilon$-greedy and encourage persistent exploration of promising high-value regions, S2Q employs a Softmax-based behavior policy during training. This policy samples a sub-value function index $k$ based on the $Q^*$ values of the identified optimal and suboptimal actions:
$P_t = (P_{0,t}, \dots, P_{K,t}) := \text{Softmax}\left( Q^*(s_t, \tau_t, a^*_{0,t})/T, \dots, Q^*(s_t, \tau_t, a^*_{K,t})/T \right)$
where $T$ is a temperature parameter. After sampling $k \sim P_t$, agents execute actions from $Q^{sub}_k$ using an $\epsilon$-greedy rule ($a^i_t \sim \epsilon\text{-greedy}(Q^i_k)$). This ensures that actions corresponding to higher $Q^*$ values are explored more frequently, facilitating faster adaptation when optimality shifts.

**Coordinated Execution via Communication During Training**
The exact computation of $P_t$ requires global state information, and all agents must agree on the same $k$ to consistently execute actions from $Q^{sub}_k$. To enable this, S2Q integrates a centralized encoder-decoder architecture *during training*:
1. **Encoder ($E_\psi$):** Maps the concatenation of all agents' local histories ($\tau_t$) to a latent representation ($z_t$).
2. **Decoder ($D_\psi$):** Reconstructs the global state ($\hat{s}_t$) and predicts an approximate distribution ($\hat{P}_t$) over the sub-value functions.
The encoder-decoder is trained using a loss function $L_{latent}(\psi)$ that combines a cross-entropy loss for $\hat{P}_t$ against $P_t$ and a mean squared error loss for $\hat{s}_t$ against $s_t$. During training, agents sample $k \sim \hat{P}_t$ and synchronize their choice.

**Overall Training Strategy:**
A sampling strategy is implemented where, with a probability $p=0.5$, the sub-value index is fixed to $k=0$ for the entire episode, ensuring consistent learning of the global optimum. Otherwise, $k$ is sampled from $\hat{P}_t$ at each timestep for exploration. The total S2Q loss combines $L_{successive}$, $L_{Q^*}$, and $L_{latent}$.

**Decentralized Execution at Test Time:**
A key feature of S2Q is that communication is not required during decentralized execution by default. Since $Q^{sub}_0 (= Q_{tot})$ is trained to capture the optimal action, agents can simply follow the greedy policy of $Q^{sub}_0$ without needing to coordinate on $k$ or communicate.
A variant, **S2Q-Comm**, is introduced for communication-critical tasks where the latent variable $z_t$ is provided to each individual utility $Q^i_k$ during both training and execution, allowing for explicit information sharing.

This methodology combines a novel approach to learning and retaining multiple high-value actions with a targeted exploration strategy and a practical communication mechanism during training, aiming to address dynamic optimality shifts in MARL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates S2Q across challenging Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) benchmarks, including the StarCraft II Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC) and Google Research Football (GRF). The key findings and results are as follows:

#### 4.1. Performance Analysis

- **Superior Performance on SMAC-Hard+ and GRF:** S2Q consistently outperformed various baselines, including QMIX, WQMIX, DOP, FOP, PAC, RiskQ, MARR, and MASIA, in communication-free scenarios from SMAC-Hard+ and GRF.
 - S2Q demonstrated faster convergence and achieved higher asymptotic win rates.
 - This advantage was particularly evident in exploration-intensive scenarios (e.g., SMAC-Hard+ 6h vs 8z and 3s5z vs 3s6z, and challenging GRF tasks), where conventional methods struggled to adapt to shifting optimal joint actions.
- **Effectiveness in Communication-Critical Tasks (SMAC-Comm):** The S2Q-Comm variant, which utilizes communication during both training and execution, showed substantial gains in SMAC-Comm scenarios requiring tight coordination (e.g., 5z vs 1ul and bane vs hM).
 - In simpler SMAC-Comm tasks (e.g., 1o 2r vs 4r), S2Q-Comm performed comparably to baselines.
 - The improvements were attributed to S2Q's successive sub-value learning and synchronized function selection rather than solely information sharing, as indicated by comparisons with QMIX-Comm.
- **Robustness to Stochasticity:** Additional experiments on SMACv2 (randomized team compositions) and SMAC-Hard (Mixture Opponent) benchmarks demonstrated that S2Q consistently outperformed all baselines, suggesting its effectiveness in environments with increased stochasticity.
- **Generality Across CTDE Methods:** The S2Q framework was successfully extended to other CTDE methods like VDN and QPLEX, yielding significant performance improvements over their respective baselines, especially in exploration-demanding environments (e.g., 6h vs 8z).

#### 4.2. Trajectory Analysis

- **Adaptation to Shifting Policies:** A detailed trajectory analysis in the SMAC-Hard+ 6h vs 8z scenario illustrated S2Q's dynamic adaptation.
 - Early in training, 'move' actions were optimal, while 'hit' actions were tracked as suboptimal.
 - As training progressed, the optimal policy shifted towards more aggressive 'hit' strategies, indicated by an increasing hit rate and win rate.
 - S2Q successfully adapted by gradually increasing the execution frequency of 'hit' actions via its Softmax-based behavior policy, allowing $Q_{tot}$ ($Q^{sub}_0$) to rapidly adjust to the new optimum.
- Similar adaptive behaviors were observed in other scenarios, such as SMAC-Hard (Mixture Opponent) 5m vs 6m Hard (shifting from greedy attacks to repositioning) and GRF academy 4 vs 3 (shifting from long shots to approaching or passing).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Importance of Coordinated Sub-value Selection:**
 - `S2Q oracle` (using true Softmax distribution) achieved the highest performance, setting an upper bound.
 - `S2Q independent` (sampling sub-value index `k` independently without coordination) suffered a substantial performance drop, emphasizing the critical role of accurately estimating Softmax probabilities and synchronizing agent choices.
- **Contribution of Core S2Q Mechanisms:**
 - `S2Q no wTD` (removing WQMIX-based weighted TD learning) maintained strong performance, suggesting that the successive sub-value learning and execution are more central to S2Q's gains than the auxiliary weighting scheme.
 - `S2Q no soft` (removing Softmax-based execution) and `S2Q random` (uniformly sampling `k`) showed significant performance degradation, highlighting the necessity of actively tracking and prioritizing the execution of valuable suboptimal actions.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
 - **Number of Sub-networks (K):** $K=2$ generally yielded the best performance, striking a balance between exploring diverse suboptimal actions and maintaining learning stability. $K=0$ (no suboptimal actions) was insufficient, while $K=3$ sometimes introduced excessive variance.
 - **Softmax Temperature (T):** A moderate temperature ($T=0.1$) was optimal, balancing focused sampling with sufficient exploration. Very low $T$ led to overly deterministic behavior, while high $T$ promoted excessive but potentially unguided exploration.
 - **Suppression Constant ($\alpha$):** $\alpha=1.0$ proved optimal, ensuring sufficient suppression of previously identified actions to find meaningful suboptimal alternatives without forcing exploration of overly irrelevant actions.
 - **Weighting Factor ($w_c$):** A relatively high $w_c=0.9$ maintained effective TD-error propagation, demonstrating robustness to moderate changes.

#### 4.4. Computational Complexity

- S2Q introduced a moderate increase in computational overhead (e.g., approximately 3.5% to 8.4% more training time per 1M steps for K=2 compared to QMIX).
- GPU memory consumption also increased moderately with K (e.g., 248 MB more for S2Q with K=2 in MMM2).
- Crucially, S2Q achieved target win rates significantly faster than QMIX, indicating that the performance gains and faster convergence outweigh the modest increase in computational and memory resources.

Overall, the findings indicate that S2Q effectively addresses the challenge of dynamic optimality shifts by explicitly learning and leveraging alternative high-value actions through its successive sub-value functions and Softmax-guided exploration. This approach leads to more adaptable, robust, and efficient learning in complex multi-agent environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Successive Sub-value Q-learning (S2Q) holds several points of significance and carries potential impacts on the field of cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing a Fundamental Limitation:** S2Q directly confronts a long-standing challenge in value decomposition-based CTDE methods: their susceptibility to premature convergence and inability to adapt efficiently when the optimal policy shifts dynamically during training. This limitation is often exacerbated by restrictive monotonicity constraints and inefficient exploration strategies.
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** By explicitly learning and retaining multiple high-value, suboptimal actions, S2Q enables a more nuanced understanding of the value landscape. This capacity allows the main Q-function ($Q_{tot}$) to rapidly pivot to newly optimal actions as the environment or policy estimates evolve, making the learning process more robust to non-stationarity inherent in MARL.
- **Improved Exploration Efficiency:** The Softmax-based behavior policy, guided by the estimated $Q^*$ values of multiple optimal and suboptimal actions, offers a more directed and persistent exploration strategy than conventional $\epsilon$-greedy methods. This active prioritization of promising regions in the joint action space facilitates faster discovery of global optima and more effective learning.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** The provision of Theorem 4.1, which guarantees that S2Q's successive learning procedure can indeed identify the $k$-th suboptimal joint action under certain conditions, provides a stronger theoretical basis for the method compared to purely heuristic approaches.
- **Practical Decentralized Execution:** S2Q's default design allows for fully decentralized execution without requiring communication, as the $Q_{tot}$ (which is $Q^{sub}_0$) is sufficient for greedy action selection. This offers a practical advantage for real-world deployment where continuous communication might be costly or infeasible. For communication-critical tasks, the S2Q-Comm variant provides a flexible alternative.
- **Generality:** The demonstrated ability of S2Q to enhance the performance of other foundational CTDE methods like VDN and QPLEX highlights its generality as an algorithmic improvement that can be integrated into existing MARL frameworks.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Cooperative MARL:** S2Q contributes to the development of more capable and generalizable cooperative MARL agents. Its ability to handle dynamic optimality shifts can unlock new levels of performance in complex coordination tasks.
- **Applications in Dynamic Environments:** The method's enhanced adaptability makes it particularly promising for real-world applications where the environment dynamics, team compositions, or task objectives can change over time. This includes domains like:
 - **Robotic Swarms:** Adapting to changing task priorities, damaged robots, or new environmental obstacles.
 - **Autonomous Driving/Traffic Management:** Responding to unforeseen traffic patterns, accidents, or dynamic road conditions.
 - **Resource Allocation:** Optimizing resource distribution in systems with fluctuating demands or capacities.
 - **Gaming and Simulation:** Developing more sophisticated and adaptive AI agents in complex strategic games.
- **Reduced Training Time and Computational Cost (Effective):** Despite a moderate increase in raw computational resources per step, S2Q's significantly faster convergence to high-quality policies can ultimately lead to reduced overall training time and a more efficient use of computational resources to achieve target performance levels.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The concept of explicitly tracking and leveraging suboptimal policies could inspire further research into diverse exploration strategies, meta-learning for adaptation to shifting objectives, and more sophisticated methods for modeling the multi-modal nature of value functions in MARL.

In summary, S2Q represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and adaptive multi-agent systems by addressing a core challenge in MARL, offering both theoretical grounding and empirical effectiveness across diverse benchmarks.
