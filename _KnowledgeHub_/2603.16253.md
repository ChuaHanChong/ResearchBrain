## Problem

- Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs) produce ambiguous step scores, making it difficult to differentiate between genuine logical errors and issues stemming from unreliable visual grounding.
- This ambiguity results in systematic verification errors, specifically false positives due to hallucinated visual premises and false negatives when correct statements are incorrectly perceived as ungrounded.
- Current solutions like tool-integrated verification incur high computational costs, limiting their scalability for tasks such as Best-of-N reranking of long reasoning traces.

## Method

- Utilizes a framework where policies generate step-wise visual checklists of premises and a constraint extractor predicts structured visual facts from the input image and question.
- Employs a consistency-to-reliability module that matches checklist claims against the extracted constraints, aggregating them into a candidate-level visual reliability score.
- This reliability score then serves as a smooth gating factor to calibrate base step rewards, attenuating them for visually dependent steps with unreliable premises.

## Results

- EVPV-PRM achieved a 67.46% Macro-F1 score on VisualProcessBench, outperforming prior PRMs, and as a plug-in, it improved Macro-F1 for external VLM/LLM judges (e.g., +6.94 points for Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-instruct).
- Demonstrated consistent improvements in Best-of-N reranking accuracy, increasing up to +9.78 points over the Pass@1 baseline for InternVL2.5-38B models on multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that structured visual evidence and its accurate alignment are essential, with corrupting relational structure causing a 7.64-point drop in Macro-F1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual premise reliability from logical step correctness, specifically through structured visual evidence, is crucial for improving VL-PRM trustworthiness.
> - The performance gains of premise verification are causally linked to the fidelity of the structured visual constraints used.
> - The lightweight, judge-agnostic calibration layer effectively enhances diverse black-box VLM/LLM step judges without requiring retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of multimodal reasoning, particularly concerning the reliability of Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs). Modern Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are capable of generating multi-step solutions to complex problems involving both visual perception and symbolic reasoning, such as multimodal mathematical reasoning. However, these solutions can be prone to errors, where a single misinterpretation of visual information can lead to a cascade of incorrect but locally coherent reasoning steps.

Process Reward Models (PRMs) have emerged as a method for process supervision, scoring intermediate reasoning steps to facilitate techniques like Best-of-N reranking, guided search, and post-training of MLLMs. While VL-PRMs have demonstrated utility in improving performance under test-time scaling, their step scores often lack interpretability. A low reward for a step may indicate a genuine logical error or stem from unreliable visual grounding, either by the policy generating the solution or by the verifier itself. This ambiguity contributes to systematic verification errors, specifically false positives (rewarding statements based on hallucinated visual premises) and false negatives (penalizing correct statements that appear to be visually ungrounded to the verifier). This issue degrades the effectiveness of both reranking and error localization.

Prior work has explored various aspects of PRMs, including stronger verification procedures, improved learning objectives, and tool-integrated verification. Tool-integrated verifiers can query external tools for visual evidence to mitigate confirmation bias. However, per-step tool calls can be computationally expensive, limiting their applicability for long reasoning traces at Best-of-N scales.

This research positions itself as complementary to these existing directions. Rather than proposing changes to PRM architecture or training signals, it introduces a lightweight, test-time calibration framework. This framework explicitly addresses the reliability of visual premises by decoupling it from the logical correctness of a step. It provides a grounding-aware calibration layer on top of a step judge, aiming to enhance PRM reliability and calibration, which aligns with broader concerns in the field regarding PRM trustworthiness. By focusing on explicit visual premise verification, the work aims to improve the robustness of multimodal reasoning systems against perceptual misinterpretations, a known challenge for MLLMs in tasks involving fine-grained perception like counting, geometry, and structured reading.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the reliability and interpretability of Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs) by explicitly verifying the visual premises upon which reasoning steps depend. The motivation for this objective stems from several key challenges and observations:

1. **Ambiguity in VL-PRM Scores**: Current VL-PRMs evaluate intermediate reasoning steps, but a low reward score is inherently ambiguous. It can signal a genuine error in logical reasoning or an issue with the visual grounding of the statement, where either the generating policy or the verifier itself has misinterpreted or hallucinated visual information. This entanglement makes it difficult to ascertain the true nature of a step's error.
2. **Systematic Verification Errors**: The ambiguity in visual grounding leads to two types of systematic verification errors. First, false positives occur when a PRM rewards a fluent, syntactically correct reasoning step that, in reality, relies on a visually hallucinated premise (e.g., assuming a non-existent feature in an image). Second, false negatives arise when a PRM penalizes a logically sound statement because it fails to reliably ground a correct visual premise, leading to an incorrect judgment. These errors compromise the effectiveness of PRMs in reranking candidate solutions and localizing errors within reasoning traces.
3. **Dominance of Visual Misinterpretation**: Empirical analysis, such as that conducted on VisualProcessBench, indicates that visual misinterpretation constitutes a substantial fraction of step-level errors in multimodal reasoning. This highlights the critical need for mechanisms that can specifically identify and account for visual grounding issues.
4. **Cost of Existing Solutions**: While tool-integrated verification methods can independently query visual evidence to reduce confirmation bias, they often involve per-step tool calls. This approach incurs significant computational cost, making it impractical for applications requiring the evaluation of numerous long reasoning traces, such as Best-of-N reranking, where efficiency is paramount.
5. **Need for Decoupling Premise Reliability from Step Correctness**: To address these issues, the research is motivated by the need for a framework that can explicitly decouple the reliability of visual premises from the overall correctness of a reasoning step. By independently assessing whether the visual facts asserted or implied by a step are truly present and accurate in the image, the framework aims to provide a more nuanced and accurate evaluation signal.

In summary, the core motivation is to improve the diagnostic capability and performance of VL-PRMs by introducing a lightweight, test-time framework that specifically verifies visual premises, thereby leading to more interpretable rewards, reduced verification errors, and enhanced overall multimodal reasoning performance without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, Explicit Visual Premise Verification (EVPV), is a lightweight, test-time framework designed to calibrate multimodal step rewards by explicitly verifying the reliability of visual premises. It is judge-agnostic, meaning it can be applied to various underlying step verifiers. The methodology consists of three main stages, supported by training specific modules:

#### 3.1. EVPV Framework Stages

1. **Step-wise Visual Checklist:**
 - The policy (the model generating the solution) is prompted to accompany each reasoning step ($s_t$) with a minimal, explicit declaration of any visual premise it depends on. This declaration, $d_t$, is either a verifiable visual assertion (e.g., "the radius is 2," "AB is perpendicular to CD") or `null` if the step is purely symbolic.
 - This generates a visual-dependency indicator, $\nu_t = \mathbb{I}[d_t \neq \text{null}] \in \{0, 1\}$.
 - The collection of all non-null declarations forms a visual checklist, $V = \{v_j\}_{j=1}^M$, which serves as the explicit interface for premise verification.

2. **Structured Visual Evidence (Constraints):**
 - A dedicated constraint extractor, $E_{\phi}$, processes the input image ($I$) and question ($q$) once per instance to predict a structured set of visual facts, $C = E_{\phi}(I, q) = \{c_k\}_{k=1}^K$.
 - These constraints follow a unified JSON schema, encompassing numeric readings (lengths, angles), relations (parallel, perpendicular, equality), and compositional structure (part-whole, attachments).
 - Crucially, $C$ is computed only once per $(I, q)$ pair and then reused across all steps and candidate solutions for that instance, ensuring scalability for Best-of-N reranking.

3. **Consistency-to-Reliability:**
 - A type-aware matching function, $m(\cdot)$, assesses whether each claim $v_j$ from the visual checklist is supported by the extracted constraints $C$. This yields a per-claim support score, $p_j \in$. The matching incorporates numeric tolerance and entity/relation alignment.
 - These individual support scores are aggregated into a single candidate-level visual reliability score, $r \in$, using a smoothed geometric mean. This aggregation method is designed to be highly sensitive to catastrophic premise failures, causing $r$ to drop sharply if any claim is strongly unsupported.

#### 3.2. Judge-Agnostic Step Rewards with Reliability Gating

- **Unified Judge Interface:** EVPV operates on a base step reward, $R^{\text{base}}_t \in [-1, 1]$, provided by an arbitrary judge module $J$.
- **EVPV-PRM (Our Trained Verifier):** For the primary system, a trained probabilistic step verifier, $V_{\theta}$, outputs a correctness probability $u_t \in$, which is then mapped to $R^{\text{base}}_t = 2u_t - 1$.
- **EVPV as a Plug-in:** EVPV can also calibrate binary step decisions ($R^{\text{base}}_t \in \{-1, +1\}$) from external, black-box VLM/LLM judges (e.g., GPT, Gemini) by providing the predicted structured constraints $C$ as additional evidence to the judge.
- **Reliability Gating:** The reliability score $r$ is converted into a smooth gating factor, $\alpha(r) = \sigma(\beta(r - \tau))$, where $\sigma$ is the logistic function, $\tau$ is a reliability threshold (set to 0.5), and $\beta$ controls sharpness (set to 10).
- The final step reward, $R_t$, is computed as $R^{\text{base}}_t$ if the step is not visually dependent ($\nu_t = 0$), and $\alpha(r) R^{\text{base}}_t$ if it is visually dependent ($\nu_t = 1$). This mechanism attenuates rewards for visually dependent steps when $r$ is low (unreliable premises) and preserves base rewards when $r$ is high.

#### 3.3. Trajectory Scoring for Best-of-N Reranking

- The gated step rewards, $\{R_t\}_{t=1}^T$, for a candidate solution $S$ are aggregated into a trajectory score.
- Rewards are first mapped to positive values, $\tilde{R}_t = \epsilon + \frac{R_t+1}{2}$, and then a geometric-mean trajectory score is computed. This magnitude-sensitive aggregation ensures that reliability gating directly influences candidate ranking.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference

- **Constraint Extractor ($E_{\phi}$):**
 - Initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B.
 - Trained via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on 26,454 image-question pairs from geometry- and table-centric subsets of VisualPRM400K. A strong teacher model (Qwen3-vl-235b-a22b-instruct) generated pseudo-gold structured constraints ($C^{\star}$) in JSON format.
 - Further refined using a DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) stage on 5,832 instances, where the teacher generated preference pairs ($C^+, C^-$).
- **Step Verifier ($V_{\theta}$ - EVPV-PRM):**
 - Fine-tuned from the same Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B backbone.
 - Trained as a probabilistic step verifier on 19,490 step-labeled trajectories from VisualPRM400K, using binary cross-entropy loss.
 - Importantly, VisualProcessBench is reserved solely for evaluation, avoiding training-test contamination.
- **Policy Model:** The policy generating solutions is not trained in this work; it is only prompted at inference time to produce steps and their associated visual checklist items.
- **Inference Pipeline:** For each instance, $E_{\phi}$ predicts $C$ once. For each candidate solution, the policy's checklist is parsed, $r$ is computed by matching against $C$, base step rewards are obtained from the judge (either $V_{\theta}$ or an external prompted judge), reliability gating is applied, and the gated rewards are aggregated for reranking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence across several experiments supporting the effectiveness and causal mechanisms of EVPV.

#### 4.1. Step Verification on VisualProcessBench (Exp-1)

- **EVPV-PRM Performance:** The proposed EVPV-PRM achieves an overall Macro-F1 score of 67.46% on VisualProcessBench, outperforming prior multimodal PRMs such as VisualPRM-8B (62.00%), QWEN-VL-PRM-7B (58.60%), and TIM-PRM-8B (61.70%). This indicates enhanced step-level discrimination in scenarios involving visual uncertainty.
- **EVPV as a Plug-in:** When attached as a plug-in calibration layer, EVPV improves the Macro-F1 scores for several strong external VLM/LLM judges. For instance, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-instruct's Macro-F1 increased by +6.94 points, Gemini 2.5 Pro by +1.51 points, and doubao-seed-1.6-vision by +2.14 points. These gains suggest that premise-aware calibration is broadly beneficial for black-box judges without requiring their retraining.

#### 4.2. Best-of-N Reranking in Downstream Benchmarks (Exp-2)

- EVPV-PRM consistently yields improvements in Best-of-8 (BoN@8) reranking accuracy across InternVL2.5 policy models (8B, 26B, and 38B scales) on six multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- **Overall Gains:** EVPV-PRM improves the overall BoN@8 accuracy over the Pass@1 baseline by +8.83 points for InternVL2.5-8B, +9.52 points for InternVL2.5-26B, and +9.78 points for InternVL2.5-38B. These gains are also generally higher than those achieved by VisualPRM.
- **Perception-Intensive Tasks:** The improvements are particularly pronounced on benchmarks that are visually intensive, such as MathVista, WeMath, and LogicVista, aligning with EVPV's design to address visual grounding errors.

#### 4.3. Perception Evidence Quality and Its Causal Impact (Exp-3)

- **Perception Interventions:** Experiments demonstrated that providing oracle perception (structured description) substantially improved policy accuracy, while removing the image (text-only) sharply decreased performance. EVPV-PRM's trajectory scores showed a monotonic ordering aligned with perception quality (oracle > noisy > normal > text-only), indicating its sensitivity to visual evidence.
- **Causal Curve via Constraint Corruption:** Introducing controlled noise by randomly flipping a fraction of fields in the extracted constraints led to a monotonic degradation of VisualProcessBench Macro-F1 (Figure 4). This provides interventional evidence that the performance gains from EVPV are directly driven by the fidelity of the structured visual constraints used for premise verification, rather than incidental prompt effects.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Exp-4)

- **Importance of Structured Evidence:** Replacing structured constraints with "caption-only" descriptions resulted in a 4.08-point drop in overall Macro-F1. Completely removing constraints (`facts = ∅`) caused a 5.35-point drop. Crucially, shuffling the facts to corrupt relational structure led to a sharper decline of 7.64 points. These results indicate that the benefit of EVPV stems from structured, matchable visual facts and their accurate alignment, not merely from the presence of additional textual context.
- **Role of Direct Visual Input:** Making the judge text-only while retaining JSON constraints reduced Macro-F1 by 12.53 points. Removing both visual input and JSON constraints led to a 19.23-point drop. Severe evidence loss through "drop-facts" corruption caused a 31.69-point performance collapse, demonstrating the necessity of the underlying visual information and the robust constraint extraction for EVPV's calibration to function effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Explicit Visual Premise Verification (EVPV) represents an advancement in improving the reliability and interpretability of multimodal reasoning systems, particularly those employing Process Reward Models (PRMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Reliability of PRM Scoring:** EVPV directly addresses the critical problem of ambiguous step scores in Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs). By explicitly decoupling visual premise reliability from the correctness of symbolic reasoning steps, it enables a more accurate diagnosis of errors. This reduces both false positives (rewarding hallucinated visual premises) and false negatives (penalizing correct grounded statements), leading to more trustworthy PRM evaluations.

2. **Improved Best-of-N Reranking:** The enhanced reliability of step scores translates into more effective Best-of-N reranking. By accurately identifying solutions that rely on erroneous visual premises, EVPV-PRM can better discriminate between high-quality and flawed reasoning traces, leading to consistent gains in downstream performance metrics across various multimodal reasoning benchmarks. This is particularly impactful for applications where multiple candidate solutions are generated and the best one needs to be selected.

3. **Lightweight and Scalable Design:** EVPV's test-time framework is designed for efficiency. By extracting structured visual constraints only once per instance and reusing them across all steps and candidate solutions, it avoids the high computational cost associated with per-step tool calls, a limitation of some prior tool-integrated verification methods. This makes EVPV a practical solution for deployment in scenarios requiring scalable evaluation.

4. **Judge-Agnostic Calibration Layer:** EVPV functions as a plug-in calibration layer that can be applied to various existing multimodal or large language models acting as step judges. This broad compatibility extends the benefits of premise verification to black-box systems without requiring extensive retraining, offering a versatile tool for improving the grounding capabilities of diverse AI models.

5. **Causal Evidence for Grounded Reasoning:** The controlled corruption experiments provide empirical, interventional evidence affirming that the fidelity of visual perception evidence directly drives the quality of verification. This finding reinforces a fundamental principle in grounded reasoning research and provides insights into the mechanisms underlying multimodal AI performance.

6. **Direction for Future Research:** The work highlights several areas for future exploration, including the development of step-local reliability signals, external visual dependency detectors to improve robustness against policy under-reporting, and methods for enhancing the fidelity and coverage of constraint extractors beyond teacher distillation.

In essence, EVPV contributes to building more transparent and robust multimodal AI systems. Its ability to temper overconfident step rewards under uncertain visual perception holds particular promise for applications in domains like scientific discovery, engineering design, and education, where precise visual interpretation and reliable logical reasoning are paramount.
