# PokeVLA: Empowering Pocket-Sized Vision-Language-Action Model with Comprehensive World Knowledge Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> PokeVLA introduces a lightweight Vision-Language-Action model (1.22B parameters) that achieves state-of-the-art performance in language-instructed robot manipulation. It integrates comprehensive world knowledge through specialized pre-training, multi-view geometry alignment, and goal-aware semantic segmentation, resulting in an 83.5% success rate on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark and improved robustness in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- General-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have a domain gap with robotic manipulation tasks, leading to inefficient action learning.
- Existing VLA models often rely on directly using hidden states from large foundation models, resulting in high computational costs and sub-optimal action learning.
- There is a lack of multi-view spatial consistency and fine-grained guidance in current VLA models, hindering complex task generalization and precise manipulation.

## Method

- **PokeVLA** introduces a ==two-stage training paradigm==: pre-training a compact VLM (**PokeVLM**) on a Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone, followed by ==VL-Action post-training==.
- PokeVLM is pre-trained on a large-scale (2.4M samples) ==multimodal embodied dataset==, curated for general understanding, grounding, affordance, and reasoning.
- VL-Action post-training integrates ==multi-view goal-aware semantic segmentation==, ==geometry alignment== (using **VGGT** during training), and an enhanced action head to inject manipulation-relevant representations.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art total success rate of **83.5%** on the LIBERO-Plus simulation benchmark with a **1.22B**-parameter model, outperforming baselines.
- Demonstrated superior transferability, achieving **79.3%** success rate on LIBERO-Plus when trained solely on LIBERO data, significantly surpassing prior methods.
- Attained **81.25%** average success rate across eight real-world robotic manipulation tasks and maintained **63.0%** under various perturbations, showing enhanced robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A comprehensive, curated multimodal embodied dataset effectively bridges the domain gap between general VLMs and specific robotic manipulation requirements.
> - Goal-aware semantic segmentation, acting as an auxiliary task, provides precise, fine-grained guidance and improves cross-view consistency for manipulation targets.
> - Leveraging a 3D geometric foundation model (VGGT) during training allows implicit learning of 3D structural information, enhancing geometric perception without runtime overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied manipulation has seen advancements with the emergence of foundation models, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation. Early VLA models, such as RT-1 and Octo, were trained on large-scale robotic datasets. More recent efforts, including RT-2, OpenVLA, and RoboFlamingo, have explored fine-tuning large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robotic trajectories to transfer general knowledge to robotic control.

Despite these developments, existing VLA methods encounter several limitations. A primary challenge is the domain gap that arises when directly applying VLMs, pre-trained on general web-scale data, to the specific requirements of embodied manipulation. These models often lack fine-grained, manipulation-relevant representations, leading to inefficient action learning and high computational costs. Further, many approaches lack robust multi-view spatial consistency, which is crucial for handling high-level language instructions involving absolute or relative positions in 3D environments. There is also a deficit in high-level knowledge prediction, meaning models often lack precise guidance toward manipulation targets.

Previous research has attempted to address these issues. DreamVLA and ReconVLA have sought to infuse VLM understanding into action experts by forecasting world knowledge or reconstructing target regions. VLA-Adapter utilizes a bridging attention mechanism to inject VLM hidden states into the action head. Efforts to enhance spatial awareness include SpatialVLA, PointVLA, GeoVLA, and BridgeVLA, which typically introduce additional input modalities or external foundation models, often at the cost of increased computational latency. Spatial Forcing offers an alternative by implicitly aligning vision embeddings during training. Goal-aware VLAs have explored generating subgoals or reconstructing dynamic regions to improve task comprehension. However, a lightweight and comprehensively robust VLA model that effectively overcomes the domain gap, ensures multi-view spatial consistency, and provides fine-grained target guidance without substantial computational overhead remains an active area of research.

PokeVLA positions itself within this landscape by developing a lightweight VLA foundation model designed to overcome these challenges. It aims to efficiently infuse task-specific knowledge, improve spatial and goal awareness, and enhance overall robustness within a compact architecture, distinguishing itself from larger models while addressing the identified shortcomings of prior approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a lightweight yet capable foundation model for embodied manipulation, named PokeVLA, that effectively integrates vision-language understanding with action learning. This objective is driven by the need to address several critical challenges observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

1. **Bridging the Domain Gap in Pre-trained Knowledge**: Current VLMs, while powerful in general visual-language understanding, often exhibit a significant domain gap when directly applied to embodied robotic manipulation tasks. Their general-purpose knowledge is frequently misaligned with the specific spatial, semantic, and action-oriented requirements of robotic environments. This misalignment can lead to inefficient action learning and subpar performance in fine-grained manipulation tasks. The motivation is to infuse VLMs with specialized embodied knowledge to make them more effective and efficient for robotic applications, without compromising their general understanding capabilities.

2. **Enhancing Multi-View Spatial Consistency**: Robotic manipulation often relies on multi-view visual inputs, but existing VLA models frequently lack consistent spatial understanding across these views. This deficiency hinders their ability to generalize to complex language instructions involving absolute or relative positions of objects, as the models struggle to form a coherent 3D understanding of the scene from 2D images. A key motivation is to develop mechanisms that enforce and learn robust multi-view spatial consistency, enabling more accurate and generalizable spatial reasoning for robotic actions.

3. **Providing High-Level Knowledge Prediction and Fine-Grained Guidance**: Many VLA models lack the ability to predict or explicitly incorporate high-level, task-relevant knowledge, such as the precise location or affordance of a manipulation target. Without fine-grained guidance, models can be distracted by irrelevant visual information, reducing robustness and efficiency. The motivation is to introduce mechanisms that guide the model's attention towards the specific manipulation targets and their interaction properties, thereby enhancing task comprehension and action precision.

4. **Improving Efficiency and Computational Cost**: Existing VLA models, particularly those built on large language model backbones, often incur high computational costs, which can limit their deployment in real-world, resource-constrained robotic systems. This research is motivated by the desire to create a lightweight VLA model that achieves high performance and robustness without the prohibitive computational requirements of larger models.

To achieve these objectives, PokeVLA is specifically designed to be (1) lightweight, (2) endowed with rich embodied knowledge (e.g., target localization, spatial awareness, affordance prediction), and (3) capable of effectively injecting manipulation-relevant representations into the action learning process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PokeVLA employs a two-stage training paradigm designed to imbue a compact Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model with comprehensive world knowledge and manipulation-relevant representations.

#### 3.1. VLM Pre-training

The first stage focuses on pre-training an embodied Vision-Language Model (VLM) called PokeVLM.

#### 3.1.1. VLM Pre-training Data Collection

To bridge the gap between general VLMs and robotics, a large-scale, curated multimodal embodied dataset of approximately 2.4 million samples was constructed. This dataset is categorized into four types:
- **General Understanding**: Utilizes LLaVA-Instruct-665K for broad multimodal comprehension, including object recognition and image captioning.
- **Grounding**: Integrates data from RoboPoint, RefSpatial, RoboRefit, and Robospatial datasets. This provides pointQA and boxQA tasks to enhance spatial awareness and object localization in tabletop embodied scenarios.
- **Affordance**: Leverages subsets from HOVA-500K (Ego4D, Epic100) and MoloAct datasets. These provide information on manipulation traces and operation points from a first-person perspective, aiding in understanding action affordances.
- **Reasoning**: Gathers embodied reasoning subsets from RefSpatial and the agibot subset from Cosmos to strengthen the VLM's reasoning capabilities for embodied tasks.
All point coordinates in the dataset are normalized to a range, and annotations are refined. Language descriptions for word-level annotations are enriched using templates and large language models. Data sampling is performed to balance task distribution during pre-training.

#### 3.1.2. VLM Model

PokeVLM is built upon the Prismatic-VLM framework. It consists of:
- A Qwen2.5-0.5B language model backbone.
- An MLP projector for mapping visual features to the language model space.
- A dual-component visual encoder comprising pre-trained SigLIP (for high-level open-vocabulary semantic information) and DinoV2 (for enhanced spatial perception).
Visual features from DINO and SigLIP encoders are concatenated and projected. The model undergoes end-to-end training via next-token prediction using the collected multimodal data. Pre-training parameters include 2 epochs, 8 GPUs, an effective batch size of 128 (4 per GPU with 4 accumulation steps), AdamW optimizer (2e-5 learning rate with linear warm-up and cosine decay), and gradient clipping (max norm 1.0).

#### 3.2. VL-Action Post-training

The second stage adapts the pre-trained PokeVLM into a VLA model for manipulation, building upon the OpenVLA-OFT architecture. This stage integrates high-level semantic and spatial information into the action learning process.

#### 3.2.1. Goal-Aware Manipulating

This component introduces manipulation target segmentation as an auxiliary task to provide fine-grained guidance and ensure consistent understanding of target objects across multiple viewpoints.
- **Embedding-as-mask Paradigm**: A special learnable token `<SEG>` is added to the VLM's vocabulary. Its embedding (`h_seg`) captures contextual scene information and target details.
- **Segmentation Model**: A 2D segmentation foundation model, SAM, is used. Its image encoder extracts dense visual features (`f_sem`) from both base and wrist camera views.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Decoding**:
 - **Coarse Stage**: `h_seg` acts as a text prompt for SAM's prompt encoder (`E_prom`) to generate sparse and dense embeddings. These are fed into a coarse mask decoder (`D_c`) with `f_sem` to produce semantic logits maps (`F`) for each viewpoint.
 - **Fine-grained Stage**: `F` serves as a mask prompt, combined with `h_seg`, and fed into a fine-grained mask decoder (`D_f`) to generate refined pixel-level prediction masks (`M_hat`).
- **Training Objectives**: The segmentation loss (`L_seg`) is calculated using a combination of sigmoid focal loss and Kullback-Leibler Divergence loss between predicted and ground truth masks.

#### 3.2.2. Geometry Alignment

To provide precise geometric perception without increasing inference latency, a powerful 3D geometric foundation model, VGGT, is utilized exclusively during the training phase.
- VGGT extracts target geometric features (`f_geo`) from multi-view images.
- The last-layer hidden states corresponding to the VLM's visual tokens (`h_v`) are projected by a lightweight projector (`P`) and aligned with `f_geo` using cosine similarity. This constitutes the geometry alignment loss (`L_geo`), enabling the VLA's intermediate representations to learn 3D structural information.

#### 3.2.3. Action Head: From VLM to VLA

The action head is designed to efficiently inject the learned manipulation-relevant representations into the action space.
- **Inputs**: The action head receives geometrically enhanced visual hidden states (`h_v`), semantically refined `<SEG>` embeddings (`h_seg`), action query embeddings (`h_q`) from the VLM backbone, and the current robot proprioceptive state (`s_t`).
- **Architecture**: It comprises L layers, each featuring self-attention over action latents (`a_t`), and cross-attention between `a_t` and `[h_q, MLP(s_t)]`, `h_v`, and `h_seg`.
- **Output**: The final action latent is processed through layer normalization and an MLP to produce a chunk of future actions (`A_hat_t`).
- **Training Objectives**: An L1 loss (`L_action`) is minimized between the predicted and ground-truth actions.
- **Overall Training Objective**: The total loss is a weighted sum: `L = L_action + λ_seg L_seg + λ_geo L_geo`, with `λ_seg = 0.2` and `λ_geo = 0.4`.

Post-training is conducted on 8 GPUs with a per-GPU batch size of 8 (global batch size 64) for 150K steps using AdamW and LoRA, with a learning rate of 1e-4 and a cosine annealing schedule with warm-up.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PokeVLA encompassed VLM performance, VLA simulation benchmarks (LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus), detailed ablation studies, and real-world robot experiments.

#### 4.1. VLM Performance

PokeVLM, the pre-trained vision-language model, was evaluated on spatial grounding (Where2Place, RefSpatial) and general understanding (CV-Bench) tasks. Results indicated a substantial improvement in spatial grounding capabilities compared to the original Prismatic-VLM. For instance, PokeVLM's performance on Where2Place's Point, Bbox, and Location tasks, and RefSpatial's Placement and Unseen tasks, significantly surpassed the baseline. A modest improvement in general vision-language understanding (CV-Bench) was also observed. Visualizations on Where2Place demonstrated PokeVLM's ability to accurately localize spatial areas described in instructions, even without direct training on that benchmark, indicating generalization.

#### 4.2. VLA Simulation Benchmark Performance: LIBERO Benchmark

On the standard LIBERO benchmark, PokeVLA (1.22 billion parameters) achieved a total success rate of 98.2%. This performance matches that of the strongest concurrent methods while outperforming many larger or comparable models. PokeVLA achieved near-saturated success rates across Spatial (99.6%), Object (99.6%), and Goal (98.4%) task suites. Notably, on the challenging Long suite, requiring long-horizon planning, it reached 95.2% success, exceeding approaches like CoT-VLA (69.0%) and World-VLA (54.1%).

#### 4.2.2. LIBERO-Plus Benchmark

The more challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, featuring diverse environmental perturbations, was used for further evaluation.
- **Fine-tuned on LIBERO-Plus**: When fine-tuned directly on LIBERO-Plus data, PokeVLA achieved a new state-of-the-art overall success rate of 83.5%. The model demonstrated consistent robustness across various perturbations, with high success rates under visual appearance changes like lightning conditions (99.0%) and background textures (99.3%). It also exhibited strong cross-viewpoint consistency (98.2% under camera viewpoint shifts) and achieved the best performance under robot initialization perturbations (52.9%).
- **Direct Transfer from LIBERO**: In a direct transfer setting, where models were trained only on the standard LIBERO dataset and evaluated on LIBERO-Plus without adaptation, PokeVLA attained a 79.3% total success rate. This significantly outperformed baselines such as OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%). PokeVLA showed pronounced gains under camera viewpoint shifts (84.7% vs. 56.4%), language instruction variations (84.8% vs. 79.5%), and sensor noise (89.8% vs. 75.8%), indicating strong transferability and generalization. Its performance with a compact parameter budget also highlighted its parameter efficiency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study and In-depth Analysis

A series of ablation studies investigated the contribution of each proposed component and the categories of pre-training data.
- **Component Contribution**:
 - **VLM Pre-training**: Led to consistent and significant improvements across all task suites and perturbation types, increasing the total success rate from 78.2% to 82.9%. It particularly enhanced performance on the Goal suite and under language instruction perturbations, also mitigating failures from robot initial pose variations.
 - **Geometry Alignment**: Improved robustness for long-horizon tasks and visual perturbations (lighting, object layout), validating its role in strengthening 3D scene understanding.
 - **Goal-Aware Segmentation**: Demonstrated substantial gains, especially under lighting, background, noise, long-horizon tasks, and camera viewpoint perturbations. This suggests its effectiveness in guiding model attention to relevant regions and ensuring cross-view consistency.
 - The full PokeVLA model achieved the highest overall success rate (85.3%), indicating a synergistic effect among the modules.
- **Pre-training Data Contribution**:
 - Removing **grounding data** led to significant performance drops in goal and long-horizon tasks, highlighting the importance of contextual localization.
 - Excluding **affordance data** weakened robustness against robot initialization perturbations, demonstrating its role in learning object interaction properties.
 - Omitting **reasoning data** caused a decline in performance under language perturbations, suggesting its contribution to parsing diverse instructions.
 These results collectively indicate that a multi-dimensional embodied pre-training data system (grounding, affordance, reasoning) is crucial for comprehensive VLA robustness.
- **Goal-Aware Segmentation Analysis**:
 - The **coarse-to-fine decoding** paradigm showed that the coarse stage captures holistic foreground object relationships with higher attention to manipulation targets, while the fine-grained stage refines these into precise semantic segmentations with strong cross-view consistency.
 - The learned representations exhibited **high consistency and robustness**, maintaining accurate segmentation over long temporal sequences and under various perturbations (background, noise, lighting, camera viewpoint changes), which assists in goal-following and robustness in challenging environments.
 - Visualizations of **attention maps** demonstrated that goal-aware segmentation effectively guides the model's attention to specific manipulation targets and affordance regions, contributing to the generation of high-quality actions.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

Real-world experiments were conducted using a UFACTORY xArm7 robot with two Realsense D435 cameras and 3,000 demonstrations across 60 tasks involving 97 diverse objects.
- **Original Setting**: PokeVLA achieved an 81.25% average success rate across eight diverse tasks, outperforming VLA-Adapter (68.75%) and OpenVLA-OFT (20.00%). It showed superior spatial understanding (e.g., in tasks requiring "rightmost" or "topmost layer" identification) and improved color reference comprehension.
- **Perturbed Setting**: Under five types of perturbations (robot initial pose, object interference, background, lighting, unseen language instructions) on two tasks, PokeVLA maintained a 63.0% average success rate (excluding the original setting), a 20% improvement over VLA-Adapter (43.0%). Its advantage was most notable under robot initial pose and lighting disturbances, confirming the robustness of its learned manipulation-related representations.
- **Qualitative Results**: Visualizations of real-world tasks confirmed PokeVLA's ability to generalize to complex instructions involving spatial, color, and semantic understanding. The goal-aware segmentation consistently provided accurate segmentation results and guided the model's attention to task-relevant regions, supporting successful task completion over long horizons in real environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PokeVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that demonstrates performance typically associated with larger architectures, despite its compact size. The findings suggest that a systematically designed training framework can enable lightweight models to achieve robust and generalized manipulation capabilities.

The primary contributions of this work are:
- The creation of a large-scale embodied multimodal dataset, encompassing approximately 2.4 million samples, for the purpose of pre-training a compact VLM. This dataset facilitates the acquisition of embodied priors for manipulation while maintaining general visual-language understanding.
- The development of a novel methodology for learning manipulation-relevant representations. This includes multi-view consistent learning of manipulation targets, geometric feature alignment, and an action head designed for efficient injection of these representations into the action learning process.
- Comprehensive experimental validation in both simulated and real-world environments. These experiments substantiate the efficacy and necessity of the proposed approach in integrating embodied priors and learning manipulation-relevant representations for robotic manipulation.

The significance of PokeVLA lies in several aspects. It demonstrates that a lightweight VLA model (1.22 billion parameters) can achieve performance comparable to state-of-the-art models and exhibit superior generalization capabilities. The proposed two-stage framework effectively addresses critical limitations of previous VLA research, such as the domain gap between general-purpose VLMs and embodied tasks, the lack of multi-view spatial consistency, and the absence of fine-grained high-level task guidance. The model's robustness under diverse perturbations (including camera viewpoint shifts, robot initial pose variations, language instruction changes, lighting conditions, sensor noise, and object layout alterations) indicates its potential for reliable deployment in varied and challenging real-world scenarios. Furthermore, the goal-aware segmentation mechanism enhances the interpretability of the model by clearly localizing manipulation targets and demonstrating focused attention.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for the field of embodied AI and robotics. By enabling high-performance manipulation with a computationally efficient model, PokeVLA could contribute to making advanced robotics more accessible and affordable, facilitating wider adoption in applications with resource constraints. The systematic approach to integrating task-specific knowledge, spatial awareness, and goal-oriented guidance provides a robust foundation for developing generalist robot policies capable of handling complex and unconstrained manipulation tasks. The open-sourcing of code, model weights, and curated dataset scripts is expected to foster reproducibility and accelerate community progress in developing next-generation compact and robust VLA models.
