# SPHINX: A Synthetic Environment for Visual Perception and Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SPHINX is a new synthetic environment designed by researchers at RIT and the University of Washington to evaluate and advance visual perception and reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). It features 25 procedurally generated task types with verifiable ground truth, revealing that state-of-the-art LVLMs achieve only 51.1% accuracy compared to 75.4% for humans, and demonstrating that Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) significantly improves LVLM performance and generalization.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) significantly lag behind Large Language Models (LLMs) in complex reasoning tasks, particularly those involving genuine visual understanding.
- Existing multimodal benchmarks often prioritize perception-focused tasks, allow models to bypass true visual reasoning, or lack systematic evaluation of fundamental cognitive primitives essential for fluid intelligence.
- There is a need for scalable, diverse, and verifiable datasets to thoroughly diagnose LVLM limitations and facilitate advanced training paradigms like reinforcement learning.

## Method

- SPHINX introduces a novel ==synthetic environment== with a modular, programmatic framework capable of generating diverse visual reasoning data through factorized control of appearance (motifs), spatial layout (tilings), and reasoning rules (tasks).
- It features **25** distinct task types categorized into five broad families (Geometric Reasoning, Counting, Symmetry & Pattern Recognition, Sequence & Transformation, Topological & Graph Reasoning), each with a ==deterministic checker== for unambiguous ground-truth answers.
- The environment includes =='difficulty knobs'== for precise control over problem complexity, enabling tailored dataset generation and making it suitable for ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)==.

## Results

- State-of-the-art proprietary LVLMs like GPT-5 achieved only **51.1%** accuracy across SPHINX's 25 tasks, notably lower than human performance at **75.4%**.
- RLVR consistently improved performance on in-distribution SPHINX tasks and demonstrated generalization, with RL-trained models showing an average accuracy improvement across eight external visual reasoning benchmarks (e.g., **+2.8%** for Qwen3-VL-8B).
- Model accuracy consistently decreased as task complexity increased across all evaluated models, validating SPHINX's ability to provide a tunable difficulty axis for evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art LVLMs, including powerful proprietary models, still exhibit significant gaps in fundamental visual reasoning capabilities compared to human performance, especially in tasks requiring accurate low-level visual extraction and counting.
> - The modular design and verifiable ground truth of SPHINX enable systematic evaluation of cognitive primitives and provide a robust platform for diagnosing specific failure modes in multimodal AI systems.
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), applied using SPHINX-generated data, is an effective paradigm for substantially improving LVLM accuracy on in-distribution tasks, enhancing generalization to held-out tasks, and yielding consistent gains on external visual reasoning benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has observed significant advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) concerning reasoning capabilities, with reported successes in mathematical problems and logical reasoning tasks. This progress has positioned reasoning as a key benchmark for the development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Various techniques, including Chain-of-Thought prompting and post-training strategies like rule-based reinforcement learning, have contributed to these improvements.

In contrast, Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated comparatively lower capabilities in visual reasoning. Unlike text-based systems that can leverage structured prompts, LVLMs face the additional complexity of jointly parsing visual inputs and integrating them with language. Existing benchmarks for LVLMs often prioritize perception over higher-level reasoning, frequently allowing models to reduce visual inputs to text for language-based reasoning rather than engaging in genuine visual interpretation. While some recent work has explored abstract visual reasoning (AVR) in LVLMs, these efforts have not systematically evaluated fundamental perceptual primitives such as symmetry detection, mental rotation, or structured pattern matching. Cognitive science research indicates that these abilities are foundational to fluid intelligence and matrix reasoning, suggesting a need for controlled tasks that can isolate and evaluate these primitives.

The introduction of synthetic environments has been a strategy to overcome the limitations of fixed-size, limited-diversity datasets. Procedural generation allows for controlled variation, scalable dataset construction, and the systematic investigation of model performance across diverse visual and structural conditions. Examples include environments for RPM-style tasks, diagrammatic puzzles, and general generator-verifier frameworks. Reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has also shown promise in improving visual reasoning, leveraging such synthetic environments to provide scalable and precise reward signals for model training. SPHINX aims to contribute to this landscape by providing a new synthetic environment that systematically targets core cognitive primitives for visual perception and reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing SPHINX are:

1. **Address the Gap in Visual Reasoning Evaluation:** The authors observe that existing LVLM benchmarks often do not systematically evaluate core perceptual and reasoning primitives. Many benchmarks focus more on perception or allow models to bypass visual reasoning by converting visual information into text. SPHINX is motivated by the need for a benchmark that directly targets these foundational cognitive abilities, such as symmetry detection, geometric transformations, and spatial reasoning, which are crucial components of human fluid intelligence.

2. **Enable Precise and Verifiable Evaluation:** Current benchmarks can suffer from ambiguity in ground truth or limited scalability. SPHINX aims to provide programmatically generated puzzles, each paired with a deterministically verifiable ground-truth solution. This ensures unambiguous evaluation and facilitates the precise identification of model failure modes, which is essential for guiding model development.

3. **Support Large-Scale Dataset Construction:** To facilitate robust training and evaluation of LVLMs, large and diverse datasets are necessary. SPHINX is designed for procedural generation, allowing for the creation of scalable datasets that can be used for training, including reinforcement learning, mirroring the benefits observed in synthetic reasoning environments for text-based LLMs.

4. **Investigate LVLM Capabilities on Core Cognitive Primitives:** By creating tasks specifically designed around fundamental visual reasoning operations, the authors aim to quantitatively assess the current state-of-the-art LVLMs' abilities in these areas. The motivation is to benchmark how well these models perform compared to human intelligence on tasks that are designed to disentangle perception from abstract reasoning.

5. **Explore the Efficacy of Reinforcement Learning for Visual Reasoning:** The paper is motivated by the potential of RLVR to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LVLMs. By integrating SPHINX with an RL framework, the authors seek to demonstrate whether training with verifiable rewards on these procedurally generated tasks can lead to improvements in in-distribution performance and generalization to unseen tasks and external benchmarks.

In summary, the core motivation is to provide a comprehensive, scalable, and rigorously verifiable synthetic environment that specifically targets and evaluates the core cognitive primitives of visual perception and reasoning in LVLMs, thereby highlighting current limitations and exploring avenues for advancement through reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SPHINX environment is a modular framework designed for the programmatic generation of visual reasoning data with verifiable ground truth. Its design principles emphasize decoupling appearance from rule structure, achieved through three composable modules: motifs, tilings, and tasks.

#### 3.1. Design Principles

1. **Factorized Control of Variation:** Appearance (motifs), spatial layout (tilings), and reasoning rules (tasks) are independently controlled, enabling systematic exploration across perceptual diversity, geometric structures, and rule families.
2. **Verifiable Supervision:** Each generated instance includes a deterministic checker that confirms rule satisfaction and guarantees a single correct answer, eliminating ambiguity and supporting exact evaluation.
3. **Distribution and Difficulty Control:** Weighted samplers manage the mix of tasks and motifs, while difficulty parameters (e.g., number of steps, tile size) allow for fine-grained control over problem complexity.
4. **Standardized Artifacts:** Every generated sample produces a composite image, a natural-language prompt, a ground-truth answer, distractors (if applicable), and rich metadata in analysis-ready formats.

#### 3.2. Building Blocks

- **Motifs (rendered primitives):** Motifs are parameterized renderers that produce RGBA tiles based on attributes such as kind, size, count, angle, and stroke. Families include dots, rings, polygons, star polygons, crescents, and glyphs. Motifs expose attribute ranges and validity clamps, and tasks can bias selection or request asymmetric variants.
- **Geometric primitives:** Beyond motifs, SPHINX renders canonical geometric shapes like circles, n-gons, angles, polylines, grids, and Venn-style region unions to support tasks requiring spatial relations and combinatorial structure.
- **Tilings (geometric canvases):** Tilings define cell layouts and adjacency, including square, triangular, hexagonal, rhombille, and circle variants. Tiling specifications control grid size, margins, adjacency notions, and coloring regimes.

#### 3.3. Tasks

SPHINX encompasses 25 distinct task types, categorized into five broad families. Each task generates a composite image, a natural-language prompt, and a unique correct answer, often with distractors. A core principle is that questions should be visually answerable by a human directly from the image.

1. **Geometric Reasoning:** Tasks in this category probe spatial relations and geometric properties (e.g., area, perimeter, relative size). Examples include Positional Count, Shape Sorting, Stack Count, Pie Chart, and Chart Comparison.
2. **Counting:** This group focuses on counting discrete elements or measuring linear features in visual scenes. Tasks include Venn Diagram, Shape Counting, Tiles Line Length, Tiles Line Intersections, and Tiles Recoloring.
3. **Symmetry & Pattern Recognition:** These tasks require detecting symmetry, periodicity, or identifying odd-one-out patterns. Examples are Mirror Identification, Symmetry Fill, Frieze Groups, and Wallpaper Groups.
4. **Sequence & Transformation Reasoning:** This category involves temporal sequences, rotation progressions, or transformation inference. Tasks include Transform Result Identify, Transform Pair Infer, Transform Similarity Identify, Sequence Rotation, Sequence Arithmetic, and Sequence Multi-Column Arithmetic.
5. **Topological & Graph Reasoning:** These tasks involve reasoning about connectivity, paths, and assembly on tilings or grids. Examples include Tiles Geometry, Tiles Connected Component, Tiles Shortest Path, Missing Tiles, and Tiles Composition.

#### 3.4. Benchmark Construction

A benchmark dataset was curated from SPHINX, comprising 2,500 questions (100 instances per task). This benchmark was used to evaluate six proprietary LVLMs (including three GPT-5 variants, Gemini3-Flash, Claude4.5-Opus, and Claude4.5-Sonnet) and thirteen open-source LVLMs (including Qwen2.5-VL, Llama 3.2, InternVL3, Molmo2, and Qwen3-VL families). Human performance was also evaluated for comparison. Answer extraction and verification used the mathruler library, with GPT-5-mini as a fallback for ambiguous cases.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

- **Data Split:** For RLVR training, 20 tasks were designated as in-distribution, and 5 tasks (Positional Count, Tiles Recoloring, Wallpaper Groups, Sequence Multi-Column Arithmetic, Tiles Composition) were held out for out-of-distribution (OOD) testing. A training set of 32,000 samples (1,600 per in-distribution task) was generated, selected to maximize semantic similarity to evaluation samples.
- **Model Training:** Four base models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen3-VL-8B) were fine-tuned using GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with the EasyR1 framework. The reward function was a combination of binary correctness (1 for correct, 0 otherwise) and an auxiliary formatting reward, with correctness verified against ground truth using mathruler.
- **Evaluation:** RLVR-trained models were evaluated on both the IID and OOD subsets of SPHINX, as well as on eight external visual reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, LogicVista, BLINK, MMT-Bench, MMVP, and VStarBench) to assess generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. LVLM Performance on SPHINX Benchmark

- **Overall Accuracy:** GPT-5 achieved the highest overall accuracy among all evaluated LVLMs at 51.1%, but this remained 24.7% lower than human performance (75.8%). GPT-5 Mini followed with 47.1%.
- **Category-wise Performance:** GPT-5 demonstrated the strongest results in Geometric Reasoning (74.0%), Symmetry and Pattern Recognition (50.0%), Sequence and Transformation Reasoning (51.5%), and Topological and Graph Reasoning (43.0%). Claude4.5-Sonnet obtained the best Counting score at 40.2%.
- **Open-Source Models:** Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-235B achieved the highest accuracy at 41.3%, outperforming some proprietary models like Claude4.5-Opus.

#### 4.2. Human Evaluation Insights

- **Familiarity Correlation:** A strong correlation was observed between participants' self-reported familiarity with visual reasoning puzzles and their accuracy (e.g., average accuracy of 65.2% for familiarity level 1 vs. 88.0% for level 5).
- **Challenging Human Tasks:** Tasks requiring mental rotation, multi-step spatial reasoning, or fine-grained symmetry classification were particularly challenging for humans. The Frieze Group Odd-One-Out task had the lowest human accuracy (48.4%), followed by Tiles Composition, which also had the longest average response time, suggesting a genuine cognitive load.
- **Counting Tasks:** Tasks involving mental arithmetic and grid-based enumeration (e.g., Venn Diagram, Shape Counting) showed lower human scores, potentially due to the absence of external aids.

#### 4.3. Comparative Analysis (GPT-5 vs. Humans & GPT-5 Mini)

- **GPT-5 vs. Humans:** GPT-5 showed largest deficits on tile-based tasks (e.g., Tiles Line Length, Tiles Recoloring), which humans found relatively intuitive. Conversely, GPT-5 outperformed humans on the Shape Counting task and performed comparably on chart-based tasks and Tiles Composition, a task where human performance was relatively low.
- **GPT-5 vs. GPT-5 Mini:** GPT-5 generally outperformed GPT-5 Mini on tasks with explicit procedural structure, counting, or specified transformations, where it could leverage step-by-step reasoning (e.g., Stack Count, Transform Result Identify). It also exhibited a stronger ability to identify global image structure. GPT-5 Mini, however, showed advantages in tasks like Sequence Arithmetic, suggesting varying strengths in different types of counting or pattern recognition. Both models displayed weaknesses in low-level visual perception, often making errors in visual extraction despite producing coherent textual reasoning.

#### 4.4. Task Complexity vs. Model Accuracy

For 17 tasks with well-defined complexity controls, model accuracy consistently decreased as complexity increased across all evaluated models, including RLVR-trained models. This indicates that SPHINX can effectively scale task difficulty.

#### 4.5. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) Performance

- **SPHINX Test Set:** RLVR training resulted in consistent and substantial gains (ranging from +11.4% to +17.1%) in in-distribution tasks for all four base models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/3B, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B).
- **Generalization to OOD Tasks:** These gains partially transferred to held-out OOD tasks, though with greater variation. Positional Count saw the largest improvements, potentially due to structural similarity with in-distribution tasks. Tiles Recoloring remained challenging, with some RLVR models showing degraded performance.
- **External Benchmarks:** RLVR-trained models consistently improved average accuracy across eight external visual reasoning benchmarks. Performance gains were observed in 26 out of 32 model-dataset combinations, with the largest average gains for Qwen3-VL-4B (+2.8%) and Qwen3-VL-8B (+2.4%). This suggests that RLVR on SPHINX tasks enhances generalization to broader multimodal reasoning challenges.
- **Response Length:** Models trained with RLVR consistently produced shorter responses on both SPHINX and external benchmarks, a trend potentially influenced by the maximum token response length enforced during training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SPHINX represents an advancement in the evaluation and development of visual perception and reasoning capabilities in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs).

1. **Systematic Evaluation Framework:** SPHINX provides a large-scale, procedurally generated environment that systematically targets core cognitive primitives such as symmetry, transformation, and spatial operations. This addresses a recognized gap in existing benchmarks, which often emphasize perception over reasoning or lack the granularity to evaluate fundamental visual reasoning skills. The modular design, verifiable ground-truth solutions, and control over task difficulty enable rigorous and reproducible evaluation.

2. **Highlighting LVLM Limitations:** The benchmark findings clearly demonstrate that even state-of-the-art LVLMs, including advanced proprietary models, exhibit significant performance gaps compared to human abilities on these core visual reasoning tasks. This indicates that current LVLMs often struggle with accurate visual extraction and consistent application of reasoning chains, even when their textual reasoning might appear coherent. This comprehensive diagnostic value offers insights into current model limitations, particularly in distinguishing between superficial visual cues and underlying geometric or logical patterns.

3. **Advancement Through Reinforcement Learning:** The successful application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) on SPHINX tasks demonstrates a promising direction for enhancing LVLM reasoning capabilities. The observed consistent gains on in-distribution tasks and measurable improvements in generalization to external benchmarks suggest that targeted training in environments with precise, verifiable feedback can effectively advance multimodal reasoning. This opens avenues for developing more robust and generalized visual reasoning systems.

4. **Resource for Future Research:** By making SPHINX open-source (project page, code, and dataset), the authors provide a valuable resource for the research community. This can facilitate further investigations into LVLM architectures, training paradigms, and the development of new techniques to bridge the identified gap between artificial and human visual reasoning. The extensibility of SPHINX to incorporate additional task families and more complex inputs ensures its relevance for evolving model capabilities.

In conclusion, SPHINX offers a diagnostic tool and a training environment that can contribute to the fundamental understanding and advancement of machine intelligence in visual perception and reasoning.
