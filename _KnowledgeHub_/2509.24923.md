# When Greedy Wins: Emergent Exploitation Bias in Meta-Bandit LLM Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Duke University and Mila investigated LLMs trained for multi-armed bandit tasks, revealing that while both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning approaches yield low cumulative regret, they induce an emergent exploitation bias where models adopt sophisticated greedy strategies instead of robust exploration, sometimes exhibiting arithmetic degradation in novel settings.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with the fundamental exploration-exploitation trade-off in sequential decision-making, exhibiting myopic and greedy behavior.
- There is a critical gap in understanding how different training paradigms (Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning) mechanistically shape LLM exploration strategies.
- Existing research lacked comprehensive analysis of LLM generalization capabilities to novel environments and longer decision horizons in these strategic tasks.

## Method

- Trained Qwen 2.5 3B/7B Instruct models as ==meta-bandit== agents using both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- Introduced novel reinforcement learning reward designs: Strategic Reward (RL-STR) to reduce variance and Algorithmic Reward (RL-ALG) for binary imitation of an oracle policy like UCB.
- Employed a natural language input format with summarized interaction history and prompted Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for LLM decision-making.

## Results

- Both SFT and RL methods substantially improved LLM performance in Multi-Armed Bandit tasks (cumulative regret comparable to classical optimal baselines like UCB), with fine-tuned LLMs generalizing robustly to horizons **6x** longer than training and non-trivial cross-distributional transfer between Gaussian and Bernoulli environments.
- Behavioral analysis showed trained LLMs exhibited higher suffix failure rates and accelerated exploitation (higher GreedyFreq) compared to baselines, indicating a systematic shift towards greedy decision-making.
- RL-ALG policies, which learned to imitate a UCB oracle with binary rewards, consistently outperformed other learned policies and occasionally exceeded their teacher's average regret.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs, despite achieving low average regret, develop an emergent exploitation bias, adopting more sophisticated but ultimately greedy strategies rather than robust long-term exploration.
> - Reward design critically influences LLM learning; RL-ALG, by framing imitation as an RL problem, effectively simplifies credit assignment and improves performance.
> - Supervised fine-tuning can lead to overfitting and catastrophic forgetting of basic arithmetic, resulting in fragile generalization to out-of-distribution tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical challenge in the development of autonomous Large Language Models (LLMs): their ability to effectively navigate sequential decision-making problems, particularly those involving the exploration-exploitation trade-off. While LLMs exhibit sophisticated reasoning capabilities, they often fall short in tasks requiring strategic information gathering and long-term planning, frequently defaulting to short-sighted, greedy behavior. This paper positions itself within the burgeoning field of **In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL)**, where LLMs are expected to learn and adapt from their interactions with an environment.

Previous research, as cited, has explored the inherent limitations of pre-trained LLMs in Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) tasks, noting their suboptimal exploratory behavior without extensive prompt engineering (e.g., Krishnamurthy et al., 2024; Monea et al., 2024). Attempts to enhance LLM exploration capabilities have primarily focused on two paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on expert trajectories (Nie et al., 2024) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) (Schmied et al., 2025). While these studies showed improvements, a comprehensive understanding of *how* these training methods shape exploration strategies, their mechanistic differences, and crucially, their generalization capabilities to novel environments and longer horizons, has been lacking.

This paper directly bridges this gap by providing a systematic comparison of SFT and RL for training LLMs as "meta-bandit agents" (Kveton et al., 2020). It extends prior work by not only comparing performance but also deeply analyzing the *behavioral aspects* of the learned policies. Furthermore, it introduces novel reward designs for RL that enhance learning efficiency and generalization, explicitly improving upon baselines from prior work (Schmied et al., 2025). By revealing an emergent exploitation bias in trained LLMs despite achieving low regret, the authors move beyond traditional aggregate performance metrics, advocating for a more nuanced evaluation that considers the robustness of exploration strategies. This contributes significantly to the understanding of LLM limitations and guiding future research towards building truly robust and generalizable autonomous agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that while LLMs hold immense potential as autonomous agents, they frequently struggle with the fundamental exploration-exploitation trade-off in sequential decision-making. Their default behavior tends to be myopic and greedy, hindering robust performance in dynamic environments. The paper identifies a critical gap in understanding how different learning paradigms—Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL)—mechanistically shape LLM exploration strategies and how well these strategies generalize.

The key objectives of this study are:

1. **Systematically compare SFT and RL:** To investigate the impact of both supervised fine-tuning (on expert trajectories) and reinforcement learning (with tailored reward signals) on LLM exploration behavior in multi-armed bandit (MAB) tasks.
2. **Evaluate generalization capabilities:** To assess how well the learned policies generalize to significantly longer decision horizons (e.g., 6x longer than training) and out-of-distribution (OOD) environments, including different bandit families (Gaussian to Bernoulli and vice versa).
3. **Propose and evaluate novel RL reward designs:** To introduce and test two new reward signals for RL training:
 - **Strategic (Regret-Shaped) Reward (RL-STR):** Designed to reduce training variance and improve efficiency, based on the notion of immediate regret.
 - **Algorithmic Reward (RL-ALG):** A binary signal that incentivizes direct imitation of an oracle policy (like UCB) via RL, aiming to simplify credit assignment.
4. **Conduct behavioral analysis beyond aggregate metrics:** To move beyond average cumulative regret—the canonical MAB metric—and perform a deeper behavioral analysis using surrogate statistics (e.g., suffix failure rate, greedy action frequency, best arm selection distribution). The goal is to uncover the underlying decision-making strategies adopted by LLMs and determine if lower regret genuinely indicates robust exploration or merely more sophisticated exploitation.

Ultimately, the motivation is to clarify when each training paradigm is preferable, advocate for more sophisticated reward design, and promote comprehensive evaluation beyond simple performance metrics to foster truly robust exploratory behavior in LLM agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a comprehensive methodology to train and evaluate LLM agents as meta-bandit learners on Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) tasks.

**A. LLM Agent Setup:**
- **Base Models:** Qwen 2.5 3B and 7B Instruct models are used as the LLM backbone.
- **Input Representation:** At each turn, the LLM receives a natural language instruction along with a summarized interaction history. This summary includes sufficient statistics for each arm: number of pulls and average reward (as shown in Figure 1). This is noted as more effective than raw historical lists.
- **Reasoning:** The LLM is prompted to use Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, explicit thought processes, before providing its action in an `<answer>` tag.

**B. Training Paradigms:**

1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to train the LLM.
 - **Hierarchical MDP:** The problem is framed as a two-level hierarchical MDP, where the high-level policy selects an action at each turn (given the external reward), and a low-level policy generates the sequence of tokens for the response.
 - **Token-Level Advantages:** PPO is adapted to compute token-level advantages using a dual-($\gamma, \lambda$) Generalized Advantage Estimator (GAE) to account for both intra-turn (token-level) and inter-turn (episode-level) dynamics. A reward shaping term encourages valid responses.
 - **Reward Signals:** Three distinct reward signals are investigated:
 - **RL-OG (Original Bandit Rewards):** Direct use of the stochastic environmental reward ($r_t$). This poses credit assignment challenges due to stochasticity.
 - **RL-STR (Strategic Reward):** Based on immediate regret ($\tilde{r}_t = 1 - \frac{\Delta_t}{\Delta_{\text{max}}}$). This pseudo-regret based reward acts as a control variate, aiming to reduce variance and simplify credit assignment without altering the optimal policy.
 - **RL-ALG (Algorithmic Reward):** A binary signal ($r_t=1$ if the agent's action matches a UCB oracle's decision, $0$ otherwise). This enables RL-based imitation learning without explicit supervision on reasoning.

2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Data Generation:** LLMs are trained on observation-response pairs, where observations are MAB states and responses include synthetic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) demonstrations that explicitly calculate UCB values and the corresponding UCB action (Figure 2).
 - **Learning:** The model is trained to minimize cross-entropy loss between predicted and ground-truth responses, directly supervising both the reasoning and the final action.

**C. Experimental Setup:**
- **Bandit Environments:** A diverse set of 15 MAB environments is used for training and evaluation, spanning Gaussian and Bernoulli reward distributions, varying number of arms (5 to 10), and different mean/variance distributions (Table 1, A.1). Two environments (Gaussian5_Var1_MeanN0 and Bernoulli5_Uniform) are selected as training task distributions.
- **Baselines:** Standard bandit algorithms are used for comparison: Upper Confidence Bound (UCB), Thompson Sampling (TS), $\epsilon$-Greedy, and a purely Greedy policy.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Performance:** Cumulative regret (at 50 and 300 steps), time-averaged reward (AvgReward), and best arm frequency (BestArmFreq).
 - **Behavioral Analysis:** Suffix failure rate (frequency of never selecting the optimal arm again after a certain point) and greedy action frequency (GreedyFreq) are used to diagnose long-term exploration failures.
 - **Imitation Analysis:** Match rate with the oracle's decisions and average absolute difference in UCB calculations (for SFT) are used to understand imitation fidelity.

**D. Training Details:**
- **RL Training:** 64 parallel environments, 50-step rollouts, PPO updates over 500 total steps (Table 4).
- **SFT Training:** 32k transitions, 6 epochs (Table 5).

The approach combines rigorous quantitative performance evaluation with deep qualitative and mechanistic analysis of LLM behavior, particularly focusing on generalization and the nature of learned exploration strategies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings that both affirm the potential of LLMs in sequential decision-making and uncover critical limitations:

1. **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:**
 - Both SFT and RL significantly improve LLM performance on MAB tasks compared to pre-trained models. The fine-tuned agents achieve lower cumulative regret, comparable to classical optimal baselines like UCB and Thompson Sampling.
 - Learned policies demonstrate robust **length generalization**, performing well on horizons 6x longer than training (50 to 300 steps).
 - They also exhibit **non-trivial cross-distribution transfer**, generalizing from Gaussian-trained policies to Bernoulli environments and vice-versa, and to increased action spaces (5 to 10 arms). RL-trained policies generally show more robust OOD generalization than SFT, particularly for environmental feedback-based methods (RL-OG, RL-STR).

2. **Impact of Reward Design in RL:**
 - **RL-ALG (Algorithmic Reward):** This approach, where RL incentivizes imitation of an oracle (UCB) via a binary reward signal, consistently outperforms other learned policies. This is attributed to the simplified credit assignment in a reactive, myopic imitation setting. Intriguingly, RL-ALG policies sometimes *outperform* their UCB teacher.
 - **RL-STR (Strategic Reward):** The regret-shaped reward effectively improves training efficiency and policy performance in high-variance environments (e.g., Gaussian), by reducing the stochasticity of the reward signal.
 - Overall, policies optimized with UCB teacher signals (RL-ALG, SFT) consistently outperform those trained solely on direct task rewards (RL-OG), highlighting the challenge of credit assignment in long-horizon exploration.

3. **Emergent Exploitation Bias (The "Greedy Wins" Phenomenon):**
 - Despite achieving lower average regret, a deeper behavioral analysis revealed that LLM policies, especially those trained to imitate or learn optimal behavior, develop a **more sophisticated but ultimately greedier exploitation strategy**.
 - **Higher Suffix Failure Rate:** Learned agents exhibit a higher frequency of "suffix failures" (prematurely abandoning the optimal arm for the rest of the episode) compared to both pre-trained models and theoretical baselines like UCB.
 - **Accelerated Exploitation:** They reach the exploitation phase more quickly, as indicated by higher "GreedyFreq" (frequency of selecting the greedy action). The distribution of best arm selection becomes bimodal, characteristic of greedy behavior.
 - **Suboptimal UCB Variants:** RL-ALG agents, while trained to imitate UCB, often learn and explicitly state in their rationales **exploitative variants** of the UCB algorithm. For instance, instead of the standard UCB term $\sqrt{\frac{\log(t)}{N_t(a)}}$ (where $t$ is total steps), they adopt $\sqrt{\frac{\log(N_t(a)+1)}{N_t(a)}}$, which allows for earlier abandonment of seemingly unprofitable arms. This explains why they can outperform the oracle in average regret, but at the cost of long-term robustness.

4. **SFT's Strengths and Weaknesses:**
 - SFT policies show high fidelity to the UCB teacher in-domain, but this can lead to **overfitting**.
 - Crucially, SFT policies can exhibit **catastrophic forgetting of basic arithmetic skills** when exposed to out-of-distribution environments, especially those with negative rewards not encountered during training. This results in systematic calculation errors and prompts the LLM to disregard its own computed UCB values, leading to degraded generalization and higher worst-case regret in OOD settings. This explains the observed fragility in generalization for SFT compared to RL-ALG.

5. **Model Size and Learning Challenges:**
 - Smaller models (3B parameters) struggle significantly with direct RL from environmental rewards (RL-OG, RL-STR), showing stagnant performance. However, they can learn effectively with teacher guidance via SFT or RL-ALG, emphasizing the difficulty of credit assignment for smaller models in complex, long-horizon tasks.

In summary, while fine-tuning significantly boosts LLM performance in MAB, it often induces a subtle but pervasive exploitation bias. The models learn to achieve high average returns by becoming highly efficient exploiters, sometimes at the expense of robust long-term exploration and adaptability to truly novel challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the development of intelligent, autonomous LLM agents and advances our understanding of their learning dynamics in sequential decision-making.

1. **Refined Understanding of LLM Learning:** The study provides a crucial mechanistic understanding of how SFT and RL shape LLM exploration. It moves beyond superficial performance metrics to reveal that observed improvements in average regret can mask an underlying **emergent exploitation bias**. This "greedy wins" phenomenon challenges the assumption that lower regret automatically signifies robust exploratory behavior, pushing the community to adopt more nuanced evaluation metrics beyond mere average performance.
2. **Improved Reward Design for LLM-RL:** The introduction and validation of strategic (regret-shaped) and algorithmic (oracle-imitation) reward signals offer practical and effective strategies for training LLMs in complex environments. The success of RL-ALG, in particular, demonstrates the power of framing imitation learning within an RL paradigm for efficient credit assignment, especially when dealing with explicit algorithmic teachers.
3. **Guidance for Robust Agent Development:** By highlighting the trade-off between immediate returns and long-term robustness, the findings provide critical guidance for designing and training future LLM agents. Developers must consider whether their application prioritizes maximum average performance (where the learned exploitative strategies might suffice) or requires robust worst-case performance and persistent exploration. This calls for a re-evaluation of training objectives and evaluation protocols.
4. **Addressing Generalization Gaps:** The demonstration of robust generalization to longer horizons and cross-distributional settings is encouraging for the scalability of LLM agents. However, the observed fragility of SFT to OOD arithmetic tasks and the discovery of exploitative UCB variants underscore the need for models that can generalize not just in terms of task dynamics but also in foundational reasoning capabilities.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly points towards promising avenues for future work, such as:
 - **Explicitly amplifying exploration signals:** Techniques like focused replay (re-weighting experiences by information gain) or adversarial/curriculum-based environments could counter the emergent exploitation bias.
 - **Enhancing arithmetic robustness:** Addressing the catastrophic forgetting of basic arithmetic in SFT is crucial for more reliable generalization of algorithm-following agents.
 - **Fine-grained RL signals:** Exploring more granular RL signals to overcome credit assignment challenges and guide LLMs to internalize correct algorithmic logic, rather than approximate, exploitative heuristics.

In essence, "When Greedy Wins" is a call to action for the LLM research community to look deeper than aggregate performance numbers, fostering the development of truly intelligent and adaptable autonomous agents capable of robust exploration in the face of uncertainty.
