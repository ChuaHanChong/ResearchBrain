# When Greedy Wins: Emergent Exploitation Bias in Meta-Bandit LLM Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Duke University and Mila investigated LLMs trained for multi-armed bandit tasks, revealing that while both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning approaches yield low cumulative regret, they induce an emergent exploitation bias where models adopt sophisticated greedy strategies instead of robust exploration, sometimes exhibiting arithmetic degradation in novel settings.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with the fundamental exploration-exploitation trade-off in sequential decision-making, exhibiting myopic and greedy behavior.
- There is a critical gap in understanding how different training paradigms (Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning) mechanistically shape LLM exploration strategies.
- Existing research lacked comprehensive analysis of LLM generalization capabilities to novel environments and longer decision horizons in these strategic tasks.

## Method

- Trained Qwen 2.5 3B/7B Instruct models as ==meta-bandit== agents using both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- Introduced novel reinforcement learning reward designs: Strategic Reward (RL-STR) to reduce variance and Algorithmic Reward (RL-ALG) for binary imitation of an oracle policy like UCB.
- Employed a natural language input format with summarized interaction history and prompted Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for LLM decision-making.

## Results

- Both SFT and RL methods substantially improved LLM performance in Multi-Armed Bandit tasks, achieving cumulative regret comparable to classical optimal baselines like UCB.
- Fine-tuned LLMs demonstrated robust generalization to horizons **6x** longer than training and non-trivial cross-distributional transfer between Gaussian and Bernoulli environments.
- Behavioral analysis showed trained LLMs exhibited higher suffix failure rates and accelerated exploitation (higher GreedyFreq) compared to baselines, indicating a systematic shift towards greedy decision-making.
- RL-ALG policies, which learned to imitate a UCB oracle with binary rewards, consistently outperformed other learned policies and occasionally exceeded their teacher's average regret.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs, despite achieving low average regret, develop an emergent exploitation bias, adopting more sophisticated but ultimately greedy strategies rather than robust long-term exploration.
> - Reward design critically influences LLM learning; RL-ALG, by framing imitation as an RL problem, effectively simplifies credit assignment and improves performance.
> - Supervised fine-tuning can lead to overfitting and catastrophic forgetting of basic arithmetic, resulting in fragile generalization to out-of-distribution tasks.
