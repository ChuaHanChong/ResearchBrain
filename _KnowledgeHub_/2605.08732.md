# Latent Geometry Beyond Search: Amortizing Planning in World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A lightweight Goal-Conditioned Inverse Dynamics Model (GC-IDM) directly predicts actions in a frozen, pretrained latent world model, replacing computationally intensive online search. This approach achieved a 100-130x speedup in planning and matched or surpassed the success rates of established search-based planners across various tasks, while also generating significantly smoother actions.

## Problem

- Converting rapid latent world model predictions into effective actions for goal-oriented planning incurs a high computational "planning tax" due to intensive online optimization.
- State-of-the-art search-based methods, such as CEM, can require thousands of model rollouts and tens of thousands of forward passes per planning step, bottlenecking control systems.
- Existing approaches often don't fully leverage the structure of learned latent representations to simplify the control problem itself beyond merely enabling prediction.

## Method

- Introduced a **Goal-Conditioned Inverse Dynamics Model (GC-IDM)** designed to directly predict the next action from the current latent state, goal latent state, and remaining horizon.
- The **GC-IDM** is a lightweight (1.5M parameters) ==3-layer MLP==, trained offline via ==supervised regression== on a dataset of expert demonstrations, using a frozen pretrained world model's latents.
- This learned model replaces the iterative online search process of traditional ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== at test time, providing a single, fast forward pass for action selection.

## Results

- GC-IDM achieved a **100 to 130 times faster** planning rate per decision compared to CEM, simultaneously matching or exceeding its success rates in **seven out of eight** environment-protocol settings.
- Across a broader range of planners including MPPI and iCEM, GC-IDM consistently obtained the highest success rate at matched hyperparameters and maintained substantial speed advantages.
- The model produced significantly smoother actions, exhibiting **15 to 36 times lower** action jerk, and demonstrated more direct latent space trajectories towards goals compared to search-based alternatives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A sufficiently well-organized latent space, characterized by smoothness and action-sensitivity (as induced by regularization like SIGReg), can transform complex action search into a simpler, local inverse problem.
> - The "planning tax" from online search in world models is not always inherent to control, but can be amortized by inferring actions directly from a learned inverse dynamics model operating on latent states.
> - Providing multi-step horizon supervision during training is crucial for the inverse dynamics model's ability to perform effective goal-conditioned control, outperforming single-step behavioral cloning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning and control, specifically focusing on the integration of learned world models with efficient action selection. Modern vision-based world models have demonstrated an ability to learn compact and expressive latent representations of observations, enabling accurate prediction of future states. However, a persistent challenge in these systems is the computational cost associated with goal-oriented planning in the learned latent spaces. Many current approaches rely on iterative online search algorithms, such as the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), to derive action sequences from model predictions.

The paper builds upon the LeWorldModel (LeWM) framework [Maes et al., 2026], an efficient Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) that learns a world model from pixels. LeWM is characterized by its relatively small parameter count (~15M), fast training times, and the use of the SIGReg regularizer to encourage a smooth and uniform latent geometry. While LeWM improves the efficiency of the world model itself, its control component, implemented via CEM, still incurs significant computational overhead, referred to by the authors as the "planning tax."

The work distinguishes itself from prior research in several ways. While latent world models (e.g., Ha & Schmidhuber, Hafner et al.) and various test-time planning methods (e.g., CEM, MPPI, gradient-based optimization) are established, this paper does not introduce a new world model or a novel optimizer. Instead, it investigates whether the *online search* performed by these optimizers can be *amortized* into a learned inference step, by leveraging properties of the underlying latent representation.

The concept of inverse dynamics for control has been explored in various contexts, often as a mechanism for action proposal, an auxiliary prediction head, or as part of imitation learning (e.g., Agrawal et al., 2016; Pathak et al., 2017; Baker et al., 2022). GLAMOR [Paster et al., 2021] is noted as a precursor, learning recurrent inverse dynamics models from pixels to predict action sequences. However, the current work differs by predicting a single action per step with a feedforward MLP, replanning from fresh observations, and operating on the frozen latent space of a pretrained JEPA world model. Other related methods like Seer [Tian et al., 2025] and Latent Diffusion Planning [Xie et al., 2025] forecast future states before decoding actions. Mimic-video [Pai et al., 2025] demonstrates strong control from pretrained visual backbones and lightweight inverse models. Theoretical analyses (e.g., Schäfer et al., 2026; Morin et al., 2026) provide a basis for the effectiveness of simple inverse models when paired with structured latent representations. This paper contributes to this line of inquiry by empirically demonstrating that, under specific conditions of latent space regularity, a learned inverse mapping can fully replace iterative planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the computational expense of goal-oriented planning in modern vision-based world models, specifically the "planning tax" incurred by online search algorithms. The authors raise a central question: to what extent does a learned representation simplify control beyond merely enabling prediction? They investigate whether the high computational cost associated with action selection is an inherent requirement or a consequence of how control problems are typically formulated within these systems.

The motivation stems from the observation that, even with computationally efficient world models like LeWM (which can be trained on a single GPU in hours and plans faster than some foundation-model-based alternatives), the subsequent action selection via methods like CEM remains a significant bottleneck. For instance, LeWM's default CEM configuration requires 9,000 candidate rollouts and 45,000 predictor forward passes for every planning step, making search the dominant computational cost.

The authors propose a hypothesis: if a latent space is sufficiently well-organized, characterized by smoothness and action-sensitivity (properties that regularizers like SIGReg are designed to encourage in LeWM), then the task of action selection might be describable as a simpler, local inverse problem, rather than a generic combinatorial search. Under such geometric conditions, the control problem could be *amortized* into a learned inverse-dynamics mapping.

Therefore, the specific objective is to replace the iterative online search process with a lightweight Goal-Conditioned Inverse Dynamics Model (GC-IDM). This model is designed to directly map the current latent state, the goal latent state, and the remaining planning horizon to the next action. The underlying premise is that a well-regularized latent representation inherently encodes much of the structure that online planners typically attempt to recover, making it locally accessible for direct inference. The work aims to empirically validate whether such an inverse dynamics model can serve as a faithful and efficient abstraction for control in world models with structured latent spaces.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core approach of this research is to leverage a pretrained, regularized latent world model to amortize online planning into a single, learned inference step.

**Problem Formulation and Latent World Model:**
The problem is defined as finite-horizon, goal-conditioned control from observations. An agent observes `o_t`, aims to reach a goal `o_g` within a horizon `h_t`, and selects action `a_t`. The LeWorldModel (LeWM) serves as the backbone, consisting of an encoder `enc_θ` mapping observations to latent states (`z_t = enc_θ(o_t)`) and a predictor `pred_ϕ` modeling one-step latent dynamics (`z_t+1 = pred_ϕ(z_t, a_t)`). LeWM is optimized with a one-step latent prediction loss and an isotropy-inducing regularizer, SIGReg, which promotes a smooth and well-distributed latent geometry. The authors utilize a *frozen* pretrained LeWM encoder and predictor.

**Goal-Conditioned Inverse Dynamics Model (GC-IDM):**
The proposed GC-IDM replaces search-based planning. Its motivation stems from a pilot study using a pairwise inverse dynamics model, which decoded actions from consecutive latent pairs but performed poorly in planning due to the difficulty of constructing valid latent paths between distant states via linear interpolation. This suggested the need for direct goal conditioning.

GC-IDM is designed as a lightweight neural network:
- **Input:** Current latent state `z_t` (derived from `enc_θ(o_t)`), goal latent state `z_g` (derived from `enc_θ(o_g)`), and the remaining horizon `h_t`.
- **Architecture:** A 3-layer MLP with a hidden dimension of 512, LayerNormalization, GELU activation, and 10% dropout. `z_t` and `z_g` are concatenated at the input. The remaining horizon `h_t` is first normalized, sinusoidally encoded into 64 dimensions, and then processed by a 2-layer MLP to produce scaling (`γ`) and bias (`β`) terms. These terms modulate the backbone output via AdaLN-Zero modulation (`h ⊙ (1 + γ(c)) + β(c)`), applied before the final action head. The `γ` and `β` layers are zero-initialized to introduce the horizon signal gradually. The model comprises approximately 1.5 million parameters, which is about 10% of the LeWM backbone.

**Training of GC-IDM:**
- GC-IDM is trained offline using the *same demonstration dataset* that was used for LeWM. No additional environment interaction is required.
- The LeWM encoder `enc_θ` is *frozen* during GC-IDM training.
- Training examples `(z_t, z_g, h, a_t)` are created by sampling `(t, h)` from trajectories, where `h` is uniformly drawn from `[1, H_max]`. `z_t` and `z_g` are obtained by encoding observations `o_t` and `o_t+h` with the frozen `enc_θ`. `a_t` is the ground-truth action at time `t`.
- The loss function is an MSE regression: `L_gc-idm(ψ) = E[(gc-idm_ψ(z_t, z_t+h, h) - a_t)^2]`. Gradients only flow into the GC-IDM parameters (`ψ`).
- Training is fast, approximately 20 minutes per environment on a single GPU.

**Inference/Planning with GC-IDM (Closed-Loop Control):**
At test time, the GC-IDM replaces the iterative optimization loop of traditional planners.
1. The goal observation `o_g` is encoded *once* to `z_g` and cached.
2. At each environment step `t` within a given budget `T`:
 - The current observation `o_t` is *re-encoded* to `z_t`.
 - The remaining horizon `h_t = T - t + 1` is calculated (and clamped to `H_max`).
 - The GC-IDM predicts the next action `a_t = gc-idm_ψ(z_t, z_g, h_t)` in a single forward pass.
 - This `a_t` is applied to the environment.
This constitutes a closed-loop, receding-horizon control strategy, where decisions are made based on the true current state from fresh observations, without relying on predicted latent trajectories or open-loop commitments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across four benchmark environments: Two-Room (navigation), Push-T (contact-rich manipulation), OGBench-Cube (3D object manipulation), and Reacher (continuous reaching).

**Performance Against CEM:**
- **Success Rate:** GC-IDM matched or exceeded CEM's success rate in seven out of eight environment–protocol settings (evaluations with 50 and 200 samples).
 - On Two-Room, Reacher, and OGBench-Cube, GC-IDM achieved significantly higher success rates (e.g., 100% vs. 82-84% on Two-Room, 99.7-100% vs. 68-70.3% on Reacher, 98.7-99.3% vs. 67-73.3% on OGBench-Cube).
 - On Push-T, CEM showed a marginal lead at n=50 (89.3% vs. 84.7%), while GC-IDM slightly outperformed CEM at n=200 (84.2% vs. 82.5%). This environment, involving longer contact sequences, represented the most challenging scenario for local inverse recovery.
- **Planning Cost and Speedup:** GC-IDM reduced the per-decision planning cost by 100–130× compared to CEM. In terms of end-to-end wall-clock time per episode, GC-IDM was 2.2–40× faster. This substantial efficiency gain is attributed to replacing iterative search with a single, lightweight forward pass.

**Comparison with Broader Planner Family:**
- GC-IDM demonstrated superior success rates compared to MPPI, iCEM, and gradient-based trajectory optimization methods, in addition to CEM, across all four environments at matched library default hyperparameters.
 - It exceeded the best sampling baselines (iCEM or CEM, depending on the environment) by 1.7 to 29.4 percentage points.
 - First-order GradientSolver performed poorly, achieving only 2.5–32.0% success rates, suggesting its inability to recover quality actions through the LeWM predictor within default iterations.
- The planning speedups observed against CEM also extended to this broader family: GC-IDM was 103–134× faster per plan call than iCEM, 29–34× faster than MPPI, and 2.0–2.4× faster than GradientSolver.

**Trajectory Quality:**
- **Action Smoothness (Jerk):** GC-IDM produced 15–36× smoother actions across all environments. This is hypothesized to be due to its closed-loop, single-step imitation nature, which inherits continuity from expert demonstrations, contrasting with CEM's open-loop, block-based replanning that can introduce discontinuities.
- **Latent Monotonicity:** GC-IDM exhibited higher latent monotonicity, meaning the agent's latent distance to the goal decreased consistently at every step in a higher fraction of episodes (e.g., 92% vs. 73% on Two-Room, 100% vs. 71% on Push-T). This directness is attributed to the continuous re-encoding of actual observations, allowing for robust closed-loop correction.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Compute Frontier:** A comprehensive sweep of 12 CEM configurations spanning a 500× compute range revealed no CEM setting that was simultaneously faster and more successful than GC-IDM, indicating that the benefits of GC-IDM are not merely due to specific CEM tuning.
- **Goal-Distance Robustness:** GC-IDM maintained strong performance across varying start–goal offsets, particularly on Two-Room, Reacher, and OGBench-Cube. Performance on Push-T showed a more noticeable decline with increasing distance, consistent with the inherent difficulty of long, contact-rich sequences for local inverse recovery.
- **Horizon Supervision:** The remaining-horizon input `h_t` was found to be critical. Removing this supervision (e.g., setting H_max=1, akin to single-step goal-conditioned behavioral cloning) resulted in an average 42 percentage point drop in success, which could not be recovered by increasing network width or depth. This indicates the horizon embedding provides essential guidance for multi-step goals.
- **Data Efficiency:** Most environments (Two-Room, Reacher, OGBench-Cube) reached near-saturated performance with relatively small fractions of the training data (e.g., 5% for Two-Room and Reacher). Push-T's performance continued to improve with more data, suggesting it may not have saturated.
- **Noise Augmentation:** Unlike some prior inverse dynamics models, GC-IDM performed optimally with no input noise injection during training (σ=0). This indicates that operating on real encoder outputs during both training and testing eliminated the distribution shift that noise augmentation typically addresses.
- **Held-Out Validation:** GC-IDM maintained statistically indistinguishable success rates and speedup ratios on unseen (held-out) episodes compared to in-distribution performance, confirming its generalization capabilities and lack of reliance on memorization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research demonstrates that, when a world model's latent space is sufficiently structured and regularized, the computational burden of planning can be largely shifted from online optimization to learned inference. This finding challenges the conventional reliance on iterative, computationally expensive online search methods for action selection in model-based control.

**Key Contributions and Implications:**
- **Enhanced Efficiency:** The introduction of GC-IDM provides a method for goal-conditioned control that offers 100–130× speedup in per-decision planning cost and significant reductions in overall episode wall-clock time, without compromising success rates. This efficiency can be critical for real-time robotic applications, enabling faster control loops and potentially more responsive agents.
- **Simplified Control Pipeline:** By replacing complex online optimization algorithms (like CEM, MPPI) with a single, lightweight neural network forward pass per step, GC-IDM simplifies the control architecture, making it easier to deploy and manage.
- **Robust Performance:** GC-IDM achieves success rates that match or exceed those of established online planners across diverse tasks, indicating that a direct inverse mapping can be a highly effective control strategy given the right latent representation.
- **Improved Trajectory Quality:** The method yields smoother and more direct trajectories, which are desirable characteristics for physical systems where jerky movements can lead to wear, instability, or energy inefficiency. This is a qualitative improvement beyond mere task success.
- **Theoretical Insight:** The results suggest that well-regularized latent spaces, such as those produced by LeWM with SIGReg, inherently encode more actionable geometric information, making local inverse recovery a tractable problem. This implies that regularization of the latent space can fundamentally simplify the control problem itself.

**Potential Future Work and Impact:**
The authors identify several directions for future research, including:
- **Cross-World-Model Generalization:** Testing GC-IDM with other JEPA-style world models and latent architectures (e.g., those producing N-patch spatial features) to assess its generality.
- **Higher-Dimensional Action Spaces:** Evaluating its performance on tasks with a greater number of degrees of freedom (e.g., 7-DoF robots) to determine if the lightweight MLP architecture scales.
- **Hybrid Planning Schemes:** For tasks involving strong irreversibility or complex, long-horizon contact structures where a purely reactive policy might struggle, exploring hybrid approaches where GC-IDM provides fast proposals and traditional planners act as occasional correctors.
- **Role of Regularization:** Further investigating the specific impact of regularizers like SIGReg by comparing performance in isotropic versus non-isotropic latent spaces.

Overall, this work presents a significant advancement in bridging the gap between efficient world modeling and efficient decision-making, highlighting the potential for structured latent representations to fundamentally transform the control problem.
