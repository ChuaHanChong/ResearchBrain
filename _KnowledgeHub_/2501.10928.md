# Generative Physical AI in Vision: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey systematically reviews and categorizes generative models in computer vision that produce physically plausible outputs, establishing a taxonomy of explicit and implicit physics-aware generation methods. It details six paradigms for integrating physical simulation, revealing a trend towards functional realism, and identifies current challenges in evaluation metrics.

## Problem

- Generative AI models in computer vision often produce visually realistic content that lacks physical plausibility, failing to adhere to real-world physical laws.
- This limitation restricts their utility in applications requiring physical accuracy, such as robotics, autonomous systems, and scientific simulations.
- There was a need for a systematic and comprehensive survey to map and categorize the emerging field of physics-aware generative AI.

## Method

- Defines 'Physics-Aware Generation' (PAG) and categorizes it into explicit (PAG-E) and implicit (PAG-I) approaches.
- Establishes a comprehensive taxonomy of six paradigms for integrating explicit physical simulation into generative models, detailing their interaction with physics engines.
- Systematically reviews the components necessary for physics-aware generation, including physical materials, simulation methods, and parameter inference.

## Results

- A clear distinction between explicit (PAG-E) and implicit (PAG-I) physics-aware generation is established.
- Six distinct paradigms are identified for explicit physics integration, detailing how generative models interact with physical simulators.
- The survey highlights an increasing reliance on automatic and LLM-based methods for inferring physical parameters, moving beyond manual input.
- It confirms that conventional visual fidelity metrics are insufficient for assessing physical awareness, necessitating the development of specialized benchmarks despite challenges in standardization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative AI is moving beyond visual realism towards functionally plausible and physically coherent content, crucial for applications like robotics and autonomous systems.
> - Explicit integration of physical simulation is the dominant approach to achieve robust physical realism in generated content.
> - Evaluating physics awareness requires specialized benchmarks and metrics beyond conventional visual fidelity, as existing methods often fail to detect physical inconsistencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) has revolutionized computer vision by enabling machines to synthesize and interpret visual data with unprecedented realism. Traditional generative models, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Diffusion Models (DMs), Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), and Gaussian Splatting (GS), have primarily focused on achieving high visual fidelity in generating images, videos, and 3D/4D content. However, a significant limitation of these models, as identified by the authors, is their frequent neglect of **physical plausibility**. This means that while generated content might look realistic, it often fails to adhere to fundamental real-world physical laws, exhibiting inconsistencies in dynamics, causality, material properties, or object interactions.

This survey addresses a critical and rapidly growing subfield within generative AI: **physics-aware generation**. As generative models become more sophisticated and their potential as "world simulators" expands, integrating physical realism becomes paramount. Such models are crucial for applications where physical accuracy is non-negotiable, including robotics (e.g., training robots in realistic simulated environments), autonomous systems (e.g., predicting physically plausible scenarios), and scientific simulations.

The authors position their survey as the "first comprehensive survey" specifically focusing on generative models in computer vision that enhance the physical awareness of their outputs. They clearly delineate its scope and differentiate it from existing surveys:
- It is **orthogonal** to surveys on physics-informed machine learning (PINNs), physics-informed computer vision, and physics-informed AI, as those primarily emphasize incorporating physical principles into *model or neural architecture design* (as prior knowledge or inductive biases) rather than ensuring the *physical realism of the generation outputs*.
- It is distinct from general surveys on generative models, diffusion models, or video diffusion models, by concentrating specifically on the **physics-aware aspect**.
- It also differs from domain-specific surveys like human video or motion generation, taking a broader view of physical interactions.
- The authors acknowledge a "recent, subsequent survey" with a similar scope but from a different perspective (cognitive evolution processes of physics in video generation models), further highlighting the timeliness and increasing importance of this research area.

In essence, this survey fills a crucial gap by systematically mapping and analyzing the emerging landscape of physics-aware generative models, providing a much-needed structured framework for a fragmented field. It underscores the shift from mere visual realism to functionally plausible, physically coherent content generation, pushing the boundaries towards truly intelligent "world models."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this survey stems from a critical gap observed in the rapid advancements of generative AI in computer vision: while models excel at visual fidelity, they often lack physical plausibility. This deficiency significantly limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where adherence to physical laws is essential.

The **key objectives** of this survey are therefore to:

1. **Provide a Comprehensive Overview:** Systematically review the current state-of-the-art in physics-aware generation within computer vision, offering a holistic understanding of the field's progress.
2. **Categorize and Structure Existing Methods:** Develop a structured analysis of current approaches based on how they incorporate physical knowledge. This involves distinguishing between explicit simulation and implicit learning, and further breaking down explicit simulation into various paradigms.
3. **Define Core Concepts:** Clearly define "physics-aware generation" and related concepts such as "physical simulation" and "physical understanding" to establish a common lexicon for the field.
4. **Analyze Methodologies and Approaches:** Detail the different ways generative models integrate physical principles, including the use of physical materials, simulation methods, and off-the-shelf physical engines.
5. **Discuss Evaluation Protocols:** Review existing benchmarks and metrics specifically designed to assess the physical awareness of generated content, highlighting their strengths and limitations compared to conventional visual fidelity metrics.
6. **Identify Future Research Directions:** Outline promising avenues for future development, addressing current challenges and potential advancements in areas like evaluation, explainability, foundational models, neural-symbolic integration, generative simulation engines, and applications in robotics and interdisciplinary domains.

The overarching **motivation** is to advance the capability of generative models beyond mere visual synthesis towards functioning as robust "world simulators." This capability is critical for enabling AI systems to make real-world decisions, facilitating advancements in fields such as:
- **Robotics:** Training and deploying robots in physically accurate synthetic environments.
- **Autonomous Systems:** Developing self-driving cars or drones that can predict and react to complex physical interactions.
- **Scientific Simulations:** Creating highly realistic simulations for research and development across various scientific disciplines.
- **Embodied Intelligence:** Building AI agents that can interact with and understand the physical world in a human-like manner.

By synthesizing existing knowledge, providing a clear framework, and pointing towards future challenges, the authors aim for this survey to serve as a foundational resource that streamlines research efforts and accelerates progress in the physically grounded generation for computer vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology and approach involve a systematic review and categorization of existing literature. The authors define key concepts and then establish a structured framework to organize the diverse array of physics-aware generative models.

#### 3.1. Definitions and Formulation

The paper begins by formally defining fundamental concepts:
- **Physics Simulation (PS):** The process of evolving an observation (X) into a future observation (X') using a physical model (Pθ), where θ represents physical parameters.
- **Physics Understanding (PU):** The inverse process of inferring the underlying physical model (Pθ) or its parameters (θ) from observed data (X).
- **Generation (G):** The process of creating new content (X') from an input condition (X) using a generative model (G). If this process lacks strong physical understanding, it's termed "physics-unaware generation" (PUG).

Crucially, the authors then define **Physics-Aware Generation (PAG)** as a generation process with a "strong understanding of real-world physics." They divide PAG into two main categories:
- **Physics-Aware Generation with Explicit Physical Simulation (PAG-E):** Generative models that explicitly utilize a physical simulation model.
- **Physics-Aware Generation without Explicit Physical Simulation (PAG-I):** Generative models that achieve a degree of physical awareness implicitly, without direct reliance on a physics engine.

#### 3.2. Paradigms for Explicit Physical Simulation (PAG-E)

For PAG-E, the survey meticulously identifies and categorizes six common paradigms based on the interaction between the generative model (G) and the physics simulation model (P):
1. **Generation to Simulation (GtS):** The generative model first produces content, which is then fed into a separate physical simulator for post-processing or to make it interactive/simulatable.
2. **Simulation in Generation (SiG):** The physical simulation model is integrated directly as a part or sub-module within the generative model architecture.
3. **Generation and Simulation (GnS):** Generation and simulation occur simultaneously or are tightly coupled, often sharing a common model or alternating operations.
4. **Simulation-Constrained Generation (ScG):** The physical simulation model provides constraints or knowledge that guides or regularizes the training of the generative model.
5. **Generation-Constrained Simulation (GcS):** The generative model provides constraints, guidance, or prior knowledge to the physical simulation process, often to optimize physical parameters.
6. **Simulation-Evaluated Generation (SeG):** The generated content is intended for deployment in simulation environments, and physical simulation is used primarily to evaluate the utility or functional correctness of the generated outputs.

#### 3.3. Components of Physics-Aware Generation

The survey systematically reviews the underlying components necessary for physics-aware generation:
- **Physical Simulation (Section 3):** Discusses core elements including various **physical material properties** (e.g., rigid body, soft body, fluids, granular media), different **simulation methods** (e.g., Material Point Method, Position-Based Dynamics, Finite Element Method, Newtonian Dynamics), and common **off-the-shelf physical engines** (e.g., Bullet Physics, NVIDIA Physics, Blender, Taichi).
- **Physical Understanding (Section 4):** Explores how physical parameters are obtained or inferred, categorizing approaches into:
 - **Manually-Set Physical Parameters:** Defined by domain experts.
 - **Automatically-Learned Physical Parameters:** Inferred from visual observations using data-driven models.
 - **LLM-Reasoned Physical Parameters:** Inferred by large language models (LLMs) leveraging contextual knowledge and common sense.
- **Generative Models (Section 5.1-5.5):** Briefly introduces the prevalent generative models employed in this field, including GANs, Diffusion Models (DMs), and neural rendering methods like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and Gaussian Splatting (GS), emphasizing their ability to handle diverse visual modalities (images, videos, 3D, 4D, interactive environments).

#### 3.4. Review of Physics-Aware Generation Methods

Sections 5.6 and 5.7 are the core of the review. Section 5.6 details PAG-E methods, categorizing reviewed papers according to the six paradigms outlined above and providing specific examples and their contributions. Section 5.7 covers PAG-I, focusing on emergent physical awareness in large video models, physical awareness from LLMs, physics-rich training data, and generative interactive dynamics/motion controls.

#### 3.5. Physics Evaluation

Section 6 is dedicated to evaluating physics awareness, an often-overlooked aspect. The authors discuss:
- **Benchmarks:** Text-conditioned (e.g., PhyBench, PhyGenBench, VideoPhy, WISA-32K) and visual-conditioned (e.g., Physics-IQ, PisaBench, PhyCoBench) benchmarks, noting the challenge of inconsistent physical commonsense definitions and the need for standardized ontologies (e.g., COSMOS-Reason1).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Three main approaches: human evaluation (most reliable but costly), VLM-based evaluation (automated via multimodal models), and automatic quantitative evaluation (computational metrics for motion, shape, consistency).

This systematic and structured approach allows the survey to provide a clear and comprehensive map of the diverse efforts in physics-aware generative AI, making it a valuable resource for researchers entering or already working in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main "findings" of this survey are the comprehensive, structured landscape of physics-aware generative AI it presents, detailing the various approaches, their underlying mechanisms, and the state of their evaluation.

#### 4.1. Two Main Categories of Physics-Aware Generation

The survey establishes a clear distinction between:
- **Physics-Aware Generation with Explicit Physical Simulation (PAG-E):** The majority of current research falls into this category, leveraging physics engines or differentiable simulators to ensure physical consistency. This highlights a prevailing strategy where explicit integration of physical laws is considered crucial for robust physical realism.
- **Physics-Aware Generation without Explicit Physical Simulation (PAG-I):** This emerging area focuses on models, particularly large-scale video models, that implicitly learn physical dynamics from vast amounts of data. While showing emergent reasoning capabilities, current models in this category still struggle with out-of-distribution generalization of physical laws, suggesting that brute-force scaling alone might not be sufficient for deep physical understanding.

#### 4.2. Six Paradigms for Explicit Physics Integration

A key contribution is the identification and detailed classification of six distinct paradigms for incorporating explicit physical simulation into generative models (Table 2 and Figure 5 summarize these methods):
- **Generation to Simulation (GtS):** This post-processing approach is common, especially with neural rendering techniques like NeRF and Gaussian Splatting, where generated scenes are endowed with physical properties for interaction (e.g., PIE-NeRF, PhysGaussian, VR-GS, SimAnything). This shows that existing generative outputs can be retrofitted for physical interaction.
- **Simulation in Generation (SiG):** Physics simulation acts as a direct sub-module, often by providing conditions or optimizing internal states (e.g., GPT4Motion using Blender for motion guidance, PhysDiff correcting human motions during diffusion sampling). This indicates a trend towards tighter coupling within the generation loop.
- **Generation and Simulation (GnS):** Characterized by tight coupling, often alternating or jointly learning geometry and physics (e.g., PAC-NeRF inferring geometry and physical parameters simultaneously). This represents a more unified approach to scene understanding and generation.
- **Simulation-Constrained Generation (ScG):** Physics simulation provides supervisory signals or loss functions to guide generative model training (e.g., PhysComp ensuring physical compatibility of 3D models, DiffuseBot filtering robot designs based on simulation performance). This highlights the use of physics as a powerful regularization or filtering mechanism.
- **Generation-Constrained Simulation (GcS):** Generative models guide the simulation process, typically by providing priors or optimizing physical parameters through score distillation (e.g., Physics3D, DreamPhysics using video diffusion models for material property optimization). This indicates a growing ability for generative models to inform and refine physical simulations.
- **Simulation-Evaluated Generation (SeG):** Generated content is specifically designed for deployment and evaluation within simulated environments, emphasizing functional utility (e.g., Physcene generating interactive 3D scenes for embodied AI). This underscores the move towards actionable and functional generated content.

#### 4.3. Progress in Physical Understanding and Data

The survey highlights the evolving methods for obtaining physical parameters, moving beyond manual input to automatic learning from visual data and increasingly, leveraging multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) for reasoning about material properties and plausible configurations (e.g., SimAnything, Phys4DGen). The emergence of physics-rich datasets like WISA-32K demonstrates a concentrated effort to provide models with explicit physical knowledge for training.

#### 4.4. Challenges in Evaluation

A significant finding is the inadequacy of conventional visual fidelity metrics (FID, CLIP Similarity) for evaluating physical awareness. These metrics are biased towards visual and semantic alignment but fail to detect violations of physical laws. The survey notes the recent development of specialized benchmarks (PhyBench, PhyGenBench, VideoPhy, Physics-IQ, PisaBench) that incorporate physical scenarios and rules. However, it also points out the challenge of inconsistent definitions of "physical commonsense" across different works, necessitating better standardization (e.g., COSMOS-Reason1's ontology). The reliance on costly human evaluation or the nascent capabilities of VLM-based and automatic quantitative metrics are also identified as areas needing further development.

#### 4.5. Transition from Visual to Physical Realism

Overall, the survey demonstrates a clear trend in generative AI from focusing purely on visual realism and semantic understanding ("what" and "where") to incorporating dynamic, causal, and interactive physical properties ("how" and "why"). This shift is evident in the increasing integration of physical simulation, the focus on temporal consistency, and the development of specialized evaluation metrics.

In summary, the survey successfully maps the current landscape of physics-aware generative AI, providing a clear taxonomy of approaches, highlighting successful implementations, and pinpointing critical areas for future research, particularly in standardized evaluation and the deeper integration of physics into large-scale models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Generative Physical AI in Vision: A Survey" holds significant implications for the future of artificial intelligence and its applications. Its primary significance lies in formalizing and structuring a nascent yet critical subfield, providing a roadmap for future research and development.

#### 5.1. Bridging the Reality Gap

The most profound impact of physics-aware generation is its potential to bridge the gap between virtual and physical realities. By enabling generative models to create content that not only looks real but also behaves realistically according to physical laws, this field paves the way for:
- **Enhanced World Models:** Moving beyond purely visual representations to models that can genuinely simulate real-world dynamics, interactions, and causal relationships. This is crucial for AI systems to operate effectively in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Robust AI for Real-World Applications:** Physically plausible generation is indispensable for downstream tasks in highly sensitive domains. For instance, in **robotics and embodied AI**, it enables the training of agents in high-fidelity synthetic environments that accurately mimic real-world physics, facilitating seamless sim-to-real transfer and reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection. In **autonomous driving**, it allows for the generation of diverse and physically accurate scenarios for testing and training, including rare or dangerous events.
- **Accelerated Scientific Discovery and Engineering:** In disciplines like materials science, fluid dynamics, or structural engineering, physics-aware generative models could automate and accelerate simulation design, generate novel designs that adhere to physical constraints, or even infer unknown physical properties from observational data.

#### 5.2. Advancing AI Capabilities

The pursuit of physics awareness pushes the boundaries of AI in several ways:
- **Improved Explainability:** Integrating explicit physical laws into generative models can lead to more interpretable AI. By mapping model decisions to well-understood physical principles, the generation process becomes transparent, allowing users to understand *why* certain phenomena occur or *how* specific forces influence generated content. This fosters greater trust and facilitates debugging.
- **Stronger Generalization:** Current models often struggle with out-of-distribution physical generalization. By explicitly or implicitly encoding physical laws, models can move beyond memorizing training examples to abstracting general physical rules, leading to better generalization to novel scenarios.
- **Neural-Symbolic Hybrid AI:** The field encourages the development of hybrid AI systems that combine the pattern recognition strengths of neural networks with the rigorous reasoning capabilities of symbolic AI. This fusion can yield models that are both robust and interpretable, capable of complex compositional reasoning.

#### 5.3. Guiding Future Research

The survey's detailed taxonomy and identification of future directions serve as invaluable guidance for the research community:
- **Standardized Evaluation:** The emphasis on developing better, standardized benchmarks and metrics for physical awareness is critical. This will enable objective comparisons between models and accelerate progress by providing clear targets for improvement, moving beyond subjective visual assessments.
- **Physics-Augmented Foundational Models:** The vision of augmenting large foundational models (like Vision-Language Models or LLMs) with deep physical understanding and utilizing them for multi-modal, physics-aware generation is highly impactful. This could lead to truly general-purpose "world models."
- **Generative Simulation Engines:** The concept of directly generating interactive physical simulations from high-level textual prompts represents a paradigm shift in how simulations are created, potentially automating complex design processes and making them accessible to a wider audience.
- **Interdisciplinary Applications:** The survey highlights the transformative potential beyond robotics, extending to climate modeling (e.g., NVIDIA's Earth-2 platform) and healthcare (e.g., realistic surgical simulations), underscoring the broad applicability of this research.

In conclusion, this survey is highly significant because it systematically defines, categorizes, and evaluates a nascent field that is crucial for the evolution of AI from mere data pattern recognition to a deeper understanding and interaction with the physical world. Its potential impact spans across scientific research, industrial applications, and the very foundation of artificial intelligence itself, pushing towards an era of truly intelligent and physically grounded AI systems.
