# Reference-Free Assessment of Physical Consistency in World Model-based Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Hanyang University ERICA introduced reference-free measures to evaluate the physical consistency of videos generated by world models, combining 3D and photometric consistency metrics for quantitative screening and spatio-temporal localization for qualitative diagnosis. Applying these measures improved VLA model task success rates by over 8% for filtered low-anomaly rollouts, reaching 74.6% compared to 66.2% for random selection.

## Problem

- The simulation-to-reality gap in robot learning often leads to policies trained in simulation failing to transfer reliably to physical robots.
- World model-generated videos exhibit physical inconsistencies (e.g., object morphing) that undermine the fidelity of simulated environments for VLA model evaluation.
- Existing video evaluation metrics are inadequate for quantifying pixel-level physical integrity or temporal coherence, and often require unavailable ground-truth references or costly human subjective evaluation.

## Method

- Developed a ==reference-free relative assessment== method for quantitative screening of generated videos, which combines 3D consistency (reprojection error) and photometric consistency (optical flow error) using Shannon entropy-derived weights.
- Created a reference-free absolute assessment for fine-grained spatio-temporal diagnosis, pinpointing specific frames and pixels where physical inconsistencies occur within a single video.
- Applied the methodology to evaluate world model-generated videos for OpenVLA tasks, comparing anomaly-filtered rollouts against random selections and real-world performance.

## Results

- Low-anomaly rollouts, identified by the relative assessment, achieved a mean VLA task success rate of **74.6%** (± **1.0%**), an improvement of over **8%** compared to randomly selected rollouts (**66.2%** ± **1.1%**).
- 3D consistency showed lower Shannon entropy (**2.16** for generated, **1.73** for real-world) than photometric consistency, leading to its higher weighting in the combined anomaly score.
- The absolute assessment successfully generated "Artifact Heatmaps" that visually pinpointed specific regions and moments of 3D consistency collapse within generated videos, aiding diagnostic analysis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D consistency, quantified by reprojection error, provides higher informational certainty for detecting physical anomalies in generated videos compared to photometric consistency.
> - Filtering world model rollouts based on their physical consistency scores significantly improves the reliability and accuracy of VLA model task success rate evaluations.
> - The spatio-temporal localization of inconsistencies offers valuable diagnostic information, enabling fine-grained understanding of world model failure modes for targeted improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning increasingly relies on simulation environments due to the inherent difficulties and resource limitations associated with real-world robotic systems. However, a significant challenge in this domain is the simulation-to-reality gap, where policies developed and tested in simulation often fail to transfer reliably to physical robots. Recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown high task success rates in established benchmarks like LIBERO, but struggle in more complex, recent environments.

World model-based approaches have emerged as a promising avenue to address the simulation-to-reality gap by generating visual rollouts that aim to align more closely with real-world outcomes. These models provide simulated environments for policy evaluation, exemplified by tools such as WorldGym and WorldEval. Despite their potential, a critical limitation of world model-generated videos is the presence of physical inconsistencies, such as object morphing or unnatural deformations. These artifacts compromise the fidelity of the simulated environment, making it challenging to accurately assess the true task success rates of VLA models and hindering the development of robust robotic policies.

Current video evaluation metrics, such as Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), are primarily designed to measure statistical distribution similarity between generated and real videos. They are not specifically equipped to quantify pixel-level physical integrity or temporal coherence, which are crucial for reliable robotic manipulation tasks. Other evaluation methods, like human voting (Elo ratings), are costly and subjective, while ground-truth references, often required by some metrics, are frequently unavailable for generated content. This research directly addresses these gaps by introducing reference-free measures tailored to assess the physical consistency of generated videos, thereby providing a more robust and objective framework for evaluating world model performance in the context of robot learning. The work builds upon the conceptual framework of WorldScore by focusing on specific consistency metrics relevant to physical plausibility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate reference-free measures for evaluating the physical consistency of videos generated by world models, particularly in the context of robot learning and VLA model evaluation. The work aims to combine both relative and absolute assessment approaches to provide a comprehensive evaluation of fidelity.

The motivation behind this research stems from several critical challenges in the current landscape of robot learning and generative modeling:
- **Addressing the Simulation-to-Reality Gap:** Robot learning policies often exhibit a performance disparity when transitioning from simulated environments to the real world. World models are intended to bridge this gap by creating more realistic simulation experiences. However, physical inconsistencies in generated videos undermine the fidelity of these simulations, preventing them from accurately reflecting real-world task success rates of VLA models. A key motivation is to enable world models to reproduce real-world task success rates more accurately by ensuring the physical plausibility of their generated outputs.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Evaluation Methods:** Current evaluation methods for generated videos face significant drawbacks. Ground-truth references are often unavailable for novel generative outputs, rendering metrics like FVD impractical. Human subjective evaluation, such as Elo rankings, is resource-intensive, slow, and prone to variability. This work is motivated by the need for objective, automated, and scalable evaluation methods that do not require costly human input or inaccessible ground truth.
- **Enabling Reliable Assessment of VLA Models:** VLA models require high temporal coherence and physical integrity in their visual inputs to execute tasks consistently. When world models generate physically inconsistent rollouts (e.g., object morphing), it becomes difficult to reliably assess the policy's true capabilities, as the policy might exploit these model artifacts rather than demonstrating robust task execution. The research aims to provide a mechanism to ensure that VLA reward models evaluate only high-fidelity rollouts, leading to more trustworthy performance metrics.
- **Diagnosing World Model Failures:** Beyond merely filtering out poor-quality rollouts, there is a motivation to understand *why* and *where* physical inconsistencies occur within a generated sequence. By providing spatio-temporal localization of these artifacts, the research aims to offer fine-grained qualitative diagnosis, which can inform developers on specific failure modes of world models and guide their improvement.
- **Improving Task Success Rates:** Ultimately, the motivation is to empirically demonstrate that by applying these proposed consistency measures, the task success rates of VLA models can be improved by filtering out physically implausible rollouts. This directly contributes to narrowing the simulation-to-reality gap, making simulated evaluations more predictive of real-world performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves developing two distinct but complementary assessment mechanisms: a relative assessment for quantitative screening and an absolute assessment for fine-grained qualitative diagnosis. Both approaches are designed to be reference-free, meaning they do not require ground-truth videos for comparison.

#### 3.1. Relative Assessment: Quantitative Screening

The relative assessment aims to quantitatively score generated videos based on their physical plausibility, enabling the filtering and ranking of rollouts. This method focuses on object morphing by combining two specific consistency metrics:
- **3D Consistency:** This metric quantifies the reprojection error across co-visible pixels between successive frames. It is calculated using DROID-SLAM, a deep visual SLAM system. A higher reprojection error indicates greater inconsistency in the 3D structure across frames.
- **Photometric Consistency:** This metric measures the Average End-Point Error (AEPE), which quantifies the accuracy of optical flow between consecutive frames. It is computed using SEA-RAFT, an optical flow estimation model. Higher AEPE values suggest greater photometric inconsistencies, often indicative of visual distortions or unnatural motion.

To combine these two metrics into a single anomaly score, the authors utilize Shannon entropy to determine objective weights. Shannon entropy is calculated for the distribution of scores from both real-world and generated videos for each consistency metric. The metric with lower entropy is considered more informative for anomaly detection and is thus assigned a higher weight using a stable softmax function:
$w_k = \frac{\exp(-H_k)}{\sum_j \exp(-H_j)}$
where $H_k$ is the Shannon entropy of metric $k$.
The individual scores for 3D consistency ($s_3$) and photometric consistency ($s_p$) are first z-score normalized ($\hat{s}_k = (s_k - \mu_k)/\sigma_k$) to account for differing scales and distributions. The final relative anomaly score ($S_r$) is then computed as a weighted sum:
$S_r = w_3 \cdot \hat{s}_3 + w_p \cdot \hat{s}_p$
Lower anomaly scores indicate higher physical consistency. This score is used to categorize generated rollouts into "high-anomaly" and "low-anomaly" groups for subsequent filtering and evaluation.

#### 3.2. Absolute Assessment: Qualitative Analysis

The absolute assessment provides a fine-grained, qualitative diagnosis of physical inconsistencies within a single video, pinpointing when and where failures occur. This approach specifically prioritizes 3D consistency due to its lower Shannon entropy and direct physical grounding.
- **Temporal Detection:** This step identifies the specific frame ($t^*$) where the most significant physical inconsistency occurs. It monitors the reprojection error ($e_t$) from DROID-SLAM's factor graph updates. To exclude initial system warmup, frames where $t \le 7$ are disregarded. The "error jump" ($j_t$) between consecutive frames is calculated as $j_t = |e_t - e_{t-1}|$. The frame $t^*$ is then identified as the one with the maximum error jump: $t^* = \arg \max_t (j_t)$.
- **Spatial Localization:** Once $t^*$ is identified, the method localizes the specific pixels or regions of inconsistency within that frame. It examines the residual flow $\delta_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 2}$ for each edge $i$ on DROID-SLAM's factor graph. The pixel-wise magnitude $M_i(u,v) = \|\delta_i(u,v)\|_2$ is calculated to visualize the intensity of distortions across the frame. To identify the most representative artifact, the mean magnitude $S_i$ for each edge is computed, and the edge $i^*$ with the maximum mean magnitude is selected. This results in an "Artifact Heatmap" that highlights regions of significant distortion.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The experimental evaluation focused on OpenVLA, leveraging its publicly available real-world out-of-distribution (OOD) BridgeData V2 videos featuring a WidowX robot.
- **Task Selection:** Five tasks were selected from OpenVLA, each representing a different category (visual generation, motion generation, physical generation, semantic generation, language grounding) to ensure a diverse evaluation.
- **Video Generation:** For each of the five tasks, 30 videos were generated using WorldGym and OpenVLA, with each video initialized by a unique random seed (totaling 150 generated videos).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Generated rollouts were evaluated using GPT-4o as a reward model, following the approach described in WorldGym. For relative assessment, rollouts were sorted by their anomaly scores and categorized into high-anomaly and low-anomaly groups (10 rollouts each), excluding the middle 10 to enhance distinction. A baseline of 10 randomly generated rollouts was also evaluated.
- **Cross-Model Generalization:** The robustness of the metrics was stress-tested by applying them to clips from other generative models (Sora, Grok, LucyEdit) and noise-corrupted videos, to observe their behavior under diverse and sometimes extreme visual conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Relative Assessment

The relative assessment demonstrated its utility in distinguishing between physically plausible and implausible generated videos:
- **Metric Informativeness:** Analysis of Shannon entropy showed that 3D consistency had lower entropy (2.16 for generated, 1.73 for real-world) compared to photometric consistency (2.88 for generated, 2.35 for real-world). This indicates that 3D consistency provides higher informational certainty for anomaly detection. The derived weights reflected this, with 3D consistency consistently receiving a higher weight (around 0.65-0.67) than photometric consistency (around 0.33-0.35) for both generated and real-world datasets.
- **Distribution of Anomaly Scores:** Generated videos exhibited a noticeable shift towards higher anomaly scores and wider variance compared to real-world videos (mode of -0.34 for generated vs. -0.91 for real-world). This finding supports the hypothesis that generated videos with lower anomaly scores are more likely to represent real-world physical capabilities.
- **Improved Task Success Rates:** Filtering generated rollouts based on the relative anomaly scores significantly improved the evaluation accuracy of VLA models.
 - Randomly selected rollouts achieved a mean task success rate of 66.2% (± 1.1%).
 - High-anomaly rollouts showed a slightly higher mean success rate of 70.2% (± 1.1%).
 - Low-anomaly rollouts achieved a mean success rate of 74.6% (± 1.0%), which is the closest to the real-world performance of 79.0% (± 5.5%).
 This outcome indicated an improvement of over 8% in task success rates when utilizing the low-anomaly filtered videos compared to random selection, effectively narrowing the simulation-to-reality gap for VLA model evaluation.
- **Limitations in Specific Tasks:** A limitation was observed in tasks where task failure did not involve physical contact with an object, such as "Lift Eggplant." In such cases, the absence of visual distortion could result in misleadingly low anomaly scores, even if the robot failed the semantic task. This highlights that physical consistency, while necessary, is not a sufficient condition for semantic task success.

#### 4.2. Absolute Assessment

The absolute assessment successfully provided detailed qualitative insights into physical inconsistencies:
- **Spatio-Temporal Localization:** The method generated "Artifact Heatmaps" that visually pinpointed specific regions and moments where 3D consistency collapsed within a generated video. Examples included visualizations of deformed or unnaturally thickened robot arms. These heatmaps highlighted areas of low fidelity, demonstrating the capability to localize generative model failures in both space (which pixels) and time (which frame). This provides diagnostic information for understanding the nature of world model artifacts.

#### 4.3. Challenges in Cross-Model Generalization

Applying the metrics to diverse generative models revealed inherent limitations:
- **Sora:** For videos from Sora, where synthetic artifacts were consistent throughout the sequence, the 3D consistency score did not exhibit significant fluctuations (Mean Score: 1.9326, Max Jump: 0.0063). This rendered single-frame anomaly analysis unreliable, as the method primarily detects *changes* in consistency rather than consistent, inherent anomalies.
- **Grok:** When evaluating Grok-generated sequences with surreal, non-rigid deformations (e.g., gelatinous objects), the frame with inconsistency was detected. However, the resulting heatmap was broadly distributed, lacking the precision to isolate a specific point of consistency collapse (Mean Score: 1.6282, Max Jump: 0.0041). This suggests challenges in localizing anomalies under non-rigid or diffuse deformations.
- **LucyEdit:** The detector sometimes paradoxically identified "improved" or edited frames from LucyEdit as anomalies, because they represented a deviation from the original sequence. The method struggled to isolate physical failure without a clean reference frame, even showing that some edited footage could appear "more consistent" (lower reprojection error) than natural video depending on the nature of the edit.
- **Noise-corrupted Videos:** When presented with severely noise-corrupted frames, the method frequently failed to compute scores or generate heatmaps, returning zero values for reprojection error. This indicates a collapse of DROID-SLAM's tracking mechanism, as it could not establish valid pixel correspondences under severe structural inconsistencies.

This cross-model analysis identified two fundamental challenges for SLAM-based assessment: (1) the collapse of tracking under severe structural inconsistencies, which prevents the establishment of correspondences, and (2) localization instability under non-rigid deformations or dynamic lighting conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a set of reference-free measures for assessing the physical consistency of videos generated by world models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on robot learning and generative AI:

- **Enabling Reference-Free Evaluation:** A primary significance is the development of evaluation metrics that do not require ground-truth references. This is critical for assessing outputs from modern generative models where ground truth is often unavailable, costly to obtain, or subjectively defined. This capability provides a scalable and automated alternative to laborious and subjective human evaluation.
- **Improving Reliability of VLA Model Evaluation:** By quantitatively filtering out physically inconsistent rollouts, the proposed relative assessment mechanism enhances the fidelity of simulated environments for VLA model evaluation. This ensures that VLA reward models assess policies based on physically plausible interactions, thereby providing a more accurate representation of a policy's real-world capabilities. The demonstrated improvement of over 8% in task success rates for low-anomaly rollouts directly contributes to narrowing the simulation-to-reality gap.
- **Diagnostic Capabilities for World Models:** The absolute assessment, with its spatio-temporal localization of artifacts through heatmaps, offers a powerful diagnostic tool for developers. It allows for a fine-grained understanding of *when* and *where* world models fail to maintain physical consistency, providing actionable insights for model refinement and targeted improvement. This moves beyond simple pass/fail evaluation to deep introspection of model behavior.
- **Scalable Alternative to Manual Inspection:** For verifying robot-centric visual rollouts, particularly in large-scale simulation environments, manual inspection is infeasible. This work offers a scalable, automated framework for preliminary screening, allowing researchers to focus on high-fidelity simulations.
- **Potential for Reward Signal Integration:** The derived relative anomaly score holds potential as a novel reward signal for refining world models themselves. By integrating this consistency measure into the training loop, world models could be incentivized to generate more physically consistent and realistic visual outputs.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The challenges identified in cross-model generalization—specifically the limitations of SLAM-based tracking under severe structural inconsistencies, non-rigid deformations, or dynamic lighting—highlight important directions for future research. This includes exploring more robust tracking mechanisms, incorporating semantic or contact-aware signals into consistency metrics, and extending the evaluation to a broader range of VLA and world models. Addressing these limitations could lead to even more generalizable and powerful consistency assessment tools.

In conclusion, this research provides practical, automated tools to enhance the trustworthiness and utility of world model-generated videos for robot learning, fostering the development of more robust and transferable robotic policies.
