# SONIC: Supersizing Motion Tracking for Natural Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed SONIC, a system that scales physics-based motion tracking using over 100 million frames of motion data and 32,000 GPU hours to create a generalist humanoid controller. The system facilitates natural, robust whole-body movements and supports diverse real-time applications including multimodal commands and integration with high-level AI models.

## Problem

- Sim-to-real humanoid control has not seen the scaling gains of foundation models observed in other AI domains.
- Existing humanoid control policies are typically small, task-specific, and require laborious, manual reward engineering for new behaviors.
- Developing a generalist humanoid controller flexible enough for diverse real-world applications (e.g., teleoperation, multimodal commands) remains challenging.

## Method

- Identified and leveraged ==motion tracking== as a scalable foundational task, utilizing dense supervision from over **100 million** frames of diverse human motion-capture data.
- Supersized the training across network size (up to **42M** parameters), dataset volume (**700 hours**), and compute (**32k GPU hours**) using a universal control policy and ==PPO==.
- Developed a unified ==encoder-quantizer-decoder== architecture, a ==generative kinematic motion planner==, and integration with multimodal models (**GENMO**) for various input interfaces and real-time deployment.

## Results

- Scaling data volume, model size, and compute consistently improved motion imitation performance, with the **100M**-frame dataset providing the most substantial gains.
- SONIC significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines in tracking out-of-distribution motions, achieving up to **99.6**% success rates and lower tracking errors in simulation.
- The controller demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer to a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, executing all **50** diverse trajectories without failure, and achieved a **95**% success rate for mobile manipulation when integrated with a GR00T N**1.5** VLA model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling motion tracking with massive data and compute directly yields emergent capabilities, improved generalization, and robustness in humanoid control, akin to breakthroughs in other foundation models.
> - Motion tracking provides a scalable, reward-free learning objective that can replace manual reward engineering for a wide array of humanoid behaviors.
> - A universal token space and cross-embodiment architecture enable a single policy to effectively handle diverse input modalities (VR, video, text, music) and facilitate integration with higher-level AI planning.
