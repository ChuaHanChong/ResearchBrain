# DetToolChain: A New Prompting Paradigm to Unleash Detection Ability of MLLM

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents DetToolChain, a novel prompting paradigm that enables powerful Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like GPT-4V and Gemini to perform zero-shot object detection with enhanced precision. The method combines a detection-specific Chain-of-Thought with a toolkit of visual processing and reasoning prompts, resulting in significant performance improvements across open vocabulary, described, referring expression, and oriented object detection benchmarks, often surpassing prior state-of-the-art zero-shot approaches.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with precise object detection, particularly for challenging instances like occluded, small, or rotational objects, despite their advanced visual intelligence.
- Finetuning MLLMs for detection is computationally expensive, requires vast manual data preparation, and is impractical for state-of-the-art closed-source MLLMs like GPT-4V.
- Prior prompting methods for MLLMs often rely on pre-extracted bounding boxes from external detectors, rather than truly unleashing the MLLM's inherent detection ability.

## Method

- ==DetToolChain== introduces a novel prompting paradigm combining a =='detection prompting toolkit'== with a ==multimodal detection Chain-of-Thought (Det-CoT)== to guide MLLMs in object detection.
- Det-CoT orchestrates an iterative, step-by-step process where the MLLM generates a 'thought' to conclude or engage a tool, then constructs and appends an appropriate prompt to the message history.
- The toolkit comprises ==Visual Processing Prompts== (e.g., Ruler Marker, Image Split, Box Marker) that preprocess images with visual aids, and ==Detection Reasoning Prompts== (e.g., Problem Insight Guider, Self-Verification Promoter) that guide MLLM internal reasoning for refinement.

## Results

- DetToolChain improved baseline GPT-4V and Gemini performance by **2**0%** to 50%** across various detection metrics, significantly outperforming prior zero-shot methods.
- On Open Vocabulary Object Detection (MS COCO), zero-shot GPT-4V+DetToolChain surpassed CORA by **+**22.7%** AP50** on Novel classes and achieved better AP50 (**+6.4%**) than supervised Faster R-CNN-R50 on COCO val2017.
- For Described Object Detection (D-cube dataset), GPT-4V+DetToolChain improved upon the best prior method FIBER-B by **+**14.5%** AP** on FULL settings, demonstrating enhanced ability to comprehend complex descriptions for localization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like GPT-4V and Gemini possess an inherent, yet untapped, zero-shot object detection capability that can be effectively unlocked through strategic prompting.
> - Integrating visual processing prompts (e.g., ruler markers, image splits) directly into the image input, combined with reasoning prompts, provides MLLMs with the necessary 'prior knowledge' and 'reasoning capabilities' to perform precise localization.
> - An iterative Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm tailored for detection (Det-CoT) enables MLLMs to decompose complex detection tasks, dynamically apply specialized tools, and progressively refine bounding box predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-3, Gemini, InternLM, and Qwen have revolutionized natural language understanding and generation. The integration of visual encoders with these LLMs has led to the emergence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), capable of tasks like image captioning and visual question answering, demonstrating capabilities akin to human visual intelligence. However, despite these strides, the paper highlights a significant gap: the potential of MLLMs in *precise object detection tasks* remains largely underestimated and underexplored.

Existing MLLMs often struggle with accurately predicting precise coordinates for objects, especially in challenging scenarios like highly occluded objects, small objects, or rotational objects. This limitation severely constrains their real-world applicability in crucial domains such as defect detection in manufacturing or sports analysis.

Prior efforts to enhance MLLM detection capabilities broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Finetuning MLLMs:** Approaches like BuboGPT, Kosmos-2, Shikra, Qwen-VL, SPHINX, and Ferret have attempted to improve detection by finetuning MLLMs on extensive datasets of high-quality image-text instruction pairs containing detailed location information. While yielding considerable improvements, this method is computationally expensive, requires vast manual effort for data preparation, and, critically, is infeasible for closed-source, state-of-the-art MLLMs like GPT-4V and Gemini, which are often the most powerful.
2. **Prompting MLLMs:** Some intuition-based prompting methods (e.g., RedCircle, FGVP, SCAFFOLD, SOM, CPT) have advanced regional comprehension and spatial understanding. However, their effectiveness in *precise object detection* remains largely underexplored. Furthermore, many of these methods for visual grounding or referring expression comprehension often rely on *pre-extracted bounding boxes or segmentation masks* from high-quality, dedicated detectors or segmentors (like SAM or other SOTA object detectors), meaning they do not truly unleash the MLLM's *inherent* detection ability but rather leverage external tools.

This paper positions **DetToolChain** as a novel prompting paradigm that directly addresses these limitations. It seeks to unlock the *zero-shot object detection ability* of powerful, often closed-source, MLLMs by designing a new "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) specifically tailored for detection, combined with a "detection prompting toolkit." This approach is motivated by the belief that current MLLMs possess an inherent, yet untapped, zero-shot detection potential that can be unleashed through strategic prompting, bypassing the need for computationally intensive finetuning or reliance on external pre-trained detectors. It explicitly differentiates itself by not using any pre-trained models for detection or segmentation, focusing purely on instructing the MLLM.

The work also builds upon the general concept of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which has significantly boosted the reasoning capabilities of LLMs by enabling step-by-step problem decomposition. While multimodal CoT variants (e.g., Multimodal CoT, DDCoT, HoT, Spatial CoT) exist for general multimodal reasoning, the specific application of CoT for *object detection* within MLLMs, especially incorporating visual detection priors, has been a largely unexplored area. DetToolChain fills this gap by introducing a detection-tailored CoT that progressively refines detection results through an iterative, tool-using process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **unleash the zero-shot object detection ability of multimodal large language models (MLLMs)**, specifically powerful closed-source models such as GPT-4V and Gemini.

The motivations behind this objective are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing MLLM Limitations in Detection:** Despite their advanced visual intelligence, current MLLMs exhibit poor performance when asked to provide precise coordinates for objects, especially challenging instances like highly occluded, rotational, or small objects. This precision deficit significantly limits their practical utility in real-world computer vision applications.
- **Overcoming Finetuning Obstacles:** The conventional approach of finetuning MLLMs for detection is resource-intensive (manual data curation, high computational cost) and impractical for state-of-the-art, closed-source MLLMs, which are currently the most capable. A prompting-based solution offers a viable alternative.
- **Leveraging Inherent MLLM Potential:** The authors hypothesize that MLLMs, with their vast knowledge and reasoning capabilities, inherently possess the capacity for precise localization. The challenge lies in effectively communicating detection tasks and guiding their inherent visual and spatial reasoning.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition and SOTA Detectors:** The proposed DetToolChain draws inspiration from how humans solve complex visual tasks—by breaking them down into simpler subtasks, employing measurement tools (like rulers or compasses), and iteratively refining their understanding. It also mimics the progressive refinement of bounding boxes seen in advanced object detection algorithms like DETR and SparseRCNN.
- **Developing a Generalizable Zero-Shot Detector:** The goal is to create a prompting paradigm that can transform MLLMs into effective zero-shot detectors across a spectrum of detection tasks without requiring task-specific instruction tuning, thereby offering greater adaptability and lower deployment costs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DetToolChain introduces a novel prompting paradigm that orchestrates a "detection prompting toolkit" with a "multimodal detection Chain-of-Thought (Det-CoT)" to guide MLLMs in object detection. The core idea is to enable MLLMs to decompose complex detection tasks, apply specialized prompts, and progressively refine their predictions.

#### 3.1. Detection Chain-of-Thought (Det-CoT)

Det-CoT serves as the orchestrator, managing the entire detection process in an iterative, step-by-step manner (Algorithm 1 in the paper).
1. **Formatting the Query:** The raw input query (image and natural language instruction) is first transformed into a suitable template for the MLLM.
2. **Iterative Prompting Loop:**
 - The MLLM processes the current message history and generates a "thought" – a decision to either conclude the task or engage a specific tool from the toolkit.
 - If a tool is chosen, the MLLM constructs the appropriate prompt for that tool, which might involve processing the image (e.g., adding visual markers) or generating reasoning cues.
 - This new prompt, along with the processed image/information, is appended to the message history, and the process reiterates.
3. **Returning Final Response:** The loop continues until the MLLM indicates the final detection results, which are then extracted using a string extractor.

This iterative process allows for task decomposition, dynamic tool selection, and progressive refinement of bounding box predictions, akin to human problem-solving and modern detection algorithms.

#### 3.2. Detection Prompting Toolkits

The toolkit consists of two main types of prompts, strategically designed to provide MLLMs with the necessary "prior knowledge" and "reasoning capabilities" for detection:

**a) Visual Processing Prompts:** These prompts actively preprocess the input image to facilitate better perception and localization by the MLLM.
- **Regional Amplifier:**
 - **Image Split:** Divides the image into distinct parts, helping the MLLM focus on smaller regions.
 - **Zoom In:** Provides close-up views of specific regions, crucial for detecting small or fine-grained objects.
- **Spatial Measurement Standard:** These overlay visual aids directly on the image, enabling MLLMs to "read" coordinates rather than solely inferring them.
 - **Ruler Marker:** Overlays linear graduations, providing a translational reference for precise X-Y coordinate reading.
 - **Compass Marker:** Overlays rotational references, specifically beneficial for oriented object detection to infer angles.
- **Scene Image Parser:** These prompts mark predicted information directly on the image to aid contextual understanding and refinement.
 - **Box Marker, Centroid Marker, Convex Hull Marker:** Visualize predicted object locations and shapes in different formats (rectangle, center point, boundary points), helping the MLLM confirm or refine its spatial understanding, especially for irregularly shaped or occluded objects.
 - **Scene Graph Marker:** Connects the centers of detected objects to highlight their spatial relationships, encouraging the MLLM to leverage contextual reasoning (e.g., object co-occurrence or relative positioning).

**b) Detection Reasoning Prompts:** These prompts guide the MLLM's internal reasoning process, enabling it to diagnose predictions, infer from context, and self-correct.
- **Problem Insight Guider:** Helps the MLLM identify potential difficulties (e.g., small objects, occlusion) and suggests relevant visual processing prompts or strategies.
- **Spatial Relationship Explorer:** Prompts the MLLM to analyze and leverage spatial relationships between objects to refine bounding boxes, ensuring common sense consistency (e.g., a person on a surfboard).
- **Contextual Object Predictor:** Encourages the MLLM to infer the presence of likely co-occurring objects based on common sense, helping to mitigate object hallucination or missing detections.
- **Self-Verification Promoter:** Prompts the MLLM to critically evaluate its own predictions for accuracy and consistency, and ultimately decide when the detection task is complete.
- The paper also alludes to integrating other reasoning techniques like "debating" and "self-debugging" to further enhance reasoning reliability.

By combining these specialized prompts within a flexible, iterative Chain-of-Thought framework, DetToolChain enables MLLMs to tackle complex detection challenges in a zero-shot manner, leveraging their inherent capabilities without specific finetuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DetToolChain demonstrates remarkable effectiveness in unlocking the zero-shot object detection abilities of MLLMs, significantly outperforming baselines and often surpassing state-of-the-art methods across various detection tasks without any instruction tuning.

- **Overall Performance Uplift:** The framework consistently improves the performance of baseline GPT-4V and Gemini models by 20% to 50% across different detection metrics. This highlights its broad applicability and power.

- **Open Vocabulary Object Detection (MS COCO Novel/Base/All classes):**
 - GPT-4V with DetToolChain achieved exceptional results, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art method, CORA, by **+22.7% AP50 on Novel classes, +4.1% AP50 on Base classes, and +9.0% AP50 on All classes**. This demonstrates its superior ability to recognize and precisely locate objects, even those unseen during any traditional training phase.
 - **Comparison to Supervised Detectors (COCO val2017):** In a striking finding, zero-shot GPT-4V+DetToolChain (AP: 34.5, AP50: 64.8, AP75: 31.5) not only significantly surpassed its own baseline (AP: 0.3, AP50: 1.4, AP75: 0.0) but also achieved better **AP50 (+6.4%)** than supervised Faster R-CNN-R50 and even outperforming DETR-R50 and VisionLLM-R50. The higher AP50 (ease of detecting most objects) but comparatively lower AP75 (less precise bounding boxes at higher IoU thresholds) suggests that while it can generally locate objects well, refining very tight boundaries remains challenging without specific training data annotations.

- **Described Object Detection (D-cube dataset):**
 - DetToolChain enabled GPT-4V and Gemini to significantly outperform existing methods. GPT-4V+DetToolChain improved upon the best prior method, FIBER-B, by **+14.5% AP on FULL settings, +16.0% AP on PRES settings, and +10.2% AP on ABS settings**. This highlights MLLMs' enhanced ability to comprehend complex, often negative or absence-related, descriptions and accurately localize the described objects.

- **Referring Expression Comprehension (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg):**
 - GPT-4V+DetToolChain showed dramatic improvements over the GPT-4V baseline, for instance, by **+44.53% Accuracy@0.5 on RefCOCO val set**. It also performed competitively, and often better, than other zero-shot methods and significantly narrowed the gap with instruction-tuned MLLMs. The authors attribute this to the effectiveness of visual processing prompts like `Ruler Marker` in helping MLLMs understand spatial relationships conveyed by directional language. Notably, unlike other zero-shot methods that use external object detectors for proposals, DetToolChain allows MLLMs to *inherently predict and refine* bounding boxes.

- **Oriented Object Detection (HRSC2016 dataset):**
 - The framework substantially improved baseline results, with GPT-4V+DetToolChain achieving **+0.50 mIoUp and +0.51 mIoUr** over its baseline. The ablation study specifically identified the `Compass Marker` as the most impactful tool for this task, leading to +0.34 mIoUp and +0.25 mIoUr performance gains over the GPT-4 baseline, by making it easier for MLLMs to interpret object orientation.

- **Ablation Studies and Further Analysis:**
 - The studies demonstrated the individual efficacy of various prompts. `Ruler Marker` and `Compass Marker` proved crucial for general and oriented object detection, respectively, by providing clear spatial references.
 - `Image Split` and `Zoom In` were highly effective for small object detection, allowing MLLMs to focus on fine-grained details. `Contextual Object Predictor` also helped reduce missing small objects.
 - For multi-instance and multi-object scenarios, `Scene Graph Marker` and `Spatial Relationship Explorer` improved performance by leveraging spatial reasoning among objects.
 - `Convex Hull Marker` and `Self-Verification Promoter` aided in detecting occluded objects by helping to delineate irregular shapes and refine predictions.
 - Crucially, DetToolChain's strategic combination of these tools yielded superior performance compared to using individual tools or other generic Chain-of-Thought approaches (Zero-shot CoT, Few-shot CoT, Multimodal CoT), validating its tailored design for detection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DetToolChain framework represents a significant leap forward in empowering Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for object detection, offering several profound implications for research and application:

- **Unlocking Zero-Shot Object Detection:** The most significant contribution is the definitive demonstration that state-of-the-art MLLMs like GPT-4V and Gemini possess an inherent, zero-shot object detection capability that can be effectively unleashed through intelligent prompting. This fundamentally shifts the paradigm from expensive, data-intensive finetuning to dynamic, prompt-based instruction for complex vision tasks.
- **Reduced Reliance on Finetuning and Specialized Data:** By achieving impressive detection performance without instruction tuning or reliance on pre-trained external detectors/segmentors, DetToolChain offers a powerful alternative to current labor-intensive and computationally expensive methods. This democratizes access to advanced detection capabilities, especially for closed-source MLLMs, and accelerates rapid prototyping and deployment.
- **Enhanced MLLM Interpretability and Refinement:** The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm inherent in DetToolChain promotes a step-by-step reasoning process. This not only improves precision through progressive refinement but also enhances the interpretability of MLLM decisions, as the intermediate steps of problem decomposition, tool selection, and self-diagnosis become transparent.
- **Broader Real-World Applications:** The ability of MLLMs to perform precise object detection in a zero-shot manner opens up a vast array of practical applications. This includes, but is not limited to, industrial defect detection, real-time sports analysis, autonomous systems (e.g., self-driving cars, robotics), medical image analysis, and surveillance, where new objects or scenarios frequently appear, bypassing the need for continuous retraining.
- **New Research Avenues in Multimodal AI:** This work pioneers a powerful direction in multimodal AI, emphasizing the synergy between sophisticated prompting strategies and the inherent capabilities of large foundation models. It encourages further research into designing specialized "toolkits" and "reasoning chains" for MLLMs to tackle a broader spectrum of complex, grounded visual tasks beyond general understanding.
- **Advancing Human-AI Interaction:** The approach of instructing MLLMs with visually augmented prompts (like rulers and compasses) mirrors how humans use tools to augment their perception and reasoning. This could lead to more intuitive and effective ways for humans to interact with and guide AI systems for complex visual tasks.

Despite its groundbreaking nature, the paper also acknowledges limitations that highlight future research directions. These include operational efficiency challenges due to sequential processing, potential inconsistencies in MLLM output formatting, the scalability requirement for large MLLMs, and the associated computational costs. Addressing these challenges will further solidify the utility and widespread adoption of this promising prompting paradigm. Ultimately, DetToolChain represents a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to become truly versatile and precise detectors in a dynamic, open-world setting.
