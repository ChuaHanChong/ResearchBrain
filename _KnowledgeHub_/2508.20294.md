# Dynamics-Aligned Latent Imagination in Contextual World Models for Zero-Shot Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> The Dynamics-Aligned Latent Imagination (DALI) framework extends model-based reinforcement learning to achieve zero-shot generalization in latent contextual environments by self-supervised inference of dynamics-aligned context representations. It demonstrated performance increases of up to 96.4% over context-unaware baselines in complex extrapolation tasks, encoding physically consistent environmental factors.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents struggle with zero-shot generalization across diverse, unseen environmental conditions, particularly when critical context parameters are latent and unobservable.
- Existing contextual RL methods often rely on explicit context conditioning, which is impractical or impossible in real-world scenarios where ground-truth context is unavailable.
- Standard model-based RL architectures, such as DreamerV3, suffer from information bottlenecks in their recurrent states, limiting their ability to explicitly disentangle and adapt to diverse latent contexts efficiently.

## Method

- **DALI** extends ==DreamerV3== by introducing a self-supervised context encoder that infers dynamics-aligned latent context representations from short interaction histories.
- The context encoder is optimized using a forward dynamics loss to predict future observations and a cross-modal regularization loss to align with DreamerV3's stochastic state.
- Inferred latent contexts are integrated into the DreamerV3 world model and policy, either shallowly (DALI-S) or deeply (DALI-D), to explicitly condition dynamics and behavior for zero-shot generalization.

## Results

- DALI-S-χ achieved up to +**96.4%** performance gain over the context-unaware Dreamer-DR baseline in the Ball-in-Cup extrapolation task (Pixel modality, IQM **0.2730**).
- In Ball-in-Cup extrapolation, DALI-S-χ outperformed ground-truth context-aware baselines (cRSSM-S/D) by **33.8%** to **63.9%** in the Featurized modality (IQM **0.3720**).
- Counterfactual analysis demonstrated that perturbations to learned latent context dimensions resulted in physically plausible changes in imagined trajectories, such as altered gravity-string dynamics in Ball-in-Cup and modified actuator strength for locomotion in Walker Walk.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The recurrent state in standard world models like DreamerV3 acts as an information bottleneck, making efficient and rapid context inference challenging and requiring long interaction histories.
> - DALI's dedicated context encoder enables efficient context identifiability from short temporal windows, theoretically demonstrating a sample complexity gain of O(T/K) compared to standard approaches.
> - The learned latent context representations in DALI are interpretable and capture physically consistent mechanistic factors of the environment, enabling physically plausible counterfactual predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability of reinforcement learning (RL) agents to generalize across diverse, unseen environmental conditions without requiring costly retraining is a significant challenge in the field, particularly for real-world applications. Contextual Markov Decision Processes (cMDPs) provide a formal framework for modeling tasks with varying dynamics, often governed by latent parameters such as friction, gravity, or object mass.

Existing approaches to contextual RL frequently rely on explicit context conditioning, where the agent or model is provided with ground-truth context variables (e.g., specific friction coefficients). While effective in controlled environments, these methods typically scale poorly and become impractical in unstructured real-world settings where such explicit context information is latent, difficult to measure, or unavailable. This necessitates the development of RL agents capable of inferring and adapting to hidden contexts in a self-supervised manner.

Model-based RL (MBRL) algorithms, such as the Dreamer family (DreamerV3 in particular), have demonstrated notable sample efficiency by learning predictive models of environment dynamics and performing planning entirely within these learned latent spaces. Previous work has explored Dreamer for zero-shot generalization through explicit context conditioning. However, these methods still depend on direct context supervision.

This research distinguishes itself by focusing on self-supervised inference of *latent* contexts. Unlike most meta-RL approaches that typically require fine-tuning on new tasks for adaptation, this work aims for zero-shot generalization by learning structured latent representations that are transferable across environments without additional adaptation. By extending the DreamerV3 architecture, the paper addresses its limitation in generalizing across diverse latent contexts, which often stems from its inability to retain critical environmental information in its standard recurrent state representations. The proposed Dynamics-Aligned Latent Imagination (DALI) framework introduces a novel mechanism for inferring these latent contexts through dynamics alignment, thereby bridging perception and control without explicit context supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust zero-shot generalization in reinforcement learning agents operating within partially observable contextual Markov Decision Processes (cMDPs) where environmental contexts are latent. The motivation stems from several key challenges and limitations of existing methods:

1. **Implicit Context Handling in Real-World Scenarios**: Real-world robotic and agent control tasks often involve dynamic environmental conditions (e.g., varying surface friction, gravity, or object properties) that are not directly observable or easily quantifiable. Current contextual RL methods often assume direct access to these context variables, which is impractical or impossible in many real-world applications. The core motivation is to allow agents to infer these variations implicitly from their interactions with the environment, using sensory feedback and observed dynamics.

2. **Limitations of Context-Unaware World Models**: While model-based RL agents like DreamerV3 are proficient in learning and planning within fixed environments, their standard architectures are not designed to explicitly disentangle and represent diverse latent contexts. This limitation hinders their ability to generalize effectively to environmental conditions that significantly differ from those experienced during training, leading to performance degradation in zero-shot generalization tasks. DreamerV3's recurrent state, in its default configuration, compresses all episode information, including context, states, and noise, into a fixed-size representation, creating an information bottleneck that impedes rapid adaptation to novel contexts.

3. **Need for Self-Supervised Context Inference**: To overcome the reliance on explicit context variables, a critical motivation is to develop a framework that can learn actionable context representations in a self-supervised manner. This involves distilling contextual factors from observation-action histories into compact embeddings that can then condition the agent's world model and policy.

To address these motivations, DALI aims to:

- **Infer Latent Contexts**: Develop a self-supervised context encoder that can extract stable and meaningful representations of latent environmental parameters from short interaction histories.
- **Enhance World Model Adaptability**: Integrate these inferred latent context representations into the DreamerV3 world model and policy architecture, enabling it to explicitly reason about and adapt to varying dynamics.
- **Achieve Zero-Shot Generalization**: Facilitate robust adaptation to unseen contextual variations (extrapolation) without requiring additional training or fine-tuning.
- **Provide Theoretical Justification**: Establish theoretical foundations demonstrating the necessity and efficiency of a dedicated context encoder for robust generalization, specifically addressing information bottlenecks and sample complexity in partially observable cMDPs.
- **Validate Physical Consistency**: Demonstrate that the learned latent context space is interpretable and exhibits counterfactual consistency, meaning that perturbations to context-encoding dimensions result in physically plausible changes in imagined future trajectories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Dynamics-Aligned Latent Imagination (DALI), extends the DreamerV3 model-based reinforcement learning architecture to achieve zero-shot generalization by inferring latent contexts from agent-environment interactions.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Contextual Markov Decision Processes (cMDPs) and DreamerV3

The work formalizes the problem using a cMDP framework, where an environment is defined by a tuple $M = (S, A, O, C, R, P, E, \mu, p_C)$. Here, $S$, $A$, and $O$ represent state, action, and observation spaces, respectively, and $C \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ is a continuous context space from which latent contexts $c \in C$ are drawn per episode. The agent receives observations $o_t$ but does not directly observe the state $s_t$ or context $c$. The transition function $P$ varies with $c$, while the reward function $R$ remains fixed. Zero-shot generalization is defined as learning a policy using a training context distribution $p_{train}(c)$ that maximizes expected return for contexts drawn from an unseen evaluation distribution $p_{eval}(c)$.

DALI builds upon DreamerV3, an MBRL algorithm that learns a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) to simulate environment dynamics. The RSSM encodes observations $o_t$ into a compact latent state $s_t = \{h_t, z_t\}$, where $h_t$ is a deterministic recurrent state and $z_t$ is a stochastic state. During training, $z_t$ is inferred conditioned on $o_t$ (posterior inference), and during imagination, future states are predicted without observations (prior prediction). An actor-critic architecture then learns policies within this latent space.

#### 3.2. Context Encoder for Dynamics-Aligned Representations

DALI introduces a self-supervised context encoder, $g_\varphi$, which maps a history of observations and actions $(o_{t-K:t}, a_{t-K:t-1})$ to a context representation $z_t$. $K$ denotes the temporal window length.

- **Forward Dynamics Alignment**: To ensure $z_t$ captures environmental dynamics, $g_\varphi$ is optimized with a forward dynamics loss ($L_{FD}$):
 $L_{FD}(\varphi) = E\left[ \left\|o_{t+1} - f_{w_\varphi}(o_t, a_t, z_t)\right\|_2^2 \right]$
 where $f_{w_\varphi}$ predicts the next observation $\hat{o}_{t+1}$ given the current observation $o_t$, action $a_t$, and context $z_t$. Minimizing this loss compels $z_t$ to encode contextual factors essential for accurate dynamics prediction.

- **Cross-Modal Regularization**: A cross-modal loss ($L_{cross}$) is introduced to align $z_t$ with DreamerV3's posterior stochastic state $z_t \sim q_\theta(z_t|h_t, o_t)$. This loss enforces bidirectional alignment:
 $L_{cross}(\varphi) = E\left[ \|z_t - W_z z_t\|_2^2 \right] + E\left[ \|z_t - W_{z'} z_t\|_2^2 \right]$
 where $W_z$ and $W_{z'}$ are linear maps. This regularization aims to enhance the robustness of $z_t$ by leveraging the observation-informed representation of $z_t$, while avoiding redundant trajectory-specific information from $h_t$.

- **Total Loss**: The context encoder is trained using a combination of these objectives: $L_{total}(\varphi) = L_{FD}(\varphi) + \lambda_{cross} L_{cross}(\varphi)$.

#### 3.3. Integrating Context into DreamerV3

Two strategies are proposed for incorporating the inferred context $z_t$ into the DreamerV3 architecture:

- **Shallow Integration (DALI-S)**: The inferred context $z_t$ is appended only to the observation embedding within the world model's encoder: $z_t \sim q_\theta(z_t|h_t, o_t, z_t)$. Other world model and policy components remain unchanged. This approach allows context information to propagate indirectly through the recurrent state.

- **Deep Integration (DALI-D)**: $z_t$ is integrated more pervasively into the world model and policy. The deterministic recurrent state $h_t$ and all predictors (reward, continuation, decoder) are directly conditioned on $z_t$. The actor and critic networks also explicitly condition on $z_t$ for policy optimization.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Insights

The paper provides theoretical analysis grounded in cMDPs with continuous, Lipschitz-continuous, and $\beta$-mixing dynamics, noisy observations, and an exploratory policy.

- **Information Bottleneck in DreamerV3**: The standard DreamerV3 RSSM's recurrent state ($h_{RSSM,t}$) compresses all episode information, leading to an information bottleneck. This makes context inference slow and prone to losing crucial contextual cues, requiring long interaction histories ($T \gg K$) to disambiguate contexts.

- **DALI's Efficiency**: DALI's context encoder explicitly decouples context inference. Due to the $\beta$-mixing property of the environment, a short temporal window ($K$ transitions) is sufficient to capture near-optimal context information ($I(c; z_t) \ge (1-\delta)h(c)$). This results in a sample complexity gain of $O(T/K)$ compared to DreamerV3, as DALI effectively leverages local histories.

- **Reduced Information Bottleneck**: DALI's sequence model, particularly with deep integration, retains more context information ($I(c; h_{DALI,t}) \ge I(c; h_{RSSM,t}) - \epsilon(K)$, where $\epsilon(K) = O(e^{-\lambda K/2})$) than DreamerV3's RSSM, implying a reduction in the information bottleneck.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DALI focused on zero-shot generalization across contextual variations in the DMC Ball-in-Cup and Walker Walk tasks from the CARL benchmark. Evaluations were conducted using Featurized (low partial observability) and Pixel (high partial observability) observation modalities under Interpolation, Extrapolation, and Mixed generalization regimes. Performance was assessed using Interquartile Mean (IQM) and Probability of Improvement (PoI).

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Generalization Performance

- **Ball-in-Cup (challenging extrapolation)**:
 - **Interpolation**: All methods achieved high IQM scores (0.92–0.95). DALI-S-$\chi$ was competitive.
 - **Extrapolation**: DALI-S-$\chi$ demonstrated significant performance gains.
 - **Featurized**: IQM of 0.3720, representing a +87.9% gain over the context-unaware Dreamer-DR baseline, +63.9% over cRSSM-S, and +33.8% over cRSSM-D.
 - **Pixel**: IQM of 0.2730, indicating a +96.4% gain over Dreamer-DR, +45.9% over cRSSM-S, and +12.8% over cRSSM-D.
 - The ground-truth context-aware baselines (cRSSM-S/D) performed worse in extrapolation, suggesting potential overfitting to training contexts. The low absolute IQM scores reflect the extreme OOD context ranges tested.
 - **Mixed Regime**: DALI-S-$\chi$ led in the Featurized modality (IQM 0.6830), while cRSSM-D excelled in the Pixel modality (IQM 0.6250), benefiting from ground-truth context for visual dynamics.

- **Walker Walk (less extreme extrapolation)**:
 - **Interpolation**: High scores for all methods (0.94–0.97). DALI-S performed best in the Featurized modality (IQM 0.9710).
 - **Extrapolation**: DALI-S showed performance improvements.
 - **Featurized**: IQM of 0.7810, achieving +4.0% over Dreamer-DR, +11.3% over cRSSM-S, and +4.3% over cRSSM-D.
 - **Pixel**: cRSSM-S achieved the highest IQM (0.7770), indicating the benefit of ground-truth context for robust visual feature extraction. DALI-S achieved 0.7580.
 - **Mixed Regime**: Context-aware baselines (cRSSM-D Featurized, cRSSM-S Pixel) generally led, while DALI methods showed moderate gains over Dreamer-DR.

#### 4.2. Generalization Trends Across Environments and Modalities

- **Cross-Modal Regularization Effects**: In Ball-in-Cup, DALI-S-$\chi$ (with $L_{cross}$) consistently outperformed DALI-S (FD loss alone), suggesting the regularization improved context inference for nonlinear pendulum dynamics, especially with noisy pixel inputs. In contrast, DALI-S generally outperformed DALI-S-$\chi$ in Walker Walk, indicating that simple forward dynamics prediction was sufficient for contexts where actuator strength linearly scaled joint torques. This highlights the task-specific utility of regularization.
- **Shallow Integration Efficacy**: Shallow Integration, as implemented in DALI-S and DALI-S-$\chi$, performed strongly across experiments. Its design, wherein the inferred context $z_t$ primarily influences the world model's encoder, was observed to implicitly regularize the world model, which potentially mitigated overfitting to noisy $z_t$ estimates, particularly beneficial in OOD settings.

#### 4.3. Validating Physically Consistent Counterfactuals

To ascertain if the learned latent space encodes mechanistic factors, perturbations were applied to individual dimensions of the inferred context $z_t$, and the resulting counterfactual imagined trajectories were analyzed.

- **Ball-in-Cup (Gravity-String Dynamics)**: The top-ranked latent dimension, $z_6$, in DALI-S-$\chi$ was identified as encoding coupled gravity-string dynamics.
 - **Pixel Modality**: Perturbing $z_6$ in zero-action rollouts (isolating passive dynamics) resulted in imagined ball trajectories exhibiting both shorter string length (ball hovering higher) and higher acceleration (faster swing cycles), consistent with increased gravitational effects.
 - **Featurized Modality**: Quantitative analysis of ball Z-position and Z-velocity confirmed these observations: reduced oscillation amplitude and earlier/higher velocity peaks for the perturbed trajectory, verifying physically plausible alterations.

- **Walker Walk (Actuator-Driven Recovery)** (Supplementary Section D.2): Perturbations to the top-ranked latent dimension $z_3$ in DALI-S were found to influence the Walker's recovery and locomotion.
 - **Pixel Modality**: An agent whose $z_3$ was perturbed (representing enhanced actuator strength) was able to recover from a fall and initiate forward locomotion in imagined rollouts, whereas the unperturbed agent failed to recover.
 - **Featurized Modality**: Torso height dynamics confirmed that the perturbed trajectory achieved sustained elevation, aligning with successful recovery and locomotion due to increased actuator strength.

These counterfactual experiments suggest that DALI's context encoder learns interpretable representations aligned with underlying physical parameters, which contributes to its generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DALI framework addresses a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning: zero-shot generalization to unseen environmental conditions with latent contexts. Its contributions and potential impacts are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Generalization for Real-World RL**: DALI's ability to achieve significant performance gains, particularly in extrapolation tasks, often surpassing baselines that rely on ground-truth context, represents a step toward more robust and adaptable RL agents for real-world applications where explicit context information is typically unavailable. This is particularly relevant for robotics and autonomous systems that must operate in dynamic and uninstrumented environments.

- **Self-Supervised Latent Context Inference**: The core innovation of inferring latent context representations directly from interaction histories in a self-supervised manner reduces the need for expensive context engineering or supervision. This makes DALI a more scalable and practical approach for complex, real-world problems.

- **Mechanistic Understanding and Interpretability**: The demonstration of physically consistent counterfactuals is significant for the interpretability of learned latent representations. It shows that DALI's context encoder captures meaningful, mechanistic factors of the environment (e.g., gravity, string length, actuator strength), which can lead to a deeper understanding of how RL agents adapt and make decisions. This capability offers a pathway toward building more transparent and trustworthy AI systems.

- **Theoretical Foundations for Efficiency**: The theoretical results concerning context identifiability, sample complexity, and the reduction of information bottlenecks provide a rigorous understanding of why DALI is effective. By demonstrating that short interaction windows are sufficient for efficient context inference, the work contributes to the theoretical underpinning of efficient generalization in cMDPs.

- **Architectural Simplicity and Robustness**: The empirical success of the Shallow Integration strategy highlights that even lightweight modifications to world model architectures can yield substantial benefits. Its implied regularization effect, which helps mitigate overfitting to noisy context estimates, offers practical guidance for designing robust contextual RL systems.

- **Future Research Directions**: The work opens avenues for future research, including:
 - Further theoretical exploration into how context representations explicitly influence policy learning and robustness.
 - Investigation of different integration strategies and their regularization properties.
 - Extension of DALI to more complex environments with sparse rewards or very high-dimensional observation spaces, where assumptions like $\beta$-mixing might be more challenging to satisfy.
 - Addressing practical aspects such as training stability and hyperparameter sensitivity to facilitate broader deployment.

In summary, DALI represents a methodological advancement in contextual reinforcement learning, providing a robust and efficient framework for zero-shot generalization by inferring and leveraging dynamics-aligned latent context representations.
