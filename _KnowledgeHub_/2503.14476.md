# DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ByteDance Seed, Tsinghua University, and The University of Hong Kong developed DAPO, an open-source reinforcement learning system for large language models. The system achieved 50 points on the AIME 2024 dataset with a Qwen2.5-32B model, surpassing the prior 47-point score of DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B while requiring 50% fewer training steps.

## Problem

- There is a lack of transparency and reproducibility in state-of-the-art LLM reinforcement learning (RL) systems, as key technical details are often concealed by leading research groups.
- Large-scale LLM RL training for complex, long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tasks faces significant practical challenges, including entropy collapse, reward noise, and training instability.
- Eliciting and enhancing sophisticated reasoning capabilities in LLMs requires specialized RL approaches beyond standard alignment techniques.

## Method

- DAPO, an open-source LLM reinforcement learning system, builds on ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a rule-based reward model and without a KL divergence penalty.
- It introduces four core techniques: ==Clip-Higher== for mitigating entropy collapse, ==Dynamic Sampling== for improving sample efficiency by filtering zero-gradient samples, ==Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss== for balanced gradient contribution, and Soft Overlong Punishment for refined length-aware reward shaping.
- A carefully curated dataset, ==DAPO-Math-17K==, was created with integer-transformed answers to enable precise, verifiable rule-based reward signals for mathematical reasoning.

## Results

- DAPO trained a Qwen2.5-32B base model to achieve **50 points** on the AIME 2024 dataset, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B which scored **47 points**.
- The system demonstrated enhanced training efficiency, achieving its performance with **50%** fewer training steps compared to the prior method.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Dynamic Sampling was the most impactful component, contributing 8 points to the final score, while Clip-Higher, Soft Overlong Punishment, and Token-level Loss also provided cumulative improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of specific algorithmic refinements (Clip-Higher, Dynamic Sampling, Token-level loss, Overlong Reward Shaping) is critical for stable and efficient large-scale LLM RL training, especially for long Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
> - Maintaining a healthy level of policy entropy and ensuring consistent, effective gradients by filtering out zero-gradient samples are crucial for improving performance and accelerating convergence.
> - Reinforcement learning can foster the emergence of new, complex reasoning behaviors in LLMs, such as self-reflection and error correction, which were not explicitly present in the base model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself directly within the cutting-edge of Large Language Model (LLM) research, specifically addressing the challenge of eliciting and improving complex reasoning abilities. The current landscape is dominated by increasingly capable LLMs that exhibit remarkable reasoning for tasks like competitive mathematics (e.g., AIME) and coding.

**Key Trends and Challenges:**
- **Emergence of Reasoning:** LLMs, when scaled and fine-tuned, have shown unprecedented reasoning capabilities, often through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting.
- **Role of Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL has emerged as a core technique for refining these reasoning abilities, enabling behaviors such as self-verification and iterative refinement. Prominent examples cited include OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek's R1, which demonstrate superior performance in complex reasoning tasks.
- **Reproducibility Crisis in LLM RL:** A significant bottleneck in the current research landscape is the *lack of transparency* and *difficulty in reproducing* the results of state-of-the-art LLM RL systems. The paper explicitly states that "key technical details... are concealed" in existing reports, leading to community struggles in replicating RL training outcomes. This echoes a broader reproducibility crisis often seen in areas involving massive computational resources and proprietary techniques. The paper cites several community efforts attempting to address this gap, underscoring its urgency.
- **RLHF vs. Reasoning RL:** While Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has been widely used for aligning LLMs with human preferences (e.g., making them safer, more helpful), this paper focuses on using RL to *activate and enhance inherent reasoning capabilities*. The motivation is not just alignment but capability elicitation, which necessitates a different approach to reward modeling (rule-based for verifiability) and training stability.

**DAPO's Contribution to the Landscape:**
DAPO directly tackles the reproducibility and transparency issues. By fully open-sourcing its algorithm, training code, and a carefully curated dataset, it aims to:
- **Democratize Access:** Provide the broader research community with a working, scalable, and high-performing RL system for LLMs, moving beyond theoretical discussions to practical implementation.
- **Set a New Standard for Transparency:** Offer a blueprint for how complex, large-scale RL training for LLMs can be conducted and shared, fostering more collaborative and verifiable research.
- **Advance State-of-the-Art:** Not only aims for reproducibility but also pushes the performance frontier, demonstrating superior results on AIME 2024 compared to existing models like DeepSeek-R1, often with greater efficiency. This positions DAPO as a leading open-source solution in the domain of LLM reasoning.

In essence, DAPO emerges as a timely and critical intervention in a field marked by rapid advancements but hindered by opacity. It seeks to bridge the gap between proprietary breakthroughs and democratized scientific progress, empowering more researchers to build upon and further develop advanced LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of DAPO is driven by clear objectives and strong motivations rooted in the current state of LLM reinforcement learning:

**Primary Objectives:**
1. **Develop a State-of-the-Art Large-Scale LLM RL System:** The core objective is to create an RL system capable of eliciting and enhancing complex reasoning behaviors in LLMs to an industry-leading level. This is explicitly demonstrated by targeting and surpassing existing state-of-the-art performance on competitive reasoning tasks like AIME 2024 using a Qwen2.5-32B base model.
2. **Achieve Superior Performance and Efficiency:** Specifically, the goal is to outperform DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (which achieved 47 points on AIME) and to do so with greater training efficiency (e.g., 50% fewer training steps).
3. **Fully Open-Source the System:** This encompasses the algorithm (DAPO), the training code (built on the `verl` framework), and a carefully curated dataset (DAPO-Math-17K). This open-source commitment is central to the project's mission.

**Core Motivations:**
1. **Address Lack of Transparency and Reproducibility:** The most significant motivation highlighted in the paper is the prevailing secrecy surrounding the technical details of state-of-the-art LLM RL systems. Reports from OpenAI (o1) and DeepSeek (R1) are cited as withholding critical "key recipe" details, making it extremely difficult for the broader academic and research community to reproduce their impressive results. This leads to wasted effort, hinders scientific progress, and creates a knowledge asymmetry.
2. **Overcome Practical Challenges in Large-Scale RL Training:** The authors note that their initial attempts with naive GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) yielded significantly lower performance (30 points on AIME vs. DeepSeek's 47 points). A thorough analysis revealed specific, recurring issues such as:
 - **Entropy Collapse:** The policy quickly becoming deterministic, limiting exploration.
 - **Reward Noise:** Inaccurate or confusing reward signals, particularly for truncated or perfectly correct/incorrect samples.
 - **Training Instability:** Fluctuations and degradation in performance during the training process.
 These practical hurdles underscore the need for refined algorithms and system design.
3. **Democratize Advanced LLM Capabilities:** By providing a fully transparent and accessible system, the authors aim to "democratize solutions with industry-level RL results," enabling more researchers to engage with and build upon large-scale LLM RL, fostering innovation across the community.
4. **Reveal Valuable Insights:** Through the open-sourcing effort and detailed analysis of their techniques, the authors seek to "reveal valuable insights to large-scale LLM RL" that can benefit future research and development. This includes insights into training dynamics and the emergence of reasoning behaviors.

In summary, DAPO is motivated by a desire to push the boundaries of LLM reasoning capabilities while simultaneously championing openness and reproducibility in a field that desperately needs it. It addresses both performance gaps and transparency deficits.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization) builds upon existing reinforcement learning principles, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), while introducing four critical, interlinked techniques to address the challenges of large-scale LLM RL for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tasks.

**Foundational Elements:**
- **Base Algorithm:** The system uses GRPO as its foundational RL algorithm. GRPO, a variant of PPO, removes the need for a separate value function by estimating advantages in a group-relative manner, normalizing rewards within a batch of generated responses.
- **Rule-Based Reward Modeling:** Unlike many RLHF approaches that rely on learned reward models, DAPO employs a simple, verifiable rule-based reward. For mathematical tasks, this means a reward of +1 for a correct answer and -1 for an incorrect answer. This approach is chosen to avoid the "reward hacking" problem often associated with complex, learned reward models and to provide clear, objective signals for activating reasoning.
- **Removal of KL Divergence:** Standard PPO/GRPO often includes a KL divergence penalty to keep the updated policy close to the old or reference policy. DAPO explicitly removes this term. The rationale is that for long-CoT reasoning tasks, the model's distribution *needs* to diverge significantly from its initial state to acquire new, complex reasoning patterns, making a strong KL constraint counterproductive.

**Four Key Techniques of DAPO:**

1. **Clip-Higher (Decoupled Clipping):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Entropy collapse, where the LLM's policy quickly becomes deterministic, limiting exploration and diversity in generated responses. This happens because the upper clipping bound in standard PPO/GRPO (which tries to prevent too-large updates) makes it hard for low-probability "exploration" tokens to significantly increase their probabilities, even if they lead to high rewards.
 - **Approach:** DAPO decouples the clipping range for importance sampling ratios into a lower bound ($\epsilon_{low}$) and a higher bound ($\epsilon_{high}$). While $\epsilon_{low}$ is kept standard (e.g., 0.2), $\epsilon_{high}$ is increased (e.g., 0.28). This "raises the ceiling" for probability increases, allowing the model to more aggressively explore and elevate the probabilities of potentially beneficial, low-probability tokens, thereby maintaining higher policy entropy and promoting diversity.

2. **Dynamic Sampling:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Gradient-decreasing problem and sample inefficiency. When all responses for a given prompt in a batch are either perfectly correct (reward = 1) or perfectly incorrect (reward = -1), the group-relative advantage (Equation 4) becomes zero. This results in zero policy gradients for those prompts, effectively reducing the number of useful samples in a batch and leading to larger gradient variance and dampening of signals.
 - **Approach:** The system dynamically over-samples prompts and filters out those where all generated outputs are either 100% correct or 0% correct. Sampling continues until the batch is fully filled with prompts that yield non-zero advantages (i.e., where at least one response is correct and one is incorrect, or there's a mix of different reward values). This ensures every prompt in a batch contributes effective gradients, improving sample efficiency and training stability, and leading to faster convergence.

3. **Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Imbalance in gradient contribution for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) responses. The original GRPO calculates loss at the sample level (averaging token losses within a sample, then across samples), meaning each *sample* has equal weight regardless of its length. This can disproportionately reduce the influence of tokens within very long, high-quality responses and fail to adequately penalize undesirable patterns (e.g., gibberish, repetition) in excessively long, low-quality responses.
 - **Approach:** DAPO switches to a token-level loss calculation. Instead of averaging within samples first, it aggregates losses directly across all tokens in the batch. This means longer sequences (with more tokens) naturally have a greater influence on the overall gradient update. This ensures that every token's contribution to the loss is directly proportional to its impact on the policy, improving the learning of reasoning-relevant patterns and better suppressing undesirable long-form generation.

4. **Overlong Reward Shaping:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Reward noise introduced by truncation. In RL training, responses exceeding a maximum length are typically truncated and often assigned a punitive reward. This can be problematic if a long, valid reasoning path is penalized simply due to length, confusing the model.
 - **Approach:** DAPO first explored "Overlong Filtering" (masking the loss for truncated samples), which stabilized training. It then proposes **Soft Overlong Punishment**. Instead of a hard penalty, a "punishment interval" is introduced: if a response exceeds a `max_length - cache_length` threshold but is still within `max_length`, it receives a soft, length-proportional penalty. This guides the model to avoid excessively long responses without harshly punishing potentially sound but long reasoning processes, reducing reward noise.

**Dataset Transformation:**
- To ensure accurate, rule-based reward signals and minimize parsing errors, the **DAPO-Math-17K** dataset was created. It comprises 17,000 mathematical prompts from web and competition sources.
- A key step involves **transforming answers into integers**. For example, an answer like "$a + \sqrt{b} / c$" would be transformed into "$a+b+c$" by modifying the problem statement itself, making verification straightforward. This transformation uses a Chain of Thought framework and few-shot examples to guide an LLM to perform the reformulation.

**Training Details:**
- The system is implemented using the open-source `verl` framework.
- It utilizes the AdamW optimizer with a constant learning rate, linear warm-up, and specific batch sizes for rollouts and training (512 prompts, 16 responses per prompt).
- Evaluation on AIME uses `avg@32` (averaging over 32 repetitions) for result stability.

This comprehensive methodology demonstrates a deep understanding of the practical challenges in scaling LLM RL, providing concrete, empirically validated solutions to improve training stability, efficiency, and ultimately, performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the AIME 2024 dataset using the Qwen2.5-32B base model yielded significant findings, demonstrating the effectiveness of the DAPO algorithm and its constituent techniques.

**Overall Performance:**
- **State-of-the-Art AIME Score:** DAPO successfully trained the Qwen2.5-32B base model to achieve **50 points** on AIME 2024.
- **Superior to Previous SoTA:** This score directly outperforms the previous state-of-the-art for a comparable model size, DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, which achieved 47 points.
- **Enhanced Efficiency:** Crucially, DAPO achieved this higher performance using only **50% of the training steps** required by DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, highlighting its improved sample efficiency and convergence speed. Figure 1 visually confirms this faster convergence.

**Ablation Study (Contribution of Individual Techniques):**
Table 1 provides a clear breakdown of how each proposed technique contributes to the overall performance improvement, starting from a naive baseline:
- **Naive GRPO:** Achieved a baseline of **30%** accuracy on AIME 2024, indicating the initial challenges without the DAPO innovations.
- **+ Overlong Filtering:** Improved performance to **36%** (+6 points), demonstrating the initial positive impact of addressing reward noise from truncated samples.
- **+ Clip-Higher:** Further increased accuracy to **38%** (+2 points), validating its role in promoting exploration and preventing entropy collapse.
- **+ Soft Overlong Punishment:** Added another **3 points** for a total of **41%**, showing that a refined length-aware penalty is more effective than simple filtering.
- **+ Token-level Loss:** Contributed **1 point** for a total of **42%**. Although a smaller numerical gain, the authors note its critical role in enhancing training stability and leading to a "healthier" increase in response length, preventing issues like gibberish.
- **+ Dynamic Sampling (Full DAPO):** The final and most impactful addition, boosting performance by a substantial **8 points** to reach **50%**. This highlights its effectiveness in ensuring consistent, effective gradients by filtering out zero-gradient samples, also leading to faster convergence (Figure 6).

**Training Dynamics Insights (Figure 7):**
The paper provides valuable insights into key intermediate metrics during RL training, which serve as essential monitoring indicators:
- **Mean Response Length (Figure 7a):** The length of generated responses is closely tied to performance. An increase in length generally allows for more complex reasoning and exploration. However, the authors observe that length does not always continuously increase and can stagnate or decline, serving as an indicator of potential training deterioration.
- **Reward Score (Figure 7b):** The reward on the training set generally increases stably, indicating the model's ability to fit the training data distribution when given reliable reward signals. However, the authors find that the final training reward often correlates poorly with validation accuracy, suggesting potential overfitting to the training set.
- **Generation Entropy (Figure 7c) and Mean Probability (Figure 7d):** These metrics are crucial for monitoring the model's exploration capability.
 - The `Clip-Higher` strategy is shown to successfully address initial entropy collapse, maintaining a healthy level of exploration.
 - Maintaining a slow upward trend in entropy is found to be conducive to model performance, indicating a balanced exploration-exploitation trade-off. Conversely, excessively low entropy means limited exploration, while excessively high entropy can lead to repetitive or nonsensical generations.

**Qualitative Findings (Case Study):**
A particularly interesting qualitative finding is the **emergence of reflective behavior** during RL training (Table 2, Table 3). The authors observed that:
- In early training stages, the model rarely exhibited self-checking or reflection.
- As training progressed, the model started to show distinct behaviors of reflection and backtracking within its reasoning process, identifying and correcting its own errors.
This indicates that RL algorithms not only reinforce existing successful reasoning patterns but can also **give rise to entirely new modes of reasoning** that were not explicitly present in the base model or initially trained examples. This offers profound insights into the learning mechanisms of LLMs and their ability to acquire sophisticated cognitive processes.

In summary, DAPO delivers a new state-of-the-art performance in mathematical reasoning for its model class, demonstrating the combined power of its novel techniques. Furthermore, the detailed ablation studies and analysis of training dynamics provide critical empirical evidence and practical guidelines for future large-scale LLM RL research. The observation of emergent reasoning capabilities is a significant step towards understanding how LLMs truly learn.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DAPO project carries substantial significance and potential impact across several dimensions of LLM research and development:

1. **Democratization of Advanced LLM RL:** This is arguably the most significant contribution. By fully open-sourcing the algorithm, training code, and a high-quality dataset, DAPO directly addresses the transparency and reproducibility crisis plaguing state-of-the-art LLM RL. It lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, enabling academic institutions and smaller research groups to experiment with and build upon industry-level RL techniques without access to proprietary information or immense resources for reverse-engineering. This can accelerate research, foster innovation, and diversify the pool of contributors in a field often dominated by large tech companies.

2. **Establishment of a New State-of-the-Art and Benchmark:** DAPO's achievement of 50 points on AIME 2024 with Qwen2.5-32B, surpassing DeepSeek-R1 while requiring fewer training steps, establishes it as a leading solution for reasoning-focused LLM reinforcement learning. The open-sourced nature means this new performance benchmark is verifiable and reproducible, serving as a robust foundation for future comparisons and improvements.

3. **Practical Solutions to Core RL Challenges:** The four key techniques (Clip-Higher, Dynamic Sampling, Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss, Overlong Reward Shaping) offer highly effective and empirically validated solutions to pervasive problems in large-scale RL training, such as entropy collapse, gradient starvation, length bias, and reward noise. These insights and techniques are not merely theoretical; they are practical recipes for making RL effective and efficient in real-world LLM applications. Their detailed exposition will guide future algorithm design for diverse long-CoT tasks.

4. **Advancing the Understanding of LLM Capabilities and Emergence:** The qualitative observation of emergent reflective behaviors (self-checking and backtracking) during RL training is profoundly significant. It suggests that RL isn't just optimizing pre-existing behaviors but can actively induce and shape new, complex cognitive strategies in LLMs. This opens new avenues for research into:
 - **Interpretability:** How do these reasoning patterns emerge?
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Can specific RL setups be designed to foster such emergent properties more efficiently?
 - **General Intelligence:** What are the fundamental mechanisms through which LLMs acquire higher-order reasoning?
 This finding moves beyond simply improving performance to deepening our understanding of artificial intelligence itself.

5. **Blueprint for Industry-Academia Collaboration:** The successful collaboration between ByteDance and Tsinghua University, leveraging ByteDance's `verl` framework, provides a strong example of how industry resources and engineering expertise can be effectively combined with academic rigor to produce impactful, open-source research. This model can inspire similar collaborations to tackle other complex AI challenges.

6. **Broader Applicability:** While focused on competitive mathematics, the principles and techniques developed in DAPO are highly likely to be transferable to other domains requiring complex, multi-step reasoning, such as code generation and debugging, scientific discovery, and complex problem-solving. This means DAPO's contributions could pave the way for more generally capable and robust LLMs across a wide array of applications.

In conclusion, DAPO is a landmark contribution that simultaneously pushes the state-of-the-art in LLM reasoning, provides crucial transparency and reproducibility to a historically opaque field, and offers foundational insights into the mechanisms of emergent intelligence in large models. Its open-source nature ensures its impact will resonate widely within the global AI research community.
