# DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ByteDance Seed, Tsinghua University, and The University of Hong Kong developed DAPO, an open-source reinforcement learning system for large language models. The system achieved 50 points on the AIME 2024 dataset with a Qwen2.5-32B model, surpassing the prior 47-point score of DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B while requiring 50% fewer training steps.

## Problem

- There is a lack of transparency and reproducibility in state-of-the-art LLM reinforcement learning (RL) systems, as key technical details are often concealed by leading research groups.
- Large-scale LLM RL training for complex, long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tasks faces significant practical challenges, including entropy collapse, reward noise, and training instability.
- Eliciting and enhancing sophisticated reasoning capabilities in LLMs requires specialized RL approaches beyond standard alignment techniques.

## Method

- DAPO, an open-source LLM reinforcement learning system, builds on ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a rule-based reward model and without a KL divergence penalty.
- It introduces four core techniques: ==Clip-Higher== for mitigating entropy collapse, ==Dynamic Sampling== for improving sample efficiency by filtering zero-gradient samples, ==Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss== for balanced gradient contribution, and Soft Overlong Punishment for refined length-aware reward shaping.
- A carefully curated dataset, ==DAPO-Math-17K==, was created with integer-transformed answers to enable precise, verifiable rule-based reward signals for mathematical reasoning.

## Results

- DAPO trained a Qwen2.5-32B base model to achieve **50 points** on the AIME 2024 dataset, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B which scored **47 points**.
- The system demonstrated enhanced training efficiency, achieving its performance with **50%** fewer training steps compared to the prior method.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Dynamic Sampling was the most impactful component, contributing 8 points to the final score, while Clip-Higher, Soft Overlong Punishment, and Token-level Loss also provided cumulative improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of specific algorithmic refinements (Clip-Higher, Dynamic Sampling, Token-level loss, Overlong Reward Shaping) is critical for stable and efficient large-scale LLM RL training, especially for long Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
> - Maintaining a healthy level of policy entropy and ensuring consistent, effective gradients by filtering out zero-gradient samples are crucial for improving performance and accelerating convergence.
> - Reinforcement learning can foster the emergence of new, complex reasoning behaviors in LLMs, such as self-reflection and error correction, which were not explicitly present in the base model.
