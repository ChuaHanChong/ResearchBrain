# Driving in the Occupancy World: Vision-Centric 4D Occupancy Forecasting and Planning via World Models for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Drive-OccWorld adapts a vision-centric 4D occupancy forecasting world model to end-to-end autonomous driving planning. It combines a BEV history encoder, a memory queue with semantic- and motion-conditional normalization, and an action-conditioned auto-regressive world decoder, coupled to an occupancy-based planner. It sets new state-of-the-art occupancy/flow forecasting and safer planning on nuScenes, Lyft-Level5, and nuScenes-Occupancy.

## Problem

- Existing driving world models mostly target video/data generation or representation pretraining, neglecting how forecasting could directly improve end-to-end planning safety and robustness.
- Prior 3D occupancy-forecasting world models rarely support flexible, controllable action conditioning, limiting their use for downstream action-controllable generation tasks.
- Prior planning-integrated world models such as Drive-WM rely on image-based reward functions that do not exploit the environment's geometric 3D occupancy structure.

## Method

- ==Semantic- and motion-conditional normalization== module in the memory queue modulates historical BEV embeddings via adaptive affine transforms conditioned on semantic labels and ego/agent motion flow.
- **Drive-OccWorld**'s ==auto-regressive World Decoder== injects diverse action conditions (velocity, steering angle, trajectory, commands) through a unified ==Fourier-embedding== conditional ==cross-attention== interface for controllable future occupancy and flow forecasting.
- Occupancy-based planner combines agent-safety, road-safety, and learned-volume BEV cost terms with a ==BEV-refinement cross-attention== step to select and refine trajectories for continuous forecasting-and-planning rollout.

## Results

- Outperforms Cam4DOcc by **9.5%** mIoU_f and **5.1%** VPQ_f on nuScenes, and by **6.1%** mIoU_f and **5.2%** VPQ_f on Lyft-Level5 for inflated GMO/flow forecasting.
- Achieves a **4.3%** fine-grained occupancy forecasting improvement on nuScenes-Occupancy, with mIoU gains of **5.9%** and **5.1%** over Cam4DOcc for current and future GMO.
- Planning beats UniAD by relative **33%**, **22%**, and **9.7%** on L2 at 1s/2s/3s respectively, and ablations confirm each cost factor and normalization component contributes measurable gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting low-level action conditions (trajectory, velocity) improves future forecasting more than high-level commands, suggesting fine-grained motion cues carry more predictive world-state signal.
> - Using predicted rather than ground-truth trajectories as action conditions during training/testing can improve forecasting quality by constraining BEV features to account for ego motion, despite worse raw planning accuracy.
> - Coupling occupancy forecasting with an occupancy-grounded cost function (rather than image-level rewards) is a generalizable pattern for turning generative world models into safer closed-loop planners.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within autonomous driving (AD): **end-to-end learning, world models, and 4D occupancy forecasting**.

Historically, AD systems have relied on **modular pipelines**, breaking down the complex task into distinct stages such as perception, prediction, and planning. While robust, these systems can suffer from error propagation between modules and may lack global optimization. Recent advancements have seen a shift towards **end-to-end models**, which directly map raw sensor data to driving actions or trajectories in a unified pipeline, promising better generalization and holistic optimization. However, a key challenge for these end-to-end methods is their **deficiency in generalization and safety robustness**, often attributed to insufficient "world knowledge" for forecasting dynamic environments.

Enter **world models**, a concept gaining significant traction in AI, particularly for embodied agents. A world model allows an agent to learn a compressed, predictive representation of its environment, envisioning potential future states under various actions. In autonomous driving, initial applications of world models primarily focused on **data generation** (e.g., generating high-fidelity driving videos to augment training data) or **pretraining paradigms** (e.g., pre-training visual encoders by forecasting future sensor data like point clouds). Examples include GAIA-1, Drive-Dreamer, and ViDAR, which primarily aimed at enhancing downstream perception tasks or generating realistic driving scenarios.

This paper identifies a critical gap: **most existing world models for AD do not directly integrate their powerful future forecasting capabilities with end-to-end planning to enhance safety and robustness**. While some pioneering works, like Drive-WM, have explored integrating generated driving videos with image-based reward functions for planning, they often do not fully exploit the rich **3D geometric features** of the environment, which are crucial for safe navigation.

"Driving in the Occupancy World" addresses this gap by proposing a novel framework, Drive-OccWorld, that adapts a vision-centric **4D occupancy forecasting** world model to **end-to-end planning**. 4D occupancy forecasting represents the environment as a grid of occupied voxels (3D) that evolves over time (4D), often including semantic and motion information. This offers a more comprehensive understanding of the environment, including both static structures and dynamic agents, at a fine-grained level, compared to traditional object detection or segmentation. By combining this rich 4D future prediction with an occupancy-based planner, this work aims to imbue end-to-end AD systems with explicit world knowledge and foresight, thereby enhancing their safety, robustness, and interpretability in complex, dynamic driving scenarios. It pushes the boundaries of how world models can directly inform and improve the core decision-making process of autonomous vehicles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Drive-OccWorld is to **integrate a vision-centric 4D occupancy forecasting world model with an end-to-end planning framework for autonomous driving**, thereby enhancing the safety, robustness, and generalization capabilities of self-driving systems.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges in the current autonomous driving landscape:

- **Limitations of Current End-to-End AD Models:** While end-to-end autonomous driving models offer the promise of simplified pipelines and holistic optimization, they frequently struggle with **generalization ability and safety robustness**. This is largely attributed to their **insufficient world knowledge** for accurately forecasting complex, dynamic environments, leading to potential unsafe or unpredictable behaviors in novel situations.
- **Underutilized Potential of World Models:** Existing world models for autonomous driving have predominantly focused on either **generating synthetic data** for training or **pretraining perception modules**. While valuable, this focus neglects the direct application of a world model's core strength: its ability to **envision and simulate potential future states** based on various ego actions. The authors argue that leveraging this predictive capacity directly in the planning loop is a crucial, yet underexplored, avenue.
- **Need for Comprehensive Environmental Understanding:** Safe autonomous driving necessitates a deep understanding of the surrounding environment, including not only the location of objects but also their **semantic properties, fine-grained geometry, and future motions**. Current perception outputs might be insufficient for robust planning, especially in dynamic scenarios. 4D occupancy forecasting provides a rich, spatiotemporal representation of the environment, offering a more complete "world state" for decision-making.
- **Desire for Action-Controllable Generation:** For an autonomous agent to effectively plan, it must be able to anticipate how the world will evolve under *its own different potential actions*. This requires a world model that can generate diverse future scenarios conditioned on various ego actions (e.g., accelerating, braking, turning left/right). Such a capability not only facilitates planning but also enables the development of powerful neural simulators for testing and data generation.
- **Enhancing Explainability and Safety:** By explicitly forecasting future environmental states (occupancy and flow), the world model provides a more interpretable basis for the planner's decisions. The planner can then evaluate potential trajectories against these forecasted states using well-defined cost functions, leading to more transparent and inherently safer motion planning.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond world models as mere data generators or pretrainers, proposing to harness their full potential as **predictive engines integrated directly into the core decision-making loop of autonomous vehicles**. This integration aims to create an intelligent agent that can "think ahead," anticipating consequences of its actions and navigating safely in complex, dynamic real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Drive-OccWorld is presented as a vision-centric 4D forecasting and planning world model for autonomous driving, built upon an auto-regressive framework. It comprises three main components: a History Encoder, a Memory Queue with novel normalization, and a World Decoder integrated with an Occupancy-based Planner.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture (Auto-Regressive Framework)

The system operates auto-regressively, predicting the next state and optimal trajectory iteratively. It takes historical sensor observations and ego trajectories as input to generate future states and actions.
- **World Model (W):** Generates plausible future states (semantic occupancy and flow) given historical observations and ego actions.
- **Occupancy-based Planner (P):** Selects the optimal ego trajectory by evaluating future predictions from W using a cost function. The selected trajectory then serves as the action condition for the world model's next rollout.

#### 3.2. 4D Forecasting with World Model Components

1. **History Encoder (W_E):**
 - **Function:** Processes historical multi-view camera images to extract multi-view geometry features and transforms them into Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) embeddings.
 - **Implementation:** Leverages a BEVFormer-based encoder, a state-of-the-art architecture for lifting 2D image features to a 3D BEV representation, known for its effectiveness in autonomous driving perception tasks.

2. **Memory Queue (W_M) with Semantic- and Motion-Conditional Normalization:**
 - **Function:** Accumulates historical BEV features, enhancing them with semantic and dynamic information.
 - **Semantic-Conditional Normalization:** Addresses the "ray-shaped patterns" in raw BEV embeddings (where points along a 3D ray correspond to a single image pixel), which can hinder semantic discrimination.
 - It first applies layer normalization to BEV embeddings.
 - Then, it modulates these embeddings using an adaptive affine transformation (scale and shift parameters, γ_s, β_s) derived from *voxel-wise semantic predictions*. A lightweight head predicts semantic labels, which are encoded as one-hot embeddings and convolved to generate the affine parameters. This process enhances the semantic discriminability of BEV features.
 - **Motion-Conditional Normalization:** Accounts for the movements of both the ego vehicle and other agents.
 - **Ego-motion:** Ego-pose transformation matrices (rotation and translation) are flattened and encoded via MLPs to generate affine parameters (γ_e, β_e), compensating for ego-vehicle movement.
 - **Agent-motion:** Predicts voxel-wise 3D backward centripetal flow (F, pointing from current voxel to previous instance center) and encodes it into affine parameters (γ_f, β_f) for fine-grained motion-aware normalization, crucial for dynamic objects.
 - **Mechanism:** Both normalizations apply affine transformations to `LayerNorm(F_bev)` using learned `γ` and `β` parameters, making the BEV features more representative of semantic and dynamic changes over time.

3. **World Decoder (W_D):**
 - **Function:** An auto-regressive transformer that predicts future BEV embeddings (and subsequently, future semantic occupancies and flows) based on historical features from W_M and anticipated action conditions.
 - **Inputs:** Learnable BEV queries, historical BEV embeddings (via temporal cross-attention), and action condition embeddings (via conditional cross-attention).
 - **Architecture:** Comprises deformable self-attention (for contextual relationships), temporal cross-attention (to extract features from multi-frame historical embeddings, with ego-motion aware alignment), conditional cross-attention (to inject action information), and a feedforward network.
 - **Outputs:** Future BEV embeddings, which are then passed to prediction heads (using channel-to-height operation) to forecast semantic occupancy (S) and 3D backward centripetal flow (F).

#### 3.3. Action-Controllable Generation

- **Diverse Action Conditions:** The model supports various forms of ego actions:
 - **Velocity:** (v_x, v_y) in m/s.
 - **Steering Angle:** Converted to curvature in m⁻¹.
 - **Trajectory:** (Δx, Δy) in meters, representing ego movement to the next timestamp.
 - **Commands:** High-level intentions like "go forward," "turn left," "turn right."
- **Unified Conditioning Interface:** All heterogeneous action conditions are first encoded via **Fourier embeddings** (to represent conditions at high frequencies) and then concatenated. These combined embeddings are fused through learned projections to align dimensions, serving as input to the conditional cross-attention layers within the World Decoder. This method enables flexible and efficient integration of diverse actions for controllable generation.

#### 3.4. End-to-End Planning with World Model

- **Occupancy-based Planner (P):** Designed to select optimal trajectories using the fine-grained future occupancy and flow predictions from the World Model.
- **Occupancy-based Cost Function:** A comprehensive function with multiple factors to ensure safe planning:
 - **Agent-Safety Cost:** Penalizes trajectories that overlap with (or are too close to) grids occupied by other agents (pedestrians, vehicles).
 - **Road-Safety Cost:** Penalizes trajectories that deviate from the drivable road area, extracted from occupancy predictions.
 - **Learned-Volume Cost:** A learnable 2D cost map generated from the future BEV embeddings (F_bev^+t) to provide a more comprehensive, context-aware evaluation of occupancy grids.
- **Trajectory Selection:** A trajectory sampler generates candidate trajectories. The planner then selects the optimal trajectory by minimizing the total cost function (summation of the above factors).
- **BEV Refinement:** The selected trajectory is further refined. It's encoded and concatenated with a command embedding to form an "ego query." This query performs cross-attention with the future BEV embeddings (F_bev^+t) to extract fine-grained environmental representations, which are then used by MLPs to predict the final refined trajectory.
- **Planning Loss (L_plan):** Includes a max-margin loss (to encourage the expert trajectory to have lower cost than other candidates), a naive L2 loss for imitation learning, and a collision loss to penalize planned trajectories that intersect with obstacles. Critically, during training and testing for planning, the *predicted trajectories* are used as action conditions, preventing ground-truth leakage and enabling the model to learn from its own predictions.

#### 3.5. Loss Functions

The overall optimization combines:
- **Normalization Loss (L_norm):** Cross-entropy for historical semantic probabilities and L1 loss for historical 3D backward centripetal flow, enhancing memory representations.
- **Forecasting Loss (L_fcst):** L1 for future flow predictions and a combination of cross-entropy, Lovász, and binary occupancy losses for future semantic occupancy predictions.
- **Planning Loss (L_plan):** As described above.

This comprehensive methodology allows Drive-OccWorld to not only predict the future states of the driving environment at a fine-grained 4D level but also to directly leverage these predictions for making safe and informed planning decisions in an end-to-end manner, while also supporting controllable generation of scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate Drive-OccWorld's superior performance across 4D occupancy and flow forecasting, as well as in end-to-end trajectory planning.

#### 4.1. 4D Occupancy and Flow Forecasting Performance

- **Inflated GMO and Flow Forecasting (nuScenes, Lyft-Level5):** Drive-OccWorld achieved state-of-the-art results.
 - On nuScenes, it surpassed previous methods (e.g., Cam4DOcc) by **9.4% in weighted mIoU for future occupancy (~mIoU_f)** and **5.1% in Video Panoptic Quality (VPQ_f)** for flow predictions.
 - Similar significant gains (6% in ~mIoU_f and 5.2% in VPQ_f) were observed on the Lyft-Level5 dataset.
 - This indicates a strong capability to forecast future world states, including both dilated occupancy patterns of general movable objects (GMO) and their dynamic motions (3D backward centripetal flow).
- **Fine-Grained GMO Forecasting (nuScenes-Occupancy):** For predicting more detailed voxel labels, Drive-OccWorld also outperformed existing methods across all time intervals, demonstrating its ability to forecast fine-grained world states.
- **Fine-grained GMO and GSO Forecasting (nuScenes-Occupancy):** Drive-OccWorld achieved the best performance. Specifically, `Drive-OccWorld_P` (conditioned on predicted trajectories) outperformed Cam4DOcc by **5.9% (mIoU_c)** and **5.1% (mIoU_f)** for general movable objects (GMO) at current and future timestamps, respectively, highlighting its accuracy in locating dynamic objects.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations confirm the model's ability to generate plausible 4D occupancy and flow predictions at various future timestamps, accurately modeling vehicle lane changes, pedestrian crossings, and ego-vehicle following behavior even in challenging conditions like nighttime.

#### 4.2. Controllability under Diverse Action Conditions

- **Impact of Action Conditions:** Injecting any action condition (velocity, steering angle, trajectory, commands) consistently improved forecasting results compared to a baseline without such conditions.
- **Low-Level vs. High-Level Conditions:** Low-level conditions (trajectory, velocity) significantly enhanced *future* forecasting (`~mIoU_f`), suggesting they help the world model better understand ego-environment interaction over time. High-level commands (e.g., "go forward") had a greater impact on current moment prediction (`mIoU_c`) but less on distant future predictions.
- **Predicted vs. Ground-Truth Trajectories:** While using ground-truth (GT) trajectories resulted in slightly better *planning* results, surprisingly, using *predicted* trajectories (from the planner P, denoted as `Drive-OccWorld_P`) slightly *improved occupancy and flow forecasting quality* compared to using GT actions (`Drive-OccWorld_A`). This is attributed to planning constraints associated with predicted trajectories, enabling the planner to cross-attend between trajectories and BEV features, which refines BEV features to account for ego motion and improves perception. It also suggests effective learning from its own predicted actions during training.
- **Qualitative Controllability:** The model successfully simulated different future occupancies based on various ego motions, such as changing steering angles or velocities, demonstrating its potential as a neural simulator for AD.

#### 4.3. End-to-End Planning Performance

- **Superior Planning Performance:** Drive-OccWorld_P achieved state-of-the-art planning performance on nuScenes across different evaluation protocols (NoAvg and TemAvg) in terms of L2 error and collision rates.
- **Significant L2 Error Reduction:** Using the NoAvg protocol, `Drive-OccWorld_P†` showed remarkable relative improvements compared to `UniAD†`, with a **33% reduction in L2@1s, 22% in L2@2s, and 9.7% in L2@3s**.
- **Enhanced Safety:** The model also demonstrated competitive collision rates, with `Drive-OccWorld_P‡` achieving very low collision rates comparable to the best methods, even when using ego status in planning and revised collision rate calculations.
- **Effectiveness of Continuous Forecasting and Planning:** The strong performance, especially at distant future timestamps, underscores the effectiveness of leveraging the world model's capacity to accumulate world knowledge and envision future states for robust motion planning.
- **Qualitative Planning Results:** Visualizations demonstrated the planner's ability to generate safe trajectories, avoiding preceding cars (including in rainy conditions) and yielding to pedestrians, confirming the efficacy of the occupancy-based cost function.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Conditional Normalization:** All components (semantic, ego-motion, agent-motion aware normalization) contributed positively. Ego-motion aware normalization yielded the largest gain in future occupancy mIoU (1.9%), emphasizing the importance of ego status. Agent-motion aware normalization specifically improved flow forecasting (VQP*f by 0.9%).
- **Action Conditioning Interface:** Cross-attention proved more effective for injecting action conditions than additive embeddings. Fourier embedding further improved performance by encoding conditions at high frequencies.
- **Occupancy-based Costs:** Each factor of the cost function (Agent-Safety, Road-Safety, Learned-Volume costs) contributed to safe planning. The agent-safety constraint was particularly critical, as its absence led to a significantly higher collision rate. BEV refinement also played a vital role in enhancing trajectory quality.

Overall, the findings strongly validate Drive-OccWorld's design choices, showcasing its capability to provide advanced 4D environmental understanding and to translate that understanding into safer and more accurate driving plans.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Drive-OccWorld represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving research, particularly in the realm of integrating predictive world models directly into decision-making. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging a Critical Gap in AD World Models:** The paper successfully moves beyond using world models solely for data generation or pretraining. By integrating a 4D occupancy forecasting world model directly with end-to-end planning, Drive-OccWorld addresses the crucial challenge of enabling autonomous agents to "think ahead" and anticipate future environmental states based on their own actions. This shifts the paradigm towards more proactive and informed decision-making.
- **Enhanced Safety and Robustness for Autonomous Driving:** By leveraging fine-grained 4D occupancy and flow forecasts, the system gains a much richer understanding of the future environment. This foresight allows the planner to evaluate potential trajectories more thoroughly against explicit safety constraints (e.g., avoiding collisions with dynamic agents, staying on the road), leading to demonstrably safer and more robust motion planning, especially in complex and dynamic scenarios.
- **Improved Generalization:** The accumulation of "world knowledge" within the model's memory and its ability to predict plausible future states contribute to better generalization. This is vital for autonomous vehicles operating in diverse and unpredictable real-world conditions, reducing deficiencies that plague many current end-to-end systems.
- **Increased Explainability of Decisions:** Unlike black-box end-to-end planners, Drive-OccWorld's explicit forecasting of future 4D occupancy and flow provides a clear, interpretable basis for the planner's decisions. When a trajectory is chosen to avoid a predicted collision, the underlying environmental prediction is visible, which is crucial for certification, debugging, and human trust in autonomous systems.
- **Foundational for Neural Simulation and Data Generation:** The action-controllable generation capability of Drive-OccWorld is a powerful feature. It allows for the simulation of diverse future scenarios conditioned on specific ego actions. This positions Drive-OccWorld as a **neural simulator** for autonomous driving. This has immense potential for:
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Creating high-fidelity, varied training data to augment real-world datasets, which are often expensive and limited.
 - **Extensive Testing and Validation:** Allowing AD systems to be tested against a vast array of simulated scenarios, including rare or hazardous ones, to identify weaknesses and improve safety.
 - **Reinforcement Learning for AD:** Providing a realistic, differentiable environment for training reinforcement learning agents to learn complex driving policies.
- **New State-of-the-Art Performance:** The significant improvements across multiple benchmarks for both 4D occupancy forecasting and end-to-end planning set new performance standards, demonstrating the practical effectiveness of the proposed approach.
- **Flexible and Adaptable Framework:** The unified conditioning interface allows for various types of action inputs (from low-level kinematics to high-level commands), making the framework adaptable to different planning strategies and downstream applications.
- **Practical Industry-Academic Collaboration:** The collaboration between Zhejiang University and Huawei Technologies underscores the potential for this research to translate into real-world applications and influence the development of commercial autonomous driving solutions.

In conclusion, Drive-OccWorld pushes autonomous driving intelligence forward by granting vehicles the ability to robustly anticipate the future and plan accordingly, a critical step towards truly safe, reliable, and generalized autonomous navigation. Its impact could resonate across research into predictive control, neural simulation, and the next generation of end-to-end autonomous driving systems.
