# Orbit: A Unified Simulation Framework for Interactive Robot Learning Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> ORBIT is a modular open-source robot-learning framework built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, pairing GPU-accelerated PhysX 5.1 rigid and FEM deformable physics with ray-traced photorealistic sensing. A World/Agent abstraction with actuator models, multi-rate sensors, and motion generators lets one scene serve many tasks. It ships 16 robot platforms, 4 sensor modalities, 10 motion generators, and 20+ benchmark tasks, reaching about 10x rigid and 3x deformable throughput over prior frameworks.

## Problem

- Vision-centric simulators render well but abstract away low-level contact and grasping, so policies trained in them never confront realistic physical interaction.
- Robotics physics engines such as Isaac Gym or SAPIEN omit physically-based rendering, deformable-body solvers, and ROS support, forcing researchers to stitch per-project tooling.
- CPU-vectorized frameworks exhaust memory near 200-300 parallel environments, and existing Isaac environment suites bind to one RL library with little modularity beyond RL.

## Method

- **World/Agent** abstraction: World holds robots, sensors, objects, and markers; Agent is a computation graph of perception and action nodes, so a graph cut plus extrinsic goal defines any task.
- ==Actuator groups== with ==DC-motor== and ==series-elastic actuator== models sit between joint controllers and the simulator, injecting delays and torque saturation; per-sensor and per-node timers give multi-rate operation.
- Batteries-included feature set: ==GPU differential IK==, operational-space and joint control, CPU ==RMPFlow== and ==OCS2== planners, keyboard/gamepad/spacemouse teleoperation, robomimic-format collection, wrappers to rl-games, RSL-RL, stable-baselines3.

## Results

- GPU parallelization yields roughly **10x** rigid-body and **3x** cloth throughput over robosuite, ManiSkill2, and DEDO, which crash near **200-300** environments; physics peaks around **125,000 FPS**.
- PPO reaches **50,000-75,000 FPS** with RSL-RL and rl-games at **2048** environments versus **6,000-18,000** for stable-baselines3, despite identical hyperparameter settings.
- Behavior cloning on Franka-LiftCube from **2000** teleoperated demos scores **0.43** success under changed initial and goal states while BC-RNN scores **0.87**; ANYmal-D locomotion transferred to hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating world from task via a computation-graph cut lets one scene serve many tasks and action spaces, mirroring how extrinsic rewards specify goals in reality.
> - Memory, not compute, caps CPU-vectorized simulation; end-to-end GPU pipelines scale environment counts an order of magnitude but shift the bottleneck to camera rendering.
> - Fidelity has a price: finer cloth meshes raise accuracy while cutting throughput, so simulation realism should be tuned per task rather than maximized.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robot simulation frameworks is an ongoing area of research critical for advancing robot learning, enabling iterative development, large-scale data generation, and safe testing without physical hardware. An ideal robot simulator is characterized by fast and accurate physics, high-fidelity sensor simulation, diverse asset handling, and user-friendly interfaces for task and environment integration. Historically, existing simulation platforms have presented trade-offs among these characteristics.

For instance, simulators primarily focused on computer vision, such as Habitat and ManipulaTHOR, often provide advanced rendering capabilities but simplify low-level physical interactions like grasping. Conversely, physics-centric simulators, including Isaac Gym and SAPIEN, offer efficient rigid-body contact dynamics but may lack physically-based rendering (PBR), deformable object simulation, or out-of-the-box Robotic Operating System (ROS) support. CPU-based physics engines like MuJoCo and Bullet, used in frameworks such as MetaWorld and RoboSuite, require CPU clusters for parallelization and typically focus on rigid-body manipulation. Simulators for deformable bodies, like DEDO and SoftGym, often employ particle-based dynamics or finite-element methods (FEM) but may have limited tooling for complex scenarios.

NVIDIA's Omniverse Isaac Sim, a recent development, aims to address these limitations by offering GPU-accelerated real-time PBR and an advanced physics engine. This work, ORBIT, builds upon Isaac Sim, seeking to consolidate the capabilities of this underlying technology into a unified and modular framework for robotics research. By doing so, ORBIT aims to reduce the effort duplication commonly observed when researchers develop custom tooling for diverse robot learning applications within a simulator. The project positions itself as a comprehensive platform designed to support various research paradigms, including reinforcement learning (RL), learning from demonstrations (LfD), and motion planning, by providing a "batteries-included" experience with a wide array of robots, sensors, and pre-defined tasks. The comparison with other frameworks (Table I in the paper) indicates that ORBIT strives to offer a broader spectrum of features, including GPU-accelerated rigid and deformable body physics, PBR, ray-tracing, a variety of sensors (RGBD, Semantic, LiDAR, Contact), diverse robotic platforms (fixed-arm, mobile, legged), and flexible scene authoring methods (procedural, mesh-scan, game-based).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for developing ORBIT stems from the observed limitations and fragmentation within existing robot simulation platforms, specifically the trade-offs between physical accuracy, visual fidelity, operational speed, and ease of use. The emergence of advanced simulators like NVIDIA Isaac Sim, with its GPU-accelerated physics and photo-realistic rendering capabilities, presented an opportunity to overcome these challenges. The authors recognized a need for a consolidated framework that could leverage these advancements without requiring individual researchers to invest substantial effort in building the necessary infrastructure for robot learning.

The key objectives of ORBIT are:

1. **Unified and Modular Framework Design:** To create an open-source, modular framework that facilitates the efficient creation of robotic environments. This design aims to simplify the process of defining new and complex tasks by providing abstractions that mirror real-world robotic systems.
2. **Leveraging Advanced Simulation Capabilities:** To exploit the latest simulation technologies provided by Isaac Sim, specifically its GPU-accelerated real-time Physically Based Rendering (PBR) for photo-realistic scenes and state-of-the-art physics engine (PhysX SDK 5) for high-fidelity rigid and deformable body simulation.
3. **Comprehensive Asset and Tooling Provision:** To offer a "batteries-included" experience for roboticists by integrating models for a wide variety of robotic platforms (fixed-arm, mobile manipulators, legged robots), diverse sensors (RGB, depth, semantic, LiDAR, force), and motion generators. This objective aims to lower the entry barrier for using the framework.
4. **Standardized Benchmark Tasks:** To provide a suite of benchmark tasks of varying complexity, ranging from single-stage operations like cabinet opening to multi-stage tasks such as room reorganization and cloth folding. These tasks serve both as benchmarks for algorithmic evaluation and as examples for users to design new environments.
5. **Support for Diverse Learning Paradigms:** To enable research across various robot learning domains, including reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning (IL), representation learning, and task and motion planning. The framework is designed to support diverse observation and action spaces required by these paradigms.
6. **High Throughput and Efficiency:** To enable the training of reinforcement learning policies and the collection of large demonstration datasets in a time-efficient manner by leveraging GPU-based parallelization inherent in Isaac Sim.
7. **Sim-to-Real Transfer Facilitation:** To design interfaces and abstractions that support the inclusion of actuator and noise models, different sensor update frequencies, and varied control architectures to bridge the simulation-to-reality (sim-to-real) gap, thus making trained policies more robust for real-world deployment.
8. **Community Collaboration and Extensibility:** To establish a common platform that fosters interdisciplinary collaborations within the robotics community and to ensure the framework's modularity allows for easy extension with new tasks, robots, and applications in the future.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ORBIT is designed as a unified and modular open-source framework built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, which itself runs on the Omniverse platform and utilizes PhysX SDK 5 for physics and RTX for rendering. The methodology centers on creating a hierarchical abstraction that separates the "World" (the simulated environment) from the "Agent" (the decision-making intelligence), mirroring real-world robotics.

**Core Abstractions:**

- **World:** This abstraction represents the physical environment.
 - **Scene Design:** The world can be constructed using multiple methods: procedural generation (script-based), scanned meshes (e.g., Replica dataset), or interactively via Isaac Sim's graphical user interface (GUI). This flexibility aims to combine the benefits of realistic architectural layouts from 3D scans with the ease of interactive scene creation.
 - **Robots:** Robots are central components, defined by their articulation, actuator models, and low-level joint controllers. ORBIT uses the Universal Scene Description (USD) format for robot models, providing tools for converting other formats (URDF, OBJ, STL) to USD. A unified interface (e.g., `LeggedRobot`) supports various robots within the same category. Actuator models (e.g., Direct Control motors, Series Elastic Actuators) are integrated to simulate real-world characteristics like delays and torque saturation, which are crucial for sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Sensors:** ORBIT unifies Isaac Sim's physics-based (range, force, contact) and rendering-based (RGB, depth, normals, semantic) sensors under a common interface. It includes a custom sensor management system that configures only necessary sensors for a given task and allows individual sensors to update at different frequencies, addressing a common discrepancy between simulation and reality.
 - **Objects:** Passive entities in the world, with support for randomization of textures and physics properties (e.g., friction). The framework also accounts for active motion components within objects, such as hybrid models for refrigerator hinges (initially stiff, then free).
 - **Visualization Markers:** Programmatic addition of primitive shapes (axes, spheres, meshes) for debugging and visualizing goal states or coordinate frames.

- **Agent:** This abstraction represents the control and perception pipeline of the embodied system.
 - **Computation Graph:** An agent comprises various nodes forming a computation graph. Nodes are categorized as perception-based (e.g., RGB-D to point-cloud conversion) or action-based (e.g., task-level commands to joint commands).
 - **Modularity and Frequency Control:** Each node has an internal timer to control its update frequency, addressing the asynchronous nature of real-world robotic systems. Information flow between nodes occurs synchronously via Python, avoiding communication overheads.

- **Learning Task:** This separate module defines the goal, reward functions, and reset conditions for a specific task. This separation allows the same `World` definition to be used for multiple tasks, similar to how tasks are specified via extrinsic rewards in the real world. The task definition can also integrate specific agent computation nodes, enabling different control architectures (e.g., task-space control at a lower frequency, joint control at a higher frequency) within a unified framework.

**Included Features and Tools:**

- **Robot Diversity:** Supports 4 mobile platforms, 7 robotic arms (6-DoF, 7-DoF), and 6 end-effectors, with tools to compose complex systems like mobile manipulators.
- **I/O Devices:** Integration with input devices like Keyboard, Gamepad, and Spacemouse for teleoperation and demonstration data collection.
- **Motion Generators:** Provides GPU-based implementations for differential inverse kinematics (IK), operational-space control (OSC), and joint-level control for efficient parallel execution. CPU-based planners like RMP-Flow and OCS2 are also included. Pre-trained policies for legged locomotion are available.
- **Benchmark Tasks:** A suite of over 20 tasks, including rigid object manipulation (e.g., grasping, screwing, stacking), deformable object manipulation (e.g., cloth folding), locomotion, and in-hand manipulation. These tasks allow easy switching of robots, objects, observations, action spaces, and domain randomization parameters.

**Workflow Support:**

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Provides wrappers for popular RL frameworks such as `rl-games`, `RSL-rl`, and `stable-baselines-3`, making the environment agnostic to the specific learning library.
- **Teleoperation and Imitation Learning (IL):** Offers interfaces for collecting demonstration data using I/O devices, compatible with frameworks like `robomimic`.
- **Motion Planning:** Supports both hand-crafted state machines for sequential planning and interactive motion planning workflows through the GUI, incorporating grasp generation and motion previewing.
- **Sim-to-Real Deployment:** Demonstrates mechanisms for deploying policies on real robots:
 - **ZMQ:** For lightweight communication of joint commands from ORBIT to a real-time kernel controlling a Franka Emika robot (upsampling 60Hz commands to 1000Hz).
 - **ROS:** For integration with existing robotic software stacks, showcased with a locomotion policy deployed on an ANYmal-D quadruped.

**Evaluation Methods:**

- **Simulation Accuracy:** Quantitatively evaluated deformable body simulation accuracy by comparing simulated damped oscillations of a clamped silicone beam to real-world motion capture data.
- **Simulation Throughput:** Compared ORBIT's performance against other frameworks (RoboSuite, ManiSkill2, IsaacGymEnvs, DEDO) for rigid and deformable body tasks, measuring frames per second (FPS) across varying numbers of parallel environments, using a standardized hardware setup.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents ORBIT as a functional and comprehensive simulation framework, demonstrating its capabilities through various evaluations and exemplar workflows.

**Framework Offerings:**
ORBIT provides a significant collection of resources out-of-the-box: 16 robotic platforms, 4 sensor modalities, 10 motion generators, over 20 benchmark tasks (11 rigid, 13 deformable, 2 locomotion), and wrappers for 4 learning libraries.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) Performance:**
Experiments on Franka-Reach and Franka-Cabinet-Opening tasks using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) showed variations in training speed across different RL frameworks. GPU-optimized frameworks like RSL-rl and rl-games achieved throughputs of 50,000-75,000 frames per second (FPS) with 2048 parallel environments. In contrast, stable-baselines3, which relies on CPU for environment interaction, achieved 6,000-18,000 FPS. This highlights the benefit of ORBIT's GPU-accelerated design when integrated with similarly optimized learning libraries. The framework also allowed for easy switching of action spaces, such as joint position or joint velocity control, impacting policy learning curves.

**Teleoperation and Imitation Learning (IL):**
For the Franka-LiftCube task, the framework facilitated the collection of 2000 demonstration trajectories using teleoperation. Policies trained with Behavior Cloning (BC) and BC with an RNN (BC-RNN) were evaluated. BC-RNN showed higher robustness, achieving 100% success in the unchanged evaluation setup and 88% success when both initial and goal states were varied, compared to 87% for BC. This demonstrates ORBIT's utility for collecting high-quality datasets and training robust IL policies.

**Motion Planning:**
ORBIT demonstrated support for both hand-crafted policies (state machines for sequential planning, shown across various tasks in Fig. 6) and interactive motion planning. The interactive workflow allowed users to select objects, preview grasp poses generated by an image-based grasp generator, visualize robot motion sequences via an RMP controller, and then execute the motion. This capability supports traditional robotics methodologies and human-in-the-loop task design.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
Successful sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated for two distinct robotic platforms:
- **Franka Emika Arm:** Joint commands were sent from ORBIT via ZeroMQ (ZMQ) at 60Hz, upsampled to 1000Hz on the robot's real-time kernel. This setup allowed for teleoperation, state machine execution, and waypoint tracking with obstacle avoidance using both the Franka Emika hand and an Allegro hand. The successful real-world execution, including simultaneous object lifting, suggested a realistic contact simulation.
- **ANYmal-D Quadruped:** A locomotion policy trained entirely in ORBIT, incorporating domain randomization (e.g., base mass, random pushes), was deployed on a real ANYmal-D robot using its ROS software stack. The policy successfully transferred, indicating the viability of ORBIT's simulated contact dynamics and its suitability for contact-rich tasks in real-world environments.

**Simulation Accuracy (Deformable Bodies):**
A quantitative comparison of a clamped silicone beam's deformation under gravity showed that ORBIT's simulation results closely matched real-world motion capture data. The damped oscillations observed in the simulation followed the physical behavior, demonstrating the accuracy of the FEM-solver for deformable bodies within Isaac Sim.

**Simulation Throughput:**
Throughput comparisons revealed significant performance advantages:
- **Rigid Body Tasks:** GPU-based parallelization in ORBIT (and IsaacGymEnvs) achieved approximately 10 times higher throughput compared to CPU-based vectorized frameworks (RoboSuite, ManiSkill2). While CPU-based frameworks were limited by memory around 200-300 environments, ORBIT scaled efficiently to thousands of environments, reaching up to 125,000 FPS for physics simulation.
- **Deformable Body Tasks:** For cloth manipulation, ORBIT obtained approximately 3 times higher throughput than DEDO. The study also showed that increasing the mesh resolution of the cloth, while improving accuracy, inversely affected throughput, demonstrating the computational trade-offs involved.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ORBIT represents an advancement in simulation frameworks for robotics, with several implications for the broader research community:

- **Accelerated Robot Learning Research:** By providing a unified, modular, and high-performance platform, ORBIT has the potential to significantly accelerate research in robot learning, including reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and motion planning. The ability to run thousands of environments in parallel on GPUs enables faster iteration cycles for policy training and large-scale data collection.
- **Reduced Entry Barrier:** The "batteries-included" approach, offering a diverse range of pre-integrated robots, sensors, motion generators, and benchmark tasks, aims to lower the technical barrier for new researchers entering the field of robotics and robot learning. This reduces the time and effort traditionally required to set up simulation environments.
- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The framework's design emphasizes features critical for successful sim-to-real transfer, such as the inclusion of actuator and noise models, support for varying sensor and control frequencies, and high-fidelity physics and rendering. Demonstrated transfers to real Franka and ANYmal robots indicate the framework's capability to bridge the simulation-reality gap, which is crucial for deploying learned policies on physical hardware.
- **Advanced Simulation Fidelity:** Leveraging NVIDIA Isaac Sim's GPU-accelerated PhysX 5 for rigid and deformable body dynamics, coupled with RTX ray-tracing for physically-based rendering, provides a level of fidelity that can capture complex physical phenomena and generate photo-realistic sensory data. This is particularly impactful for tasks involving contact-rich manipulation and deformable objects, where accurate simulation has historically been challenging.
- **Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** As an open-source platform, ORBIT aims to serve as a common infrastructure, fostering collaboration across various research areas in robotics. Its modularity and extensibility encourage community contributions, potentially leading to a richer ecosystem of tasks, robots, and algorithms.
- **Standardized Benchmarking:** The provision of a suite of benchmark tasks, with the flexibility to easily switch between different robots, objects, and configurations, offers a standardized basis for evaluating and comparing different robotic algorithms, promoting reproducible research.
- **Facilitating Complex Task Design:** The flexible scene authoring capabilities (procedural, mesh-scan, GUI-based) and hierarchical abstractions simplify the design of complex, multi-stage tasks, allowing researchers to explore more intricate robotic behaviors.

In summary, ORBIT's comprehensive features, high performance, and explicit focus on sim-to-real transfer positions it as a significant tool for advancing the state of the art in robot learning and the development of intelligent robotic systems.
