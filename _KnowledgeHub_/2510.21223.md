# Model Merging with Functional Dual Anchors

> [!summary] Summary
> Functional Dual Anchors (FDAs) introduce a method for model merging that operates by modeling knowledge in the input-representation space, rather than the parameter space. This approach generates synthetic inputs whose induced gradients align with task vectors, leading to consistent performance improvements over parameter-centric methods and effectively refining models merged by existing techniques across vision and language tasks.

## Problem

- Existing model merging methods primarily operate in the parameter space, facing challenges with parameter inconsistency and conflicts when combining diverse task-specific knowledge.
- There is a need for efficient post-training strategies to consolidate knowledge from multiple specialized (finetuned) models into a single, unified model without the high cost of full multi-task retraining.
- Parameter-space task vectors can be sensitive to initialization and prone to drift, limiting the robustness and reliability of merged models.

## Method

- The paper proposes ==Functional Dual Anchors (FDAs)==, synthetic inputs constructed by matching their induced gradients on a pretrained model to the ==task vector== (parameter difference) of a finetuned model, effectively projecting task knowledge into the input-representation space.
- A principled ==initialization scheme for FDAs==, derived from linear model analysis, ensures efficient and stable convergence during their construction, limiting energy in the tail subspace.
- Once constructed, FDAs are used to optimize the merged model parameters by minimizing ==representation discrepancies== across tasks, or to refine existing parameter-centric merged models by fine-tuning their task vector adjustments.

## Results

- FDAs consistently outperform basic parameter-centric methods like Task Arithmetic, achieving an average performance improvement of nearly **18%** on ViT-B/16 and **15.4%** in average GLUE score on RoBERTa-Large.
- FDAs are complementary to existing data-free parameter-centric merging methods, providing further performance improvements of up to **~5.10%** on ViT-B/16, **~13%** on RoBERTa-Large, and **10%** on auto-regressive models when used as a refinement step.
- The layer-wise FDA construction and adaptation strategy, combined with effective initialization schemes like "Linear Weight Sampling," enables practical application and scalability to large foundation models across diverse vision and language tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling knowledge in the input-representation space via synthetic inputs (FDAs) offers a robust and flexible alternative to direct parameter-space manipulation for model merging, effectively bridging multi-task training and post-hoc merging paradigms.
> - FDAs exhibit a long-tailed singular value spectrum and their high-energy subspace progressively aligns with that of real task-specific data, indicating they capture salient functional characteristics.
> - FDA-induced parameter adaptations (gradients) align with those induced by real data, validating their effectiveness as a proxy for real data in guiding model changes and mitigating representation bias in merged models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the critical and rapidly evolving area of **model merging**, an efficient post-training strategy for consolidating knowledge from multiple specialized (finetuned) checkpoints of a shared foundation model into a single, unified model. This approach is gaining traction due to the proliferation of large foundation models and the need to adapt them to diverse downstream tasks without the prohibitive cost of full multi-task retraining or sequential continual learning.

**Comparison to Related Fields:**
- **Multi-task Learning (MTL):** MTL aims to train a single model on multiple tasks simultaneously from scratch. Model merging, in contrast, is a *post-training* strategy, combining *already finetuned* models. This offers flexibility as individual models can be finetuned independently.
- **Continual Learning (CL):** CL focuses on learning new tasks sequentially while retaining performance on previously learned tasks. Model merging, however, primarily focuses on consolidating knowledge from *contemporaneously finetuned* models into one at a specific point in time, rather than sequential adaptation.

**Limitations of Existing Model Merging Approaches:**
The prevailing paradigm in model merging operates in the **parameter space**. Methods typically compute "task vectors" (the difference between finetuned and pretrained parameters) and then combine these vectors, often with scaling or adjustments, to form a merged model.
- **Data-free methods:** These approaches manipulate task vectors based on intrinsic properties of the parameter space (e.g., magnitude, similarity, orthogonality, subspace structure) (Ilharco et al., 2022; Yadav et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2024; Du et al., 2024; Xiong et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2025b; Gargiulo et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025).
- **Data-driven methods:** These utilize task-specific data (or proxies thereof) to guide adjustments to task vectors, often through iterative optimization or distillation (Matena & Raffel, 2022; Jin et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023, 2024; Wei et al., 2025a; Xu et al., 2025).

A fundamental challenge for all parameter-space methods is **parameter inconsistency** and **conflicts** arising from diverse task-specific knowledge encoded in different parameter updates. These methods primarily focus on *mitigating* these conflicts within the parameter space.

**Novelty of this Work:**
This paper introduces a fundamentally different perspective: instead of operating in the parameter space, it proposes to model the **input-representation space**. By generating synthetic inputs (Functional Dual Anchors or FDAs) whose induced gradients align with task vectors, the method effectively projects task-specific knowledge into a functional space. This is a significant shift, as it offers a new conceptual framework for knowledge consolidation that is potentially more robust to parameter inconsistencies. It also implicitly bridges the gap between post-hoc merging and joint multi-task training, where knowledge integration inherently occurs through the interaction with data in the input space. The concept builds upon insights from dataset distillation and iterative teaching, where input-space modeling has proven effective for knowledge transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Functional Dual Anchors (FDAs) are multifaceted:

1. **Address Parameter Inconsistencies and Conflicts:** Existing model merging methods are primarily confined to the parameter space. While effective, they are limited by the inherent conflicts and inconsistencies that arise when attempting to directly combine diverse knowledge encoded as parameter offsets. The authors aim to circumvent these limitations by proposing an alternative approach.
2. **Shift Knowledge Modeling to Input-Representation Space:** The core motivation is to model task-specific knowledge in the input-representation space rather than the parameter space. The authors hypothesize that modeling in the input space can be more robust and flexible, drawing parallels to how joint multi-task training implicitly integrates knowledge through data inputs.
3. **Bridge Multi-task Training and Post-hoc Merging:** By constructing synthetic inputs that elicit similar functional shifts as task-specific finetuning, FDAs aim to emulate the effect of joint multi-task training within a post-hoc merging framework. This allows for the benefits of both approaches: the flexibility of post-hoc merging with the integrated knowledge representation of joint training.
4. **Enhance Robustness and Flexibility of Merged Models:** The authors observe that parameter-space task vectors can be sensitive to initialization and prone to drift. By capturing functional shifts in the input space, FDAs are expected to offer greater robustness to such variations, leading to more reliable model merging.
5. **Develop a Principled Initialization Scheme:** Recognizing the non-convex nature of optimizing synthetic inputs, a key objective is to derive an effective initialization strategy for FDAs to ensure efficient and stable convergence during their construction.
6. **Complement Existing Parameter-Centric Methods:** Beyond serving as a standalone merging strategy, the authors aim to demonstrate that FDAs can enhance existing parameter-space model merging techniques, acting as a refinement step to further improve their performance by leveraging input-space knowledge.
7. **Leverage Prior Successes in Input-Space Modeling:** The motivation is reinforced by the empirical success of input-space modeling in related fields like dataset distillation, iterative teaching, dataset condensation, and continual learning, suggesting its potential applicability to model merging.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Functional Dual Anchors (FDAs) framework operates in two distinct stages: (1) FDA construction and (2) parameter optimization using FDAs.

#### 3.1. FDA Construction: Knowledge Projection via Gradient Matching

The first stage focuses on creating synthetic inputs (FDAs) that effectively capture the functional shift induced by a task-specific finetuned model (φ(θ_i)) relative to the pretrained model (φ(θ_0)). This is achieved by matching the gradients induced by these synthetic inputs on the pretrained model to the corresponding task vector (τ_i = θ_i - θ_0).

- **Objective Function (Equation 1):** For each downstream checkpoint φ(θ_i), a set of `n` synthetic input points `{x_ij}` is constructed by minimizing the cosine distance between two gradient vectors:
 `min_{x_i1...,x_in} cos_dist(∇_θ Σ_j Dist(φ(θ, x_ij), φ(θ_i, x_ij)) |θ=θ_0, τ_i)`
 Here, `Dist(·)` measures the representation discrepancy between the pretrained model's output and the finetuned model's output on the synthetic inputs. Cosine distance is primarily used, implying a focus on directional alignment of representations.
- **Gradient-based Construction (Equation 2):** The objective is optimized using gradient descent in the input data space (`X`), where model parameters are fixed. This iterative process refines the synthetic inputs.
- **Linear Model Analysis for Initialization:** To mitigate issues like slow convergence or poor generalization due to non-convexity, a simplified linear model analysis is performed. This analysis reveals that optimization has minimal effect on components corresponding to near-zero eigenvalues of `∆Wᵀ∆W` (where `∆W = W_i - W_0`). This insight leads to the **Initialization Principle**: an effective initialization strategy should limit the energy of the initialization point within the tail subspace spanned by the task vector.
- **Initialization Strategies:**
 - **Linear Weight Sampling:** Initializes `x_ij` by sampling a row vector from the weight matrix `W_i` of the finetuned model. This approach naturally aligns with the idea of avoiding excessive tail energy, as weight matrices often exhibit long-tailed spectra.
 - **Scaled Gaussian Sampling:** Initializes `x_ij` from a standard normal distribution scaled by a coefficient `σ`. This ensures coverage of the entire input space while `σ` directly controls the total energy, indirectly limiting tail energy.

#### 3.2. Parameter Optimization: Leveraging FDAs for Multi-task Model Merging

Once the FDAs for all tasks are constructed, they are used to update the model parameters.

- **Direct Adaptation (Equation 5):** The merged model parameters `ˆθ` are initialized with the pretrained parameters `θ_0` and then optimized to minimize the total representation discrepancy across all tasks and their respective FDAs:
 `min_θ Σ_i Σ_j Dist(φ(θ, x_ij), φ(θ_i, x_ij))`
 This process leverages the FDAs as synthetic training data to guide the model towards a multi-task optimal state.
- **Refinement of Parameter-Centric Methods (Equation 6):** FDAs can also be used to refine task vectors obtained from existing parameter-centric merging methods (e.g., TA, TSV, WUDI). Given an already merged model `φ(θ_0 + Σ_i ϕ_i(τ_i))`, the adjustment functions `ϕ_i` are optimized to minimize representation discrepancies on FDAs, effectively fine-tuning the parameter-space merging:
 `min_{ϕ(τ_i)} Σ_i Σ_j Dist(φ(θ_0 + Σ_i ϕ_i(τ_i), x_ij), φ(θ_i, x_ij))`

#### 3.3. Practical Implementation for Foundation Models

- **Layer-wise Construction and Adaptation:** To handle the computational complexity of second-order gradients and the sheer size of foundation models (e.g., Transformers), a layer-wise strategy is employed. The model, parameters, and task vectors are partitioned, and FDAs are constructed and adapted layer by layer.
- **Shape of FDAs:** FDAs `x_ij` have dimensions `token_num × embedding_dim`. The number of anchors (`n`) and `token_num` are hyperparameters explored in ablation studies.
- **The Scale Coefficient (σ):** The scaling coefficient `σ` in Gaussian initialization is critical for convergence, balancing between suppressing tail energy and maintaining sufficient head energy for optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Functional Dual Anchors (FDAs) across diverse tasks and model architectures yields several significant findings:

#### 4.1. Understanding FDA-Encoded Knowledge

- **Long-tailed Spectrum Structure:** FDAs, during optimization, evolve to exhibit a long-tailed singular value spectrum, similar to real data. This suggests that the optimization process guides FDAs to distribute energy effectively, with less energy allocated to "tail" components. Initializations with lower tail energy (e.g., weight-based or appropriate σ) lead to faster convergence in FDA construction.
- **Subspace Alignment with Real Data:** The high-energy subspace spanned by FDAs gradually aligns with that of real task-specific data. This indicates that FDAs effectively learn to capture the most salient functional characteristics relevant to the task, albeit not necessarily by precisely mimicking the input manifold of real data.
- **Alignment of Induced Adaptation:** FDAs induce parameter adaptations (gradients) that increasingly align with adaptations induced by real data. This is observed by projecting FDA-induced adaptations onto non-negative cones spanned by sampled parameter updates from real data. This confirms that FDAs serve as a valid proxy for real data in guiding model changes.
- **Mitigation of Representation Bias:** Adaptation with FDAs effectively mitigates representation bias in both pretrained and merged models, as measured by representation discrepancies on real data.

#### 4.2. Performance on Vision and Language Models

Comprehensive experiments across vision (CLIP Vision Encoder with ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14 on 8 datasets), natural language (RoBERTa-Base/Large on GLUE benchmark), and auto-regressive models (WizardMath-13B, LLaMA-2-13B-code-Alpaca) demonstrate the effectiveness of FDAs.

- **Significant Improvement over Baselines:** FDAs consistently and significantly outperform basic parameter-centric methods like Task Arithmetic (TA). For instance, on ViT-B/16, FDA (Pretrained, Weight) achieves an average performance of 87.26, a nearly 18% improvement over TA's 73.94. Similarly, on RoBERTa-Large, FDAs show a 15.4% improvement in average GLUE score over TA.
- **Competitive Performance:** FDAs achieve performance comparable to, and often surpassing, many data-based model merging methods (e.g., RegMean, Fisher Merging, AdaMerging, ProDistill), despite FDAs being *synthetic* data.
- **Complementarity with Parameter-Centric Methods:** A key finding is that FDAs are complementary to existing data-free parameter-centric merging methods (TA, TSV, WUDI). Incorporating FDAs to refine models merged by these methods consistently improves their performance (e.g., ~5.10% on ViT-B/16, ~13% on RoBERTa-Large, and 10% on auto-regressive models over TA). This suggests that input-space knowledge can effectively fine-tune parameter-space combinations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Initialization Schemes:** "Linear Weight Sampling" performs best, followed by "Scaled Gaussian Sampling" with an optimally chosen `σ` (e.g., `σ = 10^-2`). This validates the Initialization Principle.
- **Shape of FDAs:** Generally, larger numbers of anchors (`n`) and `token_num` lead to better FDA quality (higher multi-task performance). However, for RoBERTa-Base, increasing `token_num` beyond a certain point caused a slight decrease in overall performance, largely attributed to a single dataset (STSB), indicating task-specific sensitivities.
- **Distance Functions:** Cosine distance is most effective for FDA construction, outperforming L1 and L2 distances. The choice of distance function matters more for construction than for adaptation.
- **Optimization Steps:** More optimization steps generally lead to higher-quality FDAs. High-quality FDAs can be obtained efficiently, often within 40 steps, indicating the robustness and efficiency of the FDA construction process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Functional Dual Anchors (FDAs) marks a significant methodological advancement in the field of model merging, shifting the paradigm from purely parameter-space manipulation to **input-representation space modeling**. This novel perspective offers several critical implications and potential impacts:

1. **A Robust Alternative to Parameter-Space Merging:** FDAs provide a fundamentally different way to conceptualize and execute model merging. By focusing on the *functional shifts* induced by finetuned models rather than direct parameter combinations, FDAs inherently address challenges like parameter inconsistencies and conflicts. This could lead to more robust and reliable merged models, especially as foundation models grow in complexity and heterogeneity of finetuning tasks.

2. **Bridging Theoretical Gaps:** The framework explicitly connects post-hoc model merging with the principles of joint multi-task training and knowledge transfer in the input space. This theoretical bridge not only offers new insights into how knowledge is consolidated but also opens avenues for cross-pollination of ideas between these previously distinct research areas. Techniques from multi-task learning or data distillation could inspire further advancements in FDA construction and application.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The demonstrated ability of FDAs to significantly improve multi-task performance, both independently and when combined with existing parameter-centric methods, highlights their practical utility. This improvement, often approaching or exceeding that of data-based methods without requiring real task data during merging, indicates enhanced generalization capabilities for the merged models.

4. **Flexibility and Scalability for Large Models:** The layer-wise construction and adaptation strategy makes FDAs practically applicable to large-scale foundation models (e.g., Vision Transformers, RoBERTa, LLaMA-2). This scalability is crucial for leveraging the full potential of these models in diverse real-world applications without incurring massive retraining costs.

5. **New Research Directions in Synthetic Data Generation:** FDAs are a form of synthetic data, specifically designed to capture functional knowledge. This work could spur further research into tailored synthetic data generation for various model adaptation and knowledge transfer tasks. Understanding the properties of these "functional anchors" (e.g., their intrinsic dimension, relationship to task difficulty, and interpretability) could lead to more powerful and efficient learning strategies.

6. **Potential for Broader Impact:** The ability to efficiently merge specialized models into a single, capable multi-task model has broad implications across various domains, including:
 - **Resource Optimization:** Reducing the need for separate deployments of many finetuned models, thus saving computational resources and simplifying model management.
 - **Model Personalization:** Consolidating user-specific preferences or domain adaptations into a single personalized model.
 - **Continual Learning Enhancements:** Although distinct, insights from FDA could inform techniques for consolidating knowledge in continual learning scenarios.

In conclusion, "Model Merging with Functional Dual Anchors" presents a compelling new direction for knowledge consolidation in deep learning. By ingeniously shifting the focus to the input-representation space, the authors offer a robust, flexible, and powerful framework that not only improves the state-of-the-art in model merging but also opens exciting avenues for future research at the intersection of model understanding, synthetic data, and efficient model adaptation.
