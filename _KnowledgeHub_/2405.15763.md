# FreeMotion: A Unified Framework for Number-free Text-to-Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> FreeMotion unifies single- and multi-person text-to-motion synthesis by decomposing the joint motion distribution into a recursive chain of conditional distributions, factored into a generation module (single-person diffusion) plus an interaction module (global self-attention over any number of condition motions). Trained only on two-person data, it generalizes to three and four people and outperforms InterGen on the InterHuman benchmark.

## Problem

- Prior single-person and two-person motion models fit either the marginal or the two-person joint distribution separately, unable to run inference for both cases with one network.
- No text-annotated multi-person motion dataset beyond pairs exists, and existing network designs are not scalable, so no method generates motion for more than two individuals at inference.
- Spatial control methods proven for single-person motion have no established way to integrate into multi-person interaction models for fine-grained joint-level control.

## Method

- ==Conditional motion decomposition==: reformulate n-person joint distribution as a recursive product of single-person conditional distributions via the ==chain rule==, enabling number-free generation.
- **Interactive Block**: stacked ==self-attention== module with random masking of condition hidden states, injected into a frozen generation module through ==zero-initialized linear layers== in a ==ControlNet==-style two-stage training scheme.
- Explicit plus implicit spatial guidance: ==classifier-guidance== L2 trajectory correction combined with a masked linear-injection pathway lets single-person spatial control transfer to multi-person control without redesign.

## Results

- On InterHuman two-person synthesis, FreeMotion achieves FID **6.740** versus InterGen's **13.404** and Top-1 R-Precision **0.326** versus **0.264**, the best among all compared baselines.
- On re-annotated single-person synthesis, FreeMotion improves FID from **23.415** (InterGen) to **12.975** and Top-1 R-Precision from **0.206** to **0.264**.
- Ablation shows adding interactive descriptions boosts the full model's FID from **8.792** to **6.740**, while the generation-module-only variant instead degrades from **8.842** to **10.749**, confirming the decoupled design's benefit.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating a fixed-cardinality joint generation problem as a recursive conditional-generation chain lets a single model scale to unseen population sizes at inference without retraining.
> - Self-attention with random condition masking is more flexible than cross-attention for interaction modeling because it naturally handles a variable number of condition inputs.
> - Decoupling a base generative module from a frozen-and-adapted interaction module (ControlNet-style) can prevent conflicting text distributions from degrading a jointly finetuned single network.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion synthesis is a rapidly evolving area within computer vision and graphics, with broad applications in animation, virtual reality, gaming, robotics, and human-computer interaction. The primary goal is to generate realistic and semantically consistent human movements based on various input signals, with text being a particularly intuitive and versatile modality.

Historically, text-to-motion (T2M) synthesis began with a focus on **single-person motion generation**. Early methods often employed VAE (Variational Autoencoder) structures to align text and motion in a shared latent space (e.g., TEMOS, Guo et al.). More recently, **diffusion models** have emerged as the dominant paradigm for T2M, offering superior diversity and realism (e.g., MDM, Motion Diffuse, MLD). These models typically condition a denoising process on text embeddings, often extracted using pre-trained large language models like CLIP. Advances in this area have focused on improving fidelity, diversity, and controllability for individual motions, including detailed trajectory control (e.g., GMD, OmniControl).

A natural progression has been towards **multi-person motion synthesis**, which introduces significant complexity due to the need to model interactions, spatial relationships, and temporal coordination between individuals. Initial efforts in multi-person motion often relied on motion graphs or inverse kinematics, but recent advancements have seen deep learning models, particularly those leveraging attention mechanisms, making strides. For T2M specifically, two-person motion synthesis has been the frontier, with works like ComMDM and InterGen pioneering this area. ComMDM demonstrated few-shot fine-tuning of single-human models, while InterGen introduced a large-scale two-person text-annotated dataset (InterHuman) and a shared-weight diffusion network with cross-attention to model interactions.

However, the existing landscape for T2M has critical limitations that FreeMotion directly addresses:
1. **Universality:** Most models are specialized for either single-person or two-person scenarios and cannot simultaneously infer both, let alone a variable number of individuals. This creates a fragmented ecosystem.
2. **Scalability:** Generating motion for *more than two* individuals has remained largely unexplored and unachievable. This is primarily due to the scarcity of text-annotated multi-person datasets for larger groups and the non-scalability of network architectures designed for fixed numbers of participants.
3. **Controllability for Multi-person:** While spatial control has advanced for single-person motion, integrating precise spatial guidance into multi-person generation models, especially those handling interactions, poses a non-trivial challenge.

FreeMotion positions itself as a crucial advancement by tackling these exact limitations. It proposes a **unified framework** that achieves **number-free motion synthesis**, meaning it can generate motions for any number of individuals (single, two, three, or more) within a single model. This is a significant leap beyond the fixed-person-count limitations of prior art. The paper innovatively leverages the power of diffusion models and draws inspiration from conditional control mechanisms like ControlNet, but adapts them to the unique challenges of recursive, variable-person-count motion generation. By reframing multi-person generation as a sequence of conditional single-person generations, FreeMotion offers a scalable and universal solution that pushes the boundaries of text-driven human motion synthesis towards more complex and realistic scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the FreeMotion project is to develop a **unified framework capable of "number-free" text-to-motion synthesis**, addressing the significant limitations of existing methods in universality, scalability, and multi-person spatial control.

The motivation for this ambitious goal stems from several critical shortcomings in the current state of text-to-motion (T2M) research:

1. **Lack of Universality:** Current T2M models are typically specialized. They are either designed to generate motion for a single individual or for a fixed pair of individuals. This means that a separate model is often required for each scenario, making it impossible to apply a single model to synthesize motions for varying numbers of people simultaneously. This fragmentation hinders the development of general-purpose motion generation systems.

2. **Limited Scalability to Multiple Individuals:** The ability to generate motions for more than two individuals based on textual descriptions has been an unmet challenge. This limitation is twofold:
 - **Data Scarcity:** Large-scale, high-quality text-annotated datasets for multi-person motions (especially for groups larger than two) are virtually non-existent.
 - **Non-scalable Network Designs:** Existing network architectures for two-person motion synthesis (e.g., those relying on fixed cross-attention mechanisms between two entities) are not inherently scalable to an arbitrary or larger number of participants without extensive redesign or re-training. This makes generating motions for three, four, or more people practically impossible with prior approaches.

3. **Difficulty in Multi-person Spatial Control:** While some progress has been made in achieving fine-grained spatial control for single-person motion generation, extending these control signals to multi-person scenarios remains a complex task. The challenge lies in how to effectively incorporate spatial constraints for multiple interacting bodies while maintaining the realism and coherence of their movements.

To overcome these fundamental problems, the authors propose a novel rethinking of the multi-person motion synthesis process. Their core insight is to transform the complex task of modeling a joint distribution of *n* individuals, p(x1..., xn), into a sequence of conditional single-person generations. By decomposing the joint distribution using the chain rule of probability – p(x1..., xn) = p(x1) * Product(p(xi+1 | xi..., x1)) – the problem is reframed. This means generating the first person's motion, then the second's conditioned on the first, and so on, recursively. This paradigm offers a pathway to number-free generation, as it reduces the problem to learning a *conditional single-person motion synthesis* model that can account for any number of prior individuals.

Ultimately, FreeMotion is motivated by the need for a more practical, flexible, and comprehensive T2M system that can seamlessly handle diverse scenarios—from a lone individual dancing to a group of people interacting—and provide precise spatial control, thereby greatly expanding the utility of synthetic human motion in advanced applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FreeMotion introduces a novel unified framework for number-free text-to-motion synthesis, predicated on decomposing multi-person motion generation into a series of conditional single-person generations. This approach tackles the challenges of scalability and universality by leveraging a diffusion model architecture augmented with specialized modules for generation and interaction, alongside a two-stage training strategy and integrated spatial control.

#### 3.1. Core Paradigm: Recursive Conditional Generation

The fundamental idea is to decompose the joint probability distribution of *n* individuals' motions, p(x1..., xn), into a product of conditional probabilities: p(x1) * Product(p(xi+1 | xi..., x1)). This allows for a recursive generation process:
1. Generate the motion for the first individual (x1) based solely on the text prompt.
2. Generate the motion for the second individual (x2) conditioned on the text and the previously generated motion x1.
3. Continue this process, generating xi+1 conditioned on the text and all previously generated motions (x1..., xi), until all *n* individuals' motions are synthesized.
This paradigm transforms the complex multi-person task into sequential conditional single-person motion synthesis, inherently supporting any number of individuals.

#### 3.2. FreeMotion Framework Architecture

The framework consists of two main decoupled modules: a Generation Module and an Interaction Module.

- **Generation Module:** This module is responsible for synthesizing diverse single-person motion based on a given text prompt. It is a **Transformer-based diffusion network**, akin to state-of-the-art single-person T2M models (e.g., MDM). Its role is to denoise a Gaussian noise input into a coherent human motion sequence, guided by text features extracted using a pre-trained CLIP model. Text features are injected into adaptive layer normalization within the attention layers.

- **Interaction Module:** Inspired by ControlNet, this module is designed to inject condition signals (motions of other individuals) into the generation process of the current person. When generating motion for x1, this module is inactive. When generating x2, x1 becomes the condition. When generating x3, x1 and x2 become conditions, and so on.
 - It takes the noised motion of the current target person (e.g., x1_t) and the N-1 condition motions (x2..., xN) as inputs.
 - All inputs are first encoded into hidden states via a shared linear layer.
 - The core of the interaction module is a stack of **K novel Interactive Blocks**. Each block employs **global self-attention**, which is crucial for accommodating a variable number of motion conditions.
 - An Interactive Block first applies a self-attention module to the noised hidden state of the target person.
 - It then randomly masks some of the condition hidden states (to enable training for variable numbers of conditions and single-person fallback).
 - The masked condition hidden states are concatenated with the output of the first self-attention module and fed into a second global self-attention module. This allows the model to learn interactive information between the noised motion and the conditions.
 - The outputs of these interactive blocks are then linearly projected and **added to the outputs of corresponding denoiser blocks within the Generation Module**. The initial linear layers are zero-initialized, ensuring the interaction module doesn't interfere with the pre-trained generation module at the start of its training.

#### 3.3. Training Process

The training is divided into two stages:

- **Stage 1: Generation Module Training:**
 - The Generation Module is trained for single-person motion generation.
 - To obtain single-person descriptions from multi-person text, a **Large Language Model (LLM)** is employed to transform multi-person motion descriptions into corresponding descriptions for each individual.
 - The module learns to synthesize diverse and vivid single-person motions conditioned on these individual text prompts.

- **Stage 2: Interaction Module Training (Conditional Motion Modeling):**
 - The parameters of the Generation Module are **frozen**.
 - The Interaction Module is trained using multi-human motion data.
 - For a given set of N multi-person motions [x1, x2..., xN], their order and descriptions are randomized. One motion (e.g., x1) is selected as the target to be noised and reconstructed, while the remaining N-1 motions (x2..., xN) serve as clean motion conditions.
 - The noised x1 is fed into the Generation Module. The noised x1 and the N-1 condition motions are fed into the Interaction Module.
 - The Interaction Module's output, representing interactive information, is injected into the Generation Module to guide the denoising of x1.

#### 3.4. Motion Representation and Loss Functions

- **Motion Representation:** FreeMotion adopts the **non-canonical representation** proposed by InterGen, which preserves global joint positions and velocities, local rotations, and foot-ground contact features. This representation is crucial for maintaining accurate relative spatial relationships in multi-person scenarios.
- **Loss Functions:**
 - **Stage 1:** Total loss (L1) includes reconstruction loss (L_rec), foot contact loss (L_foot), joint velocity loss (L_vel), and bone length loss (L_bl).
 - **Stage 2:** Total loss (L2) includes all losses from Stage 1, plus a **masked joint distance map (DM) loss (L_dm)** to explicitly model interactions between individuals.

#### 3.5. Spatial Control

FreeMotion seamlessly integrates spatial control for multi-human motions by applying it to the underlying single-person generation process. It utilizes both explicit and implicit guidance:

- **Explicit Guidance:** A desired spatial location `s` (e.g., joint trajectory) is provided. Classifier guidance is used to perturb the predicted motion at each denoising step, correcting the bias between the predicted motion and the target spatial location using an L2 distance metric.
- **Implicit Guidance:** The spatial signal `s` is fed into an independent linear layer, and its output is added to the noised motion's hidden state. Random frames and joints are masked during training to enhance robustness.

By decoupling generation and interaction, and by allowing the recursive nature, FreeMotion provides a highly flexible and powerful framework for generating and controlling human motions for any number of individuals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the superior performance, versatility, and novel capabilities of the FreeMotion framework.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Comparison with Baselines:** FreeMotion was primarily compared against InterGen, the previous state-of-the-art for two-person T2M, and a modified version, InterGen*, which was retrained to allow for both single and two-person inference. Other single-person T2M models (TEMOS, T2M, MDM, ComMDM) were also included for context, though they lack multi-person capabilities.
- **Superiority in Two-Person Synthesis:** On the InterHuman dataset for two-person motion synthesis, FreeMotion significantly outperformed InterGen* and all other baselines across almost all metrics. For instance, FreeMotion achieved the best R Precision (Top 1: 0.326 vs. InterGen*'s 0.264), the lowest FID (6.740 vs. InterGen*'s 13.404), and competitive MM Dist, Diversity, and MModality scores. This indicates that FreeMotion generates more realistic, diverse, and text-consistent two-person motions.
- **Superiority in Single-Person Synthesis:** When evaluating single-person motion synthesis (using LLM-generated individual descriptions), FreeMotion again showed clear advantages over InterGen*. FreeMotion achieved better R Precision (Top 1: 0.264 vs. InterGen*'s 0.206) and a lower FID (12.975 vs. InterGen*'s 23.415), demonstrating its effectiveness in generating high-quality single-person movements.
- **Powerful Generality:** The ability of FreeMotion to outperform specialized models (InterGen* for two-person, and others for single-person) in their respective domains while simultaneously supporting both single and multi-person inference underscores its "powerful generality."

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed decoupled architecture and the importance of high-quality interactive descriptions.
- **Generation Module (GM*) vs. FreeMotion*:** When only single-person texts were used for training the interaction module (FreeMotion*), its performance was similar to a finetuned Generation Module (GM*). This suggested that while the architecture is sound, the *quality* of interaction data is paramount.
- **Impact of Interactive Descriptions (InterDes):** Introducing high-quality two-person interactive descriptions (InterDes) dramatically improved FreeMotion's performance, while a simple finetuning of GM with InterDes led to a performance drop. This highlights:
 - The crucial role of rich, interactive data for learning multi-person interactions.
 - The efficiency of FreeMotion's decoupled generation and interaction modules in leveraging all available information for training, preventing performance degradation when new, complex data types (like interactive descriptions) are introduced.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons provided strong visual evidence supporting the quantitative findings.
- **Single-Person Motion Synthesis:** FreeMotion generated single-person motions that were more consistent with textual descriptions. For example, it could produce an "obvious squatting pose" when instructed to "grab onto another person's waist," a detail missed by InterGen*.
- **Two-Person Motion Synthesis:** FreeMotion exhibited better interaction coordination and comprehensibility for complex text. It successfully generated synchronized "rock-paper-scissors" motions, accurately reflecting both players' actions, whereas InterGen* struggled with simultaneity and hand usage. For "fencing practice," FreeMotion showed more balanced and defensive actions from both individuals, unlike InterGen* which focused predominantly on kicking. The authors attribute this to the decoupled design, where the generation module handles complex text comprehension, and the interaction module ensures better synchronization.
- **Three-Person Motion Synthesis (Novel Contribution):** Crucially, FreeMotion demonstrated the ability to generate **three-person motions** with varying levels of interaction (low, middle, high) despite being trained exclusively on two-person motion data. This is a significant finding, showcasing the inductive bias of the recursive conditional generation paradigm and the scalability of the global self-attention-based interactive block. This ability to generalize to a higher number of individuals without direct training is a breakthrough.
- **Controllable Motion Synthesis:** The framework successfully integrated spatial control for multi-person motions (from two to four individuals). Despite the spatial control module being trained for single motions, it maintained effectiveness when generating multi-person scenarios, demonstrating that the interaction module did not "damage" the spatial control capabilities. This allows users to control global locations and trajectories for multiple interacting characters.

In summary, the results convincingly establish FreeMotion as a robust, universal, and scalable solution for text-to-motion synthesis, significantly advancing the state of the art by enabling number-free and spatially controllable multi-person motion generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FreeMotion represents a significant stride forward in the field of human motion synthesis, offering a universal and scalable solution that addresses long-standing limitations. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking the "Number Barrier" in T2M:** The most impactful contribution is the achievement of **number-free motion synthesis**. Prior methods were constrained to generating motions for a fixed number of individuals (typically one or two). FreeMotion unifies these tasks, allowing a single model to generate motions for single, two, three, or even more individuals based on text. This eliminates the need for specialized models for different group sizes, drastically improving the flexibility and practicality of T2M systems.

2. **Scalability to Arbitrary Number of Individuals:** By rethinking multi-person motion generation as a recursive conditional process and designing an interaction module with global self-attention, FreeMotion demonstrates the ability to scale to groups larger than those seen during training (e.g., generating three- and four-person motions after training only on two-person data). This is a crucial breakthrough, as the scarcity of multi-person datasets for larger groups has been a major bottleneck. The framework provides a viable path towards generating complex crowd or group dynamics from natural language.

3. **Enhanced Controllability for Multi-person Scenarios:** The seamless integration of spatial control for multiple individuals is highly valuable. While spatial control existed for single-person motions, applying it to interacting groups without compromising realism or coherence was challenging. FreeMotion enables precise control over the global location and trajectory of individual participants within a multi-person scene, which is essential for directing complex animated sequences or robotic interactions.

4. **Decoupled and Efficient Architecture:** The proposed decoupled Generation and Interaction Modules offer an elegant and efficient way to model conditional motion. This architecture allows for better comprehension of complex textual descriptions (via the generation module) and more accurate synchronization and interaction modeling (via the interaction module), leading to higher fidelity and more consistent results. The two-stage training strategy also optimizes the learning process by first establishing robust single-person generation before fine-tuning for interactions.

5. **Broad Applications:** The capabilities introduced by FreeMotion have profound implications across various industries:
 - **Animation and Film Production:** Animators can rapidly prototype complex multi-character scenes from text, significantly reducing manual effort and production time.
 - **Video Game Development:** Developers can generate diverse and dynamic crowd behaviors, character interactions, and NPC movements more easily, enhancing immersion and gameplay.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Creation of more realistic and interactive virtual environments with multiple autonomous agents.
 - **Robotics:** Generating natural and human-like interaction trajectories for collaborative robots or humanoids operating in multi-agent environments.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** Developing more intuitive interfaces where users can instruct multiple virtual characters through natural language.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** FreeMotion opens up new avenues for research, including:
 - Exploring more sophisticated mechanisms for decomposing multi-person text descriptions into individual actions.
 - Investigating more complex and nuanced interaction modeling beyond what can be learned from two-person data, perhaps through symbolic reasoning or more advanced LLM integration.
 - Scaling to even larger groups or crowds, potentially incorporating scene context or environmental constraints.
 - Combining number-free motion with other modalities like audio or music for more expressive and responsive group performances.

Despite acknowledging limitations such as potential text-movement mismatches from LLM-generated descriptions and interpenetration issues with very complex multi-person prompts due to limited training data, FreeMotion provides a robust foundational framework. Its innovative approach fundamentally changes how we can conceptualize and execute text-to-motion synthesis for dynamic multi-agent scenarios, promising to accelerate advancements in intelligent content creation and human-centric AI.
