# EvoLM: In Search of Lost Language Model Training Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic analysis of multi-stage large language model training dynamics investigates how design choices across pre-training, continued pre-training, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning impact model capabilities, providing a transparent framework and introducing outcome reward model scores as a reliable proxy for generative task evaluation.

## Problem

- A lack of systematic, transparent, and reproducible understanding of the intricate scaling behaviors and design trade-offs in the multi-stage training lifecycle of large language models (LLMs).
- Existing scaling laws primarily focus on the pre-training phase, neglecting the complex interactions and effects of subsequent continued pre-training, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning.
- Difficulty in reliably evaluating downstream generative reasoning tasks post-training, as traditional language modeling metrics like perplexity become less indicative.

## Method

- Developed **EvoLM**, a transparent experimental framework and ==model suite==, by training over 100 LLaMA-2 based models (0.5B, 1B, 4B parameters) from scratch with full control over hyperparameters and data at each stage.
- Systematically investigated the impact of computational budgets and model sizes across four sequential training stages: pre-training, continued pre-training (CPT), supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL).
- Implemented a comprehensive evaluation protocol for upstream cloze tasks and downstream generative reasoning tasks (both in-domain and out-of-domain), utilizing an outcome reward model (ORM) score as a new proxy metric.

## Results

- Pre-training beyond **80x**-**160x** model size for the studied configurations shows diminishing returns, and in some cases, degradation in downstream task performance.
- A **5%** replay budget of general-domain data during continued pre-training effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, preserving both upstream and downstream performance.
- Outcome Reward Model (ORM) scores exhibit strong predictive power for downstream task accuracy (Pearson correlation **0.62**-**0.84**), serving as a more reliable unsupervised validation metric than perplexity during post-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Excessive general-domain pre-training and supervised fine-tuning lead to diminishing returns and can degrade performance on certain downstream tasks, suggesting saturation points.
> - Continued pre-training on domain-specific data induces catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge, which is effectively mitigated by small replay budgets (e.g., 5%) of pre-training data.
> - Reinforcement learning primarily increases the probability of sampling high-quality rollouts by sharpening confidence in already-correct outputs, rather than fundamentally enhancing reasoning capabilities.
