# Beyond Memory: Harnessing Long-Horizon Agents with Explicit Belief States

> [!summary] Summary
> PoS (Progression of States) is an inference-time framework that gives an LLM agent an explicit, task-conditioned belief state to act from, instead of a stored or compressed interaction history. The belief holds an Entity-State-Relation world model plus epistemic and achievement gaps. A Belief Sentinel validates every update, and a health score detects Belief Trapping and triggers tailored recovery. PoS ranks first on four benchmarks with three backbones.

## Problem

- Memory-based context methods such as raw trajectories, compression and hierarchical working memory keep a transformed record of past evidence, not a coherent, checkable estimate of the current world.
- Prior explicit-belief agents let the LLM write its own belief updates unchecked, so conflicting states or unsupported inferences persist and corrupt later decisions.
- Existing handling of unproductive interaction, such as truncating trajectories during RL or exhaustion gates, stops stalled behavior rather than restoring task progress within the ongoing episode.

## Method

- ==Task-conditioned belief==: a world state of Entities, States and Relations with provenance and confidence, plus the goal, epistemic gaps and achievement gaps, with an active gap focusing each action.
- **Belief Sentinel**: a validator that audits each candidate belief update for internal conflicts and contradictions with observed evidence, returns issues for evidence-based revision, and labels step-level progress.
- **Trapping-Aware Recovery**: a health score built from gap persistence, progress stagnation and belief recurrence detects trapping, diagnoses a Static, Cycle or Drift pattern plus the blocked gap type, then composes recovery constraints.

## Results

- PoS beats the strongest baseline on all four benchmarks under all three backbones, for example ALFWorld **88.81%** vs **72.39%** and RCA-100 joint accuracy **38.83%** vs **28.16%** with Qwen3.7-Plus.
- Removing consistency validation costs up to **14.93 points** on ALFWorld and **11.81** on LOCA-Bench, while removing trapping diagnosis and recovery costs **4.85-6.79 points** on RCA-100.
- On LOCA-Bench PoS holds stable from 96K to 256K context, leading the strongest baseline by **10.67-16.00 points** at 256K, but total tokens rise **5.06x** on RCA-100.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - With strong long-context models, compressing or reorganizing history does not reliably beat raw trajectories, because the information it discards can cancel the benefit of removing redundancy.
> - Tracking what remains unknown separately from what remains to be achieved lets a failure diagnosis choose a targeted recovery, which beats simply prompting the agent to recover.
> - Spending inference compute on keeping a verified estimate of the current state can pay off more than minimizing tokens, though the overhead is large and still needs reducing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

In the ALFWorld task "Put a hot mug in the cabinet," the agent has to satisfy two requirements: a temperature and a location. In one illustrated run, an agent working from its Raw Trajectory, the transcript of its interaction history, misses the heating requirement and repeatedly examines the cabinet. It fails after 50 steps. The paper's method, PoS, keeps track of what is true now and what still needs doing, rather than leaving the agent to reconstruct that from a transcript. PoS tracks "make the mug hot" as an active requirement, recovers, and completes the task in 17 steps. This is one illustrative pair of runs. It does not show that recovery alone caused the difference.

The underlying problem is that an agent's actions change the world. Meanwhile, a growing interaction history mixes evidence about past states with judgments that may no longer hold.

### 2. A belief that can guide the next step

PoS maintains a belief: the agent's explicit, task-conditioned account of the current world and unresolved requirements. It is neither a transcript nor a claim of perfect knowledge. Its world model records task-relevant Entities, their States, and Relations. It also records two kinds of gaps:

- **Epistemic gaps** are what the agent still needs to learn.
- **Achievement gaps** are what it still needs to do.

For the mug, "make the mug hot" is an achievement gap. The Task Agent selects an active gap while retaining the broader task context.

The belief is updated in a loop:

1. After an action and observation, the agent proposes a belief update.
1. The Belief Sentinel checks for conflicts within the belief and for claims the interaction evidence does not support.
1. The agent revises before the update becomes the next decision context.

Validation and progress assessment are separate jobs, because a belief can be internally consistent but still stalled.

Method overview, not a measured result. Evidence leads to belief modeling. The task agent reads the belief and active gap to choose an action. The Sentinel validates a candidate transition and records progress, and a health estimate can trigger diagnosis and recovery.

### 3. When activity stops advancing the task

PoS calls continued action without meaningful progress toward the goal **Belief Trapping**. Over a recent window, it checks three things:

- whether requirements persist,
- whether transitions are being labeled as non-progress, and
- whether the world state relevant to the active gap recurs.

Gap persistence alone is not enough to declare a trap. The signal combines persistent gaps with stagnation or recurrence.

Once a trap is detected, PoS classifies two things:

- the pattern, as Static, Cycle, or Drift;
- the blocked requirement, as epistemic or achievement.

The resulting constraints target both dimensions. For example, they may tell the agent to stop repeating an ineffective transition and seek discriminating evidence, or to cause a goal-relevant state change. The constraints guide the Task Agent. They do not prescribe the tool call or supply the answer.

The direct test of this mechanism compares factorized recovery against a generic recovery prompt, using Qwen3.7-Plus. The numbers below are final task performance, not recovery success rates:

### 4. Benchmark results and cost

On ALFWorld, Qwen3.7-Plus with PoS achieved 88.81% overall task success. The strongest evaluated baseline for that backbone, LongHorizon-Harness, reached 72.39%. Overall success is the share of the 134 valid-unseen tasks on which the environment confirms goal completion. All methods used the same benchmark interface and action budget.

PoS had the highest reported overall metric in all 12 benchmark–backbone settings. These results cover four named benchmarks and three evaluated backbones. They are not a general guarantee for all agents or tasks.

PoS also adds inference-time work. With Qwen3.7-Plus on RCA-100, the numbers per episode compare as follows:

- **Joint accuracy** rises from 24.27% with Raw Trajectory to 38.83% with PoS.
- **Task Agent tokens** fall from 355.70K to 281.20K.
- **Total tokens** rise from 355.70K to 1,800.13K, because the added belief and Sentinel calls make the total higher.

This is a token tradeoff. It is not a claim of lower elapsed time or computation.

### 5. State, then progress

A record of actions shows what happened. A maintained, checked account of current conditions and unresolved requirements can guide what to do next. It can also reveal when the current course is no longer advancing the task.
