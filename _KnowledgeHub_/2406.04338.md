# Physics3D: Learning Physical Properties of 3D Gaussians via Video Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Physics3D introduces a framework to infer diverse physical properties for 3D Gaussian-represented objects by distilling priors from video diffusion models, enabling more realistic physics-aligned simulations. It achieves higher video quality (PSNR 14.72, SSIM 0.59) and user-preferred motion realism compared to previous methods, particularly by incorporating viscoelastic material modeling.

## Problem

- Existing 3D generative models primarily focus on surface features, neglecting the inherent physical properties crucial for realistic interactive dynamics.
- Traditional physics simulation struggles with complex 3D object generation and issues like large deformations, while prior learning-based methods were limited to specific material types or required manual parameter tuning.
- Accurately predicting the diverse and intricate physical attributes of real-world objects from visual data for plausible simulation remains a complex challenge.

## Method

- Proposes a unified simulation-rendering pipeline based on ==3D Gaussians==, extending the physical parameter space to incorporate viscoelastic properties.
- Utilizes an MLS-==MPM== simulator with a custom viscoelastic material model, which decomposes the deformation gradient into elastoplastic and viscoelastic components for enhanced realism.
- Learns optimal physical parameters by distilling realistic motion priors from a pre-trained ==video diffusion model== using ==Score Distillation Sampling== (==SDS==).
- Augments internal object volumes with additional Gaussians to improve simulation realism for solid objects.

## Results

- Physics3D generated realistic movements for objects with complex textures, demonstrating plausible deformation with a balance of oscillation frequencies and accurate damping effects.
- It achieved higher average video quality scores across metrics like PSNR (**14.72**), SSIM (**0.59**), and MS-SSIM (**0.49**), outperforming previous physics-based 3D simulation methods.
- User studies indicated a strong preference for Physics3D, with higher scores in action coherence (**8.95**), motion realism (**8.57**), and overall quality compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both viscoelastic and elastoplastic modules for diverse material dynamics and the internal particle filling strategy for maintaining solid object integrity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Viscoelastic material modeling, incorporating both elastic and viscous behaviors, is crucial for capturing the complex, time-dependent deformations and damping observed in real-world objects.
> - Leveraging video diffusion models through Score Distillation Sampling provides an effective way to automatically infer plausible physical parameters and dynamics from visual data.
> - Decomposing the deformation gradient into elastoplastic and viscoelastic components within an MLS-MPM simulator enables a robust and generalized simulation of diverse material types.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D computer vision has witnessed advancements in reconstructing and generating static 3D assets, with increasing interest in extending these capabilities to 4D dynamics. Contemporary 3D generative models commonly emphasize surface features such as color and shape. However, this focus presents limitations in modeling realistic interactive dynamics, which are critical for applications like virtual/augmented reality and animation. Accurate simulation of object behavior in these environments necessitates an understanding and incorporation of inherent physical properties.

Traditional physics simulation methods, which often rely on continuous physical dynamic equations and body-fixed meshes, face challenges in generating complex 3D objects and are prone to issues related to nonlinearity, large deformations, or fractures. Recent developments in implicit and explicit 3D representation techniques, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, have prompted efforts to integrate rendering with simulation.

Previous works like PhysGaussian attempted to incorporate physics-based attributes (e.g., velocity, strain, stress) into 3D Gaussian kernels for unified simulation and rendering. A limitation of PhysGaussian was the requirement for manual pre-design of physical parameters, a process that can be laborious and imprecise. Building upon this, PhysDreamer aimed to automate parameter estimation by learning object dynamics from video generation models to infer a single physical material parameter, specifically Young's modulus. However, this approach primarily addressed hyper-elastic materials and encountered difficulties with the complex, composite nature of real-world objects, such as plastics, metals, or non-Newtonian fluids, which exhibit behaviors beyond a single elastic parameter.

Physics3D positions itself to address these limitations by proposing a more generalized approach. It aims to learn a diverse set of physical properties for 3D objects, moving beyond single elastic parameters to include viscoelastic characteristics. By distilling physical priors from video diffusion models and employing a more comprehensive material model, Physics3D seeks to enhance the fidelity and generalizability of physics-aligned dynamics simulation for a broader spectrum of real-world materials, thereby bridging the gap between simulated and real-world object behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while 3D generation models have advanced rapidly, they largely neglect the inherent physical properties that govern object behavior in reality. This omission restricts their applicability in scenarios requiring realistic interactive dynamics, such as virtual reality, augmented reality, and animation. To achieve accurate and plausible physics-aligned dynamics, it is essential to predict and integrate the material properties that dictate how objects respond to forces and deformations. However, precisely predicting the diverse materials of real-world objects is a complex task due given the intricate nature of their physical attributes.

Physics3D was developed with the following key objectives:
- **Develop a novel method for learning diverse physical properties**: The core objective is to move beyond simple surface characteristics and enable models to infer intrinsic physical attributes of 3D objects.
- **Design a highly generalizable physical simulation system**: The aim is to create a system capable of simulating a broad range of materials with high fidelity, contrasting with previous methods that were limited to specific material types.
- **Incorporate viscoelastic material modeling**: A central goal is to extend the physical parameter space beyond elasticity to include viscosity. This allows the simulation to capture complex material behaviors such as damping and time-dependent deformation, which are prevalent in many real-world objects like plastics, foams, or biological tissues. This expands on prior work that primarily focused on elastic properties.
- **Distill physical priors from video diffusion models**: The research seeks to leverage the understanding of realistic object materials embedded within large-scale video diffusion models. By distilling these priors, the system aims to automatically infer plausible physical parameters without manual specification.
- **Demonstrate efficacy with both elastic and plastic materials**: A practical objective is to validate the method's performance across various material types, showcasing its ability to generate realistic dynamics for both purely elastic objects and those exhibiting more complex, inelastic behaviors.
- **Bridge the gap between physical reality and virtual neural space**: Ultimately, the work aims to provide a framework for better integrating realistic physical principles into virtual environments, enhancing the authenticity and interactivity of generated 3D content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Physics3D proposes a unified simulation-rendering pipeline built upon 3D Gaussians, designed to learn physics dynamics from a video diffusion model. The methodology involves several key steps:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Physical Parameter Expansion

The input to the system is a static representation of an object using 3D Gaussians. The objective is to estimate the physical attributes of each Gaussian particle and generate physics-plausible motions. While previous methods often focused on parameters like mass (m), Young’s modulus (E), and Poisson’s ratio (ν) for elastic objects, Physics3D extends this by incorporating additional parameters essential for viscoelasticity. These include viscosity coefficients (ν_v and ν_d) and Lamé coefficients (λ and μ). The mass of each particle (m_p) is pre-calculated from a constant density (ρ) and particle volume (V_p), and Poisson’s ratio is assumed to be constant across the object. The Lamé parameters (λ, μ) control elastic deformation, while the viscosity coefficients (ν_v, ν_d) govern viscous resistance to rapid deformation, enhancing the model’s capacity to capture viscoelastic behaviors. For simplicity, ν_v and ν_d are taken as equal in this work.

#### 3.2. Viscoelastic Material Point Method (MPM) Simulation

Physics3D employs an MLS-MPM simulator, which discretizes the material into deformable particles and uses a background grid for force computations. The simulation process for a single sub-step (S) updates particle position, velocity, deformation gradient, and affine momentum based on current states, physical properties (θ), and time step (Δt).
The deformation gradient (F), which measures local rotation and strain, is decomposed into two parallel components: an elastoplastic part (F_E F_P) and a viscoelastic part (F_N F_V). For convenience, F_P is set to the identity matrix (I). The total internal stress (σ) is the sum of stresses from these two components: σ = σ_E + σ_N.

- **Model for Elastoplastic Part**: This component is responsible for pure elastic deformation and potential plasticity (though simplified to pure elastic in this work). The internal stress σ_E is computed from F_E. The fixed corotated constitutive model is chosen, with an energy function ψ(F_E) defined as μ_E Σ_i (σ_{E,i} - 1)^2 + (λ_E/2)(det(F_E) - 1)^2, where σ_{E,i} are the singular values of F_E. The Cauchy stress tensor σ_E is derived from this energy function. The deformation gradient F_E is updated via F^{n+1}_E = (I + Δt∇v^n)F^n, where ∇v^n is the velocity gradient.

- **Model for Viscoelastic Part**: This component captures both elastic and viscous behaviors. The total strain F is given by F = F_N F_V. Only F_N contributes to the internal stress σ_N (or Kirchhoff tensor τ_N = det(F_N)σ_N), while F_V affects the update rule for F_N, acting as a dissipator for strain. The Kirchhoff tensor τ_N relates to the log principle Kirchhoff tensor ϵ_N (logarithm of singular values of F_N) via τ_N = 2μ_N ϵ_N + λ_N tr(ϵ_N)I. The update of F_N follows a trial-and-correction procedure: an initial trial strain F^{n}_{N,tr} = (I + Δt∇v^n)F^n_N is computed, then corrected based on a dissipation potential ψ_V (τ_N) = (1/(2ν_d))|dev(τ_N)|^2 + (1/(9ν_v))(τ_N · 1)^2. This results in an update rule for ϵ_N: ϵ^{n+1}_N = A(ϵ^{n}_{N,tr} - Btr(ϵ^{n}_{N,tr}) · 1), where A and B are learned parameters implicitly related to viscosity.

- **MPM Loop**: The simulation proceeds through a Particle-to-Grid (P2G) transfer, where particle mass and momentum are mapped to grid nodes. Grid velocities are then updated using the total internal stress (σ_E + σ_N), external forces, and gravity. Finally, a Grid-to-Particle (G2P) transfer projects updated grid velocities back to the particles, updating their positions and states.

#### 3.3. Optimization with Video Diffusion Model

After iterating the MPM simulation, a sequence of time-evolving 3D Gaussians is obtained. These Gaussians are rendered from a fixed camera viewpoint to produce a sequence of reference images (I_rt). To optimize the physical parameters (θ), Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) is employed, leveraging a pretrained video diffusion model (Stable Video Diffusion).

- **SDS Loss**: The SDS loss gradient is computed as ∇_θ L_SDS = E_{t,p,ϵ} [ w(t)(ϵ_ϕ(I^p_t; t, I^r_t, Δp, y) - ϵ) ∂I^p_t/∂θ ]. Here, w(t) is a time-dependent weighting function, ϵ_ϕ(·) is the predicted noise from the 2D diffusion prior, Δp represents the relative camera pose change, and y denotes the conditioning (e.g., image or text). This loss guides the optimization of physical parameters by aligning rendered dynamic sequences with the realistic motion priors learned by the video diffusion model.

- **Internal Particle Filling**: To enhance simulation realism, particularly for solid objects, an optional partial filling strategy is implemented. Internal volumes of selected objects are augmented with additional Gaussians. These "filled" Gaussians are optimized concurrently with the SDS process using an L2 rendering loss: L_Fill = (1/T) Σ_{t=1}^T λ||I^p_t - I^r_t||^2_2, which aims to ensure the rendered images of the filled object match the reference. The two loss components (SDS and L_Fill) are optimized separately.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The process begins with reconstructing 3D Gaussians from multi-view images. For complex scenes, a segmentation step isolates foreground objects. Internal particle filling is then performed. Each Gaussian kernel is associated with a distinct set of physical properties for optimization. The foreground region is discretized into a 50^3 grid. The MPM simulation employs 400 sub-steps within each temporal interval between video frames, corresponding to a sub-step duration of 1e-4 seconds. Optimization for each object typically requires approximately 5 minutes on an NVIDIA A6000 (48GB) GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents both qualitative and quantitative assessments to evaluate Physics3D’s performance against existing methods and through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Realistic Motion Generation**: Visualizations demonstrate Physics3D’s capability to simulate the movement of objects with complex textures, resulting in physically plausible outcomes. Examples include the deformation of a carnation, alocasia plant, telephone cord, and beanie hat under external forces. Additional results in Appendix A.1 show successful simulation for materials like rubber, fluffy bread, and fabric.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: Comparisons using space-time slices reveal differences in simulated motion characteristics:
 - **PhysDreamer**: This method, focused on elastic properties, generated periodic oscillations with subtle damping, which the authors indicate as less realistic than Physics3D's output.
 - **PhysGaussian**: Exhibited unrealistically low-frequency movements, attributed to inaccurate parameter settings.
 - **DreamGaussian4D**: Lacked explicit physical priors and produced motion described as unrealistically constant and low-magnitude periodic.
 - **Physics3D**: Demonstrated a balance of high- and low-frequency oscillations, along with more realistic damping effects, aligning more closely with observed real-world object behaviors.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

Quantitative evaluation was conducted using video-quality metrics on rendered videos from a fixed perspective.
- **Performance Metrics**: Physics3D achieved higher average scores across all reported metrics compared to PhysDreamer and PhysGaussian:
 - PSNR: 14.72 (Physics3D) vs. 13.89 (PhysDreamer), 13.86 (PhysGaussian)
 - SSIM: 0.59 (Physics3D) vs. 0.55 (PhysDreamer), 0.57 (PhysGaussian)
 - MS-SSIM: 0.49 (Physics3D) vs. 0.37 (PhysDreamer), 0.39 (PhysGaussian)
 - VMAF: 0.59 (Physics3D) vs. 0.52 (PhysDreamer), 0.59 (PhysGaussian)
 These higher scores indicate improved video quality and motion consistency in Physics3D's generated dynamics.

#### 4.3. User Study

A user study involving 30 volunteers assessing 20 rounds of video samples (from Physics3D, PhysDreamer, PhysGaussian, DreamGaussian4D, and real-captured videos) further supported the qualitative and quantitative findings. Volunteers selected preferred videos based on quality, realisticity, alignment with the input 3D object, and fluency.
- **User Preference**: Physics3D was preferred by users and received higher scores in all evaluated aspects:
 - Action Coherence: 8.95 (Physics3D) vs. 8.76 (PhysDreamer), 7.82 (PhysGaussian)
 - Motion Realism: 8.57 (Physics3D) vs. 7.73 (PhysDreamer), 6.89 (PhysGaussian)
 - Overall Quality: 9.05 (Physics3D) vs. 7.89 (PhysDreamer), 6.93 (PhysGaussian)

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies investigated the contribution of Physics3D’s core components:
- **Without Viscoelastic Part**: Removing this component resulted in a deficiency in sustained damping and rebound effects. In scenarios with minimal external disturbances, energy dissipation occurred rapidly, leading to less realistic damping behavior.
- **Without Elastoplastic Part**: The absence of this component led to a lack of elasticity. Objects exhibited a greater susceptibility to shape deformation and displayed more fluid-like behaviors rather than solid-body elasticity.
- These findings underscore the importance of both elastoplastic and viscoelastic modules for capturing the diverse dynamics of physical objects accurately.
- **Without Internal Filling**: When internal volumes were not filled (e.g., in a cake object), the hollow parts were prone to collapse during simulation.
- **With Internal Filling**: The internal filling strategy contributed to more realistic and smooth object deformation under external forces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Physics3D represents an advancement in the integration of physical principles into 3D generative models. By addressing the limitations of prior work that primarily focused on surface features or simplified physical models, this research contributes to the development of more realistic and interactive virtual environments.

The primary significance lies in its ability to learn and simulate various physical properties, encompassing both elasticity and viscosity, for a broad range of materials. This generalization capability, achieved through a viscoelastic Material Point Method and the distillation of physical priors from video diffusion models, enables the system to generate dynamic behaviors that more closely approximate real-world object interactions. The observed balance of oscillation frequencies and realistic damping in simulated motions suggests a higher fidelity compared to existing methods.

The potential impact of Physics3D spans several application domains:
- **Realistic Simulations**: The enhanced ability to model diverse material properties can lead to more accurate and believable simulations across various fields, including engineering, product design, and scientific research.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality**: By enabling virtual objects to respond with plausible physical dynamics, Physics3D could significantly improve immersion and interactivity in VR/AR experiences, making virtual environments feel more tangible.
- **Animation and Content Creation**: Animators and content creators could leverage this framework to generate more natural and diverse object movements and deformations without extensive manual rigging or parameter tuning, streamlining production workflows.
- **Robotics and Material Science**: A deeper understanding of material behavior in a differentiable simulation framework could inform the design of robotic interactions with objects and potentially aid in predicting the response of novel materials.

The paper also acknowledges limitations, such as the current requirement for manual intervention in defining the scope of movable objects and filling ranges in complex, entangled environments. Addressing these limitations through automated segmentation and more comprehensive physics system modeling is proposed as future work, which could further broaden the applicability and efficiency of Physics3D. The work lays a foundation for future research that aims to bridge the physical world with neural representations for a wider range of interactive virtual experiences.
