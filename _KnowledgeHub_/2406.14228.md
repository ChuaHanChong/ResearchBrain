# EvoAgent: Towards Automatic Multi-Agent Generation via Evolutionary Algorithms

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoAgent extends a single specialized LLM agent into a multi-agent system by treating agent settings as an evolving population. LLM-simulated crossover, mutation, quality-check selection, and result integration create diverse child agents without human design. It works with any agent framework and improves reasoning, multimodal, interactive, and planning tasks.

## Problem

- Most LLM agent and multi-agent frameworks rely on handcrafted roles, task scopes, skills, and prompts, so scaling the number of agents demands expensive human labor.
- Automatic agent generators such as AgentVerse and AutoAgents still impose fixed human-designed pipelines, like planner-observer or recruit-decide-act-evaluate, which restrict task scope and generality.
- No generic paradigm existed to automatically turn an arbitrary existing specialized agent framework into a multi-agent system, and prompting strategies like Self-Refine or SPP falter on complex planning or weaker models.

## Method

- Initialization treats any existing agent framework as the parent individual and designates its settings (role, skills, prompts) as ==evolvable variables==, making the approach framework-agnostic and applicable to MetaGPT or AutoGen.
- ==Crossover== has an LLM inspect parent results and update the skills needing improvement, while ==Mutation== makes children distinct from parents yet capable, yielding N' candidate agents per iteration.
- A ==Selection== stage uses an ==LLM quality-check== to keep N inherited-but-distinct children and discard duplicates. ==Results Update== then integrates child outputs with the prior result over T iterations.

## Results

- On Logic Grid, Trivia Writing and Codenames, EvoAgent (1,3) with GPT-4 scores **77.0**, **84.4** and **84.5**, beating AgentVerse and AutoAgents. With GPT-3.5 it reaches **71.5**, **60.8** and **79.4**.
- On TravelPlanner, GPT-4 final pass rate rises from a direct baseline of 2.2 to **7.2** using EvoAgent, with micro commonsense pass **31.4** versus 27.5. It also improves ScienceWorld: GPT-4 27.97 to **30.42**.
- Ablations show removing the quality-check module lowers scores at population size above one (commonsense **68.9** down to about **62.7** for N=3). EvoAgent also beats Overgen, Suggest, and PromptRefine variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolutionary operators simulated by an LLM over natural-language agent settings give a non-parametric, framework-agnostic way to scale up agent diversity without extra human design.
> - Diversity control matters: without a quality check, larger populations spawn near-duplicate agents and degrade output quality, so selection is essential rather than optional.
> - Adding agents can bias the initial agent toward user preferences over commonsense rules, and gains shrink on long trajectories and cost more tokens, so multi-agent scaling has real limits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) have ignited a new frontier in artificial intelligence: the development of autonomous agents capable of solving complex tasks. Initially, research focused on single LLM-based agents, endowing them with skills like planning, tool use, and memory to enhance their capabilities. However, many real-world problems are inherently complex and often exceed the capacity of a single agent. This limitation has spurred a growing interest in **multi-agent systems**, where multiple specialized LLM-based agents collaborate to tackle challenging missions, akin to human teams.

Existing multi-agent frameworks, such as MetaGPT, Generative Agents, AutoGen, and CAMEL, have demonstrated impressive performance by simulating human-like behaviors and orchestrating specialized roles. These systems rely on *human-designed* elements, including predefined character roles, task scopes, specific skills, and meticulously crafted prompt settings. While human expertise is undeniably valuable in guiding LLMs, this reliance on manual design presents significant limitations:
1. **Limited Functional Scope:** Handcrafted designs inherently restrict the types and diversity of agents that can be created.
2. **Scalability Issues:** Manually designing and maintaining numerous agents becomes prohibitively expensive and time-consuming as the complexity and scale of tasks increase.
3. **Lack of Adaptability:** The rigidity of human-designed frameworks limits their flexibility in handling unexpected challenges or novel scenarios.

This paper directly addresses these critical limitations. It identifies a significant gap in the current landscape: the absence of a generic paradigm for *automatically* generating diverse and effective multi-agent systems from existing specialized agents. Previous attempts at automated agent generation, like AgentVerse and AutoAgents, still embed strong human-designed architectural constraints, limiting their true generality and functional scope.

EVOAGENT enters this landscape by proposing a novel, generic, and automated approach to agent generation, moving beyond the handcrafted limitations. By formulating agent generation as an evolutionary process, it seeks to democratize the creation of multi-agent systems, making them more adaptable, scalable, and powerful for a wider array of complex, open-world, and real-world planning tasks. This work, therefore, represents a significant step towards truly autonomous and self-organizing LLM-based agent communities, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve collectively.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **EVOAGENT**, a novel and generic method for **automatically extending specialized LLM-based agents into multi-agent systems** with the aim of significantly improving their task-solving capabilities.

The core motivations driving this work stem directly from the identified limitations of current LLM-based agent frameworks:

- **Overcoming Human Design Dependence:** The authors observe that existing multi-agent systems are "heavily dependent on human-designed frameworks," which "greatly limits the functional scope and scalability of agent systems." This manual effort is expensive and limits the ability to scale up the number and diversity of agents. EVOAGENT seeks to alleviate this bottleneck by automating the generation process.
- **Enhancing Task-Solving Capability:** The ultimate goal of building multi-agent systems is to tackle complex tasks that a single agent cannot. By automatically generating a diverse population of specialized agents, EVOAGENT aims to foster emergent abilities and synergize expertise, leading to superior performance in challenging scenarios.
- **Achieving Generality and Adaptability:** Current automated agent generation methods often impose specific architectural requirements or task-specific pipelines, limiting their broad applicability. A key motivation for EVOAGENT is to provide a "generic method" that can be "generalized to any LLM-based agent framework" and "applied to any agent framework" without extra human effort or preconditions. This universality is crucial for wider adoption and impact.
- **Mimicking Natural Evolution:** Inspired by the efficiency and adaptability of natural evolution in human society, the authors are motivated to simulate this process to automatically create a diverse "population" of agents. This biological analogy underpins the entire methodological design of EVOAGENT.

In essence, EVOAGENT aims to shift the paradigm from human-centric, bespoke multi-agent system design to an automated, evolutionary-inspired generation process, thereby unlocking greater scalability, diversity, and effectiveness for LLM-based agents in increasingly complex real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EVOAGENT formulates the problem of multi-agent generation as an **evolutionary process**, drawing inspiration from biological evolution, particularly evolutionary algorithms (EAs). The core idea is to treat existing specialized LLM agents as "individuals" whose "settings" (such as roles, skills, and prompts) can "evolve" across generations to create a diverse and effective "population" of agents. The entire process is designed to be largely automatic, leveraging LLMs themselves to simulate the evolutionary operators.

The methodology consists of a four-stage pipeline:

1. **STEP 1: Initialization**
 - **Starting Point:** EVOAGENT begins with a pre-defined LLM-based agent framework (e.g., MetaGPT, AutoGen). This initial agent serves as the "initial individual" or "parent" from which the evolutionary process commences.
 - **Evolvable Variables:** The system settings of this initial agent – including its assigned role, specialized skills, and prompt configurations – are identified as the variables subject to evolution.
 - **Generality:** A key aspect of this step is that EVOAGENT does not impose any structural prerequisites on the initial agent framework, making it broadly applicable to various existing agent designs.

2. **STEP 2: Crossover & Mutation**
 - **Evolutionary Operators:** This stage implements two primary evolutionary operators: Crossover and Mutation, both facilitated by LLMs.
 - **Crossover:** Parent agents first generate results for a given user query. An LLM then analyzes these results to identify areas for improvement in the agents' skills. Based on this feedback, the LLM "updates" the settings of the parent agents, generating "child agents" with refined skill sets. This mimics the recombination of genetic material to produce potentially superior offspring.
 - **Mutation:** To ensure diversity and prevent convergence to local optima, a Mutation operation is applied. An LLM compares the newly generated child agents with their parents and intentionally modifies the child agents' settings. The goal is to introduce new variations or "characteristics" while preserving the agents' core task-solving capabilities, ensuring that the generated agents are distinct.
 - **Iterative Process:** These operations are repeated across successive iterations, with agents generated in previous generations potentially contributing to the next.

3. **STEP 3: Selection**
 - **Quality Control:** After Crossover and Mutation generate multiple candidate agents, a selection mechanism, similar to "survival of the fittest," is applied.
 - **LLM-based Quality Check:** An LLM acts as a "quality-check module." It evaluates whether the newly generated agents (from the mutation step) meet specific criteria, such as being distinct from previous agents and maintaining their task-solving capability.
 - **Population Control:** From the larger pool of candidates, a fixed number (N) of high-quality agents are selected to form the evolved population for the current iteration. This ensures that only effective and diverse agents proceed.

4. **STEP 4: Results Update**
 - **Integration and Refinement:** The selected, evolved agents are tasked with generating their own candidate results for the user query.
 - **Natural Selection Analogy:** An LLM then integrates these new candidate results with the best result from the previous iteration. This process is likened to "natural selection," where the most optimal elements from the diverse contributions are combined to produce a superior, consolidated final result.
 - **Iterative Refinement:** Steps 2 to 4 are iterated until a predefined target number of agents or iterations is reached, continuously refining the agent population and the task solution.

By leveraging the generative and reasoning capabilities of LLMs for each evolutionary operation, EVOAGENT provides a unique and flexible framework for automatically creating and optimizing multi-agent systems, without manual intervention in agent design or role assignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and generality of EVOAGENT were thoroughly evaluated across a diverse set of tasks, spanning traditional NLP, multi-modal reasoning, interactive scientific problem-solving, and real-world complex planning.

#### 4.1. NLP and Multi-Modal Tasks

- **Benchmarks:** Logic Grid Puzzle (reasoning), Trivia Creative Writing (knowledge-intensive), Codenames Collaborative (reasoning), and MMMU (Multi-discipline Multi-modal Understanding and Reasoning).
- **Models:** Llama2-13B, GPT-3.5, GPT-4 (for NLP); GPT-4V, Gemini-Pro (for MMMU).
- **Baselines:** Direct (0-shot), Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Self-Refine, Solo Performance Prompting (SPP), AgentVerse, AutoAgents.
- **Results:**
 - EVOAGENT consistently and significantly improved LLM performance across all NLP tasks, outperforming both traditional prompting strategies (CoT, Self-Refine, SPP) and other pre-defined multi-agent generation frameworks (AgentVerse, AutoAgents). For instance, with GPT-4, EVOAGENT achieved 77.00% on Logic, 84.40% on Writing, and 84.53% on Code, often being the best or second-best performer.
 - EVOAGENT demonstrated strong generalization, providing consistent improvements even with weaker LLMs (e.g., Llama2-13B), where SPP often showed limited effectiveness.
 - In the MMMU multi-modal task, while Self-Refine and SPP degraded performance compared to CoT, EVOAGENT generated domain-specific agents that improved multi-modal models' ability to address scientific questions across all difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard).

#### 4.2. Interactive Scientific Solving Simulation

- **Benchmark:** ScienceWorld (complex interactive environment requiring long-term memory, sub-task decomposition, and scientific knowledge).
- **Models:** GPT-3.5, GPT-4.
- **Results:** EVOAGENT (set to generate one new agent per step for efficiency, denoted as EVOAGENT$_{(1,1)}$) successfully extended interactive agents to multi-agent systems in ScienceWorld. It consistently improved LLM performance in solving complete scientific tasks in dynamic, open-world environments. The most substantial improvements were observed in short-trajectory tasks, indicating potential challenges with very long contexts for multi-agent collaboration, which the authors note as future work.

#### 4.3. Real-World Scenarios (Complex Planning)

- **Benchmark:** TravelPlanner (evaluating language agents in real-world complex planning with multiple constraints).
- **Models:** Mistral-7B, GPT-3.5, Gemini-Pro, GPT-4.
- **Baselines:** Direct, CoT, ReAct, SPP, Self-Refine.
- **Results:**
 - EVOAGENT (set as EVOAGENT$_{(1,3)}$ to align with Self-Refine's iterations) significantly outperformed all baselines in generating high-quality travel plans. It consistently showed higher commonsense constraint pass rates, hard constraint pass rates, and final pass rates, especially with stronger models like GPT-4, where it achieved the best scores across almost all metrics.
 - The results highlighted that human-designed prompting strategies are often insufficient for complex planning, whereas EVOAGENT's automatic generation of specialized agents (e.g., culinary experts, transportation experts) led to plans more aligned with user preferences and commonsense rules.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (on TravelPlanner)

- **Impact of Agent Generation:** EVOAGENT significantly outperformed variants that merely generated multiple results (Overgen) or only provided suggestions (Suggest), confirming the value of *generating specialized agents*. Prompt refinement was also less effective than full agent generation.
- **Quality-Check Module:** The quality-check module was crucial, especially when generating more than one agent per iteration (N>1). Without it, agent diversity could decrease, leading to similar or unsuitable agents and a reduction in travel plan quality (e.g., causing "All-in" strategy to perform poorly due to excessively lengthy, non-compliant plans).
- **Population Size and Iterations:** Increasing population size and iteration number generally improved model alignment with user preferences, but sometimes at the cost of commonsense adherence, suggesting an inherent bias in the initial agent's output refinement.
- **Selection Strategies:** The "All-in" strategy (integrating all candidate results) performed optimally when the quality-check module was included, indicating that diverse contributions from well-vetted agents are beneficial. Without the quality-check, the "PK" (pairwise comparison) strategy sometimes performed better, suggesting it partially compensated for the lack of explicit quality control.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that EVOAGENT is a robust, effective, and generic method for automatically expanding specialized agents into collaborative multi-agent systems, consistently yielding superior performance across various tasks and LLM backbones compared to existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EVOAGENT represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM-based autonomous agents, offering a paradigm shift from manual design to automated generation of multi-agent systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Automation of Multi-Agent System Design:** The most profound impact of EVOAGENT is its ability to **automatically generate specialized agents** and extend existing agent frameworks into multi-agent systems without extensive human intervention. This addresses a critical scalability and labor-cost bottleneck in current research and development. By automating the creation of diverse roles and skill sets, EVOAGENT frees designers from the tedious and resource-intensive process of manually crafting agent personas and prompt engineering.
- **Enhanced Task-Solving Capabilities:** The experimental results consistently demonstrate that EVOAGENT significantly improves the performance of LLM-based agents across a wide range of complex tasks, including knowledge-intensive reasoning, multi-modal understanding, interactive scientific problem-solving, and real-world planning. This indicates that automatically generated multi-agent collaborations can indeed lead to higher-quality, more comprehensive, and more compliant solutions, proving the utility of specialized agent collective intelligence.
- **Generality and Adaptability:** EVOAGENT's formulation as an evolutionary process makes it "generic" and "applicable to any LLM-based agent framework." This high degree of adaptability is a crucial advantage, as it means researchers and developers are not constrained by specific architectural designs. They can leverage their existing single-agent frameworks and seamlessly extend them into multi-agent configurations, fostering wider adoption and applicability across diverse domains.
- **Novel Application of Evolutionary Algorithms to LLMs:** The paper successfully demonstrates a powerful synergy between large language models and evolutionary algorithms. By using LLMs to simulate complex biological processes like mutation, crossover, and selection, EVOAGENT showcases a novel and effective approach to optimizing and evolving discrete structures (agent settings/prompts), a challenging area for traditional optimization methods. This opens new avenues for combining meta-heuristic search with the generative capabilities of LLMs for various AI problems.
- **Broader Real-World Applications:** The strong performance in real-world complex planning tasks like TravelPlanner highlights EVOAGENT's potential for practical applications where nuanced constraints and diverse expertise are required. This could revolutionize areas like personalized planning, complex problem-solving in industrial settings, and even the creation of more dynamic and intelligent conversational AI systems.
- **Foundation for More Autonomous AI:** By pushing towards automatic agent generation, EVOAGENT contributes to the broader vision of more autonomous and self-organizing AI systems. It suggests a future where AI systems can dynamically adapt, create, and deploy specialized sub-agents to tackle unforeseen challenges, mirroring the adaptability of biological and social systems.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also point to avenues for future research:
- **Token Cost:** Generating multiple specialized agents naturally incurs higher token costs compared to a single agent. Future work could explore more token-efficient evolutionary strategies or distillation techniques.
- **Comparison Scope:** Limited extensive comparisons with *all* existing multi-agent systems due to the substantial design effort required for each benchmark. This suggests a need for standardized multi-agent evaluation frameworks that simplify comparisons.
- **Manual Evaluation:** The quality of generated agents was not manually evaluated, though their performance on complex tasks serves as an indirect validation. Direct human assessment of agent diversity and quality could provide deeper insights.
- **Long Context and Bias:** Observed diminishing returns and potential biases in long-trajectory tasks or when integrating results, indicating that effective context management and bias mitigation in LLM-driven multi-agent systems remain areas for improvement. Leveraging longer-context LLMs might alleviate some issues.

Despite these limitations, EVOAGENT lays a significant foundation for building more autonomous, flexible, and capable LLM-based multi-agent systems, marking a notable step towards more generalized and adaptive artificial intelligence.
