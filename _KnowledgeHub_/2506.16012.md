# DualTHOR: A Dual-Arm Humanoid Simulation Platform for Contingency-Aware Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> DualTHOR is a physics-based household simulator extending AI2-THOR to dual-arm humanoid robots (Unitree H1, AgiBot X1), adding humanoid inverse-kinematics solvers for continuous motion, a 356-task bimanual suite, undo/redo state replay, and a probabilistic contingency mechanism that samples action outcomes like breakage or spillage. Evaluations show leading vision-language planners reach only about 37-42% success on dual-arm-essential tasks.

## Problem

- Household simulators like AI2-THOR, ProcTHOR and iGibson restrict embodiments to wheeled bases and single arms, so bimanual coordination and humanoid reach constraints go untested.
- Prior platforms execute skills as instantaneous flash-like state transitions, hiding continuous motion and denying agents any signal about skill progression or partial execution.
- Deterministic transition assumptions guarantee success after every action, so agents never face breakage, spillage or jamming and cannot learn recovery, widening the sim-to-real gap.

## Method

- ==Unity==-based **DualTHOR** extends ==AI2-THOR== with H1 and X1 humanoid assets, per-robot ==IK solvers== (decoupled 3x3 pose for X1, whole-body 4x4 for H1) and ==cubic-spline joint interpolation== for continuous motion.
- A ==probabilistic contingency mechanism== maps each object-dependent action to state-conditioned categorical outcomes (success, broken, spill, half-open, stuck, nothing happens) with user-configurable probabilities, such as 80/10/10 when grasping a filled mug.
- A 356-task suite splits into dual-arm essential, dual-arm optional and single-arm categories, backed by ==undo/redo state replay== for branching trajectory collection and fluid dynamics plus collision rendering.

## Results

- Across **356** tasks, **10** rooms and **50** trials each, best planner DAG-Plan reaches **36.54%** (X1) and **41.53%** (H1) on dual-arm-essential tasks versus **55.47%**/**57.86%** on single-arm tasks.
- Open-source VLMs lag far behind: InternVL2.5-8B scores **9.71%** and Qwen2.5-VL-7B **18.33%** on X1 dual-arm-essential tasks, against GPT-4o's **23.56%** and Gemini-1.5-Pro's **25.31%**.
- Raising skill failure rates collapses performance: DAG-Plan falls from **36.54%** to **11.69%** on dual-arm-essential and **55.47%** to **31.42%** on single-arm tasks when low-level success drops to **20%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-level planners silently assume near-perfect low-level skills; benchmarks with deterministic execution overstate robustness, so stochastic outcome models belong in any planning evaluation.
> - Embodiment-aware structure helps: graph-based dual-arm plan representation shows the smallest single-to-dual-arm degradation, suggesting morphology should enter the planner's representation, not just the prompt.
> - Failures cluster on reachability and arm-occupancy reasoning, hinting that training data of sequential single-arm demonstrations teaches models to ignore future-step spatial constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied intelligent agents capable of performing complex interactive tasks in dynamic real-world environments is a central challenge in embodied AI. Over recent years, significant advancements in simulation platforms, such as AI2-THOR, Habitat, VirtualHome, and Isaac Gym, have contributed to enhancing the capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by providing diverse tasks and interaction complexities for training. These platforms have played a role in generating embodied data, improving scene understanding, and reducing the sim-to-real gap for certain robotic deployments.

However, existing simulation environments primarily feature simplified robot morphologies, typically wheeled robots or single-arm manipulators. While these platforms have increased task, scene, and object diversity, they often bypass the complexities of low-level execution. Many simulators employ instantaneous or "flash-like" state transitions, which assume deterministic success for actions and omit the continuous physical processes and potential failures inherent in real-world robot operations. This simplification limits the transferability of VLM-based planning to physical robots, where stochasticity and errors are common.

A parallel trend in robotics research involves the emergence of humanoid dual-arm robots. These robots offer expanded task capabilities, enabling more efficient, fine-grained, and realistic interactions compared to their single-arm counterparts. Despite this development, simulation platforms capable of supporting these complex morphologies and their bimanual control requirements, especially for long-horizon planning in household settings, remain limited. Current humanoid robot simulation efforts, such as HumanoidBench and Isaac Gym, primarily focus on low-level control policies and often lack detailed background rendering, making them unsuitable for evaluating high-level planning for dual-arm tasks within visually rich environments.

DualTHOR positions itself within this landscape by addressing the identified gaps: the absence of detailed simulation for dual-arm humanoid robots in household environments and the lack of a mechanism to account for the stochastic nature of low-level action execution. It aims to bridge these limitations by integrating realistic humanoid robot models, advanced physical simulation, and a contingency mechanism into a widely used household simulation framework (AI2-THOR).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing DualTHOR stems from two fundamental limitations in existing embodied AI simulation platforms:
1. **Simplified Robot Morphologies**: Current platforms largely restrict embodied agents to simplified forms, such as wheeled robots or single-arm manipulators. This limitation hinders the development of VLMs capable of operating more advanced humanoid dual-arm robots, which offer a broader range of manipulation capabilities crucial for complex real-world tasks. The authors aim to integrate realistic dual-arm humanoid robots to enable more sophisticated and generalizable embodied agents.
2. **Lack of Realistic Low-Level Execution**: Most prior simulators abstract away the continuous and often uncertain nature of low-level robot skill execution. They typically employ instantaneous transitions that guarantee success, thereby overlooking potential failures, continuous perception of skill progression, and the need for adaptive re-planning. This deterministic approach creates a significant sim-to-real gap, compromising the transferability of learned policies to physical robots.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the DualTHOR project are:
- **To develop a physics-based simulation platform** that supports complex dual-arm humanoid robots in interactive household environments. This includes integrating real-world robot assets and ensuring compatibility with the simulated environment.
- **To introduce a diverse task suite** specifically designed for dual-arm collaboration, allowing for evaluation of coordinated bimanual control, parallel action execution, and complex manipulation scenarios.
- **To implement advanced low-level control mechanisms**, including humanoid-specific inverse kinematics (IK) functions, to ensure continuous, smooth, and physically plausible action execution. This is intended to provide realistic observation variations throughout the interaction process.
- **To incorporate a probabilistic contingency mechanism** that simulates potential execution errors and unexpected outcomes during low-level actions. This feature aims to bridge the sim-to-real gap by introducing real-world stochasticity, thereby challenging agents to develop robustness and re-planning capabilities.
- **To provide a comprehensive benchmark** for evaluating the robustness, generalization, and high-level planning abilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in complex, realistic household environments with contingencies. The goal is to identify current limitations of VLMs in these challenging scenarios and guide future research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DualTHOR is a physics-based simulation platform built upon an extended version of AI2-THOR, leveraging the Unity engine. Its methodology integrates several key components to achieve a realistic and challenging environment for humanoid dual-arm robots.

#### 3.1. Physics Engine and Visual Perception

The platform utilizes the Unity engine, which supports parallel execution of bimanual actions, interpolation-based motion for continuous transitions, and high extensibility for object modeling and scene configuration. Visual perception is provided through a multi-camera system, including first-person head-mounted cameras (up to 360-degree panoramic view) and dynamically adjusted third-person cameras (rear, frontal, lateral views). Each camera offers omnidirectional rotation and a 160-degree horizontal field of view. Beyond RGB input, DualTHOR provides depth images aligned with each visual frame, offering geometric information essential for 3D scene reconstruction and object localization (Appendix A.1). Scenes are composed of distinct rooms, each with unique object sets, and all objects are re-rendered to ensure compatibility with humanoid robot heights (Figure 2, Appendix A.3).

#### 3.2. Humanoid Robots

DualTHOR supports two primary humanoid robot embodiments: the Unitree H1 and AgiBot X1 (Figure 7).
- **Unitree H1**: Characterized by strength and stability, making it suitable for tasks requiring heavy lifting or high-force interactions.
- **AgiBot X1**: Known for its dexterity and precision, enabling fine motor control for delicate manipulations.
Both robots are equipped with dual-arm configurations to facilitate bimanual manipulation. The simulator is designed for extensibility, with other humanoid agents such as Unitree G1 planned for integration (Appendix A.2).

#### 3.3. Task Categories and Action Set

The simulator offers a comprehensive range of interactive actions, divided into object-independent and object-dependent categories (Appendix A.4, Table 5).
- **Object-independent actions**: Include navigational (MoveAhead, MoveBack, MoveLeft, MoveRight, RotateLeft, RotateRight) and perceptual (OBSERVE, CROUCH, STAND) behaviors.
- **Object-dependent actions**: Enable precise object manipulation (PICKUP, OPEN, FILL, TOGGLE, LIFT, PLACE, TELEPORT). These actions support parallel execution for dual-arm operations.

Tasks are categorized based on the necessity of dual-arm involvement (Appendix A.5, Table 6):
- **Dual-arm essential tasks**: Require both arms (e.g., lifting heavy objects, holding open a container while placing an item).
- **Dual-arm optional tasks**: Can be done with one arm but benefit from dual-arm coordination for efficiency or realism (e.g., grasping multiple items, operating a coffee machine).
- **Single-arm tasks**: Simpler interactions, often adapted from AI2-THOR.

A task replay mechanism with "Undo" and "Redo" functionalities is implemented to manage task history and enable efficient exploration of alternative trajectories without restarting the simulator, thus reducing data collection inefficiencies.

#### 3.4. Low-Level Control

Control commands are executed through a closed-loop system, where Python backend sends serialized JSON action commands to the Unity engine. Unity parses these commands and uses an AgentMovement component to interface with the Inverse Kinematics (IK) system.
- **IK System**: Implemented as a cross-platform HTTP service. Unity computes interaction points on objects, transforms their coordinates to the robot's base frame, and sends this information to the IK server. The server solves the IK function, returning required joint configurations.
- **Motion Control**: Cubic spline interpolation is applied to joint angles to ensure smooth and continuous motion. OmniManip is utilized for pose computation and IK solving, supporting realistic grasping trajectories with dexterous hand models.
- **Robot-Specific IK**: The X1 robot uses a decoupled IK approach, solving independently for each arm with a simplified pose matrix. The H1 robot employs a whole-body coordination model, solving for both arms simultaneously using a 4x4 homogeneous transformation matrix and integrating current joint angles and velocities for dynamic optimization, particularly for balance.

#### 3.5. Contingency Mechanism

To simulate real-world uncertainty, DualTHOR incorporates a probabilistic contingency mechanism for object-dependent actions (Figure 3).
- **Probabilistic Outcomes**: Actions are mapped to multiple potential outcomes with defined probabilities (e.g., picking up a "pourable" cup: 80% success, 10% spill, 10% break). These outcomes are modeled using categorical distributions (Appendix A.6, Table 7).
- **State-Dependent Execution**: Actions are constrained by the current state of objects (e.g., a "Cooked" ingredient cannot be "COOKed" again), preventing unrealistic sequences. This mechanism aims to train agents to reassess states, adapt plans, and recover from failures.
- **Continuous Environmental Transitions**: Beyond discrete contingencies, DualTHOR enhances realism by continuously updating scene rendering to reflect dynamic changes (e.g., a sink gradually filling with water, Figure 5). This provides detailed feedback on action effects, challenging VLMs to adapt to continuous environmental changes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on 356 tasks across 10 distinct room environments, involving 68 unique objects. Tasks were divided into dual-arm essential, dual-arm optional, and single-arm categories. Performance was evaluated on both X1 and H1 robots over 50 trials per task, using proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro), open-source VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL2.5-8B), and prompt-enhanced VLMs (LLM-Planner, RAP, DAG-Plan, with GPT-4o as base for DAG-Plan).

#### 4.1. VLM Understanding of Dual-Arm Tasks

- **Overall Performance**: Results presented in Table 2 indicate that current VLMs exhibit limitations in long-horizon planning for dual-arm scenarios. The H1 robot generally achieved higher average success rates than the X1, attributed to its larger physical structure and broader interaction range, which can simplify object localization and navigation.
- **Dual-Arm Essential Tasks**: Performance on dual-arm essential tasks was consistently the lowest across all baselines. This highlights a significant challenge for current VLMs in comprehending and executing coordinated bimanual control. Such tasks demand complex reasoning, including temporal sequencing of object interactions, effective arm selection, and precise target positioning. A common failure mode observed was the inability to reach an object because an arm was already occupied or positioned incorrectly, necessitating complex re-planning for navigation and manipulation.
- **Bimanual Structure Information**: For dual-arm optional tasks, DAG-Plan demonstrated the smallest performance drop compared to single-arm tasks among the baselines. This suggests that incorporating dual-arm structural information can contribute to improved high-level planning for VLMs, although the overall success rate for these tasks still lagged behind single-arm tasks, indicating room for further improvement.

#### 4.2. VLM Handling of Contingency and Re-planning

- **Impact of Skill Failure Rates**: Table 3 presents results from experiments where tasks were subjected to varying low-level skill failure rates: Easy (100% success), Medium (50% success), and Hard (20% success). All baselines, even for single-arm tasks, exhibited a significant performance degradation under Medium and Hard conditions.
- **Robustness Limitations**: This finding indicates that the high-level planning components of current VLMs lack robustness and are highly dependent on high success rates from low-level control policies. In real-world deployments, where execution failures are common, such reliance would lead to persistent unsafe or ineffective behaviors.
- **Re-planning Challenges**: DAG-Plan, despite being a state-of-the-art method, responded to unexpected events by constructing new nodes and restarting the planning process from scratch. This reactive approach suggests limited proactive robustness and adaptive re-planning capabilities. The experiments showed that VLMs struggle with complex re-planning scenarios, such as needing to locate a new object if the original one breaks due to a contingency, or dynamically adjusting robot position based on interaction feedback (Figure 10).
- **Spatial and Temporal Reasoning**: Figure 9 showcases a plan failure where DAG-Plan failed to account for the bimanual configuration and spatial relationships of objects. For example, it generated a plan to move from a successful two-arm interaction to a failed one because it did not consider the interactive reachability of the left arm when the right arm was occupied. This suggests that VLMs, possibly due to training on sequential embodied datasets, tend to overemphasize the current interaction target and neglect future spatial and temporal constraints for bimanual tasks.

#### 4.3. Realistic Environment Transitions

DualTHOR’s implementation of continuous rendering updates, such as a sink gradually filling with water (Figure 5), demonstrated its ability to provide detailed action effects. This feature allows VLMs to better comprehend scene transformations and assess action execution in a more immersive and realistic environment, thereby challenging them to adapt to continuous changes. Figure 8 further illustrates continuous action control, providing richer data compared to instantaneous transitions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DualTHOR represents a notable contribution to the embodied AI research landscape by addressing several critical limitations of existing simulation platforms.

#### 5.1. Advancing Embodied AI Simulation

The platform’s primary significance lies in its support for dual-arm humanoid robots, moving beyond the simplified morphologies prevalent in other simulators. By integrating realistic robot assets like Unitree H1 and AgiBot X1, along with specialized inverse kinematics solvers, DualTHOR facilitates the development and evaluation of VLMs for more complex, bimanual manipulation tasks previously unaddressed in household-scale simulations. The re-rendering of objects to align with humanoid robot scales further enhances the realism and practicality of the simulated environment.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap

A key innovation is the probabilistic contingency mechanism, which introduces real-world stochasticity and potential failure modes during low-level action execution. This contrasts with deterministic transitions in many simulators, providing a more truthful representation of physical interactions. By forcing agents to encounter and recover from errors (e.g., a mug breaking, coffee spilling), DualTHOR helps train VLMs that are robust and adaptable, capabilities that are crucial for successful deployment on physical robots. The continuous rendering updates, reflecting dynamic scene changes like fluid dynamics, also enhance the fidelity of the simulation, offering richer data for VLM comprehension of action effects.

#### 5.3. Establishing a Challenging Benchmark

DualTHOR provides a novel and challenging benchmark tailored for household dual-arm tasks. The experimental results reveal that current open-source and proprietary VLMs struggle significantly with dual-arm coordination and exhibit limited robustness when faced with contingencies. Specifically, performance drops were observed in dual-arm essential tasks and under conditions of increased low-level skill failure rates. These findings highlight the current limitations of VLMs in spatial-temporal reasoning for bimanual tasks and their reactive rather than proactive re-planning capabilities.

#### 5.4. Driving Future Research

The identified shortcomings of existing VLMs in DualTHOR underscore the necessity for future research to develop more sophisticated embodied AI agents. The platform encourages advancements in:
- **Bimanual Coordination**: Enhancing VLMs' ability to understand and coordinate two arms for complex, interdependent tasks.
- **Robust Planning**: Developing planners that are resilient to execution-level failures and capable of intelligent, timely re-planning, rather than merely restarting.
- **Contextual Reasoning**: Improving VLMs' understanding of spatial relationships, reachability constraints, and the temporal dependencies of actions in dynamic environments.
- **Data Generation**: The platform offers a robust foundation for generating diverse trajectory data, including failure cases, which can be invaluable for training more capable VLMs.

#### 5.5. Future Directions

The authors acknowledge limitations and outline future work, including the development of flexible asset generation tools, support for a broader range of robots (e.g., Unitree G1), implementation of non-grid-based positioning, more controllable failure modes, and multi-room environments. The intention to incorporate multi-agent cooperative evaluation also indicates a path toward even more complex and realistic scenarios, further extending DualTHOR's impact on the field of embodied AI.
