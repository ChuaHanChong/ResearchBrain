# DualTHOR: A Dual-Arm Humanoid Simulation Platform for Contingency-Aware Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at CAS, AgiBot, BAAI, and Peking University introduce DualTHOR, a physics-based simulation platform extending AI2-THOR for dual-arm humanoid robots (Unitree H1, AgiBot X1). The simulator includes a dual-arm task suite, humanoid-specific inverse-kinematics solvers, and a novel contingency mechanism that injects probabilistic execution failures, exposing the limited robustness of current VLM planners in realistic household scenarios.

## Problem

- Most embodied AI simulators rely on simplified single-arm or wheeled robot morphologies, ignoring the dual-arm humanoid platforms that increasingly dominate real-world robotics research.
- Existing simulators bypass low-level execution stochasticity via "flash-like" instantaneous transitions, undermining sim-to-real transferability of VLM planners.
- There is no standardized benchmark to evaluate high-level VLM planning capabilities for dual-arm collaboration under realistic failure conditions.

## Method

- **DualTHOR** extends ==AI2-THOR== with the ==Unity physics engine==, humanoid-specific ==inverse-kinematics (IK)== functions, and task-reversal capabilities, supporting Unitree H1 and AgiBot X1 humanoids as primary embodied agents.
- A dual-arm task suite where two arms can execute distinct parallel actions or collaborate on a single complex task (e.g., "toggle and get a cup of water", "pick and open a gift box", "lift heavy objects").
- A ==contingency mechanism== that incorporates probabilistic execution failures during action operation (e.g., breaking a cup, spilling coffee), simulating real-world uncertainties while maintaining reproducibility.
- Extended physical simulation including object collisions and ==fluid dynamics== (pouring, filling) to reduce the sim-to-real gap for VLM-based planners.

## Results

- Current VLMs exhibit **limited robustness** when handling dual-arm coordination tasks, with planning failures concentrated in contingency-rich scenarios.
- The contingency mechanism reveals failure modes in VLM planners (poor re-planning under unexpected events) that are masked by simulators with instantaneous transitions.
- Both open-source and proprietary VLMs show **constrained generalization** on long-horizon dual-arm household tasks, motivating dedicated training for contingency-aware planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling continuous low-level execution with probabilistic failures, rather than flash-like transitions, is essential for evaluating VLM planners destined for real-world deployment.
> - Dual-arm humanoid morphology enables a fundamentally broader task space than single-arm or wheeled platforms — a critical missing piece in prior simulators.
> - Contingency-aware planning is an under-evaluated capability of current VLMs; DualTHOR provides a standardized testbed for studying it.
