# SE-Agent: Self-Evolution Trajectory Optimization in Multi-Step Reasoning with LLM-Based Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative effort introduces SE-Agent, a self-evolutionary framework for LLM-based agents that optimizes multi-step reasoning trajectories via iterative revision, recombination, and refinement. This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on SWE-bench Verified, improving Pass@1 by up to 112% for Llama-3.1-70b-Instruct and reaching 61.2% with Claude-3.7-Sonnet.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based agents in multi-step reasoning often underexploit rich feedback from interaction trajectories.
- Current methods typically treat individual reasoning trajectories independently, limiting search space diversity and leading to structurally similar, suboptimal solutions.
- Agents frequently get stuck in local optima due to a lack of cross-trajectory learning and an inability to explore genuinely diverse solution paths.

## Method

- SE-Agent proposes a ==Self-Evolution framework== treating trajectories as 'genotypes': the ==Revision operation== enhances individual trajectories through multi-planning exploration, mutation, and LLM-based reflection to identify and address weaknesses.
- The ==Recombination operation== facilitates collective evolution by combining strengths from different trajectories via crossover, transfer learning, and global restructuring to synthesize new paths.
- The ==Refinement operation== evaluates trajectories using a multi-dimensional reward function (task completion, reasoning quality, efficiency) and selects top-performing, diverse trajectories for the next iteration.

## Results

- SE-Agent consistently outperformed all baselines across five different LLMs on SWE-bench Verified, achieving state-of-the-art performance for open-source agents.
- It delivered substantial relative Pass@1 improvements, including **+112%** for Llama-3.1-70b-Instruct (**15.4%** to **32.6%**) and **+80%** for GPT-4o (**22.4%** to **40.4%**).
- An ablation study confirmed that all core modules (Revision, Recombination, Refinement) are essential for performance, with the Revision operation playing a particularly critical role in initial trajectory diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing entire reasoning trajectories, rather than just individual steps or generating more samples, enables agents to learn and improve at a higher conceptual level.
> - A self-evolutionary paradigm, inspired by genetic algorithms, can effectively overcome LLM limitations like 'tunnel vision' and foster genuine diversity in problem-solving approaches.
> - Strategic cross-trajectory learning through explicit recombination operations is crucial for synthesizing novel solutions and leveraging collective insights across different reasoning attempts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses critical challenges within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents, particularly their capability in multi-step reasoning and complex task execution. The broader landscape sees LLMs transitioning from mere text generators to autonomous agents capable of interacting with environments and utilizing tools. This evolution is crucial for tackling real-world problems that cannot be solved in a single generative step.

Existing approaches to multi-step reasoning, such as the widely adopted ReAct framework and advanced search methods like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), have demonstrated remarkable capabilities. However, the authors identify a significant limitation: these methods often treat individual reasoning trajectories (sequences of states and actions) as independent entities. This leads to several issues:
1. **Underexploited Trajectory Feedback:** The rich feedback inherent in an agent's interaction history is not fully leveraged.
2. **Lack of Search Space Diversity:** Even with diverse sampling strategies, LLMs tend to converge on structurally similar solution paths, limiting true exploration and leading to homogeneous, often suboptimal, outcomes. This "tunnel vision" or "mode collapse" is a known challenge in generative models.
3. **Redundant Reasoning:** Without cross-trajectory learning, agents might repeatedly attempt similar flawed paths.

SE-Agent positions itself as a novel solution that goes beyond merely optimizing individual steps or sampling more trajectories. It introduces a *self-evolutionary paradigm* that operates at the trajectory level, allowing agents to iteratively optimize their entire problem-solving process. This contrasts with other agent enhancement methods, which might focus on improved planning (GoalAct, MPO), multi-agent collaboration (Collaborative Voyager, RGD), memory mechanisms (MemGPT, ReAct), or self-critique (Reflexion, CRITIC). While sharing conceptual similarities with self-play and expert iteration in reinforcement learning, SE-Agent explicitly manipulates *complete reasoning trajectories* rather than isolated state-action pairs, aiming for more holistic improvements.

The choice of SWE-bench Verified as a benchmark is significant. SWE-bench is a highly challenging, real-world benchmark for automated software engineering, requiring agents to resolve actual GitHub issues. Success on such a benchmark pushes the boundaries of LLM agents' applicability to complex, practical problems, moving beyond simpler code generation tasks. By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance, SE-Agent contributes a robust and generalizable method for improving agent capabilities in a domain with high industrial relevance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SE-Agent is to develop an LLM-based agent capable of generating high-quality reasoning trajectories for complex, multi-step tasks. Specifically, the goal is to find an optimal policy that maximizes the expected reward for a given task, where reward is defined by task completion, reasoning quality, and efficiency.

The motivation stems from the observed limitations of current LLM-based agents in multi-step reasoning:
- **Inefficient Use of Interaction Trajectories:** The problem-solving trajectories, rich with feedback, are not fully utilized to guide agents towards better solutions. This wasted information represents a significant missed opportunity for improvement.
- **Limited Search Space Exploration:** Existing methods, including sophisticated tree search algorithms like MCTS, often treat trajectories independently. This independence leads to a lack of diversity in explored solution paths, as LLMs inherently gravitate towards high-probability patterns, resulting in similar, redundant reasoning and suboptimal outcomes. The analogy of "converging on structurally similar trajectories that differ only in surface-level expressions" highlights this critical issue.
- **Suboptimal Problem Solving:** The combination of underexploited feedback and limited search space diversity means agents frequently get stuck in local optima or fail to discover the most effective problem-solving strategies, especially for intricate tasks.

To address these challenges, SE-Agent aims to:
1. **Expand the Search Space:** Move beyond local optima by intelligently exploring diverse solution paths, guided by insights derived from previous trajectories.
2. **Leverage Cross-Trajectory Inspiration:** Efficiently enhance performance by combining strengths and lessons learned across multiple distinct reasoning attempts, thereby mitigating the impact of suboptimal paths and fostering more robust solutions.
3. **Achieve Continuous Self-Evolution:** Enable agents to incrementally improve their reasoning quality through an iterative optimization process, akin to natural evolution, making the system more adaptable and effective over time.

In essence, the motivation is to enable LLM-based agents to learn *how to learn* from their own problem-solving experiences in a more profound and structured way, leading to genuinely novel and superior solutions for complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SE-Agent proposes a novel **Self-Evolution framework** that iteratively optimizes an agent's reasoning processes through systematic manipulation of interaction trajectories. The core idea is to treat trajectories not as isolated attempts, but as "genotypes" that can be revised, recombined, and refined to evolve superior "phenotypes" (solutions). This process is conceptualized as a specialized form of genetic algorithm tailored for LLM-based agents.

The framework operates through three fundamental, iterative operations:

#### 3.1. Overview of SE-Agent

The process begins with generating an initial pool of diverse pilot trajectories (Γ₀) for a given task. Each trajectory (τ) is a sequence of states and actions. Instead of simply picking the best initial trajectory, SE-Agent enters an iterative evolutionary loop, where each iteration (k) produces an improved generation of trajectories (Γₖ). The goal is to converge on a high-rewarding trajectory (τ*).

#### 3.2. Revision Operation

This phase focuses on enhancing individual trajectories through introspection and targeted improvement.
- **Generating Initial Trajectories:**
 - **Multi-Planning Exploration:** Generates diverse initial trajectories (Γ_plan₀) by systematically varying planning strategies, prompting techniques, and reasoning approaches (e.g., five distinct planning prompts are provided in Appendix A.1.1). This ensures broad coverage of the solution space.
 - **Mutation-Based Diversification:** Further expands the pool (Γ_mutate₀) by applying controlled mutations to existing trajectories, introducing targeted variations in steps, actions, or conclusions.
- **Reflection and Revision:** For each generated trajectory, a critical reflection process (prompted by LLMs as shown in Appendix A.1.2) analyzes its strengths, weaknesses, and potential for improvement. This identifies logical inconsistencies, underdeveloped reasoning, and proposes alternative approaches. Based on these reflections, revised trajectories (τ'ⱼ) are generated, eliminating redundancies and incorporating better strategies. This embodies "planning origin and reflective evolution."

#### 3.3. Recombination Operation

This crucial phase facilitates collective evolution by enabling cross-trajectory learning, drawing insights from multiple paths.
- **Crossover:** Identifies high-performing segments from different trajectories (τ_j1, τ_j2) and combines them to create hybrid trajectories (τ_cross_new) that inherit the strengths of multiple "parents" (prompted as shown in Appendix A.2).
- **Transfer Learning:** Systematically transfers knowledge and effective strategies from successful trajectories (reference pool) to enhance less developed or suboptimal paths (target trajectory) (prompted as shown in Appendix A.2).
- **Restructuring:** Reorganizes reasoning trajectories based on a comprehensive global analysis of the entire trajectory pool (Γₖ). This involves discovering abstract patterns, common subgoals, and synthesizing a completely new, optimized trajectory (prompted as shown in Appendix A.2).

#### 3.4. Refinement Operation

This final phase focuses on trajectory optimization and selection.
- **Evaluation Function:** A multi-dimensional reward function `Reward(τ, t)` evaluates trajectory quality:
 - `TaskCompletion(τ, t)`: Measures effective task resolution, combining `AutoEval` (rule-based structural validation, e.g., non-empty patch, sufficient code edits) and `ExpertEval` (LLM-based quality assessment).
 - `ReasoningQuality(τ)`: Assesses logical coherence, depth, and robustness of the reasoning process.
 - `Efficiency(τ)`: Quantifies computational efficiency (reasoning steps, resource utilization).
- **Selection and Convergence:** A strategic selection mechanism iteratively updates the trajectory pool (Γ_k+1). It retains top-performing trajectories based on reward scores while also ensuring diversity by considering trajectory dissimilarity. This process continues until a predefined number of evolution cycles is completed or convergence criteria are met (e.g., marginal improvement). The final output is the highest-rewarding trajectory (τ*). This mechanism embodies "collective competition and genetic emergence."

The integration of LLMs within each operation (reflection, revision, crossover, transfer, restructuring, and evaluation) is key. The provided prompts illustrate how LLMs are used to analyze trajectories, identify critical steps, conclude features of solutions, and then synthesize new, improved reasoning paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SE-Agent was conducted on **SWE-bench Verified**, a highly challenging benchmark consisting of 500 real-world GitHub issues requiring functional bug fixes. The performance was measured using **Pass@1** (percentage of issues resolved on the first attempt) and **Pass@5** (percentage resolved within five attempts).

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison

- SE-Agent consistently **outperformed all baselines** across all five evaluated LLMs, including both open-source (DeepSeek-V3-0324, Qwen-2.5-72b-Instruct, Llama-3.1-70b-Instruct) and closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet).
- When integrated with **SWE-Agent** (a CodeAct-based framework), SE-Agent delivered substantial relative improvements:
 - **+112%** for Llama-3.1-70b-Instruct (Pass@1: 15.4% -> 32.6%)
 - **+80%** for GPT-4o (Pass@1: 22.4% -> 40.4%)
 - **+51%** for Claude-3.7-Sonnet (Pass@1: 40.6% -> 61.2%)
- Against the stronger **SWE-Search** (an MCTS-based framework), SE-Agent still achieved average relative gains of **+30%**.
- Notably, SE-Agent achieved **state-of-the-art performance** among open-source agents on SWE-bench Verified, reaching **61.2% with Claude-3.7-Sonnet** and **80.0% with Claude-4-Sonnet** (the latter reported with the latest SWE-Agent baseline). These results validate the generalizability and effectiveness of SE-Agent across diverse model families and its ability to significantly boost agent performance.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- An ablation study investigated the contribution of each core module (Revision, Recombination, Refinement). The results (Figure 2) showed that **all designed modules are important** for SE-Agent's performance, as removing any one led to a decrease in Pass@1.
- The **Revision operation was particularly effective**, highlighting its critical role in generating a diverse set of initial trajectories, which is foundational for subsequent recombination and overall performance enhancement.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis (Venn Diagram and Case Study)

- A Venn diagram (Figure 3) comparing resolved issues demonstrated that SE-Agent uniquely solved **12 issue instances** that none of the other compared models (SWE-Agent, SWE-Search, or ablated SE-Agent variants) could address. This underscores its complementary problem-solving strength and robustness.
- A detailed **case study (SCIKIT-LEARN #14629)** illustrated how SE-Agent successfully overcame "tunnel vision" that plagued traditional ReAct/MCTS-based agents. While traditional agents fixated on surface-level errors in `_validation.py`, SE-Agent's self-evolution mechanism enabled it to explore diverse regions of the patch space, discover the root cause in `multioutput.py`, and implement a more profound, root-level repair. This demonstrates SE-Agent's ability to generate genuinely novel solutions rather than minor variants.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Analysis

- SE-Agent achieved near-optimal performance with **just 10 candidate trajectories** (Figure 4, left), indicating its efficiency in trajectory-based search through inter-trajectory interactions.
- Under the same API cost budgets, SE-Agent consistently **outperformed baselines** (Figure 4, right), validating its efficiency and effectiveness even with resource constraints.

In summary, the findings robustly support that SE-Agent's trajectory-level self-evolution framework offers a highly effective and efficient method for enhancing LLM-based agents, particularly in complex multi-step reasoning tasks like software bug fixing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SE-Agent framework represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM-based agents, offering a novel paradigm for enhancing their multi-step reasoning capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Agent Learning

SE-Agent introduces a fundamental shift from optimizing individual decisions or merely sampling multiple outcomes to *evolving entire reasoning trajectories*. By treating trajectories as manipulable entities that can be revised, recombined, and refined, it enables agents to learn and improve at a higher conceptual level. This move beyond "next-token prediction" for local edits to "trajectory-level evolution" for global problem-solving strategy is a key conceptual leap.

#### 5.2. Overcoming Core LLM Limitations

The framework directly addresses critical limitations of current LLM agents, namely the lack of true search space diversity and the underutilization of rich interactive feedback. By strategically intervening at the trajectory level, SE-Agent allows LLMs to escape local optima and explore genuinely diverse solution paths, leading to more robust and accurate outcomes than traditional multi-sampling or MCTS approaches. This has profound implications for problems where "tunnel vision" or premature convergence is a common failure mode.

#### 5.3. State-of-the-Art Performance on Real-World Tasks

Achieving significant, sometimes more than double, relative improvements on SWE-bench Verified, a benchmark of real-world GitHub issues, demonstrates SE-Agent's practical utility. This performance boost is not only impressive but also highlights the potential for LLM-based agents to tackle increasingly complex software engineering tasks autonomously, from bug fixing to feature implementation, with higher success rates. This directly contributes to the vision of truly autonomous AI software developers.

#### 5.4. Generalizability and Modularity

SE-Agent is designed as a "plug-and-play module," indicating its modularity and generalizability across different LLMs (both open and closed-source) and potentially different agent architectures. This means that as base LLMs continue to improve, SE-Agent can continue to extract additional performance gains, ensuring its relevance and applicability in the long term. The fact that its effectiveness is not tied to a specific base model's capabilities but rather to the *process* of reasoning makes it a universally beneficial enhancement.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Agent Development

The self-evolutionary principles introduced by SE-Agent lay foundational groundwork for future agent designs. The ability of agents to critically reflect on their own processes, learn from past mistakes and successes (even across different attempts), and synthesize new strategies autonomously is a crucial step towards more intelligent, adaptable, and robust AI systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Automated Software Engineering:** Revolutionize how software bugs are fixed and new code is developed, potentially leading to faster development cycles, higher code quality, and reduced human effort in routine maintenance.
- **Complex Problem Solving:** Extend to other domains requiring multi-step reasoning, such as scientific discovery, strategic planning, robotics, and creative design, where iterative refinement and diverse exploration are crucial.
- **Enhanced AI Capabilities:** Push the boundaries of what LLM-based agents can achieve, making them more reliable and capable tools for complex decision-making in real-world environments.
- **Research Direction:** Open new avenues for research into evolutionary algorithms applied to LLM reasoning, meta-learning for agents, and methods for quantifying and fostering genuine diversity in AI-generated solutions.

In conclusion, SE-Agent offers a powerful and elegant solution to a core challenge in LLM agent research. By enabling agents to self-evolve their reasoning trajectories, it not only achieves state-of-the-art performance but also provides a versatile framework for building more intelligent and adaptable autonomous systems in the future, with significant implications across various fields of AI and beyond.
