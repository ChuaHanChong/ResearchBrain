# PaLM: Scaling Language Modeling with Pathways

> [!summary] Summary
> Google Research developed PaLM, a 540-billion parameter dense Transformer model, demonstrating that continued scaling with efficient infrastructure leads to substantial improvements and emergent capabilities in few-shot learning. The model achieved state-of-the-art results across numerous NLP benchmarks, multi-step reasoning, code generation, and multilingual tasks, leveraging the new Pathways training system for unprecedented efficiency.

## Problem

- To rigorously investigate if performance gains from scaling large language models (LLMs) would continue, plateau, or lead to qualitatively new emergent abilities beyond previous models like GPT-3.
- The challenge of efficiently training extremely large, dense Transformer models across thousands of accelerator chips without the efficiency drawbacks of traditional pipeline parallelism.
- Achieving a single generalist LLM capable of high performance across diverse tasks including complex reasoning, code generation, and multilingual understanding without extensive task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

- Developed **PaLM**, a 540-billion parameter ==dense Transformer== model with architectural improvements like ==SwiGLU activation== and ==multi-query attention==, trained on a diverse 780 billion token corpus.
- Utilized Google's new ==Pathways ML system== to enable ==pipeline-free training== of PaLM 540B on 6144 TPU v4 chips across two DCN-connected pods, achieving high ==Model FLOPs Utilization==.
- Evaluated the model extensively using ==few-shot learning== on hundreds of benchmarks, critically employing ==chain-of-thought prompting== for complex reasoning tasks.

## Results

- PaLM 540B achieved a Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) of **46.2%** on **6144** TPU v4 chips, surpassing the efficiency of prior large language models.
- Set few-shot state-of-the-art on **28 out of 29** English NLP benchmarks and outperformed average human performance across **58** BIG-bench tasks, with emergent capabilities observed on **25%** of BIG-bench tasks.
- Achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on multi-step reasoning tasks with chain-of-thought prompting (e.g., **58%** on GSM8K) and competitive performance in code generation (e.g., **76.2%** pass@100 on HumanEval) and multilingual NLP.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continued scaling of dense Transformer models yields sustained performance improvements and can unlock 'discontinuous improvements' or emergent capabilities on certain complex tasks.
> - The Pathways system enables unprecedented training efficiency (46.2% MFU) for hyperscale models by orchestrating thousands of accelerators without pipeline parallelism, crucial for future AI development.
> - Chain-of-thought prompting significantly enhances multi-step reasoning in LLMs, allowing them to achieve state-of-the-art results on tasks traditionally requiring specialized architectures or fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of PaLM is a direct continuation and significant acceleration of the trend towards scaling large language models (LLMs), which has defined much of the progress in NLP over the past few years. This research builds upon several foundational works and addresses key questions emerging from the LLM paradigm:

- **Evolution of Language Models:** Early transformer-based models like BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) and T5 (Raffel et al., 2020) demonstrated remarkable performance through pre-training on large text corpora, primarily via masked language modeling or span corruption, followed by task-specific fine-tuning. However, these models often required substantial labeled data for each new task.
- **The Rise of Few-Shot Learning:** A pivotal shift occurred with GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020), which introduced the concept of few-shot learning using extremely large autoregressive (decoder-only) language models. GPT-3 showed that by scaling model size, LMs could perform diverse tasks with only a few examples (or even zero examples) provided as part of the prompt, without requiring parameter updates. This significantly reduced the data and computational burden for adapting models to new applications.
- **Post-GPT-3 Scaling Race:** Following GPT-3, several models continued to push the boundaries of scale and efficiency, including GLaM (Du et al., 2021), Gopher (Rae et al., 2021), Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al., 2022), Megatron-Turing NLG (Smith et al., 2022), and LaMDA (Thoppilan et al., 2022). These models explored various avenues for improvement:
 - **Increased Model Size:** Continuously growing the number of parameters.
 - **More Training Tokens:** Training on larger and more diverse datasets.
 - **Cleaner Data:** Focusing on higher quality training corpora.
 - **Sparse Activations:** Introducing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures (like GLaM) to increase model capacity without proportionally increasing computational cost per token.
- **PaLM's Contribution:** PaLM strategically positions itself within this landscape by focusing on *dense* model scaling while introducing a novel, highly efficient training infrastructure. Unlike GLaM which explored sparse activations, PaLM doubles down on the dense Transformer architecture, pushing it to an unprecedented 540 billion parameters. Crucially, it highlights "Pathways" as a new ML system that enables this scale with exceptional training efficiency, addressing one of the biggest bottlenecks in LLM development. The paper directly compares PaLM's performance against its contemporaries, aiming to demonstrate that the benefits of scaling, particularly dense scaling, are far from saturated and can unlock emergent capabilities, especially in complex reasoning. It also uniquely addresses multilingual and code-specific capabilities in a single general-purpose model, expanding the scope of what such LLMs can achieve.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the PaLM project are multifaceted, aiming to advance both the theoretical understanding and practical capabilities of large language models:

- **Further Understanding the Impact of Scale:** The core motivation is to rigorously investigate the impact of increasing model scale on few-shot learning capabilities. By training a 540-billion parameter, densely activated Transformer, the researchers sought to determine if the performance gains observed with previous LLMs would continue, plateau, or even lead to qualitatively new "emergent" abilities.
- **Demonstrate Breakthrough Capabilities:** A key objective was to achieve state-of-the-art few-shot learning results across a comprehensive suite of benchmarks, including language understanding, generation, code tasks, and especially multi-step reasoning. The researchers hypothesized that sufficient scale, combined with effective prompting techniques like chain-of-thought, could unlock unprecedented performance, potentially surpassing fine-tuned state-of-the-art models.
- **Validate the Pathways System:** A significant motivation was to conduct the first large-scale real-world test of Google's new ML system, Pathways. This involved demonstrating its ability to efficiently train a single, extremely large model across thousands of accelerator chips (6144 TPU v4 chips across multiple pods) in a pipeline-free manner, addressing the critical infrastructure challenges of hyperscale ML.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation and Analysis:** Beyond raw performance, the project aimed for a holistic understanding of PaLM's characteristics. This included:
 - **Scaling Behavior:** Analyzing whether performance improvements were continuous (log-linear) or discontinuous (emergent capabilities).
 - **Multilingual Prowess:** Thoroughly evaluating performance on translation and multilingual NLP tasks, despite a relatively smaller proportion of non-English training data.
 - **Code Generation:** Assessing its proficiency in text-to-code, code-to-code translation, and code repair.
 - **Ethical Considerations:** Conducting an extensive analysis of representational bias (gender, occupation, race, religion), toxicity in open-ended generation, and training data memorization, along with a discussion of broader ethical implications and mitigation strategies.

In essence, the project was motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of what is possible with dense LLMs, enabled by cutting-edge infrastructure, and to provide a deeper empirical understanding of their capabilities, limitations, and societal implications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of PaLM involved a sophisticated blend of model architecture design, a vast and carefully curated training dataset, a novel distributed training infrastructure, and rigorous evaluation methodologies.

- **Model Architecture:** PaLM employs a **standard Transformer model in a decoder-only setup** (autoregressive, left-to-right prediction). Key architectural modifications aimed at improving quality and training/inference speed include:
 - **SwiGLU Activation:** Replacing standard activation functions (ReLU, GeLU, Swish) with SwiGLU for improved quality.
 - **Parallel Layers:** A "parallel" Transformer block formulation (Attention and MLP applied in parallel to LayerNorm(x), then added to x) for ~15% faster training speed, with no quality degradation at large scales.
 - **Multi-Query Attention:** Sharing key/value projections across attention heads to significantly reduce memory cost during autoregressive decoding, without impacting quality or training speed.
 - **RoPE Embeddings (Rotary Position Embeddings):** Used instead of absolute or relative position embeddings for better performance on long sequences.
 - **Shared Input-Output Embeddings:** A common practice to reduce parameters.
 - **No Biases:** Omitting biases in dense kernels and layer norms to increase training stability for large models.
 - **SentencePiece Vocabulary:** A 256k token vocabulary, lossless and reversible, designed to support multiple languages and preserve whitespace (crucial for code).
 The models were trained at three scales: 8.63B, 62.50B, and 540.35B parameters, all dense.

- **Training Dataset:** A high-quality corpus of **780 billion tokens** was used for pre-training. This dataset is a diverse mixture, representing a wide range of natural language uses, and based on datasets used for LaMDA and GLaM. Its composition is:
 - 50% Social media conversations (multilingual)
 - 27% Filtered webpages (multilingual, quality-scored)
 - 13% Books (English)
 - 5% GitHub (source code, copyleft licenses excluded, deduplicated, 24 languages)
 - 4% Wikipedia (multilingual)
 - 1% News (English)
 All three model scales were trained on exactly one epoch of this data, with careful mixing proportions to avoid data repetition in subcomponents. English comprised approximately 78% of the training tokens.

- **Training Infrastructure:** This is a crucial aspect of PaLM's success, leveraging Google's cutting-edge ML systems:
 - **JAX and T5X:** The training and evaluation codebase are built on these frameworks, providing a robust and flexible environment.
 - **TPU v4 Pods:** All models were trained on Google's custom Tensor Processing Unit v4 accelerators.
 - **Pathways System:** This novel ML system enabled **pipeline-free training of PaLM 540B on 6144 TPU v4 chips across two DCN-connected (Datacenter Network) TPU Pods**. This is a significant advancement over previous approaches that often relied on pipeline parallelism (which has drawbacks like "pipeline bubbles" and higher memory bandwidth demands). Pathways achieves this through a client-server architecture, two-way data parallelism at the pod level (each pod computes gradients on half the batch, then exchanges gradients with the other pod), and optimized DCN utilization to handle bursty cross-pod gradient transfers efficiently. This resulted in an impressive **46.2% Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU)**, a new metric proposed by the authors for cleaner comparison of system efficiency, which contrasts with the less consistent hardware FLOPs utilization (HFU).

- **Training Setup:** Standard practices for LLMs were largely followed:
 - **Optimizer:** Adafactor (a variant of Adam) without factorization, using parameter scaling.
 - **Learning Rate Schedule:** 10^-2 for 10,000 steps, then decayed at 1/√k.
 - **Other Hyperparameters:** Momentum β1=0.9, dynamic β2=1.0 - k^-0.8, global norm gradient clipping (1.0), dynamic weight decay (lr^2.0), auxiliary z-loss.
 - **Sequence Length:** 2048 tokens, concatenated without padding.
 - **Batch Size:** Dynamically increased during training (e.g., from 512 to 2048 for PaLM 540B) to balance sample efficiency with TPU efficiency.
 - **Stability:** Noteworthy mitigation for loss spikes in the largest model involved restarting from earlier checkpoints and skipping specific data batches.
 - **Reproducibility:** Bitwise deterministic training framework and dataset pipeline.
 - **Dropout:** Not used during pre-training.

- **Evaluation Methodology:**
 - **Few-shot Learning:** Primary evaluation method (0-shot, 1-shot, few-shot), following the GPT-3 paradigm, on hundreds of diverse benchmarks.
 - **Benchmarks:** Included 29 English NLP tasks (e.g., TriviaQA, SuperGLUE), 150+ BIG-bench tasks (logical reasoning, translation, QA, math), multi-step reasoning tasks (e.g., GSM8K, StrategyQA), code generation/repair (HumanEval, MBPP, TransCoder, DeepFix), multilingual NLP (MLSum, WikiLingua, TyDiQA), and machine translation (WMT).
 - **Chain-of-Thought Prompting:** Critically used for reasoning tasks, where models are prompted to generate intermediate reasoning steps before the final answer, significantly enhancing performance.
 - **Finetuning:** Also performed on selected benchmarks (SuperGLUE, GEM, TyDiQA) to compare against specialized SOTA models.
 - **Bias and Toxicity Analysis:** Investigated representational bias (Winogender, co-occurrence analysis for gender, race, religion) and toxicity in open-ended generation using Perspective API and RealToxicityPrompts.
 - **Memorization and Contamination Analysis:** Quantified exact training data reproduction and assessed overlap with evaluation datasets to rule out inflated results.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The PaLM paper presents a wide array of significant findings, primarily showcasing the continued benefits of large-scale, dense language modeling and the capabilities of the Pathways training system.

- **Unprecedented Training Efficiency with Pathways:**
 - PaLM 540B was trained on **6144 TPU v4 chips across two pods** with a remarkable **46.2% Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU)**. This MFU significantly surpasses previous LLMs (e.g., GPT-3 at 21.3%, Gopher at 32.5%, Megatron-Turing NLG at 30.2%), demonstrating Pathways' ability to enable highly efficient, pipeline-free training at extreme scales.

- **Continued and Discontinuous Improvements from Scaling:**
 - PaLM 540B achieved **state-of-the-art few-shot results on 28 out of 29 widely evaluated English NLP benchmarks**, often by significant margins, over models like GPT-3, GLaM, Gopher, Chinchilla, and LaMDA. This critically demonstrates that scaling improvements have not plateaued.
 - On **BIG-bench**, PaLM 540B (5-shot) outperformed the *average human performance* across 58 common tasks.
 - A significant finding was the observation of **"discontinuous improvements"** on roughly 25% of BIG-bench tasks. For these tasks (e.g., English proverbs, logical sequence), performance steeply increased when scaling from 62B to 540B, suggesting that certain advanced capabilities *emerge* only after a critical model scale is reached.

- **Breakthrough Capabilities in Reasoning:**
 - When combined with **"chain-of-thought prompting,"** PaLM 540B achieved **state-of-the-art accuracy on multi-step reasoning tasks** (e.g., 58% on GSM8K), outperforming prior SOTA models that often used task-specific fine-tuning, domain-specific architectures, and verifiers. This highlights that generating intermediate reasoning steps can drastically improve the quality of predictions, even for tasks traditionally modeled as categorical.
 - PaLM demonstrated exploratory capabilities in explicitly interpreting and explaining complex reasoning chains, such as explaining jokes or performing logical inferences.

- **Outstanding Performance in Code Generation:**
 - PaLM 540B achieved **state-of-the-art or highly competitive results** on various coding tasks, including text-to-code (HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K-Python), code-to-code translation (TransCoder), and code repair (DeepFix).
 - Notably, PaLM achieved comparable performance to specialized code models like Davinci Codex (e.g., 76.2% pass@100 on HumanEval) despite being a generalist language model trained on significantly less Python code (2.7B tokens vs. 100B+ for Codex). Fine-tuning (PaLM-Coder) further boosted performance (88.4% pass@100 on HumanEval).
 - Scaling effects were consistent across code tasks, with performance improving from 8B to 540B parameters.

- **Strong Multilingual Understanding:**
 - Despite non-English data comprising only ~22% of the training corpus, PaLM showed **strong multilingual capabilities**. In few-shot translation, it outperformed previous LLMs and, for certain language pairs (German-English, Romanian-English), even surpassed prior supervised baselines.
 - It achieved competitive performance in multilingual natural language generation (summarization, data-to-text) and question answering (TyDiQA-GoldP), bridging the gap with finetuned SOTA encoder-decoder models in several instances.

- **Analysis of Bias, Toxicity, and Memorization:**
 - **Bias:** Accuracy on the Winogender coreference task improved with scale (PaLM 540B set new SOTA in few-shot settings), but **representational biases persisted**, with lower performance on "gotcha" (non-stereotypical) examples. Co-occurrence analysis revealed associations of certain identities (e.g., Islam with "terrorist," "violent," "radical"), consistent across model scales.
 - **Toxicity:** In open-ended generation, toxicity levels in PaLM's continuations correlated highly with prompt toxicity, suggesting a "like-to-like" response style. Toxicity slightly increased from 8B to 62B/540B models.
 - **Memorization:** Larger models exhibited a higher rate of exact training data memorization, strongly correlated with the frequency of exact repetition in the training corpus. Most memorized content was formulaic or boilerplate.
 - **Data Contamination:** Thorough analysis indicated that data contamination with evaluation benchmarks did not meaningfully inflate PaLM's reported results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PaLM paper marks a significant milestone in the field of large language models, with far-reaching implications for AI research, development, and deployment.

- **Validation of the Scaling Hypothesis:** PaLM unequivocally demonstrates that continued scaling of dense Transformer models, both in terms of parameters and efficient infrastructure, leads to substantial and even emergent improvements in few-shot learning across a broad spectrum of tasks. The observation of "discontinuous improvements" is particularly impactful, suggesting that simply making models larger can unlock entirely new capabilities that are not predictable from smaller-scale trends. This fuels further investment in larger models.
- **Pathways as a Game-Changer for Hyperscale ML:** The successful deployment of Pathways to train PaLM 540B is a testament to Google's prowess in distributed systems. Pathways addresses critical efficiency bottlenecks, enabling training on thousands of accelerators without the compromises of pipeline parallelism. This innovation is not just for language models; it represents a foundational shift that could unlock the training of even larger, potentially multimodal, models (as implied by the paper's conclusion about Pathways' broader vision). This efficient infrastructure is essential for the future of AI.
- **Advancement in Reasoning Capabilities:** The breakthrough performance in multi-step reasoning, especially when empowered by chain-of-thought prompting, is profoundly significant. It moves LLMs beyond mere pattern matching towards capabilities that appear to involve more structured "thought" processes. This finding suggests a pathway for developing more robust and interpretable AI systems, where models can not only provide answers but also explain their reasoning. This has immediate applications in education, scientific discovery, and complex problem-solving.
- **Towards Generalist AI Models:** PaLM's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse domains—natural language understanding, generation, code, and multilingual tasks—within a *single, dense* model is a strong step towards the development of generalist AI. This contrasts with approaches that rely on specialized architectures or extensive fine-tuning for each domain. A highly capable generalist model simplifies development, reduces resource requirements for new tasks, and offers broad applicability.
- **Enhanced Multilingual and Code Capabilities:** The strong performance in multilingual translation and understanding, even with a relatively modest proportion of non-English training data, highlights the cross-lingual transfer learning capabilities of large models. Similarly, PaLM's competitive performance in code generation tasks demonstrates its utility in software engineering, suggesting that LLMs can serve as powerful co-pilots for developers.
- **Heightened Importance of Responsible AI:** The detailed analysis of bias, toxicity, and memorization underscores the critical importance of responsible AI development alongside capability advancements. While the paper identifies persistent biases and potential harms, it also contributes to the methodology for analyzing these issues. This reinforces the need for ongoing research into mitigation strategies, robust ethical guidelines, and transparent model documentation (model cards, datasheets) before real-world deployment. The finding that prompt style heavily influences toxicity also provides valuable insights for safe interaction design.
- **Future Research Directions:** PaLM's success opens several important avenues for future research. The paper explicitly calls for further investigation into the optimal trade-offs between model size and training data quantity (e.g., comparing 62B parameters on 7T tokens vs. 540B on 780B tokens), the impact of data quality and mixing, architectural innovations (like combining dense and sparse models), and pre-training objectives. The Pathways system is posited as a foundation for exploring modularized, multimodal systems, potentially integrating diverse AI capabilities under a unified infrastructure.

In conclusion, PaLM is a landmark achievement, not only for its impressive performance benchmarks but also for pushing the boundaries of distributed ML infrastructure and deepening our understanding of emergent capabilities in large-scale AI. It sets a new standard for what generalist language models can achieve, while also highlighting the ongoing responsibility to address their ethical implications.
