# SpikePingpong: Spike Vision-based Fast-Slow Pingpong Robot System

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and BAAI developed SpikePingpong, a robot system that integrates spike vision with a "Fast-Slow" architecture and imitation learning for precise table tennis play. The system achieved a 12.34 mm Mean Absolute Error in ball interception, a 92% success rate for 30 cm target zones, and a 78% success rate for 100 sequential returns, outperforming human players and baseline robotic systems.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with high-speed object manipulation tasks, such as table tennis, which require millisecond-level perception and precise control under dynamic uncertainties.
- Existing robotic table tennis systems often rely on simplified physics models, struggle with real-world complexities like ball spin, or face a significant sim-to-real gap.
- Traditional vision systems have limitations like motion blur and latency, hindering accurate real-time ball trajectory prediction and precise striking for strategic placement.

## Method

- A "==Fast-Slow==" system perception framework combines rapid physics-based prediction (System 1) with refined neural error correction using high-frequency spike camera data (System 2) to predict ball trajectories.
- An "Imitation-based Motion Planning And Control Technology (IMPACT)" module learns optimal robot arm striking policies from expert demonstrations, enabling strategic ball placement beyond simple interception.
- The system decomposes the table tennis task into distinct interception and striking phases, utilizing an ABB IRB-120 6-DOF robotic arm and a multi-camera setup for vision.

## Results

- The Fast-Slow system achieved a Mean Absolute Error of **12.34** mm and an RMSE of **13.85** mm for ball hittable position prediction, significantly improving over physics-only (MAE **44.13** mm) and RNN-based methods.
- SpikePingpong demonstrated an average **92%** success rate within 30 cm target zones and **70%** within 20 cm high-precision zones for single-target returns, surpassing human average (**53%** at 30 cm) and baselines.
- The system achieved a **78%** success rate for 100 sequential returns to randomly ordered targets and showed robustness, maintaining **74%** success in out-of-distribution launcher positions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating rapid, physics-based trajectory prediction with a learning-based neural calibrator (Fast-Slow architecture) effectively compensates for real-world physical effects and sensor noise, improving contact precision.
> - Imitation learning allows robots to acquire complex, strategic manipulation skills like targeted ball placement for competitive gameplay, rather than just basic interception.
> - High-frequency spike cameras provide critical, ground-truth data for training the neural error correction system, which then operates as a lightweight predictor without real-time spike feedback during deployment.
