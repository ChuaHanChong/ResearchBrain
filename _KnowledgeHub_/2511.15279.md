# Look, Zoom, Understand: The Robotic Eyeball for Embodied Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> EyeVLA turns a pre-trained VLM into a vision-language-action policy that drives a real pan-tilt-zoom camera. Given one RGB image plus an instruction, it emits hierarchically tokenized pan/tilt/zoom actions in a single autoregressive pass, trained via pseudo-labeled grounding data, IoU-filtered iterative SFT with bounding-box chain-of-thought, and GRPO reinforcement learning. It reaches 96% task completion on 50 real scenes.

## Problem

- Vision-language models consume passively supplied static images, so they cannot decide where to look or at what scale to sense under pixel and spatial budgets.
- Fixed monocular or RGB-D cameras trade wide-area coverage against fine detail, failing on distant small text or task-decisive structures without inefficient whole-robot locomotion.
- Prior vision-language-action work targets manipulation motor commands, and active-perception systems use predefined grids, software cropping, or cascaded modules that accumulate error.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical action encoding== decomposes pan, tilt, and zoom into decimal digits, then into a {5,2,1} canonical coin basis, adding 43 action tokens to the vision-language model vocabulary.
- Pseudo-label engine maps grounding-dataset bounding boxes to camera actions via a Random Forest, then three iterative supervised fine-tuning rounds filter samples by IoU thresholds 0.7 then 0.95 and substitute ground-truth boxes.
- ==Bounding-box chain-of-thought== forces explicit target localization before action tokens; ==Group Relative Policy Optimization== (**GRPO**) then refines the policy on real demonstrations with combined IoU and per-axis error rewards.

## Results

- Final model reaches **2.04°** pan error, **1.68°** tilt error, **65.37** zoom error, and **96%** completion across 50 held-out scenes over five runs.
- Baselines lag badly: Random Forest with ground-truth boxes gets **36%**, direct Qwen2.5-VL prompting **12%**, and grounding plus hard-coded geometric mapping **70%** completion.
- Ablations confirm each part: removing IoU filtering drops completion from **72%** to **56%**, reinforcement learning lifts **92%** to **96%** and mean box IoU from **0.91** to **0.93**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete structured action tokens beat continuous regression inside autoregressive language backbones; the continuous-output variant overfit badly and scored near-zero completion on held-out scenes.
> - Forcing an interpretable intermediate localization step before action prediction grounds control decisions in visual evidence and gives a cheap quality filter for pseudo-labels.
> - Cheap synthetic labels can carry most of the learning burden if a small real-data reinforcement stage afterwards corrects hardware tolerances and label-distribution collapse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), embodied AI, and active perception in robotics. In recent years, VLMs have demonstrated advancements in tasks such as zero-shot recognition, instruction following, and multimodal reasoning by understanding visual content and textual instructions. However, these models traditionally operate as passive observers, processing static, pre-supplied images without the agency to control the visual information acquisition process.

Embodied AI and robotics require systems that can actively interact with and perceive their environment. A crucial challenge for robots is the need to acquire fine-grained visual details (e.g., reading small text, identifying specific connectors) from varying distances, often without the inefficiency of physical locomotion. Current robotic systems typically rely on fixed cameras, which inherently present a trade-off between wide-area coverage and the resolution required for minute details. This limitation means powerful VLM backbones often receive insufficient or redundant visual evidence for precision-sensitive tasks.

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have extended VLMs for embodied control, primarily focusing on robotic manipulation by predicting end-effector or joint-level motor commands to interact with objects. While these models address "how to act on the world," they largely overlook the preceding problem of "how to acquire the most informative observation."

Active perception in robotics has also been explored, with systems designed to actively select viewpoints or explore environments. Some approaches integrate VLMs for uncertainty-driven exploration or language-guided next-best-view selection. However, many of these systems rely on predefined discrete action spaces, cascaded modular designs, or software-based viewpoint search, often lacking tight, end-to-end integration between multimodal reasoning and physical camera actuation.

This work addresses the identified gap by shifting the focus from passive visual consumption to active, goal-directed visual information acquisition. It proposes a unified framework that empowers an embodied agent with a Pan-Tilt-Zoom (PTZ) camera to actively control its viewpoint and focal length based on natural language instructions. This distinguishes it from manipulation-focused VLA models and static active vision systems, positioning it as a novel approach to integrate active perceptual control directly into the VLM paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a robotic system to actively adjust its visual perception capabilities based on natural language instructions, moving beyond the limitations of passive, static image processing. Specifically, the authors aim to develop a framework that, given an initial RGB image from a PTZ camera and a natural language instruction, can predict precise pan, tilt, and zoom adjustments to acquire a maximally informative view for the specified task.

The motivation for this work arises from several identified deficiencies in current embodied AI and vision systems:

1. **Reconciling Wide-Area Coverage with Fine-Grained Detail:** Fixed RGB-D cameras struggle to simultaneously offer a broad view of an environment and the high-resolution detail necessary for tasks requiring the perception of small, distant, or intricate objects (e.g., reading labels, distinguishing small components). This fundamental limitation impedes their efficacy in complex, open-world robotic applications.
2. **Addressing Passive Perception in VLMs:** Despite significant advancements in semantic understanding, modern VLMs primarily operate on passively supplied, static images. They lack the autonomy to decide where to focus attention, at what scale to sense, or how to dynamically reallocate sensing resources to resolve ambiguities or acquire critical information. This passivity creates a bottleneck when the quality of semantic inference depends on obtaining specific, high-fidelity visual evidence.
3. **Inefficiency of Physical Locomotion:** In scenarios where fine-grained details are required, relying on an entire robot to physically move closer to a target object can be highly inefficient in terms of time, energy consumption, and the computational overhead associated with path planning and navigation. Adjusting a camera's viewpoint and zoom offers a more agile and efficient alternative for perceptual tasks.
4. **Extending VLA Capabilities to Perceptual Action:** While existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have focused on robotic manipulation, the authors argue that "before a robot can manipulate the world, it must first master perceptual action." This work defines a new task—language-guided active visual perception—where the action space directly controls camera observation rather than physical interaction, laying a foundational step for more comprehensive embodied intelligence.
5. **Overcoming Data Scarcity for Embodied Control:** Training VLA models for physical camera control traditionally requires extensive real-world action-annotated data, which is expensive and difficult to scale. A key motivation is to develop a data-efficient pipeline that can leverage pre-trained VLM knowledge and synthetic data to achieve robust real-world performance with minimal real-world samples.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to transform visual perception in embodied systems from a passive input-consumption process into an active, goal-driven mechanism, thereby enhancing the robot's ability to understand and interact with its environment more intelligently and efficiently.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named EyeVLA, is designed to enable language-guided active visual perception through a unified autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. It integrates visual perception, language understanding, and physical camera control within a single pipeline.

**Overall Approach:**
EyeVLA operates on a hardware platform called the Robotic EyeBall (REB), which consists of a two-axis pan-tilt mount and a zoomable camera. At inference, it receives an initial wide-field RGB image and a natural language instruction. The system then predicts a compact action triplet (pan, tilt, zoom adjustments) and actuates the physical camera to acquire a zoomed-in, task-relevant view in a single forward pass. The system is built upon the Qwen2.5-VL (7B) framework, adapting it for joint perception and control.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is formalized as learning a policy $\pi$ that predicts a camera adjustment action $a = (\Delta\theta_1, \Delta\theta_2, \Delta z)$—representing horizontal pan, vertical tilt, and zoom magnitude changes, respectively—from an initial image $I_0$ and language instruction $L$. The objective is to maximize the task-relevant information gain $R(I_1(a), L)$ in the resulting view $I_1$.

**Hierarchical Action Encoding:**
A core challenge is representing continuous camera adjustments ($\Delta\theta_1, \Delta\theta_2, \Delta z$) within a discrete, tokenized language modeling architecture efficiently. EyeVLA addresses this with a hierarchical action encoding scheme that minimizes token count while preserving semantic coherence.
1. **Digit Decomposition:** A target angle (or zoom value) $x$ is decomposed into its decimal digits $x = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1} x^{(\ell)} \cdot 10^\ell$, where $\ell$ denotes the digit level (units, tens, etc.).
2. **Basis Set Encoding:** Each digit $x^{(\ell)}$ is then encoded as a non-negative integer linear combination of a basis set $D = \{5, 2, 1\}$. A greedy algorithm (selecting the largest denomination not exceeding the remaining value) is used, which is proven to yield a globally optimal solution (minimal token count) for digits 0-9.
3. **Tokenization:** The encoded digits are then represented as discrete tokens within the VLM's vocabulary. This design ensures theoretical optimality in token usage per digit, constant-time encoding/decoding, and structural alignment with physical camera control (higher digits for coarse, lower for fine adjustments).
 - For example, values like pan and tilt (up to $\pm 29^\circ$) typically require at most two digit levels, achieving an average token length of 2.3, significantly more efficient than uniform discretization (e.g., one token per degree).
 - The Qwen2.5-VL tokenizer is extended with 43 new action tokens, distinguishing between positive/negative pan/tilt and positive-only zoom.

**Data Generation and Iterative Refinement:**
To overcome the high cost of manual real-world data collection, EyeVLA employs a data-efficient pipeline:
1. **Initial Data Collection:** 500 real-world demonstration samples are manually collected by human operators adjusting the REB system to achieve task-relevant views based on natural language instructions. This dataset is split into 450 training and 50 test scenes.
2. **Pseudo-Label Engine:** To scale data, pseudo-labeled samples are synthesized from existing large-scale grounding datasets (e.g., Rexverse-2M).
 - Bounding box (bbox) coordinates from these datasets are mapped to simulated pan and tilt actions by normalizing center coordinates.
 - Zoom actions are modeled using the ratio of the bbox area to the total image area, before and after isotropic cropping to maximize the bbox area (simulating an optimal zoom).
 - 50,000 samples with small target area ratios (representing distant or occluded targets where PTZ is most useful) are selected.
 - A Random Forest model, pre-trained on a subset of the real-world demonstrations (300 samples), is used to generate continuous pseudo-labels for $\Delta\theta_1, \Delta\theta_2, \Delta z$, which are then encoded into discrete action tokens.

**Training Strategy: Iterative Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
The training procedure consists of two phases:
1. **Phase 1: Iterative Supervised Alignment with CoT Guidance:**
 - The vocabulary-extended VLA model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) undergoes SFT using the generated pseudo-labeled samples. The Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder and vision-language projector are frozen to preserve pre-training knowledge, while the language model backbone and action token embeddings are updated.
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Guidance:** To aid learning, the model is trained to first predict a 2D bounding box for the target before generating action tokens. This explicit localization step provides an interpretable intermediate representation, grounding the action prediction in visual evidence.
 - **Iterative Refinement:**
 - **SFT1:** Initial training on the full synthetic dataset.
 - **SFT2:** The SFT1 model is used to infer actions on the synthetic samples. Only samples where the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes is greater than 0.7 are kept. The predicted bboxes are replaced with ground-truth bboxes in these filtered samples, and a second round of SFT is performed.
 - **SFT3:** This refinement is repeated with an even stricter IoU threshold of 0.95 to ensure higher quality pseudo-labels.
2. **Phase 2: Policy Refinement via Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - After iterative SFT, RL is applied to the 450 real-world demonstration samples to align the policy with real-world action distributions and enhance robustness against hardware tolerances and scene geometry.
 - **GRPO Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed, using group-normalized advantages and clipped probability ratios for stable training, along with a KL divergence penalty to maintain proximity to the initial VLM policy.
 - **Reward Function:** The total reward is an average of four sub-rewards: $R_{IoU}$ (IoU between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes) and $R_{\theta_1}, R_{\theta_2}, R_{zoom}$ (penalties for absolute errors in each action dimension, defined with linear ramps around tolerance thresholds).

This hybrid training pipeline efficiently transfers broad knowledge from synthetic data to grounded camera control, with real-world samples providing critical corrective feedback through RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EyeVLA involved quantitative comparisons against baselines and ablated variants, using both Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for action prediction and a human-judged Task Completion Rate (CR) on a physical robotic system.

**Experimental Settings:**
- **Dataset:** 500 real-world demonstration samples were collected, split into 450 for training (for RL phase) and 50 held-out test scenes (no scene overlap).
- **Pseudo-Data:** 50,000 pseudo-labeled synthetic samples from the Rexverse-2M dataset were used exclusively for the SFT pre-training phase.
- **Hardware:** Evaluations were conducted on the physical Robotic EyeBall system.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):** Reported for $\Delta\theta_1$ (pan), $\Delta\theta_2$ (tilt) in degrees (°), and $\Delta z$ (zoom) in raw zoom units (approximately 90 units = 1x zoom). Lower MAE indicates better performance.
 - **Task Completion Rate (CR):** Assessed through human evaluation over five independent runs on the 50 real-world test scenes. A trial was successful if the target object was clearly recognizable and centered in the resulting frame. Higher CR indicates better performance.

**Quantitative Comparison (Table 1 & 2):**
The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the EyeVLA framework and its iterative training components:

- **Overall Performance:** EyeVLA (RL3), the fully trained model including three stages of SFT and RL, achieved the lowest MAE across all action dimensions (pan: 2.04°, tilt: 1.68°, zoom: 65.37 units) and the highest average task completion rate of 96%.
- **Baseline Comparison:**
 - A traditional Machine Learning (ML) baseline (Random Forest, even with ground-truth bounding boxes as input features) achieved only a 36% CR and substantially higher MAEs across all dimensions.
 - Directly prompting Qwen2.5-VL to predict PTZ actions (Qwen2.5-VL (Direct)) yielded a 12% CR, indicating off-the-shelf VLMs lack direct physical camera control reasoning.
 - Augmenting Qwen2.5-VL with grounding followed by a hard-coded geometric mapping (Qwen2.5-VL (Grounding)) improved CR to 70%, but still significantly underperformed EyeVLA (96%). This highlights the advantage of an end-to-end learned approach for nuanced control.
- **Progressive Improvement:** The results showed a consistent improvement across the SFT stages. SFT1 already surpassed the ML baseline for $\Delta\theta_2$ and zoom MAE, demonstrating the generalization capacity derived from the VLM backbone. Subsequent SFT2 and SFT3 stages, particularly with IoU filtering, further reduced MAEs and increased CRs.

**Ablation Study:**

- **IoU-Controlled Data Filtering (Table 1, Figure 7):** Removing IoU-based filtering during SFT (e.g., SFT2(N) vs. SFT2(Y)) led to a degradation in all metrics. For instance, $\Delta\theta_1$ MAE increased from 3.97° to 4.91°, and CR dropped from 72% to 56%. This indicates that filtering out low-quality pseudo-labels based on IoU is critical for the iterative refinement process and for preventing noise from harming training. Replacing predicted bboxes with ground-truth bboxes further enhanced the model's ability to localize targets.
- **Reinforcement Learning (Table 1 & 3):** The RL post-training stage significantly contributed to the final performance. Comparing SFT3 to RL3, the $\Delta\theta_1$ error reduced from 2.94° to 2.04°, and the average CR improved from 92% to 96%. RL consistently improved the mean IoU of predicted bounding boxes (e.g., SFT3 IoU 0.91 to RL3 IoU 0.93). This suggests that RL, using real-world reward signals, helps correct biases accumulated from pseudo-label training and promotes more robust, physically grounded action outputs, especially for actions like zoom where the reward function encourages exploration within a wider tolerance window.
- **Action Encoding Analysis:** Direct continuous regression for action prediction was attempted but resulted in severe overfitting and near-zero task completion on the test set. This confirmed that the hierarchical discrete token representation is essential for the model's generalization capabilities within the language modeling architecture.

**Visualizations (Figure 3 & 4):**
Visual examples demonstrated the system's ability to effectively adjust the camera to view challenging targets, such as reading text on a tissue box or identifying a pen inside a small box, showcasing how iterative training progressively refines the model's performance in real-world scenarios.

In summary, the main findings indicate that EyeVLA, through its unified autoregressive architecture, hierarchical action encoding, and iterative SFT with CoT guidance and RL, achieves a high level of performance in language-guided active visual perception using a relatively small amount of real-world data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work establishes a new approach for language-guided active visual perception in embodied AI, offering several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Embodied Perception:** EyeVLA introduces a paradigm where visual perception is active and goal-driven, rather than passive. By enabling an embodied agent to intelligently decide "where to look and at what scale to sense," it transforms how robots acquire visual information, moving from mere consumption of static frames to closed-loop, task-aware acquisition. This capability is critical for robots operating in dynamic, open-world environments.
2. **Bridging the VLM-Embodiment Gap:** The research effectively bridges a gap between the advanced semantic understanding of Vision-Language Models and the practical requirements of embodied agents. It demonstrates that VLM capabilities can be extended beyond passive image interpretation to direct control over sensing hardware, enabling a more integrated and intelligent form of interaction with the physical world.
3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Capabilities for Robotics:** By allowing robots to dynamically adjust their perceptual focus (pan, tilt, zoom), EyeVLA offers a more efficient alternative to physical locomotion for acquiring fine-grained details. This can lead to significant savings in time, energy, and computational resources, particularly in tasks requiring precise identification of distant or small objects (e.g., inventory management, quality inspection, assembly, medical assistance).
4. **Data-Efficient Learning for Embodied Control:** The proposed data-efficient training pipeline, which combines pseudo-label generation from large grounding datasets with iterative IoU-controlled refinement and reinforcement learning on a small set of real-world samples, provides a practical pathway for developing sophisticated embodied policies without the prohibitively high cost of large-scale real-world data collection. Achieving 96% task completion with only 500 real-world samples is a notable demonstration of this efficiency.
5. **Unified Multimodal Reasoning and Control:** The integration of visual perception, language understanding, and physical camera control within a single autoregressive Vision-Language-Action model represents a step towards truly unified multimodal reasoning and control. This approach minimizes accumulated errors often seen in modular, cascaded systems, leading to more robust and reliable performance.
6. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** The concept of a "Robotic Eyeball" and the demonstrated capabilities of EyeVLA lay a foundation for developing more adaptable and intelligent embodied AI systems. Future research can build upon this to explore more complex active perception strategies, multi-modal sensor integration, and dynamic task-specific information gathering, ultimately contributing to robots that can understand and interact with their environment with greater autonomy and precision.
