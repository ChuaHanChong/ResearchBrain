# RoboFactory: Exploring Embodied Agent Collaboration with Compositional Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboFactory presents a framework for multi-agent embodied systems that automates training data generation through the application of compositional constraints, including logical, spatial, and temporal rules. The system, comprising RoboBrain and RoboChecker modules, enables multiple robots to collaborate on complex manipulation tasks, demonstrating improved success rates and providing insights into policy architecture design on a new multi-agent benchmark.

## Problem

- Gathering training data for multi-agent robotic systems is inefficient and costly, often requiring simultaneous remote operation by multiple human operators.
- Multi-agent systems face increased complexity in task planning, space management, and agent scheduling compared to single-agent systems.
- Existing single-agent methods (e.g., Behavioral Cloning, Offline RL) and LLM-based planning approaches lack the structured constraints necessary for coordinating multiple physical agents effectively.

## Method

- **RoboFactory** introduces a compositional constraints framework comprising logical, spatial, and temporal constraints to guide multi-agent interaction, collision avoidance, and action synchronization.
- The framework features RoboBrain, an LLM-based module that generates sub-goals and textual constraints from a global task description, and RoboChecker, which validates robot actions against these constraints.
- This architecture automates the generation of multi-agent training data without requiring simultaneous human operators, improving data collection efficiency.

## Results

- Introduction of spatial and temporal constraints significantly improved multi-agent task success rates in the RoboFactory benchmark.
- Diffusion policies trained on data generated with compositional constraints showed reasonable performance across multi-agent tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing specific constraints led to significant performance degradation, demonstrating their critical role in data quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compositional constraints are essential for generating high-quality multi-agent training data, with temporal constraints particularly improving task execution and reducing episode length.
> - The choice of policy architecture impacts multi-agent performance; local view and separate policy architectures can achieve better results than shared policies in certain tasks.
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can be leveraged to effectively generate sub-goals and textual compositional constraints for complex multi-agent tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

In today's rapidly evolving field of robotics and AI, there's a growing need for multiple embodied agents to collaborate effectively on complex tasks that are beyond the capabilities of single robots. The RoboFactory paper addresses this challenge by introducing a novel approach to multi-agent embodied systems through compositional constraints.

As shown in the figure above, RoboFactory enables multiple robots to collaborate on complex tasks like photographing an object, by incorporating logical, spatial, and temporal constraints. These constraints guide the robots in how they should interact with objects, avoid collisions with each other, and coordinate their actions over time.

Unlike previous approaches that focus primarily on single-agent systems or high-level planning, RoboFactory bridges the gap between high-level task planning and low-level control in multi-agent scenarios. The work aims to address a critical bottleneck in developing collaborative robotic systems: the inefficient process of generating training data, which traditionally requires multiple human operators to simultaneously control robots.

### 2. The Challenge of Multi-Agent Embodied Systems

Training policies for collaborative robotic tasks presents unique challenges that are not adequately addressed by existing methods:

1. **Data Collection Inefficiency**: Gathering training data for multiple robots requires simultaneous remote operation by multiple human operators, making it costly and time-consuming.

2. **Increased Complexity**: Multi-agent systems face added complexity in task planning, space management, and agent scheduling compared to single-agent systems.

3. **Coordination Requirements**: Effective collaboration requires robots to maintain logical consistency, avoid collisions, and synchronize their actions temporally.

Existing approaches like Behavioral Cloning (BC) and Offline Reinforcement Learning (ORL) have proven effective for single-agent systems but don't translate well to multi-agent scenarios. Similarly, while Large Language Models (LLMs) show promise in generating plans and trajectories, they lack the structured constraints necessary for coordinating multiple physical agents.

### 3. Compositional Constraints Framework

The core innovation of RoboFactory is its compositional constraints framework, which consists of three types of constraints:

1. **Logical Constraints** define rules and relationships governing valid robot behaviors, focusing on high-level logic such as how robots should interact with objects. For example, a gripper might need to be perpendicular to an object for proper grasping.

2. **Spatial Constraints** specify physical or spatial boundaries to prevent collisions and ensure safe operation. These are essential when multiple robots occupy the same workspace.

3. **Temporal Constraints** impose time-related rules to enable efficient collaboration through synchronization, deadlines, and sequential dependencies. These determine when agents should act simultaneously or in sequence.

These constraints work together to enable safe, efficient, and logical multi-agent collaboration while automating the data generation process. By incorporating these constraints, RoboFactory addresses the key challenges in multi-agent robotic manipulation.

### 4. RoboFactory Framework Architecture

The RoboFactory framework consists of two primary modules:

1. **RoboBrain**: This module takes a global task description and generates sub-goals for each agent along with textual compositional constraints. It leverages the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to understand the task requirements and break them down into manageable sub-tasks for different agents.

2. **RoboChecker**: This module constructs constraint interfaces based on the textual constraints and the current multi-agent state. It ensures that agents don't violate any constraints while executing the generated trajectories.

The workflow begins with a task description (e.g., "Grab the steak and use the camera to photograph it with 4 embodied agents"). The RoboBrain processes this task, along with multi-agent information (observations and states), to generate subgoals and constraints. These constraints are then converted into interface representations, which the RoboChecker uses to monitor and validate robot actions.

This architecture enables the automated generation of multi-agent training data without requiring simultaneous human operators, significantly improving efficiency in data collection.

### 5. Constraint Interface Generation

To transform abstract textual constraints into concrete criteria that can interact with the real world, RoboFactory employs four physical representation methods:

1. **Interaction Position and Direction**: For logical constraints, the system identifies key interaction points and directions for proper manipulation of objects.

2. **3D Occupancy Construction**: For spatial constraints, the system creates 3D occupancy maps to represent regions that robots should avoid to prevent collisions.

3. **Dynamic Occupancy Modeling**: For temporal constraints, the system models how occupancy changes over time to enable coordinated movement.

These representations allow the system to convert high-level constraints like "Agent 1's gripper is perpendicular to the steak" into specific geometric criteria that can be verified during execution.

The constraint interface is formalized with a set of functions that can be automatically invoked to check violations:

- Interaction validity (VI): Checks if an agent is interacting with an object correctly
- Direction validation (VD): Ensures the interaction direction is appropriate
- Spatial occupancy (VSO): Prevents collisions among agents
- Temporal scheduling (VS): Manages synchronization and sequencing

### 6. The RoboFactory Benchmark

To evaluate the effectiveness of compositional constraints and multi-agent policies, the authors created the RoboFactory benchmark:

This benchmark consists of collaborative manipulation tasks requiring multiple robots, including:

1. **Align Camera**: One agent picks up an object while others align a camera to it
2. **Place Food**: One agent lifts a pot lid while another places food in the pot
3. **Stack Cube**: Agents work together to stack colored cubes
4. **Lift Barrier**: Multiple agents simultaneously lift a barrier

The benchmark is built on the ManiSkill simulator, providing a standardized platform for evaluating different approaches to multi-agent collaboration. The tasks are designed to require different types of coordination, from sequential actions to simultaneous movements, testing the full range of collaborative capabilities.

### 7. Experimental Results

The experiments validate the necessity of compositional constraints in multi-agent data generation and evaluate the performance of diffusion policies on the RoboFactory benchmark:

1. **Constraint Effectiveness**: The introduction of spatial and temporal constraints significantly improved task success rates. Temporal constraints were particularly important for enhancing data quality by streamlining task execution and reducing episode length.

2. **Policy Performance**: Diffusion policies trained on data generated with compositional constraints showed reasonable performance on multi-agent tasks. However, the results also highlighted challenges in facilitating effective collaboration among multiple agents and learning long-term temporal dependencies.

3. **Constraint Ablation**: Removing certain constraints led to significant performance degradation, confirming their essential role in generating high-quality training data.

### 8. Policy Architecture Exploration

The research evaluated different architectural designs for multi-agent systems based on imitation learning:

1. **Global View + Shared Policy**: All agents share the same policy and observe the entire scene.
2. **Local View + Shared Policy**: Agents share a policy but each has its own local view.
3. **Global View + Separate Policy**: Each agent has its own policy but observes the entire scene.
4. **Local View + Separate Policy**: Each agent has its own policy and local view.

The results revealed that local view and separate policy architectures achieve better performance in certain tasks. Shared policy architectures struggled when they needed to infer which agent should currently execute an action. This highlights the importance of policy architecture design in multi-agent systems.

### 9. Significance and Impact

The RoboFactory framework makes several significant contributions to the field of multi-agent embodied systems:

1. **Automated Data Collection**: The framework addresses a key challenge in training multi-agent systems by automating the data collection process, reducing the need for multiple human operators.

2. **Benchmark for Research**: The RoboFactory benchmark provides a standardized platform for evaluating and comparing different methods for multi-agent manipulation.

3. **Practical Applications**: The focus on real-world decision-making and low-level manipulation makes the research relevant to various applications, including manufacturing, medical assistance, and domestic robotics.

4. **Safety and Efficiency**: The compositional constraints ensure that multi-agent systems operate safely and efficiently, preventing collisions and optimizing task execution.

5. **Insights for Policy Design**: The exploration of different policy architectures offers valuable guidance for designing future multi-agent systems.

### 10. Conclusion

RoboFactory represents a significant advancement in the development of collaborative robotic systems by introducing compositional constraints that enable efficient generation of training data for multi-agent tasks. The framework bridges the gap between high-level planning and low-level control, providing a comprehensive solution for training robots to work together effectively.

The research demonstrates that by incorporating logical, spatial, and temporal constraints, it's possible to automate the data generation process for multi-agent systems without requiring multiple human operators. This not only improves efficiency but also enhances the quality of the generated data, leading to better-performing robotic policies.

As robotic systems become increasingly prevalent in various domains, the ability to coordinate multiple robots efficiently will be crucial. RoboFactory provides a foundation for developing such collaborative systems, paving the way for more complex and capable robotic assistants in the future.
