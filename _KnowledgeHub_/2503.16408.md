# RoboFactory: Exploring Embodied Agent Collaboration with Compositional Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboFactory presents a framework for multi-agent embodied systems that automates training data generation through the application of compositional constraints, including logical, spatial, and temporal rules. The system, comprising RoboBrain and RoboChecker modules, enables multiple robots to collaborate on complex manipulation tasks, demonstrating improved success rates and providing insights into policy architecture design on a new multi-agent benchmark.

## Problem

- Gathering training data for multi-agent robotic systems is inefficient and costly, often requiring simultaneous remote operation by multiple human operators.
- Multi-agent systems face increased complexity in task planning, space management, and agent scheduling compared to single-agent systems.
- Existing single-agent methods (e.g., Behavioral Cloning, Offline RL) and LLM-based planning approaches lack the structured constraints necessary for coordinating multiple physical agents effectively.

## Method

- **RoboFactory** introduces a compositional constraints framework comprising logical, spatial, and temporal constraints to guide multi-agent interaction, collision avoidance, and action synchronization.
- The framework features RoboBrain, an LLM-based module that generates sub-goals and textual constraints from a global task description, and RoboChecker, which validates robot actions against these constraints.
- This architecture automates the generation of multi-agent training data without requiring simultaneous human operators, improving data collection efficiency.

## Results

- Introduction of spatial and temporal constraints significantly improved multi-agent task success rates in the RoboFactory benchmark.
- Diffusion policies trained on data generated with compositional constraints showed reasonable performance across multi-agent tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing specific constraints led to significant performance degradation, demonstrating their critical role in data quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compositional constraints are essential for generating high-quality multi-agent training data, with temporal constraints particularly improving task execution and reducing episode length.
> - The choice of policy architecture impacts multi-agent performance; local view and separate policy architectures can achieve better results than shared policies in certain tasks.
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can be leveraged to effectively generate sub-goals and textual compositional constraints for complex multi-agent tasks.
