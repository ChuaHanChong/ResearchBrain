# Code as Worlds: Agentic Discovery of Executable World Representations for Physical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Code-as-World represents physical scenes as executable code covering composition, dynamics and appearance. An agent builds these worlds from text or real video with a propose-instantiate-execute-render-verify loop, refining each hypothesis against the evidence. Verified worlds supply quantitative supervision for training vision-language models. Code-as-World-VL-9B scores 55.4 MRA on QuantiPhy, above Gemini-3.1 Flash at 54.8.

## Problem

- Video generators predict future pixels without representing causes, so contradictory internal explanations can yield equally plausible futures, and a visually plausible future is not necessarily physically correct.
- 3D reconstruction preserves geometry and appearance but entangles persistent structure with dynamics, so it does not explain physical behavior such as why an object falls when support is removed.
- Language captures semantics but cannot precisely encode continuous quantities like trajectories, contacts and physical parameters, and real videos rarely carry labels for hidden metric quantities such as velocity or mass.

## Method

- ==Executable World Representation==: a world is a ==code triple== of physical composition (objects, geometry, mass, friction), dynamic evolution (initial states, forces, events) and visual appearance (camera, lighting, materials), run through a physics simulator.
- ==Agentic discovery loop==: an LLM agent proposes or edits the world, compiles it to simulator parameters, executes it, renders and projects depth, masks and tracks, then verifies key frames and feeds discrepancies back, up to K rounds.
- ==Two-phase VLM training==: supervised image-space measurement first, then ==GRPO== on world-level questions from text-driven and video-driven verified worlds, rewarding scale-normalized numeric accuracy plus unit correctness and format.

## Results

- On QuantiPhy-validation, Code-as-World-VL-4B, 9B and 27B (reasoning) reach **50.6**, **55.4** and **58.6** average MRA, beating Gemini-3.1 Flash (**54.8**), ChatGPT-5.1 (**48.4**) and Qwen3-VL-32B (**40.2**).
- With a matched five-evaluation budget, the iterative discovery loop beats Best-of-5 independent sampling on Visual Alignment, Object IoU, Traj-ADE and Accuracy@2%D, using verifier signals distinct from the reported metrics.
- The image-space-only 4B model scores **44.2** average MRA. Adding executable-world supervision lifts the 4B to **50.6**, which shows the world-derived data carries the gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Code is a useful physical representation because it is compositional, editable and quantitatively exact. Simulator execution turns it into a checkable hypothesis and a source of exact labels.
> - Iterating on verifier feedback uses compute better than independent sampling, so build verification into world construction rather than only generating many candidates.
> - Simulator-verified synthetic worlds can supervise metric perception in a small VLM enough to beat larger proprietary models. The caution is that the loop may settle on a plausible but mechanistically wrong world.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Understanding and reasoning about the physical world is a fundamental aspect of artificial intelligence. Existing approaches to modeling the physical world primarily fall into three categories: pixel-level prediction, geometric reconstruction, and language-based abstraction. Each of these paradigms captures certain aspects of the physical world but exhibits limitations in providing a complete representation that simultaneously supports semantic understanding, structural composition, and temporal prediction with underlying mechanisms.

- **Pixel-level models:** Video generative models, for instance, excel at predicting future observations, generating realistic and diverse content. However, they often optimize for visual plausibility without explicitly representing the underlying physical causes of changes. This can lead to ambiguities where multiple contradictory internal explanations might yield visually similar, but not necessarily physically correct, futures. Such models struggle to disentangle factors like object identity, physical state transitions, interactions, or viewpoint changes.

- **3D reconstruction models:** Approaches involving 3D reconstruction, inverse graphics, and dynamic scene representations can faithfully reproduce observations by recovering geometry, viewpoint, and appearance. While these provide strong constraints on information preservation, they do not inherently guarantee interpretability. A latent representation might reproduce an observation without explicitly revealing the causal factors governing the world's evolution, such as why an object falls or how it interacts with others.

- **Natural language models:** Language-based representations capture high-level concepts in a compact, compositional, and transferable manner, enabling semantic understanding. They can describe entities, actions, and semantic relations while abstracting irrelevant visual details. However, natural language is inherently limited in expressing the continuous and quantitative aspects of physical states, such as precise geometry, trajectories, contact relationships, or physical parameters. It serves as an effective semantic interface but not a complete physical state representation.

The paper argues that physical intelligence requires a representation that integrates these capabilities: possessing semantics akin to language, structure like reconstruction, and the ability to model temporal evolution similar to generative models. Such a representation should go beyond merely describing or reproducing the world to explicitly capture the entities, states, and mechanisms that generate observations. This work posits that existing vision-language models, despite their descriptive capabilities, frequently lack explicit representations of underlying mechanisms—like object states, physical parameters, and governing dynamics—which are crucial for robust physical reasoning and responding to interventions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce **Code-as-World**, a novel paradigm for representing physical worlds through executable world representations (EWRs). The authors motivate this by highlighting the need for representations that can capture the "mechanisms" underlying physical phenomena, rather than merely describing or predicting "phenomena" observed at the surface level. Humans reason about the physical world using abstract concepts (objects, mass, gravity) and can generalize knowledge across different situations, which necessitates compact and generalizable representations that abstract away incidental observational details while preserving mechanistic structure.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:
1. **Addressing the Phenomenon–Mechanism Dichotomy:** Modern vision-language models can describe physical events but often lack an explicit representation of the underlying causal mechanisms. This limitation hinders their ability to reliably reason about how the world evolves or responds to interventions. Code-as-World aims to bridge this gap by providing a representation that explicitly specifies physical composition, dynamic evolution, and visual appearance as executable code.
2. **Developing Compact, Quantitatively Grounded, and Controllable Abstractions:** The goal is to move beyond pixel-level, latent-feature, or natural-language representations to an executable code format. This code is intended to be compact, quantitatively grounded (explicitly representing states, physical parameters, and governing dynamics), and controllable (allowing inspection, modification, and execution of different components).
3. **Enabling Agentic Discovery of EWRs:** Recognizing that obtaining such representations from incomplete multimodal observations (e.g., natural-language descriptions or real-world videos) is an inverse problem, the work aims to develop an "agentic discovery loop." This loop is inspired by abductive reasoning, where an agent iteratively proposes, executes, renders, verifies, and refines executable world hypotheses to best explain the available evidence.
4. **Providing Scalable Physical Supervision for Vision-Language Models:** A concrete application and motivation is to use these verified executable worlds to provide scalable physical supervision for training vision-language models (VLMs) on quantitative physical reasoning tasks. These tasks require models to connect visual evidence with metric states of the underlying world, which often lack real-world annotations. Code-as-World aims to generate this crucial supervision.

In essence, the research seeks to establish executable code as a foundational representation for physical intelligence, enabling models to "understand" the physical world by capturing its underlying mechanisms and providing a framework for their automated discovery and utilization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Executable World Representations (EWR)

Code-as-World represents the physical world through executable code, termed Executable World Representations (EWRs), denoted as $p = (C, E, A)$. This structured and compositional abstraction aims to explicitly represent entities, relations, physical parameters, and events as operations over states, abstracting incidental observational details.

- **Physical composition (C):** This component specifies the static and relatively stable physical properties of entities in a scene. It includes objects, their geometry, metric dimensions, and physical attributes such as mass, friction, and gravity. Environmental structures (e.g., floors, walls) are also included as static physical entities capable of participating in support, contact, and collision.
- **Dynamic evolution (E):** This describes how the world changes over time, including initial object states, temporal changes, key events, and simulation duration. Executing this component with the composition leads to a complete state trajectory, detailing contacts, collisions, velocity changes, and termination conditions.
- **Visual appearance (A):** This component describes how the physical world is observed and presented. It includes camera parameters, backgrounds, materials, lighting, frame rates, resolutions, and rendering configurations. It determines the visual presentation of the physical trajectory without altering the underlying physical process.

This implementation allows for inspection, modification, and independent execution of different EWR components, providing a foundation for downstream reasoning, simulation, verification, and data generation. MuJoCo is utilized as the underlying physical simulation platform.

#### 3.2. Agentic Discovery Loop

To construct EWRs from incomplete multimodal observations, Code-as-World employs an agentic discovery loop inspired by abductive reasoning. This loop iteratively refines EWR hypotheses against input evidence.

- **Evidence Construction:**
 - **Text-driven world construction:** Given natural language descriptions, an agent extracts explicit entities, spatial relations, physical events, and intended outcomes as structured semantic evidence. Combining physical priors with default conditions, it initializes an EWR hypothesis. For downstream applications, a video generation model enriches the resulting executable world with visual realism (objects, materials, backgrounds, lighting) while maintaining physical alignment.
 - **Video-driven world abstraction:** For real videos, the agent extracts visual evidence such as depth maps, instance masks (using SAM3), and object tracks (using VGGT-Omega). A 3D object generation model (SAM3D) constructs meshes for segmented objects. This evidence helps recover spatial positions, scales, and dynamic states, forming an executable 3D scene. Candidate EWR rollouts are projected back into the input view and refined based on consistency with observed geometry, depth, masks, and trajectories.

- **Agentic Discovery Loop (Propose–Instantiate–Execute–Render–Verify):**
 1. **Propose/Update:** At each iteration, a discovery agent (an LLM) proposes or updates an EWR based on the multimodal evidence, current hypothesis, and structured feedback from the previous iteration.
 2. **Instantiate:** The EWR is compiled into simulator-ready parameters, conforming to the specific simulator interface (e.g., MuJoCo).
 3. **Execute:** The simulator executes these parameters to produce a complete state trajectory, explicitly recording object states, contacts, collisions, and event outcomes.
 4. **Render & Project:** The trajectory is rendered into predicted visual observations. For video input, simulated states are projected into depth maps, instance masks, and image-plane trajectories.
 5. **Verify:** Predicted and input evidence are compared at key frames. For text input, verification focuses on semantic and physical constraints. For video input, it compares RGB appearance, depth, masks, and trajectories. Discrepancies are aggregated into structured feedback (∆) which guides the agent's next proposal.
The loop continues until the EWR sufficiently explains the input parsimoniously or the iteration budget is exhausted, at which point the hypothesis is either accepted or rejected. The same simulation interface is used across modalities to produce synchronized videos and physical states.

#### 3.3. Application to Quantitative Physical Reasoning

The verified executable worlds generated by Code-as-World serve as scalable physical supervision for training vision-language models (VLMs) on quantitative physical reasoning. This involves inferring physical quantities (e.g., object size, velocity, acceleration) from monocular videos, a task often lacking real-world annotations.

- **Problem Formulation:** Given a monocular video and a quantitative physical question, the model predicts a numerical answer. For world-space queries, a reference quantity with a known world-space value is provided for metric calibration.
- **Image-Space Measurement Grounding:** Pixel-level supervision is generated from existing visual datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, RefCLEF, GOT-10K) by converting bounding boxes, masks, and object tracks into quantitative question-answer pairs (e.g., object extent, position, displacement, velocity, acceleration in pixel units). Models are initially trained on this data (L_pix) using supervised fine-tuning to localize, measure, and track objects.
- **World-Space Physical Calibration from Verified Executable Worlds:** After image-space grounding, verified EWRs provide direct generation of quantitative question-answer pairs with exact world-space labels. Object geometry, camera parameters, and time-varying physical states from EWRs allow precise answers. These questions (D_text from text-driven, D_video from video-driven) are combined for training. Models are optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), with a reward function that combines scale-normalized numerical accuracy with auxiliary rewards for unit correctness and response format. Text-driven worlds offer exact numerical supervision, while video-driven worlds provide visual generalization to real-world appearances. Joint training leverages both for accurate physical supervision and robust visual generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Agentic Discovery Performance

The agentic discovery loop demonstrates iterative improvements in reconstructing executable worlds from video inputs.
- **Quantitative Improvements:** Over five rounds, static quality and most motion-fidelity metrics consistently improve. The agentic discovery loop (after five rounds) outperforms the "Best-of-5" independent sampling baseline across multiple metrics: Visual Alignment, Object IoU, Traj-ADE (trajectory position error), Velocity-ADE (inter-frame displacement error), and Accuracy@2%D (percentage of valid observations within 0.02D). For instance, Visual Alignment increases from 0.54 (one-shot) to 0.62 (round 5), and Traj-ADE decreases from 4.5 to 1.5. This indicates a more effective use of computational resources compared to independent sampling.
- **Qualitative Analysis and Controllability:** Code-as-World successfully generates physically grounded videos from semantic descriptions and abstracts executable worlds from real-world videos (Figures 6 and 7). Moreover, the recovered EWRs allow for controllable resimulation. Users can modify physical quantities (e.g., initial velocity direction), initial conditions, or camera configurations, and the system renders these edited rollouts into realistic videos while preserving the specified physical evolution (Figure 4).
- **Sim-to-Real Fidelity:** Text-driven sim-to-real video generation shows improved distributional realism (lower JEDi and TRAJAN scores) compared to raw simulator renders, while maintaining motion agreement with original simulator trajectories. This indicates that the video generation model enhances visual realism without altering the underlying physical evolution encoded by the EWR.

#### 4.2. Image-Space Measurement Grounding

Code-as-World-VL models achieve robust performance in image-space measurements.
- **Performance:** Both 4B and 9B "Image-Space" variants show strong image-plane measurement capabilities across referring-expression datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, RefCLEF) and GOT-10K.
- **Reinforcement from World-Space Training:** The full Code-as-World-VL models (which include World-Space training) further improve performance on all five image-space benchmarks compared to their "Image-Space only" counterparts. This suggests that World-Space training not only preserves but strengthens the underlying image-space grounding capability, indicating a complementary relationship between the two training stages.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Physical Reasoning on QuantiPhy

Code-as-World-VL demonstrates strong and competitive performance on the QuantiPhy benchmark for quantitative physical reasoning.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Code-as-World-VL-9B achieves the best average performance among direct-answer variants, surpassing substantially larger open-weight models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) and competing with leading proprietary models like Gemini-3.1 Flash. The 4B variant also outperforms several larger open-weight baselines.
- **Reasoning Capability:** A larger, reasoning-enabled Code-as-World-VL-27B variant further exceeds the direct-answer 9B variant and the strongest proprietary baseline (average MRA of 58.6 vs. Gemini-3.1 Flash's 54.8). This model produces chain-of-thought reasoning traces, illustrating its measurement and calibration steps (Figure 10), and suggesting that the framework scales effectively to larger reasoning models.
- **Consistent Strength Across Categories:** The consistent performance across QuantiPhy's 2S, 2D, 3S, and 3D subsets indicates that the gains are not limited to specific quantities or scene configurations, but rather reflect a more reliable connection between visual measurements and metric scale/motion.
- **Parameter Scaling:** The average MRA on QuantiPhy increases with model size, from the 4B to 9B direct-answer variants, and further with the 27B reasoning-enabled model, illustrating favorable scaling behavior (Figure 9).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the three data sources (Image-Space measurement data D_pix, text-driven executable worlds D_text, and video-driven executable worlds D_video) confirms their complementary benefits for quantitative physical reasoning.
- **Complementary Benefits:** For both 4B and 9B models, adding either D_text or D_video to D_pix improves QuantiPhy performance over using D_pix alone. Combining both D_text and D_video yields the best average performance, indicating that exact simulator supervision and real-video alignment provide distinct and complementary signals beyond image-space grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Code-as-World" paradigm represents a shift in physical intelligence research, moving beyond purely descriptive or predictive models towards representations that explicitly capture the underlying mechanisms of the physical world. This approach, which expresses physical composition, dynamic evolution, and visual appearance as executable code, offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Mechanism-Grounded Reasoning:** By providing compact, quantitatively grounded, and controllable abstractions of physical worlds, Code-as-World enables more explicit, verifiable, and generalizable physical intelligence. This contrasts with previous approaches that largely infer physical understanding through task-specific outputs, leaving the underlying representations implicit. The executable nature allows agents to query, intervene on, and verify world states directly, fostering a deeper understanding of "why" phenomena occur.
- **Scalable Physical Supervision:** The ability to automatically discover and verify executable world representations from multimodal observations (text or video) allows for the scalable generation of high-quality physical supervision. This addresses a significant challenge in quantitative physical reasoning tasks, where real-world annotations for underlying physical quantities are scarce. The success of Code-as-World-VL on QuantiPhy, outperforming proprietary models, highlights the practical value of this synthetic supervision for training vision-language models.
- **Enhanced Controllable Generation:** Code-as-World decouples world dynamics from visual rendering. This separation offers the potential for physically consistent video generation, allowing for coherent long-horizon generation and controllable interventions without needing to reconstruct every visual variant. The ability to edit physical parameters and initial conditions in the executable world and then re-render them realistically could lead to more flexible content creation and simulation.
- **Advancement in Embodied Intelligence:** The structured and agent-native nature of EWRs could support a "System-2-like" form of embodied intelligence. Agents might construct explicit world models, reason over possible futures, and deliberate on actions before execution, rather than relying solely on reactive policies. By providing a shared abstraction across passive observations, simulated environments, and real-world interaction, EWRs could facilitate the transfer of physical knowledge across different settings, potentially accelerating scalable embodied learning.
- **Extensibility to Broader Physical Phenomena:** While the current implementation primarily focuses on rigid-body dynamics, the Code-as-World paradigm is designed to be extensible. Code's expressiveness could provide a common interface to specialized simulators for fluids, deformable bodies, combustion, fracture, and other complex physical phenomena. This opens avenues for connecting decades of progress in graphics and computational physics to broader physical intelligence.
