# Compositional 3D-aware Video Generation with LLM Director

> [!summary] Summary
> The C3V framework, developed by Microsoft Research Asia and Chinese universities, introduces a compositional 3D-aware approach to text-to-video generation by explicitly composing concepts in 3D space. This method achieves higher Q-Align and CLIP scores compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, offering improved control over individual elements and complex scene handling.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-video generation methods, largely operating in 2D, struggle with precise control over individual elements like object appearances, motions, and camera viewpoints.
- Maintaining consistent object representations across different viewpoints and complex motions is challenging for current 2D-based video generation systems.
- Generating videos with multiple objects often leads to difficulties in maintaining coherent spatial relationships and independent control over each object.

## Method

- Introduces Compositional 3D-aware Video generation (C3V), a framework that explicitly composes concepts in 3D space rather than operating purely in 2D.
- Employs a three-stage pipeline: LLM-driven task decomposition, multi-modal LLM-based trajectory generation lifted to 3D, and fine-grained composition using 2D diffusion priors and ==Score Distillation== Sampling.
- Leverages Large Language Models as 'directors' to orchestrate the decomposition of complex textual prompts and the generation/composition of 3D representations.

## Results

- C3V achieved higher Q-Align and CLIP scores compared to state-of-the-art methods, including 4D-FY, Comp4D, and VideoCrafter2, indicating improved visual quality and semantic consistency.
- The framework demonstrated flexible control over individual concepts, robust handling of complex scenes with multiple objects, and controllable camera movements due to its 3D representation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of each component, particularly highlighting the effectiveness of the step-by-step trajectory estimation and fine-grained composition refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adopting a compositional approach in 3D space, similar to human perception, is crucial for fine-grained control and consistency in video generation.
> - Large Language Models can effectively decompose complex prompts into manageable components and orchestrate object trajectories in 3D environments.
> - Combining 3D scene representation (3D Gaussian Splatting) with 2D diffusion priors (Score Distillation Sampling) allows for both spatial consistency and realistic appearance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The field of text-to-video generation has rapidly evolved with the advancement of diffusion models and large language models. However, existing approaches often struggle with precise control over individual elements within generated videos - controlling object appearances, motions, and camera viewpoints remains challenging. This paper introduces a paradigm shift by proposing Compositional 3D-aware Video generation (C3V), a framework that explicitly composes concepts in 3D space rather than operating purely in 2D.

The research represents a collaboration between Microsoft Research Asia and leading Chinese universities, combining industrial expertise in large-scale model development with academic innovation in algorithmic design. The approach leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) as "directors" to orchestrate the generation and composition of 3D representations, enabling unprecedented control over video generation from textual descriptions.

### 2. Background and Motivation

Traditional text-to-video generation methods face several fundamental limitations. Most existing approaches operate in 2D space, making it difficult to maintain consistent object representations across different viewpoints and complex motions. When generating videos with multiple objects, these methods often struggle to maintain coherent spatial relationships and individual object control.

The human understanding of the world is inherently compositional - we perceive scenes as combinations of objects, backgrounds, and motions that can be manipulated independently. This compositional nature suggests that video generation should also operate on individual concepts that can be combined flexibly.

Recent advances in 3D representation learning, particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have demonstrated the ability to create high-quality 3D scenes from 2D observations. Simultaneously, Large Language Models have shown remarkable capabilities in understanding complex instructions and decomposing tasks into manageable components. The C3V framework capitalizes on these developments by combining LLM-driven task decomposition with 3D compositional generation.

### 3. The C3V Framework

The C3V framework operates through three distinct stages, each addressing a specific aspect of compositional video generation:

1. **Task Decomposition**: Breaking down complex textual prompts into individual concepts
2. **Trajectory Generation**: Determining coarse-grained motion paths for objects
3. **Fine-grained Composition**: Refining object placement and appearance using 2D diffusion priors

This multi-stage approach allows the system to handle complex scenes with multiple objects while maintaining fine-grained control over each element. The framework's strength lies in its ability to leverage the best aspects of each component - LLMs for high-level reasoning, 3D representations for spatial consistency, and 2D diffusion models for realistic appearance.

### 4. Task Decomposition with LLM

The first stage involves decomposing a complex textual description into sub-prompts that describe individual concepts. For example, the prompt "In a Magician's magical cabin alone in a serene forest, an alien walking on the floor" would be decomposed into:

- Scene: "a Magician's magical cabin alone in a serene forest"
- Object: "an alien"
- Motion: "walking on the floor"

The LLM (specifically GPT-4V) serves as the task decomposer, using carefully crafted instructions to identify and separate these concepts. Once decomposed, each concept is processed by specialized expert models:

- **3D Scene Generation**: Creates 3D representations of static environments using pre-trained models
- **Object Generation**: Generates 3D object representations using 3D Gaussian Splatting
- **Motion Generation**: Retrieves appropriate motion sequences from libraries (e.g., SMPL-X parameters for human motion)

This decomposition approach enables modular control over each aspect of the scene, allowing for independent manipulation of backgrounds, objects, and motions.

### 5. Trajectory Generation

The second stage focuses on determining how objects move through the 3D space. Rather than relying on simple kinematic equations or pre-defined motion patterns, the framework employs a multi-modal LLM to estimate realistic trajectories based on the scene context.

The trajectory generation process uses a step-by-step estimation approach:

1. **Starting Point Estimation**: The LLM identifies the initial position of the object
2. **Ending Point Estimation**: Determines where the object should conclude its motion
3. **Intermediate Point Generation**: Creates a smooth path between start and end points

This decomposed approach proves more effective than direct trajectory estimation, as shown in the comparison figure. The LLM receives visual context through bounding box representations of objects, enabling it to make spatially-aware decisions about movement patterns.

The trajectory is initially estimated in 2D pixel coordinates but is subsequently lifted to 3D world coordinates using monocular depth estimation. This process ensures that object movements respect the 3D geometry of the scene.

### 6. Fine-grained Composition

The final stage refines the composition using 2D diffusion priors to ensure realistic appearance and natural object interactions. This stage addresses the challenge of making the composed 3D scene appear natural and consistent with real-world image distributions.

The fine-grained composition process employs Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), a technique that distills knowledge from pre-trained 2D diffusion models into 3D representations. The optimization focuses on three key transformation parameters:

- **Scale**: Adjusting object sizes to fit naturally in the scene
- **Location**: Fine-tuning object positions for realistic placement
- **Rotation**: Optimizing object orientations for natural appearance

The SDS process works by rendering the 3D scene from multiple viewpoints and comparing these renderings against what a pre-trained diffusion model (such as Stable Diffusion) would expect for the given text prompt. The gradients from this comparison are used to optimize the transformation matrices of objects in 3D space.

This approach ensures that while maintaining 3D consistency, the final composed scene adheres to the natural image distribution learned by large-scale 2D diffusion models, resulting in realistic and visually appealing videos.

### 7. Results and Evaluation

The C3V framework demonstrates significant improvements across multiple evaluation metrics. Quantitative assessments show higher Q-Align scores and CLIP scores compared to state-of-the-art methods including 4D-FY, Comp4D, and VideoCrafter2. These metrics measure both visual quality and semantic consistency with input prompts.

Qualitative results showcase the framework's ability to handle complex scenarios involving multiple objects with distinct motions and interactions. The system successfully generates videos where objects maintain their individual characteristics while interacting naturally with the environment and each other.

Key advantages demonstrated include:

- **Flexible Control**: Individual concepts can be edited independently without affecting others
- **Complex Scene Handling**: Multiple objects with different motion patterns can coexist naturally
- **Viewpoint Control**: The 3D representation enables controllable camera movements
- **Long Video Support**: The compositional approach scales naturally to longer sequences

Ablation studies confirm the importance of each component, particularly highlighting the effectiveness of step-by-step trajectory estimation and the necessity of fine-grained composition refinement.

### 8. Significance and Impact

The C3V framework represents a paradigm shift in video generation technology with implications extending beyond academic research. The explicit 3D compositional approach addresses fundamental limitations of existing 2D-based methods while enabling new applications.

**Technical Contributions**:
- First framework to explicitly compose concepts in 3D space for video generation
- Integration of LLM reasoning with 3D representation learning
- Step-by-step trajectory estimation methodology
- Effective combination of 3D consistency with 2D diffusion priors

**Practical Applications**:
- Content creation for entertainment and advertising
- Virtual and augmented reality experiences
- Educational material development
- Game asset generation
- Interactive storytelling platforms

The framework's modular design also opens possibilities for future extensions, including support for additional object types, physics-based dynamics, and multi-scene narratives.

### 9. Conclusion

The C3V framework introduces a compositional approach to 3D-aware video generation that leverages LLMs as directors for orchestrating complex video creation tasks. By explicitly operating in 3D space and decomposing generation into manageable components, the system achieves unprecedented control over individual elements while maintaining overall scene coherence.

The three-stage pipeline - task decomposition, trajectory generation, and fine-grained composition - demonstrates how different AI technologies can be effectively combined to address complex generative tasks. The results show clear improvements in both quality and controllability compared to existing methods, suggesting that compositional 3D approaches may become the standard for future video generation systems.

This work establishes a foundation for more sophisticated video generation applications and opens avenues for research in compositional AI systems, 3D-aware generation, and multimodal reasoning. As the technology matures, it promises to transform how we create and interact with digital video content across numerous domains.
