# Multi-Step Visual Reasoning with Visual Tokens Scaling and Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers develop VTS-V (Visual Token Scaling with Verification), a framework that enables multimodal language models to perform iterative visual reasoning through dynamic interaction with visual tools, achieving 6.90-point improvements on BLINK benchmark for GPT-4o and consistent gains across open-source models (4.18-7.58 points) through a Markov Decision Process formulation where a reasoner proposes visual actions from ten tool types (depth, cropping, OCR, segmentation) while a verifier trained on 301K preference pairs via direct preference optimization determines reasoning termination, supported by a novel VTS-SFT dataset of 315K tool-grounded reasoning trajectories that enables open-source models to transition from static "look once" visual encoding to adaptive "look deeper and verify" inference patterns with theoretical guarantees for bounded reasoning steps.

## Problem

- Current multimodal large language models use static visual inference, processing entire images into fixed visual tokens upfront, limiting their ability to iteratively refine understanding or adapt to visual ambiguities during reasoning
- Existing visual reasoning approaches focus primarily on scaling text tokens or using predefined visual tools in fixed pipelines, neglecting dynamic scaling of visual tokens based on reasoning context
- Available datasets lack multi-step, tool-grounded reasoning trajectories with preference labels needed to train models for dynamic visual interaction and verification

## Method

- Formulate visual reasoning as a ==Markov Decision Process== with a ==reasoner== that proposes visual actions and a ==verifier== that evaluates reasoning quality and determines termination
- Introduce ==ten diverse visual tools== (grounding, depth, zoom, visual search, crop, OCR, segmentation, captioning, similarity, overlay) that enable fine-grained visual interaction
- Create VTS-SFT dataset with **315K** verified tool-grounded reasoning trajectories and VTS-DPO dataset with **301K** preference pairs for training reasoner and verifier components respectively
- Train verifier using multi-step ==Direct Preference Optimization== to learn when reasoning should terminate based on diminishing reward differences between consecutive states

## Results

- GPT-4o with VTS-V achieves **69.15%** average accuracy on BLINK benchmark, outperforming base GPT-4o (**62.25%**), Chain-of-Thought (**64.96%**), Set-of-Mark (**60.13%**), and Visual Sketchpad (**66.78%**) approaches
- Fine-tuned open-source models show consistent improvements: Qwen2.5VL-7B increases from **49.07%** to **56.65%**, Qwen2VL-7B from **48.01%** to **52.19%**, and LLaMA3.2-11B from **47.98%** to **50.32%** on BLINK
- Ablation studies confirm verifier's critical role, with removal causing accuracy drops across all models (e.g., Qwen2.5VL drops from **54.44%** to **47.09%** without verifier)
- Generalization validated across diverse benchmarks including V*Bench, MMStar, and MathVista, with enhanced interpretability through explicit step-by-step visual evidence backing each reasoning decision

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Open-source models require supervised fine-tuning on tool-grounded trajectories before they can effectively utilize verifier signals and external visual tools, unlike closed-source models that demonstrate inherent tool-use capabilities
> - Dynamic visual token scaling enables models to focus computational resources on relevant image regions rather than uniformly processing all pixels, leading to more efficient and accurate reasoning
> - Verifier-guided termination prevents infinite reasoning loops while ensuring sufficient exploration, with theoretical guarantees that total reasoning steps form a finite stopping time under mild conditions
> - Complex compositional tasks benefit most from iterative visual reasoning, with largest improvements observed in counting, functionally-correlated, and visually-correlated reasoning categories

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), visual reasoning, visual programming, and verifier design. It tackles a fundamental limitation of current MLLMs: their static inference paradigm for visual content.

**Current Landscape & Identified Gaps:**
- **Static Visual Inference:** Most MLLMs process an entire image into a fixed set of visual tokens upfront. This "static inference" limits their ability to iteratively refine understanding, adapt to context, or recover from ambiguities (e.g., occlusion, missing details) during reasoning. The paper contrasts this with human perception, which is dynamic, selective, and feedback-driven—we iteratively inspect regions, zoom, and seek new evidence.
- **Limitations of Existing Visual Reasoning Approaches:**
 - **Text-focused Scaling:** Previous works like Shikra (Chain-of-Thought, CoT) and V* have improved reasoning by scaling *text tokens* or using visual anchors (e.g., Set-of-Mark, Scaffolding), but have largely neglected dynamic scaling of *visual tokens*.
 - **Limited Visual Tool Use:** Visual programming approaches (e.g., Visprog, ViperGPT) enable LLMs/VLMs to generate code for external vision tools. While Visual Sketchpad introduced multi-step capabilities, these methods are typically limited to 1-2 steps and lack the "reflective refinements" or model agency in choosing subsequent visual observations that complex tasks require. They often employ fixed tools in predefined pipelines, rather than allowing the model to decide *what* to observe next or *how* to focus visual attention.
- **Verifier Design Challenges:** While verifiers have been used to enhance language model reasoning (e.g., providing reward signals, evaluating solutions), existing verifiers are often task-specific or require tailored training datasets, lacking a unified, theoretically grounded framework for multi-step visual interaction.

**Bridging the Gaps:**
This work directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel framework that enables MLLMs to perform *inference-time visual token scaling* guided by a *verifier*. By formulating the problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), the authors introduce a structured approach where a "reasoner" proposes visual actions and a "verifier" evaluates these actions and determines when to terminate reasoning. This empowers the model to dynamically interact with visual content, performing fine-grained, context-aware reasoning by iteratively querying and refining its visual understanding. The introduction of a new dataset (VTS-SFT and VTS-DPO) specifically designed for tool-augmented, multi-step visual reasoning with preference labels further distinguishes this work by providing the necessary resources to train such dynamic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from the observed gap between the static visual processing capabilities of current MLLMs and the dynamic, adaptive nature of human visual reasoning. The authors argue that despite their impressive generalization, MLLMs fundamentally struggle with tasks requiring iterative refinement or context-sensitive exploration of visual information because they encode the entire image into fixed visual tokens at the outset.

The key objectives of this work are explicitly stated as threefold contributions:

1. **Develop an Expressive and Theoretically Grounded Framework:** The primary objective is to introduce a novel framework that allows MLLMs to engage in iterative, verifier-guided reasoning over images. This is formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) comprising a "reasoner" (proposing visual actions) and a "verifier" (evaluating actions and managing termination). A theoretical guarantee for bounded reasoning steps is also sought.
2. **Create a New Dataset for Tool-Augmented Visual Reasoning:** Given the absence of suitable datasets, a crucial objective is to construct a two-part dataset: VTS-SFT (supervised reasoning trajectories with tool use) and VTS-DPO (preference-labeled reasoning comparisons). These datasets are essential for effectively training both the reasoner and the verifier to support dynamic visual interaction.
3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Results and Demonstrate Interpretability:** The final objective is to comprehensively evaluate the proposed method across diverse visual reasoning benchmarks. This aims to demonstrate significant performance improvements over existing approaches, emphasizing not only accuracy gains but also the enhanced interpretability of the reasoning processes due to the explicit step-by-step, visual evidence-backed approach.

In essence, the authors aim to move MLLMs from a "look once" static paradigm to a "look deeper and verify" dynamic paradigm, enabling more robust, efficient, and human-like visual understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, Visual Token Scaling with Verification (VTS-V), revolves around an iterative reasoning process guided by a reasoner-verifier cooperation system, formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).

**Problem Formulation as MDP:**
- **State (`s_h`):** Represents the history of previous reasoning steps, including image, question, and prior actions/observations.
- **Planning (`t_h`):** The VLM (reasoner) generates textual planning for the current step.
- **Action (`a_h`):** Based on the planning, the VLM chooses a specific visual module/tool from a predefined action space.
- **Observation (`o_h`):** The chosen visual module executes, returning a visual observation (e.g., depth map, cropped image).
- **Verification (`r*`):** After each step, a verifier decides whether to continue reasoning or terminate.
- **Reasoning Sequence (`τ`):** The complete sequence of states, plans, actions, and observations.

**Two Main Components:**
1. **Reasoner (`R_θ`):** A pre-trained VLM (e.g., GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL, LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct) augmented with plug-and-play visual tools.
 - **Action Space:** Comprises ten diverse visual tools: `GroundingAction`, `DepthAction`, `ZoomInAction`, `VisualSearchAction`, `Crop`, `OCR`, `ImageSegment`, `ImageCaptioner`, `SimilarityComputing`, and `Overlay`. These tools allow the model to interact with and enrich visual content.
 - **Training:** Fine-tuned using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the **VTS-SFT dataset**. The loss function minimizes cross-entropy for generating correct textual plans and tool actions given the current state.
2. **Verifier (`r*` / `V_φ`):** A function derived from a base VLM (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) that assesses the quality of reasoning trajectories.
 - **Training:** Trained using **multi-step Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** on the **VTS-DPO dataset**. DPO (building on the Bradley-Terry model) aims to learn a reward function such that preferred trajectories (correct ones) have higher rewards than unpreferred ones (incorrect ones). The verifier operates by comparing the quality of successive reasoning steps.
 - **Termination Condition:** During inference, the reasoner continues generating steps as long as the reward difference between consecutive states (`|r_phi(s_h+1) - r_phi(s_h)|`) is above a predetermined threshold `ε`. If the difference drops below `ε`, it signals diminishing returns, and the reasoning terminates.

**Dataset Construction (VTS-SFT and VTS-DPO):**
This is a critical part of the methodology, as no existing dataset provided the necessary multi-step, tool-grounded reasoning traces with preference labels.
1. **Initial Data Collection:** Image-question pairs are sampled from the single-image portion of the LLaVA-OneVision (LLaVA-OV) dataset, ensuring diversity across 83 task types.
2. **Trajectory Generation:** Qwen-2.5-VL-72B (a powerful MLLM) is used in a few-shot prompting setting to generate multi-step reasoning trajectories that include calls to the predefined visual tools.
3. **Preprocessing and Filtering:** Raw trajectories undergo cleanup (flattening, merging, removing malformed examples, filtering trivial or excessively long ones).
4. **Correctness Verification (LLM-as-a-Judge):** The same Qwen-2.5-VL-72B model acts as an "LLM-as-a-judge" to filter out trajectories whose final answers are incorrect. This results in 315K high-quality, verified trajectories.
5. **VTS-SFT Dataset:** These 315K verified, tool-grounded trajectories form the VTS-SFT dataset, used for supervised fine-tuning of the reasoner.
6. **VTS-DPO Dataset:** For each correct trajectory (`τ_w`) in VTS-SFT, the model is prompted to generate a *suboptimal* trajectory (`τ_l`) by instructing it to proceed with incorrect reasoning steps from an intermediate point. These correct/incorrect pairs (`s_1, τ_w, τ_l`) form the 301K preference pairs for DPO training of the verifier.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
The paper formally proves that the total number of reasoning steps (`H_τ`) for the VTS-V algorithm is a stopping time and, under mild conditions, is finite with probability 1. This ensures that the iterative reasoning process is well-behaved and avoids infinite loops.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that VTS-V significantly enhances visual reasoning capabilities across various benchmarks and model architectures.

**1. VTS-V Improves Performance of Closed-Source Models (GPT-4o):**
- On the challenging BLINK benchmark (13 subtasks), GPT-4o + VTS-V achieved an average accuracy of 69.15%.
- This represents a substantial improvement over the base GPT-4o (62.25%, +6.90 points), GPT-4o + Chain-of-Thought (CoT) (64.96%, +3.30 points), Set-of-Mark (SoM) (60.13%, +9.02 points), and Visual Sketchpad (66.78%, +2.37 points).
- It even slightly surpassed GPT-4o + MMFactory (68.90%, +0.25 points), a strong tool-using framework.
- The most significant gains were observed in complex compositional tasks like Counting (+18.33), Functionally-Correlated (+12.91), and Visually-Correlated (+7.56), indicating VTS-V's effectiveness in fine-grained and structured visual reasoning.

**2. VTS-V Enhances Open-Source Models After Fine-Tuning:**
- Open-source models (Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaMA3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct) also showed consistent improvements when fine-tuned on the VTS-SFT dataset and then evaluated with VTS-V.
 - Qwen2VL-7B improved from 48.01% to 52.19% (+4.18).
 - Qwen2.5VL-7B improved from 49.07% to 56.65% (+7.58).
 - LLaMA3.2-11B improved from 47.98% to 50.32% (+2.34).
- These gains were observed across various subtasks, confirming the generalizability of VTS-V.

**3. Critical Role of VTS-SFT Dataset for Open-Source Models:**
- A crucial finding was that applying VTS-V directly to *un-fine-tuned* open-source models often degraded performance (e.g., Qwen2VL-7B dropped from 48.01% to 38.54%) or prevented valid tool-use actions (LLaMA3.2-11B).
- This suggests that base open-source models lack the intrinsic ability to correctly interact with verifier signals or external tools. However, after supervised fine-tuning on the VTS-SFT dataset, these models not only recovered but surpassed their original performance, enabling reliable tool interaction. This underscores the necessity of the VTS-SFT dataset for training open-source VLMs in dynamic visual reasoning.

**4. Generalization Across Diverse Benchmarks:**
- VTS-V's effectiveness extended to general benchmarks like V*Bench, MMStar, and MathVista.
- After full VTS-V training (SFT + DPO), models consistently improved: Qwen2.5VL-7B to 75.13% on V*Bench, Qwen2VL-7B to 66.67% on V*Bench, and LLaMA3.2-11B to 60.21% on V*Bench.
- While LLaMA3.2-11B showed a drop on MathVista (from 30.10% to 18.81%), the authors attribute this to the base LLaMA model's bias towards MathVista and weaker general performance, rather than a limitation of VTS-V itself.

**5. Verifier's Contribution (Ablation Study):**
- Ablation studies confirmed the verifier's critical role. Removing the verifier (`w/o verifier`) consistently led to accuracy drops across all open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5VL from 54.44% to 47.09%). This highlights the verifier's importance in filtering suboptimal reasoning paths and improving overall decision quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Multi-Step Visual Reasoning with Visual Tokens Scaling and Verification" paper makes a significant contribution to the field of multi-modal AI, particularly in pushing the boundaries of how MLLMs process and understand visual information.

**Key Significance:**
- **Paradigm Shift in MLLM Inference:** The most profound impact is the shift from a static, one-shot visual encoding paradigm to a dynamic, iterative, and verifiable approach. This more closely mimics human perception, enabling MLLMs to "look deeper" and refine their understanding as reasoning unfolds.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By allowing models to actively query and observe specific visual details through a diverse set of visual tools, VTS-V significantly improves performance on complex, fine-grained visual reasoning tasks that previously challenged conventional MLLMs. The consistent state-of-the-art results across various benchmarks (BLINK, V*Bench, MMStar, MathVista) attest to this.
- **Increased Interpretability and Grounding:** The step-by-step, tool-grounded reasoning traces provide a clearer, more interpretable account of how the model arrives at its answers. Each step is explicitly justified by visual evidence or tool output, enhancing trust and debugging capabilities in AI systems.
- **Theoretical Foundations for Iterative Reasoning:** The formulation of the problem as an MDP with theoretical guarantees for bounded reasoning steps provides a robust framework for developing complex, multi-step AI behaviors, addressing concerns about uncontrolled iterative processes.
- **Valuable Data Resource:** The creation and public release of the VTS-SFT and VTS-DPO datasets are crucial. These resources provide the necessary high-quality, tool-grounded reasoning trajectories and preference labels, which were previously lacking. This will undoubtedly accelerate further research and development in dynamic visual reasoning and tool-augmented MLLMs.
- **Efficiency Potential:** While not the primary focus, dynamic scaling also holds the potential for more efficient computation by allowing models to focus resources only on the most relevant parts of an image, rather than uniformly processing all pixels.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements presented in this paper have broad implications for future AI systems:
- **More Robust and Reliable MLLMs:** Enabling MLLMs to perform fine-grained, context-aware visual reasoning makes them more robust to ambiguity, occlusion, and missing details, leading to more reliable applications.
- **Advanced Applications:** This framework can unlock more sophisticated applications, from highly accurate visual question answering and image-grounded dialogue to complex robotics tasks requiring iterative perception and scientific analysis demanding deep visual inspection.
- **Foundation for AI Agents:** The ability for an AI to dynamically interact with its visual environment, make decisions, and verify them positions VTS-V as a foundational component for developing more intelligent and autonomous AI agents capable of complex visual understanding and problem-solving.

**Limitations Acknowledged:**
The authors acknowledge that the framework can be further adapted to support an even broader set of visual tools and more diverse reasoning formats. Future work will also focus on enhancing the verifier's adaptability across tasks and integrating it more tightly with downstream applications for improved robustness.

In conclusion, VTS-V represents a significant step towards MLLMs that can "think with their eyes," bridging the gap between static machine perception and dynamic human visual cognition, and offering a robust, interpretable, and effective framework for next-generation visual reasoning.
