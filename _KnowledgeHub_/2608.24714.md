# GaussianWAM: Distilling Geometry and Semantics from 3D Gaussian Fields into World-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GaussianWAM is a training-time enhancement framework for World-Action Models. It builds a 3D Gaussian field from multi-view observations, binding frozen geometry-model depth and CLIP semantic features to shared Gaussian primitives, then renders semantic, depth, and coverage targets that are distilled into the WAM's current-observation latents. All teachers and heads are dropped at inference. LIBERO-Plus success rises from 52.05% to 71.29% on FastWAM.

## Problem

- World-action model video latents are optimized for visual prediction only, so they stay geometrically under-constrained despite manipulation requiring reliable 3D scene structure and spatial relations.
- Language conditioning of video generation does not force visual tokens to carry spatially localized, object-relevant semantics, leaving latents weakly grounded in what regions actually represent.
- Independent geometry and semantic teachers give heterogeneous features in different spaces and viewpoints, while explicit 3D/4D world models add encoders, rendering, and inference cost.

## Method

- ==Gaussian teacher construction== lifts frozen VGGT-Omega depth, confidence, and camera parameters into a dense ==3D Gaussian field== whose 64-dimensional primitive attributes are initialized from frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 patch features.
- Multi-view ==differentiable splatting== fits the field for 50 iterations under cosine semantic, L1 depth, coverage, and regularization losses, caching rendered semantic, depth, alpha, and validity maps offline.
- **GaussianWAM**'s ==Gaussian distillation== attaches lightweight semantic, depth, and alpha heads to final-layer current-observation tokens, applying masked cosine and L1 losses only inside the validity mask; heads are removed at inference.

## Results

- On LIBERO-Plus, FastWAM improves **52.05%** to **71.29%** overall, with the largest shifts under noise (**+39.46**), lighting (**+29.11**), and camera (**+29.03**); Cosmos Policy goes **71.52%** to **77.30%**.
- Ablations show depth supervision matters most (removal drops overall to **66.28%**), semantics next (**68.06%**), alpha least (**70.99%**); Gaussian unification beats direct CLIP+VGGT distillation, **71.29%** versus **69.37%**.
- On RoboTwin Random, Cosmos Policy rises **7.10%** to **14.40%** and Clean **34.40%** to **68.90%**; real bimanual UR7e tasks improve FastWAM's average success from **30.00%** to **40.00%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A shared 3D spatial carrier for heterogeneous teachers beats independent 2D feature losses, since binding geometry and semantics to the same primitives adds a measurable 1.62-point gain.
> - Representation enrichment can be purely training-time: teachers, rendering, and auxiliary heads all vanish at deployment, so robustness gains cost zero extra inference computation.
> - Supervision placement matters; final-layer distillation outperforms layer 10 or 20, and geometric depth cues contribute more to distribution-shift robustness than semantic cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of robotic control, specifically addressing challenges in World-Action Models (WAMs) and video-action models. These models represent a paradigm shift from conventional vision-language-action (VLA) models by jointly learning future visual dynamics and action generation. WAMs leverage video generative models to acquire representations from temporal dynamics, using future scene evolution as a representation-learning signal for action generation.

Despite the advancements in WAMs, their learned video latent representations are primarily optimized for visual prediction and reconstruction in 2D image or latent space. This approach often results in representations that lack explicit preservation of cross-view geometric structure and spatially localized, object-relevant semantic information. Robotic manipulation tasks, which inherently operate in three-dimensional space, require a robust understanding of scene geometry and spatial relationships. Prior WAMs may therefore exhibit limitations in precise spatial interaction. While language instructions can condition video and action generation, this does not inherently guarantee that visual tokens consistently correspond to task-relevant objects and their spatial locations.

Previous attempts to incorporate geometry and semantics into WAMs include distilling knowledge from multiple pre-trained teachers or extending WAMs to explicit 3D/4D world modeling. However, these approaches face limitations. Independent teachers often produce heterogeneous features that are difficult to unify spatially. Explicit 3D/4D models typically incur increased training complexity and inference costs due to additional 3D encoders, decoders, or rendering modules.

GaussianWAM addresses these limitations by proposing a training-time representation-enhancement framework. It differentiates itself by using a 3D Gaussian field as a unified spatial carrier to organize heterogeneous geometric and semantic supervision from existing foundation models. This approach aims to inject structured geometric and semantic knowledge into WAM representations without altering their inference architecture or introducing additional runtime overhead, thereby maintaining the efficiency of the original policy. The work builds upon recent developments in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as an efficient 3D scene representation and its extensions to incorporate high-dimensional features from foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of GaussianWAM are:

1. **To enhance the spatial grounding of World-Action Models (WAMs):** Existing WAMs primarily learn representations optimized for 2D visual reconstruction or prediction. The authors identify a gap where these representations often lack explicit constraints to preserve cross-view geometric structure, which is crucial for precise 3D robotic manipulation. The objective is to imbue WAM representations with a more reliable understanding of scene geometry and spatial relationships.
2. **To integrate spatially localized, object-relevant semantic information:** Current WAM video latents are not explicitly optimized to retain semantic information directly associated with objects and their spatial positions. While language conditioning exists, it does not guarantee fine-grained semantic grounding in the visual latent space. The motivation is to enable WAMs to better capture not only "where objects are in 3D space" but also "what the corresponding visual regions represent" by integrating rich semantic priors from visual foundation models.
3. **To unify heterogeneous geometric and semantic knowledge:** Geometric and semantic teachers typically output disparate features in different representation spaces, viewpoints, and confidence levels. A key motivation is to overcome the limitations of independent feature distillation by providing a shared spatial carrier that can consistently associate geometric and semantic information with the same physical 3D structures across multiple views. The 3D Gaussian field is proposed for this unification.
4. **To achieve enhancement without sacrificing inference efficiency:** Explicitly extending WAMs to 3D/4D world models or adding dedicated spatial enhancement modules often introduces additional computational overhead during inference. A crucial objective for GaussianWAM is to introduce structured geometric and semantic supervision *only during training*, ensuring that the original WAM inference pipeline remains unchanged, thus preserving its efficiency and deployment cost.
5. **To provide a generalizable framework:** The authors aim for a framework that can be applied to different WAM architectures, demonstrating its broad applicability rather than being specific to a single model design. This is motivated by the diversity in existing WAM architectures, such as dual-expert Mixture-of-Transformers and unified diffusion Transformers.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between 2D-centric WAM representations and the 3D, semantically rich world of robotic manipulation, doing so in an inference-efficient and architecturally agnostic manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GaussianWAM is a training-time 3D Gaussian field enhancement framework designed to distill geometric and semantic information into World-Action Models. The core methodology involves constructing a 3D Gaussian teacher from multi-view observations, rendering structured supervision signals from this teacher, and then distilling these signals into the WAM's internal visual representations during training. Critically, all Gaussian-related modules are removed at inference time.

The methodology can be broken down into the following stages:

#### 3.1. Gaussian Teacher Construction

For each training sample, a dense 3D Gaussian teacher is built from synchronized multi-view observations.
- **Geometry Extraction:** A frozen geometry foundation model, VGGT-Omega, is used to estimate dense depth maps, depth confidence, camera intrinsics, and extrinsics for each view. These are resized to a 14x14 teacher grid. Invalid or low-confidence pixels are filtered to create a geometry-valid mask, and the remaining valid pixels are back-projected into 3D space.
- **Visual-Semantic Feature Extraction:** A frozen CLIP image encoder (ViT-B/16) extracts dense visual-semantic features. These patch tokens are reshaped into a spatial feature map, resized to the same teacher grid, and projected to a 64-dimensional representation. This step aligns geometric and semantic information at corresponding image locations before 3D lifting.
- **Gaussian Field Initialization:** A Gaussian field $G = \{g_i\}_{i=1}^N$ is initialized using the geometry-valid 3D points. Each valid location initializes a Gaussian primitive $g_i = \{x_i, r_i, s_i, o_i, z_i\}$, encoding 3D center ($x_i$), rotation ($r_i$), spatial scale ($s_i$), opacity ($o_i$), and the corresponding 64-dimensional visual-semantic feature ($z_i$). This binds 3D geometry and visual-semantic information within shared primitives in a common 3D coordinate system.
- **Multi-view Gaussian Fitting:** The initialized Gaussian field is rendered into each observed camera view using a differentiable depth-aware Gaussian renderer. This produces rendered visual-semantic feature maps ($\hat{F}_v$), depth maps ($\hat{D}_v$), and accumulated Gaussian coverage maps ($\hat{A}_v$). An optimization process adjusts Gaussian parameters (centers, rotations, scales, opacities, and optionally semantic features) to align $\hat{F}_v$ with extracted CLIP features, and $\hat{D}_v$ with estimated depth, using cosine distance for features and $\ell_1$ loss for depth. A coverage objective encourages high coverage at valid geometric locations and suppresses spurious coverage. Regularization is also applied to Gaussian parameters. This fitting stage runs for 50 iterations per sample.
- **Offline Teacher Cache:** After fitting, the optimized Gaussian field is rendered to produce cached targets: semantic feature map ($T_{sem}$), rendered depth ($T_{depth}$), accumulated Gaussian coverage ($T_{\alpha}$), and a final validity mask ($T_{valid}$). The validity mask ensures supervision is applied only at locations with reliable geometry and non-negligible Gaussian coverage. For multi-view WAMs, these per-view targets are composed into a multi-view spatial layout, then cached offline to avoid redundant computation during policy training.

#### 3.2. Gaussian Distillation

The cached Gaussian representation is distilled into the WAM's internal visual representation during policy training.
- **Auxiliary Prediction Heads:** Three lightweight auxiliary prediction heads ($\Phi_G = \{\phi_{sem}, \phi_{depth}, \phi_{\alpha}\}$) are attached to the WAM's final-layer hidden tokens corresponding to the current visual observations ($H_L^{cur}$). These heads predict the cached semantic features, depth, and accumulated alpha.
- **Gaussian Distillation Objective:** The distillation loss $L_G = \lambda_{sem}L_{sem} + \lambda_{depth}L_{depth} + \lambda_{\alpha}L_{\alpha}$ is computed. $L_{sem}$ uses masked cosine distance, while $L_{depth}$ and $L_{\alpha}$ use masked $\ell_1$ losses. All losses are evaluated only over the valid locations identified by $T_{valid}$.
- **Overall Training Objective:** The total training objective for the WAM becomes $L_{train} = L_{WAM} + \lambda_G L_G$, where $L_{WAM}$ is the original WAM training objective.
- **Inference-Time Removal:** After training, the entire Gaussian teacher, the rendering modules, and the auxiliary prediction heads are completely removed. The WAM then operates with its original forward path and computational cost, benefiting from the enhanced representations learned during training.

#### 3.3. Application to WAM Architectures

GaussianWAM is instantiated on two distinct WAM architectures:
- **FastWAM-style Dual-Expert MoT:** For FastWAM, which uses separate video and action experts, Gaussian distillation is applied to the spatial tokens of the final-layer video expert hidden states corresponding to current visual observations. This enhanced video representation is then available to the action expert through the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) interaction.
- **Cosmos-Policy-style Unified DiT:** For Cosmos Policy, which processes observations, world variables, and actions within a unified diffusion Transformer, Gaussian distillation is applied directly to the final-layer hidden tokens corresponding to the current visual observations within the shared Transformer backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative and qualitative evidence to support the effectiveness of GaussianWAM.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus Benchmarks:**
 - **FastWAM:** GaussianWAM improved FastWAM's overall success rate on LIBERO-Plus from 52.05% to 71.29%. On the standard LIBERO benchmark, success increased from 96.6% to 97.6%. Significant gains were observed under distribution shifts: camera (+29.03 percentage points), lighting (+29.11), noise (+39.46), and robot appearance (+18.73).
 - **Cosmos Policy:** GaussianWAM improved Cosmos Policy's overall success rate on LIBERO-Plus from 71.52% to 77.30%. Gains were noted for camera, robot, language, noise, and layout shifts, indicating the method's applicability beyond a single WAM architecture.
- **RoboTwin Benchmark:**
 - **FastWAM:** Maintained Clean performance (70.50%) and improved Random success from 0.70% to 1.60%.
 - **Cosmos Policy:** Improved Clean success from 34.40% to 68.90% and Random success from 7.10% to 14.40%. These results suggest enhanced robustness under randomized conditions beyond the LIBERO environment.
- **Real-world Manipulation:**
 - On a bimanual real-robot platform, GaussianWAM improved the average success rate of FastWAM from 30.00% to 40.00% across standard, layout, camera, and visual-noise variations, demonstrating transferability to real-world scenarios.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Gaussian-field unification:** Direct 2D distillation of CLIP semantic features and VGGT depth without a Gaussian field achieved an overall LIBERO-Plus success rate of 69.37% (compared to 52.05% for baseline FastWAM). GaussianWAM (without alpha supervision) further improved this to 70.99%, suggesting that unifying heterogeneous teacher signals through a shared 3D Gaussian representation provides an advantage over direct multi-teacher supervision. The inclusion of rendering-derived alpha supervision further increased performance to 71.29%.
- **Effect of Gaussian supervision components:** Removing any individual component of the Gaussian supervision degraded overall performance on LIBERO-Plus. Removing semantic supervision reduced success from 71.29% to 68.06%. Removing alpha supervision reduced it to 70.99%. Removing depth supervision resulted in the largest drop, to 66.28%, indicating the importance of explicit geometric information. This suggests that geometry, visual semantics, and Gaussian coverage provide complementary supervision.
- **Effect of supervision depth:** Applying Gaussian distillation to the final Transformer layer (layer 30) of FastWAM yielded the highest overall LIBERO-Plus performance (71.29%), compared to intermediate layers (layer 10: 67.86%, layer 20: 68.44%).

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

- **Geometric Representation Probing:** After training, frozen GaussianWAM-enhanced backbones exhibited clearer scene structure and more coherent depth responses when probed with a lightweight depth predictor, compared to baseline models. This suggests stronger geometric grounding.
- **Visual-Semantic Representation Visualization:** t-SNE visualizations of final-layer visual-semantic representations showed that GaussianWAM-enhanced models produced more compact local clusters and clearer separation among semantic groups, indicating improved visual-semantic organization in the learned latent space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GaussianWAM contributes to the field of robotic control by addressing limitations in how World-Action Models (WAMs) represent their environment. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Representation Learning:** The core impact is the substantial improvement in the geometric and visual-semantic grounding of WAM representations. By explicitly binding heterogeneous geometric (depth, camera parameters) and semantic (CLIP features) information within a unified 3D Gaussian field, GaussianWAM enables WAMs to learn more robust and spatially aware representations. This is critical for robotic manipulation tasks that require precise interaction with 3D objects.
2. **Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** The consistent performance gains observed across LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin (especially under "Random" settings), and real-world experiments under various distribution shifts (camera, lighting, noise, layout) indicate that GaussianWAM significantly enhances the robustness and generalization ability of WAMs. This is a crucial factor for deploying robots in diverse and unpredictable real-world environments.
3. **Inference-Efficient Design:** A major practical advantage of GaussianWAM is its training-time-only enhancement strategy. By removing all Gaussian-related teachers and auxiliary prediction modules at inference, the method avoids introducing any additional computational overhead or changes to the original WAM deployment architecture. This makes it a practical solution for real-world robotic systems where low latency and computational efficiency are paramount.
4. **Generality and Compatibility:** The successful application of GaussianWAM to two distinct WAM architectures (FastWAM and Cosmos Policy) demonstrates its generality as an enhancement framework. This suggests that the approach can potentially benefit a broad range of existing and future WAMs, irrespective of their specific architectural design.
5. **Unified Spatial Supervision:** The approach of using a 3D Gaussian field as a "unified spatial carrier" for heterogeneous supervision signals provides a novel and effective way to overcome the challenges of integrating diverse teacher models. This mechanism could inspire similar approaches for other representation learning tasks where multi-modal or multi-source supervision needs to be spatially coherent.
6. **Insights into Representation Depth:** The ablation study on supervision depth provides valuable insights, suggesting that applying structured geometric and semantic distillation to the final layers of WAM backbones is most effective for overall task performance. This could inform future designs of representation learning pipelines for robotic control.

In conclusion, GaussianWAM offers a practical, robust, and generalizable method for strengthening the underlying representations of World-Action Models. Its ability to inject crucial 3D geometric and semantic understanding without compromising inference efficiency positions it as a significant contribution towards more capable and deployable robotic manipulation systems.
