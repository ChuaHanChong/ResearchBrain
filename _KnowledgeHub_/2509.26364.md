# Data-to-Energy Stochastic Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Edinburgh developed an extension of Schrödinger Bridge (SB) algorithms to situations where one or both distributions are defined by unnormalised densities, enabling "data-to-energy" and "energy-to-energy" stochastic transport. Their method, which incorporates learnable diffusion coefficients and off-policy reinforcement learning, achieved performance comparable to data-to-data approaches on synthetic benchmarks and demonstrated effective conditional image generation in latent spaces, sometimes yielding lower FID scores than rejection sampling.

## Problem

- Existing Schrödinger Bridge (SB) algorithms, such as Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF), typically require direct access to samples from both the source and target marginal distributions.
- Many distributions of practical interest, particularly in scientific computing and generative modeling, are defined by unnormalised probability densities (energy functions) from which direct sampling is computationally expensive or intractable.
- Practical implementations of continuous-time stochastic dynamics often suffer from inaccuracies introduced by time discretization, limiting the fidelity of learned processes.

## Method

- Extended the ==Iterative Proportional Fitting== (IPF) framework to handle target distributions defined by unnormalised densities (data-to-energy and energy-to-energy settings) by adapting maximum likelihood objectives with a conditional variance-based loss.
- Integrated off-policy reinforcement learning techniques, including replay buffers, Langevin updates to buffer samples, and reverse trajectory reuse, to stabilize and improve training convergence when explicit samples are unavailable.
- Introduced learnable diffusion coefficients for both forward and backward stochastic differential equations (SDEs) to mitigate errors arising from time discretization in the Euler-Maruyama approximation.

## Results

- Trainable diffusion coefficients yielded competitive or improved performance in terms of Wasserstein-2 distance and Path KL on synthetic 2D benchmarks compared to fixed-variance methods, especially with fewer time discretization steps.
- The data-to-energy IPF achieved performance quantitatively comparable to its data-to-data counterpart on 2D distribution transport, demonstrating successful learning of stochastic bridges to unnormalised targets.
- Applied to outsourced sampling in latent spaces of generative models, the method generated high-quality images adhering to target classes (e.g., lower FID scores than rejection sampling for CIFAR-10) while preserving unconstrained visual features, as confirmed by qualitative analysis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning the diffusion coefficient alongside the drift function can significantly improve the accuracy of time-discretized Schrödinger Bridge algorithms, compensating for approximations.
> - Off-policy sampling strategies, such as replay buffers and reverse trajectory reuse, are essential for effectively training stochastic processes to match unnormalised target densities without direct sample access.
> - Data-to-energy Schrödinger bridges can perform conditional generation while preserving unconstrained semantic content (e.g., image style) better than generic diffusion samplers, offering an advantage for tasks like image-to-image translation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated within the field of generative modeling, specifically focusing on stochastic dynamical systems for transforming distributions. Modern generative models, such as diffusion models (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021b) and flow matching (Liu et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2023), have demonstrated capabilities in generating high-fidelity data across various modalities including images, video, audio, and text. These approaches can be interpreted as specific instances of the broader problem of learning optimal stochastic dynamics between two arbitrary probability distributions.

This broader problem is formally known as the Schrödinger Bridge (SB) problem (Schrödinger, 1931; 1932). The SB problem involves finding a stochastic process that transports one marginal distribution to another while minimizing a specific transportation cost, typically expressed as a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to a reference process. It is considered a generalization of optimal transport (Monge, 1781; Kantorovich, 1960) to the stochastic domain, with connections to optimal control theory.

Computational approaches to the SB problem include the Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) algorithm (Fortet, 1940; Vargas et al., 2021; De Bortoli et al., 2021). IPF iteratively refines forward and reverse stochastic processes until they converge to the optimal bridge. While existing IPF variants have advanced the computational feasibility of SB, a significant limitation persists: they typically require direct access to samples from *both* the source and target marginal distributions. This "data-to-data" setting restricts their applicability to scenarios where samples from both distributions can be readily obtained.

In many real-world and scientific applications, target distributions are often described by unnormalised probability densities or energy functions (e.g., Bayesian posteriors, statistical physics models), from which direct sampling is computationally expensive or intractable. Recent research has addressed this by developing "diffusion samplers" that learn to sample from such energy-based models (Zhang & Chen, 2022; Vargas et al., 2023; Berner et al., 2024). These methods often leverage techniques from off-policy reinforcement learning to effectively navigate the landscape of unnormalised densities.

This paper bridges the gap by extending the IPF framework to scenarios where one or both marginal distributions are provided as unnormalised densities, rather than requiring explicit data samples. This generalization makes the Schrödinger Bridge problem applicable to a broader class of problems, integrating concepts from data-free diffusion sampling into the optimal stochastic transport framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general method for modeling Schrödinger bridges when one or both of the target marginal distributions are specified by their unnormalised densities (energy functions), without requiring access to direct data samples. This addresses a fundamental limitation of existing Schrödinger bridge algorithms, which primarily operate in a data-to-data setting.

The motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

- **Expanding Applicability of Schrödinger Bridges**: Many distributions of interest in various scientific and engineering domains, such as posterior distributions in Bayesian inference, configurations in statistical mechanics, or learned distributions in energy-based models, are naturally defined by an energy function or an unnormalised density. However, direct sampling from these distributions can be intractable. By enabling Schrödinger bridge inference for these "data-free" distributions, the proposed method significantly broadens the practical utility of SB algorithms.
- **Generalizing Iterative Proportional Fitting**: The IPF algorithm is a theoretically sound and computationally effective approach for solving the SB problem. The development of a "data-to-energy" or "energy-to-energy" IPF generalizes this established framework, allowing it to tackle a wider range of distribution transport problems. This involves adapting the maximum likelihood training objectives of traditional IPF to accommodate unnormalised densities.
- **Leveraging Advances in Diffusion Samplers**: Recent progress in training diffusion models to sample from unnormalised densities has demonstrated the effectiveness of certain loss functions (e.g., variance-based losses) and off-policy reinforcement learning strategies. The research is motivated by the idea of porting these successful techniques to the more general Schrödinger bridge context.
- **Improving Time Discretization Accuracy**: The practical implementation of continuous-time stochastic dynamics often relies on time-discretized approximations (e.g., Euler-Maruyama). The authors hypothesize that learning the diffusion coefficient of the dynamics, in addition to the drift, can help mitigate errors introduced by this discretization, drawing inspiration from related findings in diffusion samplers.
- **Enabling Data-Free Conditional Generation**: A specific motivating application is the problem of conditional data generation, particularly "image-to-image translation" or "outsourced sampling" in latent spaces of generative models. Here, one aims to transform a prior distribution (e.g., a Gaussian latent prior) to a posterior distribution conditioned on specific attributes (e.g., an image class), where the posterior is typically defined by an unnormalised density involving a likelihood term. A data-free SB method could offer a scalable solution that also preserves relevant semantic content during translation.

Based on these motivations, the paper outlines three key contributions:
1. **Development and Demonstration of Data-to-Energy/Energy-to-Energy IPF**: To successfully learn stochastic bridges with low transport cost between synthetic data and energy-based distributions, validating the core methodological extension.
2. **Investigation of Trainable Diffusion Coefficients**: To demonstrate that learning the diffusion coefficient, alongside the drift, can enhance the performance of time-discretized IPF algorithms.
3. **Application to Outsourced Sampling**: To apply the data-to-energy IPF to the problem of sampling posterior distributions in latent spaces of generative models, thereby establishing a data-free method for image-to-image translation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology developed in this paper extends the Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) algorithm to enable the inference of Schrödinger bridges (SB) when one or both marginal distributions are given by unnormalised densities.

#### 3.1. Schrödinger Bridge Problem Background

The SB problem seeks a stochastic process P_t that transports a source distribution p_0 to a target distribution p_1, while minimizing the KL divergence to a reference process Q_t. The solution P_t is typically an Itô Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) with a learnable drift function F and a fixed diffusion coefficient σ_t, derived from the reference process. The KL divergence acts as a dynamic transport cost. As σ_t approaches zero, the SB problem converges to dynamic optimal transport.

#### 3.2. Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF)

IPF is an iterative procedure to solve the SB problem. It maintains two SDEs, one in forward time (P_t) and one in reverse time (P_t), which are iteratively updated.
- **Initialization**: The forward process is initialized to the reference process Q_t.
- **Backward Step (Eq. 3a)**: Update the backward process (P_t^(n+1)) by finding the process closest (in KL) to the previous forward iterate (P_t^n) that satisfies the marginal constraint at t=0 (p_0). This is equivalent to maximum likelihood training of the backward SDE (P_t^(n+1)) given trajectories sampled from the forward SDE (P_t^n) initialized at p_0 (Eq. 6a).
- **Forward Step (Eq. 3b)**: Update the forward process (P_t^(n+1)) by finding the process closest (in KL) to the current backward iterate (P_t^(n+1)) that satisfies the marginal constraint at t=1 (p_1). This is equivalent to maximum likelihood training of the forward SDE (P_t^(n+1)) given trajectories sampled from the backward SDE (P_t^(n+1)) initialized at p_1 (Eq. 6b).
Upon convergence, the forward and backward processes become time-reversals of each other and solve the SB problem.

#### 3.3. Time Discretization and Trainable Variance

To make IPF computationally tractable, the continuous-time SDEs are approximated by discrete-time Markov chains using the Euler-Maruyama scheme (Eqs. 5a, 5b).
- **Data-to-Data IPF (Algorithm 1)**: In the traditional setting, samples from p_0 and p_1 are available. The maximum likelihood objectives (Eqs. 6a, 6b) are used, involving sampling trajectories from the current process and maximizing the log-likelihood of these trajectories under the process being updated.
- **Trainable Diffusion Coefficients**: A key methodological extension is to learn not only the drift functions (F) but also the diffusion coefficients (σ^2) of both forward and backward processes. This is achieved by replacing the fixed variances in the Euler-Maruyama transition kernels (Eqs. 5a, 5b) with neurally parameterized functions (σ_θ^2, σ_φ^2). This aims to mitigate errors arising from time discretization, inspired by results in diffusion samplers.

#### 3.4. Data-to-Energy Schrödinger Bridges (Algorithm 2)

The central contribution is addressing the scenario where p_1 is an unnormalised density (e^(-E1(x))/Z) without sample access.
- **Backward IPF Step (Eq. 6a)**: This step remains unchanged as it requires samples from p_0, which are assumed to be available.
- **Forward IPF Step (Eq. 6b Replacement)**: Since samples from p_1 are unavailable, the maximum likelihood objective cannot be directly applied. Instead, the paper proposes a conditional variant of the "log-variance" (L_LV) loss (Richter et al., 2020), adapted from diffusion sampling literature.
 - **L_LV Objective (Eq. 7, 8)**: This objective minimizes the variance of a log-ratio term over a batch of trajectories (τ^(i)) sharing the same initial point (x_0). The log-ratio compares the forward process probability, the unnormalised target density p_1(x_1), and the backward process probability. The objective does not require knowledge of the normalizing constant Z of p_1.
 - **Off-Policy Training Methods**: To effectively optimize the L_LV objective, particularly for complex, high-dimensional target distributions, off-policy reinforcement learning techniques are employed:
 - **Replay Buffer**: A buffer stores past final samples (x_1) from forward trajectories. During training, x_1 samples are drawn from this buffer, and a reverse trajectory is sampled from the current backward model (P_φ) to obtain a corresponding x_0. This strategy guides training towards high-density regions of p_1.
 - **Reverse Trajectory Reuse**: The reverse trajectory (from x_1 in the buffer to x_0) is included alongside on-policy forward trajectories starting from that x_0 in the batch for L_LV computation. This provides "successful" trajectories that reach p_1.
 - **Langevin Updates**: Periodically, samples in the replay buffer are refined using a few steps of unadjusted Langevin dynamics on the target density p_1, to correct for potential biases or inaccuracies of the learned sampler.
 - **Mixed Training Policy**: A combination of on-policy sampling (from p_0 and the current forward model) and off-policy sampling (via the replay buffer and reverse trajectories) is used, controlled by an "off-policy ratio."

#### 3.5. Energy-to-Energy Generalization

The methodology extends to the "energy-to-energy" setting where both p_0 and p_1 are given by unnormalised densities. In this case, both the forward and backward IPF steps utilize the variance-based loss (Eq. 7, 19), with separate replay buffers and off-policy strategies for each marginal.

#### 3.6. Outsourced Sampling Application

The data-to-energy IPF is applied to Bayesian posterior sampling in the latent space of generative models.
- **Problem**: Sample from p(x|y) ∝ p(x)r(x,y), where p(x) is a prior from a generator f(z) and r(x,y) is a constraint.
- **Approach**: The problem is pulled back to the latent space, aiming to sample from p(z|y) ∝ p(z)r(f(z),y). The source distribution is the tractable latent prior p(z), and the target is the unnormalised posterior p(z|y).
- **Method**: The data-to-energy IPF learns a bridge from the latent prior p(z) to the latent posterior p(z|y), where p(z) provides samples and p(z|y) provides an energy function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings based on experiments across synthetic 2D distributions and high-dimensional generative model latent spaces.

#### 4.1. Benefits of Trainable Variance

Experiments on synthetic 2D benchmarks (Gauss ↔ GMM, Gauss ↔ Two Moons, Two Moons ↔ GMM) were conducted to compare existing data-to-data SB methods with the proposed log-likelihood (LL) method, both with fixed and learned diffusion coefficients.
- **Improved Performance**: Table 1 indicates that training the diffusion coefficient ("LL learnt var.") generally yields competitive or improved performance in terms of Wasserstein-2 distance (W2_2) and Path KL compared to methods with fixed variance (e.g., "LL fixed var." and other established SB algorithms like DSBM, SDE, [SF]^2M).
- **Correction for Discretization Error**: Figure 2 and Table 4 illustrate that learning the variance allows for more accurate modeling, particularly when the number of time discretization steps (K) is small. This suggests that the trainable diffusion coefficient helps to compensate for the inaccuracies introduced by coarse time discretization.

#### 4.2. Data-to-Energy and Energy-to-Energy Schrödinger Bridge

To validate the proposed data-to-energy IPF, a bridge between a Gaussian and a GMM distribution was trained. The data-to-energy version, which only had access to samples from the Gaussian and the energy function of the GMM, was compared against a data-to-data version with full sample access.
- **Comparable Performance**: Figure 3 visually demonstrates the convergence of the learned processes over IPF iterations for data-to-data, data-to-energy, and energy-to-energy settings. Table 4 quantitatively shows that the data-to-energy model achieved performance comparable to the data-to-data model in terms of W2_2 and Path KL.
- **Energy-to-Energy Viability**: Preliminary results for the energy-to-energy setting (between two GMMs, bottom row of Figure 3) also indicate successful learning of stochastic transport, suggesting the generalizability of the approach.

#### 4.3. Outsourced Schrödinger Bridge

The data-to-energy IPF algorithm was applied to the problem of sampling from posterior distributions in the latent spaces of pretrained generative models (VAE on MNIST, StyleGAN and SN-GAN on CIFAR-10). The objective was to translate a Gaussian latent prior to a reward-reweighted posterior defined by a classifier.
- **Image Generation Quality (Table 2)**: For CIFAR-10, FID scores were computed between images decoded from latents sampled by the trained SB model and real images of the target class. Notably, the transported samples sometimes exhibited lower FID scores than samples obtained through rejection sampling (considered a ground truth posterior). This suggests that the SB approach can generate high-quality samples that adhere to the target posterior. The ELBO values provided a measure of how well the model fit the target density.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4, Figures 6-13)**: The visual results demonstrate that the learned Schrödinger bridge not only transforms images to match the target class but also tends to preserve "style" features or semantic content that is not explicitly constrained by the target class (e.g., background, object orientation, digit thickness). This observed property, where unconstrained features are maintained while constrained features are transformed, indicates a specific advantage over generic diffusion samplers for conditional generation tasks like image-to-image translation.

#### 4.4. Ablations of Off-Policy Techniques

A detailed ablation study was performed on the SN-GAN outsourced sampling problem for CIFAR-10 (Table 3) to evaluate the contribution of various off-policy reinforcement learning components.
- **Replay Buffer and Langevin Updates**: Incorporating a replay buffer and applying Langevin updates to buffer samples significantly improved the Path KL metric, indicating a closer approximation to the optimal bridge.
- **Reverse Trajectory Reuse**: Reusing backward trajectories for loss computation further improved Path KL, achieving the lowest value among the ablations. However, this sometimes led to a negative impact on the mean log-reward, suggesting a trade-off between minimizing transport cost and achieving high fidelity to the target density modes.
- **Off-Policy Ratio**: Exploring different off-policy ratios and annealing schedules showed varying effects, highlighting the sensitivity and importance of balancing exploration and exploitation in the training process.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Analytical Solution

The proposed SB algorithm was tested on the problem of finding the bridge between two Gaussian distributions, for which an analytical solution exists (Bunne et al., 2023).
- **Accuracy**: Figure 5 shows that both the data-to-energy and data-to-data SB algorithms achieved Wasserstein-2 distances to the analytical solution comparable to the DSBM method (De Bortoli et al., 2021) across different time steps of the trajectory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of generative modeling and stochastic optimal transport, specifically by addressing a significant limitation of existing Schrödinger Bridge (SB) algorithms.

**Generalization of Schrödinger Bridge Theory and Application**: The primary significance lies in extending the Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) framework to the "data-to-energy" and "energy-to-energy" settings. This enables the computation of stochastic bridges when target distributions are defined by unnormalised densities rather than requiring direct samples. This generalization significantly broadens the applicability of SB to numerous real-world problems in scientific computing, machine learning, and artificial intelligence, particularly in areas like Bayesian inference, sampling from energy-based models, and statistical physics, where direct sampling from complex distributions is often intractable.

**Methodological Contributions in Stochastic Process Learning**: The integration of off-policy reinforcement learning techniques, such as replay buffers, Langevin updates, and reverse trajectory reuse, is a key methodological contribution. These techniques are crucial for stabilizing and improving the training of diffusion models and now, more generally, Schrödinger bridges in data-free environments. This work further solidifies the connection between reinforcement learning and generative modeling.

**Improved Model Accuracy through Learnable Diffusion Coefficients**: The finding that learning the diffusion coefficient, in addition to the drift function, can improve the accuracy of time-discretized SB algorithms (and potentially other diffusion-based generative models) is a valuable technical insight. This provides a mechanism to mitigate errors introduced by the discrete-time approximation of continuous processes, potentially leading to more robust and higher-fidelity models.

**Scalable Data-Free Conditional Generation and Image-to-Image Translation**: The application of data-to-energy SB to "outsourced sampling" in latent spaces of generative models offers a novel, scalable, and data-free approach to conditional generation and image-to-image translation. The observed phenomenon of preserving unconstrained semantic content during image transformation, while transforming constrained features, suggests promising applications in style transfer, controlled content manipulation, and other tasks requiring nuanced conditional generation. This could provide an alternative or complementary approach to existing methods in these domains.

**Future Research Directions**: The paper also outlines several avenues for future work, indicating its potential to inspire further research:
- **Scaling to Higher Dimensions and Arbitrary Priors**: Improving the scalability and robustness of the data-to-energy SB to handle even higher-dimensional data and more complex, non-Gaussian prior distributions would further enhance its practical utility.
- **Addressing Mode Collapse**: The challenge of mode collapse in generative models is acknowledged, suggesting that future work could focus on techniques to improve mode coverage and diversity in the generated samples.
- **Amortized Conditional Generation**: Developing models that can amortize over a distribution of conditions, rather than learning a specific model for each condition, would make the approach more efficient for diverse conditional generation tasks.
- **Discrete Schrödinger Bridges**: Exploring connections and applications to discrete Schrödinger bridge problems (e.g., in discrete state spaces) is identified as another promising direction.
- **New Applications**: Specific potential applications include text-conditional image reward fine-tuning of diffusion models, underscoring the method's potential impact on multimodal generative AI.

In summary, this research advances the theoretical and practical capabilities of Schrödinger bridges, opening new avenues for modeling stochastic dynamics in settings where conventional methods are limited. Its contributions to data-free generative modeling and conditional generation are significant, with potential implications across various scientific and engineering disciplines.
