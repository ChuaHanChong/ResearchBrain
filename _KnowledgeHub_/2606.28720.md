# CubifyGS: Object-Centric 3D Gaussian Splatting for Lifelong Dynamic Scene Maintenance

> [!summary] Summary
> CubifyGS introduces an object-centric 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that efficiently maintains dynamic scene maps by treating movable instances as reusable assets, effectively eliminating ghosting artifacts and improving reconstruction quality and update speed. The system achieves an average PSNR improvement of 35.83% and operates at 20 FPS in environments with rigid object rearrangement.

## Problem

- Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods struggle to maintain map fidelity in dynamic environments with rigid object rearrangement, often leading to persistent ghosting artifacts at previous object locations.
- Conventional gradient-based re-optimization for moved objects is computationally inefficient, requiring lengthy re-convergence and potentially destabilizing surrounding static map regions.
- Current 3DGS approaches primarily focus on static scenes or transient dynamics, lacking explicit mechanisms for managing discrete object appearance, disappearance, and movement of reusable objects.

## Method

- An object-centric ==3D Gaussian Splatting== framework (**CubifyGS**) is proposed, representing scenes with a static background and dynamic foreground objects, supported by a ==global asset library== of reusable Gaussian object templates.
- A ==spatio-temporal dynamics perception module== tracks objects, initializes new ones, and explicitly detects vanishing objects using ==hierarchical data association== and ==ray-casted occupancy analysis==.
- A lifelong scene maintenance strategy performs ==probabilistic ghost pruning== for vanished objects and ==semantic-aware asset retrieval== with two-stage alignment for newly appearing objects, complemented by ==event-triggered adaptive optimization==.

## Results

- CubifyGS achieves an average PSNR improvement of **35.83%** over the strongest baseline across five benchmark sequences, demonstrating superior reconstruction recovery and earlier elimination of ghost artifacts.
- The system operates at approximately **20 FPS** for dynamic event detection and map maintenance, which is over **40 times** faster than existing continuous-training dynamic baselines.
- It provides robust 3D object localization, with an average AP25 of **65.2**, and a near **100%** success rate for fine pose alignment of retrieved assets for initial angular discrepancies up to **25°**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting from primitive-level, gradient-based re-optimization to object-level asset management can effectively address persistent artifacts and computational redundancy in dynamic 3DGS mapping.
> - Explicitly detecting and managing object lifecycle events (vanish, appear) enables direct manipulation of Gaussian primitives through pruning and asset insertion, providing faster and cleaner map updates.
> - Concentrating computational resources on dynamic regions using focus-driven optimization ensures rapid adaptation to changes while preserving the stability and fidelity of the static environment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) and 3D scene reconstruction has seen significant advancements, particularly with the emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). 3DGS has demonstrated capabilities in high-fidelity reconstruction and real-time rendering of static environments, making it a prominent technique in dense SLAM systems. However, a significant challenge remains in maintaining these map representations in dynamic environments, particularly when rigid objects are rearranged.

Existing 3DGS-based methods predominantly assume static scenes or focus on handling transient dynamic interference (e.g., moving people or vehicles) or continuous non-rigid motion (e.g., deformable objects). These approaches often employ techniques such as motion filtering, robust optimization, or decoupling dynamic elements from the background. When faced with discrete object rearrangement—where objects are moved, appear, or disappear—these systems typically resort to a "passive re-optimization" or "erase-and-reconstruct" paradigm. This involves implicitly erasing outdated observations and gradually reconstructing the object at its new location through gradient-based optimization. This process often leads to persistent visual artifacts, such as ghosting at previous locations, and is computationally inefficient due to the need for re-convergence from scratch.

CubifyGS positions itself by addressing this specific gap: the efficient and artifact-free maintenance of 3DGS maps under rigid object rearrangement. It departs from the conventional primitive-level, gradient-based optimization for dynamic regions. Instead, it proposes an object-centric approach that treats movable instances as reusable Gaussian assets. This shifts the maintenance paradigm from reactive re-optimization to active asset management, aiming to provide more rapid updates, suppress artifacts effectively, and reduce computational overhead in scenarios common in indoor robotics. The work is complementary to spatio-temporal metric-semantic systems by focusing on photorealistic 3DGS map maintenance.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of CubifyGS is to develop a lifelong dynamic mapping framework capable of efficiently maintaining high-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splatting maps in environments characterized by rigid object rearrangement. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of current 3DGS-based methods when applied to such dynamic scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from two key issues observed in existing methods:

1. **Persistent Artifacts:** When objects are moved or removed, conventional gradient-based optimization methods struggle to rapidly "erase" the outdated representation of the object from its previous location. This leads to persistent "ghosting" artifacts—faint or blurry remnants of the object—which degrade the map's fidelity and can mislead downstream robotic tasks that rely on accurate scene representations. The implicit nature of erasing via continuous gradients is slow and often incomplete.
2. **Computational Redundancy and Slow Recovery:** Reconstructing an object from scratch at a new location using gradient-based methods is computationally intensive. The system must collect new observations, optimize numerous Gaussian parameters, and gradually converge to a new stable representation. This process is slow, especially for complex objects, and can destabilize the optimization of surrounding static regions of the map. The repeated re-optimization of known objects, simply because they have moved, represents a redundant computational effort.

To address these, CubifyGS is motivated by the insight that indoor dynamics are often object-sparse and involve reusable objects. The central idea is to shift from a passive, primitive-level re-optimization approach to an active, object-level asset management strategy. This involves:
- Explicitly representing movable objects as reusable Gaussian assets.
- Detecting object lifecycle events (appearance and disappearance).
- Updating the map through explicit operations like asset retrieval, rigid transformation, and targeted pruning rather than implicit reconstruction.
- Focusing computational resources only on regions affected by changes to ensure rapid integration and adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and approach

CubifyGS is structured as an object-centric 3D Gaussian Splatting framework designed for robust map maintenance under rigid object rearrangements. It processes continuous RGB-D streams and camera poses through several interconnected modules:

#### 3.1. A. Unified Object-Centric Gaussian Representation

The framework represents the scene as a discretized object-centric structure, differentiating between a static background and dynamic foreground objects. It also maintains a global asset library for reusable object templates.

1. **Active Scene List:** The current scene is modeled as a set of objects, $S_t = \{O_{bg}\} \cup \{O_1, \dots, O_N\}$, comprising a static background ($O_{bg}$) and dynamic foreground object instances ($O_i$). Each object $O_i$ is defined by a tuple $(G_i, F_i, B_i)$:
 - **Geometry ($G_i$):** A collection of 3D Gaussian primitives, each characterized by its mean ($\mu$), covariance ($\Sigma$), opacity ($\alpha$), and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients ($c$).
 - **Semantics ($F_i$):** A multi-view semantic feature bank containing DINO features extracted from 12 fixed canonical viewpoints ($V_{can}$) around the object. This bank aids in viewpoint-specific retrieval and matching.
 - **Spatial Bounding Box ($B_i$):** A gravity-aligned 3D bounding box that defines the object's spatial extent and pose in the world frame.

2. **Global Asset Library:** A library $L = \{A_k\}$ stores object templates in a canonical coordinate system. When an asset $A_k$ is instantiated as a scene object $O_i$, its Gaussian parameters are transformed to the world space using a rigid transformation ($T_i = [R_i|t_i]$) derived from its bounding box $B_i$. This enables immediate reuse without re-optimization.

#### 3.2. B. Spatio-Temporal Dynamics Perception

This module manages the lifecycle of object instances by tracking existing objects, initializing new ones, and detecting vanished objects.

1. **Hierarchical Tracking and Initialization:** The tracking backbone is based on a reconstruction-free paradigm (BoxFusion), treating tracking as probabilistic state estimation on the image stream.
 - **3D Object Proposals:** 3D object proposals ($P_t$) are generated from current RGB-D observations using a visual foundation model (e.g., CubifyAnything).
 - **Hierarchical Data Association:** A two-layer mechanism links proposals $P_t$ to existing instances in $S_{t-1}$:
 - **Layer 1 (3D Geometry):** Volumetric Intersection-over-Union (IoU) is computed between proposal convex hulls and existing object bounding boxes for prominent objects.
 - **Layer 2 (2D Projection):** For smaller objects prone to depth noise, 2D IoU is computed between projected 3D boundaries of existing objects and visual proposals.
 - **Bounding Box Update:** Matched objects update their bounding box $B_i$ via a Particle Filter optimizer. Unmatched proposals are queued for initialization as new objects.

2. **Vanishing Object Detection via Ray-Casting:** To explicitly detect object disappearance, a Ray-Casted Occupancy Analysis mechanism estimates the existence probability $p^{(i)}_t$ for each tracked object.
 - **Ray Classification:** For an object $O_i$ in the view frustum, rays are cast through its projected Region of Interest (ROI). Each ray is classified as Occluded, Hit, or Free based on its observed depth compared to the object's bounding box intersection interval. A "Free" state indicates the ray passed through the object's volume, suggesting disappearance.
 - **Temporal Decay Rule:** The existence probability $p^{(i)}_t$ is updated using a temporal decay rule, penalizing "Free" states heavily and allowing recovery for "Hit" states. Objects are flagged "Vanished" if $p^{(i)}_t$ falls below a threshold after a grace period.

#### 3.3. C. Lifelong Scene Maintenance Strategy

This module executes core map updates based on dynamic event detections.

1. **Probabilistic Ghost Pruning:** When an object is flagged as "Vanished," all Gaussian primitives within its bounding box $B_i$ are explicitly removed from the map. This addresses ghosting but creates geometric voids.
2. **Semantic-Aware Asset Retrieval & Alignment:** For newly appearing objects, the system queries the Global Asset Library $L$ for reusable models.
 - **Retrieval:** Cosine similarity is used to match the new object's visual feature ($f_{new}$) with the multi-view feature banks of assets in $L$. The best matching asset $A^*$ and its canonical viewpoint $k^*$ are selected if similarity exceeds a threshold. This provides coarse alignment.
 - **Fine Alignment:** The coarse alignment is refined by reformulating object pose estimation as a differentiable camera tracking problem. Gaussian parameters are frozen, and the object's relative camera pose $T$ is optimized by minimizing photometric error. A gradient approximation focusing on projected Gaussian means accelerates this process.
3. **Candidate Validation and Asset Registration:** Dynamic objects undergoing optimization are promoted to the Global Asset Library only when they meet strict convergence and coverage criteria, defined by geometric coverage (angular span of observations) and gradient convergence degree (stable gradient magnitudes over consecutive frames).

#### 3.4. D. Event-Triggered Adaptive Optimization

To address geometric voids from pruning and adapt new assets, an adaptive optimization strategy concentrates computational resources on affected regions.

1. **ROI Mask Generation:** For each change event, a 3D Region of Interest (corresponding to the object's bounding box) is projected onto the 2D image plane to create a binary interest mask ($M^{(τ)}_{roi}$).
2. **Focus-Driven Gradient Descent:** The standard 3DGS loss function is modulated by a spatial weighting map ($\Omega(u)$). Higher importance ($\gamma_{focus} \gg 1$) is assigned to pixels within the ROI mask, while a baseline weight is maintained for the static background. This ensures that gradient flow is dominated by residuals in dynamic regions, enabling rapid convergence for newly filled background areas or added objects, while preserving the stability of the static environment.

### 4. Main findings and results

The validation of CubifyGS involved a newly constructed high-fidelity dynamic benchmark in Blender, comprising four indoor scenes (Bedroom, Office, Living Room, Kitchen) with discrete rigid-object rearrangement events, augmented with real-world data from the bonn kidnapping box2 dataset. Ground truth camera poses were used for all evaluations to isolate mapping quality from tracking drift. Comparisons were made against static 3DGS mapping baselines (MonoGS, GS-ICP SLAM, SplaTAM) and one dynamic baseline (WildGS-SLAM).

**System-level Comparisons:**
- **Mapping Quality:** CubifyGS demonstrated superior reconstruction recovery, particularly in dynamic Regions of Interest (ROI) after scene changes. It achieved an average PSNR improvement of 35.83% over the strongest baseline across the five benchmark sequences. For instance, in Bedroom-1, CubifyGS improved PSNR by 7.58 dB compared to MonoGS. Qualitatively, CubifyGS removed ghost artifacts earlier and restored sharper object structures with fewer optimization iterations than baselines, leading to more consistent visual quality post-event.
- **Runtime Efficiency:** CubifyGS achieved dynamic event detection and map maintenance at approximately 20 FPS on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. In contrast, WildGS-SLAM, which continuously trains an MLP for motion detection, operated at less than 0.5 FPS, indicating CubifyGS was over 40 times faster.

**Dynamics Perception Verification:**
- **3D Object Localization:** CubifyGS significantly outperformed the single-frame CubifyAnything (CA) baseline in 3D object localization. CubifyGS achieved an average AP25 of 65.2 compared to CA's 26.7. This improvement highlights the effectiveness of CubifyGS's multi-view association and temporal aggregation for robust perception, providing reliable geometric priors for subsequent asset retrieval.

**Retrieval & Alignment Analysis:**
- **Discriminative Semantics:** t-SNE projections of DINOv3 features extracted from multi-view object observations showed well-separated clusters for distinct instances, confirming the robustness of the semantic representation against viewpoint variations.
- **Coarse Alignment Precision:** Cosine similarity matching for a query view consistently identified reference views within ±15° of the ground truth, effectively bounding the initial angular error for asset retrieval.
- **Fine Alignment Convergence:** The photometric fine alignment module demonstrated a near 100% success rate for initial angular discrepancies up to 25°, fully encompassing the ±15° coarse retrieval error bound. This indicates robust and precise pose recovery.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Efficacy of Focus-Driven Optimization (FDO):** Disabling FDO resulted in a noticeable performance drop (e.g., PSNR decreased from 20.50 to 17.58 on Livingroom-1). This confirms that actively weighting gradients in dynamic regions is critical for rapid background inpainting and asset refinement.
- **Significance of the Global Asset Library (GAL):** A "Cold Start" baseline, where GAL was disabled and new Gaussians were initialized from scratch, showed a severe PSNR drop (e.g., 4.52 dB on Livingroom-1). This indicates that the explicit retrieval and snapping of high-quality, pre-optimized assets from the GAL is essential for instant, high-fidelity scene recovery.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The development of CubifyGS represents a methodological shift in how 3DGS maps are maintained in dynamic environments, particularly those involving rigid object rearrangement. By moving from primitive-level re-optimization to object-level active asset management, the framework addresses two fundamental limitations of prior work: persistent ghosting artifacts and computational inefficiency.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:
- **Improved Map Fidelity and Consistency:** The explicit pruning of vanished objects and the direct insertion of reusable Gaussian assets enable rapid elimination of ghosting artifacts, leading to higher fidelity and more consistent map representations over time. This reduces the pathological states that could misguide downstream robotic tasks.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Responsiveness:** By leveraging a global asset library and event-triggered adaptive optimization, CubifyGS significantly reduces the computational overhead associated with reconstructing objects from scratch. This allows for faster map updates and a higher frame rate (20 FPS), which is crucial for real-time robotic applications requiring dynamic scene understanding.
- **Robustness to Discrete Changes:** The framework specifically targets discrete object rearrangement, a common challenge in indoor robotics that is often mishandled by methods designed for transient or continuous motion. Its hierarchical tracking, ray-casting-based vanishing detection, and two-stage alignment contribute to robust handling of these events.
- **New Benchmark for Dynamic Scenes:** The introduction of a new high-fidelity dynamic indoor benchmark provides a valuable resource for future research in lifelong dynamic scene mapping, enabling standardized evaluation of object-level rearrangement scenarios.
- **Foundation for Advanced Robotic Autonomy:** More accurate, up-to-date, and efficient scene maps can serve as a better foundation for advanced robotic applications such as long-term navigation, object search, mobile manipulation, and human-robot interaction in evolving environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that the current study focuses on rigid object rearrangement with reusable, fixed asset templates. Future work aims to:
- Extend CubifyGS to broader dynamic scenarios beyond rigid rearrangements.
- Investigate incremental asset updates and cross-session asset merging to enhance the flexibility and evolution of the asset library.
- Improve the robustness and efficiency of the perception module.
- Further evaluate how the rapid and accurate map maintenance provided by CubifyGS translates into benefits for downstream tasks, such as long-term camera localization.
