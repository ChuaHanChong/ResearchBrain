# Self-Supervised Correspondence in Visuomotor Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces self-supervised dense correspondence training to enhance visuomotor policy learning, enabling robots to acquire manipulation skills that generalize across object classes and deformable configurations with high sample efficiency. The approach demonstrated performance comparable to ground truth baselines in simulation and achieved up to 97.7% success rates on challenging real-world tasks using as few as 50 human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policy learning methods struggle with generalization to varied object configurations, deformations, and appearances due to a lack of explicit spatial correspondence training.
- Training complex visuomotor policies typically requires substantial amounts of data, leading to high sample inefficiency and hindering scalability in real-world scenarios.
- Traditional visual training paradigms (e.g., autoencoders, pose-based losses) are often limited to rigid, graspable objects or require significant human supervision beyond initial policy demonstrations.

## Method

- Integrates a self-supervised **Dense Descriptor (DD)** model, a 34-layer ResNet, to generate full-resolution descriptor images that establish ==pixel-to-pixel correspondences== between views.
- Formulates four methods for incorporating these dense correspondence models into visuomotor policy learning, ranging from fixed descriptor sets to end-to-end optimization.
- Utilizes ==behavioral cloning== for policy learning and introduces a novel ==data augmentation== technique with injected noise to enhance robustness and enable local feedback stabilization.

## Results

- In simulation, Dense Descriptor methods achieved performance comparable to policies with ground truth world state access, with DD-3D reaching **100%** success on 'Reach T only' and 'Reach T + R', and **98%** on 'Push plate'.
- On hardware, policies learned from **50-150** demonstrations achieved high success rates on challenging tasks, including **97.1%** for 'Push sugar box' and **97.7%** for 'Flip shoe (single instance)', demonstrating generalization across object classes (e.g., **77.3%** for 'Flip shoe, class-general').
- Dense Descriptor methods exhibited superior sample efficiency, with DD-2D outperforming autoencoder and end-to-end baselines with **30 demonstrations** compared to **200** on the 'Reach, T + R' task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised dense correspondence training provides a highly informative and compact visual representation, allowing policies to generalize robustly to deformable objects and novel instances within an object class.
> - Decoupling the visual representation learning from direct policy optimization, especially with a fixed pre-trained descriptor set, can be surprisingly effective and computationally efficient.
> - Data augmentation via state perturbation during imitation learning is crucial for developing closed-loop policies that can react to disturbances and maintain task objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic manipulation skills necessitates the use of vision-based policies capable of learning new tasks scalably and with minimal human supervision. Prior research in training the visual components of visuomotor policies has primarily explored methods such as end-to-end training, autoencoding, and pose-based losses.

End-to-end training allows a policy to be optimized directly from a learning signal derived from robot actions, but its performance often benefits from auxiliary signals. Autoencoders are commonly employed for unsupervised visual training to generate compact representations, sometimes in conjunction with end-to-end training or with frozen encoders. Pose-based losses utilize robot kinematics and object pose information, often assuming rigid and graspable objects, to train visual models to predict object poses. However, these conventional approaches may not fully leverage the potential of rich self-supervision derived from dense three-dimensional computer vision techniques, such as correspondence learning, which can be automated by robots without human input. Limitations of these methods include their applicability to deformable or ungraspable objects, the potential for high sample complexity, and challenges in generalizing across varied object configurations or classes.

Concurrently, various methods for acquiring vision-based closed-loop policies exist. Reinforcement learning (RL) has shown promising results, but real-world reward measurement remains a challenge, particularly for tasks beyond self-supervised grasping or those requiring assumptions about object rigidity. Sim-to-real transfer is another direction, but often demands significant engineering effort for each new simulation scenario. Imitation learning from demonstration has emerged as a viable approach for generating real-time, closed-loop visuomotor policies, yet faces challenges related to reliability, sample complexity, and characterizing generalization capabilities across varied distributions.

This work addresses the limitations of prior visual training methods by introducing a self-supervised dense visual correspondence approach for visuomotor policy learning. It aims to demonstrate that directly training for spatial correspondence can offer benefits in generalization performance and sample efficiency, particularly for challenging manipulation scenarios involving deformable, visually ambiguous, or diverse object classes, and when learning with limited demonstration data. The paper builds upon previous work in self-supervised dense visual object descriptors, extending it to dynamic environments and integrating it into visuomotor policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the utility of self-supervised correspondence training for enhancing the generalization performance and sample efficiency of visuomotor policy learning. The authors posit that integrating dense visual correspondence, a fundamental computer vision concept, into robotic visuomotor policies can address several limitations inherent in existing visual training paradigms.

The motivation for this approach stems from observations regarding prior methods:
1. **Limited Generalization:** Traditional methods like end-to-end training, autoencoding, and pose-based losses do not explicitly train for spatial correspondence. Consequently, they may struggle to generalize effectively to variations in object configuration, deformation, or appearance, as the visual features learned might not be robustly invariant to these changes. Pose-based methods, in particular, are typically restricted to rigid and graspable objects, limiting their applicability to deformable or novel objects.
2. **Sample Inefficiency:** Learning complex visuomotor policies, especially in real-world scenarios, often requires substantial amounts of data. The authors hypothesize that a visual representation grounded in dense correspondence could provide a more informative and compact input for policy learning, thereby reducing the data requirements (sample complexity) for acquiring new skills.
3. **Scalability with Human Supervision:** Achieving general-purpose manipulation demands methods that can scale efficiently, requiring minimal incremental human effort per new task or object. Self-supervised correspondence learning offers a mechanism to enrich visual representations without requiring additional human annotations or supervision beyond the initial robot demonstrations for policy learning. This contrasts with methods that might need specific object models or pose annotations.
4. **Addressing Challenging Manipulation Scenarios:** The research specifically aims to develop policies capable of handling complex real-world challenges, including:
 - **Deformable Objects:** Objects that change shape, making rigid body pose estimation inadequate.
 - **Class-General Manipulation:** Policies that can operate on novel instances within a class of objects (e.g., different shoes) rather than just a single trained instance.
 - **Visually Challenging Scenes:** Scenarios with textureless or symmetrical objects, significant clutter, moderate occlusion, and varying lighting conditions, which can confound vision systems that rely on distinct features or ideal conditions.
 - **Limited Data:** Demonstrating robust policy learning with a small number of human demonstrations (e.g., as few as 50-150).

By leveraging self-supervised dense correspondence, the authors intend to establish a visual foundation for policies that can implicitly encode object configurations and relationships robustly, thereby enabling more capable, generalizable, and data-efficient robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates self-supervised dense visual correspondence training into the visuomotor policy learning framework. The core idea is to replace or augment traditional visual feature extraction with a visual model explicitly trained to produce dense descriptors that can establish pixel-to-pixel correspondences between images.

**Visuomotor Policy Formulation:**
The policy architecture is factored into two main components:
1. **Visual Model (`f_θ_v`):** Processes high-dimensional raw images (`o_image`) into a lower-dimensional representation (`z`).
2. **Policy Model (`π_θ_p`):** Combines `z` with other robot observations (`o_robot`, e.g., robot joint states, gripper width) to output actions (`a`).
The action space consists of desired end-effector pose changes (`T_Δ,cmd`) and gripper width setpoints (`w_gripper`). `o_robot` includes 3D points on the hand, axis-angle rotation, and gripper width.

**Visual Correspondence Models for Visuomotor Policy Learning:**
The central innovation lies in how the visual model `f_θ_v` is trained. Instead of end-to-end, autoencoding, or pose-based losses, it is trained using self-supervised dense correspondence, building on prior work like Dense Object Nets.
- **Dense Descriptor Model (`f_dense_θ_v`):** This is a deep Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), specifically a 34-layer ResNet pretrained on ImageNet, which maps a full-resolution RGB image to a full-resolution descriptor image. Each pixel in the descriptor image contains a D-dimensional vector representing a "dense descriptor" for that spatial location.
- **Correspondence Function (`f_C`):** This non-parametric function takes the dense descriptor image from `f_dense_θ_v` and a set of P descriptors `{d_i}` as input. It then predicts the spatial location of these descriptors, either as 2D pixel coordinates (`K=2`) or 3D coordinates (`K=3`), forming the `z` representation for the policy.

The authors propose four distinct methods for integrating these dense correspondence models into visuomotor policy learning, all optimizing a generic policy-based loss function `L(π_θ_p(z, o_robot))`:
1. **Fixed Descriptor Set:** Only the policy parameters `θ_p` are optimized. The `f_dense_θ_v` and the initial set of descriptors `{d_i}` (sampled from a single masked reference descriptor image) are kept fixed after initial training. This method is noted for its surprising effectiveness and speed due to not optimizing the deep CNN during policy training.
2. **Descriptor Set Optimization:** Optimizes `θ_p` and the descriptor set `{d_i}`, while `f_dense_θ_v` remains fixed. This allows the policy to select which features to correspond to.
3. **End-to-End Dense Optimization:** All parameters (`θ_p` and `θ_v`) are jointly optimized. The visual model `f_dense_θ_v` is part of the end-to-end policy learning.
4. **End-to-End with Correspondence Pretraining:** A differentiable operation `g(·)` is applied directly to the output of a `f_dense_θ_v` model that was previously trained on dense correspondence. Both `θ_p` and `θ_v` are then optimized. For instance, `g(·)` could be non-parametric channel-wise spatial expectations.

**Imitation Learning Formulation:**
Behavioral cloning is employed to learn policies. Given a dataset of observation-action trajectories, the policy parameters `Θ` are optimized to minimize a behavioral cloning loss `L_BC`, which is a weighted sum of L1 and L2 norms between the expert action `a*` and the policy's predicted action `π_Θ(o_t)`.

**Training for Feedback through Data Augmentation:**
To address cascading errors common in imitation learning and enable local feedback stabilization, a novel data augmentation technique is introduced. During training, noise is injected into the robot's state (`o_robot`) to generate perturbed observation-action pairs `((o_image, ˜o_robot), a)`. This encourages the policy to command the same global-frame setpoint `a` even with slight deviations in the robot's perceived state.

**Multi-View Time-Synchronized Correspondence Training:**
Unlike prior work focusing on static scenes, this method allows training correspondence models with dynamic environments. Pixel correspondences are established between two approximately time-synchronized camera views using depth images, camera poses, and intrinsics. Object masking is performed using 3D-based background subtraction. To promote generalization with limited views and noisy depth data, image augmentations (rotation, scale, shear) and photometric-error-based rejection of correspondences are applied.

**Policy Models:**
Two standard classes of policy networks are utilized: Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) for current observations and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) recurrent networks for processing full observation sequences.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Simulation Experiments:** Used to compare generalization and sample complexity. Tasks include "Reach T only," "Reach T + R," "Push box," and "Push plate." Baselines include ground truth 3D/2D points, autoencoders (AE) with various configurations, and end-to-end (E2E) training with different vision backbones. Our methods are DD-2D (image-space spatial expectation) and DD-3D (3D-space spatial expectation using depth).
- **Hardware Experiments:** Performed on a Kuka IIWA LBR robot with a Schunk WSG 50 gripper and RealSense D415 RGBD cameras. Five challenging manipulation tasks are tested: "Push sugar box," "Flip shoe (single instance/class-general)," "Pick-then-hang hat," and "Push-then-grab plate." All hardware policies used RGB input (DD-2D) and descriptor set optimization with LSTM networks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of self-supervised dense descriptors as input for visuomotor policies and compare their performance against established baselines in terms of generalization and sample complexity in both simulated and real-world environments.

**Simulation Results:**
- **Performance Comparison (Table I):** The proposed Dense Descriptor (DD) methods demonstrated performance comparable to policies with access to ground truth world state information. Specifically, DD-3D achieved 100% success on "Reach T only," "Reach T + R," and 98% on "Push plate," closely matching or exceeding the ground truth baselines. In contrast, Autoencoder (AE) and End-to-End (E2E) baselines showed significantly lower and more varied success rates across tasks, often failing to reach 50% success on tasks like "Reach T only" and "Push plate." Attempts to enhance autoencoder performance (e.g., with masks or end-to-end training) yielded inconsistent improvements. Using a deeper (34-layer ResNet) backbone for E2E did not improve closed-loop performance, suggesting that the benefits of the proposed method stem from the correspondence training rather than merely increased model capacity.
- **Sample Complexity and Generalization (Figure 4):** On the "Reach, T + R" task, the DD-2D method with as few as 30 demonstrations outperformed both AE and E2E methods trained with 200 demonstrations. Analysis of failure modes indicated that the few failures of the DD method, similar to GT-2D, occurred when object configurations were outside the convex hull of the training data. DD-3D, utilizing 3D information, demonstrated better generalization to configurations outside the training distribution. For the "Push plate" task, which required higher precision and closed-loop feedback, DD-2D performed nearly as well as the GT-2D baseline and substantially better than AE and E2E, with DD-3D further improving performance.
- **Effect of Noise Augmentation (Table II):** The proposed noise augmentation technique for feedback training showed a notable improvement in task success. For instance, on the "Reach, T + R" task with 30 demonstrations, GT-2D improved from 5.6% to 73.3% success with noise augmentation, and DD-2D improved from 1.1% to 73.3%. This indicates its effectiveness in enhancing the robustness of behavior-cloned policies to local disturbances.

**Hardware Results:**
- **Learned Correspondences from Dynamic Scenes (Figures 5 & 6):** Despite challenges such as noisy depth sensors and imperfect calibration, the multi-view time-synchronized correspondence training successfully learned visual models capable of identifying correspondences in real-world dynamic scenes. These models demonstrated the ability to correspond pixels across object classes (e.g., different shoe types), across deformable object configurations, and in varied backgrounds.
- **Real-World Visuomotor Policies (Figure 1 & Table III):** The extensive hardware validation on challenging manipulation tasks confirmed the real-world applicability of the method.
 - **High Reliability:** Several tasks achieved high success rates, including "Push sugar box" (97.1% with disturbances) and "Flip shoe, single instance" (97.7% without disturbances) with demonstration data ranging from 50 to 94 trajectories.
 - **Generalization Capabilities:** Policies demonstrated generalization across classes of objects (e.g., "Flip shoe, class-general" achieved 77.3% success on novel shoe instances) and reaction to deformable object configurations (e.g., the hat in "Pick-then-hang hat" after disturbances).
 - **Visually Challenging Scenarios:** Tasks such as "Push-then-grab plate" were successfully executed despite visual clutter, object symmetry, and lack of texture, requiring closed-loop visual feedback and extrinsic dexterity.
 - **Data Efficiency:** All real-world policies were learned from a limited number of human demonstrations (between 50 and 150), which underscores the sample efficiency claim.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in visuomotor policy learning by introducing self-supervised dense correspondence training as a method for robust and efficient visual representation learning. The findings indicate that this approach addresses several key challenges in robotic manipulation, offering notable benefits compared to prevalent vision-based baselines.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that by training visual models explicitly for dense spatial correspondence, robots can acquire highly capable policies that:
- **Exhibit Enhanced Generalization:** The learned policies generalize effectively across object classes, adapt to deformable object configurations, and operate in visually complex environments (e.g., textureless, cluttered, occluded scenes). This capability is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured real-world settings where object appearance and state are highly variable.
- **Achieve High Sample Efficiency:** The method enables the learning of complex manipulation skills with a relatively small number of human demonstrations (as few as 50 demonstrations), reducing the human effort and data collection burden typically associated with training robotic policies. This contrasts with methods requiring hundreds or thousands of demonstrations.
- **Attain Robustness:** The integration of correspondence training, coupled with a novel data augmentation technique for feedback, contributes to policies that are more stable and capable of reacting to disturbances and maintaining task objectives in a closed-loop manner.
- **Leverage Self-Supervision:** The core visual training process is self-supervised, meaning it does not require additional human labeling or supervision beyond the initial robot demonstrations for the policy itself. This inherent scalability makes it a promising direction for developing more autonomous robot learning systems.
- **Reach Near Ground-Truth Performance:** In simulation, the method's performance closely approaches that of policies granted access to ground truth world state information, suggesting it extracts highly relevant and accurate spatial information from raw images.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for the field of robotics. It provides a foundation for developing more general-purpose manipulation capabilities in robots, moving beyond tasks with rigid, known objects to address a wider array of real-world scenarios. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in areas such as manufacturing, logistics, and service, where adaptability and robust object handling are critical. Furthermore, by improving sample efficiency and reducing reliance on extensive human supervision, this research contributes to making robot learning more accessible and scalable. Future research could explore integrating these spatial correspondence representations with object instance segmentation or higher-level task understanding to further expand the scope of robotic intelligence.
