# G3Flow: Generative 3D Semantic Flow for Pose-aware and Generalizable Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> G3Flow provides a framework for generative 3D semantic flow, enabling robotic manipulation policies to integrate geometric precision with robust semantic understanding for pose-aware and generalizable object tasks. The approach achieved an average success rate of 68.3% in pose-aware manipulation and 50.1% in cross-object generalization, outperforming baseline methods by 22.1% and 18.4% respectively.

## Problem

- Current 3D imitation learning methods often lack explicit semantic understanding, hindering tasks that require interaction with specific object parts or precise pose control.
- Robotic manipulation policies struggle to generalize effectively to objects that are geometrically diverse but semantically similar, requiring extensive retraining for new instances.
- Existing semantic field approaches face practical limitations, including the need for manual keypoint selection, multi-view setups for complete field generation, and inconsistency under occlusion.

## Method

- **G3Flow** constructs a real-time, object-centric, and occlusion-robust 3D semantic flow by combining multi-view observations, 3D generative models, and vision foundation models.
- A complete digital twin of the object is created from multi-view RGB images, from which a canonical 3D semantic point cloud is generated in a virtual environment using DINOv2 features.
- The pre-generated semantic flow is dynamically maintained by robustly tracking the object's 6D pose in real-time using FoundationPose, ensuring spatial alignment and completeness even under occlusion, and then conditioning a diffusion policy with this flow.

## Results

- G3Flow achieved an average success rate of **68.3%** in terminal-constrained (pose-aware) manipulation tasks, outperforming 3D Diffusion Policy by **22.1%**.
- In cross-object generalization tasks, G3Flow attained an average success rate of **50.1%**, surpassing the strongest baseline by **18.4%** and demonstrating robustness to geometric and scale variations.
- The framework operates at **34.04** Hz on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090, approximately 6 times faster than GenDP, while providing a more complete and robust semantic field, especially under occlusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating complete and occlusion-robust 3D semantic flow, derived from digital twins and foundation models, substantially improves a robot's ability to perform manipulation tasks requiring precise pose awareness.
> - Maintaining semantic consistency through dynamic object tracking allows policies to generalize more effectively across geometrically diverse objects and categories with limited training data.
> - The use of foundation models for 3D generation and semantic feature extraction, combined with a lightweight pose tracker, enables real-time and annotation-free semantic understanding suitable for robotic deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing challenges in imitation learning that utilize 3D perception. Recent advancements in imitation learning have enabled robots to perform a variety of tasks, with methods often relying on either image-based (2D) or 3D representations like point clouds or voxels. Image-based methods can face limitations in capturing precise geometric relationships, which impacts fine manipulation and sample efficiency. 3D imitation learning, including diffusion-based policies, has shown promise in capturing geometric information and modeling multi-modal distributions for few-shot learning.

However, a recurring limitation across many geometry-centric 3D methods is the absence of crucial semantic understanding. Tasks such as placing a shoe with a specific orientation or using tools require the robot to identify and interact with semantically meaningful parts of an object (e.g., the toe of a shoe, a tool's handle). Furthermore, these methods often struggle with generalizing to objects that are semantically similar but geometrically diverse.

To address this, the concept of 3D semantic fields has emerged, aiming to integrate rich semantic information with geometric features. Previous works like D3Fields introduced dynamic 3D descriptor fields, and GenDP explored category-level generalization through semantic fields. Despite these efforts, existing semantic field approaches typically require manual keypoint selection, multi-view setups for complete field generation, and encounter difficulties in maintaining semantic consistency during dynamic interactions, especially under occlusion caused by the robot's own arm.

G3Flow positions itself to bridge the gap between geometric precision and semantic comprehension by proposing a method for constructing and maintaining real-time, object-centric, and occlusion-robust 3D semantic representations. It aims to overcome the limitations of prior semantic field approaches by leveraging recent advances in foundation models for 3D generation, semantic feature extraction, and robust pose tracking, thereby enhancing both pose-aware manipulation and cross-object generalization capabilities of diffusion policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance robotic manipulation policies by integrating robust semantic understanding with geometric precision, specifically for tasks that demand accurate spatial control and generalization across object variations.

The motivation for developing G3Flow stems from several limitations observed in current imitation learning paradigms for robotic manipulation:

- **Limited Semantic Understanding in Geometry-Centric Methods:** While 3D imitation learning methods, particularly those based on diffusion policies using point clouds or voxels, excel at capturing geometric information and modeling complex action distributions, they often lack explicit semantic understanding. This deficiency becomes problematic in tasks requiring robots to identify and interact with specific, semantically defined parts of an object (e.g., the handle of a cup, the head of a tool, the toe of a shoe). Purely geometric representations may treat all points equally or categorize them based solely on shape, failing to differentiate functional or semantically important regions.
- **Challenges in Pose-Aware Manipulation:** Many real-world manipulation tasks require precise pose control, where the orientation or specific placement of an object is critical (e.g., placing a bottle upright, orienting a shoe correctly). Without semantic understanding, geometry-centric policies may achieve approximate placement but struggle with the nuanced, semantically driven alignment requirements.
- **Difficulty in Cross-Object Generalization:** Current methods often exhibit limited generalization capabilities when encountering objects that are geometrically diverse from their training set but semantically similar (e.g., different types of shoes, various tools). A policy trained purely on geometric features might not recognize that a new, differently shaped object still possesses a "handle" or a "toe" that requires a specific manipulation strategy. Semantic understanding is crucial for transferring learned skills to novel instances within the same category or across semantically related categories.
- **Practical Limitations of Existing Semantic Field Approaches:** Previous attempts to introduce semantic fields into robotic manipulation, such as D3Fields and GenDP, have faced practical hurdles. These include the necessity for manual keypoint selection (requiring human intervention and expertise), the need for multi-view camera setups for complete field generation (increasing hardware complexity), and difficulties in maintaining semantic consistency, particularly when objects are partially occluded by the robot's arm or other environmental elements during dynamic interaction. Such occlusions lead to incomplete observations and hinder reliable feature acquisition for downstream policies.
- **Need for Real-time, Robust, and Annotation-Free Solutions:** To be practical for real-world robotics, a semantic representation system must operate in real-time, be robust to dynamic environmental changes and occlusions, and ideally, eliminate the need for laborious manual annotations. Existing approaches often fall short in one or more of these aspects.

G3Flow aims to address these motivations by proposing a foundation model-driven framework that constructs a real-time, object-centric, and occlusion-robust 3D semantic flow without manual annotations. This flow is intended to provide complete semantic understanding, even under occlusions, thereby enabling imitation learning policies to achieve higher precision in pose-aware tasks and greater generalization across object variations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

G3Flow introduces a novel framework for generative 3D semantic flow, designed to provide real-time, object-centric, and occlusion-robust semantic representations for robotic manipulation. The approach integrates 3D generative models, vision foundation models, and robust pose tracking within a diffusion policy framework. The methodology operates in two main phases: initial semantic flow construction and dynamic semantic flow maintenance, which then informs a G3Flow-enhanced diffusion policy.

#### 3.1. Initial Semantic Flow Construction (Offline/Initialization Phase)

This phase focuses on generating a complete and high-fidelity 3D semantic representation of the target object.

- **Object-Centric Exploration:** To overcome limitations of single-view observations (e.g., incomplete geometry due to self-occlusion or robot arm occlusion), an active exploration strategy is employed. The robot's wrist camera systematically captures multi-perspective RGB observations around the target object. Initially, Grounded-SAM is used to detect object bounding boxes and masks from a global camera view, providing initial object point clouds and spatial coordinates, which guides the robot's exploration path.
- **Object 3D Model Generation:** The collected multi-view RGB observations are fed into a foundation model-based 3D asset generator (e.g., Rodin). This model reconstructs a high-quality digital twin of the object. A key advantage is the model's ability to leverage embedded prior knowledge to accurately infer and complete occluded or unseen parts of the object (e.g., a mug's hidden handle), thus providing a geometrically complete object representation. The quality of the generated digital twin is verified against observed views for consistency.
- **Virtual Semantic Flow Generation:** The digital twin is then utilized in a virtual environment to generate comprehensive semantic features.
 - Unlike real cameras that provide noisy or incomplete depth information, the virtual space allows for the generation of high-resolution RGBD observations from arbitrary viewpoints.
 - Multi-view RGB observations synthesized in the virtual space are processed by DINOv2 (a vision foundation model) to extract rich semantic feature maps. These features capture both low-level geometric details and high-level semantic information.
 - To enhance computational efficiency while retaining critical information, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to compress these high-dimensional features (from 384 to 5 dimensions). The PCA model is trained on features from the training dataset in virtual space.
 - By combining these multi-view, dimension-reduced semantic features with precise virtual depth information, a complete 3D semantic point cloud is generated. This point cloud is then uniformly sampled to K points using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to obtain `P_init`. This `P_init` serves as the canonical, complete semantic representation, robust to real-world sensing limitations.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Semantic Flow Maintenance (Online/Real-time Phase)

Once the initial semantic flow is constructed, its accuracy and completeness must be maintained as the robot manipulates the object in the real world.

- **Spatial Alignment via Object Tracking:**
 - At the start of manipulation, Grounded-SAM, guided by task descriptions, detects and segments the target object from single-perspective RGB images. Combined with depth data and the pre-generated digital twin, FoundationPose computes the initial 6D pose matrix (`M_init`) of the object.
 - During active manipulation, FoundationPose continuously updates the object's pose (`M_update`) in real-time.
 - The initially generated semantic point cloud (`P_init`) is then dynamically transformed using the updated pose information (Equation 1: `P_T_update = [(M_c2w * M_update) * (M_c2w * M_init)^-1] * P_T_init`). This process ensures continuous spatial alignment of the semantic flow with the physical object.
 - This approach leverages FoundationPose's robustness to occlusions and the inherent completeness of the digital twin-derived semantic flow. Instead of re-detecting and re-extracting features at each timestep (which could introduce compounding errors and be computationally expensive), the high-quality feature point cloud is directly transformed, preserving completeness even under partial observations or occlusions by the robot arm.

#### 3.3. G3Flow-Enhanced Diffusion Policy

The maintained semantic flow is integrated into a diffusion-based imitation learning policy to guide manipulation actions.

- **Conditional Feature Acquisition:** The policy takes three distinct types of information as input, processed through separate MLP encoders:
 - **Semantic features (`f_s`):** Derived from the dynamically transformed semantic flow (`P_T_update`), providing rich object-centric semantic understanding (shape: `K, 3 + D_feat`).
 - **Scene features (`f_r`):** Extracted from real-time point cloud observations of the environment (shape: `K, 3`).
 - **Robot state features (`f_p`):** Encoded from the current robot's joint states.
 This multi-modal input enables the policy to reason about both the semantic and geometric aspects of the task, along with the robot's configuration.

- **Conditional Denoising Process:** The decision-making process is formulated as a conditional denoising operation. A denoising network (`ε_θ`) iteratively transforms random Gaussian noise (`a_K`) over K steps to predict the final action (`a_0`). This process is conditioned on the acquired semantic (`f_s`), scene (`f_r`), and robot state (`f_p`) features (Equation 2). This structure allows the policy to model complex action distributions while maintaining stability.

- **Training Procedure:** The policy is trained using an objective that predicts the noise added to expert actions (MSE loss, Equation 3). A DDIM scheduler is used for noise scheduling. The integration of `f_s`, `f_r`, and `f_p` in the loss function allows the policy to learn from demonstration data by leveraging the combined geometric precision and semantic understanding provided by G3Flow.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated G3Flow across five manipulation tasks from the RoboTwin benchmark, assessing its performance on both terminal-constrained manipulation (pose-aware) and cross-object generalization tasks. The experiments compared G3Flow with 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), DP3 with color information, and 2D Diffusion Policy (DP) baselines, trained on 100 expert demonstrations and evaluated over 3 random seeds with 100 test episodes per seed.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Pose-aware Manipulation Tasks (Terminal Constraint Control)

For tasks requiring precise pose-aware manipulation (Shoe Place, Dual Shoes Place, Tool Adjust, Bottle Adjust), G3Flow consistently outperformed all baseline methods. These tasks used objects geometrically similar to the training set to primarily test pose-awareness, rather than generalization.

- **Overall Performance:** G3Flow achieved an average success rate of 68.3% across these tasks, which is 22.1% higher than the next best method, 3D DP (46.2%).
- **Specific Task Improvements:**
 - **Shoe Place (T):** G3Flow achieved 83.0% success, significantly higher than 3D DP (54.0%) and 3D DP w/ color (58.0%), demonstrating improved understanding for correct shoe orientation.
 - **Dual Shoes Place (T):** G3Flow achieved 24.0% success, nearly doubling the success rate of the strongest baseline, 3D DP (13.0%), indicating enhanced ability to maintain consistent orientation accuracy in bi-manual tasks.
 - **Tool Adjust (T):** G3Flow reached 84.3% success, outperforming 3D DP w/ color (70.3%) and 3D DP (43.3%).
 - **Bottle Adjust (T):** G3Flow achieved 82.0% success, exceeding the average of baselines by over 38% for upright pick.
- **Conclusion:** The semantic understanding provided by G3Flow assisted the policy in comprehending and executing pose-aware requirements more effectively, particularly in tasks demanding precise object orientation and spatial relationships.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Generalization Performance (Cross-object Generalization)

For tasks designed to test generalization across geometrically distinct objects (Shoe Place, Dual Shoes Place, Diverse Bottles Pick, Tool Adjust), G3Flow exhibited robust generalization capabilities. These tasks used objects geometrically distinct from the training set, with limited training exposure, to specifically assess generalization.

- **Overall Performance:** G3Flow achieved an average success rate of 50.1% across these tasks, which is 18.4% higher than the strongest baseline, 3D DP (31.7%).
- **Intra-class Generalization:** In tasks with geometrically distinct unseen instances of the same object category (e.g., different shoe designs, varying bottle shapes), G3Flow maintained high performance. This suggests that G3Flow's semantic understanding enables effective operational generalization even with geometric diversity within a category.
- **Cross-category Generalization:** For the Tool Adjust (G) task, which involved semantically similar but geometrically different tools, G3Flow achieved a 70.7% success rate. This was 13.4% higher than the best baseline, confirming the method's ability to transfer learned operational skills (e.g., grasping handles and picking upright) across different, unseen tool categories.
- **Scale Variation:** In the Diverse Bottles Pick (G) task, G3Flow successfully generalized to bottles of varying sizes with a consistent grasp success rate of 51.3%, demonstrating robustness to geometric scale variations while preserving semantic understanding.
- **Conclusion:** G3Flow provides semantic information that empowers policies to generalize effectively across different object categories, variations, and scales, even with limited training data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Efficiency:** G3Flow achieved a decision-making frequency of 34.04 Hz on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090, which is approximately 6 times faster than GenDP (6.89 Hz) and significantly faster than DP with Scene-Level DINOv2 (9.52 Hz). This real-time performance is attributed to the one-time generation of digital assets and the use of a lightweight pose-tracking network for semantic flow maintenance.
- **Quality of Semantic Field:** G3Flow enhanced success rates by 22.6% and 41.2% over scene-level features and by 9.3% and 3.7% over D3Fields in the Shoe Place (T) and Dual Shoes Place (T) tasks, respectively. This improvement is linked to G3Flow's object-centered visual inputs, which reduce background noise, and its tracking-based semantic flow, which maintains robust object representations even under occlusion. Visualizations indicated that scene-level features struggled with occlusions, and D3Fields had inaccuracies in occluded regions, whereas G3Flow generated a complete and high-quality semantic field via digital twins.
- **Vision Foundation Models (VFMs):** An ablation study on the Shoe Place (T) task demonstrated that DINOv2, the VFM used in G3Flow, outperformed CLIP and SAM. G3Flow with DINOv2 achieved 83.0% success, compared to 72.3% for CLIP and 74.7% for SAM. The paper suggests this is because CLIP prioritizes image-text alignment over spatial understanding, while SAM focuses excessively on detailed features rather than relevant part-level characteristics required for manipulation tasks.

#### 4.4. Visualizations

Visualizations of G3Flow across the five evaluation tasks confirmed that the real-time semantic flow maintained temporal coherence and spatial alignment during manipulation. Distinct colors in the visualizations represented different semantic features (e.g., orientation-critical regions, functional parts), and the semantic flow remained complete and stable even under partial occlusions by robot arms or objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

G3Flow presents a methodological contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by proposing a framework that explicitly addresses the integration of geometric precision with semantic understanding. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robotic Dexterity and Precision:** By providing real-time, object-centric semantic flow, G3Flow enables robotic policies to better comprehend and execute tasks requiring fine-grained control and specific object orientations. This is demonstrated by the superior success rates in terminal-constrained manipulation tasks, suggesting a step towards more dexterous manipulation capabilities.
- **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The framework's ability to maintain a consistent semantic understanding across varying object geometries allows policies to generalize more effectively to unseen objects and categories. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured or dynamic environments where they encounter novel instances of known object types or entirely new objects. The demonstrated performance in cross-object generalization tasks indicates a reduced need for extensive object-specific retraining.
- **Robustness to Occlusion:** A significant practical advantage of G3Flow is its ability to generate and maintain a complete semantic representation even when the object is partially occluded. By leveraging digital twins and robust pose tracking, the system bypasses the limitations of real-world partial observations, which is common during physical interaction. This enhances the reliability and applicability of robotic systems in complex manipulation scenarios.
- **Annotation-Free Semantic Understanding:** The reliance on foundation models for 3D generation and semantic feature extraction eliminates the need for manual annotations or keypoint selections, a common bottleneck in developing semantic representations for robotics. This automation streamlines the development process and makes the system more scalable.
- **Real-time Practicality:** The reported computational efficiency, with a decision-making frequency suitable for real-time robotic control, indicates the framework's potential for practical deployment. This addresses a critical requirement for interactive robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** G3Flow establishes a basis for further research in semantic-aware robotic manipulation. The paper identifies future directions, including extending the framework to articulated objects and multi-object interactions, and continuously optimizing 3D modeling for dynamically interacting objects. These areas represent significant challenges in advanced robotics.
- **Broader Applications:** The principles demonstrated by G3Flow could have broader implications beyond imitation learning, impacting areas such as human-robot collaboration, autonomous assembly, and service robotics, where understanding object semantics and handling variability are paramount. The ability to integrate high-fidelity geometric and semantic information could unlock new levels of autonomy and adaptability for robotic systems.
