# Enabling Robust Cloth Manipulation via Inference-Time Simulator-in-the-Loop Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> A robotic system for manipulating deformable cloth objects was developed, integrating a high-fidelity physics simulator, an RGB-native state estimator, and an online motion planner. This framework enables real-time error correction during execution, achieving high success rates on diverse cloth manipulation tasks, including folding and disturbance recovery, in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects like cloth is challenging due to their complex dynamics, high-dimensional configuration spaces, and susceptibility to error accumulation.
- Current robot policy learning methods, such as imitation learning and foundation models, often lack the capacity for autonomous error correction during real-world deployment.
- Directly employing physics simulators for online reasoning in cloth manipulation faces hurdles related to simulator fidelity, accurate real-to-sim state recovery from partial observations, and real-time computational constraints.

## Method

- The research proposes a closed-loop 'real-to-sim-to-real' pipeline that combines the FLASH deformable object simulator, an RGB-native state estimation module, and a prior-guided ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) controller.
- An RGB-native module, trained entirely on synthetic data, reconstructs a sparse, simulation-compatible cloth state from a single RGB image using a DINOv2 visual encoder and canonical tokens.
- The MPPI controller refines nominal action sequences from an offline base policy by sampling and evaluating candidate actions through parallel rollouts in the FLASH simulator, applying a task-specific cost function for optimization.

## Results

- The full framework achieved high success rates on real-world cloth tasks, including 9/10 for single-arm diagonal folding and 8/10 for dual-arm symmetric folding, demonstrating superior performance over ablated components.
- The RGB-native state estimator achieved significantly lower reconstruction errors (e.g., **2.33** mm² MSE for towels) and faster inference latency (e.g., **7.4** ms) compared to point-cloud or depth-based baselines.
- The system showed robust generalization across various garment assets, different folding tasks, and recovery from human-induced disturbances, maintaining high success rates of 7/10 to 8/10 without additional dynamics retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A physically accurate and computationally efficient deformable object simulator is essential for viable online inference and effective action refinement in real-world cloth manipulation.
> - Achieving precise and rapid synchronization between the real and simulated cloth states from limited sensory input (e.g., a single RGB image) is critical for the simulator to provide meaningful feedback.
> - Integrating offline policy proposals with real-time physics-based optimization provides robust error correction, significantly improving the reliability of robotic manipulation of deformable objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deformable object manipulation, particularly with cloth, presents substantial challenges for robotic systems due to factors such as infinite-dimensional configuration spaces, continuously evolving local geometry, pervasive self-occlusion, and complex frictional contact. These characteristics significantly complicate both accurate state estimation and robust action reasoning.

Current dominant paradigms in deformable object manipulation include imitation learning and robot foundation models. These approaches learn policies by fitting to expert demonstrations and robot interaction data, showing promise in task-level generalization. However, a significant limitation of these methods at the deployment stage is their restricted capacity to autonomously correct execution errors. Minor deviations in perception, grasp selection, timing, or action prediction can accumulate through contact-rich cloth dynamics, leading the system away from the intended task state. This highlights a need for inference-time mechanisms that can refine policy proposals and recover from accumulated errors.

High-fidelity deformable-object simulators have emerged as a potential foundation for physically grounded inference-time reasoning. Prior work has explored using simulators to generate data for learned dynamics models, often paired with full-state reconstruction from partial observations (e.g., point clouds). This approach introduces an additional approximation layer through the learned predictor, which may be tied to its training distribution.

This paper positions itself by arguing for the direct use of physical simulators as the forward dynamics model for inference-time reasoning. This approach aims to circumvent the need for task- or asset-specific dynamics training and to eliminate the approximation error associated with learned predictors, leveraging the simulator as a general physical reasoning engine. While simulator-in-the-loop (SIL) optimization has demonstrated effectiveness in rigid-body manipulation, where state and contact are more tractable, its direct application to real-world cloth manipulation from partial observations (e.g., a single RGB input) presents a more complex challenge. The work addresses the full "real-to-sim-to-real" loop for cloth, focusing on the integrated challenges of simulator fidelity and speed, accurate real-to-sim state recovery, and efficient rollout representation for online planning under real-time constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question motivating this research is whether deformable-object simulators can be directly employed as inference-time forward models for real-world cloth manipulation. This question arises from the limitations of existing policy learning methods, such as imitation learning and robot foundation models, which often lack the ability to correct execution errors that inevitably accumulate during the manipulation of highly deformable objects like cloth. The complex dynamics of cloth, characterized by continuous deformation, self-occlusion, and frictional contact, make it particularly susceptible to such accumulated errors.

To address this overarching question, the authors identify and aim to solve three coupled system-level challenges necessary for establishing a functional real-to-sim-to-real loop for cloth manipulation:

1. **Simulator Fidelity, Numerical Stability, and Rollout Efficiency**: A core objective is to identify and integrate a deformable-object simulator that offers a practical balance among physical fidelity, numerical stability, and computational efficiency. The simulator must be realistic enough to accurately predict cloth behavior, stable enough to handle complex contact dynamics over repeated rollouts, and fast enough to support online trajectory evaluation within a robot's control budget. The motivation here is that an inadequate simulator would lead to misaligned actions or excessive computational costs, rendering online refinement impractical.
2. **Recovery of Simulation-Compatible Cloth States from Practical Observations**: The research aims to develop a method for accurately translating real-world observations into a simulation-compatible cloth state. This involves tackling the difficulties of high-dimensional, continuously deforming, and often self-occluded cloth from limited sensory input (specifically, a single RGB image). The motivation is that precise synchronization between the real and simulated cloth states is fundamental for the simulator to provide meaningful feedback for action refinement. Without accurate state estimation, the optimized actions would be misaligned with the physical scene.
3. **Refinement of Nominal Policy Trajectories into Robust Closed-Loop Actions**: The final objective is to design an online planning mechanism that can effectively refine nominal action sequences (generated by an offline-distilled policy) into robust closed-loop actions. This mechanism must operate under limited online computational budgets while preserving manipulation-relevant deformation and contact behaviors. The motivation is to provide an inference-time error correction capability, allowing the robot to adapt to unmodeled dynamics, perception errors, and unexpected disturbances, thereby increasing the reliability and success rate of cloth manipulation tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the fragility of open-loop policies in deformable object manipulation by integrating real-time physics-based inference. The work aims to construct a unified simulator-in-the-loop framework that achieves this by carefully selecting and integrating a simulator, developing a robust real-to-sim perception module, and implementing an efficient online control strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates a deformable object simulator, an RGB-native real-to-sim state estimator, and a prior-guided Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller into a closed-loop real-to-sim-to-real pipeline. The system operates through both offline training and online execution phases.

**System Overview (Receding Horizon Loop):**
At each control step *t*, the robot captures a single RGB image *I<sub>t</sub>*. This image is processed by the real-to-sim module to reconstruct a simulation-compatible cloth state, *ˆs<sub>t</sub>*. This reconstructed state is then used to synchronize the simulator. An offline-distilled policy provides a nominal action sequence, *Ū<sub>t</sub>*, over a specified horizon. The MPPI controller refines this nominal sequence by sampling candidate actions, evaluating them through parallel rollouts in the FLASH simulator, and then executing the first action of the refined sequence, *a*<sup>⋆</sup>*<sub>t</sub>*. This process repeats in a receding-horizon manner.

**A. Simulator Backbone:**
The FLASH simulator serves a dual role: generating synthetic data for real-to-sim training and acting as the inference-time rollout engine for MPPI. FLASH models cloth as a triangular mesh, with vertices as degrees of freedom and mesh connectivity encoding local deformation. It employs non-smooth Newton iterations for frictional contact resolution and utilizes GPU-friendly sparse matrix operations for stable and efficient rollouts. This choice was informed by comparative evaluations indicating FLASH's superior balance of physical fidelity, numerical stability, and rollout efficiency compared to other simulators like Isaac Sim, Newton, and Genesis.

**B. RGB-native Real-to-Sim Reconstruction (Offline Training, Online Inference):**
This module, *f<sub>θ</sub>*, recovers a sparse, simulation-compatible cloth state, *ˆs<sub>t</sub>*, from a single RGB image. The state is represented by the deformed 3D positions of *N* material vertices on the cloth mesh.

1. **Simulator Asset Construction:** For each garment (e.g., towel, long-sleeve shirt), a fixed-topology rest-shape mesh is reconstructed offline from RGB-D frames. This mesh defines the *N* material vertices whose positions are predicted by the module and used by the simulator.
2. **Synthetic Supervision:** The module *f<sub>θ</sub>* is trained purely on synthetic data generated within FLASH. This involves simulating diverse manipulation episodes with randomized cloth poses and configurations. Each rendered frame, captured from camera poses matching the real setup, is paired with the ground-truth 3D positions of the *N* mesh vertices, creating a dataset *D<sub>syn</sub>*.
3. **Network Architecture:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** A frozen, pretrained DINOv2 encoder extracts dense patch features from the input RGB image. A learnable linear layer projects these features to a model dimension.
 - **Canonical Tokens:** *N* learnable canonical tokens, *C* = {*c<sub>i</sub>*}<sup>N</sup><sub>i=1</sub>, are maintained, one for each mesh vertex. These tokens are initialized based on the rest-shape coordinates of their respective vertices and are optimized during training to encode persistent identity and canonical location.
 - **Fusion Backbone:** The canonical tokens and visual tokens are concatenated and jointly processed by self-attention layers. This allows canonical tokens to aggregate visual evidence and exchange structural context.
 - **Decoder:** An MLP decoder maps each fused canonical token feature to its predicted deformed 3D position (*ˆp<sub>t,i</sub>*).
4. **Training Objective:** The model is supervised using a smooth-L1 loss, minimizing the mean squared error between predicted and ground-truth vertex positions. Only the projection layer, canonical tokens, attention layers, and decoder are trained, while DINOv2 remains frozen.
5. **Robust Augmentation:** To improve sim-to-real robustness, perturbations are applied during training:
 - Image-space randomization (photometric, geometric, lighting) on the rendered RGB images.
 - Latent-level noise and masking, including random masking around sampled grasping points, to train the model to infer cloth state from incomplete visual evidence.

**C. Prior-Guided MPPI Refinement (Online Control):**
This component refines a nominal action sequence provided by an offline base policy.

1. **Prior-Informed Sampling:** Given the synchronized cloth state *ˆs<sub>t</sub>*, the controller samples *K* candidate action sequences, *U*<sup>(k)</sup>*<sub>t</sub>*, by adding Gaussian noise (*ϵ*<sup>(k)</sup>*) to the nominal proposal *Ū<sub>t</sub>*.
2. **Rollout Evaluation:** Each candidate sequence is rolled out over a horizon *H* in parallel using the FLASH simulator. The simulator predicts the physical consequences of each action sequence.
3. **Cost Function:** Each rollout *τ*<sup>(k)</sup>*<sub>t</sub>* is assigned a task cost *S*(τ*<sup>(k)</sup>*<sub>t</sub>*) based on a task-specific objective (e.g., folding goal, measured by keypoint alignment error) and regularization terms (e.g., workspace limits, table penetration, action smoothness).
4. **Weight Calculation:** MPPI converts these rollout costs into normalized weights (*w<sub>k</sub>*) using a softmax function with a temperature parameter (*λ*).
5. **Refined Action Sequence:** The optimized action sequence, *U*<sup>⋆</sup>*<sub>t</sub>*, is computed as a weighted average of the candidate action sequences, where weights are derived from their respective costs.
6. **Receding-Horizon Execution:** The robot executes only the first action of the optimized sequence, *a*<sup>⋆</sup>*<sub>t</sub>* = *U*<sup>⋆</sup>*<sub>t</sub>*. The real-to-sim-to-real loop then repeats with a new RGB observation. This continuous correction allows the system to recover from local execution errors and cloth-state deviations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented a comprehensive evaluation of its proposed simulator-in-the-loop framework, focusing on the individual components and the overall system performance in real-world cloth manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulator Backbone Evaluation

A comparative study of deformable object simulators (FLASH, Isaac Sim, Newton, Genesis) was conducted on a single-arm diagonal folding task.
- **FLASH** consistently achieved the highest success rates (95–100%) across various numbers of parallel environments (*K* = 64 to 512), with low keypoint alignment errors (1.5–2.1 cm) and low mean per-vertex MSE (3–4 cm²). It demonstrated a balance of control accuracy, computational efficiency, and physical fidelity.
- **Isaac Sim** was marginally faster per simulation step (1.5–1.9x) but yielded lower success rates (below 60%), higher keypoint errors (6–13 cm), and significantly higher per-vertex MSE (30–300 cm²), indicating less precise control and fidelity.
- **Newton** exhibited slower performance than Isaac Sim, comparable or lower success rates (below 60%), and notable numerical instabilities (large standard deviations in errors).
- **Genesis** failed to produce stable folds and was excluded from detailed quantitative comparison.
- **Conclusion:** FLASH was identified as the most suitable simulator, providing a physically accurate and computationally viable oracle for online MPPI cloth folding.

#### 4.2. State Estimation Evaluation

The RGB-native real-to-sim module was evaluated against point-cloud-based DPM and depth-based DeFM on towel and long-sleeve shirt assets.
- **Our RGB-native module** achieved the lowest reconstruction errors across all metrics (mean per-vertex MSE, Chamfer Distance, Earth Mover’s Distance) and the fastest inference latency. For a towel, it showed an MSE of 2.33 mm² (approx. 1.5 mm RMS error) and a latency of 7.4 ms. For a long-sleeve shirt, MSE was 24.23 mm² (approx. 4.9 mm RMS error) with 12.4 ms latency.
- **Baselines** demonstrated higher reconstruction errors and/or slower inference speeds. For instance, DPM had significantly higher latency (318.5 ms for towel, 512.9 ms for shirt).
- **Conclusion:** The RGB-native module accurately reconstructs cloth states, maintaining material-point consistency and overall cloth shape, which is crucial for faithful simulator initialization.

#### 4.3. Real-World Pipeline Evaluation

The full framework and its variants (replacing the state estimator, simulator backbone, or controller) were tested on real-world single-arm diagonal folding and dual-arm symmetric folding tasks.
- **Full Pipeline:** Achieved the highest success rates: 9/10 for single-arm diagonal folding and 8/10 for dual-arm symmetric folding.
- **Component Ablation:**
 - Replacing the RGB-native state estimator with DPM reduced success rates to 6/10 (single-arm) and 3/10 (dual-arm).
 - Replacing the FLASH backend with Isaac Sim resulted in success rates of 5/10 (single-arm) and 4/10 (dual-arm).
 - Executing the base policy directly without MPPI refinement yielded the lowest success rates: 3/10 (single-arm) and 1/10 (dual-arm).
- **Conclusion:** The results indicate a tight coupling between state synchronization, simulator fidelity, and online refinement. Weakening any single component negatively impacted the entire real-to-sim-to-real loop, confirming the necessity of each integrated part.

#### 4.4. Generalization and Robustness

The framework's ability to generalize and withstand disturbances was evaluated on long-sleeve shirt folding, shorts folding, reverse-diagonal towel folding, and recovery from mid-fold human disturbances.
- **Performance:** The system maintained high success rates (7/10 to 8/10) across these diverse tasks and perturbation scenarios.
- **Conclusion:** The framework demonstrated robustness to variations in garment assets, folding tasks, and unexpected external perturbations without requiring additional dynamics retraining.

#### 4.5. Real-to-Sim Augmentation Ablation (Appendix C)

An ablation study on training-time data augmentation for the RGB-native module showed that while augmentation had minimal impact on synthetic validation accuracy, it was critical for real-world deployment. Without augmentation, the module was less robust to real-image noise, sim-to-real visual shift, and occlusions, leading to lower closed-loop success rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation of deformable objects by introducing a functional simulator-in-the-loop (SIL) framework for real-world cloth manipulation. Its significance stems from several key aspects:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Deformable Objects**: The research successfully demonstrates a comprehensive real-to-sim-to-real loop for cloth, a domain traditionally challenging due to high-dimensional state spaces and complex contact dynamics. This extends the SIL paradigm, which has previously been more prevalent in rigid-body manipulation, to a much more complex class of objects.
2. **Enabling Robust Execution for Learned Policies**: A primary impact is providing an inference-time error correction mechanism for offline-distilled policies. By continuously refining nominal actions through online physics inference, the framework mitigates the accumulation of errors from perception, action prediction, and unmodeled dynamics. This addresses a critical limitation of imitation learning and foundation models, which often lack robustness during real-world deployment on deformable materials.
3. **Holistic System Design**: The paper highlights the interconnected importance of its three core pillars: a high-fidelity and efficient deformable simulator (FLASH), an accurate and fast RGB-native real-to-sim state estimation module, and a prior-guided online optimization strategy (MPPI). The ablation studies clearly demonstrate that the effectiveness of the system relies on the robust performance and synergistic integration of all these components.
4. **Practical Real-World Performance**: The framework demonstrates strong performance on various real-world cloth manipulation tasks, including different types of folding and recovery from disturbances. Its ability to generalize across new garment assets and folding variants without task-specific dynamics retraining suggests a step towards more versatile and capable robotic cloth manipulation systems.
5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work lays a groundwork for future developments in deformable object manipulation. The identified limitations, particularly concerning the scalability of deformable dynamics rollouts and per-asset training requirements for generalization to novel garments, point to clear and impactful directions for future research. This includes exploring distributed simulation, image-conditioned mesh foundation models, and category-level real-to-sim modules to further enhance scalability and generalization capabilities.

In summary, this research contributes to the field by providing a robust and practical solution for real-time robotic cloth manipulation. It leverages the power of physics simulation for online inference, thereby enhancing the reliability and error recovery capabilities of robotic systems interacting with complex, deformable environments.
