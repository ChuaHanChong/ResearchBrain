# Can We Generate Images with CoT? Let's Verify and Reinforce Image Generation Step by Step

> [!summary] Summary
> This work comprehensively investigates applying Chain-of-Thought reasoning strategies to autoregressive image generation, demonstrating how verification and reinforcement mechanisms can significantly enhance image quality and text-to-image alignment. The proposed approach achieves a 24% improvement over the Show-o baseline on the GenEval benchmark, surpassing Stable Diffusion 3 by 15%.

## Problem

- Autoregressive image generation models often struggle with inconsistent decoding paths and difficulties in accurately reflecting complex compositional details from text prompts.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, highly successful in large language and multi-modal models for understanding tasks, had not been thoroughly explored for its applicability to generative tasks like image synthesis.
- Existing generative models typically operate without explicit step-by-step reasoning, verification, or self-correction mechanisms comparable to CoT in other AI domains.

## Method

- The research integrates test-time computation using specialized reward models (Outcome Reward Model, Process Reward Model, and the novel ==Potential Assessment Reward Model - PARM==) to verify and select optimal image generation paths.
- ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)== and iterative DPO are applied to fine-tune the generative model (Show-o), aligning its outputs with preferred image examples for specific text prompts.
- A ==reflection mechanism (PARM++)== is introduced, enabling the generative model to self-correct and iteratively refine images based on detailed textual feedback regarding detected misalignments.

## Results

- The ultimate configuration, integrating PARM with iterative DPO for both reward guidance during training and test-time verification, enhanced the Show-o baseline from **53%** to **77%** on the GenEval benchmark, marking a **24%** improvement.
- This best-performing method achieved **77%** on GenEval, significantly outperforming Stable Diffusion 3 (**62%**) by **15%**, with notable gains in challenging categories such as 'Two Obj.', 'Colors', 'Position', and 'Attribute binding'.
- The proposed DPO, fine-tuned ORM, and PARM methods demonstrated plug-and-play capabilities, consistently improving performance across other sequential autoregressive generative models like LlamaGen-3B and Janus-Pro-7B.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating CoT-inspired verification and reinforcement strategies during both training and inference can substantially improve autoregressive image generation quality and text alignment, presenting a new paradigm beyond architectural scaling.
> - The development of specialized reward models, such as PARM, is crucial for effectively assessing and guiding image generation at intermediate steps, as generic reward models struggle with the unique characteristics of visual intermediates (e.g., blurriness).
> - Combining preference alignment (via DPO) during training with reward-model-guided test-time verification and reflection yields greater performance gains than either strategy applied in isolation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper significantly contributes to the evolving landscape of generative AI by bridging a critical gap between the highly successful Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in large language and multi-modal models (LLMs/LMMs) and the domain of image generation.

Historically, CoT reasoning has revolutionized LLMs by enabling them to tackle complex understanding and reasoning tasks (e.g., mathematics, science, coding) through explicit, multi-step problem-solving. While generative AI, especially diffusion models (like Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, PixArt-α), has made tremendous strides in producing high-fidelity images, these models typically operate in a less transparent, end-to-end manner without explicit step-by-step *reasoning* or *verification* in the same vein as CoT.

The paper focuses on **autoregressive image generation** models (e.g., Show-o, LlamaGen, MaskGiT), which, unlike diffusion models, generate images token-by-token in a sequential manner, akin to how LLMs generate text. This architectural similarity provides the crucial entry point for applying CoT-style strategies. The authors argue that this token-by-token decoding inherently creates a "reasoning path" that can be verified and reinforced.

By introducing and thoroughly investigating test-time computation (verifiers), preference alignment, and self-reflection mechanisms to autoregressive image generation, this work pioneers a new paradigm for enhancing image quality and controllability. It challenges the conventional approaches that primarily focus on architectural scaling or improved diffusion processes by demonstrating that cognitive-inspired reasoning mechanisms can yield substantial performance gains. This positions the work as a crucial step towards making generative models not only capable of producing aesthetically pleasing images but also capable of generating images that logically align with complex, compositional prompts through a verifiable and self-correcting process. It offers a complementary perspective to diffusion models, particularly in scenarios demanding intricate compositional control and robust alignment with text prompts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to provide the **first comprehensive investigation into whether Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning strategies, widely explored and successful in large models for complex understanding tasks, can be effectively applied to verify and reinforce autoregressive image generation scenarios.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Gap between Understanding and Generation:** CoT has demonstrated remarkable success in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs and LMMs for tasks requiring multi-step thinking. However, its applicability to generative tasks, specifically image generation, remains largely unexplored. The authors are motivated to transfer these proven reasoning strategies to a new domain.
- **Leveraging Architectural Similarities:** Autoregressive image generation models, like LLMs, quantize data into discrete tokens and iteratively predict content conditioned on previous outputs. This inherent step-by-step decoding process closely mirrors the multi-step nature of CoT, making it a natural candidate for adaptation.
- **Addressing Limitations in Current Image Generation:** Despite advancements, autoregressive image generation models often struggle with inconsistent decoding paths and difficulties in accurately reflecting complex compositional details (e.g., object attributes, relative positions, counting) in generated images. The authors seek to improve the robustness and reliability of these models.
- **Exploring Novel Enhancement Methods:** The paper aims to explore alternative avenues for improving image generation quality beyond merely scaling model parameters or refining sampling techniques. By introducing verifier-based test-time computation and preference alignment, the authors investigate whether post-training and inference-time strategies can unlock new levels of performance.
- **Developing Specialized Tools for Image CoT:** Recognizing the unique characteristics of image generation (e.g., blurry intermediate steps), the authors are motivated to develop specialized reward models that can effectively assess and guide the visual generation process, overcoming the limitations of generic reward models designed for text or mathematical reasoning.
- **Enabling Self-Correction and Reflection:** Inspired by human cognitive processes, the work seeks to introduce mechanisms for self-evaluation and refinement in image generation, allowing models to identify and correct unsatisfactory outputs proactively.

Ultimately, the paper aims to provide unique insights into the future advancement of image generation by integrating robust reasoning and verification principles, moving towards more intelligent and controllable generative AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic investigative approach, building upon a baseline autoregressive image generation model and progressively integrating and evaluating CoT-inspired strategies.

**Baseline Model and Evaluation:**
- **Baseline Model:** **Show-o**, a recent discrete generative model known for unifying multimodal understanding and generation, is chosen as the foundational model.
- **Evaluation Benchmark:** Performance is rigorously assessed on **GenEval**, a challenging text-to-image generation benchmark designed to evaluate visual quality, image-text alignment, and crucially, accurate object attributes and co-occurrence (e.g., counting, colors, positions, attribute binding).

**Three Core Strategies Investigated:**

1. **Scaling Test-Time Computation with Reward Models (Verifiers):**
 - **Outcome Reward Model (ORM):** Evaluates the final generated image.
 - **Zero-shot ORM:** Utilizes **LLaVA-OneVision (7B)** as a powerful LMM, prompted to give a binary 'yes/no' assessment of image-text alignment.
 - **Fine-tuned ORM:** Curates a large dataset (288K text-to-image ranking examples) by prompting GPT-4 for diverse text prompts, generating images with Show-o, and labeling them 'yes/no' using LLaVA-OneVision. LLaVA-OneVision is then fine-tuned on this dataset.
 - **Mechanism:** Employs a "best-of-N" selection strategy, where the reward model scores multiple candidate images, and the highest-scoring one is chosen.
 - **Process Reward Model (PRM):** Evaluates intermediate generation steps.
 - **Zero-shot PRM:** Also uses LLaVA-OneVision with a modified prompt to assess intermediate images.
 - **Fine-tuned PRM:** Curates a 300K step-wise text-to-image ranking dataset. Due to the blurriness of early-stage intermediate images, an **automated annotation approach** (inspired by Math-Shepherd) is used: for each intermediate step, Show-o generates multiple paths, and if any final image from these paths receives a 'yes' score from the ORM, the intermediate step is labeled 'yes'. This fine-tunes LLaVA-OneVision to better interpret blurry images.
 - **Mechanism:** Guides the generation step-by-step by selecting the best-of-N candidate at each intermediate stage.

2. **Reinforced Preference Alignment via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):**
 - **DPO Ranking Data Curation:** Reuses the 'yes/no' labels from the ORM training data to create 10K paired examples (preferred 'yes' image vs. dispreferred 'no' image for the same prompt).
 - **DPO Alignment:** Applies **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** to fine-tune Show-o. The objective is to increase the likelihood of preferred images and decrease that of dispreferred ones, aligning the model's output preferences.
 - **Iterative DPO:** After an initial DPO alignment, the newly aligned model generates new images, which are then re-annotated and used to create a refined DPO dataset (7K samples) for a second round of training, enhancing alignment iteratively.

3. **Integration of Preference Alignment and Test-time Verifiers:**
 - **DPO with Reward Model Guidance:** Integrates the fine-tuned ORM as an explicit reward model during the DPO training process, providing online preference feedback to the generation model.
 - **Verification after DPO Alignment:** Applies the fine-tuned ORM (using best-of-N selection) directly to the outputs of the DPO-aligned model, leveraging the refined knowledge distribution from DPO and the selection power of verifiers.
 - **Verification after DPO with Reward Model Guidance:** Combines both previous approaches, using a reward-guided DPO during training and a reward-based verifier during inference.

**Novel Reward Models for Autoregressive Image Generation:**

- **Potential Assessment Reward Model (PARM):**
 - **Motivation:** Addresses ORM's lack of step-wise granularity and PRM's issues with blurry early-stage and overly similar later-stage intermediate images.
 - **Three Progressive Tasks:**
 1. **Clarity Judgment (120K data):** Adaptively determines if a partially generated image is visually clear enough to be assessed (e.g., skipping blurry early steps).
 2. **Potential Assessment (80K data):** For clear steps, assesses the potential of the current path to lead to a high-quality final image, pruning low-potential paths.
 3. **Best-of-N' Selection (200K data):** Performs a final "best-of-N'" selection among the remaining high-potential paths (similar to ORM).
 - **Data Curation:** A new 400K ranking dataset is curated, specifically designed for these three tasks, including static labels for clarity judgment and automated labels for potential assessment based on final image quality.

- **Potential Assessment Reward Model ++ (PARM++):**
 - **Motivation:** Inspired by human reflection, to enable self-correction in image generation.
 - **Reflection Mechanism:**
 1. After PARM selects an optimal image, PARM++ performs a **Reflection Evaluation** to check final image-text alignment.
 2. If misalignment is detected, it provides a **detailed textual analysis of discrepancies** (using GPT-4o to annotate negative examples).
 3. This feedback (original prompt, suboptimal image, misalignment reasons) is fed back to the generative model for **iterative self-correction**, refining the image until it meets the criteria (max 3 iterations).
 - **Data Curation:** An additional 120K instances (total 520K) are curated for the reflection task, with GPT-4o providing reasons for misalignments.
 - **Self-correction Fine-tuning:** Show-o is fine-tuned on a dataset of 10K instance groups (text prompt, low-quality image, high-quality image, reflection reasons) to learn to refine images based on textual feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating the substantial benefits of integrating CoT reasoning strategies into autoregressive image generation:

- **Effectiveness of Individual CoT Strategies:**
 - **Test-time verification** using reward models significantly boosts generation performance. The **fine-tuned ORM** achieves a notable **+10%** improvement over the baseline (from 53% to 63% on GenEval), confirming that selecting reliable decoding paths is crucial.
 - In contrast, the **fine-tuned PRM** yields only marginal improvements (**+2%** to 55%), highlighting its struggles with blurry early-stage and indistinct later-stage intermediate images in image generation, unlike in LLM reasoning.
 - **Fine-tuning reward models** with curated ranking data consistently **outperforms zero-shot variants** and demonstrates better scalability with increased N in best-of-N selection (steeper curves in Figure 3).
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** effectively reinforces generation performance, achieving **+9%** (to 62%). **Iterative DPO** further extends these gains to **+11%** (to 65%), surpassing even the best individual test-time verifier (fine-tuned ORM).

- **Complementarity of Integration Strategies:**
 - Combining preference alignment and test-time verifiers consistently leads to **greater improvements** than either strategy alone.
 - The **third integration method** (PARM with iterative DPO, applied for both reward guidance during training and test-time verification) achieves the most substantial gains, outperforming DPO alignment alone by **+10%** and verification alone by **+12%**. This underscores the versatility of reward models in guiding training and selecting outputs.

- **Superiority of Proposed Reward Models (PARM & PARM++):**
 - **PARM** proves to be the **best-performing reward model** across various strategies. As a stand-alone test-time verifier, it significantly outperforms fine-tuned ORM by **+6%** (from 63% to 67%), demonstrating its effectiveness in adaptively assessing image generation steps.
 - When integrated, PARM consistently yields higher scores than fine-tuned ORM, solidifying its role as a robust and versatile reward model for autoregressive image generation.
 - The ultimate configuration, integrating **PARM with iterative DPO for both reward model guidance and test-time verification**, enhances the Show-o baseline from 53% to **77%** on GenEval, marking a **+24% improvement**. This result **surpasses Stable Diffusion 3 by a significant +15%** (SD3 scores 62%).
 - Notably, this best configuration achieves substantial gains in challenging categories such as 'Two Obj.', 'Colors', 'Position', and 'Attribute binding', emphasizing its robustness in handling complex compositional prompts.

- **Generalization to Other Autoregressive Models:**
 - The proposed methods (DPO, fine-tuned ORM, and PARM) demonstrate **model-agnostic and plug-and-play capabilities**, consistently improving performance across other sequential autoregressive generative models, namely LlamaGen-3B and Janus-Pro-7B, without architectural modifications or retraining.

- **Impact of PARM++ with Reflection:**
 - The **reflection mechanism in PARM++** significantly enhances image quality. While the self-correction fine-tuning for the generative model initially causes a minor **-2%** drop in baseline performance, enabling the reflection process results in a substantial **+10%** improvement over PARM without reflection (achieving 70% on GenEval in a DPO-agnostic setting).
 - Qualitative examples confirm that PARM++ effectively identifies and self-corrects issues such as unrealistic structures, incorrect object counts, wrong colors, and improper object layouts, leading to higher fidelity and better alignment with prompts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a pivotal step in the field of generative AI, particularly text-to-image synthesis, with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering the Integration of CoT in Image Generation:** This is the *first comprehensive empirical study* to explore the application of Chain-of-Thought reasoning strategies to autoregressive image generation. It successfully demonstrates that verification and reinforcement mechanisms, previously confined to LLMs/LMMs for understanding tasks, can be effectively adapted to creative generation.
- **A New Paradigm for Image Synthesis:** By achieving a **+24% improvement** on a challenging benchmark (GenEval) and **outperforming advanced diffusion models like Stable Diffusion 3 by +15%**, the work establishes a new, competitive paradigm for enhancing image generation beyond traditional architectural scaling or diffusion-based methods. This opens new research avenues, suggesting that "reasoning" is not just for understanding but also for effective creation.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Fidelity:** The ability to verify and reinforce generation step-by-step, combined with a reflection mechanism, offers unprecedented control over the image generation process. This directly addresses known weaknesses of generative models in handling complex compositional prompts, object attributes, and spatial relationships, leading to significantly higher fidelity and semantic alignment with user intentions.
- **Introduction of Specialized Reward Models:** The development of **PARM and PARM++** is a crucial innovation. These specialized reward models overcome the limitations of generic ORM/PRM in the unique context of image generation's intermediate steps (blurriness, similarity), providing more accurate and adaptive guidance. This demonstrates the necessity of domain-specific reward mechanisms for optimal performance.
- **Towards More Intelligent Generative AI:** The reflection and self-correction capabilities introduced by PARM++ signify a move towards more intelligent and autonomous generative systems. Models are no longer passive output devices but can actively assess their creations, diagnose errors, and iteratively refine their outputs, mirroring human cognitive processes. This could pave the way for more robust and reliable AI tools in creative industries.
- **Broader Applicability and Plug-and-Play Nature:** The demonstrated generalization of DPO, fine-tuned ORM, and PARM to other sequential autoregressive generative models (LlamaGen, Janus-Pro) highlights their modularity and "plug-and-play" nature. This means the benefits of CoT reasoning can extend across a wider range of existing and future autoregressive generative architectures.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This study provides a strong foundation and unique insights, encouraging further exploration into integrating complex reasoning, planning, and multi-agent systems from the LLM domain into other generative AI tasks. It suggests a future where generative models are not only creative but also highly logical and self-aware.

In conclusion, this research significantly pushes the boundaries of text-to-image generation by demonstrating the profound impact of applying Chain-of-Thought reasoning. It offers practical tools and a conceptual framework that could inspire a new wave of highly capable, verifiable, and self-correcting generative AI systems.
