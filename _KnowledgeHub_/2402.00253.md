# A Survey on Hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey from Huawei Technologies comprehensively defines and categorizes hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), synthesizing current evaluation methods, root causes, and mitigation strategies to provide a structured overview of this critical issue. The work maps hallucination types, identifies its origins across the LVLM pipeline components, and reviews diverse solutions being developed.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) like MiniGPT-4 and LLaVA exhibit 'hallucination,' a critical issue where generated text factually misaligns with the visual content of an image.
- LVLM hallucinations extend beyond simple object presence to include incorrect attributes and relationships, significantly impeding their reliability and trustworthiness for practical applications.
- The rapidly evolving field lacked a comprehensive, structured overview defining this complex problem, categorizing its symptoms, consolidating evaluation methods, analyzing root causes, and reviewing mitigation strategies.

## Method

- The authors conducted a comprehensive survey to systematically define and categorize LVLM ==hallucination==, developing a ==taxonomy for its symptoms==.
- They structured a review of existing evaluation methodologies and benchmarks for LVLM hallucination, distinguishing between ==generative and discriminative== approaches.
- The survey provides a ==pipeline-centric analysis== of the root causes of hallucinations in LVLMs and systematically reviews and categorizes existing mitigation strategies, linking them to their corresponding causes.

## Results

- The survey formally defines LVLM hallucination as a 'disagreement between factual visual content and corresponding textual generation,' distinguishing its multifaceted symptoms including cognitive and semantic errors.
- It identifies **two** main evaluation approaches (non-hallucinatory generation and hallucination discrimination) and categorizes current benchmarks, noting trends towards generative benchmarks covering broader hallucination types.
- **Four** primary root causes for LVLM hallucination are detailed: issues from training data (bias, irrelevance), vision encoder limitations (resolution, fine-grained semantics), modality aligning module simplicity (connection, token constraints), and inherited LLM issues (context attention, stochastic sampling, capability misalignment).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucination in LVLMs is a multifaceted phenomenon manifesting as cognitive errors (judgment, descriptive inaccuracies) and semantic errors concerning objects, attributes, and relations, distinct from purely linguistic model issues.
> - The root causes of LVLM hallucinations are traceable to specific components of the model pipeline, including data biases, vision encoder limitations, simple modality alignment modules, and inherited issues from the large language model component.
> - Current mitigation strategies directly correspond to these identified root causes, targeting improvements in data quality, vision encoding, modality alignment training, LLM decoding, and post-processing techniques.
