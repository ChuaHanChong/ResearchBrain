# A Survey on Hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey from Huawei Technologies comprehensively defines and categorizes hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), synthesizing current evaluation methods, root causes, and mitigation strategies to provide a structured overview of this critical issue. The work maps hallucination types, identifies its origins across the LVLM pipeline components, and reviews diverse solutions being developed.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) like MiniGPT-4 and LLaVA exhibit 'hallucination,' a critical issue where generated text factually misaligns with the visual content of an image.
- LVLM hallucinations extend beyond simple object presence to include incorrect attributes and relationships, significantly impeding their reliability and trustworthiness for practical applications.
- The rapidly evolving field lacked a comprehensive, structured overview defining this complex problem, categorizing its symptoms, consolidating evaluation methods, analyzing root causes, and reviewing mitigation strategies.

## Method

- The authors conducted a comprehensive survey to systematically define and categorize LVLM ==hallucination==, developing a ==taxonomy for its symptoms==.
- They structured a review of existing evaluation methodologies and benchmarks for LVLM hallucination, distinguishing between ==generative and discriminative== approaches.
- The survey provides a ==pipeline-centric analysis== of the root causes of hallucinations in LVLMs and systematically reviews and categorizes existing mitigation strategies, linking them to their corresponding causes.

## Results

- The survey formally defines LVLM hallucination as a 'disagreement between factual visual content and corresponding textual generation,' distinguishing its multifaceted symptoms including cognitive and semantic errors.
- It identifies **two** main evaluation approaches (non-hallucinatory generation and hallucination discrimination) and categorizes current benchmarks, noting trends towards generative benchmarks covering broader hallucination types.
- **Four** primary root causes for LVLM hallucination are detailed: issues from training data (bias, irrelevance), vision encoder limitations (resolution, fine-grained semantics), modality aligning module simplicity (connection, token constraints), and inherited LLM issues (context attention, stochastic sampling, capability misalignment).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucination in LVLMs is a multifaceted phenomenon manifesting as cognitive errors (judgment, descriptive inaccuracies) and semantic errors concerning objects, attributes, and relations, distinct from purely linguistic model issues.
> - The root causes of LVLM hallucinations are traceable to specific components of the model pipeline, including data biases, vision encoder limitations, simple modality alignment modules, and inherited issues from the large language model component.
> - Current mitigation strategies directly correspond to these identified root causes, targeting improvements in data quality, vision encoding, modality alignment training, LLM decoding, and post-processing techniques.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in artificial intelligence have been largely driven by the emergence of powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4, LLaMA, and LLaMA2. These models have demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in natural language understanding and generation, revolutionizing various text-based applications. A natural progression in AI research has been to extend these impressive linguistic abilities to encompass visual information, leading to the development of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). Models like MiniGPT-4, LLaVA, and LLaVA-1.5 represent the vanguard of this multimodal paradigm, integrating visual features with LLMs to address complex vision-language tasks like visual question answering and image captioning.

Despite their promising performance, a pervasive and critical challenge that significantly impedes the practical implementation of both LLMs and, more acutely, LVLMs is "hallucination." In the context of LLMs, hallucination refers to the generation of factually incorrect or nonsensical information. For LVLMs, this issue is compounded: "hallucination" denotes a misalignment between the factual content presented in an image and the corresponding textual content generated by the model. While earlier work on image captioning models addressed object presence hallucination, LVLMs, leveraging the extensive expressive capabilities of LLMs, exhibit more diverse and severe forms of hallucination, extending beyond object existence to include incorrect attributes and relations.

This survey paper is highly relevant and timely because it synthesizes the burgeoning body of research specifically on hallucination in LVLMs. As the field rapidly evolves, a comprehensive overview is essential to clarify definitions, categorize symptoms, consolidate evaluation methodologies, analyze root causes, and review mitigation strategies. By providing a structured and detailed understanding of this complex problem, the paper serves as a critical reference for researchers and developers navigating the challenges of building reliable and trustworthy multimodal AI systems. It directly addresses the gap in consolidated knowledge, setting the stage for more focused and effective future research in an area that is crucial for the safe and practical deployment of LVLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a comprehensive and structured investigation into the phenomenon of "hallucination" in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). The authors aim to establish a clear overview of the problem, facilitate future research, and ultimately contribute to the development of more reliable and efficient LVLMs.

More specifically, the key objectives articulated in the paper include:

- **Clarification of Concept:** To precisely define what constitutes "hallucination" in the context of LVLMs, outlining its various symptoms (e.g., judgment errors, descriptive inaccuracies, and factual errors concerning objects, attributes, and relations). The survey also highlights the unique challenges inherent in LVLM hallucinations compared to those in purely linguistic models.
- **Evaluation Landscape:** To outline and categorize the existing benchmarks and methodologies specifically designed for evaluating hallucinations unique to LVLMs. This involves distinguishing between methods assessing non-hallucinatory content generation and those evaluating hallucination discrimination.
- **Root Cause Analysis:** To conduct a thorough investigation into the underlying causes of LVLM hallucinations, drawing insights from different components of the LVLM pipeline, including training data, vision encoders, modality connection modules, and the large language models themselves.
- **Mitigation Strategy Review:** To critically review and categorize existing methods proposed for mitigating hallucinations, linking these strategies back to their corresponding identified causes.
- **Future Directions:** To discuss open questions, current challenges, and potential future research directions for addressing hallucinations within LVLMs.

The overarching motivation for this work stems from the recognition that while LVLMs hold immense practical implementation potential, "hallucination" poses a significant challenge that impedes their utility and trustworthiness. By dissecting this complex issue, the authors seek to "provide insights for the development of LVLMs," understand their current limitations, and offer "important guidance for future research and the development of more reliable and efficient LVLMs." The survey is motivated by the critical need to advance the field beyond mere performance metrics to address fundamental issues of factual accuracy and reliability, which are paramount for real-world applications of AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the authors' methodology is centered on a systematic and comprehensive review, synthesis, and categorization of existing research related to hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models. The approach is structured to provide a holistic understanding of the problem space, from its definition and evaluation to its causes and mitigation.

The key aspects of their methodology include:

- **Conceptual Clarification and Taxonomy Development:** The authors begin by meticulously defining "hallucination" within the LVLM context, distinguishing it from related concepts in LLMs. They develop a multi-faceted taxonomy for hallucination symptoms, categorizing them from both a cognitive perspective (judgment vs. description errors) and a visual semantics perspective (object, attribute, and relation errors). This clear conceptualization forms the foundation for the subsequent analysis.
- **Structured Literature Review for Evaluation:** The paper systematically reviews existing evaluation methods and benchmarks. This involves categorizing methods into "non-hallucinatory generation" (quantifying hallucinatory content) and "hallucination discrimination" (binary judgment of hallucination presence). Benchmarks are similarly classified as "discriminative" or "generative," with a comparative analysis of their sizes, targeted hallucination symptoms, and metrics. This structured review provides a critical overview of how hallucinations are currently measured.
- **Pipeline-Centric Causal Analysis:** The authors adopt a pipeline-oriented approach to dissect the root causes of hallucinations. They map potential causes to the different components of a typical LVLM architecture:
 - **Data:** Analyzing issues like data bias and annotation irrelevance in training datasets.
 - **Vision Encoder:** Investigating limitations related to visual resolution and capturing fine-grained semantics.
 - **Modality Aligning Module:** Examining how the simplicity of connection modules and limited token constraints contribute to misalignment.
 - **Large Language Model (LLM):** Identifying inherited issues from the LLM component, such as insufficient context attention, stochastic sampling, and capability misalignment.
 This systematic breakdown allows for a granular understanding of where hallucinations originate.
- **Categorization of Mitigation Strategies:** Corresponding to their causal analysis, the authors categorize existing mitigation methods. These strategies are grouped by the component they target (data, vision encoder, connection module, LLM) or by the stage of application (post-processing). This structured presentation of solutions helps in understanding the diverse approaches being taken to combat hallucinations.
- **Identification of Open Challenges and Future Research:** Based on their comprehensive review, the authors identify remaining challenges and delineate promising future research directions. This forward-looking aspect is crucial for a survey paper, guiding subsequent investigations in the field.
- **Maintenance of an Online Repository:** A practical aspect of their methodology is the commitment to maintaining a GitHub repository (https://github.com/lhanchao777/LVLM-Hallucinations-Survey) with relevant references. This ensures the survey remains a living document in a fast-evolving research area, providing continuous updates and accessibility to the latest developments.

In essence, the methodology involves an exhaustive review of published literature, followed by the creation of detailed taxonomies and analytical frameworks to categorize and explain the complex phenomenon of LVLM hallucinations, their causes, and current solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results (from the Survey's Synthesis)

The survey provides a comprehensive synthesis of the current state of research on hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models, yielding several key findings across definition, evaluation, causes, and mitigation:

#### 4.1. Definition and Symptoms of LVLM Hallucination

- **Core Definition:** Hallucination in LVLMs is fundamentally defined as a "disagreement between factual visual content and corresponding textual generation." This distinguishes it from pure textual hallucinations in LLMs.
- **Multifaceted Symptoms:** LVLM hallucinations are not monolithic. They manifest in:
 - **Cognitive Errors:** Deficiencies in judgment (e.g., incorrect true/false answers to visual queries) and inaccuracies in describing visual information (e.g., misrepresenting scene details).
 - **Semantic Errors:** Specifically categorized as errors related to:
 - **Objects:** Generating non-existent objects.
 - **Attributes:** Providing incorrect descriptions of object properties (e.g., color, size, hair length).
 - **Relations:** Making inaccurate assertions about spatial or conceptual relationships between objects.
- **Unique Challenges:** The multimodal nature of LVLMs introduces complexities in detecting hallucinations across various semantic dimensions, the causes are often intertwined between visual and linguistic modalities, and mitigation strategies require compound approaches.

#### 4.2. Evaluation Methods and Benchmarks

- **Two Main Evaluation Approaches:**
 - **Non-Hallucinatory Generation:** Measures the proportion of hallucinatory content in generated outputs. This includes:
 - *Handcrafted Pipeline Methods:* Rely on rule-based or external modules (e.g., CHAIR, CCEval, FAITH-SCORE) to analyze generated text against visual ground truth.
 - *Model-based End-to-End Methods:* Utilize advanced LLMs (e.g., GPT-4 based evaluation like GAVIE, MMHal-Bench) or fine-tuned models (e.g., M-HalDetect, HaELM) to directly assess hallucinations.
 - **Hallucination Discrimination:** Assesses the LVLM's ability to identify whether a given statement (often a VQA-style question) is hallucinatory (e.g., POPE, CIEM, NOPE).
- **Benchmarking Trends:**
 - Discriminative benchmarks (POPE, NOPE, CIEM) primarily focus on object existence and use accuracy metrics.
 - Generative benchmarks (M-HalDetect, GAVIE, FAITHScore, MMHal-Bench) are more prevalent and extend their scope to include attribute and relation hallucinations, employing more complex and varied metrics.
 - Comprehensive benchmarks like AMBER integrate both discriminative and generative tasks.

#### 4.3. Root Causes of LVLM Hallucinations

The survey identifies four primary sources of hallucinations, corresponding to different stages and components of the LVLM pipeline:
- **From Data:**
 - **Data Bias:** Imbalance in training data (e.g., overwhelming "Yes" answers in QA pairs) and data homogeneity leading to limited understanding of visual instructions.
 - **Annotation Irrelevance:** Issues with synthesized instruction data, where generated long instructions may not accurately correspond to fine-grained visual content.
- **From Vision Encoder:**
 - **Limited Visual Resolution:** Lower image resolution used during training limits the encoder's ability to perceive fine details, exacerbating hallucinations.
 - **Fine-grained Visual Semantics:** CLIP-derived vision encoders, optimized for salient objects, often fail to capture subtle visual cues like background details, object counts, or precise relations.
- **From Modality Aligning Module:**
 - **Connection Module Simplicity:** The use of simple structures (e.g., linear layers) for modality alignment hinders comprehensive multimodal connection, increasing hallucination risk.
 - **Limited Token Constraints:** Modules like Q-Formers, while efficient, may use a restricted number of tokens, preventing the full encoding of visual information and leading to information loss.
- **From LLM (Inherited):**
 - **Insufficient Context Attention:** LLMs may over-focus on partial textual context during decoding, ignoring crucial input visual information or prioritizing fluent but inaccurate language patterns.
 - **Stochastic Sampling Decoding:** The inherent randomness introduced by stochastic decoding strategies, while beneficial for diversity, can amplify hallucination risks.
 - **Capability Misalignment:** Disparity between the LLM's pre-training capabilities and the expanded requirements during instruction tuning can push the model to generate content beyond its reliable knowledge boundaries.

#### 4.4. Mitigation Strategies

The paper categorizes current mitigation methods, often directly addressing the identified causes:
- **Mitigation for Data:**
 - **Bias Mitigation:** Strategies like contrastive instruction tuning (CIEM) and incorporating diverse negative samples (LRV-Instruction, Ferret).
 - **Annotation Enrichment:** Constructing richly annotated datasets that provide fine-grained supervision (M-HalDetect, GRIT, EMMA, HallE-Switch).
- **Mitigation for Vision Encoder:**
 - **Scaling-up Vision Resolution:** Training models with higher resolution images (Qwen-vl, Monkey, InternVL), although resource-intensive.
 - **Perceptual Enhancement:** Integrating additional perception modalities (VCoder) or pre-trained models for spatial and scene graph information (Spatial Awareness Enhancing).
- **Mitigation for Connection Module:**
 - **Connection Module Enhancing:** Upgrading simple connection modules to more capable structures (LLaVA-1.5, QLLaMA).
 - **Alignment Training Optimizing:** Explicitly adding learning objectives to reduce the gap between visual and textual tokens (HACL) or using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for modality alignment (LLaVA-RLHF).
- **Mitigation for LLM:**
 - **Decoding Optimization:** Modifying decoding strategies to ensure better attention to visual context and reduce over-reliance on language priors (OPERA, VCD).
 - **Aligning with Human Training:** Employing human preference-aligned training methods like RLHF (LLaVA-RLHF) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO variants like FDPO, HA-DPO, RLHF-V) to favor non-hallucinatory responses.
- **Mitigation via Post-processing:**
 - Applying external modules or operations to refine and edit generated outputs to remove inaccuracies (LURE, Woodpecker).

These findings collectively map out the landscape of LVLM hallucination research, from identifying its manifestations to diagnosing its origins and surveying the solutions developed thus far.
