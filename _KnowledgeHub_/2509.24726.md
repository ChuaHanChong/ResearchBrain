# Socratic-Zero : Bootstrapping Reasoning via Data-Free Agent Co-evolution

> [!summary] Summary
> SOCRATIC-ZERO introduces a data-free framework enabling large language models to autonomously improve their reasoning capabilities through the co-evolution of Solver, Teacher, and Generator agents. This system creates adaptive, high-quality training curricula from minimal seed examples, achieving state-of-the-art performance in mathematical reasoning and demonstrating transferability to general cognitive tasks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art LLMs for reasoning heavily rely on massive, high-quality human-annotated datasets, creating a fundamental bottleneck for scalability and accessibility.
- Current LLM training paradigms are static, unable to adapt the curriculum to evolving model capabilities or exploit rich feedback for targeted weakness remediation.
- Existing data synthesis methods often struggle with inconsistent data quality and lack dynamic adaptation to a model's current abilities, leading to suboptimal training signals.

## Method

- A ==multi-agent co-evolutionary framework== (Solver, Teacher, Generator) bootstraps reasoning skills from minimal seed examples; the Solver refines its policy via ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==, learning from feedback the Teacher generates based on its own attempts.
- The Teacher (a fixed, high-capacity LLM) verifies solutions, refines problems based on Solver failures, and dynamically adapts the curriculum by categorizing problems and generating new, targeted challenges based on the Solver's =='zone of proximal development'==.
- The Generator learns to mimic the Teacher's problem design strategy via ==Value-Weighted Supervised Fine-Tuning (WSFT)==, using a utility function to ensure generated problems are optimally challenging for the current Solver.

## Results

- The Socratic-Solver-8B achieved **56.1%** average accuracy across seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks, representing a **+20.2** percentage point gain over prior data synthesis methods.
- The Socratic-Generator-32B produced synthetic data with a **95.6%** validity rate and enabled a student LLM to achieve superior downstream training effectiveness (**37.72%**) compared to data generated by larger commercial LLMs (e.g., GPT-5, Claude-4.1-Opus).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of initial supervised fine-tuning for foundational capabilities and validated the effectiveness of the Gaussian reward function in guiding the Generator to produce optimally challenging problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous, data-free co-evolution of specialized LLM agents can effectively generate high-quality training data and adaptive curricula, significantly reducing reliance on expensive human annotation.
> - Integrating preference learning (DPO) with dynamic curriculum adaptation driven by a 'Teacher' and a 'Generator' learning problem-design principles enables continuous, targeted self-improvement in reasoning.
> - Maintaining an optimal challenge level for the learner (Solver), guided by an adaptive curriculum that provides problems at its 'zone of proximal development', is crucial for efficient and robust skill bootstrapping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of sophisticated reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) is a paramount objective in AI research. Recent breakthroughs, particularly in mathematical reasoning, have demonstrated impressive problem-solving prowess. However, as the authors keenly observe, these advancements are predicated on the availability of "massive, high-quality datasets—typically human-annotated and thus difficult to scale." This dependency creates a critical bottleneck, limiting the accessibility and autonomous evolution of LLMs beyond human-curated knowledge.

Socratic-Zero emerges as a direct response to this fundamental challenge, aiming to shift the paradigm from human-dependent, static data pipelines to fully autonomous, dynamic, and adaptive learning systems. The paper positions itself within several key areas of contemporary LLM research:

- **Data Synthesis:** Existing methods like LLM2LLM and WarriorMath have explored using LLMs to generate training data, sometimes with "deficiency-aware" mechanisms. More ambitiously, Absolute Zero and R-Zero have ventured into fully autonomous self-play paradigms. Socratic-Zero builds upon these by introducing a robust "quality control mechanism" and an "adaptive curriculum generation" strategy that current methods often lack, thus addressing the inconsistency and low-value sample issues.
- **Data Distillation:** Efforts like Orca and policy distillation aim to transfer capabilities from powerful teacher models to smaller student models. While these methods leverage teacher feedback, Socratic-Zero goes further by enabling students (Solvers) to actively refine their reasoning based on preferences and allowing a Generator to strategically distill the *teacher's problem-design strategy*—a more active and dynamic distillation than passively accepting solutions. Crucially, Socratic-Zero's curriculum is not static but dynamically adjusts to the Solver's evolving capabilities.
- **Preference Learning:** This area, spearheaded by techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Reward-Weighted Supervised Fine-Tuning (RWSFT), focuses on aligning model behavior with desired outcomes. While these methods have shown promise in self-correction (e.g., Self-Refine), the authors identify a gap: the lack of "unified, co-evolving frameworks for feedback generation and validation." Socratic-Zero explicitly addresses this by integrating DPO within a multi-agent system where the Teacher actively generates tailored feedback and new problems, forming a truly closed-loop self-improvement mechanism.

In essence, Socratic-Zero represents a significant step towards enabling LLMs to autonomously develop and refine complex reasoning skills with minimal human intervention and data, moving beyond the current limitations of costly data annotation, static curricula, and passive distillation. It seeks to unify and enhance the strengths of data synthesis, distillation, and preference learning within a coherent, self-improving multi-agent framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Socratic-Zero is to develop a "fully autonomous framework that generates high-quality training data from minimal seed examples through the co-evolution of three agents." This overarching goal is driven by several critical motivations stemming from the limitations of current LLM training paradigms:

1. **Overcome Data Scarcity and Cost:** State-of-the-art LLMs for reasoning tasks heavily rely on "massive, high-quality datasets," often "human-annotated and thus difficult to scale." This leads to prohibitive costs and creates a "fundamental bottleneck" for developing more advanced reasoning capabilities. Socratic-Zero aims to eliminate this dependency, requiring only "minimal seed examples" (starting from just 100 questions).

2. **Address Static Curriculum Limitations:** Current methodologies are "entrenched in a static paradigm," where datasets are fixed, and curricula are handcrafted. This approach cannot adapt to the "evolving model capabilities during training" or "exploit rich feedback signals for targeting specific weaknesses." Socratic-Zero seeks to create a "self-improving curriculum" that dynamically adapts, ensuring that training signals remain maximally informative.

3. **Improve Data Quality and Adaptability in Synthetic Generation:** While data synthesis offers an alternative, existing methods "struggle with inconsistent data quality and an inability to dynamically adapt to the evolving capabilities of the model, leading to suboptimal training signals." The framework is motivated to ensure the generation of "high-quality" and optimally challenging problems through its Teacher and Generator agents.

4. **Advance Autonomous Reasoning:** The ultimate motivation is to achieve "truly autonomous reasoning improvement." By operationalizing the Socratic method into a computational framework, the authors aim to enable LLMs to learn and evolve their reasoning abilities continuously without external human supervision for data generation or curriculum design.

In summary, the core motivation is to break free from the reliance on expensive, static, human-curated data, and instead establish a dynamic, autonomous system where LLMs can collaboratively generate their own high-quality learning curriculum and continuously improve their reasoning skills, mirroring an effective pedagogical process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Socratic-Zero is a "paradigm-shifting framework" built on the philosophical analogy of the Socratic method of learning through guided questioning. It operationalizes this concept into a multi-agent co-evolutionary system comprising three distinct LLM agents: the Solver, the Teacher, and the Generator. The entire system is designed as a closed-loop, self-improving mechanism, starting from a minimal set of seed problems and operating without external human-annotated data thereafter.

#### 3.1. The Three Co-Evolving Agents

1. **Solver (S):** This agent (parameterized by π_θ_S) is the learner. Its primary objective is to generate correct reasoning paths for given problems.
 - **Online Optimization:** At each iteration, the Solver attempts to solve problems from the current curriculum. It generates *k* multiple solution attempts.
 - **Preference Learning (DPO):** The Solver refines its reasoning policy using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). It learns from "preference feedback" constructed by the Teacher, which labels the Solver's own attempts as "winning" (correct) or "losing" (incorrect). This process maximizes the likelihood of preferred solutions over rejected ones.
 - **Curriculum Adaptation:** The Solver's failures are critical for driving the evolution of the curriculum.

2. **Teacher (T):** This is a fixed, high-capacity LLM (specifically, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507). It acts as a static "oracle" with two deterministic functions:
 - **Verification Function `V(q, y)`:** Judges the correctness of a solution `y` for a problem `q`. This is crucial for generating preference pairs for the Solver and for assessing the quality of generated problems.
 - **Problem Refinement Function `G(q, y_fail)`:** Creates a new, instructive problem-solution pair (`q'`, `y'_ref`) by revising an original problem `q` based on a failed Solver attempt `y_fail`. This function is central to dynamically adapting the curriculum.
 - **Adaptive Curriculum Design:** The Teacher categorizes problems based on the Solver's success rate into "Mastered," "Learning," and "Too Difficult" zones. It then strategically generates new problems: more complex variants from "Mastered" problems and targeted error-fixing problems from "Learning" problems, ensuring the curriculum remains within the Solver's "zone of proximal development." Problems from the "Too Difficult" zone are temporarily excluded to avoid counterproductive difficulty spikes.

3. **Generator (G):** This agent (parameterized by π_θ_G, specifically Qwen3-32B) learns to mimic and scale the Teacher’s problem refinement strategy.
 - **Offline Distillation:** The Generator is trained via Value-Weighted Supervised Fine-Tuning (WSFT). It observes the Teacher's generation of high-utility problems based on Solver failures.
 - **Utility Function `U(q'|π_θ_S)`:** To ensure an "effective curriculum" with problems of "desirable difficulty," the Generator's training is weighted by a utility function. This function scores new problems `q'` based on the *current* Solver's estimated success rate (aiming for a target success rate `μ=0.5` for optimal challenge, using an unnormalized Gaussian). This allows the Generator to internalize the Teacher's expert curriculum design principles, producing problems optimally challenging for the current Solver.

#### 3.2. The Co-Evolutionary Loop

The framework operates in a continuous, iterative cycle:
1. The Solver attempts problems from the current curriculum (D_t).
2. The Teacher verifies the Solver's attempts, generating preference data for DPO and identifying failures (F_t).
3. The Solver updates its policy (π_θ_S) using DPO on the collected preference data.
4. The Teacher leverages the Solver's failures (F_t) to generate new, instructive, and increasingly challenging problems (D_new).
5. The Generator observes this Teacher's problem generation process and distills its strategy using WSFT, weighted by the utility function, to learn to produce high-quality, optimally challenging problems (thereby creating new problems at scale).
6. The curriculum (D_t) is augmented with these newly generated problems (D_t+1 = D_t ∪ D_new).
7. All problems in the curriculum are dynamically recategorized based on the updated Solver's performance, allowing "too difficult" problems to re-enter the learning pool as the Solver improves.

#### 3.3. Key Supporting Mechanisms

- **Minimal Seed Data:** The process starts with only 100 seed questions sampled from the MATH dataset, ensuring diversity and appropriate initial difficulty.
- **Initial SFT:** Solver models undergo an initial LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a small dataset (1,500 problems) to provide foundational reasoning capabilities, which ablation studies confirm is critical.
- **Robust Evaluation:** Solver performance is measured by Mean@32 accuracy (32 independent solution attempts). Problem validity and correctness are ensured through a "dual-verification mechanism" combining rule-based answer extraction (MathRule) and semantic validation by the Teacher LLM.
- **Quality Control:** An explicit problem quality control mechanism prevents the propagation of erroneous or ill-posed problems, involving Teacher self-verification and problem filtering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Socratic-Zero achieved remarkable empirical performance across a spectrum of mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in autonomously bootstrapping and refining LLM capabilities from minimal seed data.

#### 4.1. Solver Performance

- **Superior Mathematical Reasoning:** The Socratic-Solver-8B achieved an impressive average accuracy of **56.1%** across seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AMC23, AIME24-25, Olympiad, MATH-500, Minerva, and GSM8K). This represents a substantial **+20.2 percentage point gain** over prior data synthesis methods such as Static Augmentation and LLM2LLM (which achieved 40.7% and 40.9% respectively).
 - Particularly noteworthy were the gains on highly competitive problems: +19.1 points on AIME-24 and +16.1 points on AIME-25, showcasing improved performance on challenging tasks.
- **Cross-Architecture Generalization:** The effectiveness of Socratic-Zero was validated across different model architectures.
 - On GLM4-9B-base, it achieved **52.3%** average accuracy (+9.5 points over SFT baseline).
 - On Qwen3-14B-base, it reached **60.3%** average accuracy (+8.5 points over SFT baseline). These consistent gains underscore that the Socratic-Zero principles transcend specific model families and enhance fundamental reasoning.
- **Transfer to General Reasoning:** Improvements in mathematical reasoning successfully transferred to broader cognitive abilities, resulting in a **+6.02 points average improvement** across BBEH, MMLU-Pro, and SuperGPQA benchmarks. This suggests that the framework cultivates more generalized reasoning skills.

#### 4.2. Generator Performance and Data Quality

- **High Problem Validity:** The Socratic-Generator-32B demonstrated a remarkable **95.6% validity rate** for generated problems, significantly outperforming its base model (Qwen3-32B at 89.1%) and rivaling the validity rates of much larger, proprietary models like GPT-5 (95.8%), Gemini-2.5-Pro (94.2%), and Claude-4.1-Opus (96.9%). This confirms the Generator's ability to produce high-quality, solvable problems.
- **State-of-the-Art Downstream Training Effectiveness:** Synthetic data produced by the Socratic-Generator-32B enabled a student LLM (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) to achieve **37.72% downstream training effectiveness** (average accuracy on the seven mathematical benchmarks).
 - Remarkably, this performance **outperformed data generated by leading commercial LLMs**, including Qwen3-235B-A22B (37.13%), DeepSeek-V3.1-671B (36.62%), GPT-5 (36.62%), Gemini-2.5-Pro (37.20%), Grok-4 (37.01%), and Claude-4.1-Opus (37.63%). This is a significant finding, as the Socratic-Generator-32B is substantially smaller than many of these commercial models, highlighting the power of specialized, adaptively generated data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Initial SFT:** An ablation study confirmed the critical role of initial supervised fine-tuning. The Solver trained with SFT showed an **18.38 percentage point improvement** on AIME-24 by Stage 3, compared to a mere 2.34 percentage point gain without SFT—a 7.9 times greater improvement. SFT provides essential foundational capabilities for subsequent reinforcement learning.
- **Optimal Reward Function:** The designed Gaussian reward function (N(μ=0.5, σ=0.2)) for the Generator, which incentivizes problems at the frontier of the Solver's capabilities (around 50% success rate), achieved the best performance. Alternative linear or differently parameterized Gaussian functions consistently yielded inferior results, validating the strategic choice of the reward signal for adaptive curriculum generation.

#### 4.4. Curriculum Stability and Diversity

- **Adaptive Difficulty:** The Solver's mean reward evolution showed initial decline then recovery (e.g., 52.1% to 48.7% then 50.1%), indicating that the Teacher adaptively increased curriculum difficulty as the Solver improved, maintaining an optimal challenge level.
- **Stable Generator Output:** The Generator consistently produced high-reward problems (around 50% success rate) with minimal fluctuation (1.3% range), demonstrating stable learning of the optimal difficulty zone.
- **Diversity Preservation:** Multi-domain seed initialization and high-temperature sampling at critical stages ensured continuous problem diversity throughout the curriculum evolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Socratic-Zero represents a significant leap forward in the field of autonomous AI development, with profound implications for the future of LLM reasoning capabilities and beyond.

1. **Breaking the Data Bottleneck:** The most immediate impact is the framework's ability to "eliminate dependency on large-scale external datasets" for advanced reasoning tasks. By bootstrapping from only 100 seed questions, Socratic-Zero offers a scalable and cost-effective pathway to developing sophisticated LLMs, democratizing access to high-quality training methodologies currently limited by expensive human annotation. This is particularly crucial for niche domains or low-resource languages where human-labeled data is scarce.

2. **Paradigm Shift to Autonomous Learning:** Socratic-Zero instigates a shift from a human-centric, static data engineering paradigm to a truly "autonomous reasoning improvement" loop. It demonstrates that AI systems can generate their own high-quality, adaptive curricula, learn from their own successes and failures, and dynamically evolve their capabilities. This mirrors a more biological or pedagogical learning process, where a learner (Solver) is guided by an expert (Teacher) and a curriculum designer (Generator) in a self-sustaining cycle.

3. **Enhanced Reasoning and Generalization:** The substantial performance gains in mathematical reasoning (+20.2 points average over baselines) indicate a fundamental improvement in LLM's core reasoning abilities. Furthermore, the demonstrated transferability to general reasoning benchmarks (+6.02 points average) suggests that the developed skills are not merely task-specific but contribute to broader cognitive intelligence, paving the way for more robust and versatile AI.

4. **Efficiency through Specialization:** The remarkable finding that a smaller, specialized Socratic-Generator-32B can produce higher-quality training data than much larger, general-purpose commercial LLMs (including GPT-5 and Claude-4.1-Opus) underscores the power of targeted, co-evolutionary training. This highlights a potential future where models might not need to be exorbitantly large to achieve superior performance, but rather strategically specialized and adaptively trained within self-improving ecosystems. This has significant implications for computational efficiency and environmental sustainability in AI training.

5. **Foundation for Broader AI Evolution:** While currently focused on mathematical reasoning, the underlying principles of a multi-agent, co-evolutionary framework with dynamic curriculum generation and preference-based learning are domain-agnostic. The authors explicitly envision extending this framework to "broader domains including scientific discovery, real-world decision making, and complex system modeling." Socratic-Zero thus lays a theoretical and empirical foundation for future AI systems that can continuously explore, learn, and adapt in open-ended environments.

The paper's innovative approach, robust empirical validation, and significant performance gains position Socratic-Zero as a highly impactful contribution, pushing the boundaries of autonomous AI and offering a promising blueprint for the next generation of self-improving intelligent systems.
