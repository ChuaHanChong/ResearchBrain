# V-Dreamer: Automating Robotic Simulation and Trajectory Synthesis via Video Generation Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> V-Dreamer is a fully automated pipeline turning a language instruction (optionally a real photo) into a simulation-ready 3D scene and executable expert trajectory. An LLM plus Flux/SAM3/SAM3D build physics-validated layouts; a video generation model dreams the manipulation, which CoTracker3, VGGT and TAPIP3D lift into 3D end-effector trajectories. Policies trained on this data reach 36.96% success on unseen mugs and transfer zero-shot to real hardware.

## Problem

- Real-world teleoperated demonstration collection is prohibitively expensive, slow, and cannot scale to long-tail manipulation scenarios needed for generalist robot policies.
- Existing generative simulators depend on fixed asset libraries and hand-designed tasks, capping visual, semantic, and geometric diversity available for sim-to-real transfer.
- Video generation models produce plausible motion but stay in 2D pixel space, violating kinematic and geometric constraints, so no executable robot trajectory results.

## Method

- ==Semantic-to-Physics Scene Synthesis==: Qwen-Max writes an asset manifest, Flux renders assets, SAM3 segments, SAM3D lifts to meshes, Genesis enforces ==AABB collision checks== and gravity-aligned layout.
- ==Video-Prior-Based Trajectory Generation==: after a dynamic settling phase, Qwen-Image-Edit style-refines the initial frame and Wan2.2-I2V-Flash with negative prompts synthesizes rigid-body manipulation video.
- ==Sim-to-Gen alignment==: mask-restricted ==CoTracker3== point tracking plus ==VGGT== metric depth fused by ==TAPIP3D== into 3D motion, then Graspgen grasp poses and ==inverse kinematics== yield executable end-effector trajectories.

## Results

- Policy success on **10** held-out unseen mugs scales non-linearly with data: **3.46%** at **500** trajectories, **25.90%** at **1,000**, peaking at **36.96%** with **2,500** synthesized demonstrations.
- The engine produces roughly **600** LeRobot-formatted trajectories per hour on an 8xRTX 4090 workstation, replacing manual teleoperation as the data bottleneck.
- A one-shot real policy from a single synthesized trajectory scored **50%** under visual distractors, **20%** on novel objects, **15%** under spatial perturbation, **0%** under camera occlusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video foundation models can serve as motion priors for control if paired with mask-restricted tracking and metric depth to ground pixels into 3D.
> - Zero success under camera occlusion is diagnostic evidence that a policy runs closed-loop visual servoing rather than replaying an open-loop trajectory.
> - Synthetic demonstration scale shows a sharp threshold, not smooth gains: below a coverage floor of the visual-spatial distribution, imitation policies fail catastrophically.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic manipulation policies capable of operating in unstructured environments is a central challenge in robotics. Current approaches, particularly those reliant on imitation learning, are often constrained by the availability of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality training data. Collecting such data in the real world is resource-intensive, expensive, and difficult to scale to a wide variety of scenarios.

Simulation offers a scalable alternative for data generation, but traditional simulators face limitations. They typically rely on fixed asset libraries and manually designed tasks, which restrict the semantic and visual diversity necessary for robust generalization and effective sim-to-real transfer. This leads to a "reality gap" where policies trained in simulation may not perform adequately on physical hardware.

The broader research landscape has seen the emergence of generative foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and diffusion models, which encapsulate extensive world knowledge regarding object semantics, spatial relationships, and visual affordances. Prior works have explored using these models for various purposes, including generating static 3D scenes (e.g., GenSim, RoboGen, Holodeck) or high-level task plans. Video generation models have also demonstrated the ability to produce visually plausible motions. However, a significant bottleneck remains: these generative approaches rarely yield fully executable robotic tasks, often due to issues with geometric consistency, kinematic constraints, or a lack of physical grounding in the generated visual data. Extracting precise, physically grounded 3D trajectories from 2D pixel-space video predictions for direct robot control presents a persistent challenge.

V-Dreamer addresses this gap by proposing a fully automated, full-cycle pipeline that integrates these generative capabilities to synthesize dynamic, physically feasible manipulation trajectories and environments from natural language instructions. It aims to overcome the limitations of both real-world data collection and existing simulation environments by creating a scalable data engine that can generate diverse, policy-compliant demonstrations without manual human supervision, thereby enhancing data availability for generalizable robot learning and robust sim-to-real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of V-Dreamer is to develop a fully automated framework capable of generating open-vocabulary, simulation-ready manipulation environments and executable expert trajectories directly from natural language instructions. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity for Generalist Robots:** Training general-purpose robotic manipulation policies, particularly with methods like imitation learning, necessitates vast quantities of diverse training data. Real-world data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and does not scale efficiently to the long-tail of variations in objects, scenes, and tasks. V-Dreamer aims to provide a high-throughput, automated solution to alleviate this data bottleneck.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Simulators:** Conventional robotic simulators are often limited by predefined asset libraries and manually specified task parameters. This restricts the visual and semantic diversity of generated data, which is crucial for training policies that can generalize to novel objects and unstructured environments in the real world. The motivation is to move beyond fixed asset repositories to enable truly open-vocabulary scene diversity.
- **Leveraging Generative Foundation Models:** The recent advancements in large language models and diffusion models (for image and video generation) have demonstrated their capacity to capture complex semantics and visual patterns. V-Dreamer is motivated by the potential to harness this implicit knowledge to automate scene construction and motion synthesis, reducing the reliance on hand-crafted rules or expert demonstrations.
- **Bridging the Gap between Visual Generation and Physical Execution:** While video generation models can produce visually plausible motions, these often lack the physical grounding and kinematic feasibility required for robotic execution. A key motivation is to bridge this representational gap by developing a robust mechanism to translate abstract visual predictions from video generation models into precise, physically valid, and executable 3D robot trajectories.
- **Enabling Robust Zero-Shot Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ultimate goal for generalist robots is to perform tasks on unseen objects in novel real-world settings. V-Dreamer is motivated to generate data that inherently supports zero-shot generalization in simulation and facilitates zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, minimizing the need for real-world fine-tuning or extensive domain randomization tuning.
- **Automating the Full Data Synthesis Cycle:** Many prior works focus on isolated aspects of data generation (e.g., static scene generation or high-level planning). V-Dreamer seeks to automate the entire pipeline, from natural language instruction to a simulation-ready scene and an executable expert trajectory, without human intervention, thereby creating a unified and streamlined data synthesis process for robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-Dreamer operates as a fully automated framework, converting natural language instructions into simulation-ready environments and executable expert trajectories. The methodology comprises three interconnected stages: Semantic-to-Physics Scene Synthesis, Video-Prior-Based Trajectory Generation, and Sim-to-Gen Alignment.

**A. Semantic-to-Physics Scene Synthesis**
This module translates an open-ended semantic instruction into a physically interactive 3D rigid-body simulation through a multi-stage procedural generation pipeline:

1. **Semantic Parsing and Asset Manifest:** A Large Language Model (Qwen-Max) acts as a high-level semantic planner. It decomposes the natural language prompt into a structured JSON Asset Manifest, specifying object categories and stylistic elements. To improve downstream reconstruction, negative constraints are introduced (e.g., prohibiting highly reflective surfaces). This stage focuses on object-level manifest generation without explicit background or layout.
2. **Generative Visual Synthesis:** For each entry in the Asset Manifest, 2D image assets are generated using the Flux diffusion model. Prompts are augmented with modifiers like "studio lighting" and "solid white background" to minimize environmental bias and baked-in shadows. Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM3) performs background removal using a hybrid segmentation scheme (semantic-first, with a vision-based fallback for suboptimal masks).
3. **Memory-Efficient 3D Reconstruction:** To address the computational bottleneck of lifting high-resolution 2D assets to 3D meshes, a memory-efficient pipeline is implemented. This involves dynamically offloading encoder and decoder sub-networks between the CPU and GPU during inference, enabling the reconstruction of complex, high-polygon geometries on consumer-grade hardware by optimizing VRAM usage at the cost of computation time.
4. **Physically Grounded Layout:** After generating individual 3D object meshes, they are assembled into a coherent and physically valid scene. An LLM infers approximate real-world metric dimensions for each object, and heuristics correct any distortions from 3D lifting. A foundation model then generates a scene layout as a JSON specification, positioning objects. Finally, the Genesis physics engine enforces a constraint-based layout using AABB collision checking and gravity alignment. Anchors (e.g., tables) are placed on the ground plane, while accessories are positioned on supporting surfaces with procedural spatial jittering to resolve bounding-box overlaps.

**B. Video-Prior-Based Trajectory Generation**
This module leverages implicit motion priors from video foundation models to synthesize context-aware manipulation trajectories:

1. **Scene Stabilization and Refinement:** The procedurally generated scene undergoes a brief dynamic settling phase within the Genesis simulator to resolve micro-penetrations, capturing the stabilized state as the initial frame (I$_0$). Style refinement is then applied to I$_0$ using Qwen-Image-Edit to obtain a Style-Refined Initial Frame (I'$_{0}$). This step allows for automatic visual diversity (e.g., material perturbations) or user-specified stylization.
2. **Video Generation:** I'$_{0}$ is fed into the Wan2.2-I2V-Flash video generation model to synthesize a continuous temporal sequence of the specified manipulation task. Targeted negative prompts are employed to suppress undesirable visual artifacts like camera motion, object deformation, and background flicker, encouraging the diffusion model to focus on rigid-body kinematics and physical plausibility.
3. **3D Motion Extraction (Sim-to-Gen Alignment):** To extract actionable 3D controls from the 2D pixel domain:
 - SAM3 is re-run on the visually processed initial frame (I'$_{0}$) to obtain a high-quality mask of the target object.
 - Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) estimates per-frame depth, providing metric cues for 3D lifting.
 - CoTracker3 tracks uniformly sampled 2D points within the object mask across frames, yielding dense, object-consistent 2D trajectories.
 - These tracked points are then fused into a coherent 3D motion trajectory using the TAPIP3D module. This mask-restricted tracking approach aims for higher precision compared to full-frame dense tracking.
4. **Post-processing and IK:** Lightweight post-processing removes outlier tracks and suppresses temporal jitter. Graspgen generates a feasible robotic grasp pose for the target object. Inverse kinematics (IK) maps the resulting 3D motion to the robot's end-effector trajectory, yielding a physically executable trajectory τ = {(p$_t$, q$_t$)}$^{T}_{t=0}$, where p$_t$ is the end-effector pose and q$_t$ is the gripper state.

**C. Sim-to-Real Alignment**
V-Dreamer continuously runs the pipeline to generate a large-scale, high-fidelity synthetic dataset D = {(τ$_i$, O$_i$)}$^{N}_{i=1}$, where each expert trajectory τ$_i$ is paired with a diverse semantic and visual context O$_i$. For tasks with multiple steps, the LLM decomposes the instruction into sub-tasks, and the generation pipeline iterates for each.

For real-world deployment, a sim-to-real transfer protocol is adopted:
- A snapshot of the real target scene can be used as an alignment input to instantiate a scene-matched, style-refined initial frame (I'$_{0}$) in simulation.
- The simulator is calibrated to match real camera parameters and robot workspace bounds.
- A policy trained solely on D is deployed zero-shot on physical hardware without human demonstrations or target-domain fine-tuning.
- Optional coarse bounding geometries of novel objects are reconstructed in simulation for background collision checking, while the core manipulation behavior is driven by the learned generalized representations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate V-Dreamer's capabilities in terms of scalability and data quality, semantic and geometric diversity, and physical grounding and executability for sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Scalability and Data Quality (Q1)**
V-Dreamer demonstrated high-throughput data generation, producing approximately 600 LeRobot-formatted trajectories per hour on a workstation equipped with 8 NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs. Qualitative visualizations illustrated V-Dreamer's capacity to synthesize coherent demonstrations for tasks of varying complexity, including simple pick-and-place and more complex cooperative mobile manipulation tasks. The generated videos and corresponding extracted end-effector trajectories indicated structured, executable scene-action pairs. Additionally, generated scenes exhibited substantial variation in textures, metric scales, and spatial layouts.

**B. Semantic and Geometric Diversity (Q2)**
The pipeline generated a diverse training corpus encompassing 40 distinct target objects, 40 receptacle objects with varying geometries, textures, and scales, and 20 unique table settings with randomized surface materials. This diversity was intended to support generalization.

Evaluation in simulation involved training policies with varying sizes of synthesized datasets (500 to 2,500 trajectories) and testing them on a held-out set of 10 novel mug instances with unseen shapes, handles, and textures. The results showed a correlation between the scale of the synthetic data and the policy's success rate. A policy trained on 500 trajectories exhibited a 3.46% success rate, which increased to 25.90% with 1,000 trajectories. The performance peaked at 36.96% success rate with 2,500 synthesized demonstrations. These results suggest that policies trained on V-Dreamer's data can generalize to geometrically unseen objects in simulation, and that increasing the scale of the synthesized data leads to improved downstream learning performance.

**C. Physical Grounding and Executability (Q3) and Real-World Experiments**
To assess physical grounding and executability, policies trained with V-Dreamer-generated data were deployed on a physical 6-DoF Piper robotic arm. The evaluation focused on a zero-shot sim-to-real protocol, where a policy trained on a *single* V-Dreamer-synthesized trajectory (for a simple block-to-bowl task) was transferred to the real world without further real-world demonstrations or fine-tuning.

The one-shot policy demonstrated non-trivial robustness under various perturbation conditions:
- **Visual Distractors:** Achieved a 50% success rate when unmodeled background clutter was present in the camera's field of view.
- **Object Generalization:** Achieved a 20% success rate when manipulating out-of-distribution target objects such as an apple, tape roll, mango, pear, and bottle, indicating limited but present generalization to novel objects not seen during training.
- **Spatial Perturbation:** Achieved a 15% success rate when initial object and goal positions were shifted.
- **Sensor Occlusion:** Resulted in a 0% success rate when the camera view was blocked, confirming that the policy relied on closed-loop visual feedback rather than open-loop trajectory playback.

These real-world experiments indicate that the trajectories generated by V-Dreamer are physically grounded, kinematically feasible, and directly usable for robotic control, even under an extreme low-data, one-shot supervision regime. This provides evidence for the utility of V-Dreamer in bridging the reality gap for robot manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

V-Dreamer introduces a significant advancement in the field of robot learning by presenting a fully automated, full-cycle pipeline for instruction-driven, open-vocabulary robotic manipulation data synthesis. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Automated Data Generation:** V-Dreamer addresses the critical data bottleneck in robot learning by automating the entire process from natural language instruction to simulation-ready environments and executable expert trajectories. This capability reduces the reliance on manual teleoperation, which is a slow, expensive, and non-scalable method of data collection. By generating approximately 600 trajectories per hour, it offers a high-throughput solution that can dramatically increase the scale and diversity of training data.
- **Open-Vocabulary Scene Synthesis:** Unlike prior generative simulation methods that are constrained by fixed asset libraries, V-Dreamer synthesizes novel 3D geometries and photorealistic textures from scratch using diffusion models. This enables true open-vocabulary scene diversity, unconstrained by predefined asset repositories, which is crucial for training robots that can operate in varied real-world environments.
- **Novel Video-Prior-Based Behavior Synthesis:** The framework introduces a method for generating expert trajectories by leveraging video generation models as rich motion priors. This approach moves beyond hand-crafted heuristics or traditional motion planners, offering a pathway to synthesize diverse, context-aware manipulation behaviors. The robust Sim-to-Gen visual-kinematic alignment module is instrumental in translating visually plausible motions from video models into physically feasible and executable robot end-effector trajectories.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The empirical results demonstrate that policies trained on V-Dreamer's synthetic data exhibit robust zero-shot generalization to geometrically unseen objects in simulation. Furthermore, the framework enables effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with high data efficiency (using a single synthesized demonstration). This suggests that the generated data is not merely visually coherent but also physically grounded and directly applicable for real-world robot control, mitigating the reality gap.
- **Foundation for Generalist Robot Learning:** By providing a scalable, diverse, and physically grounded data engine, V-Dreamer has the potential to accelerate research and development in generalist robot learning. It can facilitate the training of more robust and adaptive robotic policies capable of handling a wider range of objects and tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work also outlines clear limitations and future research avenues, such as developing automated physics-aware trajectory filtering, extending the framework to articulated and deformable objects, and incorporating tighter physical feedback during the generation and grounding processes. These directions suggest a pathway for further improving the physical realism and sim-to-real robustness of generative data synthesis for robotics.

In summary, V-Dreamer contributes a comprehensive, automated framework that bridges the gap between high-level language instructions, generative visual models, and low-level robot control, offering a scalable solution to the data bottleneck that hinders the development of generalist robotic manipulation systems.
