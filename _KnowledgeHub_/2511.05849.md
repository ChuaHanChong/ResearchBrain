# EGG-SR: Embedding Symbolic Equivalence into Symbolic Regression via Equality Graph

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Texas at El Paso and Purdue University developed Egg-SR, a unified framework that embeds symbolic equivalence into modern symbolic regression algorithms via equality graphs. This approach improved accuracy for Monte Carlo Tree Search, Deep Reinforcement Learning, and Large Language Model-based methods, achieving, for instance, <1E-6 NMSE with Egg-MCTS on trigonometric datasets while maintaining stable training and minimal computational overhead.

## Problem

- Symbolic regression (SR) is an NP-hard problem, leading to an exponential growth in the search space for mathematical expressions.
- Current SR algorithms redundantly explore functionally equivalent but syntactically distinct expressions, wasting computational resources.
- A unified and scalable method to integrate symbolic equivalence (e.g., using equality graphs) into diverse modern learning-based SR frameworks was lacking.

## Method

- Egg-SR introduces a module that extends ==equality graphs== (e-graphs) to compactly represent symbolically equivalent expressions for grammar-based symbolic regression.
- For Monte Carlo Tree Search (Egg-MCTS), symbolic equivalence is embedded via e-graph-based backpropagation to simultaneously update statistics for equivalent paths, preventing redundant exploration.
- For Deep Reinforcement Learning (Egg-DRL), the framework uses an e-graph-based policy gradient estimator that aggregates probabilities and rewards across equivalent generated sequences to stabilize training.
- For Large Language Models (Egg-LLM), the framework enriches feedback prompts with multiple equivalent expressions extracted from e-graphs, guiding the LLM toward more informed predictions.

## Results

- Egg-MCTS consistently discovered expressions with lower median Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) (e.g., <1E-6 on trigonometric datasets) and maintained a broader and deeper search tree compared to standard MCTS.
- Egg-DRL achieved smaller NMSE values for discovered expressions and exhibited a reduction in the variance of the estimated objective, leading to more stable policy updates.
- Integrating Egg into LLM-SR (GPT-**3.5**, Mistral) enabled the discovery of higher-quality expressions with lower NMSE values on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution scientific benchmarks.
- The e-graph representation demonstrated substantially less memory consumption for equivalent expressions and contributed minimal runtime overhead to the overall DRL pipeline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equality graphs efficiently encode symbolic equivalence, allowing for compact representation of functionally identical expressions by sharing common sub-expressions, leading to significant memory savings.
> - Integrating equivalence into MCTS results in a tighter regret bound by effectively reducing the branching factor of the search tree, focusing computational effort on genuinely distinct paths.
> - Aggregating rewards for equivalent expressions in DRL provides an unbiased gradient estimator with lower variance, contributing to more stable and efficient policy updates.
> - The Egg module introduces negligible computational overhead to the overall symbolic regression pipeline, making its integration practical and scalable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Symbolic regression (SR) is a field concerned with uncovering underlying mathematical expressions or physical laws from experimental data. It holds significance in AI-driven scientific discovery, with applications spanning various scientific domains. Contemporary SR methods often frame the search for optimal expressions as a sequential decision-making process, involving the prediction of grammar rules, traversal sequences of expression trees, or executable strings. However, a fundamental challenge in SR is its NP-hard nature, leading to an exponential growth in the search space as the complexity of expressions increases.

A particular aspect contributing to this computational challenge is symbolic equivalence. Many expressions, while syntactically distinct, represent the same mathematical function (e.g., `log(x_1^2 x_2^3)`, `log(x_1^2) + log(x_2^3)`, and `2 log(x_1) + 3 log(x_2)`). Existing SR algorithms typically treat these equivalent variants as unique, leading to redundant exploration of the search space and inefficient learning. While the concept of symbolic equivalence is central to areas like program synthesis and mathematical reasoning, and equality graphs (e-graphs) have been introduced to compactly represent sets of equivalent variants by sharing common sub-expressions, their integration into modern learning-based symbolic regression frameworks remains an underexplored direction. E-graphs have found applications in program optimization, dataset generation for equivalent expressions, and aspects of genetic programming (like duplicate detection and simplification). However, a unified framework enabling diverse modern symbolic regression algorithms to leverage e-graphs for accelerated learning has not been extensively developed. This paper addresses this gap by proposing such a framework to systematically integrate symbolic equivalence into learning algorithms for symbolic regression.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Egg-SR, a unified framework that embeds symbolic equivalence into a class of modern symbolic regression methods, thereby accelerating the discovery of accurate expressions.

The core motivations for this work stem from several challenges inherent in symbolic regression:

1. **Computational Complexity due to Search Space Size:** Symbolic regression is an NP-hard problem, and the search space of candidate expressions grows exponentially with data dimensions and expression length. This makes the task computationally challenging for existing algorithms, which often spend significant resources exploring functionally identical but syntactically different expressions.
2. **Redundant Exploration:** Current SR algorithms typically treat symbolically equivalent expressions as distinct outputs. This leads to redundant computational effort, as the algorithms re-evaluate, re-explore, and re-learn properties for expressions that ultimately represent the same underlying function. For example, if `log(x_1^2 x_2^3)` is explored and its fitness calculated, an algorithm might later independently explore `2 log(x_1) + 3 log(x_2)` and duplicate the effort, rather than recognizing its equivalence and leveraging prior knowledge. This redundancy slows down the learning process.
3. **Scalability of Equivalence Representation:** Explicitly maintaining and comparing all possible equivalent variants of symbolic expressions is combinatorially expensive and not scalable in terms of time and memory, as the number of variants can grow exponentially with expression length. A compact and efficient representation of equivalence is needed to overcome this.
4. **Lack of Unified Integration:** While equality graphs (e-graphs) offer a compact way to encode equivalent expressions by sharing sub-expressions, a unified framework that allows diverse modern SR algorithms (such as Monte Carlo Tree Search, Deep Reinforcement Learning, and Large Language Models) to effectively interact with and leverage e-graphs for accelerating *learning* has been lacking. Existing applications of e-graphs in SR have been somewhat disparate or focused on specific aspects like simplification.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the Egg-SR framework are:

- **For Monte Carlo Tree Search (Egg-MCTS):** To prune redundant exploration of equivalent subtrees within the search space, thereby focusing computational effort on genuinely distinct paths.
- **For Deep Reinforcement Learning (Egg-DRL):** To aggregate rewards across equivalent generated sequences, which aims to stabilize training and improve the efficiency of policy updates by reducing the variance of the gradient estimator.
- **For Large Language Models (Egg-LLM):** To enrich the feedback prompts provided to LLMs with multiple equivalent expressions, guiding the model to make more informed predictions in subsequent iterations.

Furthermore, the authors aim to provide theoretical justifications for the benefits of integrating symbolic equivalence: demonstrating a tighter regret bound for Egg-MCTS compared to standard MCTS and a reduction in the variance of the gradient estimator for Egg-DRL compared to conventional DRL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Egg-SR framework is built upon the concept of equality graphs (e-graphs) and integrates this data structure into three distinct modern symbolic regression paradigms: Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), and Large Language Models (LLMs).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The paper first defines a symbolic expression as a connection of input variables and coefficients via mathematical operators, represented using a context-free grammar. This grammar defines non-terminal symbols, terminal symbols (variables, coefficients), production rules (mathematical operations), and a start symbol.
Symbolic equivalence is defined under a rewrite system (R), a set of rewrite rules (LHS { RHS) that transform one expression into another. Two expressions are equivalent if one can be transformed into the other via a finite sequence of rewritings using R. The set of rewrite rules used in this work includes common mathematical identities (e.g., commutative laws, distributive laws, exponential/logarithmic identities, and various trigonometric identities).
The overall goal of Symbolic Regression is to find an optimal expression `phi*` that minimizes a loss function (e.g., Normalized Mean Squared Error, NMSE) between the expression's predictions and the observed data, with coefficients typically optimized numerically.

#### 3.2. Egg: Equality Graph for Grammar-based Symbolic Expression

The core component of the framework is the "Egg" module, which extends e-graphs to support grammar-based symbolic expressions for unified integration.
- **Definition:** An e-graph consists of e-classes, each containing a set of e-nodes that represent symbolically equivalent sub-expressions. E-nodes encode mathematical operations and reference child e-classes for their operands.
- **Construction:** An e-graph is initialized with a sequence of production rules representing an input expression. Rewrite rules are applied iteratively through "equality saturation." This involves matching the left-hand side (LHS) of a rule against sub-expressions in the e-graph, creating new e-classes and e-nodes corresponding to the right-hand side (RHS), and then merging these new e-classes with existing matched ones. This process compactly encodes multiple equivalent expressions by sharing common sub-expressions.
- **Extraction:** After saturation, the e-graph can yield representative expressions. The paper mentions two strategies: cost-based extraction (minimizing a user-defined cost function to find simplified expressions) and random-walk sampling (stochastically traversing the e-graph to generate a batch of equivalent expressions). For learning, the latter is primarily used to generate a batch of equivalent variants.
- **Interaction with SR algorithms:** The Egg module generates a subset of equivalent variants for a given expression, which are then used by the SR algorithms to inform their learning process.

#### 3.3. Embedding Symbolic Equivalence into Symbolic Regression via Egg

- **Egg-MCTS (Monte Carlo Tree Search):**
 - **Standard MCTS:** Operates by iteratively performing Selection (using UCT scores to traverse the tree), Expansion (generating child nodes from a leaf), Simulation (performing rollouts to estimate rewards), and Backpropagation (updating rewards and visit counts along the traversed path).
 - **Egg-based Backpropagation:** This is the key modification. When MCTS explores a path, the partial (or complete) expression associated with that path is used to construct an e-graph. This e-graph is saturated with rewrite rules, and then several distinct equivalent sequences (paths) are sampled. If these equivalent paths exist in the MCTS search tree, their visit counts and reward estimates are updated *simultaneously* with the originally explored path. This mechanism, analogous to transposition tables, prevents redundant exploration of equivalent subtrees.
 - **Theoretical Justification:** The paper states that Egg-MCTS offers a tighter regret bound compared to standard MCTS, as it effectively reduces the branching factor of the search tree.

- **Egg-DRL (Deep Reinforcement Learning):**
 - **Standard DRL:** Employs a neural sequential decoder to generate expressions by sampling production rules. The model learns a policy `p_theta(tau)` to maximize the expected reward (e.g., `1/(1+NMSE(phi))`) of generated expressions. Training uses a policy gradient estimator `g(theta)`.
 - **Egg-based Policy Gradient Estimator:** For each sequence `tau_i` sampled by the decoder, an e-graph is constructed to represent `tau_i` and its equivalents. From this e-graph, `K-1` additional equivalent sequences are sampled. The revised policy-gradient estimator `g_egg(theta)` then aggregates the probabilities of all `K` equivalent sequences (including the original `tau_i`) that share the same reward.
 - **Theoretical Justification:** The paper posits that Egg-DRL yields an unbiased gradient estimator with strictly lower variance than the standard DRL estimator, leading to more stable and efficient policy updates.

- **Egg-LLM (Large Language Models):**
 - **Standard LLM-SR:** Involves three steps: Hypothesis Generation (LLM generates candidate expressions based on a prompt), Data-Driven Evaluation (candidates are evaluated for fitness), and Experience Management (LLM receives feedback on previous predictions to refine future generations).
 - **Egg-based Feedback Prompt:** The Python functions generated by the LLM are parsed into symbolic expressions and then transformed into e-graphs. Equivalent expressions are extracted from these e-graphs and summarized into enriched feedback messages. This richer feedback, incorporating functionally equivalent expressions, is then included in the prompt for subsequent LLM iterations, aiming to improve the quality and accuracy of predictions.

#### 3.4. Connection to Existing Methods

The authors acknowledge other SR approaches (e.g., layer-wise symbolic networks, Transformer-based end-to-end models) and discuss how Egg might be integrated or how their work differs (e.g., focusing on grammar-based expressions versus network representations). They also note that their work is orthogonal to other knowledge-guided discovery methods like those incorporating physical unit constraints or user hypotheses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate Egg-SR's performance across various benchmarks and case studies, demonstrating its advantages over existing approaches.

#### 4.1. Overall Benchmarks

- **Impact of Egg on MCTS:**
 - **Accuracy:** Egg-MCTS consistently discovered expressions with lower median Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) compared to standard MCTS across trigonometric datasets (Table 1), in both noiseless and noisy settings. For instance, on a (2,1,1) dataset, Egg-MCTS achieved <1E-6 NMSE (noiseless) compared to MCTS's 0.006.
 - **Search Space Exploration:** Egg-MCTS maintained a broader and deeper search tree over learning iterations (Figure 3, Left). This indicates that by identifying and sharing statistics among equivalent paths, Egg-MCTS more effectively explores a larger and more diverse search space, leading to improved accuracy. The improvement is attributed to the effectiveness of rewrite rules in covering trigonometric identities.

- **Impact of Egg on DRL:**
 - **Accuracy:** Expressions discovered by Egg-DRL achieved smaller NMSE values than standard DRL across all tested trigonometric datasets, under both noiseless and noisy conditions (Table 1).
 - **Training Stability:** Egg-DRL exhibited a reduction in the variance of the estimated objective over training iterations (Figure 3, Right). This is attributed to the aggregation of information from symbolic variants generated via the e-graph, resulting in more stable gradients and policy updates.

- **Impact of Egg on LLM:**
 - **Accuracy:** Integrating Egg into LLM-SR (using GPT-3.5 and Mistral models) enabled the LLMs to discover higher-quality expressions, measured by lower NMSE values on both In-Distribution (IID) and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) test sets across scientific benchmark problems (Oscillation I, Oscillation II, Bacterial growth, Stress-Strain) (Table 2). The enriched feedback prompts incorporating equivalent expressions generated by Egg are credited for this improvement.

#### 4.2. Case Analysis

- **Space Efficiency of Egg:**
 - The e-graph representation demonstrated substantially less memory consumption compared to a traditional array-based approach for representing equivalent variants of expressions like `log(x_1 * ... * x_n)` and `sin(x_1 + ... + x_n)` (Figure 4). While the array-based method showed exponential memory growth with the number of variables `n`, the e-graph effectively compacts representation by sharing common sub-expressions, highlighting its memory efficiency.

- **Time Efficiency of Egg with DRL:**
 - Benchmarking the runtime of the Egg module within the Egg-DRL pipeline (Figure 5) revealed that Egg contributes minimal computational overhead. Its runtime is negligible compared to more computationally intensive steps like fitting coefficients in symbolic expressions or updating neural network parameters (for both LSTM and Transformer decoders). This indicates the practical feasibility of incorporating Egg without significantly slowing down the overall learning process.

- **Additional Visualizations of E-graph Construction:**
 - The paper provides visualizations of e-graph constructions for complex expressions from the Feynman dataset, illustrating how the Egg module can simplify and transform scientific expressions using various rewrite rules. This further supports the module's effectiveness in practical settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Egg-SR, a unified framework that fundamentally integrates symbolic equivalence into modern symbolic regression (SR) algorithms. This work holds several significant contributions and potential impacts.

### 6. Significance

- **Addressing a Core Challenge:** The work directly addresses the fundamental computational challenge in SR posed by the exponential growth of the search space, specifically highlighting the redundancy caused by functionally equivalent but syntactically distinct expressions.
- **Unified Framework:** By integrating the equality graph (e-graph) into three diverse and prominent SR paradigms—Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), and Large Language Models (LLMs)—Egg-SR provides a versatile and unified solution, rather than ad-hoc modifications for individual algorithms.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** The theoretical justifications, demonstrating a tighter regret bound for Egg-MCTS and a lower variance gradient estimator for Egg-DRL, provide a formal basis for the framework's improved efficiency and stability. These guarantees suggest that Egg-SR can converge faster and more reliably.
- **Empirical Validation:** Consistent empirical improvements across all tested SR methods (MCTS, DRL, LLM) and various benchmarks underscore the practical effectiveness of embedding symbolic equivalence via Egg. The ability to discover more accurate expressions within the same time budget is a key achievement.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The case studies demonstrate that the e-graph representation offers significant space efficiency by compactly encoding equivalent expressions, and the Egg module introduces negligible time overhead into the learning pipeline, making it a practical and scalable solution.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Scientific Discovery:** By enabling SR algorithms to more efficiently explore the space of physical laws, Egg-SR could accelerate the discovery of governing equations from experimental data across various scientific and engineering disciplines. This can lead to new insights and better predictive models in fields like physics, chemistry, and biology.
- **Advancement of AI for Science:** The framework contributes a critical computational tool to the growing field of AI-driven scientific discovery, enhancing the capabilities of automated systems to perform complex scientific reasoning tasks.
- **Improved SR Algorithms:** The modular "Egg" component can serve as a foundation for improving existing and developing new SR algorithms. Its principles of equivalence-aware learning could be extended to other SR methodologies, such as genetic programming, or integrated with other knowledge-guided learning approaches (e.g., incorporating physical unit constraints).
- **Reduced Computational Resources:** More efficient search and learning processes mean that complex SR tasks might be achievable with fewer computational resources or in shorter timeframes, making advanced scientific discovery more accessible.
- **Richer Model Understanding:** By systematically exploring and presenting equivalent expressions, Egg-SR could offer scientists a richer understanding of the underlying phenomena, providing alternative formulations that might be more intuitive or reveal different aspects of a physical law.

In conclusion, Egg-SR represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and efficient symbolic regression systems, bridging the gap between compact symbolic representations and modern learning-based search strategies with both theoretical and empirical backing.
