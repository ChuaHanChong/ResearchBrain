# Geometry-aware 4D Video Generation for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A 4D video generation model is introduced that predicts future RGB frames and 3D pointmaps, achieving both temporal coherence and cross-view geometric consistency for robot manipulation. This system enables the extraction of accurate 6DoF robot end-effector trajectories, leading to an average task success rate of 0.64 across simulation tasks, surpassing prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models struggle to simultaneously maintain temporal coherence and 3D geometric consistency, particularly in dynamic, multi-object robot manipulation scenarios.
- Traditional pixel-based models often lack 3D structure understanding, leading to visual artifacts, while current 3D-aware models are limited to simpler, static scenes.
- Robotic applications require spatio-temporally aligned predictions that generalize to novel viewpoints and facilitate precise 6DoF robot pose extraction for effective manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A 4D video generation model extends the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) framework to predict future multi-view RGB frames and corresponding 3D pointmaps.
- Geometry-consistent supervision is integrated by training a dedicated ==VAE== for pointmaps and applying a cross-view pointmap diffusion loss, ensuring one view's pointmaps are predicted in the reference view's coordinate frame.
- Multi-view ==cross-attention== is incorporated into the ==U-Net== ==diffusion model=='s decoders, enabling information transfer between views to enhance 3D alignment and accuracy during pointmap prediction without explicit camera pose inputs at inference.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved superior 4D video generation quality, demonstrating lower FVD for RGB temporal coherence, better depth prediction (lower AbsRel, higher 21), and stronger cross-view 3D consistency (higher mIoU) across simulation and real-world tasks.
- The model yielded a significantly higher average robot task success rate of 0.64 across three simulation tasks, substantially outperforming baselines such as Dreamitate (0.12) and Diffusion Policy (0.12).
- The framework effectively generalizes to novel viewpoints and can accommodate additional input views at inference time without retraining, maintaining good visual quality and cross-view consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly enforcing 3D geometric consistency through cross-view pointmap supervision is crucial for generating spatially aligned and realistic 4D videos essential for robot interaction.
> - The multi-view cross-attention mechanism within the diffusion model is vital for learning accurate 3D correspondences and enabling robust pointmap projection between different camera viewpoints.
> - Generating geometry-aware multi-view RGB-D videos allows for direct and accurate 6DoF robot end-effector pose extraction using off-the-shelf trackers, improving the reliability and performance of robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in the field of video generation: simultaneously achieving temporal coherence and 3D geometric consistency in synthesized videos, particularly within the context of robot manipulation.

Traditional pixel-based video generation models, often relying on recurrent networks or generative adversarial networks, have seen significant advancements with the integration of diffusion models. These models, such as those extended with temporal layers or latent diffusion techniques, frequently excel at capturing short-term motion and visual fidelity in RGB videos. However, they often lack an explicit understanding of 3D scene structure, leading to artifacts like flickering, object deformation, or disappearance when viewed from different perspectives. This limitation makes them less suitable for applications requiring precise spatial reasoning, such as robotics.

Conversely, 3D-aware approaches in video generation prioritize geometric constraints but are typically restricted to simpler, static backgrounds or single-object scenarios. These methods, while enforcing spatial consistency, often struggle to scale to the complexity of multi-object, dynamic scenes common in robot manipulation. Prior work in 4D (spatial-temporal) video generation has sometimes addressed temporal and spatial consistency separately, for instance, by combining a video model with a novel view synthesis model, or by focusing on single-object videos.

In the broader context of generative models for robot planning, there is a growing interest in using video generation to simulate future outcomes, enable action planning, and facilitate adaptation to new scenarios. However, extracting accurate and robust robot policies from generated videos remains difficult if the videos lack physical plausibility, smooth motion, or precise 3D geometry. Existing methods for extracting robot actions from videos often rely on learned inverse dynamics models, behavior cloning, or RGB-based pose tracking. These approaches benefit significantly from high-fidelity, geometrically consistent visual predictions.

This research aims to bridge the gap between temporally coherent pixel-based models and geometrically consistent 3D-aware models. It proposes a framework that unifies strong temporal modeling with robust 3D geometric consistency, specifically targeting multi-object, dynamic robot manipulation scenes. By generating 4D videos (RGB-D sequences) that are both temporally coherent and spatially consistent across multiple camera views, the work provides a foundation for more reliable and generalizable robot manipulation policies, particularly in scenarios involving novel viewpoints or complex interactions. The ability to implicitly learn cross-view scene representations without requiring camera poses at inference time is a notable contribution to making these models practical for real-world robotic deployment where precise camera calibration for novel views can be challenging.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a 4D video generation model capable of predicting future RGB-D video sequences that are simultaneously temporally coherent and geometrically consistent across multiple camera viewpoints. This capability is motivated by the need to enhance robot planning and interaction in complex physical environments.

**Detailed Objectives and Motivation:**

1. **Improve Robot Dynamics Understanding:** Robots require a robust understanding and predictive model of the physical world's dynamics to effectively plan and interact. Anticipating how environments change due to object motions or occlusions over extended time horizons is critical for complex manipulation tasks. Current video generation models offer a paradigm for learning such dynamic visual models by forecasting future observations.

2. **Address Limitations in Existing Video Generation:** A significant challenge in current video generation is producing videos that are both realistic and physically plausible enough for precise robot policies.
 - **Temporal Coherence:** Ensuring smooth, causally consistent motion over time is crucial. Pixel-based models often perform well in this aspect but may generate artifacts that lack 3D structural understanding.
 - **3D Consistency:** Preserving object geometry and spatial correspondences across different viewpoints is equally important. Many models either capture one at the expense of the other, or 3D-aware models are limited to simple scenes, failing to scale to realistic multi-object manipulation scenarios. The lack of 3D consistency can lead to flickering, deformation, or object disappearance, which are detrimental to robot perception and planning.

3. **Enable Robust Generalization to Novel Viewpoints:** In robotic applications, even minor shifts in camera viewpoints can cause visuomotor policies to operate out of distribution and lead to failures. A model that can generate spatio-temporally aligned future video sequences from novel viewpoints, given initial observations, is highly valuable. This eliminates the need for recalibration or explicit camera pose inputs during inference, improving adaptability.

4. **Facilitate Accurate Robot End-Effector Trajectory Extraction:** The ultimate goal for robotic applications is to extract precise robot actions. If generated videos are geometrically consistent and provide depth information, they can be directly used by off-the-shelf 6DoF pose trackers to recover robot end-effector trajectories. This allows for the synthesis of robot manipulation policies that generalize across different camera perspectives.

5. **Develop a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** To rigorously assess the performance of 4D video generation models in robotic contexts, there is a need for benchmarks that include diverse camera viewpoints in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks. This enables a thorough evaluation of generation quality, 3D consistency, and generalization capabilities.

In summary, the motivation stems from the limitations of current video generation models for robotics, specifically their inability to simultaneously achieve robust temporal and 3D geometric consistency, and the practical challenges of deploying robots in varied camera setups. The research aims to overcome these limitations by introducing geometry-aware supervision and cross-view architectural components within a video diffusion framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a 4D video generation framework that integrates temporal coherence with 3D geometric consistency, enabling spatio-temporally aligned predictions across time and varying viewpoints. The approach builds upon a diffusion-based video generation backbone and enhances it with novel geometry-consistent supervision.

**Problem Statement Refinement:**
The model takes initial RGB-D observations from two camera views, $v_n$ and $v_m$, at time $t$. From these, it predicts future RGB frames and 3D pointmaps, {O$_{t+1}$..., O$_{t+h}$} and {X$_{t+1}$..., X$_{t+h}$} respectively, for both views. The key distinction is that pointmaps from view $v_m$ are predicted in the coordinate frame of $v_n$, allowing for direct comparison and consistency enforcement.

#### 3.1. Diffusion-Based Video Generation Backbone

The framework adopts the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) architecture, known for its performance in generating temporally coherent video sequences.
- **RGB Video Processing:** Historical RGB frames are first encoded into a latent space using a pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encoder. A U-Net-based diffusion model then operates on these latents to predict future latent representations, which are subsequently decoded back into RGB frames using the VAE decoder. The training objective for RGB generation follows a standard denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) loss, aiming to predict the original clean latent data from a noisy input at each diffusion step.
- **Pointmap Processing:** In parallel, pointmaps are processed. A Pointmap VAE, initialized from the pretrained RGB VAE and fine-tuned on pointmap data, encodes historical pointmaps into a latent space. The diffusion model then forecasts future pointmaps in this latent space, which are decoded by the Pointmap VAE decoder.

#### 3.2. Geometry-Consistent Supervision

To enforce 3D consistency across views, the method integrates a cross-view pointmap supervision strategy adapted from DUSt3R.
- **Cross-View Pointmap Prediction:** The model predicts a sequence of future pointmaps for the reference view ($v_n$) in its native coordinate frame. Simultaneously, it predicts future pointmaps from the second camera view ($v_m$), but expresses them in the coordinate frame of $v_n$. This results in a sequence of projected pointmaps {X$_{m \to n_{t+1}}$..., X$_{m \to n_{t+h}}$}.
- **3D Diffusion Loss:** During training, a 3D diffusion loss is applied at each future time step to both the native view $v_n$ pointmap latents and the projected view $v_m \to v_n$ pointmap latents. This loss encourages alignment between the predicted pointmaps from different views when transformed into a common coordinate system.
- **Inference Advantage:** While camera poses are required during training to facilitate the projection between views, a significant advantage at inference time is that the model can directly predict pointmaps from a novel view $v_m$ in the coordinate frame of $v_n$ without needing camera poses as input. This bypasses extrinsics calibration challenges for novel camera setups.

#### 3.3. Multi-View Cross-Attention for 3D Consistency

A crucial architectural component is the introduction of multi-view cross-attention.
- **Asymmetric Decoders:** Unlike RGB video prediction where each view might be processed independently, pointmap prediction requires explicit cross-view alignment. To achieve this, the U-Net diffusion model employs two separate decoders (with independent weights): one for $v_n$ and one for $v_m$.
- **Information Transfer:** Cross-attention layers are inserted between these decoders. Specifically, intermediate features from the decoder of view $v_n$ (the reference view) are passed as key and value to the corresponding cross-attention stage in the decoder of view $v_m$. The query originates from $v_m$'s decoder features. This allows $v_m$'s decoder to attend to and incorporate geometric cues from $v_n$, promoting accurate pointmap prediction in the reference coordinate frame and enhancing cross-view geometric consistency, especially under viewpoint variations.

#### 3.4. Joint Temporal and 3D Consistency Optimization

The overall training objective combines the strengths of both RGB-based temporal modeling and pointmap-based 3D consistency.
- **Combined Loss Function:** The full loss is the sum of diffusion losses for RGB video prediction (for both $v_n$ and $v_m$) and the 3D diffusion loss for pointmap prediction (for $v_n$ and $v_m \to v_n$), weighted by a hyperparameter $\lambda$. This joint optimization encourages both high temporal coherence and strong cross-view 3D consistency.
- **Gripper-Region Re-weighting:** To improve prediction quality in areas critical for robot manipulation, a re-weighting mechanism is applied to the diffusion loss. Binary masks of the robot gripper region are downsampled to match the latent space resolution, and loss contributions in these regions are effectively doubled, encouraging higher accuracy for gripper pose estimation.

#### 3.5. Robot Pose Estimation from 4D Videos

The generated 4D videos (RGB-D sequences) are leveraged for downstream robotic tasks.
- **Off-the-Shelf Pose Tracker:** An off-the-shelf 6DoF pose tracking model, FoundationPose, is used. It takes predicted RGB-D frames from a single view, a binary mask of the target object (e.g., robot gripper), camera intrinsics, and the object's CAD model as input.
- **Trajectory Extraction:** The tracker outputs estimated 6DoF poses ($T_t \in SE(3)$) for the robot end-effector at each time step. Pose estimation is performed independently on both views, and the result with the highest confidence score is selected.
- **Gripper State Inference:** Gripper openness is inferred by segmenting the left and right gripper fingers, projecting their pixels to 3D space using the predicted RGB-D, and measuring the distance between their centroids.
- **Open-Loop Execution:** The recovered trajectories are used to control the robot in an open-loop manner, with subsequent inference performed using updated RGB-D observations after each action.

#### 3.6. Datasets and Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Data:** Experiments are conducted on three table-top manipulation tasks within the LBM physics-based simulator. The dataset includes 25 demonstrations per task, each recorded from 16 diverse camera poses (12 for training, 4 for testing), totaling 400 videos per task. Cameras are sampled from the upper hemisphere around the workstation.
- **Real-World Data:** A multi-task real-world dataset comprising four manipulation tasks, each with 20 robot teleoperation demonstrations, is used. Synchronized RGB-D sequences are captured from two FRAMOS D415e cameras.
- **Training and Inference:** The model is trained for approximately 60 epochs on 4 NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs. Inference for 10 future frames takes approximately 30 seconds on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results evaluating the proposed 4D video generation model across various metrics and tasks in both simulated and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. 4D Video Generation Quality

Evaluation was performed across three key aspects: RGB video generation quality (Fréchet Video Distance - FVD), depth generation quality (absolute relative error - AbsRel, and threshold accuracy - $\delta_1$), and cross-view 3D point consistency (mean Intersection-over-Union - mIoU on object masks).

- **Overall Superiority:** The proposed method (OURS) consistently demonstrated superior performance across all metrics and tasks in both simulated and real-world datasets (Table 1). It achieved lower FVD scores, indicating higher temporal coherence and visual fidelity for RGB videos. For depth generation, it exhibited lower AbsRel and higher $\delta_1$, signifying more accurate depth predictions. Crucially, OURS achieved the highest mIoU scores, demonstrating strong cross-view 3D consistency.
- **Importance of Multi-View Cross-Attention:** The ablation study with `OURS w/o MV attn` revealed a significant degradation in 3D consistency (lower mIoU) and depth prediction quality, particularly for the projected view ($v_m$). This indicates that the multi-view cross-attention mechanism is essential for the model to learn 3D geometric correspondences and accurately transform pointmaps into the reference view's coordinate frame. Qualitative results (Figure 3) showed `OURS w/o MV attn` exhibiting inconsistent gripper poses across views.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - `4D Gaussian` performed notably worse across all video, depth, and consistency metrics. It produced blurry RGB frames and inaccurate depth predictions from unseen viewpoints, indicating limitations in novel view synthesis from a single generated video.
 - `SVD` and `SVD w/ MV attn` (which do not predict depth or have geometric supervision) naturally lacked 3D consistency. While `SVD w/ MV attn` attempted information transfer via cross-attention on RGB features, it still resulted in noticeable gripper mismatches and generally worse RGB generation quality compared to OURS, highlighting that RGB features alone are insufficient for robust 3D consistency.
- **Real-World Performance:** After fine-tuning on a multi-task real-world dataset, the proposed model successfully generated high-fidelity multi-view RGB-D sequences that accurately captured visual appearance and depth over time, outperforming baselines in real-world scenarios (Table 1, Figure 4, Figure 10).

#### 4.2. Robot Policy Results

The utility of the generated 4D videos was evaluated by extracting robot end-effector trajectories for manipulation tasks and assessing the task success rate on unseen camera views.

- **Higher Task Success Rate:** The proposed method achieved a significantly higher average task success rate (0.64) across three simulated manipulation tasks compared to all baselines (Table 2).
- **Baseline Performance Analysis:**
 - `Dreamitate` (an SVD-based method using RGB only for pose tracking) exhibited poor performance (average 0.12). The lack of depth information and geometric consistency supervision in its generated videos led to inaccurate pose estimations and high failure rates in downstream robot policies.
 - `Diffusion Policy (DP)` (an RGB-conditioned behavior cloning method) struggled to generalize to unseen viewpoints (average 0.12), suggesting that conditioning on multi-view RGB features alone is insufficient for learning view-invariant actions without explicit geometric modeling.
 - `DP3` (an RGB-D behavior cloning method using global 3D point clouds) showed marginal improvement on one task (`StoreCerealBoxUnderShelf`) but not on others (average 0.25). This indicates that while 3D information is beneficial, end-to-end learning from point clouds might not fully capture the precision needed for manipulating small objects, and it still struggled with grasping failures.
- **Conclusion on Policy:** The findings indicate that direct gripper pose tracking on the geometry-consistent RGB-D videos generated by the proposed method leads to higher action accuracy and improved grasping success, substantiating the value of geometry-aware 4D video generation for robotic control.

#### 4.3. Pose Tracking Error Analysis (Appendix A.6)

An analysis of FoundationPose applied to ground-truth RGB-D images showed generally low MSE errors (around or below 0.001) for gripper pose tracking. This suggests that the pose tracker itself is highly accurate given good quality input, implying that the failures observed in baseline robot policies are primarily attributable to the quality and consistency of their generated video data rather than the pose tracking component.

#### 4.4. Scaling to More Input Views (Appendix A.7)

The framework was evaluated for its ability to incorporate more input views at inference time without retraining. For the `StoreCerealBoxUnderShelf` task, incorporating a third predicted view resulted in a slight degradation in performance but still maintained good visual quality and cross-view consistency (Table 4). This indicates that the model can extend to more views by performing additional forward passes, albeit with a linear increase in inference time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of video generation for robotics, addressing the critical need for spatio-temporally consistent and geometrically accurate predictions of dynamic scenes. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Enhanced Robot Manipulation Capabilities:** The ability to generate 4D videos that are both temporally coherent and geometrically consistent across camera views directly enhances a robot's capacity for planning and interaction. By providing high-fidelity RGB-D sequences, the model enables more accurate 6DoF pose estimation of robot end-effectors using off-the-shelf trackers. This precision is crucial for complex manipulation tasks, leading to higher success rates in challenging scenarios.

2. **Robustness to Novel Viewpoints:** A key practical advantage is the model's capacity to generate future RGB-D videos from novel viewpoints without requiring camera poses as input during inference. This capability significantly simplifies the deployment of robots in varied environments, removing the need for arduous extrinsic camera calibration for every new camera setup. This generalization potential is critical for practical robotic applications.

3. **Advancements in Dynamic Scene Understanding:** The introduction of geometry-consistent supervision via cross-view pointmap alignment forces the model to learn a shared 3D scene representation. This implies a deeper understanding of the underlying physical world dynamics and object geometry, moving beyond purely pixel-level predictions. Such an understanding is fundamental for developing more intelligent and adaptive robotic systems.

4. **Foundational Framework for Future Research:** The proposed framework unifies temporal modeling with 3D consistency, offering a robust approach to 4D video generation. This work provides a foundation upon which future research can build, especially in areas requiring precise visual dynamics for agents interacting with the physical world. The benchmark introduced (simulated and real-world with diverse viewpoints) further facilitates and standardifies evaluation in this domain.

5. **Addressing Data Challenges in Robotics:** While the method requires RGB-D video datasets with varying camera viewpoints for training, the demonstrated performance highlights the value of such data. Future work could leverage advancements in depth estimation from RGB images to ease real-world data collection, making this powerful approach more accessible.

6. **Potential for More Reactive Policies:** Although the current inference speed of the video generation model is relatively slow for real-time closed-loop planning, the architecture is compatible with faster generative models (e.g., flow matching, autoregressive transformers). Integrating these could lead to more reactive robot policies, expanding the practical applicability of geometry-aware video generation in dynamic robotic control loops. Additionally, hierarchical planning structures could mitigate inference costs by using sparse predicted frames as subgoals.

In summary, this research contributes a novel and effective method for 4D video generation that simultaneously addresses critical limitations of existing models for robotic applications. Its impact lies in enabling more capable, generalizable, and robust robot manipulation by providing robots with a geometrically grounded predictive understanding of their environment.
