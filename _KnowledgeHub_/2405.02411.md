# The Call for Socially Aware Language Technologies

> [!summary] Summary
> A position paper arguing that NLP's persistent failures on bias, trust, and safety stem from a single missing capacity: social awareness, defined as sensitivity to social factors, interactions, and implications of language use. The authors propose social factors/interaction/implication as an organizing framework, argue LLMs do not yet possess this capacity despite surface fluency, and lay out five research directions for building and measuring socially aware language technologies.

## Problem

- Bias, toxicity, trust, and fairness failures in NLP models are usually treated as separate problems rather than symptoms of one shared root cause.
- NLP models prioritize syntax, grammar, and lexicon (langue) while largely ignoring the concrete social context of actual language use (parole).
- LLM benchmarks for theory-of-mind and psychometric traits rest on flawed premises, since token-likelihood generation is not evidence of genuine social reasoning.

## Method

- **Social-factors/interaction/implication framework**: a three-layer taxonomy (who speaks to whom under what norms; the exchanges between humans and systems; the downstream societal impact) that structures what counts as social awareness in NLP.
- ==Four grounding considerations (C1-C4)==: social reasoning must be grounded in socially diverse data, extend to interactive and multimodal settings, adapt during interaction, and remain interpretable.
- ==Five-point research agenda==: operationalizing measurement, grounding evaluation in social-science behavioral expectations, inferring social context for reasoning, deploying in practical applications, and studying societal-level effects.

## Results

- Cites empirical work showing LLMs struggle on quantified social-signal tasks such as relation classification and inappropriate-content detection, evidencing a current capability gap.
- Draws on prior findings that theory-of-mind question-answering in LLMs is ill-posed since model answers stem from word-likelihood generation, not attributed mental states.
- References psychometric-reliability studies showing LLM responses to personality-style questionnaires are internally inconsistent across repeated administrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Surface fluency on language tasks should not be mistaken for social competence; the two capacities can diverge sharply and require separate evaluation.
> - Borrowing psychological or psychometric test instruments to probe LLMs is unreliable unless the underlying premise, that responses reflect complex inner states, actually holds for the model.
> - Progress on social awareness will require new interactive, cross-disciplinary evaluation paradigms, not incremental extensions of static language benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Natural Language Processing (NLP) has undergone significant advancements, particularly with the proliferation of large language models (LLMs) based on Transformer architectures. These models have demonstrated improved performance across various tasks, including machine translation, sentiment analysis, and conversational agents. However, alongside these technological strides, there has been increasing identification of persistent and exacerbated problems, such as algorithmic bias, toxicity generation, issues of trust, and fairness concerns. These challenges indicate that despite advanced linguistic capabilities, current NLP systems often perform inconsistently across diverse users and social contexts.

This paper positions itself within the evolving discourse on responsible AI and human-centered NLP, arguing that many of these identified flaws share a common underlying cause: a fundamental lack of *social awareness*. Historically, NLP development has focused predominantly on abstract linguistic patterns (e.g., syntax, grammar, lexicon), a trajectory the authors liken to Ferdinand de Saussure's concept of "langue" while neglecting the concrete, individual use of language, "parole." The introduction of LLMs, while powerful, is argued to have amplified this gap, as their training primarily emphasizes linguistic correlations rather than the nuanced social dynamics that govern human language use.

The work differentiates itself from related concepts such as personalization (which focuses on individual users), computational sociolinguistics (which uses NLP to analyze social phenomena), Theory of Mind (ToM, which infers mental states), and human-centered NLP (which emphasizes user-centered design). While acknowledging overlap, the paper posits that socially aware language technologies represent a distinct, broader paradigm that integrates understanding of social factors, interactional dynamics, and societal implications directly into model design and evaluation. It advocates for a return to a more holistic view of AI, reminiscent of early AI goals that aimed for human-like behavior, which inherently presumed a degree of social awareness. The paper suggests that, following Moravec’s paradox, where "easy things are hard, and hard things are easy" for AI, social awareness represents one of the "hard" problems that NLP must now tackle after mastering many of the "easy" information-based tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to argue that a significant portion of the challenges facing modern NLP, particularly those exacerbated by LLMs, stems from a deficiency in social awareness. The authors aim to conceptualize and advocate for the integration of social awareness into language technologies.

The motivations for this work are detailed as follows:
- **Addressing Fundamental Flaws:** The paper contends that issues like bias, toxicity, and fairness are not isolated problems but symptoms of a deeper systemic lack of understanding of the social environment in which language operates. By framing these issues under the umbrella of social awareness, the authors seek to provide a unifying perspective for their mitigation.
- **Improving Application Quality:** Integrating social awareness is posited to enhance the naturalness, usefulness, and safety of NLP applications. For instance, systems lacking social awareness may produce stilted dialogues, disregard social or cultural taboos, or provide inappropriate responses, diminishing user experience and utility.
- **Unlocking New Capabilities:** Beyond addressing existing problems, incorporating social awareness is expected to open avenues for novel NLP applications that require nuanced understanding of human social interactions, goals, and contexts.
- **Providing a Comprehensive Framework:** The paper is motivated by the need to offer a structured discussion of perspectives and practices for socially aware language technologies. It is intended to serve as a guide for NLP researchers, practitioners, HCI scholars, and social scientists interested in the societal impacts of language technologies. This involves moving beyond simply recognizing social factors to a deeper engagement with how NLP systems interact within and influence social and physical environments.
- **Encouraging Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The complexity of social awareness necessitates insights from various disciplines, including psychology, sociology, and behavioral economics. The paper aims to motivate cross-disciplinary engagement to build more robust and contextually appropriate language technologies.
- **Promoting Responsible Development:** By highlighting the implications of NLP systems on society, the authors emphasize the necessity of responsible development and deployment, particularly in assessing biases, ensuring global inclusivity, and mitigating risks such as misinformation and job displacement.

In essence, the core motivation is to steer NLP towards a paradigm where technological sophistication is coupled with social consciousness, enabling systems to understand and respond to the intricate social signals and implications inherent in human language use.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a perspective paper, which means it does not present new empirical data or a novel model but rather provides a conceptual framework, synthesizes existing knowledge, and offers an argumentative stance on a critical direction for the field. Its methodology is primarily conceptual and analytical.

The approach taken involves:

1. **Defining Socially Aware Language Technologies:** The authors establish a working definition: "the study and development of language technologies from a social perspective, allowing NLP systems to understand and respond to social signals expressed in language and broader physical and social environments." This definition emphasizes systems exhibiting emotional intelligence, cultural competence, and perspective-taking abilities.

2. **Conceptual Framework (Figure 1):** A central aspect of the methodology is the introduction of a three-layered conceptual structure for social awareness:
 - **Social Factors:** This inner layer encompasses aspects like the speaker, receiver, their social relationship, context, social norms, culture, ideology, and communicative goals. The paper argues that understanding these factors can motivate socially informed tasks, enhance representations, and guide LLM steerability.
 - **Interaction:** This middle layer refers to the social exchanges between humans and NLP systems, governed by relational, organizational, and cultural norms. It draws on social science theories (e.g., social influence, social norms, interactionism) to highlight how language emerges from social exchange and how social interactions reveal dynamics such as power, trust, and user expectations.
 - **Implication:** This outer layer addresses the impact of an NLP system on society, including both positive and negative effects. This involves assessing biases, considering global populations, investigating misinformation, examining job displacement, and aligning with human preferences and societal values.

3. **Differentiation from Related Concepts:** To precisely delineate the scope of "socially aware language technologies," the authors systematically compare and contrast it with adjacent concepts:
 - **Personalization:** Social awareness is broader, encompassing collective social and cultural groups, while personalization focuses on the individual.
 - **NLP in a Social Context / Computational Sociolinguistics:** This field uses NLP to *analyze* language in social settings; socially aware NLP aims to *incorporate* social understanding into model design.
 - **Theory of Mind (ToM):** While related, ToM focuses on inferring mental states, whereas social awareness is a broader construct encompassing understanding and appropriate response to social dynamics.
 - **Human-Centered NLP:** Human-centered NLP emphasizes user-centered design; socially aware NLP adds the dimension of appropriate interaction with the broader social environment beyond individual user needs.
 - **Human-like AI:** Aims to mimic human interactions; social awareness additionally emphasizes *appropriate and considerate* interactions.

4. **Identifying Key Considerations for Building Socially Aware NLP:** The paper outlines four strategic considerations (C1-C4) for future development:
 - **C1: Grounding in Socially Diverse Data:** Models require exposure to diverse persons, values, and relationships to learn effective representations and reason about social information, moving beyond categorical stereotypes to bespoke understanding.
 - **C2: Extension to Interactive Contexts, Diverse Cultures, and Non-Text Modalities:** Social awareness must operate beyond static text, integrating interaction, cultural nuances, and multimodal inputs (e.g., vision, audio).
 - **C3: Adaptability:** Models need to adapt to different contexts and evolve their social understanding during interaction.
 - **C4: Interpretability/Explainability:** Understanding how models utilize social awareness in their reasoning processes is crucial.

5. **Critique of Current LLMs:** The authors argue that current LLMs, despite their linguistic prowess, are not yet socially aware. This is supported by highlighting five areas requiring significant progress:
 - Operationalization and measurement of social awareness beyond traditional benchmarks.
 - Alignment with behavioral expectations derived from social science theories.
 - Improved inference and explicit use of social context in reasoning.
 - Effective deployment of socially aware behaviors in practical applications.
 - A deeper understanding of how these technologies affect people and society.

6. **Historical and Future Outlook:** The paper traces a historical trajectory, noting AI's early holistic goals and subsequent specialization that sidestepped social awareness (linking to Moravec's paradox). It projects a future where NLP re-integrates social awareness, necessitating new tasks, metrics, and interdisciplinary collaboration, drawing parallels with the re-introduction of "extraneous" factors in linguistics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a perspective paper, the "findings" are primarily conceptual arguments and proposed frameworks rather than empirical results. The main conceptual findings and arguments presented are:

1. **Identification of a Core Deficit:** The paper identifies a fundamental lack of *social awareness* as a common underlying cause for many pervasive problems (e.g., bias, toxicity, fairness) in modern NLP, especially amplified by Large Language Models (LLMs). It argues that current models prioritize linguistic form over social context.

2. **Definition and Framework for Social Awareness:** It proposes a structured definition of socially aware language technologies, emphasizing the understanding and response to social signals within broader physical and social environments. This is operationalized through a three-part conceptual structure:
 - **Social Factors:** Encompassing aspects like speaker/receiver identity, relationship, context, norms, culture, and communicative goals.
 - **Interaction:** Focusing on the social exchanges, governed by relational and cultural norms, and revealing dynamics like trust and power.
 - **Implication:** Addressing the societal impact, including biases, global effects, misinformation, and human-AI alignment.

3. **Differentiation and Uniqueness of Social Awareness:** The paper systematically distinguishes socially aware NLP from related fields like personalization, computational sociolinguistics, Theory of Mind, and human-centered NLP, establishing it as a distinct and crucial area of focus that integrates understanding of social dynamics directly into language technology design.

4. **Critique of Current LLMs' Social Capabilities:** Despite their advanced language generation abilities, the paper argues that current LLMs are not inherently socially aware. Their performance on "social" tasks is often superficial, lacking genuine understanding of social factors or the capacity for appropriate social reasoning. This is evidenced by their struggles in operationalizing social awareness, aligning with social science behavioral expectations, inferring social context, deploying socially aware behavior in applications, and understanding broad societal implications.

5. **Strategic Considerations for Development:** Four key considerations are proposed for building socially aware NLP: the necessity of grounding in diverse social data, extending to interactive/multimodal/cultural contexts, ensuring adaptability during interaction, and providing interpretability of social reasoning.

6. **Call for a Paradigm Shift:** The paper concludes that achieving social awareness requires a fundamental re-alignment of NLP research, moving beyond purely linguistic tasks and traditional benchmarks. It advocates for new tasks, metrics, and, critically, interdisciplinary collaboration with social sciences to embed social intelligence into language models.

In summary, the paper's core finding is a strong conceptual argument for recognizing and explicitly integrating social awareness as a distinct and essential dimension into NLP research and development, providing a framework for how this integration might be approached.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper's call for socially aware language technologies carries significant potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **Enhancing the Utility and Safety of NLP Systems:** By integrating social awareness, NLP models could move beyond merely processing language to understanding its context and implications. This could lead to systems that are more polite, culturally sensitive, empathetic, and less prone to generating harmful, biased, or inappropriate content. For instance, conversational agents could engage in more natural and contextually appropriate dialogues, and machine translation systems could better handle cultural nuances and avoid reinforcing stereotypes.

2. **Mitigating Existing Harms and Biases:** A primary motivation for the paper is to address persistent issues like algorithmic bias, toxicity, and fairness. By explicitly modeling social factors, interactions, and implications, developers could create systems that are more aware of the potential for harm, leading to more equitable and just outcomes for diverse user populations globally, not just those in dominant linguistic or cultural contexts.

3. **Broadening the Scope of NLP Applications:** Socially aware NLP could unlock new application areas that currently fall short due to a lack of social intelligence. This includes improved therapeutic AI, coaching systems, more inclusive technologies for people with disabilities, and complex social games where understanding player mental states and social implications is crucial.

4. **Interdisciplinary Integration and Advancement:** The paper strongly advocates for increased collaboration between NLP researchers and social scientists (e.g., psychologists, sociologists, HCI scholars). This integration could enrich NLP with theoretical insights and empirical findings from human social behavior, leading to more robust and human-aligned AI. Conversely, it could also provide social scientists with advanced tools for analyzing complex social phenomena.

5. **Influencing the Broader AI Landscape:** The principles of social awareness are not exclusive to NLP. The paper highlights its relevance to other AI modalities, such as computer vision (interpreting emotions, social interactions from visual data) and robotics (enabling safe and effective human-robot interaction, e.g., eldercare robots). Developing socially aware NLP could therefore set precedents and offer methodologies for building more socially intelligent AI across the entire field.

6. **Reframing Research Agendas and Evaluation Paradigms:** The paper argues for a fundamental shift in how NLP research is conducted, proposing new tasks, metrics, and evaluation strategies that move beyond traditional performance benchmarks. This could lead to a re-evaluation of what constitutes "success" in NLP, emphasizing ethical, social, and human-centric outcomes.

7. **Ethical Development and Policy Considerations:** The paper acknowledges the significant risks associated with developing powerful, socially aware systems, including cultural misunderstandings, enforcement of biases, privacy violations, misinformation, and over-reliance. By bringing these implications to the forefront, it underscores the need for a strong ethical framework and informs future policy and regulation for AI development.

In essence, the paper posits that embracing social awareness is not just an optional enhancement but a critical, necessary step for NLP to mature responsibly, deliver on its full potential, and genuinely align with human societal values and interactions.
