# A Causality-aware Infer-diagnose-refine Framework for Test-time Modality Adaptation in VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> An Infer-Diagnose-Refine (IDR) framework offers a training-free, model-agnostic method for test-time modality adaptation in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, dynamically adjusting visual importance using causal inference. This approach consistently improves task success rates across various VLA backbones and environments, including an 18.8% increase in average success rate on real-world robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle to dynamically adapt the importance of visual observations during diverse robot manipulation phases, leading to suboptimal performance.
- The contribution of visual information varies across different environments and VLA model architectures, necessitating costly re-training for each new scenario to maintain effectiveness.
- Existing test-time adaptation techniques for VLA models are typically limited to specific failure types or involve computationally expensive re-training/fine-tuning processes.

## Method

- The **Infer-Diagnose-Refine (IDR)** framework is proposed, a model-agnostic and training-free pipeline that operates at test time to refine VLA model actions.
- ==Causal inference== principles are employed to diagnose the dynamic importance of visual observations, quantifying their causal effect on action predictions by comparing factual and ==zero-padded counterfactual inputs==.
- A ==Gated Residual Fusion== mechanism refines actions by selectively applying visual corrections and a ==bounded proprioceptive regularizer==, guided by the diagnosed causal effects to adapt modality importance.

## Results

- IDR improved performance across all four VLA backbones tested on in-domain (LIBERO) and out-of-domain (SIMPLER, CALVIN) simulation benchmarks, with a notable gain of **+6.33%** for X-VLA in SIMPLER VA settings.
- Real-world experiments on a dual-arm ARX5 platform showed an average success rate increase from **56.5%** to **75.3%** (**+18.8%**), and a reduction in average completion time from **53.6s** to **41.5s** for successful trials.
- The framework enabled a **33.3%** full-completion rate on a challenging 5-step real-world long-horizon task (Organize Table), compared to **0%** for the baseline VLA model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The importance of visual observations in VLA models is highly dynamic, fluctuating with different manipulation phases, environmental contexts, and inherent model architectural biases.
> - Simple zero-padding interventions can effectively serve as a proxy for causal interventions, allowing for accurate, training-free diagnosis of modality importance at test time.
> - Selectively applying corrections based on the diagnosed causal effects of visual input, combined with a carefully bounded proprioceptive regularizer, stabilizes low-level control while improving task-level performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models represent an evolving paradigm for robot manipulation, enabling robots to execute tasks based on language instructions, visual observations, and proprioceptive states. These models are typically trained on large-scale datasets and have demonstrated performance across various environments. A central challenge in VLA models involves the effective fusion of multimodal information, particularly how to dynamically adjust the importance of visual observations during task execution. Robot manipulation tasks are dynamic, encompassing phases such as long-distance movements and close-range interactions, where the relevance of visual input can fluctuate significantly.

Prior research has identified that visual information does not contribute uniformly to VLA models. Studies have shown that the roles of vision and proprioception can vary across environments and during motion-transition phases. Some existing methods attempt to estimate visual importance and dynamically utilize visual information during the training phase. Other test-time adaptation approaches for VLA models focus on specific types of failures, such as language-conditioning collapse or distractor-induced grounding errors, often through counterfactual comparisons or evolutionary diffusion.

This paper distinguishes itself by exploring a model-agnostic, training-free framework for adjusting visual importance at *test time* for *frozen* VLA models. It frames the dynamic importance of visual observations using principles from causal inference. While causal inference has been applied in deep learning for tasks like understanding model behavior, addressing language bias, or reducing domain shifts, its application in VLA models for general test-time modality adaptation, specifically focusing on the causal effects of visual observations on action predictions, represents a distinct approach. This work aims to provide a broad, generalizable strategy for improving VLA models without requiring re-training, targeting dynamic adaptation across various manipulation stages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a model-agnostic framework that can refine action predictions of frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models at test time by dynamically adjusting the importance of visual observations. This is motivated by the observation that the contribution of visual information to VLA models is not static but varies significantly across different stages of robot manipulation tasks, model architectures, and operational environments.

The authors identify several specific observations that underscore this motivation:
1. **Dynamic Visual Importance in Manipulation Phases:** Robot manipulation involves dynamic processes where the importance of visual observations changes. For instance, close-range interactions may heavily rely on vision, while long-distance movements might depend more on proprioceptive states. Existing VLA models often struggle to adapt to these shifts, leading to suboptimal performance.
2. **Environmental Influence on Visual Importance:** The empirical analysis shows that the same VLA model can exhibit different visual importance patterns when deployed in varied environments. For example, a model might be proprioception-dominant in one benchmark but vision-dominant in another. This suggests a need for an adaptive mechanism that can account for environmental shifts without requiring model re-training for each new setting.
3. **Architectural Dependence of Visual Importance:** Different VLA model architectures exhibit distinct patterns of visual information utilization. Some models may be inherently more vision-dominant due to their reliance on rich vision-language representations, while others might show stronger proprioceptive effects if proprioceptive states are integrated earlier in their architecture. A flexible adaptation framework should be able to accommodate these inherent architectural biases.
4. **Avoidance of Retraining:** Current approaches that dynamically utilize visual information often involve re-training or fine-tuning, which can be computationally expensive and time-consuming, especially for large-scale VLA models. The motivation here is to provide a "training-free" solution that only incurs inference-time computation, thereby making it practical for real-world deployment and continuous adaptation.

Based on these motivations, the research seeks to address two key challenges:
1. **Effectively Estimating Dynamic Visual Importance:** How can the time-varying importance of visual observations for a given VLA model be accurately estimated at test time, without prior knowledge or training? The authors propose framing this estimation through the lens of causal inference, specifically by diagnosing the causal effects of visual observations on action predictions.
2. **Leveraging Estimation for Action Refinement:** Once visual importance is estimated, how can this information be effectively leveraged to refine action predictions during execution? The goal is to develop a mechanism that can adaptively recalibrate actions while preserving the established behavior patterns of the frozen VLA model, preventing "blind execution" (over-reliance on proprioception) while avoiding excessive perturbations to low-level control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is the **Infer-Diagnose-Refine (IDR)** framework, a model-agnostic and training-free pipeline designed for test-time modality adaptation in VLA models. IDR conceptualizes the dynamic importance of visual observations as their causal effects on the predicted actions.

**A. Infer-Diagnose-Refine Framework Overview**
The IDR framework operates in three sequential stages at each timestep:

1. **Infer:** The framework first infers action predictions under both factual (original inputs) and counterfactual (intervened inputs) scenarios. This involves selectively manipulating input modalities to observe their isolated effects.
2. **Diagnose:** Subsequently, the causal effects of specific modalities (e.g., visual observations) are diagnosed by quantifying the differences between factual and counterfactual action predictions. This quantification provides an estimate of the dynamic importance of that modality.
3. **Refine:** Finally, based on the diagnosed causal effects, the initial (base) action prediction is refined. This refinement mechanism dynamically adjusts modality importance to produce a more robust and context-aware action.

**B. Causality-Aware Action Refiner**

To instantiate the IDR framework, the authors design a "causality-aware action refiner." This refiner utilizes causal inference principles to dynamically adjust visual importance during action execution while aiming to preserve the VLA model's prior behavior.

1. **Causal Formulation:**
 The VLA model's action prediction is framed as a conditional distribution $p(A | V, S, L)$, where $V$ is visual observation, $S$ is proprioceptive state, and $L$ is language instruction. In the causal formulation, visual observation ($V$) and proprioceptive state ($S$) are treated as input factors ($X_1, X_2$), and language instruction ($L$) serves as the fixed contextual variable ($C$). This allows for analyzing the causal effects of $V$ and $S$ on the action ($A$) under a given language instruction.

2. **Infer: Zero-padding Interventions:**
 - **Factual Base Action:** For a frozen VLA model $\pi_{\theta^*}(\cdot)$, the factual base action $a_{\text{base},t}$ at timestep $t$ is computed using the original multimodal inputs $(v_t, s_t, l)$:
 $a_{\text{base},t} = \pi_{\theta^*}(v_t, s_t, l)$.
 - **Counterfactual Actions:** To isolate modality effects, zero-padding interventions are applied:
 - **Visual Intervention (do(V = 0_v)):** The visual input $v_t$ is replaced with an all-zero tensor $0_v$ of the same dimension. The resulting counterfactual action $a_{\text{no img},t}$ represents the prediction without visual information:
 $a_{\text{no img},t} = \pi_{\theta^*}(0_v, s_t, l)$.
 - **Proprioceptive Intervention (do(S = 0_s)):** Similarly, the proprioceptive input $s_t$ is replaced with $0_s$. The counterfactual action $a_{\text{no prop},t}$ represents the prediction without proprioceptive information:
 $a_{\text{no prop},t} = \pi_{\theta^*}(v_t, 0_s, l)$.
 These counterfactual actions provide the basis for dynamic importance estimation.

3. **Diagnose: Norm-based Quantification:**
 - **Action Deviations:** The directional causal effect of each modality is captured by the deviation between the factual and counterfactual actions:
 - $\Delta_{\text{img},t} = a_{\text{base},t} - a_{\text{no img},t}$ (deviation due to visual input absence)
 - $\Delta_{\text{prop},t} = a_{\text{base},t} - a_{\text{no prop},t}$ (deviation due to proprioceptive input absence)
 - **Causal Effect Magnitude:** The magnitude of these deviations is quantified using the $L_2$ norm:
 - $E_{\text{img},t} = \|\Delta_{\text{img},t}\|_2$
 - $E_{\text{prop},t} = \|\Delta_{\text{prop},t}\|_2$
 A larger $E_{\text{img},t}$ indicates a greater reliance on visual inputs. These magnitudes serve as adaptive diagnostic signals for dynamic visual importance.

4. **Refine: Gated Residual Fusion:**
 The core objective is to adaptively regulate visual importance without disrupting the VLA model's prior. This is achieved through a Gated Residual Fusion mechanism:
 - **Dynamic Gating Mechanism:** A gate $g_t$ is defined using an indicator function $I[\cdot]$ and an intervention threshold $\tau$:
 $g_t = I[E_{\text{img},t} < \tau]$.
 When $E_{\text{img},t}$ (visual causal effect) is below $\tau$ (meaning visual cues are underutilized), the gate activates ($g_t=1$). This triggers the injection of a correction to compensate for suppressed visual importance. Otherwise, the gate remains inactive ($g_t=0$), preserving the base action. The threshold $\tau$ is set to the mean causal effect of visual observations under the baseline model.
 - **Bounded Proprioceptive Regularizer:** To prevent high-frequency control jitter from proprioceptive corrections, a regularizer is introduced:
 - $w_{\text{prop},t} = \beta \cdot \min(1, \frac{E_{\text{prop},t}}{E_{\text{img},t} + \epsilon})$: a dynamic scaling factor based on the relative magnitudes of proprioceptive and visual effects.
 - $u_{\text{prop},t} = \text{clip}(\Delta_{\text{prop},t}, -\lambda, \lambda)$: the proprioceptive residual is clipped within a range $[-\lambda, \lambda]$ for smooth actuation.
 - **Final Refined Action:** The final action $a_{\text{final},t}$ is a sum of the base action and a gated, weighted combination of the visual correction and the regularized proprioceptive correction:
 $a_{\text{final},t} = a_{\text{base},t} + g_t \cdot (\alpha \Delta_{\text{img},t} + w_{\text{prop},t} \cdot u_{\text{prop},t})$.
 Here, $\alpha$ controls the visual refinement strength. This mechanism selectively applies visual correction guided by the diagnosed causal effects while stabilizing low-level control with a bounded proprioceptive term.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on multiple VLA backbones (π0.5, X-VLA, VLA-Adapter, OpenVLA-OFT) across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, SIMPLER, CALVIN) and real-world tasks.

**A. Observations from Test-time Causal Diagnosis:**
The analysis of normalized visual importance ratio ($R_{\text{img}} = E_{\text{img}} / (E_{\text{img}} + E_{\text{prop}} + \epsilon)$) yielded three observations:
1. **Environment Shifts Visual Importance:** The same VLA model (e.g., X-VLA) exhibited varying visual importance across different environments (e.g., proprioception-dominant in LIBERO, but vision-dominant in Google SIMPLER settings).
2. **Model Architectures Shape Visual Importance:** Within the same benchmark (LIBERO), different VLA models showed distinct visual importance patterns. Some were vision-dominant (e.g., π0.5, VLA-Adapter), while X-VLA was proprioception-dominant, suggesting architectural designs influence modality reliance.
3. **Manipulation Phases Alter Visual Importance:** Within a single task execution, the visual importance patterns fluctuated, particularly around critical events like gripper closing and opening, highlighting the dynamic nature of visual relevance during task progress.

These observations support the core hypothesis that visual importance is dynamic and context-dependent, necessitating a causality-aware, test-time adaptation strategy.

**B. Simulation Experiments:**
- **In-domain Evaluation (LIBERO):** IDR improved performance across all four VLA backbones. The largest gains were observed on VLA-Adapter (+1.40%) and π0.5 (+1.25%). X-VLA, which was already proprioception-dominant, showed a smaller gain (+0.45%), indicating less room for visual-guided correction.
- **Out-of-domain Evaluation (SIMPLER and CALVIN):** IDR demonstrated improved robustness under distribution shifts.
 - On SIMPLER, a notable gain of +6.33% was achieved in the Variant Aggregation (VA) setting for X-VLA, where visual adaptability is critical.
 - On CALVIN (transfer from A-C to unseen environment D), IDR improved the average completed subtasks (from 4.22 to 4.44) and the 5/5 completion rate (from 70.5% to 77.3%) for X-VLA, demonstrating effectiveness in long-horizon tasks. Similar improvements were observed for VLA-Adapter.

**C. Real-World Experiments:**
Evaluations on a dual-arm ARX5 platform using a Qwen3-VL-4B-based VLA model for tasks like Grab Cola, Fold Clothes, Sweep Trash, and Organize Table.
- **Overall Improvement:** IDR increased the average success rate from 56.5% to 75.3% (+18.8%).
- **Efficiency:** Despite requiring three forward passes, IDR reduced the average completion time among successful trials from 53.6s to 41.5s. This suggests that improved action quality can lead to more direct and efficient task execution, compensating for the increased inference latency.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** Significant gains were observed in tasks requiring fine-grained visual guidance and contact-rich manipulation, such as Fold Clothes L3 (+25.0%), Grab Cola L2 (+25.0%), and Sweep Trash (+21.9%).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** On the challenging 5-step Organize Table task, IDR achieved a 33.3% full-completion rate compared to 0% for the baseline, and increased the average completed steps from 2.17 to 4.00.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Intervention Strategies:** Zero-padding interventions yielded the best performance compared to Gaussian noise and mean-value padding, suggesting that complete and deterministic removal of modality information provides a cleaner counterfactual signal.
- **Correction Scale (α) and Intervention Threshold (τ):**
 - Performance peaked at a moderate correction scale ($\alpha = 0.08$), with larger values degrading performance due to excessive correction.
 - The optimal intervention threshold ($\tau$) was found to be near the mean visual causal effect, demonstrating that selective intervention is critical. Uniform intervention across all timesteps (e.g., by setting a very high $\tau$) resulted in performance degradation.
- **Modules of Action Refiner:** Both the selective gate and the bounded proprioceptive regularizer were found to be essential for stable refinement. Removing these components or their sub-components (clip bound, adaptive weighting) reduced performance. Reversing the intervention direction (negated $\alpha, \beta$) also degraded performance, confirming that the improvement stems from steering the action in the correct causal effect direction.
- **Which Modality Guides Refinement:** A proprioception-guided variant on X-VLA (a proprioception-dominant model) resulted in a significant performance drop (from 96.05% to 77.35%), particularly on object manipulation tasks. This suggests an asymmetry: while vision offers task-level corrections based on scene semantics, proprioception-guided refinement can disrupt low-level control stability, even for models that inherently rely more on proprioception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents the Infer-Diagnose-Refine (IDR) framework, a training-free and model-agnostic approach for test-time modality adaptation in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The significance of this work lies in its novel application of causal inference to dynamically adjust the importance of visual observations during robot task execution, addressing a fundamental challenge in VLA model deployment.

The potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The consistent improvements observed across diverse VLA backbones, simulation benchmarks (in-domain and out-of-domain), and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that IDR can reliably boost manipulation accuracy and improve long-horizon task completion. This is particularly relevant for scenarios involving visual and environmental variations, where VLA models often face distribution shifts.
- **Practical Deployment of Frozen VLA Models:** By providing a training-free adaptation mechanism, IDR increases the practical utility of pre-trained, frozen VLA models. This alleviates the need for costly and time-consuming re-training or fine-tuning when deploying models in new environments or for slightly varied tasks. It enables existing models to be more adaptive and robust to unforeseen dynamic conditions.
- **Theoretical Advancement in VLA Understanding:** The framework formalizes the dynamic importance of visual observations as causal effects, providing a principled method for diagnosing how visual inputs influence action predictions. The empirical observations regarding how environments, model architectures, and manipulation phases alter visual importance contribute to a deeper understanding of VLA model behavior.
- **Reduced Development Cycle and Cost:** The training-free nature of IDR means that developers can leverage existing VLA models more effectively, reducing the computational resources and time typically associated with adapting policies to new contexts. This could accelerate the development and deployment of generalist robot policies.

The primary limitation identified is the computational overhead of requiring three forward passes per control step, which increases inference latency. However, the authors demonstrate that improved action quality can lead to faster overall task completion in real-world scenarios by reducing unnecessary movements. Future work exploring the distillation of these online causal diagnostics into the model during training could potentially enable implicit, modality-aware adaptation without the test-time computational overhead, further broadening the applicability and efficiency of this approach.
