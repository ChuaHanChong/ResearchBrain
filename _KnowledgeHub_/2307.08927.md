# Multi-Stage Cable Routing through Hierarchical Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Berkeley AI Research Lab developed a hierarchical imitation learning system for multi-stage cable routing using a Franka Panda robot, enabling robust manipulation of deformable cables and effective failure recovery by integrating learned low-level clip insertion primitives with a high-level action sequencing policy. The system achieved a 50% success rate for 3-clip routing and demonstrated rapid adaptation to novel conditions through interactive fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Complex, multi-stage robotic manipulation tasks are hindered by compounding errors, where individual primitive failures drastically reduce overall task success probability.
- Manipulating deformable objects like cables presents challenges due to their infinite state space, complex non-linear dynamics, and the critical need for continuous vision-based perception.
- Traditional flat end-to-end learning methods often fail for long-horizon tasks, particularly when requiring diverse failure recovery strategies in real-world settings.

## Method

- A hierarchical imitation learning system was developed, comprising a learned low-level policy for vision-based cable insertion into a single clip and a learned high-level policy for sequencing actions.
- The low-level policy was trained on human teleoperation data, including specific recovery trials, to enable self-correction for local mistakes, utilizing end-effector mounted cameras and extensive data augmentation.
- The high-level policy orchestrates a mix of learned and scripted primitives, dynamically selecting actions based on visual observations and a primitive history embedding to achieve multi-stage cable routing and compensate for imperfections.
- An interactive fine-tuning scheme allows rapid adaptation of the high-level policy to novel or out-of-distribution scenarios with minimal human demonstrations.

## Results

- The learned low-level single-clip insertion policy achieved a 46% success rate, outperforming a scripted baseline and demonstrating that recovery demonstrations improve performance from 18% to 46%.
- The full hierarchical system attained 79%, 58%, and 50% success rates for 1, 2, and 3-clip routing tasks respectively, significantly surpassing flat end-to-end learning (0% for 3 clips) and hand-designed state machines (5% for 3 clips).
- Interactive fine-tuning with only 10 demonstrations led to substantial performance improvements for out-of-distribution clip configurations (e.g., from 0% to 15/30 for 3-clip OOD) and enhanced reliability for in-distribution tasks (from 50% to 85%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training low-level policies with explicit recovery demonstrations significantly enhances their robustness and ability to self-correct local errors during complex manipulation.
> - A learned high-level policy, capable of intelligent primitive sequencing and failure recovery, is crucial for mitigating compounding errors and achieving high success rates in long-horizon, multi-stage tasks.
> - View-invariant observations from end-effector mounted cameras, coupled with rigorous data augmentation, are essential for developing generalizable vision-based controllers for deformable object manipulation.
