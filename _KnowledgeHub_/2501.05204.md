# Design and Control of a Bipedal Robotic Character

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Disney Research and Walt Disney Imagineering developed a bipedal robotic character that balances dynamic mobility with artist-directed, expressive motion. Their approach integrates artistic vision directly into mechanical design and uses reinforcement learning for robust motion control, enabling a physically expressive character capable of engaging public performances.

## Problem

- Traditional robotics research often prioritizes functional capabilities (e.g., speed, efficiency) over artistic expressiveness, character, and audience engagement, which are crucial for entertainment applications.
- Developing bipedal robotic characters for entertainment requires balancing dynamic mobility with the ability to execute artist-directed, expressive motions.
- Integrating artistic design and engineering in robotics development often lacks a cohesive workflow, leading to design constraints that limit creative expression.

## Method

- A holistic workflow integrates animation authoring, mechatronic design, reinforcement learning for control, and a real-time runtime system, with artistic vision guiding mechanical design.
- A compact 3kg bipedal robot was designed with 5 DoF per leg and a 4 DoF neck/head, optimized for character and expressive motion rather than general-purpose mobility.
- A reinforcement learning-based control architecture, divided into perpetual, periodic, and episodic motion policies, was trained in simulation to enable robust execution of artist-directed movements.

## Results

- The bipedal robot successfully executed a range of expressive motions, including a distinctive 'wide' walk and specific choreographed sequences, while maintaining stability against disturbances.
- Simulation-trained control policies transferred effectively to the physical robot, demonstrating robust performance across different scenarios with minimal real-world adjustment.
- Public deployments showcased the robot's ability to convey character and emotion, with audiences connecting with its movements and attributing intentions, validating the integrated art-engineering approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating artistic vision as a primary driver, rather than a secondary consideration, significantly shapes the mechatronic design and overall character expressiveness of an entertainment robot.
> - A modular control architecture leveraging reinforcement learning, separated into perpetual, periodic, and episodic motion policies, enables robust execution of artist-directed movements while maintaining stability.
> - A carefully designed puppeteering interface, coupled with an animation engine that blends motions and respects physical limitations, allows operators to achieve nuanced real-time performances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Entertainment robotics presents a unique challenge at the intersection of artistic expression and technical engineering. While traditional robotics research often focuses on functional capabilities like speed, efficiency, and robustness, entertainment robots must prioritize expressiveness, character, and audience engagement. This paper by researchers from Disney Research and Walt Disney Imagineering presents a comprehensive approach to designing and controlling a bipedal robotic character that balances dynamic mobility with expressive, artist-directed motion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The researchers developed a small bipedal robot with a distinctive character, focusing on how it moves and interacts with audiences. What sets this project apart is its holistic integration of artistic vision with technical implementation, creating a robot whose design is driven primarily by creative goals rather than purely functional requirements. The project was completed in less than a year, highlighting the effectiveness of the team's integrated workflow.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Integrating Art and Engineering

The development process involved a close collaboration between mechanical engineers and animators, with a workflow that unites four key elements:

1. **Animation Authoring**: Animators defined the character's motion style, creating both periodic motions (like walking) and episodic motions (specific actions or sequences)
2. **Mechatronic Design**: Engineers developed a robot that could physically execute the desired motions while maintaining the character's aesthetic
3. **Reinforcement Learning**: AI techniques were used to train control policies that enable robust execution of the artistic motions
4. **Runtime System**: A real-time system allows operators to puppeteer the robot, blending different motions and controlling the character during performances

The design process began with animators exploring the character through traditional animation tools, which helped determine the robot's morphology and movement style. This artistic vision informed the mechanical design constraints, rather than the other way around. This approach inverts the typical robotics development process, where design often precedes animation.

#### 3.2. Mechatronic Design

The robot features a compact design optimized for character and expressiveness:

- 5 degrees of freedom (DoF) in each leg, allowing for expressive walking motions
- 4 DoF neck and head system for natural gaze control
- Show functions including actuated antennas, illuminated eyes, and a headlamp
- Built primarily with off-the-shelf hardware modules and 3D-printed components
- Total weight of approximately 3 kg and standing height of 35 cm

The team focused on simplicity over complexity, selecting the minimum number of joints needed to achieve the desired artistic expressions. This approach helped manage project complexity while still achieving an expressive character.

When designing the locomotion capabilities, the team focused on a distinctive walking motion rather than general-purpose mobility. The robot employs a "wide" walking style with legs splayed outward and a slight rocking motion reminiscent of an animated character.

#### 3.3. Control Architecture

The researchers developed a control architecture based on reinforcement learning (RL) that enables the robot to execute artist-directed motions while maintaining stability. Their approach divides the control problem into three separate policies:

1. **Perpetual motion policy**: Handles standing and balancing in place
2. **Periodic motion policy**: Controls walking gaits at different speeds and turning rates
3. **Episodic motion policy**: Manages specific animation sequences

This division allowed the team to train specialized policies for each motion type, resulting in more precise control. The policies were trained in simulation using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, with reward functions designed to encourage both accurate motion tracking and stability.

#### 3.4. Animation Engine

The animation engine serves as the bridge between user input and robot motion. It processes three types of animations:

1. **Background animations**: Subtle idle motions that run continuously
2. **Triggered animations**: Specific sequences activated by button presses
3. **Joystick animations**: Direct mappings from joystick positions to motion parameters

The engine blends these animations together, allowing for smooth transitions between different motions. It also synchronizes show functions (like antenna movements and eye illumination) and audio with the robot's physical motions.

A key feature of the animation engine is its ability to ensure that all generated commands respect the physical limitations of the robot. This prevents requesting impossible motions that could destabilize the robot or damage its actuators.

#### 3.5. Puppeteering Interface

The team designed an intuitive remote control interface that allows operators to puppeteer the robot in real-time. The interface uses a standard game controller, mapping different buttons and joysticks to specific robot functions:

- Left joystick controls torso orientation and posture
- Right joystick manages head and gaze direction
- D-pad and face buttons trigger specific animations and behaviors
- Shoulder buttons control walking and turning

This interface allows a skilled operator to create engaging performances by combining different motions and reactions. The researchers found that even novice operators could quickly learn to control the robot's basic functions, while experienced puppeteers could create more complex and nuanced performances.

For example, moving the left joystick left or right rotates the torso, while the right joystick controls both head and gaze direction, maintaining a natural relationship between these elements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The researchers conducted extensive testing to evaluate the robot's capabilities. In simulation, they measured how well the control policies could track reference motions while maintaining stability. The results showed that the RL-based controllers could accurately follow artistic motions while adapting to disturbances.

Real-world tests included:

1. **Stability against external pushes**: The robot could maintain balance when subjected to moderate external forces
2. **Walking over small obstacles**: The robot successfully navigated small bumps and uneven terrain
3. **Character expressiveness**: The robot demonstrated a range of expressive motions and reactions

The robot successfully transitioned between standing, walking, and performing special animations while maintaining stability. The control policies adapted to changing conditions and commands, demonstrating robust performance across different scenarios.

The team also observed that the simulation-trained policies transferred well to the physical robot, requiring minimal adjustment. This validates their approach to policy training and hardware design.

#### 4.2. Public Deployment

The ultimate test for the robot was its deployment in public settings, where it interacted with audiences. The robot was featured in several performances, including a short narrative sequence where it:

1. Walks into a scene
2. Discovers and interacts with an object (a roll of paper)
3. Expresses curiosity, surprise, and joy through its movements
4. Executes specific choreographed sequences

The robot also appeared at public events where multiple robots performed together, creating engaging group interactions that delighted audiences. These performances demonstrated the effectiveness of the puppeteering interface and the robot's ability to convey character and emotion through movement alone.

The researchers report that audiences quickly connected with the robot's character, attributing intentions and emotions to its movements. This indicates that the team successfully achieved their goal of creating a believable and engaging robotic character.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

While the project was largely successful, the researchers identified several limitations and opportunities for future improvement:

1. **Policy separation**: The division into multiple specialized policies creates training overhead. Future work could explore whether a single policy can learn multiple skills with the same level of accuracy.

2. **Puppeteering limitations**: The number of buttons and controls a human operator can effectively manage is limited. The team suggests exploring ways to embed more autonomy in the animation engine to expand the robot's expressive capabilities.

3. **Environmental awareness**: The current robot has limited perception of its environment. Adding sensors and perception capabilities could enable more interactive and responsive behaviors.

4. **Animation tools**: The team sees an opportunity to develop better tools for animators to create content for robotic characters, bridging the gap between traditional animation software and physical robot capabilities.

The researchers also note that their approach could be extended to other types of characters and morphologies, suggesting that the workflow they developed has broader applications in entertainment robotics.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

This paper presents a significant contribution to the field of entertainment robotics by demonstrating a practical approach to creating engaging and expressive robotic characters. The key innovation lies in the holistic integration of artistic vision, mechanical design, and advanced control techniques.

By prioritizing creative intent over purely functional requirements, the team created a robot with distinctive character and appeal. The reinforcement learning-based control architecture enabled robust execution of artistic motions, while the puppeteering interface allowed for real-time control of the robot during performances.

The success of the robot in public deployments validates the approach and suggests promising directions for future entertainment robotics. As robots continue to move into spaces where they interact directly with humans, the ability to create characters that engage emotionally with audiences will become increasingly important.

This research provides a valuable framework for future work in this domain, demonstrating that technical robustness and artistic expression can be successfully integrated to create compelling robotic characters. The workflow developed by the team—uniting animation, design, control, and puppeteering—offers a template for the rapid development of custom robot characters for various entertainment applications.
