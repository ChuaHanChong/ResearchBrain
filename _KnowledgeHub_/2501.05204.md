# Design and Control of a Bipedal Robotic Character

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Disney Research and Walt Disney Imagineering developed a bipedal robotic character that balances dynamic mobility with artist-directed, expressive motion. Their approach integrates artistic vision directly into mechanical design and uses reinforcement learning for robust motion control, enabling a physically expressive character capable of engaging public performances.

## Problem

- Traditional robotics research often prioritizes functional capabilities (e.g., speed, efficiency) over artistic expressiveness, character, and audience engagement, which are crucial for entertainment applications.
- Developing bipedal robotic characters for entertainment requires balancing dynamic mobility with the ability to execute artist-directed, expressive motions.
- Integrating artistic design and engineering in robotics development often lacks a cohesive workflow, leading to design constraints that limit creative expression.

## Method

- A holistic workflow integrates animation authoring, mechatronic design, reinforcement learning for control, and a real-time runtime system, with artistic vision guiding mechanical design.
- A compact 3kg bipedal robot was designed with 5 DoF per leg and a 4 DoF neck/head, optimized for character and expressive motion rather than general-purpose mobility.
- A reinforcement learning-based control architecture, divided into perpetual, periodic, and episodic motion policies, was trained in simulation to enable robust execution of artist-directed movements.

## Results

- The bipedal robot successfully executed a range of expressive motions, including a distinctive 'wide' walk and specific choreographed sequences, while maintaining stability against disturbances.
- Simulation-trained control policies transferred effectively to the physical robot, demonstrating robust performance across different scenarios with minimal real-world adjustment.
- Public deployments showcased the robot's ability to convey character and emotion, with audiences connecting with its movements and attributing intentions, validating the integrated art-engineering approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating artistic vision as a primary driver, rather than a secondary consideration, significantly shapes the mechatronic design and overall character expressiveness of an entertainment robot.
> - A modular control architecture leveraging reinforcement learning, separated into perpetual, periodic, and episodic motion policies, enables robust execution of artist-directed movements while maintaining stability.
> - A carefully designed puppeteering interface, coupled with an animation engine that blends motions and respects physical limitations, allows operators to achieve nuanced real-time performances.
