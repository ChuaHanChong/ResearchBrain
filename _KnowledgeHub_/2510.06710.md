# RLinf-VLA: A Unified and Efficient Framework for Reinforcement Learning of Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified and efficient framework, RLinf-VLA, enables scalable reinforcement learning for Vision-Language-Action models, demonstrating up to 2.27x training speedups and 20-85% higher task success rates across various robotic manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing RL-for-VLA applications are fragmented across diverse model architectures, RL algorithms, and simulators, hindering unified research and comparisons.
- Current RL frameworks for VLA models suffer from inefficiencies and significant GPU idle time due to the computational demands of online simulators competing with model inference.
- VLA models trained via imitation learning often exhibit poor out-of-distribution generalization during deployment due to distribution mismatch.

## Method

- **RLinf-VLA** provides a unified framework supporting diverse VLA architectures (e.g., OpenVLA), multiple RL algorithms (PPO, ==GRPO==), and heterogeneous robotic simulators (ManiSkill, LIBERO, RoboTwin).
- It introduces flexible GPU allocation strategies, including a novel hybrid mode with fine-grained pipelining, to optimize resource utilization and reduce GPU idle time.
- The framework integrates specific algorithmic design choices, such as multi-granularity advantage calculation, action-level value estimation, and partial resets, tailored for VLA models.

## Results

- RLinf-VLA achieved **20%** to **85%** higher success rates over baselines across ManiSkill, LIBERO (130 tasks, **98.11%** average success), and RoboTwin benchmarks.
- The framework delivered up to **2.27x** speedups in training efficiency compared to existing collocated baselines and **1.88x** speedup using hybrid pipelining for GPU-intensive simulators.
- RL fine-tuning within RLinf-VLA consistently enabled strong out-of-distribution generalization and stable convergence, even for tasks with very low initial success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal GPU allocation strategies for VLA-RL training are dependent on the simulator type (GPU-parallelized vs. CPU-parallelized), requiring flexible system support.
> - Fine-grained pipelining in hybrid GPU allocation significantly reduces 'GPU bubbles' and yields up to 1.88x speedups for GPU-parallelized simulators like ManiSkill.
> - Action-level value estimation, partial resets for PPO, and trajectory length normalization for GRPO are effective practices for improving VLA-RL performance and sample efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of RLinf-VLA is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, Reinforcement Learning (RL), and large-scale robotics simulation.

**VLA Models as Foundation Models for Robotics:** Recent years have witnessed the emergence of VLA models as a new generation of foundation models designed to unify perception, language understanding, and embodied control. These models, pretrained on vast internet-scale data and further fine-tuned on robotic demonstration datasets (e.g., OpenVLA), can map raw sensor observations and natural language instructions directly to robot actions. This paradigm has shown impressive generalization capabilities across various tasks, representing a significant step towards more general-purpose robots.

**Limitations of Imitation Learning and the Role of RL:** While VLA models trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or imitation learning are powerful, they often face challenges during deployment due to distribution mismatch between training data and real-world or novel environments. When encountering unseen states or instructions, their performance can degrade. Reinforcement learning provides a principled solution to this post-training problem. By optimizing task success through trial-and-error interaction and cumulative rewards, RL enables VLA policies to explore beyond narrow expert demonstrations, acquire corrective behaviors, and exhibit stronger out-of-distribution generalization, as indicated by recent studies (e.g., RL4VLA).

**The Current Fragmentation and Inefficiency of RL-for-VLA:** Despite RL's promise, its application to VLA models has largely been small-scale or fragmented. Existing methods often rely on disparate model architectures, RL algorithms, and evaluation protocols, making fair comparisons and the extraction of general principles difficult. Moreover, current RL-for-VLA frameworks, such as SimpleVLA-RL (which builds on VeRL, originally for LLMs), lack system-level optimizations tailored to the unique demands of embodied settings. In these environments, simulators fiercely compete with model inference and learning for GPU resources. The tightly coupled, iterative nature of online RL (model-environment interactions) often leads to significant GPU idle time and pipeline inefficiencies if the system design is not explicitly optimized for it.

**RLinf-VLA's Position:** RLinf-VLA directly addresses these critical gaps. It aims to provide a **unified platform** that standardizes the integration of diverse VLA architectures (e.g., OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT), multiple RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO), and heterogeneous simulators (ManiSkill, LIBERO, RoboTwin). Concurrently, it introduces **efficient system designs**, particularly novel GPU allocation strategies like the hybrid mode with fine-grained pipelining, to maximize resource utilization and speed up training. By doing so, RLinf-VLA positions itself as a foundational system to bridge the gap between algorithmic advancements in RL for large models (often stemming from LLMs) and the system-level demands of embodied intelligence, enabling efficient, unified, and reproducible research in this rapidly evolving domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to introduce **RLinf-VLA**, a novel framework designed to facilitate **scalable, unified, and efficient reinforcement learning training for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. The motivations for developing RLinf-VLA stem directly from the identified challenges in the current landscape of VLA and RL research:

1. **Addressing Fragmentation and Lack of Unification:** A primary motivation is the existing fragmentation across different VLA architectures, RL algorithms, and robotic simulators. Researchers often face a steep integration curve when trying to compare new methods or adapt existing ones. RLinf-VLA aims to provide a **unified interface and system abstraction** that standardizes these components, enabling seamless integration, fair comparison, and greater extensibility for future research. This includes supporting diverse VLA models, popular RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO), and a range of simulators (ManiSkill, LIBERO, RoboTwin).

2. **Improving Training Efficiency and Scalability:** Existing RL frameworks for large models, particularly those adapted from large language model (LLM) settings, often fail to account for the unique computational demands of embodied AI. Specifically, online RL for VLA models involves computationally intensive simulators that compete with model inference and training for GPU resources. This leads to substantial GPU idle time and limits scalability. A key motivation is therefore to design an **efficient system architecture** that optimizes resource allocation, especially for GPUs, to significantly speed up training and allow for tackling larger, more complex tasks.

3. **Facilitating Reproducibility and Standardized Research:** The fragmented nature of current research hinders reproducibility and the extraction of general principles. By offering a unified, open-source platform with standardized interfaces and benchmarks, RLinf-VLA seeks to **standardize and accelerate research**, making it easier for the community to reproduce results, build upon existing work, and focus on algorithmic innovations rather than system engineering.

4. **Enhancing VLA Model Performance and Generalization:** Ultimately, the framework is motivated by the desire to enable VLA models to achieve superior performance and generalization capabilities in embodied settings through effective reinforcement learning. By making RL fine-tuning more accessible and efficient, the authors aim to push the boundaries of what VLA models can achieve in terms of task success rates and robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios.

5. **Distilling Actionable Training Practices:** Through the systematic development and evaluation of RLinf-VLA, a subsidiary objective is to **identify and distill a set of actionable training practices** for effective RL-based VLA training. This provides practical guidelines for researchers and practitioners, contributing to the broader understanding of how to best apply RL in this domain.

In essence, RLinf-VLA is motivated by the critical need for a robust, efficient, and unified infrastructure to unlock the full potential of reinforcement learning for vision-language-action models, thereby accelerating progress towards more capable and general-purpose embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLinf-VLA adopts a multi-faceted approach, combining system-level optimizations with algorithmic design choices, all underpinned by a unified interface, to achieve its goals of efficiency, unification, and performance in RL-based VLA training.

**A. Reinforcement Learning Pipeline Formalization:**
The authors begin by formalizing vision-based manipulation tasks as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the agent optimizes a policy to maximize expected cumulative discounted rewards. The RL pipeline for VLA models is broken down into three components: **Generation** (inferring actions), **Simulator** (executing actions and providing observations), and **Training** (updating the policy). A key distinction from LLM-based RL is the hierarchical action formulation (Chunk → Atomic Action → Token), which the framework unifies. The substantial computational overhead of simulators on GPU and CPU resources, particularly during the iterative generation-simulation loop, is identified as a core challenge.

**B. Flexible GPU Allocation Strategies (System-Level Optimization):**
To address resource contention and maximize utilization, RLinf-VLA introduces three GPU allocation modes, with a novel contribution in the hybrid mode:

1. **Collocated Allocation:** All components (Generation, Simulator, Training) reside on the same set of GPUs. The implemented strategy modifies a "naive" collocated approach by performing offload/onload operations for Simulator and Generation only at the start and end of the rollout phase. While reducing frequent communication overhead, it's acknowledged as sub-optimal due to components waiting for each other, leading to idle GPU memory.
2. **Disaggregated Allocation:** Each component is assigned to distinct GPU partitions, ensuring dedicated resources. This is simple but suffers from GPU underutilization due to inter-component dependencies and idle periods.
3. **Hybrid Allocation with Fine-grained Pipelining (Novel):** This is RLinf-VLA's core system innovation. It allows flexible GPU assignment (e.g., Generation and Simulator on different partitions, Training on all GPUs). Crucially, it introduces **fine-grained pipelining**: a simulator instance on one GPU is partitioned into multiple sub-simulators (S^(1)..., S^(k)). These sub-simulators and the Generation component run concurrently, with actions from one sub-simulator being processed by Generation while another sub-simulator generates its observation. This reduces idle time ("GPU bubbles") caused by inter-component dependencies, providing a significant speedup, especially for GPU-parallelized simulators.

**C. Unified Interface (Abstraction and Extensibility):**
To ensure high extensibility and seamless integration, RLinf-VLA provides a unified interface:

1. **Multiple Simulators Support:** Standardized Gym-style APIs (`reset`, `step`, `chunk_step`) for diverse robotic simulators like ManiSkill (GPU-parallelized), LIBERO (CPU-parallelized), and RoboTwin. It includes features like `auto_reset` and `ignore_terminations` for flexible episode handling and supports unified observation/action formats.
2. **Multiple Models Support:** Facilitates the integration of VLA architectures (e.g., OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT) by requiring only a standardized interaction interface. It also incorporates Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for efficient fine-tuning of large models.
3. **Multiple Algorithms Support:** Designed to support various RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO) by abstracting core functions like advantage and loss computation, minimizing redundant code.

**D. Design Choices for Algorithms (Algorithmic Optimizations):**
The framework integrates specific algorithmic design choices tailored for VLA models, drawing insights from both LLM and robotics RL:

1. **Multi-Granularity Calculation Support:** Offers flexibility in advantage estimation (chunk-level vs. action-level) and log-probability computation (chunk-level, action-level, token-level) to adapt to different algorithmic requirements and action representations.
2. **PPO Specifics:**
 - **Partial Reset Support:** Allows sub-environments to reset immediately upon termination during rollouts, improving sample efficiency compared to fixed episode lengths.
 - **Critic Design:** Adopts a parameter-sharing strategy where a lightweight value head is attached to the VLA's language model component for efficient state value estimation, avoiding the computational cost of a separate large critic network.
 - **Value for Action Chunks:** Empirically, the action-level value estimation (C-dimensional value vector for each atomic action in a chunk) is found to perform better than a single scalar chunk-level value.
3. **GRPO Specifics:**
 - **Valid Action Mask:** Considers only timesteps prior to task completion, improving sample efficiency and avoiding updates on irrelevant post-completion steps.
 - **Loss Normalization by Trajectory Length:** Normalizes the policy loss by the number of valid timesteps in the Valid Action Mask setting, preventing longer trajectories from dominating gradients.
 - **Success Rate Filter:** Discards trajectory groups where all trajectories yield identical (all success or all failure) outcomes, as non-zero advantages require a mix, accelerating convergence and improving stability.

**E. Experimental Evaluation:**
The methodology includes extensive empirical evaluation across three benchmarks (ManiSkill, LIBERO, RoboTwin) to assess:
- **Policy Performance:** Task success rates, out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, and multi-task learning capabilities of models fine-tuned with RLinf-VLA using PPO and GRPO.
- **System Efficiency:** Benchmarking throughput and latency breakdown for different GPU allocation strategies, comparing against baselines like SimpleVLA-RL and demonstrating speedups.
- **Ablation Studies:** Isolating the impact of various algorithmic design choices (e.g., action-level value, partial reset, valid action mask, normalization, success rate filter) to distill actionable training practices.

By integrating these system-level and algorithmic innovations within a unified, open-source framework, RLinf-VLA provides a comprehensive and highly effective approach to scaling and optimizing RL for VLA models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with RLinf-VLA yield several significant findings regarding policy performance, system efficiency, and effective training practices.

**A. Policy Performance and Generalization:**

- **Substantial Performance Improvements:** RLinf-VLA consistently achieves significant performance gains, ranging from approximately 20% to 85% improvement over baseline models across all tested benchmarks.
 - **ManiSkill:** For OpenVLA, RL fine-tuning improves OOD success from 39.10% to 81.93% (with PPO). For OpenVLA-OFT, OOD success rises from 18.29% to 77.05% (with PPO). PPO consistently outperformed GRPO in this setting, showing stronger generalization and stability.
 - **LIBERO (130 tasks):** A single unified OpenVLA-OFT model, trained with GRPO on 130 diverse tasks across five groups, achieved an impressive average success rate of 98.11% (up from a 42.09% baseline). This demonstrates RLinf-VLA's strong capability for large-scale multi-task learning.
 - **RoboTwin:** On challenging bimanual manipulation tasks, OpenVLA-OFT with GRPO achieved an average success rate of 84.63%, representing over a 60% improvement from the 24.48% baseline. This highlights strong out-of-distribution generalization on complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Consistent Training Stability:** The training curves show stable convergence to high success rates, even for tasks with very low initial success (e.g., RoboTwin tasks starting at 3% success).

**B. System Efficiency:**

- **Optimal GPU Allocation Varies by Simulator Type:** The study conclusively shows that the best GPU allocation strategy depends on whether the simulator is GPU-parallelized or CPU-parallelized. RLinf-VLA's flexible multi-mode support is crucial for achieving high efficiency across diverse execution regimes.
 - **GPU-Parallelized Simulators (ManiSkill):** The novel **Hybrid mode with fine-grained pipelining** (pipe=2) achieved the highest throughput, demonstrating up to a **1.88× speedup** over the naive disaggregated baseline on 8 GPUs. Even with increasing GPU counts (16, 32 GPUs), it maintained a 1.61× – 1.69× advantage. This confirms the effectiveness of pipelining in reducing GPU idle time and mitigating "GPU bubbles."
 - **CPU-Parallelized Simulators (LIBERO, RoboTwin):** For these scenarios, where the CPU is the bottleneck, the collocated and hybrid (pipe=1) modes generally outperformed disaggregated and deeper pipelined modes. This is attributed to the shift in the generation-to-execution ratio and the nature of the CPU bottleneck.
- **Significant Speedups over Baselines:** When compared to existing frameworks like SimpleVLA-RL (which only supports collocated mode), RLinf-VLA achieved **1.34× – 2.27× speedups** under collocated settings for LIBERO and RoboTwin. These gains resulted from both more efficient vectorized environments during rollout and system-level optimizations (adaptive communication, removal of redundant log-probability recomputation) during training.

**C. Actionable Practices (Ablation Studies):**

- **Tips for PPO:**
 - **Action-level value estimation** consistently outperforms chunk-level estimation for VLA models with action chunks, leading to higher success rates and lower value loss (e.g., ManiSkill, LIBERO).
 - **Partial reset** substantially improves sample efficiency by resetting sub-environments immediately upon task completion, allowing for more diverse and successful trajectories per training step.
- **Tips for GRPO:**
 - **Trajectory length normalization** significantly improves performance, especially when episode lengths vary, by ensuring balanced gradient contributions from trajectories of different durations (e.g., LIBERO-Goal).
 - **Valid action mask**, which excludes post-completion actions from optimization, improves sample efficiency and performance, particularly when combined with trajectory length normalization (e.g., LIBERO-Goal). Its benefits can be task-dependent, however (less clear in ManiSkill).
 - **Success rate filtering** (discarding groups where all trajectories succeed or all fail) can improve training stability and prevent policy collapse in certain settings (e.g., OpenVLA ManiSkill), though its effectiveness is not universal across all tasks and models.
- **General Insights:**
 - **Larger rollout data size** per epoch generally leads to higher success rates and more substantial policy improvement for on-policy algorithms like PPO and GRPO.
 - **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)** itself does not drastically change peak performance but necessitates separate hyperparameter tuning, particularly for the learning rate, to ensure stable training.

These findings collectively underscore RLinf-VLA's dual strengths: as a high-performance system for VLA+RL training and as a tool for distilling practical insights into effective policy optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RLinf-VLA represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, offering a robust and practical solution to several long-standing challenges in training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with reinforcement learning (RL). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Standardization and Unification of Research:** By providing a unified interface that seamlessly integrates diverse VLA architectures, multiple RL algorithms, and heterogeneous simulators, RLinf-VLA acts as a crucial standardization platform. This will dramatically reduce the engineering overhead for researchers, allowing for fair comparisons between different methods, accelerating the development cycle for new algorithms, and facilitating the extraction of general principles in embodied RL. This unification is vital for fostering systematic progress in a field often characterized by fragmented approaches.

2. **Accelerated and Scalable Embodied AI Development:** The framework's core contribution to efficiency, particularly through its novel hybrid GPU allocation strategy with fine-grained pipelining, offers up to 2.27× speedups over existing baselines. This means that researchers can train larger VLA models on more complex tasks with significantly less time and computational resources. Such acceleration lowers the barrier to entry for large-scale RL in robotics, enabling more ambitious experiments and pushing the boundaries of what VLA models can achieve in terms of task complexity and data volume.

3. **Enhanced Reproducibility and Open Science:** RLinf-VLA's open-source release on HuggingFace and GitHub is a key enabler of reproducibility. By providing a common, well-engineered infrastructure, it ensures that research findings are more easily verifiable and extendable by the broader scientific community. This commitment to open science will foster collaboration, attract more researchers to embodied AI, and democratize access to state-of-the-art RL training tools.

4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness for VLA Models:** The empirical results clearly demonstrate that RL fine-tuning within the RLinf-VLA framework leads to substantial improvements in task success rates and out-of-distribution generalization across various benchmarks (LIBERO, ManiSkill, RoboTwin). This is a critical step towards making VLA models more capable and reliable for real-world robotic deployments, where adaptability to novel situations is paramount. The framework's ability to boost performance on complex, multi-task, and bimanual manipulation scenarios showcases its potential to unlock advanced robotic capabilities.

5. **Practical Guidance for RL-VLA Practitioners:** The extensive ablation studies conducted within the framework provide invaluable "actionable practices" for both PPO and GRPO algorithms. These distilled insights into effective critic design, rollout strategies (partial reset), advantage estimation, loss normalization, and success rate filtering offer practical guidelines for practitioners, helping them avoid common pitfalls and optimize their RL-VLA training pipelines. This contributes directly to the applied aspect of embodied AI research.

6. **Bridging the Gap between LLM and Robotics RL:** RLinf-VLA effectively adapts and optimizes RL paradigms that have seen success in large language models (like GRPO variants) for the unique system-level and environmental interaction demands of robotics. By addressing the specific challenges of simulator integration and GPU resource contention, it paves the way for cross-pollination of techniques between these two rapidly evolving subfields of AI.

In conclusion, RLinf-VLA is poised to become a foundational system in embodied AI research. Its combined emphasis on unification, efficiency, and openness will undoubtedly accelerate the development, evaluation, and practical deployment of next-generation Vision-Language-Action models, bringing the field closer to realizing truly intelligent and autonomous robotic agents.
