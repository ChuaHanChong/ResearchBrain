# ImageWAM: Do World Action Models Really Need Video Generation, or Just Image Editing?

> [!summary] Summary
> ImageWAM reconfigures pretrained image editing models to act as efficient visual backbones for robot action prediction, demonstrating that compact, instruction-conditioned visual transformation representations can replace full video generation in World Action Models. This approach achieved up to 93.56% success on RoboTwin 2.0 and 83.1% on LIBERO-Plus, while reducing inference latency by approximately 75% and computational FLOPs by about 83% compared to video-generation WAMs.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) often rely on computationally expensive video generation as a generative prior for robot action prediction.
- Full video prediction requires modeling irrelevant details like appearance and background changes, which diverts model capacity from action-relevant information.
- The inherent difficulty of generating physically consistent future videos can lead to accumulated errors that mislead downstream action prediction modules.

## Method

- Repurposes pretrained image editing models as visual backbones, extracting compact "==editing-aware representations==" from their intermediate key-value caches.
- Attaches an "action expert" module to the image editing backbone, which predicts action chunks conditioned on these instruction-guided visual transformation features.
- Utilizes a joint flow-matching objective to train the image editing branch to predict a task-relevant future endpoint frame and the action expert to generate actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **93.56%** on RoboTwin **2.0** (randomized setting) and **98.4%** on the standard LIBERO benchmark.
- Demonstrated superior performance on the challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, reaching an **83.1%** success rate with the FLUX.2 4B backbone, significantly outperforming FastWAM (**51.5%**) and OpenVLA-OFT (**69.6%**).
- Reduced inference latency by approximately **75%** and computational FLOPs by about **83%** compared to video-generation WAMs, enabling real-time operation at 69-77ms with optimizations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image editing models provide a more suitable generative prior for language-conditioned robot manipulation by focusing on instruction-guided visual modifications rather than comprehensive future video prediction.
> - Extracting compact "editing-aware representations" during the image editing process enables a significantly more efficient inference path compared to decoding dense spatio-temporal tokens.
> - The task of predicting a single future endpoint frame that summarizes the task-specified visual transformation is a more effective and less error-prone intermediate reasoning step for robot control.
