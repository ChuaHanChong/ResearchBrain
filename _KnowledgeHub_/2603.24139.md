# Tutor-Student Reinforcement Learning: A Dynamic Curriculum for Robust Deepfake Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> The Tutor-Student Reinforcement Learning (TSRL) framework introduces an adaptive curriculum where a Reinforcement Learning agent dynamically weights training samples for deepfake detectors. This approach consistently enhanced generalization across various architectures, improving average AUC by up to 4.1% on unseen manipulation techniques and data domains.

## Problem

- Deepfake detectors show significant performance degradation when exposed to novel manipulation techniques, compression artifacts, or different data domains, limiting their generalization capabilities.
- Current curriculum learning methods for deepfake detection often rely on static or pre-defined difficulty metrics, which do not adapt to the detector's evolving learning state.
- Conventional supervised training for deepfake detection applies uniform importance to all samples, leading to inefficient learning and suboptimal generalization by neglecting critical 'hard' examples.

## Method

- The ==Tutor-Student== Reinforcement Learning (TSRL) framework conceptualizes deepfake detector training as a Markov Decision Process, with an RL 'Tutor' agent dynamically guiding a 'Student' detector.
- The Tutor observes a comprehensive, history-aware state for each sample, incorporating current model features, prediction confidence, and long-term learning dynamics such as Exponential Moving Average (EMA) loss and forgetting events.
- The Tutor selects a continuous scalar weight for each sample's loss, enabling adaptive focus on informative examples, and is optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a dense, immediate reward signal that incentivizes learning progress.

## Results

- TSRL consistently improved generalization across six deepfake detection baselines, demonstrating average AUC increases on both cross-dataset and cross-method evaluation protocols.
- On cross-method evaluation, Effort + TSRL achieved the highest average AUC of **0.942**, a **2.2%** improvement, while CORE + TSRL showed the largest relative gain of **4.1%** (from **0.814** to **0.855**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dynamic, RL-learned policy in TSRL significantly outperforms static curriculum learning and standard baselines, with CORE + TSRL achieving **0.855** AUC compared to **0.817** for a static heuristic curriculum.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An RL-driven dynamic curriculum significantly outperforms static, heuristic-based curriculum learning methods, leading to substantially improved generalization in deepfake detection.
> - The adaptive sample weighting strategy enables the Student detector to learn more discriminative and disentangled feature representations, clearly separating real from fake data and organizing fake samples by their learning difficulty.
> - The framework effectively guides the student model to prioritize and focus its learning capacity on challenging examples near the decision boundary by isolating trivial samples.
