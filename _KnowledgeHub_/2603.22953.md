# Cluster-Wise Spatio-Temporal Masking for Efficient Video-Language Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> ClusterSTM, a framework developed by researchers at Harbin Institute of Technology, introduces a novel masking strategy that combines intra-frame clustering with temporal-density-based token selection and a video-text relevance reconstruction objective. This approach enhances video-language pretraining efficiency by mitigating visual information loss and temporal leakage, achieving state-of-the-art performance with improvements like 1.4% Recall@1 on MSRVTT for zero-shot text-to-video retrieval.

## Problem

- Large-scale video-language model pretraining is computationally expensive, hindering widespread application and further development.
- Existing masked visual modeling strategies for video-language tasks suffer from significant visual information loss at high masking ratios due to short training schedules.
- Masked video modeling faces temporal information leakage, where masked tokens are easily reconstructed from adjacent frames, undermining the effectiveness of representation learning.

## Method

- **ClusterSTM** proposes a masking strategy that uses ==intra-frame clustering== to select semantically diverse tokens and ==temporal-density-based filtering== to ensure strong temporal correlation among retained tokens, even with high masking ratios.
- A ==video-text relevance reconstruction== objective is introduced, training the student model to reconstruct high-level video-text relevance matrices for masked regions, generated by a teacher model (**SigLIP**).
- The pretraining objective integrates this ==masked relevance modeling loss== with standard ==video-text contrastive==, ==video-text matching==, and ==masked language modeling== losses.

## Results

- ClusterSTM achieved new state-of-the-art results in zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, improving Recall@1 on MSRVTT by **1.4%**, ActivityNet by **0.8%**, and MSVD by **1.6%**.
- It also established new state-of-the-art performance in fine-tuned text-to-video retrieval (e.g., MSRVTT R@1 increased by **1.2%** to **49.7%**) and video captioning (e.g., MSRVTT CIDEr increased by **0.5** to **64.4**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the cluster-wise spatio-temporal masking and the video-text relevance reconstruction target are critical components, with the latter providing the most substantial performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intra-frame clustering enables the preservation of holistic visual content by ensuring selected tokens represent diverse semantic regions within each frame.
> - Leveraging temporal density for token selection effectively mitigates temporal information leakage by favoring tokens with strong semantic correlation across adjacent frames.
> - Reconstructing high-level video-text relevance, rather than low-level visual features, directly fosters robust multimodal semantic alignment, leading to better performance in cross-modal tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large-scale video-language models (VLMs) has facilitated advanced generalization across various multimodal tasks by establishing semantic connections between visual dynamics and textual descriptions. However, the pretraining of these models on massive video-text datasets typically incurs substantial computational costs, presenting a significant barrier to their widespread application and further development.

Recent efforts to mitigate these computational demands have explored masked visual modeling (MVM) strategies, leveraging their efficiency in data processing and lower memory footprints. Despite these advancements, existing MVM approaches in the context of video-language pretraining encounter two primary limitations. First, they often suffer from significant visual information loss when high masking ratios are applied, which is a common strategy in MVM to achieve efficiency. Unlike masked autoencoders (MAEs) for pure vision tasks, which can compensate for such loss through extensive training (e.g., thousands of epochs), large-scale video-language pretraining typically converges within a few epochs, making prolonged training impractical. For instance, semantic masking strategies, such as those in UMT, selectively retain foreground tokens, potentially overlooking background information crucial for holistic scene understanding and accurate video-language alignment, as text descriptions frequently encompass both foreground and background elements. Lowering masking ratios, as seen in STM, preserves more tokens but reduces the efficiency benefits.

Second, masked video modeling faces the challenge of temporal information leakage. Video data inherently possess temporal correlations, allowing masked tokens to be reconstructed by referencing corresponding unmasked tokens in adjacent frames. This inter-frame redundancy can undermine the effectiveness of representation learning by making the masking task too easy. Approaches like tube masking, which apply identical masks across frames, attempt to address this by preserving tokens at the same spatial locations. However, this method assumes minimal motion, limiting its effectiveness in dynamic video scenarios.

This research positions itself within this landscape by proposing a masking strategy that aims to overcome these limitations. It seeks to design an efficient pretraining method that retains a high masking ratio while ensuring the preserved visual tokens capture comprehensive video content and exhibit strong temporal correlation, thereby mitigating both severe visual information loss and temporal information leakage.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient video-language pretraining strategy that addresses critical limitations observed in existing masked visual modeling approaches when applied to video-language tasks. The authors identify two main challenges that motivate their work:

1. **Mitigating Severe Visual Information Loss under High Masking Ratios:** Existing masked visual modeling techniques often employ high masking ratios to achieve computational efficiency. However, in the context of large-scale video-language pretraining, models typically converge rapidly (within a few epochs). Such short training schedules do not provide sufficient time to compensate for the extensive visual information loss caused by high masking. This can hinder the model's ability to learn comprehensive visual representations necessary for effective video-language alignment. The authors specifically note that previous semantic masking strategies (e.g., UMT) focus on foreground tokens, neglecting background elements that are often vital for holistic scene understanding and are frequently referenced in accompanying text descriptions. Other strategies (e.g., STM) reduce masking ratios to preserve more information, which compromises the efficiency objective. The motivation is to retain a high masking ratio for efficiency while ensuring the retained tokens are semantically rich and representative of the entire video content.

2. **Preventing Temporal Information Leakage:** Video data inherently contains strong temporal correlations between adjacent frames. In masked video modeling, this can lead to information leakage, where masked tokens can be easily inferred or reconstructed from neighboring unmasked tokens in other frames. Such leakage can reduce the learning signal, making the reconstruction task less challenging and thus hindering the model's ability to learn robust spatio-temporal representations. Traditional masking methods like random masking are particularly susceptible to this. Tube masking attempts to address this by masking entire tubes, but it relies on an assumption of minimal motion, which is not always valid in complex video content. The motivation is to design a masking mechanism that actively considers and leverages temporal relationships to ensure that retained tokens exhibit strong temporal correlation, thereby enhancing the difficulty and effectiveness of the reconstruction task.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel masking strategy that ensures retained tokens: (1) capture holistic video content, encompassing both foreground and background information, and (2) exhibit strong temporal correlation to prevent information leakage. Additionally, they aim to move beyond conventional pixel-level or feature-level visual reconstruction targets by introducing a video-text relevance reconstruction objective, motivated by the need to align high-level multimodal semantics directly, which is more relevant for cross-modal tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, ClusterSTM, integrates a novel masking strategy with a video-text relevance reconstruction objective within a teacher-student model architecture.

**Model Overview:**
The system comprises a teacher model and a student model. The teacher, a pre-trained vision-language foundation model (SigLIP), generates spatio-temporal masks and fine-grained video-text relevance matrices. The student model consists of a video encoder (ViT-B/16 initialized with UMT weights) and a text encoder (BERT-base).
Given a video-text pair, the teacher model processes the video into tokens and the text into a pooled feature. These outputs are then used to generate masks and relevance matrices. The student model takes video patch embeddings, applies the teacher-generated masks to select visible tokens, and feeds them into its video encoder. The visible and masked tokens are then passed through a spatio-temporal decoder to reconstruct full video tokens. The text sentence is processed by the text encoder. Finally, the student computes reconstructed video-text relevance matrices by interacting reconstructed invisible video tokens with text features.

**Cluster-Wise Spatio-Temporal Masking (ClusterSTM):**
This core component aims to address visual information loss and temporal leakage.
1. **Intra-Frame Clustering:** To ensure retained tokens represent holistic content, clustering is performed within each video frame. For each frame, its visual tokens are partitioned into `N_c` semantically independent clusters, where `N_c` is determined by the total number of tokens per frame and the masking ratio (`N_c = N × (1 − r)`). The Density Peaks Clustering (DPC) algorithm is used for this process. This step allows for a more distributed sampling of visual information across semantic regions of a frame.
2. **Temporal-Density-based Cluster-Wise Masking:** To mitigate temporal information leakage, a temporal density metric is introduced. For each visual token in a frame, its temporal density is computed based on its semantic distance (cosine similarity) to all tokens in adjacent frames. A higher temporal density indicates stronger semantic correlation with neighboring frames. Within each of the previously formed clusters, only the token exhibiting the highest temporal density is retained, and all other tokens in that cluster are discarded. This strategy ensures that the selected tokens maintain semantic consistency across the temporal dimension, regardless of spatial displacement. The retained tokens then form the spatio-temporal mask for the student model.

**Video-Text Relevance Reconstruction:**
This objective enhances high-level multimodal semantic alignment.
1. **Video-Text Relevance Generation:** The teacher model generates high-quality video-text relevance matrices. To improve the relevance representation beyond simple token-text interaction, the process involves aggregating a target visual token with its neighboring tokens using a sliding window and pooling mechanism. This creates a "fused visual token" that is more contextually rich. This fused token is then multiplied with the text feature from the teacher model to produce a fine-grained video-text relevance matrix for each frame. SigLIP is chosen as the teacher model due to its flexible pooling mechanism.
2. **Masked Relevance Modeling (MRM):** The student model is trained to reconstruct the video-text relevance for the masked (invisible) tokens. The masked relevance modeling loss (`L_MRM`) is computed as the L2 distance between the student's reconstructed invisible video-text relevance matrices (`e_R_inv`) and the teacher-generated ground truth relevance matrices for those same masked tokens (`b_R_inv`).

**Pretraining Objectives:**
The overall training objective combines the proposed masked relevance modeling loss with standard video-language pretraining losses:
`L = L_MRM + L_VTC + L_VTM + L_MLM`
- `L_VTC`: Video-Text Contrastive learning, aligning video and text features in a shared embedding space.
- `L_VTM`: Video-Text Matching, determining if a video-text pair is semantically related.
- `L_MLM`: Masked Language Modeling, reconstructing masked tokens in the text input.

**Implementation Details:**
The model is pretrained on a combination of WebVid-2M and CC3M datasets (approximately 5 million video-text/image-text pairs). A masking ratio of 75% for image tokens and 90% for video tokens is applied. The cutoff distance for temporal density calculation is set to the top 20% quantiles of semantic distances. Training involves 10 epochs using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-4.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ClusterSTM was evaluated across multiple video-language benchmarks, including video-text retrieval, video question answering, and video captioning.

**Zero-Shot Text-to-Video Retrieval (Table 1):**
ClusterSTM demonstrated improvements over state-of-the-art models with comparable pretraining data. It surpassed previous SOTA by 1.4% on MSRVTT, 0.8% on ActivityNet, and 1.6% on MSVD (Recall@1 metrics). While performing comparably to Singularity on DiDeMo, ClusterSTM achieved this with fewer sampled frames (4 vs. 12). When frame counts were matched, ClusterSTM outperformed STM on DiDeMo by 2.1%. These results indicate the model's generalization ability and efficiency in zero-shot settings.

**Text-to-Video Retrieval (Table 2):**
In fine-tuning scenarios, ClusterSTM achieved Recall@1 scores of 49.7% on MSRVTT (+1.2% over prior SOTA), 58.5% on DiDeMo (+1.6%), and 54.9% on ActivityNet (+1.3%). The model's performance was comparable to, or even superior to, methods that utilized substantially larger pretraining datasets, despite being trained on significantly less data (5M pairs).

**Video Question Answering (Table 3):**
ClusterSTM showed performance improvements in video QA tasks. It outperformed STM by 0.6% on TGIF-Frame, 0.8% on MSRVTT-QA, and 0.6% on ActivityNet-QA, demonstrating its capability in multimodal reasoning.

**Video Captioning (Table 4):**
For video captioning, ClusterSTM established new state-of-the-art results, achieving CIDEr scores of 64.4 on MSRVTT (+0.5 over prior SOTA) and 145.6 on MSVD (+0.2), indicating strong video understanding for generative tasks.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Masking Strategies (Table 5):** The ablation studies confirmed the benefits of the proposed masking. "Cluster-wise-S" (randomly selecting one token per cluster spatially) significantly outperformed frame-wise, random, and tube-wise masking, indicating the importance of holistic visual content preservation. "Cluster-wise-ST" (temporal-density-based cluster-wise, the proposed method) further improved performance over "Cluster-wise-S", validating the role of temporal correlation in mitigating information leakage.
- **Masking Ratios (Table 6):** Optimal performance was observed with a 75% masking ratio for image tokens and 90% for video tokens, suggesting an effective balance for data utilization and efficient pretraining.
- **Reconstruction Targets (Table 7):** The video-text relevance target yielded the best performance, surpassing reconstruction of pixels, HOG features, and deep features. This finding supports the hypothesis that aligning high-level multimodal semantic attributes is more advantageous for cross-modal tasks.
- **Pretraining Objectives (Table 8):** Each component of the pretraining objective (VTC, VTM, MLM, MRM) contributed positively to the model's performance, with MRM providing the most substantial gain, underscoring its role in enhancing joint visual-textual representation learning.
- **Effectiveness of Cluster-Wise Spatio-Temporal Masking (Table 9):** When integrated into UMT by replacing its semantic masking, the cluster-wise spatio-temporal masking strategy led to a notable performance improvement in zero-shot text-to-video retrieval (e.g., MSRVTT R@1 from 29.6 to 30.4). This indicates the masking strategy's effectiveness in capturing comprehensive visual content despite high masking ratios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ClusterSTM framework contributes to the field of video-language pretraining by addressing two identified challenges: severe visual information loss under high masking ratios and temporal information leakage due to inter-frame correlations. The introduction of intra-frame clustering combined with temporal-density-based token selection provides a mechanism to retain semantically diverse and temporally consistent visual information. This approach enables the model to operate with high masking ratios, thereby maintaining computational efficiency, while simultaneously preserving a rich learning signal.

The proposed video-text relevance reconstruction objective moves beyond conventional visual feature reconstruction by emphasizing high-level multimodal semantic alignment. The experimental results consistently demonstrate that this objective, in conjunction with the novel masking strategy, leads to superior performance across various downstream tasks, including text-video retrieval, video question answering, and video captioning. The ability of ClusterSTM to achieve state-of-the-art results with a comparable amount of pretraining data, and in some cases to rival models trained on significantly larger datasets, suggests an improvement in pretraining efficiency and the quality of learned representations.

The findings indicate that focusing on semantically relevant and temporally correlated tokens during masking, rather than random or purely spatial selections, is beneficial for learning robust video-language representations. The work offers a new perspective on designing masking strategies for multimodal representation learning. It has the potential to inspire future research into developing more scalable and temporally coherent video-language foundation models, possibly leading to more accessible and less computationally intensive pretraining paradigms for advanced multimodal AI systems.
