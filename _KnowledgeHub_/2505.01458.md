# A Survey of Robotic Navigation and Manipulation with Physics Simulators in the Era of Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey systematically analyzes how low-level physics simulator properties affect robotic navigation and manipulation performance and sim-to-real transfer in Embodied AI, providing guidance for selecting and configuring simulation tools. It categorizes existing simulators, benchmarks, and methods, highlighting the critical role of accurate physics and sensory modeling.

## Problem

- Policies trained in simulation often perform poorly in real-world robotic navigation and manipulation tasks due to the persistent "sim-to-real gap."
- Prior surveys on Embodied AI simulators lacked detailed analysis of how low-level simulator properties specifically impact sim-to-real transfer across both navigation and manipulation.
- Simulation-based evaluations frequently fail to accurately predict real-world robotic performance, leading to unreliable deployments and hindering research progress.

## Method

- The authors conduct a systematic survey analyzing how low-level physics simulator properties influence policy learning, evaluation, and sim-to-real transfer for navigation and manipulation tasks.
- The "sim-to-real gap" is dissected into perception and action-dynamics discrepancies, with common mitigation strategies categorized and reviewed.
- A comprehensive resource is provided, covering benchmark datasets, metrics, simulation platforms, and methods, along with practical guidance for tool selection and configuration.

## Results

- The survey identifies that advanced photorealistic rendering and accurate sensor noise models effectively mitigate the perception gap in navigation, while robust physics engines address the action-dynamics gap.
- For manipulation, accurate multi-point contact, friction models, and the emerging field of differentiable physics are found to be crucial for achieving successful real-world transfer of policies.
- A call is made for new evaluation standards, including procedural quality metrics, temporal safety monitoring, and sim-to-real predictivity assessments, to ensure simulated performance genuinely reflects real-world robotic behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-level simulator properties, such as collision response, contact solvers, friction models, and sensor accuracy, are critical determinants of sim-to-real transfer success in robotics.
> - Accurate physical modeling, including differentiable physics for manipulation and specialized dynamics for complex navigation, is essential for effectively reducing the sim-to-real gap.
> - Sim-to-real failures are frequently attributable to specific simulator property mismatches, necessitating more nuanced evaluation beyond simple task success rates to include safety and procedural quality.
