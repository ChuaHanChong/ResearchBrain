# Not Forgotten: Implementation and Evaluation of a Personalized Episodic Memory for the Humanoid Robot Head Kim

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from Stuttgart Media University demonstrates that equipping the humanoid robot head Kim with a personalized episodic memory module significantly boosts perceived sociability, particularly attributes like trustworthiness and warmth. This system effectively mitigates the statelessness of large language models in embodied social robotics without increasing user perceptions of disturbance or creepiness.

## Problem

- Conversational large language models (LLMs) used in social robots are stateless, preventing continuous, personalized interactions and hindering long-term human-robot relationships.
- Empirical understanding of how personalized memory in embodied robots affects user perception, especially regarding sociability and potential discomfort (e.g., 'Uncanny Valley of Mind'), is limited.
- Concerns exist that a robot's ability to recall personal details might be perceived as intrusive or akin to surveillance, rather than fostering connection.

## Method

- A lightweight ==episodic memory module== was developed and integrated into the humanoid robot head **Kim**, utilizing a ==microservice architecture== with LLM-based dialogue.
- The module stores conversational fragments as ==vector embeddings== in a Qdrant database, annotated with ==LLM-derived emotional metadata== (valence and arousal) to prioritize salient memories.
- A ==hybrid retrieval scoring function==, combining semantic similarity with factors like recency and emotional intensity, filters relevant memories for injection into the LLM's prompt, encouraging natural recall.

## Results

- The Memory-Enhanced system received significantly higher ratings on the Sociability subscale (t(42) = **-4.15**, p < **.001**, Cohen's d = **0.60**), primarily driven by increased perceived trustworthiness (d = **0.62**) and warmth (d = **0.56**).
- There was no statistically significant increase in perceived Disturbance (t(42) = **-0.05**, p = **.960**, d = **0.00**), with mean ratings remaining almost identical across conditions.
- Neither Agency nor Animacy subscales showed significant differences, and while **63%** of participants preferred the Memory-Enhanced system, this global preference was not statistically significant from chance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Personalized episodic memory serves as a social lubricant in human-robot interaction, substantially enhancing perceptions of a robot's sociability, trustworthiness, and warmth.
> - The implementation of selective, contextually appropriate recall by an embodied robot does not inherently trigger negative affective responses or increase perceived disturbance, such as creepiness or surveillance concerns.
> - Overcoming the statelessness of LLMs in embodied robotics is achievable through memory systems that balance personalization with careful retrieval mechanisms, contributing to more meaningful and sustained interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of social robots capable of maintaining persistent, meaningful relationships with users is a significant area within human-robot interaction (HRI). Such robots, envisioned for roles in companionship, therapy, and assistance, operate under an implicit social contract where users expect personal information shared to be retained across interactions. However, a prevalent challenge in current social robotics, particularly those utilizing large language models (LLMs) for conversational capabilities, is the inherent statelessness of these models. LLMs typically reset interactions or are constrained by finite context windows, leading to socially discontinuous responses over time. While this limitation might be an inconvenience for text-based chatbots, for embodied humanoid robots, whose physical form encourages attributions of agency and social capability, statelessness can violate user expectations and hinder the formation of long-term relationships.

Existing engineering solutions, such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and derivative architectures like MemGPT, enable LLMs to query external databases and inject retrieved context into prompts. Systems like Generative Agents have demonstrated that LLM-driven agents with memory streams can exhibit coherent social behavior in simulated environments. However, these solutions are often optimized for factual retrieval accuracy and evaluated primarily through functional benchmarks or virtual sandboxes. The transfer of these memory mechanisms to embodied social robotics introduces distinct concerns, particularly regarding their impact on human users' perceptions, which existing work has not adequately addressed.

The current research landscape reveals a two-fold gap in this area:
1. **Limited Empirical HRI Studies on User Perception:** Few empirical studies on memory in HRI focus on user perception, with research predominantly concentrating on the technical aspects of building memory architectures rather than how users experience them. Whether episodic memory enhances perceived social attributes like warmth, trustworthiness, or likeability in embodied agents remains an open empirical question.
2. **Uncertainty Regarding User Discomfort:** The introduction of personalized recall by an artificial agent raises concerns about potential intrusiveness. The concept of the "Uncanny Valley of Mind" suggests that attributed cognitive capabilities, including memory, might trigger eeriness if they exceed human-like expectations. Furthermore, a "memory power asymmetry," where an agent retains a complete record while the user forgets, could be perceived as surveillance rather than social attentiveness. It has been unclear whether memory-enabled robots are perceived as more socially competent, more disturbing, or both.

This paper positions itself as an initial empirical step to bridge this gap by implementing an episodic memory module on a humanoid robot head and systematically evaluating its differential impact on perceived sociability and disturbance in a controlled user study. It aims to provide empirical validation for theoretical expectations regarding the benefits of memory in HRI, specifically in an embodied context with modern LLM-based conversational systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized need for social robots to establish persistent, meaningful relationships with users. The stateless nature of large language models (LLMs), which often form the conversational core of these robots, contradicts fundamental social expectations that shared personal information should be remembered across interactions. This discontinuity in conversation is identified as a barrier to the development of robust human-robot relationships, especially for embodied agents where physical presence naturally elicits higher social attributions and expectations.

The central research question guiding this work is: **What is the differential impact of a personalized episodic memory system on the perceived sociability and the perceived disturbance of a humanoid robot, compared to a Baseline system without memory?**

To address this overarching question, the study pursued two key objectives:

1. **Develop a Lightweight Episodic Memory Module:** The first objective was to design and implement a practical episodic memory module. This module was intended to integrate vector-based semantic retrieval with an LLM-controlled dialog system and be deployable on an existing humanoid robot head. The design aimed to enable the robot to store past interactions, retrieve contextually relevant information, and inject it into the LLM's response generation prompt, thus allowing for personalized and socially continuous conversation.
2. **Empirically Evaluate User Perception of Memory-Enabled HRI:** The second objective was to conduct a controlled empirical study to assess the effects of this memory system on human users. The evaluation specifically aimed to measure two critical perceptual dimensions:
 - **Perceived Sociability:** To determine if a robot equipped with personalized episodic memory would be perceived as more sociable, encompassing attributes such as warmth, likeability, trustworthiness, and friendliness, compared to a robot without memory. This objective directly addresses the hypothesis that memory can foster stronger relational quality.
 - **Perceived Disturbance:** To investigate whether the implementation of personalized recall would elicit negative affective responses, such as feelings of creepiness, eeriness, or surveillance-related discomfort. This objective aims to test the "Uncanny Valley of Mind" hypothesis and concerns about "memory power asymmetry" in an embodied HRI context.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aimed to provide empirical evidence for the social utility of episodic memory in embodied robots, while also clarifying potential adverse perceptual effects that could impede its acceptance and integration into real-world social applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology comprised two main components: the design and implementation of the episodic memory system and the controlled user study to evaluate its effects.

**A. System Design and Architecture**

The system extended an existing robotic infrastructure with a dedicated episodic memory module.
- **Robotic Platform:** The physical embodiment was a humanoid robot head named "Kim," equipped with 14 pneumatic actuators for head movement and facial expressions. The core processing was handled by an Nvidia Jetson Orin AGX development board.
- **Overall Architecture:** A microservice-oriented design was adopted, with all components containerized using Docker and communicating via HTTP REST APIs and websockets.
 - **Base Services:** Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) via Whisper, Text-To-Speech (TTS) synthesis using XTTS, lip synchronization, and face tracking.
 - **Interaction Pipeline:** Audio input was transcribed by ASR, routed through the memory module for context retrieval, forwarded to an LLM for response generation, and then synthesized into speech with synchronized lip movements. Memory writing was offloaded to a background thread to manage latency.
- **Episodic Memory Module:** Implemented as a FastAPI microservice.
 - **Underlying LLM:** The gpt-4.1-nano model was used for response generation, with a system prompt defining its persona as an ironic, friendly android robot head.
 - **Vectorization and Storage:** Conversational text fragments were converted into 1536-dimensional vector embeddings using the OpenAI Embeddings API (text-embedding-3-small) and stored in a local Qdrant database.
 - **Emotional Annotation:** Each memory was annotated with emotional metadata (valence and arousal scores) derived from Russell’s Circumplex Model. These scores were obtained through LLM-based sentiment classification at the time of storage, with emotional intensity calculated as the Euclidean distance from the neutral origin. This allowed prioritizing emotional events during retrieval.
 - **Hybrid Retrieval Scoring:** A two-stage retrieval strategy was employed:
 1. Initial query to Qdrant using cosine similarity, over-fetching candidates by a factor of three.
 2. Re-ranking of candidates using a hybrid scoring function: `R(m) = α * sim(m) + S(m)`.
 - `sim(m)` is the cosine similarity score.
 - `α` is a heuristically calibrated scaling factor (set to 50), ensuring semantic relevance dominates.
 - `S(m)` is a memory strength score, defined as `max(0, (w_t * F_time + w_a * F_access + w_r * F_recency) * M_emotion)`.
 - `F_time`: function of elapsed time since creation.
 - `F_access`: function of retrieval count.
 - `F_recency`: function of elapsed time since last access.
 - `M_emotion`: a multiplicative reinforcement factor based on emotional intensity, preventing highly emotional memories from decaying quickly.
 - **LLM Integration:** The top-ranked memories were injected into the LLM system prompt as contextual background. The prompt included rules to encourage natural and non-intrusive use of these memories.
- **Experimental Condition Toggle:** For controlled comparison, the memory Docker container was selectively stopped for the Baseline condition (causing retrieval requests to time out) and activated for the Memory-Enhanced condition. For the Memory-Enhanced condition, the Qdrant database was pre-populated with individual user preferences collected during an initial survey.

**B. Study Design**

- **Design:** A within-subjects design was used, where each participant experienced both the Memory-Enhanced and Baseline conditions. The presentation order was randomized to mitigate order effects.
- **Format:** An asynchronous video-based online study was conducted via a LimeSurvey instance. This format allowed for strict control over stimuli (lighting, audio, robot movement), reduced social desirability bias, and enabled location-independent recruitment.
- **Procedure:**
 1. **Phase 1 (Profiling):** Participants provided informed consent and completed a questionnaire collecting six personal preferences (e.g., comfort food, hobby). These responses were manually converted into memory entries and injected into the Qdrant database for each participant. Individualized video stimuli were then generated and recorded with the robot responding to these preferences. A generic Baseline video was also created.
 2. **Phase 2 (Evaluation):** One week later, participants viewed two pre-recorded video sequences: one for the Memory-Enhanced condition (individualized, incorporating personal preferences) and one for the Baseline condition (generic responses). Both videos featured the robot responding to six identical social prompts.
- **Hypotheses:**
 - **H1 (Sociability):** Memory-Enhanced condition leads to higher ratings on the Sociability subscale.
 - **H4 (Disturbance):** Memory-enabled robot is perceived as more or less disturbing compared to a memoryless robot (directional hypothesis not explicitly stated, testing for any change).
 - **Secondary Hypotheses (H2, H3):** Agency and Animacy were assessed as additional measures.
 - **Exploratory Hypothesis (H_G):** Global overall preference between the two systems.
- **Measurement Instrument:** The Human-Robot Interaction Evaluation Scale (HRIES) was used. This scale includes 16 adjective items distributed across four subscales: Sociability (warm, likeable, trustworthy, friendly), Agency (self-reliant, rational, intentional, intelligent), Animacy (alive, natural, real, human-like), and Disturbance (creepy, scary, uncanny, weird). Items were rated on a 7-point Likert scale.
- **Participants:** N = 43 participants completed both phases, primarily university students from Stuttgart Media University. Specific demographic data were not collected to adhere to data minimization principles (GDPR).
- **Statistical Analysis:** Paired-samples t-tests were the primary inferential method for HRIES subscales, with a Bonferroni correction applied (adjusted α = .0125) for the four main comparisons (H1-H4). Effect sizes were reported using Cohen’s d. A chi-square goodness-of-fit test was used for global preference. Normality of difference scores was verified using Shapiro-Wilk tests.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the impact of the personalized episodic memory module on user perception.

**A. Reliability**
Internal consistency of the HRIES subscales was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha. All subscales demonstrated acceptable to good reliability:
- Sociability: α = .72 (Baseline) and α = .74 (Memory-Enhanced).
- Agency: α = .67 (both conditions).
- Animacy: α = .87 (Baseline) and α = .92 (Memory-Enhanced).
- Disturbance: α = .87 (Baseline) and α = .86 (Memory-Enhanced). These values support the interpretation of the subscale scores.

**B. H1: Sociability**
The Memory-Enhanced system received significantly higher ratings on the Sociability subscale compared to the Baseline system.
- A paired-samples t-test showed a statistically significant effect: t(42) = −4.15, p < .001 (Bonferroni-corrected p < .001).
- The effect size was medium-to-large (d = 0.60).
- Item-level analysis indicated that this increase was primarily driven by:
 - **Trustworthy:** M_BL = 3.21, M_ME = 4.09, d = .62 (p < .001).
 - **Warm:** M_BL = 3.07, M_ME = 3.91, d = .56 (p = .001).
 - **Likeable:** M_BL = 3.81, M_ME = 4.28, d = .33 (p = .022, a smaller effect).
- The item **friendly** showed no significant change (M_BL = 5.00, M_ME = 5.33, d = .24, p = .114), suggesting a ceiling effect in the Baseline condition.

**C. H4: Disturbance**
The Memory-Enhanced system did not significantly increase perceived disturbance.
- Mean ratings were nearly identical: M_BL = 4.22, M_ME = 4.23.
- A paired-samples t-test found no significant difference: t(42) = −0.05, p = .960 (Bonferroni-corrected p = 1.000).
- The effect size was negligible (d = 0.00).
- None of the four constituent items (creepy, scary, uncanny, weird) showed individual significance, and their effect sizes remained small (d < .25).

**D. Secondary Measures: Agency and Animacy**
Neither Agency nor Animacy subscales showed statistically significant differences between the two conditions.
- **Agency:** M_BL = 4.25, M_ME = 4.36; t(42) = −0.89, p_corr = 1.000, d = 0.11.
- **Animacy:** M_BL = 2.78, M_ME = 3.02; t(42) = −1.62, p_corr = .452, d = 0.17. Both conditions remained below the scale midpoint for Animacy.

**E. Global Preference**
When asked for an overall preference, 27 participants (63%) selected the Memory-Enhanced system, while 16 participants (37%) preferred the Baseline system. A chi-square goodness-of-fit test against an equal distribution yielded a non-significant result: χ²(1) = 2.81, p = .093. This indicates that the observed preference distribution could not be statistically distinguished from chance at the conventional significance level.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates that equipping a humanoid robot head with a personalized, LLM-integrated episodic memory module can significantly enhance perceived sociability without increasing perceived disturbance. This finding addresses a gap in the empirical understanding of memory's role in embodied HRI.

**Significance:**
- **Empirical Validation of Social Lubricant Effect:** The study provides empirical evidence that episodic memory serves as a social lubricant in HRI. The significant increase in perceived sociability (d = 0.60), particularly in attributes like trustworthiness (d = 0.62) and warmth (d = 0.56), aligns with theoretical mechanisms such as the establishment of common ground, social penetration theory, and the psychological significance of "not being forgotten." It indicates that memory contributes to a deeper sense of being known, beyond basic politeness.
- **Absence of Negative Affective Responses:** Crucially, the finding that perceived disturbance remained unchanged (d = 0.00) indicates that the implemented approach to personalized recall did not trigger privacy-related discomfort, uncanny valley effects, or surveillance concerns in this controlled context. This suggests that memory can be integrated into social robots without necessarily eliciting negative user perceptions, provided the retrieval and integration are contextually selective.
- **Design Implications for Relational HRI:** The results offer direct implications for the design of social robots. The observed impact on relational qualities like trust and warmth suggests that memory modules should be designed to communicate care and attentiveness rather than merely demonstrating technical recall. This implies prioritizing contextual selectivity and natural integration over unfiltered or forced memory references. The architectural choices, such as vector-based semantic retrieval and the hybrid scoring function, are identified as prerequisites for enabling socially appropriate recall by filtering irrelevant details and preventing intrusive references.
- **Advancing HRI Research:** This is presented as the first controlled within-subjects comparison of a memory-enabled versus a memoryless agent in an embodied HRI context using a validated instrument (HRIES). It moves beyond qualitative observations and technical benchmarks to provide quantitative insights into user perception.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Development of More Engaging Social Robots:** The findings suggest that incorporating personalized episodic memory can lead to more engaging and relationally satisfying interactions with social robots, which is critical for applications in long-term companionship, educational support, and therapy.
- **Informing Memory System Design:** The research highlights the importance of thoughtful design for memory systems in social robots, emphasizing the need for mechanisms that balance personalization with user comfort. It provides a foundation for developing memory architectures that are not only functionally capable but also socially appropriate and perceptually acceptable.
- **Addressing the LLM Statelessness Challenge:** By demonstrating a viable method to overcome the statelessness of LLMs in an embodied context without adverse effects, the study contributes to the broader effort of making LLM-powered agents more suitable for sustained social interactions.

**Limitations and Future Work (as discussed by the authors, which delineate the boundaries of the current impact):**
- **Video-based Format:** The video-based nature of the study limited ecological validity by excluding real-time dialogue and non-verbal cues, potentially impacting judgments on Agency and Animacy.
- **Single-Session Interaction:** Evaluating pre-populated preferences in a single session restricts the empirical testing of long-term factors like memory decay and the emergence of constructs like Narrative Identity.
- **Sample Characteristics:** The sample size (N=43) and demographic (university students) limit the generalizability of the findings.
- **Architectural Superiority:** While memory availability's impact is shown, the study does not definitively prove the superiority of the proposed hybrid architecture over simpler memory implementations (e.g., keyword matching).
- **Unfiltered Recall:** The current design cannot fully determine whether a system with less filtered or more extensive recall of sensitive details would trigger privacy concerns.

Future work is proposed to address these limitations, including longitudinal studies, live interaction experiments, and research into explainable memory retrieval strategies to potentially enhance perceived agency.
