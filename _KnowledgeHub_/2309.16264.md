# GAMMA: Generalizable Articulation Modeling and Manipulation for Articulated Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> GAMMA jointly learns articulation structure modeling and part-aware grasp pose affordance from point clouds of articulated objects, then uses physics-guided receding-horizon control that iteratively refines joint parameters from executed trajectories, achieving strong cross-category generalization in simulation and real Franka robot tests.

## Problem

- Prior articulation modeling methods like ANCSH normalize points into a shared coordinate space, which entangles diverse object sizes and structures and breaks joint-axis estimation across unseen categories.
- Affordance-based manipulation methods like Where2Act and VAT-Mart predict grasp actionability without explicit articulation structure knowledge, limiting generalization to novel object categories.
- Open-loop trajectory planning from a single noisy point-cloud observation accumulates joint-parameter errors that repeatedly cause manipulation failures without any correction mechanism.

## Method

- ==Articulation Modeling==: a PointNet++ backbone with segmentation, offset, and joint-parameter heads predicts per-point class, centroid offset, and axis projection, fused via DBSCAN clustering to segment parts and vote joint axis and origin.
- **Part-Aware Grasp Pose Affordance**: concatenates global geometric features, articulation parameter features, and GraspNet-proposed contact pose features through an MLP to score grasp actionability per part.
- ==Physics-Guided Adaptive Manipulation==: plans revolute or prismatic trajectories from estimated joint parameters, executes via receding horizon control, then refines parameters by minimizing Hungarian-matched error between planned and actual trajectories.

## Results

- On unseen categories **GAMMA** raises average part-segmentation AP75 from **66.0%** to **80.2%** and axis error from **26.83** degrees to **8.85** degrees versus ANCSH.
- Across pushing and pulling door and drawer tasks on unseen categories, GAMMA achieves up to **86.86%** success versus **38.06%** for VAT-Mart and **34.52%** for Where2Act.
- Ablations show removing grasp affordance drops unseen-category pulling success from **63.89%** to **53.66%**, and removing adaptive manipulation drops unseen-instance pulling from **48.06%** to **41.62%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Voting joint parameters from projected points in raw coordinate space generalizes better across object sizes than normalizing all points into one canonical coordinate frame.
> - Combining explicit physical structure priors with learned affordance outperforms affordance-only or imitation-only manipulation pipelines on unseen categories.
> - Closing the loop between planned and executed trajectories to iteratively correct kinematic parameter estimates compensates for single-view perception noise at deployment time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of articulated objects by robots is a significant challenge within the field of robotics and artificial intelligence. Articulated objects, such as doors, drawers, and cabinets, are prevalent in human environments and possess diverse geometric shapes, semantic categories, and kinetic constraints (e.g., revolute or prismatic joints). Effectively interacting with these objects requires robots to understand their underlying physical structure and how they move.

Previous research in this domain has primarily focused on several approaches:
1. **Imitation Learning:** Robots learn manipulation policies by directly observing and imitating human demonstrations. While effective in specific scenarios, these methods often struggle with distribution shifts and require extensive, diverse datasets, making them less generalizable to novel objects or situations.
2. **Specific Joint Type Manipulation:** Many works have concentrated on recognizing and manipulating articulated objects with predefined or specific joint types. These methods might estimate joint parameters or identify suitable grasp poses for trajectory planning. However, their applicability is often limited to objects within the same category or with similar kinematic structures, lacking the ability to generalize to unseen objects or cross-category scenarios.
3. **Articulation Modeling:** Advances in large-scale datasets like Shape2motion and PartNet-Mobility have spurred research into segmenting object parts and modeling articulation structures. Methods like ANCSH and ReArtNet aim to estimate per-part and per-joint poses. While achieving accuracy within known categories, generalizability across diverse object categories remains a limitation.
4. **Affordance Learning:** Research into visual affordance seeks to predict the probability of successful interaction at various points on an object. Approaches like Adaafford and Where2act use dense affordance maps, and VAT-MART proposes visual priors for actionable grasp poses. However, these often learn affordance estimation without explicit knowledge of the articulation structure, limiting their generalization across varied articulated objects.
5. **Optimal Control Methods:** Some approaches use dynamic articulation models as constraints to optimize manipulation trajectories. The success of these methods heavily relies on the accuracy of initial parameter estimation, and inaccuracies can lead to repeated failures.

The "GAMMA" framework positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical gap of **generalizability** for articulated object manipulation. It seeks to develop a unified approach that integrates robust articulation modeling, part-aware grasp pose affordance, and adaptive manipulation. Unlike prior works that often treat these components in isolation or struggle with cross-category generalization, GAMMA aims to learn a physics-informed prior from diverse articulated objects, enabling robots to quickly recognize structures, estimate parameters, predict affordances, and refine manipulation policies for novel, unseen, and cross-category objects. This integration and emphasis on generalizability represent a progression towards more versatile and intelligent robotic interaction with the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, GAMMA (Generalizable Articulation Modeling and Manipulation for Articulated Objects), that enables robots to generally model and manipulate diverse articulated objects, including those not encountered during training.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in current robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Ubiquity of Articulated Objects:** Articulated objects are a fundamental component of daily environments, ranging from simple doors and drawers to more complex appliances like refrigerators and washing machines. For robots to operate effectively and autonomously in human-centric spaces, they must be capable of interacting with these objects reliably.
2. **Diversity of Articulated Objects:** Articulated objects exhibit significant diversity in their geometrical shapes, semantic categories (e.g., cabinet vs. microwave), and underlying kinetic constraints (e.g., revolute vs. prismatic joints, varying joint axes, and origins). This variability makes it challenging for a single, fixed manipulation policy or model to generalize across the full spectrum of such objects.
3. **Limitations of Prior Work:**
 - Many existing methods either focus on specific joint types or rely on direct imitation learning, which can be data-intensive and prone to "distribution shift" when applied to novel instances.
 - Articulation modeling techniques, while capable of estimating joint parameters for known categories, often lack the ability to generalize to entirely new object categories or instances with significantly different structures.
 - Affordance learning approaches, which predict actionable grasp points, typically learn without explicit knowledge of the articulation structure, limiting their adaptability to the kinematic constraints of a diverse set of articulated objects.
 - Even optimal control methods, which leverage dynamic models, are vulnerable to inaccuracies in initial parameter estimation, leading to manipulation failures.
4. **Mimicking Human Intuition:** Humans possess an inherent ability to quickly recognize the physical structure (e.g., a pivot or a slide) of an articulated object and apply this understanding to manipulate it, even in unseen scenarios. The motivation is to imbue artificial intelligence with a similar capability, allowing robots to infer kinetic constraints and interact actively and intelligently.
5. **Need for Adaptive and Generalizable Systems:** The ultimate goal is to build general-purpose robots that can manipulate a wide range of articulated objects in varied environments without extensive re-training. This necessitates a framework that can not only model articulations and identify suitable grasp poses but also adaptively refine its understanding and actions in real-time to overcome initial modeling errors.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a method for generalizable articulation modeling from point clouds, capable of segmenting articulated parts and accurately estimating joint parameters for cross-category objects.
- Integrate part-aware grasp pose affordance into the framework to identify optimal and actionable grasp poses that respect the identified articulation structure.
- Implement a physics-guided adaptive manipulation strategy that iteratively refines the articulation model and manipulation trajectories based on real-world interaction, thereby enhancing modeling accuracy and manipulation performance over time.
- Demonstrate strong generalization capabilities of the proposed framework to unseen instances and entirely new categories of articulated objects in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GAMMA framework operates through three interconnected modules: Articulation Modeling, Part-Aware Grasp Pose Affordance, and Physics-Guided Adaptive Manipulation. The overall goal is to leverage an understanding of the physical structure of articulated objects to achieve generalizable, cross-category manipulation.

**A. Articulation Modeling**

This module is responsible for understanding the kinetic constraints of an articulated object by segmenting it into rigid parts and estimating their articulation parameters.

1. **Feature Extraction:** From a single partial point cloud observation P, point-wise features are extracted using a segmentation-style PointNet++ backbone.
2. **Head Networks:** The extracted features are processed by three specialized heads:
 - **Segmentation Head:** Predicts a per-point segmentation class (ˆc_i ∈ {0, 1, 2}) indicating whether a point belongs to a static, revolute, or prismatic part.
 - **Offset Head:** Generates offset vectors (ˆo_i ∈ R^3) that, when applied, shift each point towards the centroid of its corresponding articulated part.
 - **Joint Parameter Head:** Estimates joint parameters by regressing a projection vector (ˆv_i ∈ R^3) for each point (representing its projection onto the joint axis) and the estimated joint axis direction (ˆd_i ∈ R^3).
3. **Joint Optimization:** The parameters of the network are jointly optimized using a loss function L, which combines:
 - Focal loss (L_c) for balancing semantic distribution in segmentation.
 - Offset loss (L_o) which constrains both the L1 distance and direction of point offsets.
 - Projection vector loss (L_v) for similar L1 and direction constraints.
 - Axis direction loss (L_d) for optimizing the joint axis direction estimation.
4. **Post-Training Parameter Estimation:** After training, the network parameters are frozen for inference.
 - **Semantics-aware and Axis-aware Clustering:** Points identified as revolute or prismatic by the segmentation head are further processed. They are first shifted by their predicted offset vectors (p_i + ˆo_i) to make intra-part points spatially closer. Then, they are shifted by their projection vectors (p_i + ˆv_i) to make intra-part points spatially linear along the common axis.
 - **DBSCAN Clustering:** DBSCAN is applied to these transformed feature sets to group points into distinct part clusters.
 - **Joint Parameter Voting:** For each segmented part, the estimated per-point projection vectors and axis directions are used in a voting scheme to determine the final joint parameters (unit vector u_i, origin q_i, and joint type c_i).

**B. Part-Aware Grasp Pose Affordance**

This module extends the articulation model by predicting where and how a robot can effectively interact with the articulated object, considering its identified structure.

1. **Grasp Pose Generation:** An initial set of potential grasp poses is generated using Grasp-Net.
2. **Feature Concatenation:** For each generated grasp pose, a combined feature vector is formed by concatenating:
 - Global geometrical features (f_gg) from the PointNet++ backbone.
 - Articulation parameter features (f_ap) derived from the articulation model.
 - Contact pose features (f_gp) from a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
3. **Actionability Score Prediction:** These concatenated features are then used to predict an actionability score (s_g|P,ψ ∈). This score evaluates the feasibility and effectiveness of each grasp pose, guiding the robot to choose ideal interaction points that are suitable for manipulation given the object's articulation.

**C. Physics-Guided Adaptive Manipulation**

This module addresses the inherent inaccuracies and uncertainties in the initial articulation model derived from visual observation by introducing an adaptive, closed-loop refinement process.

1. **Trajectory Planning:**
 - **Revolute Joint:** For a contact point p on a revolute joint defined by axis u and origin q, the trajectory for a rotation angle θ is calculated using a rotation matrix and translation component. This allows planning a feasible rotational trajectory.
 - **Prismatic Joint:** For a contact point p on a prismatic joint defined by axis u, the trajectory for a translation distance δ is a simple linear movement along the axis (p + δu).
 - An open-loop trajectory τ_plan_t is generated for L steps based on the current articulation parameters ψ.
2. **Receding Horizon Control:** To mitigate the impact of errors, only the first H steps (where H < L) of the planned trajectory are executed by the robot. This executed portion constitutes the actual trajectory τ_actual_t.
3. **Adaptive Parameter Optimization:** The articulation parameters ψ are iteratively updated by minimizing the difference between the planned and actual trajectories.
 - A matching matrix C_t (H×L) is used, typically derived via the Hungarian algorithm, to find the best correspondence between actual and planned trajectory points.
 - The loss function L_t(ψ) is defined as the sum of squared L2 distances between corresponding points in the actual and planned trajectories, weighted by the matching matrix.
 - By repeatedly planning, executing, and optimizing, GAMMA continuously reduces modeling errors and improves manipulation performance over time, making the system robust to initial perceptual inaccuracies.

In summary, GAMMA integrates perception (articulation modeling and grasp affordance) with action (physics-guided adaptive manipulation) in a closed-loop learning framework, enabling robust and generalizable interaction with a wide array of articulated objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate GAMMA encompassed both simulated and real-world environments, focusing on articulation modeling accuracy and manipulation success rates, particularly for unseen and cross-category objects.

**A. Experimental Setup**

- **Environments:** The SAPIEN simulator was used for extensive training and evaluation due to its physics simulation and photo-realistic rendering. Real-world experiments were performed with a 7-DoF Franka robot equipped with an RGB-D RealSense L515 camera for point cloud sensing.
- **Datasets:** Training utilized the PartNet-Mobility dataset, comprising 562 articulated objects across 7 categories. Specifically, 4 categories (cabinet, door, refrigerator, microwave) were used for training, while 3 entirely "unseen categories" (safe, table, washing machine) were reserved for testing generalization. Additionally, "unseen instances" within the training categories were used for validation to prevent overfitting. Point clouds were generated from multiple viewpoints and states. For real-world experiments, two unseen objects (cabinet, microwave) were manipulated.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Articulation Modeling:** ANCSH, a state-of-the-art method.
 - **Manipulation:**
 - RL (TD3): A reinforcement learning baseline.
 - Where2Act: A method that selects grasping points with high actionability for short-term manipulation.
 - VAT-Mart: Utilizes 3D object-centric actionable visual priors for trajectory proposals.
 - **Ablation Studies:** Two variants of GAMMA were tested: GAMMA without adaptive manipulation (w/o adpt.) and GAMMA without grasp pose affordance (w/o afford.).

**B. Articulated Object Modeling Results**

- **Tasks:** Evaluating the ability of models to segment articulated parts and estimate joint parameters from point clouds.
- **Metrics:** Average Precision at IoU 0.75 (AP75) for instance part segmentation, Joint Type Classification Accuracy (Type Acc.), Joint Axis Error (degrees), and Joint Origin Error (cm).
- **Findings:**
 - Both ANCSH and GAMMA demonstrated high joint type prediction accuracy across most categories. However, ANCSH struggled with novel categories like "table," which have complex structures, suggesting a lack of robustness to diverse object designs.
 - GAMMA significantly outperformed ANCSH in segmenting articulated parts (higher AP75). This was attributed to GAMMA's use of a richer feature representation obtained by clustering points through both offset (towards centroid) and projection (onto joint axis) vectors, making articulated parts more distinguishable in the transformed space.
 - In estimating joint parameters, ANCSH showed success on seen objects but exhibited larger axis and origin errors, particularly in cross-category tasks. The authors suggest that ANCSH's reliance on a normalized coordinate space, which works well for similarly sized objects, becomes problematic for objects with diverse sizes and structures. In contrast, GAMMA's method of voting for joint parameters using projected points and estimated axis directions, without relying on normalization, proved more generalizable to unseen categories. For instance, in unseen categories, GAMMA achieved an average axis error of 8.85° and origin error of 8.20 cm, compared to ANCSH's 26.83° and 16.3 cm, respectively.

**C. Articulation Manipulation Results**

- **Tasks:** Four manipulation tasks (pushing door, pulling door, pushing drawer, pulling drawer) were evaluated on "unseen instances" (from training categories) and "unseen categories" (totally new objects).
- **Metrics:** Success rate (%).
- **Findings:**
 - **Baseline Comparison:**
 - RL (TD3) showed low success rates (e.g., <6%), indicating its difficulty in learning from scratch and generalizing.
 - Where2Act achieved modest improvements (e.g., around 30% for pushing tasks), but its short-term action planning limited its effectiveness in long-horizon manipulation.
 - VAT-Mart substantially improved success rates over RL and Where2Act (e.g., 53.84% for pushing door on unseen instances), demonstrating the benefit of visual action affordance and trajectory proposals.
 - **GAMMA (ours) significantly outperformed all baselines across all tasks and generalization scenarios.** For instance, in pushing a door on unseen categories, GAMMA achieved an 86.86% success rate, compared to VAT-Mart's 38.06%. Similarly, for pulling a drawer on unseen categories, GAMMA achieved 42.11% versus VAT-Mart's 29.07%. This indicates that GAMMA's integrated approach, combining robust articulation modeling, affordance, and adaptive control, contributes to superior and generalizable manipulation performance.
 - **Ablation Studies:**
 - **GAMMA (w/o afford.):** Removing grasp pose affordance (randomly choosing grasp poses) led to a significant decrease in success rates (e.g., 51.79% vs. 72.31% for pushing door on unseen instances). This highlights the crucial role of selecting actionable and feasible grasp poses informed by the articulation model.
 - **GAMMA (w/o adpt.):** Disabling adaptive manipulation (using only open-loop trajectory planning) also caused a notable reduction in success rates, particularly for "unseen instances" (e.g., 69.62% vs. 72.31% for pushing door). This confirms that iterative refinement of articulation parameters based on actual robot interaction is essential for overcoming initial modeling inaccuracies and improving manipulation robustness. The impact was sometimes less pronounced in "unseen categories," potentially because the initial model might already have a higher error floor for entirely novel objects, making the adaptive refinement relatively more critical in scenarios where initial estimates are plausible but imperfect.

**D. Real-World Experiments**

- **Tasks:** Manipulation of a real-world cabinet (pulling door and drawer) and a microwave (pulling door).
- **Findings:**
 - The model trained solely on simulated data demonstrated strong transferability to real-world point clouds, exhibiting reasonable axis and origin errors (e.g., microwave: 6.14° axis error, 7.36 cm origin error; cabinet door: 5.33° axis error, 7.7 cm origin error). This suggests good sim-to-real generalization for the perception module.
 - The overall GAMMA framework, including articulation modeling, grasp affordance, and adaptive manipulation, successfully executed pulling tasks on both a cabinet and a microwave in real-world settings. This verified the practical effectiveness and generalization ability of the proposed method beyond simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GAMMA framework represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation of articulated objects, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Generalizability:** The primary contribution of GAMMA is its ability to generalize manipulation skills to unseen objects and entirely new categories of articulated objects. This addresses a fundamental limitation in previous research, which often required extensive re-training or was restricted to specific object types. By learning a "physics-informed prior" and integrating it throughout the perception-action loop, GAMMA allows robots to interact effectively with a broader range of objects encountered in unstructured environments.
2. **Robust Articulation Modeling:** The proposed articulation modeling module demonstrates superior performance in segmenting articulated parts and accurately estimating joint parameters, particularly for diverse and previously unencountered objects. This robust understanding of an object's kinematic structure is foundational for any intelligent manipulation.
3. **Adaptive Learning for Real-World Robustness:** The inclusion of physics-guided adaptive manipulation is significant. By iteratively refining articulation parameters based on actual robot interaction, GAMMA effectively mitigates initial perceptual errors and uncertainties inherent in real-world observations. This closed-loop learning process enhances the robustness and reliability of manipulation, enabling the robot to continuously improve its understanding and performance over time.
4. **Integrated Perception-Action System:** GAMMA effectively integrates articulation modeling (perception), grasp pose affordance (perception-to-action mapping), and adaptive manipulation (action and learning from interaction). This holistic approach contrasts with methods that treat these components in isolation, leading to a more coherent and capable system for complex interaction tasks.
5. **Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The successful application of a simulation-trained model to real-world robots and objects underscores the practical utility of GAMMA. This capability is crucial for reducing the cost and time associated with real-world data collection and training, accelerating the deployment of robotic systems.
6. **Potential for Autonomous Robotic Systems:** The advancements offered by GAMMA could contribute to the development of more autonomous and intelligent robotic systems capable of performing diverse tasks in everyday environments, such as homes, offices, and warehouses. Robots equipped with such capabilities could efficiently open doors, pull drawers, or interact with appliances without human intervention or prior specific programming for each object.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a robust framework and demonstrates key principles for achieving generalizable manipulation. It could serve as a foundation for future research into more complex multi-articulated objects, more diverse joint types, and incorporating additional sensory modalities or reasoning capabilities.

In conclusion, GAMMA's ability to learn and adapt its understanding of articulated objects, coupled with its demonstrated performance in novel scenarios and real-world transfer, marks a significant step towards general-purpose robot manipulation in complex and dynamic environments.
