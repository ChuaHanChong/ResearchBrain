# Understanding World or Predicting Future? A Comprehensive Survey of World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University present a comprehensive survey of world models, proposing a dual categorization based on understanding implicit world representations and predicting future states. The work analyzes world model applications across generative games, robotics, autonomous driving, and social simulacra, while outlining critical open challenges.

## Problem

- Existing literature lacked a systematic definition of world models and a comprehensive analysis of their diverse requirements across applications.
- A persistent debate exists regarding whether a world model's core function is to understand the current world state or primarily to predict future states for decision-making.
- Recent capabilities of large multimodal AI models necessitate a re-evaluation of what constitutes a 'world model' and how current AI systems align with this concept.

## Method

- Introduced a novel categorization of world models based on two primary functions: constructing ==implicit representations== to understand the external world and ==predicting future states== to simulate and guide decision-making.
- Contextualized modern world model advancements within a historical narrative, tracing their evolution from early ==cognitive theories== to contemporary ==deep learning== paradigms.
- Systematically examined how these dual functionalities of world models are leveraged across diverse application domains, including generative games, embodied intelligence, urban intelligence, and societal intelligence.

## Results

- Provided a comprehensive overview of world model applications, demonstrating their potential in game intelligence, embodied intelligence (robotics), urban intelligence (autonomous driving), and societal intelligence (social simulacra).
- Identified critical open problems for world models, including ensuring robust physical rule adherence, enriching the social dimension, developing standardized benchmarks, and bridging the simulation-to-reality gap.
- Highlighted the need to address ethical and safety concerns related to data privacy, the potential for simulating unsafe scenarios, and accountability for AI-generated content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World models primarily serve two functions: constructing implicit internal representations of world mechanisms for informed decision-making, and explicitly simulating and forecasting future states of the world, often through visual data.
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) are emerging as powerful backbones for acquiring and leveraging extensive latent 'world knowledge' encompassing common sense, spatial, temporal, and social understanding.
> - The field is rapidly moving towards creating fully interactive and embodied 3D simulations, shifting from passive video generation to physically constrained, first-person, action-conditioned future world predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of "world models" has long been central to the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), with its roots tracing back to early AI and cognitive science. The recent explosion in capabilities of large-scale AI models, particularly multimodal large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 and advanced video generation models such as Sora, has reignited and intensified discussions around world models. These models demonstrate an unprecedented ability to capture and simulate aspects of world knowledge, prompting a critical re-evaluation of what constitutes a "world model" and how current AI systems align with this concept.

The authors explicitly position their work by identifying gaps in existing literature. They classify prior surveys into two categories: those focusing on specific applications (e.g., video processing, autonomous driving, agent-based systems) and those concentrating on the technological transition from multimodal models to world models. While valuable, these prior works often lack a systematic definition of a world model and a comprehensive analysis of the diverse requirements different real-world applications place on these models.

This survey aims to bridge these gaps by offering a comprehensive, systematic review that formally defines and categorizes world models. It contextualizes modern advancements within a historical narrative, tracing the evolution from early model-based reinforcement learning and psychological theories of "mental models" to contemporary deep learning paradigms. By emphasizing a dual perspective—"understanding the world" through implicit representations and "predicting the future" through explicit simulations—the paper provides a much-needed unifying framework. This work thus serves as a foundational resource, offering clarity and structure to a complex and rapidly developing field, enabling researchers to better comprehend the current state-of-the-art and identify promising future directions towards realizing AGI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this comprehensive survey stems from the recent surge in interest and capabilities surrounding world models, largely driven by breakthroughs in large multimodal AI, such as GPT-4 and Sora. These advancements have demonstrated an emergent capacity for AI systems to capture world knowledge and simulate physical dynamics, but simultaneously raised fundamental questions about whether they truly constitute comprehensive "world models" and how they fit into the broader quest for AGI.

The authors identify a persistent debate regarding the core definition of a world model: should it prioritize understanding the current state of the world by constructing internal representations, or should its main function be to predict future states to guide decision-making? Video generation models, for instance, align more with the predictive aspect, while models inspired by cognitive theories emphasize understanding. A systematic review is therefore deemed "timely and essential" to synthesize the latest advancements and clarify the conceptual landscape.

The key objectives of this survey are meticulously outlined:
1. **Novel Categorization System**: To introduce a structured categorization of world models based on two primary functions: (1) constructing implicit representations to understand the underlying mechanisms of the external world, and (2) predicting future states to simulate and guide decision-making. The first category focuses on internalizing world knowledge for subsequent decision support, while the second emphasizes enhancing predictive and simulative capabilities from visual perceptions.
2. **Application-Specific Analysis**: To classify how various key application domains—including generative games, autonomous driving, robotics, and social simulacra—utilize and emphasize different aspects of these two core world model functionalities.
3. **Future Research Roadmap**: To highlight critical open problems, challenges, and outline potential future research directions and trends that can enable world models to adapt to an even broader spectrum of practical applications.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to provide a clearer, more organized understanding of the world model landscape, fostering coherent research and development in this pivotal area of AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and comprehensive review methodology to survey the vast and rapidly expanding literature on world models. Their approach is structured around a novel categorization system designed to bring clarity to the field's diverse definitions and applications.

The core of their methodology involves a two-pronged functional categorization of world models:
1. **Implicit Representation of the External World (Section 3)**: This category focuses on models that construct internal, abstract representations of environmental changes to facilitate informed decision-making. The review delves into:
 - **World Models in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL)**: Examining how agents learn environment dynamics (transition models and reward functions) to support policy optimization, including techniques for representation learning in high-dimensional spaces (e.g., Ha and Schmidhuber's RWM, Dreamer series) and policy generation methods (e.g., Model Predictive Control, Monte Carlo Tree Search).
 - **World Models with Language Backbone**: Exploring the role of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) in acquiring and leveraging extensive "world knowledge" (common sense, spatial, temporal, social understanding) for decision-making. This includes direct action generation and modular usage of LLMs as components in planning systems. The section further distinguishes knowledge of the global physical world, local physical world, and human society.

2. **Future Predictions of the Physical World (Section 4)**: This category emphasizes models capable of actively simulating and forecasting future states of the world, primarily through visual and spatial data. The review covers:
 - **World Model as Video Generation**: Analyzing generative models (e.g., Sora, Cosmos, Genie) that produce realistic video sequences, showcasing their capabilities in long-term prediction, multi-modal integration, interactivity, and modeling diverse environments. The transition from passive video generation to interactive simulations is highlighted.
 - **World Model as Embodied Environment**: Investigating the evolution towards fully interactive and embodied 3D simulations. This sub-categorization distinguishes between indoor, outdoor, and dynamic environments (e.g., AI2-THOR, MetaUrban, UniSim), emphasizing the shift from static, visual-only representations to physically constrained, first-person, action-conditioned future world predictions.

Beyond this core categorization, the methodology extends to:
- **Historical Contextualization**: Tracing the evolution of world model concepts from pre-deep learning eras (Minsky's frames, Craik's mental models, Johnson-Laird's Mental Models Theory) to modern AI, thereby grounding contemporary discussions in foundational cognitive principles.
- **Application Domain Analysis (Section 5)**: Systematically examining how these dual functionalities of world models are leveraged across diverse domains such as game intelligence, embodied intelligence (robotics), urban intelligence (autonomous driving, logistics, analytics), and societal intelligence (social simulacra). This highlights the practical utility and specific requirements of world models in different contexts.
- **Identification of Open Problems and Future Directions (Section 6)**: Concluding the survey by pinpointing key challenges and outlining promising avenues for future research, including physical rule adherence, enriching social dimensions, developing robust benchmarks, bridging simulation-to-reality gaps, improving simulation efficiency, and addressing ethical/safety concerns.

The authors also provide a publicly accessible GitHub repository, consolidating representative papers and code, which enhances the utility and reproducibility of their survey. This comprehensive and structured approach allows for a deep dive into technical advancements while maintaining a high-level overview of the conceptual framework and its implications across various fields.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's comprehensive review yields several key findings and establishes a novel framework for understanding world models in the era of advanced AI.

**Core Definition and Categorization:**
The central finding is the formalization of world models as systems primarily serving two functions: (1) **constructing implicit representations** to understand the world's mechanisms, and (2) **predicting future states** to simulate and guide decision-making. This dual perspective provides a robust lens through which to analyze diverse world model approaches.

**Implicit Representation of the External World:**
- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):** The survey highlights MBRL as a foundational area, where world models learn environment dynamics (transition and reward functions) to enable planning and policy optimization. Key developments include learning latent representations for high-dimensional data (e.g., Ha and Schmidhuber's Recurrent World Model, DeepMind's Dreamer series) and leveraging planning algorithms like Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). Recent work like TD-MPC2 demonstrates scalability in continuous control tasks.
- **World Knowledge with Language Backbone:** A significant finding is the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) as powerful backbones for world model construction. LLMs, through vast pre-training, acquire rich latent world knowledge (spatial, temporal, common sense, social dynamics) which can be used for direct action generation or as modules in complex planning systems. The survey categorizes this knowledge into global physical world, local physical world, and human society, noting that while LLMs show promise in capturing these, there are still significant gaps, especially in terms of precision and robustness for real-world applications.

**Future Prediction of the Physical World:**
- **World Model as Video Generation:** This has become a dominant paradigm, with models like Sora, Cosmos, and Genie demonstrating unprecedented capabilities in generating high-quality, temporally consistent video sequences. These models explicitly simulate world evolution, understanding temporal dynamics, spatial consistency, and physical laws (though not perfectly). Key capabilities include long-term predictive ability, multi-modal integration, interactivity, and adaptation to diverse environments.
- **World Model as Embodied Environment:** The field is rapidly moving from purely visual video generation to creating interactive 3D embodied environments. These models aim to represent not just visual elements but also physical and spatial interactions. The survey categorizes these into indoor (e.g., AI2-THOR, iGibson), outdoor (e.g., MetaUrban, UrbanWorld), and dynamic environments (e.g., UniSim, Pandora, Roboscape), emphasizing the shift toward first-person, action-conditioned future world prediction with increasing integration of physical constraints.

**Application Domains:**
The survey provides a comprehensive overview of world model applications, demonstrating their transformative potential:
- **Game Intelligence:** World models are enabling generative game systems that offer unprecedented interactivity, temporal and numerical consistency, and generalization across diverse environments, moving beyond manually scripted gameplay (e.g., GameNGen, MineWorld, WHAM).
- **Embodied Intelligence (Robotics):** Crucial for equipping robots with the ability to perceive, predict, and act effectively. World models facilitate learning implicit representations (visual, 3D, task-specific via LLMs) and predicting future states for synthetic data generation, action guidance (e.g., UniPi, VPP), and policy evaluation. They are also instrumental in bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Urban Intelligence:** Applied across autonomous driving (learning implicit scene representations, end-to-end world simulators for planning), autonomous logistics (micromobility, aerial vehicles), and urban analytics (leveraging LLMs for urban scene understanding, human mobility modeling, and geographical prediction).
- **Societal Intelligence:** LLM-driven social simulacra serve as explicit world models mirroring real-world societies (e.g., Generative Agents, AgentSociety), and help agents develop implicit world models (internal representations of environment, memory, reasoning for social behaviors, e.g., Agent-Pro, GovSim).

**Functions and Challenges:**
World models function as either cloud-based environments (data engines, RL training, policy evaluators) or edge-side agent brains (latent space compression, on-device planning). However, the survey identifies critical open problems:
- **Physical Rules & Counterfactual Simulation:** Current data-driven models struggle with robustly acquiring physical laws and generalizing to out-of-distribution counterfactual scenarios, highlighting the need for hybrid approaches integrating explicit physics.
- **Enriching the Social Dimension:** Simulating realistic, complex human behavior and social interaction remains a significant challenge, requiring better integration of human behavioral theories and scalable evaluation schemes.
- **Benchmarks:** The lack of diverse, realistic, and standardized benchmarks hinders rigorous testing of generalization and specific capabilities (e.g., physical, social, embodied decision-making).
- **Bridging Simulation and Reality:** Closing the sim-to-real gap for embodied agents requires improved multi-modal, multi-task, and 3D capacities, potentially through self-reinforcing loops between world models and agents.
- **Simulation Efficiency:** The computational cost of large generative models and complex physics simulators poses a challenge, necessitating strategies for acceleration and synergy between different AI models.
- **Ethical and Safety Concerns:** Significant risks related to data privacy, the potential for simulating unsafe scenarios, and accountability for AI-generated content (e.g., misinformation, deepfakes) must be addressed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey holds significant value for the scientific community, particularly for researchers and practitioners in artificial intelligence, robotics, cognitive science, and related application domains. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Conceptual Clarity and Unification**: By formally defining and systematically categorizing world models based on their dual functions—understanding implicit representations and predicting future states—the survey brings much-needed clarity to a rapidly evolving and often ambiguously defined field. This unified framework helps researchers to better contextualize diverse approaches and fosters a more coherent dialogue across sub-disciplines.

2. **Roadmap for AGI Research**: The paper underscores the fundamental role of world models in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence. By dissecting current capabilities and highlighting limitations, it provides a clear roadmap for advancing AI towards systems capable of more sophisticated perception, prediction, planning, and truly general reasoning, which is essential for AGI.

3. **Cross-Disciplinary Synthesis**: The work masterfully bridges concepts from deep learning, reinforcement learning, generative AI, and cognitive science (e.g., mental models, theory of mind). This interdisciplinary synthesis enriches the understanding of AI's cognitive underpinnings and suggests new avenues for bio-inspired AI research.

4. **Identification of Critical Research Frontiers**: The detailed exposition of open problems—ranging from the integration of robust physical laws and the accurate simulation of human social dynamics to the development of rigorous benchmarks and efficient simulation methods—serves as a crucial guide for future research. It directs attention to areas where current models fall short and where concerted effort is most needed to unlock next-generation capabilities.

5. **Practical Guidance for Application Development**: By meticulously reviewing the application of world models in domains like generative games, autonomous driving, robotics, and social simulacra, the survey offers invaluable insights for practitioners. It illustrates how these models can transform various industries by enabling more interactive, adaptive, and intelligent systems, while also outlining the specific challenges pertinent to each application area.

6. **Emphasis on Ethical and Safety Considerations**: The inclusion of a dedicated section on ethical and safety concerns (data privacy, simulating unsafe scenarios, accountability) is particularly timely and impactful. It raises awareness about the responsible development and deployment of increasingly powerful world models, prompting researchers to integrate safeguards and ethical considerations from the outset.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing literature; it is a critical synthesis that structures the current landscape of world models, redefines their core purpose, and proactively charts a course for future innovation. It provides a foundational reference for anyone seeking to understand, develop, or apply world models in the journey toward more intelligent and autonomous AI systems.
