# Learning Human-Humanoid Coordination for Collaborative Object Carrying

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed COLA, a framework enabling humanoid robots to compliantly and coordinately carry objects with humans, leveraging a proprioception-only policy learned through a three-step training process. The system achieved a 24.7% reduction in human effort and demonstrated stable performance across diverse objects and terrains in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing human-humanoid collaboration methods are often rigid, rely on predefined heuristic rules, or lack whole-body coordination capabilities for complex tasks like object carrying.
- Integrating advanced humanoid locomotion and manipulation skills into truly compliant collaboration with humans, especially in dynamic environments, remains challenging.
- Prior approaches frequently depend on external sensors (e.g., cameras, force sensors) or explicit models of human intention, complicating real-world deployment and limiting robustness.

## Method

- A three-step learning pipeline starts with training a foundational ==Whole-Body Control (WBC)== policy for robust locomotion and general manipulation.
- A residual teacher policy is trained in a closed-loop simulation environment with privileged object information, implicitly learning collaborative dynamics and human intentions.
- The teacher's expertise is distilled into a proprioception-only student policy using behavioral cloning, enabling robust and practical real-world deployment without external sensors.

## Results

- COLA achieved superior performance in simulation, demonstrating the lowest linear velocity tracking error (**0.102** m/s) and angular velocity tracking error (**0.098** rad/s), alongside enhanced object stability.
- The framework reduced human effort by **24.7%** in simulation, achieving the lowest average external force (**12.298** N), which was further corroborated by real-world human user studies.
- Real-world experiments successfully validated COLA's ability to carry diverse objects across varied terrains and movement patterns, exhibiting implicit learning of human intention and flexible leader/follower role switching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint state offsets can effectively proxy interaction forces, allowing a humanoid to implicitly estimate and respond to physical interactions using only proprioceptive feedback.
> - The state of the carried object implicitly encodes collaboration constraints, which can be leveraged by a learning agent to infer optimal collaborative behaviors.
> - Training a residual policy on top of a pre-trained whole-body control policy efficiently enables complex collaborative skills by learning corrective adjustments rather than entirely new actions.
