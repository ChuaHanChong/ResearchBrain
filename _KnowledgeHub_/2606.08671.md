# SkillHone: A Harness for Continual Agent Skill Evolution Through Persistent Decision History

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillHone introduces an agent-facing harness for continually evolving large language model skills by maintaining a persistent, structured decision history alongside the skill artifact, integrating role-separated subagents for optimization and evaluation. This approach achieves significant performance improvements, outperforming existing skill evolution methods by up to 28.3 points on open-web deep-research benchmarks and improving accuracy by an average of 18.8 points in internal analytical scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing LLM agent skill evolution methods discard critical decision context (diagnoses, rejected alternatives, evaluation evidence) after each revision, leading to inefficient maintenance and redundant efforts.
- Current artifact-centered approaches treat an improved skill as a final product, preventing truly continual and interpretable skill evolution across multiple development sessions in dynamic environments.
- Optimization agents risk "memorization" by having direct access to unredacted evaluation assets, potentially leading to skills that are overly specific to training examples rather than generalizable.

## Method

- **SkillHone** implements an ==agent-facing harness== that manages separate skill and skill-evaluation repositories, linked by a structured, ==persistent decision history== for each revision.
- It employs ==role-separated subagents==: an ==Optimization Agent Team== with write access to skills and *redacted* evaluation reports, and an ==Evaluation Agent Team== with read access to *unredacted* evaluation data but no skill modification privileges (preventing ==memorization==).
- A ==dispatcher== routes messages and records each development step as a ==decision record== (diagnosis, candidate revision, redacted evaluation evidence, outcome), preserving the rationale for future iterations.

## Results

- SkillHone achieved the highest average scores on deep-research benchmarks in a raw open-web setting, outperforming existing baselines by up to **28.3 points** on WebWalkerQA-EN and **22.9 points** on GAIA.
- Ablation studies indicated that persistent decision history contributed more significantly (**13.4-point** performance drop without it) than role separation (**6.4-point** drop without it).
- Skills developed with SkillHone demonstrated strong cross-backbone transferability, achieving **72.4%** on GAIA with Claude Sonnet 4.6, **10.2 points** higher than the best baseline on that backbone.
- In internal deployment, SkillHone improved the accuracy of recurring analytical skills by an average of **18.8 points** across seven scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining a persistent, structured decision history of diagnoses, revisions, evidence, and outcomes is crucial for enabling continual, context-aware skill evolution and preventing redundant optimizations.
> - Role separation between skill optimization (using redacted feedback) and evaluation (using unredacted data) is vital for preventing memorization and fostering the development of generalizable, robust skills.
> - The proposed harness architecture allows agents to effectively organize complex task procedures for deep-research tasks, leading to skills that transfer well across different LLM execution backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language model (LLM)-based agents has increasingly relied on "skills" – defined as named, loadable bundles of task-specific procedures, scripts, references, and output conventions. These skills allow LLM agents to perform specialized tasks and adapt to various environments, serving as a primary unit of specialization in deployed agent systems such as Claude Code, Codex, and Hermes.

The broader research landscape for LLM agents is concerned with several aspects:
1. **Skill Acquisition and Construction**: Initial methods focus on synthesizing skills from task descriptions, examples, or documents (e.g., Skill-Creator).
2. **Skill Optimization and Improvement**: Reflective optimization methods (e.g., GEPA-style approaches like Hermes-Agent-Self-Evolution, Reflexion-style agents, DSPy) iterate on skills, prompts, tool descriptions, and code within a bounded run, aiming to produce a better artifact.
3. **Multi-Agent Systems and Collaboration**: Research explores how role specialization in multi-agent LLM systems can improve problem-solving and produce software-like artifacts (e.g., MetaGPT, SWE-style benchmarks).

This paper identifies a gap in existing skill improvement methodologies: while current approaches are effective in producing or enhancing a skill within a single optimization run, they typically treat the improved skill as a final artifact. This "artifact-centered view" discards the decision context, including diagnoses, rejected alternatives, evaluation evidence, and rationale, that led to a particular revision. Consequently, later agents attempting to further improve or maintain a skill may repeat prior optimizations, reintroduce old issues, or struggle to interpret past changes, especially as environments and APIs continually evolve.

SkillHone positions itself as an agent-facing harness that addresses this context loss. It integrates with existing agent runtimes but shifts the focus from merely producing a better skill artifact to maintaining a persistent, auditable decision history alongside the evolving skill. By doing so, it enables *continual* skill evolution across many development sessions, allowing later agents to understand the rationale behind prior changes and avoid redundant efforts, thus contributing to the robustness and maintainability of long-lived agent skills. This work extends the capabilities of skill optimization by embedding it within a structured, historical context, moving beyond single-run, scalar-signal-driven improvements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central methodological question addressed by this work is how agents can continually improve a skill across multiple development sessions without losing the rationale behind earlier changes. The paper's key objectives and motivations stem from the identified limitations of existing artifact-centered skill evolution methods.

The primary motivations are:

1. **Addressing Context Loss in Skill Evolution**: Current methods, whether synthesis-based or reflective optimization, typically discard the critical decision history once a skill revision is complete. This includes the diagnoses that motivated a revision, the alternatives considered and rejected, the specific evaluation evidence observed, and the reasons for accepting or rejecting an outcome. Without this context, later agents inherit only the latest skill artifact, making it difficult to understand the "why" behind changes. This can lead to inefficient maintenance, redundant fixes, or even regressions when environments (e.g., API rate limits, format changes) evolve over time. The paper is motivated by the need to preserve this rationale for long-lived skills.

2. **Enabling Continual, Interpretable Skill Maintenance**: Skill development is presented not as a one-shot event but as an ongoing process of creation, debugging, adaptation, and updates. This continuous loop necessitates that the rationale behind every revision remains preserved and maintainable throughout a skill's existence. The objective is to design a system that allows later agents to interpret prior revisions, evaluation results, and rejected alternatives, thereby facilitating more effective and informed future improvements.

3. **Preventing Memorization and Promoting Robustness**: When agents are exposed to unredacted evaluation assets (e.g., probe targets, validators, traces), there is a risk of "memorization" where the agent's skill optimization becomes overly specific to the training examples rather than learning generalizable procedures. A key motivation is to separate the optimization process from direct access to sensitive evaluation data, ensuring that skills improve based on generalized feedback rather than specific test-set knowledge.

4. **Improving Performance on Complex, Dynamic Tasks**: Deep-research tasks (e.g., those requiring complex search, navigation, extraction, and recovery across dynamic web sources) are highlighted as environments where skills are crucial and frequently require adaptation due to external changes (rate limits, API failures, format changes). The objective is to develop a system that can effectively evolve skills to handle such challenges, ultimately leading to improved agent performance on these difficult, real-world tasks.

To achieve these motivations, the key objectives of SkillHone are:

1. **Introduce a Harness for Continual Skill Evolution**: Develop a general agent-facing harness that maintains evolving skills along with supporting evaluation assets.
2. **Record Structured Decision History**: Implement a mechanism to record structured histories of diagnoses, proposed revisions, evaluation-side evidence, and outcomes for skill revisions. This history must be more than a mere version diff, linking changes to their motivating problems and evaluation results.
3. **Implement Role-Separated Subagents**: Design an architecture using role-bounded subagent dispatches with separated permissions. This ensures that optimization subagents (proposing revisions) only see redacted evidence, while evaluation subagents (running skills on probes) can access unredacted data but cannot directly modify the skill.
4. **Demonstrate Performance Improvement and Transferability**: Evaluate the system's ability to outperform existing skill evolution methods and commercial deep-research agents on public benchmarks and demonstrate that the developed skills transfer effectively across different LLM execution backbones.
5. **Validate Utility in Real-World Scenarios**: Deploy SkillHone in internal, tool-mediated analysis scenarios to confirm its practical utility in improving existing, recurring analytical skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkillHone is introduced as an agent-facing harness designed for continual skill evolution. Its methodology is grounded in persistent decision history and the use of role-separated subagents operating within a structured framework.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

A skill is defined as a loadable artifact encapsulating instructions, procedures, references, and output conventions for a class of tasks. SkillHone's objective is to maintain and improve this skill artifact across repeated development sessions while keeping the model backend and external tools fixed.

It manages two linked repositories:
- **Skill Repository (R_s)**: Stores the evolving skill bundle (SKILL.md, scripts, references, templates, etc.).
- **Skill-Evaluation Repository (R_e)**: Contains practice probes, oracle targets, validators, traces, redacted reports, and other evaluation assets.

Crucially, the optimization side can only observe *redacted* reports from R_e, never unredacted targets, validators, or traces. This separation prevents direct memorization of evaluation data. Accepted revisions produce a sequence of skills (S_0..., S_T) guided by practice feedback and the persistent decision history.

#### 3.2. SkillHone Harness Architecture

As depicted in Figure 2, SkillHone separates each development step into optimization and evaluation dispatches. This is formalized as M = (T_opt, T_eval, D):

- **Optimization Agent Team (T_opt)**:
 - Maintains the skill repository.
 - Subagents (e.g., Proposer, Explorer, Developer, Reviewer, Decider) can inspect the current skill, read prior decision records and failure summaries, form diagnoses, propose typed revisions, review candidate changes, and record accepted outcomes.
 - They have write access to R_s but *no access* to unredacted evaluation assets (probe targets, validators, execution traces).
 - Typical dispatches include diagnosing failures from redacted reports, integrating resources, proposing scoped revisions, and drafting patches to the current skill.

- **Evaluation Agent Team (T_eval)**:
 - Operates on the skill-evaluation repository.
 - Subagents (e.g., Executor, Diagnoser, Reporter, Auditor) can inspect oracle targets and traces, run the current skill on probe items, analyze failures, and produce redacted reports.
 - They have read access to R_e but *cannot write* to the skill repository R_s.
 - This separation ensures that practice feedback does not lead to direct memorization.

- **Dispatcher (D)**:
 - Acts as a message router between the two teams.
 - Routes redacted evidence and review requests.
 - Records accepted outcomes.
 - It is not a decision authority and does not directly edit either repository.

#### 3.3. Persistent Decision History

A core design principle is to make skill evolution persistent across sessions. This means later agents inherit not just the latest skill snapshot but also a structured record of *why* the skill changed.

Each development step is represented as a decision record:
h_t = (q_t, r_t, e_t, o_t)
- **q_t (diagnosis)**: The problem or failure mode identified.
- **r_t (candidate revision)**: The proposed change to the skill.
- **e_t (redacted evaluation evidence)**: Summarized feedback from the evaluation side.
- **o_t (outcome)**: The decision (ACCEPT, REVISE, REJECT, DEFER).

The cumulative history up to iteration t is H_t = {h_1..., h_t}. This decision record is more than a version diff; it links changes to their targeted problems, evaluation evidence, and final decisions. This allows later subagents to audit prior changes, determine if a failure is new or a similar fix was attempted, and understand why previous alternatives were rejected, thus avoiding redundant efforts and facilitating targeted improvements.

#### 3.4. Operational Flow (Algorithm 1)

1. Initialize with skill S_0, skill repository R_s, evaluation repository R_e, and an empty history H_0.
2. In each iteration (t):
 - Evaluation dispatches run the current skill (S_t) on R_e and export redacted evidence (Ẽ_t).
 - Optimization dispatches retrieve the history H_t, then propose a diagnosis (q_t) and a candidate revision (r_t).
 - Evaluation dispatches return specific redacted evidence (e_t) for the proposed revision (r_t).
 - A decision (o_t) is made: ACCEPT, REVISE, REJECT, or DEFER.
 - If accepted, S_t+1 is updated by applying r_t; otherwise, S_t+1 remains S_t.
 - The decision record (q_t, r_t, e_t, o_t) is added to the history H_t+1.
3. The final skill S_T and complete history H_T are returned.

The implementation of persistent decision history can leverage GitHub-style repository workflows, where issues record diagnoses, pull requests contain revisions, and merge/rejection decisions record outcomes, making the evolution process auditable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SkillHone encompassed public deep-research benchmarks and internal tool-mediated analysis scenarios.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Domain**: Deep research, involving information seeking, search, navigation, extraction, verification, and recovery across dynamic web sources.
- **Benchmarks**:
 - GAIA (Mialon et al., 2024): Text-only validation subset, emphasizing multi-step information seeking and factual grounding.
 - WebWalkerQA-EN (Wu et al., 2025): English split, focusing on web navigation and information extraction.
- **Evaluation Settings**:
 - **Curated search**: Deep-research agent using commercial retrieval services (a strong baseline).
 - **Raw open-web**: Agents operate without pre-integrated search tools, relying on public web access and portable skill bundles to handle public pages, free search interfaces, rate limits, and changing formats.
- **LLM Backbones**:
 - **Development-time Controller**: Claude Opus 4.6 (for feedback, diagnoses, skill revisions).
 - **Execution/Evaluation Backbone**: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (for running skills and final scores).
- **Development Data**: 20 items from the WebShaper dataset (Tao et al., 2025), disjoint from GAIA and WebWalkerQA-EN.
- **Baselines (Raw Open-Web)**:
 - **Existing-Skills**: Directly loads community skills (from ClawHub, SkillHub) without optimization.
 - **Skill-Creator**: Applies built-in iterative skill optimization.
 - **Hermes-SE**: Applies reflective skill optimization using GEPA-style evolution.
 - **SkillHone (Ours)**: Runs the full harness with role-separated subagents, redacted feedback, and persistent decision history.

#### 4.2. Main Results (Table 1)

- **Overall Performance**: SkillHone achieved the highest average scores on both benchmarks in the raw open-web setting.
 - **GAIA**: 64.6% (15.8 points higher than the deep-research agent with curated search, and 14.2 points higher than Hermes-SE).
 - **WebWalkerQA-EN**: 66.4% (3.2 points higher than the deep-research agent with curated search, and 13.4 points higher than Hermes-SE).
- **Performance on Harder Splits**: The gains were concentrated on harder splits of the benchmarks, which demand complex decision-making for searching, evidence extraction, verification, and recovery.
- **Comparison to Baselines**: SkillHone significantly outperformed existing raw open-web skill-development baselines:
 - **GAIA**: +20.5 points over Skill-Creator, +14.2 points over Hermes-SE, and +22.9 points over Existing-Skills.
 - **WebWalkerQA-EN**: +28.3 points over Skill-Creator, +13.4 points over Hermes-SE, and +16.2 points over Existing-Skills.
 - The improvement over "Existing-Skills" indicates that mere exposure to relevant skills is insufficient; SkillHone effectively organizes them into robust task procedures.

#### 4.3. Cross-Backbone Transfer (Figure 3)

- The skill bundles developed by SkillHone and baselines were tested on a different execution backbone (Claude Sonnet 4.6) without additional optimization.
- SkillHone's skill bundle transferred well, achieving 72.4% on GAIA, which was 10.2 points higher than Hermes-SE, 15.7 points higher than Existing-Skills, and 24.4 points higher than Skill-Creator on this new backbone.
- This suggests that SkillHone's gains reflect improvements in the underlying skill procedure rather than merely fitting to one specific model, indicating generalizable skill improvements.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 2)

To identify the impact of SkillHone's core mechanisms:
- **Without decision history**: Role-separated subagents were retained, but each step started from the latest skill artifact only, optimizing on a scalar signal (similar to reflective baselines).
 - Performance dropped by 13.4 points on GAIA and 10.9 points on WebWalkerQA-EN.
- **Without role separation**: Decision history was retained, but a single subagent had joint access to the skill repository and unredacted evaluation assets.
 - Performance dropped by 6.4 points on GAIA and 5.3 points on WebWalkerQA-EN.
- **Finding**: Decision history accounted for a larger share of the performance improvement than role separation, indicating its significant contribution to continual skill evolution.

#### 4.5. Deployment Study (Table 3)

- SkillHone was deployed on internal tool-mediated analysis scenarios using recurring analytical requests and seed skills.
- It improved six out of seven selected skills over their seeded versions.
- Largest gains were observed in tasks like counting (+30.0 points), aggregation (+26.3 points), structure parsing (+25.0 points), and span retrieval (+21.5 points). List filtering remained stable.
- Across all seven scenarios, SkillHone improved accuracy by an average of 18.8 points, demonstrating its practical utility in real-world, recurring analytical workflows, especially where initial procedures were underspecified.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SkillHone represents a methodological advancement in the field of LLM agent skill evolution, moving beyond artifact-centric optimization to a paradigm of continual, context-aware skill improvement.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Gap in LLM Agent Development

The work directly addresses the problem of context loss in current skill development methods. By persistently recording the rationale, diagnoses, rejected alternatives, and evaluation evidence behind each skill revision, SkillHone provides a crucial "memory" for the evolution process. This is particularly significant for:
- **Long-lived Agent Systems**: Agents deployed in dynamic real-world environments requiring continuous adaptation to changing APIs, rate limits, and data formats.
- **Maintainability and Debugging**: Future agents or human developers can audit the evolution history, understand *why* a particular change was made, and efficiently diagnose regressions or introduce targeted fixes, rather than re-deriving past decisions.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Robustness and Generalizability of Skills

The performance gains demonstrated on GAIA and WebWalkerQA-EN, particularly on harder task splits, indicate that SkillHone enables the development of more robust and sophisticated skills. The method helps agents learn how to organize complex procedures (e.g., searching, parsing, verifying, recovering from failures) rather than just acquiring individual components. The successful cross-backbone transfer further highlights that the improvements are in the skill *procedures* themselves, contributing to greater generalizability and reducing reliance on specific LLM backbones.

#### 5.3. Preventing Overfitting to Evaluation Data

The structural separation of optimization and evaluation roles, enforced by permission boundaries and redacted reports, is a significant contribution. This mechanism helps to prevent optimization agents from directly memorizing unredacted evaluation targets or traces, fostering the development of skills that generalize better to unseen tasks rather than merely performing well on specific practice probes.

#### 5.4. Practical Utility in Deployment Scenarios

The deployment study on internal tool-mediated analysis scenarios demonstrates the practical applicability and value of SkillHone beyond academic benchmarks. The average 18.8-point improvement in accuracy across diverse analytical tasks suggests that SkillHone can significantly enhance the efficacy of tool-use skills in real-world, recurring workflows. This has direct implications for enterprise applications of LLM agents, where the reliability and continuous improvement of specialized skills are critical.

#### 5.5. Broader Implications for Agent Autonomy

By enabling agents to understand and build upon their own past development history, SkillHone contributes to more autonomous and self-improving LLM agents. This capacity to learn from experience, reflect on past decisions, and avoid redundant errors is a step towards more sophisticated and human-like iterative development processes for AI systems.

#### 5.6. Limitations and Future Work

The paper notes a limitation: SkillHone currently evolves a single skill in isolation. Future work could address the joint evolution of multiple interdependent skills, including coordination across shared resources and overlapping failure modes. This suggests avenues for extending the current framework to handle more complex, interconnected agent behaviors.

In summary, SkillHone offers a principled approach to continual agent skill evolution that directly addresses the shortcomings of artifact-only methods. Its emphasis on persistent decision history and role-separated evaluation promotes more efficient, robust, and interpretable skill development, with significant potential impact on the design and deployment of advanced LLM-based agent systems.
