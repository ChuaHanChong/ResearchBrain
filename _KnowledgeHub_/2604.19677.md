# Learning Hybrid-Control Policies for High-Precision In-Contact Manipulation Under Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Oregon State University developed a reinforcement learning framework to enable robots to learn and execute hybrid position-force control policies for high-precision, in-contact manipulation under uncertainty. This approach, named MATCH, demonstrated superior robustness to state estimation noise and achieved significantly lower applied forces compared to existing methods, successfully transferring from simulation to a physical robot without fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Real-world robotic manipulation tasks often require strict force constraints, which analytical methods struggle with under sensing noise and state uncertainty.
- Current RL methods for manipulation primarily use pose control or indirect force regulation, offering limited explicit control over contact forces and potentially leading to component damage.
- Fully integrating dynamic, discrete hybrid position-force control into model-free reinforcement learning is challenging due to complex mixed discrete-continuous action spaces and inefficient training.

## Method

- A ==reinforcement learning== framework was developed to allow policies to dynamically select between ==position and force control== for each end-effector dimension.
- **Mode-Aware Training for Contact Handling (MATCH)** was introduced to improve learning efficiency by ==propagating gradients only through the active control mode== actually utilized by the low-level hybrid controller.
- A ==Supervised Selection Loss (SSL)== was implemented to guide the policy towards appropriate force/position mode selection (force control in contact, position control in free space), fostering safer exploration.

## Results

- Hybrid-control policies, particularly MATCH, achieved a **97.0%** success rate with **0.0%** peg breaks in simulated fragile peg-in-hole tasks, outperforming pose-control and variable impedance control baselines.
- MATCH demonstrated superior robustness to increasing state estimation noise in simulation, maintaining up to **~10%** higher success rates and less than half the break rates compared to pose-control policies.
- Policies trained with MATCH successfully transferred directly to a physical Franka FR3 robot, showing up to **35%** higher success rates than pose-control and applying approximately **30%** less average force than variable impedance control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly aligning the RL policy's action distribution with the underlying hybrid controller's behavior (MATCH) significantly enhances learning efficiency in mixed discrete-continuous action spaces.
> - Guiding the policy's control mode selection using ground-truth contact information (SSL) is critical for efficient exploration and robust, context-appropriate force control.
> - Learned hybrid position-force control policies can develop physically meaningful and adaptable strategies for contact-rich tasks under uncertainty, akin to expert-designed analytical approaches.
