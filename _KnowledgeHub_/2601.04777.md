# GeM-VG: Towards Generalized Multi-image Visual Grounding with Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GeM-VG, a Multimodal Large Language Model, advances multi-image visual grounding by addressing single-target limitations and diverse task scenarios. The model achieves an average performance of 74.65% on MIG-Bench, outperforming UniVG-R1 by 2.0%, and demonstrates robust multi-instance localization with 32.8% AP@50 on MC-Bench, a 9.7% improvement over Migician.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for multi-image visual grounding are biased towards single-target localization, struggling with scenarios requiring identification of multiple object instances.
- Current methods lack a unified framework to model diverse multi-image grounding tasks, especially those requiring complex cross-image reasoning or fine-grained visual distinctions that are difficult to verbalize.
- The absence of comprehensive datasets supporting multi-target localization and varied image relationships (e.g., semantic, temporal, spatial) limits the development of generalized multi-image grounding models.

## Method

- GeM-VG utilizes a ==unified output representation== for localizing both single and multiple targets within multiple images, specifying image index, object description, and bounding box coordinates.
- The work introduces ==MG-Data-240K==, a new dataset systematically categorizing multi-image grounding tasks based on cognitive demands and image relationships to support multi-target and diverse scenarios.
- A three-stage progressive training strategy (Supervised Finetuning, CoT-based SFT, ==Hybrid Reinforcement Finetuning with GRPO==) develops perception, reasoning, and multi-instance localization capabilities, with a ==reward-modulated hybrid finetuning strategy== during the GRPO phase dynamically balancing Chain-of-Thought reasoning and direct answering.

## Results

- GeM-VG achieves **74.65%** average performance on MIG-Bench, surpassing UniVG-R1 by **2.0%**, and demonstrates superior multi-instance localization with **32.8%** AP@50 on MC-Bench, outperforming Migician by **9.7%**.
- The model shows robust performance on single-image grounding (**41.1%** on ODINW, a **9.1%** improvement over Qwen2-VL-7B) and video grounding, indicating strong generalization across various visual-language tasks.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the MG-Data-240K dataset in enhancing multi-instance localization and validate the effectiveness of the multi-dimensional reward design and hybrid finetuning strategy in balancing reasoning and perception.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Developing a unified output representation and curating a systematically categorized dataset (MG-Data-240K) is critical for enabling generalized multi-image visual grounding, especially for multi-instance localization.
> - A multi-stage progressive training approach, coupled with a multi-dimensional rule-based reward function in reinforcement learning, effectively refines an MLLM's ability to localize an arbitrary number of instances and perform complex reasoning.
> - Intelligently balancing Chain-of-Thought reasoning and direct answering through reward modulation during reinforcement learning optimizes performance across tasks requiring either explicit reasoning or fine-grained visual perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual grounding has traditionally focused on tasks such as referring expression comprehension, phrase grounding, and object detection, typically within the confines of a single image. The advent of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has significantly advanced these capabilities, enabling models like Shikra, Ferret, and Kosmos-2 to perform sophisticated visual grounding in single-image scenarios by leveraging their powerful multimodal comprehension.

However, many real-world applications, such as autonomous driving, robotics, and complex user interface agents, necessitate the analysis of contextual information across *multiple* images. While some recent MLLM research has explored image-level tasks in multi-image settings (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-Next-Interleave), region-level comprehension—that is, localizing specific objects or areas within multiple images based on textual instructions—has remained relatively underexplored.

The work by Migician (Li et al. 2025) represented a foundational step in this emerging direction by introducing a benchmark for diverse multi-image grounding tasks. Following this, UniVG-R1 (Bai et al. 2025b) further enhanced reasoning capabilities in multi-image grounding through reinforcement learning. Nevertheless, a critical limitation in these preceding works is their predominant focus on *single-target* localization. Practical scenarios often involve identifying and localizing an *arbitrary number* of referring objects (e.g., "all otters in drier environments"), a challenge that existing models struggle with. Furthermore, these models sometimes face difficulties in fine-grained perception tasks where subtle visual cues are hard to articulate verbally, which can hinder Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

GeM-VG positions itself as a significant advancement by addressing these limitations, pushing towards *generalized* multi-image visual grounding. It aims to unify the modeling of diverse grounding tasks, move beyond single-target constraints to support multi-instance localization, and robustly integrate both fine-grained visual perception and complex reasoning. By doing so, GeM-VG seeks to bridge the gap between current multi-image understanding capabilities and the demands of more complex, real-world applications, setting a new standard for comprehensive multi-image visual grounding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind GeM-VG stems from the limitations observed in existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for multi-image visual grounding. While MLLMs have demonstrated impressive progress in single-image grounding and general multi-image understanding, their application to multi-image *grounding* tasks is constrained by two major factors:
1. **Single-Target Localization Bias**: Current methods predominantly focus on localizing a single target object within one or more images, failing to effectively handle scenarios where multiple instances of an object or concept need to be identified.
2. **Limited Task Diversity and Unified Modeling**: Existing approaches lack a unified framework to model a wide variety of generalized grounding tasks. This results in models that perform well on specific types of tasks but struggle with others, especially those requiring complex cross-image reasoning or fine-grained visual distinctions that are difficult to verbalize.

Given these challenges, the **key objective** of this work is to develop **GeM-VG (Generalized Multi-image Visual Grounding)**, an MLLM capable of comprehensive and generalized multi-image visual grounding.

To achieve this overarching objective, the authors articulate several specific motivations and goals:
- **Systematic Task Categorization and Dataset Creation**: To address the fragmented nature of existing tasks and the limitations of current datasets in terms of target quantity and image relationships, the authors aim to systematically categorize multi-image grounding tasks and introduce a new, comprehensive dataset, MG-Data-240K. This dataset is designed to support a broader spectrum of scenarios, including multi-target localization and diverse image relationships (semantic, temporal, spatial).
- **Unified Model for Diverse Grounding Tasks**: To overcome the constraint of task-specific models, GeM-VG is motivated by the need for a single MLLM that can robustly handle various multi-image grounding tasks, ranging from simple referential retrieval to complex reasoning-guided localization.
- **Hybrid Reasoning and Perception Capabilities**: Recognizing that different grounding tasks require distinct cognitive approaches—some benefiting from explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, others from direct, fine-grained visual perception—the authors are motivated to design a training strategy that optimally integrates and leverages the complementary strengths of both answering modes. This includes mitigating the observed phenomenon where CoT reasoning might be less effective for tasks emphasizing subtle visual cues.
- **Enhanced Multi-Instance Localization**: A primary motivation is to enable the model to accurately localize an arbitrary number of referring objects, a crucial capability for real-world applications not sufficiently addressed by prior single-target focused methods.
- **Robustness and Generalization**: Beyond specific task performance, the motivation extends to ensuring GeM-VG retains strong capabilities in general multi-image understanding and single-image grounding, demonstrating overall robustness and generalization across various visual-language tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GeM-VG model is built upon the Qwen2-VL architecture, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) composed of a vision encoder, a projection layer, and a large language model (LLM). The core approach involves a multi-stage training strategy combined with a novel dataset and a sophisticated reinforcement learning paradigm.

**A. Unified Output Representation**
GeM-VG handles both single and multiple target localizations within multiple images by using a unified output representation. This involves specifying the image index, an optional object description, and bounding box coordinates for each identified instance: `Image-N:<object_ref_start>description<object_ref_start><box_start>(x1,y1),(x2,y2)<box_end>`.

**B. MG-Data-240K Dataset**
To overcome the limitations of existing datasets (e.g., restricted target quantities, limited task scenarios), the authors curate MG-Data-240K. This dataset systematically categorizes multi-image grounding tasks based on their cognitive demands and image relationships:
- **Referential Retrieval Grounding**: Instructions explicitly identify instances without requiring cross-image comparison, but with distractors. Data sourced from D3 and COCO.
- **Cross-image Association Grounding**: Targets are identified through cross-image correspondences, further divided into:
 - **Semantic Association**: Locating objects of the same category across images, promoting multi-object localization. Data based on COCO.
 - **Temporal Association**: Perceiving temporal cues across sequential frames. Data from STAR.
 - **Spatial Association**: Understanding scenes from varying viewpoints (multi-view images). Data from Ego-Exo4D and MVTrack.
- **Reasoning Grounding**: Requires commonsense or external knowledge to locate instances. Data from MGrounding-630K.
This taxonomy and diverse data collection strategy ensure that MG-Data-240K supports a broader range of scenarios, including multi-target samples and varied image relationships.

**C. Three-Stage Progressive Training Strategy**
1. **Stage 1: Supervised Finetuning (SFT) with Short-Answer Data**: The base Qwen2-VL-7B model is first finetuned using a combination of MGrounding-630k and the newly constructed MG-Data-240K. This stage aims to establish fundamental multi-image perception and grounding capabilities. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, data for general multi-image understanding and single-image grounding is also included.
2. **Stage 2: CoT-based Supervised Finetuning**: This stage incorporates Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and direct-answer annotations, primarily from UniVG-R1 and self-constructed data. The goal is to guide the model in learning explicit reasoning processes required for complex multi-image grounding tasks, while also maintaining single-image grounding performance.
3. **Stage 3: Hybrid Reinforcement Finetuning (GRPO)**: This crucial stage refines the model's localization and reasoning capabilities using a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, an R1-like reinforcement learning method adapted for multi-image grounding.

**D. Reinforcement Learning for Generalized Multi-Image Grounding (GRPO)**
- **GRPO Adaptation**: The training process involves the policy model generating multiple candidate completions. A custom rule-based reward function evaluates these completions, and advantages are computed based on these rewards. The policy model is then updated to maximize an objective function that considers these advantages and a KL-divergence term from a reference model.
- **Rule-based Reward Function**: To handle generalized scenarios with arbitrary numbers of instances, a multi-dimensional reward function is designed:
 - **Format Reward ($R_{format}$)**: Rewards adherence to the specified output format, ensuring parsability of bounding box information.
 - **Image Reward ($R_{image}$)**: Rewards correct identification of the images containing the target(s) at a coarse image level.
 - **Precision Reward ($R_{precision}$)**: Measures the average Intersection over Union (IoU) between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes after bipartite matching, promoting accurate localization.
 - **Recall Reward ($R_{recall}$)**: Measures the proportion of ground-truth instances successfully matched by a predicted box with an IoU above a threshold ($\tau=0.5$), encouraging the model to find all relevant instances.
 - The overall reward is a sum: $R = R_{format} + R_{image} + R_{precision} + R_{recall}$.

**E. Reward-Modulated Hybrid Finetuning Strategy**
This strategy addresses the observation that CoT reasoning does not always outperform direct answering, particularly for tasks emphasizing fine-grained visual perception. It aims to leverage the complementary strengths of both modes and prevent the model from prematurely collapsing into a single answering style during RL.
- **Balancing Dual Modes (Early Stage)**: In the initial phase of RL training, a reward modulation mechanism is introduced. It adjusts the reward for completions (categorized as accurate/inaccurate CoT or direct-answer) based on the current proportion of CoT completions in a batch relative to a target balancing ratio ($\theta=0.5$). This encourages exploration and optimization of both modes.
- **Encouraging Longer Thoughts on Failure (Late Stage)**: In the later stages, the balancing strategy is removed. For *inaccurate* CoT completions, an additional length-aware reward is added, incentivizing more deliberate reasoning. This adjustment is capped to ensure that adjusted rewards of inaccurate completions do not exceed those of accurate ones, promoting deeper thinking on challenging cases.
This hybrid strategy, applied during the GRPO phase, facilitates joint optimization between CoT and direct-answer paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GeM-VG demonstrates superior performance across a wide range of visual grounding and multi-image understanding benchmarks, validating its generalized capabilities.

**A. Multi-image Grounding**
- **MIG-Bench**: GeM-VG achieves an average performance of **74.65%**, surpassing the previous leading MLLM, UniVG-R1 (72.64%), by **2.0%**. This improvement is observed across diverse subtasks, including those requiring fine-grained perception (e.g., static difference, region locating) and complex reasoning (e.g., reasoning grounding).
- **MC-Bench**: On this benchmark, which assesses the ability to localize an arbitrary number of instances, GeM-VG achieves **32.8% AP@50**. This significantly outperforms Migician (23.1%) by **9.7%** and the base model Qwen2-VL-7B (20.2%) by **12.6%**, highlighting GeM-VG's strong capability in multi-instance grounding within multi-image scenarios.

**B. Single-image and Video Grounding**
- **Single-image Grounding**: GeM-VG shows robust performance, achieving **41.1%** on ODINW (a benchmark for rare categories and multi-target scenarios), which is a **9.1%** improvement over the base Qwen2-VL-7B model and **7.8%** over UniVG-R1 (33.3%). On LLMSeg, it scores **50.90%**, slightly better than UniVG-R1 (50.60%). These results demonstrate superior multi-object localization and reasoning even in single-image contexts.
- **Video Grounding**: For video grounding tasks, GeM-VG achieves **64.41%** on ReasonVOS and **60.52%** on ReVOS, outperforming UniVG-R1 (58.73% and 60.03% respectively). This indicates improved temporal understanding and reasoning capabilities.

**C. Multi-image Understanding**
GeM-VG maintains strong capabilities in general multi-image understanding. It achieves competitive or superior results on several representative benchmarks:
- **MuirBench**: GeM-VG scores **58.20%**, outperforming Migician (57.81%) and significantly surpassing UniVG-R1 (44.77%). Notably, it outperforms Migician by **5.55%** on the Counting subtask and **2.38%** on the Grounding subtask of MuirBench, validating its effectiveness for generalized multi-image grounding tasks requiring fine-grained perception.
- **Other Benchmarks (BLINK val, MIBench, MMIU)**: GeM-VG consistently achieves superior results (52.97%, 70.16%, 55.01% respectively) compared to UniVG-R1 and often Migician, demonstrating overall strong generalization.

**D. Ablation Studies**
- **Effectiveness of Reward Design**: Experiments confirmed that combining all components of the rule-based reward (format, image, precision, and recall) leads to consistent performance improvements across diverse ODINW subsets, particularly on challenging scenarios with dense targets or occlusions. Precision and recall components were shown to be complementary.
- **Effectiveness of Hybrid Finetuning Strategy**:
 - Supervised finetuning with pure CoT data excelled in reasoning tasks but suffered in perception tasks, and vice versa for direct-answer data. A mixed SFT approach yielded better overall performance.
 - During RL, training without reward modulation led to the model collapsing into direct-answer mode and degraded reasoning performance.
 - The dual-mode balancing strategy in the early RL stage improved performance.
 - The full hybrid strategy, integrating early-stage balancing with late-stage encouragement for longer thoughts on failure, achieved the best overall performance, demonstrating its efficacy in balancing different cognitive demands.
- **Impact of MG-Data-240K**: Removing the MG-Data-240K dataset from training resulted in a significant drop of 10.4% AP@50 on MC-Bench and a 2.61% reduction in average score on MIG-Bench, underscoring its critical role in enhancing multi-instance, multi-image localization.
- **Generalizability**: The methodology was successfully applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, yielding strong results (69.68% on MIG-Bench, 39.7% on MC-Bench), confirming the generalizability of the proposed approach to other MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeM-VG work makes several significant contributions to the field of Multimodal Large Language Models and computer vision, with substantial potential impact on real-world applications:

- **Pioneering Generalized Multi-image Visual Grounding**: GeM-VG is a pivotal step towards truly generalized multi-image visual grounding. By addressing the critical limitations of single-target localization and restricted task types in prior works, it establishes a more comprehensive and unified framework for understanding and localizing objects across multiple visual inputs. This generalization is crucial for developing robust AI systems that can operate in complex, dynamic environments.

- **Robust Multi-Instance Localization**: The capability to identify and localize an arbitrary number of target instances in multi-image contexts is a major breakthrough. Many real-world scenarios involve multiple objects of interest, and GeM-VG’s superior performance on benchmarks like MC-Bench and ODINW (which feature multi-target scenarios) directly translates to enhanced applicability in domains requiring comprehensive scene understanding.

- **Advanced Training Methodology for MLLMs**: The proposed hybrid reinforcement finetuning strategy, which intelligently integrates Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with direct answering, represents a significant advancement in MLLM training paradigms. By dynamically balancing these two complementary modes and leveraging a multi-dimensional rule-based reward function, the authors demonstrate an effective way to train MLLMs that are both perceptually acute and capable of complex reasoning. This approach provides a blueprint for developing more cognitively sophisticated MLLMs.

- **Novel Data Resource**: The creation of MG-Data-240K is a valuable contribution to the research community. This systematically categorized dataset, designed to encompass diverse multi-image grounding tasks, varying numbers of targets, and different types of image relationships (semantic, temporal, spatial), fills a critical gap in existing benchmarks. It will undoubtedly serve as an essential resource for future research and development in generalized multi-image understanding.

- **Broad Real-World Applications**: The capabilities demonstrated by GeM-VG have direct and profound implications for various real-world applications:
 - **Autonomous Driving**: Enables better situational awareness by identifying and tracking multiple objects (e.g., pedestrians, vehicles, road signs) across sequential camera feeds from different viewpoints.
 - **Robotics**: Improves robot perception and interaction by allowing robots to understand complex instructions and localize multiple targets in their environment based on multi-camera inputs.
 - **GUI Agents**: Enhances the ability of AI agents to interact with graphical user interfaces by accurately locating multiple UI elements based on user commands or contextual understanding.
 - **Surveillance and Monitoring**: Facilitates more effective analysis of visual data from multiple cameras or time points to identify specific events or objects of interest.

- **Catalyst for Future Research**: This work sets a new benchmark and provides novel methodologies, encouraging further exploration into developing MLLMs with even more advanced generalized grounding capabilities. It opens avenues for research into more sophisticated reasoning mechanisms, efficient handling of larger numbers of images, and adapting these capabilities to emerging multimodal applications.

In conclusion, GeM-VG represents a substantial leap forward in the field of multimodal AI, offering a generalized solution to complex multi-image visual grounding challenges and significantly expanding the practical utility of MLLMs.
