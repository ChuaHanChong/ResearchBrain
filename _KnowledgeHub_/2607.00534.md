# Learning from Demonstration via Spatiotemporal Tubes for Unknown Euler-Lagrange Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IISc Bengaluru developed STT-LfD, a unified Learning from Demonstration framework that learns Spatiotemporal Tubes from expert demonstrations to define time-varying task precision. It employs a closed-form, model-free control strategy for unknown Euler-Lagrange systems, achieving robust execution within actuator limits and demonstrating superior computational efficiency of 0.021 ms per control step compared to baseline methods on a 7-DOF manipulator.

## Problem

- Decoupled motion learning and control in traditional LfD leads to fragility and potentially infeasible trajectories without considering robot physical constraints.
- Existing LfD methods often fail to capture the time-varying 'intent envelope' of expert demonstrations, treating all parts of a task with uniform precision.
- Current robust control strategies for Euler-Lagrange systems frequently necessitate explicit model identification or computationally intensive online optimization, hindering real-time deployment.

## Method

- A unified ==Learning from Demonstration (LfD)== framework, **STT-LfD**, that integrates data-driven motion learning with robust feedback control for unknown ==Euler-Lagrange systems==.
- Utilizes ==Heteroscedastic Gaussian Processes (HGPs)== to learn ==Spatiotemporal Tubes (STTs)== directly from expert demonstrations, capturing the task's time-varying precision requirements.
- Proposes a ==closed-form, model-free feedback controller== that guarantees robot trajectories stay within the learned STTs while adhering to ==actuator limits==, avoiding explicit system identification or online optimization.

## Results

- The framework successfully executed tasks on both a mobile robot and a 7-DOF manipulator, maintaining trajectories within learned STTs and respecting actuator limits even under external disturbances and mass variations.
- STT-LfD achieved significantly higher computational efficiency, requiring only **0.018 ms** per control step for the mobile robot and **0.021 ms** for the manipulator, outperforming baseline methods like KMP+LQR (**15.94 ms**).
- It demonstrated superior robustness to model perturbations, yielding a tracking error of **4.24 ± 0.08 mm** under a **1 kg** load variation on the 7-DOF manipulator, a substantial improvement over model-based baselines (over **50 mm** error).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expert demonstrations implicitly contain a time-varying 'intent envelope' (precision requirements) that can be probabilistically modeled by Spatiotemporal Tubes using Heteroscedastic Gaussian Processes.
> - Robust control for unknown Euler-Lagrange systems can be achieved through a closed-form, model-free strategy that relies on boundedness assumptions rather than explicit system identification or online optimization.
> - Unifying data-driven task specification via STTs with a robust feedback controller capable of real-time execution offers a more robust and computationally efficient approach to Learning from Demonstration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "STT-LfD" is situated within the field of Learning from Demonstration (LfD) for robotics. LfD seeks to enable robots to acquire complex skills by observing human or expert demonstrations, thereby avoiding the need for explicit manual programming. This approach is particularly valuable for tasks in safety-critical and unstructured environments where manual programming can be challenging or impractical.

Traditional LfD frameworks commonly employ a decoupled architecture. A high-level motion generation component, such as Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) or Kernelized Movement Primitives (KMPs), is typically responsible for producing a reference trajectory. This trajectory is then passed to a separate low-level controller, like Linear Quadratic Regulators (LQR) or Model Predictive Control (MPC), which attempts to track it. This decoupled design has inherent limitations. The motion generator often operates without explicit consideration for the robot's physical constraints, potentially generating kinematically or dynamically infeasible trajectories. Furthermore, traditional trackers tend to treat all points along a reference trajectory with uniform importance, failing to capture the time-varying precision requirements of a task—an "intent envelope" implicitly expressed by the expert. For example, some task segments may require high precision (narrow tolerance), while others allow for greater variability (wider tolerance). These issues can result in fragility, poor robustness to disturbances, and a need for explicit system identification or online optimization, which can be computationally intensive.

Previous research in spatiotemporal tubes (STTs) has primarily focused on satisfying formally specified temporal logic constraints through optimization- or learning-based tube synthesis. However, these formulations have not typically addressed the construction of STTs directly from expert demonstrations in an LfD context, nor have they explicitly accounted for actuator constraints in a model-free manner for unknown Euler-Lagrange systems.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a unified LfD framework that integrates motion learning with control. It aims to overcome the rigid decoupling of prior methods by learning a data-driven safety specification—the spatiotemporal tube—directly from demonstrations. The framework further develops a closed-form, model-free control strategy that explicitly respects actuator limits and ensures robustness against unknown dynamics and external disturbances. This positions the work as an advancement in LfD that seeks to provide a more robust, computationally efficient, and physically aware method for transferring expert skills to robotic platforms, particularly for systems with unknown Euler-Lagrange dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop STT-LfD, a unified Learning from Demonstration (LfD) framework that integrates motion learning with robust control for unknown Euler-Lagrange systems. This framework aims to transform expert demonstrations into a time-varying intent envelope, expressed as a Spatiotemporal Tube (STT), and to synthesize a control law that safely and robustly executes the task.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations observed in existing LfD architectures:

- **Rigid Decoupling of Motion Learning and Control:** Traditional LfD approaches typically separate the process of generating a desired motion from the process of controlling the robot to execute that motion. Motion generators often produce reference trajectories without considering the robot's physical constraints (e.g., joint limits, actuator capabilities). Subsequently, low-level controllers treat these trajectories as fixed references, attempting to track them uniformly. This decoupling can lead to fragility, as the robot may be unable to track the generated trajectory, especially under disturbances or when operating close to its physical limits.
- **Absence of an "Intent Envelope":** Expert demonstrations inherently contain varying levels of precision and flexibility across different phases of a task. For instance, a human might exhibit tight control during a delicate manipulation phase but allow for more variance during a free-space movement. Traditional LfD methods often fail to capture this time-varying spatial tolerance, which the authors term the "intent envelope." Instead, they typically learn a single, precise reference trajectory or a uniform error band, which does not reflect the nuances of human expertise.
- **Requirement for Explicit System Identification and Online Optimization:** Many existing control strategies, such as LQR, MPC, or those based on Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), require an explicit model of the robot's dynamics (system identification) or involve computationally intensive online optimization. For complex or unknown Euler-Lagrange systems, obtaining accurate models can be difficult, and real-time optimization can lead to high computational loads, making deployment on resource-constrained hardware challenging.
- **Lack of Actuator Constraint Consideration in Prior STT Formulations:** While Spatiotemporal Tubes have been used to define admissible behavior, previous implementations in control contexts have often not explicitly incorporated actuator limits. Disregarding these limits can lead to control actions that are physically impossible for the robot, compromising safety and task execution reliability.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines three main contributions:
1. **Unified Task Specification:** To propose an LfD framework that learns Spatiotemporal Tubes (STTs) directly from expert demonstrations using heteroscedastic Gaussian Processes. These learned STTs are designed to capture the time-varying precision requirements of the task, defining admissible task executions as time-varying regions rather than fixed trajectories.
2. **Closed-Form Control under Input Constraints:** To develop a computationally efficient, closed-form control strategy that ensures the robot's trajectory remains within the learned STTs while respecting actuator limits. This control method is designed to operate without requiring explicit system identification or online optimization, promoting real-time applicability.
3. **Experimental Validation:** To validate the proposed framework through hardware experiments on distinct robotic platforms (a mobile robot and a 7-DOF manipulator). The objective of these experiments is to demonstrate significant improvements in robustness to disturbances and computational speed compared to existing baseline methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The STT-LfD framework integrates data-driven motion representation with feedback control for Euler-Lagrange systems with unknown dynamics. The methodology involves three main stages: system definition and problem formulation, learning spatiotemporal constraints from demonstrations, and synthesizing a closed-form controller.

#### 3.1. System Definition and Problem Formulation

The system under consideration is an *n*-dimensional Euler-Lagrange (EL) system, defined by the equation:
$M(x)\ddot{x} + V(x, \dot{x}) + G(x) = \tau + w(t)$
Here, $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the state, $\tau(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the control input, and $w(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is an unknown external disturbance. The mass matrix $M(x)$, Coriolis and centrifugal terms $V(x, \dot{x})$, and gravity vector $G(x)$ are all assumed to be unknown (Assumption 1). To facilitate control synthesis despite unknown dynamics, the framework adopts several boundedness assumptions:
- **Assumption 2:** The external disturbance $w(t)$ is bounded within a known range, i.e., $-\bar{w} \preceq w(t) \preceq \bar{w}$.
- **Assumption 3:** Given control bounds, there exists a positive constant $m$ such that $m\bar{\tau} \preceq M^{-1}\bar{\tau}$.
- **Assumption 4:** The terms $M^{-1}(V+G)$ are bounded, i.e., $\underline{V}_M \preceq M^{-1}(V+G) \preceq \bar{V}_M$.
- **Assumption 5:** The inverse mass matrix scales the disturbance, i.e., $-\|M^{-1}\|\bar{w} \preceq M^{-1}w \preceq \|M^{-1}\|\bar{w}$.
These assumptions establish theoretical bounds that are used to prove feasibility and guarantee control performance without requiring explicit identification of these parameters.

The problem statement is to learn an STT $\Gamma(t)$ from expert demonstrations and design a closed-form control input $\tau(t)$ such that the system trajectory $x(t)$ remains within $\Gamma(t)$ for a finite time horizon $T$, the control input respects actuator bounds $|\tau(t)| \preceq \bar{\tau}$, and the task is executed robustly despite unknown dynamics and disturbances.

#### 3.2. Learning Spatiotemporal Constraints

The framework learns Spatiotemporal Tubes (STTs) directly from expert demonstrations to represent the task's nominal behavior and its time-varying precision requirements.

- **Data Collection and Temporal Alignment:** Multiple expert demonstrations of the same task are collected as time-series measurements of the robot's state. To account for natural human variability in execution speed, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is employed as a preprocessing step. DTW aligns the time axes of the demonstrations, mapping them onto a common temporal grid while preserving their overall spatial geometry. This ensures temporal consistency, which is crucial for time-dependent task representations.
- **HGP-based Spatiotemporal Tube Construction:** Given the temporally aligned demonstrations, Heteroscedastic Gaussian Processes (HGPs) are used to construct the STTs. For each state dimension $j \in [1;n]$, the desired time-varying behavior is modeled as $x_j(t) \sim \mathcal{GP}(\mu_j(t), \sigma^2_j(t))$, where $\mu_j(t)$ is the posterior mean and $\sigma_j(t)$ is the posterior standard deviation. Unlike standard (homoscedastic) GPs, HGPs allow the noise variance (and thus the uncertainty) to vary with time. This capability is utilized to capture the "intent envelope," where narrow tube segments represent task-critical regions requiring high precision (low variance), and wider segments represent flexible regions (high variance).
 The one-dimensional STT for the $j$-th state is defined as $\Gamma_j(t) := [\mu_j(t) - \lambda\sigma_j(t), \mu_j(t) + \lambda\sigma_j(t)]^\top$. The full $n$-dimensional STT $\Gamma(t)$ is then the Cartesian product of these intervals across all state dimensions. The scaling parameter $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is user-defined and controls the tube width, influencing the trade-off between capturing demonstration variability and maintaining tracking precision. A theorem (Theorem 3.2) formally establishes that any trajectory remaining within the learned STT is considered a consistent execution of the demonstrated task, up to bounded deviations governed by $\lambda$.

#### 3.3. Controller Synthesis

A closed-form feedback controller is designed in a two-stage approach to guarantee forward invariance of the learned STTs while respecting actuator constraints. The control design is inspired by funnel-control methodologies.

- **Stage I: Velocity-level Control:** To ensure that the state $x_j(t)$ remains within $\Gamma_j(t)$, a reference velocity vector $v_r(x, t)$ is defined. This reference velocity is inversely proportional to the normalized error $\epsilon_x(x,t) := [x_j(t) - \mu_j(t)] / (\lambda\sigma_j(t))$ through a diagonal matrix of bounded transformation functions $\Psi(\epsilon_x)$. As the state approaches the tube boundaries, the reference velocity pushes it back towards the center of the tube.
- **Stage II: Acceleration-level Control:** To ensure smooth tracking of the reference velocity $v_r(t)$, the velocity error $e_v(t) := v(t) - v_r(t)$ is constrained within exponentially decaying funnel constraints $\rho_v(t)$. This means that $|e_v(t)| \prec \rho_v(t)$. The final control input $\tau(t)$ is then formulated based on the normalized velocity error $\epsilon_v(t) := \text{diag}(\rho_v)^{-1}e_v(t)$ and the maximum permissible torque $\bar{\tau}$ using the same bounded transformation function $\Psi$. Specifically, $\tau(t) = -\text{diag}(\Psi(\epsilon_v(t)))\bar{\tau}$. The transformation function $\Psi$ (e.g., tanh) ensures control inputs remain within bounds.
- **Feasibility Conditions:** For the controller to guarantee tube invariance and bounded control, specific feasibility conditions are derived:
 - **Stage I Feasibility:** The maximum permissible velocity $\bar{v}$ must be sufficiently large to accommodate the maximum speed implied by the demonstrations ($\bar{\Gamma}$) and reject worst-case velocity perturbations ($p_v$), i.e., $\bar{v} \succeq \bar{\Gamma} + p_v$.
 - **Stage II Feasibility:** The maximum permissible torque $\bar{\tau}$ must be sufficient to overcome worst-case Coriolis, gravity, and disturbance terms, as well as ensure the decay of velocity error, i.e., $\bar{\tau} \succeq \frac{1}{m} [\max(-\underline{V}_M, \bar{V}_M) + m_i\bar{w} + \mu_v(p_v - q_v) + \bar{a}_r]$, where $\bar{a}_r$ bounds the derivative of the reference velocity.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Guarantees

Theorem 4.1 formalizes that, given initial conditions within the tube and funnels, and if the feasibility conditions (8) and (9) hold, the closed-form controller $\tau(t)$ guarantees that (i) the system state $x(t)$ evolves within the STTs for all time, and (ii) the control input $\tau(t)$ remains within the prescribed actuator limits. The proof relies on a contradiction argument for both stages, demonstrating that the state and velocity error cannot cross their respective boundaries without violating the feasibility conditions or the definition of the control law. A key aspect is that the control framework is "model-free," meaning it does not require explicit identification of system parameters for implementation, only knowledge of their bounds for theoretical guarantees.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of the STT-LfD framework was conducted on two hardware platforms: an omnidirectional mobile robot (TurtleBot, AgileX LIMO) and a 7-DOF Franka Research 3 manipulator. The experiments were designed to evaluate the framework's ability to handle spatiotemporal ambiguity, maintain robustness against unknown dynamics and disturbances, and demonstrate computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Execution Behavior and Tube Invariance

- **Spatiotemporal Intent Capture:** For both the mobile robot (figure-eight trajectory) and the manipulator (alphabet "W" drawing), the learned STTs effectively captured the time-varying intent of the expert demonstrations. Narrower tube segments were observed in regions requiring high precision (e.g., start/end points, sharp corners), reflecting low variability in expert behavior. Conversely, wider tube segments appeared in more flexible task phases (e.g., larger free-space movements, straight strokes), where demonstrations naturally exhibited greater variability. The temporal indexing of the STTs successfully resolved spatiotemporal ambiguities, such as distinguishing between distinct phases at the crossing point of the figure-eight trajectory.
- **Robustness to Disturbances and Dynamic Variations:** The STT-LfD framework demonstrated consistent performance under various perturbed conditions.
 - **Mobile Robot:** The figure-eight trajectory was successfully reproduced on different platforms (TurtleBot and AgileX LIMO) using the same learned STTs, without controller modification or explicit system identification. The system also maintained task execution within the STTs when a 1 kg mass variation was applied and when external jerky disturbances were introduced.
 - **7-DOF Manipulator:** Similarly, for the "W" drawing task, the manipulator's trajectory remained within the learned STTs despite experiments with varying mass matrices (by attaching water bottles) and manually applied external jerks. The controller successfully modulated joint torques to adapt to these changes and maintain tube invariance.
- **Bounded Control Inputs:** Throughout all experiments, the control inputs generated by the STT-LfD controller remained within the prescribed actuator limits for both platforms, indicating safe and physically feasible operation.

#### 4.2. Computational Efficiency

- **Offline STT Generation:** The offline computation of STTs was efficient, requiring approximately 5.655 seconds for the 2-DOF mobile robot and 5.794 seconds for the 7-DOF manipulator. This parallel computation across state dimensions contributed to its efficiency.
- **Online Execution Speed:** The closed-form nature of the controller resulted in high online computational efficiency. It required only 0.018 ms per control step for the mobile robot and 0.021 ms per control step for the manipulator. This indicates strong computational scalability with increasing system dimensionality.

#### 4.3. Sensitivity Analysis of Tube Width Parameter (λ)

A sensitivity study on the mobile robot's figure-eight task demonstrated the trade-off governed by the $\lambda$ parameter. As $\lambda$ increased from 0.2 to 2.4, the Root Mean Square (RMS) tracking error increased approximately linearly, which is consistent with the wider admissible execution region provided by larger tubes. Concurrently, the RMS control effort decreased approximately exponentially, as the controller required less aggressive corrective action to keep the trajectory within the more spacious boundaries. A value of $\lambda = 1.2$ was empirically selected, balancing tracking precision and control effort, and corresponding to capturing approximately 77% of expert demonstration variance.

#### 4.4. Comparative Performance Analysis

STT-LfD was quantitatively compared against several baseline controllers (KMP+LQR, DMP+MPC, NODE-CLF-CBF, and S2NNDS) using the 7-DOF manipulator in a Gazebo simulation environment, with identical task specifications, control frequency, input limits, and mass variation. Hardware validation was only performed for STT-LfD due to observed performance degradation of baselines under model perturbations and their lack of formal forward-invariance guarantees.

- **Robustness to Model Perturbations:** Under a 1 kg load variation, STT-LfD exhibited significantly superior robustness, with a tracking error of 4.24 ± 0.08 mm. In contrast, model-based baselines (KMP+LQR, DMP+MPC) saw their tracking errors increase substantially to over 50 mm. Even other robust learning-based methods (NODE-CLF-CBF, S2NNDS) showed increased errors (18.78 mm and 27.39 mm, respectively) under the same perturbation. This performance difference is attributed to STT-LfD's ability to compensate for unknown Euler-Lagrange terms in real-time without explicit identification.
- **Computational Efficiency:** STT-LfD demonstrated significantly higher computational efficiency, requiring 0.021 ms per control step. This was orders of magnitude faster than KMP+LQR (15.94 ms), DMP+MPC (22.73 ms), NODE-CLF-CBF (1.17 ms), and S2NNDS (1.49 ms). This speed advantage is a direct result of the closed-form nature of its controller, which avoids online optimization or iterative neural network evaluations.

The results collectively indicate that STT-LfD effectively translates probabilistic task specifications from demonstrations into robust, safe, and computationally efficient hardware execution. The framework mitigates the modeling mismatch common in decoupled designs, with the learned STT serving as a dynamic safety specification and the controller ensuring adherence to expert intent within physical limits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The STT-LfD framework contributes to the field of robotic Learning from Demonstration by addressing several limitations of existing approaches, offering potential advancements in robot autonomy and safety.

- **Unified and Robust Task Specification:** The development of Spatiotemporal Tubes (STTs) from heteroscedastic Gaussian Processes directly addresses the challenge of capturing the "intent envelope" in LfD. By representing demonstrations as time-varying admissible regions rather than rigid trajectories, the framework allows for a more nuanced and robust interpretation of expert behavior. This is significant because it provides robots with a flexible yet constrained understanding of a task, differentiating between critical precision requirements and areas allowing for greater variability.
- **Model-Free and Computationally Efficient Control:** The proposed closed-form control strategy is a notable advancement as it guarantees forward invariance of the learned STTs and respects actuator limits without requiring explicit system identification or computationally intensive online optimization. This "model-free" characteristic simplifies deployment in environments where dynamic models are unknown or difficult to obtain, while the computational efficiency enables real-time execution on resource-constrained robotic hardware. This broadens the applicability of LfD to latency-sensitive tasks and systems.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Disturbances and Unknown Dynamics:** The experimental validation demonstrates STT-LfD's ability to maintain performance under significant external disturbances and variations in system dynamics (e.g., changes in mass matrix, external jerks). This level of robustness, particularly when compared to model-based and some learning-based baselines, is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and uncertain real-world environments where unforeseen perturbations are common. The framework’s ability to operate without precise knowledge of system parameters makes it a more practical solution for real-world scenarios.
- **Improved Safety and Consistency:** By enforcing that robot trajectories remain within the learned STTs and respecting actuator bounds, the framework contributes to safer robot operation. The STTs act as a data-driven safety specification, ensuring that any executed motion is consistent with the expert's demonstrated intent and within the physical capabilities of the robot. This consistency is vital for tasks requiring reliable and predictable performance.
- **Broad Applicability:** The successful implementation on diverse robotic platforms—a mobile robot and a 7-DOF manipulator—indicates the generalizability of the STT-LfD framework across different robot kinematics and operational contexts. This suggests its potential for a wide range of applications, from mobile navigation to complex manipulation tasks in industrial or service robotics.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as extending the framework for online adaptation to dynamic environments (e.g., obstacle avoidance, multi-robot coordination), developing systematic methods for $\lambda$ selection, and automatically adapting temporal parameterization, suggests continued research in making LfD systems more autonomous, adaptive, and practical for increasingly complex and dynamic settings. This work lays a foundation for more intuitive and reliable robot skill acquisition.
