# Bridging Simulation and Reality: Cross-Domain Transfer with Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting (S2GS) offers a novel representation for robust sim-to-real policy transfer in robotic manipulation, extracting object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features. This approach, developed by researchers at Xi'an Jiaotong University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, enabled a Diffusion Policy to achieve high success rates, such as 80-87%, across pick, push, and stack tasks in real-world environments with unseen objects.

## Problem

- Robotic policies trained in simulation exhibit reduced performance or failure in real-world environments due to a significant "domain gap" in visual appearance, object properties, and environmental complexities.
- Current sim-to-real transfer methods, such as domain randomization and adaptation, demand extensive tuning and large datasets, while calibration is engineering-intensive and lacks generalization.
- Existing 3D scene representations (e.g., NeRFs, point clouds) struggle to efficiently and accurately extract the domain-invariant spatial features crucial for robust robotic control and real-time operation.

## Method

- Propose **S2GS** (==Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting==) which extracts clean, ==object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features== from multi-view images for robotic policies.
- **S2GS** constructs 2D feature representations from input images, maps them to a unified 3D semantic space using ==feature-level Gaussian Splatting==, and employs ==semantic retrieval== to filter background noise.
- Integrate the extracted compact spatial features (e.g., centroid) with a ==Diffusion Policy== as input, allowing the policy to focus solely on task-relevant information.

## Results

- In simulation, **S2GS**-derived features enabled a Diffusion Policy to match ground-truth state performance with **100%** success for PickCube and PushCube, and **97%** for StackCube.
- Policies trained with **S2GS** achieved strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, yielding average success rates of **86.7%** for Pick and Push tasks and **80.0%** for Stack tasks on unseen real-world objects.
- **S2GS** demonstrated superior appearance quality and geometric accuracy over 3D Gaussian Splatting, with a **2.46 dB** higher PSNR and **0.12** higher SSIM, due to better alignment with object surfaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extracting object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features via S2GS can reduce the sim-to-real gap to a degree where policy performance approaches that of using ground-truth state inputs.
> - 2D Gaussian primitives within S2GS offer superior appearance quality and geometric accuracy compared to 3D Gaussian Splatting, enhancing the precision of spatial feature extraction from object surfaces.
> - Leveraging semantic understanding (via CLIP and SAM) within the representation allows for effective filtering of irrelevant background information, enabling the policy to focus on critical target objects.
