# Bridging Simulation and Reality: Cross-Domain Transfer with Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting (S2GS) offers a novel representation for robust sim-to-real policy transfer in robotic manipulation, extracting object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features. This approach, developed by researchers at Xi'an Jiaotong University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, enabled a Diffusion Policy to achieve high success rates, such as 80-87%, across pick, push, and stack tasks in real-world environments with unseen objects.

## Problem

- Robotic policies trained in simulation exhibit reduced performance or failure in real-world environments due to a significant "domain gap" in visual appearance, object properties, and environmental complexities.
- Current sim-to-real transfer methods, such as domain randomization and adaptation, demand extensive tuning and large datasets, while calibration is engineering-intensive and lacks generalization.
- Existing 3D scene representations (e.g., NeRFs, point clouds) struggle to efficiently and accurately extract the domain-invariant spatial features crucial for robust robotic control and real-time operation.

## Method

- Propose **S2GS** (==Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting==) which extracts clean, ==object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features== from multi-view images for robotic policies.
- **S2GS** constructs 2D feature representations from input images, maps them to a unified 3D semantic space using ==feature-level Gaussian Splatting==, and employs ==semantic retrieval== to filter background noise.
- Integrate the extracted compact spatial features (e.g., centroid) with a ==Diffusion Policy== as input, allowing the policy to focus solely on task-relevant information.

## Results

- In simulation, **S2GS**-derived features enabled a Diffusion Policy to match ground-truth state performance with **100%** success for PickCube and PushCube, and **97%** for StackCube.
- Policies trained with **S2GS** achieved strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, yielding average success rates of **86.7%** for Pick and Push tasks and **80.0%** for Stack tasks on unseen real-world objects.
- **S2GS** demonstrated superior appearance quality and geometric accuracy over 3D Gaussian Splatting, with a **2.46 dB** higher PSNR and **0.12** higher SSIM, due to better alignment with object surfaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extracting object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features via S2GS can reduce the sim-to-real gap to a degree where policy performance approaches that of using ground-truth state inputs.
> - 2D Gaussian primitives within S2GS offer superior appearance quality and geometric accuracy compared to 3D Gaussian Splatting, enhancing the precision of spatial feature extraction from object surfaces.
> - Leveraging semantic understanding (via CLIP and SAM) within the representation allows for effective filtering of irrelevant background information, enabling the policy to focus on critical target objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning consistently faces the challenge of sim-to-real cross-domain transfer. Policies trained in simulated environments frequently exhibit reduced performance or outright failure when deployed in real-world settings due to inherent discrepancies, termed the "domain gap." This gap arises from variations in visual appearance, object properties, and environmental complexities between simulated and physical environments.

Traditional approaches to mitigate this domain gap include:
- **Domain Randomization:** Introducing variability in simulation parameters (e.g., textures, lighting, dynamics) to improve policy robustness. However, this often requires extensive tuning and diverse data, potentially leading to overly conservative policies.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Learning mappings between simulated and real domains, often through techniques like image translation or aligning perception and dynamics distributions. These methods can also demand considerable data and tuning.
- **Sim-to-Real Calibration:** Adjusting simulator parameters (e.g., sensors, materials, dynamics) to more accurately reflect the real world. This approach is typically engineering-intensive and may not generalize to unseen real-world variations.

In parallel, advancements in representation learning have sought to generate features that are robust to domain shifts. Early methods utilizing RGB-based neural networks struggled with semantic and spatial consistency across multiple viewpoints. Point cloud representations explicitly encoded 3D structures but often suffered from sparsity and difficulty in embedding high-level semantic features. More recently, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) offered high-fidelity implicit 3D representations with multi-view consistency and semantic information, but their computational cost and slow rendering speeds rendered them impractical for real-time robotic control.

The introduction of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) marked a development in real-time, high-fidelity 3D scene representation and rendering, leading to its application in robotic tasks such as grasp detection and photorealistic simulation. However, 3DGS can exhibit inconsistencies in depth perception and may not perfectly align with object surfaces. 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) emerged to address these geometric inconsistencies by representing scenes with 2D Gaussian primitives that better conform to object surfaces.

This research paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying a persistent limitation: existing approaches often struggle to efficiently and accurately extract *domain-invariant spatial features* that are critical for robust sim-to-real transfer. While methods like diffusion-based visuomotor policies have enhanced generalization, they frequently rely on dense RGB observations, remaining susceptible to clutter and appearance variability. The presented work proposes a novel representation, Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting (S2GS), to address this by focusing on extracting clean, object-centric, and domain-invariant spatial features, building upon the advantages of 2DGS while integrating semantic understanding. The aim is to provide a more reliable and efficient input for robotic policies, thereby bridging the simulation-to-reality gap more effectively than previous methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust method for cross-domain transfer in robotic manipulation by addressing the significant domain gap between simulated and real-world environments. The authors hypothesize that this gap can be effectively bridged if policies are trained using domain-invariant features in simulation, and these same features can be reliably extracted and provided as input during real-world deployment.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and limitations of existing approaches:

- **Persistent Domain Gap:** Robotic policies trained exclusively in simulation often fail in real-world scenarios due to discrepancies in visual appearance, object properties, and environmental complexities. This limits the practical applicability of simulation-based learning.
- **Limitations of Current Transfer Methods:**
 - **Domain Randomization and Adaptation:** These methods often require extensive hyperparameter tuning and large, diverse datasets, making them resource-intensive.
 - **Sim-to-Real Calibration:** This approach demands significant engineering effort and may not generalize well to the broad range of variations encountered in real-world settings.
 - **Representation Learning:** Previous representation methods, such as RGB-based, point cloud, and NeRF-based approaches, have their own drawbacks. RGB-based methods struggle with multi-view consistency; point clouds are often sparse and lack high-level semantics; and NeRFs, despite their fidelity, suffer from high computational costs and slow rendering speeds, which are unsuitable for real-time robotic control. These methods generally struggle to efficiently and accurately extract the necessary domain-invariant spatial features.
- **Cognitive Neuroscience Inspiration:** The authors draw an analogy from human cognitive neuroscience, where humans can perform learned manipulation skills in unfamiliar environments. This generalization ability is linked to the brain's capacity to extract stable and invariant visual representations, focusing on essential features while filtering out irrelevant information. Applying this principle to robotics, the authors aim to enable robotic policies to leverage similar domain-invariant spatial features (e.g., object centroids, surface normals, orientations) for robust generalization.
- **Need for a Specific Representation:** Motivated by these points, the research seeks to develop a representation that:
 - Accurately extracts domain-invariant spatial features of task-relevant objects.
 - Offers high editability to flexibly remove irrelevant background content and environmental distractions, allowing the policy to focus on critical targets.
 - Provides strong real-time performance to meet the demands of online robotic control in real-world environments.

Therefore, the key objective is to introduce Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting (S2GS) as a novel representation that can effectively reduce the domain gap in cross-domain policy transfer by providing clean, object-centric, and domain-invariant spatial features, thus enhancing the reliability and generalization of robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting (S2GS) to extract domain-invariant spatial features for robust cross-domain policy transfer. This representation is then integrated with a Diffusion Policy for downstream learning and evaluated in both simulation and real-world environments.

**S2GS Architecture Overview:**
S2GS takes multi-view images as input to construct a 2D feature representation for each frame. These features are then mapped into a unified 3D semantic space using feature-level Gaussian Splatting. A semantic retrieval mechanism subsequently filters out redundant background information, retaining only task-relevant object features. From these, domain-invariant spatial features are extracted, serving as input for the policy. The system also supports dynamic scene updating.

**Core Components of S2GS:**

1. **Appearance Modeling:** The visual fidelity of rendered images ($\hat{I}$) against input images ($I$) is maintained using a combined L1 loss and D-SSIM term, given by $L_{rgb} = (1 - \lambda)L_1(\hat{I}, I) + \lambda L_{D-SSIM}(\hat{I}, I)$, with $\lambda=0.2$.

2. **Hierarchical Semantic Extraction:** To capture semantics at different granularities, the method distills hierarchical semantic features using pre-trained vision foundation models:
 - CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) is used to obtain raw semantic features ($f^I_{raw}$) from the input image.
 - SAM (Segment Anything Model) generates masks ($\{M_I\}$) for different regions.
 - **Global Semantic Feature ($f^I_g$):** Obtained by averaging semantic vectors of pixels within each region, ensuring consistency for the same object.
 - **Local Semantic Feature ($f^I_l$):** Derived by cropping images based on masks, feeding them to CLIP to get object semantic features ($f^I_{obj}$), and then remapping these to the original image space.

3. **Semantic Feature Rendering and Modeling:**
 - Each 2D Gaussian primitive ($G_i$) is augmented with a learnable, low-dimensional semantic feature vector ($f_i$).
 - Semantic features are rendered using an $\alpha$-blending process: $f(u) = \sum_{i \in N} T_i \alpha_i f_i$.
 - A shallow MLP decoder ($\Psi$) maps the rendered features ($\hat{f}$) back to the original high-dimensional space, predicting both global ($\hat{f}_g$) and local ($\hat{f}_l$) semantic features.
 - These predicted features are supervised against their ground-truth counterparts using a cosine similarity loss: $L_f = sim(\hat{f}_g, f^I_g) + \lambda_l sim(\hat{f}_l, f^I_l)$, where $\lambda_l=0.5$.

4. **Geometry Regularization:** To enhance geometric accuracy, local consistency of normal and depth is enforced using a loss term: $L_n = \sum_u \omega(u) (1 - N(u) \cdot N_d(u))$. Here, $N(u)$ is the rendered normal, $N_d(u)$ is the depth normal derived from the rendered depth $D(u)$, and $\omega(u)$ is the rendering weight.

5. **Total Loss Function:** The overall optimization combines these components: $L = L_{rgb} + \lambda_f L_f + \lambda_n L_n$, with weights $\lambda_f = 0.1$ and $\lambda_n = 0.05$.

**Task Target Object Spatial Feature Extraction:**
Once the S2GS field is constructed, target objects are identified via natural language queries using CLIP. The process involves a two-stage refinement:
1. **Instance Clustering:** DBSCAN clustering is applied to the positions of initial object Gaussian primitives to filter noise and identify coherent object regions (parameters: $\epsilon=0.02m$, min_samples=15).
2. **Geometric Completion:** The largest cluster is selected as the target object, and convex hull completion is applied to fill any segmentation gaps.
Finally, a compact spatial feature, typically a centroid representation, is derived from the aggregated Gaussian primitives of the target object, serving as input for the diffusion policy.

**Diffusion Policy Integration:**
A Diffusion Policy is adopted as the downstream learning algorithm. It is conditioned on the object-centric spatial features ($s$) extracted by S2GS and the current robot state (end-effector pose, $q$). The policy denoises a sequence of random noisy actions into a feasible action sequence using a conditional denoising network ($\epsilon_\theta$), optimized with an MSE loss to predict the noise added during the forward diffusion process.

**Dynamic Scene Updating:**
To maintain an accurate scene representation during manipulation, which typically involves object movement, the method incorporates a dynamic updating mechanism. Assuming object rigidity, motion is parameterized by an SE(3) transformation (translation and rotation). These motion parameters are optimized by minimizing the L1 loss between the rendered and input images, focusing on the masked region of the moving object. Robotic manipulation information is integrated as a motion prior to further refine the optimization.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Simulation:** Training is performed in ManiSkill2 with a 7-DOF Franka Panda robot. To facilitate transfer to a 6-DOF UR5 robot, joint angle information is excluded from state inputs.
- **Real-world Deployment:** A UR5 robotic arm is used. An Intel RealSense D435i camera on the gripper performs an initial panoramic scan to construct the S2GS field (7,000 optimization steps for efficiency). A second fixed D435i camera continuously updates the 2DGS representation.
- **Tasks:** PickCube, PushCube, and StackCube are evaluated in both simulation and real-world environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the effectiveness of S2GS in sim-to-real transfer across various robotic manipulation tasks, focusing on spatial feature extraction, policy performance, and geometric accuracy.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

Experiments in the ManiSkill2 simulation environment compared S2GS against baseline methods using different input modalities for a Diffusion Policy (DP). The tasks included PickCube, PushCube, and StackCube.

- **RGB-based Baselines:**
 - **DP-RGB (raw RGB input):** Achieved success rates of 0.76 for Pick, 0.89 for Push, but a low 0.04 for Stack, indicating difficulties with multi-object reasoning and clutter.
 - **DP-RGB + GS (RGB with Gaussian rendering for background filtering):** Showed improvements (0.91 for Pick, 0.92 for Push, 0.29 for Stack), demonstrating the benefit of removing background distractions.
 - **DP-RGB + GS + S2GS (RGB with semantic enhancement):** Further boosted performance to 1.0 for Pick and Push, and 0.94 for Stack, suggesting the value of semantic integration.
- **S2GS-derived Features:**
 - **DP-S2GS (pure S2GS-derived spatial features):** Achieved success rates of 1.0 for Pick, 1.0 for Push, and 0.97 for Stack. This performance matched the ground-truth state input.
- **State-based Upper Bound:**
 - **DP-State (ground-truth state input):** Achieved 1.0 for Pick, 1.0 for Push, and 0.97 for Stack.

These simulation results indicate that S2GS-derived spatial features can effectively replace raw visual inputs, providing cleaner, task-relevant information that leads to performance functionally equivalent to using ground-truth states, thereby reducing potential interference from raw RGB data.

#### 4.2. Real-world Results

The policies trained in simulation with DP-S2GS were directly deployed on a UR5 robotic arm in real-world environments without fine-tuning. Experiments involved a variety of unseen household objects differing in shape, material, and appearance from those in simulation.

- **Overall Performance (Table 2):** S2GS achieved high average success rates across tasks:
 - Pick tasks: 86.7% (e.g., "Pick the wood" 5/5, "Pick the egg" 5/5).
 - Push tasks: 86.7% (e.g., "Push the can" 5/5, "Push the bucket" 5/5).
 - Stack tasks: 80.0% (e.g., "Put the egg in bowl" 5/5, "Put the egg in red can" 4/5).
- **Specific Task Challenges:**
 - "Pick the strawberry" (3/5 success): Attributed to the strawberry's irregular surface and limitations of the two-finger gripper in achieving a force-closure grasp.
 - "Push the mouse" (3/5 success): Due to the mouse's partially symmetric geometry, leading to uneven torque response when pushed off-center.
 - "Put the wood on can" (3/5 success): Performance dropped when the can was upright, increasing vertical placement difficulty and introducing a domain gap beyond the model's adaptation capacity for tall objects.

These real-world results demonstrate S2GS's capability to enable robust cross-domain transfer and maintain strong task performance in complex real-world scenarios, even with objects significantly different from training data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study compared the appearance quality of S2GS with traditional 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) after 7,000 optimization steps.

- **Appearance Quality Metrics (Table 3 & Figure 6):**
 - S2GS achieved a PSNR of 23.68 dB, outperforming 3DGS (21.22 dB) by 2.46 dB.
 - S2GS achieved an SSIM of 0.861, compared to 3DGS's 0.741 (an increase of 0.12).
 - S2GS showed a lower LPIPS score of 0.202, compared to 3DGS's 0.310 (a reduction of 0.108).
This improvement in rendering quality for S2GS is attributed to the 2D Gaussian primitives' ability to better align with object surfaces, leading to sharper reconstructions, improved boundary definition, and fewer artifacts compared to 3DGS.

#### 4.4. Dynamic Scene Updating

The dynamic scene updating mechanism achieved real-time tracking during manipulation tasks, with translation errors at the millimeter level. The semantic filtering mechanism reduced computational overhead by focusing updates on task-relevant regions. Optimization of motion for 10 steps per frame was deemed suitable for robotic control.

In summary, the findings indicate that S2GS successfully extracts object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features, which, when used with a Diffusion Policy, significantly improves sim-to-real transferability and achieves high, stable task performance in complex real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Semantic 2D Gaussian Splatting (S2GS) as a novel representation method with implications for robotic manipulation and sim-to-real transfer. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing the Sim-to-Real Domain Gap:** The core contribution of S2GS is its explicit focus on extracting object-centric, domain-invariant spatial features. By providing these features as input to robotic policies, the method aims to inherently reduce the discrepancies between simulated and real-world environments. This approach offers an alternative to methods that rely heavily on extensive domain randomization, complex domain adaptation, or meticulous sim-to-real calibration, which often require significant resources and engineering effort.

- **Enhanced Policy Generalization and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate that policies trained with S2GS-derived features exhibit robust generalization from simulation to the real world, maintaining high task success rates even with unseen objects and environments. This suggests that S2GS can enable robots to perform learned manipulation skills more reliably in diverse and unstructured real-world settings. The ability to filter out task-irrelevant background distractions contributes to this robustness by allowing the policy to focus on critical target objects.

- **Efficiency and Real-time Capability:** S2GS is designed to operate in real-time, which is a critical requirement for online robotic control. The approach achieves efficient construction of semantic fields and supports dynamic scene updates during manipulation, addressing a major limitation of computationally intensive methods like NeRF. The lightweight optimization process and semantic filtering mechanism contribute to its practical applicability.

- **Improved Geometric and Semantic Representation:** The ablation study indicates that S2GS provides superior appearance quality and geometric accuracy compared to 3D Gaussian Splatting, primarily due to its 2D Gaussian primitives better aligning with object surfaces. This improved reconstruction quality, combined with hierarchical semantic feature extraction, offers a richer and more precise understanding of the scene and target objects.

- **Potential for Broader Applications:** While demonstrated for manipulation primitives (pick, push, stack), the principles of S2GS could extend to other robotic tasks requiring robust 3D perception and semantic understanding, such as assembly, navigation, or human-robot interaction in dynamic environments. The method's ability to query objects using natural language also opens avenues for more intuitive and flexible human interaction with robotic systems.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work acknowledges limitations, such as the current lack of integrated surface normals, which are crucial for contact-sensitive tasks. This highlights potential directions for future research, suggesting that further refinement of the domain-invariant feature set could expand the method's capabilities and impact on even more complex manipulation scenarios.

In summary, S2GS represents a step towards more practical and deployable robotic policies by providing an efficient, accurate, and semantically rich representation that bridges the gap between simulation and reality, thereby reducing the barriers to real-world robotic system development.
