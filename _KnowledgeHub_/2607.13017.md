# FlowWAM: Optical Flow as a Unified Action Representation for World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces FlowWAM, a framework that utilizes optical flow as a unified, video-native action representation for World Action Models (WAMs). It achieves state-of-the-art success rates in robot manipulation tasks and significantly improves the reliability of action-conditioned world modeling by providing dense, cross-frame motion cues.

## Problem

- Existing action representations for World Action Models (WAMs) either lack robot-agnosticism (numerical tokens), fine-grained spatial motion cues (latent actions), or dense temporal action encoding (static image-space actions).
- A modality gap exists between robot-specific control signals and the pixel-level visual processing of general-purpose pretrained video generators used in WAMs.
- Training robust robot policies requires extensive, labeled demonstration data, which is costly and limits scalability.

## Method

- **FlowWAM** encodes ==optical flow== fields into an RGB image format using ==HSV encoding==, making it visually compatible with standard video frames and processable by shared visual encoders.
- A ==dual-stream diffusion transformer==, built on a pretrained video generator, jointly models RGB video and HSV-encoded optical flow, supporting both policy generation and ==action-conditioned world modeling==.
- A ==two-stage training strategy== decouples general motion learning from action-unlabeled human videos (EgoDex) and embodiment-specific action decoding from robot demonstration data (RoboTwin 2.0).

## Results

- FlowWAM achieved a **92.94%** success rate on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, outperforming existing VLA and WAM baselines in robot manipulation tasks.
- It demonstrated the highest overall **EWMScore of 63.71** on the WorldArena benchmark, with an **18.4%** relative improvement in **Trajectory Accuracy** for action-conditioned video prediction.
- In real-world robot experiments, FlowWAM achieved an average success rate of **75.7%** across single and dual-arm manipulation tasks, showing particular effectiveness for bimanual coordination.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optical flow provides a dense, spatially grounded, and video-native representation that effectively closes the modality gap between control signals and visual priors in WAMs.
> - Learning motion priors from large-scale, action-unlabeled human videos through optical flow significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization.
> - A shared generative model simultaneously predicting future RGB frames and corresponding optical flow fields enables a unified framework for both policy learning and reliable world modeling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is actively developing methods for more capable and generalizable control. Within this domain, two prominent paradigms are Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies and World Action Models (WAMs). VLA policies typically leverage semantic knowledge acquired from large-scale image-language pretraining, beneficial for grounding instructions. In contrast, WAMs aim to utilize pretrained video generators, which intrinsically encode motion and temporal dynamics, to facilitate control. This work specifically focuses on advancing WAMs.

A central challenge in WAMs is establishing an effective action representation. This representation must meet two criteria: it needs to align with the visual input format expected by pretrained video generators and it must convey sufficient cross-frame motion cues for precise control. Existing approaches to action representation in WAMs have presented limitations:
- **Numerical action tokens:** While offering precision, these are often robot-specific, which hinders transferability across different robotic platforms. They also do not align natively with the pixel space of video generators.
- **Learned latent actions:** These representations aim for robot-independence by learning from frame transitions. However, their abstract nature may not preserve the dense, spatially grounded motion cues often required for fine-grained control.
- **Image-space actions:** Methods like action ray maps, masks, or multi-view action images render control signals into a visual form. While these visually ground action information, they typically function as static, frame-level conditioning signals that indicate *where* actions occur, rather than directly representing *how* each part of the embodiment moves across frames over time. Consequently, they do not provide a dense, temporally evolving action representation that integrates with future video predictions.

This research identifies a gap in existing WAMs regarding a video-native action representation that simultaneously provides dense, cross-frame motion cues, aligns with video generator inputs, and is decodable into executable control. Prior work has used optical flow as an auxiliary signal or an intermediate step in manipulation pipelines. This paper, however, re-envisions optical flow as a primary, unified action modality that is deeply integrated within the generative latent space of a WAM, an area that has been largely underexplored. FlowWAM positions itself as a development addressing this gap by proposing optical flow as a solution to create a unified, motion-centric, and visually-aligned action representation for WAMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate optical flow as a unified, video-native action representation for World Action Models (WAMs). This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and aims to achieve specific objectives:

**Motivation:**

1. **Leveraging Video Generator Strengths:** WAMs benefit from the motion and temporal dynamics inherently captured by pretrained video generators. However, effectively translating these video priors into executable robot control requires an action representation that is compatible with the video domain.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Action Representations:**
 - **Numerical actions** (e.g., joint torques, end-effector poses) are precise but are robot-specific and do not natively integrate with the pixel-level visual processing of video generators, creating a modality gap.
 - **Learned latent actions** offer abstraction and robot-agnosticism but can be too abstract to provide the dense, spatially grounded motion cues necessary for robust and fine-grained manipulation.
 - **Image-space action conditions** (e.g., masks, ray maps) are visually grounded but often represent static spatial cues (where actions occur) rather than dynamic, cross-frame motion (how parts move), limiting their utility as a continuous action representation that co-evolves with predicted videos.
3. **Need for Dense, Cross-Frame Motion Cues:** Successful robot manipulation, particularly in tasks involving continuous contact and intricate movements, relies on capturing the precise, frame-by-frame motion of the robot and its interaction with the environment. Current visual action representations often lack this dense temporal motion structure.
4. **Enabling Scalable Pretraining:** Acquiring large amounts of robot demonstration data with explicit action labels is resource-intensive. An action representation that can be derived from raw, action-unlabeled videos (e.g., egocentric human manipulation videos) would enable more scalable and data-efficient pretraining strategies for WAMs.

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Uniformity and Video-Native Representation:** To establish optical flow as an action representation that shares the same image format as RGB videos. This objective aims to close the modality gap between control signals and pretrained visual priors, allowing seamless integration within video generative models.
2. **Dual-Mode Functionality:** To develop a framework that uses optical flow for two core modes of WAM operation:
 - **Policy Mode:** Generate future optical flow predictions that can be decoded into executable robot actions, effectively serving as a motion plan for control.
 - **World-Model Mode:** Use provided optical flow sequences as a conditioning signal to guide the generation of future RGB videos, enabling faithful action-conditioned prediction for planning and evaluation.
3. **Reliability and Spatially Grounded Conditioning:** To demonstrate that dense optical flow provides a robust, spatially grounded conditioning signal for video prediction, leading to more faithful and reliable action-conditioned world modeling compared to sparse action tokens or static visual cues.
4. **Scalability through Unlabeled Data:** To enable the learning of motion priors from large-scale action-unlabeled video datasets by virtue of optical flow being extractable from any video sequence without requiring explicit action labels.
5. **Empirical Validation:** To empirically demonstrate that the flow-based action representation improves manipulation success rates in policy mode and enhances the accuracy and reliability of world models in action-conditioned prediction, across both simulated and real-world robotic platforms.

By addressing these motivations and pursuing these objectives, FlowWAM aims to create a more effective and versatile foundation for embodied AI and robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowWAM proposes a dual-stream diffusion framework that leverages optical flow as a unified action representation. This approach integrates action prediction and world modeling within a shared generative model.

#### 3.1. Unified Video-Flow Generation

- **Flow RGB Encoding:** The core idea involves converting the numerical optical flow field, which records per-pixel displacement (u, v), into an RGB image format using an HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) encoding. In this encoding, hue represents the direction of motion, and saturation represents its magnitude. This transformation makes the flow representation visually identical in format to standard RGB video frames, enabling it to be processed by the same visual encoders and video generators. A magnitude cap (25 px) and noise threshold (0.5 px) are applied to normalize and clean the flow data.
- **Dual-Stream Architecture:** FlowWAM utilizes a pretrained video generator (specifically, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, a 5-billion parameter image-to-video diffusion transformer). RGB frames and their corresponding HSV-encoded flow frames are independently encoded by the same frozen VAE encoder. The architecture features stream-specific patch embedding layers and output heads for RGB and flow. Critically, all transformer blocks within the diffusion transformer (DiT) are shared between the two streams. RGB and flow tokens are concatenated for joint self-attention operations within each layer, allowing deep spatiotemporal interaction, and then split back. Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) are applied independently to each stream. This design ensures that the model can leverage the rich motion priors of the pretrained video generator while processing both visual modalities.
- **Two Operating Modes:** The dual-stream DiT supports two primary operational modes:
 - **Policy Mode:** Given a reference image and a language instruction, the model jointly generates future RGB and flow latents. Both streams are initialized from Gaussian noise (except for the first frame, which is conditioned on the clean VAE encoding of the reference observation). An action expert then decodes intermediate features from the denoised flow latents into executable robot actions.
 - **World-Model Mode:** In this mode, a desired motion trajectory is provided as a sequence of clean, VAE-encoded flow latents. Only the RGB latents are initialized from noise and then denoised. The model generates future RGB video frames that are consistent with the provided motion trajectory, enabling action-conditioned prediction.

#### 3.2. Action Prediction from Flow Latents

- **Action Expert:** A dedicated transformer, termed the "action expert," is responsible for translating the predicted dense RGB and flow latents into low-level robot actions. This expert transformer cross-attends to the per-layer hidden states of both the RGB and flow streams from the dual-stream DiT. It also conditions on the current proprioceptive state of the robot and the language instruction (via a T5 text encoder).
- **Action Chunk Prediction:** The action expert predicts an N-step action chunk (e.g., a 32-step chunk of 14D joint positions velocity) using a flow-matching objective.
- **Stochastic Latent Conditioning:** To mitigate the domain gap between training (where the expert sees clean latents) and inference (where it sees potentially noisy generated latents), a noise mixing strategy is employed during training. On 50% of training steps, noise is added to the latents fed to the video generator, and the noise level is passed as an auxiliary embedding to the action expert.

#### 3.3. Training

FlowWAM employs a two-stage training strategy to decouple general motion representation learning from embodiment-specific action decoding:

1. **Stage 1: Action-Free Motion Pretraining:**
 - **Data Source:** EgoDex, a dataset of egocentric human manipulation videos *without* robotic action labels.
 - **Objective:** Only the video generation loss (L_video) is optimized. This loss is a weighted combination of RGB and flow stream losses, both based on a flow matching objective. Motion-aware reweighting is applied to the flow loss to emphasize active motion regions over static backgrounds.
 - **Trainable Parameters:** Only the dual-stream DiT parameters are updated. The VAE and text encoder remain frozen.
 - **Purpose:** To learn manipulation-centric motion priors from large-scale, unlabeled video data.

2. **Stage 2: Joint Embodied Training:**
 - **Data Source:** RoboTwin 2.0, a dataset of robot demonstrations with action labels.
 - **Initialization:** The video generator is initialized from the Stage 1 checkpoint.
 - **Objective:** The full objective L = L_video + λ_a * L_action is optimized. L_action is an independent flow-matching loss supervising the action expert to predict ground-truth action chunks.
 - **Trainable Parameters:** The full DiT, the flow stream components, and the action expert are updated.
 - **Multi-View Input:** RGB observations from head, left-wrist, and right-wrist cameras are tiled into a 320x384 "T-shape" input layout. Flow supervision is primarily provided for the head-camera region using robot-only replay videos (where robot joint actions are replayed in a simulator to isolate embodiment motion and extract clean flow targets).

This methodology aims to create a unified and scalable framework where optical flow serves as the central, video-native action representation, facilitating both robust policy learning and reliable world modeling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates FlowWAM across manipulation policy, action-conditioned world modeling, and real-world robot platforms, supported by ablation studies and qualitative visualizations.

#### 4.1. Decodability for Control (Manipulation Policy)

- **Performance on RoboTwin 2.0:** FlowWAM achieved a 92.94% success rate in the Clean setting and 92.14% in the Random setting on RoboTwin 2.0, a benchmark of 50 bimanual tasks. This performance surpassed both Vision-Language-Action (VLA) baselines (e.g., π₀.₅, X-VLA) and other World Action Model (WAM) baselines (e.g., Motus, GigaWorld-Policy, X-WAM, Fast-WAM). The consistent advantage suggests that dense image-space displacement fields provide a more explicit motion plan for the action expert compared to discrete action tokens.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablations on RoboTwin 2.0 indicated that replacing the flow representation with numerical action vectors reduced the success rate to 69.8%. Substituting HSV-encoded flow with raw (u, v) tensors, which are not in the expected image format, resulted in a 72.3% success rate. These findings emphasize that the HSV-encoded flow format, compatible with the VAE and DiT, is critical for performance. Removing motion-aware reweighting or stochastic latent conditioning for the action expert also led to performance degradation (83.9% and 82.1% respectively), affirming the contribution of these design choices.
- **Flow Decodability Correlation:** A Pearson correlation coefficient of r = -0.81 was observed between the error in FlowWAM's predicted flow (compared to RAFT-extracted pseudo-ground-truth flow) and the task success rate across the 50 RoboTwin tasks. This strong negative correlation provides quantitative evidence that policy gains are directly related to improvements in the quality of flow prediction, supporting the premise that flow is effectively decodable into correct robot actions.

#### 4.2. Scalability via Action-Unlabeled Video Pretraining

- Pretraining FlowWAM on action-unlabeled human egocentric videos from EgoDex demonstrated further performance gains, particularly under the more visually robust Random settings. This aligns with the expectation that motion priors learned from diverse human video can enhance generalization.
- Even without this action-unlabeled pretraining, FlowWAM still outperformed VLA baselines, suggesting that the flow stream itself contributes substantial benefits, with pretraining acting as an amplifier.

#### 4.3. Reliability for World Modeling (Action-Conditioned World Modeling)

- **Performance on WorldArena:** FlowWAM achieved the highest overall EWMScore of 63.71 on WorldArena, a benchmark for evaluating embodied world models.
- **Trajectory Accuracy:** FlowWAM showed an 18.4% relative improvement in Trajectory Accuracy, a diagnostic metric measuring how faithfully a generated rollout follows a given action. Text-conditioned and numerical-action-conditioned baselines performed considerably lower in this aspect, while image-space actions yielded only partial improvements. FlowWAM's strong performance in this metric indicates that dense flow conditioning provides a precise per-pixel motion field that effectively steers video generation to follow the requested action trajectory.
- **Other Metrics:** FlowWAM also achieved the highest Depth Accuracy and maintained competitive performance in other visual quality metrics like Subject and Background Consistency, indicating that reliability gains do not compromise visual quality.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablations on WorldArena showed that different conditioning signals yielded varied EWM Scores: Text only (49.31), Numerical actions (54.18), Raw (u,v) flow (56.72), and Image actions (masks) (57.84). The full RGB flow (65.23) significantly outperformed these alternatives, confirming that the combination of per-pixel motion and a video-native representation is crucial for effective world modeling.

#### 4.4. Real-World Manipulation

- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** FlowWAM was evaluated on real-world single-arm Franka Research 3 tasks (e.g., Stack Bowls, Place in Drawer) and dual-arm ARX 5 tasks (e.g., Fold Towel, Stack Bowls).
- **Success Rates:** FlowWAM achieved the highest success rate on every real-world task, averaging 75.7% across the suite, outperforming baselines π₀.₅ (61.4%) and Motus (57.1%).
- **Bimanual Coordination:** The performance gap between FlowWAM and baselines was more pronounced on dual-arm tasks. This suggests that the flow representation is particularly effective for coordinating multiple robot parts, as it represents coupled arm motion directly in image space rather than solely through low-dimensional joint states.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

- Qualitative visualizations in policy mode demonstrated that predicted flow concentrated on the moving embodiment along coherent, goal-directed trajectories, while the RGB stream preserved object context. The decoded actions consistently led to successful task completion across randomized scenes.
- In world-model mode, generated rollouts faithfully followed the motion prescribed by the conditioning flow, maintaining object identity and static background consistency over frames.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FlowWAM introduces optical flow as a foundational action representation, representing a notable development in the domain of embodied AI and robot manipulation. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Action Representation for WAMs:** By establishing optical flow as a unified, video-native action representation, FlowWAM addresses a critical gap in existing World Action Models. It provides a dense, cross-frame motion signal that directly aligns with the visual processing capabilities of pretrained video generators, unlike prior numerical, latent, or static image-space action representations. This can lead to more intuitive and effective integration of actions within generative models.
- **Enhanced Performance in Control and World Modeling:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that FlowWAM achieves state-of-the-art performance in both manipulation policy execution (on RoboTwin 2.0 and real-world platforms) and action-conditioned world modeling (on WorldArena). This indicates that the flow-based approach is not only conceptually sound but also practically effective, leading to higher success rates in complex tasks and more reliable future prediction.
- **Scalability and Data Efficiency:** The ability to extract optical flow from raw, action-unlabeled videos (e.g., egocentric human demonstrations) is a significant advantage. This enables large-scale action-free pretraining, reducing the reliance on costly, robot-specific labeled demonstration data. This enhanced scalability can accelerate the development of more generalist robot policies and world models.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The performance gains observed, particularly under randomized settings in RoboTwin 2.0, suggest that the flow-based representation contributes to improved visual robustness and generalization. By encoding precise pixel-level motion, FlowWAM can adapt better to variations in object pose, lighting, and backgrounds.
- **Applicability to Complex Manipulation:** The effectiveness of FlowWAM in real-world settings, including challenging dual-arm manipulation tasks requiring bimanual coordination and longer horizons, highlights its practical utility. Representing coupled arm motion directly in image space through flow offers advantages over relying solely on low-dimensional joint states for complex coordination.
- **Unified Framework for Embodied AI:** The dual-stream diffusion framework, which jointly models RGB and flow with a shared pretrained video generator, provides a cohesive and efficient architecture. This unified approach simplifies the development and training of WAMs by treating action prediction and world modeling as a single, consistent video-to-video generation task.

**Potential Future Impact:**

The principles demonstrated by FlowWAM could pave the way for future research directions. These include scaling action-free pretraining to even larger, internet-scale video datasets, which could unlock unprecedented levels of generalizability for robotic agents. Furthermore, extending flow-based planning to significantly longer temporal horizons could enable robots to perform more complex, multi-stage tasks with greater autonomy and foresight. Overall, FlowWAM offers a robust foundation for building more capable, adaptable, and data-efficient robotic systems.
