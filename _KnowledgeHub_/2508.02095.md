# VLM4D: Towards Spatiotemporal Awareness in Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCLA, Microsoft, UCSC, and USC introduce VLM4D, the first benchmark and dataset specifically designed to evaluate spatiotemporal (4D) reasoning in Vision Language Models (VLMs). The study finds a significant performance gap between state-of-the-art VLMs and human observers in comprehending dynamic spatial and temporal interactions.

## Problem

- Current Vision Language Models (VLMs) struggle with spatiotemporal awareness, failing to robustly understand dynamic interactions involving object movements, rotations, and perspective shifts over time and space.
- VLMs lack the ability to implicitly or explicitly reconstruct a four-dimensional (3D space + time) representation of dynamic scenes.
- Existing VLM evaluation benchmarks primarily focus on static images or general video comprehension, neglecting the dynamic interplay of motion and temporal evolution inherent in real-world videos.

## Method

- The ==VLM4D benchmark== is proposed, comprising 1000 real-world and synthetic videos with over 1800 human-annotated ==spatiotemporal question-answer pairs==, explicitly targeting 4D reasoning capabilities.
- A comprehensive evaluation of ==23 state-of-the-art VLMs== (including proprietary and open-source models) is conducted in a zero-shot setting to assess their spatiotemporal understanding.
- Potential solutions like ==spatiotemporal supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== and ==4D feature field reconstruction== are explored to identify promising directions for enhancing VLM performance.

## Results

- Google's Gemini-2.5-Pro achieved the highest VLM accuracy at **62.0%**, while OpenAI's GPT-4o followed at **57.5%**, both significantly below human performance (**98.8%**).
- Targeted supervised fine-tuning on the VLM4D real-world dataset improved Qwen 2VL (7B) accuracy from **38.3%** to **54.5%** and Qwen 2.5VL (7B) from **43.4%** to **55.6%**, demonstrating the value of focused training.
- Enhancing InternVideo2-8B with 4D feature fields using Feature4X resulted in higher accuracy (**37.4%** for CoT) compared to original 2D video inputs (**36.0%** CoT), suggesting benefits of explicit 4D representations despite computational intensity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial performance gap exists between VLMs (top 62.0% accuracy) and human observers (98.8% accuracy) in spatiotemporal reasoning, indicating a fundamental lack of 4D awareness in current models.
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting does not consistently improve VLM accuracy for spatiotemporal tasks and often reveals flaws in reasoning, showing a disconnect between visual perception and linguistic knowledge.
> - An analysis of existing video instruction tuning datasets reveals a critical lack of fine-grained, accurate spatiotemporal annotations, hindering VLMs' ability to learn robust 4D grounding.
