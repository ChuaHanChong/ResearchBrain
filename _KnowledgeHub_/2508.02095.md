# VLM4D: Towards Spatiotemporal Awareness in Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCLA, Microsoft, UCSC, and USC introduce VLM4D, the first benchmark and dataset specifically designed to evaluate spatiotemporal (4D) reasoning in Vision Language Models (VLMs). The study finds a significant performance gap between state-of-the-art VLMs and human observers in comprehending dynamic spatial and temporal interactions.

## Problem

- Current Vision Language Models (VLMs) struggle with spatiotemporal awareness, failing to robustly understand dynamic interactions involving object movements, rotations, and perspective shifts over time and space.
- VLMs lack the ability to implicitly or explicitly reconstruct a four-dimensional (3D space + time) representation of dynamic scenes.
- Existing VLM evaluation benchmarks primarily focus on static images or general video comprehension, neglecting the dynamic interplay of motion and temporal evolution inherent in real-world videos.

## Method

- The ==VLM4D benchmark== is proposed, comprising 1000 real-world and synthetic videos with over 1800 human-annotated ==spatiotemporal question-answer pairs==, explicitly targeting 4D reasoning capabilities.
- A comprehensive evaluation of ==23 state-of-the-art VLMs== (including proprietary and open-source models) is conducted in a zero-shot setting to assess their spatiotemporal understanding.
- Potential solutions like ==spatiotemporal supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== and ==4D feature field reconstruction== are explored to identify promising directions for enhancing VLM performance.

## Results

- Google's Gemini-2.5-Pro achieved the highest VLM accuracy at **62.0%**, while OpenAI's GPT-4o followed at **57.5%**, both significantly below human performance (**98.8%**).
- Targeted supervised fine-tuning on the VLM4D real-world dataset improved Qwen 2VL (7B) accuracy from **38.3%** to **54.5%** and Qwen 2.5VL (7B) from **43.4%** to **55.6%**, demonstrating the value of focused training.
- Enhancing InternVideo2-8B with 4D feature fields using Feature4X resulted in higher accuracy (**37.4%** for CoT) compared to original 2D video inputs (**36.0%** CoT), suggesting benefits of explicit 4D representations despite computational intensity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial performance gap exists between VLMs (top 62.0% accuracy) and human observers (98.8% accuracy) in spatiotemporal reasoning, indicating a fundamental lack of 4D awareness in current models.
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting does not consistently improve VLM accuracy for spatiotemporal tasks and often reveals flaws in reasoning, showing a disconnect between visual perception and linguistic knowledge.
> - An analysis of existing video instruction tuning datasets reveals a critical lack of fine-grained, accurate spatiotemporal annotations, hindering VLMs' ability to learn robust 4D grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as a transformative class of AI, demonstrating remarkable abilities in integrating linguistic and visual reasoning. Initially, much of the foundational work on VLMs focused on static image understanding, leveraging massive datasets of paired image-text data. Subsequent advancements expanded their capabilities to video understanding, tackling tasks such as video captioning, event recognition, and general scene comprehension. These models often operate by aggregating 2D visual features across temporal sequences, aiming to infer events and relationships.

However, despite these strides and training on colossal datasets, the authors identify a critical limitation: current VLMs fundamentally struggle with **spatiotemporal awareness**—the ability to understand dynamic interactions involving object movements, rotations, perspective shifts, and their coherence over time and space. This is precisely where human cognition excels; we effortlessly track complex trajectories, disentangle object motion from camera motion, and interpret physical phenomena in a 4D (3D space + time) context.

The existing research landscape for VLM evaluation has followed a similar trajectory. Initial benchmarks focused on 2D image-based visual question answering. As VLMs evolved to handle video, benchmarks expanded to general video comprehension tasks, assessing temporal coherence and semantic understanding of events. While some recent efforts have begun to explore 3D visual-spatial intelligence, these have largely been confined to *static* 3D scenes, neglecting the dynamic interplay of motion and temporal evolution inherent in real-world videos.

"VLM4D" positions itself as a pioneering work that directly addresses this glaring gap. By introducing the first benchmark explicitly designed to evaluate **4D (3D space + time) intelligence** in VLMs, it pushes the boundaries beyond mere temporal coherence or static 3D understanding. The paper argues that current models, despite their impressive performance on other tasks, lack the deep spatiotemporal grounding necessary for robustly interpreting dynamic real-world environments. This work is a crucial call to action for the AI community to pivot towards developing VLMs with more sophisticated spatial and temporal reasoning capabilities, which are indispensable for advanced applications in embodied AI, robotics, and intelligent agents interacting with dynamic surroundings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind VLM4D stems from the observation that while state-of-the-art Vision Language Models (VLMs) exhibit impressive capabilities in integrating visual and linguistic information, they remain surprisingly inept at understanding dynamic spatiotemporal interactions in a human-like manner. Humans effortlessly perceive, track, and interpret motion, spatial relationships, and temporal changes from various perspectives. This "spatiotemporal cognition" is fundamental to comprehending physical phenomena, object interactions, and causal relationships in our dynamic world. In contrast, current VLMs, despite being trained on vast datasets, fail to translate their general video understanding into robust spatiotemporal reasoning.

The authors highlight a key challenge: VLMs struggle with the implicit or explicit reconstruction of a **four-dimensional (4D) representation**—3D space + time—of dynamic scenes. A compelling example provided is a car moving forward and turning left from its own perspective, which, from a camera's viewpoint, might appear as a combination of rightward movement and recession due to camera rotation. Humans can seamlessly disentangle these complex dynamics by integrating multiple visual cues, but VLMs, relying on aggregated 2D features across time, often provide incorrect interpretations. This discrepancy underscores a significant gap between human and machine spatiotemporal understanding.

Given this critical deficiency, the key objectives of the VLM4D research are threefold:

1. **Propose the first benchmark VLM4D:** To explicitly and rigorously evaluate the spatiotemporal (4D) reasoning capabilities of Vision Language Models. This benchmark aims to provide a standardized tool to characterize and challenge existing models.
2. **Introduce a novel, meticulously curated dataset:** This dataset comprises diverse real-world and synthetic video sequences, carefully paired with spatiotemporal question-answer (QA) annotations. These annotations emphasize translational and rotational motions, perspective awareness, and motion continuity, directly targeting the identified limitations.
3. **Analyze critical limitations and explore solutions:** To systematically evaluate contemporary VLMs on the benchmark, identify their fundamental deficiencies in spatiotemporal reasoning, and conduct experiments exploring potential solutions. This objective aims to highlight challenges and outline clear directions for impactful future research, thereby encouraging deeper exploration into improving VLMs' spatial and temporal grounding.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to catalyze research towards developing VLMs with enhanced "4D intelligence," paving the way for more capable and reliable visual intelligence systems that can truly comprehend and interact with dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VLM4D benchmark is meticulously constructed to rigorously evaluate the spatiotemporal reasoning abilities of VLMs. It comprises 1000 videos paired with over 1800 question-answer (QA) pairs, designed to jointly assess spatial and temporal understanding.

**Benchmark Construction:**
Unlike prior work that often leverages LLMs/VLMs for initial benchmark generation, VLM4D prioritized **direct human annotation** due to the unreliability of automated methods for fine-grained spatiotemporal reasoning. This human-centric approach was then augmented by LLMs for multiple-choice answer generation, followed by extensive quality control.

- **Real Video Data Collection:** Videos were sourced from datasets known for rich spatiotemporal characteristics and diverse motion.
 - **Egocentric data:** Primarily from Ego4D, offering first-person perspectives coupled with individual actions.
 - **Exocentric data:** From DAVIS and YouTube-VOS, providing third-person views.
 - To minimize confounders, videos were pre-processed by temporal segmentation, centering around the most relevant action, resulting in average durations of 3-8 seconds.
- **Synthetic Video Data Generation:** This component addresses the need for 4D reasoning on generated content.
 - **Backbone:** Cosmos, a world-foundation model, was used for video generation.
 - **Motion Control:** Techniques from were incorporated to modify diffusion forward steps, enforcing object localization constraints and ensuring consistency between motion-oriented prompts and generated object directions.
 - Average duration for synthetic videos was 5 seconds. A manual verification step filtered low-quality videos.
- **QA Generation and Quality Control:**
 - **Primary:** Human annotations for question-answer pairs.
 - **Augmentation:** LLM (GPT-4o) generated alternative answers for multiple-choice (MC) questions.
 - **Quality Assurance:** A three-round, multi-person cross-checked verification process was applied to filter ambiguous videos and refine vague or incorrect QA pairs, ensuring precise spatial and temporal alignment.
 - **Synthetic QA:** For synthetic videos, two types of questions were generated: directional questions (derived from generation prompts) and False Positive (FP) questions (querying non-existent objects to assess critical thinking), both following a specific format ("What direction is the <Object Name> moving?"). An LLM (GPT-4o) generated MC answers, followed by manual review.
- **Assessing Human Performance:** A baseline was established by having participants answer 100 randomly sampled questions, with accuracy aggregated to approximate human spatiotemporal reasoning.

**Categorizing Spatiotemporal Performance:**
The benchmark categorizes performance along several dimensions:

- **Perspective:** Egocentric (first-person) vs. Exocentric (third-person).
- **Motion Types:** Translational Movement (TM), Rotational Movement (RM), Spatiotemporal Counting (STM – requiring complex reasoning for object counting during motion), and False Positives (FP – assessing critical thinking for non-existent events).

**Evaluation Setup:**
- **Benchmark Models:** A comprehensive set of 23 state-of-the-art VLMs was evaluated, including both proprietary (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4, Grok-2-Vision) and open-source models (e.g., Llama 4, DeepSeek-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5, InternVideo 2.5/2, Phi-4-multimodal/3.5-vision, Pixtral, VideoLLama3, LLaVA series). Models ranged from 2B to 72B parameters.
- **Evaluation Settings:** All evaluations were performed in a zero-shot setting, using either the full video or a set of sampled frames, followed by a prompt.
- **Inference Settings:**
 - **Direct Output (DO):** Models provide an immediate answer.
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Models generate intermediate reasoning steps before inferring the final answer.
- **Metrics:** Accuracy was the primary metric for multiple-choice questions. LLM-as-Judge (using GPT-o3 and o4-mini) was employed to assess VLM outputs, particularly for CoT, where reasoning and final answers might conflict. Judgments were cross-checked and manually resolved for disagreements.

**Probing Future Solutions:**
The authors explored two promising directions for enhancing spatiotemporal understanding:

1. **Spatiotemporal Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Qwen 2VL (7B) and Qwen 2.5VL (7B) were fine-tuned on subsets of the VLM4D real and synthetic data, evaluating improvements in accuracy.
2. **4D Feature Fields Reconstruction:** InternVideo2-8B was enhanced using Feature4X to construct a 4D feature field from the VLM's 2D feature space. Performance was compared using original 2D videos, rendered novel global-view videos, and reconstructed global feature fields as input modalities on a subset of the DAVIS 2016 dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation on the VLM4D benchmark reveals several critical insights into the spatiotemporal reasoning capabilities of modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

**Significant Performance Gap with Human Baseline:**
A stark finding is the substantial performance disparity between VLMs and human observers. While human accuracy reached an impressive 98.8%, even the top-performing VLM, Google's Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieved an overall accuracy of only 62.0%. OpenAI's GPT-4o followed at 57.5%. This enormous gap (over 30 percentage points) underscores that current VLMs fundamentally lack sophisticated 4D (3D space + time) awareness, operating significantly above random chance (24.1%) but far from human intuition.

**Proprietary Models Outperform Open-Source, but with Notable Exceptions:**
Generally, proprietary models demonstrated superior performance over their open-source counterparts. Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-4o led the overall benchmark. Among open-source models, image-based VLMs like Meta's Llama-4-Scout-17B (54.1%) and Llama-4-Maverick-17B (53.8%) showed strong performance, even surpassing some video-supported models. Alibaba's Qwen2.5-VL-72B (53.0%) and Shanghai AI Lab's InternVideo2.5-8B (52.7%) were top performers in the video-supported open-source category. This indicates that while specialized video architectures are being developed, general-purpose image VLMs are also making significant progress in multimodal reasoning, possibly due to broader training or architectural efficiencies.

**Lack of Generalized Spatiotemporal Understanding:**
The results showed significant performance variations across different data types and perspectives. Models struggled differently with real versus synthetic data, and with egocentric (first-person) versus exocentric (third-person) perspectives. This suggests that current models lack a truly generalized spatiotemporal understanding, instead exhibiting more localized proficiencies that do not robustly transfer across diverse dynamic scenarios.

**Limitations of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:**
The study found no consistent or significant advantage of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting over Direct Output (DO) in improving VLM accuracy for spatiotemporal tasks. Deeper analysis of CoT responses revealed common flaws: models often generated irrelevant information, arrived at conclusions inconsistent with their own reasoning processes, or exhibited a disconnect between their visual perception and linguistic knowledge. While larger models sometimes attempted human-like reasoning strategies, their execution often fell short, highlighting a fundamental deficiency in processing and integrating spatiotemporal cues effectively.

**Deficiencies in Existing Video Instruction Tuning Datasets:**
An extensive analysis of popular supervised fine-tuning (SFT) VLM datasets (over 2 million samples) revealed a critical issue: existing video captions predominantly operate at a "scene" level, lacking the fine-grained temporal, spatial, and object-level details necessary for robust spatiotemporal grounding. Although string-matching identified a high prevalence of spatiotemporal descriptors (e.g., "left," "right," "moving"), manual evaluation indicated that less than 10% of these detected labels were actually accurate or precise in describing motion dynamics. This underscores the inadequacy of current dense captioning approaches for capturing nuanced spatiotemporal relationships.

**Promising Directions for Future Solutions:**
The paper explored two avenues for improving spatiotemporal awareness:

1. **Spatiotemporal Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Targeted SFT on the VLM4D real-world dataset led to significant accuracy improvements for models like Qwen 2VL (7B) (from 38.3% to 54.5%) and Qwen 2.5VL (7B) (from 43.4% to 55.6%). This demonstrates that focused training on high-quality spatiotemporal data can enhance performance. Interestingly, the addition of synthetic data did not consistently improve performance over using real data alone, suggesting that the quality and fidelity of synthetic data are crucial.
2. **4D Feature Fields Reconstruction:** Enhancing InternVideo2-8B with a 4D feature field (using Feature4X) resulted in higher accuracy (37.4% for CoT and 29.0% for DO) compared to using original 2D video (36.0% CoT, 24.3% DO) or global-view video inputs. This improvement is attributed to the inherent structural benefits of 4D representations and the avoidance of rendering artifacts associated with RGB reconstruction. However, this method currently requires computationally intensive per-scene optimization, limiting its generalizability.

These findings collectively highlight that while VLMs are powerful, their spatiotemporal understanding remains a significant bottleneck, primarily due to insufficient data quality and a lack of explicit mechanisms for 4D scene representation and reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VLM4D represents a significant contribution to the field of Vision Language Models and broader AI research. Its impact is multifaceted, ranging from establishing a new evaluation standard to guiding future research and enabling more sophisticated AI applications.

**Establishing a New Standard for Evaluation:** VLM4D is the first benchmark explicitly designed to rigorously evaluate the **4D (3D space + time) reasoning capabilities** of VLMs. By focusing on dynamic spatiotemporal interactions, it fills a critical void in existing evaluation suites that largely overlook nuanced motion understanding, perspective shifts, and temporal coherence in conjunction with spatial awareness. This benchmark will serve as a crucial tool for objectively assessing the progress of future VLMs and ensuring that advancements are not just superficial but address fundamental cognitive abilities.

**Exposing Core Limitations of Current VLMs:** The comprehensive evaluation across a wide array of state-of-the-art models, both proprietary and open-source, unequivocally demonstrates that even the most advanced VLMs are significantly behind human performance in spatiotemporal reasoning. This finding is profoundly important as it challenges the assumption that scaling models and data will automatically lead to human-level "4D intelligence." It highlights a fundamental deficiency, suggesting that current architectural designs and training paradigms may not be inherently equipped to build robust internal representations of dynamic 3D environments over time.

**Identifying Data Deficiencies:** The detailed analysis of popular video instruction tuning datasets reveals a critical bottleneck: the lack of fine-grained, accurate spatiotemporal annotations. This indicates that current data collection and captioning methods are insufficient for teaching VLMs the precise motion dynamics and contextual understanding required for real-world scenarios. This insight provides a clear roadmap for data scientists and researchers to focus on developing higher-quality, spatiotemporally rich datasets.

**Guiding Future Research Directions:** VLM4D does not merely point out problems; it also offers concrete directions for impactful future research. The promising results from targeted supervised fine-tuning and the exploration of 4D feature fields reconstruction suggest viable pathways forward. This work encourages the community to explore:
 - Novel architectural designs that can explicitly or implicitly reconstruct coherent 4D scene representations.
 - Advanced data annotation techniques to capture fine-grained spatiotemporal information.
 - Integration of physics-informed models or structured latent scene representations to imbue VLMs with a more intuitive understanding of dynamic environments.
 - Cross-disciplinary inspiration from cognitive science and neuroscience to understand how humans effortlessly achieve this level of spatiotemporal reasoning.

**Enabling More Capable AI Systems:** The ability to robustly understand spatiotemporal dynamics is not just an academic challenge; it is a prerequisite for a multitude of advanced AI applications.
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics:** For robots to interact intelligently and safely in complex, dynamic human environments, they need to accurately perceive object movements, predict trajectories, and understand the consequences of actions in 4D space.
 - **Interactive AI Systems:** Virtual assistants or augmented reality applications that truly understand dynamic visual environments will offer richer, more intuitive user experiences.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Accurate prediction of other vehicles' and pedestrians' movements is paramount for safety and efficiency.
 - **World Modeling:** Developing truly predictive world models for generative AI or simulation requires a deep understanding of how objects move and interact over time.

In conclusion, "VLM4D" serves as a crucial milestone in the journey towards genuinely intelligent visual agents. By rigorously evaluating and dissecting the spatiotemporal limitations of current VLMs, it provides the necessary framework and insights to inspire and direct future innovations, ultimately pushing the boundaries of AI towards a more human-like comprehension of our dynamic world.
