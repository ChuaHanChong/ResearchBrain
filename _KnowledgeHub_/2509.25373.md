# From Perception to Cognition: A Survey of Vision-Language Interactive Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey analyzes Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) through a "From Perception to Cognition" framework, charting their evolution, challenges, and future directions by examining the integration between visual information acquisition and higher-order reasoning. It identifies a shallow and incoherent integration between perception and cognition as a key bottleneck in current MLLMs.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limitations such as hallucination and biases, stemming from a disjointed integration between visual perception and cognitive reasoning.
- Prior surveys on MLLMs often categorize models or tasks but lack a unified analytical framework to understand the interplay between perception and cognition.
- Current MLLMs struggle to translate low-level pixel processing into robust, coherent internal world models for advanced reasoning.

## Method

- The authors introduce a =="From Perception to Cognition" framework==, deconstructing vision-language understanding into distinct yet interdependent layers: ==Perception== and ==Cognition==.
- Challenges and existing methodologies for MLLMs are ==systematically categorized== and analyzed under this framework, addressing issues from ==low-level visual extraction== to ==dynamic reasoning==.
- The paper reviews relevant ==benchmarks== and applications to evaluate how well MLLMs perform on tasks requiring different levels of perceptual and cognitive capabilities.

## Results

- Proprietary models generally outperform open-source counterparts in complex cognitive tasks, attributed to their scale and advanced architectures like Mixture-of-Experts.
- Open-source models like InternVL3 and Qwen2.5-VL exhibit strong performance in targeted areas through innovations such as process supervision and dynamic resolution.
- Current MLLMs still fall considerably short of human performance in highly specialized cognitive tasks, underscoring ongoing limitations in knowledge integration and reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust cognitive abilities in MLLMs are fundamentally contingent on the quality of their low-level visual perception and fine-grained vision-language alignment.
> - The field is transitioning towards dynamic perception and dynamic forensics, allowing MLLMs to actively re-examine visual evidence and engage in iterative, multi-step problem decomposition.
> - Hallucinations in MLLMs can be attributed to identifiable deficiencies within either the perceptual (e.g., weak visual extraction) or cognitive (e.g., static memory, poor task decomposition) layers.
