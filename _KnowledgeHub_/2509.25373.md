# From Perception to Cognition: A Survey of Vision-Language Interactive Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey analyzes Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) through a "From Perception to Cognition" framework, charting their evolution, challenges, and future directions by examining the integration between visual information acquisition and higher-order reasoning. It identifies a shallow and incoherent integration between perception and cognition as a key bottleneck in current MLLMs.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limitations such as hallucination and biases, stemming from a disjointed integration between visual perception and cognitive reasoning.
- Prior surveys on MLLMs often categorize models or tasks but lack a unified analytical framework to understand the interplay between perception and cognition.
- Current MLLMs struggle to translate low-level pixel processing into robust, coherent internal world models for advanced reasoning.

## Method

- The authors introduce a =="From Perception to Cognition" framework==, deconstructing vision-language understanding into distinct yet interdependent layers: ==Perception== and ==Cognition==.
- Challenges and existing methodologies for MLLMs are ==systematically categorized== and analyzed under this framework, addressing issues from ==low-level visual extraction== to ==dynamic reasoning==.
- The paper reviews relevant ==benchmarks== and applications to evaluate how well MLLMs perform on tasks requiring different levels of perceptual and cognitive capabilities.

## Results

- Proprietary models generally outperform open-source counterparts in complex cognitive tasks, attributed to their scale and advanced architectures like Mixture-of-Experts.
- Open-source models like InternVL3 and Qwen2.5-VL exhibit strong performance in targeted areas through innovations such as process supervision and dynamic resolution.
- Current MLLMs still fall considerably short of human performance in highly specialized cognitive tasks, underscoring ongoing limitations in knowledge integration and reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust cognitive abilities in MLLMs are fundamentally contingent on the quality of their low-level visual perception and fine-grained vision-language alignment.
> - The field is transitioning towards dynamic perception and dynamic forensics, allowing MLLMs to actively re-examine visual evidence and engage in iterative, multi-step problem decomposition.
> - Hallucinations in MLLMs can be attributed to identifiable deficiencies within either the perceptual (e.g., weak visual extraction) or cognitive (e.g., static memory, poor task decomposition) layers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are pushing the boundaries towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) by integrating the sophisticated symbolic reasoning of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the perceptual acuity of Computer Vision (CV) models. However, the paper identifies a critical bottleneck in current MLLMs: a "shallow and incoherent integration" between information acquisition (Perception) and reasoning (Cognition). This fundamental disconnect leads to prominent issues like hallucination and biases, revealing that the ability to process raw pixels does not automatically translate into a coherent internal world model.

This survey positions itself by acknowledging prior MLLM surveys that have offered foundational overviews, specialized in reasoning, or focused on fine-grained visual reasoning. However, it distinguishes itself by introducing a *novel and unified analytical framework*: "From Perception to Cognition." Unlike existing surveys that might categorize models or tasks, this work deconstructs interactive reasoning into two interdependent layers – Perception and Cognition – to analyze the *root causes* of model failures. This framework provides a more foundational perspective, elucidating the intrinsic limitations of current MLLMs and offering a structured lens through which to understand the field's evolutionary trajectory. It asserts that advancements in Perception are a necessary prerequisite for building truly robust Cognition in MLLMs, thereby offering a holistic roadmap for future development. The work addresses a crucial need for a systematic, integrated understanding of how these two core capabilities interact and influence the overall performance and trustworthiness of MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this survey are clearly articulated:
1. **Introduce a Novel Unified Analytical Framework:** The core objective is to propose and utilize the "From Perception to Cognition" framework. This framework aims to deconstruct the complex process of vision-language interactive understanding into two distinct yet interdependent layers: Perception (accurate visual information extraction and fine-grained alignment with textual instructions) and Cognition (higher-order, proactive, multi-step, goal-oriented reasoning, characterized by an "observe-think-verify" loop).
2. **Systematically Dissect and Address MLLM Challenges:** Guided by this framework, the authors aim to systematically analyze the key bottlenecks of current MLLMs at both the perceptual and cognitive layers, such as weak visual information extraction, limited vision-language interaction, challenges in task decomposition, and memory-based reasoning leading to hallucinations.
3. **Survey Cutting-Edge Methodologies:** The paper seeks to review the landscape of advanced methods developed to overcome these challenges, covering techniques that enhance low-level visual representations, improve high-level reasoning paradigms (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, dynamic reasoning), and bridge the gap between perception and cognition.
4. **Review Critical Benchmarks and Applications:** It aims to provide a thorough analysis of relevant benchmarks and applications across diverse domains (scientific problem-solving, medical diagnosis, diagram understanding, video understanding, sentiment analysis) to assess how current models perform on tasks requiring different balances of perceptual and cognitive skills.
5. **Delineate Future Research Directions:** Finally, the survey aims to identify promising future research avenues, such as unified vision encoders, latent reasoning, generative reasoning, tool-augmented reasoning, cross-image relation reasoning, and real-world cognitive evaluation, to illuminate the path toward building next-generation MLLMs capable of deep understanding and genuine world interaction.

The overarching motivation is to provide the research community with a clear, structured perspective for understanding the intrinsic limitations of current MLLMs and to offer a comprehensive guide for advancing the field towards more intelligent, human-like AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this survey is structured and comprehensive, following its proposed "From Perception to Cognition" framework. The authors systematically organize their analysis into distinct sections:

1. **Introduction and Core Framework Definition:** The paper begins by establishing the context of MLLM advancements and their limitations. It then formally defines its central "Perception" and "Cognition" concepts, elaborating on their interrelationship and outlining the "observe-think-verify" loop essential for cognitive processes.
2. **Problem and Challenge Analysis (Section 2):** This section identifies specific challenges within MLLMs, categorizing them directly under the Perception and Cognition layers:
- **Perception Challenges:** Weak low-level visual information extraction and limited interaction between visual and textual information.
- **Cognition Challenges:** Difficulties in decomposing complex tasks and reliance on one-shot, static evidence, leading to forgetting and hallucinations.
3. **Methodology Survey (Section 3):** This is the core of the survey, where existing research methods are systematically reviewed and classified based on which layer's challenges they address:
- **Perception Enhancement:** Explores techniques to enhance low-level visual perception (e.g., individual encoder optimization, multi-encoder integration, knowledge distillation) and improve vision-language alignment (e.g., projection layer improvements, task-specific fine-tuning, prompt tuning, dynamic visual search mechanisms).
- **Cognition Enhancement:** Delves into methods for enhancing problem decomposition (e.g., imitation learning, curriculum learning, preference learning, automated data synthesis, inference-time search algorithms) and mitigating hallucinations through dynamic forensics (e.g., endogenous visual evidence injection via single/multiple forward passes, and exogenous visual evidence injection via external tools).
4. **Applications and Benchmarks (Section 4):** This section provides a thorough review of how the capabilities of MLLMs are evaluated in practice. It covers diverse domains, including scientific problem-solving, medical diagnosis, diagram understanding, video understanding, and sentiment analysis. For each domain, it discusses key benchmarks, their specific challenges, and presents performance comparisons of leading proprietary and open-source models.
5. **Future Directions (Section 5):** The survey concludes by outlining promising research avenues derived from the identified gaps and emerging trends, such as unified vision encoders, latent reasoning, generative reasoning, tool-augmented reasoning, cross-image relation reasoning, and real-world cognitive evaluation.

Throughout the survey, the authors leverage a structured taxonomy, tables, and illustrative figures (e.g., Fig. 1 showing the evolution, Fig. 2 outlining the survey structure, Fig. 3 illustrating the perception-cognition loop) to clarify complex concepts and organize the vast body of literature. The explicit connection of methods and challenges to the Perception-Cognition framework ensures a coherent and insightful analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, the "findings" are the synthesized insights and trends observed across the MLLM research landscape. The key findings and results presented are:

1. **The Foundational Role of Perception:** A central finding is the increasing consensus that a precise and fine-grained Perception layer is a prerequisite for advanced Cognition in MLLMs. Early models suffered from coarse-grained visual encoders (like CLIP-ViT) that lacked fine-grained recognition and spatial localization, limiting their ability to support complex reasoning.
2. **Advancements in Visual Encoders:** Significant progress has been made in enhancing low-level visual perception through:
- **Individual Encoder Optimization:** Newer models (MetaCLIP, SigLip, Eva-CLIP) improve fine-grained representation, while DINOv2 strengthens geometric and structural understanding through self-supervised learning.
- **Multi-Encoder Integration:** Combining complementary strengths of different encoders (e.g., CLIP and DINOv2) via static fusion (FerretV2, SPHINX) or dynamic Mixture-of-Experts (MoME, MoVA) architectures, or distilling their capabilities into a single efficient encoder (Radio, UNIC).
3. **Enhanced Vision-Language Alignment:** Methods now focus on task-driven alignment:
- **Projection Layer Improvements:** Dynamic and adaptive projection layers (ChartMoE, LLaVA-Octopus, Ovis) replace static MLPs to extract task-relevant visual features.
- **Task-Specific Fine-Tuning & Prompt Tuning:** Instruction fine-tuning (MATCHA, LLaVA-Med) and parameter-efficient prompt tuning (VPGTrans, TVP) are used to adapt models to specific visual tasks.
- **Instruction Encoding & Output Architecture Enhancements:** Encoding coordinates, boxes, or masks directly into prompts (Shikra, Kosmos-2, GLaMM) and introducing special tokens (<SEG>) for pixel-level outputs (LISA, GSVA) facilitate more precise interactive understanding.
4. **Emergence of Dynamic Perception:** To overcome static visual processing, models are being endowed with dynamic perception capabilities (V*, DyFo, FaST), allowing them to actively and iteratively re-examine visual evidence, akin to an "iterative visual search loop."
5. **Evolution of Problem Decomposition (Cognition):** MLLMs are moving beyond single-step reasoning towards robust, multi-step problem decomposition:
- **Training Paradigms:** Supervised fine-tuning with process supervision (Multimodal-CoT, Visual CoT), curriculum learning (LLaVA-CoT), and preference learning (UV-DPO, VLM-R1) are being used to internalize decomposition skills.
- **Automated Data Synthesis:** To overcome data scarcity, automated methods (Cogcom, Mint-CoT) synthesize large-scale, high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets with interleaved visual and textual evidence, using Vision-to-Text, Text-to-Vision, or Interleaved approaches. Bootstrapped methods (MC-CoT, GCOT) further refine data generation.
- **Inference-Time Search:** Algorithms like Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) are being adapted for multimodal tasks (VisuoThink, Socratic-MCTS) to explore multiple reasoning paths and improve solution robustness.
6. **Dynamic Forensics During Reasoning:** To mitigate hallucinations and integrate visual evidence dynamically:
- **Endogenous Evidence Injection:** Methods either use a single forward pass with dynamic attention re-weighting (Look-back, MINT-CoT) or employ multiple forward passes to actively re-encode specific regions of interest (CogCoM, DeepEyes, SIFThinker).
- **Exogenous Evidence Injection:** MLLMs are treated as agents that call external tools (e.g., visual experts, segmentation models) to gather evidence, forming an "observe-act-decide" loop, trained via in-context learning or fine-tuning (MM-ReAct, ViperGPT, LLaVA-Plus, TACO).
7. **Benchmark Landscape & Model Performance:**
- **Proprietary Model Dominance:** Proprietary models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o, Med-PaLM M) generally demonstrate superior and more balanced performance across various benchmarks, especially in complex cognitive tasks, often attributed to larger scale, advanced architectures (like MoE), and sophisticated internal reasoning mechanisms.
- **Open-Source Strengths:** Open-source models (InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL, STORM) show exceptional performance in specific areas due to architectural innovations (e.g., process supervision in InternVL3, dynamic resolution and time encoding in Qwen2.5-VL, temporal encoders in STORM).
- **Cognitive Gaps:** A significant gap persists between even the best models and human performance on highly specialized cognitive tasks, such as complex pathological understanding (PathVQA) or open-ended scientific problem-solving, highlighting limitations in knowledge integration and innovative reasoning.
- **Evaluation Challenges:** The evaluation ecosystem is fragmented, often lacking transparency for proprietary models, and susceptible to data contamination and "shortcut" learning, making fair comparisons difficult.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the MLLM research community:

1. **A Unifying Framework:** The "From Perception to Cognition" framework offers a clear, structured, and intuitive lens through which to understand the capabilities and limitations of MLLMs. This unification is crucial for moving beyond ad-hoc solutions and guiding research towards foundational improvements in vision-language interaction. It provides a common language and conceptual model for discussing complex multimodal reasoning.
2. **Structured Taxonomy of Methodologies:** By organizing a vast and rapidly expanding body of literature into a coherent taxonomy, the survey clarifies the relationships between seemingly disparate research efforts. Researchers can quickly identify state-of-the-art methods addressing specific perceptual or cognitive challenges, understand their underlying principles, and avoid redundant efforts. This fosters more targeted and efficient research.
3. **Elucidation of Developmental Dependencies:** The survey explicitly highlights that high-level cognitive abilities are fundamentally dependent on the quality of low-level visual representation and fine-grained visual-language alignment. This unified perspective emphasizes that advancements in Perception are not isolated but integral and sequential stages for achieving breakthroughs in Cognition, thereby offering a strategic roadmap for MLLM development.
4. **Diagnosis of Core Challenges (e.g., Hallucination):** By mapping issues like hallucination to specific deficiencies at either the perceptual (e.g., weak visual extraction) or cognitive (e.g., static memory, poor decomposition) level, the survey provides a clearer diagnosis of root causes. This clarity is essential for developing effective mitigation strategies.
5. **Comprehensive Benchmark Analysis:** The detailed review and comparative analysis of benchmarks across diverse applications offer valuable insights into current model performance, strengths, and weaknesses. It highlights areas where MLLMs still fall short of human-level intelligence, particularly in highly specialized or open-ended cognitive tasks, underscoring the need for further research.
6. **Guiding Future Research:** The concrete future directions outlined in the survey provide a clear agenda for the next generation of MLLMs. Topics such as unified visual encoders, latent reasoning, generative reasoning, and tool-augmented reasoning represent high-impact areas that could fundamentally bridge the perception-cognition gap, leading to more robust, controllable, and genuinely intelligent multimodal AI agents capable of understanding and interacting with the world in a human-like manner.

In summary, this survey serves as an invaluable reference for both newcomers and experienced researchers, offering a structured, up-to-date overview of progress and a strategic guide for future work in interactive vision-language reasoning, ultimately contributing to the advancement of MLLMs towards achieving true artificial general intelligence.
