# ACE: A Cross-Platform Visual-Exoskeletons System for Low-Cost Dexterous Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, San Diego developed ACE (A Cross-platform visual-Exoskeletons system), a low-cost teleoperation solution combining exoskeleton-based wrist tracking with vision-based hand pose estimation for dexterous robot control. The system demonstrated an average end-effector accuracy of 3mm and enabled the training of robot policies that successfully executed complex manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robot teleoperation systems are often prohibitively expensive.
- Current teleoperation solutions are frequently restricted to specific robot platforms or end-effectors.
- Many systems limit the level of dexterity achievable for complex manipulation tasks.

## Method

- ==The University== of California, San Diego developed **ACE**, a low-cost, cross-platform visual-exoskeleton system for robot teleoperation.
- ACE integrates a 3D-printed 6-DOF exoskeleton for wrist pose estimation with webcams for vision-based hand pose detection, processed by MediaPipe.
- The system costs approximately $150 to build and features modular components for easy assembly, size adjustments, and multi-platform compatibility.

## Results

- The ACE system achieved an average end-effector accuracy of only 3mm during a tape measure test, demonstrating high precision in movement replication.
- In simulation tests, ACE consistently showed shorter task completion times, higher reaching speeds, and better success rates compared to the GELLO baseline.
- Policies trained using data collected via ACE successfully enabled robots to perform various real-world manipulation tasks, including vacuuming a keyboard, serving candies, and grasping dolls.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining exoskeleton-based wrist tracking with vision-based hand pose estimation enables precise, dexterous robot control that is also affordable.
> - A modular, 3D-printable design using low-cost components significantly reduces the barrier to entry for advanced teleoperation systems, making them accessible to a wider range of researchers.
> - Flexible control modes for different robot platforms and end-effectors, including wrist-to-end-effector mappings and specific gripper/hand controls, allow for broad applicability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Robot teleoperation systems are essential tools for collecting high-quality data to train robots in complex manipulation tasks. However, existing solutions often come with significant limitations: they can be prohibitively expensive, restricted to specific robot platforms, or limited in the dexterity they enable. Researchers from the University of California, San Diego have developed a novel teleoperation system called ACE (A Cross-platform visual-Exoskeletons system) that addresses these challenges.

ACE combines the precision of exoskeleton-based systems with the flexibility of vision-based approaches, resulting in a low-cost solution that works across various robot platforms and end-effectors. This hybrid approach enables highly accurate control for dexterous manipulation while maintaining affordability and adaptability.

### 2. System Design

The ACE system features a modular design built around 3D-printed exoskeletons equipped with low-cost webcams. The hardware components include:

- **Exoskeleton Structure**: A 6-degree-of-freedom (DOF) joint system (1 DOF base joint, 3 DOF elbow joint, and 2 DOF wrist joint) that captures the operator's arm and wrist movements.
- **Camera Mount**: Webcams mounted near the wrist to capture hand poses and finger movements.
- **Modular Components**: Parts connected via magnetic connectors for easy assembly, size adjustments, and component replacement.
- **Control Board**: Processes joint angle readings from encoders and calculates wrist pose using forward kinematics.

The system costs approximately $150 to build, making it significantly more affordable than commercial alternatives that can cost thousands of dollars. The modular design accommodates users of different sizes and allows for quick adjustments or component replacements.

### 3. Working Mechanism

ACE operates through a two-part process to translate human movements into robot actions:

1. **Wrist Pose Estimation**: The system reads joint angles from the exoskeleton encoders and calculates the wrist pose using forward kinematics. This provides highly accurate position and orientation data for the operator's wrist.

2. **Hand Pose Detection**: The webcams capture images of the operator's hand, which are processed using MediaPipe to detect 21 hand keypoints. These keypoints are transformed into the wrist frame to provide consistent hand pose data regardless of wrist orientation.

The system then maps these human inputs to robot control signals through:

3. **Pose Mapping**: The operator's wrist pose is mapped to the robot's end-effector pose, addressing challenges such as workspace mismatches and scale differences between human and robot movements.

4. **End-Effector Control**: Depending on the end-effector type (gripper or hand), the system translates hand pose data into appropriate control signals, enabling fine-grained manipulation.

### 4. Control Modes

To accommodate various robots and tasks, ACE implements several control modes:

**Wrist-to-End-Effector Mapping Modes**:
- **Normal Mode**: Directly transfers end-effector positions to the robot, suitable for situations where the operator and robot face the same direction.
- **Mirror Mode**: Designed for face-to-face operation with the robot, particularly useful for larger robots or when direct visual feedback is needed.
- **Bimanual Mode**: Specialized mode for two-handed tasks that require high precision coordination.

**End-Effector Control Modes**:
- **Gripper Mode**: For parallel-jaw grippers, where finger positions are mapped to gripper opening/closing actions.
- **Hand Mode**: For anthropomorphic hands, where the operator's finger movements are retargeted to corresponding robot hand joints.

These flexible control modes allow ACE to adapt to different robot platforms including fixed-base arms, quadrupeds, and humanoids, as well as various end-effectors from simple grippers to complex robotic hands.

### 5. Performance Evaluation

The ACE system demonstrates impressive performance in both precision and efficiency:

**End-Effector Accuracy**: When tested by having both the operator and robot measure distances with a tape measure, the system showed an average error of only 3mm, confirming high precision in movement replication.

**Target-Reaching Efficiency**: Compared to the GELLO baseline in simulation tests, ACE consistently demonstrated:
- Shorter time costs for completing tasks
- Higher reaching speeds
- Higher effective ratios (proportion of movement contributing to task completion)
- Better success rates across varying workspace and target sizes

These results indicate that ACE enables more intuitive and efficient teleoperation, particularly for precision tasks where accuracy is crucial.

### 6. Applications in Imitation Learning

One of the most significant contributions of ACE is its ability to efficiently collect high-quality demonstration data for imitation learning. The system was used to train robot policies for various real-world tasks:

**Demonstrated Tasks**:
- Vacuuming a keyboard
- Serving candies
- Wiping a whiteboard
- Grasping dolls
- Putting tennis balls in a container

Policies trained on demonstrations collected with ACE achieved high success rates across these diverse tasks, demonstrating the system's ability to transfer dexterous human skills to robots. The trained policies successfully reproduced complex manipulation sequences, showcasing ACE's value for robot learning applications.

### 7. Advantages Over Existing Systems

ACE offers several advantages over existing teleoperation systems:

1. **Cross-Platform Compatibility**: Unlike platform-specific systems, ACE works with various robot types (humanoids, quadrupeds, arm-hand, arm-gripper) and end-effectors (anthropomorphic hands, grippers).

2. **Cost-Effectiveness**: At approximately $150, ACE is significantly more affordable than commercial alternatives that can cost thousands of dollars.

3. **Dexterity**: The combination of precise wrist tracking and vision-based hand pose estimation enables fine-grained control for complex manipulation tasks.

4. **User-Friendliness**: The modular design accommodates users of different sizes with minimal calibration required.

5. **Maintainability**: Magnetic connections allow for easy assembly, disassembly, and component replacement.

These advantages make ACE particularly valuable for research labs and educational institutions with limited budgets but requiring advanced teleoperation capabilities.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its strengths, ACE has some limitations that could be addressed in future work:

1. **Camera Mount Limitations**: The current camera mount can hinder certain hand movements and increase rotational burden. Future designs could explore alternative camera placements or tracking methods.

2. **Software Improvements**: Enhanced inverse kinematics solvers could improve robot arm maneuverability, while more refined hand pose estimation could enable more accurate finger control.

3. **Integration with Other Platforms**: Future work could explore integrating ACE with platforms like Vision Pro to extend its capabilities and user interface options.

4. **Bimanual Coordination**: While ACE supports bimanual operation, there's room for improvement in coordinating two-handed tasks, particularly for tasks requiring high precision.

### 9. Conclusion

The ACE system represents a significant advancement in teleoperation technology, offering a low-cost, cross-platform solution for dexterous robot control. By combining the precision of exoskeleton-based systems with the flexibility of vision-based approaches, ACE enables accurate and intuitive control across various robot platforms and end-effectors.

Its successful application in collecting high-quality data for imitation learning demonstrates its potential impact on robot learning research. By making advanced teleoperation more accessible, ACE contributes to the democratization of robotics, allowing more researchers and educators to explore complex manipulation tasks and advance the field of robot learning.

The modular, adaptable design of ACE, coupled with its impressive performance in precision and efficiency, positions it as a valuable tool for the robotics community, particularly for those seeking affordable solutions for dexterous teleoperation and imitation learning data collection.
