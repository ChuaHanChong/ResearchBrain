# ACE: A Cross-Platform Visual-Exoskeletons System for Low-Cost Dexterous Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, San Diego developed ACE (A Cross-platform visual-Exoskeletons system), a low-cost teleoperation solution combining exoskeleton-based wrist tracking with vision-based hand pose estimation for dexterous robot control. The system demonstrated an average end-effector accuracy of 3mm and enabled the training of robot policies that successfully executed complex manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robot teleoperation systems are often prohibitively expensive.
- Current teleoperation solutions are frequently restricted to specific robot platforms or end-effectors.
- Many systems limit the level of dexterity achievable for complex manipulation tasks.

## Method

- ==The University== of California, San Diego developed **ACE**, a low-cost, cross-platform visual-exoskeleton system for robot teleoperation.
- ACE integrates a 3D-printed 6-DOF exoskeleton for wrist pose estimation with webcams for vision-based hand pose detection, processed by MediaPipe.
- The system costs approximately $150 to build and features modular components for easy assembly, size adjustments, and multi-platform compatibility.

## Results

- The ACE system achieved an average end-effector accuracy of only 3mm during a tape measure test, demonstrating high precision in movement replication.
- In simulation tests, ACE consistently showed shorter task completion times, higher reaching speeds, and better success rates compared to the GELLO baseline.
- Policies trained using data collected via ACE successfully enabled robots to perform various real-world manipulation tasks, including vacuuming a keyboard, serving candies, and grasping dolls.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining exoskeleton-based wrist tracking with vision-based hand pose estimation enables precise, dexterous robot control that is also affordable.
> - A modular, 3D-printable design using low-cost components significantly reduces the barrier to entry for advanced teleoperation systems, making them accessible to a wider range of researchers.
> - Flexible control modes for different robot platforms and end-effectors, including wrist-to-end-effector mappings and specific gripper/hand controls, allow for broad applicability.
