# Neural Predictor-Corrector: Solving Homotopy Problems with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Hunan University and Westlake University developed the Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC) framework, which applies reinforcement learning to unify and automate predictor-corrector solvers for diverse homotopy problems. This approach replaces manual heuristics with adaptive policies, leading to significantly enhanced computational efficiency, such as a 70-80% reduction in corrector iterations and 80-90% reduction in runtime in robust optimization, while maintaining solution accuracy across various tasks.

## Problem

- Solvers for homotopy problems rely on manually designed, task-specific heuristics for step sizes and termination criteria, leading to suboptimal performance and extensive manual tuning.
- Diverse problems solvable by homotopy, such as robust optimization and polynomial root-finding, lack a unified algorithmic framework, leading to fragmented research and bespoke solutions.
- Previous learning-based methods for homotopy often require per-instance training or address only specific components, limiting their generalization across different problem instances.

## Method

- Introduce the **Neural Predictor-Corrector** (NPC) framework, which formulates the ==predictor-corrector== process of homotopy methods as a Markov Decision Process.
- Utilize a neural network to learn adaptive policies for dynamically controlling predictor step sizes and corrector termination criteria, replacing traditional hand-crafted heuristics.
- Implement amortized training to develop a single, generalized policy that can be deployed efficiently on diverse, unseen problem instances within a class after one-time offline training.

## Results

- NPC significantly improved computational efficiency across tasks, achieving 70-**80%** fewer corrector iterations and 80-**90%** reduced runtime in robust optimization while maintaining solution accuracy.
- For polynomial root-finding, NPC+HC reduced iterations by up to **82%** (e.g., from 39 to 7 for Katsura10) and runtime by up to **70%** (e.g., **2.22ms** to **0.65ms** for Katsura10) with a **100%** success rate.
- The framework consistently demonstrated superior computational efficiency and numerical stability across all four diverse homotopy problem classes (robust optimization, global optimization, polynomial root-finding, and sampling) without compromising solution accuracy compared to classical and specialized baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A wide range of problems, including robust optimization and sampling, can be unified under the homotopy paradigm, sharing a common predictor-corrector algorithmic structure.
> - Reinforcement learning offers a powerful approach to automate and optimize the sequential decision-making process within non-differentiable numerical procedures like predictor-corrector methods.
> - Amortized training enables a learned policy to generalize across diverse problem instances within a class, providing a scalable and efficient alternative to problem-specific heuristics or per-instance learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a class of challenging problems that can be formulated under the homotopy paradigm, a general principle spanning robust optimization, global optimization, polynomial root-finding, and sampling. Solvers for these problems commonly employ a predictor-corrector (PC) structure, which involves predicting the next step along a solution trajectory and then correcting that prediction.

Historically, these predictor-corrector methods have been developed independently within each problem domain. This independent evolution has led to a reliance on manually designed heuristics for critical parameters such as step sizes and termination criteria. These heuristics are often suboptimal, task-specific, and require considerable manual tuning.

Previous attempts to incorporate learning into homotopy workflows have generally focused on specific components or required per-instance training. For example, some learning-based methods have been applied to Gaussian homotopy or polynomial root-finding, but they typically lacked a unified framework or the ability to generalize across different problem instances without retraining. Similarly, reinforcement learning (RL) has been utilized to learn optimizers or adapt algorithmic parameters in certain optimization and sampling tasks. However, these applications have not addressed the comprehensive predictor-corrector control problem across diverse homotopy classes, nor have they leveraged amortized training to produce a single policy transferable across multiple instances of a problem type.

This research aims to unify these disparate approaches under a single framework by recognizing their shared underlying homotopy and predictor-corrector structure. By doing so, it proposes a general neural solver that replaces manual heuristics with automatically learned policies, thereby seeking to improve efficiency, stability, and generalization beyond the capabilities of existing classical and learning-based methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in current solvers for homotopy problems. While the homotopy paradigm offers a general principle for tackling complex problems by transforming them into simpler, solvable forms, the practical algorithms, often based on a predictor-corrector (PC) structure, exhibit several drawbacks:
- **Suboptimal and Task-Specific Heuristics:** Current PC solvers rely on hand-crafted heuristics for determining step sizes and termination rules. These heuristics are typically developed for specific problems or problem classes, making them suboptimal when applied broadly and necessitating extensive manual tuning for each new instance or variant. This lack of adaptability hinders efficiency and robustness across diverse problem landscapes.
- **Lack of Unification:** Methods implementing the homotopy paradigm have largely evolved independently across domains such as robust optimization, global optimization, polynomial root-finding, and sampling. This independent development has prevented the recognition of a common underlying structure and, consequently, the design of a general-purpose solver. The absence of a unified framework leads to fragmented research efforts and bespoke solutions for each problem.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
- **Unified Framework for Homotopy Problems:** To systematically unify diverse problems, including robust optimization, global optimization, polynomial system root-finding, and sampling, under the homotopy paradigm. This unification aims to demonstrate that their respective solvers fundamentally share a common predictor-corrector structure, thus enabling a generalized approach rather than problem-specific solutions.
- **Development of a Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC):** To introduce a novel reinforcement learning-based framework, named Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC), that replaces hand-crafted heuristics in PC solvers with automatically learned, adaptive policies. This involves formulating the policy selection process as a sequential decision-making problem.
- **Enhanced Generalization through Amortized Training:** To implement an amortized training mechanism within the NPC framework. This mechanism is designed to facilitate one-time offline training over a distribution of problem instances, yielding a policy capable of efficient, training-free deployment on new, previously unseen instances from the same problem class.
- **Demonstration of Performance and Stability:** To evaluate the NPC framework across multiple representative homotopy problems, demonstrating its ability to generalize effectively, achieve superior computational efficiency, and maintain higher numerical stability compared to existing classical and specialized baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on unifying diverse problems under the homotopy paradigm and developing a Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC) framework guided by reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Homotopy Paradigm as a Unified Perspective

The paper begins by defining the homotopy paradigm as a general principle for solving a complex target problem, g(x), by continuously interpolating from a simpler source problem, f(x). This interpolation is represented by a homotopy function H(x, t), where H(x, 0) = f(x) and H(x, 1) = g(x). The solution of the target problem is found by tracing an implicit solution trajectory x*(t) as t varies from 0 to 1.

#### 3.2. Predictor-Corrector Algorithm

The practical algorithm for tracing this trajectory is the Predictor-Corrector (PC) method. This method involves two steps at each stage of the interpolation:
- **Predictor:** Proposes the next homotopy level (t) and provides an initial estimate of the solution (x) at that level.
- **Corrector:** Iteratively refines the predicted solution to ensure it accurately lies on the true solution trajectory, preventing error accumulation.
Traditional PC methods rely on fixed, hand-designed heuristics for determining the step size of the predictor and the number of iterations or convergence tolerance for the corrector.

#### 3.3. Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC)

The core innovation, NPC, replaces these heuristic rules with adaptive policies learned by a neural network. The entire predictor-corrector process is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **State (s):** At each homotopy interpolation level, the agent observes a state composed of:
 - **Homotopy Level:** The current position 't' along the interpolation path.
 - **Corrector Statistics:** The iteration count and the attained tolerance from the previous corrector step. This information reflects the convergence efficiency and the deviation from the predicted trajectory.
 - **Convergence Velocity:** The relative change in an optimality metric (e.g., objective value for optimization/root-finding, Kernelized Stein Discrepancy for sampling) between consecutive homotopy levels, indicating the rate of change of the solution.
- **Action (a):** Based on the observed state, the NPC's neural network outputs a two-part action:
 - **Step Size (Δt):** Determines how far the predictor advances along the homotopy path.
 - **Corrector Termination:** Specifies either a convergence threshold (ϵ) or a maximum number of updates (i_max) for the corrector.
The NPC solver operates in an iterative loop. In each iteration, the neural network determines the predictor's step size and the corrector's termination criteria. The predictor then advances the homotopy level and provides an initial solution estimate, which the corrector refines. Performance statistics from the corrector are collected and integrated into the next state observation.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning for NPC

Reinforcement learning (RL) is employed to train the NPC, chosen due to the non-differentiable nature of the predictor-corrector procedure and the long-term dependencies of sequential decisions (early choices impact the entire trajectory).
- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), an on-policy algorithm, is utilized for its suitability in continuous state and action spaces. Policy and value functions are parameterized by multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Reward Function:** Designed to balance accuracy and efficiency:
 - **Step-wise Accuracy (r_acc_t):** Rewards accurate trajectory tracking, based on convergence velocity or relative error change.
 - **Terminal Efficiency Bonus (r_eff):** Rewards completing the task with fewer corrector iterations, calculated as T_max - T, where T is the total corrector iterations and T_max is an upper bound.
 The cumulative reward is defined as R = (Σ λ1 * r_acc_t) + λ2 * r_eff, with scaling coefficients λ1, λ2 to adjust the balance.
- **Amortized Training:** A key aspect of the approach is amortized training. The NPC agent is trained offline over a diverse distribution of problem instances within a given class. This allows the learned policy to generalize efficiently to new, unseen instances from the same problem class without requiring per-instance fine-tuning or additional training during deployment.

#### 3.5. Representative Homotopy Problems and PC Implementations

The paper illustrates the applicability of NPC across four problem types:
1. **Robust Optimization (Graduated Non-Convexity, GNC):** GNC defines a homotopy that smooths a non-convex loss function to a convex one (t=0) and gradually reintroduces non-convexity (t=1). The predictor progresses by increasing non-convexity, and the corrector typically uses a non-linear least squares optimizer (e.g., Levenberg–Marquardt).
2. **Global Optimization (Gaussian Homotopy, GH):** GH smooths the target function by convolution with a Gaussian kernel. The predictor reduces the kernel bandwidth, while the corrector refines the solution at each stage using an optimizer like a momentum method with a zeroth-order gradient approximation.
3. **Polynomial Root-Finding (Homotopy Continuation, HC):** HC constructs a linear homotopy between a simple polynomial system with known roots and the complex target system. The predictor extrapolates the next root along the path (e.g., using Padé approximation), and the corrector refines it using Gauss-Newton or Newton iteration.
4. **Sampling (Annealed Langevin Dynamics, ALD):** ALD defines a homotopy between a simple source distribution (e.g., Gaussian) and a complex target distribution. The predictor schedules intermediate distributions, and Langevin dynamics serves as the corrector to refine samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC) framework on four representative homotopy problems, demonstrating its performance against classical and specialized baselines.

#### 4.1. Problem 1: Robust Optimization via Graduated Non-Convexity (GNC)

NPC was applied to point cloud registration (95% outliers) and multi-view triangulation (50% outliers). The NPC model was trained on a single dataset (Aquarius) to test generalization.
- **Accuracy:** NPC achieved comparable accuracy to Classic GNC in both tasks. For instance, on point cloud registration, NPC maintained similar rotation and translation errors (log(E_R), log(E_t)) to Classic GNC. In multi-view triangulation, it also maintained comparable 3D point reconstruction errors (log(E_p)).
- **Efficiency:** NPC significantly improved GNC's efficiency. In point cloud registration, it reduced corrector iterations by approximately 70-80% and runtime by 80-90%. In multi-view triangulation, it showed substantial reductions in iterations and runtime (e.g., for 'reichstag', from 142 iterations in Classic GNC to 21 in NPC+GNC, and runtime from 81.98ms to 14.18ms).
- **Stability/Generalization:** The IRLS baseline, tailored for specific tasks, performed poorly on triangulation, indicating a lack of generalization. NPC, however, maintained robustness and efficiency across tasks, demonstrating its generalizable nature.

#### 4.2. Problem 2: Global Optimization via Gaussian Homotopy (GH)

NPC was evaluated on 2D non-convex functions (Ackley, Himmelblau, Rastrigin) and a 10D Ackley function. The NPC agent was trained on randomized Ackley functions.
- **Accuracy:** NPC+GH achieved comparable or superior solution quality (f(x*)) to Classic GH and other baselines. For example, on Himmelblau, both Classic GH and NPC+GH reached f(x*) = 0.00, while SLGH_d and PGS occasionally failed (f(x*) of 2.57 and 1.18 respectively).
- **Efficiency:** NPC+GH demonstrated a substantial reduction in iterations and runtime. For the 2D Ackley, iterations dropped from 501 (Classic GH) to 359 (NPC+GH), and runtime from 16.25ms to 12.31ms. The learning-based CPL method had significantly higher training times that overshadowed any inference efficiency.
- **Stability:** SLGH_d and PGS sometimes failed to reach optimal solutions, indicating challenges with fixed-schedule homotopy methods on certain landscapes. NPC, conversely, showed robustness by consistently converging to the optimum.

#### 4.3. Problem 3: Polynomial Root-Finding via Homotopy Continuation (HC)

NPC was tested on polynomial system benchmarks (Katsura10, Cyclic7, Noon5, Chandra9) and a computer vision task (UPnP). The NPC agent was trained on polynomial systems from a 4-view triangulation task.
- **Accuracy:** NPC+HC consistently tracked all target solutions with a 100% success rate, matching Classic HC and Simulator HC.
- **Efficiency:** NPC+HC significantly reduced both iterations and runtime. For Katsura10, iterations decreased from 39 (Classic HC) to 7 (NPC+HC), and runtime from 2.22ms to 0.65ms. For UPnP, iterations reduced from 53 to 29, and runtime from 8.25ms to 3.86ms. Simulator HC’s runtime was not directly comparable due to different implementation languages.
- **Generalization:** NPC provided a general-purpose solver without task-specific pre-training, in contrast to Simulator HC, which relied on pre-trained networks for specific problems.

#### 4.4. Problem 4: Sampling via Annealed Langevin Dynamics (ALD)

NPC was evaluated on sampling from complex distributions: 40-mode Gaussian mixture model (GMM), 10-dimensional funnel distribution, and 4-particle double-well (DW-4) potential. The NPC agent was trained on a 10-mode GMM.
- **Accuracy:** NPC+ALD achieved Wasserstein-2 distance (W2) and Kernelized Stein Discrepancy (KSD) values comparable to Classic ALD, indicating high-quality sample approximations. For the 40-mode GMM, NPC+ALD's W2 was 11.91 (Classic ALD: 11.57), and KSD was 0.0040 (Classic ALD: 0.0037).
- **Efficiency:** NPC+ALD required significantly fewer iterations and reduced runtime. For the 40-mode GMM, iterations dropped from 410 to 110, and runtime from 1353.16ms to 772.34ms. The iDEM baseline, while sometimes achieving lower W2, involved extensive per-task computation, making direct runtime comparisons difficult.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study of RL State Components

An ablation study on GNC point cloud registration showed that removing any single component from the RL state (homotopy level, corrector tolerance, corrector iteration count, convergence velocity) led to an increase in corrector iterations. This indicates that all state components contribute to efficiently guiding the solver, with corrector statistics being particularly informative.

#### 4.6. Analysis of Efficiency-Precision Trade-Off

The study showed that NPC-accelerated methods achieved higher precision for a given number of iterations compared to classical GNC and ALD, which require manual tuning. The learned NPC policy was able to identify an optimal operating point that balanced predictor step size and corrector tolerance, leading to a substantial reduction in iterations at comparable precision.

In summary, the findings indicate that NPC is a generalizable, efficient, and stable framework for solving diverse problems under the homotopy paradigm. It consistently reduces computational cost without compromising accuracy and outperforms existing approaches in various contexts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of numerical solvers by addressing fundamental limitations in the application of the homotopy paradigm. The significance and potential impact of the Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC) framework can be summarized as follows:

- **Unification of Diverse Problem Domains:** The work is presented as the first to systematically unify a range of problems—including robust optimization, global optimization, polynomial system root-finding, and sampling—under the overarching homotopy paradigm. This unification identifies a common predictor-corrector (PC) structure across these seemingly disparate fields, facilitating a generalized approach to solver design rather than requiring bespoke solutions for each problem type. This integrated perspective could foster cross-disciplinary research and development.

- **Automation of Heuristic-Driven Processes:** By introducing NPC, the research offers a method to replace manually engineered heuristics for step sizes and termination rules with adaptive, learned policies. Traditional PC methods often rely on parameters that are difficult to tune, suboptimal, and task-specific. NPC's ability to automatically discover efficient strategies through reinforcement learning mitigates these issues, potentially reducing the development effort and expertise required for new applications.

- **Enhanced Efficiency and Stability:** Empirical results across diverse benchmarks demonstrate that NPC consistently outperforms classical and specialized baselines in computational efficiency (e.g., significantly fewer corrector iterations and reduced runtime) while maintaining comparable or superior solution accuracy. Furthermore, NPC exhibits improved numerical stability, which can be critical for robust problem-solving in sensitive applications. This improvement could translate into faster computation times for scientific simulations, industrial optimization, and data analysis.

- **Robust Generalization through Amortized Training:** The implementation of an amortized training mechanism is a significant contribution. It enables the NPC agent to undergo one-time offline training on a distribution of problem instances and subsequently deploy the learned policy efficiently on new, unseen instances without further training. This capacity for out-of-the-box generalization makes NPC a practical and scalable solution for real-world scenarios where continuous fine-tuning is impractical.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This paradigm establishes a precedent for integrating reinforcement learning with classical numerical methods, opening new avenues for research. It suggests that learning-based policy search can serve as a practical, generalizable, and efficient alternative to traditional heuristic strategies. Future work could extend homotopy methods to an even broader array of optimization and sampling problems, investigate adaptive reward scaling mechanisms, or explore more complex neural architectures for policy learning.

In conclusion, NPC provides a generalized, efficient, and stable framework for a class of challenging problems, potentially accelerating scientific discovery and engineering solutions that rely on robust and efficient numerical computations.
