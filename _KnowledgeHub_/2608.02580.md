# Ego2Robot: Scalable Robot Data Synthesis from Egocentric Human Data

> [!summary] Summary
> The Ego2Robot pipeline generates a large-scale synthetic robot dataset by converting egocentric human manipulation videos for pretraining vision-language-action models. Joint pretraining on this synthesized data combined with real robot demonstrations consistently improves out-of-distribution generalization across visual, scene, embodiment, and semantic shifts, and enhances real-robot performance on long-horizon manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Large-scale robot manipulation datasets are difficult and expensive to collect, limiting the diversity and generalization of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Existing methods leveraging human egocentric data often require additional robot data or are not scalable for comprehensive robot policy pretraining.
- Current evaluation benchmarks often aggregate various perturbation factors, making it challenging to isolate specific causes of generalization failures in robot policies.

## Method

- Developed **Ego2Robot**, a scalable pipeline that converts egocentric human manipulation videos into robot-specific training data, supporting 15 diverse robot morphologies.
- The pipeline includes stages for ==action alignment== (human hand poses to robot end-effector trajectories), ==visual alignment== (rendering robots into inpainted scenes after human arm removal), and ==multi-level quality curation==.
- Implemented a ==disentangled evaluation protocol== to assess generalization across specific dimensions: visual appearance, scene layout, embodiment morphology, and task semantics.

## Results

- The Ego2Robot pipeline generated **18,561 hours** of synthetic robot training data from approximately **1,940 hours** of human egocentric video, spanning 15 robot morphologies.
- Pretraining with a 1:1 mix of Ego2Robot and real robot data improved the success rate on the RoboTwin Randomized benchmark by **2.6 percentage points** (to **53.5%**) and achieved up to a **12.1 percentage point** gain on the EBench benchmark.
- Generalization improved across all disentangled axes, with notable gains in visual appearance (**+7.6%**), cross-embodiment transfer (**+11.2%** for UR5-WSG), and unseen object instances (**+10.7%**), and demonstrated improved real-robot performance with **13-14 percentage point** gains on complex tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric human manipulation videos, when processed through an embodiment-aware synthesis pipeline, contain valuable and transferable manipulation priors for robot policies.
> - Scaling data synthesis to include multiple robot morphologies during pretraining effectively enhances cross-embodiment generalization and visual robustness for target robot policies.
> - Jointly pretraining VLA models on Ego2Robot synthesized data and real robot data consistently improves out-of-distribution generalization, surpassing performance achieved with robot-only pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robot manipulation, particularly with vision-language-action (VLA) models, have demonstrated capabilities through large-scale robot demonstration pretraining. However, a significant constraint on the generalization ability of these systems is the limited scale and diversity of available robot training data. Collecting such data is costly, labor-intensive, restricted by hardware availability, and inherently limited in interaction diversity.

Egocentric human manipulation videos present a potential solution to this data scarcity. They offer the possibility of massive-scale collection, encompassing diverse objects, environments, and task variations that capture manipulation priors. While prior research has explored leveraging human data for robot learning through methods such as visual pretraining, reward learning, or motion priors, these approaches often still necessitate robot data to bridge the embodiment gap between humans and robots. More direct methods involve co-training robot policies on both egocentric human and robot data or converting human demonstrations into robot-format data via retargeting and rendering. The latter, specifically the retarget-and-render approach, has demonstrated effectiveness for individual tasks at smaller scales.

This work addresses a research gap by investigating whether this retarget-and-render methodology can be scaled to provide substantial pretraining benefits for VLA models, specifically in improving their out-of-distribution generalization capabilities. It extends prior work by scaling the synthesis process to generate a significantly larger dataset across multiple robot morphologies.

Furthermore, the paper relates to data augmentation in robot learning. While existing data augmentation techniques, such as simulation-based generation or cross-embodiment visual transfer (e.g., RoviAug, Mirage), primarily focus on robot-to-robot domain gaps, this research extends the scope to the more substantial ego-to-robot domain gap.

The evaluation framework also contributes to the broader landscape of robot generalization benchmarks. Current benchmarks often bundle various perturbation factors, making it challenging to isolate the causes of performance degradation. This work introduces a disentangled evaluation protocol, building on existing benchmarks like RoboTwin 2.0, to provide a more granular analysis of generalization across distinct perturbation axes, including visual, scene, embodiment, and semantic shifts, while supporting dual-arm and cross-embodiment evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate ego-to-robot synthesis as a scalable paradigm for robot policy pretraining. The core hypothesis is that egocentric human manipulation videos, despite differences in embodiment, contain transferable interaction regularities that can complement robot data, provided they are appropriately aligned.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are:

1. **Addressing Robot Data Limitations:** Current large-scale robot datasets, despite significant efforts, are expensive to collect and inherently limited in their scale and diversity due to hardware availability, teleoperation costs, and restricted interaction scenarios. This limits the generalization ability of pre-trained VLA models.
2. **Leveraging Egocentric Human Data:** Egocentric human videos offer a massive, diverse, and readily available source of manipulation demonstrations. These videos capture rich manipulation priors that are difficult or costly to obtain solely with robot platforms. The motivation is to convert this abundant human data into an effective format for robot training.
3. **Developing a Scalable Data Synthesis Pipeline:** A key objective is to design and implement an end-to-end pipeline, named Ego2Robot, capable of systematically converting egocentric human manipulation videos into robot-specific training data. This pipeline needs to address the technical challenges of:
 - **Action Alignment:** Retargeting human hand motions to robot end-effector kinematics.
 - **Visual Alignment:** Visually replacing human arms with rendered robot embodiments within the original scene context.
 - **Quality Curation:** Filtering and refining the synthesized data to ensure high quality and relevance for robot learning.
 - The pipeline is motivated to support diverse input formats, including both pre-annotated and raw egocentric videos, and to be adaptable to multiple robot morphologies.
4. **Constructing a Large-Scale Ego-to-Robot Dataset:** Based on the scalable pipeline, a major objective is to produce the largest ego-to-robot dataset to date. This dataset is intended to be a resource for large-scale pretraining of robot policies.
5. **Rigorous Generalization Evaluation:** Motivated by the limitations of existing benchmarks that provide aggregate generalization metrics, a crucial objective is to develop a disentangled evaluation protocol. This protocol aims to isolate and independently test generalization across specific dimensions: visual appearance, scene layout, embodiment morphology, and task semantics. This allows for a granular analysis to determine precisely where the benefits of ego-to-robot synthesized data manifest.
6. **Demonstrating Pretraining Benefits:** The ultimate motivation is to empirically demonstrate that joint pretraining on a combination of Ego2Robot-synthesized data and real robot data leads to consistent improvements in out-of-distribution generalization performance for robot policies, rather than merely increasing the volume of training data.
7. **Validating Real-World Utility:** To confirm the practical value of the approach, an objective is to validate the benefits of using Ego2Robot-synthesized data for real-robot deployment on complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Ego2Robot pipeline converts egocentric human manipulation videos into embodiment-specific robot training data through three main stages: action alignment, visual alignment, and quality curation. The pipeline supports two input paths for egocentric videos.

**Input Paths:**
- **Path A (Annotated Ego Datasets):** Utilizes egocentric datasets that already include hand pose annotations, such as ANT, EgoDex, ViTRA, and EgoVerse.
- **Path B (Pure Video no hand pose):** Designed for unannotated egocentric videos. This path first estimates hand poses using WiLoR for per-frame 3D hand reconstruction, followed by DynHaMR for temporal optimization of MANO parameters. Hand detections are filtered by SAM 3-generated hand masks, and cross-frame association is performed. Long videos are further segmented into discrete subtasks with natural language descriptions using a VLM (Qwen3.5).

**1. Action Alignment:** This stage transforms human hand poses into robot end-effector trajectories.
- **Hand-to-Gripper Retargeting:** A compact parallel-gripper representation is derived from 21 hand keypoints. A virtual fingertip is defined, and the tool center point (TCP) is calculated as the midpoint between the thumb tip and the virtual fingertip. The gripper opening width is the distance between these points. The grasp orientation (a right-handed orthonormal frame) is established using the jaw line (thumb tip to virtual fingertip) as the z-axis, the wrist-to-fingertip direction to define the jaw plane, and the gripper-normal axis (y-axis) perpendicular to it.
- **Temporal Smoothing:** High-frequency noise from per-frame hand detection is mitigated using Savitzky-Golay filtering for positions and widths, and Gaussian-weighted SLERP for orientations. Velocity filters are applied to remove sudden detection jumps.
- **Action Speed Alignment:** Human manipulation speeds are typically faster than robot teleoperation. To align speed distributions, per-source frame subsampling is applied (e.g., ANT and EgoDex to 60% of original frame rate, EgoVerse to 45%, ViTRA to 25%).

**2. Visual Alignment:** This stage replaces human arms with rendered robot arms within the original scene.
- **Arm Segmentation:** SAM 3 is employed to segment human arm regions in each frame, ensuring temporal consistency through propagation and post-processing.
- **Hand Removal:** ProPainter performs temporally consistent video inpainting to remove the segmented human arms, reconstructing the background.
- **Robot Base Pose Search:** A critical challenge is determining the robot base placement. This is formulated as an optimization problem to find a base pose T_base that maximizes kinematic feasibility for the target robot morphology across a trajectory of retargeted end-effector poses. Candidate base placements are generated via grid search around the trajectory centroid, constrained by the robot's maximum reach, and evaluated by an inverse kinematics (IK) solver. The placement with the highest IK feasibility rate, while also considering a penalty for operating too close or too far from the kinematic reach limit, is selected. This search is conducted independently for each of the 15 supported robot morphologies (including Panda, UR5e, ARX-L5, xArm7, etc.).
- **Inverse Kinematics and Rendering:** Once the optimal base placement is determined, IK is solved frame-by-frame using MuJoCo, and the robot is rendered from the original camera viewpoint.
- **Depth-Aware Compositing:** The rendered robot is composited into the inpainted scene using depth information. The robot's arm body is always composited, while gripper pixels undergo per-pixel depth comparison with the scene depth (estimated via Depth Anything V3) to handle occlusions, with special consideration for dilated hand mask regions to prevent visual artifacts. Deterministic color randomization is applied to the rendered robots.

**3. Quality Curation:** A multi-level filtering process ensures data quality.
- **L1 (Pipeline-internal):** Filters frames based on IK failures, self-collisions, action outliers, or insufficient robot visibility/workspace coverage.
- **L2 (Statistical):** Applies post-hoc statistical filters to remove trajectories with extreme action values, sudden discontinuities, or an excessive ratio of invalid frames.
- **L3 (VLM Consistency):** A vision-language model (Qwen3.5) audits the synthesized videos to verify semantic consistency between the rendered robot actions and the original human manipulation intent, discarding inconsistent episodes.

**Data Generation:** The pipeline processes approximately 1,940 hours of egocentric video data from four sources (ANT, EgoDex, ViTRA, EgoVerse). After conversion across 15 robot morphologies and quality curation, this yields 18,561 hours of synthetic robot training data. Each data sample includes a robot-composited video frame, corresponding camera-frame EEF action (3D position delta, 3D rotation delta, 1D gripper), camera parameters, and a text instruction. Camera-frame relative EEF actions are used to unify data from various camera setups and robot morphologies.

**Evaluation Framework:**
To assess generalization, the authors extend RoboTwin 2.0 with 11 independent perturbation settings and camera-frame relative EEF support, and complement it with EBench, which features 7 precision tabletop tasks from a higher viewpoint. The evaluation is structured along four disentangled generalization dimensions:
1. **Visual Appearance:** Background texture, lighting variation, and robot color (random hue shifts).
2. **Scene Layout:** Table height, distractor objects, and camera viewpoint offset.
3. **Embodiment:** Zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer to distinct robot morphologies (ARX-X5, UR5-WSG, Franka Panda) from the default Aloha-Agilex, with aligned initial EEF poses.
4. **Task Semantics:** Generalization to unseen object instances (same category, different appearance) and paraphrased natural language instructions.

**Model and Training:** A VLA architecture with Qwen3.5-4B as the vision-language backbone and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action head is employed. The model predicts 32-step action chunks using flow matching. Pretraining configurations include robot-only data (~6,565 hours) and Ego2Robot-synthesized data mixed with robot data at 1:3, 3:1, and 1:1 ratios. All models undergo 200K pretraining steps. Finetuning is performed on RoboTwin's 50 clean-setting tasks or EBench's training set for 50K steps. Real-robot experiments are conducted on an ARX ACone platform for 5 long-horizon tasks, using a combination of teleoperated and Ego2Robot-synthesized data for finetuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the efficacy of Ego2Robot for robot policy pretraining and generalization.

1. **Large-Scale Data Generation:** The Ego2Robot pipeline successfully converted approximately 1,940 hours of egocentric human manipulation videos into 18,561 hours of synthetic robot training data, spanning 15 robot morphologies. This represents a significant expansion of available training data, making it the largest ego-to-robot dataset reported to date.

2. **Improved Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:**
 - Pretraining with a mix of Ego2Robot-synthesized and real robot data consistently improved OOD generalization. The Ego2R+Robot (1:1) configuration achieved a 53.5% success rate on the RoboTwin Randomized benchmark, an increase of 2.6 percentage points over the robot-only baseline (50.9%). This configuration also showed a 5.9 percentage point improvement on clean tasks, reaching 68.1%.
 - The benefits were observed to be substantial with mixing ratios of 3:1 and 1:1 (Ego2R:Robot), while a 1:3 ratio yielded more marginal gains.

3. **Specific Generalization Gains Across Perturbation Axes:**
 - **Visual Appearance:** The 1:1 mix led to improvements across all visual factors, including background (+3.7%), lighting (+7.6%), and robot color (+6.4%). This suggests that the diversity in scenes within egocentric videos and the multi-morphology rendering contributed to visual robustness.
 - **Scene Layout:** Moderate gains were observed, with camera offset robustness improving by 5.9 percentage points (1:1 mix). This is attributed to the varied viewpoints present in egocentric video data.
 - **Embodiment Transfer:** The approach demonstrated benefits for cross-embodiment generalization. The ARX-X5 performance improved by 7.1 percentage points (1:1 mix), and UR5-WSG peaked with an 11.2 percentage point gain (3:1 mix). These improvements are directly linked to exposure to 15 different robot morphologies during pretraining. However, Franka Panda showed limited improvement (remaining below 7%), indicating a larger kinematic or visual domain gap for this particular embodiment.
 - **Task Semantics:** Generalization to unseen object instances improved from 29.3% to 40.0% (a 10.7 percentage point increase at 3:1 mix), stemming from the diverse object interactions captured in egocentric videos. Robustness to paraphrased natural language instructions also increased to 68.5% (a 5.4 percentage point gain at 1:1 mix), benefiting from broader linguistic diversity.

4. **Confirmation on EBench:** The EBench benchmark, which features a camera viewpoint more similar to egocentric perspectives, showed the most substantial gains. The 3:1 Ego2R+Robot mix achieved the best EBench score of 51.7%, representing a 12.1 percentage point improvement over the robot-only baseline. This indicates that benefits from co-training can be amplified when there is a partial match in viewpoint bias.

5. **Ablation Studies Validating Pipeline Components and Scaling:**
 - **Pipeline Value:** Raw egocentric data alone (without robot rendering) for pretraining achieved 28.1% on RoboTwin Randomized. Processing this data through the Ego2Robot pipeline (even with a single morphology) improved performance to 31.7% (+3.6%), validating the effectiveness of the action and visual alignment stages.
 - **Morphology Scaling:** Increasing the number of synthesized robot morphologies from 1 to 15 progressively improved performance (from 31.7% to 33.5%), indicating that morphological diversity in pretraining data enhances generalization.
 - **Raw Ego Data Contribution:** Combining 15-morphology Ego2R data with raw egocentric data further increased performance to 37.3%, suggesting that raw ego data serves as an additional "morphology" that enriches pretraining diversity.

6. **Real Robot Deployment Success:**
 - On an ARX ACone platform, the "Mix + Ego2R Play" configuration demonstrated superior performance across all five long-horizon manipulation tasks.
 - Notable gains included 14 percentage points on "Put Blocks" and 13 percentage points on "Insert Screw" over the robot-only baseline.
 - The results indicate that even casually recorded egocentric videos, when processed through the Ego2Robot pipeline, can be converted into effective training signals that consistently improve real-robot policy performance, complementing pretraining on general Ego2R data and a small number of teleoperated demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in addressing the foundational challenge of data scarcity and lack of diversity in robot learning, particularly for training generalizable robot manipulation policies.

1. **Scalable Data Generation Paradigm:** Ego2Robot offers a scalable and systematic paradigm for generating robot training data from readily available egocentric human manipulation videos. This pipeline's capability to process large volumes of human video and render it across 15 robot morphologies, resulting in 18,561 hours of synthetic data, demonstrates a pathway to overcome the limitations of expensive and resource-intensive real robot data collection.

2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The work empirically demonstrates that incorporating Ego2Robot-synthesized data into pretraining significantly improves the out-of-distribution generalization of robot policies. The disentangled evaluation protocol provides a nuanced understanding, showing specific gains in robustness to visual appearance changes, varied scene layouts, different robot embodiments, and semantic variations in tasks. This suggests that the synthesized data fosters invariance and cross-distribution robustness, which are critical for deploying robots in varied real-world environments.

3. **Bridging the Human-Robot Embodiment Gap:** The methodology effectively bridges the substantial embodiment gap between human hands and robot grippers through sophisticated action and visual alignment techniques. This enables the transfer of manipulation priors learned from human demonstrations to robot control, expanding the scope of transferable skills.

4. **Rigorous Evaluation Framework:** The introduction of a disentangled generalization benchmark is an important contribution to the field. By decoupling perturbation axes, researchers can gain finer-grained insights into the strengths and weaknesses of robot policies and identify which specific types of data or pretraining strategies are most effective for different generalization challenges. This framework can guide future development in robot learning benchmarks.

5. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The validation on real-robot platforms for long-horizon tasks underscores the practical utility of Ego2Robot. The finding that even casually recorded "ego play" videos can be effectively converted into training data to boost real-robot performance indicates a low-cost, accessible method for enhancing robot capabilities in deployment scenarios. This could accelerate the development and deployment of robust robotic systems for various applications.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The Ego2Robot pipeline and the resulting large-scale dataset provide a valuable resource for the research community. They lay a foundation for exploring further improvements in robot learning from human data, such as incorporating dexterous multi-finger hands, developing more advanced generative models for visual fidelity, and extending the framework to an even broader range of tasks and embodiment configurations.

In conclusion, this research presents a method for efficiently scaling robot training data through human-centric video synthesis, leading to improved generalization and practical performance in robotic manipulation. It has the potential to significantly impact the trajectory of robot learning by leveraging the abundant and diverse human manipulation experience.
