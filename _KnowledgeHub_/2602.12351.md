# LongNav-R1: Horizon-Adaptive Multi-Turn RL for Long-Horizon VLA Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> LongNav-R1, developed by the University of Michigan, introduces a multi-turn reinforcement learning framework that optimizes Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models for long-horizon navigation. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on object-goal navigation benchmarks, showing superior success rates and zero-shot generalization to unseen categories and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end VLA navigation models predominantly rely on single-turn imitation learning, which leads to myopic decision-making, behavioral rigidity, and lack of causal reasoning for long-horizon tasks.
- Current VLA systems struggle to recover from errors and explore diverse trajectories, as they strictly imitate expert demonstrations rather than optimizing for long-term goal success.
- Applying multi-step reinforcement learning to large VLA models for long-horizon tasks faces significant challenges in temporal credit assignment and computational overhead due to the need for a separate value function in traditional actor-critic methods.

## Method

- **LongNav-R1** reformulates VLA navigation as a continuous ==multi-turn conversation== between the policy and environment, enabling autoregressive action generation conditioned on full historical context and leveraging ==KV cache reuse== for efficiency.
- Introduces ==Horizon-Adaptive Policy Optimization (HAPO)==, a novel critic-free advantage estimation framework that uses a temporal-aware Gaussian kernel to provide precise temporal credit assignment for dense, step-level rewards.
- Implements ==online token pruning== to manage the high volume of visual tokens in long-horizon trajectories, retaining only informative tokens to maintain computational efficiency during training and inference.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art success rates on object-goal navigation benchmarks (e.g., **76.0%** SR on HM3D V1, **83.7%** SR on HM3D V2), outperforming existing methods, including those using additional sensor modalities.
- Demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization on open-vocabulary object-goal navigation, outperforming traditional transformer-based RL methods by approximately **30%** and improving an SFT baseline's SR by **8.7%** with only **4,000** RL iterations.
- HAPO with a temporal kernel significantly improves performance on long-horizon tasks (>200 steps), achieving **15.4%** SR where SFT and sparse-reward REINFORCE++ fail (**0.0%** SR), while maintaining efficient inference at approximately **5 frames per second**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-turn reinforcement learning, coupled with dense, step-wise rewards, is crucial for VLA policies to develop causal reasoning, understand sequential dependencies, and effectively learn error recovery strategies in long-horizon navigation.
> - Accurate temporal credit assignment for long-horizon tasks can be efficiently achieved without a separate critic network by regressing a non-parametric baseline from the local rollout buffer using a time-aware kernel.
> - Optimizing VLA models through direct online interaction with the environment and dense rewards promotes the generation of diverse trajectories and adaptability, overcoming the limitations of pre-recorded imitation learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence is advancing towards enabling robots to navigate and interact with physical environments. Historically, robotic navigation systems have relied on modular pipelines, segmenting the task into separate components such as perception, mapping, and planning. While these modular approaches provided structured solutions, recent developments have shifted towards end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These VLA models leverage large-scale pre-training from vision-language models (VLMs) and large language models (LLMs) to directly map complex visual observations and linguistic instructions to actions, aiming for more general and adaptable semantic navigation.

Despite these advancements, current end-to-end VLA navigation approaches, particularly those based on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), exhibit limitations in long-horizon tasks. Existing state-of-the-art methods often adopt a single-turn imitation learning paradigm. This paradigm treats navigation as a series of isolated action predictions based on immediate local context, neglecting sequential dependencies and the causal effects of historical interactions. This can lead to agents that lack causal reasoning, struggle to recover from errors, and develop behavioral rigidity due to strict imitation of expert demonstrations rather than optimizing for long-term goal success. Such systems tend to be myopic and brittle when faced with novel or complex long-horizon scenarios.

This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) framework. While RL has been applied to LLMs for alignment and reasoning, adapting it effectively for embodied, multi-step robotic decision-making, especially for long-horizon tasks, remains a challenge. Specifically, the problem of temporal credit assignment – accurately attributing a long-term outcome to individual actions across many steps – is a significant bottleneck. Standard actor-critic RL methods can be computationally intensive for large VLA models, while existing critic-free methods developed for LLMs are often designed for single-turn tasks with sparse, outcome-level rewards, failing to capture the evolving temporal dynamics of multi-step robotic tasks.

LongNav-R1 contributes to this landscape by proposing an end-to-end multi-turn RL framework that explicitly models the sequential nature of navigation. It introduces a novel critic-free advantage estimation method, Horizon-Adaptive Policy Optimization (HAPO), which is designed to handle temporal credit assignment in long-horizon VLA tasks efficiently. By reformulating navigation as a continuous multi-turn conversation and optimizing VLA models directly through online interaction with dense, step-level rewards, this work aims to enhance causal reasoning, promote diverse trajectory generation, and improve robustness, thereby bridging a gap towards more capable embodied agents for complex, long-horizon navigation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end VLA model capable of performing robust and efficient long-horizon navigation in complex and unknown environments. The motivation stems from several critical limitations observed in existing VLA navigation systems:

1. **Addressing Deficiencies of Single-Turn Imitation Learning:** Current state-of-the-art VLA models largely rely on a single-turn imitation learning paradigm, where actions are predicted based on immediate observations and a global instruction. This approach views navigation as a sequence of independent steps, neglecting the causal relationships between actions over an extended trajectory. As a result, agents often struggle with:
 - **Lack of Causal Reasoning:** They fail to understand how early-stage exploration influences later-stage goal attainment, treating each step in isolation.
 - **Behavioral Rigidity:** By strictly imitating expert demonstrations, these models are prone to distribution shifts and exhibit limited adaptability. They lack the capacity to recover from errors or explore alternative, potentially more efficient, paths.
 - **Myopic Decision-Making:** Without optimizing for the cumulative long-term outcome, agents tend to make locally optimal decisions that may not contribute to the overall success of a long-horizon task.

2. **Enabling Multi-Step Decision-Making and Online Interaction:** To overcome the rigidity and myopia of imitation learning, the authors propose reformulating navigation as a continuous multi-turn conversation between the VLA policy and the environment. This multi-turn framework is motivated by the need for:
 - **Comprehensive State and Objective Awareness:** Allowing the VLA policy to explicitly account for the causal effects of historical interactions and sequential future outcomes.
 - **Learning from Online Interactions:** Direct learning through reinforcement from online interactions encourages agents to explore diverse trajectories and behaviors, moving beyond the limitations of pre-recorded human demonstrations.

3. **Solving Temporal Credit Assignment in Long-Horizon RL for VLAs:** While multi-turn RL offers advantages, applying it to large VLA models for long-horizon tasks presents a significant challenge in temporal credit assignment. The relative contribution of an action to the final objective varies greatly over time, making it difficult to accurately attribute rewards.
 - **Computational Overhead of Actor-Critic Methods:** Standard actor-critic RL methods, such as PPO, require learning a separate value function, which can incur prohibitive computational overhead when integrated with large VLA models, especially for long sequences.
 - **Limitations of Critic-Free Methods:** Existing critic-free methods, often used in LLM training (e.g., GRPO, REINFORCE++), are typically optimized for single-turn tasks with sparse, outcome-level rewards. They lack the mechanism to capture the fine-grained temporal dynamics and varying horizon lengths inherent in multi-step robotic decision-making.

In summary, the key motivations are to enable VLA models to perform effective long-horizon navigation by addressing the causal reasoning, behavioral rigidity, and temporal credit assignment problems prevalent in current approaches, ultimately aiming for more robust, generalizable, and sample-efficient embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LongNav-R1 is an end-to-end multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to optimize Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models for long-horizon navigation tasks. The approach leverages a novel Horizon-Adaptive Policy Optimization (HAPO) for efficient temporal credit assignment.

#### 3.1. Multi-Turn RL Formulation of VLA Policy

The core of LongNav-R1 reformulates the navigation process as a continuous multi-turn conversation between the VLA policy and the embodied environment. Instead of single-turn predictions, actions are generated autoregressively, conditioned on the global instruction `I`, the full history of observations and actions ` {v_1:t-1, a_1:t-1} `, and the current observation `v_t`. This multi-turn design offers two primary advantages:
1. **KV Cache Reuse:** It allows for efficient reuse of the Key-Value (KV) cache, reducing computational overhead during long training and inference sequences.
2. **Causal Reasoning:** Explicitly accounts for causal dependencies of prior interactions, enabling the model to attribute the final trajectory outcome to individual actions.

#### 3.2. Horizon-Adaptive Policy Optimization (HAPO)

HAPO is a critic-free advantage estimation framework developed to address the temporal credit assignment challenge in long-horizon VLA navigation. It eliminates the need for a separate value network by regressing a baseline directly from the local rollout buffer using kernel regression.

- **General Kernel-Based Advantage Estimation:** HAPO models critic-free baselines as non-parametric value function approximations. For each action `a_t^i` at timestep `t` in trajectory `τ^i`, the advantage `A_t^i` is defined as `A_t^i = G_t^i - V_K_B(s_t^i)`. `G_t^i` is the discounted return from timestep `t`. The baseline `V_K_B(s_t^i)` is computed via leave-one-out kernel regression over the rollout buffer `B`. This formulation uses a similarity kernel `K(·, ·)` and a feature mapping `f(·)` to estimate values, adapting to task objectives through specialized kernel designs. The leave-one-out construction avoids self-bias in estimation.
- **Temporal-Aware Kernel Design:** To specifically account for the sequential structure and varying horizon lengths in navigation, HAPO introduces a time-aware kernel. It defines the discrete time step `t` as the feature `f(s_t^i) = t` and employs a Gaussian kernel `K(x, x') = exp(−|x−x'|^2 / (2σ^2))` with bandwidth `σ`. This enables the baseline to capture the reward's temporal dynamics, providing temporal precision comparable to a critic without the computational cost.
- **Comparison with REINFORCE++:** HAPO generalizes existing methods. REINFORCE++, for example, corresponds to a constant kernel and features defined only at the final outcome state, leading to a uniform baseline across all actions and timesteps. HAPO's time-aware kernel provides a more precise, horizon-adaptive baseline.

#### 3.3. LongNav-R1: Multi-Turn Navigation Pipeline

The overall pipeline comprises three iterative stages:

1. **Interactive VLA Rollout:**
 - **State Encoding:** At each timestep `t`, the current visual observation `v_t` is processed by a vision encoder to extract visual tokens `x_t`. The previous action `a_t-1` is embedded into a textual token `e_t-1`.
 - **Online Token Pruning:** To manage the high volume of visual tokens in long-horizon trajectories, LongNav-R1 implements online token pruning. It retains only informative visual tokens by applying a binary selection mask `m_t`. A token is retained if its maximum similarity to historical KV cache elements falls below a predefined threshold `δ`, indicating novel information. The sparse visual tokens `f_t` are then used to update the state `s_t` along with the history cache and action token.
 - **Action Prediction:** Given the encoded and pruned state `s_t`, the VLA policy `π_θ` predicts the next action `a_t` as a categorical distribution over a discrete action space via next-token prediction. The agent executes `a_t` in the environment, leading to a state transition, a reward `r_t`, and a new observation `v_t+1`. This loop generates a complete trajectory `τ`.

2. **Horizon-Adaptive Advantage Estimation:**
 - **Return Estimation:** For each trajectory `τ^i` in the rollout buffer, empirical returns `G_t^i` are computed at each step `t` using a discount factor `γ`. The step-level reward `r_t` is defined based on the geodesic distance to the goal, providing dense feedback. `γ` is calibrated based on environmental semantic density.
 - **Kernel-Based Baseline:** A non-parametric baseline `V_t^i` is regressed using the temporal Gaussian kernel (Equation 9) over returns in the rollout buffer. This provides temporal smoothing and helps mitigate variance in long-horizon tasks.
 - **Dense Advantage Estimation:** The advantage `A_t^i` at each timestep is calculated as `A_t^i = G_t^i - V_t^i`, providing dense, informative process signals for policy learning.

3. **Training Strategy:**
 - **Balanced IL Warm-up:** An off-the-shelf VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B) is first fine-tuned using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on human demonstrations (Habitat-Web dataset). The objective is to minimize negative log-likelihood (Equation 10). Uniform sampling of trajectories is used to mitigate "stop bias" and length bias inherent in naive sampling.
 - **Online Multi-Turn RL Training:** Following SFT, the agent transitions to an online RL phase using a PPO-like objective (Equation 11). This objective maximizes the expected cumulative reward, employing importance sampling, gradient clipping (`ϵ_l`, `ϵ_h`), and KL divergence regularization (`βD_KL`) against a reference model (`π_ref`) to ensure stable optimization. The critical distinction is the use of HAPO-derived advantages `A_t^i` instead of batch averages, enabling precise credit assignment for stochastic dynamics and error correction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding LongNav-R1's performance, efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art (SOTA)

- **Object-Goal Navigation:** LongNav-R1 demonstrates superior performance on the HM3D V1, HM3D V2, and MP3D object-goal navigation benchmarks. It achieves success rates (SR) of 76.0%, 83.7%, and 63.0% respectively, outperforming existing SOTA methods across all datasets and metrics (SR and SPL). Notably, LongNav-R1, using only RGB observations, surpasses methods that utilize additional modalities like odometry and depth sensors, indicating strong generalizability and robustness to real-world conditions where such sensors may be noisy. It also outperforms UniNavid, a prior end-to-end VLA model, in path efficiency (7.2% improvement), which is attributed to its RL training.

- **Open-Vocabulary Object-Goal Navigation (Zero-Shot):** LongNav-R1 exhibits strong generalization capabilities on the HM3D-OVON benchmark. Despite being trained exclusively on six object categories in HM3D, it achieves SOTA zero-shot performance on OVON (which contains 379 object categories) without any fine-tuning. It surpasses traditional transformer-based RL methods by approximately 30% and also outperforms Nav-R1, a single-turn RL baseline, emphasizing the benefit of the multi-turn RL approach in capturing sequential dependencies for long-term objectives.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning:** Ablation studies compare HAPO variants with zero-shot, SFT, and REINFORCE++ training strategies. HAPO variants consistently outperform others. Specifically, RL with dense, step-wise distance-to-goal rewards (used in HAPO) shows significant improvement over SFT, whereas RL with sparse binary outcome rewards (used in REINFORCE++) does not, underscoring the importance of dense reward shaping for multi-horizon robotics tasks.
 - On overall performance, SFT achieves 64.3% SR, REINFORCE++ (sparse rewards) achieves 47.4% SR, while HAPO (σ=30) achieves 73.0% SR.
 - For tasks with >200 steps, SFT and REINFORCE++ fail (0.0% SR), while HAPO (σ=30) achieves 15.4% SR, indicating its critical role in unlocking long-horizon navigation.

- **Effectiveness of Horizon-Adaptive Advantage Estimation:** Comparing HAPO variants with different temporal kernel bandwidths (σ) demonstrates that a restricted temporal window (e.g., σ=30) yields smaller value estimation error and improved performance compared to an infinite window (uniform baseline, σ=∞). HAPO with σ=30 achieves 73.0% SR, while HAPO with σ=∞ achieves 71.6% SR, confirming the necessity of temporal-adaptive advantage estimation for complex navigation dynamics.

- **Training Statistics Analysis:** The training process involves an SFT warm-up phase followed by an RL phase. SFT initially provides rapid learning (0% to 64.3% SR), but performance plateaus. The RL phase successfully breaks this plateau, improving performance by 8.7% with only 4,000 additional iterations (trajectories). This efficiency is attributed to the multi-turn design, which allows learning from numerous decision-making points per sample, and on-policy rollouts for effective error correction.

- **Computation Analysis:** LongNav-R1 demonstrates high computational performance. For a 400-step sequence, inference time is approximately 0.23 seconds, achieving about 5 frames per second (FPS). This efficiency is facilitated by KV cache reuse in the multi-turn setup and online token pruning, which restricts attention to informative tokens, making the model suitable for real-world deployment.

#### 4.3. Case Studies and Qualitative Analysis

- **SFT vs. LongNav-R1:** Visualizations show that LongNav-R1 successfully and efficiently navigates to distant, cross-floor goals (e.g., finding a sofa), a task where SFT often fails. LongNav-R1's native handling of cross-floor transitions highlights its adaptability.
- **Real-World Deployment (Zero-Shot):** The robot equipped with LongNav-R1 successfully locates an "elevator," an unseen object category, in a real-world office environment, demonstrating zero-shot real-world effectiveness.
- **Generalization across Object Categories:** Performance analysis across HM3D, MP3D, and OVON reveals no significant performance gap between trained and unseen object categories, even for semantically distant ones. This suggests that LongNav-R1 learns generalizable navigation capabilities rather than mere memorization.
- **Long-Horizon Exploration:** Visualizations of trajectories ranging from 81 to 354 steps illustrate LongNav-R1's ability to sustain long-horizon exploration, locate distant targets, and avoid repetitive paths. It maintains effective active perception strategies (peeking, panoramic scanning) to capitalize on promising regions and avoid dead ends.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly for robotic navigation, through the development of LongNav-R1. The key contributions are manifold and have substantial implications:

1. **Enabling Long-Horizon Navigation for VLA Models:** LongNav-R1's multi-turn reinforcement learning framework addresses fundamental limitations of prior VLA models, which typically struggled with long-horizon tasks due to their single-turn imitation learning paradigm. By reformulating navigation as a continuous, interactive dialogue, the framework allows VLA policies to develop causal reasoning, understand sequential dependencies, and optimize for cumulative, long-term objectives rather than immediate actions. This capability is crucial for robots operating in complex, real-world environments that demand multi-step planning and recovery from errors.

2. **Efficient Temporal Credit Assignment:** The introduction of Horizon-Adaptive Policy Optimization (HAPO) is a notable methodological contribution. HAPO's critic-free advantage estimation, utilizing a temporal-aware kernel, provides precise temporal credit assignment without the prohibitive computational overhead associated with auxiliary critic networks in large models. This innovation makes it feasible to train large VLA models with dense, step-level rewards, which are essential for stable and efficient learning in complex robotic tasks, a challenge that bottlenecked prior multi-turn RL approaches for VLAs.

3. **Enhanced Generalizability and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate that LongNav-R1 achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse object-goal and open-vocabulary navigation benchmarks. Its ability to outperform methods using additional sensor modalities (depth, odometry) while relying solely on RGB input underscores its robustness and potential for deployment in varying real-world conditions. Furthermore, the strong zero-shot generalization to unseen object categories and environments, including real-world scenarios, indicates that the model learns generalizable navigation skills rather than merely memorizing task-specific behaviors.

4. **Improved Sample Efficiency and Training Stability:** The multi-turn design, combined with HAPO and online token pruning, contributes to superior sample efficiency. The ability to improve performance significantly with a relatively small number of RL iterations (4,000 trajectories for an 8.7% SR boost) compared to SFT baselines trained on millions of data points highlights a more efficient learning paradigm. Computational analysis shows that the architectural optimizations, such as KV cache reuse and online token pruning, maintain efficient inference times, making LongNav-R1 practical for real-world deployment.

5. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI Research:** LongNav-R1 provides a robust "recipe" for training end-to-end VLA policies, offering a pathway for developing more capable embodied agents. The work also opens avenues for future research, such as exploring advanced memory mechanisms (selective KV cache eviction, spatially aware global memory) to handle even more extreme-horizon tasks, and extending the framework to broader applications like mobile manipulation or integrating world models for enhanced real-world learning.

In conclusion, LongNav-R1 advances the state of embodied navigation by effectively integrating multi-turn reinforcement learning with VLA models, demonstrating a viable approach to overcome critical challenges in long-horizon tasks. Its contributions enhance agent intelligence, efficiency, and adaptability, moving closer to human-level performance for autonomous robots.
