# FlowVid: Taming Imperfect Optical Flows for Consistent Video-to-Video Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowVid presents a video-to-video synthesis framework that achieves high temporal consistency and prompt alignment by utilizing imperfect optical flows as soft conditions in conjunction with spatial information. The method demonstrates significant efficiency improvements, generating 4-second videos 3.1x to 10.5x faster than prior state-of-the-art techniques.

## Problem

- Direct application of text-to-image diffusion models to individual video frames results in temporal flickering and inconsistency.
- Existing video-to-video synthesis methods using explicit optical flow are limited by the inherent inaccuracies of pre-trained flow estimation models, leading to error propagation and undesirable artifacts.
- Implicit motion handling via spatial-temporal attention in video diffusion models often fails to achieve consistent temporal coherence across frames.

## Method

- Adapted a spatial-controlled image-to-image diffusion model (similar to ==ControlNet==) for video by inflating 2D layers into pseudo-3D and incorporating ==temporal self-attention==.
- Integrated explicit temporal conditions, including flow-warped video, backward occlusion masks, and a constant first-frame latent, as flexible, soft guidance rather than rigid constraints.
- Implemented an 'edit-propagate' inference strategy, allowing the first frame to be edited by any existing image-to-image model before propagating these changes consistently to subsequent frames.

## Results

- Generated 4-second (120 frames, 512x512) videos in approximately **1.5** minutes on a single A-100 GPU, demonstrating **3.1x** to **10.5x** speed improvements over state-of-the-art methods.
- Achieved the highest user preference at 45.7% in human evaluations for overall quality, robustness, and editing capabilities, significantly outperforming TokenFlow (40.4%), Rerender (10.2%), and CoDeF (3.5%).
- Exhibited strong prompt alignment (highest CLIP Pro-Ali score of **33.20**) and high temporal consistency (CLIP Tem-Con score of **97.08**), supporting diverse applications like stylization, object swaps, and local modifications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Imperfect optical flow can be effectively utilized as a soft condition to guide motion and structure, mitigating the negative impact of its inherent estimation errors.
> - Jointly leveraging explicit temporal information (flow-warped video, occlusion masks) with additional spatial conditions (e.g., depth maps) improves structural coherence and rectifies inaccuracies from optical flow.
> - V-parameterization is crucial for preventing unnatural global color shifts across frames and maintaining consistent appearance over time in video diffusion models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-guided video-to-video (V2V) synthesis aims to modify input videos based on text prompts, holding wide applications in areas such as short-video creation and the film industry. Significant progress has been observed in text-guided image-to-image (I2I) synthesis, largely driven by large pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models. However, V2V synthesis presents an additional challenge due to the temporal dimension, which often leads to inconsistent flickering between frames when I2I models are applied independently.

Prior efforts to improve temporal consistency in V2V synthesis can be broadly categorized into two main approaches. The first involves inflating image models with spatial-temporal attention mechanisms (e.g., Tune-A-Video, TokenFlow), where motion is implicitly retained within the attention modules. While these methods offer some improvements, they do not consistently achieve the desired level of temporal coherence. The second approach utilizes explicit optical flow guidance from videos to derive pixel correspondence, which can then be used to generate occlusion masks for inpainting (e.g., Rerender, Hu's VideoControlNet) or to construct canonical image representations (e.g., CoDeF). However, a significant limitation of these flow-based methods is their reliance on the accuracy of optical flow estimation. When optical flow is derived from pre-trained models, it often contains imperfections, leading to problematic hard constraints that can propagate errors and produce undesirable outcomes.

FlowVid positions itself within this landscape by acknowledging the utility of optical flow for motion guidance while specifically addressing its inherent imperfections. Unlike methods that strictly adhere to optical flow, FlowVid integrates flow information as a flexible, soft condition in conjunction with spatial conditions. This approach aims to leverage the benefits of optical flow for temporal continuity without being rigidly bound by its inaccuracies, thereby seeking to achieve a more robust and consistent V2V synthesis. It builds upon the architecture of spatial-controlled I2I models (like ControlNet) but extends them with specific temporal conditioning, including flow-warped video, occlusion masks, and information from the first frame.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a consistent video-to-video (V2V) synthesis framework that can modify input videos according to given text prompts while maintaining strong temporal coherence across frames.

The core motivation stems from the limitations of existing V2V synthesis methods. While image-to-image (I2I) synthesis has advanced significantly, directly applying I2I models to individual video frames typically results in temporal flickering and incoherence. Current V2V approaches, which either implicitly handle motion through spatial-temporal attention or explicitly use optical flow as a rigid constraint, still face challenges. Methods relying on strict adherence to optical flow often produce unsatisfactory results because pre-trained flow estimation models frequently contain inaccuracies, particularly in complex motion scenarios or regions with occlusions. For instance, imprecise flow estimations for moving limbs can lead to persistent errors in generated video frames (as illustrated in Figure 2).

Given these challenges, the specific objectives and motivations behind FlowVid include:

1. **Harnessing Optical Flow with Imperfection Handling:** To utilize the benefits of optical flow for propagating motion and structural information across frames, but without treating it as an infallible, strict constraint. The motivation is to design a system that can leverage optical flow's guidance while simultaneously mitigating the negative impact of its inevitable estimation errors.
2. **Integrating Joint Spatial and Temporal Conditions:** To combine the explicit temporal clues from optical flow with additional spatial conditions (e.g., depth maps). The motivation is that spatial conditions can provide a corrective or complementary structural reference, allowing the model to rectify inaccuracies introduced by imperfect optical flow and thus achieve more structurally coherent results.
3. **Enabling a Flexible "Edit-Propagate" Workflow:** To decouple the initial editing of the first frame from the subsequent propagation of edits. This allows users to leverage any existing and evolving I2I model for editing the initial frame, providing significant flexibility. The motivation is to enhance the adaptability and applicability of the V2V system by not requiring a specialized first-frame editor.
4. **Achieving High-Quality and Consistent Outputs:** To generate synthesized videos that exhibit strong temporal consistency, accurate alignment with the target text prompt, and overall visual quality, surpassing the performance of current state-of-the-art methods.
5. **Improving Efficiency:** To develop a V2V synthesis pipeline that is computationally efficient, enabling faster video generation compared to existing approaches, which can be time-consuming due to per-video optimization or sequential processing.
6. **Supporting Lengthy Video Generation:** To implement an autoregressive mechanism that allows for the coherent generation of extended video clips, addressing a practical need for longer content creation.
7. **Versatile Application Support:** To design a framework capable of handling a diverse range of video editing tasks, including global stylization, object swaps, and localized modifications, demonstrating broad utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowVid proposes a consistent V2V synthesis framework by jointly leveraging spatial conditions and temporal optical flow clues. The model is built upon an inflated spatial-controlled I2I diffusion model, similar to ControlNet, but with enhancements for temporal processing and a specific strategy for handling optical flow.

#### 3.1. Inflating Image U-Net to Accommodate Video

The core of FlowVid's architecture involves adapting a U-Net based latent diffusion model for video processing. This adaptation includes:
- **Pseudo-3D Layers:** All 2D layers within the convolutional modules of the U-Net are modified into pseudo-3D layers to process temporal sequences.
- **Temporal Self-Attention:** An additional temporal self-attention layer is incorporated to model relationships across frames.
- **Spatial-Temporal Self-Attention:** The existing spatial self-attention layers are adapted to become spatial-temporal self-attention layers. For a given frame I_i, the query feature is derived from I_i, while key and value features are obtained by concatenating information from the first frame (I_1) and the preceding frame (I_{i-1}). This mechanism helps to preserve appearance consistency over time.
- **ControlNet Integration:** The framework integrates an additional ControlNet U-Net for processing spatial conditions. The output of this ControlNet U-Net is then merged into the main, expanded U-Net.

#### 3.2. Training with Joint Spatial-Temporal Conditions

The model is trained using a combination of spatial conditions and a unique approach to integrating optical flow information:
- **Optical Flow Estimation and Warping:** Given an input video sequence I, optical flow is calculated between the first frame (I_1) and all subsequent frames (I_i) using a pre-trained model (UniMatch). Both forward (F_{1→i}) and backward (F_{i→1}) flows are obtained. A forward-backward consistency check is then applied to derive corresponding occlusion masks (O^{fwd} and O^{bwd}). The backward flow (F_{i→1}) and occlusion mask (O^{bwd}_{i→1}) are used to warp the first frame (I_1) to create a flow-warped i-th frame (I^W_i). These warped frames are intended to replicate the structure of I_i but with pixels sourced from I_1, including potentially blank occluded regions.
- **Condition Integration:** The sequence of flow-warped frames (I^W) is encoded into a latent representation (f). This latent `f` is then concatenated with the noisy input latent `z_t` for the diffusion process. The first layer of the U-Net is augmented to accommodate the increased channel dimensions. Similarly, `f` is integrated into the spatial ControlNet U-Net.
- **Loss Function:** The training utilizes a modified Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) loss function, incorporating the spatial conditions (c) and the flow information (f) as additional inputs to the U-Net: `L_FlowVid = E[||ϵ - ϵ_θ(z_t, t, τ, c, f)||^2_2]`.
- **Crucial Design Choices:**
 - **v-parameterization:** The model uses v-parameterization instead of the standard ϵ-parameterization. This choice was empirically found to prevent unnatural global color shifts across frames.
 - **Additional Conditions:** Beyond flow-warped video, two other elements are integrated to enhance performance:
 - **Constant First Frame Video:** The first frame (I_1) is processed as a constant video sequence (I_{1st} = {I_1..., I_1}), converted to a latent representation, and concatenated with `z_t`. This provides consistent texture guidance.
 - **Binary Occlusion Masks:** The backward occlusion masks (O = {O^{bwd}_{1→1}..., O^{bwd}_{N→1}}) are resized to latent dimensions and concatenated with `z_t`. These masks explicitly signal occluded regions, guiding the model's generation process.

#### 3.3. Generation: Edit the First Frame, Then Propagate

FlowVid employs an "edit-propagate" strategy during inference:
- **First Frame Editing:** The initial frame (I_1) of the input video is edited using any prevalent I2I model based on the target prompt (τ'), resulting in an edited first frame (I'_1).
- **Propagation:** The edits are propagated to subsequent frames:
 - Optical flow (F_{i→1}) and occlusion masks (O^{bwd}_{i→1}) from the *original input video* I are used to create a flow-warped *edited* video (I'^W) from I'_1.
 - I'^W is encoded to a flow latent (f).
 - Spatial conditions (e.g., depth maps) extracted from the *original input video* are also provided.
 - A randomly initialized Gaussian noise (z_T) is denoised by the trained model, guided by the flow latent (f), spatial conditions (c), and the target prompt (τ').
 - DDIM denoising with 20 sampling steps is used, along with classifier-free guidance and the Zero SNR noise scheduler.
 - The denoised latent (z_0) is then decoded to pixel space.
- **Autoregressive Generation for Lengthy Videos:** For generating videos longer than a single batch, an autoregressive mechanism is used. The last frame of the current generated batch (I'_N) serves as the starting point for the subsequent batch.
 - **Color Calibration:** To counteract a "grayish effect" that can accumulate over long autoregressive sequences due to the lossy nature of the encoder/decoder, a global color calibration is applied. Each generated frame's mean and variance are adjusted to match those of the initial edited frame (I'_1).
 - **Self-Attention Feature Fusion:** To further preserve the original structure and motion, self-attention maps obtained from DDIM inversion of the input video with its original prompt are pre-saved. During generation with the target prompt, the keys and values in the self-attention modules are replaced with these stored maps.
- **Post-processing:** The synthesized keyframes are then interpolated using an off-the-shelf frame interpolation model, such as RIFE, to achieve the desired output frame rate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FlowVid was trained on 100k real videos from Shutterstock and evaluated against state-of-the-art methods, including Rerender, CoDeF, and TokenFlow, using 25 DAVIS videos and 115 manually designed prompts, as well as 50 Shutterstock videos with 200 prompts.

- **Flexibility:** The model demonstrates flexibility by seamlessly integrating with existing I2I models for first-frame editing, supporting diverse applications such as global stylization, object swaps, and local edits (Figure 1).
- **Efficiency:** FlowVid exhibits notable efficiency gains:
 - It generates a 4-second video (120 frames, 512x512 resolution, 30 FPS) in approximately 1.5 minutes on a single A-100 GPU.
 - This makes it 3.1x faster than CoDeF (4.6 minutes), 7.2x faster than Rerender (10.8 minutes), and 10.5x faster than TokenFlow (15.8 minutes).
 - The efficiency is attributed to FlowVid's batch processing of multiple frames simultaneously for keyframe generation, contrasting with Rerender's sequential approach. Additionally, its use of a non-reference-based interpolation model (RIFE) is faster than Rerender's reference-based Eb-Synth.
- **High-Quality Output:**
 - **User Study:** In a human evaluation involving five participants, FlowVid was preferred 45.7% of the time. This significantly outperformed TokenFlow (40.4%), Rerender (10.2%), and CoDeF (3.5%). Users reported FlowVid to be more robust and to achieve better editing capabilities and overall video quality.
 - **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 5):** FlowVid generated outputs with superior prompt alignment and visual quality compared to per-frame I2I applications (which suffered from flickering), CoDeF (blurry outputs, especially with large motion), Rerender (failed to capture large motions, color shifts), and TokenFlow (occasionally struggled with prompt adherence and introduced flickering artifacts).
 - **CLIP Scores (Table 2 in Appendix):** FlowVid achieved the highest Prompt Alignment (Pro-Ali) score of 33.20, indicating strong adherence to text prompts. Its Temporal Consistency (Tem-Con) score of 97.08 was the second highest, slightly trailing TokenFlow (97.30), with the paper noting that TokenFlow's high temporal consistency might sometimes correlate with less significant video modification.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Condition Combinations (Figure 6):** The study demonstrated the importance of integrating multiple conditions. Using only spatial controls resulted in a 9% winning rate against the full model. Adding flow-warped video significantly improved it to 38%. Further incorporating flow occlusion masks increased it to 42%. The full model, including spatial controls, flow-warped video, flow occlusion masks, and the first frame, provided the best overall performance, with the first frame offering better texture guidance, particularly in highly occluded areas.
 - **Spatial Control Type (Figure 7):** Different spatial conditions proved suitable for different tasks. Canny edge maps, which retain more fine details, were better for tasks requiring strong structural preservation like stylization. Depth maps, offering more flexibility, were more effective for object swap tasks where greater scene change and editing freedom were needed.
 - **Parameterization (Figure 8):** The choice of v-parameterization over ϵ-parameterization was crucial, as ϵ-prediction was observed to lead to unnatural global color shifts across frames, a phenomenon mitigated by v-parameterization.
- **Autoregressive Evaluation (Figure 4):** The implementation of global color calibration was effective in preserving consistent color across lengthy videos generated autoregressively, preventing a progressive "graying" effect.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FlowVid makes several contributions to the field of video-to-video synthesis, with implications for content creation, visual effects, and broader research into diffusion models.

The primary significance lies in its effective resolution of the temporal consistency challenge in V2V synthesis. By explicitly addressing the imperfections of optical flow—a common limitation in existing flow-guided methods—FlowVid offers a more robust and reliable approach to integrating motion information. This soft conditioning strategy, combined with spatial controls, represents a methodological advancement over rigid flow adherence, demonstrating how noisy auxiliary inputs can be harnessed for generative tasks.

The framework's demonstrated flexibility to work with any existing image-to-image (I2I) model for first-frame editing significantly enhances its adaptability and utility. This decoupled "edit-propagate" design allows FlowVid to leverage ongoing advancements in I2I research without requiring specialized re-training, thereby future-proofing a key component of its pipeline. This could accelerate innovation in both I2I and V2V domains by fostering interoperability.

Furthermore, FlowVid's superior efficiency, as evidenced by its significantly faster generation times compared to state-of-the-art methods, is a critical practical advantage. Faster generation reduces computational costs and turnaround times, making high-quality V2V synthesis more accessible and practical for a wider range of users, from professional content creators in the film industry to casual users creating short videos.

The high qualitative and quantitative performance, validated through extensive user studies and objective metrics (CLIP scores), indicates that FlowVid produces visually compelling results with strong temporal coherence and accurate prompt adherence. Its ability to support various applications, including stylization, object swaps, and local edits, broadens its potential impact across diverse creative and industrial applications.

The implementation of an autoregressive generation mechanism with effective color calibration also addresses the challenge of creating lengthy, consistent videos, expanding the scope of V2V synthesis beyond short clips.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Content Creation:** Empowering artists, filmmakers, and social media creators with a powerful tool for visual storytelling, allowing for consistent stylization, object manipulation, or scene alterations in video.
- **Democratization of Video Editing:** Making advanced video editing capabilities more accessible to users without extensive technical expertise, similar to how I2I models have democratized image editing.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The unique approach to handling imperfect optical flow as a soft condition provides a valuable blueprint for future research in incorporating diverse, potentially noisy, auxiliary guidance signals into complex generative models.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as dependence on accurate first-frame edits and challenges with large occlusions or extremely fast motion. These areas represent avenues for future research to further enhance the robustness and capabilities of V2V synthesis systems.
