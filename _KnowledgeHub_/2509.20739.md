# Decision-Driven Semantic Object Exploration for Legged Robots via Confidence-Calibrated Perception and Topological Subgoal Selection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) developed a decision-driven framework for semantic object exploration, enabling legged robots to leverage camera-centric perception. The system dynamically integrates calibrated semantic evidence and a compact topological memory for robust subgoal selection, achieving a 4.8 percentage-point improvement in semantic accuracy and a 2.1 percentage-point gain in global node selection accuracy in simulated environments, while demonstrating practical deployment on a physical quadruped robot.

## Problem

- Traditional geometry-centric SLAM methods are often unstable and resource-intensive for legged robots due to dynamic motion, ground impacts, and unstable viewpoints.
- Existing mapping representations do not directly support semantic decision-making for task-driven object exploration in open-world environments.
- Semantic perceptions from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can be noisy and heterogeneous, requiring better mechanisms to handle uncertainty for downstream robotic decisions.

## Method

- A ==confidence-calibrated semantic evidence arbitration== mechanism that integrates scene-level and object-level visual cues, suppressing noise and mapping confidences to a unified posterior score.
- A ==controlled-growth semantic topological memory== that compactly represents exploration history with 3D node positions, semantic labels, and exploration potential, avoiding dense metric maps.
- A ==semantic utility-driven subgoal selection== strategy that considers semantic relevance (via ==LLM==), fused confidence, exploration potential, and travel cost to make real-time, executable decisions.

## Results

- The confidence-calibrated semantic evidence arbitration mechanism improved average Semantic Accuracy (SA) by **4.8** percentage points in simulation, achieving **90.1%**.
- The semantic utility-driven subgoal decision mechanism increased Global Node Selection Accuracy (GNSA) by **2.1** percentage points over baselines, reaching **85.8%**.
- The full framework successfully completed object exploration tasks in diverse simulated environments and demonstrated practical deployment on a Unitree Go1 quadruped robot in **five** real-world settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust semantic exploration for legged robots benefits from a decision-driven approach that prioritizes actionable semantic information over precise geometric reconstruction.
> - A compact, dynamic topological memory, managed with controlled growth and pruning, is more effective for long-horizon semantic exploration than computationally intensive dense mapping.
> - Calibrating and arbitrating noisy, heterogeneous VLM evidence, then balancing that reliability against semantic gain, exploration value, and reachability cost in a multi-factor utility function, yields more optimal, executable subgoal selections.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous navigation and exploration for mobile robots in unstructured environments is a critical area of robotics research, with applications spanning search-and-rescue, logistics, and environmental monitoring. Traditionally, robotic navigation has relied heavily on geometry-centric Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) pipelines, which build dense metric maps using sensors such as LiDAR or multi-sensor fusion. While effective in structured settings, these geometry-driven approaches often face challenges with legged robots due to their rapid motion, frequent ground impacts, and viewpoint instability, which can compromise the robustness of dense SLAM representations. Furthermore, these systems demand substantial computational resources and expensive sensors, limiting their deployment on lightweight or cost-sensitive platforms that primarily use cameras.

The paper addresses a recognized gap where geometry-centric mapping does not directly support semantic decision-making required for task-driven semantic object exploration in open-world scenarios. It positions open-world semantic object exploration as fundamentally a decision-making problem, rather than solely a mapping or localization task. This perspective contrasts with conventional methods by emphasizing the transformation of noisy and heterogeneous semantic observations into stable, actionable exploration decisions.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) have enabled robots to extract rich semantic cues from visual data and natural language instructions. While these models offer potential for open-vocabulary object understanding and semantic reasoning, existing approaches often concentrate on perception or recognition, with less emphasis on explicit decision modeling, closed-loop execution, or fine-grained obstacle avoidance. Moreover, many VLN evaluations are primarily conducted in simulation or static environments, raising questions about their applicability to real-world, dynamic legged robot platforms.

This work specifically aims to bridge this gap by focusing on decision-driven semantic exploration using camera-centric sensing and constrained computational resources. It departs from dense geometric reconstruction, advocating for compact topological representations that are more suitable for long-horizon, goal-directed exploration, and naturally integrate semantic information. The paper contributes to the evolving landscape of semantic robotics by proposing mechanisms that explicitly handle the uncertainty and complementarity of different semantic cues and by demonstrating practical deployment on real legged robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable legged robots to perform effective decision-driven semantic object exploration in open-world environments. This involves developing a navigation paradigm that prioritizes decision-making based on semantic information rather than relying on dense geometric reconstructions.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and limitations in current robotic exploration systems:
1. **Limitations of Geometry-Centric SLAM for Legged Robots:** Existing SLAM-based pipelines, while robust in structured settings, are often fragile when applied to legged robots. The dynamic nature of legged locomotion, characterized by rapid motion, ground impacts, and unstable viewpoints, complicates accurate geometric mapping and pose optimization. Moreover, these systems typically require high-cost sensors (e.g., LiDAR) and significant computational power, which may not be feasible for lightweight or cost-sensitive platforms predominantly equipped with cameras.
2. **Mismatched Representations for Semantic Tasks:** For task-driven semantic object exploration, constructing a globally consistent dense metric map is often computationally intensive and does not directly inform semantic decisions, such as identifying where to explore next or which semantic targets to pursue. There is a need for representations that are compact, support semantic integration, and are suited for long-horizon, goal-directed exploration.
3. **Uncertainty in Semantic Perception:** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) provide robust open-vocabulary object understanding and semantic reasoning, their outputs can be noisy and heterogeneous under real-world conditions (e.g., motion blur, occlusion). Current methods often treat these outputs as raw perception results, with insufficient attention to modeling their uncertainty, integrating complementary cues (scene-level vs. object-level), and calibrating them for downstream decision-making.
4. **Lack of Explicit Decision Modeling for Exploration:** Many approaches in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) focus on perception or end-to-end policies, often relying on implicit decision processes or heuristic rules for subgoal selection. There is a need for explicit, interpretable, and real-time decision rules that can transform uncertain semantic evidence into executable actions, particularly for challenging platforms like legged robots that require fine-grained obstacle avoidance and real-time constraints.
5. **Real-World Deployment Challenges:** Much of the existing research in semantic navigation is evaluated in simulation or static environments, leaving open questions regarding practical deployment on real mobile robots, especially legged platforms that must contend with dynamic motion, real-time safety, and environmental variations.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper are:
- To propose a **confidence-calibrated semantic target arbitration mechanism** that integrates scene-level and object-level visual cues to produce reliable and executable exploration targets despite partial observations.
- To introduce a **controlled-growth semantic topological memory** that compactly represents exploration history and facilitates long-horizon semantic decision-making without dense metric maps.
- To design a **semantic utility-driven subgoal selection strategy** that jointly considers semantic relevance, evidence confidence, exploration potential, and travel cost to enable real-time execution.
- To validate the proposed approach through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating **cross-platform deployability and practical feasibility** on legged robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed decision-driven semantic object exploration framework operates as an "evidence-memory-decision-execution" pipeline, designed for legged robots using camera-centric sensing.

**A. Confidence-Calibrated Semantic Evidence Arbitration:**
This module addresses the challenge of integrating heterogeneous semantic evidence from different sources.
1. **Scene-level Semantic Evidence:** Uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B to process RGB images and a natural language instruction to generate scene-level proposals (image-plane locations, semantic labels) and a global scene-level confidence score.
2. **Object-level Semantic Evidence:** Employs GroundingDINO-T for open-vocabulary object detection, producing bounding boxes, text-aligned labels, and detection confidences for specific objects.
3. **Calibration and Arbitration:**
 - **Confidence Calibration:** Both scene-level and object-level confidences are processed through a monotonic calibration function (Eq. 1) to suppress low-confidence noise and map values onto a unified posterior scale.
 - **Posterior Scoring:** Candidate targets are formed by aligning object detections with the instruction and considering scene proposals. A posterior score (Eq. 2) is calculated for each candidate, combining calibrated object confidence, calibrated scene confidence, spatial consistency (Intersection over Union between scene region and object bounding box), and a depth-based feasibility indicator (κ_t) to penalize unreachable targets. Hyperparameters control the relative contributions of these factors.
 - **Target Selection:** The candidate with the maximum posterior score is selected as the final target (t*), and its normalized fused confidence (C_f) is computed (Eq. 3).
 - **Executable Target Location:** The 2D image-plane location of the selected target (p_t) is determined by a confidence-weighted interpolation between the object-level box center and the scene-level proposal location (Eq. 4), if both are available. Otherwise, the available location is used. The output is (p_t, L_t, C_f).

**B. Controlled-Growth Semantic Topological Memory:**
This module maintains a compact, dynamic graph representation (G = (V, E)) of the explored environment.
1. **Graph Structure and Node State:** Each node v_i ∈ V stores its 3D world position (P_v_i), semantic label (L_v_i), fused confidence (C_f_v_i) from the arbitration module, and an exploration potential (P_explore(v_i)). A master node (v_m) is established at the starting viewpoint. 3D node candidates are generated by back-projecting the 2D target pixel (p_t) and its depth (D_t(p_t)) using camera intrinsics and odometry/IMU (Eq. 5), without global pose-graph optimization.
2. **Controlled Growth and Connectivity:** To manage memory size, a new node is inserted only if its 3D position is sufficiently far from any existing node (Euclidean distance > δ_d) and its fused confidence exceeds a threshold. Otherwise, the observation is merged into the nearest existing node, and its attributes, including confidence, are updated via an exponential moving average (Eq. 6). Edges are added to spatial neighbors within a radius (δ_r) or to k-nearest nodes, with traversal costs approximated by Euclidean distance or short-horizon reachability.
3. **Exploration Potential Update and Pruning:** The exploration potential (P_explore(v_i)) of a node is initialized based on fused confidence and decays (Eq. 7) when the node is revisited or its surroundings are observed. Nodes with low exploration potential and low semantic confidence are periodically pruned, and spatially adjacent nodes with consistent semantics may be merged to maintain memory compactness.

**C. Semantic Utility-Driven Subgoal Decision:**
This module selects the next executable subgoal from the topological memory.
1. **Candidate Set and Semantic Relevance:** Candidate nodes (V_c) are filtered based on a minimum exploration potential threshold (τ_p) (Eq. 8). For each candidate, its semantic label, fused confidence, and estimated travel cost from the robot's current position are retrieved. A Large Language Model (LLM), specifically GPT-4, is used to assess the semantic relevance (S_LLM_v) between the robot's natural language instruction (ℓ) and the candidate node's semantic label (L_v).
2. **Semantic Utility and Greedy Selection:** The semantic utility (U(v)) of each candidate node is computed using a composite function (Eq. 9) that combines:
 - Semantic relevance (S_LLM_v)
 - Fused confidence (C_f_v)
 - Exploration potential (P_explore(v))
 - An inverse exponential of the travel cost (d(P_robot, P_v)).
 Hyperparameters (α, β, η, γ) control the weighting of these factors. The next subgoal (v*) is selected greedily as the node maximizing this utility score, and its 3D position (g_t) is passed for execution (Eq. 10).

**D. Execution Interface and Motion Realization:**
This module translates the selected subgoal into safe, real-time actions for legged robots.
1. **Local Obstacle-aware Command Generation:** The 3D subgoal (P_g_t) is projected into the current camera view. An end-to-end vision-based local planner, Viplanner, is used to generate linear and angular velocities ([v_p, ω_p]^T) based on depth observations and the projected subgoal. This ensures collision avoidance.
2. **Policy-based Motion Realization:** Reinforcement learning-based locomotion policies serve as low-level controllers. These policies take the robot's current state and the commanded velocities to output joint-level actions. A lightweight wrapper incorporates recent execution history to stabilize motion during complex transitions. This decoupled design allows for platform-agnostic high-level semantic decision-making and platform-specific low-level motion control.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted in both simulation (Isaac Sim environments: CARLA Town, Warehouse) using multiple quadruped and humanoid robot models, and real-world environments using a Unitree Go1 quadruped robot equipped with a RealSense D435i RGB-D camera. Onboard processing uses a Jetson AGX Orin for planning and control, communicating with a workstation (NVIDIA RTX4090) for VLM/LLM inference. Evaluation metrics included Semantic Accuracy (SA), Global Node Selection Accuracy (GNSA), Obstacle Avoidance Success Rate (OASR), Success Rate (SR), and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed mechanisms across various metrics in both simulated and real-world environments.

**A. Results of Evidence Arbitration for Decision:**
The confidence-calibrated semantic evidence arbitration mechanism was evaluated for its ability to produce reliable semantic inputs for decision-making (Table I).
- **Performance Comparison:** Qwen2.5-VL (scene-level) and GroundingDINO/YOLO-World (object-level) alone showed instability. A naive fusion of Qwen and GroundingDINO offered limited improvement.
- **Proposed Method's Advantage:** The proposed confidence-calibrated arbitration consistently achieved the highest Semantic Accuracy (SA) across all five simulation scenes. It improved the average SA from 85.3% (Qwen+GroundingDINO) to 90.1%, representing a +4.8 percentage-point gain.
- **Interpretation:** This finding indicates that the arbitration primarily enhances decision quality by filtering unreliable semantic cues and enforcing spatial consistency, rather than merely improving raw recognition rates. This stabilization of heterogeneous semantic evidence provides more robust inputs for subsequent decision stages.

**B. Utility-Driven Subgoal Decision on Topological Memory:**
The efficacy of the semantic utility-driven subgoal decision mechanism was assessed by its Global Node Selection Accuracy (GNSA) and Obstacle Avoidance Success Rate (OASR) (Table II).
- **Baseline Limitations:** A Bayesian Probability approach (confidence-only ranking) showed limited decision stability, especially in cluttered environments where high-confidence nodes might be hard to reach.
- **LLM Integration:** Incorporating GPT-4 for semantic relevance improved GNSA, demonstrating the value of language model reasoning in aligning decisions with task instructions.
- **Proposed Method's Advantage:** The full utility-driven strategy, which jointly considers semantic relevance, fused confidence, exploration potential, and traversal cost, further improved GNSA. It increased the average GNSA from 83.7% (HOV-SG, a strong baseline) to 85.8%, a +2.1 percentage-point gain.
- **Interpretation:** This suggests that explicitly balancing semantic gain, evidence reliability, exploration value, and reachability cost leads to more executable and optimal subgoal selections, reducing oscillations from noisy semantic cues.

**C. System-Level Object Exploration Experiments:**
The full framework's performance was evaluated in system-level object exploration tasks (Table III, Fig. 3, Fig. 4).
- **Simulation Results:** The system demonstrated stable performance in five diverse simulated environments (Garden, Sidewalk, Road, two Warehouse scenarios) across different legged robot platforms. It achieved high Success Rates (SR) and competitive Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). Failures were primarily attributed to unstable semantic evidence or limitations in local reachability. Successful runs showed trajectories close to geodesic paths, indicating coherent decision sequences.
- **Real-World Results:** Deployed on a Unitree Go1 robot in five real-world environments (Office, Showroom, Laboratory, Living Room, Garden), the framework consistently completed object exploration tasks. While performance metrics decreased compared to simulation, attributed to real-world challenges like perception noise, motion blur, and illumination variations, the system maintained functional robustness across diverse indoor and outdoor settings. This demonstrates the practical feasibility and deployability of the decision-driven exploration mechanism on real hardware.

**D. Runtime Efficiency Analysis:**
The system's modular architecture and event-triggered execution scheme were analyzed (Table IV).
- **Hierarchical Execution:** High-level semantic modules (Qwen-VL, GroundingDINO, Evidence Arbitration, Topology Update, Utility Decision) are invoked on demand when the robot reaches a stable viewpoint. VLM/LLM inference introduced a latency of approximately 3-3.5 seconds per viewpoint update.
- **Real-time Execution Layer:** The low-level execution layer (Trajectory Tracking at 12 Hz, Motion Policy at 50 Hz) operates continuously in real-time.
- **Interpretation:** The decoupled design ensures that large model latency primarily affects the decision refresh interval, not the stability of motion control. The system is more sensitive to the low-level control frequency, which operates at real-time rates.

**E. Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies quantified the impact of core decision mechanisms on SR and SPL (Table V).
- **Baseline Performance:** A baseline system (scene-level perception, confidence-based ranking) achieved an SR of 35% and SPL of 23.5%, demonstrating the limitations of unstable semantic observations without the proposed components.
- **Impact of Evidence Arbitration:** Introducing semantic evidence arbitration alone improved SR to 45% and SPL to 30.8%, highlighting its role in providing more reliable decision inputs.
- **Impact of Utility-Driven Decision:** Further incorporating the utility-driven subgoal decision mechanism increased SR to 55% and SPL to 34.2%.
- **Interpretation:** The results confirm that both semantic evidence arbitration and utility-driven subgoal decision contribute synergistically to improving the reliability and efficiency of exploration planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic autonomy by proposing a decision-driven semantic object exploration framework tailored for legged robots. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Shift in Navigation Paradigm:** The work represents a shift from traditional geometry-centric navigation to a decision-driven, semantic-first approach. By focusing on how semantic information can be reliably translated into actionable exploration decisions, it addresses a fundamental challenge for robots operating in open, unstructured environments where task relevance often outweighs precise geometric mapping. This paradigm can potentially reduce the reliance on expensive high-fidelity sensors and computationally intensive dense SLAM, making autonomous navigation more accessible for resource-constrained platforms.

2. **Robust Semantic Decision-Making:** The proposed confidence-calibrated semantic evidence arbitration mechanism offers a method for effectively integrating and stabilizing heterogeneous and noisy semantic cues from both scene-level and object-level perception. This is crucial for real-world deployment, where perception is often imperfect. The utility-driven subgoal selection strategy provides a robust and interpretable method for balancing multiple factors (semantic relevance, confidence, exploration potential, and travel cost) to make informed exploration choices.

3. **Feasibility for Legged Robots:** The successful deployment and validation of the framework on real legged robots (Unitree Go1) in diverse indoor and outdoor environments demonstrate its practical feasibility. Legged robots present unique challenges due to their dynamic motion and viewpoint instability, which often degrade perception systems. By showing that a compact semantic topological memory, coupled with robust decision-making, can enable goal-directed exploration on these platforms, the research paves the way for their increased autonomy in complex scenarios.

4. **Foundation for Future Work:** The paper highlights current limitations, such as dependence on perceptual stability and validation in moderately scaled/static environments. This provides a clear direction for future research, including incorporating temporal consistency modeling, explicit uncertainty propagation, and evaluating generalization in more complex, dynamic, and larger-scale real-world settings. This foundational work can serve as a basis for developing more advanced and robust semantic navigation systems for autonomous agents.

5. **Broad Application Potential:** The capabilities demonstrated, such as exploring unknown spaces, interpreting scene semantics, and searching for task-relevant objects, are highly relevant for a range of applications. These include search-and-rescue operations where robots must identify specific items in disaster zones, warehouse logistics requiring robots to locate and retrieve objects, and environmental monitoring tasks where specific features or items need to be identified and inspected. The ability to perform open-vocabulary object exploration driven by natural language instructions broadens the applicability of autonomous systems in human-robot interaction contexts.
