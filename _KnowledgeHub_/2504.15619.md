# AdaViP: Aligning Multi-modal LLMs via Adaptive Vision-enhanced Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from USTC develop AdaViP, a preference optimization framework that reduces hallucinations in multimodal language models by incorporating vision-based preference pairs and adaptive optimization, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks including Object HalBench and AMBER while surpassing GPT-4V on certain trustworthiness metrics.

## Problem

- Existing preference learning methods for MLLMs focus primarily on language preferences while neglecting visual context
- MLLMs frequently generate responses that contradict or ignore important visual information (hallucinations)
- Prior DPO-based alignment methods apply preference optimization uniformly, without adaptively balancing the influence of visual versus language-based preference signals during training

## Method

- ==Vision-based preference pair construction== using cascade of ==visual foundation models== to create modified images
- ==Adaptive optimization strategy== that dynamically balances vision and language-based preferences during training via ==DPO==
- Unifies vision- and language-based preferences into a single distribution via a modified ==DPO== loss inspired by the ==Plackett-Luce model==, weighting rejected samples by their reward-value ratio

## Results

- Outperforms existing DPO methods and hallucination-specific baselines across multiple benchmarks (Object HalBench, AMBER, MMHal-Bench), achieving superior trustworthiness compared to GPT-4V on certain metrics
- Ablation studies confirm both vision-based pairs and adaptive optimization contribute to improvements
- Analysis shows successful transition from visual-heavy to balanced preference integration during training

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Removing key visual elements from images creates effective preference pairs for training
> - Adaptive weighting between visual and language preferences improves optimization
> - Models initially rely more on visual preferences before achieving balanced integration

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): hallucination. Hallucination, in this context, refers to the generation of responses that contradict or are not supported by the visual input. The work builds upon the existing research on aligning MLLMs with human preferences using techniques like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

Here's how it fits into the broader research landscape:

- **Hallucination Mitigation in MLLMs:** There's a growing body of research focused on mitigating hallucinations in MLLMs. Existing methods can be broadly categorized into:
 - Data cleaning strategies to improve the quality of training data.
 - Techniques to enrich visual representations within MLLMs.
 - Test-time augmentation methods to enhance the model's sensitivity to visual details.
 - Preference alignment approaches, which use human or AI feedback to train models to generate more accurate responses.
 The AdaViP approach falls into the preference alignment category but distinguishes itself by focusing specifically on visual context.

- **Preference Learning for MLLMs:** Preference learning, particularly DPO, has become a popular approach for aligning LLMs and MLLMs with human preferences. Existing DPO-based methods for MLLMs primarily focus on language-based preferences, often neglecting the importance of visual cues.
 - AdaViP extends these methods by incorporating vision-based preference pairs, ensuring that the model pays more attention to visual context.
 - It also introduces an adaptive optimization strategy to dynamically balance the influence of vision and language-based preferences, a novel approach in this area.

- **Visual Foundation Models:** The paper leverages the power of pre-trained visual foundation models like CLIP, Recognize Anything Model (RAM), GroundingDINO, Segment Anything Model (SAM), and Big LaMa for image understanding, object detection, segmentation, and removal. This aligns with the trend of utilizing large-scale pre-trained models to enhance the performance of downstream tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The main objectives of this paper are:

- **To address the limitations of existing DPO-based methods for MLLMs that primarily focus on language preferences and neglect visual context.** The authors argue that this leads to inadequate attention to visual details and, consequently, hallucinations.
- **To develop a novel preference optimization framework (AdaViP) that enhances MLLMs' sensitivity to visual information and reduces hallucinations.**
- **To improve the accuracy and contextual relevance of MLLM outputs by incorporating vision-based preference pairs and an adaptive optimization strategy.**

The key motivations behind this work are:

- The need to improve the trustworthiness and reliability of MLLMs by mitigating hallucinations.
- The recognition that visual context is crucial for accurate understanding and response generation in multimodal scenarios.
- The desire to overcome the limitations of existing preference learning methods that overemphasize language preferences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed AdaViP framework consists of two main components:

- **Vision-based Preference Pair Construction:**
 - The authors create vision-based preference pairs by modifying the original image to remove key visual elements.
 - They leverage a cascade of visual foundation models (RAM, GroundingDINO, SAM, and LaMa) for object recognition, detection, segmentation, and removal.
 - The process involves:
 - Identifying objects in the image using RAM.
 - Generating bounding boxes for these objects using GroundingDINO.
 - Segmenting the objects using SAM.
 - Removing the segmented objects using Big LaMa to generate a candidate set of perturbed images.
 - The image with the least similarity to the preferred response (measured using CLIP) is selected as the rejected image.

- **Adaptive Preference Optimization:**
 - The authors propose an adaptive strategy to dynamically balance the influence of vision and language-based preference pairs during optimization.
 - They unify the vision and language-based preferences into a single distribution using a modified DPO loss function inspired by the Plackett-Luce model.
 - The adaptive loss function assigns different weights to each rejected sample based on the reward value ratio between the preferred and rejected samples.
 - This allows the model to adaptively optimize the preferences by focusing more on samples where the implicit reward model misclassifies the rejected sample as better than the preferred one.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of AdaViP in mitigating hallucinations and improving MLLM performance:

- **AdaViP outperforms existing DPO-based methods and hallucination-specific baselines on several benchmarks (Object HalBench, AMBER, and MMHal-Bench).**
- **AdaViP achieves state-of-the-art performance in trustworthiness, even surpassing the proprietary model GPT-4V on some metrics.**
- **Ablation studies show that both the vision-based preference pair construction and the adaptive optimization strategy contribute to the overall performance improvements.**
- **Analysis of weight and reward dynamics during training reveals that the model initially relies more on visual preferences but gradually transitions towards a more balanced integration of visual and language-based information.**

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its ability to address a critical limitation of existing MLLMs: hallucinations. By enhancing the model's sensitivity to visual context and adaptively balancing vision and language-based preferences, AdaViP offers a promising approach for improving the trustworthiness and reliability of MLLMs.

The potential impact of this work includes:

- **Improved accuracy and reliability of MLLMs in various applications, such as image captioning, visual question answering, and multimodal dialogue systems.**
- **Reduced hallucinations and more contextually relevant responses in MLLMs.**
- **A framework for incorporating visual information more effectively into preference learning for MLLMs.**
- **Potential for extending the approach to other modalities, such as video and audio.**
- **Contribution to the development of more robust and trustworthy AI systems that can effectively leverage multimodal data.**
