# Calibri: Enhancing Diffusion Transformers via Parameter-Efficient Calibration

> [!summary] Summary
> Calibri enhances Diffusion Transformer (DiT) performance in generative tasks by introducing a parameter-efficient calibration method for DiT blocks. This approach consistently improved image generation quality and reduced inference steps by up to 3x across state-of-the-art models, showing decisive preference in human evaluations.

## Problem

- Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks exhibit uneven contributions to model performance, with some potentially introducing detrimental artifacts.
- Standard DiT architectures are sub-optimally weighted, preventing them from achieving their maximum generative quality.
- Existing visual generative model alignment methods typically rely on computationally expensive full model fine-tuning.

## Method

- **Calibri** proposes a ==parameter-efficient calibration== approach that optimizes a minimal set of scalar coefficients (approximately $10^2$) for ==DiT== architectural components.
- The calibration process is framed as a ==black-box optimization== problem, solved using ==Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES)== to maximize a scalar reward function.
- Three levels of granularity for internal-layer calibration (block, layer, and gate scaling) are introduced, along with an ensemble method for combining calibrated models.

## Results

- Calibri consistently improved key metrics (HPSv3, ImageReward, Q-Align) across FLUX.1-dev, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium, and Qwen-Image, reducing required inference steps by up to **3x** (e.g., SD-3.5M from **80 to 30 steps**) while improving or maintaining quality.
- Human user studies (200 users, **5,600** assessments) showed a decisive preference for Calibri's outputs in terms of overall quality and text alignment, confirming perceptual gains.
- Calibri achieved alignment performance comparable to Flow-GRPO by modifying only 216 parameters, demonstrating its parameter efficiency compared to Flow-GRPO's **18.78** million, and further improved already aligned models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Selectively disabling certain DiT blocks can sometimes improve image generation quality, suggesting some layers contribute sub-optimally or introduce artifacts.
> - A simple re-weighting of each DiT block's output with a single learned scalar coefficient consistently enhances overall model performance.
> - The inherent sub-optimal weighting in standard DiT architectures can be significantly improved through post-hoc calibration of constituent blocks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Tokhchukov et al. is situated within the rapidly evolving field of visual content generation, specifically focusing on diffusion models. Recent advancements in this area, exemplified by models such as Stable Diffusion 3 and FLUX, have largely moved from the traditional UNet architecture towards Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). This architectural shift, often coupled with innovative techniques like flow matching, has established DiTs as a standard for tasks ranging from text-to-image synthesis to instruction-guided image editing and video generation.

The paper builds upon existing research into the interpretability of diffusion model backbones. Previous studies, such as Stable Flow and FreeFlux, have indicated that despite their uniform architectural design, individual DiT blocks contribute unevenly to the overall model performance. These works identified "vital layers" whose exclusion significantly impacts model outputs, suggesting that not all layers are equally critical or even beneficial.

Furthermore, this work relates to the area of visual generative model alignment, where models are fine-tuned to human preferences using reward models. Conventional alignment methods, often inspired by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and including techniques like Reward Backpropagation, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Differentiable Diffusion Preference Optimization (DDPO), typically require computationally expensive full model fine-tuning. Calibri aims to provide a parameter-efficient alternative to these methods by optimizing only a small subset of parameters. By addressing the observed sub-optimality of DiT block contributions, Calibri seeks to enhance generative quality and efficiency within this established framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to uncover and leverage the latent potential within Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) to significantly improve their performance in generative tasks. The motivation for this objective stems from a detailed analysis of the denoising process within DiT architectures, which revealed two key insights:

Firstly, the authors' analysis, building on prior work suggesting uneven contributions of DiT blocks, found that selectively disabling certain blocks could, counter-intuitively, sometimes *improve* generation quality rather than degrade it. This observation suggests that some DiT blocks might introduce detrimental artifacts or contribute sub-optimally to the final output. To evaluate this systematically, a framework was devised using the Qwen 3 model to generate diverse text prompts, which were then used with the FLUX model to produce baseline images. Subsequent ablations involved bypassing individual DiT layers by multiplying their output residual connection by zero. Evaluating the Image Reward scores for these ablated sets confirmed that certain layer removals enhanced image quality, providing empirical support for the hypothesis of uneven and potentially suboptimal layer contributions.

Secondly, extending this line of inquiry, the researchers discovered that a straightforward re-weighting of each DiT block's output—specifically, by multiplying it with a single learned scalar coefficient—consistently enhanced the model's performance beyond its original configuration. This experiment involved generating images with various scaling coefficients (from 0 to 1.5) for individual blocks and evaluating their Image Reward scores. The results indicated the existence of an optimal scaling factor for each block that improved performance.

These findings collectively led to the central hypothesis that the standard DiT architecture is sub-optimally weighted. Therefore, its performance can be significantly improved through a simple, post-hoc calibration of its constituent blocks. Based on this, the core objective was to propose Calibri, a parameter-efficient approach specifically designed to calibrate the contributions of DiT's architectural components. This calibration process is framed as a black-box optimization problem aimed at maximizing the quality of model outputs as measured by a reward model, while modifying a minimal number of parameters (approximately 10^2). A secondary, yet significant, objective was to demonstrate that Calibri could also reduce the number of inference steps required for image generation, thereby improving both efficiency and quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Calibri, aims to enhance Diffusion Transformer (DiT) generative capabilities through parameter-efficient calibration. The approach is structured around a black-box optimization framework to identify optimal scaling coefficients for DiT components.

**Preliminaries on Diffusion Transformer Architecture:**
The paper details two primary DiT block types:
- **Standard DiT block:** Comprises Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and feed-forward (FF) layers, each applying LayerNorm and modulated by time embeddings using α, β, γ vectors.
- **MM-DiT block:** An extension for multimodal data, processing textual and visual tokens in parallel. Inter-modal communication is restricted to a MultiModal Attention Layer, with separate modulation vectors for each modality.

**Calibri Problem Formulation:**
The calibration process is formulated as an optimization problem where the goal is to find an optimal parameter vector `c*` that maximizes a scalar-valued reward function `R(c)`, which measures the diffusion transformer's performance.

**Search Space for Calibration Parameters:**
The calibration parameters `c` are a union of output-level calibration weights `ω` and internal-layer calibration parameters `{s_i}`. The calibrated model output is expressed as `ω * f_s_θ(x, t, p)`, where `f_s_θ` denotes the model with internal scaling. Three levels of granularity for internal-layer calibration are introduced:
1. **Block Scaling:** A coarse method uniformly adjusting the outputs of Attention and MLP layers within the same architectural block using a shared `s` coefficient.
2. **Layer Scaling:** A finer granularity approach that adjusts individual Attention and MLP layers within a block with distinct `s` coefficients, offering greater flexibility.
3. **Gate Scaling:** Specific to multimodal architectures like MM-DiT, this calibrates distinct gates for visual and textual tokens to optimize their interaction dynamics.

**Calibration Parameter Search Procedure:**
To identify optimal calibration coefficients, Calibri employs the **Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES)**. This is a gradient-free optimization algorithm that iteratively refines a sampling distribution (a multivariate Gaussian `N(μ, σ^2C)`) based on the evaluation of candidate solutions by the reward function. The procedure involves:
1. Sampling candidate solutions from the Gaussian distribution.
2. Generating sample batches using these candidates.
3. Evaluating the candidates using a reward model (HPSv3, Q-Align, ImageReward).
4. Updating the mean vector, step-size (σ), and covariance matrix (C) of the sampling distribution to favor higher-performing candidates.

**Calibri Ensemble:**
Calibri also introduces an ensemble method, where `N` differently calibrated models are aggregated into a single sampler. The ensemble output is a weighted sum of the individual calibrated model outputs. The optimization problem is reformulated to find optimal calibration parameters for all `N` models simultaneously. This ensemble framework is shown to seamlessly integrate with Classifier-Free Guidance, where `N=2` models (conditional and unconditional) are calibrated.

**Implementation Details:**
Experiments utilized train and test prompts from T2I-compbench++. HPSv3 was used to track image preference and Q-Align for image quality during training. CMA-ES hyperparameters included an initial sigma of 0.25 and 4 + ⌊3 ln d⌋ candidates (where d is the search space dimension). A fixed bucket size of 16 images and resolution of 512 were used, with 15 inference steps during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key findings regarding the effectiveness and efficiency of Calibri:

**Motivational Experiments:**
The initial experiments demonstrated that DiT blocks have uneven contributions; removing certain blocks could enhance image quality, suggesting some introduce detrimental artifacts. Furthermore, a simple re-weighting of each block's output with a learned scalar consistently improved performance, highlighting the sub-optimal weighting of the standard DiT architecture.

**Calibri Design Decisions:**
- **Search Space Granularity:** Three levels of granularity were evaluated (block, layer, gate scaling). While gate scaling sometimes achieved the highest HPSv3 reward, layer scaling consistently yielded more robust improvements across multiple reward functions (Table 1, Figure 5). The training speed varied significantly, with block scaling being the fastest and gate scaling the slowest due to the increased number of parameters.
- **Calibri Ensemble:** Applying Calibri Ensemble (e.g., combining two calibrated models) consistently increased the HPSv3 reward across all inference steps (Figure 6). A significant finding was the reduction in the optimal number of sampling steps required, shifting from 30-50 steps in baselines to only 10-15 steps with Calibri Ensemble, thereby reducing computational load and inference time. This method also generalizes Skip Layer Guidance.

**Performance Across Different Backbones:**
Calibri was evaluated on three state-of-the-art text-to-image models: FLUX.1-dev, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD-3.5M), and Qwen-Image.
- **Quantitative Improvements:** Calibri consistently improved key metrics (HPSv3, ImageReward, Q-Align) across all baseline models (Table 2).
- **Efficiency Gains:** Calibri dramatically reduced the required inference steps for high-quality generation. For example, FLUX improved from 30 to 15 steps, SD-3.5M from 80 to 30 steps, and Qwen-Image from 100 to 30 steps (Table 2), all while achieving superior or comparable quality.
- **Qualitative & Human Evaluation:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 7) visually reinforced the improved output quality. A large-scale human user study (200 users, 5,600 assessments) decisively preferred Calibri's outputs in terms of overall preference and text alignment, confirming genuine perceptual gains beyond mere metric improvement (Table 3). Calibrated models were 2–3.3 times faster.

**Combination with Alignment Methods:**
Calibri was tested in conjunction with Flow-GRPO, an existing alignment method.
- **Enhanced Alignment:** When applied to a base SD-3.5M model, Calibri optimized for PickScore, improving not only PickScore but also HPSv3 and Q-Align. Its performance was comparable to Flow-GRPO, despite Calibri modifying only 216 parameters compared to Flow-GRPO's 18.78 million (Table 4).
- **Additive Benefits:** Calibri further improved models already aligned by Flow-GRPO (e.g., a PickScore-aligned checkpoint), demonstrating its utility in enhancing pre-aligned models. It also effectively integrated with Flow-GRPO when optimizing for different metrics like GenEval, boosting overall performance (Figure 8).

**Calibration Cost:**
The cost for calibrating a model using Calibri ranged from 32 to 356 NVIDIA H100 GPU-hours (Table 5). This is a one-time, offline cost. The convergence speed depended on both the search-space size and the quality of the pre-trained model; stronger models generally converged faster. For example, calibrating Flux with block scaling took 32 H100 GPU-hours and resulted in an approximate 2x permanent inference speed-up.

**Limitations and Diversity:**
The supplementary material acknowledges that Calibri’s reliance on reward models, which can be insensitive to subtle generation artifacts, might lead to suboptimal coefficient selection (Supplementary A). However, it also demonstrates that Calibri maintains generation diversity comparable to original models, unlike some other optimization methods like Flow-GRPO which can reduce it (Supplementary B). Furthermore, Calibri's effectiveness is not tied to a specific reward model; calibration with strong reward models like HPSv3 or PickScore leads to broad quality improvements across various metrics, indicating it is a general-purpose enhancement rather than a "reward hacking" technique (Supplementary C). The CMA-ES optimizer was shown to be substantially more efficient than gradient-based Flow-GRPO for calibrating Calibri coefficients (Supplementary D).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Calibri represents a notable advancement in enhancing Diffusion Transformer-based generative models, with several significant implications:

**Parameter-Efficient Performance Enhancement:** Calibri demonstrates that substantial improvements in generative quality can be achieved by modifying a minimal set of parameters (approximately 10^2). This contrasts sharply with computationally intensive full model fine-tuning approaches common in visual generative model alignment, making high-quality model enhancement accessible and cost-effective for a broader range of researchers and practitioners.

**Improved Generative Quality and Efficiency:** The consistent improvement across various state-of-the-art text-to-image models (FLUX, SD-3.5M, Qwen-Image), validated by both objective metrics and human evaluation, underscores Calibri's effectiveness. Crucially, its ability to significantly reduce the number of inference steps required for high-quality outputs leads to faster and more computationally efficient image generation. This efficiency gain is vital for real-world applications where computational resources and time are critical constraints.

**Deeper Understanding of DiT Architectures:** The motivational experiments, which revealed the uneven functional contributions of DiT blocks and the potential for quality improvement through simple re-weighting, offer valuable insights into the architectural sub-optimality of standard DiT designs. This can inform future architectural improvements and design principles for diffusion models.

**Complementary to Existing Alignment Methods:** Calibri's demonstrated capability to integrate with and further boost the performance of models already aligned with methods like Flow-GRPO highlights its versatility. It can act as an additional layer of optimization, allowing for combined benefits or the targeting of different quality aspects beyond a single alignment objective.

**Practical Applicability:** The lightweight design, one-time offline calibration cost, and the resulting permanent inference-time speed-up make Calibri a practical and attractive solution for developers working with diffusion models. It provides a straightforward path to improve model performance without incurring continuous, heavy computational overhead.

**Future Research Directions:** While effective, the acknowledged limitation regarding the sensitivity of current reward models to certain generation artifacts points to a critical area for future research. Advancements in reward modeling that can more accurately capture human preference for subtle details would likely maximize Calibri's potential, leading to even more robust and perceptually flawless generative outputs.
