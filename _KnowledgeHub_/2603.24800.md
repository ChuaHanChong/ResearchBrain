# Calibri: Enhancing Diffusion Transformers via Parameter-Efficient Calibration

> [!summary] Summary
> Calibri enhances Diffusion Transformer (DiT) performance in generative tasks by introducing a parameter-efficient calibration method for DiT blocks. This approach consistently improved image generation quality and reduced inference steps by up to 3x across state-of-the-art models, showing decisive preference in human evaluations.

## Problem

- Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks exhibit uneven contributions to model performance, with some potentially introducing detrimental artifacts.
- Standard DiT architectures are sub-optimally weighted, preventing them from achieving their maximum generative quality.
- Existing visual generative model alignment methods typically rely on computationally expensive full model fine-tuning.

## Method

- **Calibri** proposes a ==parameter-efficient calibration== approach that optimizes a minimal set of scalar coefficients (approximately $10^2$) for ==DiT== architectural components.
- The calibration process is framed as a ==black-box optimization== problem, solved using ==Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES)== to maximize a scalar reward function.
- Three levels of granularity for internal-layer calibration (block, layer, and gate scaling) are introduced, along with an ensemble method for combining calibrated models.

## Results

- Calibri consistently improved key metrics (HPSv3, ImageReward, Q-Align) across FLUX.1-dev, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium, and Qwen-Image, reducing required inference steps by up to **3x** (e.g., SD-3.5M from **80 to 30 steps**) while improving or maintaining quality.
- Human user studies (200 users, **5,600** assessments) showed a decisive preference for Calibri's outputs in terms of overall quality and text alignment, confirming perceptual gains.
- Calibri achieved alignment performance comparable to Flow-GRPO by modifying only 216 parameters, demonstrating its parameter efficiency compared to Flow-GRPO's **18.78** million, and further improved already aligned models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Selectively disabling certain DiT blocks can sometimes improve image generation quality, suggesting some layers contribute sub-optimally or introduce artifacts.
> - A simple re-weighting of each DiT block's output with a single learned scalar coefficient consistently enhances overall model performance.
> - The inherent sub-optimal weighting in standard DiT architectures can be significantly improved through post-hoc calibration of constituent blocks.
