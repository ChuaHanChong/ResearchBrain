# GET-Zero: Graph Embodiment Transformer for Zero-shot Embodiment Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> GET-Zero introduces a Graph Embodiment Transformer (GET) that enables robot policies to generalize zero-shot to unseen robot morphologies, including structural and geometric variations. This approach, incorporating explicit graph encoding and a self-modeling loss, yields a 20% improvement in zero-shot generalization for complex dexterous manipulation tasks compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing robot policies require extensive retraining for even minor hardware modifications, limiting their adaptability and scalability.
- Learned robot policies often lack inherent mechanisms to adapt to structural variations (e.g., missing or added joints) within a robot's kinematic chain.
- Developing embodiment-aware policies for complex tasks like dexterous manipulation is challenging due to the difficulty in defining unified action spaces across diverse robot designs.

## Method

- Introduces the Graph Embodiment Transformer (GET) architecture, which explicitly encodes the robot's kinematic graph structure (spatial and parent-child relationships) into the self-attention mechanism.
- Utilizes an embodiment-aware distillation process, training GET with behavior cloning from multiple embodiment-specific expert policies to efficiently consolidate knowledge.
- Incorporates a self-modeling loss that trains the network to predict forward kinematics (3D joint positions), enhancing the understanding of robot spatial properties for improved generalization.

## Results

- Achieved a 20% improvement in zero-shot generalization for both new graph and new link length variations on dexterous manipulation tasks compared to baseline methods.
- Demonstrated successful transfer from simulation to a physical LEAP Hand, maintaining zero-shot generalization capabilities to unseen physical embodiments.
- Reduced the average per-joint forward kinematics prediction error to 0.82mm, confirming the efficacy of the graph encoding mechanism in understanding spatial relationships.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding the robot's graph connectivity into a transformer's attention mechanism significantly improves zero-shot generalization to structural variations.
> - The self-modeling loss, when combined with robust graph encoding, enhances zero-shot performance by encouraging a strong internal representation of robot kinematics.
> - Linearized graph representations, such as those from Depth-First Search, are sensitive to structural changes, leading to poor generalization across varied robot morphologies.
