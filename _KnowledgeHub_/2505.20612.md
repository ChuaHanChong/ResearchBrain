# Roboflow100-VL: A Multi-Domain Object Detection Benchmark for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Roboflow and Carnegie Mellon University introduce Roboflow100-VL (RF100-VL), a multi-domain object detection benchmark designed to evaluate Vision-Language Models' generalization to out-of-distribution concepts and diverse imaging modalities. The benchmark, which includes multi-modal annotation instructions, reveals that state-of-the-art VLMs exhibit significantly reduced performance, with zero-shot accuracy below 2% on some medical imaging datasets.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) benchmarks primarily focus on common objects, failing to rigorously assess generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) concepts, tasks, or imaging modalities found in real-world scenarios.
- Many current VLM benchmarks emphasize visual question answering (VQA) or common sense reasoning rather than thoroughly evaluating fine-grained object detection capabilities in novel or specialized contexts.
- Models trained on internet-scale data often struggle with rare concepts, scientific names, context-dependent classes, or non-standard imaging (e.g., X-rays, thermal), due to the long-tail distribution of pre-training data.

## Method

- Developed ==Roboflow100-VL==, a large-scale benchmark of **100** object detection datasets (**564** classes, **164,149** images, >**1.3M** annotations) from Roboflow Universe, specifically curated for novelty and OOD challenges across diverse domains like medical, industrial, and aerial.
- Generated ==multi-modal annotation instructions== for each dataset, comprising a few visual examples and rich textual descriptions per class, using GPT-4o with subsequent human refinement, to guide concept alignment for models.
- Conducted comprehensive evaluations of state-of-the-art ==open-vocabulary detectors== and multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) across zero-shot, few-shot, semi-supervised, and fully-supervised learning regimes.

## Results

- State-of-the-art models like GroundingDINO showed a considerable mAP reduction from **49.2** on ODinW-13 to **15.7** on RF100-VL, with medical imaging datasets yielding less than **2%** zero-shot accuracy.
- Specialized open-vocabulary detectors achieved higher performance than generalist MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL 72B), which often performed worse than smaller specialist models in zero-shot settings.
- Few-shot fine-tuning of GroundingDINO outperformed YOLO variants by over **1**0%** mAP**, and semi-supervised learning techniques achieved comparable or superior performance with only **10%** of labeled data, demonstrating data efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Roboflow100-VL presents a substantially more challenging evaluation for VLM generalization than existing benchmarks, exposing significant performance drops for state-of-the-art models on OOD concepts.
> - Specialized open-vocabulary object detectors consistently outperform generalist Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in object detection, as MLLMs struggle to effectively leverage the rich multi-modal annotation instructions provided.
> - Few-shot learning through fine-tuning, particularly for models like GroundingDINO, demonstrates greater efficiency in learning new concepts compared to in-context prompting, indicating that large-scale pre-training aids efficient adaptation.
