# Safe and Steerable Geometric Motion Policies for Robotic Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SafePBDS introduces a framework for robotic dexterous manipulation that provides hard safety guarantees and external steerability by extending Pullback Bundle Dynamical Systems with Pullback Control Barrier Functions and a Task Manifold Action Interface. This approach successfully enabled 92.5% grasp success on diverse objects and robust palm-down in-hand reorientation on a 23-DOF robotic platform.

## Problem

- Reconciling diverse geometric objectives and hard constraints for robotic dexterous manipulation in real time, often involving soft objectives (e.g., null-space redundancy) and hard constraints (e.g., collision avoidance).
- Existing vector field policies like Pullback Bundle Dynamical Systems (PBDS) treated safety as soft objectives, which could be violated, and lacked an interface for external control or steering.
- Traditional methods (data-driven, trajectory optimization) struggle with real-time responsiveness, safety guarantees, or generalizability for complex dexterous tasks.

## Method

- SafePBDS extends Pullback Bundle Dynamical Systems (PBDS) by integrating Pullback Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) that convert task manifold safety conditions into hard linear constraints on robot acceleration.
- It introduces a Task Manifold Action Interface, enabling external high-level policies to inject low-dimensional residual motions to steer the robot's behavior without compromising safety.
- A two-stage Quadratic Program (QP) is used at each control step to first compute an optimal autonomous safe acceleration, then to find the final steered acceleration that respects all hard safety constraints.

## Results

- Simulation experiments demonstrated that SafePBDS successfully drove a robot out of unsafe regions while converging to goals and maintained chart invariance for both ECBF and BCBF.
- The system achieved a 92.5% success rate for 4-finger grasping across 20 diverse household objects and a 94.4% success rate for 3-finger grasping on a 23-DOF robotic arm and hand.
- SafePBDS enabled robust palm-down in-hand reorientation on hardware, performing over 360 degrees of yaw rotation under various conditions, marking the first model-based achievement for this challenging task without learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric consistency from Pullback Bundle Dynamical Systems can be combined with hard safety guarantees via Pullback Control Barrier Functions, ensuring robust constraint adherence across different charts and metrics.
> - Low-dimensional external control inputs can effectively steer complex, high-dimensional robotic manipulation behaviors while rigorously preserving safety guarantees, bridging autonomy and higher-level guidance.
> - Principled, model-based control frameworks are capable of achieving advanced dexterous manipulation capabilities, such as robust palm-down in-hand reorientation, without relying on extensive data-driven methods or specialized hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic dexterous manipulation necessitates the continuous reconciliation of objectives and constraints defined across diverse geometric spaces. For instance, a robot controlled within a $\mathbb{R}^7$ configuration manifold may be required to track end-effector poses on SE(3) while adhering to obstacle avoidance margins in $\mathbb{R}$. This complex control problem involves both soft objectives (e.g., null-space redundancy resolution) and hard constraints (e.g., collision avoidance, force closure). Real-world applications further demand real-time responsiveness to perturbations and modeling inaccuracies.

Current approaches to this problem typically fall into three categories:
1. **Data-driven methods:** Approaches such as Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and behavior cloning can learn complex behaviors. However, they often lack generalization across tasks or embodiments and can fail unpredictably outside their training distributions, raising concerns about safety guarantees. Vision-language-action models (VLAs) show promise for arm-gripper manipulation but face challenges with multi-fingered hands due to the complexity of data collection and motion.
2. **Trajectory optimization:** These methods explicitly incorporate constraints and dynamics to generate certifiably safe motion plans. However, they are computationally intensive, susceptible to local minima, require specialized engineering, and are not typically suitable for real-time adaptation to online perturbations at high control frequencies.
3. **Vector field policies:** These policies map a state to a desired velocity or acceleration through analytically defined vector fields, enabling fast reactive behavior and potential certification of properties like stability. Riemannian Motion Policies (RMPs) and Optimization Fabrics are examples in this category that compose per-task vector fields. However, existing formulations often lack geometric consistency (e.g., chart-invariance in RMPs) or treat safety as a soft objective. These local methods can also become trapped in local minima, necessitating integration with global planning or learning-based methods.

Pullback Bundle Dynamical Systems (PBDS) is a vector field policy framework that achieved geometric consistency by extending Simple Mechanical Control Systems (SMCS) to arbitrary Riemannian manifolds. It composes desired task behaviors by minimizing a weighted sum of task-acceleration tracking errors. However, PBDS had two significant limitations:
- **Soft safety constraints:** Safety-related objectives were incorporated as weighted costs in the optimization, meaning they could be violated under conflicts with other tasks. Such objectives could also become ill-defined in unsafe regions.
- **Lack of action interface:** The framework provided no direct mechanism for a higher-level policy or external input to steer the system, limiting it to autonomous behaviors defined at design time.

This work, SafePBDS, is positioned to address these specific limitations of PBDS, aiming to provide hard safety guarantees and external steerability while preserving the foundational geometric and compositional properties. It contributes to the field by enabling robust, certifiably safe, and externally controllable dexterous manipulation within a geometrically consistent framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing SafePBDS stems from the shortcomings of existing motion generation frameworks for robotic dexterous manipulation, particularly the limitations of its predecessor, PBDS. The overarching objective is to create a motion generation framework that can continuously reconcile objectives and constraints defined on heterogeneous geometric spaces in real time, with an emphasis on certifiable safety and external steerability.

The key specific objectives and motivations are:

1. **To provide hard, certifiable safety guarantees:** Traditional PBDS treated safety conditions as soft objectives, which could be violated when competing with other tasks, and could become ill-defined near unsafe regions. The objective of SafePBDS is to integrate **hard safety constraints** that are guaranteed to be satisfied at all times, preventing catastrophic failures, especially critical in physical human-robot interaction or complex contact-rich tasks. This requires converting task manifold safety conditions into strict, enforceable constraints on configuration manifold accelerations.

2. **To enable recovery from unsafe states:** The inability of prior metric-based PBDS safety approaches to define behavior upon constraint violation motivated the need for a mechanism that can actively drive the system *out* of unsafe regions once entered, rather than merely attempting to avoid entry.

3. **To introduce an action interface for higher-level control:** PBDS was purely autonomous, meaning it executed pre-defined behaviors without external intervention. A crucial objective for practical application is to allow higher-level policies (e.g., from a planner or learned model) to inject **low-dimensional residual motions** to steer or modify the robot's behavior in real time. This interface should preserve the system's safety guarantees and default to autonomous behavior when no external input is provided.

4. **To maintain geometric consistency and compositional properties:** A core strength of PBDS is its foundation in Riemannian geometry, ensuring chart-invariance and principled composition of tasks defined on arbitrary manifolds. SafePBDS aims to extend this framework without compromising these fundamental geometric properties.

5. **To validate the framework on complex dexterous manipulation tasks:** The practical utility of such a framework is best demonstrated on challenging real-world problems. Specific objectives include achieving high success rates in dexterous grasping of diverse objects and enabling robust in-hand reorientation, particularly in difficult palm-down configurations, which has historically relied heavily on less certifiable learning-based methods.

In summary, SafePBDS seeks to overcome the limitations of soft safety and fixed autonomy in PBDS by introducing novel pullback control barrier functions and a task manifold action interface, thereby producing a geometrically consistent, certifiably safe, and steerable framework for robotic dexterous manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SafePBDS builds upon the Pullback Bundle Dynamical Systems (PBDS) framework, extending it with two main innovations: Pullback Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for hard safety guarantees and a Task Manifold Action Interface for external control. The core idea is to express both autonomous behaviors and safety requirements as tasks on arbitrary task manifolds, which are then pulled back to the robot's configuration manifold and combined via a quadratic program (QP) to compute optimal, safe accelerations.

#### 3.1. Foundation: Pullback Bundle Dynamical Systems (PBDS)

The method uses PBDS as its basis. PBDS constructs motion policies by designing stable mechanical control systems (SMCSs) on individual task manifolds ($N_i$) and combining them. An SMCS for a task $i$ is defined by a task map $f_i: M \rightarrow N_i$ (mapping the configuration manifold $M$ to $N_i$), a Riemannian metric $g_i$ on $N_i$, a potential function $\Phi_i: N_i \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, and a dissipative force map $F_{D,i}: TN_i \rightarrow T^*N_i$. These components define a desired task acceleration, $\dot{\gamma}^a_{\dot{\sigma},i}(0)$, on $N_i$.

PBDS then formulates a weighted least-squares problem to find a configuration manifold acceleration $\ddot{\sigma}$ that minimizes the sum of squared errors between the desired task accelerations and the actual task accelerations induced by $\ddot{\sigma}$:
$$ \ddot{\sigma}(t) = \arg \min_{a \in D_{\dot{\sigma}(t)}} \sum_{i=1}^K \frac{1}{2} \|Z_i(a) - S_i(\dot{\sigma}(t))\|^2_{F^*_i w_i} $$
where $S_i$ maps the robot state to the desired task acceleration, $Z_i$ maps a candidate configuration acceleration to the resulting task acceleration, and $w_i$ is a weighting pseudometric for task priority. This formulation is geometrically consistent, but its safety constraints are soft and it lacks an external action interface.

#### 3.2. Innovation 1: Pullback Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)

SafePBDS integrates hard safety guarantees by incorporating Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) defined on task manifolds. The key is to "pull back" these task manifold safety conditions to the configuration manifold, where they act as linear constraints on the robot's acceleration. The paper assumes surjective submersive task maps, implying the system is fully actuated on the task manifold.

The safety function $h_0: N \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ defines a safe set $C_0 = \{x \in N | h_0(x) \ge 0\}$. For second-order systems like an SMCS, the control input enters through acceleration, meaning $h_0$ typically has a relative degree of 2. Two CBF variants are derived:

1. **Exponential CBF (ECBF):** The ECBF approach enforces a linear constraint on the second time derivative of $h_0$. The core idea involves ensuring that the second derivative $\ddot{h}_0$ is sufficiently large to prevent $h_0$ from becoming negative, based on the current values of $h_0$ and $\dot{h}_0$.
 The configuration manifold coordinate acceleration $\ddot{\sigma}$ affects $\ddot{h}_0$ through the geometric kinematics relating configuration and task manifold accelerations:
 $$ \ddot{h}_0 = \frac{\partial^2(h_0 \circ f)}{\partial \sigma^i \partial \sigma^j} \dot{\sigma}^i \dot{\sigma}^j + \frac{\partial(h_0 \circ f)}{\partial \sigma^i} \ddot{\sigma}^i $$
 Substituting this into the ECBF condition ($\ddot{h}_0 \ge -\kappa_2 \dot{h}_0 - \kappa_1 h_0$) yields a linear inequality constraint on $\ddot{\sigma}$:
 $$ \frac{\partial(h_0 \circ f)}{\partial \sigma^i} \ddot{\sigma}^i \ge - \frac{\partial^2(h_0 \circ f)}{\partial \sigma^i \partial \sigma^j} \dot{\sigma}^i \dot{\sigma}^j - \kappa_2 \dot{h}_0 - \kappa_1 h_0 $$
 This constraint is affine in $\ddot{\sigma}$ and notably independent of any Riemannian metric on the task manifold. The gains $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$ are chosen for stability and convergence, subject to initial state conditions.

2. **Backstepping CBF (BCBF):** The BCBF approach avoids computing higher-order derivatives of $h_0$ directly. Instead, it constructs a CBF $h(x, \dot{x})$ on the tangent bundle $TN$ by introducing an auxiliary safe velocity field $\tilde{\xi}: N \rightarrow TN$ on the task manifold $N$. This safe velocity field is derived using a half-Sontag formula to ensure forward invariance of $C_0$ under first-order dynamics. A candidate CBF is then defined as $h(x, \dot{x}) = h_0(x) - \frac{\epsilon}{2} \| \dot{x} - \tilde{\xi}(x) \|_N^2$.
 The time derivative $\dot{h}$ involves $\ddot{x}$ and thus $\ddot{\sigma}$ through the geometric kinematics. The BCBF condition $\dot{h} \ge -\alpha(h(x, \dot{x}))$ translates to another linear inequality constraint on $\ddot{\sigma}$, provided the velocity error $e_x = \dot{x} - \tilde{\xi}$ is non-zero. If $e_x = 0$, the constraint is satisfied by the design of $\tilde{\xi}$. Unlike ECBF, BCBF is metric-dependent, as it involves gradients and norms on the task manifold $N$.

These pullback CBF constraints form a set $A_{safe}$ of permissible accelerations, ensuring that the robot's configuration manifold acceleration keeps all active task manifold safety functions above their respective thresholds.

#### 3.3. Innovation 2: Task Manifold Action Interface

To enable external steerability, SafePBDS introduces an action interface that allows a high-level policy to inject low-dimensional residual motions. This is achieved by adding a new term to the PBDS optimization objective:
$$ \ddot{\sigma}(t) = \arg \min_{a \in D_{\dot{\sigma}(t)} \cap A_{safe}} \sum_{i=1}^K \frac{1}{2} \|Z_i(a) - S_i(\dot{\sigma}(t))\|^2_{F^*_i w_i} + \sum_{l=1}^L \frac{1}{2} \|Z_l(a) - Z_l(\bar{a}) - u^\sharp_l\|^2_{F^*_l w_l} $$
Here, $u_l \in T^*N_l$ is the force-like input provided by the user/high-level policy on a control task manifold $N_l$. $u^\sharp_l$ converts this input to an acceleration-level quantity. The term $Z_l(\bar{a})$ represents the task acceleration induced by the autonomous safe acceleration $\bar{a}$, effectively making $u^\sharp_l$ a residual force that shifts the autonomous behavior.

This formulation ensures three desiderata:
1. The action space can be lower-dimensional than the configuration space.
2. All configuration dimensions can be controlled if control task maps collectively form a submersion.
3. When $u_l = 0$ for all $l$, the system recovers its autonomous, safe behavior, as $\bar{a}$ is a minimizer of the action-augmented objective.
Importantly, the action interface preserves safety because the optimization is still constrained by $A_{safe}$.

#### 3.4. Overall Control Loop (Algorithm 1)

At each control step, SafePBDS performs the following:
1. Initialize the safe acceleration set $A_{safe}$ to the entire tangent space.
2. For each active ECBF safety task, compute its linear constraint on $\ddot{\sigma}$ (Theorem IV.1) and intersect it with $A_{safe}$.
3. For each active BCBF safety task, compute its linear constraint on $\ddot{\sigma}$ (Theorem IV.3) and intersect it with $A_{safe}$.
4. Solve a QP (QP1) to find the optimal *autonomous* safe acceleration $\bar{a}$ by minimizing the PBDS objective subject to $a \in A_{safe}$.
5. Solve a second QP (QP2) to find the final *steered* acceleration $\ddot{\sigma}$ by minimizing the action-augmented objective (which includes $\bar{a}$) subject to $a \in A_{safe}$.
6. The resulting $\ddot{\sigma}$ is returned to a low-level controller (e.g., impedance control) for execution.

This methodology combines the geometric consistency of PBDS with certifiable safety and external steerability into a single, real-time-capable framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SafePBDS framework was evaluated through extensive simulation experiments and hardware experiments on a dexterous manipulation platform.

#### 4.1. Simulated S$^2$ Double Integrator

Experiments on a point robot on S$^2$ validated fundamental theoretical properties:

- **Metric Dependence and Chart Switching:** ECBF trajectories were observed to be metric-independent, consistent with theoretical derivations, leading to consistent avoidance behavior irrespective of the underlying manifold metric. In contrast, BCBF trajectories exhibited metric dependence, producing visibly different avoidance paths based on whether a round or flat metric was assumed for the chart. BCBF also maintained a larger clearance due to its velocity tracking error penalty. Chart switching experiments, where the robot transitioned between North and South pole stereographic charts, demonstrated that SafePBDS produced consistent embedding-space behavior across chart choices for both ECBF and BCBF, confirming its chart invariance.
- **Safety Recovery:** When trajectories were initialized within an unsafe region (inside an obstacle), both ECBF and BCBF successfully drove the system out of the unsafe region and converged to the goal while respecting safety. The ECBF followed a more direct path to the safe boundary, while the BCBF maintained a larger clearance, consistent with its more conservative design.
- **Steered SafePBDS:** Using an identity control task map on the active chart, an action interface was demonstrated to select between topologically distinct avoidance paths around an obstacle. A tangential action perpendicular to the start-goal geodesic could steer the robot to either side of the obstacle. Even when subjected to a large, adversarial action continuously pushing the robot towards the obstacle center, SafePBDS prevented constraint violation and maintained safety, demonstrating robust clipping by the CBF. Chart invariance under action inputs was also verified.

#### 4.2. Simulated 7-DOF Robot Arm

Experiments on a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm in MuJoCo validated pullback CBFs for workspace obstacle avoidance and the action space for behavior modality selection.

- **Autonomous SafePBDS:**
 - **6-DOF Pose Tracking:** The full SafePBDS system, with obstacle avoidance ECBFs active, successfully deflected the arm around a spherical obstacle to reach a goal end-effector pose. An ablation without the obstacle CBF resulted in the arm passing through the obstacle, confirming the efficacy of the obstacle CBF.
 - **Random Orientation Tracking:** Across 50 randomly generated scenarios, the full SafePBDS system maintained safety in all runs (minimum obstacle clearance of +0.010 m). The ablation without the obstacle CBF violated the constraint in 11 of 50 runs (minimum clearance of -0.080 m), while still reaching the target orientation.
- **Steered SafePBDS:** With only end-effector position tracking (leaving 4 redundant degrees of freedom), a brief scalar action on the first joint successfully steered the arm to opposite sides of a workspace obstacle, allowing selection between different homotopy classes of safe paths. Both trajectories converged to the same goal position while maintaining safety, demonstrating how simple, transient inputs can guide complex behaviors without compromising safety.

#### 4.3. Hardware Experiments: 23-DOF Franka Panda–Allegro Hand Platform

Hardware validation focused on dexterous grasping and in-hand reorientation.

- **Dexterous Grasping:**
 - **4-Finger Grasping:** SafePBDS achieved a 92.5% overall success rate (111/120 trials) across 20 diverse household objects, with 15 objects achieving 100% success. Failures and partial successes were concentrated among heavier objects with small vertical extents, where pose estimation errors and high grasp forces pushed the system to its limits. The policy used a combination of joint limit, force closure (based on l* metric), fingertip lift-distance, pairwise finger-finger distance, table avoidance, and various link-object ECBFs, alongside per-finger fingertip-to-object distance and average-fingertip centering action tasks.
 - **3-Finger Grasping:** The flexibility of the framework allowed for a 3-finger grasping ablation where one finger was intentionally excluded. By simply switching the active force closure ECBF to a 3-finger variant and retargeting the excluded finger's action, the system achieved a 94.4% success rate (34/36 trials) across 3 test objects. This demonstrated the framework's ability to reliably adapt to task modifications with minimal policy changes.
- **Palm-Down In-Hand Reorientation (IHR):**
 - This challenging task, requiring the object to remain securely grasped, was addressed by leveraging offline simulation pre-planning to discover feasible finger gaiting sequences. A depth-first tree search explored finger-object contact states, generating sequences of lift-and-drop primitives. Each primitive was forward-simulated under SafePBDS, ensuring force closure of the three in-contact fingers and managing the moving finger.
 - The pre-planned open-loop motion sequences were then successfully deployed on hardware. SafePBDS achieved over 360 degrees of yaw rotation in both clockwise and counter-clockwise directions. This robustness was demonstrated under three conditions: static arm with no payload, static arm with incrementally added weight (up to 98g), and a swinging arm with loose weight (70g) inside the bottle. This result represents the first model-based achievement of robust palm-down in-hand reorientation on a fully actuated general-purpose dexterous hand without relying on machine learning or mechanical compliance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SafePBDS represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between geometrically principled motion generation and the practical requirements of safety and steerability.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Certifiable Safety in Geometrically Consistent Frameworks:** SafePBDS directly addresses a critical limitation of previous PBDS formulations by incorporating hard, certifiable safety guarantees through pullback Control Barrier Functions (CBFs). This enables the system to rigorously prevent constraint violations, such as collisions or loss of force closure, even in complex, contact-rich scenarios. This is a substantial advantage over data-driven methods that often lack formal safety guarantees.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Steerability:** The introduction of a task manifold action interface provides a mechanism for higher-level policies or human operators to inject control inputs, thereby steering the robot's behavior. This effectively combines the benefits of autonomous, reactive control with the flexibility of external guidance, while rigorously preserving safety. The ability to select between topologically distinct paths or adapt to task changes with simple, low-dimensional actions is a significant practical advantage.
- **Robustness in Dexterous Manipulation:** The successful hardware validation on a 23-DOF arm-hand system for complex tasks like grasping diverse household objects and palm-down in-hand reorientation highlights the practical robustness of SafePBDS. Achieving 92.5% grasp success and demonstrating consistent in-hand reorientation under varying conditions (e.g., object weight, arm motion) without reliance on machine learning or mechanical compliance indicates a high degree of reliability for real-world deployment.
- **Foundational for Model-Based Dexterity:** For palm-down in-hand reorientation, SafePBDS enabled the first model-based solution to achieve over 360 degrees of rotation in both directions. This demonstrates that principled, model-based approaches can achieve dexterity comparable to or exceeding current learning-based methods in specific challenging scenarios, with the added benefit of safety certification.
- **Modular and Composable Design:** By extending PBDS, SafePBDS inherits its modularity. Tasks and safety constraints can be defined on their natural geometric manifolds and then composed, simplifying the design of complex behaviors.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Safer Robot Deployment:** The hard safety guarantees provided by SafePBDS can lead to safer robots, enabling their deployment in environments with humans or in tasks where failure has high costs. This could accelerate the adoption of dexterous manipulators in industries requiring high reliability.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration:** The steerable nature of SafePBDS could facilitate more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration, allowing operators to guide robots with simple commands while the underlying system ensures safety and handles low-level complexities.
- **Foundation for Hybrid AI Approaches:** SafePBDS provides a robust, geometrically grounded "lower-level" control layer that could be effectively combined with "higher-level" AI decision-making (e.g., reinforcement learning, large behavior models). Learned policies could provide the action inputs, benefiting from the safety and geometric consistency guarantees of SafePBDS, potentially leading to more robust and certifiable hybrid systems.
- **Advancements in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The framework's ability to handle force closure and other contact-rich constraints with certifiable safety could drive progress in areas such as assembly, manufacturing, and surgical robotics, where precise and safe contact interactions are paramount.
- **Reduced Development Costs for Complex Tasks:** By providing a structured, model-based approach to complex dexterity, SafePBDS might reduce the need for extensive data collection and reward engineering often associated with learning-based methods, potentially lowering development costs and time for new manipulation tasks.

#### 5.3. Identified Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Local Minima:** Like other vector-field policies, SafePBDS is a local motion generation method and can become trapped in local minima. Escaping these requires intervention from a higher-level policy providing action inputs.
- **Kinematic Model:** The current formulation is kinematic and relies on a lower-level tracker or impedance controller to realize commanded accelerations, rather than reasoning directly about robot dynamics and contact forces.
- **Assumption of Surjective Submersion:** The pullback derivations assume surjective submersive task maps, limiting its applicability to fully or over-actuated systems and excluding tasks with singularities or non-smooth task maps.

Future work suggested includes:
- Combining the action interface with more advanced decision-making policies (e.g., reinforcement learning, vision-language-action models).
- Relaxing the assumption of surjective submersion to handle a broader class of objectives and robot configurations.
- Incorporating robot dynamics and contact forces directly into the framework to reason about interaction forces alongside geometric safety.

Overall, SafePBDS makes a substantial step towards more reliable, flexible, and certifiably safe robotic dexterous manipulation, laying groundwork for future integrations with advanced AI and more dynamic physical interactions.
