# Robo-ABC: Affordance Generalization Beyond Categories via Semantic Correspondence for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Robo-ABC is a training-free framework that gives robots cross-category grasp affordance. It mines contact points from egocentric human videos into an affordance memory, retrieves visually and semantically similar objects with CLIP, then transfers contact points to novel objects using emergent semantic correspondence in pre-trained diffusion features, feeding AnyGrasp. It lifts affordance success rate 31.6% over end-to-end baselines and grasps novel objects at 85.7%.

## Problem

- Prior affordance learners trained end-to-end on human videos predict contact regions only for in-domain object instances and viewpoints, failing on unseen objects and categories.
- Semantic-correspondence methods for manipulation generalize mostly within a single category, or need extra training, part segmentation, or user-supplied goal images to transfer.
- Cross-category manipulation from point clouds demands large annotated datasets and reliable actionable-part segmentation, and existing generalist agents stay confined to known object instances.

## Method

- **Affordance memory**: a hand-object detector plus skin segmentation locate hand-object contact pixels in egocentric videos; a homography chain maps them to a nearby unoccluded, Laplacian-sharpest frame, cropped and stored.
- Retrieval stage: langsam crops the target object, a ==CLIP== image encoder embeds it, and cosine similarity fetches the top-k most similar memory objects, within category or across all categories.
- Correspondence transfer: ==DIFT== diffusion U-Net features match retrieved contact points onto the new object, with eight rotation/flip augmentations to fix orientation mismatch; ==AnyGrasp== candidates nearest the point become the executed 7-DOF pose.

## Results

- On the AGD20K-derived benchmark Robo-ABC hits **60.7%** success rate versus **29.1%** for LOCATE, with NSS **0.516** vs **0.283** and distance-to-mask **0.045** vs **0.103**.
- Real-robot grasping over seven object categories reached **85.7%** success against VRB's **68.6%**, with the largest gain on badminton racket (**5/5** versus **2/5**).
- Ablations: CLIP-B32 retrieval with DIFT scored best; success rose from **56.4%** to **60.7%** as memory grew **18** to **51** categories, and **54.5%** to **60.7%** with top-5 retrieval.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Retrieval-plus-correspondence over a small memory can replace end-to-end affordance training: foundation-model features supply the generalization, so the memory needs breadth, not scale.
> - Diffusion features beat DINO-ViT for cross-category point matching, and they are orientation-sensitive; cheap test-time rotation/flip augmentation recovers much of the lost accuracy.
> - Memory diversity only helps if the correspondence model is strong: weaker backbones degrade past 36 categories as retrieval gets distracted by irrelevant matches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Robotic manipulation in unstructured environments remains one of the most challenging problems in robotics. While humans can intuitively understand how to interact with novel objects by drawing on past experiences, robots typically struggle when encountering objects they haven't seen during training. The paper "Robo-ABC: Affordance Generalization Beyond Categories via Semantic Correspondence for Robot Manipulation" addresses this fundamental limitation by proposing a framework that enables robots to generalize interaction knowledge across object categories without requiring additional training or manual annotations.

The research, conducted by a collaborative team from Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, Tsinghua University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Shanghai AI Lab, introduces a method that learns from readily available human interaction videos and transfers this knowledge to novel objects through semantic correspondence mapping. This approach represents a significant step toward achieving "open-world embodied intelligence" where robots can adapt to new scenarios autonomously.

### 2. Problem and Motivation

Current robotic manipulation systems face a critical generalization problem. Traditional approaches either rely on precise models of known objects or require extensive data collection and training for specific scenarios. These methods often fail when robots encounter out-of-distribution objects or situations not covered during training. The challenge becomes even more pronounced when trying to achieve cross-category generalization - enabling a robot to apply knowledge learned from one type of object to a completely different category.

The motivation for Robo-ABC stems from human cognitive abilities. When humans encounter a new object, they can instinctively understand how to interact with it by drawing parallels to familiar objects. For example, someone who has never seen a badminton racket can still grasp it effectively by recalling their experience with tennis rackets. This ability to generalize affordances - the action possibilities that objects provide - across categories is what the researchers aim to replicate in robotic systems.

The key insight is that the internet contains vast amounts of human interaction videos that could serve as a rich source of affordance knowledge. However, effectively extracting and transferring this knowledge to robots operating in real-world scenarios requires sophisticated methods for understanding semantic correspondences between different objects.

### 3. The Robo-ABC Framework

The Robo-ABC framework consists of four interconnected stages that work together to enable cross-category affordance generalization:

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Affordance Collection from Human Videos

The framework begins by building an "affordance memory" from egocentric human-object interaction videos, primarily sourced from the EpicKitchens-100 dataset. Affordances are represented as contact points between human hands and objects, which capture the essential interaction locations.

The system uses an off-the-shelf hand-object interaction detector to identify frames where contact occurs. Through skin segmentation, it pinpoints the exact intersection areas between hands and objects, from which multiple contact points are randomly sampled. To handle occlusion issues common in contact frames, the system employs homography matrices to map contact points back to clearer, unobstructed frames within a temporal window. A Laplacian operator helps select the optimal frame to minimize motion blur.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Similar Object Retrieval

When a robot encounters a new target object, the system crops and encodes the object image using CLIP, which has proven effective for this task. The encoded feature vector is compared against all objects in the affordance memory using cosine similarity.

The retrieval strategy adapts based on whether the target object belongs to a category already present in memory. For seen categories, the system retrieves the most similar object from that specific category. For entirely novel categories, it finds the most similar object across all categories in memory, enabling true cross-category generalization.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Semantic Correspondence Mapping

This stage represents the core innovation of Robo-ABC. The system leverages the emergent semantic correspondence capabilities of pre-trained diffusion models to map contact points from retrieved objects to target objects.

The process extracts Diffusion Features (DIFT) from both source and target images. For each contact point on the source object, the algorithm identifies the pixel in the target image whose diffusion features have the highest similarity to the source point's features. To ensure robustness against orientation differences, the system applies eight rotation and flipping transformations to the source image and selects the best-matching result.

#### 3.4. Stage 4: Real-World Deployment

The final stage integrates the inferred contact points with AnyGrasp, a robust 7-DOF grasping system. AnyGrasp generates multiple grasp candidates from the scene's point cloud, and the robot selects the candidate whose position is closest to the predicted contact points. This combination of high-level semantic understanding with low-level grasping primitives enables effective real-world deployment.

### 4. Experimental Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates Robo-ABC's effectiveness across multiple dimensions:

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Affordance Generalization

On a custom evaluation dataset covering "hold" and "open" actions with both seen and unseen object categories, Robo-ABC achieved a remarkable 60.7% success rate. This represents a substantial 31.6% improvement over LOCATE, the best-performing baseline method (29.1%). The system also showed superior performance on secondary metrics, with NSS scores of 0.516 versus LOCATE's 0.283, and DTM scores of 0.045 versus 0.107.

#### 4.2. Cross-Category Performance

The per-category analysis reveals consistent improvements across diverse object types, from bottles and drawers to more complex items like badminton rackets and wine glasses. This consistency demonstrates the framework's ability to handle varied object geometries and interaction patterns.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robot Experiments

Deployed on Franka Panda robots, Robo-ABC achieved an 85.7% success rate in cross-category object grasping tasks across seven diverse object categories. This significantly outperformed the VRB baseline's 68.6% success rate, validating the framework's practical applicability in complex real-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies identified optimal components for each stage:
- **Retriever Encoder**: CLIP-B32 proved most effective for memory retrieval
- **Semantic Correspondence**: DIFT (Diffusion Features) outperformed alternatives like SD-DINO and DINO-VIT
- **Memory Size**: Performance generally improved with larger memory sizes for capable models
- **Retrieval Strategy**: Using multiple top-k similar images enhanced performance at the cost of inference time

### 5. Technical Innovation

The primary technical innovation lies in Robo-ABC's exploitation of pre-trained diffusion models for semantic correspondence. Unlike previous approaches that required additional training or manual annotations, this method leverages the inherent semantic understanding already captured in foundation models.

The framework's ability to handle orientation differences through multi-transformation analysis represents another significant advancement. By applying eight different rotations and flips to source images, the system can successfully transfer affordances even when objects are presented in different orientations.

The integration strategy with existing grasping systems (AnyGrasp) demonstrates how high-level semantic reasoning can be effectively combined with proven low-level manipulation primitives. This modular approach allows the system to benefit from advances in both semantic understanding and physical manipulation.

### 6. Significance and Impact

Robo-ABC represents a significant advancement toward generalizable robotic manipulation. The framework's ability to achieve cross-category affordance transfer without additional training or manual annotation addresses a fundamental limitation in current robotic systems. The 31.6% improvement in success rates over existing methods, combined with strong real-world performance (85.7% success rate), demonstrates both theoretical and practical value.

The approach's reliance on foundation models positions it well for future improvements. As diffusion models and other foundation models continue to advance, Robo-ABC's performance is likely to improve correspondingly. This scalability makes it a valuable long-term solution for robotic manipulation challenges.

The work also opens new research directions in leveraging internet-scale data for robotics. By demonstrating how human interaction videos can be systematically converted into robotic knowledge, the research provides a pathway for robots to benefit from the vast repository of human experience available online.

Perhaps most importantly, Robo-ABC moves the field closer to achieving "open-world embodied intelligence" where robots can adapt to novel situations without extensive retraining. This capability is essential for deploying robots in unstructured real-world environments where they will inevitably encounter objects and scenarios not covered in their training data.

The framework's modular design and integration with existing systems like AnyGrasp also demonstrates how semantic reasoning advances can be incorporated into current robotic platforms, facilitating practical adoption and further development.
