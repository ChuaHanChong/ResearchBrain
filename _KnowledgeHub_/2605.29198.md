# Guidance Contrastive Token Credit Assignment for Discrete Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Guidance Contrastive Policy Optimization (GCPO), an algorithm that refines group-advantage-based reinforcement learning by assigning fine-grained, per-token credit using a contrastive guidance signal derived from Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG). GCPO demonstrates improved performance in both text-to-image generation and multimodal reasoning tasks, consistently outperforming baselines.

## Problem

- Existing group-advantage-based reinforcement learning (RL) methods, such as GRPO and DAPO, apply a uniform sample-level reward across all tokens within a generated sequence, neglecting their varying contributions.
- The uniform credit assignment limits the precision of learning, especially in tasks requiring fine-grained semantic understanding like chain-of-thought reasoning or text-to-image generation, where some tokens are more critical than others.
- There was a need for a mechanism to estimate and incorporate token-level importance into policy optimization objectives without adding the complexity of a separate value network.

## Method

- **GCPO** calculates a per-token contrastive guidance score using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the model's predicted token distributions conditioned on a positive prompt and a negative prompt.
- The raw KL divergence scores are transformed into stable importance weights in the range [0, 1] using a rank-based normalization method inspired by histogram equalization.
- These normalized per-token importance weights scale the per-sample advantage in the Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==) objective, ensuring that more semantically critical tokens receive stronger learning signals.

## Results

- In text-to-image generation, GCPO achieved a GenEval score of 0.89, surpassing the base model (0.80) and GRPO (0.85), showing particular gains in counting and color attribution tasks.
- For multimodal reasoning, GCPO consistently outperformed GRPO, DAPO, and VPPO across visual math (MathVerse, MathVision, MM12k) and generic visual reasoning benchmarks (LogicVista, MMMU-Pro) using Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B and Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B models.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of KL divergence as the metric, histogram equalization for normalization, and the 'generate a wrong answer' negative prompt for language generation, along with visual evidence of improved semantic alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) signals, traditionally used for inference, can be repurposed as an intrinsic, fine-grained measure of token-level importance for policy optimization during training.
> - The divergence between model predictions under positive and negative prompt conditions provides an effective proxy for identifying tokens most sensitive and relevant to the conditioning prompt.
> - A rank-based normalization approach (histogram equalization) for KL divergence scores ensures robust and consistent token credit assignment across diverse generative tasks and input scales.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an effective post-training methodology for enhancing generative models across various domains, including mathematical reasoning, code generation, and text-to-image synthesis. Among these RL approaches, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and its variants, such as Direct Advantage Policy Optimization (DAPO), have gained prominence due to their scalability, efficiency, and stability, particularly in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal contexts. A fundamental aspect of GRPO is its use of a group-normalized, per-sample reward to calculate advantages, which simplifies the training process by obviating the need for a separate value network, unlike methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that rely on Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) for token-level supervision.

However, a key limitation of GRPO and similar group-advantage-based methods is their uniform broadcasting of sample-level rewards to all tokens within a sequence. This uniform credit assignment fails to account for the heterogeneous contributions of individual tokens. For instance, in chain-of-thought reasoning, certain tokens represent critical calculations or logical steps, while others serve as mere fillers. Similarly, in text-to-image generation, specific image regions are semantically linked to the textual prompt, whereas background areas hold less relevance. This discrepancy motivates the field of "token credit assignment," which endeavors to develop mechanisms for estimating and incorporating token-level contributions into policy optimization. Prior work in this area has explored various heuristics, including token confidence, gradient information, entropy, and statistical significance tests, to derive token-level importance weights. Some approaches, such as VPPO, have extended this concept to multimodal settings by emphasizing visually grounded tokens.

Concurrently, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) has become a standard inference technique in text-to-image diffusion models, augmenting model predictions by contrasting outputs under positive (conditional) and negative (unconditional) prompts. This technique is known to improve image quality and text-image alignment. While less widely adopted in language models, CFG-style guidance has shown potential for improving reasoning in LLMs. The current work leverages the insights from CFG, particularly the observation that not all tokens are equally affected by contrastive guidance, to develop a more fine-grained credit assignment mechanism within the RL framework. It seeks to generalize this concept beyond its traditional application in image generation to multimodal language tasks, positioning itself as a general and scalable strategy for discrete policy learning that addresses the uniform credit assignment problem inherent in group-advantage-based RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel algorithm for discrete policy optimization that facilitates fine-grained, per-token credit assignment, thereby improving upon existing methods that uniformly broadcast sample-level advantages. The core motivation stems from a recognized limitation in prevalent reinforcement learning algorithms, such as GRPO and DAPO, which, despite their scalability and efficiency, assign uniform credit to all tokens within a generated sequence based solely on the overall sample reward. This uniform assignment disregards the differential importance of individual tokens, leading to a potentially suboptimal learning signal.

The authors identify two specific scenarios to highlight this deficiency:
1. **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning**: In tasks requiring complex reasoning, certain keywords or phrases within a reasoning trace are pivotal for the correctness of the final answer (e.g., mathematical calculations), while others are merely structural or filler words (e.g., punctuation, linking verbs).
2. **Text-to-Image Generation**: When generating images from text prompts, specific visual regions directly correspond to entities described in the prompt, bearing significant semantic weight, whereas other regions constitute less relevant background elements.

The central insight driving this work is derived from Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), an inference technique widely used in text-to-image models. The authors observe that, during CFG-guided inference, tokens corresponding to semantically critical regions in an image exhibit a larger divergence between predictions made under positive (textual prompt) and negative (unconditional) conditions. This divergence, or "guidance contrast," serves as an indicator of a token's importance.

Therefore, the key objectives and motivations are:
- To **address the uniform credit assignment problem** in group-advantage-based RL by enabling a more precise, token-level credit assignment.
- To **leverage the principles of classifier-free guidance** to quantify the semantic importance of individual tokens by contrasting model predictions under positive and negative conditioning.
- To **integrate this contrastive guidance signal** into the reinforcement learning objective, specifically by scaling per-sample advantages at the token level, allowing the learning process to focus on crucial tokens or regions.
- To **extend the applicability of this approach** beyond text-to-image generation to multimodal chain-of-thought reasoning tasks in vision-language models (VLMs), even in scenarios where CFG is not part of the standard inference pipeline.
- To **develop a robust normalization strategy** for the derived token importance weights to ensure stable and consistent optimization behavior across varying contexts and scales of raw divergence values.
- To **demonstrate empirically** that this novel approach, Guidance Contrastive Policy Optimization (GCPO), consistently outperforms existing baselines (GRPO, DAPO, and VPPO) across diverse benchmarks, validating its effectiveness as a general and scalable optimization strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Guidance Contrastive Policy Optimization (GCPO), modifies the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework by introducing a mechanism for per-token credit assignment based on contrastive guidance.

#### 3.1. Contrastive Guidance

The core of GCPO is the computation of a token-level importance weight, termed "contrastive guidance" ($\eta_{i,t}$). For each token $y_{i,t}$ in a sampled sequence $y_i$, given a positive prompt $x$, the model computes its probability $\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}|x, y_{i,<t})$. Simultaneously, it computes the probability under a negative prompt $x^-$, denoted $\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}|x^-, y_{i,<t})$. The contrastive guidance $\eta_{i,t}$ is then defined as the KL divergence between these two distributions:
$$ \eta_{i,t} = D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}|x, y_{i,<t})||\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}|x^-, y_{i,<t})) $$
This KL divergence quantifies the difference between the model's prediction under positive and negative conditioning, serving as an indicator of how semantically critical a token is to the positive prompt. A larger $\eta_{i,t}$ implies a greater influence of the positive prompt, suggesting higher importance for that token.

#### 3.2. Normalization

Since the raw KL divergence $\eta$ is unbounded (ranging from 0 to $\infty$) and can vary significantly across tokens, prompts, and sequences, direct application as a weight is unstable. The authors propose a rank-based normalization technique inspired by histogram equalization, rather than common methods like softmax or min-max normalization, which are sensitive to outliers. The normalized weight $\eta_{normalized}$ is computed using the cumulative distribution function $F$:
$$ \eta_{normalized} = F(\eta) = P(x; x < \eta) $$
Here, $P(x; x < \eta)$ represents the percentage of tokens within the same sequence that have a KL divergence value smaller than $\eta$. This ensures that the highest KL value in any sequence normalizes to 1, the lowest to 0, and median values to 0.5, creating a consistent distribution of normalized weights across different samples and facilitating smoother optimization.

#### 3.3. Guidance Contrastive Policy Optimization Objective

With the normalized token-level importance weights $\eta_{normalized,i,t}$, GCPO modifies the standard GRPO objective. Instead of uniformly broadcasting the per-sample advantage $\hat{A}_i$ to all tokens, GCPO scales it to obtain a per-token advantage $\hat{A}_{GCPO,i,t}$:
$$ \hat{A}_{GCPO,i,t} = \eta_{normalized,i,t} \hat{A}_i $$
The final GCPO objective function is then given by:
$$ J_{GCPO}(\theta) = E\left[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \min\left( w_{i,t}(\theta) \hat{A}_{GCPO,i,t}, \text{clip}(w_{i,t}(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) \hat{A}_{GCPO,i,t} \right) \right] $$
where $w_{i,t}(\theta)$ is the importance weight for trust-region updates, as in GRPO. This objective emphasizes learning signals in tokens deemed more important by the contrastive guidance.

#### 3.4. Extending to Language Generation

Applying GCPO to multimodal reasoning tasks with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) presents specific challenges, as CFG is not a standard inference technique for LLMs, and they do not naturally accept empty strings as negative prompts.
- **Negative Prompt Construction**: The authors experimented with several negative prompt constructions. The most effective approach for language generation was found to be augmenting the original question $x$ with a suffix instruction: "please generate a wrong answer." This effectively contrasts the probability of a token belonging to a correct answer (given $x$) with its probability of belonging to an incorrect answer (given $x$ and the negative suffix).
- **Bayesian Interpretation**: This choice of negative prompt has a Bayesian interpretation, where the ratio of probabilities $\pi_\theta(y|x) / \pi_\theta(y|x^-)$ reflects the odds of an implicit classifier determining if a token contributes to a correct versus an incorrect answer.
- **Comparison with VPPO**: The paper contrasts GCPO with VPPO, a related work focusing on multimodal reasoning. VPPO constructs negative prompts by masking parts of input images and uses a hard filter (top 40% visually dependent tokens) for credit assignment. GCPO argues for its broader applicability by capturing both visual and general reasoning through its negative prompt design and employing continuous rank-based weighting, which is less restrictive than VPPO's hard filtering.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

Extensive ablation studies were conducted to validate design choices:
- **Divergence Metric**: KL divergence was compared against Information Gain and Absolute Difference, with KL divergence demonstrating superior empirical performance.
- **Normalization Method**: Histogram equalization was compared against softmax and min-max normalization, confirming that histogram equalization provided the best results by ensuring a smooth and consistent distribution of weights.
- **Choice of Negative Prompts (for language)**: Various negative prompt constructions were evaluated for multimodal reasoning tasks. The strategy of using a negative suffix ("generate the wrong answer") consistently outperformed other options, including those from prior work like VPPO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GCPO was evaluated through extensive experiments across text-to-image generation and multimodal reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating its ability to enhance model performance by enabling fine-grained token credit assignment.

#### 4.1. Text-to-Image Generation

Experiments were conducted using an autoregressive image generation model, Janus-Pro-7B, and evaluated on the GenEval benchmark, which provides verifiable rewards for text-to-image alignment.
- **Overall Performance**: GCPO achieved an overall GenEval score of 0.89, representing a substantial improvement of 0.09 points over the base Janus-Pro-7B model (0.80). This also surpassed the GRPO baseline (0.85) and the previously reported RL method Janus-Pro-R1 (0.86) on the same base model.
- **Competitive Standing**: GCPO's performance (0.89) was comparable to or even slightly exceeded that of significantly larger state-of-the-art models such as Qwen-Image-2507 (20B, 0.87) and BAGEL (14B, 0.88), suggesting its efficiency in leveraging parameters.
- **Subcategory Improvements**: Notable performance gains were observed in specific GenEval subcategories, particularly "counting" (improved from 0.56 to 0.84) and "color attribution" (improved from 0.66 to 0.83). These improvements align with the hypothesis that GCPO effectively focuses learning signals on key image regions critical for understanding quantitative and attributive aspects of prompts.
- **Qualitative Results**: Visual examples provided in the paper illustrate that images generated by GCPO-tuned models more accurately represent the semantic content and specific attributes described in user prompts compared to those from base models or GRPO-tuned models.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Reasoning

GCPO was extended to language generation tasks within multimodal reasoning, using Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B and Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B as base models, and trained on the ViRL39K dataset. Evaluation was performed on visual math reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MM12k) and generic visual reasoning tasks (LogicVista, MMMU-Pro).
- **Consistent Outperformance**: GCPO consistently outperformed GRPO, DAPO, and VPPO baselines across all evaluated benchmarks and both base VLM architectures.
 - For Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B, GCPO achieved scores such as 71.7 (MathVerse), 83.1 (MM12k), and 39.3 (MMMU-Pro), surpassing DAPO and VPPO in all metrics.
 - Similarly, for Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B, GCPO obtained 84.1 (MathVerse), 81.3 (MM12k), and 55.3 (MMMU-Pro), again showing superior results over DAPO and VPPO.
- **Enhanced General Reasoning**: The improvements observed were particularly pronounced in generic visual reasoning tasks (LogicVista and MMMU-Pro) compared to VPPO. This indicates that GCPO's continuous weighting, based on contrastive guidance and its specific negative prompt design, is more effective in capturing logical reasoning and world knowledge aspects alongside visual dependencies, which is a limitation of VPPO's visually-dependent token filtering.
- **Training Stability**: Analysis of training dynamics showed that GCPO consistently maintained higher validation accuracy on MM12k compared to DAPO and GRPO throughout the training process, with the performance gap widening over time.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments validated the specific design choices of GCPO:
- **Divergence Metric**: KL divergence was empirically determined to be the most effective metric for contrastive guidance, yielding the highest scores on GenEval (0.89) and MM12k (83.1) compared to Information Gain (0.88, 82.9) and Absolute Difference (0.84, 75.5).
- **Normalization Method**: Histogram equalization, the proposed rank-based normalization technique, proved superior, achieving 0.89 on GenEval and 83.1 on MM12k. This contrasted with softmax (0.86, 81.0) and min-max normalization (0.85, 62.5), demonstrating its robustness in handling varying scales of KL divergence.
- **Negative Prompts for Language**: The strategy of augmenting the original prompt with "generate the wrong answer" was found to be the most effective negative prompt construction for multimodal reasoning, leading to the highest MM12k score (83.1) among all tested options, including alternative constructions and those adopted by VPPO.

#### 4.4. Visualizations

Visualizations of guidance heatmaps confirmed that GCPO accurately identifies and emphasizes semantically relevant regions in generated images (e.g., objects described in prompts) and critical keywords within reasoning traces, aligning with the core objective of fine-grained credit assignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Guidance Contrastive Policy Optimization (GCPO), which represents an advancement in discrete policy optimization for generative models. Its primary significance lies in addressing a fundamental limitation of existing group-advantage-based reinforcement learning (RL) methods, such as GRPO and DAPO, by moving beyond uniform sample-level reward broadcasting to enable fine-grained, per-token credit assignment.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Enhanced Learning Signals**: By contrasting model predictions under positive and negative prompts, GCPO generates token-level advantages proportional to semantic importance. This allows the RL process to focus computational resources and learning on critical tokens, leading to more precise and informative optimization signals.
- **Improved Generative Model Performance**: The consistent outperformance of GCPO against strong baselines (GRPO, DAPO, and VPPO) across diverse benchmarks, including text-to-image generation (GenEval) and complex multimodal reasoning (MathVerse, MathVision, MM12k, LogicVista, MMMU-Pro), indicates its effectiveness. The ability to match or exceed larger state-of-the-art models with a smaller base model highlights its parameter efficiency and optimization prowess.
- **Generalizability of Contrastive Guidance**: The work demonstrates that the concept of classifier-free guidance, originally prominent in text-to-image synthesis, can be successfully adapted and generalized to other autoregressive generation tasks, including language-only or multimodal language reasoning, even when CFG is not explicitly used during inference. This expands the practical utility of contrastive principles in RL.
- **Robust Normalization Strategy**: The introduction of rank-based normalization (histogram equalization) for KL divergence is a pragmatic methodological contribution. It addresses the challenge of dynamic scales in token importance weights, making GCPO more stable and less reliant on extensive hyperparameter tuning across different tasks and data distributions.
- **Broader Applicability**: GCPO offers a generalizable framework for token credit assignment in any prompt-dependent RL setting where a meaningful contrast between positive and negative conditioning can be established. This paves the way for future research to explore its application in other domains of discrete policy optimization.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advanced Generative Capabilities**: GCPO can lead to generative models with higher fidelity, better alignment with instructions, and improved reasoning capabilities, particularly in complex multimodal scenarios. This could accelerate progress in AI applications requiring precise generation and nuanced understanding.
- **Efficient Model Training**: By focusing learning on critical tokens, GCPO may contribute to more efficient training of large generative models, potentially reducing the computational resources and time required to achieve high performance.
- **New Research Directions**: The findings encourage further exploration into sophisticated token credit assignment mechanisms and the adaptation of inference-time guidance techniques for policy optimization across various model architectures and tasks. The identified limitations regarding very small models' comprehension of negative prompts also suggest a future direction for incorporating external teacher models.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations:**
The authors acknowledge two main limitations: GCPO is restricted to reinforcement learning tasks that are strictly prompt-dependent, and its effectiveness in multimodal understanding tasks assumes a base model possesses initial capabilities to differentiate correct and incorrect answers. Smaller models might struggle to adequately respond to negative prompts. Furthermore, the authors include a broader impact statement, noting that finetuned models, if used improperly, could be trained for malicious actions or inherit biases and hallucinations from their base models, recommending against non-research use.
