# Token Assorted: Mixing Latent and Text Tokens for Improved Language Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta AI and collaborators developed Token Assorted, a method that combines discrete latent tokens with text tokens for Large Language Model reasoning. This approach enhances reasoning accuracy on synthetic and mathematical benchmarks while reducing reasoning trace length by an average of 17%.

## Problem

- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs significantly improves accuracy but generates lengthy textual traces, leading to high computational costs during training and inference.
- Much of the textual content in CoT traces serves for linguistic coherence rather than conveying core reasoning information, resulting in inefficiency.
- Existing efforts to compress or internalize reasoning traces often involve complex multi-stage training, incur high computational costs, or lead to performance degradation.

## Method

- A two-stage approach uses a ==Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)== to abstract initial Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps into ==discrete latent tokens==, with a compression rate of **16:1**.
- Pre-trained LLMs are fine-tuned using a ==hybrid representation== where the beginning of CoT traces is replaced with these discrete latent tokens, while the rest remains explicit text.
- A ==randomized, left-to-right replacement strategy== during fine-tuning allows the LLM to quickly adapt to the new latent tokens by stochastically mixing varying degrees of abstraction in training samples, avoiding complex curriculum learning.

## Results

- The method consistently outperformed baselines (Sol-Only, CoT, iCoT, Pause Token) across synthetic and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, achieving an average accuracy improvement of **2.9 to 4.5** percentage points on Llama models (1B, 3B, 8B).
- It reduced reasoning trace length by an average of **17%** compared to the full CoT baseline across all mathematical benchmarks, while simultaneously achieving higher accuracy.
- On synthetic benchmarks, the approach yielded substantial accuracy gains, for instance, **+19.8%** on Keys-Finding Maze and **+18.7%** on ProsQA, with significantly shorter reasoning traces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mixing abstract discrete latent tokens with explicit text tokens creates a 'bridge' that allows LLMs to leverage both high-level summaries and detailed textual information for more effective reasoning.
> - The randomized partial replacement strategy during fine-tuning facilitates robust and efficient adaptation to an extended vocabulary including novel latent tokens in a single training stage.
> - Compressing verbose textual CoT allows the model to focus attention more effectively on crucial tokens (e.g., numerical values, mathematical operations), leading to improved internal reasoning efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated their remarkable capabilities in various tasks, with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting emerging as a cornerstone for enhancing complex reasoning and planning. By explicitly outlining step-by-step thought processes in text, CoT enables LLMs to achieve higher accuracy across diverse domains, from mathematical problem-solving to logical inference. However, this effectiveness comes with a significant trade-off: the generation and processing of lengthy CoT reasoning traces consume substantial computational resources. Much of the text in these traces often serves for linguistic coherence rather than conveying core reasoning information, leading to inefficiency.

This paper addresses this crucial challenge by proposing a novel hybrid approach that seeks to optimize both the efficiency and accuracy of LLM reasoning. It positions itself against several existing lines of work:

- **Explicit CoT Prompting and Training with CoT Data:** The paper acknowledges the success of explicit CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022b; Kojima et al., 2022) and the even greater performance gains achieved when models are *trained* on high-quality CoT data (Nye et al., 2021b; Lehnert et al., 2024). The current work aims to retain these benefits while mitigating the associated computational costs.
- **Efforts to Mitigate CoT Length:** Previous research has explored ways to compress or internalize reasoning traces. Hao et al. (2024) investigated reasoning in a *continuous* latent space, while Deng et al. (2024) explored "implicit CoT" through iterative elimination of intermediate steps, resembling curriculum learning. Goyal et al. (2023) introduced "pause tokens" to delay generation and allow for additional computation. Pfau et al. (2024) explored "filler tokens" for computational tasks. The authors argue that these existing approaches often involve complex multi-stage training procedures, incur significant computational costs, or have not yet demonstrated strong performance when integrated into modern LLMs on real-world datasets.
- **Alternative Latent Space Approaches:** While some works (Pagnoni et al., 2024; Barrault et al., 2024) leverage continuous latent spaces for tokenization-free architectures or sentence embedding, this paper specifically focuses on the direct use of *discrete* latent tokens for abstracting reasoning steps.

The "Token Assorted" approach distinguishes itself by:
1. **Discrete Latent Abstraction:** Utilizing discrete latent tokens generated by a Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) to abstract initial reasoning steps, offering a compressed representation. This contrasts with continuous latent space methods.
2. **Hybrid Representation:** Mixing these latent tokens with conventional text tokens. This partial abstraction acts as a "bridge" between the abstract and detailed representations, allowing LLMs to leverage both high-level summaries and explicit textual details.
3. **Simplified Training Procedure:** Introducing a randomized replacement strategy during fine-tuning that mixes latent and text tokens. This single-stage, efficient adaptation mechanism contrasts with the multi-stage curriculum learning approaches often employed by other methods to handle new tokens or compressed representations.

By proposing a method that consistently outperforms baselines in both accuracy and token efficiency across diverse benchmarks, this work contributes significantly to the ongoing quest for more efficient, yet powerful, LLMs capable of sophisticated reasoning, moving closer to the goal of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Token Assorted" stems from a critical bottleneck in the advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks: the computational inefficiency introduced by lengthy Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces. While CoT has demonstrably improved LLM reasoning and planning capabilities, the explicit, step-by-step textual outputs generate very long input sequences. A significant portion of these textual tokens, while essential for human readability and linguistic coherence, often do not contribute directly to the core informational content of the reasoning process. Processing these verbose inputs demands substantial computational resources during both training and inference.

The key objectives of this research are to:

1. **Reduce Reasoning Trace Length:** Develop a method to significantly decrease the length of reasoning traces without compromising, and ideally improving, reasoning accuracy. This directly addresses the computational burden.
2. **Propose a Hybrid Reasoning Representation:** Introduce a novel way to represent the reasoning process by combining abstract, discrete latent tokens with explicit text tokens. This hybrid approach aims to capture the essence of early reasoning steps in a compressed form while retaining the detail of later, critical steps.
3. **Enable Efficient Adaptation to New Tokens:** Design a training procedure that allows LLMs to effectively and quickly adapt to an extended vocabulary that includes unseen latent tokens, avoiding complex and computationally expensive multi-stage curriculum learning strategies.
4. **Enhance LLM Reasoning Performance:** Ultimately, improve the overall reasoning capabilities of LLMs on a variety of tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing CoT-based methods and other compression techniques.

In essence, the authors aim to strike an optimal balance between the expressivity of explicit reasoning and the efficiency of compact representations, thereby making sophisticated LLM reasoning more practical and scalable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Token Assorted" methodology consists of a two-stage training procedure designed to enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to reason with a hybrid representation of latent and text tokens.

**Stage 1: Learning Latent Discrete Tokens**

The first stage focuses on abstracting the reasoning steps (Chain-of-Thought, C) of an input sample (X = P ⊕ C ⊕ S, where P is prompt, S is solution) into a sequence of discrete latent tokens (Z). For this, the authors employ a **Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)** architecture (Van Den Oord et al., 2017).

- **VQ-VAE Components:**
 - **Encoder (f_enc):** A causal Transformer that encodes a sequence of `L` text tokens (a chunk of the input `X`) into `L/r` continuous latent embedding vectors (`X_bar`), where `r` is a preset compression rate.
 - **Codebook (E):** A finite set of learnable `d`-dimensional vectors.
 - **Quantization Operator (q):** Replaces each continuous latent embedding vector (`x_bar`) from the encoder with its nearest neighbor vector in the codebook `E`. The index of this nearest neighbor in the codebook effectively becomes the discrete latent token.
 - **Decoder (f_dec):** Another causal Transformer that decodes the quantized latent embeddings back into the original text tokens, conditioned on the prompt embedding.
 - **Prompt Embedding (g):** Maps the prompt tokens `P` to a `d`-dimensional embedding.
- **Training Objective:** The VQ-VAE is trained with a three-part loss function:
 - **Reconstruction Loss:** `log p(X|f_dec(q(X_bar)|g(P)))` – ensures the decoded text accurately reconstructs the original input.
 - **VQ Loss:** `∥sg[X_bar] − q(X_bar)∥^2_2` – pulls the codebook vectors towards the encoder outputs. (`sg` is stop-gradient).
 - **Commitment Loss:** `β∥X_bar − sg[q(X_bar)]∥^2_2` – ensures the encoder outputs commit to a codebook vector.
- **Application:** The VQ-VAE is trained on the entire input sequence `X` but is *only applied to the CoT tokens C* in the subsequent stage to generate discrete latent tokens `Z`. The compression rate `r` (e.g., 16:1) dictates how many text tokens are condensed into a single latent token. The VQ-VAE is a relatively small model (50M parameters) and is used only once for data preparation, not during LLM training or inference.

**Stage 2: Reasoning with Discrete Latent Tokens**

In this stage, the pre-trained LLM is fine-tuned on modified samples (`X_hat`) that incorporate the latent abstractions.

- **Partial and Left-to-Right (AR) Replacement:** Instead of compressing the entire CoT trace, only the first `m` tokens of the textual CoT (`C`) are replaced by their corresponding latent abstractions (`z_1..., z_{m/r}`). The remaining textual CoT tokens (`c_{m+1}..., c_{t_c}`) are left unchanged. This "partial replacement" is a crucial design choice, as it provides a bridge between the abstract latent space and the familiar text space, aiding the LLM's adaptation. The replacement is always done from left-to-right (autoregressive).
- **Special Delimiters:** The sequence of latent tokens is encapsulated by special ` <boLatent> ` (begin-of-latent) and ` <eoLatent> ` (end-of-latent) tokens. This signals to the LLM the presence and boundaries of the abstract representation.
- **Randomized Latent Code Replacement (Key Innovation):** To facilitate fast adaptation to the new, unseen latent tokens and avoid complex curriculum learning, the authors introduce a randomized strategy. For each training sample, the number of CoT tokens to be replaced (`m`) is randomly varied. Specifically, a maximum `m_max` is sampled from a predefined set of multipliers of the chunk size `L` (e.g., `{0, 72..., 256}`), and then `m` is uniformly sampled from `[0, L, 2L..., m_max]`. This stochastic mixing of samples with varying degrees of latent abstraction allows the LLM to learn robustly from both fully textual and partially abstracted reasoning traces in a single training stage.
- **LLM Fine-tuning:** The LLM is then fine-tuned on these `X_hat` samples using a standard next-token prediction objective. During inference, the LLM directly generates these mixed tokens, including latent ones, without needing the VQ-VAE at runtime.

This two-stage approach, particularly the combination of partial, left-to-right replacement and the randomized mixing strategy, is central to the method's effectiveness in balancing efficiency and performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The "Token Assorted" approach underwent extensive evaluation across both synthetic and real-world reasoning benchmarks, consistently demonstrating significant improvements in both accuracy and token efficiency compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Performance on Synthetic Benchmarks

- **Keys-Finding Maze:** Our latent approach achieved 62.8% (1-Feasible-10 metric), an absolute improvement of +19.8% over the CoT baseline, while using significantly fewer tokens (374.6 vs 1312.0).
- **ProntoQA (Logical Reasoning):** Achieved 100% accuracy, surpassing the CoT baseline's 98.8% and doing so with much shorter reasoning traces (7.7 tokens vs 92.5).
- **ProsQA (More Difficult Logical Reasoning):** Demonstrated a substantial gain, reaching 96.2% accuracy, an improvement of +18.7% over the CoT baseline (77.5%), with only 10.9 tokens compared to 49.4 for CoT.

These results indicate that even for tasks where models are trained from scratch (T5/GPT-2), the hybrid representation with discrete latent tokens is highly effective.

#### 4.2. Performance on Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks

The method was fine-tuned on Llama models (1B, 3B, 8B) using MetaMathQA or Dart-MATH datasets and evaluated on various in-domain (Math, GSM8K) and out-of-domain (Fresh-Gaokao-Math-2023, DeepMind-Math, College-Math, OlympiaBench-Math, TheoremQA) datasets.

- **Consistent Accuracy Gains:** The latent approach consistently outperformed all baselines (Sol-Only, CoT, iCoT, Pause Token) across nearly all tasks and model sizes.
 - **Llama-3.2-1B:** Average accuracy of 17.8%, a +3.7 point improvement over the best baseline (CoT at 14.1%). Notable gains include +4.2% on Math and +6% on GSM8K.
 - **Llama-3.2-3B:** Average accuracy of 28.1%, a +2.9 point improvement over the best baseline (CoT at 25.2%). Significant improvements include +4.2% on Math, +4.1% on GSM8K, and +6.6% on Fresh-Gaokao-Math-2023.
 - **Llama-3.1-8B:** Average accuracy of 37.9%, a +4.5 point improvement over the best baseline (CoT at 33.4%). Particularly strong gains were observed on Fresh-Gaokao-Math-2023 (+13.3%), College-Math (+2.1%), and Olympia-Math (+2.6%).
- **Token Efficiency:**
 - The latent approach achieved an average reduction of **17% in reasoning trace length** compared to the full CoT baseline across all mathematical benchmarks and model sizes, while *simultaneously* achieving higher accuracy.
 - While methods like iCoT also generated shorter responses (e.g., 42.8% reduction compared to CoT), they came at a cost of significantly degraded model performance, highlighting the effectiveness of "Token Assorted" in balancing efficiency and accuracy.

#### 4.3. Ablation and Understanding Studies

- **Replacement Strategies:** The proposed AR-Replace (partial, left-to-right, randomized replacement) strategy significantly outperformed alternative replacement schemes like:
 - `All-Replace` (replacing all CoT with latent tokens): Model struggled to align, showing much lower accuracy.
 - `Curriculum-Replace` (gradually replacing tokens over epochs): Performed better than `All-Replace` but still notably worse than AR-Replace.
 - `Poisson-Replace` (randomly replacing chunks): Showed mixed results, sometimes comparable but often worse than AR-Replace, suggesting that a fixed pattern of left-to-right replacement is easier for the model to learn. This validates the importance of the "bridge" between text and latent space and the controlled introduction of abstraction.
- **Attention Weights Analysis:** The latent models developed a stronger attention to numerical values and words representing mathematical operations within the prompt. This suggests that by compressing verbose textual CoT, the model can effectively focus its attention on crucial tokens that drive the logical flow, leading to more efficient internal reasoning.
- **Compression Rate (r):** An ablation on the compression rate `r` (how many text tokens become one latent token) revealed a "sweet spot" around `r=16`. While all `r` settings (2, 16, 32) outperformed the CoT baseline in both efficiency and accuracy, `r=16` offered the best balance, indicating an optimal trade-off between compact representation and the preservation of task-critical information. Higher `r` led to fewer tokens but could lead to information loss, while lower `r` was less efficient.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the efficacy of mixing latent and text tokens with a randomized partial replacement strategy. The approach not only reduces the computational footprint of LLM reasoning but also enhances its accuracy, particularly for complex mathematical and logical tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Token Assorted" research presents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models, particularly for their application in complex reasoning tasks. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that a hybrid representation, effectively mixing abstract discrete latent tokens with explicit text tokens, can concurrently enhance reasoning capabilities and improve computational efficiency.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Breaking the Efficiency-Accuracy Trade-off:** Traditionally, efforts to compress reasoning traces (e.g., iCoT) often come at the cost of reduced performance. This work successfully breaks this trade-off, showing that shorter, hybrid reasoning traces can lead to *higher* accuracy. The average 17% reduction in trace length with simultaneous accuracy gains is a substantial practical benefit.
2. **Novel Hybrid Reasoning Paradigm:** The introduction of discrete latent tokens generated by VQ-VAE for *partial abstraction* of reasoning steps is a novel approach. Unlike purely continuous latent space methods or full textual compression, the "token assorted" strategy leverages the strengths of both representations, creating a more robust and effective learning environment for LLMs.
3. **Efficient Adaptation Mechanism:** The randomized replacement strategy for mixing latent and text tokens is a simple yet powerful innovation. It enables fast adaptation to an extended vocabulary without recourse to complex, multi-stage curriculum learning, making the fine-tuning process more straightforward and computationally less demanding.
4. **Enhanced Focus on Core Reasoning:** The attention weight analysis provides an intriguing insight: by abstracting verbose textual details, the model is guided to focus more intently on semantically crucial elements (numbers, mathematical operations). This suggests that the latent tokens effectively distill the "signal" from the "noise" in reasoning traces, leading to more efficient cognitive processing within the LLM.
5. **Broad Applicability:** The consistent superior performance across diverse benchmarks—from synthetic maze planning and logical inference to complex mathematical reasoning problems (both in-domain and challenging out-of-domain sets)—underscores the generalizability of the method.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Reduced Inference Costs:** Shorter reasoning traces directly translate to lower token generation costs and faster inference times for LLMs, making complex reasoning applications more economically viable and responsive. This is particularly important for API-based LLM services.
- **More Capable and Efficient LLMs:** By improving both accuracy and efficiency, this method contributes to the development of more powerful and practical LLMs that can tackle increasingly difficult reasoning challenges in fields like science, engineering, and finance.
- **Advancing AGI Research:** The paper touches upon the critical role of reasoning capabilities for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). By optimizing how LLMs perform and represent reasoning, this work takes a step towards more "intelligent" and resource-aware AI systems.
- **New Directions in LLM Architecture and Training:** The success of the hybrid token approach and the randomized training strategy could inspire further research into novel tokenization schemes, multi-modal reasoning representations (beyond just text and latent), and adaptive training methodologies for LLMs.
- **Interpretability and Safety:** The authors address potential safety concerns regarding opaque latent tokens by highlighting the VQ-VAE decoder's ability to translate these tokens back into human-readable format. This ensures that the reasoning process, even when partially abstracted, remains interpretable, mitigating risks and building trust in advanced AI systems.

In conclusion, "Token Assorted" offers a compelling solution to a critical bottleneck in LLM development. By intelligently blending abstract and explicit representations, it paves the way for a new generation of LLMs that are not only more capable in reasoning but also significantly more efficient to deploy and operate.
