# MMRL++: Parameter-Efficient and Interaction-Aware Representation Learning for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MMRL++ is a parameter-efficient method for adapting Vision-Language Models to downstream tasks, achieving state-of-the-art base-to-novel generalization with 85.53% base and 78.32% novel accuracy while reducing parameters by over 80% compared to its predecessor. It achieves this by fostering balanced multimodal interactions through a shared representation space and a decoupled inference strategy.

## Problem

- Adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to downstream tasks, especially in few-shot settings, is challenging.
- Existing adaptation methods often suffer from shallow-layer prompt concatenation, which can modify general concepts and hinder generalization.
- Many VLM adaptation approaches are text-centric, leading to imbalanced multimodal interactions, limited visual adaptation, and inefficient parameter usage.

## Method

- Introduces a ==shared, modality-agnostic representation space== with learnable tokens integrated into higher layers of both image and text encoders.
- Employs a ==Shared-Residual Representation Aligner (SRRA)== to significantly reduce trainable parameters via low-rank residual components and enhance cross-layer gradient interaction.
- Utilizes ==Progressive Representation Composition (PRC)== to enable deep representation tokens to assimilate instance-level information across different layers.
- Applies a ==decoupled inference strategy==, combining class and representation features for base classes while relying solely on class features for novel tasks.

## Results

- Achieves **85.53%** accuracy on base classes and **78.32%** on novel classes for base-to-novel generalization, outperforming MaPLe by over **3%** in both categories.
- Reduces the number of trainable parameters by over **80%** (from **4.992M** to **0.813M**) compared to its predecessor, MMRL, while simultaneously improving performance.
- Consistently outperforms other methods in few-shot learning across **11** datasets and demonstrates improved cross-dataset generalization, with feature visualizations showing more discriminative class boundaries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A shared, modality-agnostic representation space facilitates balanced cross-modal interactions, improving Vision-Language Model adaptation.
> - Integrating learnable tokens into higher encoder layers allows preservation of general pre-trained knowledge while enabling specific adaptation.
> - A decoupled inference strategy is crucial for balancing task-specific adaptation for base classes with robust generalization for novel classes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as powerful tools for understanding the relationships between visual and textual information. Models like CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) have demonstrated impressive zero-shot capabilities by learning joint representations of images and text through contrastive learning on vast datasets. However, adapting these pre-trained VLMs to specific downstream tasks, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios, remains challenging.

MMRL++ (Multi-Modal Representation Learning++) addresses this challenge by introducing a parameter-efficient approach for adapting VLMs to downstream tasks while maintaining strong generalization capabilities. The method focuses on creating a balanced interaction between visual and textual modalities through a shared representation space, allowing for effective knowledge transfer while preserving the pre-trained model's zero-shot capabilities.

### 2. Limitations of Existing Approaches

Current methods for adapting VLMs to downstream tasks face several limitations:

1. **Shallow-layer Prompt Concatenation**: Methods like MaPLe inject prompts into lower layers of VLMs, potentially modifying general concepts and hindering generalization.

2. **Text-centric Architectures**: Many approaches prioritize the text modality, leading to imbalanced multimodal interactions and limited visual adaptation.

3. **Class Token Overfitting**: Focusing solely on optimizing class token features during training can compromise generalization to novel classes.

4. **Parameter Inefficiency**: Some adaptation methods require a substantial number of trainable parameters, increasing computational demands and risking overfitting in few-shot scenarios.

5. **Limited Cross-Modal Interaction**: Many approaches fail to effectively leverage the interaction between modalities, reducing their ability to capture nuanced relationships.

### 3. MMRL Framework

The core of the MMRL approach is a shared, modality-agnostic representation space that bridges the visual and textual encoders. This space enables more balanced cross-modal interactions compared to existing methods.

Key components of the MMRL framework include:

1. **Learnable Representation Tokens**: A set of learnable space tokens that are projected into both the image and text encoders, serving as representation tokens.

2. **Higher-Layer Integration**: Representation tokens are integrated into higher layers of the encoders, preserving general knowledge in the lower layers.

3. **Joint Optimization**: Both class and representation features are optimized during training, allowing for a more comprehensive adaptation.

4. **Frozen Class Token Projection**: The projection layer for class tokens remains frozen to retain pre-trained knowledge.

The mathematical formulation involves:

For an image $x$ and text prompt $t$, the representation tokens $\mathcal{R}$ are injected into both encoders:

$$
f_i^l = \text{Encoder}_i^l([f_i^{l-1}; \mathcal{R}_i^{l-1}])
$$

$$
f_t^l = \text{Encoder}_t^l([f_t^{l-1}; \mathcal{R}_t^{l-1}])
$$

where $f_i^l$ and $f_t^l$ are the features at layer $l$ for the image and text encoders, respectively.

### 4. MMRL++ Enhancements

MMRL++ enhances the original MMRL framework with two key components:

1. **Shared-Residual Representation Aligner (SRRA)**: This component decomposes representation aligners into a shared aligner with low-rank residual aligners across different layers. The approach significantly reduces trainable parameters while enhancing gradient interactions across layers.

The SRRA architecture can be expressed as:

$$
\mathcal{F}^l(\mathcal{R}^l) = \mathcal{F}_{\text{shared}}(\mathcal{R}^l) + \mathcal{F}_{\text{residual}}^l(\mathcal{R}^l)
$$

where $\mathcal{F}_{\text{shared}}$ is the shared aligner and $\mathcal{F}_{\text{residual}}^l$ is the layer-specific residual aligner.

2. **Progressive Representation Composition (PRC)**: This mechanism enables deep representation tokens to assimilate instance-level information across different layers, leading to more effective adaptation.

The PRC process can be described as:

$$
\mathcal{R}^l = \mathcal{R}^{l-1} + \Delta\mathcal{R}^l
$$

where $\Delta\mathcal{R}^l$ represents the incremental information gained at layer $l$.

### 5. Architecture Overview

MMRL++ preserves the pre-trained VLM's parameters while introducing a small set of trainable parameters. The model uses a sophisticated loss function that combines:

1. A cosine similarity loss between image and text features:
 $$\mathcal{L}_{ce} = -\text{sim}(f_i, f_t)$$

2. A regularization term that aligns class and text features with the frozen VLM's zero-shot features:
 $$\mathcal{L}_{cos} = -\text{sim}(f, f_{\text{frozen}})$$

The total loss is a weighted combination:
 $$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{ce} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{cos}$$

### 6. Inference Strategy

A key innovation in MMRL++ is its decoupled inference strategy:

1. **Base Class Inference**: For base classes, both class and representation features are used, combining task-specific knowledge with general pre-trained knowledge:
 $$p(y|x) = \alpha \cdot p(y|f_c) + (1-\alpha) \cdot p(y|f_r)$$

2. **Novel Class Inference**: For novel classes, only class features are utilized:
 $$p(y|x) = p(y|f_c)$$

This dual strategy helps balance adaptation and generalization, addressing the key challenge in few-shot learning.

### 7. Experimental Results

MMRL++ demonstrates outstanding performance across a range of tasks and datasets:

Key results include:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance**: MMRL++ consistently outperforms existing methods on base-to-novel generalization, achieving 85.53% accuracy on base classes and 78.32% on novel classes, compared to 82.28% and 75.14% for MaPLe.

2. **Parameter Efficiency**: MMRL++ reduces the number of trainable parameters by over 80% compared to MMRL (0.813M vs. 4.992M), while improving performance.

3. **Few-Shot Learning**: In few-shot scenarios (1-16 shots), MMRL++ consistently outperforms other methods across 11 datasets, demonstrating robust generalization capabilities.

4. **Cross-Dataset Generalization**: When trained on ImageNet and evaluated on other datasets, MMRL++ maintains strong performance, indicating good domain generalization capabilities.

### 8. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirm the importance of various components of the MMRL++ framework:

1. **Representation Token Layer**: Placing representation tokens in higher layers (6-12) yields better performance than lower layers, confirming the importance of preserving general knowledge in lower layers.

2. **Representation Space Dimension**: A representation space dimension of 512 provides the best balance between expressiveness and overfitting.

3. **Number of Representation Tokens**: 5 representation tokens achieve optimal performance, with diminishing returns for larger numbers.

4. **SRRA and PRC Contributions**: Both components contribute to the performance improvement, with SRRA primarily improving parameter efficiency and PRC enhancing feature quality.

### 9. Visualization of Feature Spaces

Feature visualizations provide insights into how MMRL++ improves representation quality:

The visualizations demonstrate that MMRL++ produces more discriminative features with clearer class boundaries compared to zero-shot CLIP and MMRL, explaining its superior classification performance.

### 10. Conclusion

MMRL++ introduces a parameter-efficient and interaction-aware approach for adapting Vision-Language Models to downstream tasks. The method addresses key limitations of existing approaches through:

1. A shared representation space that facilitates balanced cross-modal interactions
2. The SRRA mechanism that significantly reduces parameters while maintaining performance
3. The PRC component that enhances feature quality across layers
4. A decoupled inference strategy that balances task-specific adaptation with generalization

MMRL++ achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets and few-shot learning scenarios while using significantly fewer parameters than its predecessor, MMRL. The approach demonstrates the importance of balanced multimodal interactions and parameter-efficient design in VLM adaptation, providing a strong foundation for future research in efficient multimodal learning.
