# Vision Language Models: A Survey of 26K Papers

> [!summary] Summary
> A quantitative analysis of 26,104 papers from top-tier AI conferences (CVPR, ICLR, NeurIPS 2023-2025) reveals a substantial shift towards multimodal Vision-Language Models, which increased from 16% to 40% of papers, alongside a steady expansion of diffusion models and a focus on instruction-following capabilities.

## Problem

- The overwhelming volume of research publications in rapidly evolving AI fields makes it challenging to quantitatively identify genuine trends and allocate resources effectively.
- Reliance on anecdotal evidence and qualitative observations for understanding research shifts lacks the rigor and reproducibility needed for strategic decision-making.
- A transparent, data-driven approach is needed to map the trajectory of computer vision and machine learning research, rather than subjective interpretations.

## Method

- A ==lexicon-driven quantitative survey== analyzes titles and abstracts of **26,104** papers from CVPR, ICLR, and NeurIPS (2023-2025), plus **8,424** from 2022 for longitudinal trends.
- The methodology involves text processing, categorizing papers using ==35 regular-expression-based topic labels==, and calculating prevalence and ==TF-IDF mass== for trend analysis.
- Fine-grained mining is applied to Vision-Language Models to detail specific shifts in architectures, tasks, training paradigms, loss functions, dataset usage, and co-mentioned modalities.

## Results

- Vision-Language Model (VLM) papers showed a sharp rise, increasing from **16%** of publications in 2023 to **40%** in 2025 across the analyzed conferences.
- Diffusion models continued their steady expansion, accounting for **8%** of papers in 2023 and growing to **19.2%** in 2025, with research consolidating around controllability, distillation, and speed.
- Instruction tuning saw a substantial increase of **3.9 percentage points**, becoming a primary paradigm for developing conversational VLMs, while the use of purely contrastive objectives in new VLM works markedly declined.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are becoming a central organizing paradigm, increasingly reframing classic perception tasks as instruction-following and multi-step reasoning problems.
> - Research is consolidating around large-scale pre-trained models, with parameter-efficient adaptation methods like prompt/prefix tuning and LoRA dominating VLM development.
> - Traditional learning paradigms (e.g., self-supervised learning, meta-learning) are increasingly integrated as modules within larger systems rather than being standalone contributions, while engineering concerns like efficiency and robustness gain prominence.
