# Vision Language Models: A Survey of 26K Papers

> [!summary] Summary
> A quantitative analysis of 26,104 papers from top-tier AI conferences (CVPR, ICLR, NeurIPS 2023-2025) reveals a substantial shift towards multimodal Vision-Language Models, which increased from 16% to 40% of papers, alongside a steady expansion of diffusion models and a focus on instruction-following capabilities.

## Problem

- The overwhelming volume of research publications in rapidly evolving AI fields makes it challenging to quantitatively identify genuine trends and allocate resources effectively.
- Reliance on anecdotal evidence and qualitative observations for understanding research shifts lacks the rigor and reproducibility needed for strategic decision-making.
- A transparent, data-driven approach is needed to map the trajectory of computer vision and machine learning research, rather than subjective interpretations.

## Method

- A ==lexicon-driven quantitative survey== analyzes titles and abstracts of **26,104** papers from CVPR, ICLR, and NeurIPS (2023-2025), plus **8,424** from 2022 for longitudinal trends.
- The methodology involves text processing, categorizing papers using ==35 regular-expression-based topic labels==, and calculating prevalence and ==TF-IDF mass== for trend analysis.
- Fine-grained mining is applied to Vision-Language Models to detail specific shifts in architectures, tasks, training paradigms, loss functions, dataset usage, and co-mentioned modalities.

## Results

- Vision-Language Model (VLM) papers showed a sharp rise, increasing from **16%** of publications in 2023 to **40%** in 2025 across the analyzed conferences.
- Diffusion models continued their steady expansion, accounting for **8%** of papers in 2023 and growing to **19.2%** in 2025, with research consolidating around controllability, distillation, and speed.
- Instruction tuning saw a substantial increase of **3.9 percentage points**, becoming a primary paradigm for developing conversational VLMs, while the use of purely contrastive objectives in new VLM works markedly declined.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are becoming a central organizing paradigm, increasingly reframing classic perception tasks as instruction-following and multi-step reasoning problems.
> - Research is consolidating around large-scale pre-trained models, with parameter-efficient adaptation methods like prompt/prefix tuning and LoRA dominating VLM development.
> - Traditional learning paradigms (e.g., self-supervised learning, meta-learning) are increasingly integrated as modules within larger systems rather than being standalone contributions, while engineering concerns like efficiency and robustness gain prominence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly computer vision and natural language processing, is experiencing unprecedented growth and rapid evolution. The emergence of large-scale pre-trained models, especially Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has fundamentally reshaped research paradigms. This accelerated pace of innovation often leads to an overwhelming volume of publications, making it challenging for researchers, practitioners, funding bodies, and even policymakers to identify genuine trends, assess impact, and allocate resources effectively. Anecdotal evidence and qualitative observations, while common, lack the rigor and reproducibility needed for strategic decision-making.

This paper directly addresses this challenge by providing a **quantitative, transparent, and reproducible measurement of research trends**. Instead of offering subjective interpretations, it leverages the vast corpus of accepted papers from three top-tier AI conferences—CVPR, ICLR, and NeurIPS—to empirically chart the shifts in research focus. This work is critical because it:
- **Combats Information Overload:** By processing over 26,000 papers, it distills a massive amount of information into actionable insights, helping the community navigate a complex and rapidly changing landscape.
- **Validates/Refutes Anecdotal Evidence:** It moves beyond "gut feelings" about trends, providing concrete data to confirm or challenge widely held assumptions. For instance, the "sharp rise of multimodal vision–language–LLM work" is a well-known phenomenon, but this paper quantifies its exact trajectory and prevalence.
- **Informs Strategic Planning:** For researchers, it highlights promising new directions and areas of consolidation. For funding agencies, it can inform priority setting. For educators, it can guide curriculum development.
- **Establishes a Baseline:** By releasing the methodology and lexicon, the paper creates a reproducible framework for future trend analyses, allowing for longitudinal studies and comparisons across different periods or venues.
- **Identifies "Silent" Shifts:** Beyond macro trends, it reveals subtle shifts in methodology, architectural preferences, and loss functions that might be missed by casual observation, such as the receding role of purely contrastive objectives in VLM papers.

In essence, this survey serves as a crucial navigational tool in a fast-moving scientific domain, providing an evidence-based map of where the research community's collective attention is directed.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **quantify the visible transition within the computer vision and machine learning community between 2023 and 2025**, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to provide a robust, data-driven analysis. The authors explicitly state their goal: "to quantify this transition from primary sources: the official titles and abstracts of accepted papers at CVPR, ICLR, and NeurIPS."

The core motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:
- **Identify Macro Shifts:** To accurately measure significant shifts in research focus, such as the consolidation of large-scale pre-trained models (e.g., CLIP/BLIP/LLaVA families, ViT backbones) and the ubiquitous adoption of diffusion-based generative methods.
- **Detail Fine-Grained Changes within VLMs:** To pinpoint specific evolutionary trajectories within the burgeoning Vision-Language Model (VLM) paradigm, including changes in model architectures, training practices, task formulations, and the adoption of specific tools and datasets.
- **Provide a Transparent and Reproducible Measurement:** To ensure the reliability and audibility of the findings, the authors commit to a methodology that is explicitly described and accompanied by the release of their lexicon and code. This promotes trust in the results and allows others to extend or verify the analysis.
- **Offer Actionable Insights:** Ultimately, the motivation is to provide valuable guidance to the research community, enabling researchers to better understand the current landscape, identify emerging opportunities, and anticipate future directions. This is encapsulated in the "Actionable advice for readers" section.

By systematically analyzing a massive corpus of peer-reviewed literature, the paper aims to present an objective overview of the field's trajectory, serving as a critical resource for strategic research and development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this survey is a lexicon-driven quantitative analysis of research paper titles and abstracts. It is designed for transparency and reproducibility, focusing on high-precision detection of technical terms and concepts.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

- **Sources:** The primary data consists of JSONL files containing titles and abstracts of accepted papers from three leading AI conferences: CVPR, ICLR, and NeurIPS.
- **Years Covered:** The core content analysis spans 2023, 2024, and 2025. For longitudinal trend analysis, an additional 8,424 papers from 2022 were included.
- **Volume:** A total of 26,104 abstracts from 2023-2025 were analyzed after removing empty records. The specific breakdown per conference per year is provided (e.g., CVPR 2023: 2,353 papers; ICLR 2025: 3,704 papers).

#### 3.2. Text Processing

- **Normalization:** Unicode characters are normalized, text is lowercased, and punctuation is stripped.
- **Phrase Protection:** Multi-word technical phrases (e.g., "gaussian splatting," "neural radiance fields," "vision language model") are protected as single tokens to ensure accurate matching.
- **Stopword Removal:** General stopwords and generic computer vision terms are removed to emphasize technical content.

#### 3.3. Labeling and Trend Analysis

- **Lexicon-Driven Categorization:** Each processed abstract is matched against a hand-crafted lexicon comprising 35 regular-expression categories (e.g., Diffusion, Vision-Language/LLM, 3D, Video, Robustness, Efficiency). Papers can receive multiple labels.
- **Prevalence Calculation:** The prevalence of a topic is computed as the fraction of abstracts in a given year that match a specific label.
- **TF-IDF Mass:** For aggregated direction trajectories (e.g., Figure 1 and 2), the yearly aggregated TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) mass for each direction is used, providing a normalized topic intensity.
- **Longitudinal Trends:** The inclusion of 2022 data allows for the visualization and analysis of trends over a longer four-year period.

#### 3.4. Fine-Grained Mining

- For selected high-interest areas (particularly VLMs), the methodology extends to mining sub-topics. This includes identifying:
 - **Tasks:** e.g., grounding, instruction following, VQA.
 - **Architecture Motifs:** e.g., LoRA/adapters, cross-attention, projector heads.
 - **Training Regimes:** e.g., pretrain+finetune, instruction tuning, self-supervision.
 - **Loss Functions:** e.g., contrastive, KL/distillation, cross-entropy.
 - **Named Datasets:** e.g., MS-COCO, ImageNet, LAION.
 - **Co-mentioned Modalities:** e.g., 3D, depth, audio.
 - **Specific Models:** e.g., ALIGN, LLaVA, CLIP, DINO, MoE, with detailed descriptions of their core ideas, fusion styles, backbones, objectives, and pretraining data.

#### 3.5. Caveats and Limitations

The authors explicitly acknowledge limitations to maintain transparency:
- **Lexicon-Driven:** While precise for canonical phrases, the lexicon might have recall issues, missing niche synonyms.
- **Abstract-Only Scope:** The analysis is restricted to titles and abstracts, which may lead to under-reporting of certain details (e.g., datasets, minor losses) that are only fully elaborated in the main paper body.
- **Non-Summing Percentages:** Since papers can have multiple labels, reported percentages do not sum to 100%.

Despite these limitations, the authors assert that the "longitudinal signals are consistent across venues and years," indicating the robustness of the observed trends. The release of the lexicon and methodology ensures that the findings can be audited and the analysis extended by the community.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The analysis of 26,104 papers from CVPR, ICLR, and NeurIPS (2023-2025) reveals several significant shifts and consolidations in the research landscape:

#### 4.1. Macro Trends

- **Sharp Rise of Multimodal VLM/LLM Work:** This is the most prominent trend, with the share of VLM papers soaring from 16% in 2023 to 40% in 2025. This area increasingly reframes classic perception tasks as instruction following and multi-step reasoning.
- **Steady Expansion of Generative Methods:** Diffusion models continue their growth, expanding from 8% to 19.2% of papers, with research consolidating around controllability, distillation, and speed.
- **Resilient 3D and Video Activity:** Classic 3D research remains stable, but composition methods are shifting from NeRFs to Gaussian splatting, with a growing emphasis on human- and agent-centric understanding. Video understanding also shows a steady incline, partly due to video-LLMs.
- **Decline/Plateau of Traditional Paradigms:** Classic learning paradigms like self-supervised learning (as a standalone contribution), meta-learning, GNNs, and weak supervision are declining or plateauing in relative share, increasingly integrated as modules within larger systems.
- **Rise of Engineering and Reliability Concerns:** Efficiency, robustness, out-of-distribution generalization, uncertainty, and safety are becoming more visible and diffusing across different research areas.

#### 4.2. Specific Findings within Vision-Language Models (VLMs)

- **Model Shifts:**
 - **ALIGN** remains a highly referenced family (5.1% in 2025), but **LLaVA** shows the fastest growth (0.1% to 2.7%), reflecting the pivot to instruction-following VLMs.
 - Classical backbones like **ResNet/ConvNeXt** and **ViT** are visibly shrinking in standalone visibility.
 - **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** references double, indicating growing interest in sparse capacity for scaling.
 - Models like **CLIP, BLIP, Flamingo, DINO, and Grounding DINO** are highlighted as foundational, providing universal embeddings, understanding-generation bridges, strong vision backbones, and open-vocabulary grounding, which newer LVLMs often build upon.

- **Fusion/Architectural Integration:**
 - **Lightweight Adaptation Dominates:** Prompt/Prefix tuning (14.3% in 2025) and Adapter/LoRA (4.1% in 2025) are increasingly common, reflecting a preference for parameter-efficient adaptation of strong frozen backbones.
 - **Bridging Modules Active:** Cross-/co-attention and Projector/MLP heads remain active, providing mechanisms to inject visual features into frozen LMs or map embeddings.
 - **Rebalancing of Architectures:** Encoder-decoder patterns and dual-encoder designs show a small net decline, suggesting a shift towards instruction-following and generative pipelines with tighter fusion mechanisms.

- **Task Evolution:**
 - **Reasoning/Instruction is King:** This is the fastest-growing task stratum (13.5% to 25.0%), driven by instruction-tuned LMMs that prioritize chain-of-thought localization, tool-use, and multi-step reasoning.
 - **Classic Grounding/Referring Recedes:** Once dominant, these tasks decline by 13 percentage points, as grounding becomes a sub-capability within broader instruction-following frameworks.
 - **Retrieval Stable:** Maintains a consistent share (around 8%), indicative of the continued role of dual-encoders.
 - **Captioning and VQA Decline Modestly:** Absorbed into instruction-chat settings, becoming intermediate capabilities rather than primary contributions.

- **Training Paradigms:**
 - **Pretrain + Finetune remains anchor:** Grows by +5.2 pp overall, often coupling frozen LMs with contrastively-pretrained image encoders.
 - **Instruction Tuning Surges:** A substantial increase (+3.9 pp) mirrors the pivot to conversational VLMs using synthetic multimodal dialogues.
 - **LoRA/Adapters Expand:** Driven by their efficiency and stability for fine-tuning.
 - **Self-/Weak-/Semi-supervision Declines in New Papers:** While crucial for pretraining encoders, new papers focus more on finetuning/instruction stages.

- **Loss Families:**
 - **Contrastive/InfoNCE Drops Markedly:** Recedes as the primary objective in new VLM works, reflecting the shift away from building encoders from scratch.
 - **KL/Distillation Stable/Slight Rise:** Used for compression, alignment, and transferring reasoning signals.
 - **Cross-Entropy/Ranking Objectives:** Remain important for token-level supervision and generative tasks.

- **Dataset Usage:**
 - **Legacy Benchmarks Decline:** MS-COCO and ImageNet mentions decline, suggesting fewer new papers position them as primary contributions, although they remain for sanity checks.
 - **Open-Web Sources (LAION) Persistent but not Surging:** Authors often reference them generically while focusing on instruction tuning.
 - **Task-Specific Datasets Shrink or Stabilize:** Reflecting a shift towards multi-task, instruction-style evaluations.

- **Co-mentioned Modalities:**
 - **3D/Point Cloud Uptick:** Reflects growing interest in grounding LMMs in embodied/3D settings.
 - **Image-Text Decline:** Consistent with a shift from single-pair supervision to broader instruction-style training with interleaved cues.
 - **Depth/RGB-D Stable:** Used in practical pipelines, typically via lightweight adapters.
 - **Audio/Speech and Video-Text Modest Declines:** Remain active, but often rely on existing foundations rather than stand-alone specific objectives in new VLM papers.

#### 4.3. Cross-Venue Comparison

- **CVPR:** Retains the strongest 3D emphasis (e.g., 23.1% 3D geometry mentions in 2025). Also has the highest diffusion share in 2025 (25.7%).
- **ICLR:** Shows the largest VLM share in 2025 (40.7%), comparable to CVPR (39.5%).
- **NeurIPS:** (up to 2024 data) shows an early VLM ramp (30.5% in 2024) and a respectable diffusion share (11.6%).
- **Reliability Themes:** Efficiency and robustness concerns diffuse across all venues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the AI research community, providing a robust, data-backed perspective on the current state and likely future directions. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

- **Strategic Research Guidance:**
 - **Emphasis on VLM Integration:** The most crucial takeaway is the ascendance of multimodal VLMs as an "organizing center" for research. Researchers are strongly encouraged to frame classic perception problems as instruction-following or reasoning tasks within VLM frameworks. This guides the community towards impactful problem selection and solution design.
 - **Focus on Usable Generative AI:** For generative methods, the paper highlights the shift towards controllability, speed (distillation), and reliability. This directs efforts towards making these powerful tools more practical and robust for real-world applications.
 - **Demand for Long-Context and Video Understanding:** The rising prominence of video understanding and long-context modeling points to a growing need for methods that can process extended temporal sequences efficiently while retaining reasoning capabilities.

- **Methodological Best Practices:**
 - **Parameter-Efficient Adaptation:** The dominance of prompt/prefix tuning, LoRA, and adapters establishes these as essential baselines and preferred methods for adapting foundation models, emphasizing efficiency and cost-effectiveness.
 - **Instruction Tuning as a Paradigm:** The surge in instruction tuning signals its importance for eliciting general-purpose reasoning and instruction-following abilities in multimodal models.
 - **Modular Design:** The trends in architectural integration and task breakdown suggest a move towards modular systems where strong pre-trained components (e.g., DINO-style vision backbones, frozen LLMs) are connected via lightweight bridges and tuned with instruction data.

- **Resource Allocation and Policy:**
 - **Funding Priorities:** For funding agencies, these trends provide evidence to prioritize research in multimodal AI, generative model reliability, and efficient adaptation techniques.
 - **Curriculum Development:** Educational institutions can leverage these findings to update curricula, ensuring students are equipped with skills relevant to the cutting-edge of AI research, particularly in VLMs and generative models.

- **Benchmarking and Evaluation:** The decline in mentions of traditional datasets and the rise of instruction-style evaluations suggest a shift in how model capabilities are assessed. This could encourage the development of more comprehensive, multi-task, and human-centric benchmarks.

- **Trustworthy AI:** The increasing visibility of engineering and reliability concerns (efficiency, robustness, safety, privacy) underscores their growing importance. This pushes the community towards developing AI systems that are not only powerful but also dependable and ethical, reflecting real-world deployment needs.

By providing a data-driven compass for the rapidly evolving landscape of AI research, this paper empowers the community to make more informed decisions, foster innovation in critical areas, and steer the development of AI towards more practical, efficient, and responsible outcomes.
