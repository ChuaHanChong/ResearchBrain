# EXPO-FT: Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning Finetuning for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> EXPO-FT, a reinforcement learning framework for vision-language-action (VLA) models, enables sample-efficient and reliable finetuning of pretrained VLA policies for real-world robotic manipulation tasks. The system achieved a perfect 30/30 success rate across eight challenging tasks with an average of 19.1 minutes of online robot interaction data.

## Problem

- Pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, despite strong generalization, lack the reliability needed for practical real-world robotic deployment.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning methods for VLAs are often sample-inefficient, fail to achieve high success rates, or are incompatible with expressive VLA policy architectures.
- Robot learning currently faces a gap in leveraging powerful VLA priors while simultaneously achieving the high sample efficiency and reliability required for real-world applications.

## Method

- Develops **EXPO-FT**, an off-policy ==reinforcement learning== framework that uses a lightweight edit policy to refine actions from a pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model without directly backpropagating through it.
- Adapts the RL framework to operate natively with temporally extended action chunks, aligning with the output format of modern VLA policies.
- Integrates human-in-the-loop interventions during online training to provide corrective actions and high-quality data, significantly boosting sample efficiency and guiding exploration.

## Results

- EXPO-FT achieved a perfect **30/30** success rate across all eight challenging real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating high reliability for deployment.
- The system consistently outperformed baseline methods, including HG-DAgger (22.**1/30** average) and supervised finetuning (20.**5/30** average), particularly on tasks requiring dynamic actions or high precision.
- Reliable performance was attained with an average of only 19.1 minutes of online robot interaction data per task, showcasing high sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging a strong pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) prior within a sample-efficient reinforcement learning (RL) framework is essential for achieving high reliability in real-world robotics.
> - An "edit policy" that refines actions from a frozen VLA, combined with the VLA's native action chunking, allows for stable and targeted policy improvement without the computational burden of full VLA backpropagation.
> - Integrating human-in-the-loop feedback during online RL finetuning drastically improves sample efficiency and guides exploration towards reliable policy performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: enabling robots to efficiently and reliably learn new tasks for real-world deployment. Recent advancements in large-scale imitation learning have led to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which demonstrate strong generalization capabilities across a variety of manipulation tasks. However, these pretrained VLA policies consistently exhibit insufficient reliability for practical deployment in real-world scenarios, where failures incur significant costs.

The existing research landscape for training real-world robot policies with reinforcement learning (RL) can be broadly categorized into two main approaches. The first category comprises methods that train RL policies either from scratch or by training lightweight models on top of a VLA, but without directly finetuning the VLA itself. While some of these approaches, such as HIL-SERL, have achieved high asymptotic performance and near-perfect success rates in specific settings, they do not leverage the rich semantic and behavioral priors inherent in large pretrained VLAs. Consequently, they may struggle in more complex environments or tasks with broad initial state distributions without substantial additional data.

The second category consists of methods designed to finetune pretrained VLAs, thereby inheriting their generalizable representations. However, current methods within this category often face limitations: they either fail to achieve reliable success rates required for deployment, demand an prohibitively large number of environment interactions, or both. Furthermore, many classical RL algorithms for continuous control are structured around Gaussian policies, which are not directly compatible with the more expressive policy classes, such as flow matching or diffusion models, typically employed by modern VLAs. While some recent works have developed RL algorithms tailored for flow or diffusion policies, many of these either focus on offline settings (e.g., π0.6) or use on-policy online finetuning, which can be less sample-efficient than off-policy methods. Additionally, off-policy RL methods for VLAs often involve training lightweight auxiliary policies or performing optimization in a latent space, which may not directly finetune the full VLA or integrate human feedback.

EXPO-FT aims to bridge this identified gap by developing a framework that simultaneously leverages the powerful priors of pretrained VLAs and achieves the sample efficiency and reliability necessary for practical, real-world robotic deployment. It seeks to combine the generalization benefits of VLAs with the performance optimization capabilities of RL, while addressing specific technical challenges related to VLA policy architectures and sample efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a system that enables stable, sample-efficient, and reliable reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning of pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for robotic manipulation tasks in real-world environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in the current landscape of robotic policy learning:
- **Reliability Gap in Pretrained VLAs:** Despite demonstrating strong generalization capabilities across diverse manipulation tasks, existing pretrained VLA models consistently fall short of the high reliability required for practical real-world deployment. Failures in real-world robotics are often costly and disruptive, highlighting the need for policies that can achieve near-perfect success rates.
- **Limitations of Existing RL Approaches:**
 - **RL from scratch:** Methods that train RL policies without leveraging VLA priors often require extensive data collection and cannot exploit the rich semantic and behavioral knowledge embedded in large pretrained VLAs, which can be crucial for complex or novel tasks.
 - **VLA finetuning limitations:** Current approaches for finetuning VLAs with RL often fail to achieve adequate success rates or demand prohibitively large amounts of environment interactions, making them impractical for real-world application.
- **Algorithmic Mismatch:** Traditional sample-efficient RL algorithms are often designed for simpler Gaussian policies, which are incompatible with the more expressive flow-matching or diffusion-based policy architectures commonly used in modern VLAs.
- **Need for Temporal Abstraction:** Many modern VLAs operate by predicting temporally extended action sequences (action chunks), a feature that needs to be preserved and integrated into the finetuning process for compatibility and efficiency.
- **Enhancing Sample Efficiency:** Given the high cost and time involved in real-world robot data collection, there is a strong motivation to incorporate mechanisms, such as human-in-the-loop feedback, to further accelerate learning and reduce the required online interaction time.

By addressing these points, the authors aim to unlock a new class of robot policies that are not only capable of generalist behavior but also sufficiently reliable for real-world deployment, thereby advancing the practical utility of large-scale robotic learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EXPO-FT system is designed for sample-efficient and reliable reinforcement learning finetuning of pretrained VLA models. It builds upon the EXPO algorithm and incorporates specific extensions to address the challenges inherent in finetuning VLAs in real-world robotic settings.

**Core Algorithm: EXPO Foundation**
EXPO-FT is based on EXPO, an RL algorithm developed for stable and sample-efficient training of expressive flow or diffusion policies, which aligns with the architecture of modern VLAs. EXPO operates by maintaining two parameterized policies:
- **Base Flow Policy (π_VLA):** This is the pretrained VLA model itself, which is initially trained with a supervised loss (imitation learning).
- **Lightweight Edit Policy (π_edit):** This policy is trained to maximize the Q-function. It predicts an "edit" action (ˆa) within a constrained range [−β, β]. This edit is added to the base action (a) proposed by π_VLA to form an edited action (˜a = a + ˆa). This design allows for targeted policy refinement without requiring gradient backpropagation through the large, complex VLA model.
- **On-the-Fly (OTF) Policy:** For inference and Q-function updates, EXPO uses an OTF policy that selects the action (either a base VLA action or an edited action) that yields the highest estimated Q-value.
- **Q-function Training:** The Q-function is trained using standard Temporal Difference (TD) learning.

**EXPO-FT Extensions:**
EXPO-FT extends the original EXPO formulation with two key components to enhance compatibility with VLAs and improve sample efficiency:

1. **Temporally Extended Actions (Action Chunking):**
 - Modern VLAs typically operate by predicting a chunk of H future actions (a_t:t+H) and executing C ≤ H actions at each timestep. EXPO-FT adapts its framework to this native interface.
 - The edit policy and Q-function are modified to operate over these executed action chunks (a_t:t+C).
 - The edit policy predicts a chunk of edits (ˆa_t:t+C), which are added to the VLA's proposed action chunk.
 - The loss functions for both the edit policy (Equation 4) and the Q-function (Equation 5) are adjusted to reflect this chunk-based action representation and temporal dynamics.
 - The base VLA (π_VLA) continues to be updated using its original pretraining objective, remaining mostly unmodified during RL finetuning.

2. **Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) Interventions:**
 - To further boost sample efficiency and reliability, EXPO-FT integrates human operator interventions during online training.
 - A human operator can provide corrective actions (¯a_t':t'') at any point during an action chunk or across multiple chunks. These human-provided actions directly override the robot's policy actions.
 - The executed chunks, including human interventions, are then added to the replay buffer, providing targeted, high-quality data. This mechanism helps guide exploration and reduce the learning time.

**End-to-End Training Process:**
The complete EXPO-FT training procedure for a given task involves:
1. **Environment Setup:** Configuring cameras and defining a sparse binary reward signal, which can be rule-based or learned.
2. **Initial VLA Assessment:** Evaluating the zero-shot performance of the pretrained VLA (π_VLA) on the task.
3. **Bootstrapping (if necessary):** If zero-shot performance is below a reasonable threshold (e.g., 40%), a small offline dataset of human teleoperated demonstrations is collected. The VLA is then finetuned with imitation learning on this data until it reaches a target success rate, and this data is used to initialize the replay buffer.
4. **Online RL Training:** The finetuned VLA policy then undergoes online RL training. The robot (actor) executes rollouts in the environment, with the human operator ready to intervene as needed. Policy parameters are updated either per step, per episode, or per batch of episodes, allowing flexibility based on computational resources and task requirements.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Backbone VLA:** The system employs π0.5 as the base VLA model, initialized from a task-specific LoRA supervised-finetuning checkpoint.
- **Vision Backbones:** The VLA (actor) uses its pretrained visual encoder. The critic utilizes a separate, computationally lighter ResNet-50 encoder to reduce latency during the actor-learner loop. VLA image encoder weights are frozen during RL, while critic image features are learned.
- **Learning Decoupling:** Training is decoupled into a learner (handling VLA training, inference, data management) and an actor (interacting with the real environment, executing actions, collecting data including interventions). Communication between them can be synchronous or asynchronous.
- **Reward Design:** Sparse binary rewards are defined using rule-based classifiers to detect task completion, avoiding the need for complex dense reward shaping.
- **Observations and Actions:** Multi-view RGB images (wrist-mounted and fixed side-view) and robot proprioceptive state are used as observations. Actions are end-effector Cartesian velocity and gripper velocity commands at 10 Hz.
- **Optimization:** Adam optimizer is used for all differentiable components, with specific hyperparameters for learning rates, discount factors, RedQ-style Q-ensemble, Polyak averaging coefficients, batch size, and update-to-data ratio. Image augmentations are applied to camera views during updates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates EXPO-FT on eight distinct and challenging real-world robotic manipulation tasks, comparing its performance against several prior methods. The tasks selected cover a range of challenges, including precise object alignment, handling flexible objects, and dynamic interactions (e.g., Egg Flip, String Light Routing, Candy Scoop, Cube Pick, Flower Insert, Pool Shot).

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Robot Control:** End-effector Cartesian velocity and gripper velocity commands at 10 Hz.
- **Observations:** Two 224x224 RGB images (side and wrist views) augmented with robot proprioceptive state.
- **Reward Signal:** Binary sparse rewards, determined by rule-based success detectors with over 95% accuracy.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** 30 evaluation trials per task, with success rates reported. Initial states were randomized for robustness testing. Online data usage (in minutes) was tracked.
- **Hardware:** Experiments were conducted using two NVIDIA H200 GPUs, typically involving 8,000 to 20,000 environment steps per task.

**Comparison Baselines:**
- **HIL-SERL:** A sample-efficient real-world RL system with human-in-the-loop interventions, but designed for training policies from scratch (Gaussian policies) without leveraging VLA priors.
- **DSRL:** A state-of-the-art RL method for finetuning diffusion/flow-matching policies, optimizing in a latent space, but constrained to existing modes of the prior distribution and lacking direct human intervention.
- **HG-DAgger:** A human-gated imitation learning algorithm that solicits expert corrections during online execution, but relies solely on behavioral cloning and does not optimize a reward signal or leverage suboptimal experience.
- **Supervised Finetuning (SFT):** The baseline performance of the VLA finetuned on task demonstration data prior to any online interaction.

**Key Results:**

1. **Overall Performance and Reliability:** EXPO-FT achieved a perfect success rate (30/30) across all eight evaluated tasks. This indicates a high level of reliability for real-world deployment.

2. **Outperformance of Prior Methods:** EXPO-FT consistently outperformed all compared prior methods:
 - **Average Success Rate:** EXPO-FT (30/30) significantly surpassed HG-DAgger (22.1/30 average) and SFT (20.5/30 average) across all tasks.
 - **Specific Task Challenges:**
 - On tasks requiring dynamic actions or high precision (e.g., Egg Flip, Pool Shot), EXPO-FT demonstrated substantial gains. HG-DAgger struggled due to the limitations of imitation learning in recovering from compounding errors.
 - HIL-SERL and DSRL, while effective in certain settings, exhibited significant performance drops on tasks with wider initial state distributions (e.g., Egg Flip) or those requiring highly precise motions (e.g., Pool Shot). HIL-SERL achieved an average of 5.5/30 on its evaluated subset, with 0/30 on Cube Pick and 1/30 on Pool Shot. DSRL achieved 19/30 on its subset, with 13/30 on Pool Shot. The VLA initialization in EXPO-FT provided a strong prior that aided generalization across diverse states, and the EXPO algorithm allowed for acquiring new behaviors beyond the prior's modes.

3. **Sample Efficiency:** EXPO-FT reached reliable performance with an average of 19.1 minutes of real-world online robot interaction data across all tasks. This demonstrates its high sample efficiency, attributed to the strong VLA prior, the efficient EXPO algorithm, and the integration of human-in-the-loop feedback.

4. **Training Dynamics:** Training success rate curves showed consistent improvement for EXPO-FT throughout training, while intervention rates decreased as the policy learned. Episode completion times also consistently reduced, indicating increased task efficiency and smoother policy execution over time.

In summary, the experiments establish that EXPO-FT is both general and effective across a broad spectrum of manipulation challenges. It achieves the highest performance in every evaluated setting, demonstrating the effectiveness and sample efficiency of its online RL finetuning approach for VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of EXPO-FT represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning the practical deployment of sophisticated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

**Key Significance:**

- **Bridging the Reliability Gap:** The most significant contribution of EXPO-FT is its demonstration that RL finetuning can effectively bridge the gap between the strong generalization capabilities of pretrained VLAs and the high reliability required for real-world robotic deployment. Achieving perfect task success (30/30) across a diverse suite of challenging tasks underscores this capability.
- **Practical Sample Efficiency:** EXPO-FT achieves this high reliability with remarkable sample efficiency, requiring an average of only 19.1 minutes of online robot data per task. This low data requirement makes the finetuning process economically viable and operationally practical for deployment in real-world settings, where data collection is often expensive and time-consuming.
- **Robustness Across Task Categories:** The system's consistent performance across tasks involving precise object alignment, dynamic interactions, flexible object handling, and varied initial states indicates its general applicability and robustness to different manipulation challenges. This suggests that EXPO-FT could be a general solution for adapting VLAs to a wide range of new tasks.
- **Effective Integration of Components:** The work demonstrates the synergistic benefits of combining a strong pretrained VLA prior with a sample-efficient RL algorithm (EXPO), enhanced by human-in-the-loop interventions and adapted for VLA-specific features like action chunking. This combination yields a powerful framework that leverages the strengths of each component.
- **Empirical Validation of Design Choices:** The results validate specific design choices, such as using an off-policy RL algorithm for sample efficiency, directly finetuning the VLA, incorporating human feedback, and operating over action chunks. These elements collectively contribute to the system's performance.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** By enabling fast and reliable adaptation of generalist VLA models, EXPO-FT could significantly accelerate the deployment of autonomous robots in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and service industries, where high reliability and adaptability to specific tasks are critical.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** The high sample efficiency can lead to a substantial reduction in the time and resources needed to develop and integrate new robotic skills, lowering the overall cost of robotic automation.
- **New Research Directions:** This work provides a foundation for further research into advanced RL finetuning techniques for large foundation models in robotics. It opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated human-robot interaction paradigms, automating environment resets, and improving computational efficiency for large VLA models.
- **Community Facilitation:** The release of an open-source codebase is a significant impact, as it will allow other researchers and practitioners to build upon EXPO-FT, replicate findings, and accelerate collective progress in the field of VLA finetuning and real-world RL.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its promising results, the paper acknowledges two primary limitations:
1. **Human Environment Resets:** The current pipeline requires human intervention for environment resets between episodes, which can be an operational burden at scale. Automating this reset process is identified as an important area for future work.
2. **Computational Challenge:** Finetuning large pretrained VLA models (often billions of parameters) presents computational challenges, particularly in running training and inference at high frequencies. Addressing this constraint to improve computational efficiency is another direction for future research.

Overall, EXPO-FT represents a concrete step towards making generalist VLA models practically viable for high-reliability robotic applications, potentially influencing the trajectory of robotic learning research and deployment.
