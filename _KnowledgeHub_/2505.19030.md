# RECAST: Expanding the Boundaries of LLMs' Complex Instruction Following with Multi-Constraint Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Xiaohongshu Inc. introduce RECAST, a scalable framework for synthesizing complex instruction-following datasets, and RECAST-30K, a 30,000-instance dataset with an average of 13.4 constraints per instruction. The work also presents RLVC, a reinforcement learning method, which collectively enabled models to achieve up to 32.33% average constraint satisfaction on complex tasks, surpassing larger, general instruction-tuned models while preserving general capabilities.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to accurately follow complex instructions containing a high number of explicit constraints (e.g., more than 10), limiting their reliability in real-world applications.
- Existing instruction-following datasets typically feature only 3-5 constraints, are not scalable for large-scale training, and often rely on narrowly applicable code-verifiable constraints.
- A lack of diverse, large-scale, high-quality training and evaluation data hinders the systematic assessment and enhancement of LLMs' multi-constraint instruction-following abilities.

## Method

- Developed **RECAST** (Realistic Extraction of ==Constraints== for Augmented inStruction synThesis), an efficient and scalable framework for synthesizing datasets with a significantly higher number of diverse, verifiable constraints per instance.
- Constructed RECAST-30K, a large-scale dataset of 29,939 instances, where each instruction is augmented with an average of 13.4 constraints (4.8 rule-based, 8.6 model-based) across 19 distinct types.
- Introduced RLVC (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Constraints), a method leveraging the dataset's explicit constraint verification mechanisms to provide fine-grained reward signals for simultaneous multi-objective policy optimization.

## Results

- Models fine-tuned on RECAST-**30K**-SFT achieved a **31.25%** average constraint satisfaction rate on the RECAST-Test benchmark, outperforming instruction-tuned variants and models fine-tuned on other datasets.
- The RECAST-**30K**-RLVC approach further enhanced performance, with Qwen-2.5-**7B** reaching a **32.33%** average satisfaction rate on RECAST-Test, demonstrating more pronounced gains at higher difficulty levels.
- RECAST-trained models consistently outperformed all baseline methods on external instruction-following benchmarks (IFEVAL and FollowBench) and maintained competitive performance on general capability benchmarks (GPQA for knowledge, MUSR for reasoning).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM performance consistently degrades as the number of constraints in an instruction increases, with rule-based constraints generally proving more challenging for models to satisfy than model-based ones.
> - Fine-tuning models on RECAST-30K significantly improves their ability to follow complex multi-constraint instructions, enabling smaller models to outperform larger, general instruction-tuned models.
> - Utilizing verifiable, constraint-specific rewards within a reinforcement learning framework (RLVC) further boosts LLM performance in complex multi-constraint scenarios without compromising general capabilities or transferability to other instruction-following tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across numerous Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks and are increasingly deployed in practical applications. However, a notable limitation persists in their ability to accurately follow complex instructions, particularly those containing a high number of explicit constraints (e.g., more than 10). This limitation is observed in real-world scenarios such as constitutional AI systems requiring adherence to multiple principles or enterprise assistants managing detailed business rules, where LLMs often exhibit performance degradation as instruction complexity increases.

Existing research has explored methods to evaluate and improve LLMs' instruction-following capabilities, but these efforts have primarily been constrained in scope. Prior evaluation benchmarks, often manually constructed, typically lack comprehensive training sets and are not scalable for large-scale applications. Datasets like those used in some studies impose only a small number of constraints, usually between three and five per instance, which is insufficient for rigorously challenging advanced LLMs. Furthermore, automated data construction pipelines, such as AutoIF, have tended to rely on exclusively code-verifiable constraints, thereby limiting their applicability to narrow domains and failing to capture the broader, more nuanced requirements of real-world use cases. Automated approaches that employ LLM-based instruction rewriting often produce homogenized constraints, further restricting the diversity and scalability needed for generating complex, multi-faceted instructions.

This research addresses these limitations by focusing on the generation of datasets that incorporate a significantly higher number of diverse constraints. The work aims to develop a scalable data-synthesis framework capable of creating complex instruction-following datasets to challenge and extend the current boundaries of LLMs' abilities, thereby facilitating their more reliable deployment in complex, real-world applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed deficiencies of current Large Language Models (LLMs) in accurately following complex instructions that involve multiple, explicit constraints. Despite their advancements, LLMs frequently struggle when tasked with satisfying a substantial number of requirements simultaneously, a scenario common in advanced applications such as constitutional AI, specialized enterprise assistants, and autonomous agents. This struggle limits their applicability and reliability in real-world contexts where users often craft sophisticated, multi-faceted prompts.

The authors identify specific shortcomings in existing datasets and benchmarks designed for instruction following:
- **Limited Constraint Complexity**: Most available datasets contain a small number of constraints (typically 3-5), which does not adequately represent the complexity encountered in practical applications. Datasets with more than 10 constraints per instance are largely absent.
- **Lack of Scalability**: Manually constructed datasets are not scalable for large-scale training. Automated approaches often lead to homogenized constraints, reducing diversity.
- **Narrow Domain Applicability**: Approaches relying solely on code-verifiable constraints limit the scope to specific domains, failing to capture qualitative and subjective requirements common in human communication.
- **Inadequate Evaluation and Training Resources**: The absence of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse training data for complex instruction following hinders the systematic assessment and enhancement of LLMs in this area.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Data-Synthesis Framework (RECAST)**: To propose a framework that can synthesize datasets where each example incorporates a significantly higher number of constraints than previously available benchmarks. This framework is designed to be efficient and cost-effective, leveraging signals from existing prompt-response pairs to ensure practical relevance.
2. **Construct a Large-Scale, High-Quality Dataset (RECAST-30K)**: To use the RECAST framework to create a substantial dataset (30,000 instances) spanning a wide array of 19 constraint types. This dataset aims to serve as a benchmark and training resource for improving LLMs' complex instruction-following capabilities.
3. **Demonstrate Improved LLM Performance through Fine-Tuning**: To show that models fine-tuned on RECAST-30K can substantially improve their ability to follow complex instructions, even outperforming larger, general instruction-tuned models, while retaining their general capabilities without degradation.
4. **Enable Automatic Verification of Constraint Satisfaction**: To integrate automatic verification mechanisms into the dataset, utilizing rule-based validators for quantitative constraints and LLM-based validators for qualitative ones. This verifiability provides clear feedback signals.
5. **Design a Reinforcement Learning Approach (RLVC) with Verifiable Constraints**: To leverage the verifiable nature of RECAST-30K's constraints to develop a reinforcement learning method that uses constraint-specific reward functions for simultaneous multi-objective optimization, thereby further boosting model performance on complex tasks.

In essence, the research seeks to push the boundaries of LLMs' instruction-following capacity by providing a novel, systematic, and scalable approach to data generation and model optimization for multi-constraint scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces RECAST (Realistic Extraction of Constraints for Augmented inStruction synThesis), a scalable framework for constructing complex instruction-following datasets, and RLVC (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Constraints), an optimization method utilizing the verifiability of RECAST's constraints.

#### 3.1. RECAST Framework for Data Synthesis

The RECAST pipeline operates in four main stages:

- **1. Seed Data Collection (Section 2.1)**:
 - The foundation for RECAST-30K is the Tülu 3 Persona IF dataset, which comprises 30,000 instruction-response pairs across diverse scenarios (e.g., code generation, creative writing, factual QA). This dataset was selected due to its rich content and varied task types, making it suitable for extracting diverse constraints.

- **2. Constraint Pool Construction (Section 2.2)**:
 - This stage builds a comprehensive pool of constraints categorized by their verification mechanisms.
 - **Rule-based Constraints Construction**: These are objective requirements (e.g., paragraph count, keyword inclusion/exclusion, word limits) that can be deterministically verified. Nine specialized rule-based extractors analyze seed responses for specific syntactic patterns, keyword frequencies, numerical parameters, and structural characteristics. Each rule-based constraint is paired with a programmatic verification method.
 - **Model-based Constraints Construction**: These are subjective requirements (e.g., stylistic elements, tonal qualities, content characteristics) requiring semantic understanding. This involves three steps:
 1. **Applicable Constraint Type Identification**: LLMs analyze each seed response to determine relevant constraint types from a taxonomy of 10 categories.
 2. **Constraint Generation**: LLMs generate multiple concrete constraint instances for each applicable type.
 3. **Constraint Filtering**: A filtering process verifies that each generated constraint is actually satisfied by its corresponding response. Human evaluation confirmed the accuracy of this filtering (90.1% consistency).
 - This dual approach creates a constraint pool balancing objective and subjective requirements.

- **3. Constraint-Augmented Instruction Synthesis (Section 2.3)**:
 - This stage integrates selected constraints into the original prompts to create enhanced instructions.
 - **Constraint Selection**: LLMs select a coherent and relevant subset of constraints from the pool for each original instruction, ensuring contextual appropriateness.
 - **Instruction Enhancement**: Multiple LLMs are employed to integrate these selected constraints naturally and coherently into the original instruction text.
 - **Optimal Instruction Selection**: A majority voting mechanism involving multiple LLMs evaluates and ranks candidate integrated instructions based on linguistic fluency, semantic coherence, and constraint completeness. Human evaluation confirmed the high consistency (94.6%) of LLM-selected optimal instructions.

- **4. Instruction-Consistent Response Synthesis (Section 2.4)**:
 - To ensure consistency between the new augmented instructions and their responses, this stage generates high-quality, aligned responses.
 - **Diverse Response Generation**: Multiple LLMs generate several candidate responses for each constraint-augmented instruction, fostering diversity and mitigating single-model biases.
 - **Response Quality Assessment**: A majority voting scheme (using LLMs) selects the best response based on criteria such as constraint adherence, accuracy, clarity, and conciseness. Human evaluation confirmed high quality (95.7% consistency) of LLM-selected optimal responses.

The outcome of this pipeline is **RECAST-30K**, a dataset of 29,939 instances. Each instruction in RECAST-30K is enriched with an average of 13.4 constraints (4.8 rule-based, 8.6 model-based), with some instances containing over 30 constraints. The construction cost of RECAST-30K was approximately $175.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Constraints (RLVC)

Given the explicit verifiability of constraints in RECAST-30K, the authors developed RLVC to leverage these signals for fine-grained policy optimization.

- **Constraint Verification Mechanisms (Section 3.1)**:
 - A dual-mode verification scheme is implemented:
 - **Rule-based Validators (V_rule-based)**: Programmatically verify objective requirements.
 - **LLM-based Validators (V_model-based)**: Assess subjective qualities for model-based constraints.
 - Both return a binary value (1 for satisfaction, 0 for violation).

- **Verifiable Constraints as Reward Signals (Section 3.2)**:
 - Unlike traditional holistic rewards, RLVC defines the reward for a generated response as the average satisfaction rate across all individual constraints for that instruction.
 - This provides a multi-channel, fine-grained feedback mechanism.
 - The RLVC framework integrates these constraint-specific rewards with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to guide the model towards simultaneously satisfying multiple requirements.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup (Section 4.1)

- **Evaluation Benchmarks**:
 - **RECAST-Test**: A hierarchical benchmark with four difficulty levels, defined by 5, 10, 15, or all available constraints, progressively increasing in complexity.
 - **External Instruction-Following Benchmarks**: IFEVAL and FollowBench.
 - **General Capability Benchmarks**: GPQA (knowledge) and MUSR (reasoning).
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Hard Constraint Satisfaction Rate (HSR), Rule-based Constraint Satisfaction Rate (RSR), Model-based Constraint Satisfaction Rate (MSR), and Overall Constraint Satisfaction Rate (OSR). GPT-4o was primarily used for LLM-based evaluations.
- **Baselines**: Eight open-source instruction fine-tuning datasets (e.g., Conifer, Crab, Evol-Instruct) and several leading large models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen3-235B-A22B).
- **Base Models for Fine-tuning**: Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B.
- **Training Details**: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) used LlamaFactory with specific hyperparameters. RLVC implemented with VeRL framework and GRPO, using asynchronous reward calculation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness of RECAST and RLVC in enhancing LLMs' complex instruction-following capabilities.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on RECAST-Test (Section 4.2.1, Table 1)

The RECAST-Test benchmark, with its four progressively challenging difficulty levels, demonstrated the following:

- **Performance Degradation with Increasing Constraint Complexity**: Across all models tested, performance consistently declined as the number of constraints increased from Level 1 to Level 4. This observation underscores the inherent difficulty for current LLMs in simultaneously satisfying multiple complex requirements. Rule-based constraints were identified as generally more challenging to fulfill than model-based ones. Gemini-2.5-Pro exhibited the highest average satisfaction rate among the commercial models, at 39.75%.
- **Effectiveness of RECAST-30K for Instruction Following**: Models fine-tuned on RECAST-30K via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) showed substantial improvements. For example, the Qwen-2.5-7B model fine-tuned on RECAST-30K-SFT achieved a 31.25% average satisfaction rate, outperforming both its instruction-tuned variant and models fine-tuned on other datasets. Similar improvements were observed for Llama-3.1-8B. This indicates the utility of RECAST-30K in developing robust instruction-following abilities, particularly in multi-constraint scenarios.
- **Performance Enhancement Through RLVC Optimization**: The application of RLVC further improved model performance across all difficulty levels. RECAST-30K-RLVC variants consistently surpassed their SFT counterparts, with Qwen-2.5-7B reaching a 32.33% average satisfaction rate and Llama-3.1-8B reaching 31.96%. These gains were more pronounced at higher difficulty levels (Levels 3 and 4), where constraint interactions are more complex. This outcome supports the efficacy of the constraint-specific reinforcement learning approach in optimizing for simultaneous multi-constraint satisfaction.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Additional Instruction-Following Benchmarks (Section 4.2.2, Table 2)

To assess generalization, models fine-tuned on RECAST were evaluated on two widely used external benchmarks: IFEVAL and FollowBench.

- RECAST-30K-SFT consistently outperformed all baseline methods on both benchmarks.
- RECAST-30K-RLVC further improved performance, achieving the best results for both Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B. These results suggest that the training on RECAST-30K not only enhances complex constraint following but also generalizes effectively to traditional instruction-following tasks, indicating a broader improvement rather than overfitting.

#### 4.3. General Capability Evaluation (Section 4.2.3, Table 3)

To ensure that the specialized training did not degrade general LLM capabilities, RECAST-trained models were evaluated on GPQA (knowledge) and MUSR (reasoning).

- RECAST-30K-RLVC achieved average scores of 34.64 on Llama-3.1-8B and 38.39 on Qwen2.5-7B. These scores indicate that while RECAST significantly enhances complex instruction following, it also preserves competitive performance on general reasoning and knowledge-focused benchmarks.

#### 4.4. Ablations and Analysis (Appendix D.6)

- **Impact of Constraint Type**: Experiments with only model-based or only rule-based constraints showed performance degradation on the unexposed constraint type, highlighting the importance of diverse constraint exposure during training for robust performance.
- **Impact of Constraint Number**: Training with varying maximum constraint limits (5, 10, 15) demonstrated a correlation between training constraint quantity and evaluation performance at corresponding difficulty levels. The full RECAST-30K-SFT model, exposed to diverse quantities, maintained competitive performance across all levels, suggesting effective generalization.
- **Impact of Specific RECAST Components**: Ablation studies revealed that the complete instruction-response synthesis pipeline of RECAST is crucial. Instruction-Only Enhancement led to significant performance degradation, indicating that inconsistencies between augmented instructions and original responses confuse the model. Response-Only Enhancement showed marginal impact compared to training without RECAST, emphasizing that explicit constraint specification in instructions drives performance gains.

In summary, the results collectively demonstrate that RECAST, particularly when combined with RLVC, effectively improves LLMs' ability to satisfy multiple complex constraints simultaneously without compromising their general capabilities, and generalizes to other instruction-following tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces RECAST, a framework for generating complex instruction-following datasets, and RECAST-30K, a dataset constructed using this framework, along with RLVC, a reinforcement learning approach. These contributions aim to address a current limitation of large language models (LLMs) in accurately following complex instructions containing multiple constraints.

The significance of this work is underscored by several aspects:

- **Advancing Complex Instruction Following**: The research demonstrates that models fine-tuned on RECAST-30K, even smaller models like Llama3.1-8B, can achieve performance in complex instruction following that surpasses larger, generally instruction-tuned models. This indicates a pathway for enhancing LLMs' controllability and reliability in scenarios requiring adherence to intricate rules and guidelines.
- **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Generation**: RECAST offers an efficient, scalable, and low-cost framework for synthesizing datasets with a high density and diversity of verifiable constraints. This automated approach mitigates the labor-intensive and often limited scope of manual dataset construction, making it practical for generating large-scale, high-quality training resources.
- **Introduction of Verifiable Constraints**: The integration of both rule-based and LLM-based validators provides explicit verification mechanisms for constraint satisfaction. This verifiability is fundamental for objective evaluation and, more critically, for enabling targeted model optimization.
- **Novel Reinforcement Learning Approach (RLVC)**: By leveraging these verifiable constraints as fine-grained reward signals, RLVC facilitates simultaneous multi-objective optimization. This approach offers a more precise method for guiding models to satisfy complex requirements compared to traditional holistic reward mechanisms.
- **Robustness and Generalization**: Experimental results indicate that RECAST-trained models not only excel on the novel, hierarchical RECAST-Test benchmark but also generalize effectively to established instruction-following benchmarks like IFEVAL and FollowBench. Crucially, these improvements are achieved without significant degradation of general capabilities on knowledge-based (GPQA) and reasoning-oriented (MUSR) tasks.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:

- **Improved LLM Reliability in Real-World Applications**: Enhanced complex instruction following can lead to more dependable LLM performance in critical applications such as constitutional AI, automated legal document generation, detailed business process management, and sophisticated agentic systems where precise adherence to multiple rules is paramount.
- **Facilitating Research on Controllable LLMs**: The dataset and methodologies provide valuable resources for researchers to further explore and develop techniques for making LLMs more controllable and aligned with user intent, particularly in the presence of complex, multi-faceted requirements.
- **New Benchmarking Standards**: RECAST-Test offers a more rigorous and granular benchmark for evaluating LLMs' instruction-following capabilities across varying levels of complexity, pushing the boundaries beyond datasets with fewer constraints.
- **Ethical Considerations**: While enhancing controllability, the authors acknowledge the potential for misuse (e.g., generating manipulative content). The emphasis on systematic design with explicit verification mechanisms aims to promote high-quality and safe training signals, encouraging responsible deployment.

In conclusion, this research presents a systematic advancement in addressing the challenge of complex instruction following for LLMs. By providing scalable data construction, verifiable constraints, and a targeted reinforcement learning method, it offers practical resources that could contribute to the development of more robust, reliable, and controllable LLMs for diverse real-world applications.
