# LeapAlign: Post-Training Flow Matching Models at Any Generation Step by Building Two-Step Trajectories

> [!summary] Summary
> LeapAlign introduces a post-training method for flow matching models that constructs two-step trajectories, enabling stable and efficient backpropagation of reward gradients to any generation step. This approach, combined with gradient discounting and trajectory-similarity weighting, leads to improved image quality and compositional fidelity compared to existing alignment techniques.

## Problem

- Existing direct-gradient methods for fine-tuning flow matching models face prohibitive memory costs and gradient instability when propagating through long generation trajectories.
- Many current methods struggle to effectively update crucial early generation steps, which determine global image structure, often due to computational burden or by discarding valuable gradient signals.
- Policy-gradient-based methods can introduce stochasticity and variance, while direct-gradient methods often lead to gradient explosion or limited early-step updates.

## Method

- The method constructs ==two-step leap trajectories== by selecting random timesteps and using ==latent connectors==, allowing efficient and constant-memory gradient backpropagation to any generation step.
- ==Gradient discounting== is applied to the second leap prediction, stabilizing the optimization process by controlling the magnitude of the nested gradient term while preserving its learning signal.
- A ==trajectory-similarity weighting== mechanism is introduced to emphasize leap trajectories that closely mirror the original full generation dynamics, thereby providing more meaningful gradient signals.

## Results

- **LeapAlign** consistently outperformed state-of-the-art GRPO-based and direct-gradient methods across various automatic evaluators for general preference alignment, achieving an HPSv2.1 score of **0.4092** on Flux compared to **0.3882** for DRTune.
- The method achieved a higher GenEval score of **0.7420**, surpassing MixGRPO (**0.7232**), with notable improvements in compositional alignment tasks such as 'two objects' and 'attribute binding'.
- Ablation studies confirmed that gradient discounting with **α=0.3**, trajectory-similarity weighting, and the two-step leap design were crucial for performance, enabling faster and higher reward gains while effectively fine-tuning early generation steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shortening the long generation path into a two-step differentiable 'leap' trajectory effectively addresses memory and stability issues while enabling comprehensive optimization across all generation steps.
> - The 'nested gradient' term, representing interactions between timesteps, is beneficial for learning but requires magnitude control through discounting to prevent training instability.
> - Evaluating the reward on the actual final generated image, rather than intermediate predictions, provides more reliable and faithful supervision for the fine-tuning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the post-training alignment of generative models, specifically flow matching models, with human preferences. This field has gained prominence with the development of large language models (LLMs) and text-to-image (T2I) diffusion and flow matching models.

Current post-training strategies for preference alignment broadly fall into two categories:
- **Policy-Gradient-Based Methods:** These methods, often inspired by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), are commonly used for LLMs where the generation process is discrete and non-differentiable. For flow matching models, approaches like DanceGRPO and Flow-GRPO adapt policy gradient techniques by formulating the deterministic ODE sampling as an equivalent Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE). While effective, these methods can introduce stochasticity and variance due to their reliance on policy gradients.
- **Direct-Gradient Methods:** These methods leverage the inherent differentiability of diffusion and flow matching sampling processes. They directly backpropagate reward gradients through the generation trajectory to update model weights. Examples include ReFL, DRaFT-LV, and DRTune. Direct-gradient methods can offer faster convergence and more stable training compared to policy-gradient methods.

However, existing direct-gradient methods face several challenges:
- **Prohibitive Memory Costs:** Backpropagating gradients through long generation trajectories requires substantial memory for storing intermediate activations.
- **Gradient Explosion:** Long gradient chains can lead to unstable training and gradient explosion.
- **Limited Early Step Updates:** To mitigate memory and gradient issues, many methods update only timesteps close to the final image, neglecting early steps that are critical for determining the global structure and layout of the generated image. Some methods, like DRTune, attempt early-step updates but may discard significant gradient flow by stopping gradients at model inputs.

LeapAlign aims to address these limitations by providing a direct-gradient method that can stably and efficiently propagate reward gradients to *any* generation step, including early ones, without incurring prohibitive memory costs or discarding useful gradient signals. This positions LeapAlign as an advancement in preference alignment for flow matching models, particularly in improving compositional fidelity and overall image quality by enabling comprehensive model optimization across the entire generation process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient method for aligning flow matching models with human preferences through post-training. The authors are motivated by several challenges inherent in existing direct-gradient fine-tuning techniques for these models.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Enabling Early Generation Step Updates:** A core motivation stems from the observation that early generation steps in flow matching models are crucial for establishing the global structure and layout of the final image. Existing direct-gradient methods often struggle to update these early steps effectively. This is due to the computational burden and instability associated with backpropagating reward gradients through a long sequence of ODE (Ordinary Differential Equation) sampling steps. The paper aims to overcome this limitation, allowing gradients to reach and optimize early parts of the generation trajectory.

2. **Reducing Computational Cost (Memory and Time):** Direct backpropagation through an entire multi-step generation trajectory incurs prohibitive memory costs because all intermediate activations must be stored. This makes fine-tuning large flow matching models on long trajectories impractical. The authors are motivated to reduce this computational overhead, specifically memory usage, to make direct-gradient fine-tuning more feasible.

3. **Mitigating Gradient Instability (Gradient Explosion):** Propagating gradients through numerous sequential steps in a long trajectory can lead to gradient explosion, destabilizing the optimization process. Previous methods often resort to simplifying assumptions, such as updating only one or a few late steps, or stopping gradients at model inputs, which may discard valuable learning signals. The research aims to develop a mechanism that stabilizes gradient flow, allowing for multi-step updates without encountering explosion.

4. **Preserving Useful Gradient Signals:** Some existing methods, like DRTune, address gradient explosion by completely removing the "nested gradient" term, which captures interactions and dependencies across different timesteps. The authors hypothesize that these nested gradients contain useful learning signals. A motivation is to devise a method that can control gradient magnitude while *retaining* the full gradient structure, including these nested terms, to enable more complete and effective optimization.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap in direct-gradient fine-tuning for flow matching models, allowing for comprehensive optimization across the entire generation process to improve image quality and alignment with human preferences, especially for complex compositional tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LeapAlign introduces a post-training fine-tuning method characterized by three main components: leap trajectory construction, gradient discounting, and trajectory-similarity weighting.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

At each training iteration, the method first generates a full image from noise using standard ODE sampling. From this full trajectory, two timesteps, `k` and `j` (where `k > j`), are randomly selected. These selected timesteps are then used to construct a two-step "leap trajectory" for backpropagation. The fine-tuning objective is to maximize the expected reward of the generated images, enhanced by a trajectory-similarity weighting scheme. A gradient discounting mechanism is applied to stabilize backpropagation through the leap trajectory.

#### 3.2. Leap Trajectory Construction

The core of LeapAlign is shortening the long generation path into a two-step leap trajectory.
- **One-step Leap Prediction:** Rectified flow matching models allow for a one-step leap prediction, where a future latent `x_j` can be estimated from a current latent `x_k` using the model's predicted velocity field `v_θ(x_k)`: `ˆx_j|k = x_k - (k - j)v_θ(x_k)`.
- **Two-Step Trajectory:** LeapAlign constructs two consecutive one-step leaps. The first leap predicts `ˆx_j|k` from `x_k`, and the second leap predicts `ˆx_0|j` from `x_j`.
- **Random Timestep Selection:** The timesteps `k` and `j` are randomly selected from the full generation trajectory. This enables the model to update parameters contributing to *any* generation step, including early ones crucial for global image structure.
- **Latent Connectors:** To maintain differentiability and align the predicted latent states with the actual ones from the full trajectory, "latent connectors" are introduced. For instance, `x_j = ˆx_j|k + stop_gradient(x_j - ˆx_j|k)`. This allows gradients to flow through the `v_θ` predictions while anchoring the trajectory to the actual states.
- **Benefits:** This two-step structure reduces memory cost to a constant level and permits efficient gradient backpropagation to early steps.

#### 3.3. Gradient Discounting

While leap trajectories reduce gradient length, backpropagating through two flow matching steps can still result in larger gradients, particularly from the "nested gradient" term.
- **Nested Gradient:** The full gradient `∂x_0/∂θ` (derived in Appendix G) consists of single-step gradients from `x_k` and `x_j`, and a nested gradient term `αj(k - j)(∂v_θ(x_j)/∂x_j)(∂v_θ(x_k)/∂θ)`. This nested term captures interactions across different timesteps.
- **Comparison to DRTune:** Previous work like DRTune addresses this by stopping the gradient at the model input, effectively removing the nested gradient.
- **LeapAlign's Approach:** LeapAlign proposes "gradient discounting." Instead of removal, it modifies the second leap prediction: `ˆx_0|j = x_j - jv_θ(αx_j + (1 - α) stop_gradient(x_j))`. This scales the nested gradient term by a factor `α ∈`, moderating its magnitude while preserving its learning signal. An empirically chosen `α` (e.g., 0.3) helps stabilize optimization.

#### 3.4. Fine-Tuning Objective

The objective is to maximize the reward `r(x_0)` of the generated image `x_0`.
- **Hinge-style Loss:** The chosen objective is a hinge-style loss: `L_raw = max(0, λ - r(x_0))`, where `λ` is a threshold. This encourages rewards above `λ` without driving them excessively high, which can lead to reward hacking.
- **Reward Evaluation on Final Image:** Unlike some direct-gradient methods that estimate rewards from intermediate leap predictions (e.g., `ˆx_0|j`), LeapAlign evaluates the reward directly on the *actual* final generated image `x_0`. This provides a more faithful assessment of visual and semantic quality, yielding more reliable supervision.

#### 3.5. Trajectory-Similarity Weighting

To ensure that gradients originate from meaningful trajectories, LeapAlign introduces a weighting mechanism:
- **Similarity Metric:** The similarity `w_sim` is calculated based on the average absolute difference between the predicted (`ˆx`) and actual (`x`) latent states at the two connection points (`x_j|k` vs `x_j` and `x_0|j` vs `x_0`). Distances are clamped with a minimum value `τ` to prevent over-emphasis of near-identical pairs.
- **Weighted Loss:** The final objective is `L = stop_gradient(w_sim) L_raw`. This mechanism assigns higher importance to leap trajectories that closely mirror the original full generation dynamics, thereby reducing the influence of potentially misleading gradient signals from highly deviating short trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LeapAlign was evaluated on general preference alignment and compositional alignment tasks, fine-tuning the FLUX.1-dev model and, in additional experiments, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium.

#### 4.1. General Preference Alignment

- **Overall Superiority:** LeapAlign consistently outperformed state-of-the-art GRPO-based methods (DanceGRPO, MixGRPO) and direct-gradient methods (ReFL, DRaFT-LV, DRTune) across multiple automatic evaluators (HPSv2.1, HPSv3, PickScore, UnifiedReward-Alignment, UnifiedReward-IQ, ImageReward).
 - For instance, on Flux fine-tuned with HPSv2.1, LeapAlign achieved an HPSv2.1 score of 0.4092, compared to 0.3882 for DRTune and 0.3692 for MixGRPO.
 - It demonstrated the highest average scores across most in-domain and out-of-domain metrics.
- **Robustness to Reward Models and Prompt Sets:** The method maintained its leading performance when fine-tuned with different reward models (PickScore on HPDv2, HPSv3 on MJHQ-30k) and evaluated on corresponding test sets, confirming its generality.
- **Generality Across Models:** LeapAlign also showed superior performance when applied to Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium, indicating its applicability beyond Flux.

#### 4.2. Compositional Alignment

- **GenEval Benchmark Performance:** LeapAlign significantly improved scores on the GenEval benchmark, which assesses compositional generation tasks. It achieved an overall GenEval score of 0.7420, surpassing MixGRPO (0.7232) and DRTune (0.7101).
- **Strong Gains in Key Categories:** Notable improvements were observed in categories such as 'two objects', 'colors', 'position', and 'attribute binding', which are critical for accurate compositional generation and often depend on early-stage feature development.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Qualitative comparisons illustrated that images generated by LeapAlign exhibited substantially modified global structures and compositions that more faithfully adhered to text prompts, in contrast to methods like ReFL and DRaFT-LV, which largely retained the base model's layouts.

#### 4.3. Fine-tuning Dynamics

- **Reward Growth:** Fine-tuning reward curves (e.g., HPSv2.1 and GenEval scores) showed that LeapAlign achieved faster and higher reward gains compared to DRTune and other direct-gradient methods.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Component Analysis

- **Gradient Discounting (α):** Setting the discounting factor `α` to 0.3 yielded the best performance. Removing the nested gradient (`α=0`) resulted in lower scores, while no discounting (`α=1`) led to instability. This indicates that the nested gradient is beneficial when its magnitude is appropriately controlled.
- **Trajectory-Similarity Weighting:** The proposed weighting mechanism, considering similarity at both connection points (`d_j` and `d_0`), provided the best performance, validating its role in emphasizing consistent leap trajectories.
- **Number of Leap Steps:** Two-step leap trajectories offered the optimal trade-off between performance and memory usage, outperforming both one-step and three-step variants.
- **Training Timestep Range:** Randomly selecting timesteps `k` and `j` across the full range (from noise to image) resulted in better GenEval scores than restricting selection to a later range like [0, 1/2], highlighting the importance of early-step fine-tuning.
- **Reward Model Input:** Using the actual generated image `x_0` as input to the reward model yielded superior results compared to using an estimated intermediate latent `ˆx_0|j`, reinforcing the importance of accurate reward evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LeapAlign contributes to the field of generative model alignment by addressing critical challenges in fine-tuning flow matching models with human preferences. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Comprehensive Optimization:** The most significant contribution is the ability to propagate reward gradients stably and efficiently to *any* generation step, including the crucial early steps that dictate global image composition and layout. This overcomes a long-standing limitation of many direct-gradient methods, which often neglect early steps due to computational and stability issues. This leads to more holistic model optimization.

2. **Improved Generative Quality and Alignment:** By effectively fine-tuning all generation stages, LeapAlign consistently yields superior image quality and stronger image-text alignment. This is evidenced by its leading performance across a wide range of quantitative metrics (HPSv2.1, HPSv3, PickScore, UnifiedReward, ImageReward, GenEval) and qualitative results that demonstrate enhanced compositional fidelity. This directly benefits applications requiring high-quality, precisely controlled image generation.

3. **Computational Efficiency and Stability:** The method achieves its comprehensive optimization without incurring prohibitive memory costs, thanks to its two-step leap trajectory design. The introduction of gradient discounting also stabilizes training by moderating the potentially large nested gradients, preventing explosion while preserving valuable learning signals that other methods might discard. This makes direct-gradient fine-tuning more practical for large-scale flow matching models.

4. **Generality and Robustness:** LeapAlign's effectiveness is demonstrated across different base flow matching models (Flux, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium), various differentiable reward models (HPSv2.1, PickScore, HPSv3), and diverse prompt datasets. This indicates a robust and broadly applicable fine-tuning framework.

5. **Advancing Direct-Gradient Methods:** The work provides key insights into the mechanics of direct gradient propagation in flow matching models, particularly concerning the role of nested gradients and strategies for managing their magnitude. This can inform the design of future direct-gradient-based alignment techniques for other generative models.

In terms of potential impact, LeapAlign can empower researchers and practitioners to create more controllable and aesthetically pleasing text-to-image models. Its principles might also be extended to other continuous-time generative processes, such as video generation, as suggested by the authors. The ability to precisely steer generative models towards human preferences across the entire generation trajectory opens avenues for more sophisticated content creation, artistic applications, and personalized user experiences.
