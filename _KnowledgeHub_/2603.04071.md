# SaFeR: Safety-Critical Scenario Generation for Autonomous Driving Test via Feasibility-Constrained Token Resampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harbin Institute of Technology and Chongqing Changan Automobile Company developed SaFeR, a framework for generating autonomous driving test scenarios. It combines a realism-focused motion model with a feasibility-constrained adversarial resampling approach, producing scenarios that are challenging yet solvable and kinematically realistic.

## Problem

- Existing simulation-based scenario generators struggle to simultaneously achieve adversarial criticality, physical feasibility, and behavioral realism for autonomous driving tests.
- Realism-focused generators underrepresent rare, safety-critical events, which are crucial for thoroughly stress-testing autonomous driving systems.
- Adversarial methods often generate scenarios with inevitable collisions, rendering them physically implausible or invalid for evaluating an autonomous vehicle's decision-making capabilities.

## Method

- The framework formulates scenario generation as a discrete next-token prediction problem, optimizing critical background vehicle tokens to maximize collision probability under realism and feasibility constraints.
- A realism prior is developed using a high-fidelity next-token prediction model, featuring a Multi-Head Differential Attention (MDA) mechanism to model realistic human-like behaviors and filter attention noise.
- A feasibility constraint is integrated based on the Largest Feasible Region (LFR), which is approximated via offline reinforcement learning to ensure generated scenarios are theoretically avoidable by a competent ego policy.

## Results

- SaFeR's realism prior achieved a superior realism score of 0.7730 on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD), outperforming baseline models across kinematic, interactive, and map-based metrics.
- The framework demonstrated a high Solution Rate (SR) of 0.865 on WOMD, ensuring generated scenarios are theoretically solvable, while maintaining a robust Collision Rate (CR) of 0.761, indicating effective adversarial conflict generation.
- SaFeR produced kinematically realistic adversarial behaviors, evidenced by the lowest Jensen-Shannon Divergence values for velocity (VJ 0.161) and acceleration (AJ 0.499) compared to other state-of-the-art methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework can successfully balance adversarial criticality, physical feasibility, and behavioral realism by integrating a learned realism prior with a feasibility-aware adversarial resampling mechanism.
> - The Multi-Head Differential Attention (MDA) mechanism significantly improves the fidelity of traffic behavior modeling by effectively mitigating attention noise in dense multi-agent environments.
> - Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) analysis, when approximated by offline reinforcement learning, provides a robust and dynamic measure for distinguishing between avoidable and unavoidable collision risks, enabling the generation of solvable critical scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The large-scale deployment of Autonomous Driving Systems (ADS) necessitates rigorous safety verification, a process often limited by the cost, efficiency, and inherent safety concerns of real-world testing, particularly for rare but hazardous events. Consequently, simulation-based testing has emerged as a scalable alternative. The efficacy of this approach heavily relies on the ability to systematically generate realistic and challenging scenarios.

Recent advancements in data-driven scenario generation have enhanced the realism of simulated traffic behaviors. Approaches leveraging large-scale motion datasets and paradigms such as diffusion models, continuous distribution regression, and next-token prediction have demonstrated proficiency in modeling naturalistic human driving distributions. However, when these realism-oriented generators are directly applied to safety testing, they tend to underrepresent safety-critical events, as collision-prone interactions are inherently infrequent in naturalistic datasets.

To address this imbalance, a segment of research focuses on safety-critical scenario generation by explicitly inducing adversarial behaviors from background agents. Methods in this area include adversarial trajectory perturbation, gradient-based perturbations, reinforcement learning, guided diffusion, and reverse generation. While effective in increasing collision rates, these approaches often encounter a fundamental trade-off: pursuing high criticality can lead to physically implausible or theoretically inevitable collisions, rendering the scenarios invalid for evaluating an autonomous vehicle's decision-making capabilities. Conversely, overly conservative feasibility constraints can limit adversarial strength, resulting in scenarios that are feasible but insufficiently challenging.

This tension highlights a core challenge in the field: the synthesis of scenarios that are simultaneously adversarial, high-fidelity (realistic), and physically feasible. The current work, SaFeR, aims to bridge this gap by proposing a unified framework that explicitly addresses this three-fold objective, moving beyond approaches that often prioritize one aspect at the expense of others.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for generating safety-critical scenarios for autonomous driving tests that effectively balances three conflicting criteria: adversarial criticality, physical feasibility, and behavioral realism. The authors identify a "core challenge" in achieving these objectives simultaneously within a unified framework.

Specifically, a valid safety-critical scenario is defined as one that:
1. **Induces high collision risk** when the ego vehicle executes a non-reactive (e.g., log-replay) policy. This ensures the scenario is genuinely challenging and probes the limits of an autonomous system.
2. **Remains theoretically solvable** by a competent ego policy. This criterion ensures that the generated scenario represents a *test* of the ego vehicle's capabilities rather than an unavoidable accident, which would not yield meaningful safety insights.
3. **Preserves realistic, human-like motion patterns**. This ensures that the scenarios are representative of real-world driving conditions and that the adversarial behaviors are credible and interpretable by human operators or safety engineers.

Existing methods often struggle to achieve all three. Unconstrained adversarial optimization can generate scenarios with inevitable collisions, which are physically implausible or impossible for an ego vehicle to avoid, thus invalidating their utility for system evaluation. Conversely, strict feasibility constraints can dilute the adversarial nature of scenarios, making them less challenging. Realism-focused generators, while producing naturalistic behaviors, inherently underrepresent rare, safety-critical events.

The motivation behind SaFeR is to resolve this fundamental trade-off. The authors propose integrating a learned realism prior with a feasibility-aware adversarial resampling mechanism to create a framework capable of synthesizing scenarios that are challenging, solvable, and realistic. This is crucial for developing robust and safe autonomous driving systems through comprehensive and valid simulation-based testing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SaFeR framework addresses safety-critical scenario generation by formulating traffic generation as a discrete next-token prediction problem, integrating a realism prior with a feasibility-constrained token resampling strategy.

**A. Problem Formulation**
A driving scenario is defined by map information and the temporal evolution of states for all traffic participants. The trajectory generation is reformed into a discrete sequence modeling task where the action space (acceleration `a` and yaw rate `ψ`) is discretized into a finite vocabulary of motion tokens. The generation of a scenario then becomes a product of conditional probabilities of these tokens over time. The objective for safety-critical scenario generation is to maximize the probability of a collision event involving the ego vehicle. This global objective is approximated via sequential token-level optimization, where the critical background vehicle (CBV) token at each time step is chosen to maximize the posterior collision probability, conditioned on a realism prior.

**B. Realism Prior Modeling**
A high-fidelity scenario generation model serves as the realism prior, learning the naturalistic driving distribution.
1. **Motion Tokenization:** Acceleration `a` ([-5, 5] m/s²) and yaw rate `ψ` ([-1.5, 1.5] rad/s) are discretized into 63 bins each, forming a 63x63 motion token vocabulary.
2. **Scene Encoder:** Processes map and agent state information (coordinates, velocity, yaw, length, width) to generate context embeddings and agent embeddings.
3. **Motion Decoder with Multi-Head Differential Attention (MDA):** To mitigate attention noise prevalent in standard Transformers in dense traffic, the motion decoder uses a novel MDA mechanism.
 - **Factorized Interaction Modeling:** MDA sequentially models temporal interactions (agent's own history), agent-agent interactions, and agent-map interactions. Differences in coordinates, yaws, and time are aggregated for temporal and agent-agent attention.
 - **Noise-Cancellation Property:** MDA employs a pair of parallel softmax functions. It subtracts a secondary attention map (capturing uniform noise) from the primary attention map, modulated by a learnable scalar `λ`. This design aims to filter out spurious correlations and focus on relevant interactions. `λ` is re-parameterized to synchronize learning dynamics.
 - The final output from the differential fusion block is passed through a softmax layer to obtain token probabilities, which are then converted to next states using a kinematic bicycle model.

**C. Feasibility Constraint Modeling**
To prevent the generation of inevitable collisions, SaFeR introduces a feasibility constraint based on the Largest Feasible Region (LFR).
1. **Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) Analysis:** The LFR is derived using HJR analysis. A constraint violation function `h(s_t)` is defined, indicating safety status based on the minimum distance between bounding boxes of the ego and CBV.
2. **Optimal Feasible Value Functions:** The optimal feasible state-value function `V*_h(s)` and action-value function `Q*_h(s, a)` are defined as the minimum of the maximum future constraint violation under an optimal driving policy. The zero-sublevel set of `V*_h(s)` defines the LFR, representing states from which the ego vehicle can theoretically avoid a collision.
3. **LFR Approximation:** Directly calculating `V*_h(s)` is computationally intensive and dependent on ego policy. Therefore, `V*_h(s)` is approximated using a neural network trained via offline reinforcement learning. An expectile regression approach is employed to learn the feasibility action-value function `Q_h(s, a; ϕ)` and state-value function `V_h(s; ω)`. This method decouples policy from value estimation and handles distribution shifts in offline data. The training objective minimizes `L_V_h` (for `V_h`) and `L_Q_h` (for `Q_h`), where `Q_h` targets a combination of the immediate constraint violation and the future value function.
 - Interaction data for training is collected from various ego planner configurations (e.g., SMART, DiffusionPlanner) and critical background vehicle models (NTP-based, SafeSim) to ensure broad coverage of naturalistic and safety-critical conflict patterns.

**D. Safety-Critical Token Resampling Strategy**
Leveraging the pre-trained realism prior `P_θ` and feasibility value function `V_h`, a two-stage constrained search is implemented to resample adversarial actions `w_cbv_t` for the critical background vehicle.
1. **Stage 1: Trust Region Construction:** To maintain human-like behavior, the search space for adversarial tokens is limited to `W_top-n`, the `n` most probable tokens predicted by the realism prior `P_θ`. This ensures selected tokens are inherently realistic.
2. **Stage 2: LFR-Guided Token Resampling:** Within `W_top-n`, a token `w*` is sought that maximizes adversarial criticality without violating the feasibility constraint.
 - An adversarial loss function `L(w_cbv_t)` dynamically switches objectives:
 - If the ego vehicle remains within the LFR (i.e., `V_h(s*_t+1) ≤ 0`), the loss minimizes the Euclidean distance between vehicles, increasing criticality.
 - If a candidate token pushes the ego vehicle outside the LFR (i.e., `V_h(s*_t+1) > 0`), the loss heavily penalizes this violation by adding a large constant margin `Δ`, shifting the objective to feasibility recovery (minimizing `V_h`).
 - The optimal adversarial token `w*_cbv_t` is then selected by minimizing this dynamic loss function within the trust region. This strategy aims to generate scenarios that are adversarial, high-fidelity, and feasible, avoiding theoretically inevitable accidents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental results validating SaFeR's performance in terms of realism, feasibility, and criticality.

**A. Realism Evaluation**
The NTP-based scenario generation model (which forms SaFeR's realism prior) was evaluated against several open-source approaches (diffusion models, continuous distribution regression, and other next-token prediction models) on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) and nuPlan.
- **Superior Realism:** SaFeR's model outperformed all baselines across a realism meta-metric, as well as individual kinematic, interactive, and map-based metrics on both datasets (Table II). For instance, on WOMD, SaFeR achieved a realism score of 0.7730, compared to the next best at 0.7644 (Diffusion).
- **MDA Contribution:** The Multi-Head Differential Attention (MDA) mechanism was identified as a key driver for improvements, particularly in the Interactive and Map-based metrics, indicating its effectiveness in isolating critical interaction cues in complex environments. This enhanced fidelity establishes a robust realism prior for the subsequent adversarial resampling.

**B. Feasibility Evaluation**
The physical consistency of the learned feasibility value function `V_h(s)` was validated through visualization of the Largest Feasible Region (LFR) decision boundaries.
- **Dynamic LFR:** The visualizations (Fig. 3) demonstrated that the infeasible region expanded monotonically with increasing critical background vehicle (CBV) velocity. Higher approach speeds reduced the temporal and spatial windows for ego vehicle evasion, confirming the capture of dynamic collision risks.
- **Anisotropic Properties:** The morphology of the infeasible region exhibited anisotropic behavior, deforming and rotating in alignment with the velocity vectors of interacting agents. The boundaries extended further along the longitudinal direction, consistent with vehicle braking distances and non-holonomic constraints.
- **Physical Grounding:** These results indicate that the offline-learned `V_h(s)` effectively models the complex, non-linear relationship between vehicle dynamics and collision risks, providing a physically grounded metric for distinguishing between avoidable and unavoidable interactions.

**C. Criticality Evaluation**
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4) illustrated SaFeR's ability to generate adversarial scenarios (e.g., sharp cut-ins, aggressive intersection maneuvers, sudden pull-outs) that forced ego vehicle reactions, while maintaining kinematically smooth and feasible trajectories. The Trust-Region Constraint was observed to preserve the naturalism of human driving behaviors while maximizing criticality.
- **Quantitative Results:** SaFeR was compared against state-of-the-art safety-critical scenario generation models (gradient-based, feasibility-guided, reverse generation, diffusion-based) using a dual-stage evaluation protocol.
 - **Balanced Performance:** SaFeR achieved the highest Solution Rate (SR), demonstrating that its LFR constraint effectively filters out infeasible scenarios. For example, on WOMD, SaFeR achieved an SR of 0.865, significantly higher than other methods like ADV-BMT (0.324).
 - **Strong Adversarial Effectiveness:** Concurrently, SaFeR maintained a robust Collision Rate (CR) (0.761 on WOMD), indicating effective generation of adversarial conflicts. This contrasted with methods like ADV-BMT, which achieved a high CR but a low SR, suggesting a majority of their generated collisions were unavoidable.
 - **Superior Kinematic Realism:** SaFeR also demonstrated the lowest Jensen-Shannon Divergence values for velocity (VJ) and acceleration (AJ) compared to baselines, confirming that the adversarial behaviors generated were kinematically realistic and consistent with naturalistic driving distributions (e.g., VJ of 0.161 and AJ of 0.499 on WOMD).

**D. Ablation Studies**
- **MDA and LFR Contribution:** An ablation study (Table IV) confirmed the individual contributions of MDA and the LFR constraint. Removing LFR led to the highest CR but a severe drop in SR (0.527), indicating inevitable collisions. Removing MDA degraded both SR (0.720) and kinematic realism (VJ 0.205, AJ 0.519). The complete SaFeR framework achieved the highest SR and superior kinematic fidelity while maintaining robust adversarial criticality, validating both components' necessity.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Analysis of the penalty constant M and safety distance threshold `d_th` (Fig. 5) for LFR training showed optimal performance when the mean of `V_h` stabilized closest to zero (`d_th = 0.3`, `M = 16`), ensuring accurate feasibility boundary approximation.
- **Trust Region Size:** Varying the trust region size `n` (Table V) indicated that `n = 20` provided the optimal balance between adversarial capability (CR) and behavioral realism (VJ, AJ). Larger `n` values (e.g., 50, 100) increased CR marginally but caused a sharp degradation in realism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SaFeR framework represents a notable advancement in the field of autonomous driving safety verification by addressing the complex challenge of simultaneously achieving adversarial criticality, physical feasibility, and behavioral realism in generated test scenarios.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Unified Framework for Scenario Generation:** SaFeR provides a principled, unified approach that reconciles the often-conflicting objectives of generating challenging, solvable, and realistic scenarios. This moves beyond previous methods that tended to prioritize one aspect, often at the expense of others, potentially leading to less informative or invalid safety evaluations.
- **Enhanced Realism through Differential Attention:** The introduction of the Multi-Head Differential Attention (MDA) mechanism within the realism prior contributes to more accurate and naturalistic traffic behavior modeling. By effectively mitigating attention noise in dense environments, MDA allows the model to focus on critical interaction cues, thereby generating more high-fidelity and believable motion patterns.
- **Robust Feasibility Guarantees:** The integration of a feasibility constraint derived from the Largest Feasible Region (LFR) and approximated via offline reinforcement learning is a critical contribution. This mechanism ensures that generated adversarial scenarios, while challenging, are theoretically avoidable by a competent ego policy, thus preventing the creation of "inevitable collision" scenarios that hold limited value for testing an ADS's decision-making capabilities.
- **Effective Adversarial Resampling:** The two-stage token resampling strategy combines the realism prior with the LFR constraint, providing a systematic method to induce adversarial behaviors within a human-like trust region. This allows for the generation of scenarios that actively stress test an ADS without resorting to unrealistic or impossible maneuvers.

The potential impact of SaFeR includes:
- **Improved Safety Verification:** By generating more relevant, challenging, yet solvable, and realistic test cases, SaFeR can significantly enhance the effectiveness and reliability of simulation-based safety verification for autonomous driving systems. This enables more thorough stress testing of ego vehicle policies under conditions likely to be encountered in the real world, including rare hazardous events.
- **Accelerated Development and Deployment:** The ability to systematically generate high-quality safety-critical scenarios in simulation can reduce the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and potentially dangerous real-world testing. This acceleration in the testing cycle could contribute to faster and safer development and deployment of autonomous vehicles.
- **Greater Confidence in ADS Performance:** By rigorously evaluating ADS performance against a diverse and valid set of safety-critical scenarios, SaFeR can contribute to building greater confidence in the safety and robustness of these systems, which is essential for public acceptance and regulatory approval.
