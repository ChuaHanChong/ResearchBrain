# SaFeR: Safety-Critical Scenario Generation for Autonomous Driving Test via Feasibility-Constrained Token Resampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harbin Institute of Technology and Chongqing Changan Automobile Company developed SaFeR, a framework for generating autonomous driving test scenarios. It combines a realism-focused motion model with a feasibility-constrained adversarial resampling approach, producing scenarios that are challenging yet solvable and kinematically realistic.

## Problem

- Existing simulation-based scenario generators struggle to simultaneously achieve adversarial criticality, physical feasibility, and behavioral realism for autonomous driving tests.
- Realism-focused generators underrepresent rare, safety-critical events, which are crucial for thoroughly stress-testing autonomous driving systems.
- Adversarial methods often generate scenarios with inevitable collisions, rendering them physically implausible or invalid for evaluating an autonomous vehicle's decision-making capabilities.

## Method

- The framework formulates scenario generation as a discrete next-token prediction problem, optimizing critical background vehicle tokens to maximize collision probability under realism and feasibility constraints.
- A realism prior is developed using a high-fidelity next-token prediction model, featuring a Multi-Head Differential Attention (MDA) mechanism to model realistic human-like behaviors and filter attention noise.
- A feasibility constraint is integrated based on the Largest Feasible Region (LFR), which is approximated via offline reinforcement learning to ensure generated scenarios are theoretically avoidable by a competent ego policy.

## Results

- SaFeR's realism prior achieved a superior realism score of 0.7730 on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD), outperforming baseline models across kinematic, interactive, and map-based metrics.
- The framework demonstrated a high Solution Rate (SR) of 0.865 on WOMD, ensuring generated scenarios are theoretically solvable, while maintaining a robust Collision Rate (CR) of 0.761, indicating effective adversarial conflict generation.
- SaFeR produced kinematically realistic adversarial behaviors, evidenced by the lowest Jensen-Shannon Divergence values for velocity (VJ 0.161) and acceleration (AJ 0.499) compared to other state-of-the-art methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework can successfully balance adversarial criticality, physical feasibility, and behavioral realism by integrating a learned realism prior with a feasibility-aware adversarial resampling mechanism.
> - The Multi-Head Differential Attention (MDA) mechanism significantly improves the fidelity of traffic behavior modeling by effectively mitigating attention noise in dense multi-agent environments.
> - Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) analysis, when approximated by offline reinforcement learning, provides a robust and dynamic measure for distinguishing between avoidable and unavoidable collision risks, enabling the generation of solvable critical scenarios.
