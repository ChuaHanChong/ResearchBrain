# Model-Based Diffusion Sampling for Predictive Control in Offline Decision Making

> [!summary] Summary
> MPDiffuser introduces a model-based compositional diffusion framework for offline decision-making that integrates a diffusion planner with a specialized dynamics model to ensure generated trajectories are both task-aligned and dynamically plausible. This system demonstrates improved performance on D4RL and DSRL benchmarks, achieves efficient adaptation to novel system dynamics, and shows real-world applicability for robot locomotion.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion-based methods for offline decision-making often generate dynamically implausible or infeasible trajectories, compromising reliability and safety.
- These methods struggle to effectively leverage suboptimal or heterogeneous data, potentially reproducing undesirable behaviors.
- There is a lack of robust mechanisms in current generative models to ensure both task alignment and dynamic plausibility, especially in constrained settings where policies must satisfy stringent safety constraints.

## Method

- MPDiffuser proposes a compositional framework comprising a conditional diffusion planner, a conditional diffusion dynamics model, and a flexible ranker, all trained independently.
- It employs an alternating diffusion sampling scheme that interleaves updates from the planner (for task alignment) and the dynamics model (for dynamic feasibility) during the reverse diffusion process.
- A ranking module evaluates candidate trajectories based on cumulative reward and cost, allowing for selection of the most suitable trajectory to satisfy task objectives and safety budgets.

## Results

- MPDiffuser consistently outperformed prior diffusion-based methods and other offline RL baselines on D4RL (unconstrained) and DSRL (constrained) benchmarks.
- The framework efficiently adapted to novel dynamics, such as a simulated hardware defect, by fine-tuning only the dynamics diffusion model, leading to substantial performance recovery and improvement.
- Successful real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot demonstrated improved velocity tracking and higher normalized return compared to a PPO policy, affirming its practicality and scalability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Alternating updates between a task-conditioned planner and a specialized dynamics model effectively combine task fidelity with dynamic plausibility, mitigating issues of gradient interference encountered with direct score combination.
> - Decoupling the dynamics model allows it to learn robust system transitions from diverse (including suboptimal) data independently of the planner, enhancing overall efficiency and adaptability to novel system dynamics.
> - Integrating a flexible ranking module with dynamically feasible trajectories is crucial for robust constrained decision-making, enabling effective balancing of safety and reward objectives and outperforming post-hoc projection methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence and machine learning is increasingly focused on developing decision-making policies that are both effective in achieving goals and reliable in their execution, particularly in complex and safety-critical real-world scenarios. Classical control methods often rely on explicit modeling and optimization, which can be computationally intensive and limited by modeling assumptions (Rawlings et al., 2017). In contrast, recent research has explored data-driven generative models to learn complex behaviors from collected data, offering an appealing alternative when direct interaction with the environment is costly or unavailable (Janner et al., 2022; Ajay et al., 2023).

Within this data-driven paradigm, offline decision-making, where policies are learned solely from fixed datasets without further environmental interaction, has gained prominence. Generative models, especially diffusion models, have been applied to synthesize candidate state-action trajectories (Janner et al., 2022; Ajay et al., 2023; He et al., 2023). However, existing diffusion-based methods in offline reinforcement learning (RL) face several limitations. They often struggle to effectively leverage suboptimal or heterogeneous data, leading to the reproduction of undesirable behaviors (Hester et al., 2018). More critically, these methods typically operate directly in data space without explicit mechanisms to enforce system dynamics consistency. This oversight results in sampled trajectories that may be dynamically implausible or infeasible, thereby limiting their reliability and safety guarantees during real-world deployment (Garcia & Fernandez, 2015; Zhou et al., 2025). Some approaches use inverse dynamics models (Ajay et al., 2023) or post-hoc forward models (Zhou et al., 2025) to address this, but these often introduce brittleness or do not integrate dynamics deeply into the generation process.

In domains such as robotics, healthcare, and autonomous driving, policies must satisfy stringent safety constraints alongside task objectives. Enforcing these constraints offline without interaction is particularly challenging, as traditional safe RL methods can fail under distribution shifts. This work positions itself by identifying a critical gap: the lack of robust mechanisms in diffusion-based generative models for ensuring both task alignment and dynamic plausibility of synthesized trajectories in offline decision-making, especially in constrained settings. MPDiffuser aims to bridge this gap by introducing a compositional framework that actively incorporates system dynamics during the trajectory generation process, moving beyond purely data-driven synthesis to integrate model-based feasibility checks. It draws inspiration from model predictive control (MPC) by performing short-horizon planning, but leverages the generative capabilities of diffusion models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central challenge addressed by this research is the generation of reliable and effective policies for offline decision-making using diffusion models. Specifically, the authors identify that trajectories produced by existing diffusion models often suffer from misalignment with true system dynamics, which significantly compromises their utility and safety in control applications.

The motivation for developing Model Predictive Diffuser (MPDiffuser) stems from several key observations and requirements:

- **Reliability and Safety:** In many real-world applications (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving, healthcare), policies must not only achieve task goals but also guarantee safety by adhering to physical constraints. Current generative models, lacking explicit dynamics enforcement, provide limited reliability and weak safety guarantees, especially when learned from suboptimal or heterogeneous data that might contain undesirable behaviors.
- **Dynamics Consistency:** Existing diffusion-based trajectory generation methods, such as Diffuser and Decision Diffuser, primarily focus on sampling trajectories that resemble those in the training dataset. They do not intrinsically enforce the underlying physics of the system. This can lead to "hallucinated" trajectories that look plausible but are dynamically infeasible or unsafe when executed in the real environment.
- **Effective Use of Diverse Data:** Generative models can struggle to effectively leverage diverse datasets, particularly those containing suboptimal or random interactions. There is a need for a framework that can exploit low-quality data to learn system dynamics without compromising the ability to generate high-quality, task-aligned behaviors.
- **Adaptability to Novel Dynamics:** Real-world systems can experience changes in their dynamics (e.g., wear and tear in a robot). A robust decision-making framework should be able to adapt efficiently to such changes without requiring complete retraining of the entire policy.
- **Integration of Task Objectives and Constraints:** For constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs), policies must maximize rewards while satisfying cost budgets. A flexible mechanism is needed to integrate these multi-objective considerations, including the ability to balance safety and performance.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the MPDiffuser framework are:

1. **Generate Task-Aligned Trajectories:** To produce diverse state-action sequences that effectively pursue specified task objectives.
2. **Ensure Dynamic Plausibility and Feasibility:** To actively correct and refine proposed trajectories to be consistent with the true system dynamics throughout the generation process, enhancing reliability and safety.
3. **Support Constrained Decision-Making:** To provide a mechanism for generating trajectories that adhere to explicit safety budgets and cost constraints.
4. **Improve Sample Efficiency and Data Utilization:** To enable the dynamics model to learn from and leverage diverse, potentially low-quality, or previously unseen data independently of the planner, thereby improving overall efficiency and adaptability.
5. **Demonstrate Real-World Practicality:** To validate the framework's scalability to visual domains and its deployment capabilities on physical robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MPDiffuser proposes a model-based compositional diffusion framework for offline decision-making, which integrates three distinct components: a diffusion planner, a diffusion dynamics model, and a ranker. These components are trained independently and combined during inference through an alternating sampling scheme.

#### 3.1. Framework Components

- **Planner Model:** This component acts as the primary trajectory generator. It is implemented as a conditional diffusion model that learns to generate diverse state-action sequences, $\tau = (x_{1:H}, u_{0:H-1})$, conditioned on an initial state ($x_0$) and a task-specific conditioning vector ($y(\tau)$) (e.g., target return, goal). The planner is trained using a denoising score matching objective, aiming to capture multi-modal intent, task structure, and dataset priors over a planning horizon $H$. Classifier-free guidance is employed during training and inference to enforce conditional generation.

- **Dynamics Model:** This component focuses on ensuring the dynamic feasibility of generated state trajectories. Unlike the planner, it is a conditional diffusion model that operates exclusively on states. It is trained to predict state transitions conditioned on the initial state, an action sequence ($\tau_u$), and the conditioning vector ($y(\tau)$). By treating actions purely as inputs, the dynamics model specializes in capturing the system's underlying physics, aiming to provide a sharper signal for dynamics consistency compared to a model jointly predicting states and actions.

- **Ranker:** The ranker is a flexible, task-aware module responsible for evaluating and selecting the most suitable trajectories from a set of sampled candidates. It is defined as a scoring function, $\rho(\tau)$, which assigns preference values to trajectories based on desired criteria, such as maximizing reward or respecting safety budgets. The ranker can incorporate explicit domain knowledge or data-driven objectives and is crucial for integrating objectives and constraints.

#### 3.2. Alternating Diffusion Sampling

The core of MPDiffuser's trajectory generation process is an alternating diffusion sampling scheme (Algorithm 1). This procedure interleaves updates from the planner and the dynamics model during the reverse diffusion (denoising) process:

1. **Initialization:** The process starts from Gaussian noise for the state and action trajectories ($\tau_x^K, \tau_u^K$).
2. **Dynamics Step:** In each reverse step $k$ (from $K$ down to 1), the dynamics model first refines the state trajectory ($\tau_x^k$) conditioned on the current action sequence ($\tau_u^k$), the initial state ($x_0$), and the task condition ($y$). This step projects the states towards the manifold of feasible transitions. Classifier-free guidance is applied.
3. **Planner Step:** Subsequently, the planner jointly denoises both the state and action trajectories, incorporating the refined states from the dynamics step. This update restores task structure and dataset consistency. Classifier-free guidance is also applied here.
4. **Iteration:** This alternating process continues until the trajectories are fully denoised (k=0), yielding a task-aligned and dynamically plausible state-action sequence.

The alternating composition is motivated by an operator-splitting interpretation (Hairer et al., 2006). It functions as a dialogue: the planner proposes task-aligned expansions, and the dynamics model regularizes these proposals to maintain consistency with system transitions. This approach aims to balance task fidelity with dynamics feasibility in a stable manner, especially important when planner and dynamics gradients might differ in scale or curvature.

#### 3.3. Constrained Control with MPDiffuser

For constrained decision-making (Algorithm 2), MPDiffuser employs the following steps:

1. **Candidate Generation:** Multiple candidate trajectories are generated using the alternating diffusion sampling (Algorithm 1) from the current initial state and desired conditions.
2. **Evaluation:** Each candidate trajectory is evaluated for its cumulative reward and cumulative cost using learned reward and cost models (MLPs).
3. **Ranking and Selection:** The ranker filters these candidates, first identifying all "feasible" trajectories (those whose cumulative cost is below the remaining budget). If feasible trajectories exist, the one with the highest cumulative reward is selected. If no feasible trajectories are found, the ranker selects the trajectory with the minimum cumulative cost to ensure some level of safety.
4. **Flexibility:** The framework allows for flexible adaptation to different safety-reward tradeoffs by adjusting cost and return scaling parameters without necessitating retraining of the core diffusion models.

#### 3.4. Rationale Behind Algorithm Development

The theoretical justification for MPDiffuser's alternating sampling scheme involves formulating trajectory generation as a constrained optimization problem. The goal is to find a target distribution $q$ that minimizes the expectation of negative log likelihood under a dynamics distribution $p_{dyn}(\tau|x_0)$ while remaining close to the planner distribution $p_{pl}(\tau|x_0)$ in terms of KL divergence. The solution to this constrained problem is an exponential tilting of the planner distribution: $q^*(\tau|x_0) \propto p_{pl}(\tau|x_0) p_{dyn}(\tau|x_0)^\lambda$.

While directly sampling from $q^*$ is intractable, its score function $s_{q^*}$ can be approximated as a sum of individual scores: $s_{q^*}(\tau_k, k) \approx s_{p_{pl}}(\tau_k, k) + \lambda s_{p_{dyn}}(\tau_k, k)$. The alternating update scheme is presented as an approximation to sampling from this combined score, inspired by operator-splitting methods like Lie–Trotter splitting. This approach mitigates issues arising from gradient interference between planner and dynamics components that might occur with direct score combination. The dynamics model is argued to provide a more accurate local estimate of the true dynamics score due to its specialization, allowing the alternating updates to effectively correct the planner's biases towards dynamics consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MPDiffuser spanned various benchmarks and real-world scenarios, demonstrating its capabilities in unconstrained and constrained offline decision-making, dynamics consistency, adaptability, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. Offline Decision Making (D4RL Benchmarks)

- **Performance:** MPDiffuser consistently outperformed prior diffusion-based methods (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, D-MPC) and other offline RL baselines (BC, DT, COMBO, IDQL) on D4RL benchmark tasks (e.g., Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Kitchen). The alternating planner–dynamics sampling scheme was shown to produce trajectories better aligned with the dataset distribution and dynamics, leading to improved performance.
- **Ranking Module:** The inclusion of a ranking module (MPDiffuser+Rank), which selects among multiple candidate trajectories, provided a modest but consistent performance gain, particularly in tasks requiring longer-horizon planning like Kitchen, by selecting higher-return trajectories.

#### 4.2. Leveraging Random Data for Dynamics Learning

- **Efficiency:** In the FetchPickAndPlace task, training the dynamics model with additional random (suboptimal) trajectories significantly improved MPDiffuser's success rate, even while the planner was trained solely on expert data. This demonstrated that the compositional framework allows the dynamics model to exploit diverse, inexpensive data to enhance feasibility and performance, a benefit not observed in D-MPC, whose dynamics model is only applied post-hoc.

#### 4.3. Dynamics Consistency of Sampled Trajectories

- **Feasibility:** On FetchPickAndPlace, MPDiffuser exhibited stronger dynamics consistency, characterized by lower mean state error between generated state trajectories and their open-loop simulated counterparts, compared to Decision Diffuser and a planner-only baseline. While D-MPC achieved comparable state deviation, MPDiffuser achieved a notably higher success rate, indicating a more effective balance between task fidelity and dynamic feasibility through its integrated dynamics model.

#### 4.4. Adapting to Novel Dynamics

- **Adaptability:** When tested on a Walker2d environment with a simulated hardware defect (limited ankle joint torque), MPDiffuser maintained significantly higher returns than Diffuser and D-MPC. Crucially, MPDiffuser efficiently adapted to the new dynamics by fine-tuning *only* its dynamics diffusion model with a small amount of "play" data, leading to substantial performance recovery and improvement. This highlights the benefit of isolating and independently updating the dynamics component.

#### 4.5. Constrained Offline Decision Making (DSRL Benchmarks)

- **Safety-Performance Balance:** MPDiffuser consistently achieved high returns while adhering to specified cost constraints on the DSRL benchmark tasks (e.g., velocity and Safety Gym tasks). It demonstrated competitive performance against and often outperformed other constrained offline RL methods (COptiDICE, CPQ, BCQ-Lag, CDT) by effectively balancing safety and reward objectives. The framework also allowed for flexible adaptation to different safety–reward tradeoffs without retraining, simply by adjusting cost and return scale parameters.
- **Importance of Dynamic Feasibility for Ranker:** In a Pendulum environment with a hard velocity constraint, MPDiffuser sustained high success rates as the number of sampled trajectories increased. In contrast, a planner-only approach's performance degraded with more samples due to selecting dynamically infeasible (hallucinated) high-return trajectories. SafeDiffuser, which uses projection-based safety, achieved substantially lower success rates, underscoring the necessity of dynamic feasibility enforcement during generation rather than just post-hoc projection.

#### 4.6. Extending MPDiffuser to Visual Domains

- **Scalability:** A preliminary study on the Pendulum environment with image observations showed that MPDiffuser, operating on a learned latent space via a convolutional autoencoder, achieved higher average returns compared to Decision Diffuser, Planner-only, and Diffuser baselines. This indicated the framework's potential for scalability to high-dimensional visual inputs.

#### 4.7. Robot Locomotion with Unitree Go2

- **Real-World Applicability:** In simulation, MPDiffuser achieved the highest average return while staying under the cost limit for a Unitree Go2 quadruped. Real-world deployment on the Unitree Go2 (using an onboard Jetson Orin) further validated its practicality. With system-level optimizations (single-sample DDIM, action chunking, asynchronous planning, warm-starting), MPDiffuser successfully tracked a constant velocity command more accurately and achieved a higher normalized return with zero cost compared to a PPO policy.

#### 4.8. Analysis

- **Effect of Alternating Updates:** Analysis revealed that MPDiffuser's performance gains were not merely due to an increased number of denoising steps. MPDiffuser (100 alternating steps) substantially outperformed both Planner (100 steps) and Planner (200 steps) on D4RL medium-replay tasks, with comparable runtime to Planner (200), attributing the improvement to the compositional planner–dynamics sampling.
- **Alternation vs. Score Combination:** Alternating updates consistently outperformed a direct combined-score update (convex combination of planner and dynamics scores) on D4RL hopper environments. This was attributed to gradient interference and instability when combining scores directly, whereas alternation provided more stable and effective guidance.
- **Limitations:** A controlled experiment demonstrated that MPDiffuser's performance degrades significantly under a strong distribution mismatch between the planner's training data (medium-expert) and the dynamics model's training data (medium-replay). This suggests that the dynamics module can "over-correct" trajectories if its training distribution is too different from the planner's proposals, highlighting a practical guideline for compatible data distributions rather than a fundamental limitation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Model Predictive Diffuser (MPDiffuser) represents a significant step towards enhancing the reliability and safety of offline decision-making systems, particularly those leveraging generative diffusion models. Its compositional framework addresses a critical limitation of previous diffusion-based approaches: the generation of dynamically inconsistent trajectories.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Safety in Control:** By actively enforcing dynamics consistency through an interleaved sampling process, MPDiffuser generates trajectories that are not only task-aligned but also dynamically plausible. This directly translates to increased reliability and safety, which is crucial for deployment in safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving, robotics, and healthcare, where errors can have severe consequences.
- **Improved Performance in Offline RL:** MPDiffuser consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art diffusion-based methods and other offline RL baselines across a range of unconstrained and constrained benchmarks. This provides a more robust and effective tool for learning high-quality policies from static datasets.
- **Efficient Data Utilization:** The compositional design allows the dynamics model to leverage diverse and even low-quality or random data to learn robust system transitions, independently of the planner. This improves sample efficiency and can alleviate the burden of collecting large quantities of expert or optimal data, making offline learning more accessible and practical.
- **Rapid Adaptability to System Changes:** The ability to fine-tune only the dynamics model in response to novel system dynamics or hardware defects enables rapid and efficient adaptation. This is a crucial feature for real-world robotic systems that encounter changing environmental conditions or system wear.
- **Flexible Constraint and Objective Handling:** The integrated ranking module provides a flexible mechanism to incorporate and balance task objectives and safety constraints. This allows for tailored behavior generation across a spectrum of safety-reward tradeoffs without requiring full model retraining.
- **Scalability and Real-World Applicability:** Demonstrations in visual domains and successful real-world deployment on a quadrupedal robot (Unitree Go2) affirm MPDiffuser's scalability to complex inputs and its practicality for robotic control, overcoming computational challenges through system-level optimizations.

Potential future impacts could include:

- **Advancing Safe AI Systems:** MPDiffuser's principles could contribute to the development of safer AI agents by systematically embedding dynamic feasibility and constraint satisfaction into generative policy learning.
- **Hybrid Control Architectures:** The work could inspire new hybrid control architectures that combine the data-driven flexibility of generative models with the robustness of model-based approaches, potentially leading to more advanced predictive control strategies.
- **Foundation for Online Learning and Exploration:** The dynamics module within MPDiffuser could be extended to inform online exploration strategies or facilitate adaptive control in dynamic environments.
- **Application in Complex Sensory Environments:** The demonstrated scalability to visual inputs suggests broad applicability for control tasks relying on high-dimensional sensory data, paving the way for more sophisticated vision-based robotic manipulation and navigation.
