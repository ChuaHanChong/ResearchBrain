# TIPSv2: Advancing Vision-Language Pretraining with Enhanced Patch-Text Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> TIPSv2 advances vision-language pretraining by enhancing dense patch-text alignment through a modified masked image modeling objective, iBOT++, and an efficient head-only EMA teacher strategy. The model family achieves state-of-the-art results on various dense image-text tasks while maintaining strong performance on global and image-only benchmarks, simultaneously reducing training costs.

## Problem

- Current vision-language models struggle to achieve strong performance simultaneously in both global (image-level) and dense (patch-level) understanding.
- Existing pretraining methods for large models do not consistently induce precise pixel- or patch-level alignment with text, which is essential for tasks like open-vocabulary segmentation.
- Standard self-supervised learning often relies on a full-model exponential moving average (EMA) teacher, leading to significant memory and computational costs.

## Method

- Introduced iBOT++, a modified ==masked image modeling== objective that applies supervision to all patch tokens (masked and unmasked) to ensure representation consistency and preserve local semantics.
- Implemented a head-only EMA strategy, updating only the projection heads of the teacher via EMA while sharing the student and teacher encoders, significantly reducing memory and computational overhead.
- Incorporated multi-granularity text captions by dynamically sampling from detailed Gemini Flash captions and simpler PaliGemma captions to provide richer, more robust textual supervision.

## Results

- The iBOT++ objective improved zero-shot segmentation performance on ADE**150** from **3.5** mIoU (with iBOT) to **17.6** mIoU for a TIPS ViT-g model, demonstrating enhanced patch-text alignment.
- The head-only EMA strategy reduced trainable parameters by **42**% for a ViT-B model, effectively maintaining performance with reduced resource requirements and sometimes yielding minor improvements.
- TIPSv2 L/**14** achieved state-of-the-art **24.7** mIoU on ADE**150** for zero-shot semantic segmentation, outperforming previous bests and DINOv2, and demonstrated competitive performance across 9 tasks and **20** datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilled smaller models can achieve superior patch-text alignment compared to their larger teacher models, indicating that applying loss on all tokens and random initialization are critical for strong fine-grained alignment.
> - The `L_CLIP` contrastive loss provides sufficient stability for the vision encoders, making a simplified 'head-only' EMA teacher feasible without significant performance degradation.
> - Multi-granularity captions enhance both global and dense image-text alignment by balancing learning difficulty and descriptive detail, leading to more robust model representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual representation learning has undergone significant transformations due to the advancements in large-scale self-supervised and vision-language pretraining methods. Current leading approaches generally fall into two categories: weakly-supervised contrastive or sigmoid methods (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP, Perception Encoder), which are known for robust image-text alignment and zero-shot capabilities; and self-supervised learning (SSL) methods (e.g., DINO, iBOT), which typically excel at spatial understanding for dense downstream tasks like depth estimation or segmentation.

A notable challenge within this landscape is the development of a unified representation that simultaneously demonstrates strong performance in both global (image-level) and dense (patch-level) understanding. Existing state-of-the-art models often optimize for one capability at the expense of the other. For instance, DINOv2 exhibits proficiency in dense vision tasks but lacks inherent text alignment for vision-language capabilities, while PE-core provides robust image-text alignment but shows comparatively weaker performance on dense tasks.

Recent unified approaches, such as TIPS and SigLIP2, have made progress in integrating these capabilities. However, the paper identifies that even these contemporary models continue to face difficulties in maintaining precise pixel- or patch-level alignment with text, a capability considered essential for advanced applications like open-vocabulary segmentation. Efforts like DINO.txt and DINOv3 have attempted to address this by incorporating supplementary text encoders, but still exhibit limitations in multimodal task performance, suggesting that grounded alignment with text at a fine-grained level remains an unresolved issue. This is further corroborated by observations that the final transformer layers in some models tend to function as global contrastive "decoders," rather than preserving local semantics.

This work positions itself as an advancement in vision-language pretraining by specifically targeting the enhancement of dense patch-text alignment, building upon and addressing the limitations of previous unified models. It explores methods to integrate strong spatial awareness directly into the pretraining process, aiming to overcome the observed trade-offs between global and dense understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the capability of foundational vision-language models in aligning dense patch representations with text embeddings of corresponding concepts. This is articulated as a critical issue that existing models struggle with, despite recent advancements in vision-language pretraining.

The motivation for this work stems from several detailed observations and perceived limitations in the current state of vision-language models:

1. **Addressing Inconsistent Patch-Text Alignment in Large Models:** The authors observed a trend where larger flagship vision encoders, including prior state-of-the-art models, surprisingly underperform their smaller, distilled counterparts in tasks requiring fine-grained patch-text alignment, such as zero-shot semantic segmentation. For example, the TIPS ViT-g model, a large pretrained model, exhibited significantly weaker zero-shot segmentation performance compared to the smaller TIPS ViT-L model, which was distilled from ViT-g. This discrepancy suggested that the pretraining recipes for large models, while effective for global or image-only tasks, did not consistently induce strong patch-text alignment. This performance gap indicated a need for modifications to pretraining strategies to ensure that larger, more powerful models also excel at dense understanding.

2. **Leveraging Insights from Distillation Processes:** The research investigated the factors contributing to the superior patch-text alignment observed in distilled models. Through ablation studies, it was discovered that applying supervision across all patch tokens (rather than only masked ones) during a patch-level distillation procedure significantly boosted grounding alignment. This insight, particularly the importance of training on visible tokens and random initialization of the student vision encoder, motivated the researchers to integrate similar mechanisms directly into the pretraining phase to foster stronger alignment from the outset.

3. **Improving Masked Image Modeling (MIM) for Dense Alignment:** Existing MIM objectives, like iBOT, typically focus on reconstructing masked regions, providing limited direct supervision for visible tokens. The hypothesis was that this limited supervision could impede the preservation of local semantics and, consequently, dense patch-text alignment. A key motivation was to modify the MIM objective to enforce representation consistency across all patches, thereby anchoring student patch representations more effectively to the teacher’s.

4. **Enhancing Pretraining Efficiency and Effectiveness:** Alongside improving alignment, the research aimed to develop a more efficient pretraining framework. Standard self-supervised learning often relies on a full-model exponential moving average (EMA) teacher, which incurs significant memory and computational costs. A motivation was to simplify the EMA setup, reducing resource requirements while maintaining or improving model performance, thereby enabling more accessible and scalable training of vision-language models.

5. **Increasing Robustness through Multi-Granularity Text Supervision:** The quality and granularity of image captions directly influence the learning of vision encoders. Existing caption sources (e.g., web alt-text, PaliGemma) were identified as having limitations in descriptive detail. The motivation was to leverage richer, more diverse synthetic captions to provide a broader range of textual descriptions, which could enhance the robustness and comprehensive understanding of the vision encoder.

By addressing these specific motivations, the work sought to develop a new family of image-text encoder models, TIPSv2, capable of robust performance across a wide spectrum of downstream applications, with a particular focus on excelling in dense image-text alignment tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TIPSv2 framework builds upon the architecture and pretraining methodology of its predecessor, TIPS, which integrates contrastive image-text learning with self-supervised learning. The core methodology involves several novel components designed to enhance patch-text alignment, efficiency, and robustness.

**Foundation (TIPS Recap):**
The underlying method employs an image encoder (`f`, a Vision Transformer) and a text encoder (`g`, a standard Transformer). The image encoder generates both a global embedding (`e_g`) and a set of patch embeddings (`{e_1..., e_N}`). The training objective (`L`) combines three loss components:
- **Image-Text Contrastive Loss (`L_CLIP`):** Aligns `e_g` with text embeddings (`e_t`) using an InfoNCE loss. TIPS specifically uses two separate global CLS embeddings, one for web alt-text and one for synthetic captions, to capture different granularities of information.
- **Global-Level Self-Distillation Loss (`L_DINO`):** A DINO-style loss where student global embeddings from multiple image crops are compared to the teacher's global embedding of the original image.
- **Patch-Level Masked Image Modeling (MIM) Loss (`L_iBOT`):** An iBOT-style loss where the student reconstructs masked image patches by matching features of a teacher processing the unmasked image. Supervision is applied only to masked tokens.
- **Distillation in TIPS:** In TIPS, distillation to smaller models involves replacing the EMA teacher with a frozen larger teacher and removing masking for the patch-level loss.

**TIPSv2 Innovations:**

1. **Bridging Pretraining and Distillation (Discovery Phase):**
 The research began by investigating why distilled student models (e.g., TIPS ViT-L) unexpectedly outperformed their larger teacher models (e.g., TIPS ViT-g) on zero-shot semantic segmentation. Ablation studies on a ViT-L self-distillation baseline identified key factors:
 - **Loss on Visible Tokens:** Lowering the masking ratio during distillation, especially eliminating it entirely, significantly increased patch-text alignment. This indicated that applying the patch-level loss to *all* tokens, not just masked ones, was crucial.
 - **Random Initialization:** Initializing the student vision encoder randomly (from scratch) rather than with pretrained weights was found to be essential for the student to achieve superior alignment during distillation. Pretrained initialization eliminated the distillation advantage. This suggested that the model needed to escape its initial convergence region to learn effectively during distillation.

2. **iBOT++ (Enhanced Masked Image Modeling Objective):**
 Inspired by the distillation findings, iBOT++ modifies the original `L_iBOT` objective to enforce representation consistency on *all* patch tokens, both masked and unmasked.
 - **Mechanism:** The `L_iBOT++` loss, given by `L_iBOT++ = - Σ_{i=1 to N} h_t(f_t(I)_i)^T log h_s(f_s(I_mask)_i)`, applies supervision to every patch `i`, regardless of whether `m_i` (masking indicator) is 1 (masked) or 0 (visible).
 - **Rationale:** While iBOT only reconstructs masked tokens, allowing visible tokens to behave arbitrarily, iBOT++ explicitly anchors visible student patch representations to the teacher's, thus preserving local semantics directly during pretraining. It retains a high masking ratio (e.g., 75%) to benefit from MIM while extending supervision to visible tokens.

3. **Head-only EMA (Efficient Temporal Ensembling):**
 To improve training efficiency, TIPSv2 introduces a simplification to the exponential moving average (EMA) setup.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of maintaining an EMA copy of the entire teacher network (`f_t`), only the projection head network (`h_t`) is updated via EMA from the student head (`h_s`). The teacher vision encoder (`f_t`) is directly set equal to the student vision encoder (`f_s`).
 - **Rationale:** The presence of the `L_CLIP` contrastive loss already provides a stable signal that prevents the collapse of the vision encoders (`f_s`, `f_t`). Therefore, the computationally expensive full EMA for the encoder is deemed redundant.
 - **Benefit:** This approach drastically reduces memory overhead and training throughput requirements (e.g., 42% reduction in trainable parameters for ViT-B models). Preliminary experiments indicated that completely removing EMA (sharing both encoder and projector) led to training instability.

4. **Multi-Granularity Text Captions:**
 To provide richer and more robust textual supervision, the captioning strategy was expanded.
 - **Mechanism:** In addition to noisy web alt-text captions and PaliGemma synthetic captions, TIPSv2 incorporates highly detailed synthetic captions generated by Gemini Flash.
 - **Strategy:** During training, for the second CLS token (following TIPS' dual CLS setup), captions are randomly alternated between the detailed Gemini captions and the simpler PaliGemma captions. The first CLS token is supervised by web alt-text.
 - **Rationale:** This multi-granularity approach balances the need for comprehensive detail (from Gemini) with the avoidance of trivializing the contrastive loss (which can happen with overly detailed captions, hindering robust representation learning). This dynamic sampling strategy aims to optimize learning difficulty and detail extraction.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Training Data:** WebLI dataset (116M images, filtered subset from TIPS).
- **Synthetic Captions:** PaliGemma and Gemini 1.5 Flash.
- **Pretraining Stages:** A low-resolution stage (90k steps, batch size 8192, 224 resolution global crop, 98 resolution local crops) followed by a high-resolution adaptation stage (9k steps, batch size 4096, 448 resolution global crop, 140 resolution local crops).
- **Augmentations:** Random resizes, crops, and flips.
- **Optimizer:** Adafactor.
- **Model Sizes:** Pretrained at ViT-g scale, then distilled to smaller ViT-B, ViT-L, and SO variants.
- **Evaluation:** Comprehensive evaluation on 9 tasks across 20 datasets, keeping pretrained image-text representations frozen.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that the proposed TIPSv2 methodology leads to consistent improvements in vision-language pretraining, particularly enhancing dense patch-text alignment and training efficiency.

#### 4.1. Distillation Insights and iBOT++ Effectiveness

- **Distillation Phenomenon:** A key finding was that smaller models distilled from larger teachers (e.g., TIPS ViT-L from ViT-g) significantly surpassed their teachers in patch-text alignment tasks like zero-shot segmentation (Table 1). This indicated that the distillation process imparted superior dense alignment capabilities not fully present in the original pretrained teacher.
- **Factors for Distillation Success:** Ablation studies on distillation revealed that two factors were crucial for strong patch-text alignment: applying loss on all tokens (removing masking) and randomly initializing the student vision encoder (Table 2).
- **iBOT++ Performance:** Integrating iBOT++ into the pretraining recipe, which applies the patch-level loss to all tokens (masked and unmasked), resulted in substantial improvements. For a TIPS ViT-g model, iBOT++ led to a significant increase in zero-shot segmentation performance on ADE150 (from 3.5 mIoU to 17.6 mIoU, Table 3), demonstrating enhanced patch-text alignment directly during pretraining. Qualitative visualizations (Figure 9) corroborated cleaner segmentation maps with iBOT++ compared to iBOT.
- **Generalizability of iBOT++:** Experiments extending iBOT++ to the CLIP architecture (ViT-L backbone) showed consistent performance gains across various tasks, including zero-shot segmentation, image-only tasks (segmentation, depth), and image-text retrieval (Tables 9, 10, 11). This suggests that iBOT++ is a generalizable improvement for vision-language pretraining.
- **Optimal Masking Ratio for iBOT++:** Ablations confirmed that a 75% masking ratio remained optimal for iBOT++ during pretraining, underscoring the continued importance of Masked Image Modeling (MIM) for robust representation learning (Table 12).

#### 4.2. Head-only EMA Efficiency

- The head-only EMA strategy, which updates only the projection heads of the teacher via EMA while sharing the student and teacher encoders, retained the majority of the performance compared to full EMA (Table 4). This adaptation reduced training parameters by 42% for a ViT-B model, demonstrating significant resource savings without substantial performance degradation. In some cases, it even resulted in minor improvements, such as on zero-shot segmentation (Table 4).

#### 4.3. Multi-Granularity Text Captions Impact

- Leveraging multi-granularity captions (alternating PaliGemma and Gemini Flash captions for the second CLS token) was shown to improve both global (Image-to-Text, Text-to-Image Retrieval) and dense (zero-shot segmentation) image-text tasks (Table 4, 13). This approach balanced learning difficulty and detail extraction, contributing to more robust model representations.

#### 4.4. Overall TIPSv2 Benchmarking (ViT-g/L scale)

- **Dense Image-Text Tasks (Zero-shot Segmentation):** TIPSv2 achieved state-of-the-art results across PC59, PC60, VOC21, and ADE150 datasets (Table 5). For example, on ADE150, TIPSv2 L/14 reached 24.7 mIoU, outperforming previous bests like TIPS L/14 (20.8 mIoU) and DINOv2 (dino.txt) L/14 (20.6 mIoU), even against methods employing more expensive protocols.
- **Global Image-Text Tasks (Retrieval, Zero-shot Classification):** TIPSv2 demonstrated strong competitive performance, achieving the best or second-best results in 5 out of 7 evaluations (Table 6). It outperformed larger ViT-G models like PE-core on multiple COCO and long-text DOCCI retrieval tasks.
- **Image-only Tasks (Segmentation, Depth, Normals, Fine-grained Retrieval, ImageNet Classification):** TIPSv2 achieved the best or second-best results in 7 out of 9 evaluations (Table 7). It showed particular strength in dense understanding tasks, improving segmentation on PASCAL and depth estimation on NYUv2. While slightly lower in ImageNet classification compared to some specialized models, this was noted as a trade-off for optimizing general-purpose capabilities.
- **Comparison with DINOv3:** Against the recent DINOv3 (ViT-L scale), TIPSv2 outperformed it in 4 out of 6 comparable metrics, despite DINOv3's teacher being trained with substantially more parameters and data (Table 8).
- **Qualitative Assessments:** PCA maps (Figures 5, 7, 8) illustrated that TIPSv2 patch embeddings yielded smoother, more spatially coherent, and semantically focused features compared to other vision-language pretraining methods, with well-delineated objects.

#### 4.5. TIPSv2 Model Family Performance

- The entire TIPSv2 model family (ViT-B, ViT-L, ViT-SO, ViT-g) demonstrated strong performance across all benchmarks, with the ViT-g model being pretrained and the smaller variants distilled from it (Table 15).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TIPSv2 research introduces several advancements that hold significance for the field of vision-language pretraining and its downstream applications:

1. **Enhanced Dense Image-Text Alignment:** The most significant contribution is the substantial improvement in precise patch-text alignment. By identifying and addressing the limitations of existing pretraining methods, particularly the iBOT objective, TIPSv2 enables models to develop a more fine-grained understanding of the spatial relationships between visual features and textual concepts. This enhanced capability is crucial for advanced tasks such as open-vocabulary segmentation, where accurately grounding text to specific image regions is paramount. This advancement pushes the boundaries of how well vision-language models can "see" and "understand" at a local level.

2. **More Robust and Versatile Visual Representations:** The integration of iBOT++, head-only EMA, and multi-granularity captions collectively leads to the creation of more robust and general-purpose image-text encoders. These models are capable of performing strongly across a wide range of tasks, including dense prediction, image-text retrieval, and zero-shot classification, indicating a balanced improvement in both global and local understanding. This versatility makes TIPSv2 models suitable for a broader spectrum of real-world multimodal applications.

3. **Increased Training Efficiency:** The introduction of the head-only EMA mechanism represents a practical and impactful contribution to the efficiency of large-scale vision-language pretraining. By significantly reducing the memory overhead and computational cost associated with maintaining a full EMA teacher network, TIPSv2 makes the training of advanced models more accessible and sustainable. This efficiency gain can accelerate research and development in the field, allowing researchers and practitioners to experiment with larger models or longer training schedules with fewer computational resources.

4. **Novel Insights into Pretraining and Distillation:** The detailed investigation into why distilled models sometimes surpass their teachers in dense alignment provides valuable insights into the mechanics of representation learning. The findings regarding the importance of loss on all tokens and random initialization during distillation inform the design of more effective pretraining strategies, specifically leading to iBOT++. These insights could guide future architectural designs and training paradigms for developing vision-language models with superior spatial grounding.

5. **Competitive Performance Against State-of-the-Art:** TIPSv2 consistently achieves competitive or superior performance across a comprehensive suite of 9 tasks and 20 datasets, outperforming many contemporary vision encoders, including larger models and those using more resource-intensive evaluation protocols. This empirical validation positions TIPSv2 as a strong baseline and a new state-of-the-art method in several critical benchmarks.

6. **Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** The release of the code and models associated with TIPSv2 provides a valuable resource for the research community, fostering reproducibility and enabling further innovation. The improved capabilities in dense understanding can underpin the development of more sophisticated AI systems for applications requiring detailed visual comprehension, such as robotics, autonomous driving, medical imaging analysis, and complex content generation and editing.
