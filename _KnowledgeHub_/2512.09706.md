# Training One Model to Master Cross-Level Agentic Actions via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and National University of Singapore researchers developed CrossHA, a unified agentic model trained with a three-stage reinforcement learning pipeline to autonomously select optimal action granularities at each step. The model achieved state-of-the-art performance on over 800 Minecraft tasks, outperforming single-action-space baselines and demonstrating improved generalization and efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing agentic AI models are typically confined to static, predefined action spaces, limiting their adaptability to dynamic environments.
- Manually designed action policies and translation layers are brittle and struggle with unexpected environmental states or multi-modal interaction requirements.
- The optimal action granularity often varies within a single task, requiring agents to dynamically switch between high-level and low-level actions for maximum success and efficiency.

## Method

- A unified ==agentic model==, **CrossHA**, is introduced, designed to autonomously select the most effective action interface for each step of a task trajectory.
- A three-stage reinforcement learning pipeline is developed: Mixed-Space Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for foundational multi-space understanding.
- Single-Turn Reinforcement Learning (STRL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for strategic one-step action space selection, followed by Multi-Turn RL (MTRL) to optimize for episodic success and execution efficiency.

## Results

- CrossHA achieved state-of-the-art performance in Minecraft across 800+ tasks, with an overall **58.7%** Finished Tasks and **54.6%** Average Success Rate.
- The model notably surpassed single-action-space baselines, achieving **94.7%** FT on Mine Blocks, **66.6%** FT on Kill Entities, and **83.3%** FT on Craft Items.
- CrossHA exhibited a smaller generalization gap between in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks, showcasing enhanced robustness and effective knowledge transfer to unseen scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning fine-tuning on large pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models facilitates scalable out-of-distribution generalization, even when trained on a limited number of tasks.
> - Dynamically switching between heterogeneous action spaces accelerates convergence and improves asymptotic performance in multi-turn reinforcement learning compared to agents confined to a single action space.
> - An intermediate Single-Turn RL stage acts as an effective warm-start, significantly enhancing the efficiency and convergence speed of subsequent multi-turn trajectory optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of agentic artificial intelligence (AI) is undergoing a transition from manually engineered, complex workflows for pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to the development of native agentic models through extensive post-training. Current native agents are typically characterized by their mastery of specific, predefined action spaces. For instance, GUI agents interact via mouse and keyboard events, Deep Research agents utilize API calls, Tool-Calling agents integrate with Model Context Protocol (MCP) services, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models execute embodied robotic commands.

However, a fundamental limitation in prior research is the necessity for distinct action space designs for different tasks, often requiring manual selection and definition. This static reliance on a single action space presents two main challenges: first, specialized action policies or translation layers can be brittle, failing when encountering unexpected environmental states (e.g., CAPTCHA verification blocking an API call or a robotic policy lacking precision); second, manual assignment of action spaces restricts an agent's flexibility in complex scenarios demanding multi-modal interactions. While some recent efforts have attempted to bridge disjoint action spaces, for example by unifying GUI and API-based actions or merging heterogeneous trajectories during training, these often involve complex and potentially brittle orchestration pipelines.

This research addresses the observation that the optimal action space can vary not only across different tasks but also within a single task at a step-by-step level. A truly generalist agent would therefore require the ability to dynamically switch between action granularities, from high-level APIs to low-level primitives, to maximize both success rates and efficiency. This paper positions itself as an advancement in this landscape by proposing a unified model that autonomously learns to select the most appropriate action space at each step of a trajectory, moving beyond static definitions or heuristic switching rules. It builds upon prior work on generalist agents with multi-action spaces but introduces reinforcement learning to explicitly optimize these transitions, treating action-space selection as a learnable decision variable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a unified agentic model, termed CrossHA, capable of mastering multiple heterogeneous action spaces and autonomously selecting the most effective interface for each step of a task trajectory. This objective is motivated by the inherent limitations of existing agentic AI paradigms, which largely confine agents to static, predefined action spaces.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are:

1. **Overcoming Rigidity in Action Spaces:** Current agents are often specialized for a single type of interaction (e.g., exclusively APIs, GUI events, or robotic commands). This rigidity limits their adaptability in dynamic environments where the optimal granularity of interaction varies based on context. The authors aim to bridge this gap by enabling an agent to operate across a spectrum of action spaces.
2. **Enabling Adaptive Action Switching without Human Rules:** A significant challenge is to allow the agent to autonomously decide whether to use a high-level, efficient action (like an API call) or a low-level, precise action (like a GUI manipulation). The motivation is to move beyond human-specified rules or heuristics, which are often complex, brittle, and difficult to design for diverse, open-ended environments. CrossHA seeks to learn this adaptive switching behaviour directly from experience.
3. **Balancing Efficiency and Precision:** The optimal action choice often involves a trade-off. High-level actions can be highly efficient but may lack the precision needed for specific sub-tasks (e.g., navigating a CAPTCHA). Conversely, low-level actions offer precision but can be computationally expensive and time-consuming. A key objective is to train CrossHA to dynamically balance these factors, prioritizing efficiency when appropriate and precision when necessary, within a single trajectory.
4. **Improving Generalization and Robustness:** Statically defined action spaces restrict an agent's flexibility and lead to brittleness, as a failure in one specific action layer can cap the overall success rate. By learning to dynamically switch, the agent is expected to be more robust to environmental variations and generalize better to unseen tasks and situations, as it can leverage alternative interaction modes when a primary one fails or is suboptimal.
5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance in Complex Environments:** The research aims to demonstrate the practical benefits of this approach through extensive experiments in a challenging, open-world environment (Minecraft). The objective is to show that CrossHA can significantly outperform baselines confined to fixed action spaces, exhibiting superior generalization and efficiency in long-horizon reasoning tasks.

In summary, the motivation stems from the observed inadequacy of single-action-space agents in dynamic, multi-modal environments, leading to the objective of developing a unified, learning-based approach for adaptive, step-level action space selection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces CrossHA, a unified agentic model designed to autonomously select the most appropriate action space within a trajectory. This capability is developed through a three-stage reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the state space $S$ is combined with a composite action space $A = \bigcup_{x=1}^N A_x$. Each subspace $A_x$ represents a distinct class of actions, ranging from low-level motor controls to high-level motion primitives, each executed by a specific interface $C_x$. The agent's objective is to maximize an expected return $J = E[\sum_t (r_t - \lambda_x \text{cost}(a_t))]$, where $r_t$ is the immediate reward and $\lambda_x \text{cost}(a_t)$ penalizes the computational or operational cost associated with different action granularities (e.g., token length or execution time).

**Three-Stage Training Pipeline:**

1. **Stage 1: Mixed-Space Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
 - **Objective:** To provide the model with a foundational understanding of executing actions across diverse, heterogeneous action spaces. This stage teaches the model the syntax and semantics required to generate valid actions in various formats.
 - **Approach:** A base Vision-Language Model (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, fine-tuned on Minecraft VQA and captioning datasets to form $M_{base}$) is fine-tuned using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on a balanced dataset ($D_{mix}$) containing trajectories from multiple action subspaces. This unified data exposure enables the model ($M_{mix}$) to decode and generate actions from a composite action space without modal interference, laying the groundwork for subsequent decision-making. At this stage, the model does not autonomously select action spaces; it merely learns to process and generate actions within them.

2. **Stage 2: Single-Turn Reinforcement Learning (STRL)**
 - **Objective:** To empower the model to autonomously select the most appropriate action space for immediate, one-step decisions, transitioning from stochastic capability to strategic selection.
 - **Warm-up: Diversity-Enhanced SFT:**
 - Standard datasets typically lack explicit labels for optimal action spaces. To address this, prompts are constructed to encourage the model to generate candidate actions across all available action spaces.
 - A rejection sampling approach filters these candidates, retaining only actions that successfully execute the task (verified by the environment or a parser) as ground-truth annotations.
 - The model is then fine-tuned on this rebalanced, multi-space dataset, resulting in $M_{cs1}$. This model can generate valid actions across spaces but its selection is observed to be stochastic, not strategic.
 - **Optimization via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - GRPO, a stable and resource-efficient RL algorithm, is employed. Unlike PPO, GRPO uses group statistics of multiple sampled outputs to estimate the advantage, eliminating the need for a separate value function network.
 - Each example in the dataset is treated as a one-step decision problem. A deterministic parser $g : A \rightarrow R$ maps an action string to its canonical raw representation.
 - The reward function $r(\hat{a}, a^*) = 1 \text{ if } g(\hat{a}) = g(a^*)$ is action-space agnostic, granting credit if the parsed raw action matches the ground truth, irrespective of the generated action space.
 - The STRL objective is to maximize this expected reward, enabling $M_{cs1}$ to learn to select the action space that most reliably yields the correct raw action. The resulting model is denoted as $M_{strl}$.

3. **Stage 3: Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning (MTRL)**
 - **Objective:** To cultivate robust, trajectory-level action-space selection, explicitly optimizing for episodic task success and overall execution efficiency over long horizons.
 - **Initialization via Self-Training ($M_{cs2}$):**
 - To accelerate training, the action-space preferences learned by $M_{strl}$ are distilled into a supervised format.
 - $M_{strl}$ performs inference over the initial dataset $D$. For each example $(x, a^*)$, if $M_{strl}$'s prediction $\hat{a}(x)$ is semantically consistent with $a^*(x)$ (i.e., $g(\hat{a}(x)) = g(a^*(x))$), then $\hat{a}(x)$ replaces the original label. Otherwise, $a^*(x)$ is retained. This creates a relabeled dataset $D_{strl}$.
 - The base model is then fine-tuned on $D_{strl}$, resulting in $M_{cs2}$. This model initializes the MTRL stage with a strong prior for appropriate action-space selection.
 - **Trajectory Optimization:**
 - $M_{cs2}$ is fine-tuned using multi-turn RL on a curated task set $T$.
 - A binary episodic reward $r(\tau) = 1\{\text{success}(\tau)\}$ is used, where $\tau$ is a generated trajectory.
 - The objective is to maximize $J(\theta) = E_{x \sim T} E_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} [r(\tau) - \lambda l_\theta(\tau)]$. The term $\lambda l_\theta(\tau)$ penalizes the total number of tokens produced, encouraging the model to prefer concise (e.g., high-level API) action spaces over verbose ones when both lead to success.
 - Multi-Turn GRPO is applied, adapting the single-turn formulation to handle sequential interactions by assigning the trajectory-level reward to all intermediate responses. The final model is CrossHA ($M_{mtrl}$).

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Environment:** Minecraft (version 1.16.5) is used as an open-world testbed, providing RGB images as observations.
- **Action Space:** A discrete, human-like interface comprising mouse movements, clicks, and keyboard commands (as detailed in Appendix A). The heterogeneous action spaces explored include Raw Actions, Language Skills, Motion Actions, Grounding Actions, and Latent Actions (as detailed in Appendix B).
- **Benchmark:** OpenHA benchmark suite, containing over 800 tasks categorized into Mine Blocks, Craft Items, and Kill Entities.
- **Metrics:** Finished Tasks (FT) and Average Success Rate (ASR).
- **Training Details:** Initialized with Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct. Training datasets are constructed using SAM-based grounding and MineCLIP-based motion generation. MTRL is trained on 30 tasks (10 from each OpenHA category) for over 80 iterations, involving over 6,400 environment interactions per iteration on 8 NVIDIA A800-SXM4-80GB GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CrossHA in the Minecraft environment, encompassing over 800 tasks, yielded several findings.

**Performance Comparison with Baselines:**
CrossHA achieved state-of-the-art performance across all three main task categories (Mine Blocks, Kill Entities, Craft Items), outperforming both instruction-conditioned policies and hierarchical agents.
- **Mine Blocks:** CrossHA achieved 94.7% FT and 45.2% ASR.
- **Kill Entities:** CrossHA achieved 66.6% FT and 58.1% ASR.
- **Craft Items:** CrossHA achieved 83.3% FT and 72.7% ASR.
- **Overall (All Tasks):** CrossHA reached 58.7% FT and 54.6% ASR.

**Advantages Over Single-Action-Space Agents:**
Baselines confined to a single action space exhibited specialized performance. For example, GroundingHA showed high performance on Kill Entity tasks (90.1% FT), MotionHA performed better on Mine Block tasks, and RawHA showed advantages in Craft Item tasks. However, these specialized agents did not achieve strong, uniform results across all categories. CrossHA, by contrast, demonstrated balanced performance, achieving high success rates across all task types, indicating that it effectively coordinates and selects suitable action spaces based on context.

**Impact of Reinforcement Learning for Generalization:**
The study found that RL fine-tuning consistently led to gains in both ASR and FT compared to SFT-only agents. Despite being trained on only 30 tasks, the improvements generalized to over 800 evaluation tasks, particularly for fine-grained control domains like Craft Item. This suggests that RL, when applied to large pre-trained VLA models, contributes to scalable Out-of-Distribution (OOD) generalization.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Impact of Mixed Action Spaces:** Comparing CrossHA with single-space baselines (GroundingHA and MotionHA), CrossHA demonstrated faster convergence and higher asymptotic performance during Multi-Turn RL (MTRL). This indicates that the ability to dynamically select from heterogeneous action spaces provides greater flexibility and improves the data efficiency of the RL process.
- **Necessity of the STRL Stage:** The inclusion of the Single-Turn RL (STRL) stage was found to significantly enhance training efficiency and accelerate convergence in the subsequent MTRL stage. Models trained with STRL achieved higher success rates across a broader range of tasks compared to variants without STRL, suggesting that STRL provides an effective warm-start for action-space selection.

**Generalization Evaluation (In-Distribution vs. Out-of-Distribution):**
CrossHA exhibited robust performance on OOD tasks and maintained a smaller generalization gap between In-Distribution (ID) and OOD tasks compared to single-space RL baselines (e.g., RawHA-RL, MotionHA-RL). While single-space agents often achieved very high ID performance (e.g., RawHA-RL at 96.2% on Craft Items), their performance dropped sharply on OOD tasks. CrossHA, performing strongly on ID tasks (83.7% on Craft Items, 68.8% overall), demonstrated a more consistent performance profile, indicating that dynamic action-space selection helps mitigate overfitting and improves the transfer of learned environment knowledge to unseen tasks.

**Qualitative Case Studies:**
Case studies (e.g., Kill Sheep, Chop Tree, Craft Enchanting Table) illustrated CrossHA's adaptive action selection. For instance, in "Kill Sheep," the agent used efficient Motion Actions for initial navigation, blended Motion, Grounding, and Raw Actions for approaching, and relied on sustained Grounding and Raw Actions for precise interaction. This contextual switching closely mirrored human behavioral patterns, suggesting semantic coherence in the agent's decision-making process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents CrossHA, a unified agentic model capable of mastering heterogeneous action spaces and autonomously selecting the most effective interface at each step. This work represents a methodological advancement in agentic AI, moving beyond predefined action spaces and brittle heuristic-based switching mechanisms.

The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Advancement in Agent Adaptability:** By enabling dynamic, step-level action space selection, CrossHA enhances the adaptability of AI agents in complex and dynamic environments. This capability allows agents to seamlessly transition between high-level, efficient actions (e.g., API calls) and low-level, precise actions (e.g., GUI manipulations or atomic movements) as context dictates. This adaptability is crucial for handling tasks that inherently require multi-modal interactions and varying granularities of control.
2. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The brittleness associated with fixed or manually orchestrated action policies is a significant limitation in current agentic systems. CrossHA's learning-based approach to action-space selection improves robustness by allowing the agent to dynamically leverage alternative interaction modes when a primary one is suboptimal or fails. The experimental results demonstrate superior generalization to a large number of unseen tasks, indicating that learning to switch action spaces helps mitigate overfitting and transfers knowledge more effectively across diverse scenarios.
3. **Enhanced Efficiency in Long-Horizon Tasks:** The inclusion of a penalty for token generation in the Multi-Turn RL objective encourages the model to prefer concise, high-level action spaces when they are sufficient for task success, thereby optimizing for execution efficiency. This capability for balancing success rate with operational cost is particularly valuable for long-horizon tasks where cumulative inefficiencies can significantly impact overall performance.
4. **Reduced Human Engineering Effort:** The paradigm shift from engineering complex workflows to training native agentic models is a major trend. CrossHA contributes to this by learning adaptive action switching without relying on human-specified rules or orchestration layers. This reduces the manual effort required to design and maintain agent behaviors, making the development of generalist agents more scalable.
5. **New Directions for Reinforcement Learning:** The introduction of a three-stage training pipeline (SFT, STRL, MTRL) utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for optimizing action space selection is a methodological contribution to reinforcement learning. It demonstrates how to effectively warm-start and progressively refine policy for complex, high-dimensional action spaces, especially when dealing with sparse rewards.
6. **Foundation for Real-World Applications:** While evaluated in the Minecraft environment, the principles demonstrated by CrossHA have potential implications for real-world robotics and general computer-use agents. The ability to autonomously adapt action granularity, manage trade-offs between speed and precision, and operate robustly across diverse interaction modalities is a fundamental requirement for deploying AI agents in complex physical and digital domains. Future work could address challenges specific to real-world settings, such as safety constraints and latency requirements.

In conclusion, CrossHA contributes to the development of more capable, autonomous, and generalist AI agents by addressing the critical challenge of dynamic action-space selection.
