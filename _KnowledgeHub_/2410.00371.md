# AHA: A Vision-Language-Model for Detecting and Reasoning Over Failures in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA, the University of Washington, MIT, and other institutions developed AHA, a Vision-Language Model that detects robotic manipulation failures and provides natural language explanations for why they occurred. The model, trained on a large-scale procedurally generated dataset of failures, demonstrated superior generalization to novel tasks and real-world scenarios, improving the performance of various downstream robotic applications.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and robotic systems often lack the ability to autonomously detect and provide detailed, natural language explanations for their own failures in manipulation tasks.
- Robots operating in real-world environments frequently encounter stochasticity and unexpected events, necessitating constant human intervention when errors occur due to the absence of self-correction capabilities.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and well-annotated datasets specifically for robotic failure modes, making it challenging to train robust failure reasoning models.

## Method

- Developed **AHA**, an ==instruction-tuned Vision-Language Model (VLM)== designed to predict task success and generate ==free-form natural language explanations== for robotic manipulation failures.
- Created ==FailGen==, a novel, automated, and scalable ==procedural data generation pipeline== that systematically perturbs successful robot trajectories in simulation to induce and label diverse failure modes.
- Instruction-tuned **AHA** using a ==multi-modal data mix== consisting of 49K+ procedurally generated failure examples from the **AHA** dataset and 765K examples from general visual question-answering datasets to ensure both specialized and general visual understanding.

## Results

- AHA-13B consistently outperformed six state-of-the-art VLMs (including GPT-4o) across simulated and real-world test datasets, showing superior performance in both binary success detection and natural language explanation generation (e.g., **0.446** ROUGE-L on AHA dataset (Test), **0.280** ROUGE-L on RoboFail real-world dataset).
- The model successfully generalized from simulated training data to real-world robot failures and different robotic embodiments, maintaining its lead over other VLMs.
- Integrating AHA's failure feedback improved downstream robotic applications: RL reward synthesis achieved **22.34%** higher task success, Task and Motion Planning saw **36.7%** higher task success, and zero-shot robot data generation increased success by **5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing failure detection as a free-form reasoning task rather than a binary classification allows VLMs to provide detailed, actionable natural language explanations for robotic errors.
> - Procedurally generating synthetic failure data (via FailGen) is effective for training VLMs for failure reasoning, with the model demonstrating generalization from simulation to real-world robotic tasks and different robot embodiments.
> - Instruction-tuning a VLM on domain-specific failure data, when combined with general VQA data, can specialize its capabilities for robotics while retaining common sense knowledge and general visual understanding.
