# Out of Sight, Still in Mind: Reasoning and Planning about Unobserved Objects with Video Tracking Enabled Memory Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Utah and Oregon State University developed memory-enabled models that integrate unsupervised video object segmentation with a relational dynamics network to allow robots to reason and plan about unobserved, novel, or reappearing objects. This approach achieved over 90% planning success rates in complex multi-object manipulation tasks with occluded objects, significantly outperforming implicit memory baselines which struggled with increasing object counts and distractor actions.

## Problem

- Robots often assume continuous observability of all objects, failing to maintain object permanence and reason about items that become occluded during multi-object manipulation.
- Existing learning-based manipulation planning frameworks lack robust mechanisms to handle long-term occlusion, the appearance of novel objects, or the reappearance of previously hidden objects.
- Prior implicit memory models struggle with long input histories and distractor actions, leading to decreased performance in tasks involving prolonged object occlusion.

## Method

- The system integrates an Unsupervised Video Object Segmentation (UVOS) module to segment, track, and assign consistent IDs to objects, including novel ones, across observations.
- Two explicit object-oriented memory models, Direct Occluded Object Memory (DOOM) and Latent Occluded Object Memory (LOOM), are introduced to represent occluded objects either by hallucinating their point clouds or propagating their latent embeddings.
- These explicit memory models are coupled with a ==transformer==-based relational dynamics model and a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) planner, enabling multi-step planning towards logical goals involving unobserved objects.

## Results

- DOOM and LOOM consistently achieved high F1 scores for relational prediction (e.g., 0.976 and 0.974) and near 1.0 planning success rates across varying object counts and up to three distractor actions in simulation.
- These explicit memory models showed a significant improvement (over 10-**20%** in F1 scores and substantially higher success rates) compared to an implicit memory baseline, which saw performance plummet with increased complexity.
- Real-world experiments confirmed the efficacy of DOOM and LOOM with high planning success rates (e.g., **19/20** trials for various object counts), demonstrating practical applicability for tasks involving occluded objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit, object-oriented memory models leveraging advanced video tracking (UVOS) are demonstrably more robust for long-term object permanence in manipulation tasks than implicit autoregressive memory models.
> - Successfully integrating unsupervised object tracking allows for seamless handling of dynamic environmental changes, including object occlusion, reappearance, and the introduction of novel objects.
> - Maintaining a consistent and explicit world state, even for unobserved objects, is critical for enabling effective long-horizon, logic-based planning in partially observable robotic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability for robots to maintain a persistent understanding of their environment, including objects that are not currently visible, is a fundamental challenge in achieving robust and intelligent robotic autonomy. This work addresses the critical problem of **object permanence** in the context of **multi-object manipulation reasoning and planning**.

Historically, robotics has tackled persistent world modeling through **mapping (SLAM)** techniques. While these approaches provide geometric representations, they often lack the object-level semantics or mutable properties necessary for complex manipulation tasks like rearranging objects. Some efforts have integrated object-level semantics into maps, but these models are typically monolithic and not conducive to learning-based manipulation planning that requires predicting action effects on specific objects.

In the realm of **learning-based manipulation planning**, many contemporary methods leverage graph neural networks or transformers to reason about inter-object relations and predict action outcomes. However, a significant limitation of these frameworks, including the authors' own prior work, is the implicit assumption that all relevant objects are continuously observable. This assumption fundamentally breaks down in realistic environments where occlusion is common.

Existing attempts to encode memory for manipulation often fall into two categories:
1. **Implicit Memory Models:** These typically use autoregressive recurrent or transformer-based neural networks to process sequential observations. While capable of sequence prediction, the authors hypothesize (and later demonstrate) that these models struggle with *long-term memory* of objects that remain occluded through many updates, as the input history grows linearly and becomes computationally intractable for long-horizon tasks.
2. **Specific Occlusion Handling:** Some works have addressed reasoning about occluded objects. However, these often come with limitations:
 - They might assume goal images or configurations instead of logic-based goals.
 - They may be restricted to simpler tasks (e.g., planar pushing with single moving objects).
 - They might rely on complete object shape knowledge or numerous engineered modules.
 - The vision community's memory models for tracking or video object segmentation primarily focus on re-identification based on appearance, not on predicting how occluded objects move due to robot actions.

This paper carves out a unique niche by proposing an **explicit, object-oriented memory model** that directly integrates an **Unsupervised Video Object Segmentation (UVOS)** algorithm into a transformer-based relational dynamics prediction framework. It moves beyond implicit memory models and addresses the limitations of prior works by:
- Providing a robust mechanism for tracking and reasoning about objects under *heavy and long-term complete occlusions*.
- Handling the *appearance of novel objects* and *reappearance of previously occluded objects*.
- Enabling *logic-based goal planning* that can involve unobserved objects.
- Applying to a *variable and a priori unknown number of objects* and diverse manipulation skills, unlike many systems that assume known object sets or simpler tasks.

By explicitly linking object permanence with a learned relational dynamics model, this research significantly advances the capabilities of robots to operate effectively in dynamic, partially observable, and realistic manipulation environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to equip robots with the ability to **reason and plan about objects that are currently unobserved due to occlusion**, a critical capability for reliable operation in complex, realistic environments. The authors are motivated by the fundamental requirement for robots, acting as assistants or caregivers, to possess a persistent memory of their world that extends beyond immediate visual perception.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Current Models:** Existing learning-based multi-object manipulation frameworks often assume full observability of relevant objects. This work aims to bridge this gap by enabling these frameworks to function effectively even when objects are occluded.
2. **Robust Object-Oriented Memory:** Develop an explicit mechanism for encoding and maintaining an object-oriented memory. The hypothesis is that such an explicit encoding, particularly one leveraging advanced video tracking, will be more robust to long-term occlusion and numerous updates compared to implicit, autoregressive memory models.
3. **Handle Dynamic Environmental Changes:** Address the challenges posed by real-world dynamics, specifically:
 - **Occluded Objects:** Reason about the existence, location, and potential movement of objects that are out of sight.
 - **Novel Object Appearance:** Integrate newly observed objects into the robot's world model without prior knowledge.
 - **Object Reappearance:** Seamlessly re-identify and incorporate objects that become visible again after a period of occlusion.
4. **Enable Complex Manipulation Planning:** Integrate this object-oriented memory into a relational dynamics prediction model to enable multi-step planning towards logical goal relations, where goals might involve currently unobserved objects. For instance, the robot should be able to plan to "place object A to the left of currently occluded object B."
5. **Generalization:** Design a system that can generalize to varying numbers of objects and different numbers of "distractor actions" (actions that move other objects while the target object remains occluded), reflecting the unpredictability of human environments.
6. **Validate against Baselines:** Empirically demonstrate the superiority of the proposed explicit memory approaches over implicit memory baselines, particularly in scenarios involving long-term occlusion.

The paper aims to provide a consistent estimate of the world state that is amenable to contemporary, learning-based approaches for manipulation planning, specifically focusing on predicting inter-object and object-environment relations and the effects of robot actions on all objects in memory, observed or unobserved.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around integrating an **explicit, object-oriented memory** into a transformer-based relational dynamics prediction framework for multi-object manipulation. The system perceives the world as a point cloud, takes actions, and predicts resulting object relations and poses.

**A. Overall Framework Structure:**
The proposed framework is a memory-based neural network that processes a current observation (point cloud Z_t), a current action (A_t, θ_t), and a *compressed memory* of past observations and actions. It then predicts the resulting relations (r'_t+1) and object poses (p'_t+1) for the next timestep. This enables multi-step planning by chaining predictions.

**B. Key Components:**

1. **Segmentation and Tracking (UVOS):**
 - At each timestep `t`, an Unsupervised Video Object Segmentation (UVOS) algorithm is employed.
 - UVOS discovers `N` object and environment segments (O^i_t) within the point cloud (Z_t).
 - It identifies new objects, assigns consistent IDs to previously tracked objects, and re-identifies objects that reappear after occlusion. The UVOS model is fine-tuned on robot arm and YCB-Video dataset annotations to improve its familiarity with the robotic environment, while still generalizing to novel object types.
 - For environment segments (e.g., table, shelf), RANSAC is used to find planar surfaces.

2. **Pose and Relation Detection (Current State):**
 - Each segment (O^i_t) is processed by a **Point Cloud Encoder** (PointConv) to generate a feature vector (P^i_t).
 - A **learned positional embedding** (I^i) encodes the unique ID of each object, randomly generated during training for generalization.
 - The segment feature and ID embedding are combined (P^i_t ⊕ I^i) and fed into a **Transformer Encoder (E)**. This transformer models the interactions between different segments, producing a latent space representation (x^i_t) for each object.
 - Three decoders predict the current state:
 - **Pose Decoder (D_p):** Predicts the 6D pose of each object (ˆp^i_t = D_p(x^i_t)).
 - **Relation Decoder (D_r):** Predicts inter-object relations (ˆr^ij_t = D_r(x^i_t, x^j_t)).
 - **Environment Identity Decoder (D_e):** Classifies whether a segment is movable or an environment (ˆe^i_t = D_e(x^i_t)).

3. **Pose and Relation Prediction (Latent Dynamics Model):**
 - A **latent space dynamics model (δ)** propagates the current latent state (x^i_t) to a predicted future latent state (x'^i_t+1) based on the executed action (A_t(i, θ)).
 - The action is encoded using the object ID `i` and continuous parameters `θ` via an MLP (M_ac_l, where `l` is the skill type). Different `δ_l` and `M_ac_l` are used for different skills.
 - The predicted future latent state (x'^i_t+1) is then decoded to predict future poses (p'^i_t+1) and relations (r'^ij_t+1). This allows estimating occluded object poses and relations even without direct observation.
 - The model can predict states H timesteps ahead by iteratively applying `δ`.

**C. Novel Memory Models for Occluded Objects:**
The paper introduces two explicit memory models:

1. **DOOM (Direct Occluded Object Memory):**
 - When object `k` is occluded, its point cloud from a previous timestep (O^k_t-1) is retrieved.
 - This point cloud is transformed using the *predicted pose* of object `k` to its estimated current location (O'^k_t).
 - This transformed point cloud (O'^k_t) is then *directly augmented* into the current observation set (Z'_t), effectively "hallucinating" the point cloud of the occluded object.
 - This augmented point cloud is then fed into the relational classifier, allowing it to detect relations involving the occluded object.

2. **LOOM (Latent Occluded Object Memory):**
 - For an occluded object `k`, its *predicted latent space embedding* (x'^k_t) from the latent dynamics model is directly copied and used as its current latent state (x^k_t) in the transformer encoder input.
 - This directly augments the *latent space representation* with information about the occluded object.

**D. Handling Novel and Reappeared Objects:**
- **Reappeared Objects:** When UVOS re-identifies an previously occluded object, its augmented memory state (either transformed point cloud for DOOM or copied latent embedding for LOOM) is removed, and the object's current observation is processed normally.
- **Novel Objects:** UVOS identifies new objects, assigns them unique IDs, and they are processed like any other observable object.

**E. Planning with DOOM and LOOM:**
- The **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)** is used for planning.
- The key modification is that the initial state for planning *encodes any unobserved objects* using either the DOOM or LOOM mechanism, enabling planning for goals involving occluded objects.

**F. Training Details:**
The training loss is a sum of three components:
1. **Current Observation Loss (L_c):** Ensures accurate prediction of pose, relations, and environment identity from the current observation.
2. **Latent Space Regularization Loss (L_ls):** Encourages the predicted latent space from the dynamics model (x'^i_t+x) to be similar to the latent space derived from actual observation (x^i_t+x).
3. **Dynamics Prediction Loss (L_d):** Ensures that the latent dynamics model accurately predicts future poses, relations, and environment identities.
The model is trained using the Adam optimizer on a synthetic dataset generated in Isaac Gym with over 20,000 skill executions, featuring varying numbers of objects, skills (push, pick-and-place, pick-and-dump), and diverse relational definitions (left, right, front, behind, above, below, contact, boundary, inside).

**G. Baseline:**
A transformer-based implicit memory model serves as a baseline, similar to. It concatenates accumulated history with a "type embedding" (indicating observability status) and processes it through an MLP and transformer to predict relations and poses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation and real-world scenarios provide strong evidence supporting the effectiveness of the proposed DOOM and LOOM memory models, particularly in comparison to an implicit memory baseline.

**A. Relational Prediction Evaluation (F1 Score):**
- **Effect of Number of Objects (Table I, Fig. 7):** DOOM and LOOM consistently achieved high F1 scores (e.g., 0.976 and 0.974 respectively for all object counts), significantly outperforming the Baseline (0.798). The performance gap widened as the number of objects increased, with DOOM/LOOM showing nearly a 20% improvement over Baseline.
- **Effect of Distractor Actions (Table I, Fig. 8):** When "distractor actions" (manipulations of other objects while the target object is occluded) were introduced, DOOM and LOOM maintained strong performance (0.909 and 0.878 F1 scores respectively), demonstrating over a 10% improvement compared to the Baseline (0.770). The Baseline's performance notably dropped with even a single distractor action, verifying the authors' hypothesis about the limitations of implicit memory.

**B. Planning to Goal Relations Success Rate Evaluation (Simulation):**
This evaluation specifically tested the models' ability to plan sequences of actions to achieve logic-based goals involving occluded objects.

- **Generalization to a Different Number of Objects (Fig. 4, left):**
 - DOOM and LOOM showed robust performance, maintaining high planning success rates (near 1.0) across scenes with 4 to 7 objects, even though the training dataset had a maximum of 5 objects. This indicates strong generalization capabilities.
 - In stark contrast, the Baseline performed reasonably well with 4 objects (around 0.9 success rate) but struggled significantly as the number of objects increased, dropping to approximately 0.2 success rate with 7 objects.
- **Generalization to a Different Number of Distractor Actions (Fig. 4, right):**
 - DOOM and LOOM maintained high planning success rates (above 0.8 and 0.9, respectively) even with 1 to 3 distractor actions, demonstrating their ability to retain long-term memory of occluded objects.
 - The Baseline's performance plummeted dramatically with distractor actions, falling below 0.2 success rate even with just one distractor action.
- **Key Insight:** The authors noted that the difference in planning success rate was more significant than the F1 score for relational prediction. This is because planning success critically hinges on *correctly reasoning about occluded objects* for goal satisfaction, a task where the Baseline models performed poorly, despite potentially adequate performance on visible objects.

**C. Real-world Planning Success Evaluation (Table II):**
Due to the poor performance of the Baseline in simulation for occlusion tasks, real-world evaluations were primarily conducted with DOOM and LOOM, with a comparison against prior work that does not handle occluded objects.

- DOOM and LOOM demonstrated strong real-world planning success, achieving rates such as 19/20 and 19/20 (across varying objects) and 14/15 and 13/15 (across varying distractor actions).
- Crucially, prior works like would achieve a 0% success rate in these scenarios because they lack the mechanisms to reason about occluded objects.

**D. Qualitative Analysis (Fig. 1, 5):**
Qualitative examples illustrated the models' capabilities:
- **Reasoning with Occluded Objects:** The robot successfully planned to manipulate a blue cup based on the occluded yellow mustard, and to place an orange in contact with an apple that was hidden inside a pitcher (even after the pitcher itself was moved).
- **Novel Object Appearance/Disappearance:** The model correctly identified newly appearing objects and planned to move them to the table boundary, while understanding that a previously seen object (Cheez-It box) had disappeared.
- **Baseline Failures:** Visualizations showcased instances where the Baseline failed by manipulating the wrong object (e.g., pushing a black object instead of a red occluded one, or picking an apple instead of an orange for a goal involving an occluded mug), whereas DOOM achieved the correct goal.

In summary, the results unequivocally support the hypothesis that explicit, UVOS-based object-oriented memory models (DOOM and LOOM) are significantly more effective and robust than implicit memory baselines for multi-object manipulation planning that involves occluded, novel, and reappearing objects, especially in the context of long-term memory and distractor actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in enabling robots to operate autonomously and intelligently in human-centric environments. The significance and potential impact of "Out of Sight, Still in Mind" can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **Bridging the Perception-Reasoning Gap for Object Permanence:** The most critical contribution is the successful integration of advanced unsupervised video object segmentation (UVOS) with a relational dynamics model for manipulation planning. This directly addresses the long-standing challenge of object permanence in robotics, allowing robots to maintain a robust mental model of their world beyond immediate visual perception. This capability is fundamental for natural and intuitive human-robot interaction where objects are frequently occluded.

2. **Robustness to Real-world Complexity:** The demonstrated resilience of DOOM and LOOM to a variable and a priori unknown number of objects, novel object appearances, object reappearance, and, most importantly, numerous "distractor actions" (manipulations of other objects while the target is occluded) significantly enhances the robustness of robotic systems. This robustness is paramount for deployment in unstructured and dynamic environments where perfect observability cannot be guaranteed.

3. **Advancement in Memory Models for Robotics:** The work empirically validates the hypothesis that **explicit, object-oriented memory** (leveraging UVOS tracking) is superior to implicit autoregressive models for managing long-term history in manipulation tasks. This provides a clear direction for future memory architectures in robot learning, suggesting that structured, explicit representations are more effective for certain complex tasks than purely end-to-end, implicit learning.

4. **Enabling Logic-Based, Long-Horizon Planning with Occlusion:** By integrating memory models into a framework that supports logical goal relations, the paper expands the scope of tasks robots can perform. A robot can now reliably plan to achieve complex goals, such as "place the blue cup to the left of the mustard" even if the mustard is currently hidden, enabling more sophisticated high-level task planning in cluttered scenarios.

5. **Foundation for Advanced Robotic Autonomy:** This research lays a crucial groundwork for various future applications:
 - **Home Assistants/Caregivers:** Robots can more effectively manage and retrieve objects in dynamic home environments (e.g., finding an item inside a cabinet or behind other objects).
 - **Industrial Logistics:** Robots can handle objects in partially visible bins or shelves, improving efficiency in warehousing and manufacturing.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Robots can better understand and adapt to human actions that might temporarily occlude objects of interest.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also clearly identifies fertile grounds for future research:
 - **End-to-End Integration:** The current planner and tracker are not fully integrated, suggesting an opportunity to train a holistic system end-to-end, potentially allowing pose predictions from DOOM to inform the tracker and further improve performance.
 - **More Complex Memory Management:** Expanding to scenarios with mobile bases would introduce new challenges for spatial memory and navigation planning.
 - **Longer-Horizon Tasks:** Applying the robust memory to even longer-horizon manipulation tasks would push the boundaries of current capabilities.
 - **Addressing Low-Level Skill Failures:** The identified failure cases (failed grasps, unstable placements) highlight the need to integrate better low-level skill execution with high-level planning, indicating a pathway towards more reliable real-world performance.

In conclusion, "Out of Sight, Still in Mind" represents a significant contribution to robot learning and manipulation by providing a robust and practical solution for robots to reason and plan with unobserved objects. Its impact is likely to be profound, paving the way for more capable, intelligent, and deployable robotic systems in complex, real-world environments.
