# Self-supervised Multi-future Occupancy Forecasting for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University and the University of California, Riverside developed Latent Occupancy Prediction (LOPR), a self-supervised framework for stochastic multi-future occupancy forecasting in autonomous driving. LOPR uses LiDAR-generated occupancy grid maps, integrates diverse sensor modalities, and operates in a learned continuous latent space to produce real-time, high-quality predictions, outperforming existing baselines and state-of-the-art models on the nuScenes and Waymo Open datasets.

## Problem

- Existing AV environment prediction methods (object-based or prior L-OGM) struggle with producing holistic, stochastic multi-future predictions, often resulting in deterministic or unrealistic forecasts.
- Prior approaches did not effectively integrate diverse sensor modalities (RGB, maps, ego-trajectory) critical for comprehensive scene understanding.
- Heavy reliance on expensive labeled data and the computational inefficiency of some latent space models limited scalability and real-time deployment.

## Method

- **LOPR** learns a low-dimensional continuous latent space for LiDAR-generated occupancy grid maps (L-OGMs) using a combined ==β-VAE and GAN== for high-fidelity representation.
- It uses a ==transformer-based network== for stochastic sequence prediction within this latent space, modeling future multimodality with a learned unobservable variable and incorporating diverse sensor inputs (RGB, maps, ego-trajectory).
- A fast single-step decoder reconstructs future L-OGMs in real-time, complemented by an optional ==diffusion-based batch decoder== for enhanced temporal consistency and detail preservation.

## Results

- LOPR consistently surpassed existing baselines and the state-of-the-art OccWorld-2D on nuScenes and Waymo datasets, showing increased performance gains with longer prediction horizons.
- The full multi-modal stochastic LOPR achieved an IS score of **6.44** at **1.5s** on nuScenes, demonstrating improved accuracy and the ability to capture diverse, semantically meaningful future scenarios.
- The single-step decoder enabled real-time performance at **9.09 Hz** (with all modalities), while an optional diffusion-based batch decoder further improved prediction fidelity (e.g., **4.88 IS** at 1.5s on nuScenes) at a higher computational cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a continuous latent space for L-OGMs via a VAE-GAN allows for robust representation and improved long-term predictive capabilities.
> - Integrating multi-modal sensor data (RGB, maps, ego-trajectory) significantly enhances prediction accuracy and enables context-aware, stochastic multi-future reasoning.
> - Operating in a continuous latent space with a transformer-based architecture achieves real-time occupancy forecasting with superior performance compared to previous methods, including those with discrete latent spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate environment prediction is a foundational requirement for the safe navigation of autonomous vehicles (AVs) in dynamic and complex urban environments. Traditional AV perception stacks often rely on a collection of independently developed, expert-designed, and learned modules such as 3D object detection, tracking, and motion forecasting. These modules frequently utilize vectorized representations of the scene, derived from human-annotated or perception-system-generated labels.

However, this object-based paradigm exhibits several limitations. First, it often produces marginalized future trajectories for individual agents rather than a holistic scene prediction that captures agent interactions, complicating integration with downstream planning modules. Second, such approaches are inherently dependent on the upstream object detection algorithms, which can be susceptible to failures under suboptimal conditions or partial observability, leading to cascading errors. Third, the reliance on extensive and costly labeled data limits dataset size and generalization capabilities in unforeseen scenarios. These drawbacks highlight a need for complementary environment modeling approaches that can operate with minimal or no reliance on expensive labeling schemes and offer greater robustness.

LiDAR-generated Occupancy Grid Maps (L-OGMs) have emerged as an alternative scene representation to address some of these challenges. L-OGMs offer a bird's-eye view representation that requires minimal data preprocessing, enabling self-supervised joint scene predictions for an arbitrary number of agents. They also demonstrate resilience to partial observability and perception detection failures. Prior work in L-OGM prediction predominantly employed ConvLSTM-based architectures, framing the task as self-supervised sequence-to-sequence learning. These methods typically operate in the raw grid cell space, producing deterministic predictions. While successful to some extent, they often result in unrealistic or blurry forecasts and do not effectively incorporate additional sensor modalities commonly available in AVs, such as RGB cameras, detailed maps, or the ego vehicle's planned trajectory.

This research introduces Latent Occupancy Prediction (LOPR) to address these identified limitations. LOPR positions itself within the landscape by moving beyond deterministic, grid-space L-OGM prediction to stochastic prediction in a learned continuous latent space. It integrates diverse sensor modalities, aiming to produce more realistic, high-quality, and contextually aware forecasts while maintaining real-time feasibility. Furthermore, by leveraging generative models and self-supervision, LOPR aligns with the growing interest in developing "foundational models" for autonomous driving that can scale with unlabeled data and provide more robust environmental reasoning. While some prior work has explored latent space prediction using discrete representations (e.g., VQ-VAE with transformers), these often incur high inference costs. LOPR differentiates itself by employing continuous latent representations to achieve real-time performance, offering an alternative to computationally intensive discrete token-based models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a self-supervised framework for stochastic multi-future occupancy forecasting for autonomous driving, specifically using LiDAR-generated occupancy grid maps (L-OGMs) as the core environmental representation.

The motivation behind developing LOPR stems from several limitations observed in existing environment prediction approaches for autonomous vehicles:

- **Inadequate capture of environmental stochasticity:** Previous L-OGM prediction methods primarily produced deterministic forecasts in the grid cell space. This approach often leads to unrealistic predictions and fails to account for the inherent uncertainty and multimodality of real-world agent behaviors and environmental evolution. Autonomous vehicles operate in dynamic settings where other agents' intentions are often unknown, necessitating a model that can predict multiple plausible future scenarios.
- **Limited integration of multi-modal sensor data:** Most prior L-OGM prediction techniques did not effectively incorporate additional sensor modalities available in modern AVs, such as RGB camera feeds, high-definition maps, and the ego vehicle's planned trajectory. These modalities provide crucial semantic information (e.g., agent type, road topology) and extend the observable context beyond the immediate LiDAR-derived occupancy grid, which is vital for more accurate and robust predictions.
- **Dependence on costly and error-prone labeling:** Object-based prediction systems and some OGM methods rely heavily on manually labeled datasets or outputs from other perception systems. This dependency leads to high annotation costs, limits data scalability, and makes the overall AV stack susceptible to cascading failures originating from errors in upstream perception or annotation. The authors advocate for a self-supervised approach using L-OGMs to mitigate these issues.
- **Computational inefficiency of existing latent space models:** While generative models operating in latent spaces have shown promise for producing high-quality samples and compressed representations, architectures combining vector-quantized variational autoencoders (VQ-VAE) with autoregressive transformers often entail significant inference times due to the large number of discrete tokens required. The motivation was to achieve high-quality predictions within a latent space while maintaining real-time feasibility for AV applications.
- **Temporal inconsistency and compression losses in decoding:** Single-step decoding of latent predictions, while fast, can lead to issues such as lack of temporal consistency between consecutive frames and the loss of fine-grained details due to compression. A mechanism to refine decoded predictions was sought to address these fidelity concerns.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives for the LOPR framework were:

1. To develop a stochastic L-OGM prediction model that operates in the continuous latent space of a generative architecture.
2. To enable conditioning of the prediction model on diverse sensor modalities, including RGB cameras, maps, and the planned ego vehicle trajectory.
3. To provide a real-time feasible prediction solution through an efficient, autoregressive transformer-based architecture and a single-step decoder.
4. To incorporate a refinement mechanism, specifically a diffusion-based batch decoder, to improve temporal consistency and mitigate compression losses in decoded predictions, even if it entails a trade-off with real-time performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Latent Occupancy Prediction (LOPR) framework is designed to generate stochastic scene predictions using ego-centric L-OGMs. The approach decomposes the prediction task into two main phases: learning an L-OGM representation in a compressed latent space and subsequently performing predictions within this latent space.

**A. Representation Learning**
The initial phase involves training a generative model to learn a low-dimensional continuous latent space for L-OGMs. Given a 2D L-OGM grid `x` (H x W), an encoder maps it to a latent representation `z` (c x h x w). A decoder then reconstructs this latent representation back into an L-OGM `x_hat`. The generative model combines elements of a β-Variational Autoencoder (β-VAE) and a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN).

- **β-VAE Component:** The objective function includes a reconstruction loss and a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term. The reconstruction loss is a combination of perceptual loss and mean squared error, aiming to ensure high-fidelity L-OGM reconstruction. The KL divergence term regularizes the latent space by minimizing the divergence between the encoder's posterior distribution `q(z|x)` and a unit Gaussian prior `p(z)`.
- **GAN Component:** The decoder also functions as the generator in a GAN setup, aiming to produce realistic L-OGMs that can deceive a discriminator. The discriminator is trained to distinguish between real L-OGMs from the dataset and reconstructed L-OGMs from the generator. This adversarial training helps produce sharp and realistic samples.
The final representation learning loss `L_rep` is a combination of the VAE and GAN objectives.

**B. Stochastic L-OGM Sequence Prediction**
With the L-OGM latent space learned, LOPR trains a stochastic sequence prediction network. This network receives a history of observed L-OGM latent representations (`z_<t`) and other optional modalities, then predicts a distribution over future L-OGM embeddings (`p_θ(z_t | z_<t)`).

- **Modeling Stochasticity:** The inherent multimodality of environment prediction is addressed by introducing an unobservable variable `s_t` to capture scene stochasticity. During training, `s_t` is sampled from a posterior inference network `q_ϕ(s_t | z_≤t)` that has access to the ground truth L-OGM representation `z_t`. At test time, `s_t` is sampled from a learned prior network `p_γ(s_t | z_<t)`. This process is autoregressively repeated for `T_F` future steps, conditioned on `T_O` past observations.
- **Objective Function:** The framework is optimized using a variational lower bound objective, which includes a term for the likelihood of predicting `z_t` given `z_<t` and `s_≤t`, and a KL divergence term between the posterior `q_ϕ(s_t | z_≤t)` and prior `p_γ(s_t | z_<t)` distributions of `s_t`.
- **Architecture:** LOPR employs a transformer decoder-based architecture. It consists of a deterministic prediction module (`P_θ`) and two inference networks (`Q_ϕ` for posterior and `Q_γ` for prior). Sinusoidal positional encodings provide temporal context. At each time step, `s_t` is sampled (from `Q_γ` at test time, `Q_ϕ` at training time) as parameters of a Gaussian distribution. The previous L-OGM latent representation `z_t-1` and `s_t` are then processed by the deterministic decoder `P_θ` using self-attention, incorporating all past representations. Latent vectors `z_t` are spatially split into patches and flattened into tokens before being processed by attention layers. Finally, predicted compressed representations are fed to the pre-trained L-OGM decoder to reconstruct future occupancy grids.

**C. Diffusion-based Batch Decoder**
To address potential temporal inconsistencies and compression losses inherent in independent single-frame decoding, LOPR includes an optional diffusion-based batch decoder. This module refines batches of single-step decoded L-OGMs (`x_hat_t-∆:t`).
- **Mechanism:** A 3D-UNet is used as a denoising model, trained to minimize the mean-squared error between predicted and ground truth noises. During training, it's conditioned on ground truth decoded frames and the rasterized frame preceding the sequence. At test time, it reconstructs from predicted embeddings, using the last observed rasterized frame for the first batch to preserve static details. This process improves temporal consistency and detail preservation but is computationally intensive.

**D. Conditioning on Other Sensor Modalities**
LOPR is designed to integrate information from multiple sensor modalities:
- **Maps and Planned AV Trajectory:** Rasterized maps (drivable area, stop lines, pedestrian crossings) and the ego vehicle's planned trajectory (normalized position (x,y,z) over time) are processed using convolutional and fully connected networks, respectively.
- **RGB Camera:** Observations from `N` surrounding RGB cameras (`c_t`) provide semantic information (e.g., agent type, orientation) and environmental context beyond the L-OGM's field of view. To overcome limited AV dataset sizes for camera-based tasks, a pre-trained DINOv2 backbone is utilized and finetuned on a deterministic L-OGM prediction task. The image processing module (Fig. 3) uses DINOv2, followed by image, spatial (aggregating across views and with L-OGM embeddings), and temporal (aggregating across time steps and with planned trajectory) attention layers. The output (`z_cam`) is then integrated into the framework via self-attention.

**E. Conditioning on Other Information**
- **Locations:** A one-hot encoding for the dataset's collection location (e.g., Singapore, USA for nuScenes) is appended to the observation sequence.
- **Sequence Augmentation:** To augment the relatively small perception datasets, techniques like mirror reflections, rotations, and sequence reversal are applied. A one-hot encoding of the augmentation type is appended to the sequence to guide the model and prevent rule violations in predictions.

**F. Prediction Extrapolation**
At test time, the prediction horizon can be extended using a sliding-window approach, where previously predicted frames serve as new observations. To handle the absence of future camera observations during extrapolation, image embeddings are randomly dropped out.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The LOPR framework was evaluated quantitatively and qualitatively on the nuScenes and Waymo Open datasets, comparing against established L-OGM prediction baselines and state-of-the-art video prediction models.

**A. Latent Space Analysis**
Experiments on representation learning losses (Table I) indicated a trade-off. While a simple autoencoder performed well in reconstruction and short-term prediction, incorporating KL and adversarial components (VAE-GAN) improved long-term prediction capabilities, which aligns with the primary objective. The VAE-GAN with augmentations achieved the best performance across reconstruction and both short- and long-term prediction horizons. Qualitative analysis (Fig. 4) demonstrated the encoder-decoder's effectiveness in reconstructing scenes, with IS scores reflecting differences in occupied cell distributions.

**B. Prediction Task**

- **General Performance (Table II):** LOPR consistently outperformed all tested baselines, including PredRNN V2, Sim.VP V2, ED3LSTM, TAAConvLSTM, PredNet, and the state-of-the-art OccWorld-2D, across both nuScenes and Waymo Open datasets. The performance gains were more substantial as the prediction horizon extended (from 1.5s to 3.0s). Qualitative examples (Figs. 5, 6) showed LOPR generating high-quality, realistic predictions, including inference of oncoming vehicles and preservation of static road layouts.
- **Stochasticity (Table II, Fig. 7):** Modeling stochasticity had a positive quantitative impact, with the stochastic LOPR (7.00 IS at 1.5s on nuScenes) outperforming its deterministic counterpart (7.24 IS). The framework demonstrated the ability to capture diverse motion patterns for observed agents and infer hypothetical, previously unobserved agents, providing semantically meaningful multi-future reasoning.
- **Impact of Multi-modal Conditioning (Table III, Figs. 5, 6):** Each additional modality—planned trajectory, maps, and RGB cameras—contributed to significant numerical improvements in prediction accuracy. The best performance was achieved when all available modalities were combined (6.44 IS at 1.5s on nuScenes for stochastic LOPR with all modalities, vs. 7.20 IS without). This confirmed LOPR's ability to leverage information from these sensors, such as detecting oncoming vehicles visible only in camera feeds or understanding static road layouts from maps, which were beyond the direct L-OGM observation area. Finetuning the DINO-based camera module on a deterministic L-OGM task also proved beneficial.
- **Diffusion-based Batch Decoder (Table II, Fig. 8):** The diffusion-based batch decoder significantly improved temporal consistency and reduced compression losses, leading to substantial numerical improvements (e.g., 4.88 IS at 1.5s on nuScenes, compared to 6.36 IS for the single-step decoder with all modalities). Qualitatively, it restored fine details like trees, which were lost with the single-step decoder. However, this came at a significant computational cost, rendering it not real-time feasible (minutes per prediction) compared to the single-step decoder.
- **Real-time Feasibility (Table II):** LOPR with its single-step decoder demonstrated real-time capability, achieving prediction frequencies of 11.91 Hz (L-OGM only) and 9.09 Hz (with all modalities) for 15-frame predictions on NVIDIA L40 GPUs. This significantly surpasses OccWorld-2D (3.00 Hz) and reported performance for vanilla transformers with discrete latent spaces.
- **Baseline Comparison Rationale:** The observed superiority of LOPR is attributed to its ability to address the limitations of prior work. Baselines often lacked stochasticity, operated solely in grid cell space, and did not integrate multi-modal inputs. This led to a gradual loss of detail and disappearance of dynamic objects in their predictions. Even LOPR's deterministic variant outperformed baselines over longer horizons, with stochasticity and multi-modal conditioning further widening the performance gap. The performance against OccWorld-2D also highlights the efficacy of continuous latent representations in achieving faster inference while maintaining competitive or superior accuracy compared to discrete codebook transformers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LOPR framework introduces several significant advancements and holds substantial potential impact for autonomous driving research and deployment:

- **Enhanced Safety and Robustness:** By providing stochastic, multi-future predictions, LOPR enables AVs to anticipate diverse possible evolutions of the environment, including the uncertain intentions of other agents. This more comprehensive and realistic understanding of future scenarios is critical for safer navigation, particularly in complex urban settings, allowing planning modules to account for multiple contingencies. Its resilience to partial observability and perception detection failures, inherent in L-OGM representation, further enhances system robustness.
- **Improved Scene Understanding through Multi-modality:** The effective integration of RGB cameras, detailed maps, and planned trajectories allows LOPR to infer contextual information beyond what is available from LiDAR alone. This capability to "see" around corners, identify agent types, and understand road layouts contributes to a richer and more accurate environment model, leading to more informed decision-making for the AV.
- **Reduced Reliance on Costly Labeling and Foundational Model Potential:** LOPR's self-supervised nature, leveraging L-OGMs, significantly reduces the need for expensive human annotation or reliance on potentially erroneous upstream perception labels. This aligns with the emerging paradigm of foundational models in AI, suggesting that L-OGM prediction, possibly combined with RGB video prediction, could serve as a powerful unsupervised pre-training objective for autonomous driving, facilitating scalability with vast amounts of unlabeled sensor data.
- **Real-time Feasibility for Practical Deployment:** The ability of LOPR's single-step decoder variant to generate high-quality, stochastic, multi-modal predictions in real-time (approximately 9-11 Hz) is crucial for practical implementation in autonomous vehicles. This makes it a viable candidate for integration into current AV stacks, providing immediate improvements to environment prediction.
- **Advancement in Latent Space Prediction:** LOPR demonstrates that continuous latent representations can achieve superior performance and significantly faster inference compared to discrete codebook-based transformer models in occupancy prediction tasks. This offers a compelling alternative for efficiently processing and predicting high-dimensional sensor data.
- **Pathway for High-Fidelity Refinement:** Although the diffusion-based batch decoder is not yet real-time feasible, its demonstrated ability to significantly enhance temporal consistency and reduce compression losses provides a clear pathway for achieving even higher fidelity predictions. Future research, potentially leveraging advancements in fast diffusion models (e.g., consistency models), could bridge the gap to real-time high-fidelity output.
- **Broad Applicability and Extensibility:** The framework's modularity and the expressive power of its latent space suggest broad applicability. The authors themselves propose extensions to 3D occupancy prediction, occlusion inference, and path planning, indicating that LOPR could serve as a versatile foundation for various perception and planning tasks in autonomous driving.

In summary, LOPR represents a notable step forward in environment prediction for autonomous driving by combining stochasticity, multi-modal conditioning, and efficient latent space operation, delivering a robust, accurate, and practical solution with significant potential for future development.
