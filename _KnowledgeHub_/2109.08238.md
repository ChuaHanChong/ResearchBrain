# Habitat-Matterport 3D Dataset (HM3D): 1000 Large-scale 3D Environments for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> The Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) dataset provides 1,000 large-scale, high-fidelity indoor environments, featuring 34-91% fewer reconstruction artifacts and 20-85% higher visual fidelity compared to prior datasets. Training embodied AI agents on HM3D resulted in superior generalization and up to 100% success on the Gibson-test dataset, empirically validating the data scaling hypothesis.

## Problem

- Existing 3D reconstruction datasets (e.g., Matterport3D, Gibson) were limited in scale, often containing fewer than 600 scenes, hindering robust agent generalization.
- Prior datasets suffered from significant reconstruction artifacts like missing surfaces, holes, or untextured regions, creating unrealistic visual and geometric cues for embodied agents.
- Visual fidelity of rendered images from existing datasets often showed a domain gap compared to real-world captures, complicating the transfer of learned policies to physical robots.

## Method

- Acquired **HM3D**'s 1,000 real-world indoor spaces from diverse global locations using ==Matterport Pro2 depth sensors==, ensuring a broad range of architectural styles and furnishings.
- Employed Matterport's proprietary ==3D reconstruction pipeline==, followed by over 800 hours of rigorous ==human curation== to ensure high completeness, diversity, and physical integrity of the scenes.
- Anonymized personally identifiable information and explicitly packaged the dataset to be ==Habitat-ready== for seamless integration with the high-performance ==Habitat simulator==, including ==navigation meshes==.

## Results

- HM3D offers **1,000** building-scale scenes (**112.5k m²** navigable space), representing **1.4 to 3.7 times** larger coverage than previous building-scale datasets like MP3D and Gibson.
- The dataset exhibits a **34-91%** reduction in reconstruction defects and **20-85%** higher visual fidelity (measured by FID and KID metrics) compared to other photorealistic 3D datasets.
- Agents trained exclusively on HM3D achieved **100%** success on the Gibson-test dataset and outperformed the next-best agents by **3-4 SPL points** on the challenging MP3D-test dataset for PointGoal navigation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling the quantity and quality of 3D environment datasets empirically correlates with improved embodied AI agent performance and generalization, showing a strong near-linear relationship (Pearson ρ = 0.88) between navigable area and PointNav SPL.
> - High-fidelity and near-complete 3D environments significantly reduce the sim-to-real gap, enabling agents to learn more robust and transferable skills that generalize exceptionally to unseen environments.
> - Training on a sufficiently large and diverse dataset allows agents to achieve pareto-optimal performance, consistently outperforming agents trained on smaller or lower-quality datasets across various evaluation benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) is a critical advancement in the rapidly evolving field of embodied AI. Embodied AI agents, designed to perceive, navigate, and act within physical environments, heavily rely on realistic 3D datasets for training and evaluation. Before HM3D, the research landscape for 3D indoor environments was characterized by several types of datasets, each with limitations that this work aims to address:

- **Synthetic/CAD-based datasets:** These environments (e.g., AI2-Thor, VirtualHome, Habitat 2.0's synthetic scenes) offer high control over scene composition and object interaction. However, they often lack the visual and geometric diversity, architectural complexity, and realistic clutter found in the real world, leading to a significant "sim-to-real" gap. Objects tend to be reused, and their rendered appearance may not perfectly mimic reality.
- **3D Reconstruction datasets (Real-world scans):** These datasets aim to capture the authenticity of real-world spaces.
 - **Room-scale datasets:** Early efforts like SceneNN and ScanNet provided high-quality scans but were limited to individual rooms or regions, restricting training of agents for navigation in larger, multi-room, or multi-floor settings.
 - **Building-scale datasets:** Datasets such as BuildingParser, Matterport3D (MP3D), and Gibson were pioneering in providing reconstructions of entire buildings. However, they suffered from critical limitations:
 - **Limited Scale:** Even the largest (Gibson, 571 scenes) was relatively small compared to the diversity of real-world architecture.
 - **Incompleteness & Artifacts:** Many scans in these datasets had significant reconstruction errors, "holes," or untextured regions, creating unrealistic scenarios for agents (e.g., "seeing through walls"). The authors note that only a fraction of Gibson scenes were considered high quality.
 - **Visual Fidelity:** While based on real scans, the visual quality of rendered images often still presented a domain gap compared to actual camera captures.
- **Floorplan and Panorama datasets:** Datasets like Zillow Indoor provide floorplans and 360-degree panoramas but lack full 3D mesh reconstructions of interiors or detailed object appearance, making them less suitable for embodied navigation that requires depth and geometric understanding.

HM3D positions itself as a direct response to these limitations by offering **1,000 building-scale, high-fidelity, near-complete, and diverse real-world 3D reconstructions**. It fills a critical gap by providing a dataset that combines the scale needed for robust agent training with the visual and geometric realism essential for bridging the sim-to-real gap. By being "Habitat-ready," it also integrates directly into an existing, powerful embodied AI simulation ecosystem, making it immediately impactful for the research community. This work aims to set a new standard for physically realistic environments, thereby catalyzing the development of more capable and generalizable embodied AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) dataset is to provide a significantly improved and expanded resource for the embodied AI research community. The authors' motivation stems directly from the recognized shortcomings of existing 3D indoor environment datasets, which have become a bottleneck for advancing the capabilities of intelligent agents.

The key objectives and motivations are:

1. **To provide a large-scale collection of 3D environments:** The existing building-scale datasets were limited in the total number of scenes and the overall navigable area. HM3D aims to dramatically increase this scale, offering 1,000 unique building-scale reconstructions, which translates to a substantially larger and more diverse set of spaces for agents to explore and learn within. This addresses the need for more training data to achieve robust and generalizable agent behaviors.
2. **To ensure high completeness of 3D reconstructions:** Prior datasets frequently suffered from reconstruction artifacts such as missing surfaces, holes, or untextured regions. These imperfections create unrealistic visual and geometric cues that can hinder agent learning and make environments less representative of the real world. A core objective of HM3D is to provide near-complete reconstructions with significantly fewer such defects.
3. **To achieve high visual fidelity:** The accuracy of rendered images from simulated environments is crucial for developing agents that can transfer effectively to real-world scenarios (reducing the "sim-to-real" gap). HM3D aims to offer visual fidelity that closely matches real-world camera captures, making the simulated experience as realistic as possible for visual perception tasks.
4. **To offer diverse scene categories and geographical locations:** Learning in a homogenous set of environments can lead to agents that struggle to generalize. HM3D seeks to provide a broad spectrum of indoor spaces—from multi-floor residences to offices, restaurants, and shops—across a diverse set of geographic locations. This diversity is crucial for training agents that are robust and adaptable to various real-world settings.
5. **To facilitate embodied AI research within the Habitat simulator:** By being explicitly "Habitat-ready" and providing pre-packaged meta-data, the dataset aims to seamlessly integrate with the high-performance Habitat simulator. This objective is to reduce the logistical barriers for researchers, allowing them to focus on agent development rather than environment setup.
6. **To validate the data scaling hypothesis for embodied AI:** A key motivation is to empirically demonstrate that increasing the scale and quality of 3D datasets directly leads to improved performance and generalization for embodied AI agents, similar to what has been observed in passive perception tasks.

In essence, HM3D is motivated by the need for a comprehensive, high-quality, and accessible dataset that pushes the boundaries of realism and scale, thereby empowering the next generation of embodied AI agents to learn and operate more effectively in complex real-world indoor environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of the Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) dataset involved a multi-stage approach, combining commercial scanning technology, a robust reconstruction pipeline, and extensive human-in-the-loop curation to ensure high quality and diversity.

1. **Data Acquisition:**
 - The dataset comprises 1,000 real-world indoor spaces, including multi-floor residences, stores, offices, and other private interiors.
 - These spaces were originally scanned by Matterport Inc. users using the **Matterport Pro2 tripod-based depth sensor**. This professional-grade sensor is known for its ability to capture high-resolution RGB-D data.
 - The scans originated from a diverse set of 38 countries and 181 geographic regions, ensuring a broad range of architectural styles, furnishing, and clutter.

2. **3D Reconstruction Pipeline:**
 - Matterport Inc.'s proprietary reconstruction pipeline was used to process the raw RGB-D data. This pipeline performs crucial steps such as aligning the RGB-D data, generating 3D surface meshes, and applying textures to create photorealistic 3D models of the environments. This process is critical for producing the high-fidelity textured meshes that characterize HM3D.

3. **Dataset Curation and Quality Assurance (800+ hours of human effort):**
 - To ensure the quality and utility of the 1,000 scenes, a rigorous two-stage annotation and verification process was employed from a larger pool of candidate scenes:
 - **Stage 1 (Volunteer Annotation):** A group of 15 volunteer annotators rated each candidate scene on a 1-5 quality scale. Their assessment considered several factors crucial for embodied AI research:
 - Presence of reconstruction artifacts (holes, cracks, missing surfaces).
 - Realism and density of furnishing.
 - Number of closed doors (impacting navigability).
 - "Interactive potential" (presence of objects an agent could interact with).
 - Quantitative metrics characterizing scene navigability to detect disconnectedness issues.
 - **Stage 2 (Expert Curation):** Three expert annotators then collated scenes, prioritizing those with high ratings. They further ensured diversity in the final selection, particularly focusing on the distribution of the number of floors per scene. This dual-stage human curation was essential for selecting high-quality scenes that are physically complete and diverse.

4. **Privacy and Ethical Considerations:**
 - Matterport Inc. worked in collaboration with the Habitat team to obtain consent from users to contribute their scans for academic and non-commercial AI research.
 - A critical step involved obscuring personally identifiable information (e.g., faces, names, documents, license plates) from the scans. Human reviewers previewed images, annotated personal information, which was then blurred using pixel-wise masks. The 3D scans were then recreated using this anonymized data, adhering to privacy standards.

5. **Habitat Integration:**
 - The dataset is explicitly "Habitat-ready," meaning it comes pre-packed with meta-data and support necessary for seamless integration with the Habitat simulator, a high-performance platform for embodied AI. This includes navigation meshes and other properties required for efficient simulation.

6. **Quantitative Analysis and Agent Experiments:**
 - The authors performed extensive quantitative analyses to compare HM3D with existing datasets across metrics like floor area, navigable area, navigation complexity, scene clutter, reconstruction completeness, and visual fidelity (using FID and KID metrics against real images).
 - For embodied AI evaluation, they used the **PointGoal Navigation (PointNav)** task, a standard benchmark. Agents were trained using a standard DD-PPO algorithm with ResNet-50 and LSTM architecture, leveraging RGB-D and GPS+Compass inputs. Experiments were conducted on HM3D, Gibson, and Matterport3D, including cross-dataset generalization tests and an investigation into the data scaling hypothesis by training on subsets of HM3D.

This comprehensive approach, from professional scanning and reconstruction to meticulous human curation and integration with a leading simulator, underpins HM3D's claims of superior scale, completeness, and fidelity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a compelling case for HM3D as a superior dataset for embodied AI through both comprehensive dataset characteristic comparisons and empirical evaluations with navigation agents.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics and Comparison

- **Scale:** HM3D significantly surpasses existing datasets. It features 1,000 building-scale scenes, totaling 112.5k m² of navigable space. This is 1.4 to 3.7 times larger than previous building-scale datasets like MP3D and Gibson. The dataset includes over 10,600 rooms and 1,920 building floors, with individual scenes ranging from 49m² to 2,172m² and up to 8 floors and 93 rooms. This unprecedented scale provides a vast and diverse training ground.
- **Completeness of Reconstruction:** HM3D exhibits drastically fewer reconstruction artifacts. Quantitative analysis showed a 34-91% reduction in defects (missing surfaces, holes, untextured regions) compared to other photorealistic 3D datasets (Replica, MP3D, Gibson, ScanNet). For instance, HM3D has more than three times as many scenes with less than 5% views exhibiting artifacts compared to the full Gibson dataset (560 vs. 175 scenes). This high completeness reduces unrealistic visual cues that can hinder agent learning.
- **Visual Fidelity:** Images rendered from HM3D boast significantly higher visual fidelity. Measured using FID and KID metrics against real camera-captured images from Gibson and MP3D, HM3D renders showed 20-85% higher fidelity than other datasets. This is crucial for narrowing the sim-to-real gap, allowing agents trained in simulation to generalize better to the real world.
- **Diversity and Complexity:** The dataset contains a wide range of environments, including multi-floor residences, offices, restaurants, and shops, from 38 countries. Metrics for scene clutter and navigation complexity demonstrate that HM3D scenes are comparable to or exceed the complexity of other building-scale datasets (e.g., MP3D, Gibson), offering challenging layouts for navigation tasks.

#### 4.2. Embodied AI Agent Performance (PointGoal Navigation)

The authors benchmarked PointGoal navigation agents, a standard task in embodied AI, using HM3D and other datasets (MP3D, Gibson 4+, Gibson).

- **Pareto-Optimal Performance:** HM3D-trained agents exhibited 'pareto-optimal' performance. They achieved the highest success rates and Shortest Path Length (SPL) scores across *all* evaluation datasets (HM3D, Gibson, and MP3D test sets), regardless of whether they were trained with RGB or depth inputs. No agent trained on other datasets could match this consistent performance across all test environments.
- **Exceptional Generalization:** Agents trained solely on HM3D demonstrated remarkable generalization capabilities:
 - They achieved 100% success on the Gibson-test dataset (with depth inputs), effectively "solving" this benchmark.
 - On the more challenging MP3D-test dataset, HM3D-trained agents outperformed the next-best agents (trained on Gibson) by 3-4 SPL points. This highlights HM3D's richness and diversity, enabling agents to adapt to previously unseen scene appearances and layouts.
- **Robustness to Episode Difficulty:** HM3D-trained agents showed superior adaptability to increasing episode difficulty (measured by geodesic distance between start and goal locations) on MP3D-test, outperforming other agents, especially on harder episodes. This indicates that the diverse and complex layouts within HM3D foster more robust navigation policies.
- **Validation of Data Scaling Hypothesis:** Crucially, the experiments demonstrated a strong, near-linear correlation (Pearson coefficient ρ = 0.88) between the total navigable area in the training dataset and the PointNav performance (SPL). This empirically validates the data scaling hypothesis for embodied AI, confirming that providing larger and higher-quality datasets directly leads to better-performing and more generalizable agents.

In summary, the findings establish HM3D as a new benchmark for 3D environments, not only due to its intrinsic qualities (scale, completeness, fidelity) but also because of its tangible positive impact on embodied AI agent learning, leading to superior performance and generalization across diverse tasks and environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) dataset represents a significant milestone in the field of embodied AI, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Setting a New Standard for 3D Environments:** HM3D dramatically raises the bar for 3D indoor environment datasets available for academic research. By offering unprecedented scale (1,000 building-scale scenes, 112.5k m² navigable space), superior reconstruction completeness (34-91% fewer artifacts), and higher visual fidelity (20-85% closer to real images), it provides a more realistic and robust foundation than any prior dataset. This new standard will push the entire field to develop more sophisticated and robust embodied AI agents.

2. **Accelerating Embodied AI Research:** The dataset's sheer size and quality directly address a major bottleneck in embodied AI—the lack of realistic and diverse training data. Researchers now have access to environments that better mimic the complexity and appearance of the real world, enabling them to:
 - Train more generalizable agents for tasks like visual navigation, object search, and instruction following.
 - Develop and test algorithms under more realistic perceptual and navigational challenges.
 - Explore multi-task learning and long-horizon planning in complex, multi-floor structures.

3. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The enhanced visual fidelity and reduced reconstruction artifacts of HM3D are crucial for mitigating the "sim-to-real" gap. Agents trained in these high-fidelity simulated environments are more likely to transfer their learned skills effectively to physical robots operating in real-world settings, thereby accelerating the practical deployment of embodied AI.

4. **Empirical Validation of Data Scaling:** The paper's empirical validation of the "data scaling hypothesis" for embodied AI (showing a strong, near-linear correlation between dataset size and agent performance) provides critical guidance for future research. It reinforces the importance of investing in large-scale, high-quality data collection efforts, suggesting that further scaling could lead to even more capable agents. This insight can drive resource allocation and research priorities in the community.

5. **Catalyst for Advanced Tasks:** While initially benchmarked with PointGoal navigation, the rich geometry and photorealism of HM3D lay the groundwork for more complex embodied AI tasks. Future extensions with semantic annotations and physical attributes, as suggested by the authors, could enable tasks such as ObjectGoal navigation, object rearrangement, and complex human-robot interaction scenarios. Its "Habitat-ready" nature ensures immediate usability within a leading simulation platform, further amplifying its impact.

6. **Open Access and Community Resource:** The availability of HM3D for academic, non-commercial research makes it a valuable community resource. This open access will foster widespread adoption and collaborative innovation, democratizing access to high-quality 3D data that was previously scarce.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including potential biases in data acquisition (restricted to Matterport Pro2 users/regions) and the dataset's current focus on static environments without semantic annotations or dynamic objects. Future work is envisioned to address these by augmenting HM3D with semantics, object retrieval capabilities, and support for dynamic, changing environments, further bringing simulated training environments closer to the fluidity of the real world.

In conclusion, HM3D is not just another dataset; it is a foundational research artifact that will significantly advance the capabilities of embodied AI by providing an unprecedented level of realism, scale, and diversity. It is poised to catalyze new discoveries and accelerate the development of truly intelligent agents capable of navigating and interacting with our complex physical world.
