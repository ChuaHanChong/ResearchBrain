# Humanoid Occupancy: Enabling A Generalized Multimodal Occupancy Perception System on Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at X-Humanoid and GigaAI developed "Humanoid Occupancy," a multimodal perception system for humanoid robots that generates 3D occupancy and semantic maps. It achieved an mIoU of 55.73% and a rayIoU of 61.32% on a new panoramic dataset, providing comprehensive environmental understanding for complex humanoid tasks.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack comprehensive and generalized visual perception systems, facing challenges in sensor layout, data collection, and semantic-geometric understanding for complex environments.
- Existing perception paradigms, including those based on occupancy grids, are often tailored for autonomous driving and do not meet the near-field, decimeter-level accuracy, and dense annotation requirements for humanoid interaction.
- Diverse humanoid tasks (manipulation, locomotion, navigation) require versatile perception that can provide rich texture, precise geometry, and global semantic understanding simultaneously.

## Method

- Implemented a unique sensor layout on the Tienkung humanoid robot, combining 6 RGB cameras and a 40-line 360-degree LiDAR sensor on the head to mitigate blind spots and kinematic interference.
- Constructed the first large-scale panoramic occupancy dataset for humanoid robots, collected via a wearable device and featuring detailed point-wise semantic annotations and dynamic object handling.
- Developed a multimodal fusion network that processes camera and LiDAR inputs using a BEV paradigm, integrating distortion-aware cross-attention and temporal fusion for robust 3D occupancy and semantic map generation.

## Results

- The Humanoid Occupancy system achieved an mIoU of **55.73%** and a rayIoU of **61.32%** with 2 aggregated frames, demonstrating competitive performance in 3D semantic segmentation and depth-axis consistency compared to existing BEV perception models.
- The proposed distortion-aware projection strategy improved mIoU to **47.92%** and rayIoU to **53.97%** in single-frame camera-only settings, outperforming traditional undistortion preprocessing while reducing latency.
- Multimodal fusion of camera and LiDAR inputs significantly enhanced perception (**55.73%** mIoU, **61.32%** rayIoU) compared to camera-only (**50.37%** mIoU) or LiDAR-only (**48.61%** mIoU) approaches, underscoring the benefits of combining diverse sensor data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Occupancy representation effectively encodes both 2D planar distribution and detailed 3D structural/semantic attributes, making it a robust and extensible visual representation for humanoid robots.
> - Incorporating camera lens distortion parameters directly into the view transformation during multimodal feature sampling enhances perception accuracy and avoids the latency of traditional undistortion methods.
> - Temporal aggregation of multi-frame BEV features improves performance, with optimal results achieved using two historical frames, suggesting a balance between leveraging past information and managing accumulated pose errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of visual perception systems for humanoid robots is an active area of research, evolving from single-sensor configurations to increasingly complex multimodal fusion architectures. Early systems frequently relied on monocular or stereo cameras, which presented limitations in perception range and depth sensing for complex environments. Subsequent advancements integrated RGB-D cameras, LiDAR, and panoramic cameras to provide more comprehensive environmental data.

Current perception paradigms for humanoid robots include vision-based systems leveraging camera arrays and deep learning, multimodal fusion systems combining diverse sensor inputs, end-to-end perception and decision-making frameworks, and spatial representation-based systems utilizing occupancy grids, point clouds, or meshes. Vision-based approaches, exemplified by robots like Boston Dynamics' Atlas, focus on object detection, semantic segmentation, and 3D reconstruction. Multimodal fusion, employed by robots such as Agility Robotics' Digit, combines complementary sensor strengths to enhance accuracy and robustness, particularly for dynamic obstacles and varying lighting. End-to-end systems integrate perception with control, often using reinforcement or imitation learning, though they demand extensive datasets and computational resources.

This paper positions its work within the context of spatial representation-based perception, specifically advocating for occupancy-based representations. Occupancy grids, which encode both semantic and geometric information in a structured format, are noted for their utility in downstream tasks like navigation and manipulation. While occupancy prediction has seen significant advancements in autonomous driving, particularly with large-scale datasets like OpenOccupancy and Occ3D, its application in humanoid robotics remains comparatively limited. Autonomous driving datasets typically focus on long-range perception (50-200m) with meter-level resolution, which contrasts with the near-field, decimeter-level accuracy and dense annotations required for humanoid interaction in human-centric, dynamic, and cluttered environments. Humanoid robots also face unique challenges such as frequent occlusions and articulated motions, necessitating tailored solutions for sensor layout, data annotation, and multimodal fusion.

The research presented here aims to bridge this gap by introducing a multimodal occupancy perception system designed specifically for the unique requirements and structural characteristics of humanoid robots, thereby contributing to the development of robust and generalized visual modules for this platform.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a generalized multimodal occupancy perception system for humanoid robots, termed "Humanoid Occupancy," that enables comprehensive environmental understanding for various downstream tasks. This objective is driven by several key challenges and specific requirements inherent to humanoid robotics:

1. **Challenges in Humanoid Robot Perception:** Humanoid visual perception systems face challenges including designing optimal multimodal sensor layouts for complex environments, efficiently collecting and annotating diverse large-scale datasets, and achieving generality and standardization in visual system design. The unique structural characteristics of humanoids introduce issues such as kinematic interference, data scarcity, and limitations in representational capacity, which can restrict their effective deployment.

2. **Diverse Task Requirements:** Humanoid robots are designed for three core tasks: manipulation, locomotion, and navigation. Each task imposes distinct perception requirements:
 - **Manipulation** requires rich texture and geometric information for precise object recognition and handling.
 - **Locomotion** emphasizes terrain geometry perception for stable movement in varied environments.
 - **Navigation** demands a global understanding of environmental semantics and spatial geometry for efficient path planning.
 These diverse demands necessitate a versatile and robust perception system capable of providing comprehensive environmental data.

3. **Rationale for Occupancy Representation:** The selection of occupancy as the core visual representation paradigm is motivated by its advantages over traditional methods:
 - **Rich Information Encoding:** Occupancy representation directly encodes the occupancy status of each spatial unit (voxel or grid), capturing both 2D planar distribution and detailed 3D structural and semantic attributes. This is considered superior to representations like Bird's Eye View (BEV), which typically focus on ground projections and lack vertical and high-level semantic information.
 - **Multimodal Data Fusion Suitability:** Occupancy representation is naturally suited for integrating information from diverse sensors (RGB, depth, LiDAR) within a unified spatial grid, facilitating comprehensive environmental understanding.
 - **Generality and Extensibility:** Compared to point clouds or meshes, occupancy offers greater generality and extensibility in data structure, semantic annotation, and interfaces for downstream tasks, aiding large-scale data processing and model training.
 - **Direct Support for Downstream Tasks:** The dense spatial distribution output by occupancy directly supports path planning, obstacle avoidance, and manipulation tasks, enhancing robot adaptability in complex environments.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need to overcome existing limitations in humanoid robot perception by developing a unified, efficient, and information-rich system based on occupancy representation, specifically tailored to address the challenges of sensor integration, data acquisition, and semantic-geometric understanding for complex real-world operation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Humanoid Occupancy system is implemented using a three-stage visual system architecture: hardware design, dataset construction, and a multimodal fusion network.

#### 3.1. Hardware Design and Sensor Layout

The system is implemented on the Tienkung humanoid robot. The sensor suite consists of 6 RGB cameras and a 40-line 360-degree omnidirectional LiDAR sensor.
- **Camera Configuration:** The 6 RGB cameras are arranged with one in the front, one in the back, and two on each side. Each camera provides a horizontal field of view (FOV) of 118 degrees and a vertical FOV of 92 degrees.
- **LiDAR Configuration:** The LiDAR sensor offers a 360-degree omnidirectional scan with a vertical FOV of 59 degrees.
- **Placement Strategy:** The entire sensor suite is mounted on the humanoid robot's head. This configuration aims to provide robust mapping and semantic understanding of the surrounding environment, while mitigating perception blind spots caused by the robot's kinematic structure and minimizing interference during various operational scenarios.

#### 3.2. Data Acquisition and Annotation Pipeline

To address the difficulty and high cost of collecting data directly with humanoid robots, a wearable device was developed.
- **Wearable Device:** Human data collectors wear a device with the identical sensor configuration (6 cameras and 1 LiDAR) used on the humanoid robot. This approach reduces collection costs and difficulties.
- **Collection Protocol:** Data collectors are required to be approximately 160 cm tall to ensure sensor height distribution matches that of the robot. A neck stabilizer is used to prevent head shaking and maintain horizontal stability of the sensors during data collection.
- **Dataset Composition:** The collected data is categorized into home, industrial, and outdoor scenes, comprising 180 training clips and 20 evaluation clips, with each clip containing 200 frames.
- **Annotation Process:**
 - **Semantic Categories:** Different point-wise semantic categories are defined for each scene type (e.g., pedestrian, robot, chair, floor for home scenes; conveyor, static objects for industrial scenes; vehicle, road, building for outdoor scenes).
 - **Dynamic Object Annotation:** Bounding boxes are annotated for dynamic objects like pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles. For "special posture pedestrians" (those not fully representable by bounding boxes), additional point-by-point annotation within the bounding box is performed to distinguish the pedestrian from other objects.
 - **Static Object Annotation:** After removing dynamic targets from each clip, the remaining static points from multiple frames are superimposed. Point-level semantic annotation is then performed, referencing the projection of the point cloud onto the images.
 - **Occupancy Ground Truth Generation:** The annotated static superimposed point cloud is aligned to the ego coordinate system of each frame. Dynamically annotated foreground points (from bounding boxes) are then merged into the point cloud according to their respective postures in each frame. The merged point cloud is directly voxelized to produce the final 3D occupancy ground truth, which includes both occupancy status and semantic labels. Poisson reconstruction is not utilized in this process.

#### 3.3. Multi-Modal Fusion Network

The network processes multimodal inputs (LiDAR point cloud and 6 camera images) using a Bird's Eye View (BEV) paradigm for feature extraction and fusion. Sensor data is transformed into a gravity-aligned egocentric reference frame to align with BEV assumptions. The model architecture consists of separate encoders for each modality, a multimodal fusion module, a temporal fusion module, and a prediction head.

- **Camera Feature Encoder:** A shared convolutional neural network backbone, pre-trained ResNet50 with FPN, extracts features from the 6 camera images. Input images are raw, distorted pinhole camera images, as de-distortion processing is omitted to reduce system latency.
- **LiDAR Feature Encoder:** PointPillar is employed to construct BEV features from the LiDAR point cloud. Raw points are voxelized into non-empty pillars, and their attributes are encoded via an MLP. These pillar-wise features form a sparse pseudo-BEV feature map, which is then processed by a 2D convolutional backbone into a dense BEV representation.
- **Multimodal Fusion:** Inspired by DeepFusion, LiDAR-to-camera cross-attention is used. LiDAR BEV features serve as queries, while camera convolutional features serve as keys and values. Deformable Attention is used for feature sampling to accurately describe the projection relationship. Pinhole camera distortion projection is directly incorporated during sampling to account for distorted camera features, aiming to reduce the impact of image distortion on occupancy prediction. This fusion combines LiDAR's geometric information with camera's semantic information.
- **Historical Feature Fusion:** To leverage temporal information and maintain efficiency, a BEVDet4D-style dynamic feature queue stores BEV features from previous timesteps. Historical BEV feature maps are aligned to the current ego coordinate frame using relative ego-motion transformation and bilinear interpolation-based warping. Aligned historical features are concatenated with the current frame's BEV features along the channel dimension. A BEV encoder, comprising ResNet with residual blocks and FPN, processes these multi-temporal features.
- **Prediction Head:** Following FlashOcc, the 2D BEV features are transformed into 3D voxel space through channel-to-height redistribution. FPN features are upsampled and merged via concatenation and convolution. The consolidated BEV feature map is reshaped into 3D voxels by splitting channels into predefined height bins. A 3D convolution block then processes these 3D voxel features.
- **Loss Function:** The network is optimized using a combination of focal loss ($\mathcal{L}_{focal}$), Lovasz-softmax loss ($\mathcal{L}_{ls}$), and affinity losses for scene-wise ($\mathcal{L}_{geo}^{scal}$) and class-wise ($\mathcal{L}_{sem}^{scal}$) metrics (geometric IoU and semantic mIoU).
- **Training Details:** The model is implemented using the mmdetection3d framework and trained on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for 20 epochs with a batch size of 4, using an AdamW optimizer with a cosine annealing learning rate scheduler. Temporal information is integrated from the second epoch onwards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Humanoid Occupancy system was evaluated on a proprietary multi-modal dataset collected from 6 cameras and 1 LiDAR sensor, using mIoU (mean Intersection over Union) for semantic segmentation accuracy and rayIoU for depth-axis consistency.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

The model's performance was benchmarked against existing BEV perception models (BEVDet, FBOcc, BEVFusion) in both single-frame and multi-frame settings.
- **Single-Frame Performance:**
 - Humanoid Occupancy (C+L, 1 frame) achieved an mIoU of 52.79% and a rayIoU of 60.49% with 40.5M parameters.
 - In comparison, BEVDet (C, 1 frame) showed 47.93% mIoU, FBOcc (C, 1 frame) 47.37% mIoU, and BEVFusion (C+L, 1 frame) 53.98% mIoU. While BEVFusion shows a slightly higher mIoU, Humanoid Occupancy demonstrates a comparable rayIoU while utilizing fewer parameters (60.6M for BEVFusion vs. 40.5M for Humanoid Occupancy).
- **Multi-Frame Performance (2 frames):**
 - Humanoid Occupancy (C+L, 2 frames) achieved an mIoU of 55.73% and a rayIoU of 61.32% with 40.5M parameters.
 - This represents an improvement over its single-frame performance and generally surpasses other methods in the 2-frame setting (e.g., BEVDet 47.95% mIoU, FBOcc 46.70% mIoU, BEVFusion 53.21% mIoU).
 - The results indicate that the proposed model provides competitive performance on the benchmark metrics, often with a reduced parameter count compared to the evaluated baselines.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

**Ablation of Distortion Handling Strategies:**
Three strategies for handling camera image distortion were compared:
- Using raw pinhole distorted images directly: 46.23% mIoU, 52.54% rayIoU.
- Traditional image undistortion preprocessing: 47.41% mIoU, 53.46% rayIoU. This method introduced significant latency.
- Proposed distortion-aware projection (encoding lens distortion parameters directly into view transformation during sampling): 47.92% mIoU, 53.97% rayIoU. This method showed superior performance and avoided the latency associated with explicit undistortion.

**Ablation of Temporal Information (Number of Aggregated Frames):**
The impact of aggregating historical frames was investigated:
- 1 frame: 52.79% mIoU, 60.49% rayIoU.
- 2 frames: 55.73% mIoU, 61.32% rayIoU. This configuration achieved the highest performance.
- 3 frames: 55.11% mIoU, 60.53% rayIoU.
- 4 frames: 54.30% mIoU, 60.63% rayIoU.
Performance degradation was observed when incorporating more than two historical frames, which the authors attributed to the accumulation of pose errors during robotic motions.

**Ablation of Sensor Modalities:**
The contribution of different sensor modalities was assessed:
- Camera-only (C): 50.37% mIoU, 55.98% rayIoU. This configuration showed relatively better semantic differentiation.
- LiDAR-only (L): 48.61% mIoU, 59.01% rayIoU. This configuration provided good occupancy prediction but poorer semantic differentiation due to lack of rich visual cues.
- Multimodal fusion (C+L, 2 frames): 55.73% mIoU, 61.32% rayIoU. The fusion approach demonstrated the highest performance, indicating that it effectively combines LiDAR's geometric precision with camera's rich semantic information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Humanoid Occupancy system presents a series of contributions to the field of humanoid robotics and environmental perception.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Generalized Multimodal Perception System:** The work introduces an integrated hardware and software framework that leverages occupancy-based representations for comprehensive semantic and geometric environmental understanding tailored for humanoid robots.
- **Novel Sensor Layout Strategy:** The system incorporates a sensor layout strategy designed to mitigate perception blind spots and kinematic interference common in humanoid platforms, enhancing the robot's overall perceptual coverage.
- **First Panoramic Occupancy Dataset for Humanoids:** The creation of a dedicated panoramic occupancy dataset for humanoid robots addresses a recognized data scarcity problem. This dataset includes detailed point-wise semantic annotations and dynamic object handling, providing a valuable resource for future research and benchmarking in this specialized domain.
- **Robust Multimodal Fusion Network:** The proposed network architecture effectively integrates multimodal (camera and LiDAR) features using distortion-aware cross-attention and incorporates temporal information, leading to robust perception in complex environments.
- **Demonstration on a Humanoid Platform:** The system's implementation and evaluation on the Tienkung humanoid robot platform demonstrate its practical applicability and effectiveness in real-world scenarios.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancing Humanoid Robot Environmental Perception:** By addressing challenges specific to humanoid kinematics and operational contexts, the system contributes to enhancing the robots' ability to understand and interact with complex environments. The fine-grained spatial reasoning enabled by occupancy maps is directly relevant for tasks such as precise foot placement, object manipulation, and safe navigation in cluttered human environments.
- **Foundation for Standardized Visual Modules:** The development of a generalized perception system and an associated dataset can establish a technical foundation for standardizing universal visual modules for humanoid robots. This standardization could streamline development efforts across different robot platforms and accelerate their integration into diverse real-world applications.
- **Facilitating Deployment in Complex Scenarios:** Improved environmental understanding through occupancy perception is a prerequisite for more autonomous and adaptable humanoid robots. This work has the potential to facilitate the widespread deployment of humanoid robots in various complex settings, including homes, industrial facilities, and outdoor environments.
- **Resource for Future Research:** The released panoramic occupancy dataset serves as a benchmark and a foundation for future research in areas such as 3D semantic segmentation, occupancy prediction, and multimodal fusion specifically for humanoid platforms.

The authors indicate future work directions include exploring omnidirectional perception and mapping using state-of-the-art computer vision reconstruction techniques, further expanding the dataset, refining temporal fusion strategies, and deploying the system across diverse humanoid platforms to enhance robustness and standardization in robotic visual perception.
