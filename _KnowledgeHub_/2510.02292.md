# From Behavioral Performance to Internal Competence: Interpreting Vision-Language Models with VLM-Lens

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM-LENS provides a unified, extensible toolkit for systematically extracting and analyzing the internal representations of diverse Vision-Language Models. It enables a shift from evaluating behavioral performance to understanding a VLM's internal competence, as demonstrated by insights into how models encode primitive concepts and process ambiguous multimodal inputs.

## Problem

- Current VLM evaluation primarily relies on superficial, exact-match accuracy metrics, overlooking the richness of internal representations and potentially leading to misleading assessments.
- The research landscape lacks comprehensive and extensible interpretability toolkits for VLMs, making systematic cross-model analysis challenging due to proprietary nature or limited model support.
- Researchers face substantial hurdles in extracting intermediate representations from diverse VLM architectures beyond basic layer-wise outputs.

## Method

- VLM-LENS employs PyTorch's ==`hook` mechanism== to extract internal representations from any specified layer during a VLM's forward pass, capturing output tensors.
- It provides a ==unified, YAML-configurable interface== that abstracts model-specific complexities, streamlining model loading, preprocessing, and inference across diverse architectures.
- The toolkit supports ==16 base VLMs and over 30 variants==, offering isolated, model-specific environment setups for easy installation and reproducibility.

## Results

- Probing experiments revealed significant differences in internal competence across VLMs, with Qwen2-VL-7B and MiniCPM-o achieving near-perfect accuracy for primitive concepts, while LLaVA-1.5-7B showed weaker competence.
- Concept similarity analysis with Stroop-like stimuli demonstrated that lexical content (the written word) can influence VLM color perception more prominently than the actual font color, with background color being the most dominant visual cue.
- System evaluation showed generative VLM inference times ranging from **0.092s/instance** (MiniCPM-V-2) to **0.623s/instance** (InternVL) and peak GPU memory usage varying from **7GB** to over **34GB**, indicating architectural efficiency differences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluating internal competence, not just behavioral performance, is crucial for truly understanding VLM capabilities and limitations, distinguishing genuine understanding from shortcut learning.
> - Standardizing access to VLM internal states significantly lowers the technical barrier for interpretability research, accelerating scientific understanding and improvement of these models.
> - Different VLMs exhibit varied internal competence in encoding primitive concepts like color, material, and shape within their hidden states, complementing traditional output-based evaluations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have propelled them to the forefront of AI research, enabling a wide array of applications from multimodal understanding and robotics to world modeling. However, the paper identifies a critical gap in the existing research landscape: while VLMs exhibit impressive performance on various benchmarks, their evaluation predominantly relies on superficial, exact-match accuracy metrics. This approach often overlooks the richness of information embedded in their hidden representations and can lead to misleading assessments due to models exploiting "shortcuts" rather than truly understanding.

This work addresses two major deficiencies in the current VLM research paradigm:
1. **Limited Competence Analysis:** Current VLM benchmarks, such as visual question answering (VQA) and image captioning, primarily gauge "performance" – what the model outputs. VLM-LENS aims to facilitate "competence analysis" – understanding *how* and *why* a model arrives at an output, by peering into its internal mechanisms and the generalizable knowledge it possesses. This shift is crucial because high behavioral performance does not always equate to robust, human-like understanding or reasoning, as highlighted by issues like shortcut learning and failures on simple synthetic data.
2. **Underdeveloped Interpretability Tools for VLMs:** Compared to their text-only (e.g., TransformerLens) or vision-only counterparts, comprehensive and extensible interpretability toolkits for VLMs are significantly underdeveloped. Existing VLM-specific tools are often proprietary, insufficiently documented, or limited to supporting a single model, making systematic, cross-model analysis challenging and engineering-intensive. Researchers often face substantial hurdles in extracting intermediate representations beyond basic layer-wise outputs (like attention maps or pre-normalization features) from diverse, often bespoke, VLM architectures.

VLM-LENS directly bridges these gaps by providing a **unified, extensible, and user-friendly toolkit** for extracting internal representations from a wide range of open-source VLMs. It positions itself as an essential infrastructure layer, enabling deeper, more scientifically rigorous analyses that move beyond superficial performance metrics. By standardizing the extraction process, VLM-LENS democratizes access to VLM internals, allowing researchers to apply advanced interpretability methods (e.g., probing, neural circuit inspection, knowledge tracing, saliency analyses) that were previously difficult to implement across heterogeneous VLM ecosystems. The toolkit acknowledges the growing interest in understanding multimodal models (e.g., works on hidden states, output probabilities, and specific VLM interpretability tools like LVLM-Intrepret), but differentiates itself by offering broad model coverage and extensibility, unlike existing single-model solutions. This contribution is vital for accelerating the development of more reliable, trustworthy, and genuinely intelligent VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind VLM-LENS stems from the recognition that while Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved remarkable performance, their internal workings remain largely opaque, hindering both their improvement and responsible deployment. Current evaluation methods predominantly focus on observable, task-specific accuracy, which is insufficient for a comprehensive understanding of what VLMs actually "know" or how they make decisions. This limitation leads to concerns about model robustness, susceptibility to biases, and the potential for exploiting spurious correlations rather than deep understanding.

Against this backdrop, the authors outline several key objectives for VLM-LENS:

1. **Enable Systematic Benchmarking, Analysis, and Interpretation of VLMs:** The primary objective is to move beyond simple performance metrics to facilitate a more in-depth understanding of VLM capabilities and limitations. This involves shifting the focus from "behavioral performance" (what the model outputs) to "internal competence" (the underlying knowledge and decision-making processes).
2. **Support Extraction of Intermediate Outputs from Any Layer of Open-Source VLMs:** A fundamental goal is to provide researchers with granular access to the internal states of VLMs. This includes not just the final layer outputs, but also hidden representations from any intermediate layer during the forward pass, such as attention maps or features before layer normalization, which are crucial for mechanistic interpretability.
3. **Provide a Unified, YAML-Configurable Interface:** Recognizing the architectural diversity and varying dependencies of different VLMs, a key objective is to abstract away model-specific complexities. The toolkit aims to offer a consistent, user-friendly interface configured via YAML files, simplifying model loading, preprocessing, and inference across a wide range of models without requiring extensive boilerplate code.
4. **Ensure Broad Model Coverage and Extensibility:** To be a truly useful community tool, VLM-LENS aims to support a diverse set of state-of-the-art open-source VLMs (currently 16 base models and over 30 variants) and be easily extensible. This means designing the core logic such that new models can be added with minimal engineering effort, fostering community contributions.
5. **Address Model-Specific Environmental Support Challenges:** Different VLMs often rely on distinct, and sometimes conflicting, library versions and environments. A practical objective is to provide isolated, model-specific environment setups that are easy to install, ensuring consistency and reproducibility.
6. **Accelerate Community Efforts in Understanding and Improving VLMs:** By open-sourcing VLM-LENS, the authors intend to lower the technical barrier for VLM analysis, enabling researchers and practitioners to conduct more fine-grained and rigorously controlled studies. Ultimately, the goal is to foster a collaborative environment that leads to a deeper understanding, diagnosis, and subsequent improvement of VLM architectures and capabilities.

In essence, VLM-LENS is motivated by the critical need for transparent and interpretable AI, particularly in the rapidly evolving field of multimodal models, to build more robust, reliable, and trustworthy systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLM-LENS is designed as a modular and extensible toolkit, built on PyTorch's native functionalities, to provide standardized access to the internal states of diverse Vision-Language Models. Its methodology focuses on abstracting complexities while ensuring flexibility and broad compatibility.

**Core Mechanism:**
The toolkit leverages PyTorch's `hook` mechanism. This powerful feature allows developers to register custom functions that execute during the forward (or backward) pass of a neural network module, providing access to the input and output tensors of that module. This is the fundamental enabler for extracting intermediate representations from any specified layer.

**Design Philosophy and Implementation:**
1. **Unified Interface and YAML Configuration:** The toolkit's design prioritizes ease of use. A central interface (`src/main.py`) orchestrates operations based on user-defined YAML configuration files. These files allow users to specify model architecture, path, desired output database, input data directory, prompts, and crucially, the specific modules (layers) from which to extract tensors. This abstracts away model-specific loading and preprocessing logic, making cross-model analysis consistent.
2. **Model-Specific Environmental Support:** To circumvent the common issue of conflicting dependencies among different VLM libraries, VLM-LENS provides dedicated, isolated environment setups for each supported model. These environments can be installed with a single `pip install` command, promoting reproducibility and reducing setup friction.
3. **Extensible Architecture:** The toolkit employs an object-oriented design where each model-specific implementation inherits from a base class (`src/models/base.py`). This base class standardizes common operations like model loading, preprocessing, and inference, while allowing individual models to override or customize these functions as needed (e.g., for unique chat templates or specialized forward passes). This inheritance model ensures that adding new VLMs requires minimal modification to the core toolkit logic.
4. **Pipeline Implementation:**
 - **Initialization:** A model and its accompanying preprocessor (e.g., for image resizing, tokenization, prompt formatting) are loaded based on the YAML configuration.
 - **Hook Registration:** For each specified layer in the configuration, a forward hook is registered. This hook captures the output tensor of that layer during the model's forward pass.
 - **Inference and Data Extraction:** The preprocessed image-text input is fed through the VLM. As the data flows through the network, the registered hooks extract the intermediate tensors.
 - **Database Storage:** The extracted tensors, along with rich metadata (model name, architecture, image path, prompt, label, layer name, tensor dimensionality), are systematically stored in a SQLite3 database. This standardized schema facilitates efficient querying and subsequent analysis.
 - **Hook Unregistration:** After extraction, all forward hooks are unregistered to prevent unintended side effects or contamination in subsequent inference calls.
 - **Module Inspection:** A utility argument (`--log-named-modules`) allows users to easily list all available modules within a model, aiding in the identification of desired layers for hooking.

**Supported Models:**
VLM-LENS offers extensive coverage, supporting 16 base VLMs and over 30 variants, including popular models like BLIP-2, CLIP, CogVLM, InternVL, LLaVA, MiniCPM, Paligemma, and Qwen2-VL, ensuring broad applicability.

**Demonstrative Experiments (Usage Examples):**
To showcase its capabilities, the paper presents two analytical experiments:

1. **Probing for Primitive Concepts:**
 - **Dataset:** A custom dataset built on CLEVR, defining six categorical splits (color, material, number, shape, size, boolean) representing primitive object attributes.
 - **Approach:** For each input (image + text) and ground-truth label, VLM-LENS extracts mean-pooled intermediate representations from chosen VLM layers (middle and last). A lightweight, two-layer perceptron probe (with ReLU activation and 512 hidden units) is then trained on these representations to predict the ground-truth label. Hyperparameters are tuned via k-fold cross-validation.
 - **Control:** A "control probe" trained on randomly shuffled labels serves as a baseline to ensure the main probe is decoding meaningful information rather than memorizing spurious patterns. A statistically significant advantage of the main probe indicates the VLM encodes task-relevant information.

2. **Concept Similarity (Stroop Effect Adaptation):**
 - **Stimuli:** Specially constructed images featuring deliberate incongruities between lexical content (e.g., word "red"), font color (e.g., green ink), and background color (e.g., blue canvas), inspired by the human Stroop effect.
 - **Prompt:** An ambiguous instruction: "Describe the color in this image in one word."
 - **Reference Prototypes:** Intermediate layer representations are extracted for canonical "primitive color concepts" (e.g., images of pure red) to serve as reference points.
 - **Analysis:** Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to the reference embeddings to identify dimensions capturing color variation. The hidden representations of the Stroop images are then projected into this PCA space, and their cosine similarity to the reference color concepts is computed. This allows for comparing "matched" (e.g., word "red" with red reference) vs. "mismatched" similarities across different cues (lexical, font, background) and model layers.

These examples highlight how VLM-LENS enables fine-grained analysis of VLM internals, providing insights into their representational capabilities and grounding of concepts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VLM-LENS toolkit was demonstrated through two analytical experiments, revealing significant insights into the internal representations of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), alongside a systematic evaluation of the toolkit's performance.

#### 4.1. Probing for Primitive Concepts

This experiment evaluated how well VLMs encode primitive concepts (like color, material, shape) in their hidden states, using a CLEVR-based dataset and lightweight probes.
- **Model Competence Variation:** Significant differences in internal competence were observed across VLMs. Models like Qwen2-VL-7B and MiniCPM-o demonstrated near-perfect accuracy on many concept splits when probed, with a statistically significant advantage over control probes (trained on random labels). This suggests strong encoding of these concepts.
- **Weaker Competence:** In contrast, LLaVA-1.5-7B, while still showing some statistically significant differences from control, exhibited weaker competence across several splits, indicating that these concepts are less robustly represented in its internal states, despite its overall task performance.
- **Salient Features and Layer Depth:** Across all evaluated models, "color" emerged as the most salient feature, consistently yielding high probe accuracy in both middle and last layers. More complex attributes like "material," "number," and "shape" were better handled by models with strong instruction-following and multimodal understanding capabilities (e.g., Qwen2-VL, MiniCPM-o, InternVL), particularly when probing the last-layer representations. This suggests a progressive refinement of conceptual information through the network layers.
- **Beyond Accuracy:** The probing results effectively complement traditional accuracy-driven benchmarks by providing a detailed, interpretable understanding of *what* is represented internally, rather than just *what* the model outputs.

#### 4.2. Concept Similarity (Stroop Effect Adaptation)

This experiment investigated how VLMs ground the notion of color when presented with ambiguous, incongruent cues, akin to the human Stroop effect. The analysis focused on LLaVA-1.5-7B.
- **Encoding of Multiple Cues:** VLM-LENS successfully revealed that all three types of color information—lexical content (the word itself), foreground font color, and background color—are reliably encoded within the model's hidden representations. A clear separation between "match" and "mismatch" conditions (where the VLM's internal representation aligned or conflicted with a primitive color concept) was observed across various layers.
- **Hierarchy of Influence:**
 - **Background Color Dominance:** The background color produced the strongest contrast between matched and mismatched examples, indicating it is the most prominent visual cue influencing the VLM's internal color grounding.
 - **Surprising Lexical Prominence:** Interestingly, the lexical content (the written word, e.g., "red") showed a more prominent separation between matched and mismatched conditions than the *actual font color* of the word. This suggests that the textual modality's influence on color perception within the VLM can be stronger than directly perceived visual attributes of text itself, even in a visually ambiguous context.
- **Distributed Representation of Color:** The analysis indicated that color information is not encoded in a single, simple linear direction within the representation space. Instead, a sufficient number of Principal Component Analysis (PCA) components were required to achieve a clear separation between matched and mismatched color concepts, implying a more distributed and complex representation.

#### 4.3. System Evaluation

The toolkit's performance was evaluated on a subset of the MSCOCO dataset, assessing inference time and memory usage across various supported models.
- **Performance Variability:**
 - **CLIP:** Demonstrated the fastest inference time (0.025s/instance) and lowest memory footprint (617MB). However, its contrastive learning architecture makes it less directly comparable to generative VLMs.
 - **Generative VLMs:** Inference times varied significantly, from MiniCPM-V-2 (0.092s/instance) to InternVL (0.623s/instance), even for models with similar parameter counts (e.g., 7B class). This suggests substantial architectural and optimization differences impact real-world performance.
 - **Memory Usage:** Peak GPU memory usage ranged widely, from MiniCPM-V-2 (7,154.23 MB) to Molmo (34,403.26 MB). Even with lower precision points (bfloat16, float16), models like LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2-VL still consumed considerable memory (>24GB), highlighting that memory efficiency is highly architecture-dependent.
- **Practical Implications:** These performance statistics offer valuable guidance for users to make informed decisions regarding hardware selection and advanced inference techniques when utilizing VLM-LENS for their research.

Overall, the findings underscore that VLM-LENS is not merely a technical utility but a powerful research instrument capable of uncovering systematic differences in how VLMs process and represent multimodal information internally, going far beyond what can be gleaned from behavioral output alone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLM-LENS represents a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the broader landscape of interpretable AI. Its significance lies in addressing critical methodological and technological gaps that currently impede a deep understanding and robust development of these complex multimodal systems.

**Key Significance Points:**

1. **Shift from Performance to Competence:** The most profound significance of VLM-LENS is its enablement of a paradigm shift in VLM evaluation—moving from merely assessing "behavioral performance" (output accuracy) to meticulously analyzing "internal competence" (the underlying knowledge, reasoning, and representational mechanisms). This is crucial for distinguishing between genuine understanding and superficial pattern matching or shortcut exploitation. The demonstrative experiments vividly illustrate how models performing well on standard benchmarks can still exhibit weaknesses in representing primitive concepts or handling ambiguous cues internally.
2. **Standardized and Unified Interpretability Infrastructure:** By providing a unified, YAML-configurable interface to extract intermediate representations from a wide array of state-of-the-art open-source VLMs, VLM-LENS drastically lowers the barrier to entry for VLM interpretability research. It eliminates the substantial engineering effort previously required to adapt disparate models and their unique dependencies for internal analysis, a problem often faced when extending tools like TransformerLens.
3. **Acceleration of Scientific VLM Understanding:** The toolkit will accelerate community efforts in understanding, diagnosing, and improving VLMs by making rigorous scientific analysis more accessible. Researchers can now systematically probe for various cognitive capabilities, investigate neural circuits, trace knowledge flow, and identify representational biases across diverse VLM architectures with unprecedented ease. This will foster a more data-driven and mechanistic understanding of *how* and *why* VLMs behave the way they do.
4. **Enabling Advanced Interpretability Methods:** While VLM-LENS currently focuses on extraction, its design as an extensible framework allows for seamless integration with various advanced interpretability and analysis methods, including future community contributions for attention interpretation, neural circuit discovery, and gradient-based saliency analyses. This positions it as a foundational tool for a burgeoning area of research.
5. **Informing Model Development and Robustness:** VLM-LENS offers VLM developers a powerful diagnostic tool. By easily extracting and analyzing internal states, developers can pinpoint specific layers or modules where conceptual understanding falters, identify sources of shortcut learning, and guide fine-tuning or architectural modifications to enhance model robustness and reliability. The findings from the Stroop-like experiment, for instance, revealing the surprising prominence of lexical content over font color, can inform how multimodal inputs are weighted and integrated during VLM training.
6. **Resource Optimization:** The detailed system evaluation (inference time and memory usage) provides practical guidance for researchers on hardware selection and optimization strategies, which is critical given the computational demands of VLMs.

**Potential Impact:**

- **More Trustworthy AI:** A deeper understanding of VLM internals can lead to the development of more transparent, explainable, and trustworthy AI systems, crucial for deployment in sensitive applications.
- **Improved VLM Architectures:** Insights gained from VLM-LENS can directly inform the design of future VLM architectures, leading to models that are not only high-performing but also genuinely understand and reason about multimodal data.
- **Enhanced Debugging and Bias Detection:** The toolkit will empower researchers to effectively debug VLM failures and identify potential biases embedded in their internal representations, promoting fairer and more ethical AI.
- **New Research Avenues:** By democratizing access to VLM internals, VLM-LENS is likely to spawn entirely new research avenues in areas like multimodal cognitive science, comparing human and AI perception, and developing novel interpretability methods specifically tailored for multimodal transformers.
- **Stronger Community Collaboration:** As an open-sourced project, VLM-LENS is poised to foster strong community contributions, collaboratively expanding its capabilities and solidifying its position as a central tool for VLM analysis.

In conclusion, VLM-LENS is more than just a software utility; it is a critical scientific instrument designed to shed light on the black box of Vision-Language Models. By enabling rigorous, fine-grained analysis of internal competence, it paves the way for the development of more intelligent, robust, and interpretable multimodal AI systems.
