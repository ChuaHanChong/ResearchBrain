# PO-PDDL: Learning Symbolic POMDPs from Visual Demonstrations for Robot Planning Under Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed PO-PDDL, a symbolic POMDP formulation extending PDDL, and a pipeline to learn these models from visual robot demonstrations. The system enabled 100% task success and superior cumulative rewards on long-horizon real-world manipulation tasks while reducing planning time by over 50% compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Robot task planning in real-world environments is hindered by pervasive perception and action uncertainties.
- Standard PDDL formulations assume fully observable and deterministic settings, making them inadequate for real-world robotic applications.
- Manually constructing POMDP models is labor-intensive, and learning them from high-dimensional visual robot demonstrations poses significant challenges in state abstraction, stochasticity, and partial observability.

## Method

- A novel symbolic language, **PO-PDDL**, extends PDDL to explicitly model observations, beliefs, stochastic transitions, and partial observability, maintaining LLM-friendly syntax.
- A two-stage learning pipeline constructs complete PO-PDDL domains, including predicates, ==stochastic operators==, and observation functions, directly from visual robot demonstrations.
- Partial observability is identified by analyzing contradictions between inferred symbolic states from reconstructed trajectories and direct visual observations.

## Results

- The system learned complete and correct POMDP models in 291 seconds from 29 visual demonstrations, demonstrating lower learning costs than alternative explicit model-learning approaches.
- PO-PDDL achieved **100%** task success and the highest cumulative rewards across five long-horizon real-world robot manipulation tasks, outperforming all baselines.
- Planning times were reduced by an average of **50.4%** to **85.7%** compared to baselines, attributed to efficient Bayesian belief updates and selective VLM invocations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified symbolic POMDP formulation (PO-PDDL) effectively bridges the gap between structured PDDL planning and principled uncertainty modeling, leveraging LLM capabilities.
> - A two-stage learning pipeline, starting with a fully observable domain and then incorporating partial observability based on data-driven inconsistency detection, is effective for learning complex POMDP models from visual demonstrations.
> - Explicitly modeling action constraints and differentiating between passive and active observation types are critical for robust belief-space planning in real-world settings.
