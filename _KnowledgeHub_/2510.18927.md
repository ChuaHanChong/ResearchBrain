# BAPO: Stabilizing Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning for LLMs via Balanced Policy Optimization with Adaptive Clipping

> [!summary] Summary
> BAPO introduces an adaptive clipping mechanism for off-policy Reinforcement Learning in Large Language Models, which dynamically re-balances optimization signals and preserves policy entropy. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance on AIME reasoning benchmarks, outperforming comparable open-source models and demonstrating competitiveness with proprietary systems.

## Problem

- Off-policy Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Language Models (LLMs) suffers from optimization instability, leading to erratic training and performance collapse, particularly with increasing data staleness.
- A rapid and sharp decline in policy entropy during off-policy RL reduces the LLM's exploratory capacity, driving it towards over-exploitation and potentially hindering the discovery of better behaviors.
- Fixed clipping mechanisms in PPO-like objectives lead to imbalanced optimization, disproportionately penalizing negative samples while blocking beneficial updates from low-probability positive tokens that are crucial for entropy preservation.

## Method

- ==BAPO== employs a novel ==adaptive clipping mechanism== that dynamically adjusts the upper (`c_high`) and lower (`c_low`) bounds of the PPO objective for each update step and batch.
- The adjustment aims to ensure that the contribution of positive signals to the policy gradient loss meets a predefined ==target threshold (`ρ₀`)==, thereby re-balancing the influence of positive and negative samples.
- This iterative adjustment process expands `c_high` to include more low-probability positive tokens and adjusts `c_low` to filter out excessively negative tokens, promoting ==entropy maintenance== and preventing gradient explosions.

## Results

- BAPO achieved significantly more stable training dynamics, maintaining stable policy entropy, steady gradient normalization, and balanced positive token contributions, preventing the collapse observed in baselines.
- The method demonstrated superior robustness to data staleness, consistently outperforming base models, standard clipping, and empirically tuned asymmetric clipping on both AIME 2024 and AIME 2025 benchmarks.
- BAPO-trained BP-Math-32B models scored **87.1** on AIME 2024 and **80.0** on AIME 2025, setting state-of-the-art among open-source models of comparable scale and achieving results competitive with proprietary systems like Gemini-2.5-Flash-Thinking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimization in PPO-like objectives for LLMs is often imbalanced, with negative-advantage samples disproportionately dominating policy gradient updates, which can lead to instability and suppressed exploration.
> - A newly derived "Entropy-Clip Rule" reveals that fixed clipping systematically blocks entropy-increasing updates from low-probability positive tokens, leading to a continuous decline in policy entropy.
> - Preliminary experiments with asymmetric clipping confirmed that increasing the upper bound (`c_high`) improves performance and counteracts entropy decline, motivating the need for an adaptive mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the forefront of research into **Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models (RL for LLMs)**, a field that has seen tremendous growth and impact in recent years. RL, particularly through methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or AI Feedback (RLAIF), has become the cornerstone for aligning LLMs with human preferences and enhancing their capabilities across complex tasks such such as reasoning, coding, and agentic decision-making.

Within RL for LLMs, **off-policy reinforcement learning** is gaining increasing attention due to its promise of improved sample efficiency and tolerance to data staleness. These characteristics are particularly attractive for LLMs, given their massive scale, the high cost of data generation (e.g., human annotations), and the logistical challenges of on-policy data collection. However, applying off-policy RL to LLMs introduces substantial practical and theoretical hurdles, primarily revolving around **optimization instability** and the **decline of policy entropy**. These issues often manifest as unstable training, exploding gradients, and a bias towards over-exploitation, which cripples the model's exploratory capacity.

The paper directly addresses these critical challenges, building upon and distinguishing itself from prior work in several ways:
- **PPO-like Algorithms:** The current standard for RL for LLMs often employs PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) or its variants like GRPO (Group-based Reinforcement Policy Optimization). PPO's clipping mechanism is central to its stability by implicitly enforcing a trust region. This paper delves into the limitations of *fixed* clipping in off-policy settings.
- **RL Stability and Entropy Preservation:** A significant body of recent work aims to stabilize RL for LLMs and maintain exploration. Prior approaches include:
 - **Dynamic Clipping and Sampling:** DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) introduced techniques like Clip-Higher and dynamic sampling to improve performance.
 - **Token-level Focus:** Wang et al. (2025a) explored optimizing only high-entropy tokens, while DCPO (Yang et al., 2025b) adjusted clipping based on token prior probabilities.
 - **Entropy Regularization:** Other works (He et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025) systematically investigate maintaining entropy stability.
 - **Asymmetric Clipping:** Roux et al. (2025) and Arnal et al. (2025) also explored asymmetric clipping, albeit in a more rigid or manually specified manner.

BAPO distinguishes itself by offering a **holistic optimization perspective**. Instead of relying on empirical tuning or fixed asymmetric bounds, it theoretically derives the **Entropy-Clip Rule** from the PPO objective and combines this insight with an observed imbalance in loss contributions. This allows BAPO to dynamically and adaptively adjust clipping bounds, not just for individual tokens but across the entire policy update, to achieve a better balance of positive/negative signals and preserve entropy. By providing a principled, adaptive mechanism that outperforms existing methods and even leading proprietary systems, BAPO represents a significant advancement in making off-policy RL for LLMs more robust, efficient, and broadly applicable in modern AI infrastructures, such as those employing partial rollouts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **stabilize and enhance the effectiveness of off-policy Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models (LLMs)**. While off-policy RL offers significant advantages in terms of sample efficiency and tolerance to data staleness—crucial for training colossal LLMs—its application in this domain has been plagued by substantial challenges.

The primary motivations for developing BAPO stem from two critical issues identified through extensive empirical and theoretical analysis:

1. **Optimization Instability and Collapse in Off-Policy RL:** Preliminary experiments, particularly shown in Figure 2, vividly demonstrate that as data staleness increases (a common characteristic of off-policy settings), LLM training becomes highly unstable. This instability manifests as erratic training reward curves, exploding gradient norms, and ultimately, a sudden collapse in performance. This directly hinders the reliable and scalable deployment of off-policy RL for LLMs.

2. **Sharp Decline in Policy Entropy:** Concurrently with optimization instability, off-policy RL consistently leads to a rapid decrease in policy entropy (Figure 2). A sharp decline in entropy signifies reduced exploratory capacity, driving the policy towards over-exploitation. This prevents the LLM from discovering novel, potentially better, behaviors and can lead to performance plateaus or local optima, limiting the model's overall reasoning capabilities.

To understand the root causes of these problems, the authors conducted a comprehensive analysis, revealing two key insights that directly motivated the BAPO design:

- **Insight 1: Imbalanced Optimization Dominated by Negative-Advantage Samples:** The policy gradient updates in PPO-like objectives are often disproportionately influenced by samples with negative advantages. As shown in Figure 4, negative samples constitute a majority both in number and in their contribution to the policy-gradient loss. This imbalance arises because models tend to generate longer, often incorrect, trajectories on difficult queries, thus producing more negative tokens (Figure 6). This excessive penalization of negative actions can suppress even neutral or correct behaviors, and critically, the accumulation of low-probability negative tokens can trigger gradient explosions, further destabilizing training.
- **Insight 2: The Entropy-Clip Rule and Fixed Clipping's Detrimental Effects:** Through theoretical derivation and empirical validation, the authors identified an "Entropy-Clip Rule" for PPO-like objectives (Equation 6). This rule reveals that the fixed clipping mechanism (e.g., `[1-ε, 1+ε]`) systematically blocks updates from many low-probability positive tokens while over-penalizing low-probability negative tokens (Figure 3, left; Figure 5). Crucially, these low-probability positive tokens are essential for increasing entropy and promoting exploration. By consistently excluding such entropy-increasing updates, fixed clipping inevitably sharpens the policy's output distribution, leading to a continuous decline in entropy and, consequently, crippled exploratory capacity.

In summary, the key objectives for BAPO were to design a method that could explicitly and adaptively address these two core issues: (1) **re-balance the contributions of positive and negative tokens** to prevent gradient explosions and ensure meaningful updates, and (2) **preserve policy entropy** to maintain the model's exploratory capability and prevent premature over-exploitation and collapse.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology behind BAPO (BAlanced Policy Optimization with Adaptive Clipping) is centered on dynamically adjusting the clipping bounds in PPO-like objectives to address the identified issues of imbalanced optimization and entropy collapse.

#### 3.1. Validation Experiment: Asymmetric Clipping

Before introducing BAPO, the authors performed preliminary experiments using a manually set **asymmetric clipping range**. This involved varying the upper bound (`c_high`) and lower bound (`c_low`) of the PPO clipping mechanism. Figure 7 demonstrates that increasing `c_high` (which allows more low-probability positive tokens to contribute to updates) improves performance and counteracts entropy decline. Conversely, relaxing `c_low` (allowing more low-probability negative tokens) degrades performance and accelerates entropy collapse. This preliminary validation confirms the hypothesis that asymmetric clipping can indeed influence training dynamics and entropy, thereby motivating the need for an adaptive mechanism.

#### 3.2. BAPO: BAlanced Policy Optimization with Adaptive Clipping

Building on these insights, BAPO introduces a novel adaptive clipping mechanism. The core idea is to dynamically adjust `c_high` and `c_low` for each update step and batch, rather than using fixed or manually tuned values.

The adaptive adjustment in BAPO is driven by a simple yet effective objective: to ensure that the contribution of positive signals to the policy gradient loss meets a predefined target threshold, `ρ₀`. Formally, for each update, BAPO aims to find `c_high` and `c_low` such that:

$| \sum_{A_t > 0} \pi_{\theta^{\text{rollout}}}(y_t) \cdot \min(r_t \cdot A_t, \text{clip}(r_t, 0, c_{\text{high}}) \cdot A_t) | / | \sum_{A_t} \pi_{\theta^{\text{rollout}}}(y_t) \cdot \min(r_t \cdot A_t, \text{clip}(r_t, c_{\text{low}}, c_{\text{high}}) \cdot A_t) | \ge \rho_0$

Where $A_t$ is the advantage at time step $t$, $r_t$ is the importance weight, and $\text{clip}(r_t, c_{\text{low}}, c_{\text{high}})$ limits $r_t$ to the interval $[c_{\text{low}}, c_{\text{high}}]$.

The dynamic adjustment process (Algorithm 1) works as follows:
1. **Initialization:** Clipping bounds `c_low` and `c_high` are initialized to their minimum allowed values ($a^-$ and $a^+$).
2. **Iterative Adjustment:** In a loop, `c_high` is gradually increased by a step size `δ₁` until it reaches its maximum bound ($b^+$). If `c_high` can no longer be increased, `c_low` is then increased by `δ₂` until it reaches its maximum bound ($b^-$). This iterative process continues until the positive token contribution `ρ` meets or exceeds the target `ρ₀`.
3. **Policy Update:** Once the adaptive clipping bounds are determined, the LLM policy $\pi_\theta$ is updated by maximizing the PPO-like objective using these dynamically adjusted `c_low` and `c_high`.

**Key Benefits of BAPO's Approach:**
- **Re-balancing Contributions:** By dynamically expanding `c_high`, BAPO encourages the inclusion of more low-probability positive tokens, directly addressing the imbalance problem by boosting positive signal contributions. Simultaneously, by adjusting `c_low`, it can filter out excessively negative tokens that risk gradient explosion.
- **Entropy Preservation:** The inclusion of low-probability positive tokens (which often have high importance sampling weights when the target policy shifts away from the behavior policy) and the controlled exclusion of low-probability negative tokens directly align with the "Entropy-Clip Rule." This mechanism effectively promotes entropy maintenance, ensuring the model retains its exploratory capacity and prevents over-exploitation.
- **Adaptive and Automated:** Unlike methods requiring manual tuning of asymmetric bounds, BAPO automatically adjusts `c_high` and `c_low` based on real-time loss contributions. This simplifies hyperparameter management and makes the training process more robust and efficient.
- **Mitigation of Tail Degradation:** By setting a target contribution `ρ₀` for positive tokens, BAPO prevents an uncontrolled surge of positive signals that could lead to overfitting on easy problems and neglect of challenging ones, thereby mitigating "tail degradation."

BAPO effectively combines theoretical insights (Entropy-Clip Rule) with empirical observations (imbalanced loss) into a practical and adaptive algorithm that fundamentally improves the stability and performance of off-policy RL for LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results presented in the paper provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness and robustness of BAPO across various off-policy scenarios, model scales, and benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Stable and Fast Training Dynamics

- **Improved Stability:** As shown in Figure 9, BAPO significantly stabilizes the optimization process compared to baseline methods. It achieves rapidly increasing training rewards, maintains stable policy entropy (preventing the sharp decline observed in baselines), ensures steady gradient normalization (avoiding explosions), and consistently increases the contribution of positive tokens to the loss. This demonstrates a much-improved balance between exploration and exploitation.
- **Adaptive Clipping in Action:** Figure 8 visually confirms the dynamic nature of BAPO's clipping bounds. Both the upper and lower bounds (`c_high` and `c_low`) fluctuate during training, adapting to the evolving policy and data, which is a key differentiator from static clipping mechanisms.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Data Staleness

- Figure 11 illustrates BAPO's superior performance under different degrees of data staleness (2x and 4x). On both AIME 2024 and AIME 2025 benchmarks, BAPO consistently outperforms the base model, standard clipping, and even an empirically tuned asymmetric clipping approach (Clip w/ [0.8, 1.28]). This highlights BAPO's enhanced robustness in off-policy settings where stale data is prevalent.

#### 4.3. State-of-the-Art Performance on AIME Benchmarks

- **Significant Improvements over Baselines:** BAPO delivers substantial performance gains across different model scales (Figure 1, Table 1). For the 32B BP-Math model, BAPO outperforms the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline by 2.7 and 1.9 points on AIME 2024 and AIME 2025, respectively, and provides noticeable improvements over GRPO. For the 7B BP-Math model, the gains are even larger (3.9 and 3.5 points over SFT on AIME 2024 and AIME 2025). This demonstrates BAPO's efficacy in leveraging RL to boost LLM capabilities.
- **SOTA among Open-Source Models:** BAPO-trained models achieve state-of-the-art results among open-source models of comparable scale.
 - **32B Models:** BP-Math-32B BAPO scores 87.1 on AIME 2024 and 80.0 on AIME 2025, significantly outperforming Qwen3-32B (81.4/72.9) and SkyWork-OR1-32B (82.2/73.3).
 - **7B Models:** BP-Math-7B BAPO achieves 70.8 on AIME 2024 and 62.5 on AIME 2025, surpassing SkyWork-OR1-7B (70.2/54.6).
- **Competitive with Proprietary Systems:** Remarkably, BAPO models demonstrate competitiveness with leading proprietary systems:
 - BP-Math-32B BAPO (87.1/80.0) not only outperforms larger models like DeepSeek-R1 (79.8/70.0) but also achieves results comparable to or better than o3-mini (79.6/76.7) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Thinking (82.3/72.0) on certain metrics.
 - BP-Math-7B BAPO (70.8/62.5) yields results on par with Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking (73.3/53.5), showcasing its strong performance even at a smaller scale against commercial offerings.

#### 4.4. Performance in Partial Rollout Scenarios

- BAPO exhibits greater robustness and more stable optimization when applied in partial rollout settings (Figure 12), a common technique in modern AI infrastructures to improve LLM RL efficiency. This demonstrates BAPO's practical utility in realistic, off-policy deployment scenarios.

#### 4.5. Generalizability Across Architectures

- Experiments on Llama-based models (OctoThinker-Llama3.2-3B-Long-Zero) also confirm BAPO's effectiveness. As shown in Table 2 and Figure 13 (Appendix A), BAPO yields more competitive results (e.g., 5.4% vs. 2.5% on AIME 2024) and exhibits greater training stability, indicating its broad applicability beyond Qwen-based architectures.

In conclusion, the main findings highlight that BAPO successfully addresses the core instability issues in off-policy RL for LLMs, leading to significantly more stable and efficient training, and ultimately, state-of-the-art performance on challenging reasoning benchmarks, even outperforming some leading proprietary models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of BAPO carries significant implications for the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models and its broader applications.

### 6. Enabling Robust Off-Policy RL for LLMs

BAPO directly tackles a critical bottleneck: the instability and entropy collapse in off-policy RL for LLMs. By providing a robust and adaptive mechanism, BAPO makes off-policy methods more viable and reliable. This is crucial for scaling LLM training, as off-policy learning allows for greater sample efficiency and better utilization of stale data, reducing the substantial computational and data collection costs associated with LLMs.

### 7. Pushing LLM Performance Boundaries

The impressive state-of-the-art results on challenging reasoning benchmarks like AIME demonstrate BAPO's ability to significantly enhance LLM capabilities. Outperforming both leading open-source models and even competing with some large-scale proprietary systems (like o3-mini and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Thinking) highlights that BAPO can unlock higher levels of performance in complex reasoning tasks, which are vital for advanced AI applications.

### 8. Deeper Theoretical Understanding of RL for LLMs

The paper's theoretical contribution, particularly the derivation and empirical validation of the "Entropy-Clip Rule," provides a deeper understanding of how the clipping mechanism in PPO-like objectives fundamentally impacts policy entropy. This insight moves beyond empirical observations and offers a principled guide for designing more effective and stable RL algorithms. Such theoretical grounding can inspire future research into fine-grained control over exploration-exploitation trade-offs.

### 9. Practical and Infrastructural Benefits

- **Reduced Hyperparameter Tuning:** BAPO's adaptive nature reduces the need for extensive manual hyperparameter tuning (e.g., of the clipping range $\epsilon$), simplifying the deployment and use of RL for LLM practitioners.
- **Improved Infrastructure Compatibility:** Its demonstrated robustness in scenarios like partial rollouts makes it highly compatible with modern AI infrastructures designed for efficient LLM training. This directly translates to more efficient utilization of computational resources.
- **Broader Applicability:** The success across different model scales and architectures (Qwen and Llama-based models) suggests BAPO's generalizability, indicating it could be a foundational improvement applicable to a wide range of LLM fine-tuning tasks.

### 10. Future Research Directions

BAPO's approach of dynamic, contribution-aware clipping opens several avenues for future research. This includes exploring more sophisticated adaptive mechanisms, investigating how to optimally set the target positive contribution (`ρ₀`), and extending the "Entropy-Clip Rule" to other RL algorithms beyond PPO. The insights into balancing positive and negative signals and preserving entropy could also be valuable for other complex sequential decision-making problems beyond LLMs.

In conclusion, BAPO is a significant contribution that not only provides a practical, high-performance solution for stabilizing off-policy RL for LLMs but also enriches the theoretical understanding of their training dynamics. Its impact is likely to be felt in both academic research and industrial applications, paving the way for more efficient, robust, and capable large language models.
