# Boosting Reasoning in Large Multimodal Models via Activation Replay

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from multiple institutions including Nanyang Technological University and National University of Singapore found that Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) disproportionately shifts low-entropy input activations in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). They developed "Activation Replay," a training-free method leveraging this insight, which consistently boosts LMM reasoning performance across mathematical, agentic, and video tasks, achieving gains of up to 4.4% on video reasoning benchmarks and improving Pass@K scores.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods effectively enhance LMM reasoning, but the internal mechanisms by which RLVR modifies multimodal contexts and input activations remain largely unexplored.
- There is a fundamental gap in understanding how RLVR influences LMMs at the level of fine-grained internal representations, moving beyond empirical improvements.
- A lack of principled understanding hinders the development of more targeted and effective LMM reasoning enhancement strategies.

## Method

- Conducted an exploratory analysis using the "==logit lens==" technique to investigate how RLVR impacts LMM input activations, revealing differential effects on low- and high-entropy activations.
- Proposed "Activation Replay," a training-free, test-time method that optimizes learnable tokens to guide RLVR LMMs' low-entropy input activations to mimic the distributions of their corresponding base LMMs.
- The method dynamically identifies low-entropy activations and iteratively minimizes the KL divergence between manipulated RLVR and base LMM distributions for these specific activations, using gradient updates.

## Results

- Activation Replay consistently boosted performance across diverse multimodal reasoning tasks, achieving gains up to **2.6%** on MathVerse for mathematical reasoning, **2.6%** on HR4K for agentic reasoning, and **4.4%** on VideoHolmes for video reasoning.
- The method significantly improved Pass@K scores on mathematical datasets, such as MathVision MINI and MathVista, indicating enhanced reasoning coverage and capacity rather than just sampling efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that targeting low-entropy activations with an appropriate dynamic threshold (e.g., **0.4**) and sufficient guidance strength (α) is critical for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) disproportionately shifts low-entropy input activations in LMMs more significantly than high-entropy activations, indicating fundamental changes in certainty representations.
> - Maintaining fidelity to base LMM distributions for low-entropy activations is crucial for improving the perplexity of correct reasoning paths and increasing the perplexity of incorrect ones.
> - While RLVR's high-entropy activations facilitate reasoning exploration, leveraging low-entropy activations from base models through intervention can enhance overall LMM reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have advanced significantly in interpreting multimodal interactions across various data types, including images, videos, and 3D inputs. A key area of recent development for LMMs is the enhancement of their reasoning capabilities, particularly for complex scenarios like mathematics, agentic tasks, and video reasoning. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as an effective post-training paradigm for incentivizing such reasoning in LMMs, often involving long chain-of-thought verifications.

Existing research on RLVR for LMMs has focused on developing effective post-training recipes, such as tailoring chain-of-thought verification data, exploring reasoning patterns like reflection, and optimizing training efficiency. While these efforts have demonstrably improved LMM reasoning, a fundamental gap in the current understanding pertains to the internal mechanisms by which RLVR influences LMMs, specifically how multimodal contexts and input activations are modified during this post-training process.

This paper addresses this gap by investigating the effects of RLVR on LMM input activations. It leverages the "logit lens" technique, an interpretability tool primarily used for Large Language Models (LLMs), to analyze how activation distributions shift post-RLVR. By exploring these underlying mechanisms, the work aims to move beyond empirical improvements of RLVR to a more principled understanding, which can then inform the development of more effective and targeted enhancement strategies. The proposed "Activation Replay" method, a training-free approach, is positioned as a practical outcome of this mechanistic investigation, offering a novel way to boost multimodal reasoning by leveraging insights into how low-entropy activations are affected by RLVR.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to gain a deeper understanding of how Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) affects the internal workings of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), with a specific focus on the shifts in input activations. This objective is motivated by the observation that while RLVR effectively enhances reasoning in LMMs, the underlying mechanisms governing these improvements, particularly how multimodal contexts are internally represented and processed, remain largely unexplored.

Key motivations and specific objectives include:

- **Understanding RLVR's Impact on Input Activations:** The researchers hypothesize that RLVR leads to unexpected modifications in how LMMs process multimodal input contexts. They aim to systematically investigate these changes, particularly focusing on the "input activations" which fundamentally determine LMM performance, especially in long-chain reasoning scenarios.
- **Characterizing Activation Shifts using Logit Lens:** To achieve the above, the study utilizes the logit lens technique. This involves projecting intermediate activations of LMMs into the output vocabulary space to observe their token-level distributions. The motivation is to qualitatively and quantitatively measure how these top predictions and token-level distributions shift between base LMMs and their RLVR-post-trained counterparts.
- **Differentiating the Role of Low- and High-Entropy Activations:** A specific motivation is to investigate if different types of activations (e.g., low-entropy, indicating high certainty; vs. high-entropy, indicating high uncertainty) are affected differently by RLVR. Previous work suggests high-entropy tokens can drive reasoning explorations, but the role of low-entropy activations in this context is less clear. The study aims to clarify how RLVR disproportionately affects these distinct entropy levels.
- **Establishing the Association between Activation Shifts and Reasoning Performance:** The researchers seek to establish a causal or correlative link between the observed shifts in input activations (particularly low-entropy ones) and the LMMs' reasoning capabilities. This involves controlled experiments to demonstrate how modulating these shifts can impact the perplexity of correct versus incorrect reasoning paths.
- **Developing a Training-Free Reasoning Enhancement Method:** Based on the insights derived from the mechanistic investigations, a practical objective is to develop a simple, yet effective, test-time method to improve LMM reasoning. The motivation here is to provide an enhancement that does not require expensive policy optimization or architectural changes, making it readily applicable to existing post-trained LMMs. This proposed method, "Activation Replay," is designed to specifically target the regulation of low-entropy input activations.

In essence, the research is driven by a desire to move beyond black-box empirical improvements of RLVR, to uncover the internal dynamics of LMMs under RLVR training, and to translate this understanding into a generalizable and efficient reasoning enhancement technique.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research comprises both an exploratory analysis to understand the effects of RLVR on LMMs and the proposal of a novel training-free intervention method called Activation Replay.

#### 3.1. Exploratory Analysis of RLVR Effects on LMM Input Activations

The authors first conduct a systematic investigation into how RLVR impacts LMMs' internal representations, specifically focusing on input activations.

- **Background and Tools:**
 - **Notation:** LMMs are formalized as a combination of a visual encoder, a multimodal connector, and a Large Language Model (LLM) with `L` layers. Input activations at layer `l` and position `i` are denoted as `h_l,i`, with `h_0,i` referring to input tokens. Base LMM activations are `h_b_l,i` and RLVR activations are `h_r_l,i`.
 - **Logit Lens:** This interpretability technique is applied by projecting intermediate layer activations `h_l,i` to the LMM output space using a normalization function `N` and the vocabulary head `V`, followed by a softmax function, to obtain token distributions `p_l,i = softmax(V(N(h_l,i)))`. This allows for observation of how internal activations align with vocabulary predictions.
 - **Entropy:** The entropy of an activation `e_l,i = -p_l,i · log(p_l,i)` is used as a quantitative measure of uncertainty, categorizing activations into low-entropy (high certainty) and high-entropy (high uncertainty) groups.

- **Analysis of RLVR on Input Activations:**
 - **Top Predictions Shift:** A qualitative comparison is performed to observe how the top predicted tokens (via logit lens) for identical multimodal inputs differ between base LMMs and their RLVR counterparts.
 - **KL Divergence:** To quantitatively measure token-level distributional shifts, Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence `D_kl(P_base || P_rlvr)` is computed between the token distributions of base and RLVR LMM activations. This analysis is ablated by base LMM entropy percentiles to investigate differential impacts on low- and high-entropy activations.

- **Studies on Association with LMM Reasoning:**
 - **Perturbation Study:** Given a multimodal input, multiple correct and incorrect responses are sampled from a post-trained LMM. Random noises are introduced to the inputs to synthesize variations in activations, and the resulting KL divergence shifts are correlated with the perplexity of sampled correct and incorrect reasoning paths.
 - **Intervention Study:** This involves directly manipulating the forward pass of RLVR-post-trained LMMs. Activations from base LMMs are injected into the RLVR LMMs. Two combinations are tested: replacing low-entropy activations in RLVR with those from the base LMM, and vice-versa for high-entropy activations. Reasoning performance is then measured.

#### 3.2. Proposed Method: Activation Replay

Building upon the findings from the exploratory analysis, the authors propose "Activation Replay," a training-free, test-time approach.

- **Core Idea:** The method indirectly enforces RLVR LMMs' low-entropy input activations to mimic the distributions of their corresponding base LMMs. This is achieved by optimizing a set of learnable tokens.
- **Learnable Tokens:** A series of zero-initialized learnable tokens `x_1..., x_n` are introduced. These tokens augment the multimodal input tokens by element-wise addition: `ĥ_r_0,i = h_r_0,i + x_i`.
- **Low-entropy Threshold:** To identify which activations are "low-entropy," a dynamic threshold `τ` is applied. An activation `e_b_l,i` from the base LMM is considered low-entropy if `e_b_l,i < max(h_b_l) * τ`. This masking `M_l,i` ensures only selected activations are targeted.
- **Replay Function:** The learnable tokens `x_i` are optimized iteratively to minimize the token-level KL divergence between the distributions of low-entropy activations from the base LMM (`P_base`) and the manipulated RLVR LMM (`P_rlvr`). The optimization uses a gradient update: `x_i ← x_i − α∇_x_i (D_kl(P_base || P_rlvr) · M_i)`, where `α` controls the strength of the guidance. The distributions `P_base` and `P_rlvr` are derived by applying the logit lens (vocabulary head `F_v` and normalization `F_n`) to the respective activations.
- **Sampling on Manipulated Input Activations:** After the optimization process yields the vector `x_i`, these are added to the visual tokens of the RLVR LMM at test time, before any decoding (greedy or sampling), to produce the final output.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** The method is evaluated on various reasoning-enhanced LMMs, including MMR1-Math, MM-Eureka (7B and 32B), VL-Rethinker, DeepEyes (for agentic reasoning), and Video-R1 (for video reasoning).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Evaluation spans diverse multimodal reasoning tasks:
 - **Mathematics/Knowledge:** MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, DynaMath, WeMath, LogicVista, MMMU, MMMUPro.
 - **Agentic Reasoning:** HR-Bench (FCP), VisualProbe.
 - **Video Reasoning:** VideoMMMU, MLVU, MMVU, Video-Holmes.
- **Metrics:** Performance is primarily measured by task-specific accuracy. Additionally, Pass@K is used to assess broader reasoning coverage beyond greedy decoding. GPT-4o-mini serves as a judge for evaluations, with Qwen3-30B-A3B for Pass@K.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablations are conducted on the low-entropy threshold `τ` and the guidance strength `α` to understand their impact on performance. Comparisons are also made against replaying high-entropy activations, direct cross-model intervention, and static vs. dynamic thresholding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings regarding the impact of RLVR on LMM activations and the effectiveness of the proposed Activation Replay method.

#### 4.1. Impact of RLVR on LMM Input Activations

- **Shifted Top Predictions:** Qualitative analysis using the logit lens demonstrates that top predictions derived from input activations shift notably from base LMMs to their RLVR-post-trained counterparts, even for identical inputs (Figure 2). This suggests fundamental changes in how input contexts are interpreted.
- **Differential Effects on Entropy Levels:** Quantitative analysis using KL divergence reveals that low-entropy input activations are relatively more affected by RLVR, exhibiting larger distributional shifts compared to high-entropy activations (Figure 3). This indicates that RLVR disproportionately alters the representations of highly certain input elements.

#### 4.2. Association Between Activation Shifts and Reasoning

- **Perturbation Study:** Controlled experiments show a correlation between the magnitude of KL divergence shifts in low-entropy activations and the perplexity of LMM responses. Specifically, smaller shifts in low-entropy activations are associated with lower perplexity for correct reasoning paths and higher perplexity for incorrect ones (Figure 4). This suggests that maintaining fidelity in low-entropy representations is beneficial for accurate reasoning.
- **Intervention Study:** Direct intervention experiments demonstrate that replacing low-entropy activations in RLVR LMMs with those from base LMMs can improve reasoning performance. Conversely, replacing high-entropy activations from RLVR LMMs with those from base LMMs degrades performance (Table 1). This indicates that low-entropy activations from base models contain beneficial information for reasoning when combined with RLVR's high-entropy exploration.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Activation Replay

Activation Replay, a training-free test-time approach, consistently boosts multimodal reasoning performance across diverse scenarios:

- **Image-Level Mathematical and Knowledge Reasoning:** The method yields consistent performance gains across multiple mathematical and knowledge-intensive benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, DynaMath, WeMath, LogicVista, MMMU, MMMUPro). Gains are observed for various RLVR-post-trained LMMs, including MMR1-Math-v0-7B (up to 2.6% on MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B for MathVerse) and MM-Eureka-Qwen-32B (up to 3.9% on MMMUPro) (Table 2).
- **Agentic Reasoning:** For o3-like agentic tasks involving multi-turn visual search, Activation Replay enhances the performance of DeepEyes-7B on HRBench and VisualProbe benchmarks (up to 2.6% on HR4K and VisualProbe Medium) (Table 3).
- **Video Reasoning:** In complex cross-frame video reasoning scenarios, the approach improves Video-R1-7B performance across VideoMMMU, MLVU, MMVU, and VideoHolmes (up to 4.4% on VideoHolmes) (Table 4).

#### 4.4. Boosting Pass@K and Mitigating Reasoning Coverage Issues

- Activation Replay significantly boosts Pass@K scores across tested mathematical datasets (MathVision MINI and MathVista) for multiple LMMs (Figure 6, Table A2). This suggests that the method not only improves average case performance (Pass@1) but also broadens the models' reasoning coverage, addressing prior observations that RLVR primarily improves sampling efficiency without eliciting novel reasoning capacity.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Threshold (τ):** Ablations show that selecting a smaller proportion of activations for replay (lower τ, e.g., 0.4) generally yields better performance gains. Excessively large thresholds (e.g., 0.8), which would include more high-entropy activations, can lead to less satisfactory results (Table 5). This reinforces the finding that targeting low-entropy activations is crucial.
- **Strength (α):** The strength of the guidance `α` generally correlates positively with performance, with larger `α` values tending to yield better results (Table 5).
- **Comparison to High-Entropy Replay:** Replaying high-entropy activations leads to performance degradation, supporting the specific focus on low-entropy activations (Table A3).
- **Comparison to Direct Intervention:** Learnable token manipulation (Activation Replay) generally outperforms direct cross-model intervention, indicating the benefit of aligning representations through optimization (Table A4).
- **Dynamic vs. Static Thresholding:** Dynamic thresholding for identifying low-entropy activations generally performs better than a pre-computed static threshold (Table A5).

#### 4.6. Case Studies

Case studies illustrate that Activation Replay can correct intermediate reasoning steps in mathematical problems (Figure 7, Figure A1) and rectify localization and recognition errors in agentic tasks (Figures A2, A3), leading to correct final answers where the baseline RLVR LMM fails. The perturbation study also showed appropriate shifts in perplexity for incorrect responses (Figure A4).

#### 4.7. Additional Time Costs

Activation Replay introduces additional computation during test time, primarily for optimizing learnable tokens. While it adds approximately 0.15× to the overall elapsed time, this can be relatively shorter for LMMs that output very long chain-of-thought traces. In some cases, the method can even lead to comparably faster overall inference due to reduced reasoning turns or errors (Table A1).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and enhancement of reasoning capabilities in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) in several significant ways.

- **Mechanistic Understanding of RLVR:** The study provides novel insights into the internal workings of LMMs post-Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training. By revealing that RLVR disproportionately shifts low-entropy input activations and associating these shifts with reasoning behaviors, the paper moves beyond empirical observations of RLVR effectiveness to a more mechanistic understanding. This fundamental insight into how fine-grained internal representations are affected is crucial for developing more targeted and principled LMM improvements.
- **Development of a Training-Free Enhancement:** The proposed "Activation Replay" offers a simple yet effective test-time method to boost multimodal reasoning. Its training-free nature is particularly impactful as it eliminates the need for expensive and resource-intensive policy optimization or architectural modifications. This makes the method highly practical and easily deployable with existing, already post-trained LMMs, broadening its applicability across various research and industrial settings.
- **Improved Reasoning Robustness and Coverage:** The consistent performance gains across diverse and challenging reasoning tasks (mathematics, agentic interactions, video understanding) demonstrate the broad applicability and robustness of Activation Replay. Furthermore, its ability to boost Pass@K metrics suggests that the method not only improves average performance but also enhances the LMMs' capacity for exploring correct reasoning paths, potentially mitigating the "narrower reasoning coverage" often observed in RLVR models. This implies a more fundamental improvement in reasoning capacity rather than just sampling efficiency.
- **Foundation for Future Research in LMM Interpretability and Control:** By demonstrating the utility of logit lens for analyzing LMM activations and showing that targeted manipulation of specific activation types (low-entropy) can yield substantial performance improvements, this work opens new avenues for research. Future studies can explore finer-grained control over internal representations, investigate other types of activation shifts, and further refine interpretability tools for LMMs to better understand and guide their complex reasoning processes.
- **Bridging Interpretability and Performance:** The research effectively bridges the gap between LMM interpretability (using logit lens to understand internal states) and performance enhancement (developing a method based on those insights). This demonstrates a valuable pipeline for scientific discovery in the field, where understanding the "how" informs the "what to do" for improvement.
