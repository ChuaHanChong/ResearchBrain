# Constraint-Aware Diffusion Guidance for Robotics: Real-Time Obstacle Avoidance for Autonomous Racing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zürich developed Constraint-Aware Diffusion Guidance (CoDiG), a framework that integrates safety and physical feasibility constraints directly into the inference process of diffusion models for robotics. This approach enabled real-time, collision-free trajectory generation for autonomous racing, demonstrating robust obstacle avoidance with a limited training dataset.

## Problem

- Standard diffusion models in robotics often generate unsafe or dynamically infeasible trajectories due to a lack of intrinsic constraint awareness.
- Many existing diffusion-based methods require extensive offline datasets for robust generalization, which are costly and difficult to acquire in real-world robotics.
- Real-time performance for dynamic, safety-critical robotic applications like autonomous racing is typically not achieved by conventional diffusion sampling processes.

## Method

- **CoDiG** modifies the diffusion model's ==score function== during inference by adding a ==barrier gradient== term derived from defined safety constraints, ensuring generated trajectories are physically feasible and avoid obstacles.
- A ==warm-start inference== strategy initializes the denoising process by slightly perturbing the previously generated trajectory, significantly reducing the required sampling steps for real-time operation.
- The framework employs dataset augmentation with 'redundant' obstacles and a task-specific ==barrier function== to promote constraint satisfaction and near time-optimal paths with data efficiency.

## Results

- CoDiG successfully generated collision-free trajectories for real-time obstacle avoidance, achieving a **100%** success rate across **15 racing laps** over ten distinct obstacle configurations in real-world miniature autonomous racing.
- The warm-start strategy increased the sampling frequency from **0.25 Hz** to **2.5 Hz** on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU by reducing denoising steps from **1000** to **50**, meeting real-time requirements for dynamic racing.
- The generated trajectories exhibited near time-optimal properties, closely matching offline-computed optimal paths while providing increased safety clearance around obstacles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating constraint-derived barrier gradients directly into the score function during inference is an effective and data-efficient method to enforce safety and physical feasibility in diffusion-generated trajectories.
> - A warm-start strategy, leveraging temporal consistency between successive outputs, dramatically accelerates the diffusion sampling process, making it viable for high-frequency reactive planning in dynamic robotic systems.
> - Explicit constraint guidance allows diffusion models to generalize robustly to novel and dynamic environments and obstacles, maintaining safety even when trained on limited expert data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have recently achieved considerable success in various generative tasks, including image, audio, and video synthesis, due to their ability to model complex, high-dimensional data distributions. This capability has led to their increasing exploration in robotics, where they are being investigated as a means to overcome limitations of traditional policy approaches, such such as unimodal output assumptions or training instability, thereby offering a more versatile framework for learning robotic behaviors.

However, a significant challenge in deploying standard diffusion models in safety-critical robotic applications is their inherent lack of constraint-awareness and physical feasibility. Policies generated purely from data, without explicit constraint enforcement, can lead to unsafe outcomes such as collisions or dynamically infeasible trajectories, particularly when confronted with out-of-distribution scenarios. Additionally, many existing diffusion-based methods in robotics often necessitate large-scale offline datasets to ensure generalization, which can be expensive or impractical to acquire in real-world settings.

The current work by Ma et al. positions itself within this context by addressing the critical need for integrating safety constraints into diffusion models for robotics. Prior research has explored various strategies for incorporating constraints: some enforce them during training (e.g., integrating physical laws or aligning with constrained optimization paths), while others focus on inference-time enforcement (e.g., conditioning sampling, using projection steps, or incorporating model-based projections into the backward diffusion process). A third category combines both training and inference-time constraint handling.

CoDiG distinguishes itself from existing inference-time approaches by augmenting score updates with lightweight barrier gradients during the sampling process. This method avoids the computational overhead associated with projection steps, external classifiers, or auxiliary models. Furthermore, the paper introduces a warm-start strategy to enhance sampling speed and stability. A notable aspect of this research is its emphasis on real-world deployment and evaluation in dynamic environments, contrasting with many prior works that are primarily validated in simulation or quasi-static settings. This focus on real-time, safety-critical applications like autonomous racing with dynamic obstacles highlights CoDiG's contribution to enabling practical and reliable diffusion-based planning in complex robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of conventional diffusion models when applied to safety-critical robotic tasks. While these models excel at capturing intricate, high-dimensional data distributions and show promise in learning complex robotic behaviors, their standard formulations do not intrinsically enforce physical or safety constraints. This deficiency can result in generated trajectories that are unsafe (e.g., causing collisions) or dynamically infeasible, especially when operating in novel or dynamic environments not fully represented in the training data. Such outcomes are unacceptable for real-world robotic deployment. Furthermore, existing methods often rely on extensive offline datasets for robust generalization, which can be costly and difficult to collect in robotics.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a constraint-aware framework:** To propose a general-purpose, data-efficient diffusion-based framework, named Constraint-Aware Diffusion Guidance (CoDiG), that integrates safety and physical feasibility constraints directly into the trajectory generation process.
2. **Ensure constraint satisfaction with limited data:** To enable the generation of constraint-satisfying outputs even when training data is limited and to facilitate generalization to novel scenarios without requiring vast expert demonstrations.
3. **Achieve real-time performance:** To introduce strategies, specifically a warm-start inference approach, that significantly accelerate the diffusion sampling process, enabling real-time operation for high-frequency control in dynamic environments.
4. **Demonstrate real-world reliability:** To evaluate the framework in a challenging, safety-critical application, miniature autonomous racing with real-time obstacle avoidance, to validate its robustness and effectiveness in a physical system.

In essence, the research aims to bridge the gap between the generative power of diffusion models and the stringent safety and real-time requirements of robotic systems, thereby enabling their safe and practical deployment in complex, dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CoDiG framework is built upon the principles of score-based generative modeling, which formulates diffusion processes using Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) to describe both the forward noising and reverse denoising processes. The core idea is to guide the reverse-time SDE, which generates data from noise, towards constraint-satisfying regions.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of Score-Based Generative Modeling

The forward diffusion process gradually perturbs an initial data sample $x_0$ by solving an SDE, typically an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process, which drives $x_t$ towards a Gaussian distribution. The reverse-time SDE, used for generating new data, requires the score function $\nabla_x \log p_t(x_t)$, which is approximated by a neural network $s_\theta(x_t, t)$ trained via denoising score matching. For this work, a variance-preserving SDE with a specific drift term $f(x_t, t) = -\beta(t)x_t$ and diffusion term $g(t) = \sqrt{2\beta(t)}$ is employed, with $\beta(t)$ modeled as a quadratic function.

#### 3.2. Constraint-Aware Diffusion Guidance (CoDiG)

The central methodological contribution is the integration of constraints during the inference (denoising) phase.
1. **Constraint Definition:** Time-dependent constraints are defined by a function $c(x, \tau) \le 0$, which specifies the feasible region $C_\tau$. In autonomous racing, $C_\tau$ represents the obstacle-free, drivable area.
2. **Barrier Function:** A barrier function $V(x_t; C)$ is introduced, which assigns high values to infeasible data points and values close to zero within the feasible region.
3. **Modified Score Function:** Instead of directly learning a conditional distribution $p_t(x_t|C)$, which is data-intensive, CoDiG leverages the structure of the constraint by modifying the score function. Using the relationship $p_t(x_t|C) \propto p_t(x_t) e^{-\gamma_t V(x_t; C)}$, the gradient of the log-probability for the constrained distribution becomes $\nabla_x \log p_t(x_t|C) = \nabla_x \log p_t(x_t) - \gamma_t \nabla_x V(x_t; C)$.
4. **Inference-time Guidance:** This modified score function is directly substituted into the reverse SDE. This means the unconstrained score (learned by the neural network $s_\theta(x_t, t)$) is augmented by a gradient term derived from the barrier function. This additive guidance allows for constraint enforcement without requiring extensive constraint-compliant training data or architectural changes.
5. **Progressive Scheduling:** To ensure stability during denoising, the weight $\gamma_t$ of the barrier gradient is gradually increased as the diffusion time $t$ decreases from $T$ to $0$.

#### 3.3. Warm-Start Strategy

To achieve real-time performance, CoDiG employs a warm-start strategy. Unlike standard diffusion sampling, which initializes from pure Gaussian noise, the warm-start approach perturbs the *previous* output trajectory with a small amount of noise and uses this as the starting point for the next denoising process. This maintains temporal consistency between successive generated trajectories, reduces trajectory variance, and significantly decreases the number of denoising steps required, thereby accelerating inference.

#### 3.4. Autonomous Racing Case Study

The framework is evaluated in miniature autonomous racing:
1. **Dataset Construction:** Expert trajectories are generated by solving time-optimal control problems for various obstacle configurations. To improve data efficiency, a dataset augmentation technique is employed where "redundant" obstacles are added to safe regions without altering the pre-computed optimal trajectories. Trajectories are represented in a local Frenet coordinate system using lateral displacement ($\hat{y}$) and yaw angle ($\hat{\phi}$).
2. **Barrier Function Design:** The task-specific barrier function (Equation 7) comprises two parts:
 - An indicator function to penalize trajectories entering obstacle regions ($1\{\hat{y}_k \notin C_k\}$).
 - A penalty term for deviation from a nominal time-optimal trajectory, promoting dynamically feasible and near time-optimal paths and compensating for the discarded global curvature information.
3. **Diffusion Model Architecture:** A time-conditioned U-Net architecture serves as the backbone, incorporating Gaussian Fourier features for time embedding and spatial transformer modules for contextual information (e.g., reference track).
4. **Training and Inference:** The model is trained for 500 epochs. During inference, Euler-Maruyama discretization is used for the reverse SDE. The warm-start strategy is critical for achieving real-time rates by reducing the number of denoising steps. A tracking Model Predictive Control (TMPC) system is then used to follow the trajectories generated by CoDiG.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results from both simulated denoising processes and real-world experiments on an autonomous racing platform.

#### 4.1. Constraint-Aware Denoising Performance

Comparative analysis of the denoising process with and without the barrier function (Figure 1) demonstrates the effectiveness of CoDiG's constraint integration:
- **Without barrier function:** The diffusion model, despite implicit conditioning on track boundaries during training, often fails to consistently avoid obstacles, especially with limited training data. Generated trajectories can also appear unrealistic or physically implausible due to the lack of explicit curvature information from the Frenet frame transformation.
- **With barrier function:** CoDiG significantly improves the sampling process. Trajectories denoise faster, successfully avoid all obstacles, and align with physically consistent motion. The second part of the barrier function (promoting adherence to nominal time-optimality) contributes to trajectories exhibiting near time-optimal properties.

#### 4.2. Real-Time Performance and Warm-Starting

- **Initial sampling speed:** A full denoising process (1000 steps) yields a sampling frequency of 0.25 Hz, which is insufficient for real-time obstacle avoidance in dynamic racing scenarios.
- **Warm-start acceleration:** The proposed warm-start strategy, which initializes the diffusion process by perturbing the previous output, drastically reduces the required denoising steps (e.g., to 50 steps). This enables a sampling frequency of 2.5 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, satisfying the real-time requirements for racing.
- **Trajectory characteristics with warm-start:** While warm-start trajectories are slightly coarser and tend to be more conservative (staying closer to the previous path for safety), they consistently achieve obstacle avoidance and contribute to overall system stability.

#### 4.3. Real-World Obstacle Avoidance

CoDiG was successfully deployed on a real-world miniature autonomous racing platform, demonstrating robust performance:
- **Successful avoidance:** Real-world experiments (Figure 2) show CoDiG generating collision-free trajectories that effectively avoid both static and dynamic obstacles under live racing conditions.
- **Tracking accuracy:** The predicted trajectories from the Tracking Model Predictive Control (TMPC), which follows CoDiG's plans, closely align with the reference trajectories. This indicates that the planned paths are physically feasible and suitable for tracking by the vehicle, even though the TMPC operates without direct knowledge of obstacles.
- **Efficiency and stability:** The generated trajectories maintain near time-optimality, balancing safety with performance. The warm-start strategy contributes to system stability by only triggering significant replanning when an obstacle genuinely interferes with the current path.
- **Robustness:** The framework achieved a 100% success rate in obstacle avoidance across five experimental trials, each comprising 15 racing laps over ten distinct obstacle configurations.

#### 4.4. Near Time-Optimality

Comparison with offline-computed time-optimal trajectories (Figure 7) reveals a high degree of similarity between CoDiG-generated paths and optimal solutions. Minor deviations include CoDiG trajectories favoring increased clearance near obstacles for enhanced safety and slightly different turning radii in curved sections compared to the aggressive maneuvers of purely time-optimal solutions. This indicates an effective trade-off between safety and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CoDiG framework represents a step towards making diffusion models a practical tool for safety-critical robotics applications, addressing fundamental limitations in their current application.

**Addressing Core Limitations:** The primary significance of CoDiG lies in its ability to integrate explicit constraint-awareness and physical feasibility directly into the generative process of diffusion models. This overcomes a major hurdle for deploying diffusion models in real-world robotic systems, where unsafe or dynamically infeasible trajectories are unacceptable. By guiding the denoising process with barrier functions, CoDiG provides a mechanism to ensure safety and adherence to physical constraints, which is crucial for reliable operation.

**Data Efficiency and Generalizability:** The framework's approach of augmenting score updates at inference time, rather than learning conditional models from scratch, significantly reduces the need for large quantities of constraint-compliant expert data. This data efficiency is a considerable advantage in robotics, where collecting diverse and safe expert demonstrations can be costly and time-consuming. Furthermore, the explicit, gradient-based nature of constraint injection allows CoDiG to generalize to dynamic, time-varying, and novel constraints without requiring retraining, enhancing its adaptability across various tasks and environments.

**Enabling Real-Time Performance:** The introduction of a warm-start inference strategy is critical for translating the theoretical capabilities of diffusion models into practical, real-time robotic applications. Achieving a sampling frequency of 2.5 Hz, as demonstrated in the autonomous racing scenario, enables the high-frequency replanning necessary for dynamic obstacle avoidance and stable control. This acceleration strategy positions diffusion models as viable candidates for reactive planning and control loops.

**Demonstrated Real-World Reliability:** The successful deployment and 100% success rate in real-world autonomous racing experiments, involving both static and dynamic obstacles, provide empirical validation of CoDiG's robustness and practical utility. This real-world proof of concept distinguishes the work from many simulation-based studies and highlights its potential for broader adoption in robotics.

**Potential Impact:** CoDiG's contributions have implications for various robotic domains requiring safe and efficient motion planning, including autonomous driving, industrial automation, and human-robot interaction. By enabling diffusion models to generate safe, physically feasible, and near time-optimal trajectories in real-time, the framework opens avenues for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and trustworthy autonomous systems. Future work could explore extending the framework to higher-dimensional state spaces, multimodal inputs, and direct modeling of control inputs, further expanding the applicability of diffusion-based methods in robotic decision-making.
