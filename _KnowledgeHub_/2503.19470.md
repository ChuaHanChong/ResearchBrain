# ReSearch: Learning to Reason with Search for LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ReSearch framework enables Large Language Models to integrate multi-step reasoning with external search, learning interactively via reinforcement learning without supervised intermediate steps. It yields substantial performance gains on complex multi-hop question answering benchmarks and reveals emergent self-correction capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing multi-step Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approaches for LLMs rely on labor-intensive, manually designed prompts or fixed heuristics for external search, limiting scalability and adaptability.
- Training LLMs to reason with external tools traditionally requires costly supervised datasets that meticulously detail intermediate reasoning steps and search actions.
- LLMs often struggle with factual accuracy and exhibit hallucinations when addressing complex, knowledge-intensive questions, underscoring a need for more robust integration with up-to-date external information.

## Method

- ReSearch trains LLMs to dynamically incorporate internal thought processes, ==search query formulation==, and integration of retrieved information into a structured reasoning chain using specific tags.
- The framework employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, a reinforcement learning algorithm, to optimize the LLM's policy for search operations, guided by a simple ==F1-score-based reward function==.
- ==Retrieval results are masked during loss calculation==, directing the LLM's learning focus specifically to its generated reasoning steps and search queries.

## Results

- ReSearch models achieved average improvements ranging from **14.82%** to **15.81%** in Exact Match (EM) and **15.46%** to **17.56%** in LLM-as-a-Judge (LJ) compared to the strongest baselines across four multi-hop QA benchmarks.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization ability, performing effectively on diverse multi-hop datasets despite being trained exclusively on the MuSiQue training set.
- Analysis of training progression showed models increasing their response length and the average number of search operations per rollout, indicating a learned ability to engage in more extensive and iterative reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, driven by straightforward outcome-based rewards, can effectively teach LLMs to autonomously perform interactive, multi-step reasoning by leveraging external search tools.
> - Structuring search operations within the LLM's thought process using explicit tags fosters a robust and dynamic interaction with external knowledge sources.
> - Advanced reasoning capabilities, such as self-elicited reflection and self-correction of search queries, can naturally emerge from the reinforcement learning process without explicit design or training for these behaviors.
