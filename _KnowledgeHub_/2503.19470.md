# ReSearch: Learning to Reason with Search for LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ReSearch framework enables Large Language Models to integrate multi-step reasoning with external search, learning interactively via reinforcement learning without supervised intermediate steps. It yields substantial performance gains on complex multi-hop question answering benchmarks and reveals emergent self-correction capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing multi-step Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approaches for LLMs rely on labor-intensive, manually designed prompts or fixed heuristics for external search, limiting scalability and adaptability.
- Training LLMs to reason with external tools traditionally requires costly supervised datasets that meticulously detail intermediate reasoning steps and search actions.
- LLMs often struggle with factual accuracy and exhibit hallucinations when addressing complex, knowledge-intensive questions, underscoring a need for more robust integration with up-to-date external information.

## Method

- ReSearch trains LLMs to dynamically incorporate internal thought processes, ==search query formulation==, and integration of retrieved information into a structured reasoning chain using specific tags.
- The framework employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, a reinforcement learning algorithm, to optimize the LLM's policy for search operations, guided by a simple ==F1-score-based reward function==.
- ==Retrieval results are masked during loss calculation==, directing the LLM's learning focus specifically to its generated reasoning steps and search queries.

## Results

- ReSearch models achieved average improvements ranging from **14.82%** to **15.81%** in Exact Match (EM) and **15.46%** to **17.56%** in LLM-as-a-Judge (LJ) compared to the strongest baselines across four multi-hop QA benchmarks.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization ability, performing effectively on diverse multi-hop datasets despite being trained exclusively on the MuSiQue training set.
- Analysis of training progression showed models increasing their response length and the average number of search operations per rollout, indicating a learned ability to engage in more extensive and iterative reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, driven by straightforward outcome-based rewards, can effectively teach LLMs to autonomously perform interactive, multi-step reasoning by leveraging external search tools.
> - Structuring search operations within the LLM's thought process using explicit tags fosters a robust and dynamic interaction with external knowledge sources.
> - Advanced reasoning capabilities, such as self-elicited reflection and self-correction of search queries, can naturally emerge from the reinforcement learning process without explicit design or training for these behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within Artificial Intelligence, specifically Large Language Models (LLMs), Reasoning, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), with a significant contribution in the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to bridge these areas.

**The Rise of LLM Reasoning:** In recent years, LLMs have shown unprecedented capabilities in complex reasoning tasks, often facilitated by techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, where models decompose problems into intermediate steps before producing a final answer. This progress is exemplified by models such as OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1, which demonstrate impressive test-time scaling in reasoning. ReSearch acknowledges and builds upon these advancements, aiming to integrate external knowledge more effectively into these sophisticated reasoning processes.

**Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Simultaneously, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a critical paradigm to mitigate LLM hallucinations and incorporate up-to-date or domain-specific factual information by retrieving external knowledge (e.g., from search engines or databases) during generation. While effective for single-step information retrieval, designing robust multi-step RAG strategies for complex, real-world questions remains a significant challenge. Many existing multi-step RAG approaches rely on manually designed prompts or heuristics, which are labor-intensive, lack scalability, and struggle with intricate multi-hop questions requiring several iterative retrieval steps. Current methods like Iter-RetGen and IRCoT, while improving multi-step RAG, are still largely prompt-based.

**Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) has proven to be a powerful tool for enhancing LLM capabilities, particularly for alignment with human values (RLHF) and, more recently, for improving reasoning skills without explicit supervised data on reasoning steps. Models like DeepSeek-R1 have shown that rule-based reward functions can guide LLMs to develop sophisticated internal reasoning patterns autonomously. However, existing RL approaches for reasoning have primarily focused on improving *internal* thought processes, with limited exploration of how to effectively combine this reasoning with *external* knowledge retrieval in a dynamic, interactive manner.

**Bridging the Gap:** ReSearch directly addresses this gap. It proposes a novel framework that integrates reasoning with search operations as an intrinsic part of an LLM's thought process, guided by reinforcement learning. Instead of relying on pre-defined heuristics or costly supervised datasets for multi-step retrieval, ReSearch trains LLMs to *learn* when and how to perform search operations, and how to integrate the retrieved results into subsequent reasoning. This positions ReSearch as a significant advancement in making LLMs more robust, adaptive, and capable in knowledge-intensive, multi-hop question-answering scenarios. It extends the success of RL for internal reasoning to an interactive, tool-augmented environment, which is a frontier in current LLM research. The paper also notes concurrent works (Jin et al. 2025, Song et al. 2025) that are also beginning to explore RL for tool use, indicating ReSearch is at the forefront of an active research direction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the ReSearch framework is to enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to **effectively reason with external search tools via reinforcement learning, without requiring any supervised data on the intermediate reasoning steps.**

This overarching goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations:

- **Integrate Search as an Integral Part of Reasoning:** The primary objective is to treat search operations (query formulation and result assimilation) not as an external add-on, but as an integral and interactive component of the LLM's chain-like reasoning process. This means the LLM should be able to dynamically decide *when* to search, *what* to search for (based on its current thinking), and *how* to incorporate the search results to influence its subsequent reasoning steps.
- **Overcome Limitations of Manual Multi-step RAG:** Current multi-step Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approaches heavily rely on manually designed prompts or fixed heuristics to guide retrieval, which are laborious to create, difficult to scale, and often brittle when faced with diverse or complex real-world problems. ReSearch aims to replace this manual effort with an autonomous, learned strategy.
- **Address the Cost of Supervised Data:** Labeling explicit reasoning steps, especially in a multi-step RAG setting where search actions are intertwined, is extremely expensive and time-consuming. ReSearch is motivated by the success of reinforcement learning in eliciting complex behaviors from simple reward signals, thereby eliminating the need for such fine-grained supervised reasoning step data.
- **Enhance Generalizability of Reasoning-with-Search:** The goal is for the LLM to learn a generalized ability to reason with search, rather than memorizing specific patterns. By training on a single dataset and evaluating across diverse benchmarks, the authors aim to demonstrate that the learned policy is broadly applicable to various types of multi-hop questions and information retrieval needs.
- **Elicit Advanced Reasoning Capabilities:** Beyond mere information retrieval, the framework seeks to naturally elicit advanced reasoning capabilities within the LLM, such as reflection and self-correction, which are crucial for navigating complex, uncertain information environments. This would showcase the power of RL in fostering emergent intelligent behaviors.

In essence, ReSearch seeks to make LLMs not just intelligent reasoners or efficient retrievers, but intelligent **reasoner-retrievers** that can adaptively and strategically leverage external knowledge to solve complex problems, all while learning through trial and error guided by simple outcome-based rewards.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReSearch proposes a novel framework for training LLMs to dynamically integrate reasoning and external search operations using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm, without relying on any supervised data for intermediate reasoning steps.

**Core Idea: Reasoning Chain with Search Operations**
The central tenet of ReSearch is to extend the concept of a "reasoning chain" (like Chain-of-Thought) to include explicit search operations. The reasoning chain is structured with specific tags:
- `<think> ... </think>`: Encloses text-based thinking or internal reasoning steps.
- `<search> ... </search>`: Encloses the search query formulated by the LLM.
- `<result> ... </result>`: Encloses the retrieved factual information.
This structure ensures an iterative interaction where text-based thinking guides search queries, and search results subsequently inform further text-based thinking, creating a dynamic feedback loop.

**Reinforcement Learning Algorithm: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
ReSearch utilizes GRPO for training, chosen for its ability to optimize the LLM policy without requiring a separate critic model (unlike PPO).
- **Objective:** GRPO aims to maximize the probability of generating high-reward reasoning-with-search chains (rollouts).
- **Advantage Calculation:** It estimates the baseline from a *group* of rollouts, calculating a normalized advantage for each rollout within that group.
- **KL Divergence Penalty:** A KL divergence term is added to the objective function to prevent the policy from deviating too much from a reference policy (e.g., the original pre-trained LLM), ensuring training stability.

**Rollout with Search Mechanism**
The rollout generation process is iterative and interactive:
1. **Initial Generation:** The LLM generates tokens based on the input question and previous context.
2. **Search Query Identification:** When the LLM generates the `</search>` tag, the text between the last `<search>` and this `</search>` tag is extracted as the search query.
3. **External Search:** This query is then submitted to an external search environment (Wikipedia-based, using FlashRAG toolkit with E5-base-v2 retriever on Wikipedia data from Dec. 2018). The top-K (e.g., top-5) retrieval results are obtained.
4. **Result Integration:** The retrieved results are enclosed within `<result>` and `</result>` tags and appended to the current rollout.
5. **Continued Generation:** The LLM continues generating further thinking, search queries, or the final answer, using the expanded context that now includes the search results.
6. **Termination:** The process continues until the LLM generates an end-of-sentence (eos) tag (e.g., `<endoftext>`).

**Retrieval Result Masking**
A crucial methodological detail is how loss is computed. Since retrieval results are *not* generated by the LLM policy but are external inputs, these tokens are masked during loss calculation. This prevents the LLM from being inadvertently trained to reproduce search results and ensures that the optimization focuses only on the LLM's generated content (thinking and search queries).

**Training Templates**
To guide the LLM in generating the structured output required by the framework, specific prompt templates are designed:
- **Base Model Template:** A direct prompt structure for pre-trained models.
- **Instruction-Tuned Model Template:** A system prompt used with the instruction-tuned model's chat template.
These templates clearly define the roles of `<think>`, `<search>`, `<result>`, and `<answer>` tags, ensuring the model understands the desired format for interactive reasoning with search.

**Reward Modeling**
ReSearch employs a simple, rule-based reward function to guide the reinforcement learning process, avoiding the need for complex, manually crafted reward models:
- **Answer Reward:** Calculated as the F1 score between the final answer extracted from `\boxed{}` and the ground truth answer.
- **Format Reward:** A small positive reward (0.1) is given if the final answer's F1 score is zero but the rollout correctly follows the defined format (i.e., correct tag usage and `\boxed{}` in the answer). If both F1 score is zero and the format is incorrect, the reward is 0.
This structure prioritizes answer correctness while still incentivizing adherence to the interaction protocol.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base LLMs:** Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-32B, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct.
- **Training Data:** Exclusively the training set of the MuSiQue dataset (19,938 samples). This choice allows for robust evaluation of generalizability.
- **Benchmarks:** HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle – all multi-hop question answering datasets. Context documents are discarded for evaluation, relying solely on the LLM's learned search capabilities.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Exact Match (EM) and LLM-as-a-Judge (LJ) using GPT-4o-mini for more nuanced assessment of answer correctness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the significant effectiveness, generalizability, and emergent capabilities of the ReSearch framework.

**1. Superior Performance Over Baselines:**
- ReSearch models consistently and significantly outperform all baseline methods (Naive Generation, Naive RAG, Iter-RetGen, IRCoT) across all four multi-hop question answering benchmarks: HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle.
- For Qwen2.5-7B models, ReSearch shows average improvements of 15.81% in Exact Match (EM) and 17.56% in LLM-as-a-Judge (LJ) over the best baseline.
- For Qwen2.5-32B models, the average improvements are 14.82% in EM and 15.46% in LJ. These substantial gains highlight the efficacy of training LLMs to interactively reason with search through reinforcement learning.

**2. Impact of Base vs. Instruction-Tuned Models:**
- Models trained from instruction-tuned versions (e.g., ReSearch-Qwen-7B-Instruct) generally achieve better performance than those trained from base models (e.g., ReSearch-Qwen-7B), even though both are trained from scratch with RL. This suggests that the initial instruction-following capabilities embedded in instruction-tuned models provide a stronger foundation for the RL process to build upon.

**3. Strong Generalization Ability:**
- A key finding is ReSearch's impressive generalization ability. Despite being trained solely on the training set of the MuSiQue dataset, the models exhibit strong performance across diverse benchmarks with varying question types and structures. This indicates that ReSearch successfully learns a fundamental, knowledge-independent ability to reason with search, rather than overfitting to specific datasets or multi-hop patterns.

**4. Emergent Reasoning Behaviors During Training:**
- **Increased Response Length and Search Operations:** Analysis of the training process reveals progressive learning. The response length (number of generated tokens, excluding retrieval results) generally increases throughout training, indicating that models learn to engage in more extensive reasoning. Similarly, the average number of search operations per rollout consistently grows, demonstrating that the models learn to leverage multiple iterative search steps for complex questions.
- **Adaptation in 32B Models:** For 32B models, an interesting observation is an initial decrease in response length before a subsequent increase. This is hypothesized to be due to the larger models' initial reliance on their inherent knowledge, which then shifts towards more frequent search utilization upon receiving early reward signals, eventually balancing both.
- **Positive Reward Trajectory:** Training and validation rewards show a sharp increase in early training steps, followed by a gradual rise and convergence, confirming that the GRPO algorithm successfully optimizes the LLM policy towards higher-reward reasoning-with-search behaviors. Instruction-tuned models tend to have higher cold-start rewards, further supporting their stronger initial state.

**5. Self-Elicited Reflection and Self-Correction (Case Study):**
- A compelling case study demonstrates ReSearch's ability to naturally elicit advanced reasoning capabilities. In a multi-hop question, the model initially makes a suboptimal search query. However, in a subsequent `<think>` step, it explicitly acknowledges, "It appears that I made a mistake in my search query," and then corrects itself by generating a more precise search query. This "self-elicited reflection" and "self-correction" behavior is remarkable, as it was not explicitly trained for or designed into the prompt templates, but emerged organically from the reinforcement learning process.

In summary, ReSearch represents a significant step forward in training LLMs for complex, knowledge-intensive tasks by effectively merging reasoning with dynamic external search through reinforcement learning, yielding superior performance and demonstrating emergent self-improvement capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReSearch framework marks a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), with considerable potential impact across various domains.

**1. Novel Integration of Reasoning and Search with Reinforcement Learning:**
The primary significance lies in proposing and successfully demonstrating a novel framework that trains LLMs to **reason with search** as an intrinsic part of their thought process, utilizing reinforcement learning. Crucially, this is achieved *without any supervised data on intermediate reasoning steps*. This addresses a major bottleneck in developing sophisticated RAG systems, which traditionally rely on labor-intensive prompt engineering or costly step-by-step human annotations for multi-hop questions. By treating search operations as integral components of the reasoning chain, ReSearch enables a more dynamic and adaptive interaction with external knowledge.

**2. Enhanced Capabilities for Complex Knowledge-Intensive Tasks:**
ReSearch significantly improves LLMs' ability to tackle complex, multi-hop question answering. By learning *when* and *how* to perform searches and effectively integrating the results into subsequent thinking, LLMs become more adept at decomposing intricate problems and synthesizing information from multiple sources. This leads to substantial performance gains over existing prompt-based or heuristic-driven RAG methods, moving towards more robust and reliable AI systems for knowledge-intensive applications.

**3. Emergence of Advanced Reasoning Skills:**
Perhaps one of the most exciting aspects is the demonstration that ReSearch can naturally elicit advanced reasoning capabilities like **reflection and self-correction**. The observed ability of the model to recognize its own errors in search queries and subsequently refine them, without explicit instruction or predefined heuristics, highlights the power of reinforcement learning to foster complex, emergent intelligent behaviors. This finding suggests a pathway for developing more autonomous and self-improving LLM agents.

**4. Strong Generalizability:**
The framework's ability to generalize effectively across diverse multi-hop benchmarks after being trained on only a single dataset is highly significant. It implies that ReSearch is learning a fundamental, transferable "skill" of reasoning with search, rather than just memorizing task-specific patterns. This boosts the practical utility and scalability of the approach for real-world scenarios, where models encounter a wide variety of question types and information needs.

**5. Practical Implications and Potential Applications:**
- **Improved Factual Accuracy and Reduced Hallucinations:** By dynamically retrieving and incorporating external, up-to-date information, ReSearch can further mitigate LLM hallucinations and provide more factually accurate responses, enhancing the trustworthiness of AI systems.
- **Advancements in Education and Research:** The framework could power more sophisticated AI tutors, research assistants, and knowledge discovery tools that can navigate complex information landscapes and provide well-reasoned answers.
- **Enhanced Decision-Making Support:** In fields requiring access to vast and dynamic information (e.g., medicine, law, finance), ReSearch-enabled LLMs could offer more informed and verifiable insights.
- **More Transparent and Explainable AI:** The explicit `<think>`, `<search>`, `<result>` structure of the reasoning chain provides a degree of transparency into the LLM's thought process, which could lead to more explainable AI systems, fostering greater user trust.

**Potential Challenges and Limitations (as noted by authors):**
- **Reward Modeling Complexity:** The current simple F1-score based reward might not generalize to tasks requiring nuanced, longer, or subjective responses, necessitating more sophisticated reward modeling.
- **Knowledge Base Dependency:** The reliance on Wikipedia as the primary knowledge base, while standard, limits its immediate applicability to highly specialized or proprietary domain-specific information. Future work needs to explore diverse knowledge sources.
- **Computational Costs:** Reinforcement learning, especially with large LLMs, can be computationally intensive, potentially leading to higher energy consumption.

Despite these limitations, ReSearch offers a promising direction for creating more capable, reliable, and intelligent LLM-based systems. By effectively teaching LLMs to interact with and learn from external information sources through self-directed reasoning, this work pushes the boundaries of autonomous AI agents.
