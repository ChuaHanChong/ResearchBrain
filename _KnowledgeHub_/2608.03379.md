# Residual Flow Matching with Dynamic Cross-Interaction for 3D Multi-Person Motion Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage framework, Prior-Guided Residual Flow Matching (RFM), is presented for 3D multi-person motion prediction, integrating a deterministic coarse prior with a continuous-time residual flow. The model improves structural consistency and interaction modeling through a Dynamic Cross-Interaction mechanism, demonstrating competitive accuracy on diverse benchmarks like 3DPW and CMU-Syn, with reductions in metrics such as MPJPE.

## Problem

- Deterministic methods in 3D multi-person motion prediction often optimize for a single future trajectory, failing to capture the multimodal nature of human behavior.
- Continuous-time generative models (e.g., Flow Matching) struggle with computational burden and structural consistency when synthesizing high-dimensional 3D skeletal sequences directly from unstructured noise.
- Effectively modeling inter-agent interactions in generative frameworks is challenging due to the network attempting to extract social contexts from noise-corrupted representations during early integration steps, leading to spurious correlations.

## Method

- A **Prior-Guided Residual Flow Matching (PRFM)** framework decomposes motion prediction into a ==Deterministic Coarse Prior (DCP)== module for an initial kinematic anchor, and a ==Residual Flow Matching (RFM)== module for generative refinement.
- A ==Dynamic Cross-Interaction (DCI)== mechanism is introduced to adaptively modulate the strength of inter-agent message-passing based on the integration progress, suppressing interactions in noise-dominated early stages.
- A hierarchical dual-branch architecture explicitly models motion at joint, individual, and social levels, with bidirectional fusion, to decouple local skeletal topology from global multi-agent interactions.

## Results

- The framework achieves competitive prediction accuracy on 3DPW (e.g., MPJPE of **69.92mm** on 3DPW-RC), CMU-Syn, and crowded Mix datasets, showing improved fidelity in individual pose and global root-trajectory predictions.
- Ablation studies confirm that the Deterministic Coarse Prior reduces MPJPE by **+2.78mm** and the Dynamic Cross-Interaction mechanism improves MPJPE by **+0.70mm** on 3DPW-RC, validating their contributions.
- The model demonstrates strong generalizability through successful synthetic-to-real transfer from CMU-Syn to MuPoTS-3D and performance improvements when pre-trained on the AMASS database.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the generative objective by predicting motion residuals relative to a deterministic prior, rather than absolute motion from noise, improves structural consistency and stabilizes the learning process for high-dimensional skeletal data.
> - Dynamically modulating inter-agent message-passing based on the generative integration progress prevents the model from learning spurious social correlations from noisy intermediate states, enhancing the reliability of interaction modeling.
> - A dedicated architectural design that hierarchically separates and fuses local joint features with global multi-agent motion features enables a more robust capture of both individual kinematics and complex social dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D multi-person motion prediction is concerned with forecasting future skeletal configurations of multiple interacting individuals based on their historical observations. This area is critical for various downstream applications, including autonomous navigation and human-robot interaction. The task presents complex challenges related to modeling both individual kinematics and the stochastic nature of inter-person interactions.

Historically, deterministic methods, employing architectures such as recurrent neural networks, graph-based models, Transformers, or Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), have been used to model spatio-temporal dependencies. However, these methods typically optimize for a single future trajectory, which can restrict their ability to capture the inherent multimodal nature of human behavior, as identical histories can lead to diverse yet plausible futures.

More recently, continuous-time generative models, including Diffusion models and Flow Matching, have been adapted for human motion prediction. These models can generate multiple plausible future hypotheses, addressing the multimodality limitation. Nevertheless, a significant challenge with these generative models, particularly when synthesizing high-dimensional 3D skeletal sequences directly from an unstructured noise prior, is the computational burden and the potential for compromising structural consistency. The models often struggle to concurrently learn local skeletal topology and diverse future dynamics, which can lead to kinematic distortions and motions inconsistent with observed history, especially during early, noise-dominated integration steps.

Furthermore, effectively modeling inter-agent interactions within continuous-time generative frameworks presents its own set of challenges. Existing approaches often apply cross-person aggregation uniformly throughout the denoising or integration process, without modulating the interaction strength based on the integration progress. This can lead to the network attempting to extract social contexts from unreliable, noise-corrupted representations in the early stages, thereby introducing spurious correlations and potentially degrading the fidelity of multi-person motion. While temporal conditioning through timestep embeddings is standard for individual motions, explicitly aligning the magnitude of inter-person interaction with the integration progress in multi-agent contexts has remained an area with scope for further exploration.

This work addresses these limitations by restructuring the generative paradigm. It aims to improve structural consistency by incorporating a deterministic prior and to enhance interaction modeling in generative processes by introducing a dynamic, time-modulated cross-interaction mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for 3D multi-person motion prediction that can accurately forecast future skeletal configurations while addressing the limitations of existing continuous-time generative models. The motivation stems from several specific issues identified in the broader research landscape:

1. **Addressing Structural Consistency and Generative Burden:** Continuous-time generative models, such as Flow Matching, face difficulties when directly synthesizing high-dimensional 3D skeletal sequences from pure noise. This approach often requires the model to simultaneously learn complex skeletal structures and stochastic temporal variations from scratch, which can result in kinematic distortions and trajectories inconsistent with observed history.
 - **Motivation:** To alleviate this, the authors propose to decouple the forecasting objective. Instead of generating absolute motion from noise, the framework first establishes a deterministic kinematic "anchor" or "coarse prior." The generative model then focuses on predicting the *residuals* relative to this prior. This formulation simplifies the generative objective, stabilizes the learning process, and aims to preserve structural integrity.

2. **Improving Reliability of Inter-Person Interactions in Generative Models:** In continuous-time generative processes, the intermediate states during early integration steps are heavily influenced by noise. Applying unconstrained or static spatial message-passing mechanisms in multi-person scenarios at these early stages compels the network to derive social contexts from unreliable, noise-corrupted representations. This can introduce spurious correlations between agents and degrade overall motion fidelity.
 - **Motivation:** The authors are motivated to introduce a mechanism that can adaptively modulate the strength of inter-agent message-passing based on the integration progress. This mechanism aims to suppress social interactions when the latent states are predominantly noise-dominated and progressively amplify them as the states become more stable and informative. This approach seeks to ensure the extraction of more reliable social contexts and improve the fidelity of multi-person motion.

3. **Enhancing Kinematic Coherence and Hierarchical Modeling:** The challenge of multi-person motion prediction involves capturing both fine-grained individual articulated joint dynamics and complex global social interactions. Jointly encoding these without explicit separation can compromise the fidelity of individual motions.
 - **Motivation:** The research aims to design a hierarchical representation that explicitly separates and models motion at the joint, individual, and social levels. This decoupling is intended to isolate local skeletal topology from inter-agent interactions, thereby preserving structural integrity during the motion generation process.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a two-stage generative paradigm that combines a deterministic prior with a continuous-time residual flow to improve structural consistency and simplify the generative task.
- Implement a Dynamic Cross-Interaction mechanism to temporally synchronize inter-agent message-passing with the integration progress, enhancing the reliability of social context extraction.
- Design a hierarchical dual-branch architecture for motion representation, enabling decoupled modeling of local skeletal topology and global multi-agent interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed Prior-Guided Residual Flow Matching, decomposes the 3D multi-person motion prediction into a deterministic prior and generative residuals, employing three main components.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Framework Overview

The system takes historical 3D motion X of P individuals with J joints over T_h frames and aims to predict K plausible future sequences Y*_k over T_f frames. The framework addresses challenges related to spatial consistency, hierarchical motion encoding, and noise interference during continuous-time generation. It operates in two stages: a Deterministic Coarse Prior (DCP) module, followed by a Residual Flow Matching (RFM) module.

#### 3.2. Deterministic Coarse Prior (DCP)

This module generates an initial deterministic coarse prediction Y_c to serve as a kinematic anchor for the subsequent generative process.
1. **Spatial Joint Encoding:** This branch models frame-wise skeletal topology. Input 3D joint coordinates are linearly mapped to initial embeddings Z^(0)_J. These are then refined across L layers, each performing joint feature learning (φ^(l)_ind) and cross-joint learning (φ^(l)_mix). The cross-joint learning flattens per-person joint features into a unified vector, models full-body correlations, and reshapes them back.
2. **Temporal Motion Encoding:** This branch models long-horizon temporal dynamics and multi-agent interactions. The historical sequence X is transformed using Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to initialize motion embedding Z^(0)_M. In each of L layers, Z^(l-1)_M is branched into local individual features Z^(l)_loc and global social states Z^(l)_glo. A bidirectional individual-social interaction mechanism is applied, where global context (augmented by an inter-person distance embedding τ(D)) modulates local features, and aggregated local features refine the global state. Layer normalization and MLPs are used for affine parameter generation.
3. **Cross-Branch Interaction and Coarse Estimation:** Periodically (e.g., every four layers), a bidirectional gated mechanism exchanges representations between the spatial joint (Z^(l)_J) and temporal motion (Z^(l)_M) branches. After L layers, the final features from both branches are concatenated and projected to a unified latent representation Z_c. An MLP then maps Z_c to residual DCT coefficients, which are inverse DCT (iDCT) transformed to yield the coarse spatial motion residual R_c. The deterministic coarse prediction Y_c is obtained by anchoring R_c to the last observed pose X_Th (Y_c = R_c + X_Th). A frame-wise L2 distance matrix D_c of R_c is also computed. The tuple (Z_c, R_c, D_c) constitutes the deterministic prior.

#### 3.3. Prior-Guided Residual Flow Refinement

This stage employs a conditional residual flow matching framework to refine the coarse prior, focusing on capturing intricate temporal dependencies and multi-agent interactions.
1. **Residual Flow Formulation:** Instead of learning an unconstrained vector field, the framework formulates the continuous-time probability flow over motion residuals. The generative target is defined as the clean endpoint residual R^(1) = Y - X_Th. During training, an intermediate state R^(t) is constructed via linear interpolation between the clean target R^(1) and an initial noise R^(0) (R^(t) = tR^(1) + (1-t)βR^(0)). An endpoint parameterization is adopted to directly predict the clean target R^(1).
2. **Noise-Aware Motion Refinement via Dynamic Cross-Interaction (DCI):** The refinement network integrates the deterministic prior with time-varying noisy states. For an integration step t, the spatial noisy state R^(t) and time variable t are embedded and fused with the deterministic latent prior Z_c from the DCP stage to form the initial motion feature H^(0)_M. This feature is processed by L refinement layers, sharing the local-global decoupling architecture of the coarse stage.
 The DCI mechanism temporally modulates the global-to-local feature conditioning. A time-varying modulation weight g(t) = w_min + (w_max - w_min)t^γ is introduced. This function suppresses noisy social messages during early, noise-dominated stages (t → 0) by setting g(0) = w_min, and progressively restores multi-person dependencies as latent states stabilize (t → 1) by approaching g(t) → w_max. This explicit time-conditioning gates the uncertain global context while allowing the deterministic spatial prior (D_c) to remain unattenuated.
3. **Joint Refinement and Bidirectional Fusion:** A parallel joint branch, initialized by embedding the coarse estimation Y_c, operates in tandem with the motion branch. It refines joint features H^(l)_J through intra- and inter-joint mappings, and integrates with motion features H^(l)_M via a bidirectional gated exchange at each layer. This synchronized fusion aims to ensure kinematically valid and contextually aware generated sequences.
4. **Future Motion Prediction:** The final motion and joint features (H^(L)_M, H^(L)_J) are concatenated, projected via an MLP, and added to the coarse latent feature Z_c to form the refined latent representation Z_f. K parallel prediction heads decode distinct multimodal residuals R^(1)_k using iDCT. Each predicted residual is anchored to the last observed motion X_Th to generate raw K-mode future motions Y_k. Finally, a full-sequence temporal alignment module (a Transformer) refines the concatenated observed history X and predicted future Y_k, then truncates to the future horizon to yield the final predictions Y*_k.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

The model is optimized end-to-end using a joint loss function:
- An L2 loss penalizes the deviation between the coarse prediction Y_c and ground truth Y_GT.
- A Winner-Takes-All (WTA) L2 objective minimizes the error of the best-matching hypothesis among K predictions from the residual flow, to capture multi-modal futures.
- Geometric constraints penalize bone-length violations to ensure anatomical plausibility.

#### 3.5. Inference

During inference, motion generation is formulated as solving the induced Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) using a fixed-step explicit Euler solver with 10 integration steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental evaluations across multiple datasets and metrics to demonstrate the performance of the proposed framework.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

The framework achieves competitive prediction accuracy across various benchmarks, often outperforming or matching state-of-the-art baselines.
- **Intra-Dataset Forecasting:**
 - On **3DPW** (real-world, in-the-wild) and **CMU-Syn** (synthetic), the model demonstrates reductions in Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE), Joint Position Error (JPE), Aligned Pose Error (APE), Final Displacement Error (FDE), and Visibility-Ignored Metric (VIM). For instance, on 3DPW-RC, MPJPE is 69.92mm and APE is 99.05mm, which are improvements over baselines. The results on camera-motion-compensated (3DPW-RC) settings suggest effective capture of fine-grained local body articulations.
 - Performance across varying temporal horizons on CMU-Syn (short-term 1s/1s, long-term 2s/2s, extended 1s/3s) shows consistent improvements, indicating effective mitigation of error accumulation in long-term forecasting.
 - On **Mix1 (6-person)** and **Mix2 (10-person)** benchmarks for crowded environments, the method achieves low errors across all five metrics. The reductions in JPE (e.g., 40.60 on Mix1) and FDE (e.g., 28.52 on Mix1) suggest accurate individual pose and global root-trajectory predictions even under severe spatial constraints and increased social complexity. The framework appears to process expanded relational dynamics effectively.

- **Cross-Dataset Evaluation and Pre-training:**
 - **Synthetic-to-Real Transfer (CMU-Syn to MuPoTS-3D):** When trained solely on synthetic CMU-Syn data and directly evaluated on real-world MuPoTS-3D, the model yields low errors across primary spatial (MPJPE), pose-vector (VIM), and local articulation (APE) metrics.
 - **Large-Scale Pre-training (AMASS to 3DPW):** Pre-training on the AMASS database consistently improves the base model's performance on 3DPW, maintaining strong results in MPJPE, VIM, and APE. These findings indicate that the framework learns generalizable representations and is robust to domain shifts.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies conducted on 3DPW-RC and CMU-Syn (1s/1s) demonstrate the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Deterministic Coarse Prior (DCP):** Removing the DCP module (w/o DCP) leads to degraded performance (e.g., MPJPE +2.78 on 3DPW-RC), validating its role in guiding the generative process with a stable kinematic foundation.
- **Residual Flow Matching (RFM):** Replacing ODE integration with a deterministic network (w/o RFM) or formulating the flow over absolute coordinates instead of motion residuals (w/ AFM) both degrade performance. This supports the effectiveness of flow-based refinement and the stability provided by predicting motion residuals.
- **Dynamic Cross-Interaction (DCI):** Replacing the dynamic DCI mechanism with static interaction (w/o DCI, g(t)=1) results in performance degradation (e.g., MPJPE +0.70 on 3DPW-RC), highlighting the importance of time-varying modulation for scaling interaction strength along the ODE trajectory. Analysis of DCI hyperparameters confirms optimal settings for minimum/maximum conditioning strengths and transition profiles.
- **Joint Branch (JB):** Removing the joint branch (w/o JB) across both stages increases position errors, confirming its importance for explicit spatial feature extraction and skeletal integrity.
- **Full-Sequence Temporal Alignment (FTA):** Removing this module (w/o FTA) increases prediction errors, indicating its necessity for mitigating behavioral discontinuities between historical and future sequences.
- **Loss Function:** Omitting the coarse loss or the bone length constraint during training also leads to performance degradation, demonstrating the importance of these objectives for structural guidance and anatomical plausibility.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Visualizations on challenging sequences (e.g., 3DPW-RC, Mix1, CMU-Syn) illustrate that the proposed model captures complex motion intentions (e.g., bend-to-stand), maintains spatial coherence during asymmetric interactions, and yields more stable predictions in multi-agent dynamics compared to baseline methods. The two-stage generative paradigm shows that the RFM refinement effectively mitigates trajectory drift and spatial misalignment observed in the initial DCP predictions, aligning global trajectories and local body articulations closer to the ground truth.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presents a framework that advances the state-of-the-art in 3D multi-person motion prediction by addressing specific limitations of continuous-time generative models in this domain.

#### 5.1. Advancements in Motion Prediction

- **Enhanced Structural Consistency:** By introducing a deterministic coarse prior and formulating residual flow matching, the framework offers a more stable and accurate approach to synthesizing high-dimensional skeletal motions. This mitigates issues of kinematic distortion and history-inconsistent motions commonly observed when generative models predict directly from noise.
- **Improved Interaction Modeling:** The Dynamic Cross-Interaction mechanism represents a methodological contribution to continuous-time generative models, demonstrating how temporal modulation of inter-agent message passing can significantly reduce noise interference and improve the reliability of social context extraction, particularly in early generative stages.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The consistent performance across diverse real-world and synthetic datasets, including crowded scenarios, and the demonstrated transferability through zero-shot evaluation and pre-training, suggest that the learned representations are generalizable. This indicates the model's potential for deployment in varied and unconstrained environments.

#### 5.2. Methodological Contributions

- The **Prior-Guided Residual Flow Matching** framework establishes a novel two-stage generative paradigm for structured data, which could be adaptable to other complex generation tasks.
- The **Dynamic Cross-Interaction** mechanism offers a generalizable strategy for managing noise-corrupted features in continuous-time generative models involving multi-agent interactions.
- The **decoupled joint-motion architecture** with bidirectional fusion effectively balances the modeling of local skeletal topology with global multi-agent dynamics, leading to improved kinematic coherence.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact and Applications

The enhanced accuracy and robustness in predicting 3D multi-person motion have implications for several application areas:
- **Autonomous Systems:** Improved prediction of human movements can enable safer and more efficient autonomous navigation for robots and vehicles operating in human environments.
- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** More natural and adaptive human-robot collaboration can be facilitated by systems that accurately anticipate human actions and intentions.
- **Animation and Virtual Reality:** The generation of realistic and diverse multi-person motions could enhance the fidelity of animated content, virtual environments, and character control.
- **Sports Analysis and Surveillance:** Accurate motion forecasting can support performance analysis in sports and improve anomaly detection in surveillance systems.

#### 5.4. Identified Limitations and Future Work

The authors also acknowledge several limitations:
- **Inference Latency:** The iterative nature of ODE integration in the flow matching process currently incurs higher inference latency, which may be a constraint for real-time applications.
- **Hyperparameter Reliance:** The temporal schedule for the Dynamic Cross-Interaction mechanism relies on predefined hyperparameters, and it does not currently incorporate external scene constraints to adapt interaction strength more intelligently.
- **Fixed Number of Individuals:** The current architecture assumes a fixed number of interacting individuals, which restricts its flexibility in dynamically changing crowd scenarios.

Future research directions suggested include exploring distillation techniques to accelerate inference, investigating scene-aware modulation to ground human interactions within physical environments, and developing architectures that can handle a flexible number of interacting agents.
