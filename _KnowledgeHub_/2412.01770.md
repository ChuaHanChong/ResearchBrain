# Robot Learning with Super-Linear Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> The CASHER pipeline enables super-linear scaling of robot learning data with sub-linear human effort by using crowdsourced digital twins and an iterative training process for generalist policies. This approach resulted in a 62% zero-shot success rate for a manipulation task in unseen real-world kitchens and improved performance by an average of 55% through unsupervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Robot learning is constrained by the scarcity and high cost of obtaining diverse, high-quality training data.
- Existing data collection methods, such as teleoperation, scale linearly with human effort, limiting the acquisition of large datasets needed for generalist robot capabilities.
- Generating realistic and diverse data in simulation requires significant human effort from designers for scene, task, and reward creation, hindering scalability.

## Method

- **CASHER** (==Crowdsourcing and Amortizing Human Effort for Real-to-Sim-to-Real==) is introduced, a pipeline for continual data collection and training of generalist robotic policies.
- Leverages ==crowdsourced 3D reconstructions== of real-world scenes to create diverse ==digital twins== in a physics simulator, reducing the need for expert-designed content.
- Implements an ==amortized data collection== strategy where a continually improving generalist policy generates demonstrations for new simulated environments, progressively reducing the reliance on human input.

## Results

- Achieved a **62%** zero-shot success rate for the 'put object into sink' task on **8** unseen real-world kitchens after training on **56** environments, demonstrating strong sim-to-real correlation (**R²=0.92**).
- Showcased significant amortization of human effort, with the number of human demonstrations needed per new environment decreasing over time due to the generalist policy's improved performance.
- Unsupervised scanned deployment fine-tuning boosted performance by an average of **55%** on target scenes where the generalist policy initially had **≤20%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Super-linear scaling of robot learning data is achievable with sub-linear human effort by combining crowdsourced digital twins and a self-improving generalist policy.
> - Model-generated demonstrations, being consistent with learned behaviors, contribute to more robust policy learning and can gradually replace the need for human-provided data.
> - Unsupervised fine-tuning, using only a video scan of a new environment, allows pre-trained generalist policies to significantly adapt and improve performance without additional human demonstrations.
