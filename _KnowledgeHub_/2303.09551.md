# SurroundOcc: Multi-Camera 3D Occupancy Prediction for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> SurroundOcc predicts dense 3D voxel occupancy from multi-camera images via 2D-3D spatial attention that lifts multi-scale image features into 3D volumes, refined by 3D convolutions with multi-level supervision, and trains on dense pseudo-labels generated by stitching multi-frame LiDAR and Poisson reconstruction, achieving state-of-the-art occupancy prediction on nuScenes and SemanticKITTI.

## Problem

- Multi-camera 3D object detection struggles with long-tail classes and cannot describe arbitrary-shaped real-world objects outside a fixed taxonomy.
- Depth-based multi-view methods only predict the nearest occupied point per ray and cannot recover occluded regions of the 3D scene.
- Existing occupancy methods either fuse per-camera results with weak cross-camera post-processing or train on sparse LiDAR supervision, yielding sparse, inaccurate predictions.

## Method

- ==2D-3D spatial attention==: 3D volume queries project to multi-camera 2D views and use ==deformable attention== to aggregate features, preserving 3D structure instead of collapsing to BEV.
- ==Multi-scale 2D-3D U-Net==: 3D deconvolution progressively upsamples and fuses volume features across four levels, each supervised by a decayed-weight occupancy loss.
- ==Dense ground-truth generation pipeline==: separately stitches multi-frame LiDAR points for dynamic objects and static scenes, densifies via ==Poisson Surface Reconstruction==, and assigns semantics via nearest-neighbor labeling.

## Results

- On nuScenes semantic occupancy, SurroundOcc reaches **31.49** SC IoU and **20.30** SSC mIoU, beating BEVFormer (**30.50**/**16.75**) and dense-supervised TPVFormer* (**30.86**/**17.10**).
- On SemanticKITTI monocular scene completion, SurroundOcc attains **34.72** SC IoU and **11.86** SSC mIoU, outperforming MonoScene and TPVFormer despite not being designed for monocular input.
- Ablations show dense occupancy supervision is critical: sparse LiDAR supervision gives only **11.96** SC IoU / **12.17** mIoU versus **31.49**/**20.30** with dense pseudo-labels, an over **2.5x** IoU gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Volumetric occupancy is a more general 3D scene representation than depth maps or bounding boxes since it handles occlusion and arbitrary shapes without a fixed class taxonomy.
> - Label density can be manufactured cheaply from existing detection and segmentation annotations via surface reconstruction, avoiding costly manual dense-voxel annotation.
> - Preserving 3D volume structure during cross-view feature fusion, rather than collapsing to BEV early, measurably improves fine-grained geometric fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

3D scene understanding is a cornerstone of autonomous driving, with recent trends leaning towards vision-centric approaches due to their cost-effectiveness and increasing performance. This paper situates itself at the forefront of this trend, specifically addressing the limitations of existing 3D perception paradigms.

Historically, LiDAR sensors have been the gold standard for direct geometric scene capture. However, their high cost, bulk, and sparse point clouds in turn limit their widespread adoption and dense scene reconstruction capabilities. Vision-centric methods, using multi-camera setups, have emerged as a promising alternative, demonstrating competitive performance in tasks like depth estimation, 3D object detection, and semantic map construction.

The prevailing approach in vision-based 3D perception has largely centered on **3D object detection**. While effective for recognized categories, this method inherently suffers from the "long-tail problem" – the inability to effectively recognize and model objects of arbitrary shapes or infinite classes encountered in the real world. Furthermore, object detection provides discrete bounding box representations, lacking a comprehensive understanding of the entire 3D space.

Another significant area is **depth estimation**, which predicts a depth value for each pixel. Methods like SurroundDepth have advanced multi-camera depth prediction. However, depth maps only capture the nearest occupied surface along an optical ray, fundamentally failing to recover occluded parts of the scene, which is critical for robust perception.

Recent work has begun exploring **3D occupancy prediction**, which describes a 3D scene by assigning an occupied probability to each voxel in a discretized 3D space. This representation inherently offers several advantages: it ensures multi-camera geometric consistency, can recover occluded parts, and is flexible for extension to tasks like 3D semantic segmentation. Pioneering works include MonoScene, which infers dense 3D semantic scenes from monocular images, and TPVFormer, which generalizes this to multi-camera semantic occupancy. However, MonoScene struggles with multi-camera fusion, and TPVFormer's reliance on sparse LiDAR supervision often leads to sparse occupancy predictions.

SurroundOcc directly addresses these shortcomings. By proposing a multi-camera, vision-only method for *dense* 3D occupancy prediction, it pushes the boundary of scene understanding. Its key innovation lies not only in its architectural design for robust multi-camera feature fusion but also, critically, in its novel pipeline for generating **dense occupancy ground truth**. The lack of dense, high-quality 3D occupancy labels has been a major bottleneck for training such models. By stitching multi-frame LiDAR data and employing Poisson Reconstruction, SurroundOcc effectively sidesteps the expensive manual annotation problem, enabling the training of models that predict far denser and more accurate scene representations than previously possible.

Thus, SurroundOcc fills a crucial gap by offering a method that overcomes the limitations of discrete object detection, partial depth maps, and sparse occupancy predictions, providing a more comprehensive, dense, and geometrically consistent 3D understanding of the environment using readily available multi-camera imagery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the SurroundOcc paper is to develop a method capable of predicting **dense and accurate 3D occupancy of surrounding scenes using only multi-camera images as input.**

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Limitations of Existing 3D Perception Paradigms:**
 - **3D Object Detection's Semantic Limitations:** Traditional 3D object detection, while useful, is constrained by predefined classes and struggles with novel objects or those with arbitrary, complex shapes. It also provides a discrete, rather than continuous, understanding of the environment.
 - **Depth Estimation's Occlusion Problem:** Depth maps, even from multiple cameras, fundamentally struggle to reconstruct occluded areas of a scene, providing an incomplete geometric understanding. This is a significant safety concern for autonomous driving.
- **Need for a Comprehensive 3D Scene Representation:** Autonomous driving systems require a robust, fine-grained, and geometrically consistent understanding of the entire 3D environment, including free space, occupied volumes, and their semantic properties. 3D occupancy, which assigns a probability of occupation to each voxel, naturally addresses these requirements, offering an "inside-out" view of the scene. It implicitly ensures multi-camera consistency and allows for the inference of occluded regions based on surrounding context.
- **Challenges in Multi-camera Integration:** Simply fusing monocular results or using basic multi-camera feature aggregation (e.g., averaging features) has proven suboptimal for complex 3D tasks due to issues like view occlusion, varying image quality, and projection ambiguities. A sophisticated approach to integrate multi-camera information into a coherent 3D representation is necessary.
- **Scarcity of Dense 3D Occupancy Ground Truth:** A major hurdle in training models for dense 3D occupancy prediction is the lack of readily available, accurately annotated dense occupancy labels for real-world driving datasets (e.g., nuScenes provides sparse LiDAR points). Manually annotating millions of voxels is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This necessitates an automated or semi-automated method to generate such labels.
- **Advancing Vision-Centric Autonomous Driving:** By moving towards a purely vision-based approach, the research aims to capitalize on the advantages of cameras – lower cost, smaller form factor, and richer texture information – making autonomous driving systems potentially more affordable and scalable.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond partial or sparse 3D scene understanding towards a holistic, dense, and semantically rich volumetric representation, while simultaneously addressing the practical data annotation challenges required to achieve this goal with cost-effective multi-camera vision systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SurroundOcc method is built around a multi-camera 3D occupancy prediction network, coupled with a novel pipeline for generating dense occupancy ground truth.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture (SurroundOcc)

The core network architecture follows a U-Net-like encoder-decoder structure, designed to process multi-camera images and output multi-scale 3D volume features, ultimately predicting volumetric occupancy.

- **Multi-scale 2D Feature Extraction:**
 - For each of the `N` input multi-camera images `{I_1, ..., I_N}`, a 2D backbone network is used. For nuScenes, ResNet-101-DCN is employed, pre-trained with FCOS3D weights. For SemanticKITTI, EfficientNetB7 is used.
 - This backbone extracts multi-scale 2D feature maps `{X^j_i}` (where `j` denotes scale level and `i` denotes camera) which are then processed by a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) to enhance their hierarchical representation.

- **2D-3D Spatial Attention (Cross-View Feature Lifting):**
 - This is a crucial component that lifts the 2D image features into a 3D volume space. Unlike simpler methods that average reprojected features or focus only on Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) features, SurroundOcc utilizes a 3D-based cross-view attention mechanism.
 - **3D Volume Queries:** Instead of 2D BEV queries, 3D volume queries `Q` (representing points in the 3D space) are defined.
 - **Projection and Deformable Attention:** For each 3D query point, its corresponding 3D position `q_p` is projected to the 2D views `V_hit` it "hits" (is visible in), using known camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
 - **Feature Aggregation:** Deformable attention is then applied. This mechanism samples 2D features `x` around these projected 2D positions across the hit views. It computes attention weights `A_ij` based on the query and key features, allowing the network to selectively aggregate relevant information from different views and dynamically adjust sampling locations. This effectively handles varying view contributions and occlusions.
 - The output `F` of this layer is a 3D volume feature, directly capturing spatial information.

- **Multi-scale Occupancy Prediction:**
 - The 2D-3D spatial attention is applied in a multi-scale fashion, generating multi-scale 3D volume features `{F_j}`.
 - A 3D convolution network then progressively upsamples lower-resolution 3D volume features (`Y_{j-1}`) using 3D deconvolution layers and fuses them with higher-resolution features (`F_j`) via skip connections (`Y_j = F_j + Deconv(Y_{j-1})`). This U-Net-like structure helps the network learn both high-level semantic context and fine-grained geometric details.
 - **Multi-level Supervision:** Occupancy prediction is supervised at each scale `j` (`V_j`) to ensure the network learns powerful features at all resolutions. A decayed loss weight `α_j = 1/2^j` is applied, emphasizing the importance of higher-resolution predictions.
 - **Loss Functions:** Cross-entropy loss is used for both binary occupancy prediction and multi-class semantic occupancy prediction. Scene-class affinity loss is also incorporated.

#### 3.2. Dense Occupancy Ground Truth Generation Pipeline

Given the sparsity of LiDAR data and the impracticality of manual annotation, SurroundOcc introduces a novel pipeline to generate dense 3D occupancy ground truth from existing sparse LiDAR points and 3D detection/semantic segmentation labels.

- **1. Multi-frame Point Cloud Stitching:**
 - The method addresses the challenge of dynamic objects by proposing a two-stream approach: static scenes and dynamic objects are stitched separately.
 - **Separation:** For each frame, LiDAR points are divided into static scene points and movable object points using provided 3D bounding box labels.
 - **Aggregation:** Across multiple frames in a sequence, static scene segments are collected and transformed into a unified world coordinate system. Similarly, points for specific dynamic objects (identified by bounding box index) are aggregated in the world coordinate system.
 - **Merging:** For the current frame, the aggregated static scene points and object points are transformed back into the current frame's ego-pose. This results in a much denser point cloud `P` for the current frame, leveraging information from the entire sequence.

- **2. Densifying with Poisson Reconstruction:**
 - The stitched point cloud `P`, while denser, still contains holes and uneven distribution due to limited LiDAR beams.
 - Normal vectors are computed for the points.
 - **Poisson Surface Reconstruction:** This algorithm is then applied to `P` and its normals to reconstruct a dense triangular mesh `M`. Poisson Reconstruction is effective at creating watertight surfaces from noisy and incomplete point clouds, effectively filling the holes.
 - **Voxelization:** The resulting dense mesh `M` is then voxelized to obtain dense volumetric occupancy `V_d`.

- **3. Semantic Labeling with Nearest Neighbor (NN) Algorithm:**
 - Poisson Reconstruction only provides geometric occupancy, not semantic labels.
 - **Sparse Semantic Voxels:** The original sparse LiDAR points with semantic labels are voxelized into sparse semantic voxels `V_s`.
 - **Label Propagation:** For each occupied voxel in the dense geometric occupancy `V_d`, the Nearest Neighbor (NN) algorithm is used to find the closest voxel in `V_s` and assign its semantic label. This efficiently propagates semantic information to the dense occupancy grid.

This comprehensive methodology, combining an advanced multi-camera feature fusion architecture with an innovative ground truth generation pipeline, forms the core of SurroundOcc's approach to dense 3D occupancy prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on nuScenes and SemanticKITTI datasets consistently demonstrate the superiority and effectiveness of the SurroundOcc method across various tasks and metrics.

#### 4.1. 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction on nuScenes

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** SurroundOcc achieves state-of-the-art results on the nuScenes validation set for both Scene Completion (SC IoU) and Semantic Scene Completion (SSC mIoU). It significantly outperforms a range of strong baselines including MonoScene, Atlas, BEVFormer, and TPVFormer.
- **Impact of Dense Ground Truth:** A notable finding is the performance of TPVFormer when trained with dense ground truth (TPVFormer*), which improves its performance but still falls short of SurroundOcc. This highlights that while dense supervision is crucial, SurroundOcc's architectural design provides additional advantages.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Visualizations demonstrate that SurroundOcc predicts much denser and more accurate occupancy compared to competitors. It effectively captures fine details and provides a more complete reconstruction of the scene.
- **Robustness in Challenging Conditions:** The method shows impressive robustness in difficult scenarios such as rainy days and nighttime, where RGB image quality degrades. This indicates the network's ability to extract meaningful features even from suboptimal visual input and leverage the dense supervision.
- **Prediction of Occluded Parts:** SurroundOcc is shown to successfully predict occluded geometries (e.g., the backside of a car) that are not visible to the LiDAR sensor, a key advantage of volumetric occupancy prediction over depth maps.

#### 4.2. Monocular 3D Semantic Scene Completion on SemanticKITTI

- **Outperforms Monocular Baselines:** Even though SurroundOcc is designed for multi-camera input, it achieves state-of-the-art performance on the monocular SemanticKITTI test set. This indicates the strength and generalizability of its core architecture, particularly its 2D-3D feature lifting and multi-scale processing, even when the cross-view attention mechanism is effectively reduced to a single view.

#### 4.3. 3D Scene Reconstruction

- **Superior Geometric Reconstruction:** When evaluated purely for geometric 3D scene reconstruction (without semantic labels), SurroundOcc again achieves state-of-the-art performance. It significantly outperforms advanced multi-camera and monocular depth estimation methods (SurroundDepth, AdaBins, NeWCRFs), as well as other 3D reconstruction methods (Atlas, TransformerFusion) on metrics like F-score and Chamfer Distance. This verifies its effectiveness in generating high-fidelity 3D geometry.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies clearly validate the design choices made in SurroundOcc:

- **2D-3D Spatial Attention:** Crucially, spatial attention significantly outperforms simply averaging multi-camera features. Furthermore, the proposed 3D-based cross-view attention is more effective than BEV-based attention, highlighting the importance of preserving 3D spatial information during feature lifting.
- **Multi-scale Occupancy Prediction:** Both the multi-scale U-Net structure (skip connections) and multi-level supervision contribute to improved performance. Skip connections aid in learning fine-grained details, while multi-level supervision enhances 3D volume features across all scales.
- **Dense Occupancy Supervision:** This ablation is particularly impactful. Models trained with the proposed dense occupancy ground truth demonstrate vastly superior performance compared to those trained with sparse LiDAR points or even sparse multi-frame occupancy labels. This unequivocally proves the necessity and effectiveness of the novel ground truth generation pipeline.

#### 4.5. Model Efficiency

- SurroundOcc maintains competitive inference time and memory usage compared to other efficient methods like BEVFormer and TPVFormer, indicating that its superior performance does not come at an exorbitant computational cost, making it practical for autonomous driving applications.

In summary, the results firmly establish SurroundOcc as a leading method for multi-camera 3D occupancy prediction, showcasing its accuracy, robustness, and the critical role of its dense ground truth generation pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SurroundOcc paper makes several significant contributions that could have a substantial impact on the field of autonomous driving and broader 3D scene understanding research:

- **Paradigm Shift in 3D Scene Understanding:** By advocating and demonstrating the effectiveness of dense 3D occupancy prediction, SurroundOcc pushes beyond the limitations of discrete 3D object detection and incomplete depth maps. This comprehensive volumetric representation offers a more complete, geometrically consistent, and semantically rich understanding of the environment, which is crucial for complex autonomous navigation tasks.
- **Addressing the Data Annotation Bottleneck:** The most impactful contribution might be the novel pipeline for generating dense 3D occupancy ground truth from existing sparse LiDAR and semantic labels. This ingenuity circumvents the prohibitively expensive and time-consuming process of manual dense annotation, which is a major bottleneck in 3D perception research. This methodology could be adopted by other researchers, accelerating progress in various volumetric perception tasks.
- **Enhanced Robustness for Autonomous Driving:** The demonstrated ability of SurroundOcc to predict fine details and complete shapes, even in challenging conditions like rain and night, and to infer occluded geometries, directly contributes to safer and more reliable autonomous driving systems. Understanding what's behind an obstacle or accurately perceiving the environment in adverse weather is critical for planning and decision-making.
- **Flexible and Extendable Representation:** Dense 3D occupancy is a highly versatile representation. Its inherent structure makes it a strong foundation for a multitude of downstream tasks in autonomous driving, including 3D semantic segmentation, motion prediction, scene flow estimation, and advanced path planning. This could simplify system architectures by providing a unified perception output.
- **Advancement of Vision-Centric Perception:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance solely based on multi-camera images, SurroundOcc strengthens the case for vision-centric autonomous driving systems. Relying on cost-effective cameras rather than expensive LiDAR sensors can significantly reduce the overall cost of autonomous vehicles, facilitating wider adoption and deployment.
- **Methodological Innovations:** The 2D-3D spatial attention mechanism provides an effective way to lift multi-camera 2D features to a coherent 3D volumetric representation, outperforming simpler fusion strategies. The multi-scale U-Net architecture further ensures that both high-level context and fine-grained details are captured, crucial for dense predictions.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The authors' commitment to releasing their code and the generated dataset will greatly benefit the research community. This allows for reproducibility, benchmarking, and serves as a strong foundation for future research and development in dense 3D occupancy prediction.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also lays out clear future work, such as extending to occupancy flow prediction for understanding temporal dynamics and exploring self-supervised occupancy prediction to further reduce reliance on LiDAR data even for ground truth generation. This demonstrates a thoughtful roadmap for continued impact.

In conclusion, SurroundOcc represents a significant step forward in vision-based 3D scene understanding for autonomous driving. By delivering dense, accurate, and semantically rich 3D occupancy predictions through innovative architectural design and a clever ground truth generation pipeline, it offers a more comprehensive and robust perception capability with substantial practical implications for the development and deployment of autonomous vehicles.
