# RLMM-Flow: A Flow-based Mobile Manipulation Framework with Latent-Space Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> RLMM-Flow is a two-stage framework for mobile manipulation that combines a pretrained flow-based generative policy with latent-space reinforcement learning. It significantly improves trajectory quality, achieving higher task success rates and reduced collisions on complex tasks in both simulated and real-world environments by employing specialized stable post-training strategies.

## Problem

- Imitation-learned generative policies for mobile manipulation are limited by the distribution of their training data and cannot improve or discover superior behaviors beyond expert demonstrations.
- Directly applying reinforcement learning to multi-step generative policies, such as flow models, is challenging due to complexities in likelihood evaluation, credit assignment, and stable gradient propagation.
- Latent-space reinforcement learning for mobile manipulation faces difficulties with high-dimensional latent variables, heterogeneous mobile base and manipulator motions, and instability when co-training action-space and latent-space critics.

## Method

- A two-stage framework is proposed, first pretraining a ==flow policy== on expert demonstrations to acquire multimodal ==whole-body motion priors== for coordinated base and manipulator actions.
- The frozen flow policy is then enhanced by a **latent steering network**, which is optimized through ==reinforcement learning== to improve the quality of generated trajectories by steering the initial latent noise.
- Novel stable post-training strategies, including an ==action-critic warm-up== phase and ==coarse-to-fine latent steering== with a ==cascaded architecture==, are introduced to address high-dimensional latent optimization challenges.

## Results

- RLMM-Flow achieved superior performance in simulation, demonstrating up to **46%** success rate (seen tasks) and **38%** (unseen tasks), with significantly lower collision rates (**12%** seen, **32%** unseen) and joint violations (**8%** seen, **12%** unseen) compared to baselines.
- The framework successfully performed zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical mobile manipulator, achieving a **32%** success rate, **9%** collision rate, and **6%** joint violation rate, while maintaining a fast inference time of **0.21s**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the action-critic warm-up and the coarse-to-fine latent steering strategies were critical for improved stability, higher reward gains, and better constraint satisfaction during reinforcement learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured coarse-to-fine latent steering approach, coupled with a cascaded architecture, effectively manages high-dimensional latent spaces and explicitly models the dependency between mobile base and manipulator motions.
> - Warming up the action critic before joint training provides a stable and reliable value target for latent-space optimization, preventing early-stage instability and improving overall training convergence.
> - Utilizing a mixed-quality offline dataset for reinforcement learning post-training broadens the critic's value coverage, enabling robust optimization for task-specific rewards beyond the limitations of purely expert demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobile manipulation is a research area that involves coordinating the movements of a mobile base and a robotic manipulator to achieve tasks in complex 3D environments. This coordination must simultaneously satisfy multiple constraints, including goal reaching, collision avoidance, base kinematic limitations, manipulator joint limits, and trajectory smoothness.

Historically, classical motion planners have addressed these challenges by enforcing explicit constraints, but they typically require accurate models and task-specific objective functions. More recently, generative policies have emerged as an alternative, capable of learning multimodal and temporally consistent action chunks from expert demonstrations. Among these, flow-matching techniques are noted for their efficiency in generating trajectories by transporting simple noise distributions to complex action distributions.

However, a primary limitation of imitation-only generative policies is their inability to improve generated motions beyond the distribution of the provided demonstration data. This means they may struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios or fail to discover more optimal solutions. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a paradigm for optimizing planning quality through reward signals. Direct application of RL to multi-step generative policies, such as flow models, presents difficulties. These challenges stem from the complexities of likelihood evaluation, policy-ratio estimation, credit assignment, and stable gradient propagation through the continuous transport process inherent in flow-based models.

Latent-space policy improvement is a related approach that seeks to adapt frozen generative policies by steering their initial noise or latent representations. While this method can bypass some of the difficulties of direct policy optimization, its application to mobile manipulation introduces additional complexities. These include the high-dimensional nature of the latent variable representing a full-horizon action chunk, the heterogeneous roles of mobile base and manipulator motions, and the instability that can arise when simultaneously training action-space and latent-space critics, where the latent critic's early target derived from an unstable action critic can hinder convergence.

This work, RLMM-Flow, positions itself by addressing these specific challenges. It aims to combine the efficiency and multimodal nature of flow-based generative policies (learned through expert imitation) with the optimization capabilities of latent-space reinforcement learning. The paper specifically targets the stability issues in high-dimensional latent optimization that arise in mobile manipulation by introducing novel training strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for whole-body mobile manipulation that can generate coordinated base-arm trajectories satisfying complex constraints while overcoming the limitations of existing generative policy methods. The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Overcoming Imitation Learning Limitations**: The research is motivated by the observation that flow-based generative policies, while effective at learning motion priors from expert demonstrations and offering efficient inference, are inherently limited by the quality and distribution of their training data. These policies cannot discover behaviors superior to those demonstrated or improve performance beyond what is observed in the expert dataset. The objective is to enable policies to self-improve and optimize for desired task-specific rewards.

2. **Addressing Challenges in Reinforcement Learning for Generative Policies**: Directly applying reinforcement learning to complex, multi-step generative models, such as diffusion or flow policies, is challenging. The continuous nature of the transport process in flow models makes tasks like likelihood evaluation, policy-ratio estimation, and stable gradient propagation difficult compared to conventional single-step policies. The motivation is to find an alternative method for integrating reward-driven optimization without requiring direct modification or backpropagation through the entire generative policy.

3. **Stabilizing Latent-Space Reinforcement Learning for Mobile Manipulation**: Latent-space policy improvement, where a frozen generator's initial noise is steered by a separate network, offers a promising approach to the aforementioned challenge. However, applying this to mobile manipulation introduces its own set of difficulties:
 - **High-Dimensional Latent Spaces**: The latent variable must represent a high-dimensional, full-horizon action chunk for coordinated base and arm movements, making its direct optimization complex and prone to instability.
 - **Heterogeneous Motion Roles**: The mobile base and manipulator have distinct roles and kinematic properties, necessitating a structured approach to latent variable representation and optimization that captures their interdependencies.
 - **Critic Instability**: In early training stages, if both action-space and latent-space critics are updated simultaneously, the latent critic's learning target (derived from the action critic) can be unreliable, leading to oscillating or non-convergent optimization.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines three specific contributions, which serve as detailed objectives:
- To propose a flow-based framework that integrates expert pretraining with latent-space reinforcement learning for whole-body mobile manipulation.
- To develop a cascaded latent steering architecture that explicitly models the dependency between global base motion and local manipulator motion by predicting base-related latent variables before arm-related variables.
- To introduce a stable post-training strategy incorporating action-critic warm-up and coarse-to-fine latent steering to stabilize high-dimensional latent optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLMM-Flow is a two-stage framework designed for mobile manipulation, combining a pretrained flow policy with latent-space reinforcement learning.

**Stage 1: Flow Policy Pretraining**
The initial phase involves training a flow policy from expert demonstrations to acquire a multimodal prior for whole-body motions.
- **Input Representation**: The policy takes a robot-centric point cloud as input. This point cloud comprises robot-body points, target points representing the desired end-effector pose, and scene points describing the environment. Each point includes 3D coordinates and a type feature. This input is encoded by a Point Transformer and fused with the current robot state, task goal, and a flow-time embedding.
- **Action Chunk Representation**: Whole-body action chunks are represented as fixed-horizon sequences (H=50 waypoints) of mobile base (x, y, θ) and 6-DOF manipulator joint configurations. The first waypoint is anchored to the current robot configuration for continuity.
- **Flow Policy Architecture**: A Transformer-based conditional flow architecture is used. Given the observation, a noisy action chunk, and flow-time embedding, it outputs a velocity field that defines a continuous transport from a Gaussian noise distribution to the expert whole-body trajectories.
- **Training**: Rectified flow is employed for training. A logit-normal schedule for flow-time sampling is utilized, which emphasizes intermediate-noise states, contributing to the learning of coordinated high-dimensional whole-body action chunks.

**Stage 2: Latent-Space Reinforcement Learning Post-training**
After pretraining, the flow policy is frozen, and a latent steering network is optimized using reinforcement learning to improve the quality of generated trajectories.
- **Components**:
 - **Frozen Flow Policy (π_W^dp)**: Acts as a fixed generator, decoding latent variables into action chunks.
 - **Latent Steering Network (π_W)**: A Gaussian steering network that takes the scene representation as input and outputs the mean (μ_W(s)) and log variance (log σ_W(s)) of a diagonal Gaussian distribution. A latent sample (w_raw) is drawn using the reparameterization trick.
 - **Action Critic (Q_A)**: Evaluates the quality of decoded whole-body action chunks. It receives the robot-centric observation and the generated action chunk, processes them through Transformer layers, and outputs a scalar value.
 - **Latent Critic (Q_W)**: Evaluates the value of the latent variables. It shares a similar value-estimation structure with Q_A but operates in the latent space.
- **Reward Formulation**: A trajectory-level reward r(τ) is defined to evaluate various aspects of the generated whole-body trajectory. It comprises five normalized and clipped components:
 - `r_goal`: Measures terminal end-effector position and orientation error using gripper collision spheres.
 - `r_lim`: Penalizes violations of nonholonomic base kinematic constraints (e.g., lateral slip, excessive curvature).
 - `r_colli`: Penalizes collision risks using collision spheres and environment ESDFs.
 - `r_smooth`: Encourages smooth whole-body motion by penalizing second-order finite differences of collision sphere trajectories.
 - `r_joint`: Penalizes manipulator joint angles exceeding their physical limits.
 These components are scaled and weighted (e.g., `α_1` and `α_5` for goal and joint limit are 2.5, `α_2` for base limit is 2.0, `α_3` and `α_4` for collision and smoothness are 1.5).
- **Mixed-Quality Offline Dataset (D_RL)**: Unlike flow-policy pretraining that uses only high-quality expert demonstrations, RL post-training leverages a dataset comprising both high-quality and structured suboptimal trajectories. This strategy broadens the value coverage for the critics, providing supervision for lower-value actions and mitigating critic overestimation.
- **Stable Post-training Strategies**: Two key strategies are introduced to stabilize high-dimensional latent optimization:
 1. **Action-Critic Warm-up**: Before jointly training Q_W and π_W, the action critic Q_A undergoes a dedicated warm-up phase (N_pre steps). During this phase, Q_A is updated while Q_W and π_W are kept frozen. The next latent for Q_A target computation is sampled from a fixed standard Gaussian prior. This ensures Q_A develops a relatively stable action-value estimate, providing a more reliable target for latent-value distillation subsequently.
 2. **Coarse-to-Fine Latent Steering**:
 - **Cascaded Architecture**: The latent steering network uses a cascaded Transformer architecture. It first predicts base-related latent components, then conditions the prediction of arm-related components on the base prediction, capturing their dependency.
 - **Horizon-Shared Coarse Control**: In early training (before switch step t_cf), the raw latent (w_raw) is averaged along the horizon dimension, and this averaged vector is repeated across the horizon to form `w_coarse`. This `w_coarse` (with a lower effective dimension) is used, limiting the actor to learn a global motion trend.
 - **Time-Dependent Residual Refinement**: After t_cf, a time-dependent residual component (`w_res = w_raw - w_coarse`) is activated and added to `w_coarse` with a scaling factor (λ_res). This allows for high-dimensional local corrections while preserving the global trend learned earlier.
- **Training Objectives**:
 - Q_A is trained via a standard Bellman update, using a target network (`¯Q_A`).
 - Q_W is trained through value distillation, minimizing the difference between its estimate and the `sg[Q_A(s, a_w)]`, where `a_w` is the action decoded from latent `w`. A mixed distribution (`q_t(w|s)`) is used for sampling latents for Q_W to ensure exploration around both the prior and the current actor distribution.
 - π_W is updated by maximizing `Q_W(s, w_π)`, where `w_π` is the latent output from the coarse-to-fine steering, facilitating gradient propagation through the reparameterization trick.
- **Inference**: The optimized latent noise from the latent steering network is fed into the frozen flow policy to generate the final whole-body action chunk.
- **Real-world Deployment**: Policies are transferred directly from simulation ("zero-shot") to a physical mobile manipulator without any real-world fine-tuning. A low-level MPC controller tracks the generated trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated RLMM-Flow against baseline generative policies and offline RL methods on simulated "seen" tasks, "unseen" tasks (for generalization), and real-world deployments. Key metrics included success rate, collision rate, joint-limit violation rate, trajectory smoothness, inference time, base path length, and arm joint path length.

**Pretrained Generative Policies Comparison:**
- The pretrained Base Flow policy demonstrated superior safety (lower collision and joint violation rates) and smoothness compared to M2Diffuser without test-time optimization.
- Base Flow also maintained a significantly faster inference time (0.18s) than M2Diffuser without optimization (0.56s) and especially M2Diffuser with test-time optimization (3.89s).
- Test-time optimization for M2Diffuser improved performance but incurred a substantial increase in latency, indicating that Base Flow offered a more favorable quality-efficiency trade-off as a foundation for subsequent RL.

**Offline Reinforcement Learning Post-training:**
- **Action-space RL (AWR, IQL)**: These methods, which directly optimize in the full action-chunk space, yielded limited improvements over the Base Flow. In some cases, they negatively affected motion smoothness, suggesting that direct updates in high-dimensional action spaces can disrupt the coherence learned during flow pretraining.
- **DSRL-NA (Latent-space RL baseline)**: This baseline, which operates in a compact, horizon-shared latent space, demonstrated better overall performance than action-space RL methods. However, it still faced challenges related to stability due to simultaneous training of critics.
- **RLMM-Flow-QA (with Q_A warm-up)**: Incorporating the action-critic warm-up significantly improved stability and performance over DSRL-NA. It showed higher success rates (42% vs. 40% seen), lower collision rates (18% vs. 21% seen), and fewer joint violations (9% vs. 14% seen), indicating the benefit of a stable critic target.
- **RLMM-Flow-CF (with coarse-to-fine steering)**: This ablation, focusing on staged latent exploration, achieved the lowest collision rates among the ablations (15% seen), demonstrating its effectiveness in addressing constraint satisfaction.
- **RLMM-Flow (combined Q_A warm-up and coarse-to-fine steering)**: The full RLMM-Flow framework consistently outperformed all baselines and ablations across almost all metrics in both seen and unseen simulation environments.
 - It achieved the highest success rates (46% seen, 38% unseen).
 - It obtained the lowest collision rates (12% seen, 32% unseen) and joint violation rates (8% seen, 12% unseen).
 - It maintained good smoothness and motion efficiency (base and arm path lengths) while preserving fast inference times (0.21s).
 - The combined approach showed robust generalization to novel environments.

**Qualitative Observations:**
- Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) indicated that RLMM-Flow produced more coordinated base-arm motions and maintained safer obstacle clearance compared to Base Flow and action-space RL methods.
- Representative rollouts in cluttered scenes (Figure 5) further illustrated the framework's ability to navigate and manipulate effectively.

**Ablation and Stability Analysis:**
- **Q_A Warm-up**: Reward curves (Figure 6) and latent-critic loss (Figure 7) demonstrated that the Q_A warm-up led to steadier reward improvement and a more stable latent-critic training loss, validating its role in providing a consistent supervision signal.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Steering**: The reward curve for this variant showed initial improvement from the coarse stage, followed by a further boost after the fine-grained residual branch was activated, confirming the efficacy of progressive latent space control.

**Real-World Deployment:**
- Real-world experiments confirmed the simulation trends. RLMM-Flow achieved the highest success rate (32%), lowest collision rate (9%), and lowest joint violation rate (6%) among all methods tested.
- The framework successfully transferred from simulation to the real world without additional fine-tuning, maintaining its fast inference speed (0.21s).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RLMM-Flow represents an advancement in the field of mobile manipulation, specifically in the generation of complex, constrained whole-body trajectories.

**Technical Advancements in Generative Policy Optimization:**
The work offers a concrete methodology for augmenting the capabilities of imitation-learned generative policies through reinforcement learning. By combining efficient flow-based pretraining with latent-space RL, RLMM-Flow mitigates the inherent limitations of pure imitation learning, allowing policies to optimize for specific reward signals and improve beyond the demonstration data distribution. The introduced stable post-training strategies—action-critic warm-up and coarse-to-fine latent steering—address critical stability challenges in applying RL to high-dimensional latent spaces of generative models. These strategies provide a blueprint for more reliable and convergent training of such complex systems, which could be applicable to other domains beyond robotics where generative models are used for action or trajectory synthesis.

**Enhanced Robot Performance and Robustness:**
RLMM-Flow demonstrates substantial improvements in key performance metrics, including higher task success rates, significantly reduced collisions, fewer joint-limit violations, and smoother, more efficient trajectories. These improvements are achieved while maintaining fast inference times, which is crucial for real-time robotic operations. The framework's ability to coordinate heterogeneous base and arm motions effectively, guided by a multi-component reward, contributes to more dexterous and safe robot behaviors in cluttered environments.

**Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
The successful zero-shot transfer of RLMM-Flow policies from simulation to real-world hardware without any additional real-world fine-tuning is a notable achievement. This capability significantly reduces the cost and time associated with deploying complex robotic skills, as it bypasses the need for extensive real-world data collection and iterative tuning. This contributes to making advanced mobile manipulation more accessible and practical for real-world applications.

**Broadening Generalization Capabilities:**
The framework's consistent performance improvements in both "seen" and "unseen" simulated scenes, coupled with its real-world effectiveness, suggest an enhanced capacity for generalization. This implies that robots powered by RLMM-Flow could operate more reliably in novel or partially unknown environments, adapting to variations in scene layouts and obstacle configurations.

**Potential Applications:**
The impact of RLMM-Flow could be significant in various sectors requiring advanced mobile manipulation. These include, but are not limited to, industrial automation (e.g., in unstructured warehouse environments), service robotics (e.g., for elderly care or domestic tasks), logistics, and hazardous environment operations. By enabling robots to perform complex tasks more safely, efficiently, and robustly, RLMM-Flow contributes to the development of more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems, pushing closer to the deployment of general-purpose mobile manipulators.
