# Radar4D-VLM: Proposal-Grounded Temporal 4D Radar Reasoning Across Frozen Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Radar4D-VLM is a radar-only temporal vision-language model designed for reasoning from ten consecutive 4D-radar point-cloud sweeps, establishing a robust perceptual interface for autonomous driving. The research indicates that aligned language supervision did not provide a statistically stable gain in the core radar representation's predictive utility.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving perception systems degrade in adverse weather conditions (fog, rain, snow) where camera and LiDAR sensors struggle.
- Four-dimensional (4D) radar data is sparse and non-uniform, making it challenging to extract object-level semantics and complex motion for integration with language models.
- Prior radar-language integration efforts often lacked rigorous methods to attribute whether language outputs reflected genuine sensor utilization or merely interface compatibility.

## Method

- A radar-only temporal VLM architecture, **Radar4D-VLM**, processes ten consecutive 4D radar sweeps to infer scene and motion understanding.
- ==Hierarchical tokenization== (64 object, 4 scene, 1 kinematic token) organizes complex temporal radar evidence into a structured representation for fine-grained and global understanding.
- A ==parameter-efficient low-rank projector== maps radar-derived tokens to the embedding space of frozen language backbones, while parallel ==direct prediction heads== offer an auditable, language-independent evaluation.

## Results

- RTNH-compatible proposal encoder achieved **98.13%** recall for vehicle-region targets at 4 meters, outperforming fixed-lattice controls by **6.40** percentage points.
- Strict-zero radar input reduced core balanced accuracy by **0.1370** on the direct path, while scene-local shuffling caused a **0.1703** reduction, highlighting sensor dependence.
- Proposal-selected temporal tokens outperformed fixed-grid tokens by **0.0397** core balanced accuracy units on the direct path, underscoring the value of object-centric representation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligned language supervision did not provide a statistically significant improvement in the direct-head predictive utility of the shared radar representation, with 95% confidence intervals spanning zero.
> - The model's performance critically depends on actual radar content and its temporal ordering, demonstrating substantial performance drops when radar input is zeroed, shuffled, or history is reversed.
> - A structured, proposal-grounded 69-token representation of temporal 4D radar is compatible with diverse frozen language models and outperforms globally pooled or fixed-grid tokenization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems require robust perception capabilities that maintain reliability across diverse environmental conditions. While cameras and LiDAR sensors offer rich appearance and geometric information, their performance can significantly degrade in adverse weather conditions such as fog, rain, or snow. Four-dimensional (4D) millimeter-wave radar, in contrast, remains largely operational under these conditions and provides direct measurements of radial velocity, a crucial parameter for motion understanding. However, 4D radar point clouds are typically sparse, non-uniform, and pose challenges for direct connection to object-level semantics and complex motion reasoning.

The integration of radar data with language models is an emerging field, with several recent works exploring different facets. The Radar Spectra–Language Model (Pushkareva et al. 2024) focuses on aligning raw automotive radar spectra with a vision–language embedding space for retrieval and scene parsing. Talk2Radar (Guan et al. 2025) investigates language as an input query for referring-expression grounding within 4D radar point clouds. RLM (Mishra, Bansal, and Bharadia 2025) learns scene-level radar representations using simulated radar–caption pairs and structured spatial supervision. Hamilton and Heckman (2026) map K-Radar features through a frozen vision–language model for structured captioning, and RadarLLM (Lai et al. 2026) studies temporal millimeter-wave point clouds for human-motion understanding. These prior efforts demonstrate the feasibility of connecting radar and language but leave open questions regarding effective tokenization of temporal automotive radar into object-, scene-, and motion-aware representations. Furthermore, they do not consistently address the attribution challenge of whether successful language outputs reflect genuine sensor utilization, interface compatibility, or a direct benefit from aligned language supervision.

The development of general vision–language models (e.g., BLIP-2) and 3D-language models (e.g., 3D-LLM, LL3DA, Point Cloud as a Foreign Language) provides architectural context for connecting diverse modalities to large language models (LLMs). Driving-specific language research also exists, including question-answering benchmarks like NuScenes-QA and frameworks for structured reasoning and planning like DriveLM. These models emphasize the need for object-aware tokens, local–global structure, and rigorous grounding-specific evaluation. However, the unique characteristics of radar data, particularly its sparsity and direct velocity measurement, necessitate specialized approaches for effective integration with language models within the autonomous driving context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce Radar4D-VLM, a radar-only temporal vision–language model designed for reasoning from ten consecutive 4D-radar point-cloud sweeps, without reliance on camera or LiDAR input. This model aims to establish a robust perceptual interface that leverages 4D radar’s strengths, particularly its resilience to adverse visibility and direct measurement of radial velocity, to overcome the limitations of other sensor modalities.

The motivation for Radar4D-VLM stems from several key aspects:
1. **Addressing Adverse Weather Limitations:** Conventional autonomous driving perception often degrades in challenging weather. The research seeks to exploit 4D radar's inherent robustness in such conditions to enable reliable perception when other sensors are compromised.
2. **Bridging Radar Data and Language Models:** Despite the potential of 4D radar, its sparse and non-uniform data format makes it challenging to integrate with language models for high-level semantic and motion reasoning. A key motivation is to develop an interface that organizes complex temporal radar evidence into a structured format accessible to LLMs.
3. **Structured Radar Representation:** The authors aim to extract geometrically grounded object proposals from radar data and organize this evidence into a compact, hierarchical set of tokens (object, scene, and kinematic). This structure is intended to facilitate both fine-grained and global understanding of the driving environment.
4. **Parameter-Efficient Integration:** The model employs a parameter-efficient projector to map these radar-derived tokens into frozen language backbones, promoting adaptability across different LLM architectures without extensive retraining of the LLMs themselves.
5. **Joint Modeling of Driving-Relevant Attributes:** Radar4D-VLM is motivated to jointly model various crucial aspects of a driving scene, including object count, spatial distribution, motion state, collision risk, semantic category, and radial velocity, through auditable prediction heads.
6. **Controlled Attribution and Causal Interpretation:** A central motivation is to rigorously evaluate whether successful language outputs are a result of interface compatibility, actual radar data utilization, or a direct improvement from aligned language supervision. This is critical for advancing the understanding of how multimodal objectives interact and for avoiding "shortcut learning" where models leverage unintended cues. The research specifically aims to disentangle these factors through controlled experimental comparisons involving aligned, permuted, and no-language objectives, alongside radar input interventions.

In summary, the work aims to propose a novel radar-only VLM architecture that robustly processes temporal 4D radar data for autonomous driving, while employing a rigorous experimental methodology to attribute model capabilities to specific components and training objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Radar4D-VLM is designed to process ten consecutive 4D-radar sweeps (from $t-9$ to $t$) to infer scene and motion understanding. The core methodology involves transforming raw radar data into a shared physical state represented by structured tokens, which then feed both language-independent prediction heads and a frozen language backbone.

#### 3.1. Temporal 4D Radar Input and Proposal Generation

The model takes ten sweeps, $X_{t-9:t}$, as input. The current sweep, $X_t$, is fed into a frozen, train-split RTNH-compatible sparse-convolutional encoder. This encoder, trained on a subset of the K-Radar dataset, generates Top-64 proposal centers per frame, along with proposal features and priors, and a bird’s-eye-view (BEV) feature map. These proposal outputs are center hypotheses, not final detections.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Radar Tokenization

The system organizes radar evidence into a hierarchy of tokens:
- **Object Tokens (64 tokens):** For each of the 64 proposals, an object tokenizer combines its frozen semantic feature ($f^{sem}_i$), normalized center and score geometry ($g_i$), frozen class/box/direction priors ($f^{prior}_i$), and a learned temporal Doppler descriptor ($d_i$). The Doppler branch is crucial for associating nearby radar returns across the ten sweeps, capturing displacement, local radial velocity, range slope, and Doppler-alias residuals. The combined features are normalized and transformed into object tokens $o_{t,i}$.
- **Scene Tokens (4 tokens):** Global context is processed separately. A projected BEV grid, augmented with positional embeddings, forms a context representation ($C_t$). Four learned queries ($Q_s$) interact with this context through a Multi-Head Attention (MHA) mechanism to produce four scene tokens ($S_t$).
- **Kinematic Token (1 token):** This token summarizes motion across the scene. Proposal weights ($\alpha_i$) are calculated based on predicted motion probability, proposal confidence, and range. The kinematic token is then formed by aggregating the weighted object states ($\sum_i \alpha_i o_{t,i}$) and embedding various motion statistics such as weighted range, signed/absolute velocity, range slope, local Doppler, and moving-probability statistics.

These 64 object, 4 scene, and 1 kinematic token are concatenated to form a shared 69-token state representation, $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{69 \times 256}$.

#### 3.3. Dual Output Paths and Prediction Heads

The shared state $H_t$ drives two synchronized output pathways:
- **Direct Prediction Heads:** A learned state query attends over all 69 tokens to obtain a global representation ($z_t$). This $z_t$ then feeds multiple direct categorical and velocity heads. These heads predict specific driving-scene ontology attributes: moving-object count, nearest object’s sector, motion, collision risk, semantic category, and signed radial velocity. This path provides an auditable, language-independent evaluation of the radar representation.
- **Frozen Language Interface:** In parallel, a budgeted low-rank projector ($P$) maps each of the 69 radar tokens to the embedding dimension of a frozen language backbone (e.g., Qwen, Phi, Mistral, Llama, Gemma). The projected radar prefix ($P(H_t)$) is concatenated with the native token embeddings of a task prompt, enabling the frozen LLM to generate closed-set answers. The trainable parameters of this projector are capped at 1.2 million.

#### 3.4. Task and Evaluation Endpoints

The model predicts a fixed driving-scene ontology rather than unrestricted language. Tasks include moving object count, nearest object's sector, motion state, collision risk, semantic category (auxiliary), speed bin, and signed radial velocity. Macro balanced accuracy, averaging class-wise recall, is used for categorical tasks, while smooth-L1 loss is used for signed velocity regression. "Radar-only" signifies that all model inputs and inference exclude camera, LiDAR, text, tracks, and ground-truth boxes, although dataset tracks supply supervision during training.

#### 3.5. Training and Controlled Objectives

The training objective is a composite loss function:
$L = \lambda_{lang} L_{lang} + \lambda_{task} L_{task} + \lambda_{vel} L_{vel} + \lambda_{mot} L_{mot} + \lambda_{cat} L_{cat} + \lambda_{pvel} L_{pvel}$.
- $L_{lang}$: Mean answer-token negative log-likelihood for the language path.
- $L_{task}$: Categorical cross-entropy for active scene tasks from direct heads.
- $L_{vel}$: Smooth-L1 loss for signed nearest-target velocity.
- $L_{mot}$, $L_{cat}$, $L_{pvel}$: Proposal-level supervision for target match, category, and velocity.

Crucially, the study employs controlled training conditions to analyze the impact of language supervision:
- **Aligned Condition:** Uses canonical task verbalizers for $L_{lang}$.
- **Fixed Permutation Condition:** Preserves task identity and answer-set size but maps each ground-truth label to an incorrect answer string, maintaining the language forward path.
- **No-Language Condition:** Sets $\lambda_{lang} = 0$, ensuring zero projector gradients and removing the language objective.
All conditions maintain the same radar architecture, direct heads, data windows, and optimization budget, enabling a comparison of direct-head performance independently of language path agreement.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

The K-Radar dataset is used, with sequences 1–40 for training and 41–48 for development validation. A fixed set of 4,208 ten-frame windows from the validation sequences is used for all reported results. Metrics are primarily macro balanced accuracy and signed-velocity error. Statistical analysis includes equal-sequence paired effects and crossed seed–sequence bootstrap intervals to provide robust interpretations. Audit questions include proposal geometry effectiveness, interface compatibility across LLM families, sensor dependence (using interventions like radar zeroing, shuffling, history reversal, Doppler removal), and the core question of language supervision value on direct-head performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings, particularly concerning the utility of 4D radar for VLM, the role of language supervision, and the compatibility of the proposed token interface.

#### 4.1. Proposal Geometry and Target Representation

The RTNH-compatible proposal encoder effectively extracts target geometry. The Top-64 center proposals achieved 98.13% recall at 4 meters on vehicle-region targets in the K-Radar validation set. This performance exceeded that of fixed-lattice controls by 6.40 percentage points and uniform-random controls by 22.83 percentage points. The mean nearest-target distance for selected proposals was 1.057 m, compared to 2.426 m for the lattice. This indicates that the proposal mechanism effectively localizes relevant target evidence.

#### 4.2. Language Supervision Value (Primary Attribution Audit)

A central finding is a bounded non-observation of a stable gain from aligned language supervision on the shared radar representation. When comparing direct-head core balanced accuracy (independent of language output) across aligned, fixed-permutation, and no-language objectives for Qwen2.5-3B:
- The "aligned minus permuted" effect was -0.0052 (95% CI: [-0.0305, 0.0220]).
- The "aligned minus no language" effect was -0.0024 (95% CI: [-0.0231, 0.0188]).
Both 95% confidence intervals spanned zero, suggesting no statistically significant improvement in direct-head predictive utility attributable to aligned language supervision in this setting. The upper bounds (approx. 2.20 and 1.88 percentage points) indicate that minor positive effects cannot be entirely ruled out.

#### 4.3. Radar Dependence and Temporal Order

Despite the non-observation of direct language supervision benefit, the aligned models demonstrated clear dependence on radar content and temporal order. For aligned Qwen2.5-3B checkpoints:
- **Strict-zero radar** significantly reduced core balanced accuracy by 0.1215 on the language path and 0.1370 on the direct path.
- **Scene-local shuffling** (randomizing radar input order) led to reductions of 0.1532 (language path) and 0.1703 (direct path).
- **Reversing the ten-sweep history** caused smaller but consistent reductions of 0.0249 (language path) and 0.0277 (direct path).
- **Zeroing Doppler information** had a comparatively smaller and non-significant effect, with a language-path reduction of 0.0101 (95% CI crossing zero).
These interventions showed that the model actively uses radar data and its temporal sequence, and this sensor dependence was robust across permuted and no-language objectives for the direct heads. The learned direct heads achieved a core balanced accuracy of 0.5018, outperforming the deterministic RTNH+physics reference (0.3401) and macro chance (0.2833).

#### 4.4. Backbone Compatibility

The 69-token radar interface exhibited compatibility across all eight audited frozen language models from five families (Qwen, Phi, Mistral, Llama, Gemma). Language-path core balanced accuracy across all 24 matched runs (8 backbones x 3 seeds) fell within a narrow range of 0.4860–0.4995. This indicates that the token structure is consumable by various LLMs without demonstrating a monotonic trend with increasing LLM size or a definitive "best" family.

#### 4.5. Structural Ablations

Ablation studies provided insights into the utility of different architectural components:
- **Temporal History:** Replacing ten sweeps with only the current sweep consistently lowered core balanced accuracy by approximately 0.0552 on the language path and 0.0516 on the direct path, highlighting the importance of temporal context.
- **Doppler Feature:** Removing Doppler information resulted in a smaller direct-path reduction of 0.0119, with mixed effects on the language path, suggesting it is a less dominant cue than temporal history.
- **Token Structure:** Distributed 69-token representation was found to be superior. Replacing the 69 structured tokens with one globally pooled token resulted in a substantial degradation of performance (e.g., from 0.4889 to 0.3493 on the language path).
- **Proposal-Selected vs. Fixed Grid Tokens:** Proposal-selected temporal tokens outperformed fixed-grid temporal tokens by 0.0456 core balanced accuracy units on the language path and 0.0397 on the direct path, under equal-sequence pairing, indicating the value of object-centric proposals.
- **Kinematic Token:** Removing the kinematic token yielded smaller or mixed effects.

#### 4.6. LLM-Free Direct Inference

The models trained with the no-language objective could be exported for LLM-free direct inference. These exports maintained core balanced accuracy (mean 0.5014) within a registered GPU-nondeterminism envelope, demonstrating a simplified deployment path without the full language model stack.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Observations

Qualitative examples illustrated that the proposal interface successfully associates radar returns with tracked vehicles even in adverse conditions like fog and snow. Language responses, while fluent, could be partially correct or mismatched, emphasizing the need for disaggregated scoring of ontology fields.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Radar4D-VLM research contributes to the field of autonomous driving perception and multimodal AI in several ways, with implications for both research methodology and practical deployment.

#### 5.1. Methodological Advancements in Radar-Language Integration

The paper introduces a novel architecture that effectively integrates temporal 4D radar data with frozen language models. By combining proposal-grounded temporal object tokenization, global scene context, and an explicit kinematic token within a unified interface, Radar4D-VLM provides a structured approach to representing complex radar information. This is significant for making sparse and challenging 4D radar data accessible for high-level semantic and motion reasoning, which has traditionally been difficult.

#### 5.2. Rigorous Attribution for Multimodal Models

A key contribution is the development and application of a controlled attribution audit. By systematically comparing aligned, fixed-permutation, and no-language objectives while keeping other factors constant, the research rigorously tests the causal impact of language supervision. This methodology provides a framework for distinguishing between interface compatibility, sensor dependence, and actual representational improvement stemming from language supervision. This approach addresses a critical challenge in multimodal AI, where simply achieving plausible language outputs may not reflect an improved underlying representation or a genuine benefit from the language objective. The finding that aligned language supervision did not yield a stable gain in direct-head predictive utility challenges common assumptions and prompts a re-evaluation of how language objectives influence multimodal representations.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Robustness for Autonomous Driving

By demonstrating robust radar-only reasoning from temporal 4D radar sweeps, Radar4D-VLM contributes to the development of autonomous driving systems that are more resilient to adverse weather conditions. This capability is crucial for improving safety and reliability where camera and LiDAR performance is degraded. The confirmed dependence on radar content and temporal order underscores the model's ability to leverage these specific properties for perception.

#### 5.4. Practical Deployment Considerations

The ability to export LLM-free direct inference models, which retain performance within bounded drift, offers practical implications for deployment. This simplifies the computational and memory requirements, potentially enabling the use of these radar-based perception capabilities in resource-constrained autonomous vehicle platforms.

#### 5.5. Guiding Future Research

The results, particularly the bounded non-observation of direct language supervision benefit, provide guidance for future research. They suggest that further investigation is needed into how language objectives truly enhance sensor representations, rather than merely facilitating compatible output formats. Future efforts may focus on designing language supervision strategies that demonstrably improve underlying feature learning for radar or other sparse modalities. The finding also encourages the separate testing and reporting of interface compatibility, sensor dependence, and language supervision benefits in VLM research.
