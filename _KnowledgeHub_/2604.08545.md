# Act Wisely: Cultivating Meta-Cognitive Tool Use in Agentic Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba Group and Huazhong University of Science and Technology introduce the Hierarchical Decoupled Policy Optimization (HDPO) framework to enable agentic multimodal models to strategically arbitrate between internal knowledge and external tools. Their model, Metis, significantly reduces unnecessary tool invocations (e.g., from 98% to 2% on V*Bench) while simultaneously achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on various multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Agentic multimodal models often exhibit a 'meta-cognitive deficit,' struggling to judiciously decide between internal parametric knowledge and external tool invocation.
- This deficiency leads to 'blind tool invocation,' increasing latency due to serial API calls and injecting extraneous noise that can degrade reasoning performance.
- Existing reinforcement learning attempts to penalize tool use through coupled scalar rewards face an 'irreconcilable optimization dilemma,' where mild penalties are ineffective and aggressive ones suppress necessary tool usage.

## Method

- The ==Hierarchical Decoupled Policy Optimization (HDPO)== (**HDPO**) framework is introduced, employing dual, independent optimization channels for accuracy and tool efficiency.
- **HDPO** uses a ==conditional advantage estimation== mechanism for tool efficiency, computing rewards exclusively over correct responses, thereby fostering an implicit cognitive curriculum.
- A rigorous, ==meta-cognitive data curation== pipeline is developed to filter out hallucinated dynamics, isolate genuine tool necessity, and ensure environmental fidelity in training data.

## Results

- Metis achieves new state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance on multimodal reasoning benchmarks, scoring **91.1**% on V*Bench, **83.5**% on HRBench**-4K**, and **54.1**% on CharXiv (reasoning questions).
- The model substantially reduces tool invocations (e.g., from **98**% to 2% on V*Bench and **80**% to 1% on HRBench), empirically validating that strategic tool use improves accuracy and efficiency.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that HDPO with a well-tuned efficiency weight (e.g., wtool=**0.**15w_{tool}=**0.**15wtool=**0.15**) provides significant absolute gains (**+2.4**% on V*Bench, **+3.1**% on CharXiv) over standard GRPO, confirming the benefits of decoupled optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the accuracy and tool efficiency objectives eliminates destructive gradient interference and semantic ambiguity, allowing for targeted learning signals in reinforcement learning.
> - Conditioning the tool efficiency reward and advantage estimation on correctness naturally establishes a two-phase learning trajectory: first correctness, then parsimonious tool use.
> - Strategic self-reliance and targeted tool engagement, rather than reflexive invocation, directly contribute to superior reasoning performance and operational efficiency in agentic MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen significant advancements, with models demonstrating capabilities in various vision-language tasks through direct answer generation or explicit intermediate reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought). A more recent development is the emergence of "agentic" MLLMs, which can actively interact with external environments by invoking tools such as visual operations, search engines, or code interpreters. This agentic paradigm allows models to acquire fine-grained visual evidence, perform intermediate computations, and transcend the limitations of static parametric knowledge, leading to progress in complex visual reasoning, document understanding, and multi-step decision-making.

However, a noted limitation in current agentic multimodal models is a "meta-cognitive deficit." These systems often struggle to make a judicious decision between leveraging their internal parametric knowledge and querying external utilities. This deficiency frequently manifests as "blind tool invocation," where agents reflexively execute tools even when a query could be resolved directly from the raw visual context. This behavior is detrimental, leading to increased latency due to serial API invocations, and injecting extraneous noise that can degrade reasoning performance.

Existing attempts to mitigate this issue, primarily through reinforcement learning (RL) by penalizing tool usage with scalarized rewards, have encountered optimization challenges. The authors argue that this coupled reward formulation creates a dilemma: an aggressive penalty can suppress essential tool use for complex tasks, while a mild penalty is often overshadowed by the variance of the accuracy reward during advantage normalization, rendering it ineffective against tool overuse on simpler tasks. This work positions itself to address this specific meta-cognitive deficit by proposing a decoupled optimization strategy that aims to cultivate a more strategic and efficient approach to tool utilization in agentic MLLMs. It seeks to shift the focus from merely teaching models *how* to execute tools to instilling the wisdom of *when* to abstain or *when* to strategically engage them.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation of this research is to address a fundamental meta-cognitive deficit observed in current agentic multimodal models. While these models possess the capability to interact with external tools, they frequently lack the discernment to arbitrate effectively between using their internal knowledge and invoking external utilities. This leads to several problems:

1. **Blind Tool Invocation:** Current state-of-the-art models exhibit a tendency to reflexively call tools, even when tasks are intrinsically resolvable from the provided visual input alone. Empirical observations show that existing models often have high tool invocation rates (e.g., 80-90%) without necessarily achieving superior reasoning performance.
2. **Latency Bottlenecks:** Each external API invocation introduces a serial bottleneck, significantly increasing the temporal footprint of problem-solving trajectories. This means that even functionally correct systems can become practically sluggish, which is undesirable in real-world deployments.
3. **Extraneous Noise and Reasoning Degradation:** Redundant tool interactions can inject additional environmental noise into the reasoning process, potentially derailing otherwise sound reasoning trajectories and ultimately degrading the final performance.
4. **Inadequate Existing RL Paradigms:** A prevalent mitigation strategy involves penalizing excessive tool usage during reinforcement learning by scalarizing task accuracy and tool efficiency into a single reward. The authors identify this "coupled design" as an "irreconcilable optimization dilemma":
 - An **aggressive efficiency penalty** can make the model overly conservative, suppressing necessary tool use on challenging tasks and sacrificing accuracy.
 - A **mild efficiency penalty** is often "washed out" during advantage normalization (e.g., in GRPO) by the higher variance of the accuracy reward. Mathematically, the mixed advantage becomes dominated by the accuracy variance, rendering the efficiency signal asymptotically negligible for small penalty weights. This confounding of the policy gradient prevents effective learning of strategic tool use.

To transcend these limitations, the key objective is to develop an RL framework that reframes tool efficiency from a competing scalar objective to a strictly conditional one. This involves decoupling the optimization of accuracy and efficiency, thereby eliminating gradient interference and fostering a natural cognitive curriculum. The research aims to train an agent that can achieve high accuracy while significantly reducing redundant tool invocations, demonstrating that strategic tool use and reasoning performance are not a trade-off but can be attained simultaneously. The specific model, Metis, is intended to exemplify this strategic, meta-cognitive wisdom of abstention and targeted tool engagement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the Hierarchical Decoupled Policy Optimization (HDPO) framework, complemented by a rigorous data curation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation & The Reward Coupling Problem

The paper first formalizes the multi-turn tool-augmented reasoning setting, where a policy $\pi_{\theta}$ generates a group of $G$ multi-turn responses, each potentially involving $T_i$ tool interactions. Existing approaches, such as a straightforward application of GRPO, define a scalarized, coupled reward $R_{mix_i} = R_{acc_i} + \alpha \cdot R_{tool_i}$, where $R_{acc_i}$ accounts for correctness and formatting, and $R_{tool_i}$ penalizes tool usage. This $R_{mix_i}$ is then used to compute a shared advantage for policy optimization.

The authors mathematically demonstrate the inherent flaws of this reward coupling. By expanding the variance of the mixed reward, they show that the shared advantage normalization inextricably links the policy gradients of the two objectives, leading to:
- **Gradient Entanglement:** Destructive interference between accuracy and efficiency updates.
- **Semantic Ambiguity:** Inaccurate-but-efficient trajectories can yield similar scalar rewards to accurate-but-inefficient ones, neutralizing critical training signals.
- **Hyperparameter Fragility:** The effective trade-off depends on dynamic, data-dependent covariance, making $\alpha$ unstable.
Specifically, for small $\alpha$, a first-order Taylor expansion reveals that the mixed advantage $A_{mix_i} \approx \frac{\tilde{R}_{acc_i}}{\sigma_{acc}} + O(\alpha)$, illustrating that the tool efficiency gradient contribution is attenuated by the accuracy variance and effectively vanishes.

#### 3.2. HDPO: Hierarchical Decoupled Policy Optimization

HDPO addresses the reward coupling problem by maintaining two independent optimization channels.

##### 3.2.1. Dual Reward Design and Decoupled Advantages

- **Accuracy Channel:** The accuracy reward $R_{acc_i}$ combines a binary correctness score from an LLM judge ($R_{ans_i} \in \{0, 1\}$) and a format compliance bonus ($R_{fmt_i} \in \{0, 1\}$), with weights $\lambda_a=0.9$ and $\lambda_f=0.1$. Its advantage $A_{acc_i}$ is computed using standard GRPO over all $G$ rollouts in a group.
- **Efficiency Channel:** The tool reward $R_{tool_i}$ is designed for execution economy. To prevent gaming (i.e., premature termination for high efficiency), this signal is strictly conditioned on correctness. $R_{tool_i} = \frac{1}{T_i + 1}$ if $R_{ans_i} > 0$, and $0$ otherwise. This provides a monotonically decreasing reward with increasing tool calls for correct answers.
 Crucially, a conditional advantage estimation mechanism is employed. A "qualifying set" $Q$ is defined, comprising indices of rollouts with correct responses. The tool efficiency advantage $A_{tool_i}$ is then computed *exclusively* relative to other correct solutions within $Q$. If fewer than two correct rollouts exist ($|Q| < 2$), $A_{tool_i}$ is set to zero to prevent invalid comparisons.

##### 3.2.2. Hierarchical Policy Update

The final HDPO objective linearly combines the clipped surrogate losses for each channel: $L_{HDPO}(\theta) = w_{acc} \cdot L_{GRPO}(A_{acc}) + w_{tool} \cdot L_{GRPO}(A_{tool})$. This decoupling ensures that each gradient component delivers a targeted, orthogonal learning signal, eliminating destructive covariance interference.

##### 3.2.3. Algorithm Summary & The Implicit Curriculum

Algorithm 1 outlines the HDPO procedure: sampling multiple rollouts, computing orthogonal rewards, estimating $A_{acc}$ over all rollouts, estimating $A_{tool}$ exclusively over correct rollouts, and updating the policy via a weighted sum of losses. An implicit cognitive curriculum emerges: early in training, when few rollouts are correct, the accuracy objective dominates. As the model improves, more rollouts qualify for efficiency comparison, gradually scaling up the tool-parsimony signal. This naturally enforces a two-phase learning trajectory: first correctness, then efficiency.

#### 3.3. Training Data Curation

A robust empirical foundation is deemed essential. The authors identify pervasive pathologies in existing tool-augmented MLLM datasets and propose a rigorous meta-cognitive curation pipeline.

##### 3.3.1. SFT Data Curation

The SFT corpus is sourced from public trajectories and refined through three mechanisms:
- **Eradicating Hallucinated Environmental Dynamics:** Code segments are rigorously executed in a sandbox. Trajectories with execution failures or inconsistent feedback (e.g., correct output from broken code) are discarded.
- **Isolating Genuine Tool Necessity:** A zero-shot solvability baseline (Qwen3-VL-8B without tools, pass@8=1) filters out samples that can be trivially solved by direct reasoning, ensuring SFT only demonstrates necessary tool usage.
- **Multidimensional Meta-Cognitive Filtering:** Gemini-3.1-Pro acts as an automated judge to evaluate trajectories for visual relevance, reasoning coherence, and tool-use rationale. "Blind tool invocation" (e.g., meaningless image rotations) is penalized, and low-quality trajectories are discarded.

##### 3.3.2. RL Data Curation

The RL prompt set undergoes further filtering:
- **Environmental Fidelity Verification:** Prompts are assessed by a multimodal judge for image quality, question clarity, and image-text consistency; corrupted or ambiguous prompts are excluded.
- **Variance-Aware Difficulty Calibration:** Only prompts exhibiting a non-trivial mix of successes and failures (pass@8 $\in (0, 1)$ across $G=8$ rollouts) are retained, ensuring a robust and actionable gradient signal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Metis, the model trained with HDPO, demonstrates improvements across various benchmarks, particularly in balancing task accuracy with tool-use efficiency.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Tool Usage Efficiency

Metis, based on the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, establishes new state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance among open-source multimodal agents.
- **Perception and Document Understanding (Table 1):** Metis achieves 91.1% on V*Bench, 83.5% on HRBench-4K, and 82.0% on HRBench-8K. On the challenging CharXiv reasoning questions, it scores 54.1%, surpassing the previous best agentic model (DeepEyesV2 at 48.9%). These results indicate that Metis effectively leverages image cropping and search tools to resolve visual ambiguities.
- **Mathematical and Logical Reasoning (Table 2):** Metis achieves an average score of 66.9% across five datasets. Notably, on WeMath, it scores 65.2%, an absolute improvement of +26.4% over its backbone (38.8%) and surpassing previous agents like DeepEyesV2 (38.1%). This suggests that HDPO enables the model to seamlessly integrate Python code execution for complex calculations without compromising logical reasoning.
- **Tool Usage Efficiency (Figure 1):** A key finding is Metis's substantial reduction in tool invocations (e.g., from 98% for standard GRPO to 2% on V*Bench, and from 80% to 1% on HRBench) while simultaneously elevating reasoning accuracy. This empirically validates the premise that strategic tool use, characterized by minimal redundant calls, directly contributes to superior accuracy, rather than being a trade-off.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of the HDPO framework.
- **Effectiveness of Decoupled Optimization (Table 3):** Standard GRPO (equivalent to HDPO with $w_{tool}=0$) showed improvements over the base model, confirming the general benefits of RL. However, HDPO with $w_{tool}=0.15$ yielded further absolute gains, such as +2.4% on V*Bench, +2.8% on HRBench-8K, and +3.1% on CharXiv (RQ) compared to standard GRPO. This supports the hypothesis that decoupling objectives and eliminating gradient entanglement allows for suppression of noisy, redundant tool invocations, leading to higher reasoning accuracy.
- **Sensitivity to Efficiency Loss Weight (Table 3):** The study explored the impact of the tool-efficiency weight $w_{tool}$. A conservative weight ($w_{tool}=0.10$) improved performance over standard GRPO but was suboptimal compared to $w_{tool}=0.15$. An aggressive weight ($w_{tool}=0.20$) led to performance degradation. This indicates an "inverted-U" relationship, where $w_{tool}=0.15$ strikes a balance between encouraging self-reliance and enabling necessary tool queries.

#### 4.3. Meta-Cognitive Tool Arbitration

Qualitative analysis provided illustrative cases of Metis's meta-cognitive behavior:
- **Direct Reasoning (Figure 4, Figure 7 - Appendix B):** Metis demonstrates abstention from tool invocation when a query can be resolved through internal visual understanding and parametric knowledge alone (e.g., identifying LeBron James or reading clear text on a sign). This avoids latency and noise from redundant calls.
- **Targeted Code Execution (Figure 5):** When fine-grained visual analysis exceeds native resolution capabilities (e.g., comparing curves in a dense chart subplot), Metis strategically invokes code execution to crop and enlarge the relevant region, allowing precise inspection.
- **Selective Search Tool Invocation (Figure 8, Figure 9 - Appendix B):** Metis employs image search when an artwork cannot be identified visually (Figure 8) and text search when specific factual details (e.g., width of a monument's cella) are required but not inferable from the image (Figure 9). These cases highlight the model's ability to distinguish between internal recognition and external knowledge gaps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work identifies and addresses a significant meta-cognitive deficit in agentic multimodal models: the inability to judiciously arbitrate between internal knowledge and external tool use. By proposing the Hierarchical Decoupled Policy Optimization (HDPO) framework, the research offers a novel solution that effectively resolves the "reward coupling problem" prevalent in existing reinforcement learning paradigms.

The significance of this work lies in several key aspects:
- **Improved Efficiency and Accuracy:** The core finding that Metis reduces tool invocations by orders of magnitude while simultaneously elevating reasoning accuracy challenges the conventional assumption that heavy tool reliance equates to better performance. Instead, it suggests that strategic, parsimonious tool use can directly catalyze superior reasoning outcomes. This has direct implications for the practical deployment of agentic systems, as it can lead to systems that are not only more accurate but also more efficient in terms of computational resources and response latency.
- **Principled Optimization:** HDPO introduces a method for providing clean, orthogonal learning signals for task correctness and tool parsimony. The conditional advantage formulation and the resulting "implicit cognitive curriculum" (prioritizing correctness before efficiency) represent a principled approach to training agents with sophisticated decision-making capabilities. This could influence the design of future multi-objective reinforcement learning frameworks.
- **Cultivating Meta-Cognitive Abilities:** The paper emphasizes a paradigm shift from merely teaching models *how* to execute tools to cultivating the "meta-cognitive wisdom of abstention." This focus on the "when" rather than just the "how" is crucial for developing truly intelligent and robust agents that can calibrate their epistemic uncertainty and make informed decisions about resource allocation.
- **Robust Data Curation:** The rigorous, meta-cognitive data curation pipeline, designed to eradicate hallucinated environmental dynamics and isolate genuine tool necessity, highlights the importance of high-fidelity data for training reliable and efficient agents. This methodological contribution can guide future efforts in dataset development for tool-augmented learning.

The potential impact of this research extends to various domains where agentic MLLMs are being deployed or considered, including complex visual question answering, advanced document intelligence, and multi-step decision-making in interactive environments. By fostering agents that act more wisely, future systems could be more reliable, faster, and less prone to errors caused by unnecessary external interactions. This work contributes to the broader goal of developing more generally intelligent AI systems capable of strategic reasoning and efficient resource management in dynamic, tool-rich environments. Future research could build upon this meta-cognitive framework to explore more open-ended and long-horizon tasks, further enhancing the adaptability and autonomy of AI agents.
