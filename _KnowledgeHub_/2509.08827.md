# A Survey of Reinforcement Learning for Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper systematically synthesizes advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), moving beyond human alignment to focus on enhancing intrinsic reasoning capabilities through verifiable rewards. It identifies key components, challenges, and future directions for scaling RL towards Artificial SuperIntelligence (ASI).

## Problem

- The rapid success of RL in transforming LLMs into LRMs faces significant hurdles concerning computational resources, optimal algorithm design, and scalable, high-quality training data.
- Existing RL applications for LLMs primarily focused on human alignment, neglecting the potential of RL to fundamentally improve complex reasoning abilities.
- A lack of a unified framework to understand and navigate the complex, rapidly evolving landscape of RL for reasoning models hinders systematic research and development.

## Method

- The authors conduct a comprehensive literature review, systematizing the field into foundational components: ==reward design==, ==policy optimization==, and ==sampling strategies==.
- The survey introduces and emphasizes =="Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)"== as a key paradigm for enhancing reasoning in tasks with ==objective feedback==.
- It identifies and discusses foundational problems and ongoing debates, such as RL's role in =="sharpening vs. discovery"== and the interplay between ==RL and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)==.

## Results

- The survey demonstrates RL's broad impact across diverse applications, including coding, agentic tasks, multimodal understanding, robotics, and medical applications, achieving superior performance compared to non-RL methods.
- Analysis of foundational problems reveals that RL can enable "discovery" of new capabilities beyond mere "sharpening," and hybrid SFT+RL paradigms often yield the best generalization.
- Future research directions include continual RL, memory-based RL, and model-based RL, alongside architectural co-design and applications to pre-training and scientific discovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) provides a scalable path to improve complex reasoning in LLMs, especially for tasks like math and coding, by leveraging automated feedback.
> - Effective RL for LRMs relies on a careful combination of dense/unsupervised rewards, efficient off-policy optimization, and dynamic/structured sampling strategies.
> - The field is shifting from static datasets to dynamic environments and robust open-source infrastructure, which are crucial for scaling RL training for reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey positions itself at the cutting edge of Large Language Model (LLM) research, specifically focusing on the transformative role of Reinforcement Learning in enhancing reasoning capabilities, moving beyond mere human alignment. Historically, RL's primary application in LLMs, exemplified by methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), has been to align pre-trained models with human preferences, improving their helpfulness, honesty, and harmlessness (3H). While crucial for user experience and safety, these methods primarily shape behavior rather than fundamentally upgrading intrinsic reasoning skills.

This survey identifies and extensively chronicles an "emerging trend" of using RL not just for alignment, but to "incentivize reasoning itself," a paradigm referred to as **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. The paper highlights landmark achievements like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1 as exemplars of this shift, demonstrating that RL, when coupled with verifiable rewards (e.g., mathematical correctness, code unit-test pass rates), can dramatically improve models' abilities in complex logical tasks, including planning, reflection, and self-correction.

The work argues that RLVR introduces a "new scaling axis" for LLM capabilities, complementing traditional scaling laws based on data and parameter count during pre-training. By leveraging reward maximization and automatically checkable rewards, RL can overcome data limitations and enable self-generated training data, hinting at a pathway toward Artificial SuperIntelligence (ASI). This survey differentiates itself from prior works by providing a systematic synthesis of RL's role across the entire LLM training lifecycle—from reward design and policy optimization to sampling strategies and infrastructure—rather than merely treating RL-based methods as one of many reasoning strategies or focusing solely on alignment. It contextualizes the rapid progress by reviewing frontier reasoning models and by identifying foundational challenges in computational resources, algorithm design, training data, and infrastructure, thereby setting a roadmap for future research and scalability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the remarkable success of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in transforming Large Language Models (LLMs) into Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), particularly for complex logical tasks like mathematics and coding. The authors observe that RL has emerged as a foundational methodology in this transition, significantly advancing LLM capabilities beyond simple text generation or human alignment.

Despite this rapid progress, the authors identify several critical challenges that hinder the further scaling of RL for LRMs:
1. **Computational Resources:** Training large models with RL is immensely resource-intensive.
2. **Algorithm Design:** Existing RL algorithms may not be optimally suited for the unique characteristics of language models and complex reasoning tasks.
3. **Training Data:** The creation of high-quality, scalable training data, especially for verifiable reasoning, remains a bottleneck.
4. **Infrastructure:** Current infrastructure needs to evolve to support the asynchronous, interactive, and distributed nature of RL training for massive models.

In response to these motivations and challenges, the survey sets forth several key objectives:
- **Revisit Development & Reassess Trajectory:** To provide a timely review of the domain's evolution, evaluating its current state and future path.
- **Explore Scalability Strategies:** To identify and analyze approaches that can enhance the scalability of RL, specifically towards achieving Artificial SuperIntelligence (ASI).
- **Examine RL Application to LLMs/LRMs for Reasoning:** To focus on research applying RL to enhance reasoning abilities, particularly since the release of DeepSeek-R1, which marked a significant milestone in this area.
- **Systematize Foundational Components:** To dissect and analyze the core elements of RL for LRMs, including reward design, policy optimization, and sampling strategies.
- **Address Core Problems:** To discuss foundational and often controversial problems within the field, such as RL's role (sharpening vs. discovery), its relationship with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), model priors, training recipes, and reward definitions.
- **Review Training Resources:** To categorize and evaluate the available training resources, encompassing static corpora, dynamic environments, and RL infrastructure.
- **Survey Downstream Applications:** To illustrate the practical impact and versatility of RL-trained LRMs across a wide range of tasks.
- **Identify Future Opportunities and Directions:** To propose new avenues for research, algorithm development, and architectural innovations that will drive the field forward.

Ultimately, the authors hope that this comprehensive review will "promote future research on RL for broader reasoning models," guiding the community toward more effective and scalable solutions for achieving high-level intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a comprehensive **literature review and survey**, systematically synthesizing recent advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). Its methodology is entirely analytical and integrative, structured to provide a holistic overview of the field's current state, challenges, and future trajectories.

The authors' approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Defining the Scope:** The survey explicitly focuses on the application of RL to enhance *reasoning* in LLMs, distinguishing it from prior RL applications primarily aimed at human alignment (e.g., RLHF). A central concept introduced and explored is **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**, emphasizing tasks with objective, automatable feedback.

2. **Structured Organization:** The report is meticulously organized into distinct sections and subsections, allowing for a systematic breakdown of the complex interdependencies within RL for LRMs. This structure is clearly laid out in the Table of Contents and visually reinforced by Figure 1, an overview taxonomy.

3. **Preliminaries and Contextualization:**
 - The paper begins by establishing a **background** of RL concepts, mapping traditional RL components (states, actions, rewards, policy) to the language model domain.
 - It then provides a **timeline of "Frontier Models"** (Figure 4, Table 1), tracing the chronological development of state-of-the-art LRMs, categorized by their focus (LRMs, agentic LRMs, multimodal LRMs). This contextualizes the technological landscape that the survey addresses.
 - A **comparison with "Related Surveys"** clarifies the unique contributions of this work, highlighting its comprehensive, lifecycle-oriented perspective on RL for reasoning.

4. **Decomposition of Foundational Components:** The core of the survey systematically dissects the RL pipeline into three foundational components, each with its own taxonomy of approaches and representative works:
 - **Reward Design:** Covers verifiable, generative, dense, and unsupervised rewards, along with reward shaping techniques.
 - **Policy Optimization:** Discusses policy gradient objectives, critic-based vs. critic-free algorithms, off-policy optimization, and various regularization objectives (KL, entropy, length penalty).
 - **Sampling Strategy:** Examines dynamic and structured sampling methods, as well as the adjustment of sampling hyperparameters.

5. **Identification of Foundational Problems:** The authors dedicate a section to "Foundational Problems," presenting ongoing debates and unresolved issues that are critical for the field's advancement. These problems are framed as contrasts (e.g., sharpening vs. discovery, generalize vs. memorize, tricks vs. traps), encouraging deeper theoretical and empirical investigation.

6. **Review of Training Resources:** Recognizing that effective RL relies heavily on supporting resources, the survey meticulously categorizes and describes:
 - **Static Corpora:** Datasets for math, code, STEM, and agentic tasks, emphasizing quality, verifiability, and process-awareness.
 - **Dynamic Environments:** Interactive simulation environments (rule-based, code-based, game-based, model-based, ensemble-based) that enable scalable data generation and real-time feedback.
 - **RL Infrastructure:** Open-source frameworks and tools that facilitate distributed, efficient RL training.

7. **Survey of Applications:** To demonstrate practical impact, the paper extensively covers RL's application across a broad spectrum of domains: coding, agentic tasks (search, GUI), multimodal tasks (understanding, generation), multi-agent systems, robotics, and medical tasks.

8. **Future Directions:** The survey concludes by outlining promising research avenues, providing a forward-looking roadmap for innovation.

Throughout the report, the authors integrate discussions of "Takeaways" at the beginning of each subsection, providing concise summaries of key insights. The use of tables (e.g., Table 1 for open-source models, Table 2 for action/reward granularity, Table 3 for RL algorithms, Table 4 for static datasets, Table 5 for dynamic environments, Table 6 for RL infrastructure) further aids in organizing and comparing diverse research efforts. This rigorous, structured, and comprehensive approach allows the survey to provide a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners navigating the complex and rapidly evolving landscape of RL for LRMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, the paper's "findings" are primarily the systematic categorization, critical analysis, and synthesis of the current state of Reinforcement Learning for Large Reasoning Models (RL for LRMs). It highlights key advancements, identifies persistent challenges, and outlines emerging trends across various dimensions.

**Key Findings across Foundational Components:**

- **Reward Design:**
 - **RLVR is foundational:** Rule-based verifiable rewards (e.g., for math and code) provide scalable and reliable training signals, exemplified by DeepSeek-R1. The "Verifier's Law" emphasizes that automatable verification is crucial for efficient RL.
 - **Generative Rewards expand applicability:** Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) extend RL to subjective, non-verifiable tasks by providing nuanced, text-based feedback. A trend is training RMs to reason before judging, often via rubrics or co-evolving with the policy model.
 - **Dense Rewards improve efficiency:** Token, step, or turn-level rewards provide fine-grained credit assignment, improving training efficiency and stability, particularly important for long reasoning chains and multi-turn agentic tasks.
 - **Unsupervised Rewards address bottlenecks:** Methods deriving rewards from model consistency, internal confidence, self-generated knowledge, or model-agnostic heuristics/data corpora eliminate human annotation bottlenecks, enabling scalable reward signal generation, though they risk reward hacking.
 - **Reward Shaping stabilizes training:** Combining different reward types (e.g., verifiers with reward models) and using group-level baselines (like in GRPO) or Pass@K-aligned objectives enriches sparse signals and expands exploration.

- **Policy Optimization:**
 - **Critic-free algorithms are preferred for verifiable tasks:** While critic-based methods (PPO with GAE) offer token-level value signals, they incur significant computational overhead. Critic-free approaches (GRPO and its variants) are increasingly used for RLVR tasks as they only require sequence-level rewards, simplifying training and avoiding reward hacking from ill-trained critics.
 - **Off-policy methods boost sample efficiency:** Decoupling data collection from policy learning through asynchronous experience replay, offline learning, or mixed-policy methods (combining SFT with RL) improves training stability and performance.
 - **Regularization is crucial for stability:** KL regularization (against reference or old policies), entropy regularization (to prevent collapse and encourage exploration), and length penalties (to balance reasoning depth and computational cost) are vital for stable and efficient RL training of LLMs, though their optimal forms remain debated.

- **Sampling Strategy:**
 - **Dynamic and Structured Sampling are key:** Adapting prompt selection and computational budget based on online signals (difficulty, success rate) via dynamic sampling (efficiency- or exploration-oriented) maximizes data utilization. Structured sampling (e.g., tree rollouts, shared prefixes) aligns generation with problem-solving topology, improving credit assignment and sample efficiency.
 - **Hyperparameter tuning is essential:** Careful adjustment of parameters like temperature, alongside length budgeting strategies (staged context lengthening, penalties), balances exploration-exploitation and manages computational costs.

**Main Findings on Foundational Problems (Ongoing Debates):**

- **RL's Role: Sharpening or Discovery?** The debate centers on whether RL primarily refines existing capabilities ("sharpening") or generates fundamentally new ones ("discovery"). Initial evidence leaned towards sharpening, but recent studies suggest that sufficiently prolonged and stabilized RL can extend reasoning frontiers, enabling the composition of existing skills into novel behaviors ("discovery").
- **RL vs. SFT: Generalize or Memorize?** RL tends to demonstrate superior out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, especially on verifiable tasks, while SFT can lead to memorization and catastrophic forgetting on non-target tasks. However, RL is not a panacea and is sensitive to initial data. Hybrid SFT+RL paradigms are emerging as best practices.
- **Model Prior: Weak and Strong:** Base models (ee.g., Qwen) often serve as better starting points for RL than heavily instruction-tuned models, which might interfere with reward shaping. Model families show asymmetric RL responsiveness, with "mid-training" strategies (e.g., injecting high-quality math/code data into late-stage pre-training) enhancing weak priors.
- **Training Recipes: Tricks or Traps?** The field still grapples with inconsistent experimental settings and conflicting conclusions regarding various "tricks" (e.g., decoupled PPO clipping, specific normalization). A call for unified experimental protocols and explicit scalability–performance–cost curves is made to distinguish robust advancements from transient gains.
- **Reward Type: Process or Outcome?** Outcome rewards are scalable for verifiable tasks but can encourage "unfaithful" reasoning. Process rewards offer dense, interpretable guidance but are costly to annotate and prone to hacking. Combining both (e.g., via implicit process modeling or generative verifiers) is a promising direction.

**Main Findings on Training Resources and Applications:**

- **Training Resources are Evolving:** There's a clear shift from static, raw datasets to higher-quality, verifiable, synthesized data, and interactive, dynamic environments (rule-based, code-based, game-based, model-based). This is complemented by the development of robust, open-source RL infrastructure designed for distributed training and various LLM agents.
- **Broad Applications Demonstrate Impact:** RL has significantly advanced LLMs' capabilities across diverse domains:
 - **Coding:** Improved code generation, software engineering assistance, and agentic coding (e.g., SWE-Bench).
 - **Agentic Tasks:** Enhanced tool use, search, web browsing, deep research, and GUI/computer interaction.
 - **Multimodal Tasks:** Stronger understanding (image, video, 3D) and generation (image, video) by addressing data scarcity and cross-modal reasoning.
 - **Multi-Agent Systems:** Improved collaboration, reasoning, and credit assignment within LLM-based agent teams.
 - **Robotics:** Enhanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, addressing data scarcity and generalization.
 - **Medical Tasks:** Advanced medical understanding (QA, clinical coding) using verifiable rewards and emerging applications in medical generation (reports, dialogues) using DPO or rubric-based rewards.

In summary, the survey finds that RL is rapidly transforming LLMs into powerful reasoning agents by focusing on explicit reasoning capabilities, leveraging diverse reward signals, optimizing training efficiency, and expanding into a wide array of real-world applications. However, critical theoretical and practical challenges related to scalability, generalization, and reward engineering remain central to the field's future development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey, "A Survey of Reinforcement Learning for Large Reasoning Models," holds significant importance and promises substantial impact on the trajectory of Artificial Intelligence research, particularly in the realm of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the pursuit of Artificial SuperIntelligence (ASI).

**1. Comprehensive Unification and Clarification:**
The primary significance of this work lies in its comprehensive and systematic unification of the rapidly growing and often fragmented research landscape of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). By categorizing foundational components, dissecting core problems, reviewing training resources, and mapping diverse applications, the survey provides an invaluable intellectual framework. It clarifies the distinctions between RL for human alignment (RLHF, DPO) and RL for reasoning (RLVR), a crucial conceptual shift that highlights the field's evolving ambitions. This synthesis will serve as an essential reference for new and established researchers, helping them navigate the complexities and identify critical areas for investigation.

**2. Guiding Future Research Towards Scalable Reasoning and ASI:**
The most profound impact of this survey is its explicit orientation towards "scaling RL toward Artificial SuperIntelligence (ASI)." By articulating both the successes and, crucially, the foundational challenges (computational resources, algorithm design, data, infrastructure), it provides a clear roadmap for future research. The "Future Directions" section is particularly impactful, proposing concrete avenues such as:
- **Continual RL:** Essential for lifelong learning and adaptation in dynamic environments.
- **Memory-based RL:** Crucial for building agents that can learn from and generalize across experiences.
- **Model-based RL:** Fundamental for creating agents that understand and predict their environments, leading to more robust decision-making.
- **Efficient and Latent Space Reasoning:** Key to developing resource-rational LLMs that can reason effectively without excessive computational cost or in more abstract, continuous representations.
- **RL for LLMs Pre-training:** A bold direction that could redefine how foundation models are built, shifting from unsupervised next-token prediction to reward-guided learning from scratch.
- **RL for Diffusion-based LLMs:** Addresses emerging model architectures, expanding the scope of RL beyond autoregressive models.
- **RL for Scientific Discovery:** Points to the potential for LLMs to accelerate scientific progress through verifiable experimentation and feedback loops.
- **Architecture-Algorithm Co-Design:** A long-term vision for optimizing both model structure and learning algorithms simultaneously for efficiency and capability.

By highlighting these ambitious directions, the survey inspires researchers to tackle grand challenges, fostering innovation that could lead to more intelligent, autonomous, and versatile AI systems.

**3. Promoting Standardization and Reproducibility:**
The section on "Foundational Problems," particularly "Training Recipes: Tricks or Traps," implicitly and explicitly calls for greater standardization in experimental settings, reporting, and evaluation metrics. This critique of the current research ethos—where "tricks" sometimes obscure true advancements—is vital. By advocating for "unified experimental protocols, verifiable reward structures, and explicit scalability–performance–cost curves," the survey indirectly promotes more rigorous scientific practices, which is essential for accelerating progress and ensuring the reproducibility and trustworthiness of results.

**4. Bridging Academia and Industry:**
The diverse author affiliations (leading universities and a national AI lab like Shanghai AI Laboratory) underscore a practical focus. The detailed review of open-source models, datasets, and infrastructure frameworks provides concrete resources for both academic researchers and industry practitioners. This facilitates knowledge transfer and practical implementation of cutting-edge RL techniques for LLMs, accelerating the development of real-world AI applications.

**5. Advancing Practical Applications:**
The extensive review of applications across diverse domains (coding, agentic tasks, multimodal, robotics, medical) demonstrates the immediate and projected utility of RL-enhanced LRMs. From fixing software bugs to assisting in medical diagnosis and enabling autonomous robots, the potential societal and economic impact is immense. The survey not only showcases current capabilities but also identifies gaps, thereby directing future efforts towards high-impact areas.

In conclusion, this survey is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a strategic document that maps the frontier of RL for LRMs, defines the critical battles to be fought, and lays out the potential rewards. Its significance lies in its ability to consolidate understanding, inspire audacious goals, and foster the collaborative, rigorous research necessary to build the next generation of truly intelligent and reasoning AI systems.
