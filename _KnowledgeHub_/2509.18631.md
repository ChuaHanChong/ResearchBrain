# Generalizable Domain Adaptation for Sim-and-Real Policy Co-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from Georgia Tech and NVIDIA combines sim-and-real policy co-training with Unbalanced Optimal Transport to align joint observation-action distributions, allowing robots to learn manipulation policies from abundant simulation data and a small set of real-world demonstrations. The method achieves robust generalization to novel real-world scenarios across different visual modalities.

## Problem

- Robotic policies trained solely in simulation struggle in real-world environments due to discrepancies in appearance, sensors, and dynamics, known as the sim-to-real gap.
- Collecting diverse and large-scale real-world robot demonstration datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- Many domain adaptation techniques align only marginal observation distributions, which is insufficient for learning fine-grained, action-relevant features crucial for complex manipulation.

## Method

- A unified sim-and-real co-training framework integrates Optimal Transport for domain adaptation, concurrently training a policy and a visual encoder.
- Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) is applied to align the *joint distributions* of learned visual features and task-relevant states (e.g., proprioception) across simulation and real-world data.
- A temporally aligned sampling strategy, based on Dynamic Time Warping, ensures mini-batches contain behaviorally similar source and target transitions for effective UOT optimization.

## Results

- Achieved high success rates (0.73 for image, 0.77 for point cloud on average in real-world tasks), outperforming baselines in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution real-world scenarios.
- Demonstrated robust generalization to novel real-world conditions not covered by real-world demonstrations, with examples like 0.7 and 0.4 success rates on BoxInBin and Stack with novel textures, respectively.
- Confirmed the method's effectiveness across image and point cloud modalities and showed that increasing simulated data quantity led to improved out-of-distribution generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning the *joint distributions* of observations and corresponding actions or task-relevant states across domains yields a stronger, more direct signal for learning domain-invariant, behaviorally consistent features.
> - Unbalanced Optimal Transport can robustly handle the inherent data imbalance and partial overlap between extensive simulation and sparse real-world data, enabling selective alignment.
> - A temporally aligned sampling strategy is essential for forming effective mini-batches, ensuring that source and target samples within a batch are behaviorally comparable for optimal UOT-based alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses one of the most significant challenges in robot learning: the **sim-to-real gap**. Behavior cloning (BC) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for robot manipulation due to its simplicity and ability to acquire complex skills by imitating expert demonstrations. However, its effectiveness in the real world is bottlenecked by the prohibitive cost and time required to collect diverse, large-scale datasets. This limitation has fueled intense interest in leveraging simulation environments, which offer scalable, controllable, and cost-effective data generation. Yet, policies trained exclusively in simulation often perform poorly in the real world due to discrepancies in visual appearance, sensor noise, and dynamics—the "sim-to-real gap."

The paper positions itself within the context of existing strategies to bridge this gap:
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** Exposing policies to varied simulated conditions to build robustness. While effective, DR often requires careful tuning of randomization parameters to adequately cover real-world variability.
- **Data Augmentation:** Similar to DR, it aims to enhance data diversity but might not fully address fundamental domain shifts.
- **Domain Adaptation (DA) Techniques:** These methods explicitly align distributions between source (simulation) and target (real) domains, either at the pixel level (e.g., using image translation with GANs) or at the feature level (learning domain-invariant representations). While feature-level DA is more scalable for end-to-end learning, many existing approaches (e.g., MMD-based methods) primarily align marginal observation distributions. The authors argue that such marginal alignment can be insufficient for complex manipulation tasks, as it may fail to preserve action-relevant relationships crucial for fine-grained control.
- **Sim-and-Real Co-training:** This simpler approach involves training a single policy on mixed data from both domains. Recent work has shown its surprising effectiveness. However, the authors identify a critical limitation: traditional co-training typically lacks explicit mechanisms for feature space alignment across domains, potentially hindering optimal transfer and generalization, especially to out-of-distribution (OOD) real-world scenarios.

This paper builds directly upon the foundation of sim-and-real co-training by introducing an **explicit domain adaptation mechanism** into the co-training loop. It leverages **Optimal Transport (OT)**, a principled framework for aligning distributions, and specifically extends it to align *joint distributions of observations and actions/task-relevant states*. This is a crucial distinction, as aligning joint distributions provides a richer, task-relevant signal for feature learning compared to aligning marginals. Furthermore, recognizing the inherent data imbalance (abundant simulation data vs. limited real-world data), the work incorporates **Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT)** and a novel **temporally-aware sampling strategy**, addressing practical challenges that often plague sim-to-real transfer with real-world constraints.

By integrating these advanced domain adaptation techniques within a co-training framework, the paper pushes the boundaries of learning generalizable robotic policies that can effectively utilize scalable simulated data while requiring only a sparse set of real-world demonstrations. It offers a more structured and robust approach to sim-to-real than prior co-training methods and refines the application of OT for visuomotor control tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable framework for learning robotic manipulation policies that **primarily leverage abundant simulation data while requiring only a small number of real-world demonstrations.** This overarching goal is driven by the high cost and labor-intensive nature of collecting real-world robot data, contrasting sharply with the scalability and controllability of data generation in simulation.

More specifically, the paper identifies and aims to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Learn a Domain-Invariant, Task-Relevant Feature Space:** The primary challenge in sim-to-real transfer is the domain gap, especially in visual observations. For the same underlying physical state, observations from simulation (`o_src`) can look vastly different from those in the real world (`o_tgt`). The objective is to train a visual encoder (`f_phi`) that maps these diverse observations from both domains into a shared latent space (`Z`) where corresponding states have similar representations, irrespective of their origin. This latent space must also preserve information crucial for predicting robot actions.

2. **Align Joint Distributions of Observations and Actions Across Domains:** The authors argue that merely aligning marginal observation distributions (a common practice in many feature-level DA methods) is insufficient for fine-grained manipulation tasks. Their key insight is to align the *joint distributions* of observations (or their learned features) and their corresponding actions (or task-relevant states like proprioception). This provides a stronger, more direct signal for learning features that are not only domain-invariant but also behaviorally consistent. The objective is to ensure that the geometric relationships crucial for action prediction are consistent, whether the input comes from simulation or the real world.

3. **Robustly Handle Data Imbalance and Partial Overlap:** Real-world demonstrations are inherently limited compared to the vast quantities of data that can be generated in simulation. This creates a significant data imbalance (`|D_src| >> |D_tgt|`). Moreover, the state spaces covered by simulated and real demonstrations may only partially overlap, with large regions of the simulated space having no real-world counterparts. The objective is to design a domain adaptation mechanism that can robustly handle this imbalance and selective alignment without distorting the latent space or creating spurious correspondences.

4. **Enable Generalization to Novel Real-World Scenarios:** The ultimate goal is for the learned policy to perform effectively in real-world scenarios that were *not explicitly covered* by the limited real-world demonstrations during training. This includes out-of-distribution (OOD) settings in the real world (e.g., novel object textures, unseen initial poses) for which training data might only exist in simulation.

The motivation stems from the desire to unlock the full potential of simulation for robot learning. By effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap, especially regarding visual perception and policy transfer, the framework aims to make robot manipulation learning more scalable, efficient, and capable of operating in diverse, unstructured real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates Optimal Transport (OT) based domain adaptation into a unified sim-and-real co-training framework for learning generalizable visuomotor policies. The approach focuses on learning a domain-invariant and task-relevant latent feature space.

#### 3.1. Core Framework: Joint Co-Training

The system trains a single policy $\pi_{\theta}(a|z, x)$ and an observation encoder $f_{\phi}: O_{src} \cup O_{tgt} \rightarrow Z$ concurrently. The policy takes as input the learned latent features $z$ from visual observations $o$, along with low-dimensional proprioceptive information $x$ (e.g., robot joint angles, end-effector pose). The overall training objective combines a standard Behavior Cloning (BC) loss with an Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) loss:
$L(f_{\phi}, \pi_{\theta}) = L_{BC}(f_{\phi}, \pi_{\theta}) + \lambda \cdot L_{UOT}(f_{\phi})$
where $L_{BC}$ is computed over both simulation ($D_{src}$) and real-world ($D_{tgt}$) datasets, and $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter balancing the two objectives.

#### 3.2. Optimal Transport for Action-Aware Feature Alignment

A cornerstone of the method is the use of Optimal Transport (OT) to align the *joint distributions* of learned visual features and actions (or task-relevant states) across domains. This contrasts with methods that only align marginal observation distributions. The goal is to minimize the Wasserstein distance between $P_{src}(f_{\phi}(o_{src}), a_{src})$ and $P_{tgt}(f_{\phi}(o_{tgt}), a_{tgt})$.

The ground cost function $C_{\phi}$ between a source pair $(f_{\phi}(o_i^{src}), a_i^{src})$ and a target pair $(f_{\phi}(o_j^{tgt}), a_j^{tgt})$ is defined as:
$C_{\phi}((f_{\phi}(o_i^{src}), a_i^{src}), (f_{\phi}(o_j^{tgt}), a_j^{tgt})) = \alpha_1 \cdot d_Z(f_{\phi}(o_i^{src}), f_{\phi}(o_j^{tgt})) + \alpha_2 \cdot d_A(a_i^{src}, a_j^{tgt})$
where $d_Z$ is a distance in the latent space and $d_A$ is a distance in the action space. This cost function encourages the encoder $f_{\phi}$ to learn representations where visual features corresponding to similar actions are brought closer together, irrespective of their domain.
**Practical Implementation Detail:** Due to potential discrepancies in action representations or controller characteristics between simulation and real-world teleoperation, the paper pragmatically substitutes actions $a$ with proprioceptive states $x$ (e.g., end-effector pose) in the ground cost calculation. Proprioceptive states are assumed to be more consistently represented across domains and highly correlated with robot behavior.

#### 3.3. Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) for Robust Alignment

Standard OT enforces strict marginal constraints, meaning all mass from one distribution must be transported to the other. This is problematic in the sim-to-real setting due to two key issues:
1. **Data Imbalance:** $N_{src} \gg N_{tgt}$, meaning far more simulation data than real data.
2. **Partial Data Overlap:** The state spaces covered by $D_{src}$ and $D_{tgt}$ may only partially overlap, leaving many simulated states without real-world counterparts.

To address this, the method employs Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT). UOT relaxes the strict marginal constraints by introducing KL-divergence regularization terms that penalize deviations from uniform marginals. This allows UOT to perform *partial mass transport*, selectively aligning the most similar subsets of distributions while down-weighting or ignoring dissimilar/unmatched portions. The UOT loss formulation includes an entropic regularization term (for efficient Sinkhorn algorithm solution) and KL-divergence penalties for marginal relaxation.

#### 3.4. Temporally Aligned Sampling for Effective Mini-Batch Learning

The effectiveness of mini-batch OT, especially UOT, heavily relies on presenting comparable source and target samples within each batch. Randomly sampling individual transitions from D_src and D_tgt can lead to batches with unrelated samples, hindering meaningful alignment.
The paper introduces a **temporally aligned sampling strategy**. This strategy works in two stages:
1. **Trajectory-level Similarity:** It first quantifies the similarity between source and target trajectories using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) on their proprioceptive state sequences.
2. **Biased Trajectory Sampling:** It then samples pairs of trajectories, biasing the selection towards those with high DTW-based similarity.
3. **Transition Sampling:** Finally, individual transition tuples are sampled from within these selected, behaviorally similar trajectory pairs.
This two-stage process significantly increases the likelihood of sampling source and target transitions within a mini-batch that correspond to similar underlying robot states, allowing the UOT optimization to more effectively align the visual latent features.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

For point cloud observations, a 3D Diffusion Policy architecture with a PointNet encoder is used. For RGB images, Diffusion Policy with a ResNet-18 encoder is adopted. Calibration techniques are employed to minimize initial domain gaps in camera intrinsics and poses between sim and real environments. Simulation data is generated using MimicGen to create diverse trajectories from a few human demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across six challenging manipulation tasks, using both image and point cloud modalities in sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer scenarios, strongly support the proposed hypotheses.

1. **Effective Learning of Complex Manipulation Tasks (H1):** The method consistently achieved high success rates, matching or outperforming all baselines (Source-only, Target-only, MMD, Co-training) across both simulation and real-world in-distribution tasks. In real-world settings, the method achieved average success rates of 0.73 for image-based policies and 0.77 for point cloud-based policies (Tables 7 and 8 in Appendix). This demonstrates its capability to learn robust policies for intricate robot manipulation.

2. **Generalization to Target Domains Seen Only in Simulation (H2):** This is a critical finding. In "Target-OOD" (Out-Of-Distribution) scenarios, where real-world demonstrations were completely absent, the proposed method significantly outperformed all baselines (Tables 2 and 4). For instance, in real-world BoxInBin and Stack tasks with novel textures, the method achieved success rates of 0.7 and 0.4 (image-based), compared to 0.3 and 0.0 for the Co-training baseline. This highlights the framework's ability to generalize to novel real-world conditions by leveraging domain-invariant features learned from abundant simulation data. The Co-training baseline, despite benefiting from mixed data, struggles when direct overlap is missing.

3. **Broad Applicability to Multiple Observation Modalities (H3):** The performance gains were consistent across both image-based and point cloud inputs (Tables 1, 3, 7, 8). This indicates that the core mechanism of learning domain-invariant, task-relevant features via UOT is effective regardless of the specific visual modality used, broadening the applicability of the approach.

4. **Benefits of Scaling Simulation Data (H4):** An analysis on the Stack task demonstrated that increasing the quantity of simulated demonstrations (from 100 to 1000 trajectories) led to significant improvements in OOD generalization performance in the real world (Fig. 4b). This empirically validates the paper's premise that leveraging abundant, low-cost simulation data is crucial, and that the proposed method can effectively utilize this data to overcome the limitations of sparse real-world data.

5. **Achieving Shared Latent Space Alignment:** Visualizations using t-SNE (Fig. 4a and Fig. 11 in Appendix) clearly showed that the method leads to significantly better alignment and mixing of source (simulation) and target (real-world) features in the latent space compared to the Co-training baseline. This "well-mixed cluster" in the latent space confirms that the Optimal Transport objective successfully encourages the encoder to learn domain-invariant representations. Additionally, transport plan visualizations (Fig. 10 in Appendix) illustrated that the UOT effectively aligns data points corresponding to similar states across domains.

6. **Importance of Temporally Aligned Sampling:** An ablation study demonstrated that the proposed temporally aligned sampling strategy is crucial for effective learning. The "Ours w/o Sampler" variant performed poorly in OOD settings, indicating that uncoordinated mini-batch sampling for sequential data leads to suboptimal feature alignment. The proposed sampler approached the performance of an "oracle" that uses perfectly paired batches, underscoring its importance (Fig. 9).

7. **Robustness to Hyperparameters:** The method exhibited robustness to variations in key hyperparameters ($\epsilon$, $\tau$, window size) within reasonable ranges, suggesting that it does not require extensive tuning for practical deployment (Table 6 in Appendix).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and sim-to-real transfer, offering a robust and practical solution to a long-standing challenge. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Scalability and Data Efficiency:** The most immediate impact is enabling robot learning pipelines to leverage the vast, cost-effective data generation capabilities of simulation without being severely hampered by the domain gap. By requiring only a *small number* of real-world demonstrations, the method drastically reduces the bottleneck of data collection, making it feasible to train policies for a wider range of complex manipulation tasks. This is crucial for advancing robot autonomy beyond controlled lab settings.

2. **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The explicit alignment of joint observation-action distributions via Optimal Transport, coupled with the ability of Unbalanced OT to handle partial overlap and data imbalance, allows learned policies to generalize effectively to novel, unseen real-world scenarios (OOD conditions). This is a critical step towards deploying robots in dynamic and less structured environments where perfect data coverage is impossible. Policies can now infer correct actions for real-world states that were only ever encountered in simulation.

3. **Methodological Innovation in Domain Adaptation:** The integration of Unbalanced Optimal Transport within a co-training framework, specifically for aligning *joint* distributions, is a novel and powerful contribution. The development of a temporally-aware sampling strategy further refines the application of mini-batch OT to sequential robotic data, providing a practical blueprint for addressing similar challenges in other sequential decision-making domains.

4. **Broad Applicability Across Modalities and Tasks:** Demonstrating consistent performance improvements across both image and point cloud observation modalities, and a diverse suite of manipulation tasks, suggests that the underlying principles are broadly applicable. This enhances the versatility of the framework for various robotic systems and sensing modalities.

5. **Foundation for Future Robot Learning Systems:** This work lays a foundation for more intelligent and adaptable robot learning systems. By effectively disentangling domain-specific visual variations from task-relevant features, it moves closer to policies that can truly understand and interact with the physical world based on simulated experience. Future work could build upon this by extending it to address dynamics gaps, enabling transfer for more dynamic tasks, or relaxing the need for precisely aligned real-world demonstrations by consuming more unstructured data like human play data.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors** include the primary focus on addressing visual observation gaps, rather than action dynamics gaps. Additionally, the method inherits limitations from MimicGen as its simulation data generation tool, implying it is currently best suited for tasks involving prehensile and quasi-static interactions. Finally, it still requires a small number of *aligned* real-world demonstrations, presenting an avenue for future research to further relax this constraint.

In conclusion, this paper offers a robust and well-validated approach to tackling the sim-to-real problem, making significant strides in how simulated data can be effectively and scalably used to train high-performing, generalizable robot manipulation policies for the real world.
