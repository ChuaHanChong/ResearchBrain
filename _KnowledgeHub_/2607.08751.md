# DexVerse: A Modular Benchmark for Multi-Task, Multi-Embodiment Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DexVerse introduces a modular benchmark with 100 diverse tasks and multi-embodiment support to evaluate dexterous manipulation policies, providing 3,180 multi-modal expert demonstrations. Benchmarking state-of-the-art imitation learning methods on this platform revealed a mean online success rate of 34% for the best policies, indicating significant remaining challenges for general dexterous control.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation benchmarks often focus on gripper-based tasks or specialized dexterous tasks, lacking comprehensive coverage for high-degree-of-freedom multi-finger hands.
- Scaling generalist robot policies to diverse, long-horizon, and contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks across varied robot embodiments and environmental conditions is difficult due to limited task diversity and visual variation.
- There is a need for systematic evaluation of generalization across diverse interaction modes, sensory conditions, and robot embodiments that current benchmarks do not adequately provide.

## Method

- A large-scale, modular simulation environment named **DexVerse** was developed on ==Isaac Lab==, featuring 100 dexterous manipulation tasks organized into 8 categories.
- The benchmark supports 3 robot arms and 6 dexterous hands, allowing for flexible arm-hand combinations and studies on cross-embodiment generalization.
- An ==embodiment-adaptive teleoperation pipeline== using Apple Vision Pro was created to collect a multi-modal dataset of 3,180 expert demonstrations for diverse tasks.

## Results

- State-of-the-art imitation learning policies (DP3 and π₀.₅) achieved a mean online success rate of only **34%** on DexVerse, highlighting the significant challenge the benchmark poses to current methods.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, despite internet-scale pretraining, did not show a clear advantage over from-scratch policies, suggesting a distribution gap in high-DoF dexterous control.
- Tasks requiring fine contact reasoning and sub-centimeter alignment, such as 'PushT' and 'InsertPen,' consistently resulted in near-zero success rates for all evaluated policies, identifying a key area for future research.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The modular and configuration-driven design of DexVerse allows for easy extensibility, enabling the addition of new tasks, assets, and robot embodiments to promote community contributions and adaptation.
> - The provided multi-modal dataset, including proprioceptive, RGB, depth, point-cloud, and simulator state observations, supports training for various state-based, image-based, and 3D policy learning methods.
> - Benchmarking demonstrated that the most effective observation modality for robot policies is skill-dependent, with no single modality consistently dominating across all task categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation is a core capability for general-purpose robotic systems, requiring coordinated control of high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) hands and arms under complex, contact-rich interactions. Recent advancements in generalist robot policies have emerged from several areas, including image-conditioned imitation learning, scalable demonstration generation, 3D-aware policies, and vision-language-action (VLA) policies. However, a consistent challenge within this field is the difficulty in scaling these methods from isolated skills and controlled task distributions to functionally diverse, long-horizon, and contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks across varied robot embodiments and environmental conditions.

Existing benchmarks for robot manipulation primarily focus on either gripper-based manipulation or specialized dexterous tasks. Benchmarks such as CALVIN, RoboTwin 2.0, ManiSkill3, and LIBERO often emphasize long-horizon, language-conditioned, or bimanual gripper tasks, but do not fully address the intricacies of high-DoF dexterous hands. Conversely, specialized dexterous benchmarks like DexMimicGen (demonstration generation), Bi-DexHands (RL-based bimanual control), DexJoCo (task-oriented tasks), DexHoldem (real-world poker manipulation), and DexH2R (dynamic human-to-robot handover) typically focus on narrower settings and often lack features such as broad task diversity, multi-embodiment support, controllable visual variation, expert demonstrations, or parallel simulation capabilities within a unified platform.

The DexVerse benchmark addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive platform designed to jointly evaluate major axes of dexterous generalization. It aims to unify broad dexterous task coverage, multi-embodiment arm-hand control, modular extensibility, expert demonstrations, parallel reinforcement learning (RL) support, and visuomotor generalization capabilities. By integrating these elements, DexVerse provides a standardized testbed for comparing policies across diverse contact regimes, visual conditions, and embodiments, thereby facilitating the development of more general-purpose dexterous manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to establish a comprehensive benchmark that facilitates the development and evaluation of general-purpose dexterous manipulation policies. This objective is driven by several key motivations, which address existing limitations in the robotics research landscape:

1. **Systematic Evaluation of Generalization:** Building general-purpose policies requires benchmarks capable of systematically evaluating performance across diverse interaction modes, sensory conditions, and robot embodiments. Current benchmarks are limited in task and data diversity, embodiment coverage, or controllable visual variation, making it difficult to assess how well policies generalize across different settings. DexVerse aims to provide this systematic evaluation capability.

2. **Broad Task Diversity:** Dexterous manipulation encompasses a wide array of skills, from simple grasping to complex multi-stage tasks involving articulated objects, tool use, and bimanual coordination. A key motivation is to offer a significantly broader range of manipulation tasks than previously available. This includes challenging scenarios that involve contact-rich behaviors, non-prehensile control, and long-horizon planning, thereby pushing the boundaries of what current policies can achieve.

3. **Multi-Embodiment Support:** Robotic hardware varies widely in kinematics, degrees of freedom, workspace, and contact geometry. Policies trained on one robot embodiment often do not directly transfer to others. DexVerse is motivated by the need to support multiple robot arm and dexterous hand combinations, enabling researchers to study cross-embodiment generalization and develop embodiment-aware representations or policies.

4. **Controllable Visual Variation:** Robust visuomotor policies must be resilient to changes in sensory inputs, such as variations in object textures, lighting conditions, backgrounds, and camera viewpoints. These visual shifts are particularly critical in dexterous manipulation where fine-grained perception of object geometry and contact affordances is essential. The benchmark aims to provide configurable visual variations to systematically evaluate visuomotor generalization and robustness.

5. **Provision of Expert Demonstrations:** Imitation learning is a common approach for training robot policies, but collecting diverse and high-quality demonstrations for complex dexterous tasks can be challenging. A core motivation is to provide a large-scale, multi-modal dataset of expert demonstrations collected via an efficient teleoperation interface. This dataset is intended to support the training and evaluation of state-based, image-based, and 3D policy learning methods.

6. **Modular and Extensible Design:** To ensure longevity and adaptability within a rapidly evolving field, the benchmark is designed with modularity in mind. This allows for the easy addition of new tasks, assets, observation modalities, action spaces, and robot embodiments, promoting community contribution and continued expansion of the testbed.

7. **Identification of Unsolved Challenges:** By benchmarking representative state-of-the-art methods on a diverse and challenging suite of tasks, the work aims to identify the current limitations of existing policy learning approaches, particularly in areas like precise contact reasoning, fine-grained visuomotor control, and general-purpose robotic manipulation. This serves to highlight critical areas for future research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexVerse is presented as a large-scale, modular simulation environment built upon Isaac Lab, designed to address the aforementioned motivations through several key methodological components:

#### 3.1. DexVerse Environment Design

- **Task Suite:** DexVerse comprises 100 dexterous manipulation tasks organized into 8 categories: Primitive, Functional, Articulation, Non-prehensile, Contact-rich, Bimanual Coordination, Multi-goal, and Long-horizon.
 - **Primitive Tasks (9):** Basic object interaction (e.g., PickCube, StackCube).
 - **Functional Tasks (11):** Affordance-aware interaction (e.g., HammerStrike, GraspKettle).
 - **Articulation Tasks (18):** Controlling object parts and joints (e.g., OpenStapler, OpenLaptop).
 - **Non-prehensile Tasks (5):** Using pushing, sliding, or environmental contact (e.g., PushT, TakeBook).
 - **Contact-rich Tasks (8):** Precise alignment under sustained contact (e.g., InsertPeg, PlugCharger).
 - **Bimanual Coordination Tasks (5):** Coordinated control of two hands/arms (e.g., BiLiftTray, BiHandover).
 - **Multi-goal Tasks (39):** Satisfying multiple objectives simultaneously (e.g., GraspMug + PushButton).
 - **Long-horizon Tasks (5):** Temporally extended, multi-stage procedures (e.g., MakeCoffee, MicrowaveFood).
- **Task Specification:** Each task is formally defined by a tuple T = (Ω, S₀, O, A, G), specifying interactive objects (Ω), initial state distribution (S₀), observation and action interfaces (O, A), and task-level success conditions (G). Success conditions are implemented as simulator predicates.
- **Modular Architecture:** The environment uses a configuration-driven design based on Isaac Lab's manager-based interface. This architecture decouples task environments from robot embodiments, enabling reusable task templates for common logic (asset loading, state initialization, resets, success checking). Task-specific parameters define manipulated objects, target states, and sampling ranges.
- **Robot Embodiments:** DexVerse supports 3 robot arms (Franka Research 3, UR10e, xArm 7) and 6 dexterous hands (Sharpa Wave, WUJI Hand, Shadow Hand, Inspire Hand, Allegro Hand, LEAP Hand), along with floating variants for each hand. Robot configurations define arm and hand models, initial poses, action interfaces, and controller parameters, allowing for flexible arm-hand combinations.
- **Visual Variation:** Configurable visual properties are provided for visuomotor generalization studies. These include object and table materials, lighting conditions (via HDR skyboxes), background skyboxes, exposure, and color temperature. Visual properties can be sampled at reset from predefined libraries. Camera viewpoint changes are also supported.
- **Non-Visual Variations:** In addition to visual changes, DexVerse allows for randomization of object initial poses, task sampling ranges, proprioceptive and object-state perturbations, and dynamics parameters.
- **Asset Sources:** Objects and scene assets are drawn from various sources, including PartNet-Mobility, ManiTwin, NVIDIA Isaac Lab/Isaac Sim assets, AutoBio, public 3D repositories, and meshes generated from reference images using tools like Meshy.

#### 3.2. Dataset and Evaluation

- **Teleoperation Data Collection:** An embodiment-adaptive teleoperation pipeline was developed for data collection. It utilizes Apple Vision Pro via Isaac Lab's CloudXR-based XR teleoperation interface. Human wrist pose tracking guides the robot end-effector via inverse kinematics, while human hand motion is converted to target joint poses for dexterous hands using optimization-based dex-retargeting.
- **Dataset Statistics:** The dataset comprises 3,180 demonstration trajectories. For 56 single-goal tasks, 55 demonstrations were collected (50 with Shadow Hand, 1 with each of the other five hands). For 5 long-horizon tasks, 20 demonstrations each were collected.
- **Multi-modal Data:** Each demonstration records synchronized proprioceptive, RGB, depth, point-cloud, and simulator state observations. The data is stored as action-state pairs, allowing for state replay to avoid simulation divergence issues and enabling flexible regeneration of requested observation terms.
- **Imitation Learning Policy Evaluation:** Four open-source imitation learning policy families were benchmarked:
 - **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Transformers:** π₀.₅ and OpenVLA (both fine-tuned from internet-scale pretrained backbones).
 - **From-scratch Diffusion Policies:** Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3).
- **Training and Evaluation Protocol:** All policies were trained on a subset of 950 episodes (19 tasks × 50 episodes per task) from the DexVerse teleoperation corpus. Evaluation involved rolling out 50 episodes per task in the simulator under identical termination criteria, reporting the mean success rate.
- **Observation Modes for Baselines:**
 - **π₀.₅:** Two 256x256 RGB images (third-person and wrist-mounted) and natural language instruction. Proprioceptive state input was disabled.
 - **OpenVLA:** Same RGB camera set as π₀.₅, current joint position encoded by a proprioceptive projector, and language instruction.
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP):** State-based, trained on normalized proprioceptive state only (no visual input).
 - **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3):** Conditions on a workspace-cropped point cloud (from single or fused depth camera views) and proprioception (no RGB or language input).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of representative imitation learning policies on DexVerse yielded several findings regarding the current state of dexterous manipulation capabilities:

1. **Overall Challenge for Current Methods:** DexVerse presents a significant challenge for existing policies. The best-performing baselines, DP3 and π₀.₅, achieved a mean online success rate of 34%, with Diffusion Policy (DP) at 32% and OpenVLA at 19%. This indicates that current methods are far from consistently solving the diverse range of tasks in the benchmark.

2. **Limited Transfer from Internet-Scale VLA Pretraining:** Despite being initialized from large-scale web-trained backbones, the VLA policies did not exhibit a clear advantage. π₀.₅ matched the top-performing from-scratch policy (DP3), while OpenVLA performed lowest. The authors attribute this to a distribution gap between the pretraining data (web images, low-DoF action spaces) and the high-DoF multifinger control required for DexVerse tasks. The pretraining priors may contribute to perception but less effectively to the specific control manifold of dexterous manipulation.

3. **Skill-Dependent Optimal Observation Modality:** No single observation modality or representation consistently dominated across all task categories, suggesting that the most informative sensory input is skill-dependent.
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP)** was most effective on "Pick-and-Lift" tasks (mean success rate of 0.51), where object appearance and 2D image information, combined with low-dimensional state, appeared sufficient for successful grasping.
 - **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3)** performed best on "Functional Tool Use" tasks (mean success rate of 0.35), indicating that explicit point-cloud geometry can aid in localizing tool tips and regulating precise pouring or striking poses.
 - **π₀.₅** showed stronger performance on "Articulated-object Manipulation" (mean success rate of 0.35) and "Precision Contact" tasks (mean success rate of 0.29), suggesting that language conditioning and flow-matching action prediction may assist in disambiguating multi-stage subgoals and precise contact timing.

4. **Unsolved Challenges in Fine Contact Reasoning and Alignment:** Tasks requiring tight tolerances, sustained force regulation, and sub-centimeter alignment proved consistently challenging across all evaluated methods.
 - Tasks such as "PushT", "InsertPen", "SlideUtilityKnife", and "OpenLaptop" consistently resulted in near-zero success rates for all four policies (e.g., "PushT" had 0.00 success for all baselines).
 - This highlights a fundamental limitation of current behavior cloning approaches, particularly when explicit force feedback or closed-loop contact correction is not explicitly incorporated, indicating a significant area for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DexVerse represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Establishes a Unified and Challenging Benchmark:** DexVerse provides a large-scale, modular, and unified benchmark that integrates diverse task categories, multiple robot arm-hand embodiments, configurable visual variation, and multi-modal demonstration data. This addresses a critical need in the community for a comprehensive testbed to evaluate and compare dexterous manipulation policies.

2. **Facilitates Generalization Research:** By enabling systematic evaluation across various interaction patterns, sensory conditions, and hardware configurations, DexVerse directly supports research into cross-task, cross-embodiment, and visuomotor generalization. This is crucial for developing policies that are robust and adaptable to real-world complexities.

3. **Highlights Current Policy Limitations:** The benchmarking results clearly demonstrate that state-of-the-art imitation learning and vision-language-action policies are not yet capable of solving many general dexterous manipulation tasks, particularly those involving precise contact, bimanual coordination, and functional tool use. This provides empirical evidence for the remaining challenges and defines specific areas where further research is most needed.

4. **Drives Development of Advanced Dexterous Control:** By exposing the limitations of current methods, particularly in tasks requiring fine contact reasoning and sub-centimeter alignment, DexVerse is positioned to motivate the development of new control strategies, perception pipelines, and learning algorithms that can handle these complex interactions, potentially incorporating explicit force feedback or advanced contact modeling.

5. **Supports Multi-modal Learning:** The provision of a multi-modal dataset (proprioceptive, RGB, depth, point-cloud, simulator state) encourages research into how different sensory inputs can be leveraged, combined, or selected based on task requirements, contributing to the understanding of effective observation modalities for diverse manipulation challenges.

6. **Promotes Extensibility and Community Engagement:** The modular and configuration-driven design of DexVerse allows for easy expansion with new tasks, assets, and embodiments. This extensibility encourages community contribution and ensures the benchmark can evolve with the research landscape, maintaining its relevance over time.

In summary, DexVerse establishes itself as a foundational resource for advancing the capabilities of general-purpose dexterous manipulation, providing both a standardized evaluation platform and a clear roadmap for future research directions in contact-rich robotics and visuomotor learning.
