# BBN: Bilateral-Branch Network with Cumulative Learning for Long-Tailed Visual Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Megvii Technology developed the Bilateral-Branch Network (BBN) with cumulative learning to address long-tailed visual recognition, revealing that traditional re-balancing methods can damage learned feature representations while improving classifier performance. BBN consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on various long-tailed benchmarks, for instance, reducing the Top-1 error rate on CIFAR-10 (imbalance ratio 100) to 20.18%, a 2.79% improvement over the LDAM-DRW method.

## Problem

- Deep learning models perform poorly on minority classes in real-world long-tailed datasets due to imbalanced class distributions and bias towards majority classes.
- Existing class re-balancing strategies (e.g., re-sampling, re-weighting) primarily focus on improving classifier learning but often inadvertently harm the quality and generalizability of learned feature representations.
- There was a lack of a unified, end-to-end framework capable of simultaneously optimizing both robust feature representation and balanced classifier learning for long-tailed data.

## Method

- The **Bilateral-Branch Network (BBN)** is proposed, featuring two parallel branches: a 'conventional learning branch' and a 're-balancing branch', sharing most backbone weights.
- The conventional branch uses a ==uniform sampler== to learn universal feature representations from the original data distribution, while the re-balancing branch uses a ==reversed sampler== to over-sample minority classes.
- A novel ==cumulative learning strategy== with a ==parabolic decay function== dynamically adjusts a trade-off parameter, initially prioritizing representation learning and gradually shifting focus to the re-balancing branch for tail classes.

## Results

- BBN consistently achieved state-of-the-art Top-1 error rates on long-tailed CIFAR datasets, with a **2.79%** reduction over LDAM-DRW on CIFAR-10 (imbalance ratio 100), reaching **20.18%**.
- On large-scale iNaturalist 2018 and 2017 datasets, BBN outperformed existing methods, achieving **30.38%** and **34.25%** error rates respectively, showing improvements of up to **+3.94%** over LDAM-DRW (2x).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the reversed sampler for the re-balancing branch and the parabolic decay strategy for cumulative learning were optimal, validating the proposed design choices for balancing feature and classifier learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Traditional re-balancing strategies improve classifier learning but can degrade the discriminative ability and compactness of learned deep features, necessitating a careful approach to feature representation.
> - Learning robust, universal feature representations from the original data distribution is beneficial and should ideally precede or be weighted more heavily in the initial stages of training when addressing long-tailed recognition.
> - An adaptive, cumulative learning strategy that dynamically balances the contributions of representation learning and re-balancing throughout training is more effective than static or two-stage approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual recognition systems have advanced significantly with deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), largely due to the availability of large, high-quality datasets with relatively balanced class distributions, such as ImageNet, MS COCO, and Places Database. However, real-world datasets often exhibit a "long-tailed" distribution, where a small number of "head" classes account for most of the data, while a large number of "tail" classes have very few samples. This imbalance presents a challenge for deep learning models, as they tend to perform poorly on minority classes due to insufficient training data and bias towards majority classes. Recent datasets like iNaturalist, LVIS, and RPC exemplify this real-world imbalance.

Existing approaches to address long-tailed problems primarily involve class re-balancing strategies. These strategies fall into two main categories:
1. **Re-sampling methods:** These involve either over-sampling minority classes (repeating samples) or under-sampling majority classes (discarding samples) to create a more balanced training distribution.
2. **Re-weighting methods:** These assign different weights to samples or classes in the loss function, typically giving higher weights to minority classes or difficult examples to increase their influence during training.

More recent developments include two-stage fine-tuning strategies, where a network is initially trained on the original imbalanced data, and then re-balancing techniques are applied in a second fine-tuning stage. Other paradigms, such as metric learning, meta-learning, and knowledge transfer, have also been explored. This paper contributes to the area of long-tailed visual recognition by proposing a method that addresses a newly identified limitation of existing re-balancing strategies and offers a unified, end-to-end learning framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to improve recognition performance on long-tailed visual datasets by addressing a perceived limitation of existing class re-balancing strategies. The authors first investigated the working mechanism of these strategies in deep networks. Their key discovery is that while re-balancing methods effectively promote "classifier learning" (i.e., updating the weights of the classification layers to correctly separate classes), they can simultaneously "damage the representative ability" of the learned deep features (i.e., the backbone network's ability to extract generalizable and discriminative features from the original data distribution).

This discovery is based on an experimental setup that separates representation learning and classifier learning. They found that:
- When representation learning is fixed, re-balancing methods (re-weighting/re-sampling) lead to lower classification error rates, confirming their positive effect on classifier learning.
- When classifier learning is fixed, plain training with cross-entropy on original imbalanced data yields better results than representations learned with re-balancing methods, indicating that re-balancing can hurt feature learning. The feature representations learned through re-balancing methods exhibited reduced discriminative ability.

The motivation for the proposed Bilateral-Branch Network (BBN) stems directly from this insight. The authors aimed to develop a unified framework that could simultaneously optimize both representation learning and classifier learning, mitigating the adverse effects observed with traditional re-balancing strategies. The goal was to preserve the universal feature learning capability while enhancing the classification accuracy, particularly for tail classes. This motivated the design of a network with distinct branches and a strategy to adaptively balance their contributions during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose the Bilateral-Branch Network (BBN) model, designed to address both representation learning and classifier learning concurrently within a single framework. BBN consists of three main components: a conventional learning branch, a re-balancing branch, and a cumulative learning strategy.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The BBN model comprises two parallel branches: a "conventional learning branch" and a "re-balancing branch." Both branches share most of their weights (all except the last residual block in their ResNet backbone) and take input from different sampling strategies. Their feature outputs are then aggregated using a cumulative learning strategy to compute the final loss.

#### 3.2. Proposed Bilateral-Branch Structure

- **Conventional Learning Branch:** This branch is designed to learn universal patterns from the original data distribution, thereby focusing on robust representation learning. It receives input data `(xc, yc)` from a **uniform sampler**, where each sample in the training dataset is selected with equal probability within an epoch. This preserves the characteristics of the original (long-tailed) data distribution, which is hypothesized to benefit representation quality.
- **Re-balancing Branch:** This branch aims to alleviate class imbalance and improve classification accuracy, especially for tail classes. Its input data `(xr, yr)` comes from a **reversed sampler**. For the reversed sampler, the sampling probability for each class is inversely proportional to its sample size (i.e., `P_i = w_i / sum(w_j)` where `w_i = N_max / N_i`). This strategy over-samples minority classes and under-samples majority classes, effectively balancing the training mini-batches for the re-balancing branch.
- **Weights Sharing:** To promote efficiency and leverage learned representations, both branches share the weights of the backbone network, except for the final residual block. This design intends to allow the well-learned representations from the conventional branch to benefit the re-balancing branch, while also reducing computational complexity during inference.

The feature vectors `f_c` and `f_r` from the global average pooling layer of each branch are then weighted by an adaptive trade-off parameter `alpha` and `(1 - alpha)`, respectively. These weighted features are passed through separate classifiers `W_c` and `W_r` (fully connected layers) and then aggregated by element-wise addition to produce the final output logits `z = alpha * W_c^T * f_c + (1 - alpha) * W_r^T * f_r`. The softmax function converts `z` into predicted probabilities `p_hat`.

#### 3.3. Proposed Cumulative Learning Strategy

A novel cumulative learning strategy is introduced to dynamically adjust the learning focus between the two branches throughout the training process. This strategy controls the adaptive trade-off parameter `alpha`, which is calculated based on the current training epoch `T` and the maximum number of epochs `T_max`:
`alpha = 1 - (T / T_max)^2`

This parabolic decay strategy ensures that `alpha` starts at 1 (emphasizing the conventional learning branch) and gradually decreases to 0 (emphasizing the re-balancing branch) as training progresses. The rationale is to first allow the network to learn universal feature representations from the original data distribution and then progressively shift attention to the tail classes. This aims to prevent damage to the universally learned features when re-balancing is applied. The overall weighted cross-entropy loss `L` is calculated as `L = alpha * E(p_hat, yc) + (1 - alpha) * E(p_hat, yr)`, where `E` is the cross-entropy loss function. The entire network is trained end-to-end.

#### 3.4. Inference Phase

During inference, test samples are fed into both branches, yielding features `f'_c` and `f'_r`. The trade-off parameter `alpha` is fixed to 0.5, implying an equal contribution from both branches. The features are then passed through their respective classifiers, and the resulting logits are aggregated by element-wise addition to produce the final classification results.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed BBN model was evaluated through extensive experiments on four long-tailed benchmark datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 (with imbalance ratios of 10, 50, and 100), iNaturalist 2017, and iNaturalist 2018.

#### 4.1. Performance on Long-tailed CIFAR Datasets

On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets with various imbalance ratios, BBN consistently achieved the lowest Top-1 error rates compared to baseline methods (Cross-Entropy, Focal Loss, Mixup variants), two-stage fine-tuning strategies (CE-DRW, CE-DRS), and state-of-the-art methods (CB-Focal, LDAM-DRW). For example, on CIFAR-10 with an imbalance ratio of 100, BBN achieved a 20.18% error rate, which was 2.79% lower than LDAM-DRW. The two-stage fine-tuning strategies were noted to be effective, often outperforming or matching other state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.2. Performance on iNaturalist Datasets

For the large-scale iNaturalist 2018 and iNaturalist 2017 datasets, BBN also demonstrated superior performance. Using a ResNet-50 backbone, BBN achieved error rates of 33.71% and 36.61% respectively, outperforming CE-DRW, CE-DRS, CB-Focal, and LDAM-DRW. With a 2x training scheduler, BBN further reduced error rates to 30.38% (iNaturalist 2018) and 34.25% (iNaturalist 2017), showing improvements of +3.50% and +3.94% compared to LDAM-DRW (2x). This indicated the method's scalability and effectiveness on real-world, highly imbalanced and fine-grained datasets.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Different Samplers for the Re-balancing Branch:** Experiments compared the reversed sampler against uniform and balanced samplers for the re-balancing branch. The reversed sampler, which disproportionately samples tail classes, yielded a significantly lower error rate (17.82% on CIFAR-10-IR50) compared to uniform (21.31%) and balanced (21.06%) samplers. This supports the design choice of prioritizing tail classes in this branch.
- **Different Cumulative Learning Strategies (Adaptors for $\alpha$):** Various strategies for generating the trade-off parameter `alpha` were tested, including equal weight, β-distribution, parabolic increment, linear decay, cosine decay, and the proposed parabolic decay. Decay strategies (linear, cosine, parabolic) consistently outperformed constant `alpha` or incrementing `alpha` methods. The parabolic decay, `1 - (T/T_max)^2`, achieved the best performance (17.82% error on CIFAR-10-IR50). This validates the intuition that learning universal patterns should precede focusing on tail data, and the specific decay schedule is beneficial.

#### 4.4. Validation Experiments of Proposals

- **Evaluations of Feature Quality:** To validate the hypothesis that re-balancing can damage representation learning, the feature quality of BBN's conventional branch (BBN-CB) was compared to CE-trained models and re-balancing methods (RW, RS). BBN-CB achieved comparable performance to CE (58.89% error vs. 58.62% on CIFAR-100-IR50 when retraining classifiers), indicating that BBN preserves the representation capacity learned from the original distribution. BBN's re-balancing branch (BBN-RB) also showed better feature quality than standalone RW/RS, which was attributed to weight sharing. Supplementary analysis showed that re-balancing strategies led to less compact intra-class feature distributions, further supporting the claim of damaged representation.
- **Visualization of Classifier Weights:** Analysis of the `L2`-norm of classifier weights on CIFAR-10-IR50 showed that BBN's combined classifier (`BBN-ALL`) exhibited more uniform `L2`-norms across all classes compared to CE (which reflected the long-tailed distribution) or RW/RS (which had flatter but still larger standard deviations). This indicated that BBN creates a more balanced decision boundary. The conventional branch (`BBN-CB`) displayed `L2`-norm distributions similar to CE, while the re-balancing branch (`BBN-RB`) showed a reversed distribution, confirming their respective roles in learning universal features and modeling tail classes.

- **Comparisons with Ensemble Methods:** BBN was compared against ensemble models where individual models were trained with uniform, balanced, or reversed samplers. BBN consistently achieved lower error rates than these ensemble approaches across all tested datasets. This demonstrated that the integrated, weight-sharing bilateral-branch design with cumulative learning offered a more effective solution than simply combining separately trained models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of long-tailed visual recognition by empirically demonstrating a dual effect of traditional class re-balancing strategies: while they promote classifier learning, they can negatively impact the quality of learned feature representations. This finding provides a new perspective on the challenges of long-tailed data.

The proposed Bilateral-Branch Network (BBN) offers a unified and end-to-end trainable solution that addresses this dual challenge by simultaneously optimizing for both robust feature representation and balanced classifier learning. The cumulative learning strategy, with its adaptive trade-off parameter, allows the network to dynamically shift its learning focus, which is a departure from previous two-stage methods.

The consistent superior performance of BBN across multiple benchmark datasets, including large-scale and fine-grained iNaturalist datasets, indicates its practical utility. The method's first-place win in the iNaturalist 2019 large-scale species classification competition further validates its effectiveness in real-world scenarios. The findings suggest that explicitly decoupling and then adaptively combining strategies for representation and classifier learning can be a more effective paradigm for handling long-tailed data distributions. This work could influence the design of future deep learning architectures and training strategies for imbalanced datasets, potentially extending to other long-tailed problems like object detection.
