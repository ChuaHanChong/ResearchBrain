# ProtoReasoning: Prototypes as the Foundation for Generalizable Reasoning in LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The ProtoReasoning framework enhances Large Language Models' (LLMs) generalizable reasoning by training them on abstract, verifiable prototypes derived from formal logic (Prolog) and planning (PDDL). This approach led to performance improvements of up to 6.4% on planning tasks and 4.7% on logical reasoning, demonstrating that learning structural reasoning patterns facilitates cross-domain transfer.

## Problem

- Despite empirical success in complex reasoning tasks, the underlying mechanisms enabling cross-domain generalization in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) remain poorly understood.
- There is a need to develop a systematic framework to enhance LLMs' generalizable reasoning abilities beyond empirical observation.
- Current methods often lack scalable and verifiable means of generating high-quality reasoning data to train models effectively.

## Method

- The ProtoReasoning framework leverages abstract, verifiable ==prototypes== (==Prolog== for logical reasoning and ==PDDL== for planning) to represent fundamental reasoning patterns.
- An automated, model-driven pipeline constructs diverse and verifiable prototype-based reasoning problems, using interpreters like SWI-Prolog and VAL for ground-truth derivation and verification.
- A ==three-phase Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) recipe== on a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture (teacher model distillation, difficulty stratification, quality filtration) is used to train LLMs on these datasets.

## Results

- ProtoReasoning improved logical reasoning performance on Enigmata-Eval by **4.7%** (from **37.3%** to **42.0%**).
- Planning tasks showed gains of **6.4%** on Nexus-Hard (from **53.1%** to **59.5%**) and **6.3%** on Dedicated Task Planning (from **46.7%** to **53.0%**).
- The framework demonstrated out-of-domain generalization, achieving a **4.0%** gain on MMLU (from **82.7%** to **86.7%**) and a **1.0%** gain on AIME2024 (from **72.0%** to **73.0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-domain generalization in Large Language Models (LLMs) originates from shared abstract reasoning prototypes, which represent fundamental, domain-agnostic reasoning patterns.
> - Training LLMs on formal prototype representations (e.g., Prolog, PDDL) is as effective as direct natural language training for enhancing generalizable reasoning on structurally similar problems.
> - Effective generalization through prototype training requires explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, as it homogenizes and reinforces reasoning paths.
