# ProtoReasoning: Prototypes as the Foundation for Generalizable Reasoning in LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The ProtoReasoning framework enhances Large Language Models' (LLMs) generalizable reasoning by training them on abstract, verifiable prototypes derived from formal logic (Prolog) and planning (PDDL). This approach led to performance improvements of up to 6.4% on planning tasks and 4.7% on logical reasoning, demonstrating that learning structural reasoning patterns facilitates cross-domain transfer.

## Problem

- Despite empirical success in complex reasoning tasks, the underlying mechanisms enabling cross-domain generalization in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) remain poorly understood.
- There is a need to develop a systematic framework to enhance LLMs' generalizable reasoning abilities beyond empirical observation.
- Current methods often lack scalable and verifiable means of generating high-quality reasoning data to train models effectively.

## Method

- The ProtoReasoning framework leverages abstract, verifiable ==prototypes== (==Prolog== for logical reasoning and ==PDDL== for planning) to represent fundamental reasoning patterns.
- An automated, model-driven pipeline constructs diverse and verifiable prototype-based reasoning problems, using interpreters like SWI-Prolog and VAL for ground-truth derivation and verification.
- A ==three-phase Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) recipe== on a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture (teacher model distillation, difficulty stratification, quality filtration) is used to train LLMs on these datasets.

## Results

- ProtoReasoning improved logical reasoning performance on Enigmata-Eval by **4.7%** (from **37.3%** to **42.0%**).
- Planning tasks showed gains of **6.4%** on Nexus-Hard (from **53.1%** to **59.5%**) and **6.3%** on Dedicated Task Planning (from **46.7%** to **53.0%**).
- The framework demonstrated out-of-domain generalization, achieving a **4.0%** gain on MMLU (from **82.7%** to **86.7%**) and a **1.0%** gain on AIME2024 (from **72.0%** to **73.0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-domain generalization in Large Language Models (LLMs) originates from shared abstract reasoning prototypes, which represent fundamental, domain-agnostic reasoning patterns.
> - Training LLMs on formal prototype representations (e.g., Prolog, PDDL) is as effective as direct natural language training for enhancing generalizable reasoning on structurally similar problems.
> - Effective generalization through prototype training requires explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, as it homogenizes and reinforces reasoning paths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a fundamental challenge in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs): understanding and enhancing their reasoning capabilities, particularly their capacity for **cross-domain generalization**. Recent advancements in LRMs, often trained with long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), have shown impressive empirical success in complex tasks like mathematics and coding, with a surprising ability to generalize to seemingly disparate domains (e.g., STEM, creative writing, other logical puzzles). However, the underlying mechanisms for this generalization remain poorly understood.

The paper posits a novel hypothesis: cross-domain generalization stems from **shared abstract reasoning prototypes**. These prototypes represent fundamental reasoning patterns that transcend specific domains, suggesting that diverse tasks are grounded in common logical structures. This hypothesis challenges the purely "surface-level" understanding of LLM reasoning and proposes a more abstract, structural basis for intelligence transfer.

This work builds upon and contributes to several key areas:
- **Long CoT and Reasoning Models:** It extends the work on Long CoT by attempting to explain *why* it leads to generalization, moving beyond empirical observation to a mechanistic hypothesis. It leverages the success of RLVR approaches (like those used in DeepSeek-R1, Seed-Thinking-v1.5, Logic-RL) by demonstrating how highly verifiable, prototype-based data can effectively serve as the "verifiable rewards" or high-quality supervised signals needed for robust training.
- **Neuro-Symbolic AI:** The paper explicitly positions itself within the growing trend of leveraging symbolic representations within LLMs. Unlike previous work that might use code or domain-specific languages as intermediate reasoning steps (e.g., Program-Aided Language Models (PAL), Logic-LM), ProtoReasoning focuses on these symbolic representations (Prolog, PDDL) as *prototypes* for learning generalizable reasoning patterns, rather than just as tools for problem-solving. This shift emphasizes the structural isomorphism between problems.
- **Automated Data Generation and Curriculum Learning:** The framework's automated construction pipeline and verification system contribute to the broader effort of generating high-quality, scalable synthetic data for LLM training. The difficulty stratification and quality filtration steps echo principles of curriculum learning, aiming to optimize the training process.
- **Understanding Generalization:** By investigating whether training on abstract prototypes leads to better generalization than training on natural language, the paper directly contributes to the theoretical understanding of how LLMs acquire and transfer reasoning abilities. It suggests that models learn to abstract underlying problem structures, which then facilitates transfer.

In essence, ProtoReasoning aims to bridge the gap between the empirical success of powerful LRMs and a deeper theoretical understanding of their generalization capabilities, by proposing and validating the role of abstract reasoning prototypes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **understand the underlying mechanisms that enable cross-domain generalization in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)** and, based on this understanding, **develop a framework to enhance LLMs' generalizable reasoning abilities**.

The motivation for this work stems from an intriguing observation in recent LLM research:
- **Empirical Success, Lacking Explanation:** State-of-the-art LRMs, particularly those trained with Long Chain-of-Thought (Long CoT) reasoning and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), exhibit remarkable cross-domain generalization. Models trained on mathematical and coding tasks can surprisingly transfer capabilities to STEM domains or logical puzzles. While these empirical successes are significant, the "why" behind this transfer remains largely unknown.
- **The Hypothesis of Reasoning Prototypes:** The authors hypothesize that this transfer capability arises from the existence of "shared abstract reasoning prototypes." These are fundamental, domain-agnostic reasoning patterns or thinking structures that capture the essence of problems, minimizing superficial representational differences. If two problems share an underlying reasoning structure, generalization should naturally emerge.
- **Leveraging Isomorphic Reasoning:** The core motivation is to validate this hypothesis and, if true, to leverage these isomorphic reasoning processes to systematically improve LLM reasoning. By training models on problems expressed in abstract, verifiable "prototype systems," the aim is to instill a deeper understanding of fundamental logical structures, which can then generalize across various concrete problem instantiations in natural language.

Specifically, the paper seeks to demonstrate that training LLMs on formalized prototype representations (Prolog for logical reasoning and PDDL for planning) leads to:
1. **Enhanced performance** on tasks within the prototype's core domain (logical reasoning, planning).
2. **Improved cross-domain generalization** to other reasoning tasks (e.g., general reasoning, mathematics).
3. **Confirmation of the hypothesis** that learning in prototype space facilitates enhanced generalization to structurally similar problems, validating that reasoning prototypes are foundational for transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **ProtoReasoning**, a framework designed to enhance LLM reasoning by leveraging scalable and verifiable prototypical representations. The framework is applied to two core domains: logical reasoning using Prolog and planning using PDDL.

The ProtoReasoning framework consists of two key modules and a comprehensive training recipe:

#### 3.1. The ProtoReasoning Framework Overview

The core idea is to transform natural language problems into abstract prototype representations, use a robust verification system for feedback, and train LLMs on this prototype space to achieve generalizable reasoning.

#### 3.2. Logic Prototype Representations (Prolog)

Prolog (Programming in Logic) is chosen as the prototype for logical reasoning due to its declarative nature, expressiveness (capturing relational reasoning and constraint satisfaction), and verifiability via mature interpreters (SWI-Prolog).

- **Prolog-based Logic Prototype Constructor:** This is an automated, four-stage, model-driven pipeline for creating diverse and verifiable Prolog reasoning problems:
 1. **Data Initialization:** Collect natural language reasoning problems (structured Q&A, unstructured narratives).
 2. **Prototype Transformation:** Use prompt engineering with LLMs to convert natural language problems into formal Prolog representations. Standardize interpreter outputs to JSON.
 3. **Data Evolution:** Employ prompt engineering to systematically increase problem complexity while maintaining JSON output constraints.
 4. **Answer Derivation:** Use the SWI-Prolog interpreter to derive ground-truth answers. This interpreter-based approach removes reliance on manual annotations and ensures correctness, enabling large-scale, automated data generation.
 The output is a dataset $D_{Prolog} = \{⟨Q_{Prolog}, A⟩_i\}$, where $Q_{Prolog}$ is the Prolog problem and $A$ is its verified JSON answer.

- **Prolog-based Verification System:** All reference answers and model predictions are constrained to structured JSON dictionaries. This enables rigorous and consistent evaluation by comparing the JSON outputs of the Prolog interpreter with the model's generated JSON. A specific prompt template guides the model to produce output in the required format.

#### 3.3. Planning Prototype Representations (PDDL)

PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language) is chosen for planning tasks due to its ability to model state transition systems (state representations, actions with preconditions/effects, state transitions) in a way that aligns with human planning cognition.

- **PDDL-based Planning Prototype Constructor:** Problems and optimal solutions are generated using PDDL-Generator and FastDownward, based on International Planning Competition (IPC) benchmarks. Three distinct task types are created:
 1. **Plan Generation:** Model generates a complete action sequence from initial to goal state.
 2. **Plan Completion:** Model fills in missing steps in a partial action sequence.
 3. **Plan Reordering:** Model reorders unordered action steps into a valid sequence.
 The dataset is structured as $D_{PDDL} = \{⟨Q_{PDDL}, P_{ref}⟩_i\}$, where $Q_{PDDL}$ is the PDDL problem and $P_{ref}$ is the task-specific reference.

- **PDDL-based Verification System:** Unlike Prolog, PDDL requires specialized validation because multiple valid plans can exist. VAL (PDDL Plan Validator) is used, with custom procedures for each task type:
 1. **Plan Generation:** Any VAL-verified plan is accepted. For optimization tasks, the plan must also meet optimality criteria established by FastDownward.
 2. **Plan Completion:** The model's output must pass VAL verification and incorporate all actions from the given partial plan in their exact positions.
 3. **Plan Reordering:** The solution must pass VAL verification and contain the exact same set of actions as specified in $P_{ref}$.
 Similar to Prolog, specific prompt templates are used to guide model generation for these tasks.

#### 3.4. Model Training Recipe

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is employed with a three-phase process on a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture (150B total parameters, 15B activated):

1. **Teacher Model Distillation:** A high-performance LLM (Deepseek-R1) generates explicit reasoning chains (Chain-of-Thought, CoT) for the initial datasets, creating augmented datasets ($D^{Aug}_{Prolog}$, $D^{Aug}_{PDDL}$). The base model is fine-tuned on this CoT-enriched data.
2. **Difficulty Stratification:** The model from phase 1 is used with rejection sampling to classify problems by difficulty (Challenging, Intermediate, Elementary based on pass rate). Instances with 0% or 100% pass rates are excluded, and an enhanced model is trained on this stratified dataset.
3. **Quality Filtration:** A final round of rejection sampling with the refined model from phase 2 creates the definitive, high-quality training dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated significant improvements in reasoning capabilities across various benchmarks, validating the ProtoReasoning framework and its underlying hypothesis.

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The ProtoReasoning framework was evaluated against a baseline model (trained on a standard 100K diverse samples from the Seed Project). The training involved 4,196 high-quality Prolog problems and 2,424 PDDL tasks.

- **Logical Reasoning (Enigmata-Eval):** ProtoReasoning improved performance from 37.3% to **42.0% (↑4.7%)**. This suggests that training with logic prototypes effectively enhances the model's ability to apply fundamental reasoning patterns. Category-wise, the largest gain was in Crypto (+11.0%), followed by Graph (+9.0%), and Logic (+3.3%), with smaller but consistent gains across other categories.
- **Planning Tasks:** Significant improvements were observed:
 - **Nexus-Hard (Function Calling/Planning):** From 53.1% to **59.5% (↑6.4%)**.
 - **Dedicated Task Planning (Internal Seed Project):** From 46.7% to **53.0% (↑6.3%)**.
 These consistent gains indicate effective transfer of planning skills learned through prototype-based training to various manifestations of planning problems.
- **Out-of-Domain Generalization:** ProtoReasoning also demonstrated strong generalization to domains beyond its core training:
 - **MMLU (General Reasoning):** From 82.7% to **86.7% (↑4.0%)**.
 - **AIME2024 (Mathematical Reasoning):** From 72.0% to **73.0% (↑1.0%)**.
 These results confirm the effectiveness of ProtoReasoning not only in logical reasoning and planning but also in broader general knowledge and mathematical reasoning contexts.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Enigmata-Eval benchmark specifically investigated the effectiveness of prototype representations compared to natural language representations on structurally similar problems. A matched training corpus of 453 samples, available in both Prolog and natural language, was used.

- **Prototype vs. Natural Language Training:**
 - Training with **Prolog representation (+Prolog)** showed a 19.0% improvement on the "Prototype Transfer Set" (natural language versions of problems used for Prolog training) and 5.6% on the "Development Set" (unseen Enigmata-Eval problems), relative to the baseline.
 - Training with **Natural Language representation (+NL)** on the same matched problems showed a 22.9% improvement on the "Prototype Transfer Set" and 6.5% on the "Development Set."
 - **Conclusion:** The comparable performance between training with Prolog prototypes and direct natural language training on structurally similar problems confirms that prototype training is similarly effective and indeed consolidates the generalization capability of the prototype.
- **Role of CoT:** Training with Prolog prototypes *without* Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning showed dramatically reduced performance (+6.7% on transfer set, +1.1% on dev set compared to baseline), highlighting that effective generalization through prototypes requires explicit reasoning processes, as they benefit from homogenized reasoning paths.
- **Category-wise Analysis:** For categories with sufficient samples, Prolog prototype training achieved comparable or even superior performance to natural language representation, demonstrating strong generalization. An exception was the "Logic" category's transfer set, where limited samples led to unstable and slightly worse performance than the baseline, emphasizing the need for sufficient data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ProtoReasoning framework and its findings offer several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of LLM research and development:

1. **Validation of the Reasoning Prototype Hypothesis:** The most significant contribution is the empirical validation of the hypothesis that shared abstract reasoning prototypes form the foundation for cross-domain generalization in LLMs. This moves beyond merely observing generalization to providing a plausible underlying mechanism, enriching the theoretical understanding of LRM capabilities. It suggests that LLMs can learn to abstract fundamental logical structures, which is a key characteristic of general intelligence.
2. **Automated, Scalable, and Verifiable Data Generation:** ProtoReasoning introduces a robust framework for automatically constructing large-scale, high-quality, and verifiable reasoning datasets using formal languages like Prolog and PDDL. This is crucial for scaling up LLM training, as it reduces reliance on costly and error-prone human annotation, allowing for the synthesis of arbitrarily complex problems with guaranteed correctness. This approach provides a powerful alternative to traditional data collection methods for reasoning tasks.
3. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The consistent performance improvements across various logical, planning, general reasoning, and mathematical benchmarks demonstrate that training on abstract prototype representations significantly enhances LLMs' ability to transfer reasoning capabilities. This is particularly valuable for developing more robust and versatile AI systems that can adapt to new problems without extensive retraining.
4. **Guidance for Future LLM Architectures and Training:** The findings, especially the importance of CoT in conjunction with prototype training, offer insights into effective training strategies for future LRMs. It suggests that designing LLMs and training methodologies that encourage the learning and application of abstract reasoning patterns will be key to achieving more profound and generalizable intelligence.
5. **New Avenues for Neuro-Symbolic AI:** This work reinforces the value of neuro-symbolic approaches, showing how the strengths of formal logic (precision, verifiability, structure) can be synergistically combined with the pattern recognition and generative power of neural networks. It opens up avenues for more sophisticated hybrid systems where LLMs can operate at both natural language and abstract symbolic levels.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the need for a more rigorous mathematical definition of "reasoning prototypes" and deeper investigation into transfer mechanisms, its empirical successes and theoretical proposition mark a substantial step forward in understanding and improving generalizable reasoning in LLMs. The commitment to open-sourcing the datasets and reproducing results in open-source LLMs further underscores its potential to accelerate community research.
