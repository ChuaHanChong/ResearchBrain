# RSIAgent: Autonomous Exploration for Recursive Self-improvement in New Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> RSIAgent is a training-free multi-agent framework that adapts digital agents to new software environments by autonomously building reusable memory. Curriculum, actor, and verifier agents loop to propose tasks, execute code-as-policy actions, and ground outcomes in environment feedback. A broad-then-deep exploration schedule fills memory, which is then frozen for test-time reuse, lifting open-source models above frontier closed-source systems.

## Problem

- Adapting agents to new environments usually means collecting extra interaction data for further training, which is costly and infeasible in private or continuously changing environments.
- Memory-based context methods mostly store successful trajectories, so they never distill stable causal links between actions, environment conditions, and resulting outcomes into reusable structure.
- Prior agent self-improvement work depends on benchmark scores or labeled reference data as its improvement signal, which is unavailable when an agent faces an unfamiliar environment.

## Method

- Multi-agent harness splits roles: curriculum agent picks what to explore, actor agent runs ==code-as-policy== actions with evolvable memory, verifier agent grounds success judgments in environment feedback while isolated from actor memory.
- **Broad Recursive Self-exploration** runs parallel exploration waves, nominally eight projects with four concurrent, so the curriculum agent can spot knowledge gaps and widen coverage of procedures and failure patterns.
- **Deep Recursive Self-exploration** then escalates difficulty sequentially to expose hidden constraints and boundary conditions; the consolidated memory is frozen and reused at test time with updates disabled.

## Results

- On OSWorld 2.0 offline, partial score rises from **71.97** to **78.98** and binary from **37.80** to **42.68**, beating GPT-6 Astra by **6.38** points using open-source backbones.
- On Agents' Last Exam Near-term, partial moves from **83.75** to **84.82** and binary from **49.25** to **50.75**, exceeding GPT-6 Astra by **2.56** points.
- Ablation over four tasks: full RSI averages **74.54** partial versus **65.52** broad-only and **56.50** deep-only; on GameCraft-Bench quality reaches **61.28** while Play2Code degrades strong games to **51.05**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ordering matters more than depth: refinement from empty memory fell below baseline on two of four tasks, so broad coverage must precede targeted hard-case practice.
> - Most of the frontier-beating margin comes from the harness itself, since the no-RSI variant already exceeds GPT-6 on one benchmark and self-improvement adds only about one point.
> - A verifier that passes locally incomplete work writes bad rules into memory, so stored experience should carry its conditions and uncertainty, not just acceptance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

RSIAgent addresses a central problem in research on digital computer-use agents: how an agent can adapt to unfamiliar software environments, interfaces, tools, and operational constraints after the underlying model has already been trained.

Recent digital agents have improved in visual perception, planning, reasoning, and tool use. Systems such as OSWorld agents, WebVoyager, Cradle, OS-Copilot, and CodeAct-based agents demonstrate that language and vision-language models can operate desktop applications, websites, and specialized software. However, these systems often depend on pretrained knowledge, demonstrations, reinforcement learning, synthetic data, or human-generated interaction traces. Such approaches can be expensive or difficult to deploy in private, proprietary, or frequently changing environments.

A related research direction uses inference-time memory, reflection, reusable skills, and context engineering. These methods allow agents to retain successful procedures or lessons from failures without changing model parameters. RSIAgent extends this line of work by treating adaptation as an active exploration process rather than passive storage of completed trajectories. The system attempts to identify which experiences are informative, verify whether an outcome genuinely satisfies the task, and consolidate the resulting knowledge into reusable records.

The paper also connects to recursive self-improvement research, including systems that modify prompts, skills, agent architectures, or code-generation procedures. Unlike several approaches that use benchmark rewards, labeled reference data, or external performance scores to guide improvement, RSIAgent emphasizes environment feedback and task-conditioned exploration. Its stated contribution is therefore at the intersection of digital agents, persistent memory, multi-agent coordination, autonomous curriculum generation, and inference-time adaptation.

The paper uses the language of “causal discovery” to describe the acquisition of relationships among actions, environmental conditions, and consequences. In practice, the framework does not implement a formal causal inference model. Instead, it uses repeated interaction, controlled variations, failures, verification feedback, and structured memory revision to infer operational rules that are useful for future tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective is to enable a digital agent to improve its performance in a new environment without updating the parameters of the underlying language or vision-language model. The agent first explores the environment, constructs persistent environment-specific memory, and then reuses that memory for downstream tasks.

The motivation rests on several observations. First, pretrained models may know general concepts such as document editing, video processing, or CAD modeling, but they may not know the precise behavior of a particular application. Important details can include application-specific commands, file formats, coordinate systems, rendering settings, hidden state transitions, and verification requirements. Second, merely recording successful trajectories is insufficient because a procedure may work only under specific conditions. A useful memory must preserve constraints, failure modes, uncertainty, and the relationship between an action and the condition under which it is reliable. Third, exploration must be selective. Random interaction can consume substantial time without resolving the assumptions that cause failures.

RSIAgent is designed to address these issues through a recursive curriculum-action-verification loop. The curriculum agent chooses what to practice, the actor agent performs the task, and the verifier agent evaluates the result using environmental evidence. The outcome is then used to update memory and select subsequent exploration tasks.

The authors further divide exploration into two stages. Broad Recursive Self-exploration is intended to establish coverage of an environment by exploring multiple related capabilities in parallel. Deep Recursive Self-exploration then concentrates on difficult cases, unresolved uncertainties, hidden constraints, and boundary conditions. This broad-then-deep organization is intended to combine diversity with targeted refinement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Agent and Memory Formulation

The agent receives a task instruction, observes the environment, and generates actions conditioned on the task, interaction history, and durable memory. The implementation uses a **code-as-policy** formulation: actions are executable Python or Bash programs rather than individual GUI clicks. This allows the agent to inspect files, operate applications, execute scripts, and perform application-specific workflows through a common interface.

Interaction history is reset when the agent moves to a new task, while durable memory persists across tasks. Memory contains environment-specific procedures, reusable scripts, constraints, failure lessons, verification practices, and prior experience records. The model parameters remain fixed throughout exploration and evaluation.

#### 3.2. Three-Agent Harness

The framework separates responsibilities among three roles.

**The actor agent** is the primary task-solving model. It receives the task, current environment observations, execution feedback, and available memory. It generates executable actions and, after verification, proposes memory updates. It can add new procedures, revise earlier rules, remove unsupported advice, or leave memory unchanged.

**The verifier agent** independently evaluates whether the actor’s candidate satisfies the task. It does not receive the actor’s private reasoning, memory, or execution logs. Instead, it inspects the candidate and environmental evidence, including files, application state, rendered artifacts, and execution results. It returns a grounded PASS, FAIL, or, in some configurations, UNVERIFIED judgment. Verification may use checkpoint restoration or reset-and-replay so that inspection does not alter the candidate being scored.

**The curriculum agent** selects future exploration tasks. It considers the target query, current memory, prior outcomes, and actor-provided learning diagnoses. It may propose prerequisite tasks, contrastive variants, failure-driven exercises, and stress tests. It does not directly control canonical memory contents or provide the final candidate grade.

#### 3.3. Broad Recursive Self-exploration

BRS begins with a new environment and an initial memory that is generally empty. The curriculum agent proposes multiple independent projects covering different directions related to the target task. These projects are executed in parallel, with each one using the same immutable pre-wave memory snapshot and an isolated environment.

After the verifier evaluates all projects, actor contexts resume sequentially to distill and reconcile their experiences into canonical memory. The next wave is generated only after these updates have been committed. This design prevents concurrent projects from observing one another’s unfinished work while allowing later curriculum decisions to respond to the retained knowledge.

The reference configuration uses a nominal budget of eight BRS projects, with up to four executed concurrently. The budget is checked between completed waves, so an ongoing wave is not interrupted.

#### 3.4. Deep Recursive Self-exploration

DRS starts after BRS and is sequential. The actor first attempts the target task using the accumulated memory. The verifier assesses the attempt, and the actor consolidates the resulting evidence. The curriculum agent then examines the outcome, learning diagnosis, and updated memory to determine whether another practice task is justified.

After a failure, DRS may generate a practice task designed to distinguish among possible causes of failure. After a success, it may generate a contrastive or stress-test task if the procedure appears overgeneralized or insufficiently tested. The updated memory is used before the next target attempt. Exploration stops when the curriculum agent determines that further practice has insufficient expected value or when it becomes stalled.

#### 3.5. Test-Time Reuse and Evaluation

Once exploration ends, memory is frozen. The curriculum agent is disabled, host memory writeback is disabled, and the actor uses the frozen memory to solve the target task. The final result is scored by the benchmark evaluator rather than by the learning agents.

The main experiments use OSWorld 2.0’s 82-task offline subset and all 67 tasks in the Near-term split of Agents’ Last Exam. Results are reported using mean partial score and binary accuracy. Partial score measures graded task completion, whereas binary accuracy counts only tasks receiving full credit.

The default configuration uses GLM-5.3 as the actor and Kimi-K3 as the verifier and curriculum model, with these roles placed in separate contexts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance

On OSWorld 2.0, the memory-free RSIAgent harness records a partial score of **71.97%** and binary accuracy of **37.80%**. With recursive self-improvement, these increase to **78.98%** and **42.68%**, respectively. This corresponds to gains of 7.01 percentage points in partial score and 4.88 points in binary accuracy.

On Agents’ Last Exam, the baseline obtains **83.75%** partial score and **49.25%** binary accuracy. Full RSIAgent reaches **84.82%** partial score and **50.75%** binary accuracy. The improvements are smaller than on OSWorld, suggesting that the baseline harness already performs relatively well on this benchmark.

The paper compares these figures with published results from closed-source models. RSIAgent’s partial scores exceed the reported GPT-6 Astra results of **72.60%** on OSWorld and **82.26%** on ALE, as well as Claude Opus 5 scores of **70.19%** and **79.54%**. However, the comparison requires qualification. The paper states that the systems use different original harnesses and execution budgets, and the RSIAgent aggregates combine newly explored results with retained baseline scores. On ALE, RSIAgent’s binary accuracy of 50.75% is below GPT-6 Astra’s reported 52.24%. Thus, the claimed advantage is specific to the reported partial-score comparison and does not extend uniformly across all metrics.

#### 4.2. Effect of Exploration Rounds

Three OSWorld tasks show progressive improvements across RSI steps. By step eight, the video-editing task T044 reaches 100%, the presentation-repair task T049 reaches 80%, and the railway-booking task T065 reaches 100%. The authors interpret these trajectories as evidence that accumulated memory can resolve discrete bottlenecks after several rounds of practice.

The case studies illustrate different types of learned knowledge. For T049, a failed repair teaches the system that preserving dimensions does not necessarily prohibit repositioning corrupted figure boxes. A later successful practice records how to map reference geometry to slide elements and connect arrows to precise anchors. For T044, memory teaches the use of Shotcut’s native crop operation, rather than generic filters, and records checks for project structure, export settings, dimensions, and visual fidelity. For T065, exploration identifies dynamic table-width changes, the need to re-detect GUI geometry after each state transition, and the difference between a paid booking and a valid itinerary.

#### 4.3. Ablation of Broad and Deep Exploration

An ablation on four OSWorld tasks compares the full method with broad-only, deep-only, and no-RSI variants. Full RSI obtains a mean partial score of **74.54%**, compared with **65.52%** for BRS without DRS, **56.50%** for DRS without BRS, and the lower memory-free baseline.

Broad-only exploration improves over the baseline on every task in the ablation. Deep-only exploration performs below the baseline on two tasks, indicating that targeted refinement from an empty memory can be ineffective when the system lacks broad prerequisite knowledge. The results support the paper’s claim that broad coverage should generally precede focused refinement.

#### 4.4. Game Development

On 40 GameCraft-Bench tasks, RSIAgent improves the overall quality score for every tested base generator. For example, with Codex plus GPT-5.5, the baseline overall score is 52.77, RSIAgent without recursive experience reaches 57.84, and full RSIAgent reaches 61.28. With Kimi-K2.6, the corresponding scores are 31.28, 42.61, and 46.37. Similar patterns occur for GLM-5.3-Flash and Qwen3.8-27B.

Compared with Play2Code, RSIAgent provides more consistent improvements for both weaker and stronger starting games. The experiment indicates that reusable diagnostic and editing knowledge can transfer across game-development tasks. The evaluation is nevertheless limited to a random sample of 40 tasks and uses a separate configuration from the computer-use benchmarks.

#### 4.5. Failure Modes

The authors identify three main limitations in the learning loop. Insufficiently targeted exploration may fail to test the assumptions responsible for an error. Incomplete verification may approve an artifact that satisfies a local check but violates the full benchmark rubric. Unreliable memory consolidation may turn an incorrectly verified decision into a general rule, causing future failures. These findings show that recursive learning can amplify errors as well as correct them.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RSIAgent suggests that adaptation of digital agents can be achieved through structured inference-time interaction rather than immediate fine-tuning. Its main practical implication is that an agent deployed in a specialized software environment could build a reusable operational manual by exploring permitted tasks, testing procedures, and recording verified outcomes. This could reduce dependence on human demonstrations and enable adaptation to private or changing interfaces.

The framework also provides a modular way to study agent self-improvement. Curriculum generation, execution, verification, and memory consolidation can be analyzed separately, while the broad-then-deep strategy offers a general template for balancing exploration coverage against detailed stress testing. The approach may be relevant to scientific software, engineering tools, games, and other interactive domains.

Its broader usefulness depends on controlling cost and reliability. Exploration requires additional model calls, environment resets, and potentially long execution times. Performance depends on the quality of the verifier, the stopping policy, and the ability of memory reconciliation to preserve scope and uncertainty. The paper also notes risks involving unintended actions, unauthorized access, and privacy leakage. Consequently, deployment would require sandboxing, permission controls, audit logs, and verification mechanisms that are independent of the same model family responsible for acting and learning.
