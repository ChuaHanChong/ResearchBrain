# TDMPBC: Self-Imitative Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Westlake University developed TDMPBC, a self-imitative reinforcement learning framework, which significantly improved humanoid robot control. It achieved approximately a 120% increase in normalized return over baseline TD-MPC2 on HumanoidBench, successfully completing 8 out of 14 locomotion tasks within 2 million training steps.

## Problem

- Controlling humanoid robots with dexterous hands involves high-dimensional state and action spaces, making exploration and exploitation challenging for traditional reinforcement learning (RL) methods.
- The feasible regions for task accomplishment, such as maintaining an upright posture, are extremely narrow compared to the vast state space, leading to sparse rewards and sample inefficiency.
- Existing online RL algorithms exhibit limited effectiveness and efficiency when applied to complex humanoid robot control, often failing to leverage task-relevant states quickly.

## Method

- The ==Self-Imitative Reinforcement Learning (SIRL)== framework was proposed, which augments standard RL by enabling the algorithm to imitate its own potentially high-return trajectories generated during exploration.
- SIRL was integrated into ==TD-MPC2==, a model-based RL algorithm, creating **TDMPBC** (TD-MPC2 augmented with ==Behavior Cloning==) by modifying only the policy training loss function.
- A ==dynamic behavior cloning== term was introduced, whose weight is exponentially adjusted based on the cumulative return of the trajectory, thereby emphasizing learning from past successful experiences.

## Results

- TDMPBC achieved approximately a **120%** increase in normalized return compared to baseline TD-MPC2 on HumanoidBench, with a faster convergence rate and only a **5%** increase in computational overhead.
- The algorithm successfully completed **8 out of 14** locomotion tasks within **2 million** training steps, demonstrating significant improvements in learning foundational movements like walking, running, and balancing.
- While showing improvements in some whole-body manipulation tasks, TDMPBC still struggled with fine-grained dexterous hand control, often finding alternative, less precise strategies to achieve rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The cumulative return of a trajectory serves as a strong indicator of whether the robot has entered a task-relevant region, such as maintaining an upright posture.
> - Prioritizing and imitating one's own high-return trajectories can accelerate the discovery and stabilization of fundamental skills, acting as a form of self-guided exploration.
> - In complex, high-dimensional spaces like those of humanoid robots, converging to a beneficial local optimum (e.g., maintaining balance) is a crucial initial step for broader task completion.
