# 3PoinTr: 3D Point Tracks for Learning Manipulation from Unconstrained Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed 3PoinTr, a method that enables robots to learn manipulation policies from unconstrained human videos by predicting dense 3D point tracks of objects and conditioning a closed-loop policy on these tracks. This approach achieved a 25.0 percentage point higher average success rate on real-world tasks with 20 robot demonstrations compared to existing methods, effectively leveraging human video priors while mitigating the embodiment gap and occlusions.

## Problem

- Training autonomous robots for diverse manipulation tasks is costly due to the reliance on extensive teleoperated robot demonstrations.
- The "embodiment gap" makes it challenging to learn from unconstrained human videos, as human-specific motions are often impractical or unsafe for robots.
- Prior methods for learning from human videos often require restrictive conditions like choreographed movements, manual annotations, or known grasp locations, limiting data utility.

## Method

- A ==two-stage framework==, **3PoinTr**, first predicts dense ==3D point trajectories== of scene objects (excluding human hands/arms) from a single initial observation.
- A closed-loop robot policy is then trained, conditioned on these predicted 3D point tracks, using a ==Perceiver-IO encoder== for track compression and a ==U-Net-based action head== with residual cross-attention.
- The 3D point track predictor employs ==visibility-aware training==, retaining supervision for task-critical points even when partially occluded during human demonstrations.

## Results

- The 3PoinTr 3D point track predictor demonstrated superior performance on real-world tasks, achieving an average error reduction of **28%** for ADE and **44%** for 5% ADE compared to a strong baseline.
- With only **20** action-labeled robot demonstrations, 3PoinTr achieved an average success rate of **64.9%** in simulation and a **25.0 percentage point** higher average success rate in real-world tasks compared to the best baseline (DP3).
- Ablation studies confirmed that 3D point tracks, the Perceiver-IO encoder for compression, and residual cross-attention in the policy network are all vital components for high performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly focusing on 3D point tracks of scene objects, rather than human embodiment points, enables effective learning from unconstrained human videos, bypassing the embodiment gap.
> - A visibility-aware training approach for 3D point track prediction is critical for real-world application, allowing the model to retain supervision for objects that may be temporarily occluded.
> - Conditioning a closed-loop robot policy on dense 3D point track predictions provides a powerful inductive bias, leading to substantially more sample-efficient learning of manipulation skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous robotics aims to develop systems capable of performing diverse tasks across various objects and environments. A common approach for training robots involves collecting expert teleoperated robot demonstrations. However, this method is often expensive and time-consuming. Human videos represent a potentially abundant and cost-effective data source, encoding extensive physical and behavioral knowledge relevant to manipulation tasks. The challenge lies in effectively extracting these priors for robot learning.

A significant hurdle in utilizing human videos is the "embodiment gap," which refers to the kinematic differences between human demonstrators and robotic platforms (e.g., parallel-jaw grippers). These differences can lead to human manipulation strategies that are impractical or unsafe for robots. Furthermore, many existing methods that learn from videos often impose restrictive assumptions, such as requiring choreographed human motions that mimic robot kinematics, predefined keypoints, manual annotations, or known grasp locations. While 3D methods are generally preferred for capturing scene geometry and aligning with a robot's 3D action space, previous 3D approaches in this domain typically necessitate carefully curated demonstrations to mitigate the embodiment gap.

Point track representations, also known as flow or scene flow, have been explored in prior work as a means to encode scene motion in a spatially grounded and interpretable form for robot learning. Recent advancements in point-tracking foundation models have further enabled autonomous extraction of point tracks from videos. However, much of this work either relies on human videos where demonstrators precisely mimic robot kinematics (e.g., forming a claw shape with their hands), or it remains largely confined to 2D representations. The few existing 3D point track methods often cannot learn from unconstrained human videos and frequently require additional biases, such as manual annotations or predefined keypoints.

3PoinTr positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome these limitations. It aims to develop a method that can leverage *unconstrained* human videos, where demonstrators act naturally without needing to mimic robot motions, to pretrain sample-efficient robot policies. This approach intends to reduce the cost of teaching robots new tasks by addressing the embodiment gap and relaxing restrictive data collection requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, named 3PoinTr, for learning manipulation policies from unconstrained human videos. The central motivation is to reduce the dependency on expensive robot demonstrations for training autonomous robots, leveraging the abundance and low cost of human video data.

Specific objectives and the motivations behind them include:

- **Overcoming the Embodiment Gap:** Humans naturally employ manipulation strategies and motions that are often infeasible, inefficient, or dangerous for robots with different kinematics (e.g., parallel-jaw grippers). A key objective is to enable learning from human videos where the demonstrator is free to act naturally, without having to choreograph their movements to align with a robot's capabilities. This motivates the use of an intermediate representation that focuses on object motion rather than human embodiment motion.
- **Relaxing Restrictive Data Assumptions:** Current approaches often necessitate carefully curated human demonstrations, predefined keypoints, manual annotations, or known grasp locations. These requirements limit the scalability and diversity of usable video data. The motivation is to eliminate these restrictive assumptions, allowing for the use of broader, more natural human video datasets.
- **Leveraging 3D Information:** While 2D representations are common, 3D state representations have been shown to improve the sample efficiency of robot learning. The objective is to utilize 3D point tracks to capture scene geometry and align with the robot's 3D action space, thereby enhancing policy learning.
- **Developing a Robust and Flexible Representation:** The research aims to create a robust representation that is agnostic to appearance changes and can flexibly learn from "off-embodiment" demonstrations. This motivates the use of 3D point tracks as they encode scene motion in an interpretable, spatially grounded form, distinct from human-centric visual features.
- **Ensuring Closed-Loop Reactivity:** While an open-loop plan from video can provide high-level guidance, robot policies require reactivity to execution-time errors or perturbations. The objective is to combine the benefits of an open-loop task plan derived from videos with a closed-loop policy, ensuring the robot can adapt during execution.
- **Improving Sample Efficiency in Policy Learning:** Even with useful priors from videos, training robust robot policies typically requires action-labeled robot demonstrations. The goal is to maximize the utility of these limited robot demonstrations by pre-training with dense 3D point tracks, leading to higher success rates with fewer robot-specific data points.

To achieve these objectives, 3PoinTr proposes predicting dense 3D point tracks of the scene, specifically *excluding embodiment points* (human hands/arms), and then conditioning a closed-loop multitask robot policy on these tracks. This approach aims to bridge the embodiment gap by focusing on the desired object motion rather than mimicking human-specific actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

3PoinTr operates in two distinct stages: first, predicting dense 3D point trajectories from a single initial scene observation, and second, training a closed-loop robot policy conditioned on these predicted tracks.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

- **Stage 1: 3D Point Track Prediction:** Given an initial point cloud $P_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$ of $N$ visible scene points (with embodiment points removed) in the camera coordinate frame, the goal is to predict $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T \times 3}$, which represents the future 3D positions of each initial point over a fixed horizon of $T$ timesteps. The model learns a function $f_\theta: P_0 \rightarrow X$.
- **Stage 2: Track-Conditioned Policy Learning:** Given the predicted 3D point tracks $X$ (generated once from $P_0$ at the start of an episode) and a current observation $o_t = (P_t, q_t)$ (current visible scene point cloud with embodiment points removed, and current robot configuration $q_t$), a closed-loop behavior-cloning policy $\pi_\phi: (X, o_t) \rightarrow A$ is trained to predict a short chunk of $H$ actions $A \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D_A}$. The policy is re-queried on the updated $o_t$ after executing a subset of the predicted actions.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture and Training

##### 3.2.1. 3D Point Track Prediction

The 3D point track prediction network processes an input point cloud. It consists of:
- An MLP that embeds visible non-embodiment points into tokens.
- A transformer architecture with standard self-attention, applied multiple times.
- A linear prediction head that outputs the 3D trajectory for each point over the specified horizon $T$.

During training, a fixed number of random input points are subsampled from the initial point cloud. Per-sample unit-sphere normalization is applied. A key feature is the integration of visibility values ($m_i^t \in \{0, 1\}$), which indicate whether point $i$ is visible at timestep $t$. Losses are masked by these visibility values, and an L1 loss on 3D position error is computed only for visible point-timestep pairs. This visibility-aware approach allows the model to retain supervision over partially occluded, task-critical points.

##### 3.2.2. Track-Conditioned Policy Learning

The policy learning stage freezes the pre-trained 3D point-track predictor.
- **Point Track Encoding:** The predicted 3D point tracks $X$ are first processed by a Perceiver-IO architecture. Each predicted trajectory $X_i$ is embedded into a track token $F_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D_F}$ using a shared MLP. A small set of $M$ learnable query tokens then cross-attend to these per-point tokens to produce a compact set of $M$ point track tokens $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D_F}$. These tokens serve as a compressed, open-loop plan.
- **Observation Encoding:** The current point cloud $P_t$ is encoded using a PointNet-style encoder, similar to DP3. The current robot configuration $q_t$ (end-effector position, 6D continuous orientation, and gripper value) is encoded by a small MLP.
- **Global Conditioning:** The flattened point-track tokens, the point cloud features, and the robot configuration features are concatenated and projected into a global conditioning vector. This vector drives a flow-matching action head, which is based on a conditional 1D U-Net architecture.
- **Action Head with Residual Cross-Attention:** The action head predicts an action chunk $A \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D_A}$, where $D_A=10$ (3D position, 6D orientation, 1D gripper command) in the camera coordinate frame. To encourage the action head to utilize the predicted object motion from the point tracks, residual point-track-token cross-attention adapters are inserted into every residual block of the U-Net. These adapters allow the U-Net features to query the point track tokens, with the resulting cross-attention output added back to the U-Net features. The adapters are zero-initialized, meaning they initially contribute nothing, allowing the network to learn their utility during training.
- **Training Details:** Multitask training is employed with task-balanced sampling and per-task loss reweighting (inverse of mean unweighted training loss after a warmup epoch). Input point clouds and action targets are jointly normalized per sample.

#### 3.3. Data Collection

- **Simulation Data:** Robot demonstration videos are collected. Ground-truth 3D point tracks are obtained by tracking rigid body poses, with robot-specific points excluded.
- **Real-World Data:** Unconstrained human demonstration videos are collected. 2D point trajectories and visibility masks are first extracted using a 2D point tracking model. These 2D tracks are then lifted to 3D using depth maps obtained from a stereo-to-depth model. Embodiment points (human arms) are segmented using SAM3 and marked as invisible.
- **Track Processing:** For both simulation and real-world data, the 3D point trajectories (excluding embodiment points and including real-world visibility masks) are reparameterized by normalized time and resampled to fixed lengths. Coarse tracks (10-16 timesteps) are used for training the 3D point-track predictor, while finer tracks (30-50 timesteps) are used for policy training. Real-world point track prediction networks are trained exclusively on human videos.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted in both simulation and on a real UFactory xArm 7 robot with a parallel-jaw gripper. Four simulation tasks and four real-world tasks (e.g., "Open Microwave," "Fold Sock") are used, with varied object positions and orientations. Human demonstrators perform tasks naturally without mimicking the robot's kinematics. Evaluation metrics include 3D Average Displacement Error (ADE) and ADE of the 5% most moving points (5% ADE) for track prediction, and success rate (%) for policy learning.

#### 3.5. Baselines and Ablations

- **3D Point Track Prediction Baseline:** General Flow, retrained on the 3PoinTr dataset (excluding embodiment points).
- **Policy Learning Baselines:**
 - Behavior Cloning: Diffusion Policy (DP) (RGB-conditioned), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) (point cloud-conditioned).
 - Point-track-based (video pretraining): ATM (2D point tracks, trained on robot+human videos), AMPLIFY (latent video dynamics).
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigate the impact of specific design choices: "No Perceiver-IO," "No U-Net Xattn" (removing residual cross-attention), "2D Point Tracks," and "No Extra Videos" (training track predictor only on robot demonstrations).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. 3D Point Track Prediction

- **Real-World Performance:** On real-world tasks, 3PoinTr demonstrated superior performance compared to General Flow in both Average Displacement Error (ADE) and the 5% ADE metric (focusing on actively moving points). 3PoinTr achieved an average error reduction of 28% for ADE and 44% for 5% ADE.
- **Impact of Visibility-Aware Training:** The primary advantage of 3PoinTr in real-world scenarios stems from its visibility-aware training approach. General Flow discards entire trajectories if any point-timestep pair is invisible during preprocessing, which results in a loss of supervision, especially for task-critical objects that may be temporarily occluded during manipulation (e.g., paper in the "Throw Away Paper" task). 3PoinTr, by contrast, retains all trajectories and masks losses only for individual invisible point-timestep pairs, thereby preserving crucial supervision.
- **Simulation Performance:** In simulation, where all points are visible at every timestep and training data is identical, 3PoinTr's simpler single-transformer decoder architecture achieved competitive results with General Flow's more complex architecture. 3PoinTr performed better on two of the four simulation tasks (Block Stack, Open Microwave) and worse on the other two (Right Glass, Pot Lid) in terms of ADE/5% ADE.

#### 4.2. Sample-Efficient Policy Learning

- **Overall Superiority:** 3PoinTr achieved the highest success rate on every task when trained with 20 robot demonstrations. It also achieved the highest average success rate when trained with 50 and 100 demonstrations across both simulated and real-world evaluations.
- **Simulation Results:** With only 20 action-labeled robot demonstrations, 3PoinTr attained an average success rate of 64.9% across simulation tasks. This significantly surpassed baseline methods such as DP3 (29.6%), ATM (31.6%), DP (23.4%), and AMPLIFY (3.1%). The performance gap generally persisted and often widened with 50 and 100 demonstrations.
- **Real-World Results:** On real-world tasks, with 20 robot demonstrations and 50 human videos per task, 3PoinTr achieved a 25.0 percentage point higher average success rate than the best baseline, DP3. For instance, 3PoinTr achieved 20/20 successes on "Open Drawer" and "Right Glass", 18/20 on "Throw Away Paper", and 17/20 on "Fold Sock", whereas DP3 obtained 14/20, 18/20, 9/20, and 14/20 respectively. ATM exhibited notably lower success rates on these tasks.
- **Effectiveness of Priors:** The results suggest that 3PoinTr effectively extracts useful priors from 3D point track predictions.
- **Robustness to Embodiment Gap and Occlusion:** 3PoinTr's ability to outperform ATM and AMPLIFY indicates that explicitly encoding embodiment-specific points is not essential, and removing such biases can be advantageous. ATM's performance suffered from the distribution shift between human and robot demonstrations, and its per-step track re-prediction was vulnerable to occlusions, leading to unrepresented points. 3PoinTr's approach of predicting tracks once from the initial scene mitigates occlusion issues, allowing policies to maintain information about temporarily occluded objects, which was critical in tasks like "Throw Away Paper."
- **Task Specification:** The 3D point track predictions provide clear information about object motion and location, aiding tasks where objects might be small or visually indistinct in raw point clouds, thereby contributing to 3PoinTr's higher success rates in such scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of several design choices:
- **Perceiver-IO Encoder:** Removing the Perceiver-IO encoder (replacing it with mean pooling) significantly reduced average success rate across simulation tasks, indicating its importance for compressing dense point tracks into compact tokens.
- **3D Point Tracks:** Conditioning on 2D point tracks instead of 3D point tracks ("2D Point Tracks" ablation) resulted in a substantial performance decrease, confirming the benefit of 3D representations.
- **Residual U-Net Cross-Attention:** Removing the residual point-track-token cross-attention in the U-Net ("No U-Net Xattn") reduced performance, suggesting that this mechanism effectively biases the action head to utilize predicted object motion.
- **Extra Videos:** The "No Extra Videos" ablation, where the point-track predictor was trained only on robot demonstrations, still performed comparatively well, indicating that 3D point track prediction acts as a useful inductive bias on its own. However, 3PoinTr, utilizing additional human video data, achieved the highest average success rate, demonstrating the benefit of learning from actionless videos for further performance improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

3PoinTr represents an advancement in the field of robot manipulation learning, particularly in its approach to leveraging human video data. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable method for learning robot manipulation policies from *unconstrained* human videos. This addresses a core challenge in robot learning by significantly reducing the reliance on costly and time-consuming teleoperated robot demonstrations.

The potential impacts include:

- **Democratization of Robot Skill Acquisition:** By enabling robots to learn from readily available and diverse human videos, 3PoinTr could substantially lower the barrier to teaching new skills to robots. This could accelerate the deployment of autonomous systems in various domains, from industrial automation to service robotics, where custom robot demonstrations are economically prohibitive.
- **Robustness to Embodiment Differences:** The method successfully mitigates the "embodiment gap" by predicting 3D point tracks of scene objects rather than trying to directly retarget human movements to a robot. This allows human demonstrators to perform tasks naturally, providing richer and more intuitive manipulation priors without the need for choreographed motions or specific kinematic alignments.
- **Improved Generalization and Policy Robustness:** The use of dense 3D point tracks as an intermediate, appearance-invariant representation provides a robust task specification in metric space. This contributes to the development of robot policies that are more resilient to appearance variations, temporary occlusions, and distribution shifts between human demonstrations and robot execution environments. The visibility-aware prediction capability is particularly impactful for handling realistic scenarios where objects may be briefly obscured.
- **Foundation for Large-Scale Learning:** By providing an effective mechanism to extract meaningful priors from unconstrained videos, 3PoinTr lays groundwork for future research aimed at utilizing internet-scale, in-the-wild human interaction data. This could unlock access to an unprecedented volume of diverse manipulation experience for robot learning.
- **Inductive Bias for Robot Learning:** The research demonstrates that learning to predict 3D point tracks serves as a useful inductive bias for behavior cloning, even when only robot demonstrations are used for pretraining. This suggests that the representation itself offers inherent benefits for learning, beyond just enabling the use of additional video data.

While 3PoinTr offers significant contributions, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the current reliance on videos produced under relatively consistent laboratory conditions for sample efficiency. Future work on scaling to internet-scale, in-the-wild videos, handling diverse camera viewpoints and environments, and exploring adaptive temporal action selection strategies are identified as important directions to fully realize its potential.
