# SpaceTools: Tool-Augmented Spatial Reasoning via Double Interactive RL

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaceTools, a framework from NVIDIA researchers, enhances Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for metrically precise spatial reasoning and robotic control by enabling learned coordination of external computer vision and robotic tools. It leverages a Double Interactive Reinforcement Learning (DIRL) paradigm and a distributed Toolshed infrastructure, achieving state-of-the-art performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks and an 86% success rate on real-world robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack the metrically precise spatial reasoning, 3D awareness, and understanding of physical affordances required for embodied applications like robotics.
- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggles with the combinatorially large action search space when attempting to coordinate multiple heterogeneous tools, hindering effective policy discovery.
- Prior tool-augmented VLM approaches often rely on handcrafted prompting or fixed tool pipelines, limiting a VLM's adaptability and ability to discover optimal tool-use patterns for diverse tasks.

## Method

- A two-phase Double Interactive Reinforcement Learning (**DIRL**) framework trains VLMs to effectively use and coordinate multiple tools, starting with supervised fine-tuning from diverse teachers (single-tool IRL and frontier LLM demonstrations) followed by interactive RL.
- The Toolshed infrastructure, a scalable and distributed system, efficiently deploys and manages diverse, compute-heavy computer vision and robotic tools during VLM training and inference.
- The VLM is enabled to dynamically select, sequence, and call external tools (e.g., segmentation, depth estimation, 3D bounding box fitting, grasp generation) and robotic action tools to solve complex spatial reasoning and manipulation tasks.

## Results

- SpaceTools achieves state-of-the-art performance on five spatial reasoning benchmarks (RoboSpatial-Home, BLINK, RefSpatial, CVBench, BOP-ASK), surpassing baselines and frontier models like Gemini-ER 1.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and GPT-5.
- The framework enables a 7-DOF robot to achieve an **86%** success rate on real-world pick-and-place tasks, demonstrating effective transfer from learned spatial reasoning to embodied control.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both teaching phases and the second-stage interactive RL for optimal multi-tool coordination and overall performance, significantly outperforming non-interactive baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A strong initial policy, established through a 'teaching phase' that combines single-tool Interactive RL and demonstrations from frontier models, is crucial for stable multi-tool RL in a large search space.
> - Interactive Reinforcement Learning (IRL) during the 'exploration phase' allows VLMs to refine multi-tool coordination, learn adaptive strategies, and develop error recovery behaviors, leading to improved generalization.
> - A decoupled, asynchronous, and resource-isolated infrastructure (Toolshed) is essential for efficiently integrating and scaling diverse, computationally intensive vision and robotics tools with VLM training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated qualitative visual understanding capabilities. However, a limitation for their application in embodied systems, such as robotics, is their struggle with metrically precise spatial reasoning, particularly regarding geometric relationships, spatial configurations, 3D awareness, and physical affordances. Existing research has explored augmenting VLMs with tools to enhance these capabilities. Approaches have included fine-tuning on task-specific datasets, which often requires extensive data collection and engineering, or employing fixed, handcrafted prompting strategies and predefined tool pipelines, which can limit a VLM's ability to discover optimal tool-use patterns.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a method to enable VLMs to learn grounded reasoning and tool use, as demonstrated by works involving a single visual tool. However, scaling conventional RL to coordinate multiple heterogeneous tools presents a challenge due to the combinatorially large search space, which can hinder effective policy discovery. This paper addresses this gap by introducing a framework designed to enable VLMs to learn multi-tool coordination through interactive RL, while also providing a scalable infrastructure for managing diverse, compute-intensive tools. The work aims to bridge the gap between VLMs' qualitative understanding and the precise, multi-step geometric reasoning required for practical embodied applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to meet the demands of embodied applications, particularly robotics. The authors identify several motivations and specific objectives:

- **Overcoming VLM Limitations in Spatial Reasoning:** Current VLMs exhibit strong general visual understanding but often lack the metrically precise and 3D-aware spatial reasoning necessary for tasks such as estimating distances, determining object-location fit, understanding occlusions, and predicting grasping affordances. The motivation is to enable VLMs to answer complex geometric questions and support decision-making for physical actions.
- **Leveraging External Tools for Augmentation:** Instead of solely relying on internal model capabilities or extensive architectural modifications, the work proposes empowering VLMs to call external computer vision and robotics modules (tools). This approach is motivated by the potential of tools to provide precise measurements, intermediate geometric representations, and to integrate specialized computer vision models (e.g., for dense prediction) that may not be directly compatible with VLM architectures. Tools can combine the strengths of heterogeneous models to augment base-model capabilities.
- **Enabling Adaptive and Interactive Tool Use:** Prior tool-augmented approaches often rely on handcrafted prompting strategies or fixed tool pipelines. This limits a VLM's adaptability and ability to discover optimal tool-use patterns for diverse and complex spatial reasoning tasks. The motivation is to allow VLMs to learn to select, sequence, and coordinate multiple tools dynamically based on the task context, rather than following predefined scripts.
- **Addressing the Challenge of Multi-Tool Reinforcement Learning:** Applying standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) to scenarios involving numerous tools results in a prohibitively large action search space, making it difficult for agents to explore and learn effective policies. A key motivation is to develop a training paradigm that can overcome this challenge, enabling stable and effective learning of multi-tool coordination.
- **Developing Scalable Infrastructure for Interactive Training:** Interactive RL with compute-intensive tools demands efficient real-time access to these tools during training. Existing setups often tightly couple tool usage with the training loop or use web APIs that lack the throughput for VLM interactive learning with images. The motivation is to create a robust, scalable platform that can efficiently serve diverse, computationally demanding tools on demand, decoupling tool resource management from RL or inference workloads.
- **Facilitating Transfer to Embodied Control:** Ultimately, the enhanced spatial reasoning should translate into tangible actions in the physical world. A motivation is to demonstrate that VLMs, through learned tool coordination, can directly control robotic systems to perform real-world manipulation tasks, establishing a closed perception-action loop guided by language.

These motivations collectively drive the development of Double Interactive Reinforcement Learning (DIRL) and the Toolshed infrastructure, aiming to create VLMs that are both intelligently agentic and spatially precise.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a novel two-phase reinforcement learning framework, Double Interactive Reinforcement Learning (DIRL), supported by a distributed infrastructure called Toolshed.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Spatial reasoning is formulated as a sequential decision-making problem. A VLM policy, $\pi_\theta$, interacts with a set of external tools, $\mathcal{Q}_{tools}$, to respond to a user query, $\mathcal{I}$ (e.g., an image-text pair or a robotic manipulation task). The interaction proceeds in multiple turns up to a maximum $T_{max}$ steps, culminating in a final answer $A_{final}$. At each step $t$, the VLM receives the historical context $h_t$, generates a response $a_t$, which may include tool calls, and if tools are called, their outputs are appended to the context for the next turn. The VLM is structured to use `<think>`, `<tool_call>`, and `<answer>` tags to organize its reasoning, tool invocations, and final output.

#### 3.2. Double Interactive Reinforcement Learning (DIRL)

DIRL is a two-phase training framework designed to enable effective multi-tool use without suffering from the large search space of naive multi-tool RL.

- **Teaching Phase:** This phase establishes foundational tool-use capabilities through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The teaching dataset is constructed from two sources:
 - **Single-tool Interactive RL (IRL) Teacher:** A base VLM is trained using IRL to master a single "pointing" tool for spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., spatial relationship, compatibility, relative depth). This constrained environment allows IRL to converge reliably and produce grounded reasoning demonstrations. Approximately 2,000 trajectories are generated by this teacher.
 - **Universal Teacher:** A frontier model (Claude Sonnet 4.5) is prompted to solve a broader range of spatial reasoning and robot manipulation tasks using the full set of available tools (e.g., pointing, segmentation, 3D bounding box). Only trajectories leading to correct solutions are retained. Approximately 6,000 trajectories are generated this way.
 - The combined 8,000 trajectories form the teaching dataset. SFT is performed on a base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) using cross-entropy loss to imitate these teachers' reasoning and tool-use behaviors.

- **Exploration Phase:** Following SFT, Interactive Reinforcement Learning (IRL) is resumed on the SFT-initialized VLM, now with access to the full toolset across all tasks. This phase allows the model to refine its multi-tool coordination strategies through interactive exploration and feedback. The strong initialization from the teaching phase is intended to prevent exploration collapse in the large multi-tool action space.

- **Policy Update (GRPO):** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed for RL training. For each input, multiple rollouts are launched asynchronously under the current policy $\pi_\theta$. Task rewards for these rollouts are collected and standardized into relative advantages. The policy is updated by optimizing a GRPO objective that incorporates these advantages and applies KL regularization against a reference policy (the SFT-trained model) to maintain stability.

#### 3.3. Toolshed Infrastructure

Toolshed is a scalable, distributed framework developed to efficiently deploy and manage diverse, compute-heavy computer vision and robotic tools during both training and inference. Its design addresses bottlenecks by:
- **Decoupled Execution:** Tool invocations run independently of the VLM's main inference loop to avoid blocking computations.
- **Asynchronous Processing:** Multiple parallel instances can serve the same tool asynchronously, enabling high throughput.
- **Resource and Environment Isolation:** Each tool instance is assigned isolated resources and runs in a dedicated Python environment, resolving dependency conflicts.
- **Multimodal Data Passing:** Supports seamless exchange of text, images, and structured variables (e.g., 3D point clouds) between the VLM and tools, even across different hardware.

**Tools Provided:**
- **Vision Tools:** Segmentation (SAM2), two pointing detectors (RoboRefer-based `point1` and Molmo-based `point2`), monocular depth estimation (DepthPro `depth_estimator`), 3D bounding box fitting (`compute_bbox`), grasp generation (`compute_grasp`), image operations (`image_ops`), and a Python `code_executor`.
- **Robotic Tools:** `capture_image`, `capture_depth`, `execute_grasp`, and `place_object`. Mock robot tools are also provided for data generation and training, simulating successful actions.

#### 3.4. Rewards

Task-specific, normalized rewards are designed based on the correctness or geometric consistency of the final answer $A_{final}$ against ground-truth labels.
- **Multiple Choice Questions:** Binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect).
- **2D Bounding Boxes:** Mean IoU (MIoU) between predicted and ground-truth boxes.
- **Pointing:** Normalized Negative Distance to Centroid (NNDC), clipped with binary accuracy.
- **Pose Estimation:** IoU between convex hulls of predicted and ground-truth 2D projected corners.
- **Grasp Estimation:** Normalized Negative Coordinate Error (NNCE), rewarding accurate geometric grasp alignment.

The training uses Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct as the base model, with a system prompt defining tools and expected interaction formats. The LLaMA-Factory platform is used for SFT, and the VERL framework integrates Toolshed for RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the SpaceTools framework.

#### 4.1. Spatial Reasoning Performance

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Benchmarks:** SpaceTools achieves SOTA performance across a suite of spatial reasoning benchmarks, including RoboSpatial-Home, BLINK, RefSpatial, CVBench, and BOP-ASK.
 - For instance, SpaceTools surpasses Gemini-ER 1.5 by 7.5% on RoboSpatial, Claude Sonnet 4.5 by 24.4% on pose estimation, and GPT-5 by 8.3% on grasp prediction.
- **Superiority over Tool-Free Baselines:** Tool-augmented training with SpaceTools-3B yields substantially higher accuracy on all tasks compared to tool-free fine-tuning variants of the same base model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) trained on the same data. It shows a +12% improvement over tool-free SFT and +16% over tool-free RL on RoboSpatial.
- **Adaptive and Corrective Behaviors:** SpaceTools demonstrates the ability to dynamically adapt its reasoning and tool-use strategies to individual tasks. It composes multiple tools for complex tasks like pose or grasp prediction and learns corrective behaviors, such as falling back to self-estimation or switching to alternative pointing tools when a primary tool fails or provides uncertain detections. This indicates the model learns internal procedures for tool selection, ordering, and error recovery, rather than relying on fixed pipelines.

#### 4.2. Real-World Robot Manipulation

- **High Success Rate:** SpaceTools achieves an 86% success rate on real-world pick-and-place tasks using a 7-DOF robot, demonstrating effective transfer from spatial reasoning to embodied control.
- **Closed Perception-Action Loop:** The VLM orchestrates a closed perception-action loop by alternating between vision-based perception tools (e.g., pointing, segmentation, depth, grasp estimation) and robotic action tools (e.g., image capture, grasp execution).
- **Outperforms Frontier Models:** SpaceTools outperforms proprietary frontier models like GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 equipped with the same Toolshed integration, both in success rate and Time-to-First-Movement (TTFM). Other models sometimes struggle with coherent tool chaining or generating valid arguments.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study Results

- **Contribution of IRL-trained Teacher:** Removing the IRL-trained teacher from the teaching phase leads to a performance drop, particularly on tasks requiring fine spatial grounding like RefSpatial and RoboSpatial.
- **Contribution of Universal Teacher:** Removing the universal teacher also degrades performance, especially on tasks requiring multi-tool composition (e.g., pose estimation, which combines segmentation, depth, and 3D bounding box tools).
- **Importance of Stage 2 IRL:** Eliminating the Stage 2 interactive RL phase (S2-IRL) negatively impacts performance across RoboSpatial, RefSpatial, and pose tasks, indicating its role in refining multi-tool coordination.
- **Superiority of Interactive RL:** Both non-interactive baselines, Tool SFT and Tool Non-Interactive Reinforcement Learning (Tool NIRL), underperform DIRL significantly (mean improvements of +13.4% and +14.4% respectively). This suggests that interactive RL is crucial for teaching VLMs to reason over complex tool sequences.
- **Tool-IRL Generalization:** An ablation study shows that IRL with a single pointing tool, even without the full DIRL framework, leads to robust performance on its in-domain benchmark (RoboSpatial) and generalizes unexpectedly well to unseen tasks (RefSpatial), where other fine-tuning methods achieve zero accuracy.

#### 4.4. Toolshed Efficiency

- Toolshed demonstrates improved latency and throughput for compute-heavy tool pipelines compared to naive HTTP-based deployments, especially under contention. This is attributed to its decoupled execution, asynchronous processing, and resource isolation features.

#### 4.5. Enhancement of Proprietary Models

- Integrating Toolshed with proprietary VLMs like GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 shows performance gains on tasks requiring precise spatial grounding or explicit geometric reasoning (e.g., RefSpatial and pose estimation), suggesting tool feedback can mitigate limitations in spatial grounding and 3D understanding for these models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to acquire complex and metrically precise spatial reasoning capabilities through learned coordination of external tools. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Advancing Embodied AI:** By allowing VLMs to use a diverse set of vision and robotic tools, SpaceTools represents a step towards creating more capable and general-purpose embodied agents. It demonstrates that VLMs can effectively bridge the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level physical interaction, enabling direct robot control through a unified perception-action loop.
- **Scalable Tool-Augmented VLMs:** The Double Interactive Reinforcement Learning (DIRL) framework, combined with the Toolshed infrastructure, offers a scalable and efficient methodology for training VLMs to interact with and coordinate numerous compute-intensive external tools. This addresses a significant bottleneck in developing tool-using agents, particularly for visual tasks.
- **Reduced Reliance on Data-Intensive Fine-Tuning or Architectural Modifications:** The work suggests that complex spatial reasoning capabilities can be acquired through learned tool coordination rather than solely through large-scale, task-specific data collection or fundamental VLM architectural changes. This provides a more modular and potentially resource-efficient path to expanding VLM capabilities.
- **Improved Precision and Robustness:** By leveraging specialized computer vision models as tools, VLMs can access precise geometric measurements and intermediate representations, enhancing the accuracy and robustness of spatial reasoning compared to inherent VLM capabilities. The learned error recovery and adaptive tool-use strategies contribute to more reliable performance in varied scenarios.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The interactive nature of DIRL, particularly the initial interactive RL phase for grounding, appears to foster better out-of-domain generalization. This suggests that learning to interact with tools can instill more transferable reasoning skills.
- **Facilitating Research and Development:** The open-sourcing of Toolshed can provide a platform for researchers to integrate new computer vision and robotics modules with VLMs, accelerating advancements in tool-augmented reasoning and embodied robotics. It also offers a pathway for efficiently integrating new tools into existing VLM systems.

Overall, this work contributes to the development of more intelligent and capable AI systems that can reason and act effectively in complex physical environments.
