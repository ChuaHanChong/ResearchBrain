# EnvACE: Internalizing Environment Dynamics via World Rehearsal for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ENVACE from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborators introduces "world rehearsal," training a single Large Language Model (LLM) policy to act as both the agent and its environment for internalizing environment dynamics. This approach achieved an overall score of 32.91% across various agentic benchmarks and demonstrated effective test-time scaling via private simulations before committing to external execution.

## Problem

- Existing LLM agent training methods rely on costly executable environments or often inaccurate external LLM-based simulators, limiting scalability and diversity.
- Current paradigms separate environment modeling from the agent's policy, preventing agents from internalizing environment dynamics and proactively anticipating action outcomes.
- The high cost and complexity of constructing and verifying diverse executable environments for long-horizon tool use are prohibitive.

## Method

- **EnvACE** employs ==world rehearsal==, where a shared policy alternates between generating actions (acting role) and environment responses (rehearsal role) to create self-unfolded trajectories.
- It uses a modified ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== framework to jointly optimize both roles within the shared policy, leveraging trajectory-level rewards.
- The trained policy utilizes ==Test-Time Scaling (TTS)== by performing multiple "private rehearsal attempts" to simulate actions and outcomes internally before a single, committed execution in the actual external environment.

## Results

- EnvACE-8B achieved an overall score of **32.91%** across BFCL V4, τ2-Bench, and VitaBench, outperforming several environment-scaling baselines, including EnvScaler-8B (**31.92%**).
- On FinMCP-Bench, EnvACE-8B recorded the highest TF1 score of **46.78%** and tool precision of **54.04%**, showcasing strong and transferable tool-use capabilities.
- Test-Time Scaling with N=2 rehearsals improved the Overall score by **4.2%** (to **40.9%**) and enabled proactive error repair, such as preventing invalid tool calls and forbidden write operations, as demonstrated by case studies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single LLM policy can effectively learn and internalize environment dynamics by performing both acting and rehearsing roles, reducing reliance on external environments during training.
> - Joint optimization of the shared policy for both roles enables direct integration of action-response causal knowledge into the agent's decision-making process.
> - Internalized world models allow agents to privately simulate and refine actions, leading to more robust, safer, and efficient real-world interactions without immediate external consequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly being deployed as agents that interact with users and environments through tool use, demanding capabilities like dialogue management, adaptive action selection, and long-horizon planning. Current approaches to training such agents primarily fall into two categories:

1. **Interaction with real or synthesized executable environments:** This involves collecting trajectories of tool use for supervised fine-tuning or leveraging reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) in automatically synthesized environments. While providing grounded transitions, these environments are costly and complex to construct and verify, especially as their complexity scales.
2. **Interaction with external LLM-based simulators:** These simulators generate environment responses, such as tool outputs or user feedback, without requiring fully executable environments. This approach offers more flexibility and lower cost but is prone to generating inaccurate or inconsistent responses, necessitating grounding and validation from real environments.

A fundamental limitation across both paradigms is their reliance on *external* sources for environment responses. The agent's policy learns to act based on externally supplied observations, keeping the environment modeling capability separate from the acting policy. This work addresses this limitation by proposing a shift in this paradigm. Instead of relying on external environments or simulators, EnvACE introduces "world rehearsal," where the policy itself generates the environment responses. This allows the policy to internalize environment dynamics directly within its parameters, effectively creating an agent world model that supports decision-making without external interaction during training. This approach moves beyond merely predicting or consuming observations to actively *enacting* them, offering a novel pathway to scale LLM agent training beyond the constraints of external environment dependency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more scalable, effective, and autonomous method for training LLM agents, specifically by enabling them to internalize environment dynamics. The motivation behind this objective stems from several challenges inherent in existing agent training paradigms:

- **Cost and Scalability of Executable Environments:** Creating and verifying diverse and complex executable environments for long-horizon tool use is resource-intensive. As the complexity of tasks and tool sets grows, the cost and difficulty of synthesizing and maintaining correct environments become prohibitive. This limits the diversity and scale of training data available to agents.
- **Limitations of External LLM Simulators:** While LLM-based simulators offer a more flexible alternative, they often suffer from issues of accuracy and consistency. Their generated responses might deviate from real-world behavior, requiring continuous supervision or grounding from actual environments, which still ties them to external data sources. The quality of these simulators directly impacts the reliability of the agent trained with them.
- **Lack of Internalized Environment Modeling:** Existing methods treat the environment as an external entity that provides feedback. The agent's policy learns to react to these external observations but does not explicitly develop an internal model of *how* its actions influence the environment and what responses they elicit. This separation means the agent cannot proactively anticipate outcomes or reason about the consequences of its actions without querying an external source, potentially leading to less robust and generalizable behavior.
- **Desire for Self-Sufficiency and Transferability:** Inspired by the success of learned world models in other areas of reinforcement learning, the authors hypothesize that an agent capable of modeling environment responses internally would exhibit more effective decision-making and better transferability across tasks. Such an agent could rehearse actions and anticipate consequences privately, leading to safer and more efficient interactions in real environments.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives of EnvACE are:

- To introduce "world rehearsal," a mechanism where a single policy takes on both the role of an actor generating actions and an environment generating responses, thereby eliminating the need for an external environment during training rollouts.
- To propose EnvACE, an agentic reinforcement learning method that interleaves acting and world rehearsal within self-unfolded trajectories.
- To jointly optimize both roles (acting and rehearsal) within a shared policy using task-success rewards, enabling the policy to internalize the causal relationship between actions and environment responses into its parameters.
- To demonstrate that this internalized world model allows for effective test-time scaling through private rehearsal, where the agent can simulate candidate actions and their outcomes before committing to actual execution, thus avoiding costly mistakes and improving performance without additional external interaction.
- To evaluate EnvACE across a range of agentic benchmarks to demonstrate its strong and transferable performance compared to existing environment-scaling baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EnvACE employs a novel approach centered on "world rehearsal," fundamentally altering the conventional interaction loop for LLM agent training. The methodology can be broken down into three core components: World Rehearsal, Role-Wise GRPO Optimization, and Test-Time Scaling.

#### 3.1. World Rehearsal

Unlike traditional methods where an external environment or simulator provides observations, EnvACE trains a single, shared policy, denoted $\pi_\theta$, to fulfill two distinct roles:

- **Acting Role:** Given the current interaction history ($h_t$), the policy first generates an action ($a_t$) that would typically be executed in an external environment. This action is sampled as $a_t \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot | h_t, \text{ACT})$.
- **Rehearsal Role:** Immediately after generating an action, the *same policy* then plays the role of the environment. Conditioned on the current history ($h_t$) and the generated action ($a_t$), it produces an environment response ($\hat{o}_t$). This response is sampled as $\hat{o}_t \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot | h_t, a_t, \text{REHEARSE})$.

The self-generated response ($\hat{o}_t$) is then appended to the interaction history, updating it to $h_{t+1} = h_t \oplus (a_t, \hat{o}_t)$. This revised history forms the basis for the acting role to generate its next decision. This alternating act-rehearse process unfolds the entire trajectory internally. Through repeated iterations of this process, the policy's parameters ($\theta$) learn and absorb the relationship between actions and their induced environment responses, effectively internalizing environment dynamics as an "agent world model."

#### 3.2. Role-Wise GRPO Optimization

To optimize this dual-role policy, EnvACE utilizes a modified Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework:

- **Trajectory-Level Rewards:** For each task instruction ($x$), EnvACE samples a group of $K$ rollouts. Each completed rollout ($\tau_i$) receives a scalar reward ($R_i$), which evaluates task performance. This reward can be sourced from a verifiable outcome evaluator or an LLM judge.
- **Role-Specific Advantage Estimation:** All policy outputs generated within a rollout, regardless of whether they were from the acting or rehearsal role, inherit the trajectory reward $R_i$. However, for calculating advantages, EnvACE computes a *separate* reward baseline ($\mu_{x,r}$) for each role ($r \in \{\text{ACT, REHEARSE}\}$). This baseline is the average reward of all policy outputs generated under that specific role across the group of $K$ rollouts for instruction $x$. The advantage ($A_{i,m}$) for a policy output ($y_{i,m}$) is then calculated as the difference between its trajectory reward and its role-specific baseline ($A_{i,m} = R_i - \mu_{x,r_{i,m}}$).
- **Joint Policy Update:** Despite using role-specific baselines, the core policy parameters ($\theta$) are jointly updated using a clipped GRPO objective. This joint optimization is crucial, as it ensures that knowledge gained by the rehearsal role about environment dynamics is directly integrated into the acting role, reinforcing the internalization process within the shared policy.

#### 3.3. Test-Time Scaling (TTS)

After training, EnvACE leverages the internalized world model for enhanced inference-time computation before interacting with an actual external environment:

- **Private Rehearsal Attempts:** Given a new instruction, the policy performs $N$ "private rehearsal attempts." Each attempt generates an imagined trajectory ($\tilde{\tau}^{(n)}$) entirely within the policy's internal world model. The policy then self-evaluates this imagined trajectory to obtain feedback ($f^{(n)}$), which includes an assessment and suggestions for revision.
- **Modes of Information Sharing:**
 - **Parallel Mode:** All $N$ attempts are generated independently from the same initial task context. Their feedback is collected but not shared among other ongoing attempts.
 - **Sequential Mode:** Each subsequent attempt observes the trajectories and feedback from all prior attempts within the same $N$ rehearsals. This allows for iterative refinement and learning from previous imagined successes or failures.
- **Committed Execution:** After completing the rehearsal attempts, EnvACE summarizes all imagined trajectories and self-evaluations into a compact "rehearsal memory" ($m_x$). The acting role then conditions its *single, committed execution* in the *actual external environment* on this summarized memory. The crucial aspect is that these internal rehearsals do not modify the external environment.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The main experiments utilized a Qwen3-8B model backbone, trained on the CM2 dataset for 470 steps. Specific hyper-parameters included a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-6}$, a batch size of 16, and four rollouts per prompt. A Qwen3-30B-A3B model served as the LLM judge during training. Different sampling temperatures and top-p values were used for acting and rehearsal roles, with higher values for exploration in specific experiments and lower for robustness in general.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across multiple agentic benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and transferability of EnvACE.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Benchmark Suite:** EnvACE was evaluated on BFCL-v4, τ$^2$-Bench, VitaBench, and FinMCP-Bench, covering diverse tool-use, multi-turn, stateful, and financial scenarios.
- **Comparative Advantage (Table 1):** EnvACE-8B achieved an overall score of 32.91% across BFCL V4, τ$^2$-Bench, and VitaBench. This performance surpassed several environment-scaling baselines, including EnvScaler-8B (31.92%) and AWM-14B (32.54%), establishing it as a competitive method in complex agentic tasks.
 - On BFCL V4, EnvACE scored 46.04%, outperforming Qwen3-8B by 2.00% and AWM-8B by 1.75%.
 - On τ$^2$-Bench, it achieved 36.7%, exceeding EnvScaler-8B by 3.8% and AWM-14B by 6.0%.
 - On VitaBench, EnvACE scored 16.0%, leading all other 7B–8B methods, including EnvScaler-8B and ScaleEnv-8B.
- **Financial Tool Use (Table 2):** On FinMCP-Bench, EnvACE-8B achieved the highest TF1 score of 46.78%, outperforming EnvScaler-8B (43.68%) and AWM-8B (42.50%). It also demonstrated the highest tool precision (54.04%), indicating a strong balance between accurate and comprehensive tool utilization.

These results indicate that world rehearsal provides a more effective and scalable training paradigm, enabling the policy to acquire transferable tool-use capabilities across a variety of heterogeneous and specialized environments.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of World Rehearsal

- **Improved Policy Learning (Figure 3):** A controlled comparison showed that EnvACE (8B) improved the average score on τ$^2$-Bench from 31.2% (standard GRPO baseline) to 36.7%, a gain of 5.5%. This improvement highlights the benefit of world rehearsal for stateful, multi-turn interactions, where anticipating environment responses is critical for effective decision-making.
- **Internalizing Environment Dynamics:** Further ablation showed that EnvACE, which shares parameters between the acting and rehearsal roles, outperformed a variant with separate policies for each role (Per-role Policy) on τ$^2$-Bench (36.7% vs. 35.5%). This 1.2% gain supports the hypothesis that parameter sharing enables the policy to directly incorporate knowledge about action-response dynamics into its decision-making process.
- **Training Dynamics (Figure 5):** The evaluation score exhibited a consistent upward trend during training, increasing from 30.0% at step 50 to 36.7% at step 470, suggesting that world rehearsal provides a stable and progressive learning signal.

#### 4.3. Effects Across Model Scales

- **Scalability (Figure 4):** EnvACE demonstrated improved performance with increasing model capacity. Scaling from a 1.7B to an 8B backbone led to a 14.23% gain on BFCL V4 (31.81% to 46.04%) and a 21.4% gain on τ$^2$-Bench (15.3% to 36.7%). This indicates that the benefits of world rehearsal are amplified with larger models.
- **Consistent Superiority (Figure 3):** EnvACE consistently outperformed standard GRPO at both 1.7B and 8B scales, with the performance gap becoming more pronounced at the larger scale.

#### 4.4. Test-Time Scaling with World Rehearsal

- **Performance Gains at Test Time (Table 3):** With a rehearsal budget of N=2, EnvACE in parallel rehearsal mode achieved an Overall score of 40.9%, an improvement of 4.2% over the Non-TTS baseline (36.7%). Both parallel and sequential modes with EnvACE for rehearsal consistently improved performance across benchmarks (e.g., τ$^2$-Bench average improved from 31.4% to 38.0% in parallel mode).
- **Importance of Rehearsal Policy:** Rehearsal using the EnvACE model consistently outperformed rehearsal using a base model, suggesting that the internalized environment knowledge, rather than just additional inference compute, is critical for test-time gains.
- **Effect of Rehearsal Budget (Figure 6):** EnvACE consistently outperformed the base model across various rehearsal budgets (N=1, 2, 3). Increasing the budget from N=1 to N=2 generally improved performance, indicating that additional rehearsal can enhance task performance. While performance slightly decreased at N=3 relative to N=2, it remained superior to the base model's rehearsal, potentially due to context window limitations with longer rehearsal trajectories.
- **Case Studies (Figure 7, 8):** Qualitative examples illustrated EnvACE's ability to predict tool-call failures, proactively repair invalid parameters, and avoid forbidden write operations through internal rehearsal. This contrasts with baseline agents that often require several recovery steps after actual execution failures, demonstrating EnvACE's capacity for safer and more efficient decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of EnvACE and the concept of world rehearsal represent a significant advancement in the field of LLM agent training, offering several important implications and potential impacts:

- **Reduced Dependency on External Environments:** EnvACE provides a robust alternative to existing paradigms that rely on costly, complex, or potentially unreliable external environments or simulators. By internalizing environment dynamics, it substantially reduces the need for constant interaction with external data sources during training, lowering development costs and accelerating research cycles.
- **Enhanced Agent Autonomy and Robustness:** Agents trained with world rehearsal gain an internal understanding of how their actions shape environment responses. This leads to more autonomous decision-making, as agents can anticipate consequences and proactively plan, rather than merely reacting to external feedback. This capacity for "private rehearsal" before committed execution can significantly enhance the robustness and reliability of LLM agents, allowing them to detect and correct potential errors without actual interaction with the real world.
- **Improved Generalization and Transferability:** By internalizing environment dynamics into its core parameters, the policy develops a more generalized understanding of tool use and environmental interactions. This can lead to better transferability of learned skills across heterogeneous tasks and domains, as demonstrated by EnvACE's consistent performance across diverse benchmarks.
- **Scalability of Training:** World rehearsal offers a new path for scaling LLM agent training by decoupling it from the scalability constraints of external environment construction. As LLMs grow in capacity, EnvACE can leverage this increased capacity to build more sophisticated internal world models, leading to larger performance gains.
- **Enabling Safer AI Systems:** The ability to simulate actions and anticipate outcomes internally contributes to building safer AI systems. Agents can identify and avoid potentially harmful or irreversible actions in a simulated environment before taking them in a real-world setting, as illustrated by the case studies where EnvACE prevents invalid tool calls and forbidden write operations.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including investigating the effectiveness of world rehearsal on even larger LLM models and extending its application beyond tool-interactive tasks to a broader spectrum of agentic settings. This could include complex planning, multi-agent coordination, or environments with highly dynamic and uncertain state transitions.

In summary, EnvACE establishes world rehearsal as a potent mechanism for training LLM agents, enabling them to become more self-sufficient, robust, and capable by directly integrating environment modeling into their decision-making policies. This paradigm shift holds significant promise for the development and deployment of advanced AI agents in real-world applications.
