# Memory, Benchmark & Robots: A Benchmark for Solving Complex Tasks with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces MIKASA, a comprehensive benchmark suite for evaluating memory capabilities in reinforcement learning agents, specifically focusing on tabletop robotic manipulation tasks. The benchmark and extensive evaluations reveal that current online, offline, and visual-language-action models struggle with tasks demanding long-horizon spatio-temporal memory, with real-world robot experiments validating these limitations.

## Problem

- A lack of a standardized, comprehensive benchmark for systematically evaluating memory capabilities in reinforcement learning across diverse tasks and environments.
- Fragmented evaluation of memory-enhanced agents, where custom environments often accompany new algorithms, hindering direct comparison and reproducibility.
- Existing robotic benchmarks frequently overlook or inadequately address specific spatio-temporal memory requirements inherent in partially observable real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A four-type classification framework for memory-intensive RL tasks (Object, Spatial, Sequential, Memory Capacity) is proposed to standardize evaluation.
- **MIKASA**-Base unifies existing open-source memory-intensive environments under a common API to streamline reproducibility and facilitate fair comparisons.
- **MIKASA**-Robo, a novel benchmark, provides 32 memory-intensive tabletop robotic manipulation tasks built on ManiSkill3, engineered to isolate and assess specific memory-dependent skills.

## Results

- A PPO-MLP agent with full state information achieved a **100%** success rate across all 32 MIKASA-Robo tasks, confirming their solvability when complete information is available.
- Online RL baselines (PPO-LSTM, SAC, TD-MPC2) and offline RL baselines consistently showed significant performance degradation, often achieving near-zero success rates, on complex memory tasks (e.g., 5 or 9 items to remember) with visual inputs.
- Real-world experiments with a fine-tuned π0.5 model on a physical robot yielded a **10%** touch and pick success rate on a long-horizon occlusion task, indicating that the model failed to retain crucial information and isolating memory as the limiting factor.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent solvability of MIKASA-Robo tasks in fully observable settings confirms that performance degradation in partially observable modes is due to algorithmic or learning challenges, not task design.
> - Current state-of-the-art online, offline, and Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit substantial limitations in handling complex, long-horizon memory-intensive tasks, particularly with increasing task complexity or sparse rewards.
> - Real-world robotic experiments corroborate simulation findings, demonstrating that memory is a primary bottleneck for modern VLA models in tasks requiring recall across occluded intervals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Memory is a recognized component for agents addressing complex tasks characterized by temporal and spatial dependencies, particularly in scenarios with partial observability. In the field of reinforcement learning (RL), various algorithms integrate memory mechanisms. However, the existing research landscape has been characterized by a lack of a standardized and comprehensive benchmark suitable for evaluating these memory capabilities across a diverse range of scenarios. This absence has resulted in a fragmented evaluation environment, where memory-enhanced agents are frequently assessed using custom environments developed in conjunction with specific algorithmic proposals. Such bespoke evaluations limit direct comparisons of agent performance across different studies and obscure variations in agent strengths and limitations regarding distinct memory types.

Current benchmarks, such as POP-Gym, DMLab-30, and MemoryGym, primarily focus on specific aspects of memory utilization or particular problem domains. While these contributions offer insights into memory mechanisms, they do not encompass the broad spectrum of memory utilization scenarios. In the domain of robotics, where partial observability is inherent in many tasks (e.g., navigation), studies often artificially introduce partial observability into Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) or focus on fully observable scenarios. These approaches may not fully capture the complexity of real-world robotic challenges that necessitate recalling past object configurations, manipulating occluded objects, or executing multi-step procedures heavily reliant on memory. Previous attempts to classify memory-intensive environments have highlighted aspects such as temporal horizons or memory dependency pairs, but these classifications have not fully explored the link between physical interaction and spatio-temporal memory in real-world robotic tasks.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing MIKASA (Memory-Intensive Skills Assessment Suite for Agents), a benchmark designed to provide a unified framework for the evaluation of memory in RL, with a specific focus on tabletop robotic manipulation. The development of MIKASA aims to standardize evaluation, enable systematic comparison of memory-enhanced agents, and contribute to the development of more robust systems for real-world applications by bridging concepts from cognitive science with RL and robotics. The paper positions MIKASA as a comprehensive solution that offers a structured approach to memory evaluation, in contrast to existing benchmarks and concurrent efforts, such as MemoryBench, which exhibit a narrower scope or limitations in parallelization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the fragmented and often narrow evaluation of memory capabilities in reinforcement learning (RL) by introducing a comprehensive and unified benchmark. The authors identify several motivations for this work:

1. **Lack of a Universal Benchmark:** The RL field lacks a standardized benchmark to systematically assess an agent's memory capabilities across diverse tasks and environments. This absence hinders comparative analysis of memory-enhanced agents and obscures their strengths and weaknesses across different memory-intensive scenarios.
2. **Fragmented Evaluation in RL:** Existing memory-intensive environments are typically introduced alongside novel algorithms, leading to a proliferation of custom evaluation setups. This fragmentation impedes reproducibility, fair comparison, and the ability to attribute performance variations to specific memory mechanisms rather than task-specific nuances.
3. **Specific Challenges in Robotic Manipulation:** In tabletop robotic manipulation, memory is identified as essential for tasks involving partial observability, such as recalling occluded object positions, tracking object properties over time, or executing multi-step procedures. Existing robotic benchmarks primarily focus on fully observable MDPs or complex, non-atomic tasks that do not isolate specific memory requirements, thus failing to capture the domain-specific spatio-temporal memory demands.
4. **Incomplete Memory Assessment:** Even within individual studies, existing benchmarks may emphasize similar memory aspects (e.g., remembering object locations) while neglecting others (e.g., reconstructing sequential events), leading to an incomplete evaluation of an agent's memory profile. Different architectures may exhibit varying performance across different memory task types, and these discrepancies may remain undetected without a broad evaluation scope.

To address these motivations, the authors outline four specific contributions:

1. **Memory Tasks Classification:** To enable systematic evaluation, the authors propose a comprehensive framework that categorizes memory-intensive RL tasks into four distinct types. This framework is intended to simplify task selection and guide the assessment of core memory challenges in RL and robotics.
2. **MIKASA-Base – A Memory-RL Benchmark:** The introduction of MIKASA-Base, a Gymnasium-based framework, aims to consolidate widely used open-source memory-intensive environments under a common API. This contribution seeks to streamline reproducibility, facilitate fair comparisons, and promote systematic evaluation of memory in RL agents.
3. **MIKASA-Robo – Robotic Manipulation Tasks:** The development of MIKASA-Robo is intended to provide a novel, open-source benchmark comprising 32 carefully designed memory-intensive tasks for tabletop robotic manipulation. These tasks are engineered to isolate and assess specific memory-dependent skills in realistic settings.
4. **Robotic Manipulation Datasets:** To support offline RL research, the authors announce the release of expert-quality datasets containing trajectories for all 32 MIKASA-Robo memory-intensive tasks. This resource is provided to enable extensive evaluations using a range of offline RL baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the development of MIKASA, a benchmark suite designed to evaluate memory in reinforcement learning agents. The approach is structured around a classification framework for memory-intensive tasks, followed by the implementation of two distinct benchmark components: MIKASA-Base and MIKASA-Robo, along with supporting datasets and extensive experimental evaluations.

**Problem Formulation:** The work establishes Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) as the formal model for memory-intensive environments. A memory-intensive task is defined as a POMDP where a correlation horizon (ξ > 1) exists between a critical event and the need to recall information about that event.

**Memory Task Classification:** Drawing on concepts from developmental psychology and cognitive science, the authors propose a classification framework that categorizes memory-intensive tasks into four fundamental types:
1. **Object Memory:** Tasks requiring agents to retain information about object properties (e.g., color, shape) when objects are temporarily unobservable. These tasks relate to the concept of object permanence.
2. **Spatial Memory:** Tasks focusing on environmental awareness, requiring agents to remember object locations, maintain internal maps, and navigate based on past spatial information.
3. **Sequential Memory:** Tasks assessing an agent's ability to process and utilize temporally ordered information, such as recalling action sequences or maintaining order-dependent data.
4. **Memory Capacity:** Tasks challenging an agent's ability to manage multiple pieces of information simultaneously, analogous to human memory span, to evaluate information retention limits.

**MIKASA-Base Benchmark:** This component serves as a unified framework for evaluating memory-enhanced RL agents using existing open-source memory-intensive environments.
- **Design Principles:** MIKASA-Base tasks are organized into two tiers: diagnostic vector-based environments designed to isolate specific memory mechanisms, and complex image-based tasks that incorporate realistic perception challenges.
- **Task Selection:** A diverse subset of tasks from established benchmarks (e.g., Memory Cards, Numpad, BSuite MemoryLength, MiniGrid-Memory, POPGym environments) are selected and categorized according to the proposed taxonomy, then unified under a Gymnasium-based API.

**MIKASA-Robo Benchmark:** This component introduces a novel set of tasks specifically for tabletop robotic manipulation.
- **Foundation:** Built upon the ManiSkill3 framework, leveraging its parallel GPU-based training capabilities.
- **Task Design:** 32 tasks are developed across 12 categories, each targeting specific aspects of object, spatial, sequential, and memory capacity, inspired by the classification framework. Tasks include ShellGame, RememberColor, Intercept, Rotate, TakeItBack, BunchOfColors, SeqOfColors, and ChainOfColors, with varying levels of complexity (e.g., 3, 5, or 9 items to remember).
- **Training Modes:** Multiple observation modes are supported, including `state` (full oracle information), `RGB+joints` (camera images and joint states, standard for memory testing), `RGB` (visual only), `joints` (joint states only), `oracle` (task-specific data for debugging), and `prompt` (static instructions).
- **Reward Functions:** Both `dense` (continuous feedback) and `sparse` (binary task completion) reward variants are implemented.
- **Customization:** Environment parameters (e.g., number of objects, episode length, cue duration, delay intervals) are adjustable to allow for debugging, ablation studies, and curriculum learning.
- **Flexibility:** The benchmark is designed to be independent of specific robot embodiments and allows for the optional addition of complex objects or clutter to increase perceptual and manipulation complexity while preserving underlying memory requirements.

**Robotic Manipulation Datasets:** To facilitate Offline RL research, expert-quality datasets were collected for all 32 MIKASA-Robo tasks.
- **Collection Method:** Datasets consist of 1000 successful trajectories per task, generated by a PPO-MLP agent trained to 100% success rate in `state` mode with sparse rewards.
- **Data Content:** Each trajectory includes RGB images, joint states, actions, dense rewards, sparse success flags, and done flags.

**Experimental Evaluation:** A range of baseline algorithms were evaluated across MIKASA-Robo.
- **Online RL Baselines:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) backbones, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), and Temporal Difference Learning for Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC2).
- **Offline RL Baselines:** Decision Transformer (DT), Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory (RATE), Behavioral Cloning (BC), Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), and Diffusion Policy (DP). Additional baselines like DMamba and GTrXL were also tested.
- **Visual-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Octo, OpenVLA, π0, and SpatialVLA were evaluated.
- **Real-world Experiments:** The π0.5 model was fine-tuned and tested on a physical SO-101 robotic arm to validate simulation findings in a real-world setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations conducted across the MIKASA benchmark suite, utilizing various RL paradigms, yielded consistent findings regarding the capabilities of current agents in memory-intensive tasks.

**Solvability of MIKASA-Robo Tasks:**
- When agents were trained with full `state` information (MDP mode), a PPO-MLP baseline consistently achieved a 100% success rate across all 32 MIKASA-Robo tasks. This result demonstrates that the tasks are inherently solvable when complete information is available, validating the design of the benchmark and indicating that performance degradation in other modes is attributable to algorithmic limitations or learning challenges rather than task design flaws.

**Online RL Baselines (RGB+joints, Dense Rewards):**
- In the `RGB+joints` mode with dense rewards, which requires visual perception and memory, the PPO-LSTM agent generally exhibited superior performance compared to the memory-less PPO-MLP on simpler memory tasks (e.g., RememberColor-v0 with 3 colors).
- However, as task complexity increased (e.g., RememberColor-v0 with 5 or 9 colors), the performance of both PPO-MLP and PPO-LSTM agents significantly degraded, approaching near-zero success rates.
- Other online baselines, SAC and TD-MPC2, showed higher sample efficiency than PPO-MLP but also exhibited low performance on complex memory tasks, indicating that the absence of explicit memory mechanisms posed a limitation in these scenarios.

**Online RL Baselines (RGB+joints, Sparse Rewards):**
- Under `RGB+joints` observation mode with sparse rewards, both PPO-MLP and PPO-LSTM architectures consistently failed to achieve a meaningful success rate across nearly all evaluated MIKASA-Robo environments. This observation underscores the difficulty of memory-intensive manipulation tasks when only terminal feedback is provided.

**Offline RL Baselines (RGB, Sparse Rewards):**
- Evaluations with offline RL models (Decision Transformer (DT), Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory (RATE), Behavioral Cloning (BC), Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), Diffusion Policy (DP)) trained on RGB observations and sparse rewards demonstrated that none of the tested models successfully solved the majority of MIKASA-Robo tasks.
- Tasks categorized as requiring high Memory Capacity or Sequential Memory (e.g., BunchOfColors, SeqOfColors, ChainOfColors) remained largely unsolved by all offline RL baselines, even those explicitly designed for sequence modeling.
- Additional baselines, DMamba (a state-space model) and GTrXL (a gated recurrent transformer), tested in the offline setting, showed some improvements over standard DT and BC baselines but still did not match the performance of the RATE model on tasks with higher memory requirements, and generally struggled with increasing complexity.

**Visual-Language-Action (VLA) Baselines:**
- State-of-the-art VLA models (Octo-small, OpenVLA, π0, SpatialVLA) were evaluated on a subset of memory-intensive tasks. Octo-small, with a context size of 10, showed some performance on simpler tasks but degraded with increasing complexity.
- OpenVLA, particularly with a larger action chunk size (K=8), exhibited performance above random on some tasks (e.g., RememberColor3, ShellGameTouch) but also experienced performance drops on more difficult variants. Smaller chunk sizes (K=4) for OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, and π0 generally resulted in random-like performance.
- These results suggest that while chunking can offer limited compensation for a lack of explicit memory, current VLA models generally lack effective long-term memory mechanisms, leading to brittle performance on tasks with substantial memory requirements.

**Real-World Experiments (π0.5 model):**
- Real-world experiments on a physical SO-101 robotic arm, involving a fine-tuned π0.5 model, corroborated the simulation findings.
 - **Task 1 (Sanity Check - Fully Observable):** The robot achieved 100% touch success and 80% pick success on a simple pick-and-place task without memory requirements, confirming system functionality.
 - **Task 2 (Dynamic Environment - Short Horizon):** Performance degraded (63% touch, 43% pick) when dynamic scene changes occurred without long-horizon memory needs, indicating some sensitivity to dynamic conditions.
 - **Task 3 (Real-world RememberColor3-v0 - Long Horizon Occlusion):** Performance collapsed to 10% touch and 10% pick success. This decline, in comparison to Task 2, indicated that the model failed to retain crucial color information across an occluded interval, isolating long-horizon memory as the primary limiting factor.

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that existing online, offline, and VLA methods face challenges when tasks impose substantial memory requirements, particularly with increasing complexity or sparse reward signals. The consistent performance degradation observed across diverse architectures and experimental settings underscores the current limitations in temporal memory capabilities of these models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MIKASA as a unified benchmark suite is positioned to address several identified gaps in the field of reinforcement learning, particularly concerning the evaluation and development of memory-enhanced agents for real-world applications.

**Advancing Memory RL Research:** MIKASA is designed to guide and accelerate progress in the development of RL algorithms equipped with robust, long-horizon memory mechanisms. By providing a structured and comprehensive evaluation framework, it enables researchers to identify and target specific memory limitations in current architectures.

**Standardized and Comprehensive Evaluation:** The work contributes a taxonomy of memory types (object, spatial, sequential, and capacity), which provides a foundation for systematic and interpretable evaluation. This classification allows for the precise attribution of performance differences to specific memory capabilities rather than confounding factors. MIKASA-Base unifies existing memory tasks under a consistent API, reducing fragmentation and promoting reproducibility across studies.

**Addressing Robotic Manipulation Challenges:** MIKASA-Robo, with its 32 carefully designed robotic manipulation tasks, directly addresses the specific spatio-temporal memory requirements prevalent in real-world robotics. These tasks are engineered to isolate and stress specific forms of memory, which is crucial for developing robots capable of operating in complex, partially observable environments. The benchmark's flexibility to incorporate realistic object distributions and controlled clutter further enhances its applicability to real-world scenarios once foundational memory capabilities are established.

**Support for Offline RL and Imitation Learning:** The release of expert-quality datasets for all MIKASA-Robo tasks is a direct contribution to the offline RL and imitation learning communities. These datasets provide a valuable resource for training and evaluating algorithms in memory-intensive robotic settings, especially under challenging sparse reward conditions.

**Empirical Validation of Current Limitations:** The extensive experimental evaluations with online, offline, and VLA agents consistently demonstrated that current state-of-the-art methods struggle significantly when faced with substantial memory demands. The real-world experiments further validated these findings, confirming that memory, rather than embodiment or perception noise, is often the primary limitation for modern VLA models in memory-intensive tasks. This empirical evidence highlights a critical area for future research and development in RL.

**Facilitating Reproducibility and Community Engagement:** The open-source nature of MIKASA-Robo, along with detailed documentation, customization guides, and released datasets, promotes reproducibility and encourages broader community engagement. This accessibility is intended to foster collaborative research and accelerate the development of advanced memory-enhanced agents.

In summary, MIKASA aims to serve as a foundational tool for the scientific community, enabling more rigorous evaluation and informed development of memory-intensive RL systems, with a particular emphasis on their applicability to complex real-world robotic tasks.
