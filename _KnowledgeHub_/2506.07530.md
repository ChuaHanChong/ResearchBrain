# BitVLA: 1-bit Vision-Language-Action Models for Robotics Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> BitVLA introduces the first 1-bit Vision-Language-Action model for robotic manipulation, significantly reducing memory footprint and inference latency. It achieves comparable performance to larger full-precision VLA models on robotic tasks, enabling deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are large and rely on full-precision parameters, resulting in high memory footprints and significant inference latency, making them impractical for resource-constrained edge robotics.
- Post-training quantization (PTQ) for VLA models often leads to accuracy degradation and complex calibration, without being inherently aligned with the model's training dynamics.
- The potential of extreme low-bit models (like 1-bit LLMs) for multimodal VLA models remains largely unexplored, due to concerns about quantization-induced representation mismatches affecting vision-language alignment and action prediction.

## Method

- **BitVLA** was developed as a fully native 1-bit VLA model, constraining all parameters in the LLM backbone and vision encoder to ternary values ({-1, 0, 1}) for weights and INT8 for activations.
- A three-stage training paradigm was employed: multimodal training for stable vision-language initialization, a 'Quantize-then-Distill' stage to compress the vision encoder, and a robotics training stage for manipulation capabilities.
- The 'Quantize-then-Distill' stage uses a full-precision teacher to guide the quantization of a 1.58-bit student vision encoder, incorporating an auxiliary representation alignment loss to mitigate quantization-induced drift and preserve multimodal alignment.

## Results

- BitVLA achieved an average success rate of **96.0%** on the LIBERO benchmark, comparable to OpenVLA-OFT (**97.1%**) while reducing memory footprint by **11.0x** (**1.4** GB vs. **15.4** GB).
- The model exhibited **4.4x** lower inference latency (73 ms) and **4.4x** higher throughput (**341.1** Hz) compared to OpenVLA-OFT+, enabling deployment on consumer-grade GPUs.
- BitVLA demonstrated robust performance in real-world manipulation tasks and generalized effectively to out-of-distribution scenarios and novel objects in a zero-shot manner.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggressive 1-bit quantization can be successfully integrated into VLA models for robotic manipulation with competitive performance, provided a co-designed, training-time approach is used.
> - The 'Quantize-then-Distill' strategy effectively compresses the vision encoder to 1.58-bit while maintaining critical multimodal representation alignment, outperforming post-training quantization for VLA applications.
> - Large-scale robotics pre-training is crucial for BitVLA to acquire generalizable manipulation knowledge and achieve robust performance on diverse simulation and real-world tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have facilitated progress in various vision-language tasks, including visual question answering and human-agent interaction. Building on these developments, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a paradigm for learning generalist robot policies, extending VLM capabilities to robotic control. This approach leverages the representation and instruction-following abilities of VLM backbones for robotic manipulation.

However, the rapid scaling of VLA models presents challenges for practical deployment on real-world robotic systems, particularly edge platforms, which operate under strict constraints related to memory, computational throughput, and energy consumption. Existing VLA models typically employ large-scale, full-precision parameters, leading to substantial memory footprints and latency. While post-training quantization (PTQ) can reduce these demands, it often introduces accuracy degradation and requires careful calibration, and is not inherently aligned with the original training dynamics.

Concurrently, extreme low-bit modeling has shown promise in the language domain. Specifically, 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs), which constrain parameters to ternary values (e.g., {-1, 0, 1}), have demonstrated significant reductions in inference cost, memory footprint, and energy consumption while maintaining competitive performance. Examples such as BitNet b1.58 have illustrated the potential of aggressive quantization for efficiency.

The integration of such extreme low-bit models into the multimodal perception and robotic control domain, particularly for VLA models, remains largely unexplored. The challenge lies in translating the benefits of 1-bit LLMs to VLA models, where tightly coupled vision-language alignment and action prediction are susceptible to quantization-induced representation mismatch, potentially compromising end-task success. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a training-time co-design approach that integrates quantization directly into the model learning process for robotics. The work positions itself as the first to introduce a fully native 1-bit VLA model for robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that are both capable of general robotic manipulation and efficiently deployable on resource-constrained edge devices. This objective is motivated by two main challenges inherent in current VLA model development and deployment:

1. **Inefficiency of Existing VLA Models:** Contemporary VLA models are characterized by their large scale and reliance on full-precision parameters. This design results in high memory footprints and significant inference latency, rendering them impractical for deployment on embedded or mobile robotic hardware. While post-training quantization (PTQ) offers a partial solution, it frequently leads to noticeable accuracy drops and necessitates intricate calibration procedures. Furthermore, PTQ is a post-hoc operation and does not intrinsically align with the original model's optimization dynamics.

2. **Unexplored Potential of Extreme Low-Bit Models for Robotics:** Despite encouraging results in the language domain, where 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated substantial reductions in inference costs and improved hardware efficiency while preserving performance, the application of such extreme low-bit modeling to multimodal perception and robotic control is largely unexplored. VLA training involves a complex interplay of vision-language alignment and action prediction. The authors identify a specific concern that aggressive quantization could induce representation mismatches within this tightly coupled system, potentially degrading task performance.

Given these challenges, the authors adopt a "deployment-oriented view" of VLA training. Their motivation is to integrate efficiency considerations directly into the model design and optimization phases, rather than relying solely on subsequent compression techniques. The key motivations for developing BitVLA are:
- To create a VLA model where all parameters are constrained to ternary values ({-1, 0, 1}), thereby achieving a "fully native 1-bit" status for robotic manipulation.
- To leverage the compactness of 1-bit pretraining while ensuring that the model retains strong task performance comparable to larger, full-precision counterparts.
- To further reduce the memory footprint of the vision backbone, which is often a significant component, through a specialized quantization strategy.
- To demonstrate a practical pathway for co-designing efficiency and accuracy during the training of embodied policies, enabling competitive manipulation capabilities on memory-constrained edge robotic platforms.
- To fundamentally alter the arithmetic profile of the model's operations, shifting computation from floating-point multiply-accumulate operations to integer additions, which could pave the way for specialized hardware accelerators optimized for 1-bit VLAs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BitVLA is designed as a unified multimodal policy $\pi_\theta$ that takes visual observations and language context as input and generates robot actions, leveraging a fully quantized backbone. The methodology involves a specific model architecture and a three-stage training paradigm.

**A. Model Architecture**
- **LLM Backbone:** BitVLA utilizes BitNet b1.58 2B4T as its 1-bit Large Language Model backbone, where its parameters are ternary by design.
- **Vision Encoder:** The model incorporates SigLIP-L, pre-trained at 224x224 resolution, which generates 256 visual token sequences for computational efficiency.
- **Connector:** A lightweight two-layer MLP with GeLU activations projects the vision features into the language embedding space. This connector is maintained in full precision due to its minimal parameter count and memory footprint.
- **Action Head:** Continuous robot actions are decoded by an action head, which also remains in full precision.
- **Unified Policy Formulation:** The policy processes multimodal input $o_t = [I_t, q_t]$ (image observation and proprioceptive state) and language context $\ell_t$ (task instruction). Input tokens are interleaved, comprising text tokens, visual tokens from SigLIP-L, and robot state tokens derived from proprioceptive signals via a small MLP projection.

**B. Quantization of Weights and Activations**
The quantization scheme is applied to all linear layers within the vision encoder and LLM, excluding input and output embedding layers.
- **Weight Quantization ($Q_w$):** Weights are quantized to ternary values {−1, 0, 1} using an `absmean` quantizer.
 $Q_w(W) = \text{RoundClip}(\frac{W}{\alpha}, -1, 1)$, where $\alpha = \frac{1}{nm} \|W\|_1$.
- **Activation Quantization ($Q_a$):** Activations are quantized to symmetric INT8 values in [-128, 127] using a `per-token absmax` quantizer.
 $Q_a(x) = \text{RoundClip}(\frac{127x}{\beta}, -128, 127)$, where $\beta = \|x\|_\infty$.
- **RoundClip Function:** $\text{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \text{round}(x)))$ is used for clipping and rounding.
- **Forward Computation:** The output of a quantized linear layer is computed as $Y = \frac{127\alpha}{\beta} Q_w(W)Q_a(x)$. During inference, a custom kernel from BitBLAS is employed for efficient matrix multiplication of ternary weights with INT8 inputs.

**C. Training Paradigm (Three Stages)**
The training pipeline integrates quantization into the learning process across three distinct stages:

1. **Multimodal Training:**
 - **Purpose:** To establish a stable vision-language initialization.
 - **Process:** A vision-language model is initially constructed by pairing the 1-bit LLM backbone with a *full-precision* vision encoder and the lightweight connector.
 - **Steps:**
 - The connector is trained first on a small image-caption corpus (LLaVA-1.5-558k) to align visual tokens with the language embedding space.
 - The vision encoder is then frozen, and the LLM backbone and connector are optimized on a larger multimodal instruction-following dataset (10M-sample subset of MammoTH-VL).

2. **Quantize-then-Distill:**
 - **Purpose:** To compress the full-precision vision encoder into 1.58-bit weights with 8-bit activations while preserving multimodal alignment.
 - **Teacher-Student Setup:** The full-precision vision encoder from Stage 1 serves as a fixed teacher model. A 1.58-bit student encoder is initialized from this full-precision teacher.
 - **Training Focus:** Only the student vision encoder is updated during this stage. The 1-bit LLM backbone and connector are kept frozen to maintain stability.
 - **Loss Function:** The overall objective $L_{total} = L_{LM} + \lambda L_{aux}$ combines:
 - **Language Modeling Loss ($L_{LM}$):** A standard autoregressive language modeling objective calculated on answer tokens, conditioned on the 1.58-bit visual tokens and preceding text.
 - **Representation Alignment Loss ($L_{aux}$):** An auxiliary mean squared error (MSE) loss between the hidden states of corresponding layers in the full-precision teacher and the 1.58-bit student encoders. This loss is critical for mitigating representation drift caused by quantization.
 - **Quantization-Aware Training:** The student encoder utilizes the defined quantization functions and the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) for gradient approximation.

3. **Robotics Training:**
 - **Purpose:** To acquire generalizable manipulation knowledge and adapt BitVLA to downstream tasks.
 - **Model State:** Following Stage 2, both the vision encoder and LLM backbone operate with 1.58-bit weights and 8-bit activations.
 - **Pre-training:** The model is pre-trained using an autoregressive next-action prediction objective on a large-scale robotics corpus (approximately 1 million samples from Open X-Embodiment). Action dimensions are discretized into 256 bins, and training minimizes the cross-entropy loss between predicted and ground-truth action sequences.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Supervised fine-tuning is performed to adapt the pre-trained model to specific downstream tasks.
 - **Action Decoding:** Parallel decoding with action chunking (chunk size K=8) is employed to enhance inference throughput for real-time control, producing temporally coherent multi-step commands in a single forward pass. The LLM backbone retains its causal attention structure for performance and compatibility with Flash-Attention.
 - **Loss Function:** An L1 regression objective $L_{act} = \sum_{k=0}^h \|\hat{a}_{t+k} - a_{t+k}\|_1$ is used to encourage accurate multi-step action prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of BitVLA across simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

- **Simulation Performance (LIBERO Benchmark):**
 - BitVLA, with 3.0 billion parameters, achieved an average success rate of 96.0% across the LIBERO benchmark suites. This performance is comparable to OpenVLA-OFT (7.7B parameters), which achieved 97.1%, representing a 1.1% absolute reduction despite an 11.0x smaller memory footprint.
 - BitVLA outperformed other 3B-parameter baselines, such as $\pi^0$ (94.2% average success rate) and SmolVLA (88.8% average success rate).
 - On challenging long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long), BitVLA demonstrated a success rate of 92.8%, significantly outperforming $\pi^0$ (85.2%). This highlights the benefits of the robotic pre-training stage. A variant of BitVLA without robotic pre-training achieved 87.6% on LIBERO-Long, indicating the importance of this stage.

- **Memory Footprint and Inference Efficiency:**
 - BitVLA requires only 1.4 GB of memory, marking an 11.0x reduction compared to OpenVLA-OFT (15.4 GB). This enables deployment on consumer-grade GPUs with limited memory, such as the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Ti Laptop GPU (4 GB).
 - In terms of inference efficiency, BitVLA achieved a latency of 73 ms and a throughput of 341.1 Hz. This represents a 4.4x lower latency and 4.4x higher throughput compared to OpenVLA-OFT+ (321 ms latency, 77.9 Hz throughput). BitVLA also surpassed $\pi^0$ (86 ms latency, 291.6 Hz throughput) and Diffusion Policy (90 ms latency, 267.4 Hz throughput).

- **Comparison with Post-Training Quantization (PTQ):**
 - BitVLA's performance (96.0% average success rate with 1.4 GB memory) was competitive with OpenVLA-OFT models subjected to post-training quantization to INT4 (96.9% success with 4.7 GB memory) or INT8 (96.7% success with 7.7 GB memory). This comparison illustrates the advantage of integrating low-bit constraints during training, as opposed to applying post-hoc quantization.

- **Real-world Task Performance:**
 - On core manipulation tasks (e.g., "Grasp the watermelon on the table," "Put the bread into the basket," "Flip the bell upright"), BitVLA demonstrated performance comparable to the larger OpenVLA-OFT model and consistently outperformed $\pi^0$.
 - BitVLA maintained robustness and generalization capabilities under out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, including interactions with unseen objects (e.g., "Grasp Sponge") and operation under visual distractors (e.g., "Flip Bell with Tablecloth"). It generalized effectively to novel situations in a zero-shot manner, without significant performance degradation.
 - Experiments confirmed that BitVLA trained without the pre-training stage failed to learn meaningful policies in real-world tasks, achieving near-zero success rates, underscoring the critical importance of the large-scale robotics pre-training.

- **Ablation Studies and Representation Alignment:**
 - The "Quantize-then-Distill" stage effectively preserved multimodal capabilities. The 1.58-bit vision encoder (0.1 GB memory) showed only a 1.5% absolute drop in average accuracy on various VQA benchmarks (MMMU, SeedBench, MMStar, AI2D) compared to its BF16 full-precision counterpart (0.8 GB memory).
 - The representation alignment loss ($L_{aux}$) was crucial, improving the average zero-shot accuracy of the 1.58-bit encoder from 42.4% to 50.8%. Scaling the training data for this stage (from 5B to 10B tokens) further enhanced performance, mitigating quantization-induced degradation.
 - t-SNE visualizations illustrated that the internal representations of the 1.58-bit student encoder became closely aligned with those of the full-precision teacher encoder as network depth increased, validating the effectiveness of the distillation-aware training strategy in preserving semantic structure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of BitVLA presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the fields of robotics and efficient AI:

- **Enabling Edge Deployment for Robotics:** The primary significance of BitVLA lies in its ability to enable the deployment of advanced Vision-Language-Action models on memory-constrained edge robotic platforms. By achieving an 11.0x reduction in memory footprint and a 4.4x speedup in end-to-end latency compared to larger, full-precision models, BitVLA addresses a critical barrier to real-world robot autonomy. This cost-effective and efficient solution makes sophisticated manipulation capabilities accessible to hardware environments with limited resources, such as consumer-grade GPUs on mobile robots.

- **Pioneering 1-bit VLA Models:** BitVLA establishes the first fully native 1-bit VLA model for robotic manipulation. This work provides an extreme low-bit baseline for embodied policies, demonstrating the feasibility and competitive performance of integrating aggressive quantization directly into the VLA training pipeline, rather than relying on post-hoc compression. This pushes the boundaries of efficient model design in multimodal robotics.

- **Advancement in Quantization Strategy:** The proposed "Quantize-then-Distill" stage is a methodological contribution. It offers a lightweight, quantization-aware training strategy that effectively compresses the vision encoder to 1.58-bit weights while maintaining multimodal representation alignment and downstream task performance through teacher guidance. This approach could be influential for developing efficient versions of other complex multimodal models.

- **Validation of Training-Time Co-Design:** The research validates the effectiveness of a training-time efficiency-accuracy co-design approach for embodied policies. This shifts the paradigm from separate optimization (training a large model, then compressing) to an integrated process where efficiency constraints are considered during model learning. This approach can lead to more inherently efficient and robust models for resource-limited environments.

- **Implications for Hardware Acceleration:** The alteration of the model's arithmetic profile, moving from floating-point multiply-accumulate operations to predominantly integer additions for ternary weights and INT8 activations, opens new avenues for hardware-algorithm co-design. This computational shift suggests opportunities for designing specialized accelerators optimized for 1-bit VLAs, potentially leading to further improvements in energy efficiency and processing speed in robotic systems.

- **Contribution to Generalist Robotics:** By making VLA models more compact and performant on resource-limited hardware, BitVLA contributes to the broader objective of developing generalist robots. Such robots would be capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks across various environments and embodiments, moving closer to practical, autonomous robotic systems.

- **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of code and model weights fosters reproducibility, collaboration, and further research within the robotics and low-bit AI communities, accelerating advancements in the field.
