# Video Creation by Demonstration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google DeepMind and Cornell University developed δ-Diffusion, a self-supervised, two-stage approach for video creation by demonstrating actions from a source video onto a target context image. This method enhances action concept transfer and content consistency by using pre-trained video foundation models and an appearance bottleneck, achieving up to 1.00 human preference rate over baselines.

## Problem

- Existing controllable video generation methods rely on explicit signals that are either too abstract or too laborious, limiting user expressiveness for interactive video creation.
- Traditional action transfer techniques struggle with significant misalignment (appearance, subject, environment) between demonstration and target videos, making direct transfer and paired training data collection challenging.
- Generalizing complex, context-dependent actions in diverse video content is difficult, as many advanced video generation methods are domain-specific.

## Method

- Introduced **δ-Diffusion**, a two-stage, ==self-supervised training== approach for "Video Creation by Demonstration," which takes a context image and a demonstration video to generate a new video.
- Developed an "appearance bottleneck" module that uses a pre-trained ==video foundation model== (==VideoPrism==) and a feature predictor to extract action-rich control latents with minimal appearance information.
- Employed a self-supervised training paradigm where the model reconstructs subsequent video frames from an initial frame, applying action-preserving augmentations to the demonstration video to mitigate appearance leakage.

## Results

- The δ-Diffusion appearance bottleneck consistently improved action concept transferability, outperforming raw features and temporal normalization on instance-wise metrics like Hit@k and Embedding Cosine Similarity across multiple datasets.
- Human evaluators favored δ-Diffusion for visual quality, action transferability, and context consistency, showing up to **0.83** preference over WALT (text-to-video) and up to **1.00** preference over MotionDirector.
- The method successfully demonstrated auto-regressive generation, coherently concatenating multiple distinct demonstration videos to simulate complex, multi-step tasks within a consistent environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging pre-trained video foundation models for extracting implicit latent action controls offers greater flexibility and expressiveness in video generation compared to relying on explicit signals.
> - An "appearance bottleneck" that subtracts per-frame appearance features from spatiotemporal representations effectively isolates action concepts, enabling cleaner action transfer without appearance leakage.
> - A self-supervised training approach, utilizing augmentations to create misalignment between demonstration and target, is crucial for learning robust action transfer when paired training data is scarce.
