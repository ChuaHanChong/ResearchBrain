# DL-SLAM: Enabling High-Fidelity Gaussian Splatting SLAM in Dynamic Environments based on Dual-Level Probability

> [!summary] Summary
> DL-SLAM introduces a monocular 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM system that accurately tracks camera poses and reconstructs artifact-free static maps in dynamic environments. It leverages a dual-level probabilistic framework to differentiate between static and dynamic scene elements, achieving up to a 13% reduction in RMSE for camera tracking and superior rendering quality on dynamic datasets.

## Problem

- SLAM systems face degraded camera tracking and map reconstruction in dynamic environments due to the static world assumption.
- Existing dynamic SLAM methods either discard useful information from transiently static objects or mistakenly integrate them into the static map, creating artifacts.
- Robustly identifying diverse dynamic objects and their precise boundaries is challenging, impacting both tracking and map quality.

## Method

- **DL-SLAM** proposes a monocular ==3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM== system based on a novel ==dual-level probabilistic framework==.
- This framework integrates ==open-set semantic segmentation== and geometric cues to estimate dynamic probabilities at both pixel and object levels.
- A ==feedback loop== refines pixel-level probabilities using the 3D map, and an ==object-level pruning mechanism== removes globally dynamic Gaussians from the static scene representation while allowing transiently static regions to aid tracking.

## Results

- DL-SLAM achieved up to a **13% reduction in ATE RMSE**, demonstrating superior camera tracking accuracy compared to baselines on dynamic datasets like TUM RGB-D and BONN.
- The system consistently produced artifact-free 3D reconstructions and higher fidelity rendering (superior **PSNR**, **SSIM**, **LPIPS**) by effectively pruning dynamic objects from the static map.
- The object-level semantic map enables interactive scene editing and an ablation study confirmed the critical role of both pixel- and object-level probability estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-level probabilistic framework can effectively distinguish between static, transiently static, and truly dynamic regions, enabling robust tracking and artifact-free mapping.
> - Integrating open-set semantic segmentation with geometric cues provides a more generalizable and precise approach to identifying and managing dynamic objects than predefined categories.
> - Allowing transiently static parts of dynamic objects to contribute to camera pose estimation, while explicitly pruning them from the final 3D map, optimizes both tracking accuracy and map fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization And Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental technology underpinning applications such as autonomous navigation, augmented reality, and robotics. Recent advancements in scene representation, particularly with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have led to significant improvements in novel view synthesis and dense mapping capabilities. However, a common limitation across many of these modern SLAM systems is their reliance on a static world assumption. This assumption causes a degradation in camera tracking accuracy and map reconstruction quality when dynamic objects are present in the environment.

Traditional approaches to dynamic SLAM often employ semantic segmentation masks to identify and exclude objects belonging to predefined dynamic categories (e.g., persons, vehicles). While this strategy can stabilize pose estimation by treating masked regions as outliers, it introduces two primary drawbacks:
1. **Loss of valuable information:** Dynamic objects, when transiently static, can still provide useful geometric and photometric constraints for camera pose estimation. Indiscriminate removal of all masked regions discards this potentially beneficial information, which becomes particularly problematic in scenes dominated by dynamic elements where static structure is sparse.
2. **Limited generalizability:** Reliance on predefined semantic classes restricts the system's ability to handle dynamic objects outside of these categories.

A more recent development, exemplified by WildGS-SLAM, attempts to address the loss of information from transiently static objects by using per-pixel uncertainty maps. These maps weight the contribution of pixels in the tracking process, allowing regions with low uncertainty (i.e., transiently static parts of dynamic objects) to influence pose estimation. However, this approach has its own limitations:
1. **Map artifacts:** Transiently static objects, despite having low uncertainty, are erroneously integrated into the static map, leading to persistent visual artifacts in the reconstruction.
2. **Imprecise boundaries:** The uncertainty estimation in such methods often relies purely on geometric information, which can result in ambiguous object boundaries in the uncertainty maps, hindering robust identification of dynamic regions.

DL-SLAM positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome these challenges. It aims to leverage the benefits of transiently static objects for robust pose estimation while simultaneously ensuring their exclusion from the final static map, and to improve the precision of dynamic object identification, particularly at boundaries. It builds upon the efficiency and high-fidelity rendering capabilities of 3D Gaussian Splatting while addressing its specific vulnerabilities in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of DL-SLAM are to:

1. **Enhance Camera Tracking Accuracy in Dynamic Environments:** The presence of moving objects introduces incorrect data associations and geometric inconsistencies, which corrupt camera pose estimation in traditional and many modern SLAM systems. DL-SLAM aims to mitigate this interference by adaptively weighting observations based on their likelihood of being dynamic, allowing transiently static parts of moving objects to contribute constructively to pose estimation without being treated as outliers.

2. **Reconstruct Artifact-Free Static Maps:** Current methods that attempt to leverage transiently static objects often inadvertently incorporate them into the static map representation, leading to visual artifacts. A core motivation for DL-SLAM is to ensure that while dynamic objects may aid in tracking, they are categorically excluded from the final static 3D reconstruction, thereby generating high-fidelity and artifact-free maps.

3. **Improve Robustness and Generalizability of Dynamic Object Identification:** Prior approaches often depend on predefined semantic categories for dynamic object detection or rely solely on geometric cues for motion estimation. These methods suffer from limited generalizability to novel dynamic objects and can produce imprecise dynamic boundaries. DL-SLAM is motivated by the need for a more robust and precise mechanism for identifying dynamic regions, combining both semantic and geometric information.

4. **Achieve High-Fidelity Semantic Mapping:** Integrating semantic information into 3DGS-based SLAM systems is valuable for object-level scene understanding and advanced applications. However, dynamic environments introduce challenges such as temporal inconsistencies in semantic labels and occlusions, which can degrade the quality of semantic maps. DL-SLAM seeks to develop a strategy to refine semantic labels, ensuring temporal consistency and robustly completing occluded regions.

In summary, DL-SLAM aims to develop a monocular 3DGS SLAM system that can operate effectively in dynamic environments by introducing a novel dual-level probabilistic framework. This framework is designed to intelligently differentiate between static, transiently static, and truly dynamic regions, leading to improved tracking, artifact-free static map reconstruction, and high-fidelity semantic mapping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DL-SLAM employs a monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM system built upon a novel dual-level probabilistic framework. This framework integrates semantic and geometric information to identify and manage dynamic objects, facilitating robust tracking and artifact-free static map reconstruction.

#### 3.1. Gaussian Scene Representation

The scene is represented by a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians, as in standard 3DGS. Each Gaussian $g_i$ is parameterized by its mean $\mu_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$, opacity $o_i$, and RGB color $c_i$. DL-SLAM augments this representation by adding two additional attributes to each Gaussian: a dynamic probability $p_i \in$ and an explicit semantic label $\ell_i$. This extended representation enables differentiable rendering of color, depth, dynamic probability, and semantic label maps.

#### 3.2. Dual-Level Probabilistic Framework

This framework operates at both the pixel and object levels, with a feedback loop connecting them.

#### 3.2.1. Semantic Map Generation

Instead of relying on predefined dynamic categories, DL-SLAM uses an open-set approach for semantic understanding.
- **Open-set tagging:** The Recognize Anything Model (RAM) generates class tags from input images, which serve as text prompts for Grounding DINO to produce class-aware bounding boxes.
- **Fine-grained segmentation:** Mobile-SAMv2 is then used to generate fine-grained semantic label maps from these bounding boxes.
- **Temporal consistency:** To address frame-to-frame inconsistencies in segmentations, a robust data association method is implemented. This method computes a fused matching score, combining Intersection over Union (IoU) for spatial overlap and CLIP features for appearance similarity. Objects are tracked across frames by matching new detections to existing tracked objects, assigning consistent object IDs.

#### 3.2.2. Pixel-Level Probability Estimation

This module estimates a robust, pixel-wise dynamic probability map for keyframes.
- **Optical flow and epipolar error:** A Convolutional Gated Recurrent Unit (ConvGRU) estimates optical flow between consecutive keyframes. A fundamental matrix $F$ is then estimated from these correspondences. The Sampson error $e_F(u)$ is calculated for each pixel $u$ to quantify its deviation from the epipolar constraint, indicating geometric inconsistency under a static scene assumption.
- **Statistical modeling:** The squared Sampson error for a static pixel is modeled by a chi-squared distribution with two degrees of freedom. The initial pixel-level dynamic probability $\tilde{P}_{k|k-1}(u)$ is derived from the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of this distribution.
- **Semantic aggregation:** Under the assumption that pixels on a rigid object share the same motion state, pixel-level probabilities are aggregated per semantic object. For each object $o$, an aggregated 2D dynamic probability $p_{2D}^o$ is calculated as the average of the first quartile and the median of the sorted pixel probabilities within its semantic mask. This aggregated probability is then assigned to all pixels within that object's mask, forming the initial dynamic probability map $P_k$.

#### 3.2.3. Object-Level Probability Estimation

This involves lifting 2D pixel information to the 3D Gaussian representation.
- **Gaussian attribute initialization:** New Gaussians are initialized with the dynamic probability $p_i$ and semantic label $\ell_i$ from their corresponding pixel in $P_k$ and $L_k$.
- **Recency-weighted aggregation:** To counter temporal inertia and accurately reflect recent motion states, an object-level 3D dynamic probability $p_{3D}^o$ is computed using a recency-weighted average of all associated Gaussians. Gaussians created more recently receive higher weights via an exponential decay function.
- **Dynamic Gaussian pruning:** To maintain a static map, objects are designated as dynamic if their $p_{3D}^o$ exceeds a threshold for three consecutive keyframes. All Gaussians belonging to these globally dynamic objects are then removed from the scene representation.

#### 3.2.4. Probability Update (Feedback Loop)

This mechanism refines the pixel-level probability estimates using the 3D map.
- **Rendered likelihood map:** A geometrically consistent likelihood map $\hat{P}_k$ is rendered from the current 3D Gaussian representation (Eq. 3).
- **Power-law scaling:** To amplify the influence of high-probability dynamic Gaussians, a power-law scaling $\hat{P}'_k = (\hat{P}_k)^\gamma$ is applied.
- **Bayesian update:** The amplified rendered probability $\hat{P}'_k$ is fused with the initial pixel-level estimate $P_k$ using a Bayesian framework to compute a posterior probability $P_{post_k}$. This posterior probability is then propagated back to update the dynamic attribute $p_i$ of visible 3D Gaussians.

#### 3.3. Semantic Label Refinement

This strategy addresses semantic inconsistencies and occlusions in dynamic environments.
- **Online correction for inconsistencies:** A score function combining IoU and containment ratios is used to match rendered semantic masks with input masks. If a match exceeds a threshold for three consecutive keyframes, labels are merged, and Gaussians are relabeled to enforce object-level consistency.
- **Dynamic-aware densification for occlusions:** Regions with high semantic fragmentation (quantified by a pixel-wise semantic gradient) located within aggregated dynamic object masks are targeted for densification. This improves reconstruction quality in areas corrupted by dynamic objects.

#### 3.4. SLAM Process: Tracking

- **DROID-SLAM foundation:** The tracking module builds on DROID-SLAM's dense bundle adjustment (DBA) layer, which jointly optimizes keyframe poses and disparities.
- **Dynamic probability weighting:** The DBA objective function is modified to include the pixel-level dynamic probability map $P_i$ as a weighting term. Pixels exhibiting active motion are down-weighted, allowing stable regions of transiently static objects to contribute to pose estimation. Metric depth from Metric3D is also incorporated.

#### 3.4.2. Mapping

- **Incremental Gaussian map growth:** The Gaussian map is incrementally expanded with new keyframes to cover newly observed regions.
- **3D dynamic Gaussian pruning:** Unlike 2D filtering, DL-SLAM allows all pixels to initialize new Gaussians, with dynamic Gaussians pruned later in the 3D representation based on object-level probability.
- **Local window optimization:** The Gaussian map is optimized over a local window of covisible keyframes. Rendering losses (color and depth) are minimized, weighted by the dynamic probability map $P$. An isotropic loss is also included to prevent excessive elongation of Gaussians in less constrained regions. Optimization terminates when the variance of the mapping loss falls below a threshold.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DL-SLAM was evaluated on three dynamic datasets: TUM RGB-D dynamic, BONN, and Wild-SLAM iPhone, which represents unconstrained settings. The evaluation covered camera tracking accuracy (Absolute Trajectory Error - ATE RMSE and S.D.) and reconstruction/rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). Comparisons were made against various state-of-the-art SLAM methods, including traditional, NeRF-based, and 3DGS-based approaches, both static and dynamic.

#### 4.1. Tracking Performance

- **TUM RGB-D Dynamic Dataset:** DL-SLAM consistently outperformed all baseline methods across every sequence. On average, it achieved up to a 13% reduction in RMSE compared to the next-best method, indicating superior robustness in camera pose estimation in dynamic environments.
- **BONN Dataset:** Similar to TUM RGB-D, DL-SLAM attained the best overall tracking accuracy on the BONN dataset, surpassing competing approaches.
- These results collectively indicate that the dual-level probabilistic framework effectively mitigates interference from dynamic objects, leading to more robust camera tracking.

#### 4.2. Rendering Performance

- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4):** DL-SLAM demonstrated artifact-free rendering. For example, in the "wandering" sequence, WildGS-SLAM exhibited artifacts due to the inclusion of transiently static objects, which DL-SLAM successfully pruned. In the "mv_box" sequence, DL-SLAM correctly identified and removed a moving box, preventing artifacts. The method also produced precise dynamic probability maps at object boundaries, a noted improvement over WildGS-SLAM's less accurate uncertainty estimation.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 1):** DL-SLAM consistently outperformed competing methods on the Wild-SLAM iPhone and BONN datasets in terms of PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, indicating higher fidelity and photo-realistic rendering in challenging dynamic environments.
- **Interactive Scene Editing (Figure 5):** The object-level semantic map generated by DL-SLAM was shown to support interactive scene editing, allowing users to identify and remove specific instances of objects from the final map using a simple prompt.

#### 4.3. Runtime and Memory Analysis

- **Runtime (Table 4):** DL-SLAM demonstrated efficient tracking, sharing an optimized DBA layer with WildGS-SLAM, resulting in one of the lowest latencies among compared methods. While it includes a semantic segmentation module (unlike WildGS-SLAM), its overall mapping latency was lower than WildGS-SLAM, which incurs overhead from online optimization of its uncertainty network.
- **Memory (Table 4):** DL-SLAM required moderately higher GPU usage and model size compared to some baselines (e.g., RoDyn-SLAM, WildGS-SLAM). This is attributed to the additional semantic label and dynamic probability attributes stored per Gaussian. However, it was approximately 3x more compact than SGS-SLAM, which also maintains semantic attributes.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the BONN dataset validated the effectiveness of the core components:
- **Without pixel-level probability:** Led to a significant degradation in tracking accuracy (higher ATE RMSE and S.D.), confirming its crucial role in mitigating dynamic object interference for robust tracking.
- **Without object-level probability:** Resulted in lower rendering quality (lower PSNR) and retention of artifacts, demonstrating its necessity for identifying and pruning transiently static objects to achieve artifact-free maps (Figure 6).
- **Without semantic refinement:** Also showed a decrease in rendering quality (lower PSNR), highlighting its importance in resolving semantic inconsistencies and completing occluded regions for high-fidelity semantic maps.
These results confirm the individual contributions of each proposed component to the overall performance of DL-SLAM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DL-SLAM represents an advancement in Gaussian Splatting SLAM for dynamic environments. Its significance lies in its ability to reconcile the conflicting requirements of leveraging transiently static objects for robust pose estimation while simultaneously preventing their undesirable inclusion in the final static 3D map.

The introduction of a dual-level probabilistic framework, which tightly couples pixel-level dynamic probability estimation with object-level reasoning and pruning, is a novel contribution. This framework allows for a more nuanced understanding of object motion states, moving beyond binary classifications of "static" or "dynamic." By incorporating open-set semantic segmentation and a robust data association mechanism, DL-SLAM enhances its generalizability to diverse dynamic objects, an improvement over methods reliant on predefined categories.

The feedback loop between the 2D pixel-level estimates and the 3D map, guided by a Bayesian update mechanism, provides a computationally efficient way to refine dynamic probability predictions, addressing limitations of purely 2D or multi-stage geometric methods. Furthermore, the dynamic-aware semantic label refinement strategy contributes to the generation of high-fidelity and temporally consistent semantic maps, which are essential for advanced scene understanding and interaction.

The consistently superior performance in both camera tracking accuracy (up to 13% RMSE reduction) and rendering quality (higher PSNR, SSIM, lower LPIPS) across multiple challenging dynamic datasets suggests that DL-SLAM offers a more robust and reliable solution for SLAM in real-world, dynamic scenarios. The capability for artifact-free rendering and interactive scene editing further demonstrates the practical utility of the generated maps.

Potential impacts of this work include:
- **Enhanced Robotic Navigation:** More accurate and robust pose estimation in human-populated or dynamic industrial environments.
- **Improved Augmented/Mixed Reality:** Creation of more stable and realistic virtual content overlays that seamlessly interact with static real-world scenes, even when people or objects move.
- **Advanced Embodied AI:** Providing more reliable 3D scene understanding for AI agents operating in dynamic settings.
- **Foundation for 4D Reconstruction:** The authors identify modeling trajectories of dynamic objects for complete 4D scene understanding as a promising future direction, indicating that DL-SLAM could serve as a foundational step towards such comprehensive dynamic scene representations.

The moderate increase in memory footprint due to additional Gaussian attributes is a trade-off for enhanced functionality, which is within acceptable limits given the capabilities of modern hardware. Overall, DL-SLAM contributes a method that balances performance, robustness, and semantic understanding in dynamic SLAM, pushing the capabilities of Gaussian Splatting in complex real-world applications.
