# FAME: Force-Adaptive RL for Expanding the Manipulation Envelope of a Full-Scale Humanoid

> [!summary] Summary
> The FAME framework enables full-scale humanoids to maintain stable standing under varying external hand forces and diverse arm configurations by leveraging force-adaptive reinforcement learning with a latent context encoder. This approach achieved a 73.84% mean standing success rate in simulation and demonstrated stable bimanual manipulation on a Unitree H12 robot without relying on dedicated wrist force sensors.

## Problem

- Humanoids face challenges maintaining balance against varying and uncertain external forces applied to the hands, as these disturbances propagate and destabilize the lower body.
- Existing balance control methods, both model-based and DRL, often struggle with the dynamic, high-dimensional, and coupled nature of manipulation-induced disturbances.
- The need for dedicated wrist force/torque sensors increases hardware complexity and cost, hindering practical deployment in human-centric environments.

## Method

- FAME, a force-adaptive reinforcement learning framework, integrates an upper-body context encoder with a base standing policy for robust balance.
- The framework trains the policy using a curriculum of diverse upper-body poses and spherically sampled hand forces to cover a wide range of manipulation-induced disturbances.
- For deployment, FAME estimates wrist interaction forces from proprioceptive joint torque and kinematics, removing the dependency on dedicated wrist force/torque sensors.

## Results

- FAME achieved a 73.84% mean standing success rate in simulation, a notable improvement over the 29.44% of the Base policy and 51.40% of the Base+Curr policy.
- The framework successfully maintained stable standing on a physical Unitree H12 humanoid under both asymmetric single-arm and symmetric bimanual loads, qualitatively validating its real-world effectiveness where comparison policies failed.
- The approach quantitatively and qualitatively expands the humanoid's manipulation envelope, enabling stable balance across diverse arm configurations and under varied external loads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding the coupling between upper-body configuration and bimanual interaction forces into a latent context significantly improves lower-body balance adaptation against manipulation-induced disturbances.
> - Latent context adaptation proves effective for managing structured, task-relevant force and pose variations in humanoid manipulation, extending its application beyond environmental variations.
> - Online estimation of wrist interaction forces from proprioceptive joint torques and kinematics enables a sensor-free deployment strategy for force-adaptive control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are designed to interact within human-centric environments, necessitating sophisticated control strategies for bimanual manipulation while maintaining lower-body stability. This research addresses a fundamental challenge within this domain: enabling humanoids to maintain balance when subjected to varying and uncertain external forces applied at the hands, which propagate through the kinematic chain and disturb the robot's stance.

Historically, humanoid balance control has relied on traditional model-based methods, such as the Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM), Model Predictive Control (MPC), and trajectory optimization. While effective under specific conditions, these methods often struggle with highly dynamic situations and significant external disturbances, which are common during manipulation tasks.

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has emerged as an alternative approach for generating robust locomotion and balance policies for humanoid robots. Prior DRL work has achieved notable success in bipedal walking and standing, often focusing on scenarios without substantial external hand forces. However, integrating stable balance with bimanual manipulation under diverse and uncertain interaction forces remains a complex problem due to the high-dimensional, coupled nature of the disturbances, which depend on force magnitude, direction, and arm configuration.

The paper leverages and extends techniques developed to mitigate the sim-to-real gap, specifically domain randomization and latent context adaptation. Domain randomization exposes policies to varied conditions during training, while latent context adaptation, exemplified by Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), learns compact representations of state variations for online policy adaptation. These methods have primarily targeted environmental variations like terrain or mass distribution. FAME adapts this latent context encoding paradigm to the structured force variations inherent in manipulation, addressing the coupling between upper-body configuration and bimanual interaction forces.

In the realm of force-adaptive control, other research has explored dual-agent RL with force curricula (e.g., FALCON) for forceful tasks, disturbance modeling via virtual spring-damper systems (HAFO), and force-adaptive torso-tilt rewards (Thor) to maximize interaction strength. FAME differentiates itself by learning a latent representation of the force-configuration coupling for standing stability under varying and uncertain bimanual forces, rather than focusing on maximizing force application or explicitly modeling disturbances.

Regarding force estimation, while some approaches directly utilize wrist force/torque sensors or leverage torque feedback from motor-level currents, FAME introduces a sensor-free deployment strategy. It estimates wrist interaction forces from measured joint torques and kinematics, thus eliminating the need for dedicated wrist force/torque sensors. This aspect of force estimation from proprioceptive signals is situated within a broader body of work that identifies joint torque as a critical signal for DRL in locomotion.

Finally, curriculum learning has been widely adopted in robot learning to progressively expose policies to increasingly difficult scenarios, including scaling command ranges, terrain difficulty, and external perturbations. FAME integrates an upper-body pose curriculum, similar to approaches like OpenHomie, with force-based context encoding. This combination enables the policy to learn from both geometric diversity (varying arm poses) and dynamic diversity (varying interaction forces), thereby creating a more robust and adaptable balance controller.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to expand the "manipulation envelope" of a full-scale humanoid robot. The authors define the manipulation envelope as the range of admissible external hand forces and arm configurations under which the robot can maintain stable standing. Enabling humanoids to reliably maintain stable standing when subjected to varying and uncertain hand loads is identified as a fundamental challenge.

The motivation for this work stems from several interconnected issues inherent in humanoid bimanual manipulation:
1. **Interaction Force Propagation:** When external forces are applied to a humanoid's hands during manipulation, these forces do not remain localized. Instead, they propagate through the robot's kinematic chain, directly impacting and disturbing the lower-body balance. This physical coupling necessitates coordinated control of both upper and lower body.
2. **Constraint on Manipulation Envelope:** Unless actively compensated, these interaction forces restrict the feasible range of motions and loads the robot can handle, effectively limiting its manipulation capabilities. Upper-body tasks become intrinsically coupled with the robot's ability to maintain stance stability.
3. **Complex Disturbance Nature:** The disturbances induced by manipulation are high-dimensional and coupled. They depend not only on the magnitude and direction of the applied loads but also on the geometric configuration of the robot's arms and torso. This complex state space makes it difficult for standard reinforcement learning approaches to explore exhaustively during training and to adapt effectively during deployment.
4. **Need for Online Adaptation:** Real-world manipulation tasks involve dynamic and often unpredictable forces. A robust balance policy must adapt online to these real-time variations without requiring retraining or manual tuning for each new scenario.
5. **Sensor Dependency:** Traditional methods often rely on specialized sensors, such as wrist force/torque sensors, to measure interaction forces. Eliminating the need for such dedicated sensors can reduce hardware complexity, cost, and potential points of failure, making robot deployment more practical.

To address these motivations, the authors propose FAME, a force-adaptive reinforcement learning framework. FAME aims to condition a standing policy on a learned latent context that encodes both the upper-body joint configuration and bimanual interaction forces. This approach is intended to enable robust balance across diverse arm configurations and under varied external loads, thereby expanding the humanoid's manipulation capabilities in human-centered environments. A specific objective is to achieve online adaptation by estimating interaction forces from robot dynamics without requiring wrist force/torque sensors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FAME framework for force-adaptive standing consists of two primary components: an upper-body context encoder and a base standing policy. The encoder learns a compact, latent representation of the disturbances caused by upper-body configuration and bimanual forces, which then conditions the base policy to adapt its lower-body control.

**A. Training Strategy**
During training, the policy is exposed to diverse manipulation-induced disturbances through two mechanisms:
1. **Spherically Sampled Hand Forces:** External 3D forces are applied to each hand, with magnitudes sampled from a predefined range and directions uniformly sampled over a sphere. This provides an isotropic and unbiased distribution of forces, ensuring broad coverage of the 3D force space.
2. **Upper-Body Pose Curriculum:** An upper-body pose curriculum, adapted from OpenHomie, is employed. This curriculum progressively expands the range of randomized target poses for the upper-body (torso and arms) as the robot's standing quality (measured by height-tracking reward) improves. This exposes the policy to continuously varying arm configurations.

**B. Baselines for Comparison**
To evaluate the individual contributions of the curriculum and the latent context encoder, three training variants are compared:
1. **Base:** A standing policy trained with the upper-body fixed at a nominal posture, without an upper-body pose curriculum or any latent context conditioning.
2. **Base+Curr:** A standing policy trained with the upper-body pose curriculum, but without the upper-body context encoder. The policy must implicitly infer the effect of pose and force from proprioception alone.
3. **FAME:** The proposed framework, combining both the upper-body pose curriculum and the upper-body context encoder, whose latent output conditions the standing policy for force-adaptive balance.

**C. Upper-Body Pose Curriculum (Detailed)**
The curriculum uses a scalar upper-body action ratio ($\rho_a \in$), initialized to 0 and increased by a step ($\Delta\rho$) when the height-tracking reward exceeds a threshold.
- **Curriculum-scaled ratio sampling:** An auxiliary ratio ($\rho'_a$) is drawn, which concentrates near zero early in training and broadens as $\rho_a$ increases. This ensures that the policy initially learns to stabilize under small perturbations before being exposed to larger ones.
- **Sampling upper-body targets:** For each upper-body joint, a new target angle is sampled within a curriculum-scaled interval around the default posture, using the $\rho'_a$ value. Target poses are resampled every 1 second. This gradually expands the range of joint angles from small perturbations to a larger portion of the feasible joint ranges.

**D. Upper-Body Context Encoder (Detailed)**
- **Input:** The encoder takes a concatenated input vector $x_{enc}$, comprising 15 upper-body joint positions ($q_{ub}$) and 6 DoF bimanual interaction forces (3 DoF for left wrist $F_L$, 3 DoF for right wrist $F_R$).
- **Output:** It maps $x_{enc}$ to an 8-dimensional latent context vector $z_t$ using a multi-layer perceptron (MLP).
- **Force Generation (Training):** External forces are generated by sampling a magnitude $r$ from a uniform range $[r_{min}, r_{max}]$ and a direction $u$ uniformly over a 3D sphere.
- **Force Estimation (Deployment):** During deployment, wrist force/torque sensors are not required. Instead, interaction forces are estimated using robot dynamics. This involves measuring joint torques ($\tau$), computing gravity compensation torques ($\tau_g$), and mapping the residual torques into Cartesian space using the wrist Jacobian ($J$). The external force is estimated as $F_{ext} = -(J^\top)^\dagger(\tau - \tau_g)$, where $\dagger$ denotes the pseudo-inverse. The Pinocchio library is used for online computation of the Jacobian and gravity compensation torques.

**E. Base Policy (Detailed)**
- **Formulation:** The problem is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), and the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is used to learn a parameterized policy $\pi_\theta(a_t | o_t)$.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs a 12-dimensional action vector ($a_t$) representing target joint position offsets for the 12 lower-body joints (6 per leg). These offsets are added to a default lower-body posture, and the resulting target positions are tracked by a joint-space PD controller running at 50 Hz.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function is comprehensive, incorporating terms for base stability (height tracking, vertical/angular velocity, orientation, static stability), posture and joint deviation (hip, ankle, knee, joint tracking error, acceleration), feet contact and grounding (lateral distance, parallelism, slip, stumble, contact forces, contact momentum, no-fly), control smoothness and energy (action rate, second-order smoothness, joint power, torque penalty, action saturation), and safety limits (DoF position/velocity, torque limits). The reward components are adapted from for the Unitree H12.
- **Network Architecture:** The policy employs an HIM-style architecture, where the actor comprises an estimator network (E) and a follow-up policy network (N), and the critic is a separate value network (C). The actor observation space includes a stacked history of proprioceptive states and latent context, while the critic uses a privileged one-step observation augmented with base linear velocity.

**F. Domain Randomization**
Various domain randomization strategies are applied during training to improve sim-to-real transfer and robustness. These include randomization of external disturbances (e.g., pushes), actuation and observation noise (joint injection noise, actuation offset, control delay), inertial and body parameters (payload mass, body displacement, link mass scaling), contact parameters (friction coefficient, restitution), and controller parameters (PD gain scaling, initial joint position scale/offset).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates FAME in both simulation and on a physical humanoid robot to determine its effectiveness in expanding the manipulation envelope.

**A. Simulation Results**
- **Experimental Setup:** The simulation experiments involved five fixed upper-body arm configurations (C1-C5, Table VIII), designed to represent diverse poses ranging from forward-reaching to lateral and asymmetric. For each configuration, randomized external hand forces were applied (F_x, F_y $\in [-20, 20]$ N, F_z $\in [-30, 30]$ N), and the commanded base height was randomized within $[0.7, 1.0]$ m. A trial was considered successful if the robot remained upright for 10 seconds, and results were reported over 500 random configurations per scenario.
- **Performance Comparison (Table II):**
 - **Base Policy:** This policy, trained without an upper-body pose curriculum or context encoder, exhibited poor performance, achieving a mean success rate of 29.44%. It performed non-trivially only in configurations closer to symmetric arm placements (e.g., C3: 31.8%, C4: 44.2%), where forces might induce more balanced moments. However, it completely failed (0% success) in configurations introducing pronounced asymmetry or forward-reaching leverage (C1: Forward Extended, C2: Mid Sideways), indicating severe destabilization under moderate hand forces when moment arms are long or unbalanced.
 - **Base+Curr Policy:** Incorporating an upper-body pose curriculum (without the encoder) improved the mean success rate to 51.40%. This baseline generally improved performance across most configurations by exposing the policy to diverse arm poses during training. However, it sometimes underperformed the Base policy in highly asymmetric configurations (e.g., C5: 56.0% for Base+Curr vs. 71.2% for Base), suggesting that increased upper-body variability without explicit disturbance disambiguation might lead to conservative or mismatched stabilization strategies under high asymmetry.
 - **FAME Policy:** The proposed FAME framework, which combines the upper-body pose curriculum with the upper-body context encoder, achieved the strongest and most consistent performance. It significantly improved the mean standing success rate to 73.84%. FAME demonstrated high success rates even in the most challenging configurations that caused failure for the baselines (e.g., C1: 81.6%, C2: 50.0%, C5: 85.4%).
- **Conclusion from Simulation:** The results indicate that explicitly encoding the coupling between upper-body configuration and bimanual interaction forces provides a structured contextual signal. This signal enables the lower-body controller to adapt more effectively to the direction and magnitude of manipulation-induced disturbances, leading to superior stabilization and a substantial expansion of the feasible manipulation envelope (Fig. 3).

**B. Real Robot Experiments**
- **Experimental Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a Unitree H12 full-scale humanoid robot. The robot utilizes its onboard IMU for base orientation and angular velocity, and joint encoders for joint angles and velocities. The base policy runs at 50 Hz. Real-world deployment introduces challenges such as unmodeled actuator dynamics, contact uncertainty, and sensor/force estimation noise. The experiments were designed to stress the coupling between upper-body interaction forces and arm configurations. Two scenarios were evaluated:
 - **RE1 (Asymmetric Single-Arm Load):** Tests robustness to an asymmetric disturbance where one arm carries a load.
 - **RE2 (Symmetric Bimanual Load):** Evaluates stability when weight is distributed across both arms.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 4):**
 - **FAME Policy:** Under both asymmetric and symmetric loads, the robot maintained stability. Joint positions, particularly hip pitch and ankle pitch, remained close to their nominal standing configurations, and joint torques were regulated effectively.
 - **Base+Curr Policy:** In contrast, with the Base+Curr policy, the robot lost balance and fell. The joint trajectories showed significant drift from stable configurations, indicating an inability to adequately compensate for the external disturbances.
- **Conclusion from Real Experiments:** The real-robot evaluations qualitatively validate that the force-conditioned latent adaptation provided by FAME improves standing stability not only under randomized forces in simulation but also under structured, manipulation-relevant disturbances on physical hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FAME framework contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing a critical limitation in bimanual manipulation: maintaining stable balance under dynamic and uncertain external hand forces. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Expanded Manipulation Envelope:** The most direct impact is the quantitative and qualitative demonstration of an expanded manipulation envelope for a full-scale humanoid. FAME significantly increases the range of admissible external hand forces and arm configurations under which the robot can maintain stable standing. This is evidenced by a mean standing success rate of 73.84% in simulation, substantially outperforming baselines. This expanded envelope allows humanoids to undertake more complex and force-intensive interaction tasks.

2. **Robustness to Manipulation-Induced Disturbances:** The work demonstrates that explicitly encoding the coupling between upper-body configuration and bimanual interaction forces into a latent context provides a structured signal that enables robust balance recovery. This is particularly impactful for tasks where upper-body pose variations and wrist forces collectively perturb lower-body balance.

3. **Sensor-Free Deployment:** FAME introduces a practical, sensor-free strategy for estimating wrist interaction forces from measured joint torques and robot kinematics. By eliminating the need for dedicated wrist force/torque sensors, the framework potentially reduces hardware complexity, cost, and maintenance, facilitating broader adoption and deployment of humanoids in real-world settings.

4. **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** The research extends the paradigm of latent context adaptation, previously applied to environmental variations (e.g., terrain), to structured, task-relevant disturbances in manipulation. This application demonstrates the versatility of latent context encoding for enabling online policy adaptation to internal robot state variations and external interaction forces.

5. **Enabling Humanoid Autonomy in Human Environments:** By improving stability under external loads and diverse arm poses, FAME moves humanoids closer to operating autonomously and effectively in human-centered environments. This includes tasks such as carrying objects, cooperating with humans (e.g., handing over tools), pushing or pulling objects, and operating tools that involve significant interaction forces. The ability to manage these forces without losing balance is crucial for safe and efficient operation.

6. **Foundation for Future Loco-Manipulation Research:** FAME provides a robust standing foundation that can be integrated into more comprehensive loco-manipulation frameworks. A stable stance under load is a prerequisite for dynamic walking while carrying objects or performing tasks requiring sustained interaction.

In summary, FAME represents an advancement in humanoid balance control, offering a generalizable approach for enhancing the robustness and versatility of bimanual manipulation capabilities in full-scale humanoid robots, with practical implications for real-world deployment.
