# Towards Efficient LLM Grounding for Embodied Multi-Agent Collaboration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced Reinforced Advantage (ReAd), a closed-loop framework integrating Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) advantage functions to provide principled feedback for Large Language Model (LLM) planning in embodied multi-agent tasks. This approach reduces environmental interactions and LLM queries, achieving superior task success rates and efficiency across various multi-robot and cooperative benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing LLM grounding methods for embodied multi-agent tasks suffer from inefficiencies due to excessive LLM queries and costly environmental interactions.
- Traditional feedback mechanisms, like self-reflection or physical verification, often provide sparse or heuristic guidance, making it difficult to precisely attribute credit to individual agent actions.
- Evaluating the specific impact of individual agents' actions on overall team outcomes is challenging, especially with sparse reward signals.

## Method

- ReAd employs a critic network to learn multi-agent advantage functions (joint and local) that quantify the contribution of actions to the collective goal, replacing heuristic feedback.
- The framework provides theoretical grounding by extending advantage-weighted regression to multi-agent systems, enabling LLMs to refine plans by filtering actions with negative advantage scores.
- Two refinement schemes, ReAd-J (joint plan) and ReAd-S (sequential individual plan), leverage advantage scores to prompt LLMs for re-planning if proposed actions fall below a performance threshold.

## Results

- ReAd-S and ReAd-J consistently outperformed strong LLM-based baselines across DV-RoCoBench and Overcooked-AI, achieving higher success rates while significantly reducing environment steps and LLM queries.
- The method demonstrated superior robustness to unexpected environmental disturbances, maintaining **100%** success rate in 'Make Sandwich' task resets where baselines experienced significant performance drops.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the iterative plan refinement guided by advantage feedback is crucial for achieving monotonic policy improvement and robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating MARL-inspired advantage functions provides a theoretically robust and efficient feedback mechanism for LLMs, moving beyond ad-hoc or observation-heavy approaches.
> - Converting advantage-weighted re-weighting into a binary filtering mechanism for LLM prompts allows closed-source LLMs to effectively self-refine plans based on principled foresight.
> - Decomposing the joint advantage into local advantages enables finer-grained credit assignment, improving individual agent contributions to a collective outcome.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Embodied AI, and Multi-Agent Systems, addressing a critical challenge: efficiently grounding LLM reasoning for complex physical tasks involving multiple collaborative agents.

**Current Landscape:**
Large Language Models have demonstrated remarkable reasoning and planning capabilities across diverse tasks, benefiting from vast textual corpora. In Embodied AI, recent research has explored using LLMs for decision-making and planning in physical environments, often employing a closed-loop framework where LLM outputs are refined through feedback. Existing closed-loop methods generally fall into two categories:
- **Self-reflection:** LLMs evaluate their own generated plans and improve them iteratively (e.g., ReAct, Reflexion, MindAgent).
- **Physical verification:** Feedback from the external environment (e.g., success/failure, collision detection, object recognition) is used to dynamically replan. Approaches like RoCo integrate physical verification for multi-agent scenarios.

**Identified Gaps and Inefficiencies:**
Despite these advancements, current feedback mechanisms suffer from significant inefficiencies. Self-reflection often provides sparse or heuristic feedback, while physical verification necessitates frequent and often costly interactions with the physical environment. This leads to excessive LLM queries, prolonged interaction steps, and difficulties in evaluating the impact of individual actions on overall team outcomes, especially in multi-agent settings. For instance, RoCo, despite its effectiveness, is noted for its poor efficiency due to numerous interactions.

**Bridging the Gap:**
The paper critically observes that Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has successfully developed value and advantage decomposition theories to evaluate individual agents' contributions to a collective outcome and enhance coordination. However, these principled MARL mechanisms have largely been underutilized in LLM-based multi-agent collaboration. This work aims to bridge this gap by introducing MARL-inspired advantage functions as a robust and efficient feedback mechanism for LLM planning in multi-agent embodied tasks. By doing so, it proposes a more theoretically grounded and efficient alternative to the prevalent heuristic or interaction-heavy feedback loops. This positions the work as a significant step towards enabling LLMs to plan and coordinate more intelligently and efficiently in complex, real-world multi-agent environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged inefficiencies of existing LLM grounding methods for embodied multi-agent tasks. The authors highlight that traditional self-reflection and physical verification methods often lead to:
- **Excessive LLM queries:** Repeated prompting and reprocessing of information by LLMs.
- **Frequent environmental interactions:** Costly and time-consuming physical execution to gather feedback.
- **Difficulty in credit assignment:** Evaluating the specific impact of individual agents' actions on the overall team outcome, especially with sparse reward signals.

Inspired by the success of value and advantage decomposition theories in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), which provide principled ways to assess individual contributions and improve coordination, the authors pose a fundamental question: "How can we enhance the reasoning efficiency of LLMs for embodied multi-agent collaboration with theoretical supports of MARL?"

Based on this, the **key objectives** of this paper are to:
- Develop an efficient feedback and refinement algorithm that leverages multi-agent advantage functions for LLM-assisted multi-agent planning.
- Endow LLMs with the "foresight" to discern whether a proposed action truly contributes to accomplishing the final task, thereby improving plan optimality.
- Significantly decrease the number of interaction steps required by agents and the number of query rounds to LLMs, leading to a more efficient grounding process.
- Provide a strong theoretical foundation for this approach by extending existing reinforcement learning principles (like advantage-weighted regression) to the multi-agent context.

In essence, the authors aim to move beyond ad-hoc or purely observational feedback mechanisms to a principled, theoretically-backed system that allows LLMs to self-refine their collaborative plans with greater efficiency and effectiveness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Reinforced Advantage (ReAd)**, a novel closed-loop framework for efficient LLM grounding in embodied multi-agent collaboration. ReAd leverages multi-agent advantage functions, inspired by MARL, to provide a principled feedback mechanism for LLM plan refinement.

The methodology can be broken down into three main parts:

#### 3.1. Learning of Advantage Functions

The core of ReAd involves estimating two types of advantage functions using a critic network:
- **Joint Advantage Function (Aπ(s, a)):** This measures the advantage of a joint action `a` (taken by all agents) in a given state `s`. It's defined as `Qπ(s, a) - Vπ(s)`. To avoid explicitly learning `Vπ(s)`, the authors ingeniously derive `Vπ(s)` from `Qπ(s, w)` where `w` represents a joint "WAIT" action (all agents doing nothing). This means `Aπ(s, a) = Qπ(s, a) - (1/γ)Qπ(s, w)`. This is used for joint plan evaluation.
- **Local Advantage Function (A_i^π(s, a_{1:i-1}, a_i)):** This evaluates the contribution of an individual agent `i`'s action `a_i`, conditioned on the state `s` and the actions of preceding agents `a_{1:i-1}`. This concept is adopted from multi-agent advantage decomposition theories in MARL, allowing for finer-grained credit assignment.
- **Monte Carlo Estimation:** Both Q-functions (joint and local) are learned via a critic network (Q_θ) trained through regression. A dataset `D` is collected by following the LLM's initial behavior policy (`μ = π_llm`). To mitigate the out-of-distribution (OOD) problem and enhance state coverage, the dataset is augmented, for instance, by resetting the environment to otherwise unreachable states. The critic minimizes a squared error loss between Monte Carlo return estimates (R_s,a_{1:u}) and the predicted Q-values.
- **Multi-Agent Advantage Decomposition (Lemma 1):** A theoretical proof is provided, extending Kuba et al. (2022a), showing that the joint advantage function can be decomposed into a sum of local advantages of each agent, assuming sequential action selection. This decomposition is fundamental for the "Sequential Individual Plan Refinement" scheme.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Motivation for Grounding LLM

The authors provide a theoretical basis for ReAd by extending **advantage-weighted regression** (Peng et al., 2019) to multi-agent systems. The goal is to find a policy `π` that improves upon the base LLM policy `μ` by maximizing the expected improvement `η(π) = J(π) - J(μ)`. Using the performance difference lemma and a surrogate objective (approximating `η(π)` over the base policy's visitation frequencies), the optimal policy `π*` is derived. This `π*` is shown to be an exponentially weighted version of `μ`, where actions with higher advantages are given higher probabilities. This re-weighting forms the mathematical basis for ReAd's feedback mechanism.

#### 3.3. Prompting by Reinforced Advantage Feedback

ReAd converts the theoretical advantage-weighted re-weighting into a practical feedback mechanism for LLMs, especially closed-source ones like GPT-4, without direct access to action probabilities. The key insight is that, as the weighting factor `β` approaches zero, the exponential weighting effectively becomes a **binary filter**: only actions with positive advantages `(A > ϵ)` are considered.
Based on this, ReAd offers two plan refinement schemes:
- **ReAd-J (Joint Plan Refinement):** The LLM is prompted to generate a joint action `a_t` for all agents simultaneously. The critic then evaluates its joint advantage `S_ReAd-J(a_t) = A_θ(s_t, a_t)`. If the score is below a predefined threshold `ϵ`, the LLM is prompted to re-plan, receiving the low score and the "failed" plan in its history.
- **ReAd-S (Sequential Individual Plan Refinement):** Agents generate their actions `a_i^t` sequentially. For each agent `i`, the LLM is prompted to generate an action `a_i^t` that maximizes its local advantage `S_ReAd-S(a_i^t) = A_i^θ(s_t, a_{1:i-1}^t, a_i^t)`. Similar to ReAd-J, if the score is below `ϵ`, the LLM is asked to re-plan.

The refinement loop ensures that only actions exceeding the advantage threshold are executed, guaranteeing a monotonic improvement over the base LLM policy. This significantly reduces environmental interactions compared to physical verification, as undesirable plans are filtered *before* execution. The critic network, an MLP with a pre-trained BERT transformer for language features, learns to predict Q-values based on combined language and environment state features.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ReAd was conducted on two benchmarks: **Difficult Variants of RoCoBench (DV-RoCoBench)**, an extension of RoCoBench with varying difficulty levels across three multi-robot tasks (Sweep Floor, Make Sandwich, Sort Cubes), and an adapted **Overcooked-AI** environment with two scenarios (Cramped Room, Forced Coordination). The base LLM used was GPT-4-Turbo, with additional tests on Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct. Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR, higher is better), Environment Steps (ES, lower is better), and Number of LLM Queries (NQ, lower is better).

#### 4.1. Superior Performance and Efficiency

- **Dominant Across Benchmarks:** Both ReAd-S and ReAd-J consistently outperformed strong LLM-based baselines (RoCo, ReAct, Reflexion, MindAgent, Central Plan) across nearly all tasks and difficulty levels in DV-RoCoBench and Overcooked-AI.
- **Reduced Interactions and Queries:** ReAd methods achieved significantly lower ES and comparable or lower NQ compared to baselines, especially those relying on physical verification. This demonstrates that LLM planning guided by advantage functions generates more optimal plans, requiring fewer steps to task completion and fewer re-planning cycles.
- **Scaling with Difficulty:** The performance advantage of ReAd became more pronounced as task difficulty increased, highlighting its ability to handle more complex coordination challenges where baselines faltered.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Environmental Disturbances

- An extended experiment on the "Make Sandwich" task with unexpected environmental resets demonstrated ReAd-S's superior robustness. When the environment was reset mid-task without explicit prompts, ReAd-S consistently maintained a 100% success rate and lower ES/NQ, even as RoCo and its variants experienced significant performance drops, especially under more severe disruptions. This indicates that the advantage feedback mechanism allows the LLM planner to rapidly re-adjust plans and overcome misalignment issues, alleviating potential hallucination.

#### 4.3. Importance of Plan Refinement (Ablation Study)

- An ablation study compared the full, multi-step ReAd (with iterative refinement) against a single-step version (without refinement). The multi-step ReAd significantly outperformed its single-step counterpart in both success rate and efficiency (lower ES and NQ). This finding underscores that the iterative plan refinement, which ensures actions with positive advantage scores are selected, is crucial for achieving monotonic policy improvement and robust performance.

#### 4.4. Versatility with Open-Source LLMs

- When tested with Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct instead of GPT-4, ReAd-J still demonstrated a significant performance lead over other baselines using the same open-source LLM, despite a general decline in overall performance compared to GPT-4. This suggests that the ReAd framework is effective across different LLM backbones.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Insights and Limitations (Symbol Grounding)

- The authors also included an extended discussion on the symbol grounding problem, acknowledging that current benchmarks often rely on textual descriptions of object identifiers rather than true visual grounding. A GPT-4o experiment confirmed that while VLMs can describe images, they struggle with precise spatial relationships and detailed entity information, leading to inconsistencies with ground truth. This suggests a current limitation in LLMs' ability to fully perceive and reason about the physical world solely from visual input, implying that current "LLM grounding" might be over-estimated for this aspect.

In summary, ReAd establishes itself as a highly efficient, robust, and theoretically grounded approach for LLM planning in multi-agent embodied collaboration, setting a new benchmark for efficiency metrics while maintaining high task success rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Towards Efficient LLM Grounding for Embodied Multi-Agent Collaboration" holds significant implications for the fields of artificial intelligence, robotics, and multi-agent systems.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Efficiency in Embodied LLM Planning

The most immediate and impactful contribution is the substantial improvement in efficiency. By replacing computationally expensive and time-consuming physical verification or LLM self-reflection with a theoretically grounded advantage feedback mechanism, ReAd significantly reduces both the number of environment interaction steps and LLM queries. This efficiency gain is crucial for the practical deployment of LLM-powered embodied agents, making training and execution faster and more resource-effective in real-world scenarios.

#### 5.2. Principled Approach to LLM Grounding

ReAd introduces a principled framework for grounding LLMs in the physical world by integrating robust theoretical concepts from Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning, such as advantage functions and their decomposition, into the LLM planning loop. This moves beyond heuristic or ad-hoc feedback mechanisms, offering a more robust and predictable method for LLMs to learn and refine their actions, with theoretical guarantees for monotonic policy improvement.

#### 5.3. Improved Multi-Agent Collaboration

The framework directly addresses the challenges of multi-agent collaboration, particularly in credit assignment and coordinated planning. By providing agents with foresight into how their actions contribute to collective goals (via joint or local advantage scores), ReAd enables more effective and harmonious cooperation, even in complex, dynamic environments. This is a critical step towards building truly intelligent and collaborative robotic teams.

#### 5.4. Robustness to Dynamic Environments

The demonstrated ability of ReAd to adapt rapidly to unexpected environmental disturbances without explicit re-prompting or clearing history is a major step towards deploying LLMs in unpredictable real-world settings. This inherent robustness, facilitated by the critic's ability to quickly re-evaluate actions in changed states, enhances the reliability of LLM-driven agents.

#### 5.5. Broader Applicability and Scalability

The effectiveness of ReAd across diverse embodied tasks and with different LLM backbones (GPT-4 and Llama-3.1) suggests its generalizability. This framework can potentially be adapted to a wide range of embodied AI applications, from manufacturing and logistics to search-and-rescue and assistive robotics.

#### 5.6. Potential Societal Impact

The advancements offered by ReAd have profound societal implications. More efficient and robust multi-robot collaboration can lead to:
- **Increased Automation and Productivity:** Streamlined robotic operations in industries, enhancing efficiency and reducing human workload in repetitive or hazardous tasks.
- **Advanced AI Systems:** Contribution to the development of AI systems capable of more informed and effective decision-making in complex domains like healthcare (e.g., surgical assistance, diagnostic processes) or environmental management (e.g., resource optimization, disaster response).

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, particularly concerning the scalability to "many-agent" scenarios. While ReAd-S (sequential refinement) might theoretically scale better than ReAd-J (joint refinement) due to scoring individual actions, the computational cost of data collection for critic training could still be substantial. Future work includes investigating ReAd in many-agent scenarios, extending it to multi-objective and safe planning, and the crucial development of more robust benchmarks for visual-based grounding to address the "symbol grounding problem." The observed limitations of current VLMs in precisely understanding spatial relationships from images highlight a significant area for future research to bridge the gap between textual descriptions and real-world perception.

In conclusion, ReAd represents a significant advancement in grounding LLMs for embodied multi-agent collaboration, offering a principled, efficient, and robust solution with wide-ranging potential for future AI and robotic applications.
