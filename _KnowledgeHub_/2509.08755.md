# AgentGym-RL: Training LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Decision Making through Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An open-source framework, AgentGym-RL, facilitates the training of large language model agents for long-horizon decision-making through multi-turn reinforcement learning and a progressive interaction scaling strategy called ScalingInter-RL. This approach enables a 7B parameter model to achieve an average success rate comparable to or exceeding larger proprietary models across diverse environments, highlighting the impact of RL training on agentic intelligence.

## Problem

- A lack of a unified, interactive reinforcement learning (RL) framework for training LLM agents from scratch for complex, multi-turn, long-horizon tasks.
- Existing LLM agent development methods, such as prompting or supervised fine-tuning (SFT), often rely on proprietary models or human-curated data, limiting scalability, adaptability, and intrinsic self-improvement.
- Traditional RL applications for LLMs are largely confined to single-turn tasks or face instability and inefficiency issues when extended to multi-turn agent decision-making.

## Method

- Introduces **AgentGym-RL**, a modular, open-source framework with decoupled environment, agent, and training modules to support diverse multi-turn, long-horizon tasks.
- Develops ScalingInter-RL, a novel progressive interaction scaling strategy that adaptively increases the maximum number of turns during RL training to balance exploration and exploitation, improving optimization stability and efficiency.
- Integrates a unified RL pipeline that supports mainstream online RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, GRPO) and includes engineering optimizations for scalability and reliability across various realistic environments.

## Results

- An open-source **7B** model, trained with AgentGym-RL and ScalingInter-RL, achieved an average success rate that matched or surpassed leading commercial, closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro) across five diverse scenarios.
- ScalingInter-RL consistently delivered over **10%** improvement on WebArena and a 30-point improvement on TextCraft compared to baseline RL approaches, demonstrating stable and sustained reward increases throughout training.
- The RL-trained **7B** model significantly outperformed much larger open-source models (e.g., Llama3.1-**70B**, Qwen2.5-**72B** with nearly ten times the parameters) in overall accuracy, indicating the efficacy of RL over raw model scale for agent capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strategic investment in post-training and test-time computation through RL and increased interaction turns is more impactful for developing agentic intelligence than merely increasing a model's parameter count.
> - Progressive interaction scaling during RL training (ScalingInter-RL) is crucial for stable optimization and achieving robust performance in long-horizon tasks, effectively balancing initial exploitation with gradual exploration.
> - RL training yields greater performance improvements in structured environments with clear rules (e.g., TextCraft, BabyAI, SciWorld) compared to more open-ended and noisy environments like WebArena and Deep Search.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous LLM agents capable of solving complex, real-world tasks is a rapidly evolving frontier in artificial intelligence. This paper positions itself at the forefront of this trend by addressing a critical gap: the lack of a *unified, interactive reinforcement learning (RL) framework* that can effectively train LLM agents from scratch, without relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), across diverse and realistic environments.

Historically, LLMs have transitioned from mere chatbots to sophisticated agents. The prevailing approaches for developing these agents often fall into a few categories:
- **Prompting-based methods:** These rely on carefully crafted prompts to guide LLMs in using tools, performing self-reflection, planning, or self-correction. While effective, these methods typically depend on powerful, often proprietary, models (e.g., OpenAI's GPT-4o) and do not intrinsically train the underlying models to evolve intrinsic agentic capabilities through interaction. Multi-LLM collaborative frameworks also exist but often share similar limitations.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Another common approach involves collecting large-scale expert trajectories and fine-tuning LLMs to imitate these expert demonstrations. While this can imbue agents with abilities like API invocation and planning, it is often expensive, difficult to scale, and critically, the agents struggle to self-improve or adapt through active interaction with the environment.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL is a natural paradigm for training agents that learn through exploration and interaction, akin to human cognitive development. However, existing RL applications for LLMs have largely been confined to single-turn tasks, such as enhancing reasoning or aligning with human preferences (RLHF). While there have been recent attempts to extend RL to multi-turn agent tasks, these efforts often face limitations in task complexity, environment diversity, and struggle with optimization stability and efficiency, leading to suboptimal performance. DeepSeek-R1, for example, focuses primarily on single-turn reasoning tasks.

AgentGym-RL directly addresses these limitations. It seeks to bridge the gap by providing an end-to-end RL framework that is unified, modular, and flexible enough to support multi-turn decision-making across a wide array of realistic environments, crucially, *without* requiring an initial SFT step. By open-sourcing its components, this work also aims to democratize access to advanced agent training methods, moving beyond the reliance on closed-source, high-parameter models. Furthermore, the introduction of ScalingInter-RL contributes to the broader discussion on scaling compute in LLMs, specifically by exploring how progressive interaction scaling during RL training can enhance agentic intelligence more effectively than simply increasing model size.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the development of autonomous LLM agents capable of robust, intelligent decision-making over long horizons in complex, real-world scenarios. The authors are motivated by several key challenges and opportunities in the current LLM agent landscape:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Unified RL Frameworks:** The most significant motivation stems from the recognized lack of a unified, end-to-end, and interactive reinforcement learning framework specifically designed for training LLM agents. Existing multi-turn RL efforts are fragmented, limited in scope, or suffer from stability issues, preventing a cohesive approach to developing versatile agents from scratch. AgentGym-RL aims to fill this void by providing a comprehensive solution.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Current methods, whether prompting-based or SFT-driven, often rely on external human-curated data or proprietary models, limiting their scalability, adaptability, and the agents' intrinsic ability to learn through self-exploration. The authors advocate for an RL-first approach to enable agents to acquire knowledge and skills directly through interaction with diverse environments, much like human cognitive development.
3. **Addressing Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off and Optimization Stability:** Training agents for long-horizon tasks using RL is inherently challenging due to the vast action spaces, sparse rewards, and the delicate balance between exploration (discovering new strategies) and exploitation (refining known successful strategies). The authors observed that starting with a large number of interaction turns often leads to "redundant reasoning and unproductive actions" and training collapse, while consistently small interaction horizons restrict effective exploration. This motivated the development of `ScalingInter-RL` to tackle these stability and efficiency issues.
4. **Enhancing Extensibility, Scalability, and Reliability:** For an RL framework to be truly useful for cutting-edge research and development, it must be highly flexible to accommodate new agents, environments, and algorithms, capable of handling large-scale training, and robust enough for long, uninterrupted runs. These engineering considerations drove the modular design and extensive optimizations within AgentGym-RL.
5. **Democratizing Agentic AI:** A crucial objective is to empower the broader research community. By open-sourcing the complete AgentGym-RL framework—including code and datasets—the authors aim to lower the barrier to entry for developing advanced LLM agents, fostering collaborative progress and reproducible research.
6. **Matching or Surpassing Commercial Models:** Ultimately, the research seeks to demonstrate that open-source LLM agents, when trained effectively with RL, can achieve performance comparable to or even superior to larger, proprietary closed-source models across a diverse set of complex, real-world tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AgentGym-RL framework is built upon a modular, decoupled architecture designed for high flexibility and extensibility in training LLM agents for multi-turn interactive decision-making. Its core methodology revolves around a three-component structure and a novel training approach, ScalingInter-RL.

#### 3.1. AgentGym-RL Framework Architecture

The framework extends the foundational AgentGym by introducing more realistic environments, diverse online reinforcement learning algorithms, and significant engineering optimizations. It comprises three primary modules:

- **1. Environment Module:**
 - **Diverse Scenarios:** AgentGym-RL provides a broad spectrum of real-world scenarios critical for comprehensive agent evaluation and development. These include:
 - **Web Navigation (e.g., WebArena):** Tasks involving dynamic websites like online shopping, forums, or content management, requiring agents to interpret content, manipulate interfaces, and plan multi-step actions.
 - **Deep Search (RAG-based environment):** Multi-step, goal-directed queries using tools like search engines and Python interpreters, demanding information-seeking, multi-hop reasoning, and knowledge synthesis.
 - **Digital Games (e.g., TextCraft):** Interactive game environments emphasizing real-time decision-making, strategy, and adaptability.
 - **Embodied Tasks (e.g., BabyAI):** Controlling virtual bodies for navigation, manipulation, and task execution, requiring spatial reasoning and perception-action grounding.
 - **Scientific Tasks (e.g., SciWorld):** Conducting experiments and solving problems in knowledge-intensive settings, necessitating precise execution, dynamic feedback interpretation, and iterative hypothesis refinement.
 - **Standardized Architecture:** Environments are packaged as independent services, supporting multiple replicas for parallel requests. A client-server model with unified HTTP protocols exposes APIs (e.g., `/observation`, `/available_actions`, `/step`, `/reset`) to agents.

- **2. Agent Module:**
 - **Reasoning-Action Loop:** This module encapsulates the LLM agent's core intelligence, receiving observations, performing multi-turn reasoning, and generating actions (e.g., API invocations).
 - **Advanced Mechanisms:** Supports long-horizon planning, self-reflection, diverse prompting strategies, various sampling configurations, and custom reward functions, allowing for flexible agent design.

- **3. Training Module:**
 - **Unified RL Pipeline:** Provides a comprehensive pipeline for both online and offline reinforcement learning. It orchestrates trajectory collection, advantage estimation, policy optimization, and reward shaping.
 - **Algorithm Support:** Integrates mainstream online RL algorithms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Grouped Policy Optimization (GRPO), REINFORCE++, and RLOO. It also supports complementary methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and rejection sampling (AgentEvol).
 - **Scalability and Reliability:** Designed for large-scale RL, supporting distributed training across multiple nodes (multi-process and multi-node parallelism). Includes engineering optimizations like efficient batching, asynchronous logging, improved rollout parallelization (e.g., subprocess-based WebArena), and memory leak mitigation (e.g., in TextCraft, SciWorld) to ensure robust and scalable operation.
 - **Observability:** Integrated diagnostic tools provide fine-grained metrics (policy entropy, KL divergence, reward curves) and an interactive user interface for step-by-step trajectory inspection and analysis.

#### 3.2. ScalingInter-RL: Progressive Scaling Interaction for Agent RL

To address the crucial balance between exploration and exploitation and improve optimization stability in multi-turn RL training, the authors propose `ScalingInter-RL`.

- **Motivation:** While increasing inference compute for LLM reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought) shows benefits, directly applying long interaction horizons at the start of RL training often leads to instability and inefficient exploration. Conversely, perpetually short horizons limit the agent's ability to master complex tasks.
- **Core Idea:** `ScalingInter-RL` employs a progressive horizon-scaling strategy that adaptively adjusts the maximum number of interaction turns (`K`) an agent is allowed during training. The objective remains maximizing the expected terminal reward, but under a dynamically evolving interaction budget.
- **Mechanism:**
 1. **Early-Stage Exploitation:** Training begins with a small interaction horizon (`h_1`). This forces the agent to learn efficient behaviors quickly, exploiting its current policy to achieve proficiency on simpler tasks and establish a stable foundation. The constrained environment provides clearer, less noisy learning signals.
 2. **Gradual Exploration:** As training progresses, the maximum interaction horizon is monotonically increased (`h_{t+1} = h_t + δ_h`) according to a curriculum schedule, updated every `Δ` training steps. This gradual expansion incentivizes the agent to explore longer decision paths, fostering the emergence of higher-order cognitive behaviors such as planning, reflection, strategic backtracking, and diverse skill acquisition.
- **Benefits:** This staged approach effectively balances initial exploitation for stability with subsequent exploration for complexity, leading to more stable RL optimization, reduced computational resources, and ultimately, agents capable of more generalized and robust performance across long-horizon tasks.

#### 3.3. Formalization

Multi-turn interactive decision-making tasks are formally modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) `(U, S, A, O, T, r)`. Policy gradient methods are utilized to optimize the agent's policy (`π_θ`) by maximizing the expected cumulative reward (`J(θ) = E_τ∼π_θ [r(τ)]`). The policy gradient `∇_θ J(θ)` is estimated, and parameters `θ` are updated using gradient ascent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted using AgentGym-RL and ScalingInter-RL reveal significant findings regarding the training and capabilities of LLM agents:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison with Baselines

- **Significant Performance Gains:** The AgentGym-RL framework, particularly when combined with the ScalingInter-RL method, substantially enhances the agentic intelligence of open-source LLMs.
- **Matching/Surpassing Proprietary Models:** An open-source 7B model (Qwen-2.5-7B) trained with their framework and method achieved an average improvement of 33.65 points. This `Ours-7B` model (likely referring to `ScalingInter-7B`) demonstrates an overall average success rate that *matches or even surpasses* leading commercial, closed-source models such as OpenAI o3, GPT-4o, and Gemini-2.5-Pro across five diverse scenarios (WebArena, Deep Search, TextCraft, BabyAI, SciWorld) as shown in Figure 1a.
 - For example, `ScalingInter-7B` achieved an average success rate of 96.7% (BabyAI), 91.0% (TextCraft), 57.0% (SciWorld), 38.2% (Deep Search), and 26.0% (WebArena).

#### 4.2. Efficacy of ScalingInter-RL

- **Consistent Boost:** ScalingInter-RL consistently outperforms baseline RL approaches across all environments and tasks. It delivers over 10% improvement on WebArena and a remarkable 30 points improvement on TextCraft, achieving state-of-the-art results.
- **Stable and Sustained Improvements:** Training reward curves (Figure 6) show that AgentGym-RL with ScalingInter-RL yields stable, sustained, and substantial reward improvements throughout the training process across various environments.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics and Interaction Horizon (Figure 7)

- **Instability with Large Initial Horizons:** Experiments demonstrate that starting RL training with a large maximum interaction horizon (e.g., 10 turns) can lead to higher performance in early stages but quickly collapses later due to high variance, credit assignment difficulties, and overfitting to spurious behaviors. Excessive early exploration without a stable foundation is detrimental.
- **Limitations with Small Initial Horizons:** Conversely, consistently restricting interactions to a small number of turns (e.g., 5 turns) provides stable learning but limits exploration, leading to diminishing returns and a performance ceiling.
- **ScalingInter-RL as Optimal Balance:** ScalingInter-RL's progressive increase in interaction horizon effectively addresses this trade-off. It leverages initial constraints for stable foundational learning (exploitation) and then expands the horizon to encourage deeper exploration and mastery of complex tasks, resulting in higher and more efficient long-term performance and reduced computational costs.

#### 4.4. Scaling Potential: Post-training/Test-time Compute vs. Model Size (Figure 1b)

- **Superior Scaling through RL:** A key insight is that strategic investment in post-training and test-time computation (through RL) is *more impactful* than merely increasing a model's parameter count. The `Ours-7B` model, after RL training, achieves an overall accuracy of approximately 58.6%, significantly outperforming much larger open-source models like Llama3.1-70B (47%) and Qwen2.5-72B (43%), which have nearly ten times the parameters. This suggests a less steep performance gain from raw model scaling compared to targeted RL training.

#### 4.5. Impact of Environmental Structure

- **Higher Gains in Structured Environments:** RL delivers the most significant performance improvements in simulated environments with clear rules and explicit cause-and-effect relationships, such as TextCraft, BabyAI, and SciWorld. SciWorld saw an astounding boost from a baseline score of 1.50 to 50.50.
- **Moderate Gains in Open-ended Environments:** In more open-ended and noisy environments like WebArena and Deep Search (real websites, search engines), while still positive, the performance gains from RL were more moderate, indicating inherent challenges in learning optimal strategies through trial-and-error in less structured settings.

#### 4.6. Detailed Task Performance

- **Web Navigation (WebArena):** ScalingInter-7B (26.00%) surpassed GPT-4o (16.00%) and matched DeepSeek-R1-0528 (28.00%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (28.00%). Strong in "Shopping" and "CMS" sub-tasks.
- **Deep Search:** ScalingInter-7B (38.25%) outperformed GPT-4o (26.75%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (36.50%), performing comparably to DeepSeek-R1-0528 (40.25%). Achieved highest scores in NQ and TriviaQA.
- **Digital Game (TextCraft):** ScalingInter-7B (91.00%) was competitive with top proprietary models (93-94%) and was one of the few to achieve a non-zero score at Depth 4 (33.33%). RL training dramatically improved smaller models (e.g., AgentGym-RL-3B from 14.00% to 75.00%).
- **Embodied Tasks (BabyAI):** ScalingInter-7B achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) with 96.67%, outperforming OpenAI o3 (94.44%) and GPT-4o (86.67%), showing consistent mastery across diverse sub-tasks.
- **Scientific Tasks (SciWorld):** ScalingInter-7B established a new SOTA with 57.00%, significantly surpassing all other models, including OpenAI o3 (41.50%). However, the "Chem-Mix" sub-task remained intractable for all evaluated models, scoring zero, highlighting a systemic challenge in complex scientific reasoning.

#### 4.7. Test-Time Scaling for Agents (Figures 8 & 9)

- **Sequential Interaction:** Increasing interaction turns during testing consistently improves performance for all models, validating the importance of exploration and the insights behind ScalingInter-RL. The trained agent consistently outperforms baselines.
- **Parallel Sampling:** Increased parallel sampling leads to marked improvements in Pass@K performance. The RL-trained model demonstrates superior optimization capability, outperforming baselines even with small sampling budgets and showing stable, significant gains with increased samples.

#### 4.8. Performance of Different RL Algorithms (Table 6)

- **GRPO Superiority:** GRPO consistently and substantially outperforms REINFORCE++ across TextCraft, BabyAI, and Deep Search benchmarks. This is attributed to GRPO's better handling of sparse rewards and high-variance gradients through relative action advantage, leading to more stable and efficient learning. Even the 3B GRPO model achieved higher scores than the 7B REINFORCE++ model, indicating an algorithmic advantage greater than raw model scale.

#### 4.9. Case Studies (Figures 10-16)

- **Demonstrated Improvements:** RL-trained agents showed enhanced navigation (BabyAI), improved compositional task mastery (SciWorld), and adaptive web navigation strategies (WebArena, including error recovery and purposeful navigation).
- **Identified Failure Modes:** Failure cases revealed persistent issues, such as a lack of deep procedural understanding and insufficient systematic exploration in some SciWorld tasks, and "over-interaction" patterns (redundant clicking, scrolling) in WebArena, indicating areas for future refinement in efficiency and precision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AgentGym-RL framework and the accompanying ScalingInter-RL method represent a significant contribution to the field of LLM-based autonomous agents, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Democratizing High-Performance Agent AI:** By demonstrating that smaller, open-source LLMs (e.g., 7B parameters) can, through effective reinforcement learning, match or even surpass the agentic capabilities of much larger, proprietary closed-source models, this work significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing advanced AI agents. This can foster greater innovation, collaboration, and equitable access to cutting-edge AI research beyond well-resourced institutions.

2. **A Unified and Flexible Research Platform:** AgentGym-RL offers a much-needed unified, modular, and extensible framework for RL-based agent training. Its decoupled architecture, broad environment coverage, and comprehensive algorithm support provide a standardized platform that can accelerate research by simplifying experimentation, ensuring reproducibility, and facilitating the integration of new ideas from the community. The open-source nature, complete with code and datasets, is crucial for fostering a collaborative ecosystem.

3. **Novel and Robust Training Paradigm:** ScalingInter-RL provides a novel and effective solution to the long-standing challenges of exploration-exploitation balance and optimization stability in long-horizon reinforcement learning. This progressive interaction-scaling strategy is a foundational advancement that enables agents to acquire complex skills and behaviors more efficiently and reliably, preventing training collapse while encouraging diverse problem-solving.

4. **Key Insights into Agent Intelligence:** The extensive experiments yield critical empirical insights into the scaling of agentic intelligence. The finding that post-training and test-time compute (via RL and increased interaction turns/sampling) can be more impactful than merely scaling model parameters offers valuable guidance for future agent design and development. The comparative analysis of RL algorithms (GRPO vs. REINFORCE++) and the impact of environmental structure further enrich our understanding of effective agent training.

5. **Foundation for Future Agent Development:** The framework's ability to train agents "from scratch" without relying on supervised fine-tuning is a pivotal step towards truly autonomous learning agents. This moves away from imitation learning and towards agents that can self-improve through direct interaction with their environment, mimicking human-like cognitive development.

6. **Paving the Way for Complex Real-World Applications:** By offering diverse and realistic environments—from web navigation and deep search to embodied and scientific tasks—AgentGym-RL lays the groundwork for developing agents that can tackle increasingly complex problems in the physical and digital worlds. The identified failure modes also highlight specific, actionable areas for future research to refine agent robustness and efficiency.

**Future Work:** The authors acknowledge several important directions, including developing agents with stronger generalization and transfer capabilities to novel environments and tools, scaling RL training to even longer-horizon and more realistic, physically grounded tasks, and advancing multi-agent reinforcement learning. These proposed directions underscore the transformative potential of AgentGym-RL as a stepping stone towards the next generation of highly intelligent and autonomous AI agents.
