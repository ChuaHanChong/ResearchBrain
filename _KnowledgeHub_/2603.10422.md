# World2Act: Latent Action Post-Training via Skill-Compositional World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MBZUAI developed World2Act, a latent action post-training framework that transfers dynamic priors from skill-compositional World Models to Vision-Language-Action policies. The method reduces reliance on noisy pixel-space supervision, achieving improved success rates on complex manipulation tasks in simulation (e.g., 66.3% on RoboCasa) and real-world environments (6.67% average gain) while maintaining high inference speed.

## Problem

- Embodied AI policies trained with behavior cloning lack robust dynamics priors, limiting generalization to varied or novel environmental conditions.
- Existing World Model-based post-training methods rely on pixel-space supervision, making them sensitive to artifacts and hallucinations in WM rollouts, especially over long task horizons.
- World Models trained on fixed-length video clips struggle to generate temporally consistent, arbitrary-length videos for diverse robotic tasks, leading to error accumulation.

## Method

- A latent action post-training framework, **World2Act**, directly aligns VLA actions with World Model (WM) video-dynamics latents using a contrastive matching objective, bypassing pixel-space supervision.
- An automatic LLM-based pipeline decomposes high-level task instructions into atomic skill prompts, creating new skill-compositional datasets (RoboCasa-Skill, LIBERO-Skill) for stable WM training.
- A skill-compositional WM leverages these decomposed skills to generate temporally consistent, arbitrary-length video rollouts by chaining sub-videos corresponding to each atomic skill.

## Results

- World2Act achieved state-of-the-art success rates on RoboCasa (**66.3%** for Cosmos Policy with fewer demonstrations) and improved GR00T-N1.6-ft performance by **2.5%** (**72.6%**) compared to DreamGen.
- Skill-compositional World Models (WMs) consistently enhanced success rates over Base-WMs (e.g., **1.1%** gain on RoboCasa) and demonstrated faster, more stable convergence in video-action latent alignment.
- The method showed improved performance with increased post-training trajectories and strong cross-task generalization, yielding a **6.67%** average success rate increase in real-world robot experiments with minimal inference overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning VLA actions directly with World Model (WM) video-dynamics latents via contrastive learning reduces sensitivity to pixel-level noise and artifacts inherent in WM rollouts.
> - Decomposing complex robotic tasks into atomic skills using an LLM enables WMs to generate temporally consistent and stable arbitrary-length videos, overcoming limitations of fixed-length training.
> - Employing a lightweight residual policy for post-training is more computationally efficient than full fine-tuning, allowing faster integration of WM dynamic priors into VLA backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation often leverages Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, which are typically learned through behavior cloning on demonstration datasets. While effective for specific tasks, these policies frequently encounter generalization challenges when faced with environmental variations, novel contact conditions, or tasks slightly different from their training data. This performance gap suggests a deficit in robust dynamics priors within conventional VLA training paradigms.

World Models (WMs) have emerged as a mechanism to address this limitation by providing explicit representations of how robot-object interactions evolve over time. WMs can serve as a source of these dynamics priors, thereby enhancing the generalization capabilities of embodied agents. Previous research has explored WM-based post-training methods to inject these priors into VLA policies. These methods commonly supervise policy learning using signals derived from WM-generated pixel-space rollouts. Such signals often involve inverse dynamics models (IDMs) to infer pseudo-actions or reward functions computed from imagined pixel sequences.

However, a significant limitation of pixel-space supervision is its susceptibility to visual artifacts. Imperfections in WM rollouts, such as synthetic visual anomalies or inconsistent contact dynamics, can directly translate into noisy action labels or unreliable reward signals. These errors can compound over longer imagined horizons, potentially degrading VLA policy performance rather than improving it. This sensitivity creates a demand for more robust and reliable post-training approaches.

An alternative direction involves utilizing latent video representations from WMs, which are posited to capture transferable behavioral dynamics more abstractly than raw pixels. Prior work in latent-video-to-action methods, such as Unified World Models (UWM) and Cosmos Policy, typically aim to learn unified high-dimensional video-action embedding spaces. However, these complex joint embedding spaces can introduce architectural overhead and destabilize training.

WORLD2ACT positions itself within this landscape by addressing the instability of pixel-space WM supervision and the complexity of high-dimensional joint latent spaces. It proposes a latent-space post-training framework that transfers WM dynamics to VLA policies without relying on pixel-space supervision. By matching VLA action latents to WM-predicted latent dynamics targets within a shared, low-dimensional video-action latent space, WORLD2ACT aims to provide a more stable and effective method for injecting dynamics priors, thereby improving VLA generalization and robustness to WM generation imperfections.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a latent-space post-training framework, named WORLD2ACT, for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. This framework aims to transfer dynamics priors from a World Model (WM) to a VLA policy in a manner that improves generalization and robustness, particularly by circumventing the challenges associated with pixel-space supervision.

The motivation for WORLD2ACT stems from several observations regarding existing VLA training and post-training methodologies:

1. **Generalization Gap in Behavior Cloning:** VLA policies, primarily trained through behavior cloning, often struggle with generalization. They lack intrinsic dynamics priors, making them sensitive to variations in environments, task parameters, or contact conditions not explicitly covered in their training datasets. Finetuning alone on extensive datasets often proves insufficient to bridge this generalization gap.

2. **Limitations of Pixel-Space WM Supervision:** While World Models offer a promising source of dynamics priors, current WM-based post-training methods frequently rely on pixel-space supervision. This typically involves using imagined pixel rollouts from WMs to generate pseudo-actions via inverse dynamics models or to derive reward signals. The core issue with this approach is the fidelity of WM-generated pixels. World Models, especially for complex robotic scenes, can introduce visual artifacts, inconsistencies in object contacts, or other generation errors. When these imperfect pixels are used as training signals, they can directly translate into noisy action labels or misleading rewards, potentially harming the VLA policy's performance rather than enhancing it. Such errors can also compound over longer imagined trajectories, leading to unstable or detrimental post-training.

3. **Need for Stable and Efficient Dynamics Transfer:** The researchers recognize that video latents from WMs can capture transferable behavioral dynamics at a more abstract level, offering an alternative to pixel-level data. However, existing latent-video-to-action methods often depend on high-dimensional joint embedding spaces, which can increase architectural complexity and destabilize the training process. There is a need for a method that can leverage WM latent dynamics effectively without introducing substantial architectural overhead or training instability.

4. **Preserving VLA Backbone Integrity:** Heavily parameterized VLA policies, once pretrained, can be sensitive to direct finetuning, often exhibiting sample inefficiency or catastrophic forgetting. This motivates an approach that can inject new priors without extensively modifying or retraining the core VLA backbone.

In response to these motivations, WORLD2ACT seeks to:
- Induce a shared, low-dimensional video-action latent space by contrastively aligning WM-dynamics latents with action embeddings.
- Post-train the VLA policy by guiding its action representations toward WM-imagined dynamics directly in this latent space, rather than relying on decoded pixels.
- Employ a lightweight residual policy to refine the VLA's actions, leaving the main VLA backbone frozen, thereby mitigating issues of sample inefficiency and catastrophic forgetting.

By addressing these points, the work aims to provide a more robust and effective method for leveraging the dynamics knowledge embedded within World Models to enhance the generalization and performance of VLA policies for robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WORLD2ACT is designed as a two-stage post-training framework that integrates visual-dynamic priors from a frozen instruction-conditioned video World Model (WM) into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Instruction-Conditioned Video World Model

The framework utilizes Cosmos-Predict2 as the instruction-conditioned video WM. This WM operates within a structured video-VAE (Variational Autoencoder) latent space. During pre-training, ground-truth videos are encoded into latent trajectories $V^{gt} = \{V^{gt}_t\}_{t=1}^T$. A denoising network, $W_{denoise}$, is trained via flow matching to predict the vector field that transforms noisy latents toward $V^{gt}$ in this latent space. Given an initial observation $s_1$ and language instruction $\ell$, $W_{denoise}$ predicts future visual dynamics as $V = W_{denoise}(\epsilon; s_1, \ell)$, where $V = \{V_t\}_{t=1}^T$ are denoised spatial latents. In WORLD2ACT, all components of the WM are frozen, and the pre-decoding video latents $V$ are directly used as compact visual-dynamics priors. These latents describe visual evolution but do not explicitly specify robot controls, necessitating a bridge to an action-aware latent space.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Aligning Video Dynamics and Robot Actions

The first stage focuses on learning a shared, low-dimensional latent space that aligns WM latent dynamics with robot actions. This is achieved through two adapter networks and an action decoder, trained using synchronized state-action demonstrations.

- **Components:**
 - **Video Adapter ($B_v$):** A CNN-based network that maps each WM video latent $V_t$ (corresponding to a chunk of $M$ frames) to a $D$-dimensional embedding $z^v_t = B_v(V_t)$.
 - **Action Adapter ($B_a$):** An MLP-based network that partitions ground-truth action sequences $a^{gt}$ into temporally aligned chunks $\bar{a}^{gt}_t$ and maps each chunk to a $D$-dimensional embedding $z^a_t = B_a(\bar{a}^{gt}_t)$.
 - **Action Decoder ($D_a$):** An MLP-based network that maps each action chunk latent $z^a_t$ back to $M$ low-level action vectors, ensuring that the action latents preserve kinematic and temporal structure.
- **Training Objectives:** $B_v$, $B_a$, and $D_a$ are trained with two objectives:
 - **Reconstruction Objective ($L_{recon}$):** An L2 loss between the reconstructed action sequence $\hat{a}$ (obtained by unrolling decoded chunks) and the ground-truth action sequence $a^{gt}$, i.e., $L_{recon} = \|a^{gt} - \hat{a}\|^2$. This ensures that action latents are decodable back into valid actions.
 - **Contrastive Alignment Objective ($L_{contrastive}$):** A bidirectional InfoNCE loss that encourages paired video-action trajectories from the same demonstration to be close in the shared latent space, while separating them from unpaired (negative) samples in the batch. A key design choice is the use of chunk-aware trajectory alignment, where similarity is computed as the average cosine similarity over temporally aligned chunks ($sim(z^v_i, z^a_j) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \cos(z^v_{i,t}, z^a_{j,t})$). This focuses the alignment on fine-grained temporal dynamics rather than coarse task identity.
The overall loss for Stage 1 is $L = L_{recon} + L_{contrastive}$. After training, $B_v$, $B_a$, and $D_a$ are frozen to establish a stable shared latent space.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: World-Model-Guided Latent-Residual Post-Training

The second stage focuses on post-training the VLA policy using the learned shared latent space from Stage 1. To avoid catastrophic forgetting and sample inefficiency, the main VLA backbone ($\pi_{base}$, specifically GR00T-N1.6) is kept frozen. Instead, a lightweight Residual Policy $f_\theta$ is trained to predict an additive correction in the learned action-latent space.

- **Residual Policy Operation:**
 1. Given a state $s_t$ and instruction $\ell$, the frozen base VLA predicts an initial action chunk $\bar{a}_{base,t} = \pi_{base}(s_t, \ell)$.
 2. This base action chunk is mapped to the action-latent space by the frozen action adapter: $z^a_{base,t} = B_a(\bar{a}_{base,t})$.
 3. The Residual Policy $f_\theta$, implemented as a Transformer conditioned on observation tokens and the base action-latent token, refines this latent by predicting a residual correction: $\Delta z^a_t = f_\theta(s_t, z^a_{base,t})$.
 4. The final refined action latent is computed as $z^a_{final,t} = z^a_{base,t} + \Delta z^a_t$.
 5. The frozen action decoder $D_a$ decodes this final latent into an executable action chunk: $\bar{a}_{final,t} = D_a(z^a_{final,t})$. This chunk is executed for $M$ control steps before the policy is queried again.
- **Supervision for Residual Policy:**
 1. At each iteration, $B$ parallel simulator environments are used to obtain initial conditions and roll out the refined action chunks. Notably, no rewards, success labels, or differentiable state transitions from the simulator are used.
 2. For each environment instance $i$, the frozen WM ($W$) generates video latents $V_i = \{V_{i,t}\}_{t=1}^T$ conditioned on the initial observation $s_{i,1}$ and instruction $\ell_i$.
 3. The frozen video adapter maps these WM latents to video-dynamics embeddings $z^v_i = B_v(V_i)$.
 4. The Contrastive Loss ($L_{contrastive}$ from Eq. 2) is minimized between $z^v_i$ and the augmented policy's action-latent trajectory $z^a_{final,i}$. Gradients are only taken through $f_\theta$, thereby aligning the augmented policy's action latents with the WM-induced dynamics while keeping the VLA and Stage-1 modules frozen.

This two-stage approach enables WORLD2ACT to leverage the dynamics priors from a WM by operating in a latent space, directly addressing the limitations of pixel-space supervision and maintaining efficiency through a lightweight residual policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of WORLD2ACT was conducted across three simulated benchmarks (RoboCasa, LIBERO, Bridge-SIMPLER) and real-world robot experiments, with GR00T-N1.6 as the primary VLA backbone finetuned for task-alignment (GR00T-N1.6-ft).

#### 4.1. Performance on Complex Manipulation Tasks (Q1)

- **RoboCasa:** WORLD2ACT achieved a success rate (SR) of 72.6%, representing a +2.5% absolute gain over the GR00T-N1.6-ft baseline (70.1%). It also outperformed DreamGen, a pixel-space WM supervision method, by +2.1%. Against a broader set of VLA baselines, WORLD2ACT demonstrated the strongest overall performance.
- **LIBERO:** WORLD2ACT achieved a 98.1% SR. In contrast, DreamGen led to a degradation of GR00T-N1.6-ft performance to 92.1%. This degradation was linked to noisy pseudo-labels from WM artifacts, where decoded WM rollouts increased IDM action Mean Squared Error (MSE) by 18%. WORLD2ACT's latent-space approach yielded a +6.0% absolute gain over DreamGen on this benchmark.
- **Bridge-SIMPLER:** WORLD2ACT improved GR00T-N1.6-ft from 57.6% to 59.0% SR, a +1.4% absolute gain, and outperformed DreamGen.
- **Comparison with Other Post-Training Methods:** WORLD2ACT consistently outperformed other post-training baselines (e.g., BC, VLA-RFT, Ctrl-World) when applied to the GR00T-N1.6-ft model on RoboCasa, suggesting its latent-space approach provides a more effective adaptation signal.
- **Robustness to WM-capable VLAs (Appendix A):** When applied to Cosmos Policy (a VLA with built-in world-modeling capability), WORLD2ACT showed modest but consistent improvements on both LIBERO (98.5% to 98.6% SR) and RoboCasa (65.7% to 66.3% SR). This indicates that WORLD2ACT can still enhance policies that already incorporate WM structure.

#### 4.2. Impact of Latent Action Alignment and Residual Policy (Q2)

- **Correlation between Latent Alignment and Success:** During post-training, an increase in video-action cosine similarity (a measure of latent alignment) correlated with improved downstream success rates. Cosine similarity rapidly converged around 0.95, while SR improved from 70.1% to 72.6%, indicating that stronger alignment is associated with better manipulation performance.
- **Action Validity:** t-SNE visualizations demonstrated that after post-training, predicted-action latents from WORLD2ACT exhibited greater overlap with both reference-action and target-WM latent regions compared to before post-training. Quantitatively, the action-space MSE between predicted and held-out expert actions decreased from 0.034 to 0.021 (a 38.2% relative reduction), indicating improved action manifold compatibility.
- **Residual Policy Effectiveness:** The residual policy achieved a higher SR (72.6%) than LoRA finetuning (72.1% for r=32) while being 2.25 times faster to train (6.8 hours vs. 15.3 hours). This supports the efficacy of freezing the VLA backbone and learning a lightweight action-space correction.
- **Contrastive Loss Ablation:** The study highlighted the importance of both bidirectional contrast and chunk-level temporal alignment in the contrastive objective. Using unidirectional InfoNCE or a marginal loss, or collapsing chunk-wise similarity into a single global trajectory similarity, resulted in reduced success rates, emphasizing the criticality of fine-grained video-action alignment.

#### 4.3. Scaling and Generalization (Q3)

- **Scaling Post-Training Trajectories:** WORLD2ACT exhibited monotonic performance gains with increasing numbers of post-training trajectories (from 0 to 1000), reaching 72.6%. DreamGen, conversely, showed non-monotonic behavior, including performance drops, suggesting greater sensitivity to noise in pixel-space pseudo-labeling.
- **Cross-Task Generalization:** When evaluated on unseen tasks, WORLD2ACT consistently improved performance with increasing diversity of seen training tasks, yielding gains of +2.4% on GR00T-N1.6-base and +1.1% on Cosmos Policy. This suggests that generalized WM dynamics can effectively transfer to novel skills.

#### 4.4. Real-World Robot Experiments (Q4)

- On a Franka Research 3 robot across three manipulation tasks, WORLD2ACT improved GR00T-N1.6-ft by an average of 6.7% and outperformed DreamGen by 8.3%.
- Qualitative evaluation demonstrated that the system successfully executed tasks despite the presence of visual artifacts in the WM-imagined rollouts, indicating robustness of the latent dynamics transfer.

#### 4.5. World Model Training and Evaluation (Appendix E)

- The Cosmos-Predict2 WM, when trained on atomic-skill-segmented data (Skill-WM), significantly outperformed other video diffusion backbones and the base Cosmos-Predict2 model in Instruction Following (IF) and Physical Alignment (PA) metrics.
- Skill-WM consistently improved downstream success rates for WORLD2ACT compared to using a Base-WM, highlighting the benefit of training the WM on temporally consistent, skill-compositional data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WORLD2ACT offers a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a key limitation in current World Model (WM)-based post-training of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies.

**Overcoming Pixel-Space Limitations:** The primary significance lies in its ability to effectively transfer dynamics priors from WMs without relying on pixel-space supervision. This directly mitigates the problem of noisy action labels or rewards introduced by visual artifacts and inconsistencies in WM-generated pixel rollouts. The empirical results, particularly on the LIBERO benchmark where pixel-space methods degraded performance, suggest that latent WM dynamics provide a more stable and robust alternative.

**Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By leveraging latent-space dynamics, WORLD2ACT demonstrates consistent performance improvements across diverse simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa, LIBERO, Bridge-SIMPLER) and in real-world robot experiments. The observed gains in success rates, even over strong VLA baselines and other post-training methods, indicate improved generalization capabilities and robustness to environmental variations and task complexities. The positive correlation between latent alignment and task success further reinforces the effectiveness of this approach.

**Efficient and Non-Intrusive Post-Training:** The framework's design, which freezes the heavily parameterized VLA backbone and trains only a lightweight residual policy, offers significant advantages in terms of efficiency. This approach reduces the computational cost and training time compared to extensive finetuning or adapting new joint WM-policy architectures. Moreover, it avoids the risks of catastrophic forgetting, making it a less intrusive method for enhancing existing VLA systems.

**Broader Applicability:** The method's ability to improve even WM-capable VLA policies (like Cosmos Policy) suggests its complementary nature and broad applicability as an additional post-training stage. This could enable existing and future VLA models to harness the benefits of WM-induced dynamics more effectively.

**Real-World Transferability:** The successful deployment on a physical robot, demonstrating improved success rates and robustness to imperfect WM rollouts, highlights the practical potential of WORLD2ACT for real-world robotic applications. This indicates a step towards bridging the sim-to-real gap by leveraging abstract dynamic priors rather than high-fidelity visual matching.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The research also identifies several areas for future exploration. The current approach may struggle with highly contact-rich, non-prehensile tasks where subtle errors in WM dynamics can compound. The persistent domain gap in real-world success rates suggests that current WMs and VLAs may not fully capture complex physical dynamics. Additionally, the reliance on chunk-level temporal correspondence between imagined and executed trajectories could be relaxed in future work to allow for more flexible execution dynamics.

In summary, WORLD2ACT presents a significant step towards more robust, efficient, and generalizable robot learning by strategically leveraging the latent dynamics of world models, thereby circumventing the fragilities inherent in pixel-based supervision.
