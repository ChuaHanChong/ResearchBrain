# Global Prior Meets Local Consistency: Dual-Memory Augmented Vision-Language-Action Model for Efficient Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> OptimusVLA, a dual-memory augmented Vision-Language-Action model, enhances robotic manipulation by incorporating Global Prior Memory for efficient action generation and Local Consistency Memory for temporal robustness. The framework achieves higher success rates across simulation and real-world tasks, along with a 2.9x real-world inference speedup.

## Problem

- VLA models suffer from low inference efficiency due to a large distributional gap between generic noise priors and structured target action distributions, requiring numerous denoising steps.
- Current VLA models exhibit poor robustness to temporal dependencies, struggling with task phase awareness and producing jittery control due to a lack of action history context.
- Existing approaches to incorporate temporal context (e.g., long observation sequences, repeated VLM invocation) often incur high computational overhead and memory usage.

## Method

- **OptimusVLA** introduces a hierarchical VLA framework augmented with a ==Global Prior Memory== (GPM) and a ==Local Consistency Memory== (LCM).
- GPM constructs a task-level prior action distribution by retrieving semantically similar trajectories from an offline memory bank, adaptively adjusting noise scale and Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) based on retrieval confidence.
- LCM dynamically models recent action history using a consistency layer and a Mamba-based module to predict a consistency bias, which is then injected into the sampled action chunk to promote temporal coherence.

## Results

- Achieved an average **98.6%** success rate on LIBERO and **38%** on RoboTwin 2.0 Hard, outperforming existing baselines like \pi_{**0.5**} (**96.9%** and **29%** respectively).
- Delivered a **2.9x** inference speedup in real-world evaluations and up to **6.5x** faster inference in simulation, reducing the average Number of Function Evaluations to 3.2 on LIBERO from 10.0.
- Demonstrated enhanced robustness in real-world generalization tasks (**85.0%** success rate) and long-horizon tasks (**64.0%** success rate), with ablation studies confirming the independent contributions of GPM and LCM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging task-level, semantics-conditioned priors via GPM significantly narrows the prior-target distributional gap, leading to faster, more feasible action generation with fewer denoising steps.
> - A lightweight memory mechanism (LCM) effectively provides temporal awareness by modeling recent action history, enabling smoother trajectories and robust behavior in multi-stage tasks without incurring substantial computational cost.
> - Adaptive control over noise scale and NFEs, guided by the confidence in retrieved priors, balances efficiency with the necessary exploration for robust action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotic manipulation, integrating observation perception, natural-language understanding, and action generation within an end-to-end framework. These models leverage advancements in large vision-language models (VLMs) and generative policies, such as diffusion models or flow matching, to achieve robotic control. The broader landscape of VLA models can be categorized into single-stream architectures, which often autoregressively generate discretized action tokens, and hierarchical architectures, which typically combine a VLM for high-level planning with a generative policy for low-level, high-frequency action generation.

While hierarchical VLA models offer advantages in inference speed compared to purely autoregressive approaches, the action generation process remains a bottleneck. Existing generative policies, often based on Conditional Flow Matching (CFM), typically map from an isotropic noise distribution (e.g., Gaussian) to a structured action distribution. This transformation can be substantial, necessitating multiple denoising steps, referred to as Number of Function Evaluations (NFE), to produce high-quality actions. This results in inefficient inference and an increased likelihood of generating kinematically infeasible actions.

Another challenge in current VLA models is their robustness to temporal dependencies. Many models operate under a Markovian assumption, conditioning action generation solely on the current observation. This approach can hinder the agent's awareness of task progress, particularly when distinct task phases yield visually similar inputs (e.g., an unopened drawer versus a recently closed one). The lack of temporal context can also lead to inconsistent or jittery control trajectories. Previous attempts to address this issue have included concatenating long sequences of historical observations or actions, which can increase computational overhead and memory usage, and may not align with the pre-training distributions of VLA models. Other approaches have explored working memory mechanisms, but these often involve repeated invocation of a heavy VLM backbone, creating throughput bottlenecks.

This work fits into this landscape by directly addressing these identified limitations. It proposes a dual-memory augmentation to the hierarchical VLA framework, aiming to improve both the efficiency of action generation by narrowing the prior-target distribution gap and the robustness to temporal dependencies by explicitly modeling action history.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and robustness of action generation within hierarchical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. The authors identify two specific limitations in current VLA models that motivate their proposed framework:

1. **Low Inference Efficiency due to a Large Prior–Target Distributional Gap**:
 - **Problem**: Existing VLA models that employ generative policies (e.g., diffusion denoising, flow matching) typically initialize action generation from an isotropic noise distribution, such as Gaussian noise. The target, however, is a highly structured and complex action distribution specific to robotic tasks. The significant disparity between this generic noise prior and the specific target action distribution requires numerous denoising steps (high Number of Function Evaluations, NFE) to bridge the gap. This directly translates to increased inference time, making real-time control challenging. Furthermore, starting from a random, uninformative prior increases the probability that the initial generative steps produce kinematically invalid or infeasible actions, which can compromise task success.
 - **Motivation**: The authors are motivated by the observation that similar robotic tasks often share related action distributions. Instead of generating actions from an arbitrary noise prior, leveraging task-specific knowledge could provide a more informed starting point for the generative process. This would reduce the "generative path" length, decrease the required NFEs, and inherently guide the generation towards a feasible action space.

2. **Poor Robustness to Temporal Dependence**:
 - **Problem**: Many VLA models treat action generation as a Markov Decision Process, relying solely on the current observation to decide the next action. This "memoryless" approach has several drawbacks. First, it can lead to a lack of "phase awareness," where the robot struggles to distinguish between visually similar states that represent different stages of a task (e.g., the visual input for a closed drawer before opening vs. after closing). Second, the absence of historical context can result in temporally inconsistent or "jittery" control trajectories, as the policy lacks a mechanism to enforce smoothness and coherence with previously executed actions. While inputting long sequences of past observations can provide context, this often leads to substantial increases in inference overhead, memory usage, and can introduce misalignment with VLA models pre-trained on single-frame inputs.
 - **Motivation**: The authors are motivated to equip VLA models with temporal perception without incurring the high computational costs associated with long-context sequence modeling. They aim to introduce a lightweight mechanism that dynamically models the recent history of executed actions. This mechanism would allow the model to infer task progress and inject consistency constraints, thereby promoting smoother trajectories and more context-aware behavior, which are critical for robust long-horizon and multi-stage manipulation tasks.

To address these issues, the key objectives are to design and integrate two memory modules: Global Prior Memory (GPM) to provide task-level prior actions for efficient generation, and Local Consistency Memory (LCM) to ensure temporal coherence and progress awareness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, OptimusVLA, augments a standard hierarchical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model with two memory modules: Global Prior Memory (GPM) and Local Consistency Memory (LCM). The overall architecture consists of a Vision-Language Backbone, a Flow Policy, GPM, and LCM.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

Given a natural-language instruction ($\ell$) and the current observation ($O_t$), the Vision-Language Backbone (VLM) first encodes these inputs into a multimodal representation ($E_{emb}$). This representation is then used by GPM to retrieve a task-level prior action distribution and by LCM to process the previous action sequence. Finally, a flow policy uses this integrated information to generate an action chunk.

#### 3.2. Global Prior Memory (GPM)

GPM is designed to reduce the distributional gap between the generative prior and the target action distribution by providing task-level priors. It consists of three main components:

- **Prior Head**: A lightweight MLP that projects the multimodal representation ($E_{emb}$) from the VLM into a retrieval token ($z_{re}$). This head is trained using an InfoNCE objective to learn task-discriminative embeddings, ensuring that embeddings for semantically similar tasks are clustered together.
- **Memory Bank**: An offline-constructed repository storing key-value pairs of normalized task embeddings ($\tilde{z}_m$) and their corresponding full trajectories ($J_m$). When queried with $z_{re}$, it retrieves the $k$ nearest trajectories based on cosine similarity scores ($s_i$). A normalized global similarity score ($\bar{s}$) and softmax weights ($\alpha_i$) are computed from these scores.
- **Prior Construction**: For each retrieved trajectory ($J_i$), an action chunk ($C_i$) is extracted using a sliding window, aligned to the current execution progress (tracked by $\rho_t$). These chunks are then used to form a task-level prior action distribution, modeled as a moment-matched Gaussian $N(\mu, \text{diag(Var)})$. The mean ($\mu$) and diagonal covariance (Var) are computed as a weighted average of the retrieved action chunks, using the $\alpha_i$ weights. A minimum variance floor is enforced to prevent deterministic collapse.
- **Prior-Aware Sampler**: This module samples the initial action chunk ($\hat{X}_t$) for the flow policy from the constructed Gaussian prior. Crucially, it adaptively adjusts the noise scale ($\lambda$) and the Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) based on the global similarity score ($\bar{s}$). High similarity (closer to 1) leads to lower noise and fewer NFEs, indicating high confidence in the retrieved prior. Lower similarity (closer to 0) results in higher noise and more NFEs, allowing for more exploration. This sampling strategy initializes the generative flow closer to the target manifold, reducing NFEs and anchoring the process in a feasible action space. For efficiency, retrieval and weight computation are cached at the session level.

#### 3.3. Local Consistency Memory (LCM)

LCM addresses the lack of temporal awareness and jittery control by dynamically modeling action history. It comprises two lightweight structures:

- **Consistency Layer**: This layer takes the previous action chunk ($A_{t-1}$) as input and processes it using a self-attention mechanism. It captures inter-action dependencies and constraints within the chunk, producing a representation ($\hat{B}_{t-1}$).
- **Dynamic Awareness Module**: This module takes the representation $\hat{B}_{t-1}$ from the Consistency Layer and updates its internal state (implemented using a Mamba-based structure for efficient long-range dependency modeling) to predict a consistency bias ($B_t$) for the next time step.
- **Integration**: The predicted consistency bias ($B_t$) is additively injected into the action chunk ($\hat{X}_t$) sampled by GPM, forming the final policy input ($X_t = \hat{X}_t + B_t$). This mechanism introduces a learned temporal constraint into the policy, promoting smoother trajectories and providing progress awareness without requiring modifications to the VLA pre-training paradigm or incurring significant computational overhead.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

The training of OptimusVLA is a three-stage process:

1. **Stage I: Hierarchical VLA Pre-training**: A hierarchical VLA model (VLM + flow policy) is pre-trained using the architecture and protocol of $\pi_{0.5}$. During this stage, neither GPM nor LCM is active.
2. **Stage II: GPM Training**: The VLM and flow policy are frozen. The Prior Head is trained to learn task-discriminative representations using an InfoNCE objective, with a task-pair batch sampler ensuring positive and negative samples within each batch. After training, the Memory Bank is constructed.
3. **Stage III: LCM Training**: The VLM, flow policy, and GPM are all frozen. LCM is trained to predict the residual bias ($B_t^* = A_t^* - \mu_t$) between the ground-truth action chunk ($A_t^*$) and the mean prior ($\mu_t$) provided by GPM. This is done by minimizing an MSE loss. A cold-start strategy is used during training to ensure robustness during initial inference steps where no history exists.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OptimusVLA was evaluated across various simulation benchmarks and real-world scenarios, demonstrating improvements in performance and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Simulation Benchmarks

- **LIBERO**: OptimusVLA achieved an average success rate of 98.6%, surpassing existing baselines such as $\pi_{0.5}$ (96.9%) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%). It performed particularly well on the LIBERO-Long suite, which involves long-horizon tasks prone to error accumulation, achieving a 96.4% success rate. The framework also reduced the average Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) on LIBERO to 3.2, compared to 10.0 for $\pi_{0.5}$.
- **CALVIN**: In the ABC $\to$ D generalization setting, OptimusVLA attained an average episode length of 4.45, outperforming $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$. This performance is attributed to GPM's ability to retrieve semantically similar trajectories as an initialization, which aided in adapting to unseen environments and distribution shifts.
- **RoboTwin 2.0 Hard**: OptimusVLA achieved an average success rate of 38% on the Hard setting of RoboTwin 2.0, surpassing other methods like RDT (20%), ACT (2%), DP (1%), DP3 (11%), $\pi_0$ (25%), and $\pi_{0.5}$ (29%). Notably, it attained a 58% success rate on the "Stack Bowls Two" task. This performance is attributed to LCM's contribution to enforcing temporal and inter-arm consistency, which is critical for bimanual manipulation.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Real-World

- **Generalization Tasks**: On a set of real-world generalization tasks involving a GALAXEA R1 Lite robot, OptimusVLA achieved an average success rate of 85.0%, outperforming $\pi_0$ (75.0%) and $\pi_{0.5}$ (77.0%). This robustness to variations in lighting and scene is attributed to GPM's multimodal semantics-conditioned prior retrieval.
- **Long-horizon Tasks**: For real-world long-horizon manipulation tasks, OptimusVLA achieved an average success rate of 64.0%, surpassing $\pi_0$ by 52.4% (64.0% vs 11.6%) and $\pi_{0.5}$ by 12.0% (64.0% vs 52.0%). This demonstrates the model's enhanced stability and coherent bimanual coordination for complex, sequential tasks, primarily due to LCM.
- **Inference Speedup**: OptimusVLA delivered a 2.9x inference speedup in real-world evaluations compared to baselines.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of GPM**: Removing GPM resulted in a 3.3% performance drop on LIBERO-Long, a 3.8% drop in average episode length on CALVIN, and a 9.4% drop on real-world Generalization Tasks. This indicates GPM's role in improving cross-environment and cross-scene generalization by providing better initial priors.
- **Impact of LCM**: Removing LCM caused a 1.7% drop on LIBERO-Long, a 1.6% drop on CALVIN, and a 6.5% drop on real-world Generalization Tasks. This highlights LCM's contribution to maintaining temporal consistency and progress awareness.
- **GPM Memory Size**: Performance on LIBERO-Long scaled with the richness of the memory bank and the number of retrieved trajectories ($k$). Using a sufficient $k$ (e.g., 8) was shown to be important for constructing a robust prior, balancing specificity and exploratory potential.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis

- **Training Efficiency**: OptimusVLA achieved strong performance with fewer training steps compared to $\pi_{0.5}$. For example, it reached 97.6% on LIBERO-Goal in 18,000 steps, while $\pi_{0.5}$ required 26,000 steps for similar performance. This acceleration is attributed to GPM's task-level priors.
- **Inference Efficiency**: OptimusVLA demonstrated a 6.5x faster inference time and 3.1x fewer NFEs on LIBERO, and a 2.9x inference speedup in real-world tasks. These gains are attributed to the lightweight design of GPM and LCM, which reduce the required NFEs.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples illustrated that GPM enables OptimusVLA to start inference closer to the target action distribution with adaptive noise and NFEs, leading to faster inference. LCM was shown to impose consistency constraints, resulting in smoother generated trajectories and improved temporal awareness, particularly when dealing with visually similar states that can confuse policies lacking historical context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents OptimusVLA, a dual-memory augmented VLA framework that addresses two limitations in robotic manipulation: the efficiency of action generation and the robustness to temporal dependencies. By introducing Global Prior Memory (GPM) and Local Consistency Memory (LCM), the work makes contributions to the field of robotic control.

The GPM module provides a mechanism to replace generic isotropic noise priors with task-level priors retrieved from semantically similar trajectories. This approach shortens the generative path, reduces the Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) required for action generation, and lowers the incidence of infeasible samples. The adaptive noise scale and NFE schedule further optimize the inference process based on the confidence of the retrieved prior. This improvement in efficiency has implications for deploying VLA models in real-time robotic systems, where latency is a critical factor.

The LCM module enhances the temporal robustness of VLA models by dynamically modeling recent action sequences. By inferring task progress and injecting a learned consistency constraint, LCM promotes temporal coherence and smoothness in generated trajectories. This addresses the challenge of VLA policies lacking awareness of task phases or generating jittery movements when conditioned solely on current observations. This is particularly relevant for long-horizon and multi-stage manipulation tasks, as well as bimanual coordination.

The comprehensive evaluations across multiple simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN, RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world tasks demonstrate that OptimusVLA achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines. The observed improvements in success rates, particularly in generalization and long-horizon tasks, coupled with substantial inference speedups (up to 6.5x faster in simulation and 2.9x in real-world), indicate the practical utility of the proposed architecture. The ablation studies further confirm the individual contributions of GPM and LCM to these performance gains.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Improved Robotic Autonomy**: By making VLA models more efficient and robust, OptimusVLA contributes to the development of more capable and reliable autonomous robotic systems, especially for complex manipulation tasks in dynamic and varied environments.
- **Accelerated Development and Deployment**: The enhanced training efficiency and reduced inference latency could accelerate the development cycle of VLA policies and facilitate their deployment in real-world applications where rapid decision-making and smooth control are essential.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The dual-memory architecture provides a framework for integrating explicit knowledge (via GPM) and temporal reasoning (via LCM) into generative VLA policies. This could inspire further research into adaptive memory mechanisms, online memory updates, and end-to-end learning strategies that continually refine these memory components.
- **Broader Applicability**: The principles of leveraging task-level priors and local temporal consistency could potentially be adapted to other generative models in robotics or other domains where efficient and coherent sequence generation is required.
