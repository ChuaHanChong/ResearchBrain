# Towards Generalizable Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation: A Benchmark and LLM-guided 3D Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces GemBench, a new benchmark for evaluating vision-language robotic manipulation policies across four levels of generalization, and proposes 3D-LOTUS++, a modular policy. 3D-LOTUS++ combines a 3D-based motion control policy with large language models for planning and vision-language models for object grounding, achieving higher success rates on novel tasks in simulation and real-world experiments compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation policies exhibit limited generalization capabilities, often requiring extensive task-specific data and failing on novel, unseen tasks.
- Current simulation benchmarks for vision-language robotic manipulation predominantly evaluate performance on tasks seen during training, lacking systematic and progressively challenging test sets for true generalization.
- End-to-end robotic policies struggle to adapt to new tasks, while foundation models, despite strong high-level reasoning, often lack the fine-grained, precise motion control needed for intricate manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces GemBench, a new simulation benchmark built on RLBench, featuring 16 training tasks and 44 testing tasks designed across four progressively challenging levels of generalization to evaluate robustness to novel placements, rigid objects, articulated objects, and long-horizon tasks.
- Proposes 3D-LOTUS, an efficient end-to-end 3D robotic manipulation policy that uses a language-conditioned Point Cloud Transformer and a novel classification-based approach for precise position and rotation prediction.
- Develops 3D-LOTUS++, a modular framework that enhances generalization by integrating LLaMa3-8B for task planning and combining OWLv2 with SAM for robust 3D object grounding, feeding these into a modified 3D-LOTUS for motion control with category-based point features.

## Results

- 3D-LOTUS achieved a state-of-the-art 83.1% average success rate on the RLBench-18Task benchmark, demonstrating strong action execution and significantly higher efficiency with 2.2 GPU days of training compared to baselines.
- 3D-LOTUS++ significantly outperformed baselines and its base 3D-LOTUS on GemBench's generalization levels, achieving 64.5% on novel rigid objects (Level 2), 41.5% on novel articulated objects (Level 3), and 17.4% on novel long-horizon tasks (Level 4, a substantial gain over 0.3% for 3D-LOTUS).
- Real-world experiments validated 3D-LOTUS++'s practical generalization, achieving a 7.9/10 average success rate on 7 new, unseen task variations, whereas 3D-LOTUS completely failed (0/10) on these novel real-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object grounding, even when utilizing advanced vision-language models like OWLv2 and SAM, remains a primary bottleneck, particularly for distinguishing visually similar objects or fine-grained articulated parts.
> - The underlying low-level motion control policy still struggles to generalize to scenarios with highly novel robot configurations or very long, complex action sequences, even with accurate high-level planning and grounding.
> - LLM-based task planning, when operating without visual input, can make incorrect assumptions about the environment, highlighting the need for multimodal LLMs capable of visually-aware reasoning.
