# Towards Generalizable Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation: A Benchmark and LLM-guided 3D Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces GemBench, a new benchmark for evaluating vision-language robotic manipulation policies across four levels of generalization, and proposes 3D-LOTUS++, a modular policy. 3D-LOTUS++ combines a 3D-based motion control policy with large language models for planning and vision-language models for object grounding, achieving higher success rates on novel tasks in simulation and real-world experiments compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation policies exhibit limited generalization capabilities, often requiring extensive task-specific data and failing on novel, unseen tasks.
- Current simulation benchmarks for vision-language robotic manipulation predominantly evaluate performance on tasks seen during training, lacking systematic and progressively challenging test sets for true generalization.
- End-to-end robotic policies struggle to adapt to new tasks, while foundation models, despite strong high-level reasoning, often lack the fine-grained, precise motion control needed for intricate manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces GemBench, a new simulation benchmark built on RLBench, featuring 16 training tasks and 44 testing tasks designed across four progressively challenging levels of generalization to evaluate robustness to novel placements, rigid objects, articulated objects, and long-horizon tasks.
- Proposes 3D-LOTUS, an efficient end-to-end 3D robotic manipulation policy that uses a language-conditioned Point Cloud Transformer and a novel classification-based approach for precise position and rotation prediction.
- Develops 3D-LOTUS++, a modular framework that enhances generalization by integrating LLaMa3-8B for task planning and combining OWLv2 with SAM for robust 3D object grounding, feeding these into a modified 3D-LOTUS for motion control with category-based point features.

## Results

- 3D-LOTUS achieved a state-of-the-art 83.1% average success rate on the RLBench-18Task benchmark, demonstrating strong action execution and significantly higher efficiency with 2.2 GPU days of training compared to baselines.
- 3D-LOTUS++ significantly outperformed baselines and its base 3D-LOTUS on GemBench's generalization levels, achieving 64.5% on novel rigid objects (Level 2), 41.5% on novel articulated objects (Level 3), and 17.4% on novel long-horizon tasks (Level 4, a substantial gain over 0.3% for 3D-LOTUS).
- Real-world experiments validated 3D-LOTUS++'s practical generalization, achieving a 7.9/10 average success rate on 7 new, unseen task variations, whereas 3D-LOTUS completely failed (0/10) on these novel real-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object grounding, even when utilizing advanced vision-language models like OWLv2 and SAM, remains a primary bottleneck, particularly for distinguishing visually similar objects or fine-grained articulated parts.
> - The underlying low-level motion control policy still struggles to generalize to scenarios with highly novel robot configurations or very long, complex action sequences, even with accurate high-level planning and grounding.
> - LLM-based task planning, when operating without visual input, can make incorrect assumptions about the environment, highlighting the need for multimodal LLMs capable of visually-aware reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly when guided by vision and language, is rapidly evolving. The ultimate goal is to enable robots to perform a vast diversity of real-world tasks given natural language instructions, without requiring extensive, task-specific data collection. This paper positions itself at the forefront of this challenge, directly addressing two critical limitations in the current research landscape: the lack of comprehensive benchmarks for *generalization* and the struggle of existing policies to adapt to *novel tasks*.

Historically, many robotic manipulation policies relied on intensive data collection for each specific task. This approach is not scalable given the infinite variability of real-world scenarios. Consequently, research has shifted towards developing policies that can generalize. Several directions have emerged:

- **Pretraining Visual and Language Representations:** Approaches like R3M and "Real-world robot learning with masked visual pre-training" leverage large-scale web data to learn powerful visual and language embeddings. While these representations are effective for perception, they do not directly yield generalizable *policies* for action execution in novel tasks.
- **Pretraining End-to-End Robotic Policies:** More recently, efforts like RT-1, RT-2, PALM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA pretrain entire policies by combining robot data with vast web datasets. However, these methods are often constrained by the limited diversity and scale of available robot datasets, making true generalization to entirely *new* tasks challenging.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models (LLMs and VLMs):** The rise of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) has spurred a new wave of research. Models like CaP use LLMs to generate executable code, and VoxPoser composes 3D value maps for motion planning. Other works, such as SayCan and ViLa, integrate LLMs for task decomposition and high-level planning. While these approaches enhance generalization in planning and perception, they often focus on simpler tasks (e.g., pick-and-place) and may lack the precision needed for complex, fine-grained manipulation (e.g., rotating objects or screwing). They can also struggle with grounding plans in the visual world or assume predefined motion skills.

**The Benchmark Landscape:** A significant hurdle in evaluating progress towards generalization is the absence of suitable benchmarks. Early simulation-based benchmarks like RLBench-74Task and RLBench-18Task primarily assess performance on tasks *seen during training*, neglecting the crucial aspect of generalization. More recent benchmarks, including VLMbench, ALFRED, Calvin, Ravens, Arnold, VIMA-Bench, and Colosseum, have begun to incorporate some form of generalization evaluation (e.g., to new object compositions, shapes, or long-horizon tasks). However, the authors argue that these benchmarks still fall short: VIMA-Bench is limited to simpler action skills (e.g., suction gripper pick-and-place), and Colosseum emphasizes environmental perturbations (lighting, camera angles) rather than generalization to fundamentally *new task types* or object interactions.

This paper directly addresses these gaps by introducing **GemBench**, a novel benchmark designed specifically to systematically evaluate generalization capabilities across diverse action primitives and increasingly challenging levels of novelty. Furthermore, it proposes **3D-LOTUS++**, a modular policy framework that seeks to combine the strengths of precise 3D-based motion control with the powerful generalization abilities of foundation models, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior end-to-end and foundation-model-centric approaches. By offering both a robust evaluation tool and a performant method, this work aims to significantly push the boundaries of generalizable vision-language robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the state-of-the-art in vision-language robotic manipulation by enabling policies to effectively generalize to novel, unseen tasks. This primary objective is driven by several critical motivations and broken down into specific goals:

**Motivation:**

1. **Prohibitive Data Collection Costs:** Real-world robotic tasks exhibit immense diversity. Collecting sufficient demonstration data for every conceivable task or variation is practically impossible and economically unfeasible. This necessitates the development of policies that can learn from limited examples and extrapolate to new situations.
2. **Inadequate Generalization Benchmarks:** The current landscape of simulation benchmarks for vision-language robotic manipulation primarily focuses on evaluating performance on tasks already seen during training. They lack systematic and progressively challenging test sets that truly probe a policy's ability to generalize to novel objects (rigid and articulated), new object attributes, and complex long-horizon tasks. This absence hinders principled progress and makes it difficult to compare different generalization-focused methods accurately.
3. **Limitations of Current Robotic Policies:**
 - **End-to-End Policies:** Existing end-to-end policies, while capable on seen tasks, often struggle when faced with new tasks, failing to adapt their planning or properly ground new objects mentioned in instructions. This makes error diagnosis difficult.
 - **Foundation Model-based Approaches:** While large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) offer impressive generalization for high-level planning and perception, they often lack the fine-grained, precise motion control required for intricate robotic manipulation. Integrating them effectively without sacrificing control fidelity remains a challenge.

#### 2.1. Specific Objectives

1. **Develop a Comprehensive Generalization Benchmark (GemBench):** To systematically evaluate the generalization capabilities of vision-language robotic manipulation policies. This involves:
 - Incorporating a wide range of *seven general action primitives* (press, pick, push, screw, close, open, stack/put), moving beyond simple pick-and-place.
 - Defining *four progressively challenging levels of generalization*: novel placements, novel rigid objects, novel articulated objects, and complex long-horizon tasks.
2. **Propose an Efficient and Strong 3D Robotic Manipulation Policy (3D-LOTUS):** To leverage rich 3D information for accurate action prediction and establish a robust baseline. This policy aims for high performance on known tasks and improved training efficiency.
3. **Enhance Generalization with Foundation Models (3D-LOTUS++):** To overcome the generalization limitations of end-to-end policies by integrating LLMs for task planning and VLMs for object grounding within a modular framework. This aims to combine the planning and perception strengths of foundation models with the precise motion control of 3D-LOTUS.
4. **Establish New State-of-the-Art:** To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed models on both existing benchmarks (for action execution) and the new GemBench (for generalization), as well as validating performance on a real robot.

In essence, the paper aims to provide both the diagnostic tool (GemBench) and the improved solution (3D-LOTUS++) necessary to push the field towards truly generalizable, language-conditioned robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces a novel benchmark, GemBench, and two related policies, 3D-LOTUS and 3D-LOTUS++, to address the challenge of generalizable vision-language robotic manipulation.

#### 3.1. GemBench: Generalizable Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation Benchmark

GemBench is a new simulation benchmark built upon the **RLBench simulator**, chosen for its visually and physically realistic tasks and scripted demonstration generation. It features a diverse training set and a systematically designed testing set for generalization evaluation.

- **Training Tasks:**
 - Comprises 16 tasks with 31 variations, selected from existing RLBench datasets.
 - Covers seven fundamental action primitives: press, pick, push, screw, close, open, and stack/put.
 - Includes diverse objects (20), colors (20), and object parts (3), ensuring a rich initial experience for the robot.
 - Examples: "push button," "pick up cup," "screw light bulb," "close laptop lid," "open drawer," "stack blocks."
- **Testing Tasks with Four Levels of Generalization:**
 - Total of 44 tasks with 92 variations, including 23 existing RLBench tasks and 21 newly scripted ones.
 - Designed to progressively increase difficulty:
 - **Level 1 - Novel Placements:** Tests on the same 16 training tasks but with objects in new, randomly sampled positions and with new distractor objects. Evaluates robustness to minor configuration changes.
 - **Level 2 - Novel Rigid Objects:** Features 15 unseen tasks involving new rigid objects. This includes novel object-color compositions (e.g., "rose button" when only "yellow button" was seen) and novel object shapes (e.g., "lift a toy" or "star-shaped item" after training on "picking a cube").
 - **Level 3 - Novel Articulated Objects:** Consists of 18 new tasks interacting with articulated objects. Categories include novel action-part compositions (e.g., "open middle drawer" vs. "open bottom drawer" in training), novel object instances (e.g., a four-drawer unit vs. a three-drawer unit), and novel object categories (e.g., "close grill lid" vs. "close laptop lid").
 - **Level 4 - Novel Long-horizon Tasks:** The most challenging, requiring the robot to combine multiple learned actions. Includes 6 tasks (e.g., "put items in drawer" involving opening, picking, and placing multiple items in sequence).

#### 3.2. 3D-LOTUS Policy (Language-cOnditioned poinT cloUd tranSformer)

3D-LOTUS is an end-to-end 3D robotic manipulation policy designed for strong action execution, particularly on seen tasks.

- **Problem Formulation:** The policy learns $\pi(a_t | O_t, L)$, predicting action $a_t$ given observation $O_t$ (RGB-D images from K cameras, robot proprioceptive state) and language instruction $L$. The action space consists of gripper position ($R^3$), rotation ($R^3$), and open state ({0,1}). Waypoint representation is used for action sequences, with inverse kinematics for robot movement.
- **Point Cloud Preprocessing:** Multi-view RGB-D images are projected into a unified 3D point cloud in world coordinates. This cloud is downsampled and filtered to remove irrelevant points (outside workspace, on robotic arm via CAD models and joint poses), focusing on task-relevant objects and the gripper. Each point includes XYZ coordinates and additional features (RGB color, relative height).
- **Language-conditioned Point Cloud Transformer:**
 - Uses **Point Cloud Transformer v3 (PTV3)** as the backbone, leveraging a U-Net architecture with transformer layers.
 - Language (encoded by CLIP text encoder) is integrated via **cross-attention**: a cross-attention layer is added after each self-attention layer in PTV3, allowing points to attend to word embeddings. (Adaptive normalization was also explored but performed worse).
- **Action Prediction:** A novel **classification-based approach** is employed, instead of traditional regression or inefficient full-workspace classification.
 - **Position Prediction:** Discretizes each of the X, Y, Z axes into sequential bins around each point. A heatmap is predicted for these bins at each point, then concatenated across all points. The bin with the highest probability determines the position for each axis.
 - **Rotation Prediction:** Euler angles for each axis are discretized into bins, and classification is used to predict the angles.
 - **Open State Prediction:** A binary classification task.
 - Max pooling over all point embeddings is used for rotation and open state prediction. Cross-entropy loss is used for training.

#### 3.3. 3D-LOTUS++ Policy (Enhanced with Foundation Models)

3D-LOTUS++ is a modular framework that builds upon 3D-LOTUS by integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to improve generalization. This modularity disentangles planning, grounding, and control.

- **Task Planning with LLM:**
 - Uses **LLaMa3-8B** to decompose a high-level instruction $L$ into a sequence of actionable steps ($l_1, \dots, l_T$).
 - Six predefined action primitives are used in the planning (e.g., `grasp(object)`, `move_grasped_object(target)`, `push_down(object)`).
 - LLM is prompted with task requirements and in-context examples (top 20 similar examples selected using SentenceBert [?]) to guide plan generation.
- **Object Grounding with VLMs:**
 - Localizes objects described in the LLM-generated plan steps.
 - Combines **OWLv2** (open-vocabulary object detector for bounding boxes and semantic embeddings) with **Segment Anything Model (SAM)** (for precise segmentation within bounding boxes).
 - Segmentation masks are combined with RGB-D to yield 3D point clouds for each bounding box.
 - Observations of the same object from different cameras are merged based on semantic embedding and point cloud distance.
 - Text descriptions are matched to object-centric representations using CLIP text embeddings and cosine similarity.
 - **Crucially, for articulated object parts (e.g., "bottom drawer"), LLM is additionally used to predict a height range** within the VLM-detected object to provide finer-grained localization, as VLMs typically detect the whole object.
- **Motion Control (Modified 3D-LOTUS):**
 - 3D-LOTUS is adapted to take inputs from the object grounding module.
 - **Modified Point Features:** Instead of RGB colors, point features ($v_o_i$) now encode point categories: `goal object`, `goal target`, `robot`, and `obstacle`. This encourages the model to focus on geometry and enhances generalization to novel textures.
 - **Trajectory Prediction:** Predicts a sequence of actions (trajectory) for each planned step, rather than a single action.
 - A timestep embedding is concatenated with point embeddings.
 - A `stop probability` is predicted to determine when a trajectory should terminate.

The overall approach is to leverage the generalization strength of large pre-trained models for the high-level cognitive tasks of planning and grounding, while relying on a specialized, efficient 3D-based policy for precise, low-level motion control, thereby creating a robust and generalizable robotic manipulation system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate both the proposed 3D-LOTUS policy's raw action execution capabilities and 3D-LOTUS++'s enhanced generalization, validated in both simulation (GemBench and RLBench-18Task) and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance on RLBench-18Task (Seen Tasks)

- **3D-LOTUS Performance:** On the RLBench-18Task benchmark, which primarily evaluates performance on tasks seen during training, 3D-LOTUS achieves **state-of-the-art success rates (83.1% Average Success Rate)**.
- **Efficiency:** Crucially, 3D-LOTUS achieves this performance with significantly less training time (2.2 GPU days on V100) compared to other state-of-the-art methods like PerAct (128.0 days), Act3D (40.0 days), and 3D diffuser actor (67.6 days). This demonstrates its strong action execution capability and notable efficiency. It particularly excels in tasks requiring high precision, such as "insert peg," "place cups," and "stack cups."

#### 4.2. Performance on GemBench (Generalization)

The true test of generalization is on the GemBench benchmark's four levels of increasing difficulty:

- **Level 1 (Novel Placements):** 3D-LOTUS performs exceptionally well (94.3%), outperforming all baselines. This confirms its robustness to minor scene configuration changes and distractors.
- **Levels 2-4 (Generalization Challenge):**
 - **Significant Performance Drop for Baselines and 3D-LOTUS:** As expected, all state-of-the-art baselines and the end-to-end 3D-LOTUS policy show a substantial drop in performance on these levels, highlighting the inherent generalization limitations of current methods. For example, 3D-LOTUS drops to 49.9% on Level 2, 38.1% on Level 3, and a mere 0.3% on Level 4.
 - **3D-LOTUS++ Outperformance:** 3D-LOTUS++ significantly outperforms all methods, including its base 3D-LOTUS, on the more challenging generalization levels:
 - **Level 2 (Novel Rigid Objects):** 64.5% success rate for 3D-LOTUS++ vs. 49.9% for 3D-LOTUS.
 - **Level 3 (Novel Articulated Objects):** 41.5% success rate for 3D-LOTUS++ vs. 38.1% for 3D-LOTUS.
 - **Level 4 (Novel Long-horizon Tasks):** 17.4% success rate for 3D-LOTUS++ vs. 0.3% for 3D-LOTUS. This is a particularly impressive gain, demonstrating its ability to tackle multi-step, compositional tasks.
- **Level 1 Anomaly for 3D-LOTUS++:** Interestingly, 3D-LOTUS++ shows lower performance on Level 1 (68.7%) compared to 3D-LOTUS (94.3%). The authors attribute this to the zero-shot object grounding models (OWLv2 + SAM) struggling to differentiate between *some* visually similar "seen" objects (e.g., "tuna can" vs. "soup can") that the end-to-end 3D-LOTUS implicitly learns to handle from direct demonstrations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **3D-LOTUS Components:**
 - **Action Prediction:** Classification-based action prediction (discretizing position and rotation into bins) consistently outperforms regression-based methods across all generalization levels, leading to better performance and faster convergence.
 - **Language Conditioning:** Using cross-attention to integrate language information into the Point Cloud Transformer (PTV3) performs better than adaptive normalization.
- **3D-LOTUS++ Modules (Error Analysis):**
 - **Object Grounding as a Bottleneck:** Providing ground truth (GT) object grounding labels significantly improves performance, especially on Levels 1 and 2 (e.g., GT grounding boosts Level 1 from 68.7% to 92.6% and Level 2 from 64.5% to 80.1%). This highlights that the object grounding module, despite using advanced VLMs, remains a primary bottleneck, particularly for fine-grained distinction.
 - **Motion Control Limitations:** Even with ground truth task planning and object grounding, performance on Levels 3 and 4 remains suboptimal (47.8% on L3, 31.5% on L4 with full GT). This indicates that the 3D-LOTUS motion control policy itself struggles to generalize to scenarios with highly novel robot configurations (e.g., due to long sequences of actions) or extremely novel articulated object interactions.
 - **LLM Task Planning Limitations (for L4):** The LLM's task planning accuracy for Level 4 tasks can be reduced because it operates *without visual input*. This can lead to incorrect assumptions about the environment (e.g., whether a box is open or closed when asked to "take shoes out of the box"), resulting in inefficient or failed plans.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

- **3D-LOTUS:** Outperforms PolarNet on 7 seen real-world task variations (8.1/10 vs. 6.7/10 success rate), consistent with simulation results on seen tasks. However, it completely fails to generalize to 7 *new, unseen* real-world task variations (0/10 success rate).
- **3D-LOTUS++:** Successfully addresses these new real-world task variations, achieving a high average success rate of 7.9/10, demonstrating its practical generalization capability on a physical robot.

In summary, the main findings confirm that while 3D-LOTUS is a highly efficient and effective policy for known tasks, its generalization is limited. 3D-LOTUS++, by modularly integrating LLMs for planning and VLMs for grounding, significantly boosts generalization to novel tasks, setting new state-of-the-art results on the challenging GemBench benchmark and demonstrating real-world applicability. The ablation studies provide crucial insights into the remaining challenges, primarily robust object grounding and the generalization capacity of low-level motion policies in complex, long-horizon scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions that could have a substantial impact on the field of vision-language robotic manipulation and beyond:

1. **Establishes a New Standard for Generalization Benchmarking:** GemBench directly addresses a critical void in the research landscape. By providing a systematically designed benchmark with four progressively challenging levels of generalization, it offers a robust and comprehensive tool for evaluating and comparing future policies. This structured evaluation is essential for driving progress beyond mere "seen task" performance and focusing on true generalization. The public availability of GemBench, along with code and trained models, will foster collaborative research and accelerate innovation in this domain.

2. **Advances Generalizable Robotic Manipulation Policies:** 3D-LOTUS++ demonstrates a highly effective strategy for building generalizable robotic policies. By embracing a modular architecture that combines the strengths of specialized 3D-based motion control (3D-LOTUS) with the powerful planning and perception capabilities of large foundation models (LLMs and VLMs), the authors set a new state-of-the-art for generalization on complex tasks. This approach validates the hypothesis that disaggregating high-level reasoning from low-level execution can lead to more robust and adaptable robotic systems.

3. **Provides Critical Insights into Bottlenecks:** The detailed ablation studies are particularly impactful. They pinpoint specific areas that remain challenging even with advanced techniques:
 - **Object Grounding:** The finding that object grounding (even with state-of-the-art VLMs like OWLv2 and SAM) remains a significant bottleneck, especially for distinguishing similar objects or fine-grained parts, indicates a crucial area for future perception research in robotics.
 - **Motion Control Generalization:** The revelation that the underlying motion control policy (3D-LOTUS) still struggles to generalize to highly novel initial robot configurations or very long-horizon tasks, even with perfect planning and grounding, points to fundamental limitations in current reinforcement or imitation learning approaches that need to be addressed.
 - **Visually-Aware LLM Planning:** The observation that LLM-based task planning suffers from a lack of visual awareness highlights the need for multimodal LLMs that can reason about the visual state of the environment during planning.

These insights are invaluable as they guide future research efforts toward tackling the most pressing challenges, rather than merely optimizing existing paradigms.

4. **Demonstrates Real-World Feasibility:** The successful real-world experiments with 3D-LOTUS++ are highly significant. While many robotics papers demonstrate results only in simulation, the real-robot validation on *unseen* tasks provides strong evidence for the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed framework. This bridges the sim-to-real gap for generalization and enhances the credibility of the research.

5. **Promotes Practical Modularity in Robotics:** The modular design of 3D-LOTUS++ is a practical and impactful contribution. It allows researchers to independently improve different components (e.g., a better LLM for planning, a more robust VLM for grounding, or an enhanced 3D policy for control) without overhauling the entire system. This promotes a more structured development pathway for complex robotic systems.

In conclusion, this paper not only introduces a vital benchmark for evaluating generalizable robotic manipulation but also presents a compelling modular framework that significantly advances the field. By identifying and highlighting remaining bottlenecks, it charts a clear path for future research aimed at developing truly intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.
