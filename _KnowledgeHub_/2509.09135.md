# Continuous-Time Value Iteration for Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Purdue University researchers developed SPReD (Smooth Policy Regularisation from Demonstrations), a framework integrating demonstrations into reinforcement learning by using a Q-network ensemble to quantify uncertainty and apply continuous imitation weights to demonstration actions. This method improves sample efficiency and robustness to suboptimal demonstrations in sparse-reward robotics tasks, achieving up to 14x better success rates than existing baselines.

## Problem

- Discrete-time reinforcement learning (DTRL) methods struggle with high-frequency or irregular decision-making in real-world scenarios, leading to poor generalization and stability issues under varying time resolutions.
- Extending continuous-time reinforcement learning (CTRL) to multi-agent systems is hindered by the curse of dimensionality (CoD) when solving Hamilton
Jacobi
Bellman (HJB) equations for high-dimensional state spaces.
- Learning accurate centralized value functions in multi-agent settings, even with HJB-based techniques, is challenging due to non-stationarity and the potential for noisy or biased value gradients from standard Physics-informed Neural Network (PINN) training.

## Method

- A continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning (CT-MARL) framework, Value Iteration via ==PINN== (VIP), was developed, which integrates PINNs with an actor-critic architecture to approximate continuous-time value functions by solving HJB PDEs.
- A Value Gradient Iteration (VGI) module was introduced to dynamically refine approximations of value gradients during training, providing a one-step Bellman unrolling in gradient space as a target for the PINN's auto-differentiated gradient.
- The critic's objective function was enhanced with a TD-style anchor loss and the VGI loss, alongside HJB residual and terminal boundary condition losses, to provide more stable and accurate value landscape supervision.

## Results

- The VIP method consistently achieved faster convergence and higher cumulative rewards on continuous-time Multi-Agent Particle Environment (MPE) and Multi-Agent MuJoCo benchmarks, outperforming existing continuous-time RL baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the VGI module, with VIP+VGI producing value and value gradient contours that closely matched analytical LQR ground truth, unlike VIP without VGI which yielded significantly biased approximations.
- VIP demonstrated strong robustness to varying time intervals, maintaining nearly constant average returns, while a discrete-time baseline (MADDPG) experienced significant performance degradation under similar conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-informed Neural Networks (PINNs) can effectively mitigate the curse of dimensionality for HJB equation solving in continuous-time multi-agent environments, enabling scalable CT-MARL.
> - Accurate value gradient estimation is crucial for stable and effective continuous-time policy learning; the VGI module significantly improves this accuracy by providing iterative refinement, leading to better value and gradient approximations.
> - A balanced design of PINN components, including the choice of activation function (Tanh preferred for smoothness) and careful weighting of all loss terms, is essential for robust and high-fidelity value function and gradient approximations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated success in a variety of discrete-time tasks across single and multi-agent domains. However, a significant number of real-world systems, such as continuous control in robotics, autonomous driving, and market trading, operate in continuous time and demand high-frequency or irregularly timed interactions. Traditional discrete-time RL (DTRL) methods approximate these continuous processes by imposing fixed discretization steps, which introduces several limitations: non-smooth and suboptimal control with coarse timesteps, increased computational and memory costs with fine timesteps, and instability of Bellman updates when timesteps become excessively small due to approximation noise.

Continuous-time RL (CTRL) has emerged as an alternative to address these issues by replacing discrete-time Bellman recursion with differentiable value functions derived from Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equations. While CTRL offers a more direct and theoretically grounded approach for continuous systems, its application has predominantly been limited to single-agent scenarios. This limitation stems from two primary challenges. First, conventional numerical methods for solving HJB equations are prone to the curse of dimensionality (CoD), rendering them computationally intractable in high-dimensional state spaces characteristic of complex dynamical systems. Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) have been introduced as a method to mitigate CoD by embedding the underlying physics (PDEs) into the neural network's loss function. Second, even with learning-based approaches like PINNs, accurately approximating centralized value functions in multi-agent settings presents difficulties. The inherent non-stationarity introduced by multiple interacting learning agents, combined with coupled dynamics, can amplify approximation errors in value gradients, thereby destabilizing policy training.

This paper positions itself within the landscape of continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning (CT-MARL). It builds upon the advances of CTRL and PINNs for HJB equation solving, but specifically addresses the challenges of extending these to multi-agent cooperative environments, particularly focusing on the accuracy and stability of value gradient approximations. The work seeks to bridge the gap between single-agent CTRL and the complexities of multi-agent interactions, where such continuous-time formulations are largely underexplored.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research arises from the limitations of conventional DTRL methods when applied to real-world control systems that inherently operate in continuous time or require decisions at high and irregular frequencies. The approximations made by DTRL, such as fixed-interval discretizations, can lead to suboptimal, unstable, or computationally inefficient policies. Continuous-time RL, which directly models value functions as solutions to HJB partial differential equations, offers a more natural and theoretically consistent framework for these domains.

Despite the promise of CTRL, its practical application to multi-agent systems faces significant hurdles, which this paper aims to address. The key objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Overcoming the Curse of Dimensionality (CoD) in HJB Solutions:**
 - **Motivation:** HJB equations, central to CTRL, are notoriously difficult to solve numerically in high-dimensional state spaces. Conventional methods' computational complexity grows exponentially with dimension, making them impractical for systems with more than a few states, especially in multi-agent environments where the joint state space can be very large.
 - **Objective:** To leverage Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to approximate the viscosity solutions of HJB PDEs. PINNs, by incorporating the PDE structure into their loss functions, have shown potential in mitigating CoD, enabling the approximation of value functions in higher-dimensional systems.

2. **Addressing Challenges in Centralized Value Function Approximation for Multi-Agent Systems:**
 - **Motivation:** In multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), agents interact not only with the environment but also with other learning agents. This introduces non-stationarity, making it challenging to learn a stable and accurate centralized value function, even when using approaches like PINNs. Errors in value approximation, particularly in their gradients, can be amplified by the coupled dynamics inherent in multi-agent settings.
 - **Objective:** To develop a robust CT-MARL framework that can accurately learn centralized value functions under a centralized training, decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm. This includes developing mechanisms to stabilize value learning in the presence of multi-agent interactions and non-stationarity.

3. **Ensuring Accurate Value Gradient Approximations for Policy Learning:**
 - **Motivation:** The performance of continuous-time control policies is critically dependent on the accuracy of both the value function and, more specifically, its gradient (∇_xV(x)). Existing PINN-based methods, relying solely on PDE residual losses, do not consistently guarantee precise value gradients. In multi-agent systems, where policies are often derived from value gradients (e.g., through an advantage function), inaccurate gradients propagate errors, degrade the value learning process, and ultimately lead to suboptimal or unstable policy optimization.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a novel mechanism, the Value Gradient Iteration (VGI) module, that explicitly refines and ensures the consistency of value gradient approximations. By iteratively propagating and correcting gradient estimates along sampled trajectories, VGI aims to provide a trajectory-aligned correction signal that complements global PDE constraints, leading to more accurate value approximations and stronger policy learning.

In summary, the key objective is to propose a comprehensive CT-MARL framework that can effectively and stably learn continuous-time policies in complex multi-agent cooperative settings by combining PINNs with a novel value gradient refinement mechanism, thereby overcoming the limitations of current DTRL and single-agent CTRL approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Value Iteration via PINN (VIP), establishes a continuous-time actor-critic framework for solving cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning problems within deterministic systems. It leverages Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to approximate value functions and introduces a novel Value Gradient Iteration (VGI) module to enhance the accuracy of value gradient learning.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as a continuous-time multi-agent cooperative control system, M = (X, {U_i}_N_{i=1}, N, f, r, {t_k}_{k≥0}, ρ). Here, X is the global state space, U is the joint action space for N agents, f(x, u) represents the time-invariant nonlinear global dynamics (˙x = f(x, u)), r(x, u) is the shared global reward function, {t_k} represents a sequence of decision times with variable gaps, and ρ is the discount factor. The objective is to find a decentralized joint policy π = (π_1..., π_N) that maximizes the infinite-horizon discounted return, defined by the optimal global value function V(x). The framework operates under a centralized training, decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm.

#### 3.2. HJB and Policy Learning

VIP departs from discrete-time Bellman equations and instead relies on Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) partial differential equations (PDEs) to characterize the optimal value function.

- **Critic Learning with HJB:**
 - A value network, V_θ, parameterized by θ, approximates the optimal value function.
 - The HJB equation, −ρV(x) + ∇_xV(x)⊤f(x, u\*) + r(x, u\*) = 0, where u\* is the optimal control maximizing the Hamiltonian H = ∇_xV(x)⊤f(x, u) + r(x, u), serves as the theoretical basis.
 - PINNs are employed to solve this HJB PDE by minimizing a PDE residual loss: L_res = ||R_θ(x_t)||^2. The residual R_θ(x_t) is defined as −ρV_θ(x_t) + ∇_xt V_θ(x_t)⊤f(x_t, u_t) + r(x_t, u_t). This loss enforces that the approximated value function adheres to the underlying physics encoded by the HJB equation.

- **Policy Learning:**
 - An actor network generates control inputs for each agent, replacing the need for analytical optimal control laws.
 - A continuous-time advantage function, A_θ(x_t, u_t), is derived directly from the HJB residual: A_θ(x_t, u_t) = −ρV_θ(x_t) + ∇_xt V_θ(x_t)⊤f(x_t, u_t) + r(x_t, u_t). This function quantifies the advantage of a given action over the current value at a state.
 - Each agent's decentralized policy, π_ϕi(u_i | x_t), is optimized by minimizing a negative expected advantage loss: L_pi = −A_θ(x_t, u_t) log π_ϕi(u_i | x_t). This ensures policy improvement towards maximizing long-term returns.

#### 3.3. Value Gradient Iteration (VGI) Module

To address the issue of potentially inaccurate value gradients from HJB residual loss alone, particularly in high-dimensional multi-agent systems where errors can be amplified, the VGI module is introduced.

- **VGI Gradient Estimator:** The VGI module explicitly propagates value gradients along sampled trajectories and refines their estimates. The VGI estimator for the value gradient at (x_t, u_t) is defined by: ∇_xt V(x_t) = ∇_xt r(x_t, u_t)Δt + e^(-ρΔt)∇_xt f(x_t, u_t)⊤∇_x(t+Δt) V(x_t+Δt). This equation represents a one-step unrolling of the Bellman equations in the space of gradients, providing a trajectory-aligned correction signal.
- **Convergence:** The VGI update rule is shown to be a contraction mapping under conditions of a bounded dynamics Jacobian and a sufficiently small timestep Δt, guaranteeing convergence to a unique fixed point.
- **VGI Loss:** The VGI module's loss function, L_vgi = ||∇_xt V_θ(x_t) − ˆg_t||^2, minimizes the mean squared error between the automatic derivative of the PINN critic V_θ(x_t) and the VGI-generated target gradient ˆg_t. This ensures consistency between the network's computed gradients and the trajectory-derived gradient estimates.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Dynamics and Reward Models:** Instead of directly learning continuous-time dynamics ˙x = f(x, u), neural networks f_ψ and r_ϕ are trained to predict the next state x_t+Δt (L_dyn = ||f_ψ(x_t, u_t) − x_t+Δt||^2) and instantaneous reward r_t (L_rew = ||r_ϕ(x_t, u_t) − r_t||^2), respectively, from observed discrete-time transitions. The continuous-time derivative ˙x is then recovered by finite differences.
- **Anchor Loss for Critic Network:** An additional TD-style anchor loss, L_anchor = ||V_θ(x_t) − R_t||^2, is introduced to enhance the stability and accuracy of value learning. R_t = r(x_t, u_t)Δt + e^(-ρΔt)V_θ(x_t+Δt) represents a one-step return. This loss provides direct supervision on the value function itself, complementing the gradient-focused HJB residual loss, especially in infinite-horizon settings without explicit terminal conditions.
- **Overall Critic Objective:** The total critic objective combines these losses: L_total = L_res + λ_anchor L_anchor + λ_g L_vgi, where λ_anchor and λ_g are tunable weights balancing the PDE residuals, value-bootstrap anchoring, and gradient consistency. The reward model, dynamics model, and value network are trained jointly.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed Value Iteration via PINN (VIP) method against several baselines across continuous-time variants of standard multi-agent reinforcement learning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks:** The evaluation used continuous-time versions of the Multi-Agent Particle Environment (MPE) (cooperative navigation, predator-prey) and Multi-Agent MuJoCo (Ant 2×4, Ant 2×4d, Ant 4×2, Walker, Swimmer, HalfCheetah). A didactic coupled oscillator environment was also used for detailed analysis of value and gradient approximations against known ground truth.
- **Baselines:** VIP was compared against four continuous-time policy iteration baselines: CT-MBRL (ODE), Differential Policy Iteration (DPI), Integral Policy Iteration (IPI), and HJBPPO. An ablated version of VIP without the VGI module was also tested. For comparison with discrete-time methods, MATD3, MAPPO, and MADDPG were included.
- **Evaluation Questions:** The experiments aimed to assess VIP's overall efficacy, the specific contribution of the VGI module, the impact of PINN design choices (activation function, loss weighting), robustness to time discretization, and performance relative to discrete-time baselines.

#### 4.2. Results Analysis

- **Model Performance:**
 - VIP with the VGI module consistently demonstrated faster convergence and achieved higher cumulative rewards compared to all other continuous-time baselines across both MPE and MuJoCo environments (Figures 2 and 4). This supports the efficacy of integrating PINNs with the proposed learning framework.
 - The didactic coupled oscillator environment (Figure 3) showed that VIP with VGI produced value and value gradient contours that closely matched the analytical LQR ground truth, both in structure and scale. In contrast, VIP without VGI yielded significantly biased and distorted approximations.
 - An ablation study (Figure 6) of the critic loss terms (HJB residual, anchor, VGI) further underscored the critical role of each component, with the full VIP configuration demonstrating superior performance (e.g., minimum distance to prey).

- **VGI Ablation:**
 - Removing the VGI module from VIP consistently led to significantly lower cumulative rewards across all experiments (Figures 2 and 4). This finding highlights the importance of accurate value gradient refinement for effective PINN training and subsequent policy improvement in multi-agent systems, aligning with prior research.

- **PINN Design Choice:**
 - **Activation Function:** Experiments comparing ReLU and Tanh activation functions in the value network showed that Tanh consistently achieved higher accumulated rewards and more stable training than ReLU (Figures 5 and 11). This is attributed to Tanh's smoothness and differentiability, which support stable and accurate gradient flow necessary for PDE solving with PINNs. ReLU's propensity for zero-gradient regions or gradient explosion was observed to degrade learning.
 - **Loss Term Weighting:** Optimal performance was achieved when the weights for the HJB residual, anchor loss, and VGI consistency loss were properly balanced. Imbalanced weighting was found to introduce stiffness into the training dynamics of PINNs, causing dominant loss terms to converge faster but leading to overall poorer value approximations (Figure 7).

- **Time Discretization Impact:**
 - VIP demonstrated robustness to varying time intervals. In the didactic environment, VIP maintained a nearly constant average return across different integration steps, whereas the discrete-time MADDPG baseline showed significant performance degradation as the time interval increased (Figure 8). This highlights VIP's advantage in scenarios with irregular or unfixed decision frequencies.

- **Comparison with Discrete-Time Baselines:**
 - VIP outperformed widely used discrete-time MARL baselines (MATD3, MADDPG, MAPPO) in multi-agent MuJoCo tasks (Figure 9). The performance of DTRL methods deteriorated substantially in continuous-time environments. This is consistent with the understanding that DTRL's fixed-timestep assumptions lead to biased or unstable value estimates and amplified approximation errors when the underlying system evolves continuously with arbitrary time intervals.

- **Robustness to Noise (Appendix C.3):**
 - VIP maintained strong performance under no or low levels of stochastic noise injected into the dynamics. Performance degraded noticeably with mid and high noise levels, which is expected as the method is designed for deterministic continuous-time systems and does not explicitly model diffusion terms.

- **Sensitivity Check for Δt (Appendix C.4):**
 - VIP remained stable and achieved strong returns for sufficiently small step sizes. As Δt increased beyond a task-dependent threshold, performance degraded due to increasing inaccuracies in the first-order discretization, eventually violating contraction conditions and leading to sharp performance drops for very large Δt.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning, particularly for systems operating in continuous time. The proposed Value Iteration via PINN (VIP) framework offers a principled approach to tackle complex, high-dimensional cooperative multi-agent problems where traditional discrete-time methods struggle or continuous-time approaches have been limited.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to effectively integrate Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) with an actor-critic framework for continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning (CT-MARL). By leveraging PINNs, the framework addresses the long-standing challenge of the curse of dimensionality in solving Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, which are fundamental to continuous-time optimal control. This opens avenues for applying model-based RL to more complex and higher-dimensional multi-agent systems than previously feasible.

A notable contribution is the introduction of the Value Gradient Iteration (VGI) module. This innovation directly addresses a critical limitation of PINN-based HJB solvers in multi-agent contexts: the accurate approximation of value gradients. The empirical evidence demonstrates that VGI's iterative refinement of value gradients along trajectories leads to more precise value approximations and more stable and effective policy learning. This mechanism is crucial for mitigating the amplification of gradient errors caused by coupled dynamics in multi-agent settings, thereby enhancing the overall stability and performance of the learning process.

The empirical validation across continuous-time variants of standard multi-agent benchmarks, including MPE and MuJoCo, consistently shows that VIP converges faster and achieves higher accumulated rewards compared to existing continuous-time RL baselines. Furthermore, the demonstrated robustness to irregular time intervals highlights VIP's practical utility in real-world scenarios where decision-making frequencies may not be fixed. The comparison with discrete-time MARL baselines reinforces the limitations of time discretization in inherently continuous environments.

The findings regarding PINN design choices, such as the preference for Tanh over ReLU activation functions for stable gradient flow and the importance of balanced loss term weighting, provide valuable insights for future research and implementation of physics-informed neural networks in control problems.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for applications in domains such as robotics, autonomous systems, and process control, where agents must interact in continuous environments requiring fine-grained or asynchronous control. By providing a more robust and performant CT-MARL framework, this research can facilitate the development of more sophisticated and efficient intelligent multi-agent systems.

For future work, the authors identify the extension of VIP to HJI-based formulations. This would enable the framework to address competitive or mixed-motive multi-agent scenarios, moving beyond the current focus on cooperative settings and significantly broadening its applicability.
