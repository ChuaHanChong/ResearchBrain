# A Survey of Large Language Model-Powered Spatial Intelligence Across Scales: Advances in Embodied Agents, Smart Cities, and Earth Science

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey from Tsinghua University examines how large language models enable spatial intelligence across three scales - embodied agents, smart cities, and Earth science applications - while establishing connections between cognitive science principles and practical implementations through a unified analytical framework.

## Problem

- Research on LLM-powered spatial intelligence is fragmented across different domains and scales
- Lack of unified framework connecting cognitive foundations with practical applications
- Need for better understanding of LLMs' capabilities and limitations in spatial tasks

## Method

- Developed ==taxonomy== categorizing spatial intelligence into foundational capabilities and real-world applications
- Created analytical framework connecting ==cognitive science principles== to LLM implementations
- Synthesized findings across ==embodied AI==, urban intelligence, and Earth science domains

## Results

- Mapped relationships between human spatial cognition and LLM capabilities
- Identified key challenges including **topological reasoning errors** and lack of true spatial cognition
- Outlined future research directions emphasizing robust evaluation frameworks, with demonstrated applications across robotics, urban planning, and environmental monitoring

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs show promise in spatial memory and reasoning but still face challenges like hallucination
> - Interdisciplinary integration between cognitive science and AI is crucial for advancement
> - Current evaluation metrics are insufficient for assessing spatial intelligence holistically

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving research areas:

- **Large Language Models (LLMs):** The paper is motivated by the recent advancements in LLMs and their potential to revolutionize various domains beyond natural language processing.

- **Spatial Intelligence:** This field encompasses the ability to understand, reason about, and interact with spatial environments. It's traditionally studied in cognitive science, neuroscience, and AI.

- **Embodied AI:** This area focuses on developing intelligent agents that can interact with the physical world, often involving robotics, computer vision, and natural language understanding.

- **Smart Cities/Urban Informatics:** This interdisciplinary field uses data and technology to improve urban planning, infrastructure management, and the quality of life in cities.

- **Earth Science Informatics:** This field focuses on using AI and machine learning to analyze large-scale geospatial data for climate modeling, environmental monitoring, and resource management.

The paper addresses a gap in the literature by providing a unified framework for understanding spatial intelligence across different scales and disciplines. Existing studies often focus on specific applications (e.g., embodied navigation) without considering the broader connections and common principles. This survey aims to bridge this gap by synthesizing insights from cognitive science, AI, and applied domains. By doing so, this paper contributes to the development of more comprehensive and generalizable spatial intelligence models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this survey paper are:

- **Establish a Unified Framework:** To create a structured analytical framework for understanding spatial intelligence across diverse disciplines and scales, progressing from spatial memory and perception to reasoning and higher-level intelligence.

- **Synthesize Existing Literature:** To comprehensively review and synthesize the existing research on LLM applications in spatial intelligence across multiple fields, focusing on spatial memory, knowledge representation, and spatial reasoning within LLMs.

- **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** To explore key challenges and open questions in interdisciplinary spatial intelligence research, highlighting connections between embodied, urban, and global-scale intelligence and outlining promising areas for future exploration.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from:

- **The rapid advancement of LLMs:** The authors recognize the transformative potential of LLMs in spatial intelligence but also acknowledge the need for a deeper understanding of their capabilities and limitations.

- **The fragmented nature of current research:** They observe that existing studies tend to be siloed within specific domains, hindering the development of a more holistic and integrated approach to spatial intelligence.

- **The importance of interdisciplinary research:** They emphasize the need to bridge the gap between cognitive science, AI, and applied domains to advance the field of spatial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper adopts a survey-based methodology, involving a comprehensive review and synthesis of existing literature. The authors' approach can be characterized as:

- **Interdisciplinary:** Drawing insights from cognitive science, neuroscience, AI, urban planning, and Earth science.

- **Taxonomy-driven:** Developing a taxonomy of spatial intelligence in LLMs, encompassing foundational capabilities (spatial memory, knowledge, and reasoning) and real-world applications (embodied, urban, and Earth intelligence).

- **Comparative:** Analyzing the differences and connections between spatial intelligence across different scales and application domains.

- **Critical:** Identifying limitations and challenges in current research and suggesting directions for future work.

The survey is structured around the following key components:

- **Background on Human Spatial Cognition:** Reviewing human spatial intelligence research from neuroscience and cognitive science, emphasizing the role of cognitive maps and spatial schemas.

- **Taxonomy of Spatial Intelligence in LLMs:** Proposing a taxonomy that divides spatial intelligence into foundational capabilities and real-world applications.

- **Analysis of Foundational Capabilities:** Examining spatial memory, knowledge representation, and abstract spatial reasoning in LLMs.

- **Review of Real-World Applications:** Surveying the use of LLMs in embodied intelligence, urban intelligence, and Earth intelligence, highlighting specific tasks and challenges.

- **Discussion of Challenges and Future Directions:** Identifying key limitations and proposing avenues for future research, including the need for more robust evaluation metrics and interdisciplinary collaboration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey reveals several key findings:

- **LLMs show promise in spatial intelligence:** LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in spatial memory, knowledge representation, and reasoning, enabling them to perform tasks such as navigation, urban planning, and remote sensing analysis.

- **LLMs have limitations:** Despite their advancements, LLMs still face challenges in spatial intelligence, including hallucination, topological reasoning errors, and a lack of genuine spatial cognitive abilities.

- **Spatial intelligence research is fragmented:** Existing studies tend to focus on specific domains without integrating insights across disciplines and scales.

- **Interdisciplinary research is crucial:** Bridging the gap between cognitive science, AI, and applied domains is essential for advancing the field of spatial intelligence.

- **Evaluation metrics are lacking:** Current evaluation frameworks often focus on specific tasks or domains, hindering the development of a unified approach to assess spatial intelligence across diverse contexts.
