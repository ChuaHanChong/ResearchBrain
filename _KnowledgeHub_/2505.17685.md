# FutureSightDrive: Thinking Visually with Spatio-Temporal CoT for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> FutureSightDrive (FSDrive) introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in autonomous driving that generates a visual spatio-temporal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) as a unified future image. This approach, which incorporates physically plausible future scenarios, improves trajectory planning accuracy and significantly reduces collision rates.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM-based autonomous driving systems rely on textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), leading to lossy compression of visual information and a 'modality gap' between perception and action planning.
- Textual CoT fails to adequately capture critical spatio-temporal relationships and fine-grained visual details essential for safe and anticipatory driving.
- Activating visual generation capabilities in existing MLLMs for world modeling typically requires complex architectures, extensive training data, or sacrifices semantic understanding.

## Method

- The framework enables the VLA model to act as a ==world model==, generating a unified ==visual future frame (spatio-temporal CoT)== that includes predicted background and explicit physical priors like future lane dividers and 3D object bounding boxes.
- The same VLA model then functions as an ==inverse dynamics model==, planning a future trajectory autoregressively by conditioning on current observations and the generated visual CoT.
- A unified pre-training paradigm expands the MLLM's vocabulary with ==VQ-VAE image tokens== and combines Visual Question Answering (VQA) with progressive, 'easy-to-hard' future image generation to efficiently activate visual generation while preserving semantic understanding.

## Results

- FSDrive achieved state-of-the-art performance on the nuScenes dataset for end-to-end trajectory planning, with an average L2 displacement error of **0.28m** and a collision rate of **0.10%** using ST-P3 metrics.
- The framework generated future frames with a competitive Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of **10.1** on nuScenes, performing comparably to specialized diffusion models despite utilizing a smaller **2B** parameter model.
- FSDrive scored **0.57** on the DriveLM GVQA benchmark for scene understanding, outperforming prior methods like Cube-LLM (**0.50**) and OmniDrive (**0.56**), validating its unified pre-training for both generation and understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An image-based spatio-temporal CoT is more effective for autonomous driving reasoning than textual CoT, as it better preserves critical spatio-temporal relationships and fine-grained visual details, mimicking human cognitive processes.
> - Integrating explicit physical priors (lane dividers, 3D object boxes) directly into the generated visual future frames significantly enhances the physical plausibility and safety of the imagined scenarios.
> - Existing MLLMs can be efficiently empowered with visual generation capabilities for world modeling through minimal architectural changes, vocabulary expansion, and a unified, progressive pre-training strategy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of autonomous driving, specifically focusing on the integration of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and the development of sophisticated reasoning mechanisms.

**Shift towards End-to-End Autonomous Driving:** Traditionally, autonomous driving systems have relied on a modular pipeline: perception, prediction, planning, and control. While robust and interpretable, this approach suffers from error propagation and information loss between modules. The advent of MLLMs has spurred a significant interest in end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving, where models directly map sensor observations and high-level instructions to vehicle control actions. This simplifies architecture, allows for holistic optimization, and leverages vast pre-trained knowledge. FSDrive aligns with this E2E paradigm, aiming to directly generate trajectories from visual inputs.

**Limitations of Textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in MLLMs:** Many contemporary MLLM-based autonomous driving systems (e.g., DriveGPT4, EMMA) employ Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to enhance reasoning. However, these often rely on *textual* CoT, converting rich visual information into symbolic language descriptions (e.g., bounding box coordinates, natural language scene descriptions). The paper identifies this as a "lossy compression" that creates a "modality gap" between visual perception and action planning. It blurs spatio-temporal relationships and discards fine-grained visual details, which are critical for safe and anticipatory driving. FSDrive directly tackles this fundamental limitation by proposing a visual alternative.

**Role of World Models in Autonomous Driving:** World models, which predict future states of the environment, are increasingly recognized as vital for anticipatory planning. Existing world models in autonomous driving often focus on scenario generation (often leveraging diffusion models) or representation learning. Many, like GAIA-1 or DrivingGPT, use VQ-VAE-based visual tokens which can sometimes lack semantic information, potentially degrading performance in downstream understanding tasks. FSDrive contributes to this area by developing a VLA that functions as an explicit world model, generating unified *visual* future frames, and importantly, integrating physical priors directly into this visual representation.

**Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation:** A significant trend in AI is the development of single foundation models capable of both understanding (e.g., Visual Question Answering - VQA) and generating (e.g., text-to-image) different modalities. Previous attempts to unify understanding and generation in MLLMs often faced challenges: VQ-VAE-based methods struggled with semantic preservation, while ViT-based methods often required external diffusion models or massive datasets, leading to high computational costs and complex architectures. FSDrive introduces an innovative and efficient approach to activate visual generation capabilities in *existing* MLLMs with minimal architectural changes and significantly less training data, thereby making unified multimodal models more accessible and practical for autonomous driving applications. This positions FSDrive as a pragmatic advancement in the field by showing how to empower readily available MLLMs with a crucial new capability for AD.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind FSDrive stems from an observation about human cognition and a critical limitation in current MLLM-based autonomous driving systems:

**Motivation (Human Cognition Analogy):** Human drivers, when anticipating future scenarios, mentally construct visual representations of potential developments rather than converting them into linguistic descriptions for reasoning. This intuitive, direct visual thinking guides their planning. FSDrive seeks to emulate this cognitive process in autonomous vehicles.

**Addressing the Modality Gap of Textual CoT:** The primary objective is to overcome the inherent limitations of textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving. The paper argues that converting rich, continuous visual data into discrete, symbolic textual representations (e.g., bounding box coordinates or descriptive sentences) results in "lossy compression," obscures critical spatio-temporal relationships, discards fine-grained visual details, and creates a "modality gap" between perception and planning.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Introduce a Novel Visual Spatio-Temporal CoT:** Develop a reasoning mechanism that allows VLA models to "think visually" by generating a unified image representation of the future world state. This visual CoT should intrinsically capture both spatial structure and temporal evolution, thereby bridging the perception-planning gap.
2. **Empower VLAs as World Models:** Enable VLAs to function as effective world models by predicting future visual frames. These predicted frames must incorporate explicit, physically plausible priors (e.g., future lane dividers, 3D object boxes) to ensure realistic and safe future scene imagination.
3. **Develop a Unified Pre-training Paradigm:** Create an efficient pre-training methodology that simultaneously preserves the semantic understanding capabilities of existing MLLMs (via VQA) while activating their visual generation capabilities (for future frame prediction). This paradigm aims to be data-efficient and avoid complex architectural modifications.
4. **Implement Progressive Visual Generation:** Introduce a progressive, easy-to-hard curriculum for future scene generation during pre-training. This approach first generates coarse-grained structural priors to enforce physical laws before rendering the full, detailed future scene, ensuring physical consistency.
5. **Enhance Autonomous Driving Performance:** Demonstrate that this visual spatio-temporal CoT leads to improved trajectory planning accuracy, reduced collision rates, and better scene understanding, ultimately contributing to safer and more anticipatory autonomous driving systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FSDrive proposes a novel framework that empowers Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to "think visually" using a spatio-temporal Chain-of-Thought (CoT). The methodology is structured around a dual role for the VLA model and a carefully designed unified pre-training paradigm.

**Overall Framework (FSDrive):**
FSDrive takes current surround-view images and task instructions as input. The MLLM, trained for next-token prediction, first predicts a future spatio-temporal CoT. Subsequently, it generates a trajectory based on current observations and this predicted visual future.

**Dual Role of the VLA Model:**
1. **World Model:** The VLA first acts as a world model to generate a "unified future frame," which serves as the visual spatio-temporal CoT. This imagined scene combines:
 - A predicted background: Capturing the dynamic evolution of visual content and intrinsic scene development laws.
 - Explicit, physically plausible priors: Future lane dividers (rendered as red lines) and 3D object bounding boxes, explicitly overlaid on the predicted frame. These coarse-grained visual cues direct the model’s attention to drivable areas and critical objects, enforcing static and dynamic physical constraints. The visual CoT thus represents both spatial structure and temporal evolution in a single, image-based representation.
2. **Inverse Dynamics Model:** The *same* VLA then functions as an inverse dynamics model. Conditioned on the current observations and the generated visual spatio-temporal CoT, it plans a future trajectory autoregressively. This involves predicting a sequence of waypoints, directly influenced by the visually imagined future.

**Unified Pre-training Paradigm for Visual Generation and Understanding:**
To equip existing MLLMs with visual generation capabilities while preserving their semantic understanding, FSDrive employs a unique pre-training strategy:
1. **MLLM Foundation:** The method is built upon any existing MLLM that utilizes ViT-based encoders for continuous feature extraction (e.g., Qwen2-VL, LLaVA), without altering the core architecture.
2. **Vocabulary Expansion:** The crucial step is expanding the MLLM's existing text vocabulary by incorporating discrete image tokens from a VQ-VAE. This creates a unified multimodal vocabulary, allowing the MLLM to predict image tokens, which are then converted back to pixels by the VQ-VAE's detokenizer.
3. **Semantic Understanding Preservation:** To maintain the MLLM’s existing understanding capabilities, a Visual Question Answering (VQA) task (using data like OmniDrive-nuScenes) is integrated into pre-training. This ensures the model can analyze complex driving scenarios through language.
4. **Visual Generation Activation:** To enable future perception, the VLA is trained to predict future front-view frames autoregressively (Equation 4). This leverages abundant unlabeled video data (e.g., from nuScenes).

**Progressive Image Generation (during pre-training):**
Recognizing that directly generating detailed future scenes can lead to physically implausible results, FSDrive introduces a progressive, "easy-to-hard" generation method during pre-training:
1. **Structural Priors:** The VLA first infers and generates coarse-grained visual tokens representing future lane dividers (road skeleton, drivable areas) and 3D bounding boxes (key object motions). These explicit visual priors enforce static and dynamic physical constraints, respectively.
2. **Full Scene Rendering:** Only after generating these physically constrained priors are the full, detailed future frames rendered, conditioned on these intermediate visual reasoning steps (Equation 5). This teaches the model to think visually about dynamic scene evolution under physical laws.

**Training Strategy:**
The training process is divided into two stages:
1. **Stage 1: Unified Pre-training:** The model (initialized from existing MLLMs like Qwen2-VL-2B) is jointly trained on VQA tasks and future frame prediction. This stage uses data from OmniDrive-nuScenes for VQA and both unlabeled and annotated nuScenes data for progressive future frame, lane divider, and 3D detection prediction.
2. **Stage 2: Supervised Fine-tuning:** The pre-trained model is fine-tuned for specific autonomous driving tasks. This includes scene understanding using the DriveLM GVQA dataset and trajectory planning on nuScenes, where FSDrive’s spatio-temporal CoT (the unified future frame with priors) is incorporated as an intermediate reasoning step. All these tasks are trained simultaneously, allowing task-specific predictions via different prompts during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FSDrive demonstrates significant advancements across trajectory planning, future frames generation, and scene understanding, validating the effectiveness of its visual spatio-temporal CoT and unified pre-training paradigm.

**End-to-End Trajectory Planning:**
- **nuScenes Performance:** FSDrive achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on the nuScenes dataset. When using ego status (optional navigation commands and vehicle state), it surpassed previous SOTA methods across both ST-P3 and UniAD metrics (L2 displacement error and collision rate). For example, with ego status, FSDrive (Qwen2-VL-2B) achieved an average L2 of 0.28m and collision rate of 0.10% (ST-P3 metrics), and an average L2 of 0.45m and collision rate of 0.16% (UniAD metrics), outperforming contenders like EMMA* (0.32m L2, no collision reported for ST-P3) and UniAD* (0.46m L2, 0.37% collision for UniAD metrics).
- **Non-Ego Status Performance:** Even without ego status (a more challenging setting), FSDrive showed superior effectiveness compared to other autoregressive methods. It notably outperformed Doe-1, another vision-generation MLLM-based approach, demonstrating the limitations of VQ-VAE-based discrete visual features for understanding in Doe-1.
- **MLLM Agnosticism:** FSDrive's competitive performance when initialized with LLaVA-7B also indicates its broad applicability across different MLLM backbones.

- **NAVSIM Performance:** On the NAVSIM dataset, designed for challenging real-world planning scenarios, FSDrive achieved a PDMS (Planning-Driving Metric Score) of 85.1. This result surpassed prior camera-only methods such as LAW (84.6) and DiffusionDrive-Cam (83.6), highlighting its efficacy in pseudo closed-loop evaluation.

**Future Frames Generation:**
- **Competitive FID:** Despite being an autoregressive model, FSDrive achieved a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 10.1 on nuScenes for future frame generation. This is highly competitive with specialized diffusion models like GEM (10.5) and Drive-WM (15.4), especially considering FSDrive’s use of a smaller model (2B) and lower resolution for real-time driving.
- **Efficiency:** FSDrive's superior performance compared to Doe-1 (which uses the larger Lumina-mGPT 7B) further indicates that its method effectively unlocks visual generation capabilities with minimal data and model size.

**Scene Understanding:**
- **DriveLM GVQA Benchmark:** FSDrive achieved a final score of 0.57 on the DriveLM GVQA benchmark for scene understanding. This result outperformed recent methods like Cube-LLM (0.50) and OmniDrive (0.56), underscoring the effectiveness of FSDrive's unified pre-training paradigm for both generation and understanding.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Pre-training Paradigm:** Ablations confirmed the critical role of the unified pre-training. Pure VQA had negligible impact on planning. Future frame generation alone significantly improved L2 by 16.4% and collision rates by 15.8%. Including 3D detection and lane divider pre-training yielded further moderate gains. The full combined understanding and generation pre-training achieved the best performance (L2: 0.98, Collision: 0.58), validating its ability to enhance scene representation and learn physical laws.
- **Chain-of-Thought Variants:** The proposed spatio-temporal CoT showed significant improvements in collision rate (31% reduction compared to no CoT, from 0.58% to 0.40%). This was markedly better than pure textual CoT (8.6% improvement, to 0.53%) and image-text CoT (negligible additional gains over text CoT), demonstrating that unified, image-based reasoning is superior for identifying future collision risks. L2 changes were marginal across CoT variants, suggesting CoT primarily impacts safety by reducing risky situations rather than merely refining trajectories.
- **Progressive Generation:** Ablations on future frames generation confirmed that the progressive generation method (generating priors before full scenes) significantly improved FID (from 12.7 to 10.1), highlighting its role in enhancing autoregressive image generation quality and physical plausibility.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- Qualitative examples demonstrated FSDrive's ability to mitigate instruction errors through observation-based trajectory planning and future prediction when presented with incorrect instructions, showcasing its inverse dynamics modeling capability. This highlights the robustness conferred by visual CoT.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FSDrive represents a substantial advancement in autonomous driving research, particularly in how Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models reason and interact with dynamic environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Bridging the Perception-Planning Gap with Visual Reasoning:**
The most profound impact of FSDrive is its successful demonstration of a visual spatio-temporal CoT that effectively bridges the long-standing "modality gap" between perception and planning. By moving beyond symbolic, textual representations, FSDrive enables VLAs to "think visually," mimicking human cognitive processes for anticipatory reasoning. This fundamental shift from text-based to image-based intermediate reasoning is crucial for tasks requiring rich spatio-temporal understanding and fine-grained visual details, which are paramount in complex driving scenarios.

**2. Enhanced Safety and Anticipatory Driving:**
The improved trajectory accuracy and significantly reduced collision rates demonstrated by FSDrive translate directly into safer and more anticipatory autonomous driving systems. By imagining future scenarios with physically plausible lane dividers and 3D object boxes, the vehicle can better foresee potential risks and plan more robust, collision-free paths. This directly addresses one of the most critical challenges in deploying autonomous vehicles: safety assurance.

**3. Efficient Activation of Visual Generation in Existing MLLMs:**
FSDrive introduces a computationally efficient and architecturally lean method to endow existing MLLMs with visual generation capabilities. By expanding the MLLM's vocabulary with VQ-VAE image tokens and employing minimal training data, the framework avoids the extensive computational costs and complex architectural redesigns often associated with training large multimodal generative models from scratch. This makes the integration of sophisticated visual world models more accessible and practical for a wider range of autonomous driving researchers and developers.

**4. Advancements in Unified Multimodal Learning:**
The unified pre-training paradigm, combining VQA for understanding and progressive visual generation for future prediction, significantly contributes to the field of multimodal AI. It shows that MLLMs can simultaneously excel at complex semantic understanding and high-fidelity visual imagination, leading to better overall scene representation and comprehension, as evidenced by FSDrive's strong performance on DriveLM.

**5. Robustness to Imperfect Information:**
The qualitative results show that FSDrive’s observation-based planning, guided by the visual CoT, can mitigate errors even when provided with incorrect instructions. This suggests a higher degree of robustness and adaptability, allowing the system to rely more on its visual understanding of the world when explicit instructions might be ambiguous or erroneous.

**Potential Future Directions and Broader Impact:**
- **Surround-View Generation:** While current work focuses on front-view generation for real-time efficiency, extending the visual CoT to surround-view predictions would provide a more comprehensive environmental awareness, leading to even safer driving.
- **Improved Visual Quality:** Leveraging larger training datasets and more advanced unified paradigms could further enhance the robustness and fidelity of the generated visual CoT.
- **Ethical Implications:** As with all advancements in AI, especially in critical applications like autonomous driving, FSDrive’s capabilities necessitate careful consideration of ethical challenges. The ability of an AI to "imagine" future scenarios raises questions about accountability and interpretability. The development of safer, more efficient systems, as proposed by FSDrive, will need to go hand-in-hand with robust regulatory frameworks and continuous ethical scrutiny.
- **Industry Application:** The strong affiliation with Alibaba Group, through Amap and DAMO Academy, positions this research for significant real-world impact. The insights and technologies developed could be integrated into Amap's navigation and mapping services, accelerating the deployment of advanced autonomous driving features in commercial products.

In conclusion, FSDrive marks a pivotal step towards developing truly intelligent autonomous driving systems that can reason about their environment in a manner more akin to human drivers, ultimately promising a future of safer, more efficient, and more anticipatory self-driving vehicles.
