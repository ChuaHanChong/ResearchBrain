# DiffPhD: A Unified Differentiable Solver for Projective Heterogeneous Materials in Elastodynamics with Contact-Rich GPU-Acceleration

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffPhD is a GPU-accelerated differentiable Projective Dynamics solver for heterogeneous hyperelastic solids with frictional contact. It bakes stiffness-aware projective weights into the global matrix, lifts state-adaptive trust-region eigenvalue filtering onto the backward prox-map Hessian, and reuses one persistent sparse inverse factor across forward, backward and contact stages, staying stable at 100x stiffness contrast while running an order of magnitude faster.

## Problem

- Differentiable Projective Dynamics relies on a constant prefactorized global matrix that becomes ill-conditioned once Young's modulus varies 10x-100x across a mesh, stalling solves and corrupting gradients.
- Neo-Hookean energies are non-convex, and a fixed eigenvalue-projection choice (clamping or absolute value) is problem-dependent, so prior differentiable PD keeps gradient flow only in homogeneous corotated regimes.
- Existing tools cover single slices: the sparse-inverse contact solver is forward-only, and IPC-based differentiable solvers cost an order of magnitude more wall-clock than projective dynamics.

## Method

- ==Stiffness-aware projective assembly== bakes per-element Young's modulus into projective weights of the global system matrix, keeping heterogeneity structural rather than pushed into per-element local solves.
- ==Trust-region eigenvalue filtering== lifted onto the backward prox-map Hessian, with a state-adaptive per-element rule switching between absolute-value and clamping projections whose quadratic model reuses the forward PD matrix.
- Unified GPU local-global loop sharing one ==nested-dissection sparse-inverse factor== across forward, backward and frictional-contact stages, with bounded-history ==Type-II Anderson Acceleration== and a dual-gate convergence test.

## Results

- Achieves **8.69x** forward speed-up over prior differentiable PD on a **2.2M-DoF** heterogeneous crab and **33.31x** on dense codimensional cloth contact, where Cholesky and DiffPD baselines fail.
- Remains convergent up to **100x** stiffness contrast whereas prior PD diverges beyond roughly **50x**; slender-plant system identification reaches a residual loss **23.9x** smaller than the strongest baseline.
- Ablation shows GPU residency drives **2.3-2.5x** forward and **6.3x** backward gains, while the adaptive filter alone cuts bouncing-ball identification loss from **142.97** to **36.6**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding material heterogeneity structurally inside the prefactorized global matrix preserves the one-time-factorization amortization that makes projective dynamics fast, instead of burdening local solves.
> - Eigenvalue projection is a gradient-quality knob, not a speed knob: adaptive filtering reshapes the optimization landscape and reaches basins that fixed schemes never find.
> - Numerical failures surfaced only where heterogeneity, hyperelasticity and frictional contact intersect, so solvers stable on each property alone still need combined-regime validation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Differentiable simulation of soft bodies serves as a foundational component for various inverse problems in computer graphics and robotics, including system identification, trajectory optimization, and sim-to-real transfer. Projective Dynamics (PD) and its differentiable extension, DiffPD, have gained attention due to their computational advantages, specifically the amortization of linear system factorization across iterations. However, existing differentiable solvers encounter limitations when applied to complex, real-world scenarios.

The primary challenges in current differentiable soft-body simulation include:
- **Material Heterogeneity:** Real-world objects often exhibit significant stiffness contrasts within a single mesh, where Young's modulus can vary by orders of magnitude. This can lead to an ill-conditioned global stiffness matrix (A) in PD, causing iterative solvers to stall and gradients to become unreliable.
- **Hyperelasticity under Large Deformations:** Materials like flesh or rubber exhibit large, non-linear, and often volume-preserving deformations. Neo-Hookean energy, commonly used for such materials, introduces non-convexity, leading to indefinite Hessians in the local steps of solvers. The choice of eigenvalue projection (e.g., absolute-value vs. clamping) for stabilizing these Hessians is problem-dependent and can affect convergence.
- **Contact-Rich Interactions:** Simulations involving multiple bodies with frictional contact are common in robotics and character animation. Integrating these interactions into a differentiable pipeline often incurs high computational costs, particularly in the backward pass, as it requires repeated applications of the inverse global stiffness matrix (A⁻¹) against contact Jacobians.

Prior works address subsets of these challenges. For instance, FBA (Zeng et al., 2025) offers a GPU-accelerated forward solver with unified Signorini–Coulomb contact but is not differentiable. IPC-based differentiable solvers (Huang et al., 2024) provide end-to-end gradients for contact but with higher computational costs compared to PD. DiffPD (Du et al., 2021) primarily maintains gradient flow for homogeneous, corotated elastic models and often resorts to non-PD direct solvers for Neo-Hookean materials.

The central difficulty in combining these advances lies in integrating components, as individual solutions are often designed under assumptions that may be violated by other parts of a unified system. For example, trust-region filtering targets forward element Hessians, Anderson Acceleration assumes an autonomous fixed-point map, and sparse factors for A⁻¹ are typically forward-only. Resolving these conflicting assumptions while maintaining computational efficiency and gradient accuracy is a significant challenge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a unified, GPU-accelerated differentiable Projective Dynamics (PD) solver, named DiffPhD, capable of robustly simulating and optimizing systems involving heterogeneous, hyperelastic materials with complex, contact-rich interactions. This aims to overcome the combined limitations of existing differentiable solvers.

The motivation for this comprehensive approach stems from the demands of realistic applications in computer graphics and robotics:
- **Realistic Character Simulation:** Simulating characters with embedded bones (stiff) within soft tissue (compliant) requires handling extreme stiffness contrasts.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** Developing robotic grippers that can manipulate objects with spatially varying material properties necessitates accurate simulation of heterogeneous objects and their interactions.
- **Accurate Material Behavior:** Capturing the large, non-linear deformations characteristic of real-world materials like flesh or rubber requires hyperelastic models.
- **Gradient-Based Optimization:** For tasks such as system identification (determining material properties from observed motion), trajectory optimization (planning movements for soft robots), and Real2Sim transfer (calibrating simulations to real-world data), end-to-end gradient flow is essential. Current solvers often fail in these complex regimes, either due to numerical fragility (divergence) or prohibitive per-iteration computational costs.

DiffPhD is motivated by the need to enable end-to-end gradient-based optimization for scenarios previously considered intractable for differentiable solvers, such as composite creatures, soft characters interacting with stiff objects, and soft-gripper manipulation, all within a single, efficient forward-backward pass.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffPhD integrates three core technical contributions to address the challenges of heterogeneous, hyperelastic, and contact-rich elastodynamics within a unified, differentiable framework. The design centralizes around the Projective Dynamics (PD) global stiffness matrix (A), making its inversion efficient and robust.

#### 3.1. Heterogeneity via Stiffness-Aware Projective Assembly

The paper addresses material heterogeneity, where Young's modulus (E) can vary significantly across elements. Instead of introducing per-element material parameters into the local proximal operator (which can destabilize iterative solvers), DiffPhD routes heterogeneity primarily through the projective weights (`w_e`) in the global stiffness matrix A. The weights `w_e` are made proportional to the per-element Lamé parameter `μ_e` (`w_e ∝ μ_e`). This approach embeds stiffness contrast directly into A at assembly time (Eq. 31), ensuring A inherently captures the material distribution. The local proximal operator for Neo-Hookean materials (Eq. 14) consistently uses mesh-wide scalar means for Lamé parameters (`μ`, `λ`) and penalty (`k`), maintaining a uniform local map across elements. This design choice prevents Anderson Acceleration from amplifying local oscillations that would arise from element-varying local Hessians, preserving the global fixed-point contraction. Furthermore, the elastic force `f_ela(q)` is intentionally excluded from the PD right-hand side `b` to ensure the inverse of A remains robust, allowing iteration to resolve the nonlinear residual.

#### 3.2. Differentiable Hyperelasticity via Proximal-Map Trust-Region Filtering

DiffPhD enables stable Neo-Hookean gradients under large deformations by addressing the potential indefiniteness of the per-element prox-map Hessian (`H_prox_e`, Eq. 23). This indefiniteness can occur with high Poisson's ratios or large volume changes, leading to unstable backward gradients.
- **Backward Trust-Region Filtering:** A state-adaptive trust-region rule is applied *only* in the differentiable backward pass to modify `H_prox_e`. This prevents destabilization of the forward fixed-point iteration, which relies on an unmodified proximal map. The rule blends between the unprojected Hessian, eigenvalue clamping, and absolute-value filtering using a parameter `τ` (Eq. 24). The choice of `τ` is determined by a trust-region ratio (`ρ`, Eq. 28) calculated from the last converged PD increment, assessing the fidelity of A as a quadratic model. This decision is made per-element and per-iterate, targeting only problematic blocks. The quadratic model for the trust-region ratio reuses the constant PD matrix A, incurring zero additional cost.
- **Forward Stability:** To stabilize the forward iteration, DiffPhD employs Type-II Anderson Acceleration (AA) with a bounded history window (`m`). For heterogeneous meshes, `m` is set to 1 ("one-step momentum") to prevent AA from extrapolating per-element drift into mesh-level oscillations, a problem observed when `p*e` trajectories have widely varying curvatures. For homogeneous meshes, `m` is set to 5. A dual-gate convergence criterion (Eq. 22) combining step-norm and PD-residual checks prevents false convergence during contact-state transitions or at degenerate non-fixed-point energy states.
- **Stiffness-Amplified Rayleigh Damping:** To dissipate high-frequency ringing, particularly at stiff/soft interfaces, a Rayleigh-type damping force is added. This force includes per-element stiffness coefficients (`β_e`) proportional to `μ_e` (rather than inversely proportional). This choice allows aggressive damping of stiff regions, preventing ringing from propagating to soft regions while preserving soft-region dynamics. These damping terms are incorporated into A during assembly (Eq. 31) and incur no recurring per-step cost.

#### 3.3. Unified Contact-Rich GPU Forward/Backward Loop

The framework utilizes a GPU-accelerated sparse inverse factorization of A for all stages.
- **FBA Nested-Dissection Sparse Inverse:** The SPD matrix A is reordered using METIS nested dissection and then factorized into `A⁻¹ = SᵀS` (Eq. 32). The sparse factor pairs `(S, Sᵀ)` are stored in persistent GPU buffers.
- **Two-SpMV Evaluation:** Any `A⁻¹v` operation is efficiently performed as two sparse matrix-vector products (`Sᵀ(Sv)`, Eq. 33), providing an exact inverse application within floating-point precision without materializing a dense inverse.
- **Shared Factor Reuse:** Crucially, the same persistent `(S, Sᵀ)` factor pair is reused across:
 - The forward global step (`A q = b`).
 - The computation of the Delassus operator (`W = J A⁻¹ Jᵀ`) for contact, using two GPU sparse matrix-matrix products (SpMMs).
 - The differentiable backward adjoint system, where it is used to solve the `q`-block of the KKT system.
 Refactorization of A is only necessary when the topology, material properties, or damping coefficients change, significantly amortizing the cost over an entire trajectory.
- **Signorini–Coulomb Complementarity Contact:** DiffPhD integrates a non-smooth NCP formulation (Zeng et al., 2025) to handle unilateral contact, bilateral attachments, and Coulomb friction. This is solved via an iterative linearised NCP system (Eq. 36) whose `KxK` block is solved by dense LDLT. Position updates leverage the cached contact columns from the `A⁻¹` application.
- **Differentiable Backward Pass:** The adjoint method is used to propagate sensitivities. The core `q`-block of the KKT adjoint system (Eq. 38) is solved via the GPU two-SpMV using the persistent `(S, Sᵀ)` factor. The `λ`-block (for contact multipliers) is solved by dense LDLT. The local-step Jacobian (`∂b/∂q`, Eq. 42) incorporates the filtered prox-map Hessian `H_prox_e` from the trust-region mechanism, ensuring well-conditioned gradients.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DiffPhD's performance was evaluated across seventeen benchmarks, covering forward simulation stability, differentiable inverse problems (system identification, initial-state optimization, trajectory optimization), and real-world robotics transfer (Real2Sim). The core evaluations focused on heterogeneous, hyperelastic, and contact-rich scenarios where prior differentiable PD solvers typically fail or exhibit severe performance degradation.

#### 4.1. Heterogeneous Forward Simulation

- **Stiffness Contrast Handling:** On the Armadillo twist scene, DiffPhD provided stable results with stiffness contrasts up to 100x, whereas baseline DiffPD became unstable beyond approximately 50x.
- **Large-Scale Heterogeneous Bodies:** For the Crab benchmark (2.2M DoF, 10x stiffness contrast), DiffPhD achieved a forward speedup of 8.69x over DiffPD and 12.96x over MAS. Specialized forward-only solvers like Subspace MFEM struggled with contact.
- **Mesh-to-Mesh Contact:** The Gatorman scene, involving a stiff sword striking a soft sphere, demonstrated DiffPhD's ability to handle complex, transient mesh-to-mesh interactions and frictional contact without non-physical artifacts.

#### 4.2. Contact-Rich Forward Simulation

- **High-Density Codimensional Contact:** On the Napkin benchmark (50x50 mesh, up to 50% active contact), DiffPhD achieved backward pass speedups of up to 23.53x over DiffPD and 19.70x over MAS in heterogeneous configurations, demonstrating efficiency under high contact density.

#### 4.3. Differentiable Inverse Problems

- **System Identification:**
 - **Homogeneous Bouncing Ball:** DiffPhD matched the accuracy of prior solvers while maintaining efficiency, reaching a sharper loss minimum.
 - **Heterogeneous Bouncing Ball:** Successfully recovered six material parameters (Young's moduli and Poisson's ratios for three sub-regions with 3x-10x contrast) from a single trajectory, a task where baseline gradients typically desynchronized.
 - **Plant:** DiffPhD achieved a 23.9x lower residual loss compared to DiffPD and MAS on system identification for an articulated plant geometry, which typically causes baseline gradients to lose descent direction due to indefinite Hessians in slender elements.
- **Initial-State Optimization:**
 - **Heterogeneous Bunny:** DiffPhD converged to a final loss 3.05x lower than DiffPD and 2.83x lower than MAS, demonstrating robust gradient stability through bouncing contact transitions.
 - **Routing Tendon (Muscle Actuation):** DiffPhD converged to a stable controller with a final loss 457x lower than DiffPD and MAS, both of which failed to converge on this contact-free scene. This highlights the impact of the unified GPU pipeline on backward cost for muscle actuation Jacobians.
- **Trajectory Optimization:**
 - **Torus (Locomotion):** DiffPhD successfully optimized a peristaltic gait for locomotion, matching the performance of DiffPD for homogeneous contact-rich trajectory optimization.

#### 4.4. Real2Sim: Robot Manipulator

- **Extreme-Contrast Manipulation (Oreo):** DiffPhD demonstrated the capability to simulate a robot gripper closing on a multi-body stack with extreme stiffness contrasts (up to 10^8x), a scenario where DiffPD and MAS failed due to ill-conditioned PD assembly.
- **Contact-Location Recovery (Dice):** For a Real2Sim task of recovering contact location from a robotic poke, DiffPhD converged in 13 L-BFGS evaluations (approximately 13.5 minutes total wall-clock time), effectively closing the real-to-sim gap for contact behavior.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed that both GPU residency (unified local-global pipeline) and the state-adaptive trust-region filter are critical. GPU residency was identified as the primary driver for speedup (2.3-2.5x forward, 6.3x backward for Armadillo). While fixed projection schemes did not alter the optimization landscape compared to DiffPD, the adaptive trust-region filter enabled DiffPhD to reach distinct, often lower-loss, optima (e.g., 3.9x loss reduction on Bouncing Ball).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiffPhD represents a notable advancement in differentiable soft-body simulation by simultaneously addressing material heterogeneity, hyperelasticity under large deformations, and contact-rich interactions within a single, GPU-accelerated framework.

The significance of this work lies in:
- **Expanding Application Domains:** By providing a robust and efficient solver for previously challenging scenarios (e.g., materials with 100x stiffness contrasts, complex frictional contact), DiffPhD unlocks new possibilities for gradient-based optimization in computer graphics and robotics. This includes inverse design of composite materials, optimization of soft robot control, and accurate sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation tasks.
- **Improved Numerical Stability and Accuracy:** The careful integration of stiffness-aware projective assembly, state-adaptive trust-region filtering for the prox-map Hessian, and bounded Anderson Acceleration significantly enhances the numerical stability of both forward simulation and backward gradient computation, even in highly nonlinear and ill-conditioned regimes.
- **Substantial Computational Efficiency:** The unified GPU pipeline, leveraging a persistent sparse inverse factor (SᵀS) across forward, contact, and backward passes, yields order-of-magnitude speedups over prior differentiable solvers. This efficiency reduces the wall-clock time for complex optimization tasks from hours to minutes, making gradient-based approaches more practical.
- **Methodological Insights:** The work highlights that numerical failures in unified differentiable physics systems often arise from the intersection of properties rather than isolated components. It suggests a need for component-local invariants to diagnose subtle bugs, extending beyond aggregate metrics.
- **Generalizable Principles:** While specific to Projective Dynamics, the underlying principles—routing material structure into the global operator, adaptive per-element filtering of indefiniteness, and amortizing operator inversion across all solver stages—are proposed as general guidelines applicable to other differentiable physics frameworks (e.g., Newton-based, position-based dynamics).

DiffPhD provides a robust foundation for building high-fidelity digital twins whose behaviors are calibrated against real-world data, facilitating advancements in areas such as biomechanical modeling, soft robot design, and robotic manipulation.
