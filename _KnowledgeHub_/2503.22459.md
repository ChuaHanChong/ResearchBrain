# Control of Humanoid Robots with Parallel Mechanisms using Differential Actuation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework developed by researchers at Gepetto, LAAS-CNRS, and Inria introduces computationally efficient, analytically exact differential actuation models for parallel mechanisms in humanoid robots. This method enables precise control by accounting for non-linear transmissions, leading to the successful deployment of reinforcement learning policies on hardware and better utilization of the robot's full kinematic range.

## Problem

- Advanced humanoid robot hardware with serial-parallel mechanisms offers benefits but introduces complex closed-loop kinematics and non-linear transmissions.
- Conventional control algorithms and RL policies often rely on simplified serial models or constant-ratio approximations, limiting accuracy and full exploitation of hardware capabilities.
- Exact modeling of these mechanisms is computationally intensive, hindering real-time control and efficient derivative computation required by modern optimization algorithms.

## Method

- Develop a compact analytical formulation for common parallel mechanisms (planar and intricate four-bar linkages) to precisely map serial joint configurations to motor configurations.
- Derive the actuation Jacobian and its second-order derivatives, crucial for efficient derivative-based optimal control algorithms and accurate dynamic modeling.
- Implement a serial impedance transfer method and an efficient state estimator to bridge the gap between serial-space control policies and real-world parallel-actuated hardware.

## Results

- Trajectory optimization with the Actuated Serial Model enhanced performance in demanding tasks and enabled full exploitation of the robot's kinematic range by respecting true motor-space limits.
- Reinforcement Learning policies, trained on a serial model, were successfully deployed on a real Bipetto robot for robust omnidirectional walking, facilitated by the impedance transfer and state estimation.
- The state estimation and impedance transfer computations operate efficiently, completing in under 10 milliseconds for a 100 Hz control loop on a low-power onboard computer.
- The impedance transfer mechanism was shown to be critical for hardware stability, as direct torque transformations without it resulted in robot failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Analytically derived first and second-order derivatives for parallel mechanisms are computationally feasible and enable modern derivative-based control algorithms.
> - Directly modeling motor-space constraints allows controllers to fully exploit the robot's kinematic workspace, overcoming limitations of serial-space approximations.
> - A dynamic impedance transfer mechanism is essential for reliably deploying serial-model-trained RL policies onto physical robots with non-linear parallel transmissions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen advancements driven by new hardware designs, particularly the adoption of serial-parallel architectures in robot legs. These designs, exemplified by robots like Digit, H1, and G1, aim to reduce limb reflected inertia and enhance impact absorption, thereby enabling more dynamic and agile movements. However, these mechanical benefits introduce increased complexity in modeling and control due to the closed-loop kinematic chains and non-linear transmissions.

Traditional robot control frameworks, such as Whole-Body Model Predictive Control (WB-MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), typically rely on serial dynamical models and compute motions in joint space. When dealing with parallel mechanisms, several approaches have been explored in the literature:
1. **Exact Kinematic Modeling:** Directly accounting for closed-loop constraints and internal degrees of freedom. While these methods exist, their high algorithmic cost has historically limited real-time deployment, though recent advances are addressing this bottleneck.
2. **Dedicated Analytical Models:** Formulating specific analytical models for the transmission. These can reduce computational costs but are often design-specific and may not provide efficient derivatives required by modern optimization algorithms.
3. **Heuristic Models:** Using simplified models with equivalent dynamic properties.
4. **Proprietary Solutions:** Commercial robots often handle parallel mechanisms within undisclosed algorithmic layers, making it difficult for external researchers to integrate them into custom control pipelines.
5. **Constant Reduction Ratio Approximation:** A common simplification involves approximating the non-linear transmission as a constant reduction ratio. This approach reduces computational complexity but sacrifices accuracy and prevents full exploitation of the mechanism's capabilities.

For RL, integrating full robot dynamics with closed-loop mechanisms presents additional challenges, including the need for specialized GPU-accelerated simulators (e.g., Isaac Lab, MuJoCo MJX) and handling the sim-to-real gap arising from approximate contact models. Many current RL developments still rely on simplified serial models, which limit performance and transferability to real hardware.

This research addresses the gap between the mechanical advantages of serial-parallel robot designs and the computational demands of their precise control. It aims to provide a practical and efficient solution for accurately modeling and controlling these mechanisms without the computational burden of full constrained dynamics, nor the inaccuracy of constant-ratio approximations. By developing a compact analytical formulation for common parallel mechanisms and their derivatives, the authors seek to enable advanced control algorithms (WB-MPC and RL) to leverage these hardware designs effectively, ultimately improving control accuracy and robustness in dynamic humanoid locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to provide a comprehensive and computationally efficient solution for accurately accounting for serial-parallel robot designs within both Whole-Body Model Predictive Control (WB-MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) frameworks. This solution aims to integrate parallel actuation with minimal additional implementation complexity and algorithmic cost.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several factors:
- **Hardware Trends vs. Control Capabilities:** Recent advancements in bipedal locomotion hardware have increasingly adopted serial-parallel architectures in robot legs. These designs offer benefits such as reduced limb reflected inertia and improved impact absorption, facilitating more dynamic movements. However, the associated closed-loop kinematic constraints and non-linear transmissions introduce significant modeling and control complexities that are not readily handled by conventional control algorithms.
- **Limitations of Current Approximations:** A common practice in control and learning for these robots is to approximate the non-linear transmission ratio with a constant value. This simplification reduces computational burden but prevents the full exploitation of the mechanism's capabilities, leading to potential inaccuracies and sub-optimal performance. The motivation is to overcome these limitations by using an exact, yet efficient, model.
- **Computational Bottlenecks of Exact Modeling:** While methods for exactly modeling the parallel kinematics exist, they often incur substantial algorithmic costs, limiting their deployment in real-time control or large-scale optimization/learning tasks. The authors are motivated to find a middle ground: an exact model that is computationally efficient.
- **Challenges in Reinforcement Learning:** Training RL policies with full closed-loop robot dynamics requires specialized simulators and often leads to a significant sim-to-real gap, complicating deployment on physical hardware. The aim is to enable RL policies trained on simpler serial models to be effectively transferred to real robots with complex parallel mechanisms.
- **Need for Differentiable Models:** Modern WB-MPC solvers are derivative-based, requiring efficient computation of dynamic derivatives. Similarly, impedance control for RL deployments benefits from an understanding of how impedance gains transfer between joint and actuator spaces, which necessitates derivative information. The motivation includes providing analytically derived derivatives up to second order to support these advanced control techniques.
- **Exploiting Full Range of Motion:** Simplified models or conservative joint limits in the serial space can restrict the operational range of robots. By accurately modeling the parallel mechanism, the paper seeks to enable controllers to exploit the full feasible workspace, including configurations that might appear extreme in the serial space but are permissible by the actuators.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between advanced robot hardware and control algorithms by developing an accurate, differentiable, and computationally efficient model of parallel transmissions, thereby enhancing the capabilities and robustness of humanoid robot locomotion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on developing a compact analytical formulation for two common types of parallel mechanisms found in humanoid robots: planar four-bar linkages (typically used in knees) and intricate four-bar linkages (used in ankles). This formulation aims to capture the exact non-linear transmission properties while remaining computationally efficient and fully differentiable up to second order.

**A. Differential Actuation Model:**
The core of the methodology involves deriving a mathematical model that maps serial joint configurations ($q_s$) to motor configurations ($q_m$), denoted as $q_m = f(q_s)$, and subsequently deriving its first and second-order derivatives.

1. **Direct Geometry:**
 - **Four-bar linkages:** For a 1-DoF planar four-bar mechanism, the motor configuration ($q_m$) is expressed as a function of the serial joint configuration ($q_s$) through a geometric chain. This involves computing distances and angles using the law of cosines (Al-Kashi theorem) and basic trigonometry (e.g., atan2, acos) relative to a fixed point in the motor frame. The point B, representing a linkage attachment, is expressed as a forward kinematic function of $q_s$.
 - **Intricate four-bar linkages:** For a 2-DoF mechanism (e.g., ankle), the approach involves projecting each side of the non-planar mechanism into a virtual planar four-bar linkage. This allows applying a similar geometric derivation for each motor ($q_{m\alpha}$, $q_{m\beta}$) to relate it to the two serial ankle DoFs ($q_{s1}$, $q_{s2}$). This generalized formulation covers both mechanism types.

2. **Actuation Jacobian ($J_A$):**
 - The Actuation Jacobian ($J_A$) relates motor velocities to serial joint velocities ($\dot{q}_m = J_A(q_s)\dot{q}_s$) and, by power conservation, serial joint torques to motor torques ($\tau_s = J_A(q_s)^T \tau_m$).
 - $J_A$ is obtained by taking the derivative of the mapping $f(q_s)$ with respect to $q_s$. The derivation uses the chain rule, breaking down $df_{\alpha}/dq_s$ into components related to the point B's coordinates and the geometric parameters. The derivative of B with respect to $q_s$ ($B_s$) is computed using Pinocchio's analytical forward kinematics derivatives. The terms related to $q_m = q_{m1} + q_{m2}$ (where $q_{m1}$ and $q_{m2}$ are angular components) are computed by differentiating expressions involving norms and inverse trigonometric functions. The resulting formulation for $J_A$ is compact.

3. **Actuation Derivatives (Second Order):**
 - To support derivative-based optimal control, the derivatives of serial torques ($\tau_s$) with respect to robot states ($q_s$, $\dot{q}_s$) and controls (motor torques, $u=\tau_m$) are required.
 - The derivative of $\tau_s$ with respect to $\dot{q}_s$ is zero for pure torque control.
 - The derivative of $\tau_s$ with respect to $q_s$ is more complex. The product rule is applied to $\tau_s = J_A^T \tau_m$. This involves differentiating $J_A^T$ with respect to $q_s$. The expression is decomposed into terms involving $B_s$ and its second derivative ($B_{ss}$), and derivatives of the matrix $K$ (from the Jacobian derivation) with respect to geometric parameters ($\bar{l}$, $r$, $\hat{r}$). These partial derivatives are computed analytically. Pinocchio's spatial algebra is used for efficient computation of terms involving $B_{ss}$.

**B. Serial Impedance Transfer:**
The method addresses the challenge of transferring impedance control from serial joint space to actuator space, which is critical for RL policies trained with a serial model.
- A standard PD controller in serial joint space provides a desired serial torque $\tau_s^* = K_{Ps}(q_s^* - q_s) - K_{Ds}\dot{q}_s$.
- To stabilize the system, the motor-space PD controller gains ($K_{Pm}$, $K_{Dm}$) must be calculated such that the derivatives of the applied motor torque with respect to motor states match those of the desired motor torque.
- The paper derives analytical expressions for $K_{Pm}$ and $K_{Dm}$ based on the Actuation Jacobian and its derivatives, including terms that were previously neglected in simpler approaches, which ensures accuracy even when serial errors are not small.
- A motor reference position $q_m^*$ is then computed to ensure the control law is consistent, maintaining the desired motor torque.

**C. State Estimation:**
- On a real robot, only motor encoder readings ($q_m$) are available. To obtain serial joint positions ($q_s$), an inverse mapping $f^{-1}(q_m)$ is required.
- This is achieved by solving a numerical optimization problem: $q_s = \min_{\hat{q}_s} ||f(\hat{q}_s) - q_m||^2$. The gradient of this cost function is efficiently computed using $J_A$.
- Warm-starts from previous time steps ensure rapid convergence. Serial velocities $\dot{q}_s$ are obtained using the inverse of $J_A$.

**D. Implementation:**
- **Robot Model:** The methodology is implemented on the Bipetto robot, which features both four-bar (knee) and intricate four-bar (ankle) transmissions. A "Minimal Serial model" is extended into an "Actuated Serial model" using the proposed derivations.
- **Trajectory Optimization (TO):** The control problem is formulated as a Multiple Shooting Optimal Control Problem (OCP) using the FDDP algorithm in the Crocoddyl library, leveraging Pinocchio for dynamics. The derived actuation derivatives are crucial for this derivative-based solver. The framework allows setting costs and constraints directly in motor space (e.g., motor joint limits).
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Policies are trained using the Isaac Lab framework with constrained PPO (CaT). The policy outputs serial position offsets to a low-level simulated serial PD controller. For real hardware deployment, the impedance transfer method translates the serial reference position and constant serial gains ($K_{Ps}, K_{Ds}$) into motor reference positions and dynamically varying motor-space gains ($K_{Pm}, K_{Dm}$), which are then used by the robot's low-level motor controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both Trajectory Optimization (TO) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) implementations, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed differential actuation models.

**A. Trajectory Optimization Results:**
- **Comparison with Minimal Serial Model:** For relatively simple tasks, such as walking on flat terrain at a moderate speed (0.7m/s), the proposed Actuated Serial Model, which incorporates the closed-loop transmission into optimization, and a Minimal Serial Model, which approximates the transmission, yield similar performance.
- **Performance in Demanding Tasks:** The benefits of the Actuated Serial Model become apparent in more challenging locomotion scenarios, such as walking on rough terrain and climbing stairs. In these tasks, the robot's joints are pushed closer to their operational limits.
- **Exploitation of Range of Motion:** The Actuated Serial Model successfully reaches ankle configurations that would be inaccessible or constrained by simple box constraints applied in the serial joint space. By formulating limits in the actuator space, the method enables a more permissive, yet physically feasible, range of motion. This capability is illustrated by showing the ankle trajectories relative to the true motor box limits (Fig. 10), highlighting that a single set of serial joint limits cannot cover all necessary motions while preventing unfeasible ones.
- **Motor-Space Constraints:** The formulation allows for direct inclusion of constraints on motor torques and positions, which is particularly relevant for coupled ankle motors where serial joint limits may not accurately reflect actuator capabilities.

**B. Reinforcement Learning Results:**
- **Successful Hardware Deployment:** The impedance transfer mechanism (Section II-D) and state estimator (Section II-E) enabled the successful deployment of an RL policy, trained on a serial model, to a real robot (Bipetto) equipped with parallel actuation. This deployment demonstrated robust omnidirectional walking.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Control:** The state estimation and impedance transfer computations are performed in less than 10 milliseconds using pure Python code, allowing for update rates up to 100 Hz on a low-power onboard computer (Raspberry Pi 5). The system's stability is further enhanced by leveraging the actuators' low-level control loops running at much higher frequencies (kilohertz range).
- **Accurate Serial Reference Tracking:** The impedance transfer effectively translates the policy's desired serial joint behavior to the motor space. Figure 11 illustrates that even when the desired serial joint positions ($q_s^*$) occasionally fall outside typical assumed limits, the computed motor references ($q_m^*$) and dynamically adjusted motor impedance gains ($K_{Pm}, K_{Dm}$) enable the robot to accurately track the intended serial joint behavior.
- **Necessity of Impedance Transfer:** Direct application of inverse Jacobian-transformed torques ($\tau_m^* = J_A^{-T} \tau_s^*$) to the actuators without the proposed impedance transfer mechanism led to critical failures (robot falling) during hardware validation, underscoring the importance of dynamically adjusting motor-space impedance.
- **Future RL Integration:** While the RL experiments focused on flat-ground walking, the provided GPU implementations of the models $f$ and $J_A$ (using CusAdi) are expected to facilitate the integration of motor limits and more complex terrains into future RL frameworks.

In summary, the results consistently indicate that the proposed differential actuation models enhance the accuracy and robustness of robot control. In TO, they enable exploitation of the full operational workspace. In RL, they facilitate a reliable sim-to-real transfer and stable hardware deployment by accurately converting serial-space control objectives to the actuator space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers practical and significant advancements for the control of humanoid robots featuring parallel mechanisms, with implications across several aspects of robotics research and development.

**A. Enhanced Control Accuracy and Robustness:**
The primary significance lies in providing a computationally efficient and analytically exact method for modeling the non-linear transmissions in parallel mechanisms. This precision, particularly the provision of second-order derivatives, allows control algorithms (WB-MPC, RL) to account for the true dynamics of the robot. This leads to improved control accuracy, better exploitation of the robot's full kinematic capabilities, and enhanced robustness during complex or strenuous movements, such as walking on uneven terrain or climbing stairs.

**B. Bridging the Gap between Hardware Design and Control:**
The work effectively addresses a long-standing challenge in humanoid robotics: how to leverage the mechanical advantages of serial-parallel designs (e.g., reduced inertia, better impact absorption) without being hindered by their increased modeling complexity. By providing practical tools for both optimal control and learning, the research ensures that advanced hardware designs can be fully utilized by contemporary control strategies.

**C. Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer for Reinforcement Learning:**
A critical impact is the improvement in sim-to-real transfer for RL policies. By enabling policies trained on simplified serial models to be deployed reliably on complex hardware through the impedance gain conversion, the method bypasses the significant challenges associated with simulating closed-loop mechanisms and their interactions (e.g., contact models) in RL environments. This can accelerate development cycles and reduce the need for extensive hardware-in-the-loop tuning.

**D. Computational Efficiency:**
The compact analytical formulation ensures that the integration of parallel actuation models introduces negligible additional computational overhead compared to a purely serial model. This efficiency is crucial for real-time control applications and large-scale RL training, where computational cost can be a major bottleneck. The method avoids the substantial computational burden of exhaustive constrained dynamics approaches, making it accessible for onboard robot computation.

**E. Broader Applicability and Future Design Opportunities:**
The analytical models for common knee and ankle mechanisms (four-bar and intricate four-bar) are relevant to a wide range of modern humanoid robots, including Digit, H1, G1, GR1, and T1. This broad applicability suggests the framework can be adopted across various research platforms. Furthermore, by simplifying the control challenge for complex mechanisms, the work potentially opens avenues for designing future robots with even richer and more diverse non-linear actuation mechanisms, as the tools for their precise control become more readily available.

**F. Open-Source Contribution:**
The commitment to release GPU implementations of the models (using CusAdi) as open source will further accelerate research in this area, allowing other researchers and developers to integrate these advanced modeling capabilities into their own control and learning frameworks.

In conclusion, this research provides a fundamental advancement in the control of humanoid robots with parallel mechanisms. By delivering a practical, accurate, and computationally efficient solution, it significantly enhances the capabilities and deployability of modern legged robots in demanding real-world scenarios.
