# Residual Off-Policy RL for Finetuning Behavior Cloning Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ResFiT introduces a method for fine-tuning pre-trained, action-chunked Behavior Cloning policies on high-degree-of-freedom robots using sample-efficient off-policy residual reinforcement learning. This approach successfully enhances manipulation skills on a 29-DoF bimanual humanoid robot in real-world settings with sparse rewards, showing substantial performance improvements.

## Problem

- Behavior Cloning (BC) policies, despite strong initial performance, are limited by demonstration data quality and quantity, with performance often plateauing and direct fine-tuning with RL being unstable for large models.
- Real-world Reinforcement Learning (RL) for complex, high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robots is severely sample-inefficient, time-consuming, potentially unsafe, and struggles with sparse reward signals.
- A practical and robust method was needed to combine BC as a strong prior with RL for continuous improvement, particularly for modern action-chunked or diffusion-based BC policies.

## Method

- **ResFiT** is a two-phase framework: a base policy is initially trained via ==Behavior Cloning== with ==action chunking==, then frozen as a black box to preserve its learned behaviors.
- A lightweight, per-step ==residual policy== is learned via ==off-policy Reinforcement Learning==, which adds corrective terms to the fixed base policy's actions (a_t = a_base_t + a_res_t).
- The off-policy RL component incorporates techniques like high ==Update-to-Data (UTD) ratios==, ==n-step returns== (n=3), ==symmetric sampling== from online and offline data, and an ==ensemble of Q-functions (REDQ)== to ensure sample efficiency and stability with sparse rewards.

## Results

- Achieved approximately **200x** greater sample efficiency in simulation compared to on-policy residual RL (PPO), converging in **200,000** environment steps versus **40 million** steps on the BoxCleanup task.
- Demonstrated the first successful real-world RL training on a **29-DoF** bimanual humanoid robot with five-fingered hands for dexterous manipulation tasks, using only sparse binary rewards.
- Boosted real-world success rates on a WoollyBallPnP task from **14%** to **64%** with only **134** autonomous RL rollouts, and on a complex PackageHandover task from **23%** to **64%** with **343** rollouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The frozen base BC policy serves as both an implicit safety constraint, keeping robot behavior close to human demonstrations, and a strong exploration prior, which is crucial for stable and efficient RL in high-DoF environments with sparse rewards.
> - Learning per-step residual corrections, rather than directly optimizing the entire BC policy, bypasses the instability and computational burden associated with fine-tuning large, complex, action-chunked architectures.
> - Specific off-policy RL design choices, such as high UTD ratios and symmetric sampling from demonstrations, are critical for achieving the sample efficiency required for practical real-world robotic deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control has seen significant progress through two primary paradigms: Behavior Cloning (BC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Each has its strengths and limitations, and much of the current research aims to combine their benefits to achieve more robust and generalizable robotic policies.

**Behavior Cloning (BC)** has excelled in recent years, particularly with the advent of large-scale models, action chunking, and diffusion-based policies (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Diffusion Policy, ACT). These methods allow robots to learn complex visuomotor skills directly from human demonstrations, often leading to impressive performance. However, BC fundamentally relies on the quality and quantity of human data, which is expensive and labor-intensive to collect. Furthermore, human demonstrations are often suboptimal, and there is increasing evidence that policy performance can plateau even with vast amounts of demonstration data.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL)** offers an alternative by allowing agents to learn autonomously through trial and error. Deep RL has achieved remarkable successes in various domains, from games to simulated locomotion and in-hand manipulation. However, applying RL directly to real-world robots is notoriously challenging. It suffers from severe sample inefficiency, meaning it requires an immense number of interactions, which are costly, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe on physical robots. Learning from sparse rewards, common in long-horizon robotic tasks, further compounds these difficulties, especially for high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) systems.

This paper directly addresses the tension between these two paradigms. A natural direction is to **fine-tune BC policies with RL**, leveraging BC as a strong prior for safe exploration and RL for continuous improvement. However, modern BC policies are often large, complex neural networks utilizing techniques like action chunking or diffusion, making direct optimization with conventional RL methods unstable and challenging.

Previous work on **residual RL** (e.g., ResiP, Policy Decorator, EXPO) has explored learning corrective terms on top of a fixed base controller. While promising, these approaches have largely been confined to simulation, simpler single-arm tasks, or state-based observations, failing to scale to complex, high-DoF, vision-based systems in the real world. Real-world RL demonstrations have also mostly focused on single-arm robots with grippers (e.g., QT-Opt, SERL), with more complex behaviors often relying on sim-to-real transfer or human-in-the-loop approaches.

"Residual Off-Policy RL for Finetuning Behavior Cloning Policies" (ResFiT) carves out a critical niche by presenting a **sample-efficient off-policy residual RL framework** specifically designed to fine-tune modern, action-chunked BC policies for high-DoF bimanual systems directly in the real world, using only sparse binary rewards. By treating the base BC policy as a black box and learning lightweight per-step residual corrections, the authors bypass the challenges of directly optimizing massive BC architectures. This work significantly advances the state-of-the-art by demonstrating the first successful real-world RL training on a humanoid robot with dexterous hands, bridging the gap between sophisticated simulation results and practical robot deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a practical and robust method for enabling robots to learn and continuously improve their manipulation skills directly within their deployment environments. The authors aim to achieve this by synergistically combining the strengths of Behavior Cloning (BC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) through a novel residual learning framework.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations of existing approaches in robot learning:

- **Limitations of Behavior Cloning (BC):** While BC has delivered impressive visuomotor control policies, its reliance on human demonstrations presents several challenges. Data collection is manually intensive, costly, and requires significant infrastructure. Furthermore, human performance is often suboptimal, and empirical evidence suggests that BC policy performance eventually saturates, showing diminishing returns even with increasing amounts of demonstration data. This means BC alone cannot achieve truly optimal or generalized performance for complex tasks.
- **Challenges of Real-World Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Despite RL's potential for autonomous skill acquisition, its direct application to real-world robots faces formidable obstacles. RL algorithms are notoriously sample-inefficient, demanding millions of environment interactions, which is impractical for physical robots due to the time, wear, and potential safety risks involved. Learning from sparse, binary rewards (where the robot only gets a reward at the very end of a long task) further exacerbates the problem, especially for high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) and long-horizon tasks.
- **Difficulty in Combining BC and RL:** Modern, high-performing BC architectures often employ techniques like action chunking or diffusion models, resulting in policies with tens of millions to billions of parameters. Directly fine-tuning such large and complex policies using conventional RL methods is unstable and computationally challenging. There's a need for a flexible approach that is agnostic to the internal structure of the base BC policy.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the ResFiT method are:

- To create a recipe that effectively leverages pre-trained BC policies as "black-box" bases.
- To learn lightweight, per-step residual corrections on top of these fixed BC policies using RL.
- To ensure the RL process is sample-efficient enough to be feasible for real-world robotic deployment.
- To operate effectively with sparse binary reward signals, characteristic of many real-world tasks.
- To guarantee safety during online interaction, especially crucial for physical robots.
- To scale to high-degree-of-freedom bimanual systems, pushing beyond single-arm or simulation-only demonstrations.
- Ultimately, to demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in various vision-based tasks, both in simulation and, critically, on real humanoid robots with dexterous hands.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to provide a practical and robust pathway for enabling robots to learn and refine complex manipulation skills autonomously in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, Residual Off-Policy Fine-Tuning (ResFiT), is a two-phase approach that systematically combines Behavior Cloning (BC) and online Reinforcement Learning (RL) through a residual learning framework.

**Phase 1: Base Policy Training with Behavior Cloning (BC)**
The first phase involves training a base policy, denoted as $\pi_{\psi}$, using Behavior Cloning on an offline dataset of human demonstrations ($D_{demos}$). The paper highlights the importance of **action chunking**, where the policy predicts a sequence of $k$ future actions instead of a single action at each timestep. This technique is known to improve performance by mitigating compounding errors and effectively shortening the task horizon for imitation learning. The base policy is trained to maximize the log-likelihood of these action chunks from the demonstrations. Crucially, once trained, this base policy is **frozen** and treated as a black box for the subsequent RL phase, meaning its parameters are not updated further. This decision allows the framework to be agnostic to the specific architecture or parameterization of the (potentially very large) base BC policy.

**Phase 2: Fine-tuning with Off-Policy Residual RL**
The second phase introduces an online RL component to learn a residual policy, $\pi_{\theta}$, that corrects the actions proposed by the fixed base policy. Instead of learning a full policy from scratch, the residual policy learns per-step corrective terms, $a_{res}$, which are added to the base policy's actions, $a_{base}$. The final action executed by the robot is $a_t = a_{base_t} + a_{res_t}$. This residual approach offers two key advantages: it is agnostic to the base policy's internal structure, and it inherently provides a degree of safety by controlling the magnitude of the residual, keeping the robot's behavior relatively close to the (presumably safe) pre-trained base policy.

The RL component employs an off-policy actor-critic algorithm, specifically drawing inspiration from DDPG and incorporating several design decisions for sample efficiency and stability:

- **Reparameterized Actor-Critic:** The critic, $Q_{\phi}$, estimates the value of the combined action $Q_{\phi}(s_t, a_{base_t} + \pi_{\theta}(s_t, a_{base_t}))$, while the residual policy, $\pi_{\theta}$, learns to output $a_{res_t}$ based on the current observation $s_t$ and the base action $a_{base_t}$. The critic is trained using the Mean-Squared Bellman Error (MSBE) loss, and the actor (residual policy) is trained via gradient ascent on the Q-function.

- **Key Design Decisions for Sample Efficiency and Stability (Algorithm 1):**
 - **Update-to-Data (UTD) Ratio:** The method utilizes a UTD ratio greater than 1, meaning multiple model updates are performed for each data point collected from the environment. This significantly boosts sample efficiency.
 - **n-step Returns (n=3):** For calculating the target Q-values, $n$-step returns are used instead of traditional 1-step returns. This helps with long-horizon tasks and sparse rewards by incorporating more immediate reward information.
 - **Symmetric Sampling:** During the online RL phase, the training batches are sampled evenly (50/50) from both the frozen offline demonstration dataset ($D_{offline}$) and the continuously growing online replay buffer ($D_{online}$). This leverages valuable human demonstration data throughout the RL process, not just for BC pre-training.
 - **Layer Normalization on Critic:** Layer normalization is applied to the critic network to mitigate the common problem of Q-value overestimation in off-policy RL, which can lead to unstable learning.
 - **Delayed Actor Updates:** Following TD3, actor policy updates are less frequent (every 2-8 critic updates) than critic updates. This helps stabilize training by ensuring the policy is updated based on more accurate value estimates.
 - **Polyak Averaging:** Target networks for both the critic and actor are updated smoothly using Polyak averaging.
 - **Target Policy Smoothing:** Noise is added to the actions used to form the Q-target for target policy smoothing, which prevents brittle behavior caused by sharp peaks in the learned Q-function.
 - **Randomized Ensembled Double Q-Learning (REDQ):** An ensemble of $N$ Q-functions is maintained. For computing TD targets, the minimum Q-value from a random subset of this ensemble is used, while policy updates utilize the full ensemble average, further reducing overestimation bias.
 - **Vision Encoder:** For visual inputs, a shallow Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder is used, augmented with DrQ-style random shift augmentations to prevent overfitting.

**Real-World Infrastructure:**
The authors detail a robust real-world setup for training. This includes safety mechanisms like force-torque sensor limits and collision checking. To manage the bottleneck of model updates with high UTD ratios, they implement an actor-learner split, allowing data collection and scene resets to proceed concurrently with model updates. Crucially, the real-world experiments are conducted with only **sparse binary rewards** (1 for success, 0 otherwise). For evaluation, a **blind A/B testing protocol** with matched initial conditions is used to ensure fair comparison and mitigate environmental drift and evaluator bias.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling results from both extensive simulation experiments and pioneering real-world deployments, showcasing the effectiveness and practicality of the ResFiT approach.

**A. Simulation Results:**
The simulation experiments were conducted on tasks from Robomimic and DexMimicGen, spanning single-arm and bimanual manipulation, varying in DoFs, task horizons, and precision requirements, using vision and robot joint state observations with sparse binary rewards.

- **Exceptional Sample Efficiency:** A direct comparison on the BoxCleanup task revealed that ResFiT (using off-policy residual RL) achieved a remarkable **~200x boost in sample efficiency** compared to on-policy residual RL (PPO). ResFiT converged in approximately 200,000 environment steps, whereas PPO required around 40 million steps. This finding is critical for real-world applicability.
- **Near-Perfect Policy Performance:** ResFiT consistently converged to near-perfect success rates (approaching 1.0) across all simulated tasks, including the single-arm (Can, Square) and more complex bimanual tasks (BoxCleanup, CanSort, Coffee).
- **Superiority Over Baselines and Ablations:**
 - On simpler tasks like Can, most off-policy methods performed well, but ResFiT converged faster.
 - For the Square task, only ResFiT and an optimized non-residual off-policy baseline ("Tuned RLPD") achieved over 90% success, with ResFiT showing slightly faster convergence.
 - On the harder, high-DoF, and longer-horizon bimanual tasks (BoxCleanup, CanSort, Coffee), ResFiT significantly outperformed all baselines. Other methods, including "Tuned RLPD" (which uses the same off-policy RL recipe but without a base policy/residuals), "IBRL," and "Filtered BC," either collapsed to zero success, struggled to achieve high performance, or took considerably longer. This clearly demonstrated the critical role of the residual framework atop the action-chunked BC policy.
 - The "Filtered BC" baseline, which iteratively fine-tuned the BC policy with successful rollouts, showed minimal improvement over the initial BC policy, supporting the hypothesis that precision-demanding tasks benefit more from explicit value maximization provided by RL.
 - Ablation studies confirmed the importance of various design decisions:
 - The use of **Layer Normalization** was crucial for preventing the collapse of performance.
 - The inclusion of **demonstration data during online RL (symmetric sampling)** was beneficial across tasks.
 - **UTD ratios greater than 1** provided clear benefits, with optimal performance seen around UTD=4, beyond which diminishing returns occurred.
 - Using an **n-step return greater than 1 (n=3)** was essential for sparse reward tasks, though excessively large n-values could negatively impact performance due to increased bias.

**B. Real-World RL Results:**
The most significant findings came from the real-world deployment on a 29-DoF bimanual wheeled humanoid robot (Vega from Dexmate) with two five-fingered hands, performing visuomotor tasks with sparse binary rewards.

- **First Real-World RL on a Dexterous Humanoid:** To the best of the authors' knowledge, this work represents the **first successful demonstration of RL training entirely in the real world on a humanoid robot with two five-fingered hands** performing bimanual dexterous manipulation.
- **Significant Performance Boosts on Real Robot:**
 - **WoollyBallPnP:** For this pick-and-place task, the initial base ACT policy had only a 14% success rate on a challenging woolly ball (failure due to hovering and missed grasps). After just 134 autonomous RL rollouts (approximately 15 minutes of robot execution data), ResFiT boosted the success rate to **64%**.
 - **PackageHandover:** This more complex bimanual coordination task (picking up a package and handing it over) had a base policy success rate of 23%. With 343 RL episodes (approximately 76 minutes of data) in the real world, ResFiT improved the performance to **64%**.

These real-world results underscore the practicality, safety, and sample efficiency of the ResFiT approach, demonstrating its capability to significantly improve complex manipulation skills on advanced robotic platforms with limited online interaction and sparse rewards.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Residual Off-Policy RL for Finetuning Behavior Cloning Policies" (ResFiT) paper presents a highly significant advance in the field of robot learning, with profound potential impact on the deployment of intelligent robotic systems.

**1. A Practical Pathway for Real-World RL on Complex Robots:**
The most impactful finding is the successful demonstration of direct real-world RL training on a 29-DoF bimanual humanoid robot with dexterous hands. This is a pioneering achievement, as real-world RL has largely been limited to simpler robots or required extensive sim-to-real transfer. ResFiT provides a concrete, sample-efficient, and safe "recipe" for robots to learn and refine complex manipulation skills autonomously in their deployment environments. This is a critical step towards realizing more versatile and capable robots that can adapt and improve outside of controlled laboratory settings.

**2. Resolving the BC-RL Tension for High-DoF Systems:**
The work brilliantly resolves a long-standing tension between Behavior Cloning (BC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Modern BC policies, which achieve impressive performance through techniques like action chunking and large architectures, become intractable for direct RL optimization due to their high-dimensional action spaces and parameter count. By decoupling the pre-training (BC for initial skill and long-horizon reasoning) and fine-tuning (RL for precise, single-step corrections) stages, ResFiT allows for the benefits of complex BC policies without the computational burden of directly optimizing them with RL. This model-agnostic approach makes it highly versatile, applicable to various sophisticated base BC policies.

**3. Dual Role of the Base Policy: Safety and Exploration Prior:**
An important insight from the research is that the base BC policy serves a dual purpose beyond merely providing a good initialization. Firstly, it acts as an **implicit safety constraint**, guiding the residual policy to stay within a reasonable behavioral envelope, preventing unstable or aggressive actions unsuitable for real-world deployment. Secondly, it provides a powerful **exploration prior**, enabling the RL agent to learn effectively from sparse rewards in high-DoF spaces, a task that pure RL typically struggles with. This hybrid approach capitalizes on the strengths of human expertise while allowing for autonomous refinement.

**4. Enabling Dexterous and Bimanual Manipulation:**
The success of ResFiT on bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, such as `PackageHandover` with a 29-DoF humanoid robot, is a major breakthrough. Dexterous and bimanual manipulation are crucial for human-like interaction with the world and are notoriously challenging for robots. The ability to fine-tune these skills with relatively little online data (tens of minutes of real robot interaction) and sparse rewards significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing complex robotic behaviors.

**5. Impact on Frontier AI & Robotics (Amazon FAR):**
For Amazon FAR, this research holds immense practical value. It directly addresses the need for robust, adaptive, and scalable robotic solutions that can operate in dynamic real-world environments, such as fulfillment centers, logistics hubs, or potentially even customer-facing applications. The ability to quickly fine-tune robot policies with minimal human intervention and data empowers the development of more autonomous and efficient robotic systems, aligning with Amazon's strategic goals in advanced robotics.

**Potential Future Impact and Directions:**
While highly significant, the authors also acknowledge limitations, primarily that the learned behaviors may remain somewhat constrained around the base policy, potentially limiting the discovery of fundamentally new strategies. Future work could explore:
- **Relaxing the frozen base constraint:** Allowing for partial or adaptive updates to the base policy to enable more transformative skill learning while maintaining stability.
- **Policy Distillation:** Distilling the refined behaviors back into a more capable base policy, especially for multitask settings, could lead to generalist robots that continuously improve their skill repertoire.
- **Autonomous Data Collection and Feedback:** Reducing human supervision for resets and reward labeling through automatic reset mechanisms, robust success detection, and intrinsic reward shaping is crucial for scaling RL to truly autonomous and continuous skill improvement.

In conclusion, ResFiT offers a compelling and practical solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics. By combining the strengths of BC and RL in a novel residual framework, it opens up new avenues for deploying intelligent, adaptive, and dexterous robots in real-world applications, marking a significant step towards truly autonomous robot learning.
