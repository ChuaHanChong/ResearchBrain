# Reflect, Retry, Reward: Self-Improving LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Writer, Inc. researchers develop a reinforcement learning framework that trains LLMs to generate more effective self-reflections by rewarding only the reflection tokens when a model's second attempt succeeds after initial failure, achieving substantial performance gains where trained 7B parameter models outperform vanilla 72B models on function calling (APIGen) and math equation tasks (Countdown) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with binary success/failure feedback, while demonstrating minimal catastrophic forgetting (<1% degradation on MMLU-Pro, GSM8K benchmarks) and producing qualitatively superior reflections that are shorter, clearer, and more actionable compared to verbose vanilla model outputs.

## Problem

- LLMs exhibit unpredictable failures and inconsistent performance across similar tasks, creating reliability issues for real-world deployment
- Direct retraining on failed tasks is often impractical due to data scarcity and inability of even large models to generate reliable synthetic training data
- Existing self-reflection methods rely heavily on prompt quality and external supervision rather than learned capabilities

## Method

- ==Two-stage RL framework== where models attempt a task, generate self-reflection upon failure, retry the task, and receive rewards only for reflection tokens when the retry succeeds
- Uses ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with binary feedback to train models on "datasets of failures" extracted from initial unsuccessful attempts
- ==Advantage masking== for all non-reflection tokens to ensure learning focuses on reflection quality rather than task-specific answers

## Results

- Trained smaller models consistently outperform much larger vanilla models (Qwen2-7B beats Qwen2-72B on both tasks when given two attempts)
- Average performance improvements of **4.7%** on APIGen function calling and **8.6%** on Countdown math after GRPO training with self-reflection
- Minimal catastrophic forgetting with less than **1%** degradation on general benchmarks while maintaining specialized self-correction abilities

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective self-reflections are shorter and more focused rather than verbose, contradicting assumptions about chain-of-thought reasoning
> - Training on reflection process generalizes to improve first-attempt performance even when no explicit reflection is provided
> - Binary success/failure feedback is sufficient for meaningful self-improvement without requiring detailed human annotations or teacher models
