# Rectified LpJEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures with Sparse and Maximum-Entropy Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Rectified LpJEPA is introduced as a method for Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures to learn sparse and non-negative representations. This approach leverages Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization and Rectified Generalized Gaussian distributions, achieving competitive downstream performance on image classification while maintaining explicit and analytical control over representation sparsity.

## Problem

- Existing Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) primarily employ regularization strategies that favor dense, isotropic Gaussian representations, thereby limiting the diversity of learnable feature structures.
- Integrating the benefits of sparse and non-negative codes, observed in neuroscience and certain deep learning contexts for efficiency and interpretability, into JEPA frameworks with analytical control was a standing challenge.
- Preventing representational collapse in self-supervised learning requires robust regularization beyond mere invariance, with current methods often not explicitly encouraging sparsity.

## Method

- The authors propose Rectified **LpJEPA**, which integrates Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization (RDMReg) into the JEPA framework to induce sparsity and non-negativity in learned features.
- RDMReg aligns rectified feature representations (output of `ReLU(z)`) with a novel family of target distributions called Rectified Generalized Gaussian (RGG) distributions, which are constructed to model rectified random variables and explicitly feature a probability mass at zero.
- The method employs the sliced 2-Wasserstein distance on one-dimensional projections (via the Cramér–Wold device) to enforce distribution matching, while the RGG parameters (location μ and shape p) provide analytical control over the expected L0 norm (sparsity) of the representations.

## Results

- Rectified LpJEPA consistently achieved controllable sparsity, with empirical L0 norms closely tracking theoretical predictions across variations in RGG parameters and different backbone architectures.
- The method attained competitive linear probe accuracy on ImageNet-100 and ImageNet-1K (100 epochs) compared to both dense and sparse baselines, while producing significantly sparser representations.
- Learned representations exhibited improved statistical independence (lower normalized Hilbert–Schmidt Independence Criterion values) and high R´enyi information dimension entropy, indicating efficient and robust coding, along with strong transferability across various downstream datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rectified Generalized Gaussian (RGG) distributions can serve as maximum entropy distributions under expected L_p norm constraints on positive values, providing a principled theoretical target for inducing sparse and non-negative codes.
> - Applying rectification directly to the features (`ReLU(z)`) before regularization is critical for achieving a favorable sparsity-performance tradeoff, as direct matching to the RGG via the continuous mapping theorem is more effective than post-hoc rectification.
> - The framework provides analytical control over the sparsity level of learned representations, demonstrating that parameters of the target RGG distribution directly correlate with the empirical L0 norm.
