# Orthogonal Model Merging

> [!summary] Summary
> The Chinese University of Hong Kong researchers introduce Orthogonal Model Merging (OrthoMerge), a framework that merges deep learning models by operating on the Riemannian manifold of the orthogonal group rather than Euclidean space. This method preserves geometric properties of neural network weights, yielding up to a 4.0% average accuracy improvement for OFT-finetuned models and enhancing knowledge retention when combining large language models and vision-language models.

## Problem

- Existing model merging methods operate in Euclidean space, often destroying intrinsic geometric properties of neural network weights crucial for performance.
- Linear operations in Euclidean space can lead to catastrophic forgetting, exacerbate parameter conflicts, and cause magnitude collapse, hindering merged model efficacy.
- There is a need to efficiently combine diverse capabilities from a multitude of task-specific finetuned models into a single, unified model without extensive re-training.

## Method

- OrthoMerge merges models by operating on the Riemannian manifold of the orthogonal group, mapping orthogonal transformations to their Lie algebra for efficient, magnitude-corrected averaging.
- For models finetuned with standard additive methods (e.g., LoRA), an Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling strategy extracts an orthogonal component (merged geometrically) and an additive residual (merged via conventional Euclidean methods).
- A magnitude-corrected merging approach scales the averaged Lie algebra elements to prevent magnitude collapse, ensuring the preservation of adaptation intensity.

## Results

- OrthoMerge achieved the highest average accuracy (**46.25%** for Llama-3.1-8B) when merging OFT-finetuned models, outperforming individual experts and all Euclidean baselines.
- Integrating OrthoMerge variants (e.g., TIES+OrthoMerge-C) with non-OFT models improved baseline performance by up to **2.41%** and better preserved pre-trained knowledge, raising MMLU† score from **54.52%** (TIES) to **55.36%**.
- The framework successfully extended to multimodal models, with TIES+OrthoMerge-C achieving the highest average accuracy (**64.04%**) on vision-language tasks and mitigating drops in general capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conducting model merging in Riemannian space by explicitly preserving orthogonal transformations effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting and destructive interference.
> - Mapping orthogonal matrices to their corresponding Lie algebra allows for computationally efficient geometric operations while maintaining the mathematical rigor of the orthogonal group.
> - The Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling framework extends the benefits of geometry-aware merging to models not initially finetuned with orthogonal methods, broadening its applicability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to a paradigm where pretraining massive foundation models is computationally prohibitive for most, shifting focus towards finetuning smaller, task-specific models. However, this creates a challenge: managing a multitude of specialized models, each proficient in only one or a few tasks. Model merging addresses this by integrating diverse capabilities from several finetuned "expert" models into a single, unified model without requiring extensive re-training. This approach is highly appealing for its efficiency and scalability in the post-training phase of LLM development.

The current landscape of model merging methods is predominantly characterized by operations in Euclidean space. Approaches like Task Arithmetic (TA) and its variants, sparsification-based methods (e.g., TIES-Merging, DARE), and Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)-based techniques (e.g., TSV-M) all primarily operate by aggregating "task vectors"—the differences between finetuned and base model weights—using linear arithmetic. While these methods have shown utility, the authors argue that they suffer from fundamental limitations. Linear operations in Euclidean space can inadvertently destroy the intrinsic geometric properties of pretrained weights, such as hyperspherical energy or angular relationships between neurons, which are crucial for maintaining model performance and representation stability. This destruction can lead to "catastrophic forgetting" of previously learned knowledge and exacerbate parameter conflicts across tasks, ultimately hindering the merged model's overall efficacy and generalization.

"Orthogonal Model Merging" (OrthoMerge) directly addresses these limitations by introducing a fundamentally different approach rooted in Riemannian geometry. Instead of performing linear arithmetic in Euclidean space, OrthoMerge conducts merging operations on the Riemannian manifold formed by the orthogonal group. This paradigm shift positions the work as a significant departure from conventional methods, building upon the burgeoning interest in geometrically-aware deep learning and manifold-constrained optimization. By explicitly preserving geometric structures, OrthoMerge aims to unlock more robust, stable, and generalizable model merging, mitigating issues like catastrophic forgetting and destructive interference that plague existing techniques. It extends the principles seen in Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) to the merging context, and crucially, provides a mechanism (Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling) to apply these geometric benefits even to models finetuned with non-OFT methods like LoRA or full finetuning, thereby bridging a critical gap in the existing research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel model merging framework, OrthoMerge, that overcomes the limitations of current Euclidean-space merging methods by preserving the intrinsic geometric properties of neural network weights.

The key motivations driving this work stem from several critical observations and challenges in the field of LLM development:

- **Computational Prohibitions of Pretraining:** Pretraining large foundation models is becoming increasingly expensive, necessitating efficient strategies for model adaptation and integration.
- **Proliferation of Task-Specific Finetuned Models:** The success of finetuning leads to a large number of specialized models, creating a need for a scalable way to combine their diverse capabilities.
- **Limitations of Euclidean Merging:** Existing model merging methods predominantly rely on linear arithmetic in Euclidean space, which, while simple, often suffers from several drawbacks:
 - **Destruction of Geometric Properties:** Linear operations can inadvertently destroy crucial intrinsic geometric properties of weights, such as hyperspherical energy (angular relationships between neurons), which are vital for maintaining model performance and representation stability.
 - **Parameter Conflicts and Catastrophic Forgetting:** Aggregating task vectors in Euclidean space can lead to destructive interference between conflicting updates from different tasks, resulting in reduced performance, catastrophic forgetting of original capabilities, and a drift back towards the base model's performance.
 - **Magnitude Collapse:** Simple averaging in Euclidean space can cause the magnitude of task-specific adaptations to collapse due to cancellation of conflicting components, dampening the effective adaptation strength.
- **Leveraging Orthogonal Transformations:** The authors draw inspiration from Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which adapts models using multiplicative orthogonal transformations. This suggests that operating on orthogonal groups might offer a more robust mechanism for integrating knowledge.
- **Bridging the Gap for Non-OFT Models:** While OFT provides explicit orthogonal matrices, a significant portion of finetuned models use non-orthogonal methods (e.g., LoRA, full finetuning). A key motivation is to extend the benefits of geometry-preserving merging to these more common finetuning approaches.

In essence, the authors aim to introduce a principled yet efficient integration mechanism that takes into account both the direction and intensity of model adaptations, moving beyond the superficial linear aggregation to a deeper, geometry-aware merging process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OrthoMerge introduces a geometry-preserving model merging framework that operates on the Riemannian manifold formed by the orthogonal group. The methodology is split into two main scenarios: merging models finetuned with Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and merging general models finetuned with non-OFT methods.

#### 3.1. Orthogonal Merging for OFT-trained Models

For models finetuned using OFT, task-specific adaptations are explicitly represented as orthogonal matrices $\{R_i\}$.
The authors identify a "geometric dilemma":
- Direct arithmetic averaging of $R_i$ in Euclidean space (e.g., $R_{avg} = \frac{1}{N} \sum R_i$) destroys orthogonality, violating crucial geometric constraints.
- The theoretically correct Riemannian geometric mean preserves orthogonality but is computationally prohibitive for LLMs due to iterative matrix decompositions with $O(d^3)$ complexity.

To address this, OrthoMerge proposes the following:
1. **Mapping to Lie Algebra so(d):** Orthogonal matrices $R_i$ are mapped to their corresponding skew-symmetric representations $Q_i$ in the Lie algebra $so(d)$. This is achieved either directly from OFT adapter checkpoints or by solving an Orthogonal Procrustes problem (Eq. 3, 4) if only finetuned weights are available, followed by an inverse Cayley transform (Eq. 5): $Q = (R - I)(R + I)^{-1}$. The Lie algebra $so(d)$ provides a linear space where orthogonal transformations can be efficiently manipulated while ensuring the merged result remains a valid rotation.
2. **Magnitude-Corrected Merging:** A simple arithmetic mean of $Q_i$ in the Lie algebra can suffer from "magnitude collapse" due to destructive interference of conflicting task updates. To counteract this and preserve the adaptation intensity (represented by the Frobenius norm $||Q||_F$), a scaling factor $c$ is introduced (Eq. 7):
 $c = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} ||Q_i||_F}{||\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} Q_i||_F}$.
 The merged skew-symmetric matrix $Q_{merged}$ is then calculated as: $Q_{merged} = c \cdot (\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} Q_i)$ (Eq. 8).
3. **Mapping Back to Orthogonal Group:** The $Q_{merged}$ is mapped back to the orthogonal manifold to obtain the merged rotation $R_{merged}$ via the Cayley transform (Eq. 2): $R = (I + Q)(I - Q)^{-1}$.
4. **Final Merged Weight:** The final merged weight is obtained by applying the merged rotation to the base model: $W_{merged} = R_{merged} W_0$.

#### 3.2. Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling for Merging Non-OFT Models

For models finetuned with standard additive methods (e.g., LoRA, full finetuning) where explicit orthogonal matrices are absent, OrthoMerge introduces a novel Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling strategy (Algorithm 1 and 2). This framework decomposes the finetuned weights into an orthogonal component (capturing structural rotation) and an additive residual component.

1. **Orthogonal Component Extraction:** For each finetuned model $W_i$, an orthogonal matrix $R_i$ is extracted by solving the Orthogonal Procrustes problem: $\min_{R_i} ||W_{target_i} - R_i W_0||_F$, s.t. $R_i^T R_i = I$. The closed-form solution is $R_i = U V^T$, where $U\Sigma V^T = SVD(W_{target_i} W_0^T)$.
 Two strategies are proposed for defining the target matrix $W_{target_i}$:
 - **Global Decoupling:** $W_{target_i} = W_i$. This aims to extract the largest possible orthogonal component from the entire finetuned weight.
 - **Conflict-Aware Decoupling:** This strategy focuses on isolating and addressing conflicting neuron-level updates. It identifies neurons (columns) where the task-specific update $\tau_i = W_i - W_0$ opposes the global average task vector $\tau_{mean}$. The target is then defined as $W_{target_i} = W_0 + \tau_{conf_i}$, where $\tau_{conf_i}$ contains only these conflicting updates.
2. **Magnitude-Corrected Orthogonal Merging:** The extracted orthogonal matrices $\{R_i\}$ are then merged using the same Lie algebra-based, magnitude-corrected approach described in Section 4.1 to obtain $R_{merged}$.
3. **Residual Component Acquisition:** An additive residual $\rho_i$ is calculated for each task, capturing the information not modeled by the rotation: $\rho_i = W_i - R_i W_0$.
4. **Standard Merging in Euclidean Space:** The residuals $\{\rho_i\}$ are merged using conventional Euclidean-space methods (e.g., Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging) to obtain $\rho_{merged}$.
5. **Hybrid Merging:** The final merged model weight $W_{final}$ is reconstructed by applying the merged orthogonal transformation to the base model and adding the merged residual: $W_{final} = R_{merged} W_0 + \rho_{merged}$.

This hybrid approach allows OrthoMerge to leverage geometric stability for structural transformations while retaining the flexibility of additive merging for residual adjustments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical evaluation of OrthoMerge across diverse domains consistently demonstrates its superior performance compared to conventional Euclidean-space merging methods.

#### 4.1. Merging OFT-Finetuned Models

When directly merging models finetuned with Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), OrthoMerge outperforms all baseline methods (TA, TIES, DARE, TSV-M) on both Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-3B base models.
- **In-domain Performance:** OrthoMerge achieved the highest average accuracy on task-specific benchmarks (e.g., 46.25% for Llama-3.1-8B), notably surpassing the average performance of individual experts (44.71%), suggesting constructive synergy.
- **Out-of-domain Generalization:** It attained the highest average accuracy on general evaluation datasets like M-ARC and AGIEval (e.g., 41.80% for Llama-3.1-8B), indicating that preserving geometric structure robustly maintains general capabilities.

#### 4.2. Merging Non-OFT Models (Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling)

Applying the Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling framework to models finetuned with non-OFT methods (like those from MergeBench on Llama-3.2-3B) significantly enhances performance:
- **Improved Task Performance:** Integrating OrthoMerge variants (+OrthoMerge-C for Conflict-Aware, +OrthoMerge-G for Global) consistently boosted the performance of standard baselines. For instance, TSV-M+OrthoMerge-C achieved the highest average task performance of 42.07%. TIES+OrthoMerge-C improved TIES by 2.41%.
- **Mitigation of Forgetting:** OrthoMerge variants preserved pre-trained knowledge better. For example, while TIES slightly reduced MMLU† score (54.52%) compared to the base model (55.00%), TIES+OrthoMerge-C recovered and exceeded it (55.36%), showcasing better knowledge retention.
- **Vision-Language Model Extension:** The framework's effectiveness was validated on multimodal models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct). TIES+OrthoMerge-C achieved the highest average accuracy on vision-language task-specific benchmarks (64.04%). Crucially, OrthoMerge variants significantly mitigated the drop in general instruction-following (IFEval) and multimodal capabilities (MMBench) observed with vanilla merging methods, demonstrating modality-agnostic benefits.

#### 4.3. Intriguing Insights and Discussions

The paper provides compelling insights into why OrthoMerge is effective:
- **Better Loss Landscape:** Visualizations showed OrthoMerge achieving a more favorable loss location on the joint loss landscape, indicating reduced destructive interference and better preservation of model knowledge compared to Task Arithmetic.
- **Hyperspherical Energy Preservation:** By operating on orthogonal matrices, OrthoMerge inherently preserves the hyperspherical energy of the model weights, which is known to be critical for mitigating catastrophic forgetting and improving out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Spectral Regularization:** OrthoMerge implicitly regularizes the spectral norm of the weights. Since $W_{merged} = R_{merged} W_0$ and $R_{merged}$ is orthogonal, $||W_{merged}||_2 = ||W_0||_2$, preventing spectral drift that can occur with conventional Euclidean merging.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study and Analysis

- **Orthogonal Transformation Merging Strategies:** A comparison of different merging strategies for orthogonal transformations confirmed the necessity of magnitude-corrected averaging in the Lie algebra. Simple averaging of R matrices destroyed orthogonality, sequential products were unstable, and simple Q averaging suffered from magnitude collapse. OrthoMerge's approach (24.60% avg) significantly outperformed all other variants (e.g., 39.53% for Simple Average R, 40.76% for Sequential Product R, 41.01% for Simple Average Q).
- **Performance Across Number of Tasks:** OrthoMerge consistently showed superior performance when merging 2, 3, or 4 tasks, with performance gains becoming more significant as the number of merged tasks increased, highlighting its robustness and scalability.
- **Norm Distribution Analysis:** Analysis of norm distributions in the decoupling strategies showed that Global Decoupling extracts a substantial orthogonal component correlated with the full task vector, while Conflict-Aware Decoupling isolates primarily conflicting updates in the orthogonal component, leaving most information in the residuals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Orthogonal Model Merging" (OrthoMerge) framework represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) and multimodal model composition. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Model Merging:** OrthoMerge introduces a fundamentally new paradigm by shifting model merging operations from the traditional Euclidean space to the Riemannian manifold of the orthogonal group. This move is not merely an algorithmic tweak but a principled approach that acknowledges and explicitly preserves the intrinsic geometric properties of neural network weights. This could inspire a new wave of research into geometrically-aware methods for various deep learning tasks beyond just model merging.
- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting and Destructive Interference:** By preserving crucial geometric properties like hyperspherical energy and implicitly regularizing spectral norms, OrthoMerge directly addresses the long-standing challenges of catastrophic forgetting and destructive interference that plague existing model merging techniques. This leads to merged models that retain more of their specialized knowledge while also maintaining or even enhancing their general capabilities.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The consistent outperformance of OrthoMerge across diverse benchmarks, tasks, and base models (both LLMs and Vision-Language Models) is a testament to its effectiveness. The ability to achieve higher average accuracy and stronger out-of-domain generalization means that more capable and robust foundation models can be constructed from existing specialized components. This is particularly valuable as LLMs are increasingly deployed in complex, multi-functional applications.
- **Broadened Applicability through Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling:** A key impact of OrthoMerge is its extension to models not initially finetuned with orthogonal methods. The Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling strategy allows the benefits of geometric merging to be leveraged for models trained with popular techniques like LoRA or full finetuning. This significantly broadens the practical applicability of OrthoMerge, making it a versatile tool for the wider AI community.
- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** Model merging itself is motivated by the need for efficiency, avoiding costly full retraining. OrthoMerge provides a more robust way to achieve this efficiency by ensuring that the merging process is not only fast but also effective in combining knowledge without degradation, thus contributing to more scalable development cycles for complex AI systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a solid foundation for future research in several directions. It encourages further exploration of geometric deep learning principles in areas like multi-task learning, continual learning, and knowledge distillation. Investigating other relevant Lie groups or manifolds, and developing more sophisticated decoupling strategies, could lead to even more powerful and stable AI systems.

In conclusion, OrthoMerge offers a principled, effective, and broadly applicable solution to the critical problem of model merging, moving beyond linear approximations to respect the inherent geometry of neural network weights. Its potential to enable the stable, generalizable, and scalable composition of comprehensive intelligence from specialized models marks it as a significant contribution to the field of artificial intelligence.
