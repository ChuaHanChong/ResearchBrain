# LongRLVR: Long-Context Reinforcement Learning Requires Verifiable Context Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> The LONGRLVR framework enhances large language models' ability to reason over long contexts by introducing verifiable context rewards, addressing the "vanishing grounding gradient" problem in traditional outcome-only reinforcement learning. It enables smaller models like Qwen2.5-7B-1M to outperform larger, more specialized models such as LLaMA-3.1-70B on long-context benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles to effectively enhance long-context reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) due to sparse reward signals.
- Outcome-only rewards in RLVR lead to "vanishing gradients" for the contextual grounding process, preventing LLMs from learning to identify relevant information from extensive inputs.
- LLMs with extended context windows often fail to robustly retrieve and synthesize necessary information, limiting their performance on complex long-context tasks.

## Method

- **LONGRLVR** proposes a novel RLVR framework that augments the sparse answer reward with a dense, verifiable context reward, formally proving it provides a non-vanishing learning signal for the grounding process, overcoming the "vanishing grounding gradient."
- It employs a modulated F-score reward, combining an unconditional grounding signal with a synergistic success reward, and trains a factorized policy for grounding and answering.
- A multi-stage synthetic data generation pipeline creates high-quality, long-context QA datasets with ground-truth evidence annotations for training.

## Results

- LongRLVR consistently outperforms SFT and naive RLVR baselines, with Qwen2.5-**14B**-**1M** achieving scores of 88.90 on RULER-QA and 46.5 on LongBench v2.
- Smaller models fine-tuned with LongRLVR, such as Qwen2.5-**7B**-**1M**, achieve long-context reasoning performance surpassing LLaMA-3.1-**70B** and Qwen2.5-**72B**-YaRN.
- Ablation studies validate the theoretical premise, showing that the synergistic combination of context and answer rewards is vital, with an optimal blending factor "eta=**0.1**" and a recall-prioritizing "beta=2" F-score.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-only rewards in traditional RLVR are too sparse to provide effective learning signals for the contextual grounding process in long-context scenarios, leading to vanishing gradients.
> - Introducing a dense, verifiable context reward is crucial for overcoming this limitation, as it directly incentivizes the selection of relevant evidence, ensuring a robust learning signal.
> - High-quality synthetic data, specifically filtered to include challenging questions and precise grounding annotations, is essential for successfully training models to acquire strong long-context reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), and long-context processing.

**Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** The work builds directly upon the recent advancements in RLVR (e.g., Lambert et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). RLVR has shown remarkable success in enhancing LLMs' reasoning capabilities by rewarding verifiable outcomes, such as passing unit tests in programming or achieving correct mathematical solutions. Models like DeepSeek-R1 have achieved expert-level performance in these domains. The existing RLVR paradigm, however, primarily focuses on sharpening the LLM's *internal parametric knowledge* and reasoning pathways.

**Long-Context LLMs and Contextual Grounding:** A separate but related line of research focuses on extending the context windows of LLMs through architectural innovations like Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) scaling (Su et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2023). While these methods increase the *capacity* to process long contexts, studies indicate that LLMs often struggle to *effectively utilize* all information within these extended windows, especially when it requires identifying and synthesizing relevant details from vast inputs (Wan et al., 2025; Yue et al., 2025; Wen et al., 2025). This challenge is termed "contextual grounding."

**The Gap Addressed by LongRLVR:** The core contribution of LongRLVR is to bridge the gap between effective RLVR for internal reasoning and the necessity for robust contextual grounding in long-context scenarios. The authors identify that current outcome-only RLVR, while excellent for refining internal logic, is fundamentally ill-suited for tasks demanding external information retrieval and synthesis due to its *sparse reward signal*. This sparsity makes it difficult for the model to learn the intermediate step of identifying relevant evidence.

**Distinction from Related Work:**
- **Long Context Alignment:** The paper situates itself within the broader field of "long-context alignment," which involves post-training methods (SFT, DPO, RLVR) to unlock LLMs' latent long-context capabilities. LongRLVR offers an improved RLVR strategy for this alignment.
- **Long-Context LLM Agents:** The work explicitly distinguishes itself from agentic workflows (e.g., Zhao et al., 2024; Qian et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024), which tackle long contexts by breaking them into chunks and processing them sequentially via multi-turn collaboration. LongRLVR, in contrast, focuses on improving the LLM's *native* ability to reason over the *full long context* in a single pass, making it complementary to, rather than a replacement for, agentic approaches. It could potentially enhance agentic frameworks by enabling them to handle larger chunks per step.

In essence, LongRLVR targets a specific, fundamental bottleneck in applying the powerful RLVR paradigm to real-world applications requiring deep comprehension of extensive external information, thereby expanding the applicability and effectiveness of LLMs in complex long-context tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to effectively enhance the long-context reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs)**. The authors identify a crucial limitation in the existing RLVR paradigm when applied to long-context scenarios: its **reliance on an outcome-only reward signal leads to a failure in learning robust contextual grounding**.

The motivation stems from two key observations:
1. **Success of RLVR in Reasoning:** RLVR has proven highly effective in advancing LLMs' abilities in tasks like mathematics and programming by optimizing against verifiable factual outcomes. This success makes its application to broader intelligence, requiring interaction with vast external environments, highly desirable.
2. **Limitations in Long-Context Grounding:** Despite extended context windows, LLMs often struggle with "contextual grounding"—the ability to accurately retrieve and synthesize information from extensive external documents. Current RLVR, focused on internal knowledge, does not adequately address this. The authors empirically observe this failure, noting that naive RLVR with outcome-only rewards quickly leads to a stagnation in contextual recall during long-context training (Figure 1), thereby halting improvements in answer accuracy.

This empirical observation leads to the **core hypothesis and formal identification of the problem**: the outcome-only reward is too sparse to provide an effective learning signal for the *intermediate process* of identifying relevant evidence (contextual grounding). The authors formally prove that this **sparse answer reward causes "vanishing gradients" for the contextual grounding process**, making learning intractable for long sequences.

Therefore, the key objective is to **design a novel RLVR framework, LongRLVR, that augments the sparse answer reward with a dense and verifiable context reward**. This auxiliary signal is intended to directly incentivize the model to select the correct grounding information, thereby providing a robust learning gradient that overcomes the vanishing gradient problem and unlocks the full long-context reasoning potential of LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LongRLVR framework is meticulously designed to address the vanishing gradient problem in contextual grounding. Its methodology involves a refactored policy, formal theoretical analysis, a novel reward function, and a specialized data generation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Explicit Grounding Formulation

The authors first refactor the standard RLVR policy, π_θ(y | X, Q), into two explicit stages for long-context tasks:
1. **Grounding Head (π_gnd_θ(Z | X, Q)):** Responsible for selecting a subset of relevant chunks `Z` from the extensive context `X` given a question `Q`.
2. **Answer Head (π_ans_θ(y | X, Q, Z)):** Responsible for generating the final answer `y` conditioned on the selected evidence `Z`.
This factorized policy, π_θ(y, Z | X, Q) = π_gnd_θ(Z | X, Q) ⋅ π_ans_θ(y | X, Q, Z), makes the previously implicit grounding process explicit, allowing for direct supervision. The model is trained to first output chunk identifiers (`<useful chunks>`) and then the final answer (`<answer>`), as illustrated in Figure 2.

#### 3.2. The Vanishing Grounding Gradient Problem (Formal Proof)

The paper formally proves why outcome-only rewards fail in long-context grounding.
- **Assumption 1 (Sparse Answer Reward):** The expected answer reward, E[r_ans | Z], is a function `f(Z ∩ G)` of the intersection between selected chunks `Z` and ground-truth essential chunks `G`. A correct solution typically requires synthesizing a *complete set* of evidence, meaning partial information yields low or zero reward.
- **Proposition 1 (Vanishing Gradients for Grounding):** The gradient of the expected answer reward with respect to the logit `s_j` for an essential chunk `c_j ∈ G` is proportional to `Cov(f(Z ∩ G), z_j)`. Crucially, this gradient is scaled by `Pr_θ(E_j)`, where `E_j` is the event that *all other prerequisite evidence* for `c_j` to be useful is already present. In complex long-context tasks, this event `E_j` is extremely unlikely, especially early in training. Consequently, the gradient for individual chunks becomes vanishingly small, making it intractable to learn the grounding head effectively. This explains the observed plateau in contextual recall with naive RLVR.

#### 3.3. LongRLVR: Learning with a Verifiable Context Reward

To overcome the vanishing gradient problem, LongRLVR introduces a direct, dense learning signal for the grounding head.
- **Theoretical Foundation (Proposition 2):** A general additive context reward, `r_ctx(Z, G) = Σ_k∈G α_k 1{c_k ∈ Z}` (where `α_k > 0`), is introduced. The total reward is `r_total(y, Z) = r_ans(y) + r_ctx(Z, G)`. The proof demonstrates that the gradient of the expected total reward, `∇_s_j E[r_total]`, includes a term `α_j Var(z_j) = α_j p_j(1 − p_j)`. This term is **dense and independent of the rare activation event E_j**, ensuring a non-vanishing learning signal for each relevant chunk. Positive covariances between selected chunks further strengthen this signal.

- **Practical Instantiation: The Modulated F-Score Reward:** A normalized and balanced context reward is critical for stable training.
 - **F_β-score:** The F_β-score is used to measure grounding quality, balancing precision (avoiding irrelevant chunks) and recall (retrieving correct evidence). A `β=2` is chosen to prioritize recall, as missing essential evidence is often more detrimental in complex reasoning.
 - **Modulated Combination:** The final context reward is `r_ctx(y, Z, G) = η ⋅ F_β(Z, G) + (1 − η) ⋅ r_ans(y) ⋅ F_β(Z, G)`.
 - The first term (`η ⋅ F_β`) provides an **unconditional grounding reward**, a dense signal for selecting correct evidence.
 - The second term (`(1 − η) ⋅ r_ans(y) ⋅ F_β(Z, G)`) is a **synergistic success reward**. It amplifies the reward for high-quality grounding only when a correct answer `r_ans(y)` is also produced, unifying both objectives. A small `η` (e.g., 0.1) is found to be optimal, balancing direct grounding feedback with the ultimate goal of answering correctly.
- **Overall Objective:** LongRLVR maximizes the expected total reward `J(θ) = E[(X,Q,G)~D E[(Z,y)~π_θ(Z,y|X,Q) [r_ans(y) + r_ctx(y, Z, G)]]`, optimized using standard policy gradient algorithms like GRPO.

#### 3.4. Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline for Grounded QA

To train LongRLVR, a specialized dataset of (X, Q, G, y) tuples (context, question, ground-truth evidence chunks, answer) is needed. The authors develop an automated, multi-stage pipeline (Algorithm 1):
1. **Semantic Clustering and Evidence Identification:** Long documents are partitioned into chunks, embedded, and clustered thematically using DBSCAN.
2. **Per-Cluster QA Generation and Scoring:** For each cluster, a powerful generator LLM (Qwen3-235B-A22B) synthesizes candidate QA pairs, crucially *identifying the necessary evidence chunks (G)* for each. A verifier LLM then scores these candidates for clarity, fidelity, and evidence necessity. The highest-scoring QA pair per cluster is selected.
3. **Inter-Cluster Selection and Finalization:** The overall highest-scoring QA pair from across the clusters is selected for the document. A strict final filter (quality rating > 9/10) ensures high fidelity. This pipeline yields 46K high-quality, long-context QA pairs with precise grounding annotations. Crucially, the pipeline filters out "easy questions" to ensure the training data is challenging and requires robust reasoning.

#### 3.5. Training Details

LongRLVR is applied to LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B-1M, and Qwen2.5-14B-1M models. Training uses the GRPO algorithm, AdamW optimizer, and a specific learning schedule. Models are trained on the 46K synthetic data for one epoch, using hyperparameters `η=0.1` and `β=2`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LongRLVR demonstrates its exceptional effectiveness and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Consistent and Substantial Gains over Baselines

LongRLVR consistently and significantly outperforms both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and naive RLVR (GRPO) baselines across all evaluated models (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B-1M, Qwen2.5-14B-1M) and benchmarks (RULER-QA, LongBench v2, LongReason).
- **Example Performance Boost:** For Qwen2.5-14B-1M, LongRLVR boosts scores on RULER-QA from 73.17 (naive RLVR) to 88.90, on LongBench v2 from 39.8 to 46.5, and on LongReason from 73.55 to 78.42. Similar large gains are observed for the 7B and 8B models.
- These results provide strong empirical evidence that LongRLVR successfully addresses the limitations of conventional RLVR in long-context scenarios by effectively supervising the contextual grounding process. The superiority over SFT also highlights the value of RL for post-training long-context capabilities.

#### 4.2. Performance Comparable to Superior LLMs

LongRLVR not only outperforms direct baselines but also elevates the performance of base LLMs to a tier comparable to, or even surpassing, much larger and more advanced state-of-the-art models.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** Qwen2.5-7B-1M trained with LongRLVR (79.22 on LongReason) significantly outperforms LLaMA-3.1-70B (57.59) and Qwen2.5-72B-YaRN (72.76). The 14B model (46.5 on LongBench v2) also surpasses the 72B model, demonstrating remarkable efficiency in instilling long-context reasoning.
- **Rivaling Specialized Models:** The Qwen2.5-14B-1M, when trained with LongRLVR, outperforms the newer Qwen3-14B (88.90 vs 87.60 on RULER-QA, 78.42 vs 77.50 on LongReason), which benefits from a more advanced backbone and post-training strategy. Furthermore, the 14B LongRLVR model achieves performance comparable to QwenLong-L1-32B, a model specifically designed for long-context reasoning through RLVR. This underscores LongRLVR's ability to unlock superior long-context reasoning for general-purpose LLMs.

#### 4.3. Impact of Reward Components (Figure 3)

Ablation studies confirm the theoretical hypothesis regarding the importance of the verifiable context reward.
- **Answer-Only Reward:** Models trained with answer-only rewards suffer from stagnating contextual recall, which caps their final answer accuracy. This validates the "vanishing grounding gradient" problem.
- **Context-Only Reward:** Models trained solely with the F_β-score (context reward) show rapid initial gains in contextual recall and task performance. However, without the final answer reward, their downstream performance eventually degrades, as perfect grounding doesn't guarantee a correct final answer.
- **LongRLVR (Synergy):** By synergizing both context and answer rewards, LongRLVR achieves consistently improved contextual recall and stable, high-performance answer accuracy on downstream tasks, proving the essential role of both reward signals.

#### 4.4. Impact of Data Quality (Figure 4)

The quality of the synthetic training data is crucial.
- **Rejection Sampling:** Using best-rated samples (from rejection sampling) is critical, significantly outperforming models trained with median or worst-rated samples.
- **Filtering Easy Questions:** Filtering out easy questions from the training set is the most effective strategy. Conversely, filtering out *hard* questions or applying no filtering at all significantly degrades performance, indicating that challenging examples are vital for developing robust long-context reasoning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies on Hyperparameters (Figure 5)

- **Blending Factor (η):** Optimal performance is achieved at a small, non-zero `η` (0.1). `η=0` (purely synergistic reward) is suboptimal due to sparse initial signals, while `η=1` (purely unconditional grounding reward) decouples grounding from the final answer, leading to inferior results.
- **F-score Parameter (β):** An optimal `β=2` is found, moderately prioritizing recall over precision. This indicates that retrieving all necessary evidence is more critical than avoiding a few irrelevant chunks for complex reasoning.
- **Robustness to Number of Chunks:** LongRLVR shows remarkable robustness to varying numbers of chunks per document (from 16 to 128), suggesting it learns a flexible semantic grounding policy rather than relying on specific chunking strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "LONGRLVR" paper introduces a highly significant contribution to the field of Large Language Models and Reinforcement Learning, with profound potential impact.

#### 5.1. Overcoming a Fundamental Limitation in RLVR for Long Contexts

The most critical significance of this work lies in formally identifying and effectively resolving the "vanishing grounding gradient" problem inherent in applying traditional, outcome-only RLVR to long-context tasks. By proving that sparse answer rewards fail to provide adequate learning signals for contextual grounding, the authors pinpoint a core bottleneck that has limited the reasoning capabilities of LLMs in extensive textual environments. LongRLVR's verifiable context reward directly addresses this by providing a dense, intermediate signal, enabling models to learn the crucial skill of evidence identification.

#### 5.2. Unlocking Full Reasoning Potential of LLMs in Long-Context Applications

The empirical results unequivocally demonstrate that LongRLVR empowers LLMs with significantly enhanced long-context reasoning abilities. The substantial performance gains over strong baselines, coupled with the ability to rival and even surpass much larger and specialized state-of-the-art models, highlight LongRLVR's capacity to unlock previously untapped potential in LLMs. This means that LLMs can now be more reliably deployed in applications requiring deep comprehension and synthesis of information from very long documents, such as:
- **Advanced Question Answering:** Handling complex multi-hop questions over entire books, research papers, or legal documents.
- **Document Summarization and Analysis:** Generating accurate summaries or extracting key insights from lengthy reports.
- **Code Understanding and Generation:** Reasoning over large codebases or documentation.
- **Information Retrieval and Synthesis:** More effectively interacting with vast external knowledge bases.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Parameter Efficiency and Accessibility

LongRLVR's ability to enable smaller models (e.g., 7B and 14B) to outperform much larger ones (e.g., 70B and 72B) demonstrates significant parameter efficiency. This has crucial implications for the accessibility and deployment of powerful long-context LLMs. Smaller, more efficient models are cheaper to train, fine-tune, and deploy, making advanced AI capabilities more widely available to researchers and developers with limited computational resources.

#### 5.4. Robustness and Generalizability

The ablation studies confirm the robustness of LongRLVR to varying numbers of chunks and the importance of carefully designed reward components and high-quality training data. The fact that the method works across different model families (LLaMA, Qwen) further suggests its generalizability.

#### 5.5. Advancing Research in Interpretability and Controllability

By explicitly modeling and rewarding the contextual grounding process, LongRLVR implicitly promotes a more transparent and potentially more controllable reasoning pathway. When a model indicates the "useful chunks" it used, this opens avenues for better understanding *why* an LLM arrived at a particular answer, moving towards more interpretable AI systems.

#### 5.6. Future Directions and Complementary Technologies

The authors note that LongRLVR is complementary to agentic workflows. By making individual LLM passes over large chunks more effective, LongRLVR could potentially enable agentic systems to process even larger contexts or improve their efficiency by requiring fewer iterative steps. This synergy could lead to even more powerful long-context AI systems. The high-quality synthetic data generation pipeline itself is also a valuable contribution, providing a scalable method for creating specialized datasets for grounding tasks.

In conclusion, LongRLVR represents a critical step forward in making LLMs truly effective and reliable for real-world applications that demand processing and reasoning over extensive textual information. Its theoretical grounding, empirical success, and practical implications mark it as a highly impactful piece of research.
