# Policy Newton Algorithm in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers developed Policy Newton in RKHS, a second-order optimization method for reinforcement learning policies in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces. This framework overcomes the challenge of infinite-dimensional Hessians, demonstrating faster convergence and superior asymptotic rewards on continuous control benchmarks compared to first-order and parametric second-order approaches.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies represented in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS) typically rely on first-order optimization methods, which suffer from slow convergence in complex landscapes.
- Applying second-order optimization (e.g., Policy Newton algorithms) to RKHS policies is hindered by the intractability of computing and inverting the infinite-dimensional Hessian operator in function spaces.
- Previous second-order RKHS methods primarily address online learning with static data, failing to account for the dynamic data distribution shifts inherent in reinforcement learning environments.

## Method

- The Policy Newton in RKHS algorithm employs a ==cubic regularized== auxiliary objective function to implicitly handle the infinite-dimensional Hessian, avoiding direct inversion.
- It leverages the Representer Theorem to transform the infinite-dimensional RKHS update step into a tractable, finite-dimensional quadratic optimization problem with respect to a coefficient vector.
- The iterative algorithm samples trajectories, estimates first and second-order coefficients from data, solves the cubic regularized subproblem for an update direction, and updates the RKHS policy.

## Results

- The algorithm offers theoretical guarantees for convergence to a local optimum and demonstrates a local quadratic convergence rate under idealized deterministic conditions.
- Empirical validation on a toy asset allocation problem showed Policy Newton in RKHS achieving quadratic convergence and successfully escaping suboptimal local maxima, unlike conventional Policy Newton.
- On standard continuous control benchmarks (CartPole, Lunar Lander), Policy Newton in RKHS exhibited faster convergence to higher episodic rewards compared to both first-order RKHS methods and parametric second-order baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The infinite-dimensional Hessian operator in RKHS can be circumvented by reformulating the Newton step as the minimizer of a cubic regularized objective, making the problem solvable.
> - The Representer Theorem enables expressing the RKHS policy update as a finite linear combination of kernel functions, effectively reducing the functional optimization to a standard Euclidean space problem.
> - Integrating second-order curvature information into RKHS policy optimization can lead to faster convergence and the ability to escape local optima, despite the non-parametric nature of RKHS policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) frequently employs policy representations to guide agent behavior. A prominent non-parametric approach involves representing these policies within Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS). RKHS-based policies offer representational flexibility, potential for improved sample efficiency, and adaptability during learning, and have been successfully applied in areas such as meta-RL and distributional RL. However, optimization methods for RKHS policies have predominantly been limited to first-order techniques, exemplified by RKHS Policy Gradient. These first-order methods are known to exhibit slower convergence, particularly in complex optimization landscapes characterized by high curvature or ill-conditioning.

In contrast, for parametric policy representations, second-order optimization methods, such as Policy Newton algorithms, incorporate Hessian curvature information to achieve faster convergence rates and more effective update scaling. The extension of these second-order methods to RKHS policies has been challenging due to the inherent difficulties in computing and inverting the infinite-dimensional Hessian operator in function spaces. Existing research on second-order RKHS methods has primarily focused on online learning settings where data distribution remains static, a condition not met in RL due to the dynamic interaction between the policy and the environment. This work addresses a recognized gap by introducing a second-order optimization framework for RKHS-based policies in the context of reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a second-order optimization framework for reinforcement learning policies represented in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS). This framework, termed Policy Newton in RKHS, aims to overcome the limitations of current RKHS-based policy optimization methods, which are largely restricted to first-order techniques.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and recognized benefits:
- **Convergence Speed:** First-order methods, while simpler to implement, often exhibit slow convergence, especially in optimization landscapes with high curvature or narrow valleys, which are common in complex RL problems. Second-order methods, by leveraging curvature information, have the potential for faster convergence.
- **Intractability of Hessian in RKHS:** A significant hurdle for applying Newton-like methods in RKHS is the intractability of explicitly computing and inverting the infinite-dimensional Hessian operator, which corresponds to the second-order Fréchet derivative of the expected cumulative reward function.
- **Bridging a Research Gap:** Previous second-order methods in RKHS have mainly focused on online learning with static data distributions. A method capable of handling the shifting data distributions characteristic of reinforcement learning environments has been absent, limiting the applicability of more advanced optimization techniques to RKHS policies in RL.
- **Leveraging RKHS Advantages:** Despite the optimization challenges, RKHS policies offer strong representational capabilities, sample efficiency, and dynamic adjustment, making a second-order optimization approach appealing if the computational barriers can be addressed.

By introducing Policy Newton in RKHS, the authors aim to enable the benefits of second-order optimization, such as improved convergence speed and the ability to escape suboptimal local optima, for non-parametric RKHS policy representations in dynamic RL settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Policy Newton in RKHS algorithm addresses the challenge of infinite-dimensional Hessian operators by adopting a novel two-pronged approach: circumventing direct inverse Hessian computation and leveraging the Representer Theorem for dimensionality reduction.

The algorithm builds upon the standard reinforcement learning objective of minimizing the expected discounted cumulative reward, J(π), where the policy π is represented by a function `h` in an RKHS, `H_K`.

**Addressing the Infinite-Dimensional Hessian:**
In contrast to parametric RL where the policy is parameterized by a finite vector `θ` and the Hessian is a finite matrix, the Hessian in RKHS corresponds to the second-order Fréchet derivative `∇²_h J(π_h)`. This derivative is an operator mapping from `H_K` to `H_K ⊗ H_K`, and its explicit inversion is generally intractable.
The authors circumvent this direct inversion by reformulating the problem. Instead of directly computing the Newton step `Δh` using `(∇²_h J(π_h))⁻¹ ∇_h J(π_h)`, they define `Δh` as the minimizer of a cubic regularized auxiliary objective function:
`Δh = argmin_h̄∈H_K ⟨∇_h J(h_k), h̄⟩ + (1/2)⟨∇²_h J(h_k) ◦ h̄, h̄⟩ + (β/6)∥h̄∥³`
Here, `∇_h J(h_k)` and `∇²_h J(h_k)` are the first and second-order Fréchet derivatives of the objective function with respect to `h` at the current policy `h_k`. The `β/6 ∥h̄∥³` term is a cubic regularization, commonly used in methods like cubic regularized Newton to ensure global convergence properties and make the optimization well-posed without requiring Hessian positivity.

**Derivation of RKHS Gradient and Hessian Operators:**
The first-order Fréchet derivative, `∇_h J(π_h)`, is derived based on the log-policy, `log π_h(a|s) = T h(s,a) - log Z`. It is expressed as an expectation over trajectories of `∑_{t=0}^{T-1} Ψ_t(τ) T [K((s_t, a_t), ·) - E_{a'∼π_h(·|s_t)}[K((s_t, a'), ·)]]`.
The second-order Fréchet derivative, `∇²_h J(π_h)`, which represents the Hessian operator, is derived using the chain rule and involves the outer product `H_K ⊗ H_K`. Lemma 3.1 details this operator, which includes terms like `(∑ Ψ_t(τ) ∇_h log π_h(x_t)) ⊗ (∑ ∇_h log π_h(x_t))` and `∑ Ψ_t(τ) T Cov_{a'∼π(·|s_t)}[K((s_t, a'), ·)]`.

**Finite-Dimensional Transformation using Representer Theorem:**
A crucial step is making the infinite-dimensional optimization tractable. The authors apply the Representer Theorem (Lemma 3.2), which states that for a regularized functional involving empirical data, the minimizer `h` can be represented as a finite linear combination of kernel sections `h(·) = ∑ α_i K(x_i, ·)`. In this context, the RKHS update step `Δh` is expressed as `Δh = ∑_{k=1}^{NT} α_k K(x_k, ·)`, where `x_k = (s_k, a_k)` are the state-action pairs from `N` sampled trajectories of length `T`. This transforms the optimization problem from the function space `H_K` into a finite-dimensional problem in `R^(NT)` with respect to the coefficient vector `α = {α_k}`.

**Reformulated Optimization Problem:**
Theorem 3.3 presents the transformed optimization problem as a standard quadratic optimization with cubic regularization in Euclidean space:
`α* = argmin_{α∈R^(NT)} ⟨v, α⟩ + (1/2)⟨Hα, α⟩ + (β/6)∥α∥³`
Here, `v` is a vector representing the first-order coefficients and `H` is a matrix representing the second-order coefficients, both derived from the kernel evaluations of the sampled trajectory data. Their components are explicitly defined in Theorem 3.3, involving kernel terms like `K((s_i, a_i), (s_l, a_l))` and covariance terms. The dimension of this problem scales with the volume of trajectory data (`NT`).

**Algorithm 1: Policy Newton RKHS Method:**
The overall algorithm is iterative. In each iteration `m`:
1. `N` trajectories are sampled using the current policy `π_h_m`.
2. The first-order coefficient vector `v` and second-order coefficient matrix `H` are estimated from the sampled trajectories according to Theorem 3.3.
3. The cubic regularized optimization problem (Equation 7) is solved to obtain the optimal coefficient vector `α`. The authors specify the use of the conjugate gradient method for this subproblem.
4. The RKHS update step `Δh` is constructed using `α` and the Representer Theorem.
5. The RKHS function `h` and the actor policy `π_h` are updated: `h_m+1 ← h_m + ηΔh`, where `η` is the learning rate.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
The paper establishes theoretical properties for the algorithm:
- **Monte Carlo Convergence Rate (Lemma 4.1):** It shows that the Monte Carlo estimates of the first and second-order Fréchet derivatives converge at a rate of `O(1/√N)`.
- **Suboptimality and Convergence:** Under assumptions of Lipschitz continuity for the Hessian (Assumption 4.1), the algorithm is proven to converge to a local optimum (Theorem 4.5), where the expected gradient norm at a randomly chosen iteration tends to zero as `M, N → ∞`.
- **Local Quadratic Convergence Rate (Theorem 4.6):** Under idealized deterministic conditions (i.e., true gradients and Hessians are available and certain inverse operator norms are bounded), the method demonstrates a local quadratic convergence rate, in contrast to the first-order rate of Policy Gradient in RKHS.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical validations for the Policy Newton in RKHS algorithm.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Convergence to Local Optimum:** The paper provides theoretical guarantees for the algorithm's convergence, demonstrating that the expected gradient norm at a randomly selected iteration converges to zero as the number of iterations (`M`) and the batch size (`N`) tend to infinity. This implies convergence to a stationary point, which is a local optimum.
- **Local Quadratic Convergence Rate:** Under idealized conditions where true gradients and Hessians are accessible (i.e., without estimation noise from Monte Carlo sampling), the deterministic version of the Policy Newton in RKHS algorithm is shown to achieve a local quadratic convergence rate. This is a faster rate compared to the first-order convergence typically observed in gradient-based methods.

#### 4.2. Empirical Findings

**a. Quadratic Convergence in a Toy Asset Allocation Problem:**
- Experiments on a simplified Asset Allocation environment (a toy problem detailed in Appendix E) were conducted to validate the theoretical quadratic convergence properties.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Four methods were compared: conventional Policy Gradient, conventional Policy Newton, Policy Gradient in RKHS, and Policy Newton in RKHS.
- **Convergence Behavior:** Policy Newton in RKHS exhibited clear quadratic convergence as it approached the optimal policy, confirming the theoretical predictions. Both Policy Gradient in RKHS and Policy Newton in RKHS converged rapidly towards the maximum expected episodic reward.
- **Optimization Trajectory:** While the standard Policy Newton method converged faster than the conventional Policy Gradient, its training trajectory showed greater instability and ultimately converged to a suboptimal local maximum. In contrast, Policy Newton in RKHS demonstrated the ability to escape such local optima, which is attributed to the infinite-dimensional feature representation capabilities of RKHS.

**b. Training Performance in Standard RL Benchmarks:**
- The algorithm's efficacy was evaluated on two continuous observation space environments from the Gymnasium framework: CartPole and Lunar Lander. Parametric baselines (Policy Gradient and Policy Newton) used a linear model augmented with a polynomial transformation for richer feature representations.
- **CartPole:** Policy Newton in RKHS demonstrated rapid convergence to the highest episodic reward. The standard Policy Newton method achieved comparable or inferior performance, requiring more training iterations. Both Policy Gradient in RKHS and standard Policy Gradient showed limited convergence within the same training steps.
- **Lunar Lander:** Similar trends were observed in the more challenging Lunar Lander environment. Policy Newton in RKHS exhibited fast convergence to superior episodic rewards. The standard Policy Newton performed comparably or worse, and the first-order methods (both RKHS and standard Policy Gradient) required significantly more training steps to approach optimal rewards.
- **Sample Efficiency and Asymptotic Performance:** The empirical results indicate that Policy Newton in RKHS achieves faster convergence speeds and higher asymptotic episodic rewards compared to established first-order RKHS approaches and parametric second-order methods in these benchmark environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces the first second-order optimization framework specifically designed for reinforcement learning policies represented within Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS). By successfully circumventing the computational intractability of the infinite-dimensional Hessian operator through cubic regularization and leveraging the Representer Theorem to convert the problem into a finite-dimensional optimization, the research bridges a notable gap between non-parametric policy representations and advanced second-order optimization techniques in reinforcement learning.

The significance of this contribution lies in several areas:
- **Enhanced Optimization for RKHS Policies:** It provides a method that can potentially overcome the slow convergence issues inherent in first-order RKHS policy optimization methods, thereby enabling more efficient learning for policies leveraging the representational flexibility of RKHS.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** The establishment of theoretical guarantees for convergence to a local optimum and a local quadratic convergence rate under deterministic conditions provides a rigorous basis for the algorithm's efficacy.
- **Empirical Performance:** Empirical results on both a controlled toy problem and standard RL benchmarks indicate that Policy Newton in RKHS can achieve faster convergence and higher episodic rewards compared to existing first-order RKHS methods and parametric second-order approaches. Its ability to escape local optima in the asset allocation task further highlights a practical advantage.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** By improving the optimization of RKHS-based policies, this work could contribute to more robust and sample-efficient solutions in various RL domains where non-parametric representations are beneficial, such as those with complex or evolving state-action spaces.

Future work may involve exploring the robustness and stability of Policy Newton in RKHS on more complex RL problems, such as humanoid control, and investigating integrations with neural network architectures to further enhance performance in demanding scenarios. The development of this theoretical and empirically validated framework provides a foundation for subsequent advancements in second-order optimization for non-parametric policies.
