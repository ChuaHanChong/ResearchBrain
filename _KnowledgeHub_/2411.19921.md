# SIMS: Simulating Stylized Human-Scene Interactions with Retrieval-Augmented Script Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> SIMS is a hierarchical framework for physically simulated stylized human-scene interaction: an LLM with Retrieval-Augmented Script Generation plans long-term keyframe scripts, and a multi-condition physics policy (text style, scene heightmap, task goal) executes them via a finite state machine, outperforming prior kinematic and physics-based baselines on success rate, contact error, and motion diversity.

## Problem

- Kinematics-based long-term HSI methods produce penetration, floating and foot-skating artifacts and lack physical plausibility for real-time use.
- Existing physics-based HSI controllers (InterScene, UniHSI) support only a small fixed skill set and simplistic chronological or contact-only planning, lacking style diversity.
- Direct LLM script generation for long-term motion is redundant, low-diversity, and fails to maintain coherent narrative or stylistic variation across keyframes.

## Method

- **Retrieval-Augmented Script Generation (RASG)** builds a short-script database of styled keyframes, retrieves top-k ==CLIP-similarity== matches per theme, then has the LLM stitch them into coherent long-term scripts.
- A ==multi-condition physics-based control policy== conditions on proprioception, an egocentric heightmap, task goal state, and CLIP-derived language embeddings to produce style-consistent, scene-aware motions via ==goal-conditioned RL== with a ==text-conditioned discriminator==.
- A ==finite state machine== schedules seven reusable skill policies (Walk, Idle, Sit, Lie, Reach, GetUp, Carry) using keyframe-derived skill, caption, and heightmap conditions, plus a newly collected **ViconStyle** mocap dataset for stylized motions.

## Results

- SIMS achieves higher success rates and lower contact error than InterPhys, InterScene, and UniHSI on most skills, e.g. **98.1%** sit and **87.6%** lie versus UniHSI's **94.3%** and **81.5%**.
- On motion diversity, SIMS attains much lower FID and far higher APD than UniHSI (Sit APD **16.55** vs **1.14**), indicating substantially more diverse generated motions.
- Ablations show RASG lowers SBERT similarity to **0.7759** versus **0.8167** for direct LLM generation while cutting generation time from **12.2s** to **7.32s**, and removing text or heightmap conditioning degrades success rate and diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level narrative planning (LLM plus retrieval) from low-level physical control lets each component specialize, giving both stylistic richness and physical plausibility simultaneously.
> - Retrieval augmentation over a curated short-script database can outperform raw LLM generation for long-horizon planning tasks, improving both diversity and efficiency.
> - Conditioning physics controllers on language embeddings alongside scene geometry is an effective, scalable way to inject fine-grained style without dense motion-reference tracking, and new skills can be added without retraining the whole system.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The simulation of realistic and expressive human-scene interactions (HSI) is a long-standing challenge in computer graphics, robotics, and virtual reality (VR)/augmented reality (AR). The research landscape for HSI has evolved through several stages:

Initially, **kinematics-based methods** (e.g., NSM, SAMP, Humanise, AffordMotion, TesMo cited in Table 1) focused on generating human motions using geometric rules or data-driven approaches without explicitly modeling physical forces. While these methods achieved some success in generating diverse movements, they notoriously suffered from "physical artifacts" such as foot skating, object penetration, and lack of physical realism, often requiring extensive post-processing. They also typically relied on continuous keyframe control or simplistic goal positions, limiting their ability to generate complex, long-term narratives with diverse styles.

More recently, the field has shifted towards **physics-based methods** (e.g., InterPhys, InterScene, UniHSI). These approaches integrate physics simulators (like Isaac Gym) to produce motions that are inherently physically plausible, avoiding common artifacts. However, previous physics-based methods often faced limitations in **skill diversity** (restricted to a small number of specific actions), **stylistic expression** (lacking emotional or stylistic nuances), and **long-term planning capabilities** (simplistic chronological lists of actions). UniHSI, for example, introduced an LLM planner but primarily focused on contact sequences, still struggling with stylistic diversity and generalizable control.

SIMS positions itself as a crucial advancement that bridges the gaps left by previous works. It aims to overcome the simultaneous challenges of **physical plausibility**, **stylistic diversity**, and **long-term narrative coherence** in HSI. By explicitly integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for high-level script planning and coupling it with a versatile multi-condition physics-based control policy, SIMS offers a unified solution. This approach addresses the lack of expressive and diverse human behaviors that previous physics-based methods struggled with, while maintaining the physical realism absent in kinematic approaches. The paper's explicit comparison table (Table 1) clearly delineates SIMS's superior coverage across physical plausibility, planning, automaticity, style diversity, text-awareness, scene-awareness, skill scalability, and comprehensive skill incorporation, making it a state-of-the-art solution that synthesizes the strengths of prior research while mitigating their weaknesses.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SIMS framework are:

1. **To achieve both diverse style and physical plausibility in human-scene interactions:** Prior works often excelled in one aspect but fell short in the other. Kinematics-based methods offered some diversity but lacked realism, while physics-based methods provided realism but lacked diverse stylistic expression. SIMS aims to unify these two critical aspects.
2. **To enable long-term, coherent, and expressive character narratives:** Generating motions that naturally unfold over extended periods and convey rich narratives, emotions, or styles is challenging. Traditional methods often produced simplistic or disjointed action sequences. SIMS seeks to generate "long-form scripts" that provide a semantically rich foundation for motion planning, allowing characters to perform diverse skills within complex 3D scenes based on daily narratives.
3. **To bridge high-level script-driven intent with low-level control policies:** The goal is to create a hierarchical framework where abstract story themes can automatically translate into detailed, physically plausible character movements. This involves developing an intelligent planner that interprets high-level commands and a robust controller that executes them.
4. **To develop a versatile and adaptable control policy:** The low-level controller needs to be capable of executing a broad repertoire of motor skills (locomotion, HSI, dynamic object interaction) with diverse styles, while also being aware of and adapting to complex environmental geometries and task goals.
5. **To ensure scalability and extensibility:** The framework should allow for the easy integration of new skills and styles without requiring complete retraining of the entire system. This modularity is crucial for practical applications.
6. **To address the scarcity of stylized motion data:** The authors acknowledge the limited availability of datasets that combine diverse styles with detailed motion annotations. A key objective is to contribute a comprehensive dataset to facilitate future research in this area.

In essence, SIMS is motivated by the desire to create virtual characters that can "come to life" in digital environments, performing complex, expressive, and physically believable actions that align with intricate human narratives, thereby advancing fields like animation, embodied AI, robotics, and VR/AR.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SIMS introduces a novel hierarchical framework that seamlessly integrates high-level script generation with a low-level physics-based control policy, mediated by a Finite State Machine (FSM).

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Framework Overview

The system operates in two main stages:
- **High-level Script Planning**: Utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to produce coherent and stylized long-term scripts from user-provided story themes.
- **Low-level Control Policy**: Employs a multi-condition physics-based control policy to execute the generated scripts, translating keyframes into detailed, physically plausible, and stylized character motions within a 3D scene.
- **Finite State Machine (FSM)**: Bridges these two levels by managing transitions between different low-level control policies based on the keyframes from the high-level script.

#### 3.2. Short Script Database Construction

The foundation of the high-level planning is a database of "short scripts." Each short script `p` is a sequence of keyframes `f = (s, o, c, e)`, specifying:
- `s`: skill (e.g., sit, walk, carry)
- `o`: target object
- `c`: caption describing motion attributes
- `e`: emotion or style (e.g., happy, sad, tired)
Additionally, each short script has a concise one-sentence summary `u` and a distinctive style label `d`. An LLM is prompted to generate these short scripts, ensuring coherence and varied styles. CLIP embeddings of these summaries are extracted to facilitate retrieval.

#### 3.3. Retrieval-Augmented Script Generation (RASG)

To generate long-term scripts, SIMS employs a RAG-inspired method:
1. **Style Identification**: The LLM first identifies `M` styles most relevant to the user-provided story theme.
2. **Semantic Similarity Retrieval**: The user's theme is converted into a CLIP feature, used to retrieve the `top-k` short scripts for each identified style from the database, based on cosine similarity with their summaries.
3. **Summary Filtering and Long Script Creation**: The retrieved summaries are fed back to the LLM. Based on these and the scene layout, the LLM selects and logically concatenates suitable summaries and their corresponding keyframes to form a cohesive, long-term narrative. A rule-based system for skill tuples (e.g., (sit, getup)) and transition motions (e.g., walk) ensures executable and fluent sequences.

#### 3.4. Multi-Condition Controller

The low-level control policy `π(a_t | s_t, h_t, g_t, z)` is a goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework trained to produce actions `a_t` based on:
- `s_t`: Humanoid proprioception (joint angles, velocities).
- `h_t`: Egocentric heightmap, representing surrounding scene geometries for collision avoidance and interaction. This is a 12x12 grid updated when an object is close.
- `g_t`: Task-specific goal state, specifying high-level task objectives (e.g., contact with furniture, moving an object).
- `z`: Language embedding, encoded from text descriptions (captions/styles) using a re-implemented MotionCLIP model to imbue stylistic cues.

The policy is trained using a reward function `r_t = λ_style r_style_t + λ_task r_task_t`, where `r_style_t` comes from a text-conditioned motion discriminator (for style control) and `r_task_t` from task-specific rewards.

#### 3.5. Finite State Machine (FSM)

The FSM orchestrates the execution of the long-term script by switching between several reusable, task-specific policies: Walk, Idle, Sit, Lie, Reach, GetUp, and Carry. Transition rules (e.g., based on overlap time between character and target position) determine when to switch skills. The FSM incorporates heightmaps and text embeddings to ensure scene understanding and semantic control during transitions.

#### 3.6. Universal Goal Conditions

To simplify development across diverse tasks, skills are categorized into three templates:
- **Loco (Walk, Idle)**: Requires pelvis positioning at a 2D target.
- **HSI (Sit, Lie, Reach, GetUp)**: Requires a specific body joint (pelvis for sit/lie/getup, hand for reach) to contact an interactable surface on a target object.
- **DOI (Carry)**: Encourages moving a dynamic object's root to a 3D target location, considering object bounding box and target coordinates.

#### 3.7. Policy Training

Seven task-specific policies are trained. Walk, Idle, Sit, Lie, and Carry policies are provided with text conditions to capture diverse styles. Initializations involve random sampling of objects and character positions, with "Walk" motion data used for reference state initialization across all skills to facilitate transitions.

#### 3.8. Datasets

SIMS utilizes a mixture of six existing motion datasets (SAMP, COUCH, Circles, 100Style, AMASS) and introduces a new dataset, **ViconStyle**, captured with a Vicon optical motion capture system. ViconStyle aims to supplement limitations in categories and quantity of stylized motion data, specifically for actions like stylized carrying or lying. All motion clips are annotated with captions and style labels (8 categories beyond neutral: happy, angry, hurried, tired, sad, stressed, drunk, relaxed), and are augmented via left-right flipping. 3D objects and scene layouts are primarily sourced from the 3DFront dataset, with voxelization and segmentation applied for affordance surfaces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SIMS demonstrates significant advancements across multiple evaluation metrics and scenarios:

1. **Superior Physical Performance and Task Success:**
 - In comparison with SOTA physics-based methods (InterPhys, InterScene, UniHSI), SIMS achieves better or comparable Success Rates and Contact Error across various skills (Sit, Lie, Reach, Carry) (Table 3).
 - Notably, SIMS maintains strong performance even with randomly sampled text conditions, highlighting its ability to incorporate style without sacrificing physical plausibility.
 - The policies trained on a comprehensive mixture of datasets, including the new ViconStyle data, show improved success rates for skills like Lie and Carry.

2. **Enhanced Motion Diversity:**
 - SIMS significantly outperforms UniHSI and re-implemented InterPhys in terms of motion diversity, as measured by Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Average Pairwise Distance (APD) (Table 4).
 - Lower FID values indicate that SIMS-generated motions are closer to the distribution of real reference motions, suggesting higher realism and quality.
 - Higher APD values confirm that SIMS generates motions with much greater diversity compared to the nearly identical motions produced by UniHSI, showcasing its effectiveness in stylistic expression.

3. **Improved Long-Term Script Generation (RASG):**
 - The Retrieval-Augmented Script Generation (RASG) method produces more diverse scripts (lower SBERT Similarity) and is more efficient (lower Average Generation Time) compared to direct LLM generation (Table 6). This validates the effectiveness of the RAG approach in preventing redundancy and improving coherence.

4. **Strong Generalization Capabilities:**
 - SIMS policies trained solely on 3DFront objects demonstrate good physical performance (Success Rate and Contact Error) when tested on unseen objects from the PartNet dataset (Table 7). This highlights the generalization ability of the heightmap design and multi-condition control.
 - The framework's ability to scale up with new motion datasets is proven; adding more stylized data (e.g., ViconStyle for Carry) consistently improves both success rates and diversity (Tables 8, 9, 10).

5. **Robustness of Policy Settings (Ablation Studies):**
 - Ablation studies confirm the critical role of key components: policies without text embedding showed degraded APD (reduced diversity), and policies without heightmap exhibited worse performance when interacting with objects (reduced scene awareness) (Table 11). This validates the multi-condition design.

6. **Positive User Study Feedback:**
 - A user study on rendered videos revealed that SIMS significantly outperformed UniHSI across metrics such as Physical Realism, Motion Diversity, Plot Engagement, and Emotional Resonance (Table 5). This qualitative feedback underscores the perceived superiority of SIMS in delivering expressive and believable long-term animations.

7. **Qualitative Results:**
 - Figure 3 visually demonstrates the system's ability to generate complex, long-term narratives with detailed keyframes and vivid stories in intricate 3D scenes.
 - Figure 4 provides qualitative examples of individual skills (Carry, Idle, Walk, Sit, Lie) executed with diverse stylistic cues, showcasing the fine-grained control achieved by the text-conditioned policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SIMS framework represents a significant step forward in the field of human-scene interaction simulation, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Breakthrough in Expressive and Physically Plausible HSI:** SIMS successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of simultaneously achieving physical realism, diverse stylistic expression, and long-term narrative coherence. This unified solution overcomes the limitations of previous kinematic and physics-based methods, opening new possibilities for generating highly realistic and engaging virtual characters.

2. **Enhanced Control and Automation:** By leveraging LLMs with RAG for script generation, SIMS introduces an intuitive and automatic pipeline for creating complex character behaviors from high-level textual descriptions. This reduces the manual effort traditionally required in animation and simulation, making character creation more accessible and scalable.

3. **Scalability and Extensibility:** The modular design of SIMS, allowing for easy integration of new skills and styles by updating the script database and training new policies, is a crucial advantage. This ensures the framework can adapt to evolving requirements and a wider range of applications without complete system overhauls.

4. **New Data Resources:** The creation and annotation of the ViconStyle dataset, alongside the comprehensive restructuring of existing motion datasets, provides valuable resources for future research. This contribution is essential for advancing data-driven approaches to stylized motion generation, particularly in addressing the scarcity of motion data with rich emotional and stylistic labels.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Animation and Entertainment:** SIMS can revolutionize character animation in video games, film, and virtual production, enabling more dynamic, emotionally rich, and physically believable non-player characters (NPCs) and virtual actors.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** The generation of natural and stylized human interactions within virtual environments will significantly enhance immersion and user experience in VR/AR applications, from social simulations to training platforms.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** The framework's ability to generate physically plausible and context-aware human motions can inform the development of more human-like robots and embodied AI agents, particularly for human-robot interaction and tasks requiring nuanced social cues.
- **Simulation and Digital Twins:** SIMS can contribute to more realistic simulations for architectural design, urban planning, and digital twins, where understanding human behavior and interaction within physical spaces is critical.

**Future Work:**
The authors identify several promising avenues for future research, including:
- **Collecting more diverse stylized motion data:** Further expanding datasets with realistic emotions and styles remains a priority.
- **Exploring humanoid models with articulated fingers:** This would enable more detailed and realistic object manipulation.
- **Introducing multi-agent interactions:** Expanding the framework to handle multiple characters interacting with each other and the environment would unlock more complex social simulations.

Overall, SIMS establishes a robust and innovative framework that pushes the boundaries of physically simulated, stylized human-scene interactions, laying a strong foundation for future advancements in virtual character intelligence and realism.
