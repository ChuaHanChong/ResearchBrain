# RGB-D Video Generation for Improving Human-to-Robot Object Handover Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> PassGen is a Stable-Video-Diffusion pipeline that synthesizes full-body RGB-D human-to-robot handover videos, adding an Intention-Aware Temporal Face Encoder for gaze cues and morphology-based depth erosion that mimics RealSense L515 sensor voids. It builds Hand2Bot, 5,000 real-plus-generated video pairs, and a gaze-fused intention gate that reaches 90.0% accuracy, 13.6% false-positive rate, and 54/60 real UR5e trigger success.

## Problem

- Existing handover datasets are largely synthetic, with clean depth lacking edge scattering, lighting variation and motion blur, so trained models face a wide sim-to-real gap.
- Mainstream handover benchmarks use hand-centric or egocentric views, discarding gaze, head orientation and upper-body posture that precede and predict handover initiation.
- Human video generators model pose and appearance but ignore fine-grained hand-object contact and produce RGB only, while depth synthesizers over-smooth away sensor void patterns.

## Method

- **PassGen** fine-tunes a ==Stable Video Diffusion== backbone with ==LoRA==, conditioning on a ==ReferenceNet== appearance encoder plus a ==PoseNet== over ==DWPose== skeletons for physically grounded handover motion.
- An ==Intention-Aware Temporal Face Encoder== embeds ==ArcFace features== through ==temporal attention== and feed-forward blocks, injecting gaze and micro-expression tokens into the U-Net by ==cross-attention==.
- Stage II initializes depth with ==DepthCrafter==, then applies ==height-dependent morphological edge erosion== to reinstate L515 void artifacts; an ==Intention Gating== module fuses gaze confidence with object approach velocity under a monotone non-decreasing trigger.

## Results

- PassGen tops all animation baselines on Hand2Bot-Real, reaching **25.12** PSNR, **0.909** SSIM, **0.212** LPIPS, **91.32** FID and **337.59** FVD versus StableAnimator's **23.17** PSNR and **372.81** FVD.
- The full intention gate scores **90.0%** mean accuracy and **13.6%** FPR, against **95.5%** FPR with no cues, **31.8%** gaze-only and **77.3%** object-only; ablating the face encoder raises FVD to **373.61**.
- On a physical UR5e, gating gives **54/60** intention success and **2/30** false triggers versus **25/30** without it; adding **2,875** synthetic clips lifts accuracy **87.5%** to **90.0%** and unseen-object success **6/10** to **7/10**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deliberately re-injecting sensor-specific artifacts beats maximizing depth reconstruction fidelity; over-smoothed synthetic geometry causes downstream policies to overfit idealized structure.
> - Social precursors such as gaze and facial micro-expression enable proactive intent anticipation that local hand tracking cannot, cutting false triggers from 83% to 7%.
> - Generative augmentation here is credibly a variance-reduction and false-positive-suppression tool, not proof of broad manipulation generalization, given ten-trial unseen-object samples without seed statistics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Human-to-robot (H2R) object handover is a fundamental capability for establishing effective human-robot collaboration across various applications, including assistive robotics, service robots, and industrial assembly lines. Anticipating human communicative intent is a critical prerequisite for efficient and safe object transfer and complex downstream manipulation tasks.

Despite recent advancements in robotics, the development of robust and generalizable learning models for H2R handover is challenged by several limitations in existing research paradigms and datasets. A primary issue is the scarcity of large-scale, human-centric datasets that accurately reflect real-world conditions. Many existing datasets are synthetic (e.g., HandoverSim, GenH2R), offering scalability and control but failing to capture the complex noise characteristics inherent in physical sensors, such as edge scattering, lighting variations, and motion blur in depth maps. This discrepancy between synthetic and real data contributes to a significant simulation-to-reality (sim-to-real) gap, as models trained on clean synthetic geometry may not perform robustly when deployed on physical systems.

A second limitation concerns the scope of existing datasets. Mainstream benchmarks (e.g., DexH2R, DexYCB) predominantly feature egocentric or hand-centric views. While these focus on immediate hand-object interactions, they omit the broader human body context, which is crucial for natural and timely intention prediction. Global human features—such as facial expressions, gaze direction, head orientation, and upper-body posture—are recognized as important social signals that often precede and indicate handover intentions. Neglecting these cues limits the ability of robotic systems to engage in socially aware behavior.

Furthermore, state-of-the-art generative models, while advancing in human video generation, often do not adequately address the specific requirements of H2R handover scenarios. General human video generation tasks typically do not focus on human-object interactions from a robot's perspective. Existing depth synthesis pipelines prioritize visual reconstruction fidelity, often producing overly smoothed depth maps that lack the characteristic void patterns and noise distributions of standard physical sensors, further hindering their utility for training robust robotic perception systems. These gaps collectively limit the progress in developing H2R handover systems that are both effective and intuitive in shared human-robot workspaces.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the limitations of existing H2R handover datasets and generative models by constructing a comprehensive, high-fidelity dataset and developing a robust synthesis framework. The motivation stems from the recognition that current H2R systems are hampered by two critical factors: the scarcity of real-world, full-body, multimodal (RGB-D) data and the pronounced sim-to-real gap.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives are:

1. **Develop a comprehensive H2R dataset (Hand2Bot):** To overcome the data scarcity, the authors aim to create a dataset that captures full human body context, including subtle social cues like facial expressions and gaze, alongside detailed hand-object interactions. This dataset is designed to incorporate realistic sensor noise patterns from physical depth sensors, which is absent in most synthetic datasets, thereby facilitating more robust sim-to-real transfer.
2. **Propose an algorithmic solution for data generation (PassGen):** Given the prohibitive cost and overhead of manually capturing and annotating large-scale real-world multimodal data, an algorithmic solution for synthesizing high-fidelity, interactive RGB-D sequences is a core objective. This generative pipeline is intended to augment real-world data, thereby increasing dataset scale and diversity.
3. **Synthesize realistic RGB videos with social cues:** PassGen is motivated to leverage advancements in video diffusion models while specifically embedding an Intention-Aware Temporal Face Encoder (TFE). This component is designed to capture high-frequency facial micro-expressions and gaze focus, which are crucial precursors for anticipating handover intentions in H2R scenarios. The goal is to ensure hand-object consistency and integrate explicit social gaze cues into the generated RGB sequences.
4. **Simulate realistic depth sensor noise:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap, a significant motivation is to develop a method for synthesizing depth maps that accurately replicate the stochastic void patterns, edge scattering, and surface absorption characteristics of real-world depth sensors (e.g., Intel RealSense L515). This contrasts with existing depth synthesis methods that produce overly smoothed, idealized depth maps.
5. **Enable proactive and socially aware robotic behavior:** The overarching motivation is to improve the robot's ability to proactively anticipate human handover intentions, reduce false triggers during ambient human motion, and facilitate earlier intention anticipation. This is pursued through an independent Intention Gating (IG) mechanism that leverages global social cues to trigger grasp trajectories, aiming to enable more natural and safer human-robot collaboration.

In summary, the research aims to provide a unified generative and control framework that produces high-fidelity, sensor-realistic RGB-D data, thereby enabling more robust and socially aware H2R object handover systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the creation of a new RGB-D video dataset, Hand2Bot, and the development of a generative pipeline, PassGen, designed to augment this dataset with realistic synthetic data. The framework integrates advanced video generation techniques with explicit sensor noise modeling and an intention-gating mechanism for robotic control.

#### 3.1. Hand2Bot Dataset Construction

The Hand2Bot dataset is constructed to provide comprehensive, multimodal data for H2R handover. It comprises 5,000 RGB-D video pairs, with 2,125 collected from real-world interactions and 2,875 generated by the PassGen pipeline.
- **Real-world Data Collection:** An Intel RealSense L515 camera was used to capture data in 7 diverse indoor scenes involving 33 common household objects.
- **Intention-Centric Labeling:** The dataset includes approximately 20% negative cases where humans interact with objects but do not intend to hand them over, alongside positive handover cases characterized by gaze-directed object approach motions. This design aims to prevent bias toward scenarios where intention is easily inferable. Timestamp-based labels mark the exact frames where handover intention is initiated, defining the "presentation period."
- **Grasp Pose Labeling:** To support downstream robotic manipulation, 6-DoF parallel-gripper grasp poses are generated using GraspNet. The workspace mask is constrained to the object region. A vertical-alignment filtering strategy prioritizes top-down vertical grasp trajectories while ensuring kinematic feasibility and collision avoidance with human hands. A temporal consistency filter selects stable pose sequences during the presentation period.

#### 3.2. PassGen Generative Pipeline

PassGen is a two-stage pipeline designed to synthesize realistic human-object interaction RGB videos and then reconstruct corresponding depth maps with realistic noise patterns.

**Stage I: Pose-Guided RGB Video Generation**
This stage leverages a diffusion-based backbone, specifically a fine-tuned Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model, conditioned on reference identity and precise motion sequences.
- **Control Signal Integration:**
 - An Appearance Encoder (based on ReferenceNet) extracts identity features from a reference image ($I_{ref}$).
 - A lightweight PoseNet (utilizing DWPose for extraction) processes per-frame skeletons ($V_{ref}$) to provide motion guidance. These deterministic physical signals constrain the latent space.
- **Intention-Aware Temporal Face Encoder (TFE):** To embed subtle handover intentions, a dedicated TFE branch augments the pose guidance. It extracts facial embeddings using ArcFace and processes them through cascaded Temporal Attention (TA) and Feed Forward (FFN) blocks. The TFE treats the facial region as a high-frequency semantic stream, generating temporally consistent face tokens ($\tilde{F}_t$) that preserve the giver’s visual focus, a critical social signal for distinguishing intentional handovers from ambient movements.
- **Denoising and Training:** The SVD backbone is fine-tuned using the LoRA scheme on the Hand2Bot-Real dataset. The combined pose-facial guidance ($\tilde{P}_t$), integrating structural skeletons and social face tokens, is injected into the U-Net via cross-attention layers, ensuring spatial alignment and contextual validation by explicit human social intention in every generated frame.

**Stage II: Morphological Depth Video Generation**
This stage focuses on bridging the sim-to-real gap by simulating specific noise characteristics of the Intel RealSense L515 sensor.
- **Depth Initialization:** An initial affine-invariant depth map ($D_{init}$) is generated using DepthCrafter to ensure temporal smoothness and unified scale, followed by height-based linear alignment to the human mask.
- **Realistic Noise Simulation:** The authors record the noise distribution ($N_0$) from their real-world data collection. A morphology-based erosion strategy is implemented, using boundary width vectors $w(h)$ relative to pixel height $h$ to erode the generated depth ($D_{gen}$). This process approximates the boundary-level depth artifacts and void pixel patterns characteristic of the L515 sensor, producing a final realistic depth output ($D_{final}$) with inter-frame alignment.

#### 3.3. Intention-Gated Perception and Control System

An independent Intention Gating (IG) mechanism is introduced to transition from reactive grasping to proactive, socially cooperative interaction.
- **Physical Camera Configuration:** The Intel RealSense L515 sensor is strategically positioned on the UR5e robot base, tilted upwards to encompass the participant’s upper body, arm kinematics, and ensure sufficient facial spatial resolution for micro-expression and gaze tracking.
- **Multimodal Fusion Formulation:** The IG module continuously processes synchronized multimodal features to compute a unified scalar intention score $S_{intent}(t) \in$. This score is a weighted sum of the temporal gaze focus confidence ($f_{gaze}(\tilde{F}_t)$) extracted by the TFE, and the physical approaching velocity of the object ($v_{obj}(t)$) derived from masked depth boundaries. Scaling coefficients ($w_g, w_v$) balance these dimensionless and metric inputs.
- **Monotone Constraints and Sensitivity Analysis:** To prevent unstable state switching in dynamic interactions, a monotone non-decreasing constraint is enforced on the intention score: $\hat{S}_{intent}(t) = \max(\hat{S}_{intent}(t-1), S_{intent}(t))$. A parallel-jaw gripper's grasp trajectory is initiated only if $\hat{S}_{intent}(t) \geq \tau$, where $\tau$ is a safety activation threshold. A sensitivity analysis determined $\tau = 0.80$ as an optimal balance between responsiveness and false trigger suppression.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides evaluations of PassGen's generative fidelity and its utility for improving downstream robotic intention sensing, both in simulation and on a physical robot.

#### 4.1. Animation Results and Ablation Studies

- **Generative Fidelity (Table 2, Fig. 4):** PassGen demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art animation models (e.g., MagicPose, AnimateAnyone, StableAnimator) across image-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and video-level metrics (FID, FID-VID, FVD). This indicates higher spatial fidelity and temporal stability in the generated RGB sequences. Qualitatively, PassGen maintained better hand-object consistency and avoided artifacts such as object distortion or finger-mask flickering during rapid handover motions, which were observed in comparison methods.
- **Ablation Study on PassGen Modules (Table 3):** The study quantifies the contributions of the proposed conditioning modules. Removing the Temporal Face Encoder (TFE) leads to a notable drop in FVD (from 337.59 to 399.52), confirming the importance of gaze and facial consistency for realistic human-to-robot interaction synthesis.

#### 4.2. Depth Fidelity Analysis

- **Realistic Noise Simulation (Fig. 5):** The generated depth maps from PassGen intentionally incorporate morphological artifacts and noise patterns that mimic physical L515 sensors, unlike generic depth estimation methods (e.g., DepthCrafter) that produce overly "clean" edges. This characteristic is deemed critical for training robust robotic perception policies that do not overfit to idealized synthetic geometry.

#### 4.3. Intention Gating Performance and Ablation Study

- **Module Efficacy (Table 4):** An ablation study on the Intention Score Module within the Hand2Bot-Real evaluation subset revealed that the full module (combining Gaze and Object terms) achieved the best overall performance with a Mean Accuracy of 90.0% and a False Positive Rate (FPR) of 13.6%. The Gaze-only term improved accuracy (86.4%) and significantly reduced FPR (31.8%) compared to a baseline lacking both terms (95.5% FPR). This supports the integration of visual attention with spatial reaching cues for robust intention sensing.

#### 4.4. Real H2R Handover Evaluation

- **Physical Robot Deployment (Table 5):** The system was deployed on a UR5e robot with 10 distinct objects (5 seen, 5 unseen). The full module with Intention Gating (IG) achieved a Mean Intention Success Rate (ISR) of 54/60 and a low overall False Trigger Rate (FTR) of 2/30. In contrast, the baseline configuration (without IG) exhibited a high FTR of 25/30, indicating its inability to distinguish intentional handovers from ambient movements.
- **Failure Analysis:** Performance degradation in irregular and unseen object categories (ISR of 80% and 70%) was attributed to the object term of the intention score struggling with complex geometries and physical grasping challenges for the parallel-jaw gripper.
- **Safety:** The low FTR with IG active indicates the gaze-aware module effectively prevents accidental triggers, confirming its safety and necessity in shared human-robot workspaces.

#### 4.5. Downstream Utility: Real-Only vs. Augmented Training

- **Synthetic Data Augmentation (Table 6):** Training the intention prediction model with PassGen-synthesized data (Real + Syn) significantly improved downstream tracking accuracy (90.0% vs. 87.5%), reduced FPR (13.6% vs. 22.8%), and enhanced robustness for unseen objects (7/10 ISR vs. 6/10 ISR) compared to training solely on real-world data. This outcome supports that the structural pose constraints and facial signal modeling in PassGen contribute to increased physical variability and robustness.

#### 4.6. Gating Sensitivity Analysis

- **Threshold Optimization:** An offline parametric sensitivity sweep for the activation threshold ($\tau$) of the intention score determined $\tau = 0.80$ as the optimal configuration. This setting balanced maximizing intent tracking sensitivity for smooth human-robot transitions with strictly dampening the continuous frame-level FPR to 13.6%, preventing premature latching or interactive deadlocks observed with more aggressive ($\tau = 0.60$) or conservative ($\tau = 0.90$) thresholds.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses critical limitations in human-to-robot (H2R) object handover by tackling the scarcity of real-world, human-centric datasets and the simulation-to-reality (sim-to-real) gap. The contributions have several implications for the development of more capable and socially aware robotic systems.

- **Enhanced Data Availability and Quality:** The introduction of Hand2Bot, a comprehensive RGB-D video dataset, significantly augments the resources available for training H2R systems. By providing rich contextual information, including full-body posture, facial expressions, and gaze direction, the dataset enables the development of models that leverage a broader range of human social cues, moving beyond limited hand-centric views. The inclusion of negative cases and detailed intention-centric labeling further refines the dataset's utility for robust intention prediction.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The PassGen generative pipeline, particularly its morphology-based depth noise simulation, directly addresses the sim-to-real gap. By replicating realistic sensor artifacts (e.g., void patterns, edge scattering) of physical depth cameras, PassGen-synthesized data helps train robotic perception models that are more robust to real-world sensor noise, preventing overfitting to idealized synthetic geometries. This capability is relevant for deploying machine learning policies from simulated environments to physical robots.
- **Proactive and Socially Aware Robotic Interaction:** The Intention Gating (IG) mechanism, which fuses temporal gaze focus with object approaching velocity, enables robots to proactively anticipate human handover intentions. This shifts robotic behavior from reactive grasping to more intuitive and cooperative interaction. The demonstrated reduction in false trigger rates and earlier intention anticipation contribute to safer and more natural human-robot collaboration in shared workspaces, benefiting applications such as assistive robots, service robots, and industrial co-robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The Hand2Bot dataset and the PassGen framework provide a practical foundation for further research in socially aware robotic perception and proactive control. The structured annotations (6-DoF grasp poses, timestamped intention labels) can facilitate advancements in both high-level intention sensing and low-level robotic manipulation planning. The work also highlights avenues for future exploration, such as incorporating environmental diversity and conducting formalized user studies to evaluate subjective user comfort and psychological fluency, which are essential for truly natural human-robot interaction.

In essence, this work contributes to the advancement of human-robot collaboration by providing both the data and the tools necessary for training more perceptive, robust, and socially intelligent robotic systems capable of engaging in complex, intention-driven interactions.
