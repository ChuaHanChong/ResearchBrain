# Interleaved Latent Visual Reasoning with Selective Perceptual Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from a collaborative team including China University of Geosciences and the University of Southern California developed Interleaved Latent Visual Reasoning (ILVR), a framework that enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform dynamic, multi-step visual reasoning by operating entirely in a latent space. This approach reduces inference latency by 8x to 18x compared to pixel-based methods while achieving state-of-the-art performance and strong generalization across various reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing interleaved image-text reasoning approaches in MLLMs are computationally expensive due to repeated re-encoding of pixel-dense images during multi-step processes.
- Current latent visual reasoning methods are typically single-step or over-compress visual features, failing to capture dynamic intermediate state evolution or retaining crucial fine-grained perceptual details.
- The trade-off between generative fidelity and reasoning performance limits dynamic visualization methods in MLLMs.

## Method

- The ==Interleaved Latent Visual Reasoning (ILVR)== framework processes dynamic visual feedback entirely in a ==continuous latent space==, generating and updating latent representations without pixel-level re-encoding.
- A self-supervised strategy with a ==momentum teacher model== constructs context-aware queries to perform selective perceptual modeling, extracting and supervising the MLLM with only the most relevant visual features.
- A two-stage learning process trains the model first for precise perceptual grounding (==latent alignment==) and then for flexible self-generated latent states (latent relaxation) to serve as internal priors for textual generation.

## Results

- ILVR achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on in-distribution tasks, outperforming the Mirage latent reasoning method by up to **5.2%** on the COMT benchmark using the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization, surpassing Mirage by an average of **3.2%** and outperforming state-of-the-art multimodal reasoning models like VisionR1 (by **8.0%**) and PixelReasoner (by **5.9%**) on diverse out-of-distribution benchmarks.
- ILVR exhibits significantly lower inference latency, achieving an **8x** to **18x** speedup compared to competing methods by eliminating costly pixel-level re-encoding and intermediate image generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic visual reasoning in MLLMs can be efficiently achieved by interleaving textual generation with evolving latent visual representations, bypassing the computational bottleneck of explicit image processing.
> - A teacher-guided selective perceptual modeling approach can effectively preserve fine-grained visual details by focusing on context-aware feature extraction, preventing information loss from over-compression.
> - Allowing the model to self-generate and utilize latent states as internal priors (latent relaxation) during the second training stage enhances overall reasoning performance and adaptability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the forefront of research in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly concerning their ability to perform complex reasoning tasks that require both textual and visual understanding. MLLMs have shown remarkable progress in integrating vision and language, leveraging the sophisticated reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT).

The broader landscape for MLLM reasoning can be categorized into two main paradigms regarding visual feedback:
- **Interleaved Image-Text Reasoning:** This paradigm involves injecting intermediate visual information into textual CoTs to enhance understanding.
 - **Tool-based approaches:** These methods use external tools to manipulate static input images (e.g., highlighting regions, drawing lines, applying styles). While helpful for fine-grained perception, they are limited to a single visual state and cannot model dynamic, evolving scenarios or action outcomes.
 - **Generative approaches:** More recent methods enable a unified model to dynamically visualize imagined intermediate or future states. However, these often suffer from a trade-off between generative fidelity (quality of generated images) and reasoning performance. A critical limitation for both tool-based and generative approaches is the **prohibitive computational cost** associated with iteratively re-encoding pixel-dense images during multi-step reasoning.

- **Latent Reasoning:** Inspired by advancements in LLMs, this paradigm attempts to avoid costly pixel-level processing by operating in a continuous hidden space using latent representations instead of explicit images.
 - **Single-step, non-interleaved methods:** Current latent visual reasoning methods, such as LVR and Mirage, typically generate latent representations only once, either for a static region or a final state. This non-interleaved structure fails to capture the evolution of intermediate states, which is crucial for sequential tasks requiring step-by-step verification (e.g., chess puzzles, object counting where details are lost in over-compression).
 - **Over-compression of features:** Methods that heavily compress dense visual features into a limited number of latent tokens often discard crucial perceptual details, leading to inaccuracies or "hallucinations" in fine-grained tasks like dense counting.

The "Interleaved Latent Visual Reasoning (ILVR)" framework directly addresses the shortcomings of both paradigms. It aims to bridge the gap between fine-grained perceptual modeling and efficient, dynamic, sequential multimodal reasoning. By combining the benefits of interleaved reasoning (dynamic state evolution) with the efficiency of latent representations (avoiding pixel-level re-encoding), ILVR seeks to overcome the computational bottleneck of image-generating MLLMs and the static/over-compressed nature of existing latent reasoning approaches. The work positions itself as a scalable and effective solution for complex, multi-step visual reasoning in MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the Interleaved Latent Visual Reasoning (ILVR) framework are driven by the limitations observed in existing MLLM reasoning paradigms:

1. **To enable dynamic state evolution in multimodal reasoning without prohibitive computational costs:** Current interleaved image-text reasoning paradigms, whether tool-based or generative, require repeated re-encoding of pixel-dense images. This is computationally expensive and significantly hinders multi-step reasoning, making them impractical for long reasoning chains. ILVR aims to maintain the benefit of dynamic visual feedback by interleaving textual generation with evolving latent visual representations, thereby bypassing the pixel-level processing bottleneck.

2. **To unify precise perceptual modeling with dynamic latent visual reasoning:** Existing latent visual reasoning methods are often single-step and fail to capture intermediate state evolution. Furthermore, many over-compress visual features into sparse latent tokens, sacrificing fine-grained perceptual details and leading to inaccuracies (e.g., hallucinated counts). ILVR seeks to overcome this by dynamically updating latent representations to capture context-aware, step-specific visual cues while ensuring that crucial perceptual information is retained through a selective mechanism.

3. **To develop an efficient and robust framework for generalization across diverse multimodal reasoning tasks:** The authors aim for a solution that not only performs well on in-distribution tasks but also generalizes effectively to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios and various fine-grained perception and sequential planning challenges. The motivation is to provide a more versatile and scalable approach to multimodal intelligence.

In essence, the core motivation is to develop an MLLM reasoning paradigm that is both **computationally efficient** (by operating in latent space) and **perceptually precise** (by adaptively selecting relevant features), while also being **dynamically responsive** to the evolving reasoning context, thus enabling robust multi-step reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ILVR framework proposes an innovative approach to multimodal reasoning by interleaving textual generation with dynamically updated latent visual representations. Its methodology can be broken down into three key components:

#### 3.1. Interleaved Text-Latent Paradigm

ILVR operates autoregressively, generating a unified sequence `S` that alternates between discrete text tokens (`t_i,j`) and continuous latent embeddings (`z_i,k`). Special tokens `<|latent_start|>` and `<|latent_end|>` demarcate the latent visual reasoning phases.
- **Inference:** When the model generates `<|latent_start|>`, it switches to a latent generation mode for a fixed length `K`. In this mode, the hidden state from the previous timestep is directly fed as the input embedding for the current timestep, producing a sequence of `K` self-generated latent representations. After `K` latents, `<|latent_end|>` signals a return to textual reasoning, with the generated latents serving as context.
- **Training Data Construction:** Ground-truth interleaved image-text trajectories (e.g., "reasoning text → helper image → reasoning text...") are converted into supervision sequences. Each `helper image I_i` is replaced by a `<|latent_start|>` token, `K` `<|latent_pad|>` tokens, and `<|latent_end|>`. The `latent_pad` tokens are placeholders for critical visual signals extracted from `I_i` to supervise the model's generated hidden states.

#### 3.2. Interleaved Supervision Construction

To guide the model in generating meaningful latent representations, a self-supervised strategy is employed using a momentum teacher model.
- **Momentum Teacher Model:** This teacher is an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student model's parameters. This ensures stable supervision that evolves with the student's representation space.
- **Candidate Visual Feature Generation:** For a given `helper image I_i` at reasoning step `i`, the teacher's frozen vision encoder (`f_vis`) extracts a dense pool of patch features `C_i`. To counter varying information density in raw patches, an adaptive spatial aggregation step is introduced: if the number of raw patches `P_i` exceeds a threshold `L`, features are pooled over local spatial windows to form `L` semantic units, resulting in a refined candidate pool `C'_i`; otherwise, `C_i` is retained. This ensures robust features regardless of input resolution.
- **Teacher-Guided Selective Perceptual Modeling:** The teacher constructs a context-aware query `q_i` using the multimodal inputs and the reasoning history up to step `i`.
 - **Query Construction:** `q_i` fuses a global intent vector `u` (average of mean-pooled input text hidden states `r_txt` and text-guided attention over input image `r_img`) with the current textual rationales `q_text[1,i]` and, if available, previous latent rationales `z_bar[1,i-1]`. This avoids naive averaging that could dilute critical signals.
 - **Feature Selection:** Cosine similarity is computed between `q_i` and each candidate feature in `C'_i`. The top-K most relevant features are selected to form the supervision set `Z_i` for the latent tokens.

#### 3.3. Two-stage Learning

The model is trained in two stages to progressively instill interleaved latent reasoning:
- **Stage 1: Interleaved Text-Latent Joint Supervision:** This stage focuses on precise perceptual modeling. The teacher-selected features `Z_i` are used as teacher-forced inputs and regression targets for the `K` `<|latent_pad|>` tokens. The model is optimized with a joint loss:
 - **Cross-entropy loss (L_CE):** For all textual tokens.
 - **Latent alignment loss:** Encourages the student's hidden state `h_t-1` at latent token positions to match the corresponding teacher-selected feature `z_t` using `1 - cos(h_t-1, z_t)`. A balancing weight `λ_sim` is used.
- **Stage 2: Text-Only Supervision with Latent Relaxation:** In this stage, the strict latent alignment constraint is relaxed. The latent alignment loss is removed, allowing the model to freely explore and self-generate latent states. These self-generated hidden states are then fed as input for the subsequent latent positions. Optimization occurs solely on the textual part using `L_CE`, encouraging the latents to serve as internal priors for subsequent textual generation.

This multi-faceted approach ensures that ILVR learns to generate dynamic, context-aware, and perceptually rich latent representations efficiently, integrating them seamlessly into multimodal reasoning chains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted demonstrate ILVR's superior performance, efficiency, and generalization capabilities across various multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. In-Distribution (IID) Performance (Table 1)

- ILVR consistently outperforms standard baselines (Zero-shot, Direct-FT, CoT-FT) and the single-step latent method Mirage on COMT (fine-grained perception) and VSP (sequential planning) datasets.
- On the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, ILVR achieves 60.8% accuracy on COMT and 81.5% on VSP, surpassing Mirage by 4.8% and 5.5%, respectively.
- When scaled to the stronger Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, ILVR maintains a significant advantage, reaching 70.5% on COMT (+5.2% over Mirage) and 82.8% on VSP (+4.5% over Mirage).
- Notably, Stage 2 (Latent Relaxation) of ILVR consistently yields higher accuracy than Stage 1 (Latent Alignment) across all IID tasks and backbones, indicating that allowing the model to self-generate and utilize latents as internal priors improves reasoning.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization (Table 2)

- ILVR demonstrates robust generalization by outperforming standard baselines and Mirage across three OOD benchmarks: EMMA BENCH (diverse reasoning tasks), VisuLogic (visual reasoning), and Zebra-CoT (2D visual reasoning tasks).
- It achieves an average improvement of 3.2% over Mirage in the OOD setting.
- Furthermore, ILVR surpasses state-of-the-art multimodal reasoning models like VisionR1 (by 8.0% average accuracy) and PixelReasoner (by 5.9% average accuracy), despite these models often employing more complex reinforcement learning techniques.
- ILVR, trained on a smaller 10k subset of Zebra-CoT, even outperforms Bagel-Zebra (a Bagel variant fine-tuned on the *full* 180k Zebra-CoT dataset) on EMMA BENCH and VisuLogic, highlighting its efficiency and data effectiveness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3, Fig. 3, Table 4)

- **Interleaved & Selective Design:** Replacing mean pooling with teacher-guided selective perceptual modeling improves accuracy (31.5% to 32.4%). Adding the interleaved reasoning paradigm further boosts performance to 34.8%. This confirms that both selective perception (for quality supervision) and interleaved latent updates (for dynamic state evolution) are critical for ILVR's effectiveness.
- **Latent Size (K):** Experiments with varying `K` (number of latent tokens) show that `K = 8` yields the most robust performance, suggesting a moderate latent budget is optimal for capturing step-specific perceptual evidence without redundancy or insufficient capacity.
- **Alignment Weight (λ_sim):** A `λ_sim = 1` (equal weight for latent alignment and text generation) proves optimal, striking a balance between precise perceptual grounding and flexibility for subsequent reasoning.

#### 4.4. Efficiency (Fig. 5)

- ILVR exhibits significantly lower inference latency compared to competing methods, achieving 8x to 18x speedup over VisionR1, PixelReasoner, and Bagel-Zebra. This is attributed to its operation entirely in latent space, bypassing the costly pixel-level re-encoding and intermediate image generation common in other approaches.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Visualization (Fig. 4)

- Heatmaps depicting attention over relevant image patches for generated latents demonstrate dynamic, step-aligned visual focus. In navigation tasks, attention shifts with planned moves. In robotic manipulation, attention correctly shifts from the object to the target during the task sequence. This visually confirms that ILVR's latents are conditioned on evolving reasoning context, providing subgoal-adaptive localized visual cues.

In summary, ILVR robustly addresses the challenges of computational cost, dynamic state modeling, and precise perceptual grounding in MLLMs, showcasing state-of-the-art performance and remarkable efficiency across diverse and challenging reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Interleaved Latent Visual Reasoning (ILVR) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Accuracy Gap in MLLM Reasoning:** ILVR successfully unifies the benefits of dynamic visual feedback (crucial for complex, multi-step reasoning) with the computational efficiency of latent representations. By avoiding repeated pixel-level encoding, it enables MLLMs to perform sophisticated visual reasoning tasks orders of magnitude faster than methods requiring explicit image generation or re-encoding. This efficiency makes long-horizon reasoning and planning more feasible for real-world applications.

2. **Enhanced Multi-step Visual Reasoning Capabilities:** Previous latent reasoning methods were often limited to single-step operations or suffered from over-compression, leading to a loss of intermediate state information and perceptual detail. ILVR's interleaved paradigm and selective perceptual modeling overcome these limitations, allowing models to precisely track and update visual context throughout a reasoning chain. This is particularly impactful for tasks requiring sequential verification, dynamic object tracking, or fine-grained detail analysis (e.g., medical imaging diagnostics, complex robotic manipulation, advanced game-playing AI).

3. **Robust Generalization and Scalability:** The framework demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, outperforming state-of-the-art models on diverse in-distribution and challenging out-of-distribution benchmarks, even when trained on smaller datasets. This suggests that ILVR learns fundamental principles of multimodal reasoning rather than simply memorizing training patterns, paving the way for more robust and scalable MLLMs that can adapt to novel scenarios and tasks.

4. **Foundation for Future MLLM Architectures:** ILVR's success in dynamically modeling visual states within the latent space provides a new architectural blueprint for MLLMs. It suggests that future models could integrate visual perception more organically into their reasoning processes without relying on computationally intensive external tools or pixel-generating modules. This could lead to more compact, efficient, and versatile MLLMs.

**Potential Future Impacts:**
- **More Intelligent AI Assistants:** Personal and professional AI assistants could gain enhanced capabilities in understanding complex visual instructions, diagnosing problems from images, and performing multi-step visual planning.
- **Advanced Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Robots could use ILVR's dynamic visual reasoning to better understand and execute sequential tasks in complex, changing environments, improving navigation, object manipulation, and interaction with the physical world.
- **Scientific Discovery and Data Analysis:** Researchers could leverage ILVR for interpreting complex visual data in scientific domains (e.g., analyzing microscopy images, climate simulations, geological surveys) where fine-grained, dynamic understanding is critical.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors):**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to future research directions:
- **Architectural Generalization:** While promising, ILVR has primarily been validated on Qwen-VL backbones. Testing its robustness across a broader range of diverse MLLM architectures and larger parameter scales is crucial.
- **Integration with Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Directly optimizing latent trajectories using RL could further enhance multi-step planning capabilities, particularly in dynamic and interactive environments.
- **Interpretability of Latent Representations:** While attention maps offer some insight, the generated latent representations are not directly human-readable. Developing mechanisms to project these states back into pixel space would significantly improve interpretability and trust in MLLM reasoning.

In conclusion, ILVR presents a compelling solution to a core challenge in MLLM development, offering a powerful and efficient paradigm for multimodal reasoning that combines dynamic visual understanding with computational practicality. Its contributions are poised to significantly influence the design and application of next-generation MLLMs.
