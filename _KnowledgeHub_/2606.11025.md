# Flow-DPPO: Divergence Proximal Policy Optimization for Flow Matching Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Flow-DPPO introduces a trust-region reinforcement learning method for fine-tuning flow matching models, replacing noisy ratio clipping with an exact KL divergence-based mask. This approach yields superior reward optimization and generalization, significantly mitigating catastrophic forgetting and improving training stability across various generative tasks.

## Problem

- Existing online RL methods, such as Flow-GRPO and CPS, apply PPO-style ratio clipping to regulate policy updates, which is ill-suited for the high-dimensional, continuous latent spaces of flow models.
- The probability ratio, a single-sample estimate of policy divergence, introduces significant noise and bias in high-dimensional continuous spaces, leading to inconsistent constraint application and hindered optimal policy updates.
- This reliance on a noisy proxy for policy divergence contributes to issues like catastrophic forgetting, reward hacking, and limits stable multi-epoch training and sample reuse.

## Method

- **Flow-DPPO** replaces the problematic ratio clipping mechanism with a direct divergence-based masking strategy for trust-region enforcement.
- It exploits the inherent Gaussian nature of flow model's per-step policies, enabling the exact and inexpensive computation of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between old and new policies.
- An ==asymmetric divergence mask== is introduced, which only blocks gradient updates when the KL divergence exceeds a threshold AND the update direction is moving the policy further away from the old policy, thus preserving beneficial updates.

## Results

- Flow-DPPO consistently outperformed baseline methods in reward optimization (e.g., GenEval2) across various base models, including SD3.5 and FLUX2-9B, demonstrating superior performance and stable training trajectories.
- The method significantly mitigated catastrophic forgetting and out-of-domain degradation, preserving higher visual quality and compositional accuracy on unseen prompts, which correlated with maintaining lower KL divergence from the reference model.
- Flow-DPPO exhibited robust performance and sustained improvements under multi-epoch training, effectively reusing rollout samples across multiple updates and improving sample efficiency compared to baselines that degraded with frequent sample reuse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - PPO's probability ratio is a noisy and biased proxy for true policy divergence in high-dimensional continuous spaces, resulting in spurious clipping and sub-optimal policy updates.
> - The Gaussian structure of flow models' per-step policies allows for precise and computationally cheap calculation of KL divergence, offering a theoretically sound and robust measure for trust-region regulation.
> - An asymmetric divergence mask is crucial for training stability, as it selectively constrains updates to prevent excessive divergence while allowing beneficial updates to proceed unhindered.
