# Variational Neural Belief Parameterizations for Robust Dexterous Grasping under Multimodal Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Variational Neural Belief (VNB) for dexterous grasping, employing a differentiable Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to represent multimodal uncertainty in object pose and contact parameters. The framework enables risk-sensitive planning using Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), resulting in improved robust grasp success and planning efficiency in both simulation and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Dexterous grasping faces challenges from contact variability, sensing uncertainty, and external disturbances, making grasp execution stochastic and prone to catastrophic failure.
- Existing analytical and data-driven methods for grasp synthesis often assume precise contact properties or optimize for expected performance, neglecting the tail risks associated with uncertainty.
- Prior approaches modeling uncertainty, such as particle filters, suffer from high variance in tail risk estimation, computational scalability issues, and discrete stochasticity that hinders gradient-based optimization.

## Method

- A differentiable Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is used to parameterize the belief over latent physical parameters (object pose and contact parameters), enabling pathwise gradient propagation via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation and location-scale reparameterization.
- Neural networks learn the belief dynamics, including transition and observation models, to update the GMM belief over time based on actions and sensor observations.
- Robust grasping is framed as a risk-sensitive Model-Predictive Control (==MPC==) problem, optimizing a composite objective that incorporates a differentiable Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) measure to hedge against worst-case outcomes and a visual perception cost for information gathering.

## Results

- In simulations, VNB achieved **79%** robust success and **73%** perturbation survival in the nominal friction regime, matching or exceeding baseline methods like the Cross-Entropy Method (Cem) and particle filter (Pf).
- VNB demonstrated an order of magnitude reduction in planning time compared to particle-filter-based MPC, averaging 7-**8.5** seconds per episode, and exhibited lower mean absolute calibration error (|"ΔP"| "," ≤ **0.14**) for better-calibrated uncertainty estimates.
- Hardware experiments validated VNB's ability to achieve **100%** grasp-and-lift success on YCB objects, showing faster convergence (median 6 MPC steps vs. 7) and higher median terminal grasp quality compared to a Gaussian MPC baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing multimodal uncertainty with a differentiable GMM allows for direct, gradient-based optimization of risk-sensitive objectives in belief space, addressing limitations of discrete particle filters.
> - Integrating a smooth, differentiable Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) measure into the MPC objective enables the system to explicitly account for and mitigate worst-case scenarios, leading to more robust grasps.
> - Learning belief dynamics through neural networks provides a flexible and adaptive mechanism for managing uncertainty, removing the need for hand-designed process and observation models.
