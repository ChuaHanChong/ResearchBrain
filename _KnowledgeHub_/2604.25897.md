# Variational Neural Belief Parameterizations for Robust Dexterous Grasping under Multimodal Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Variational Neural Belief (VNB) for dexterous grasping, employing a differentiable Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to represent multimodal uncertainty in object pose and contact parameters. The framework enables risk-sensitive planning using Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), resulting in improved robust grasp success and planning efficiency in both simulation and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Dexterous grasping faces challenges from contact variability, sensing uncertainty, and external disturbances, making grasp execution stochastic and prone to catastrophic failure.
- Existing analytical and data-driven methods for grasp synthesis often assume precise contact properties or optimize for expected performance, neglecting the tail risks associated with uncertainty.
- Prior approaches modeling uncertainty, such as particle filters, suffer from high variance in tail risk estimation, computational scalability issues, and discrete stochasticity that hinders gradient-based optimization.

## Method

- A differentiable Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is used to parameterize the belief over latent physical parameters (object pose and contact parameters), enabling pathwise gradient propagation via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation and location-scale reparameterization.
- Neural networks learn the belief dynamics, including transition and observation models, to update the GMM belief over time based on actions and sensor observations.
- Robust grasping is framed as a risk-sensitive Model-Predictive Control (==MPC==) problem, optimizing a composite objective that incorporates a differentiable Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) measure to hedge against worst-case outcomes and a visual perception cost for information gathering.

## Results

- In simulations, VNB achieved **79%** robust success and **73%** perturbation survival in the nominal friction regime, matching or exceeding baseline methods like the Cross-Entropy Method (Cem) and particle filter (Pf).
- VNB demonstrated an order of magnitude reduction in planning time compared to particle-filter-based MPC, averaging 7-**8.5** seconds per episode, and exhibited lower mean absolute calibration error (|"ΔP"| "," ≤ **0.14**) for better-calibrated uncertainty estimates.
- Hardware experiments validated VNB's ability to achieve **100%** grasp-and-lift success on YCB objects, showing faster convergence (median 6 MPC steps vs. 7) and higher median terminal grasp quality compared to a Gaussian MPC baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing multimodal uncertainty with a differentiable GMM allows for direct, gradient-based optimization of risk-sensitive objectives in belief space, addressing limitations of discrete particle filters.
> - Integrating a smooth, differentiable Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) measure into the MPC objective enables the system to explicitly account for and mitigate worst-case scenarios, leading to more robust grasps.
> - Learning belief dynamics through neural networks provides a flexible and adaptive mechanism for managing uncertainty, removing the need for hand-designed process and observation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous grasping, a fundamental capability for autonomous robots, frequently encounters challenges due to contact variability, sensing uncertainty, and external disturbances. These factors render grasp execution stochastic, meaning that a grasp seemingly optimal under average conditions may fail catastrophically if true parameters (e.g., object pose, friction coefficients) fall into the tails of their probability distributions. Mainstream analytical grasp synthesis methods often assume precise contact properties, leading to brittle grasps susceptible to friction mismatch and pose errors. Data-driven methods, while learning grasp proposals from extensive datasets, typically optimize for expected performance without providing mechanisms for quantifying or hedging against risk from contact or pose uncertainty.

Existing approaches that explicitly model uncertainty, such as Deep Variational Bayes Filters and probabilistic grasp learning frameworks, often rely on nonparametric belief representations like particle filters. While flexible and capable of multimodal modeling, particle-based beliefs introduce limitations. These include high variance in tail risk estimation, computational scaling issues with increasing particle counts, discrete stochasticity from resampling that obstructs gradient propagation, and fidelity limitations in posterior estimation, particularly in multimodal contexts.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a variational neural belief system. It situates itself within the landscape of belief-space planning, which represents uncertainty over latent variables using probability distributions, and the emerging field of neural uncertainty representations, which leverages variational inference for differentiable uncertainty modeling. By replacing discrete particle-based beliefs with a continuous, differentiable Gaussian mixture, the work aims to enable direct, gradient-based optimization of risk-sensitive objectives, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and computationally efficient dexterous grasping strategies under realistic multimodal uncertainty.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed brittleness of robotic grasps when confronted with real-world uncertainties. These uncertainties include inexact contact parameters (friction coefficients, surface compliance, contact modes) that are inferred indirectly from noisy sensor feedback, and corrupted object pose estimates due to camera calibration drift, lighting variations, or partial observations. The paper highlights that planning algorithms must explicitly account for the full posterior distribution over these uncertain parameters, rather than merely their means, because a high expected grasp quality does not preclude catastrophic failure under adverse realizations of uncertainty.

The key objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a differentiable belief representation:** To overcome the limitations of particle filters, the authors aim to represent the belief over latent contact parameters and object pose using a continuous, differentiable distribution, specifically a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM). This representation is designed to support pathwise gradient propagation.
- **Enable risk-sensitive grasp optimization:** The objective is to formulate a grasp quality objective that explicitly considers tail outcomes. This involves integrating the Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) measure with the differentiable belief, allowing for direct, gradient-based optimization of robustness to worst-case scenarios.
- **Learn belief dynamics:** Instead of hand-crafting process and observation models, the goal is to learn neural network-based transition and observation models for updating the differentiable belief, facilitating adaptive and flexible uncertainty management.
- **Formulate robust grasping as an information-guided risk-sensitive MPC problem:** The work aims to cast grasping as a belief-space model-predictive control (MPC) problem. This framework seeks to select finger motion primitives that incrementally enhance grasp quality while actively minimizing failure risk and gathering information to reduce uncertainty, ultimately aiming for force closure under the evolving belief.
- **Improve robust grasp success and planning efficiency:** Through the proposed methodology, the authors intend to demonstrate improved robust grasp success under various uncertainties (contact parameters, external perturbations, object pose) and achieve reduced planning times compared to existing particle-filter based approaches.
- **Enhance risk calibration:** A further objective is to ensure that the learned belief accurately calibrates risk, meaning the belief-predicted failure probability aligns closely with the empirical failure rate, leading to more reliable uncertainty estimates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Variational Neural Belief (VNB) for grasping, integrates differentiable belief representation, neural belief dynamics, and risk-sensitive optimization within a Model-Predictive Control (MPC) framework.

**A. Gaussian Mixture Beliefs:**
The core of the approach involves parameterizing the belief over latent physical parameters (object pose $\xi_o$ and per-contact parameters $\psi$) as a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with $K$ diagonal-covariance Gaussian components. Each component is defined by its mixture logit $\ell_k$, mean $\mu_k$, and log-standard-deviation $\log \sigma_k$. To enable gradient propagation through the discrete component selection process, two mechanisms are jointly applied:
1. **Gumbel-Softmax Relaxation:** This technique provides a continuous, differentiable approximation of the categorical sampling process for selecting a GMM component. It yields relaxed assignment weights $\zeta_k$ that are differentiable with respect to the logits $\ell_k$.
2. **Location-Scale Reparameterization:** For each Gaussian component, samples are generated as $\theta_k = \mu_k + \sigma_k \odot \epsilon_k$, where $\epsilon_k \sim N(0, I)$. This makes the component samples differentiable with respect to their means and standard deviations.
By combining these, a single reparameterized GMM sample $\theta$ is expressed as a weighted sum of reparameterized component samples, making the entire sampling process differentiable with respect to all GMM parameters ($\ell$, $\mu_k$, $\log \sigma_k$).

**B. Neural Belief Dynamics:**
Instead of relying on hand-designed process and observation models, the system learns differentiable belief dynamics. A latent embedding $h_t$ is propagated through multi-layer neural networks:
1. **Transition Model ($f_{trans}$):** Predicts a future latent embedding $h_{pred,t+1}$ based on the current embedding $h_t$ and action $a_t$.
2. **Observation Model ($f_{obs}$):** Corrects the predicted embedding $h_{pred,t+1}$ using incoming observation $o_{t+1}$ to produce the updated embedding $h_{t+1}$.
A separate decoder network maps the updated latent embedding $h_{t+1}$ to the GMM belief parameters $\phi_{t+1}$, thereby updating the belief $b_{t+1}$. Observations $o_t$ comprise estimated object pose and covariance, tactile sensor readings, joint encoder readings, and visual reliability scores (occlusion and segmentation confidence).

**C. Pathwise Risk Gradients via Action Optimization:**
The proposed method directly optimizes a risk-sensitive objective function using pathwise gradients.
1. **Differentiable CVaR:** The Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) is used as a coherent risk measure, averaging the worst $(1-\beta)$ fraction of outcomes. The CVaR objective is made differentiable by using its dual form and approximating the non-differentiable $\max(x, 0)$ hinge function with a smooth SoftPlus function. This allows for direct computation of gradients of the CVaR with respect to the action $a$ and belief parameters $\phi$.
2. **Differentiable Multimodal Cost Function:** A composite grasp cost function $C(\theta, a)$ is defined, comprising:
 - **Closure Strength ($C_{str}$):** Penalizes low mean joint closing rates and large fingertip-to-object-center distances, promoting stable closure.
 - **Friction-Limited Robustness ($C_{\rho}$):** Encourages sufficient normal force to resist slip given uncertain friction coefficients.
 - **Contact Stability ($C_{ctc}$):** Penalizes low friction, low stiffness, and high slip velocity at contacts.
3. **Visual Perception Cost ($C_{vis}$):** This cost term is introduced to penalize visually uncertain states, incorporating factors like object pose estimation covariance, occlusion score, and segmentation confidence.
4. **Failure Probability:** The belief-space failure probability, defined as the mass of latent parameters where grasp cost exceeds a stability threshold, is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling. A smooth sigmoid approximation replaces the hard indicator function for differentiability.

**D. MPC for Grasping with Variational Beliefs:**
The system frames grasping as a risk-sensitive belief-space MPC problem. At each time step, the controller optimizes a sequence of actions $a_{t:t+H}$ over a receding horizon $H$ to minimize a composite objective function $J$. This objective blends expected cost and CVaR of the contact cost, includes the visual perception cost, and a terminal belief entropy term to encourage information gathering actions.
- **Action Optimization:** Gradient-based optimization is employed, where gradients of the objective are computed through the reparameterized belief and the smooth CVaR surrogate. To address local minima, multi-start optimization is used.
- **Per-Component Optimization:** For multimodal beliefs, actions are optimized independently for each GMM component. The action sequence associated with the lowest risk-aware cost is selected, enabling the controller to hedge against distinct contact hypotheses or pose ambiguities.
- **Hardware Implementation:** For hardware trials, object pose is estimated using ICP registration from RGB-D cameras. When multiple cameras observe the object, covariance intersection fuses the pose estimates. Tactile sensors provide feedback for contact detection and a Ferrari-Canny inspired grasp quality proxy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation involved both simulation experiments in MuJoCo and hardware experiments on a robotic arm with a multi-fingered hand.

**A. Simulation Results:**
The simulation compared the Variational Neural Belief (VNB) method against four baselines: Gaussian MPC (Gauss), Gaussian-CVaR MPC (Gauss-Cvar), Particle Filter MPC (Pf), and Cross-Entropy Method MPC (Cem). Experiments were conducted across three friction regimes (Nominal, Wide, Bimodal) and subjected to a two-phase stress test involving vertical lift and four lateral shear pulses (up to 12 N). Key metrics included success rate (SR), robust success (nominal success + perturbation survival $\geq$ 50%), perturbation survival rate (PertSurv), Ferrari–Canny quality ($\epsilon$), belief-predicted failure probability ($\hat{P}_{bel}^{fail}$), empirical failure probability ($\hat{P}_{emp}^{fail}$), calibration error ($|\Delta \hat{P}|$), and planning time.

- **Robustness and Success:** VNB generally matched or exceeded Cem across all three friction regimes in terms of robust success and perturbation survival. For instance, in the nominal regime, VNB achieved 79% robust success and 73% PertSurv, compared to Cem's 63% and 55%, respectively. Gauss and Gauss-Cvar showed 75% robust success and 69% PertSurv in the nominal regime, but their single-component Gaussian belief could not represent distinct contact hypotheses.
- **Grasp Quality Evolution:** VNB maintained higher Ferrari–Canny $\epsilon$ quality over MPC steps, especially during grasp maintenance, and remained robust under lift perturbations, whereas Cem's performance degraded in later stages.
- **Planning Efficiency:** VNB demonstrated a significant reduction in planning time compared to particle-filter based MPC (Pf). VNB's average episode time was approximately 7-8.5 seconds, which is roughly an order of magnitude faster than Pf's 32-50 seconds across regimes. Cem was faster than VNB, averaging around 5 seconds.
- **Risk Calibration:** VNB exhibited consistently low mean absolute calibration error ($|\Delta \hat{P}| \leq 0.14$) across all friction regimes, indicating better-calibrated uncertainty estimates. In contrast, Cem showed higher calibration errors (e.g., 0.55 in nominal, 0.58 in wide), often predicting near-certain failure even when empirical failure rates were moderate.

**B. Hardware Results:**
Hardware validation involved grasping three YCB objects under object pose uncertainty (ICP estimates) and structured pose perturbations across four offsets. Both VNB and Gauss achieved 100% grasp-and-lift success on this limited set of 12 trials per method.

- **Convergence and Runtime:** VNB generally converged faster and with lower dispersion. The median number of MPC steps for VNB was 6, compared to 7 for Gauss. VNB's median runtime was 11.5 seconds, while Gauss's was 14.2 seconds. VNB also showed lower coefficients of variation (CoV) for both step count (0.32 vs. 0.49) and runtime (0.41 vs. 0.78).
- **Terminal Grasp Quality:** VNB achieved a higher median terminal grasp quality proxy $\hat{\epsilon}_T$ ($1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ vs. $0.9 \times 10^{-3}$) with lower variance (CoV 0.48 vs. 1.21).
- **Contact and Slip:** Both methods achieved a median of three final contacts. Slip metrics (peak and mean slip) were comparable for the mustard bottle, with Gauss showing tighter slip dispersion on this object.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for robust dexterous grasping that addresses limitations in handling multimodal uncertainty, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts.

### 6. Significance

- **Differentiable Uncertainty Representation:** The work introduces a novel approach to representing belief over uncertain contact parameters and object pose using a differentiable Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM). This, combined with Gumbel-Softmax and location-scale reparameterization, enables pathwise gradient propagation, a capability often absent in particle-filter-based methods. This closes a critical "gradient gap" in belief-space planning, allowing for direct, gradient-based optimization of risk.
- **Enhanced Robustness through Risk-Sensitive Planning:** By integrating Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) into the planning objective and using a smooth surrogate, the method explicitly hedges against worst-case outcomes. This leads to grasps that are not just nominally successful but also robust to unexpected perturbations and adverse contact realizations, as evidenced by improved robust success and perturbation survival rates in simulations.
- **Improved Computational Efficiency:** The differentiable nature of the belief and objective allows for more efficient gradient-based optimization, significantly reducing planning time compared to sampling-based particle filter MPC. This makes the approach more practical for real-time robotic applications.
- **Better-Calibrated Uncertainty Estimates:** The method demonstrates improved calibration of risk, where the belief-predicted failure probabilities align more closely with empirical failure rates. This is crucial for reliable decision-making in safety-critical robotic tasks, as overconfident or underconfident risk estimates can lead to failures.
- **Integration of Perception and Information Gathering:** The composite MPC objective thoughtfully combines contact risk, visual perception uncertainty, and belief entropy. This encourages the robot to gather information (reduce uncertainty) while also avoiding configurations that might degrade perception, leading to a more holistic and intelligent grasping strategy.
- **Hardware Validation:** The successful deployment on a physical robot validates the transferability of the theoretical framework from simulation to real-world scenarios, even with the inherent complexities of real sensor noise and system dynamics.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Reliable Robotic Manipulation:** The core advancements in robust uncertainty handling could lead to more reliable and adaptable robotic manipulators capable of operating in unstructured, dynamic, and uncertain environments, such as logistics, manufacturing, domestic service, and disaster response.
- **Generalizability to Other Manipulation Tasks:** The methodology for differentiable, risk-sensitive planning under multimodal uncertainty is not confined to grasping. It could potentially be extended to other complex manipulation tasks requiring robust interaction with uncertain objects or environments, such as assembly, tool use, or deformable object manipulation.
- **Advancements in Robot Learning:** The use of neural belief dynamics for learning transition and observation models opens avenues for developing more adaptive and generalizable robot controllers. Future work could leverage meta-learning to quickly adapt belief dynamics across novel objects or environments, reducing the need for extensive retraining.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** With more robust and predictable grasping capabilities, robots could more safely and effectively collaborate with humans, reducing risks associated with unexpected grasp failures.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future research in several directions, including exploring alternative differentiable belief representations (e.g., normalizing flows for faster sampling), extending to other coherent risk measures, and improving tactile sensing and adaptation for compliant and low-friction materials.
