# TieBot: Learning to Knot a Tie from Visual Demonstration through a Real-to-Sim-to-Real Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> A robotic system, TieBot, learns to knot ties from human visual demonstrations through a Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework that leverages mesh representations for cloth state estimation and a teacher-student policy learning approach. The system achieved a 50% success rate in real-world tie-knotting and 70% in unseen towel-folding, showing proficiency in dexterous manipulation of deformable objects.

## Problem

- Manipulating highly deformable objects like cloth for long-horizon, bi-manual tasks such as tie-knotting presents a significant challenge due to high-dimensional state and intricate dynamics.
- Traditional Learning from Demonstration (LfD) methods struggle with the complexity of cloth manipulation, making direct action annotation or reward function design particularly difficult.
- Existing visual LfD often relies on object-centric representations (e.g., keypoints, trajectories) that are insufficient to capture the subtle deformations and complex topological changes inherent in advanced cloth manipulation.

## Method

- **TieBot** introduces a Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework, enabling robots to learn complex bi-manual cloth manipulation tasks from human visual demonstrations.
- The system employs a mesh-based representation to accurately model the deformable tie's state, utilizing a Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM) method to estimate mesh sequences from RGB-D video.
- A teacher-student learning paradigm in simulation trains a policy for grasping and pulling actions, where a teacher uses privileged information and a student learns from point cloud observations for real-world execution.

## Results

- TieBot achieved a **50%** success rate for real-world tie-knotting on a softer tie and **30%** on an unseen harder tie, successfully completing an average of **5.0** and **4.15** subgoals respectively.
- The system demonstrated broader applicability by achieving a **70%** success rate in real-world unseen towel-folding tasks.
- The Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM) method consistently outperformed baseline mesh estimation techniques, yielding lower L2 distances (e.g., 0.0248m) and maintaining topological correctness for deformable object tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mesh-based representations are crucial for accurately capturing the complex geometry, physical properties, and topological changes necessary for dexterous cloth manipulation tasks like tie-knotting.
> - Integrating physics-based simulation within a Real-to-Sim-to-Real pipeline provides a robust method for state estimation of deformable objects and facilitates effective policy learning.
> - The Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM) method, by combining local visual features with global, oriented keypoints and physics-based priors, enables accurate and topologically consistent mesh estimation for highly deformable objects even with occlusions.
