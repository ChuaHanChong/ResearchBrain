# TieBot: Learning to Knot a Tie from Visual Demonstration through a Real-to-Sim-to-Real Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> A robotic system, TieBot, learns to knot ties from human visual demonstrations through a Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework that leverages mesh representations for cloth state estimation and a teacher-student policy learning approach. The system achieved a 50% success rate in real-world tie-knotting and 70% in unseen towel-folding, showing proficiency in dexterous manipulation of deformable objects.

## Problem

- Manipulating highly deformable objects like cloth for long-horizon, bi-manual tasks such as tie-knotting presents a significant challenge due to high-dimensional state and intricate dynamics.
- Traditional Learning from Demonstration (LfD) methods struggle with the complexity of cloth manipulation, making direct action annotation or reward function design particularly difficult.
- Existing visual LfD often relies on object-centric representations (e.g., keypoints, trajectories) that are insufficient to capture the subtle deformations and complex topological changes inherent in advanced cloth manipulation.

## Method

- **TieBot** introduces a Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework, enabling robots to learn complex bi-manual cloth manipulation tasks from human visual demonstrations.
- The system employs a mesh-based representation to accurately model the deformable tie's state, utilizing a Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM) method to estimate mesh sequences from RGB-D video.
- A teacher-student learning paradigm in simulation trains a policy for grasping and pulling actions, where a teacher uses privileged information and a student learns from point cloud observations for real-world execution.

## Results

- TieBot achieved a **50%** success rate for real-world tie-knotting on a softer tie and **30%** on an unseen harder tie, successfully completing an average of **5.0** and **4.15** subgoals respectively.
- The system demonstrated broader applicability by achieving a **70%** success rate in real-world unseen towel-folding tasks.
- The Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM) method consistently outperformed baseline mesh estimation techniques, yielding lower L2 distances (e.g., 0.0248m) and maintaining topological correctness for deformable object tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mesh-based representations are crucial for accurately capturing the complex geometry, physical properties, and topological changes necessary for dexterous cloth manipulation tasks like tie-knotting.
> - Integrating physics-based simulation within a Real-to-Sim-to-Real pipeline provides a robust method for state estimation of deformable objects and facilitates effective policy learning.
> - The Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM) method, by combining local visual features with global, oriented keypoints and physics-based priors, enables accurate and topologically consistent mesh estimation for highly deformable objects even with occlusions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the domain of robotic cloth manipulation, a field recognized for its complexity due to the high-dimensional state and intricate dynamics of deformable objects. Tasks such as robotic tie-knotting represent a significant challenge within this domain, pushing the capabilities of existing robotic systems.

Traditional approaches to robotic cloth manipulation often involve either model-free reinforcement learning, learned dynamics models, or collecting and annotating data from images or simulated demonstrations. However, these methods typically focus on short-horizon tasks with simpler pick-and-place actions, such as unfolding towels or rearranging ropes. Tie-knotting, in contrast, involves long-horizon, bi-manual operations that include complex actions like flipping and rotating parts of the tie, making direct action annotation or reward function design particularly difficult.

Learning from Demonstration (LfD) offers a promising direction by allowing robots to acquire policies from expert human demonstrations, reducing the need for manual reward engineering. However, existing LfD methods also face limitations when applied to tasks like tie-knotting. Kinesthetic demonstration or teleoperation for bi-manual, complex tasks demands high operator skill and specialized equipment. While learning from visual demonstration can simplify data collection, it introduces embodiment gaps that challenge policy generalization.

Current visual LfD techniques include:
- **Pre-training neural representations from actionless videos:** These aim for general visual representations but have not demonstrated the dexterity required for complex manipulation skills like tie-knotting.
- **Leveraging object-centric representations:** Methods using object trajectories, hand poses, keypoint positions, or graph relations are sufficient for describing simple object motions but often fail to capture the complex topology and subtle dynamics inherent in advanced cloth manipulation.

This paper positions TieBot as an advancement that addresses these limitations by proposing that a mesh representation is suitable for complex cloth manipulation tasks like tie-knotting. A mesh can capture accurate geometric structures and physical properties, which are crucial for understanding and replicating the complex deformations and topological changes of a tie. By integrating this mesh-based approach within a Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework, the authors aim to leverage the benefits of simulation for state estimation and policy learning while maintaining applicability in real-world scenarios. The work is presented as the first endeavor to integrate perception, modeling, and robot learning specifically for the robotic tie-knotting task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robotic system, TieBot, capable of learning the complex task of tie-knotting from visual demonstrations provided by humans. This overarching goal is supported by several more specific objectives and motivations:

- **Tackle a High-Challenge Task:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent difficulty of the tie-knotting task itself. It is characterized by high material deformation, long-horizon manipulation sequences, and the necessity for bi-manual, dexterous actions that include flipping and rotating. Successfully addressing this task can provide insights for the broader field of robotic cloth manipulation.
- **Enable Service Robot Capabilities:** Learning tie-knotting contributes to the development of service robots that can assist individuals with activities of daily living, such as dressing, especially for the elderly or those with disabilities.
- **Leverage Learning from Demonstration (LfD):** LfD is motivated as an efficient framework for teaching complex skills without the need for manual design of task-specific reward functions, which would be particularly challenging for tie-knotting.
- **Utilize Visual Demonstration for Data Collection:** Visual demonstrations are chosen due to their ease of data collection compared to kinesthetic or teleoperation methods, especially for intricate bi-manual tasks.
- **Address Embodiment Gaps with Object-Centric Representations:** The paper aims to overcome the "embodiment gap" inherent in visual LfD by finding robust object-centric representations that robots can use to generate correct actions.
- **Propose Mesh as a Suitable Representation:** A core motivation is the hypothesis that mesh is the most appropriate representation for tie-knotting and similar complex cloth manipulation tasks. A mesh is argued to capture accurate geometric structures and physics properties, which are critical for the task's complex topology and subtle dynamics. It also disentangles irrelevant visual information (e.g., background, object colors), potentially enhancing generalization across different settings.
- **Develop a Real-to-Sim-to-Real Pipeline:** The motivation for this framework is to leverage the advantages of cloth simulation. Simulation can assist in accurately estimating the highly deformable tie's state from real-world visual demonstrations (Real2Sim) and provide a controlled environment for learning robust manipulation policies (Learn@Sim) before deploying them to the real robot (Sim2Real).
- **Refine Cloth State Estimation:** A specific objective within the Real2Sim phase is to develop a method for robustly estimating the tie's mesh with high deformation from RGB-D video, incorporating physics-based priors to maintain topological correctness.
- **Facilitate Policy Learning from Point Clouds:** Within the Learn@Sim phase, the goal is to develop a training paradigm that enables a robot to learn grasping and pulling actions from point cloud observations, a form of high-dimensional visual input, while mitigating data inefficiency through a teacher-student approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TieBot employs a Real-to-Sim-to-Real learning from visual demonstration framework for teaching a dual-arm robot to knot a tie. The methodology is structured into three main phases: Real2Sim for state estimation, Learn@Sim for policy learning, and Sim2Real for real-world execution.

**Overall Framework (Fig. 2):**
The system begins with a human demonstration video. The Real2Sim module processes this video to estimate a sequence of tie meshes, which serve as subgoals. The Learn@Sim module then uses these subgoals in a simulation environment to train a robot manipulation policy. Finally, the Sim2Real module deploys this learned policy for execution on a real dual-arm robot.

#### 3.1. Real2Sim: Mesh Estimation from Demonstration

This phase focuses on recovering the tie's mesh sequence from an RGB-D demonstration video, integrating cloth simulation for physical priors.

- **Data Preprocessing:**
 - The tie is segmented from the RGB-D video using the Track-Anything model.
 - Segmented depth images are converted into point clouds (X<sup>D</sup><sub>t</sub>).
 - A generic tie mesh model (Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t</sub>) is loaded into DiffClothAI, a cloth simulator supporting intersection-free contact.
 - The initial mesh is manually aligned with the initial point cloud, assuming full overlap.

- **Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM):** This method combines local and global visual information to iteratively estimate the tie's mesh.

 - **Local Feature Matching (LoFTR):**
 - An off-the-shelf feature matching model, LoFTR, is used to establish correspondences between consecutive RGB images (I<sub>t-1</sub> and I<sub>t</sub>).
 - These correspondences are mapped from the previous point cloud (X<sup>D</sup><sub>t-1</sub>) to the current point cloud (X<sup>D</sup><sub>t</sub>).
 - Control vertices (V) on the previous mesh (Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t-1</sub>) are identified by mapping feature points from X<sup>D</sup><sub>t-1</sub> to their nearest vertices on Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t-1</sub>.
 - DiffClothAI is then used to control these vertices to move to the corresponding positions on X<sup>D</sup><sub>t</sub>, aligning the mesh Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t</sub>.
 - **Limitation:** Vanilla local feature matching struggles with occluded regions, common in tie-knotting, leading to potential deviation in motion information.

 - **Keypoints Detection:**
 - To address occlusion and provide global structural information, five oriented keypoints are defined along the tie's surface, each with a local frame (z-direction as surface normal, x-direction along the middle skeleton, y-derived).
 - Pointnet++ is trained to predict these keypoints and their local frames on the demonstrated point clouds.
 - **Adaptive Training Data Generation:** To overcome the challenge of high-dimensional state space and sparse successful trajectories, training data is generated based on the current estimated mesh. If the Chamfer distance between the estimated mesh Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t</sub> and the demonstrated point cloud X<sup>D</sup><sub>t</sub> exceeds a threshold, the system backtracks, gathers the previous tie shape Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t-1</sub>, applies random actions in simulation to generate new training data, and retrains the keypoints prediction network.

 - **HFM Integration:** Control vertices derived from local feature matching and key vertices from keypoints detection are combined. This joint set of vertices is used to pull the mesh towards target positions specified by both local feature matching (for detailed motion) and keypoints detection (for global structural guidance), ensuring topological consistency and preventing large deviations.

- **Output:** A sequence of estimated tie meshes ({Y<sup>G</sup><sub>t</sub>}) that serve as subgoals for the policy learning phase.

#### 3.2. Learn@Sim: Policy Learning in Simulation

This phase generates feasible robotic actions in simulation to guide the simulated tie (Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t</sub>) towards the estimated subgoals ({Y<sup>G</sup><sub>t</sub>}). The focus is on learning "where to grasp" and "where to pull" the tie.

- **Pull Action Strategy:** Once grasping vertices are identified, they are pulled to the positions of the corresponding vertices on the subgoal mesh. This is not directly learned but derived from the subgoals.

- **Teacher-Student Training Paradigm:** This paradigm is adopted to simplify policy learning, especially from high-dimensional observations like point clouds.

 - **Teacher Policy (Privileged Information):**
 - Learns to select appropriate grasping points using privileged information, including the previous tie's vertices positions and the point-wise displacement for each vertex to the subgoal.
 - The action space is discrete, corresponding to selecting one or two grasping vertices from a set of candidates (e.g., 40 vertices sampled on the tie's middle line, allowing 40 single-vertex or 780 two-vertex grasp combinations).
 - Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for training.
 - The reward function (Eq. 1) is defined as:
 - C<sub>1</sub> for successful knotting.
 - -C<sub>2</sub> if a subgoal cannot be reached (distance ∥Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t</sub> - Y<sup>G</sup><sub>t</sub>∥ exceeds a threshold).
 - C<sub>3</sub> - ∥Y<sup>S</sup><sub>t</sub> - Y<sup>G</sup><sub>t</sub>∥ for intermediate steps.
 - Feasible grasping poses for specific hardware are recorded offline, automatically produced once grasping vertices are identified.

 - **Student Policy (Point Cloud Observation):**
 - Imitates the teacher policy.
 - Training data is generated by executing the teacher policy multiple times in DiffClothAI, with perturbations to mesh size and positions to enhance generalization.
 - Point clouds are rendered from these meshes in PyBullet, using camera intrinsics similar to the real-world setup, to serve as visual input.
 - Pointnet++ is used as the policy network, trained in a supervised learning manner. Its input is the rendered point cloud, and its output predicts the grasping points and placing points (which are derived from the subgoals).

#### 3.3. Sim2Real: Real-World Execution

The learned student policy is deployed and executed on a real dual-arm robot. The robot utilizes the trained policy to perceive the tie via point clouds, infer grasping and placing points, and execute the manipulation actions to knot the tie.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of TieBot was evaluated through quantitative and qualitative experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, addressing tie-knotting and other cloth manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. TieBot Performance on Tie-Knotting and Towel-Folding

- **Tie-Knotting Task (10 trials for each scenario, 6 subgoals):**
 - **Simulation (normal tie):** Achieved a 60% success rate with an average of 5.1 subgoals achieved.
 - **Simulation (larger tie, unseen):** Achieved a 30% success rate with an average of 4.3 subgoals achieved, indicating some generalization to unseen sizes.
 - **Real-world (softer tie):** Achieved a 50% success rate with an average of 5.0 subgoals achieved. Success was defined by the little end of the tie being inserted into the hole (final stage, Fig. 5).
 - **Real-world (harder tie, unseen):** Achieved a 30% success rate with an average of 4.15 subgoals achieved, demonstrating applicability to ties with different material properties.
- **Towel-Folding Task (unseen, real):** Achieved a 70% success rate with an average of 1.6 subgoals achieved.

#### 4.2. Comparison with ATM Baseline

- **Tie-Knotting Task (simulation):**
 - The ATM (Any-point Trajectory Modeling) baseline, which models tasks as point trajectories, achieved a 0% success rate and 0.0 average achieved subgoals for both normal and larger ties. This suggests that object trajectory representations may not be sufficient to capture the subtle dynamics and complex topology required for tie-knotting.
 - Further analysis on subtasks showed that ATM performed better on the first 3 simpler subtasks but still failed the last 3 involving complex topology, reinforcing the limitation of point trajectory representation for such tasks.
- **Towel-Folding Task (real):** ATM was able to learn the first folding action but struggled with subsequent actions, indicating limited capability for multi-step cloth manipulation.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM)

- **Quantitative Evaluation (L2 Distance in Simulation):**
 - HFM achieved the lowest L2 distance between the vertices of the target mesh and estimated mesh across three different deformation experiments (e.g., 0.0248m in exp1).
 - Ablated versions of HFM showed degraded performance: "Ours w/o FM" (only local feature matching) had 0.0732m, "Ours w/o KP" (local feature matching with predicted keypoints but without local frames for keypoint orientation) had 0.2424m, and "Ours w/o LF" (only predicted keypoints and local frames) had 0.0512m.
 - Coherent Point Drift (CPD) performed significantly worse (e.g., 0.1384m in exp1), indicating larger deviations from the correct state.
- **Qualitative Evaluation (Fig. 6):**
 - Visualizations confirmed that CPD and the ablated HFM versions often failed to correctly estimate the target mesh, particularly in scenarios involving complex deformations and topology changes.
 - HFM, by combining local feature matching and global keypoints, demonstrated superior ability to maintain topological correctness and capture both detailed motion and overall structure.
- **Iterative Keypoint Detection:** Experiments demonstrated the necessity of iteratively updating the keypoint detection model. The iterative method achieved a position error of 0.028m (10.68° Z-axis, 14.41° X-axis) compared to 0.183m (49.00° Z-axis, 68.76° X-axis) for a model trained on randomly sampled shapes, indicating that adaptive data generation helps overcome out-of-distribution challenges.

#### 4.4. HFM Applicability to Other Cloth Manipulation Tasks

- HFM was successfully applied to estimate meshes for a different method of knotting a tie and for a towel-folding task, visualizing the estimated states (Fig. 7). This suggests the generalizability of the HFM method across various cloth manipulation tasks.

#### 4.5. Identified Limitations

- The Real2Sim module's iterative training of keypoints detection models is computationally intensive.
- Due to hardware limitations (e.g., partial observation from a single overhead camera), the final step of inserting the little end of the tie in real-world experiments was hardcoded, limiting the fully learned aspect of the process.
- Failure cases included difficulties in rotating the entire ring structure of the tie (due to subtle tie dynamics and material properties) and challenges in perceiving and manipulating the little end of the tie due to occlusion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning deformable objects and long-horizon tasks.

- **Advancement in Deformable Object Manipulation:** The paper addresses the historically challenging problem of manipulating highly deformable objects like cloth, specifically focusing on the complex and previously underexplored task of tie-knotting. By achieving a 50% success rate in real-world tie-knotting, TieBot demonstrates a step forward in robotic dexterity with compliant materials.
- **Novel Real-to-Sim-to-Real Framework:** The proposed systematic Real-to-Sim-to-Real LfD framework provides a methodology for robots to acquire complex skills from visual human demonstrations. This approach effectively leverages the benefits of high-fidelity simulation for both robust state estimation and policy learning, bridging the gap between perception in the real world and execution by a robot.
- **Validation of Mesh Representation:** The work underscores the importance of using a detailed and physically informed representation, such as mesh, for complex cloth manipulation. Unlike simpler visual features or trajectories, meshes can accurately capture crucial geometric structures, physical properties, and topological changes, which are shown to be essential for task success and generalization.
- **Robust State Estimation for Deformable Objects:** The Hierarchical Feature Matching (HFM) method represents a significant development in cloth state estimation. By combining local feature matching with global, oriented keypoints and integrating physics priors from a differentiable simulator, HFM can track highly deformable objects accurately, even in the presence of occlusion and complex deformations. This robust estimation capability could be impactful for a wider range of deformable object manipulation tasks beyond tie-knotting.
- **Teacher-Student Learning for Complex Actions:** The application of a teacher-student training paradigm allows the robot to learn complex grasping and pulling actions from high-dimensional visual observations (point clouds). The teacher policy, leveraging privileged information, simplifies the learning of optimal actions, which are then effectively transferred to a student policy operating on more realistic sensor data, enhancing data efficiency and generalization.
- **Potential for Service Robotics:** The success in learning tie-knotting implies broader potential for service robots to assist individuals with daily living activities, such as dressing, especially for the elderly or those with motor impairments. The demonstrated applicability to towel-folding further illustrates the versatility of the approach for general household tasks.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the computational intensity of iterative keypoint detection training and challenges with partial observation, point to clear avenues for future research. Improvements in video tracking methods, more efficient perception pipelines, and the development of more dexterous and perception-rich robotic hardware could further enhance the system's capabilities.
