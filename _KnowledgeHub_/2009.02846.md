# Animated Cassie: A Dynamic Relatable Robotic Character

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of California, Berkeley, developed a framework to enable the dynamic bipedal robot Cassie to convey emotions through agile body motions. The system translates artist-designed kinematic animations into dynamically feasible and real-time executable movements, demonstrating stable execution of complex emotive sequences and novel walking height adjustments on the physical robot.

## Problem

- Most social robots are statically stable and rely on limited visual cues (e.g., facial expressions), restricting their capacity to convey a broad spectrum of emotions through full-body language.
- Dynamically stable bipedal robots offer potential for expressive motion but their complex, high-dimensional dynamics make integrating emotive motion design with real-time control challenging.
- Existing robot motion generation methods for legged robots are task-oriented or limited to slow motions, often failing to produce dynamically feasible emotive behaviors or support agile changes like walking height adjustments.

## Method

- An animation-to-real-world pipeline that uses **Blender** for intuitive, kinematic emotive motion design by animators, allowing for arbitrary motion lengths and aggressive movements.
- A segmented, ==direct-collocation== based ==nonlinear optimization== approach to convert these kinematically feasible animations into dynamically feasible trajectories for **Cassie**, preserving the emotional intent while satisfying physical constraints.
- A real-time control system, including versatile standing/walking controllers and a high-level ==automaton==, to execute the optimized motions and manage smooth, stable transitions between different control modes and motion sequences.

## Results

- The optimization framework successfully converted a **5-second** aggressive standing animation, which caused a simulated Cassie to fall when directly applied, into a dynamically feasible motion that was executed stably in simulation and on the physical robot.
- Experimental validation on the physical Cassie robot demonstrated stable execution of a "curiosity" narrative, involving aggressive transitions between dynamically optimized standing and walking motions orchestrated by the automaton.
- Cassie exhibited novel capabilities such as dynamically adjusting its walking height while moving forward, and observers inferred emotions from the combined body movements and synchronously played eye expressions on a mounted tablet.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Kinematically designed animated motions for dynamic bipedal robots require a dedicated dynamic optimization step to ensure physical feasibility, as direct application can lead to instability and falls.
> - Parameterizing walking gaits by desired velocity and walking height, and then linearly interpolating from a pre-computed gait library, enables dynamic adjustments in expressive walking behaviors.
> - A high-level automaton is critical for robustly concatenating diverse emotive motions and managing real-time transitions between different control modes (e.g., standing to walking), preventing instability from state discontinuities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader research landscape explores the development of robots capable of natural and intuitive human-robot interaction (HRI). A significant aspect of this involves enabling robots to convey emotions and personality, making them more relatable to humans.

Previous work in social robotics has predominantly focused on statically stable platforms. These robots often exhibit limited mobility, relying on simplistic visual cues such as facial expressions to communicate emotion. Examples include small mobile robots, person-sized robots with heavy bases, or stationary robotic heads. While these approaches contribute to HRI, their capacity to express emotions through body language is restricted due to their inherent static stability and often low degrees of freedom for whole-body movement.

In contrast, dynamically stable robots, particularly bipedal platforms, possess greater agility and a broader range of motion, offering the potential for more expressive and nuanced body language. However, controlling these systems for expressive behavior presents substantial challenges. Bipedal robots are characterized by their high-dimensional, nonlinear, and underactuated dynamics, often exhibiting hybrid dynamics due to intermittent ground contact. Integrating emotive motion design with the complex control requirements of such systems has largely remained unexplored.

Existing methods for robot motion generation, such as algorithmic task-oriented planning, typically result in motions that are functional but lack emotional attributes. While animation techniques from the entertainment industry have been adapted for robots to create believable behaviors, these efforts have primarily targeted statically stable platforms, with little attention to the dynamic properties of legged robots. Another approach, imitating human motion, has been used for humanoid robots to generate movements. However, such methods are often limited to slow motions, are restricted to imitation rather than creative design, and are specific to robots that can utilize Zero-Moment-Point (ZMP) stabilization. Robots with line feet, such as Cassie, cannot directly employ ZMP-based stabilization.

Research on the Cassie bipedal robot has focused on robust locomotion through methods like hybrid zero dynamics (HZD) and feedback control for standing and walking. However, these previous controllers have not addressed the design of emotive behaviors, nor have they facilitated agile motions such as changes in walking height.

This paper addresses a gap by developing a framework that enables dynamic bipedal robots to convey emotions through their agile body motions, thus introducing the concept of "Dynamic Relatable Robotic Characters." It aims to bridge advanced animation techniques with the complex dynamics and real-time control of legged robots, a domain where previous work has been constrained by either the static nature of the robot or the limitations of the motion generation methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to advance the field of human-robot interaction by enabling robots to move beyond purely task-oriented execution. The authors posit that for robots to interact meaningfully with humans, they must be relatable and capable of inciting emotion through their actions. The current limitations of most social robots, which are statically stable and primarily rely on facial expressions, restrict their ability to convey a broad spectrum of emotions. The authors identify dynamic bipedal robots as having untapped potential for expressive body language due to their agility and range of motion.

To realize this potential, the paper establishes the overarching objective of ascertaining the feasibility of utilizing a dynamic bipedal robot, specifically Cassie Cal, to perform natural, emphatic motions that can attribute a personality to the robot, thereby allowing it to be perceived as more than just a machine.

To achieve this, the authors delineate three specific sub-problems:

1. **Problem 1: Animation Tool for Emotive Behaviors:** To provide a method for designing robot behaviors with explicit emotional attributes. This involves creating kinematic motions that capture desired expressions, recognizing that these motions might initially lack dynamic feasibility for a physical robot. The objective is to empower animators, who may not possess extensive robotics knowledge, to intuitively create expressive robot movements.

2. **Problem 2: Dynamic Feasibility Conversion:** To ensure that the kinematically designed animated motions are dynamically feasible for the robot. This necessitates a process to translate the virtual animation trajectories into motions that adhere to the robot's physical constraints and dynamics, while maintaining fidelity to the original emotional intent of the animation. This is framed as an optimization problem where dynamic constraints must be satisfied while minimizing deviation from the animated input.

3. **Problem 3: Real-time Control and Balance:** To develop a real-time control system that enables the dynamic robot to execute the optimized motions while maintaining balance. This objective addresses the practical challenge of deploying complex, dynamically feasible trajectories onto a physical bipedal robot, requiring robust controllers that can handle balance and transitions between different motion sequences.

Solving these three problems is intended to provide a comprehensive framework for bringing emotive and expressive motions to dynamic robots in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes an animation-to-real-world pipeline to enable dynamic bipedal robots to express emotions through movement. This framework consists of offline animation creation and dynamics optimization, followed by online control and decision-making for real-time execution.

**A. Animation Creation (Section II):**
The first step involves creating a virtual character of Cassie in Blender, an open-source 3D creation suite. The physical Cassie robot has 20 Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF): 6 for the pelvis (3 translational, 3 rotational), 10 actuated rotational joints (5 per leg), and 4 passive joints (2 per leg) connected by leaf springs. The virtual character is "rigged" by mapping rigid body links to bones and kinematic connections to joints. To facilitate intuitive animation, target bones such as a Pelvis Target (PT), Leg Pose Targets (LPTs), and Foot Targets (FTs) are used, allowing animators to define poses by specifying end-effector positions or pelvis orientation.

An inverse kinematics (IK) problem is solved to determine joint positions from these target bone poses. This IK formulation minimizes the L2 distance between the robot's foot Cartesian positions and the FTs, while constraining the pelvis pose to the PT, leg orientations to LPTs, and incorporating Cassie's specific passive joint constraints (q5=0, q6+q4-13°=0). A custom Python plugin for Blender is developed for this, as the native IK solver does not support these internal joint constraints.

Animators then create emotive behaviors by defining keyframes, with intermediate frames interpolated by Blender. Visual cues, such as eye expressions displayed on a tablet mounted on the virtual character, are designed in conjunction with body movements. The animated joint trajectories (Q_ani) are extracted and stored, along with a tag indicating the motion's emotional attribute and control mode (standing or walking).

**B. Animation-to-Dynamics Optimization (Section III):**
The kinematically feasible animated motions (Q_ani) may not be dynamically feasible for the physical robot. To address this, a direct-collocation based nonlinear optimization approach is employed. Due to the potentially arbitrary length of animated motions, the optimization is segmented into shorter, overlapping sub-motions to manage computational complexity, while ensuring continuity between segments.

1. **Optimization for Standing:** For standing motions, the arbitrarily long animated trajectory is broken into sub-motions, each containing 'n' nodes, optimized sequentially. The optimization problem minimizes a weighted sum of motor energy consumption (||u(i)||²) and the L2 distance between the optimized poses 'q' and animated poses 'q_ani'. The weighting factor 'c' is adjusted to prioritize similarity to keyframes (higher 'c') and ensure a close match at the end of each sub-motion.
 Constraints include: time bounds, joint limits, motor torque limits, Cassie's full dynamics (D(q)¨q + C(q, ˙q)˙q + G(q) = Bu + λJᵀ(q)), kinematic constraints for feet (one fixed, the other within a bounded region), friction cone constraints for foot contact, and Center-of-Mass (CoM) projection within the support region. Continuity between sub-motions is enforced by matching pelvis position and velocity at the connection points, with an overlap (δ frames) to smooth transitions.

2. **Optimization for Walking:** For walking motions, directly imitating full-body motion while maintaining stable gaits is complex. Instead, the approach mediates imitation by optimizing a set of gait parameters 'p' = [v_x, v_y, h]ᵀ (sagittal velocity, lateral velocity, walking height) derived from the animation. The optimization generates periodic walking gaits using a hybrid model (right/left stance and impact transitions).
 The formulation, building on prior work for Cassie, includes constraints for average velocity to match 'p' and virtual constraints for hybrid zero dynamics to ensure impact-invariant motion. Additional constraints are introduced for pelvis height (q_z within a range around 'h') and step time (t, interpolated based on 'h'). This process generates a gait library 'G' comprising 1331 gaits, parameterized by Bezier polynomials, covering ranges of forward/backward speed, side-stepping speed, and walking height.

**C. Motion Library:**
After optimization, the dynamically feasible standing and walking motions are stored in a motion library. Each motion is tagged with its emotional attribute and control mode, allowing for retrieval by unique IDs.

**D. Control and Automaton for Robot Character (Section IV):**
To execute the motions in real-time and manage transitions, a control system and a high-level automaton are developed.

1. **Versatile Standing and Walking Controller:**
 - **Standing Controller (Fig. 6a):** A PD controller tracks reference joint trajectories from the motion library (q_m_d, ˙q_m_d), while lower-level control maintains feet on the ground and keeps the CoM within the support region.
 - **Walking Controller (Fig. 6b):** A desired gait (g_d) is obtained by linearly interpolating the gait library 'G' based on observed current robot gait parameters (ˆp). A gait regulator then applies corrections (δg_d) to swing leg pitch/abduction and stance leg length, proportional to the error between desired and current gait parameters (p_d - ˆp) and their derivatives. A gain scheduling module adjusts PD control gains based on the desired walking height (h_d), facilitating height changes during walking.

2. **Automaton for Robot Character (Fig. 7):** An online automaton manages the concatenation of different motions and smooth transitions between them, as well as handling switches between animation-controlled and manual radio-controlled modes. It operates as a state machine.
 Four elemental transition types are defined:
 - **Stand to Walk:** Involves changing standing height to desired walking height, pelvis lean, and lifting a leg to initiate walking.
 - **Walk to Stand:** Decelerates robot velocity to zero and switches to standing mode upon step completion.
 - **Radio from/to Animation:** Transitions from current robot states to the first frame of an animation or to radio commands, using linear interpolation of state trajectories.
 - **Animation to Animation:** Linear interpolation of states between the end of the current motion and the start of the next motion when a new motion ID is commanded.
 The automaton decides which motion to send to the controller, and when and how transitions should occur, based on a queue of motion IDs, human interaction requests, radio commands, and real-time robot feedback. The output is a blended motion trajectory sent to the real-time controllers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Optimization Results (Section V-A):**
The proposed segmented optimization framework, implemented using CFROST and IPOPT, demonstrated the capability to convert arbitrarily long animated motions into dynamically feasible trajectories for Cassie. A 5-second standing motion, for instance, could be optimized offline within approximately one hour using the robot's full-order model.

A key finding was validated through simulation. An animated standing motion that included a rapid pelvis yaw (over 50 degrees within 1 second) caused the simulated Cassie to fall when directly applied to the standing controller (Fig. 8c). This was attributed to the kinematic animation violating the robot's dynamic limits. In contrast, when the same animated motion was processed through the optimization framework, Cassie successfully performed the entire motion without falling (Fig. 8d). The optimized motion profile (solid line in Fig. 8a, 8b) showed that while it largely preserved the character of the animated motion (dash line), it smoothed rapid changes and introduced compensatory movements. For example, the optimization introduced significant pelvis roll motion, which was absent in the original animation, to compensate for aggressive changes in other degrees of freedom. The optimized motion's elapsed time was also extended by a factor of two, contributing to smoother execution. This confirmed the necessity and effectiveness of the optimization for translating aggressive animated motions into dynamically executable ones.

**B. Experimental Validation (Section V-B):**
The entire pipeline, including the automaton (200Hz) and standing/walking controllers (2kHz), was successfully deployed and tested on the physical Cassie robot. The validation focused on a "curiosity" story, where Cassie chased a laser point, requiring aggressive transitions between standing and walking.

1. **Standing Section 1:** Cassie successfully performed dynamic standing motions, leaning, turning, and squatting to keep a laser point in sight (Fig. 9a). These motions, initially aggressive in animation, were executed stably after optimization.
2. **Walking Section:** Cassie transitioned into walking, demonstrating the novel ability to adjust its walking height while moving forward to chase the laser (Fig. 9b). This capability, where the robot squat lower while walking, represents an advancement beyond previous Cassie controllers.
3. **Standing Section 2:** The robot autonomously decelerated and transitioned back to a standing posture, subsequently performing a "looking around" motion (Fig. 9c).

Throughout the experiments, the combination of body movements and synchronously played eye expressions on a tablet enabled observers to clearly infer the robot's intended emotions and the narrative of the story. The automaton was observed to be critical for stable transitions between standing and walking modes, as direct switching without the automaton's smoothing capabilities resulted in robot instability due to discontinuities in states and control policies. This validated the framework's ability to maintain balance and convey emotive content from animated sources on a dynamic bipedal robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by establishing a framework for developing "Dynamic Relatable Robotic Characters," a paradigm that leverages the agility of dynamic legged robots for emotional expression.

1. **Enabling Emotive Dynamic Bipedal Robots:** The work presents one of the first demonstrations of an animatronic dynamic legged robot, Cassie, capable of performing motions with desired emotional attributes. This extends the communicative capabilities of robots beyond static cues, allowing them to convey complex emotions and personality through full-body movement.

2. **Bridging Animation and Robotics:** The pipeline effectively connects intuitive animation tools (Blender) with the complex dynamic control requirements of legged robots. This allows animators, who may lack specialized robotics knowledge, to design expressive behaviors that can then be translated into executable motions, broadening the accessibility of robot behavior design.

3. **Robust Motion Generation and Control:** The segmented nonlinear optimization scheme provides a method to translate arbitrarily long and kinematically aggressive animated motions into dynamically feasible trajectories for complex robots like Cassie. This addresses a fundamental challenge in transferring artistic motion intent to physical constraints.

4. **Advanced Bipedal Locomotion Capabilities:** The development of a versatile walking controller capable of changing walking height during locomotion represents an enhancement to Cassie's mobility and expressive range. This feature, combined with precise velocity tracking, expands the repertoire of dynamic movements available for emotive expression.

5. **Stable Real-time Operation:** The high-level automaton provides a critical solution for robustly concatenating different motions and managing transitions between various control modes (standing, walking, animation-controlled, radio-controlled). This real-time smoothing prevents instability arising from state discontinuities, enabling the robot to execute complex sequences of emotive behaviors seamlessly and safely.

The framework developed in this paper has the potential to significantly impact human-robot interaction by creating more engaging, intuitive, and empathetic robotic companions. By allowing robots to express "personality" through dynamic actions, it could foster deeper human-robot relationships and expand the roles robots can play in social contexts, moving beyond mere task execution towards more integrated and relational interactions. Future work could build upon this foundation to develop larger libraries of nuanced and spirited motions, leading to more sophisticated human-dynamic-robot interaction scenarios.
