# PaLM-E: An Embodied Multimodal Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> PaLM-E directly incorporates real-world sensor data, such as images and state estimates, into a large language model framework to enable embodied AI systems capable of understanding and interacting with their physical environment through natural language, demonstrating high success rates in robotic manipulation and state-of-the-art performance in visual question answering.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) lack grounding in the physical world, limiting their application in embodied AI.
- Previous embodied AI approaches often kept language models separate from robotics components, leading to fragmented systems.
- Robotics research faces challenges with limited availability of diverse training data for specific tasks.

## Method

- **PaLM-E** builds on PaLM, using a unified Transformer architecture to process text and continuous sensor observations as "multimodal sentences."
- It employs specific encoders (e.g., MLPs, ViTs, OSRT) to map various modalities (state estimates, 2D images, 3D scene representations) into the language model's embedding space.
- The model is trained using a multi-task approach, co-training on robotics, visual question answering, image captioning, and language tasks, with fine-tuning the LLM showing better results.

## Results

- Achieved a **94.9%** success rate on tabletop manipulation tasks when trained with the full data mixture.
- PaLM-E-562B achieved state-of-the-art performance on the OK-VQA visual question answering benchmark.
- Demonstrated one-shot and zero-shot learning capabilities for new robotic tasks and object combinations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified architecture can effectively integrate perception, reasoning, and action generation within a single large language model.
> - Multi-task training across diverse domains (robotics, visual language, pure language) significantly improves performance and enables positive transfer learning.
> - Scaling up the language model base (e.g., to 540B parameters) helps retain language capabilities during multimodal fine-tuning and leads to emergent behaviors like multimodal chain-of-thought reasoning.
