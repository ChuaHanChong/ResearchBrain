# Improving Visual Reasoning with Iterative Evidence Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces SIEVE (Self-revisit Iterative Evidence Refinement), a framework enabling Vision-Language Models to automatically identify and inject task-relevant visual embeddings from their internal states into the reasoning chain. This method achieves substantial accuracy improvements across high-resolution visual reasoning, perception, and hallucination benchmarks, such as a 7.85% gain on V*Bench for the 4B model, without relying on external tools or image re-encoding.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) typically have a text-centric inference pipeline where visual information becomes less influential as textual reasoning extends.
- Current VLMs struggle with targeted, step-dependent revisiting of visual evidence, leading to underutilization of fine-grained visual details in complex multi-step reasoning.
- Existing solutions using external visual tools (e.g., zooming, cropping) disrupt reasoning continuity, require complex training, and introduce inference overhead due to repeated image re-encoding.

## Method

- **SIEVE** uses a self-guided visual evidence identification mechanism that automatically extracts embeddings of visually salient regions by combining ==token-level gradient saliency== with ==multimodal hidden state matching==.
- A ==reinforcement learning (RL) policy== is trained to determine when to trigger visual revisiting and which cached region embeddings to retrieve and insert into the VLM's reasoning chain.
- A comprehensive ==trajectory-level reward function== is designed to guide the RL process, incentivizing correct answers, proper output formatting, active embedding utilization, and stable, meaningful reasoning.

## Results

- SIEVE achieved consistent performance gains, including a **7.85%** overall accuracy improvement on V*Bench and up to **4.00%** on HR-Bench 8K for the Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct base model.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization, improving perception benchmarks (e.g., **12.8%** on MME-Real-Lite reasoning split for the 4B model) and reducing hallucination metrics on HallusionBench by **3.89%** for the 8B model.
- Inference time analysis showed SIEVE did not substantially increase computational overhead, and in some cases, it was faster than existing tool-invocation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs' internal visual embeddings contain sufficient information for complex visual reasoning, and the key is enabling the model to selectively access and reuse relevant localized evidence.
> - Task-relevant visual evidence can be efficiently identified and extracted from intermediate hidden states using gradient saliency and multimodal alignment without external tools or image re-encoding.
> - Reinforcement learning with a carefully designed reward function can effectively train VLMs to dynamically integrate relevant visual cues, leading to improved performance across diverse reasoning tasks and reduced hallucination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated considerable capabilities in multimodal question answering, often leveraging chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. However, the prevailing VLM inference pipeline is largely text-centric. Images are typically encoded into a fixed set of visual tokens at the initial stage, which serve as static context. As text generation proceeds autoregressively, the model's conditioning gradually shifts towards the growing textual history, diminishing the relative influence of the initial visual evidence. This dynamic often results in the underutilization of visual information, particularly in multi-step or long-horizon reasoning tasks where fine-grained visual details might be critical at intermediate stages.

To address this limitation, recent research has explored methods for integrating visual evidence more explicitly into the reasoning trajectory, drawing inspiration from human cognitive processes. A common approach involves equipping VLMs with external visual tools, such as zooming, cropping, or object detection, which generate sub-images or targeted observations for re-inspection. These tools are then fed back into the model as additional inputs, often guided by reinforcement learning (RL) policies that learn when and how to invoke them. Some advanced methods even allow VLMs to generate executable code for more flexible image manipulation.

Despite their effectiveness, these tool-augmented methods introduce several challenges. First, they can disrupt the continuity of the reasoning chain, as new image views are typically appended to the original input rather than being seamlessly integrated into specific steps of the CoT. Second, learning to repeatedly invoke external tools requires substantial amounts of training data and complex training pipelines.

Concurrently, another line of research has focused on operating within latent representation spaces or selectively using visual tokens to reduce reliance on explicit image operations. This includes methods that synthesize auxiliary visual representations, introduce learnable latent visual tokens, or dynamically select and prune visual tokens to improve efficiency. However, these paradigms often necessitate significant effort to train models to reason effectively over these newly introduced visual latents, which may differ from the native textual representation space.

This paper positions itself by challenging the necessity of generating new image views through external operations or constructing separate latent spaces for visual revisiting. It hypothesizes that the original visual embeddings, already present within the VLM's internal representations, contain sufficient information for grounded reasoning. The core bottleneck is identified as the model's limited ability to selectively reuse relevant visual evidence as generation unfolds. The proposed method, SIEVE, seeks to directly leverage these intrinsic signals by extracting and injecting task-relevant visual embeddings into the reasoning chain, thereby offering a lightweight and end-to-end solution that avoids the drawbacks of external tools and complex new latent space learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the observed limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robust visual reasoning, especially in scenarios requiring multi-step analysis or fine-grained visual grounding. While VLMs are capable of complex reasoning, their inherent text-centric inference pipeline often leads to visual information becoming less influential as the reasoning chain extends. The initial encoding of an image into static visual tokens does not facilitate targeted, step-dependent revisits to the visual evidence, leading to underutilization of critical visual cues.

Existing approaches that attempt to address this by explicitly integrating visual evidence, such as those relying on external tools (e.g., zooming, cropping), face several issues:
1. **Disruption of Reasoning Continuity:** External tool calls generate new image views that are often appended to the original input, rather than being directly integrated into the flow of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. This can fragment the reasoning process.
2. **Training Complexity and Overhead:** Learning to invoke external tools repeatedly necessitates large, specifically curated training datasets and complex VLM training pipelines, which are resource-intensive. Furthermore, these methods often incur inference-time overhead due to repeated image re-encoding.

The authors propose that VLMs already generate strong internal signals suitable for identifying and reusing visual evidence. To support this insight, a preliminary analysis was conducted where salient image regions were manually identified, and their corresponding visual embeddings were directly injected into intermediate reasoning steps. This intervention, without any additional training, resulted in a 3% accuracy improvement on the V\* benchmark, suggesting that localized embedding evidence provides a compact and effective signal that models can readily leverage.

Motivated by this observation, the key objectives of this work are:
- To develop an end-to-end self-revisit framework, termed **SIEVE (Self-revisit Iterative Evidence Refinement)**, that enables VLMs to re-engage with image evidence through their internal representations.
- To design a mechanism that automatically extracts embeddings of salient image regions and dynamically injects them into the reasoning chain when additional visual grounding is required. This process should bypass external tool calls, re-encoding, and dedicated visual processing modules, thus preserving access to fine-grained visual cues efficiently.
- To train the VLM, using reinforcement learning, to learn an effective policy for *when* to trigger visual revisiting and *which* region embeddings to retrieve and insert during the reasoning process.
- To empirically demonstrate that leveraging intrinsic signals within the VLM's hidden states can yield consistent performance improvements across various visual reasoning, perception, and hallucination benchmarks, thereby offering an efficient and scalable alternative to existing tool-orchestrated methods.

In essence, the research aims to validate the hypothesis that the original visual embeddings hold sufficient information for complex visual reasoning, provided the model can selectively access and reuse relevant localized evidence at opportune moments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core hypothesis underpinning SIEVE is that the visual embeddings generated by a VLM inherently contain sufficient information for complex visual reasoning, provided the model can access relevant localized evidence at appropriate junctures during inference. SIEVE addresses this by treating task-relevant region embeddings as reusable visual evidence and learning to integrate them through a reinforcement learning (RL) policy optimization.

The methodology comprises two primary stages: (i) Self-Guided Visual Evidence Identification and (ii) Visually-grounded Reinforcement Learning.

#### 3.1. Self-Guided Visual Evidence Identification

This stage focuses on automatically discovering and extracting embeddings of visually salient regions that are critical for reasoning, without relying on manual annotations or predefined heuristics. It involves a two-stage process:

- **Discovering Textual Anchors via Gradient Saliency:**
 - Instead of external concept taggers, SIEVE identifies textual anchors directly from the model's own predictive dynamics.
 - The primary signal is token-level gradient saliency. If a token is crucial for the model's next-step prediction, its embedding's perturbation will significantly impact the output logit.
 - Formally, for each input token embedding `h_i`, a saliency score `Sal(i) = ||∇_h_i s ⊙ h_i||_2` is computed, where `s` is the logit of the predicted next token. This formulation captures both the sensitivity of the prediction and the magnitude of the representation.
 - A stop-word filter is applied, and only content-bearing tokens whose saliency exceeds a predefined threshold are retained as textual anchors. These anchors implicitly represent semantics pivotal to the model's reasoning (e.g., objects, attributes).

- **Identifying Visual Evidence with Textual Anchors:**
 - Once textual anchors are identified, their corresponding visual regions are localized by matching the internal hidden representations of these text tokens with image patch tokens within the model's joint multimodal space.
 - The process operates on intermediate-layer representations, which prior work suggests capture richer semantic alignment. A stabilized representation `¯H` is obtained by averaging hidden states from middle layers.
 - Image patch tokens (`X`) and textual anchor representations (`q`) are extracted from `¯H`, then mean-centered and ℓ2-normalized.
 - Anchor-patch affinity is computed using cosine similarity: `s_i = cos(ˆx_i, ˆq)`, converted into a temperature-scaled softmax distribution `w_i`.
 - This distribution is used to extract spatially coherent regions: patch tokens are mapped onto a grid, and each block's score is computed as the maximum similarity within the block. The `TopK` scoring blocks (where `K=1` is chosen based on hyperparameter analysis) are selected.
 - Each selected block is expanded into a bounding rectangle. The embeddings of patches within this region are then aggregated to form a region-level snapshot `E_i`. These `E_i` are cached as reusable visual evidence alongside each training sample.

#### 3.2. Visually-grounded Reinforcement Learning

SIEVE's RL formulation enables the model to learn when and how to effectively retrieve and insert these cached region embeddings. Unlike tool-augmented methods, SIEVE reuses embeddings already produced by the vision encoder and projected into text space, avoiding image re-encoding overhead.

- **Action Space and Policy:** The policy `π_θ` selects the next action `a_t` conditioned on the original image `I` and the full interaction history `s_t`. The history `s_t` comprises the raw image followed by all preceding textual responses (`x`) and any previously inserted visual evidence (`E`) in temporal order: `s_t ≜ I || (x_1||E_1) || · · · || (x_t−1||E_t−1)`.
 - Actions `a_t` include either terminating with a final answer or triggering the insertion of cached visual evidence.
 - The selection decision can be invoked repeatedly across multiple reasoning steps until a final answer is produced or a maximum reasoning horizon is reached.

- **Trajectory-Level Reward Design:** A comprehensive reward function `R(τ)` is designed to evaluate the quality of the complete reasoning path (trajectory `τ`). It comprises four components:
 - **Result Reward (R_res):** The primary learning signal, awarded for the correctness of the final answer (binary: 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect). Weighted by `λ_1 = 0.6`.
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** Promotes well-structured outputs. For single-turn, it requires a valid reasoning chain and a final answer. For multi-turn, it additionally requires explicit embedding selection during an intermediate turn. Any structural violation yields zero reward. Weighted by `λ_2 = 0.3`.
 - **Embedding Reward (R_emb):** A bonus activated only if the model produces a correct final answer AND invokes embedding insertion at least once. This incentivizes the model to actively leverage visual evidence when beneficial. Weighted by `λ_3 = 0.5`.
 - **Action Reward (R_act):** Enhances training stability by (i) penalizing overly short reasoning traces and (ii) providing a small positive reward for committing to an action (retrieving an embedding or producing an answer), discouraging non-committal outputs. Weighted by `λ_4 = 0.2`.

- **Evidence Refresh Mechanism:** If the model fails despite utilizing cached evidence, it signals that the stored embeddings might be misaligned or insufficient. In such cases, task-relevant regions are re-identified under the current model parameters, their embeddings are re-extracted, and the evidence cache is updated. This mechanism allows the evidence to co-evolve with the policy, progressively improving visual grounding.

The training pipeline uses GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Learning for Policy Optimization) for 60 rollout steps, with a small dataset of approximately 1,500 COCO images for training. Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct serve as the base models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SIEVE demonstrates consistent performance improvements across various benchmarks and model scales, particularly in high-resolution visual reasoning tasks and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Performance on High-Resolution Visual Reasoning Benchmarks (V\*Bench, HR-Bench)

- SIEVE consistently outperformed all baseline methods, including vanilla models, GRPO-trained models, and external tool-augmented approaches (DyFo, ZoomEye, Zoom-Refine), on both V\*Bench and HR-Bench (4K and 8K resolutions).
- For the Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct base model, SIEVE achieved an overall accuracy improvement of 7.85% on V\*Bench, 3.50% on HR-Bench 4K, and 3.75% on HR-Bench 8K, compared to the vanilla baseline.
- For the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct base model, SIEVE showed an overall accuracy gain of 5.24% on V\*Bench, 2.50% on HR-Bench 4K, and 4.00% on HR-Bench 8K over its vanilla counterpart. These results indicate that reasoning with hidden-state embeddings effectively enhances performance in tasks requiring fine-grained visual understanding.

#### 4.2. Generalization Across Diverse Benchmarks

- **Perception and Reasoning:** SIEVE demonstrated consistent improvements on perception benchmarks (MME-Real-Lite, RealWorldQA) and multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, LogicVista, WeMath).
 - On MME-Real-Lite, SIEVE 4B achieved an overall gain of 8.08%, with a notable 12.8% improvement in the reasoning split. SIEVE 8B showed even higher gains, with 9.12% overall and 14.8% in the reasoning split.
 - Significant gains were also observed on WeMath, with SIEVE 8B achieving an 11.3% improvement.
- **Hallucination:** On the HallusionBench, SIEVE improved hallucination metrics (qAcc, fAcc, aAcc, Overall) for both model sizes, with a 3.89% overall gain for the 8B model. This suggests that better visual grounding can mitigate hallucination.
These results imply that injecting hidden-state embeddings during reasoning provides benefits across diverse task categories beyond high-resolution specific tasks.

#### 4.3. Visualization and Analysis of Evidence Identification

- Visualizations using the V\* dataset demonstrated that SIEVE's mechanism for retrieving bounding boxes aligned with learned region embeddings consistently identified semantically meaningful and task-relevant image regions.
- While minor localization drift could occur due to Qwen-VL's patch-based segmentation, the approach generally focused on relevant areas, providing an intuitive explanation for the observed performance gains.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Role of Inserting Embedding:** A controlled experiment showed that replacing semantically aligned region embeddings with randomly sampled ones led to a substantial degradation in performance. This confirmed that the observed gains were attributable to the semantic relevance and contextual alignment of the injected visual embeddings, not merely the act of augmenting the reasoning process with additional visual tokens.
- **Selection of Embeddings from Different Layers:** An analysis of layer-wise embedding retrieval accuracy using the Information Hit Ratio (IHR) revealed that middle layers of the VLM captured the most accurate correspondences between salient textual tokens and relevant image regions. Early layers produced noisy matches, while late layers were overly task-specialized. This justified the design choice of extracting features from intermediate representations.
- **Role of Action Rewards:** The action rewards, including signal reward and thought richness reward, were found to be critical for training stability. Without the signal reward, training rewards collapsed in later stages. Without the thought richness reward, the model tended to exploit the reward mechanism by outputting empty reasoning tags or repetitive content, leading to unstable response lengths and less substantive reasoning. Action-level rewards thus promoted meaningful, structured reasoning and stable training.

#### 4.5. Inference Time Cost

- An evaluation of average inference time per question revealed that SIEVE did not substantially increase the computational overhead compared to the baseline.
- In some cases, SIEVE was faster than tool-invocation methods (e.g., faster than ZoomRefine on Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, and fastest among tool-call methods on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct). This indicates a favorable trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness.

#### 4.6. Hyperparameter Analysis (K-value for TopK selection)

- An analysis of the `k` parameter for selecting the `TopK` scoring blocks showed that setting `k=1` yielded the best performance. Increasing `k` led to performance degradation, attributed to the accumulation of irrelevant or noisy visual information within the aggregated region embeddings when multiple regions were inserted. This supported the choice of inserting a small number of highly relevant visual embeddings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, through the introduction of the SIEVE framework, offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of multimodal reasoning with Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

**1. Paradigm Shift in Visual Re-grounding:** SIEVE challenges the prevailing paradigm that explicit external tools (like cropping or zooming) or complex new latent spaces are necessary for VLMs to effectively revisit and leverage visual information during inference. By demonstrating that intrinsic signals within the VLM's existing hidden states can be effectively harnessed, the research suggests that robust visual re-grounding can be achieved in a more self-contained and lightweight manner. This could lead to a re-evaluation of current architectural designs and training methodologies for multimodal models.

**2. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** Unlike approaches that require external tool orchestration, retrieval systems, or additional visual processing modules (which often involve re-encoding parts of an image), SIEVE reuses already computed embeddings. This eliminates the inference-time overhead associated with redundant visual re-encoding and simplifies the overall system design. The framework's ability to achieve significant performance gains with only a small training dataset (approximately 1.5k samples) further underscores its data efficiency and potential for scalable deployment in real-world applications.

**3. Improved Visual Grounding and Reasoning Capabilities:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across a diverse range of benchmarks—including high-resolution visual reasoning, general perception tasks, mathematical and logical reasoning, and even hallucination mitigation—indicate that SIEVE substantially enhances the VLM's ability to perform image-grounded reasoning. By allowing the model to dynamically refine its understanding of visual content, SIEVE enables more accurate and robust responses to complex multimodal queries.

**4. Increased Interpretability:** The visualization of extracted regions shows that SIEVE's self-guided evidence identification mechanism consistently selects semantically meaningful and task-relevant image areas. This provides a degree of interpretability into *what* visual cues the model prioritizes at different reasoning steps, offering insights into its decision-making process.

**5. Foundation for Internalized Multimodal Operations:** The success of SIEVE suggests that VLMs possess richer internal representations than previously assumed, and the key lies in developing mechanisms to effectively leverage these internal signals. This work could inspire future research into internalizing more complex multimodal operations directly within the VLM's architecture, potentially leading to more integrated and powerful reasoning systems that do not rely on external modules.

In summary, SIEVE provides a valuable contribution by offering an efficient, effective, and internally-driven mechanism for iterative visual evidence refinement in VLMs. Its implications extend to improving the performance, efficiency, and interpretability of multimodal models, potentially simplifying future research and development in complex visual reasoning tasks.
