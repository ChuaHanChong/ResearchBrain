# On Feasible Rewards in Multi-Agent Inverse Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Zurich provide a theoretical analysis of multi-agent inverse reinforcement learning, addressing challenges in defining feasible reward functions and achieving reward identifiability. They characterize feasible reward sets in entropy-regularized Markov games and establish conditions for identifying average rewards or specific components of linearly separable reward functions, including associated error propagation and sample complexity bounds.

## Problem

- Designing effective reward functions for multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is challenging due to interacting agents and potentially conflicting objectives.
- Multi-agent Inverse Reinforcement Learning (MAIRL) struggles with the ill-posed nature of inferring reward functions from expert demonstrations, particularly due to the multiplicity of Nash equilibria in Markov games.
- Extending single-agent IRL's feasible reward set definition to MAIRL using a single observed Nash equilibrium leads to 'equilibrium ambiguity,' where recovered rewards can enable new, exploitable equilibria (quantified by a Nash Gap of order (1−au)−1(1-	au)^{-1}(1−au)−1).

## Method

- The study proposes focusing on entropy-regularized Markov games, which yield unique ==Quantal Response Equilibria== (QRE), to address the equilibrium ambiguity and selection problem in MAIRL.
- It provides an explicit characterization of feasible reward functions for entropy-regularized settings, expressing an agent's reward based on its observed QRE policy, the opponent's QRE policy, value function, and transition dynamics.
- To achieve reward identifiability, the paper demonstrates that average rewards can be identified under specific rank conditions, and full reward functions can be identified (up to constants) in linearly separable reward structures by observing expert behavior across multiple environments or opponent policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simply extending single-agent IRL to MAIRL with a single Nash equilibrium observation is insufficient, as it results in an under-constrained feasible reward set that can introduce significant exploitability in the recovered game.
> - Entropy regularization effectively mitigates equilibrium ambiguity in MAIRL by yielding unique Quantal Response Equilibria, leading to more robust reward inference and higher correlation with true reward functions.
> - While general-sum MAIRL presents challenges for full reward identifiability, specific forms like average rewards or components of linearly separable rewards can be identified under certain conditions, highlighting the need for structural assumptions or varied observation contexts.
