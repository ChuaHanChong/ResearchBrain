# NeHMO: Neural Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability Learning for Decentralized Safe Multi-Arm Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The NeHMO framework from Purdue University introduces a decentralized approach to safe multi-arm motion planning, using physics-informed neural Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability to model worst-case inter-arm interactions. It achieves real-time operation and high success rates for systems up to 30 degrees of freedom, significantly outperforming centralized methods in scalability and demonstrating robust collision avoidance even against unpredictable agents.

## Problem

- Centralized multi-arm motion planning methods face exponential computational complexity, hindering real-time application for high-dimensional systems.
- Decentralized planning approaches often lack robustness against unpredictable or adversarial behaviors from other manipulators, compromising safety.
- Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) provides strong safety guarantees but is computationally infeasible for high-dimensional multi-arm systems, while neural HJR can be data-inefficient for complex articulated robots.

## Method

- Develops a neural ==Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR)== learning pipeline, enhanced with physics-informed priors like ==structural symmetry== and ==boundary condition residual learning==, to efficiently approximate worst-case safety value functions for high-dimensional multi-arm systems.
- Models inter-arm safety as a ==zero-sum differential game==, enabling individual manipulators to plan defensively against potentially adversarial or unpredictable actions of other robots.
- Embeds the learned HJR safety value function into a decentralized, ==receding-horizon trajectory optimization== framework (**NeHMO**), allowing real-time motion planning with explicit safety constraints.

## Results

- The physics-informed neural HJR learning, utilizing symmetry and residual learning, matched or surpassed baseline accuracy with **half** the training data, demonstrating enhanced data efficiency for complex systems.
- NeHMO achieved **92%** success/**2%** collision for dual-UR5 (12-DoF, **36 ms** planning) and scaled to 30-DoF systems maintaining real-time performance (~**43 ms**) with **8%** collision, while centralized methods were far slower and degraded severely at scale.
- Demonstrated robust collision avoidance in near-adversarial settings, with a single NeHMO-controlled arm achieving a **47%** success rate against a recklessly behaving arm, significantly reducing collisions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exploiting structural symmetry in Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability allows for training with significantly less data while maintaining or improving solution accuracy for multi-arm systems.
> - Learning a residual function relative to the collision boundary improves the neural HJR's ability to represent complex safety landscapes, leading to better convergence and solution quality.
> - Integrating a learned worst-case safety value function within a decentralized optimization framework provides robustness against unpredictable agent behaviors and enables scalable real-time planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces challenges in ensuring safe and efficient motion planning for multi-arm systems. These systems are characterized by high dimensionality, coupled configuration spaces, and intricate collision constraints. Existing approaches generally fall into two categories: centralized and decentralized.

**Centralized planning** methods, such as those relying on global coordination, can enforce system-wide safety by considering all robot motions simultaneously. However, their computational complexity tends to increase exponentially with the number of manipulators and their degrees of freedom (DoF), rendering them impractical for real-time applications and large-scale environments.

**Decentralized planning** offers improved scalability by allowing individual robots to plan their own motions. While this approach enhances computational efficiency, it typically relies on predicting the behaviors of other robots or assuming their adherence to a shared control policy. These assumptions may not hold in unstructured or safety-critical settings, particularly when other manipulators exhibit unpredictable or adversarial behaviors. Extending these decentralized strategies to high-dimensional articulated systems with complex collision geometries remains a challenge, as geometric abstractions designed for simpler platforms often prove insufficient.

**Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR)** provides a mathematical framework for analyzing safety-critical control by formulating safety as a differential game between a control input and a disturbance. This allows for reasoning about worst-case interactions under dynamic constraints, making it suitable for inter-arm collision avoidance under uncertainty. However, classical HJR methods suffer from an exponential increase in computational cost with system dimensionality, limiting their direct application to complex multi-arm systems.

Recent advancements have leveraged **deep learning to approximate HJR solutions**, exemplified by methods such as DeepReach. These neural network-based approaches address the dimensionality curse of traditional grid-based HJR solvers by learning the value function in a self-supervised manner, removing the need for labeled data. Despite this, they often require extensive training data to converge and may not inherently scale efficiently to the specific complexities of high-dimensional multi-arm systems. Furthermore, purely data-driven control policies can lack interpretability and structured safety guarantees, which are critical in safety-sensitive applications.

This work, NeHMO, aims to bridge these gaps by enhancing neural HJR for multi-arm systems and integrating it into a robust decentralized planning framework. It seeks to overcome the scalability limitations of centralized methods, mitigate the dependency on behavior prediction in decentralized schemes, and improve the data efficiency and applicability of neural HJR to high-dimensional, articulated robotic manipulators. The paper positions its contribution as learning structured, reachability-based safety representations that can be embedded within an optimization-based planner, thereby preserving interpretability and computational efficiency in safety-critical, real-time multi-arm operations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable decentralized framework for safe multi-arm motion planning that can operate in real-time under uncertain or unpredictable behaviors from other manipulators.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations observed in existing multi-arm motion planning methodologies:

- **Scalability limitations of centralized planners:** Centralized approaches, while capable of coordinating all arms and enforcing global safety, experience computational complexity that grows rapidly with the number of manipulators and their degrees of freedom. This inherent inefficiency restricts their application in real-time settings and for systems with many arms or complex kinematics. The authors aim to overcome this by developing a decentralized approach.
- **Vulnerability of current decentralized methods:** Existing decentralized methods often rely on explicit predictions of other arms' future behaviors or assume compliance with a shared control policy. These assumptions are problematic in unstructured environments or safety-critical scenarios where other manipulators might act unpredictably or even adversarially, potentially leading to collisions. The motivation is to create a planner that can account for worst-case interactions without requiring explicit knowledge of other agents' policies.
- **Dimensionality challenges of Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR):** While HJR offers a principled framework for modeling safety-critical control and reasoning about worst-case disturbances, classical HJR solvers suffer from exponential scaling with dimensionality. This makes their direct application to high-dimensional systems like multi-arm manipulators computationally infeasible.
- **Limitations of neural network-based HJR (DeepReach) for multi-arm systems:** Although DeepReach introduced a method to approximate HJR solutions using neural networks, thereby mitigating the dimensionality curse, it typically requires extensive training data to cover the state space and converge to a valid solution. Furthermore, the original DeepReach approach treats the problem as structure-agnostic, which may not fully exploit the inherent physical and geometric properties of articulated multi-arm systems. The motivation is to improve the data efficiency and solution quality of neural HJR specifically for the complex configuration spaces of multi-arm robots.
- **Need for integrated safety representations in planning:** Many learning-based approaches focus on end-to-end policy learning, which can lack interpretability and structured safety representations, limiting their robustness. The authors aim to learn structured, reachability-based safety representations that can be explicitly integrated into an optimization-based planner, maintaining the benefits of interpretability and computational efficiency.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the NeHMO framework are:
1. **To augment the DeepReach neural HJR learning pipeline** by incorporating physics-informed priors, specifically exploiting structural symmetry and employing boundary condition residual learning, to enhance data efficiency and scalability for high-dimensional articulated multi-robot systems.
2. **To develop a decentralized trajectory optimization framework** that effectively integrates the learned HJR safety value function to actively avoid unsafe inter-arm interactions and generate motion plans in real-time.
3. **To empirically validate the proposed framework** on challenging multi-arm motion planning tasks, including systems up to 30 degrees of freedom, heterogeneous manipulators, and scenarios involving near-adversarial or unpredictable robot behaviors, demonstrating its performance against state-of-the-art baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NeHMO framework integrates a neural Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) learning approach with a decentralized trajectory optimization strategy to facilitate safe multi-arm motion planning. The methodology is structured around modeling worst-case inter-arm safety, learning an approximation of the safety value function, and embedding this function into a real-time planning algorithm.

**A. Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability Modeling:**
The core of NeHMO's safety mechanism is HJR, which models pairwise interactions between robots as a coupled dynamical system. One robot is treated as having a control input, while the other is considered an adversarial disturbance. This formulation represents a zero-sum differential game, where one robot attempts to avoid collision (evader) and the other attempts to cause it (pursuer), thereby capturing worst-case scenarios.
The problem is defined as computing a collision-avoiding value function, `V(t, x)`, and its corresponding avoid set `V(t)`. The collision set `L` is typically defined by a signed distance function, where `l(x) ≤ 0` indicates a collision. The value function `V(t, x)` is obtained as the viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs (HJI) partial differential equation (PDE), with the terminal condition `V(T, x) = l(x)`. The zero level set of `V(t, x)` defines the boundary of the safe set, from which collision can be avoided under worst-case disturbances. This formulation also allows for the derivation of optimal control laws for both the evader and the pursuer based on the gradient of `V(t, x)`.

**B. Physics-Informed HJR Learning:**
To overcome the limitations of classical HJR solvers and enhance the scalability of neural HJR methods like DeepReach for high-dimensional multi-arm systems, NeHMO introduces two physics-informed priors during the neural network training process:

1. **Symmetry in HJR:** Multi-robot systems often exhibit physical and geometric symmetries (e.g., configurations that are geometrically equivalent). The authors formally demonstrate that if the system dynamics and boundary condition are symmetric with respect to a differentiable bijective state mapping function `f(x)`, then the HJR value function `V(t, x)` also inherits this symmetry. This property allows the training process to be restricted to a representative subset of the state space (`X_train`), effectively reducing the amount of data required for training. During inference, the value function for symmetric states in `X_sym` is derived using the inverse mapping `f^-1(x)`, thus covering the entire state space with reduced training samples.
2. **Boundary Condition Residual Learning:** Instead of training a neural network to directly approximate the entire value function `V(t, x)`, NeHMO trains it to learn a time-dependent residual function, `V_res(t, x) = V(t, x) - l(x)`. This decomposition separates the complex geometric structure of the collision boundary `l(x)` from the temporal dynamics, which simplifies the function that the neural network needs to represent. This approach is observed to improve training convergence and solution quality, particularly for articulated multi-arm systems where collision sets induce intricate boundaries in the configuration space.

**C. Trajectory Optimization:**
The learned HJR value function, `V_theta(t, x)`, is integrated as a safety constraint within a decentralized receding-horizon trajectory optimization framework. Each arm independently computes its motion plan by solving an optimization problem (Opt) over a planning horizon `t_plan`. The objective is to steer the arm towards its goal while ensuring safety for at least `t_safe` duration after executing the plan.
To account for the uncertain behaviors of other manipulators, the optimization explicitly infers the worst-case actions of other robots using the gradient of `V_theta(t, x)` (derived from the pursuer's optimal control law). This allows each arm to generate defensive motion plans without assuming knowledge of other agents' control policies.

The optimization problem (Opt) for arm 1 is formulated as:
`min_u cost(x_1(t_plan), u, x_1_goal)`
subject to:
- `x_1(t + Δt) = g_d(x_1(t), u)` (discrete dynamics for self)
- `x_i(t + Δt) = g_d(x_i(t), d_i)` (discrete dynamics for other arms)
- `τ = T - t_plan` (time remaining in HJR context)
- `d_i = arg min_d max_u ⟨∇_x V_θ(τ, x_1(t), x_i(t)), g(x(t), u, d)⟩` (worst-case disturbance for other arms)
- `V_θ(T - t_safe, x_1(t_plan), x_i(t_plan)) > ϵ` (safety constraint for each other arm `i`)
Here, `ϵ > 0` is a safety margin. A quadratic terminal cost is used, and continuous joints are not wrapped in the cost function to encourage longer routes, which may lead to more interaction. For velocity-controlled, box-constrained systems, the worst-case disturbance `d_i` has a closed-form solution, aiding computational efficiency.

**D. Decentralized Safe Multi-agent Motion Planning Algorithm:**
The overall planning algorithm operates iteratively. At each step, a robot perceives the current configurations of all other agents. This information is used to set up and solve the `Opt` problem. The resulting optimal control input is then executed for the next planning horizon. If the optimization fails or times out, a fail-safe strategy is invoked, which applies the most conservative control action (derived from the HJR value function) to avoid collision. This continuous sensing, planning, and execution cycle enables real-time adaptation to dynamic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the NeHMO framework was conducted through three sets of experiments: HJR learning validation, multi-UR5 motion planning, and challenging heterogeneous multi-arm scenarios.

**A. HJR Learning Validation**
This section assessed the effectiveness of the proposed physics-informed training pipeline for neural HJR. Comparisons were made between the original DeepReach, DeepReach with explicit boundary conditions (bc), and DeepReach with explicit boundary conditions and symmetry exploitation (bc+sym).
- **Boundary Condition Residual Learning (bc):** On the Air3D system (with a relatively simple boundary), including the explicit boundary term yielded modest improvements in `l1-error`. However, on the 2-link planar arm system, which presents more intricate collision boundaries in its articulated configuration space, the `bc` variant demonstrated a significant improvement in solution quality compared to the original DeepReach.
- **Symmetry Exploitation (bc+sym):** The `bc+sym` variant, which combines residual learning with symmetry reduction, was trained using half the data samples compared to `bc` and DeepReach (e.g., 40 million vs. 80 million samples). Despite this reduction in data, `bc+sym` consistently matched or improved upon the performance of `bc` in terms of `l1-error` on both the Air3D and Simple Arm systems. This indicates that exploiting symmetry enhances data efficiency without compromising solution accuracy.

**B. Multi-UR5 Motion Planning**
This set of experiments evaluated NeHMO's performance on various multi-UR5 motion planning tasks against several baselines.

1. **Dual-UR5 Motion Planning (12-DoF):**
 - **Performance Metrics:** NeHMO achieved the highest success rate (92%) and the lowest collision rate (2%) across 100 randomly generated scenarios, outperforming all baselines.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **CHOMP (centralized):** Achieved a competitive success rate (86%) and a collision rate of 14%. However, its mean planning time was significantly higher (570 ms) compared to NeHMO (36 ms), limiting its real-time applicability.
 - **(Opt)+DeepReach:** This baseline, using the original DeepReach solution within NeHMO's optimization framework, performed poorly (66% success, 34% collision), indicating that the original DeepReach was not sufficiently informative for high-dimensional articulated systems.
 - **Ha et al. (RL-based):** Achieved 79% success and 13% collision, with very fast planning times, but its performance was lower than NeHMO.
 - **Naive Planner:** Demonstrated 66% success and 34% collision, highlighting the need for active collision avoidance.
 - **Efficiency Trade-off:** NeHMO's mean path length was approximately 27% longer than CHOMP's. This conservatism is attributed to its worst-case interaction modeling and the choice not to wrap continuous joints in the cost function, prioritizing safety over path optimality.
 - **Real-time Operation:** NeHMO operated in real-time, achieving approximately 25Hz planning frequency.
 - **Ablation (NeHMO w/o Sym):** Removing symmetry reduction from NeHMO resulted in similar planning performance (90% success, 3% collision), but this variant required double the training data, further supporting the data efficiency benefit of symmetry exploitation.

2. **Multi-UR5 Scalability Study (18-DoF, 30-DoF):**
 - **Three UR5s (18-DoF):** NeHMO maintained a high success rate (86%) and low collision rate (5%), with a mean planning time of 41 ms. In contrast, CHOMP's success rate dropped to 68%, collision rate increased to 32%, and planning time escalated to 650 ms.
 - **Five UR5s (30-DoF):** NeHMO continued to demonstrate scalability, achieving a 66% success rate and an 8% collision rate, with a mean planning time of 43 ms. CHOMP's performance degraded significantly, with a 32% success rate, 68% collision rate, and a mean planning time of 1800 ms.
 - These results highlight NeHMO's ability to maintain real-time performance and reasonable safety guarantees as the number of manipulators and system dimensionality increase, a critical advantage over centralized methods.

**C. UR5-Kinova Challenging Motion Planning Experiment (Heterogeneous/Near-Adversarial)**
This experiment evaluated NeHMO in a heterogeneous dual-arm setup (UR5 and Kinova Gen3) under challenging coordination scenarios.
- **Naive / Naive:** When both manipulators followed naive, collision-ignoring policies, the success rate was 0% and collision rate was 100%, indicating the inherent difficulty of the tasks.
- **NeHMO / NeHMO:** When both arms utilized NeHMO for decentralized planning, the system achieved a 79% success rate and a 6% collision rate, demonstrating effective safe coordination between heterogeneous manipulators.
- **NeHMO / Naive:** In this near-adversarial scenario, the UR5 used NeHMO while the Kinova arm executed a reckless policy ignoring collisions. The UR5, using NeHMO, was able to avoid a substantial number of collisions, resulting in a 47% success rate and a 39% collision rate. This demonstrated NeHMO's defensive capabilities, significantly reducing collisions compared to the naive baseline even when one agent behaves unpredictably.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NeHMO framework introduces a decentralized multi-arm motion planning approach that addresses several persistent challenges in robotics, yielding significant implications and potential impact.

**Enhanced Scalability for High-Dimensional Systems:** The research demonstrates that NeHMO can effectively plan for multi-arm systems with up to 30 degrees of freedom (five manipulators) in real-time. This scalability is a substantial advancement over traditional centralized planning methods, whose computational complexity rapidly escalates with increased system dimensionality. The ability to manage complex, multi-robot systems efficiently unlocks potential for applications requiring large fleets of manipulators or intricate coordinated tasks.

**Robustness to Unpredictable Agent Behaviors:** By leveraging Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) to model worst-case inter-arm safety constraints, NeHMO equips individual manipulators with the capacity to plan defensively. This reduces reliance on accurate behavior prediction or strict coordination protocols from other agents. The demonstrated performance in near-adversarial scenarios, where one arm executes a reckless policy, highlights the framework's ability to enhance safety in unstructured or human-robot co-existence environments where other agents' actions may be unknown or unreliable.

**Improved Data Efficiency in Neural HJR Learning:** The integration of physics-informed priors, specifically structural symmetry exploitation and boundary condition residual learning, into the neural HJR training pipeline improves data efficiency and solution quality. This allows the neural network to learn complex safety representations for articulated multi-arm systems with fewer training samples, thereby reducing the computational resources and time required for model development. This contribution has broader implications for making neural network-based control solutions more practical and accessible.

**Decentralized Real-time Operation:** NeHMO's decentralized nature enables each arm to plan independently while considering shared safety. This not only improves scalability but also allows for continuous replanning and adaptation to dynamic environmental changes or evolving states of other robots. The real-time planning capability (approximately 25Hz for a dual-arm system) is crucial for dynamic and interactive robotic applications.

**Potential Applications:** The capabilities offered by NeHMO have implications for various robotic domains, including:
- **Coordinated Assembly:** Facilitating intricate assembly tasks where multiple manipulators operate in close proximity.
- **Shared-Workspace Manipulation:** Enabling robots to safely operate in common workspaces, for example, in manufacturing or logistics.
- **Warehouse Automation:** Enhancing the efficiency and safety of robotic fleets in automated warehouses.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Providing a foundation for safer human-robot interaction by allowing robots to anticipate and react to unpredictable human movements in shared environments.

**Foundational Contribution to Safe AI in Robotics:** While acknowledging current limitations such as the loss of formal safety guarantees due to neural network approximation and conservatism in planning, the paper lays groundwork for future research. Avenues for future work, such as post-training certification, general-sum game formulations, and extension to mobile manipulators, indicate a clear path for refining safety assurances and broadening the applicability of this approach. The work contributes to the development of more intelligent and robust autonomous robotic systems that can operate safely and efficiently in complex, dynamic, and uncertain environments.
