# ChronoFlow-Policy: Unifying Past-Current-Future Interaction Flow in Visuomotor Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ChronoFlow-Policy introduces a visuomotor policy framework that unifies past, current, and future interaction dynamics through a compact 3D keypoint representation called ChronoFlow. The policy achieves an average success rate of 72% on MetaWorld and 66% on RoboTwin 2.0 benchmarks, and demonstrates robust real-world performance in complex manipulation tasks, including deformable object handling, by effectively reasoning over temporal interaction flow.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies typically model either historical context or future dynamics independently, limiting a unified temporal understanding of interactions.
- Many manipulation tasks are non-Markovian and involve complex contact dynamics, which single-timestep observations often fail to fully capture.
- Current methods frequently struggle with deformable objects or can be hindered by redundant scene flow, leading to reduced robustness and performance in complex scenarios.

## Method

- Introduce **ChronoFlow**, a compact ==3D keypoint representation== that unifies past, current, and future interaction dynamics of the robot gripper and task-relevant objects.
- Develop **ChronoFlow-Policy** as a ==diffusion-based visuomotor model== that jointly learns ChronoFlow prediction and action sequences using a ==co-training objective==.
- The policy architecture processes point cloud observations and historical ChronoFlow to predict future ChronoFlow trajectories and corresponding robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **72%** on MetaWorld and **66%** on RoboTwin 2.0 simulation benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like DP3, 3D-FDP, and MBA.
- Demonstrated high real-world success on non-Markovian tasks (**93%** on 'Swap-Easy' Stage III, validating historical ChronoFlow) and on deformable object manipulation (**87%** on both 'Fold Towel' stages), attributed to interaction-centric keypoint modeling.
- Ablation studies highlighted the essential contributions of ChronoFlow supervision, object keypoint flows, keypoint sampling, and spatial data augmentation to the policy's real-world efficacy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling coupled object-gripper motion using sparse 3D keypoints (ChronoFlow) captures essential interaction structure more effectively than dense scene flow or object pose-only representations.
> - A co-training objective, linking ChronoFlow prediction with action generation, encourages the diffusion backbone to learn unified spatiotemporal interaction models, improving learning efficiency.
> - Integrating past, current, and future interaction flows enables the policy to resolve non-Markovian dependencies and effectively handle long-horizon and deformable object manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visuomotor policy learning, which leverages visual signals to capture object motion and interaction dynamics for action generation, constitutes a significant area of research in robotics. Existing approaches in this domain typically follow two main routes:
1. **Future dynamics prediction**: These methods aim to provide foresight for action generation by predicting how a scene might evolve. Examples include predicting future object pose trajectories (e.g.,) or scene-level point cloud dynamics (e.g.,). Some approaches use image-space prediction, such as video diffusion models or latent video representations, while others employ more structured 3D representations like object flow or dense scene flow.
2. **Historical context incorporation**: This route focuses on addressing the non-Markovian nature of many manipulation tasks by incorporating past observations for long-horizon reasoning (e.g.,). Methods in this category range from video-based representations to keyframe-based or compressed historical inputs, sometimes utilizing object-centric representations by tracking points over time.

Despite their complementary roles, these two lines of research have largely been studied in isolation. Approaches predicting future dynamics may lack explicit gripper interaction modeling or struggle with deformable objects when relying on object poses. Dense scene flow can also introduce redundant information unrelated to task-relevant interactions. Similarly, most history-aware methods focus on contextual input without explicitly modeling object-gripper interactions.

ChronoFlow-Policy addresses these limitations by introducing a unified temporal representation, ChronoFlow, which spans past, current, and future interaction dynamics. This representation focuses on sparse 3D keypoints of both objects and the gripper, explicitly modeling their coupled motion. By integrating historical context with future predictions in an interaction-centric manner, this work positions itself as an advancement in developing policies that can reason over a comprehensive temporal flow of interaction, suitable for complex and temporally-dependent manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that human intuition for manipulation tasks involves a continuous reasoning process that integrates past experiences, current perceptions, and anticipated future outcomes. Humans recall prior hand and object movements, assess their current spatial and functional relationship, and predict how objects should deform or move next to achieve a goal. Current visuomotor policies, however, typically model either historical context or future dynamics in isolation, which limits their ability to capture a unified temporal understanding of interaction.

The core problem addressed is that observations from a single timestep provide only partial information about the interaction state, and many manipulation tasks exhibit strong temporal dependencies due to contact dynamics, staged object interactions, and non-Markovian properties. Policies trained solely on instantaneous observations often struggle to capture the underlying spatiotemporal structure of object–robot interactions, leading to reduced robustness and performance in complex scenarios.

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **To propose ChronoFlow**: This involves developing a compact, interaction-centric 3D keypoint representation that unifies past, current, and future dynamics for both manipulated objects and the robot gripper. The representation aims to filter out redundant scene motion while preserving essential interaction structure.
2. **To develop ChronoFlow-Policy**: This objective involves creating a diffusion-based visuomotor policy that leverages ChronoFlow. The policy is designed to jointly learn ChronoFlow prediction and action sequences through a co-training objective. This approach aims to encourage the shared latent representation within the diffusion framework to encode temporally grounded manipulation dynamics, rather than solely action labels.
3. **To evaluate the policy's effectiveness**: The research aims to demonstrate the practical value of ChronoFlow-Policy in challenging manipulation scenarios. This includes assessing its performance on long-horizon tasks requiring multi-step execution, non-Markovian tasks demanding stage-dependent decisions based on historical context, and tasks involving deformable object manipulation where precise interaction dynamics are critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ChronoFlow-Policy methodology centers on defining and utilizing ChronoFlow, a unified representation of past, current, and future interaction dynamics based on sparse 3D keypoint trajectories. This representation is then integrated into a diffusion-based visuomotor policy.

**Problem Setup:**
The robot policy receives an observation `o_t = (p_t, q_t)` at each timestep `t`, where `p_t` is a scene-level point cloud (3D coordinates and RGB values) and `q_t` is the robot's proprioceptive state (e.g., joint positions, end-effector pose). The policy's goal is to predict a sequence of future low-level actions `a_t:t+H` over a fixed horizon `H`.

**ChronoFlow: Past-Current-Future Interaction Flow:**
ChronoFlow models interaction state using sparse 3D keypoints for both the robot gripper and task-relevant objects.
- **Gripper Keypoints Flow (`P_g_t`)**: `N_g` canonical keypoints are predefined on the gripper geometry and rigidly attached to the Tool Center Point (TCP). Their 3D positions are computed via rigid transformation from the TCP pose at each timestep, ensuring temporal consistency in the world coordinate frame.
- **Object Keypoints Flow (`P_o_t`)**: Task-relevant objects are first segmented from the initial observation using a 3D segmentation model (e.g., 3D SAM-2). Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is then applied to each segmented object to obtain a dense set of candidate keypoints. These keypoints are tracked over time using a 3D point tracker (e.g., TAPIP3D) to generate temporally consistent object-centric trajectories. For training, a subset of `N_o` object keypoints is randomly sampled per sequence.
- The union of gripper and object keypoints, `P_t = P_g_t ∪ P_o_t`, forms a sparse abstraction of the physical interaction state. Historical ChronoFlow trajectories (`P_{t-hp:t-1}`) from past observations and future ChronoFlow trajectories (`P_{t:t+H}`) for prediction are constructed from these keypoint flows.

**ChronoFlow-Policy Architecture:**
The policy factorizes action generation as `π(a_t:t+H | o_t, P_:t) = π_φ(a_t:t+H | P_t:t+H) * π_θ(P_t:t+H | o_t, P_:t)`, where `P_:t` represents historical ChronoFlow. `π_θ` models interaction dynamics evolution, and `π_φ` maps predicted interaction trajectories to actions. `π_θ` is implemented as a conditional diffusion process.

The ChronoFlow-Policy consists of the following modules:
1. **Observation Encoder**: Maps the current 3D point cloud observation (`o_t`) to a condition feature `c_t`. A PointNet-style encoder is used in simulation, and a sparse 3D encoder in real-world experiments. Proprioceptive state (if available) is embedded by an MLP and fused with the point cloud feature.
2. **ChronoFlow Encoder**: This module processes both historical ChronoFlow (`P_:t`) and the noisy future ChronoFlow (`P_k_t:t+H`) to generate ChronoFlow tokens (`Z_k_t`). It uses a multi-head cross-attention module with learnable interaction queries, with each token corresponding to a gripper or object keypoint.
3. **Joint Diffusion Denoising**: The core of the policy is a diffusion module (implemented as a Unet or Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone). It takes `c_t`, the noise timestep `k`, and `Z_k_t` as input to iteratively refine `Z_k_t` into clean interaction tokens `Z_0_t`.
4. **ChronoFlow Decoder**: A lightweight Transformer decoder converts the denoised tokens `Z_0_t` into the predicted future ChronoFlow trajectory (`ˆP_t:t+H`).
5. **Action Decoder**: This module predicts robot actions (`ˆa_t:t+H`) by concatenating `ˆP_t:t+H` with `Z_0_t` and feeding them into a lightweight transformer decoder.

**Training Objectives:**
The training objective combines ChronoFlow prediction and action learning:
- **ChronoFlow Loss (`L_CF`)**: A standard diffusion objective trains the denoising network `ϵ_θ` to recover ChronoFlow trajectories (Eq. 5), enforcing consistency in object-gripper interaction.
- **Action Loss (`L_action`)**: An MSE loss supervises the action decoder. During training, the action decoder is conditioned on partially denoised ChronoFlow tokens and an approximate ChronoFlow trajectory reconstructed from an intermediate noisy diffusion state (Eq. 7).
- **Total Objective**: `L = L_action + λ_CF * L_CF`. The ChronoFlow loss guides the diffusion backbone to learn a unified spatiotemporal interaction model, while the action loss aligns this representation with task-level control.

**Inference:**
During inference, historical ChronoFlow trajectories are obtained asynchronously using a 3D tracker. The policy starts with Gaussian noise for future ChronoFlow and iteratively denoises it through the ChronoFlow encoder and diffusion backbone to obtain clean interaction tokens. These tokens are then used by the ChronoFlow and action decoders to generate the predicted future trajectory and actions. The first 4 predicted action steps are executed in a receding-horizon manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ChronoFlow-Policy was conducted on both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, comparing its performance against various visuomotor policy baselines, including action-only, history-aware, and future-prediction methods.

**Simulation Experiments:**
- **MetaWorld**: On 7 MetaWorld tasks, ChronoFlow-Policy (Unet) achieved an average success rate of 72%, which is a substantial improvement over DP3 (30%), 3D-FDP (34%), and MBA (47%). ChronoFlow-Policy (DiT) also performed comparably at 70%. The gains were particularly noticeable in interaction-intensive tasks such as "Hand-Insert" (CFP (Unet) 67% vs. DP3 14%) and "Sweep Into" (CFP (Unet) 60% vs. DP3 15%). The results suggest that ChronoFlow's interaction-centric modeling, which explicitly represents gripper and object keypoints, provides a more expressive and localized understanding of interaction dynamics compared to dense scene flow or object pose-only representations.
- **RoboTwin 2.0**: In dual-arm manipulation on 7 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, ChronoFlow-Policy (Unet) attained an average success rate of 66%, surpassing DP3 (43%), 3D-FDP (47%), and MBA (56%). ChronoFlow-Policy (DiT) was at 63%. The policy demonstrated effectiveness in tasks requiring precise spatial localization (e.g., "Beat Block Hammer," "Click Alarmclock") and long-horizon dual-arm tasks (e.g., "Handover Microphone"), indicating its ability to capture object transition processes between grippers through explicit gripper-object interaction flow modeling.
- **Training Curves**: Learning curves indicated that ChronoFlow-Policy (Unet) generally converged faster and achieved higher or comparable final success rates than DP3 on representative simulation tasks. This suggests that ChronoFlow supervision provides a more robust training signal, facilitating efficient learning of object-gripper interaction dynamics.

**Real-World Experiments:**
Five real-world manipulation tasks were used to assess the policy's ability to handle non-Markovian dependencies, long-horizon execution, and deformable objects. Tasks were decomposed into stages for detailed analysis.

- **Q1: Does history ChronoFlow improve non-Markovian performance?**
 - On "Swap-Easy" and "Swap-Hard," tasks requiring decisions based on past states, methods without explicit history modeling (RISE, 3D-FDP, CFP w/o past) showed performance degradation in later stages. For example, in "Swap-Easy," Stage III success rates were RISE 13%, 3D-FDP 33%, and CFP w/o past 20%. In contrast, the full ChronoFlow-Policy maintained strong performance across stages (e.g., "Swap-Easy": 93%, 93%, 93%; "Swap-Hard": 94%, 94%, 61%), demonstrating that historical ChronoFlow traces effectively resolve non-Markovian dependencies.

- **Q2: Does ChronoFlow policy gain benefits from future modeling?**
 - For the long-horizon task "Prepare Breakfast," ChronoFlow-Policy achieved 93% success in Stage I and 80% in Stage II. This outperformed RISE (73%, 53%), 3D-FDP (73%, 67%), and MBA (87%, 73%). These results suggest that integrating past, current, and future interaction modeling enhances temporal reasoning and multi-step planning.

- **Q3: Can ChronoFlow Policy handle deformable object manipulation?**
 - The "Fold Towel" task, which involves highly variable geometry after the first fold, served to evaluate deformable object manipulation. RISE's success rate dropped from 80% in Stage I to 40% in Stage II. ChronoFlow-Policy achieved 87% success in both stages, outperforming prior baselines in Stage II. This performance is attributed to ChronoFlow's ability to model interaction-centric keypoint flows, which preserve towel geometry under deformation more effectively than pose-based representations.

- **Q4: Which design components of ChronoFlow are critical for real-world robustness (ablation)?**
 - Ablation studies on "Fold Towel" confirmed the importance of several ChronoFlow components:
 - Removing ChronoFlow supervision (`w/o CF`) caused Stage II performance to drop from 87% to 67%.
 - Removing object flows (`w/o ObjPoints`) led to a larger drop (87% to 47%) than removing gripper flows (`w/o GriPoints`, 87% to 80%), indicating object flows are primary for deformable tracking.
 - Disabling keypoint sampling (`w/o Sampling`) degraded performance in both stages (Stage I: 87% to 40%; Stage II: 87% to 27%), highlighting its role in spatial coverage and generalization.
 - Disabling ChronoFlow data augmentation (spatial jittering, `w/o CF-Aug`) caused Stage II success to drop significantly from 87% to 6%, indicating its criticality for robust real-world performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ChronoFlow-Policy research introduces a unified framework for visuomotor policy learning that integrates past, current, and future interaction dynamics through a compact 3D keypoint representation. This approach offers several contributions to the field of robotic manipulation.

First, the development of **ChronoFlow** as an interaction-centric 3D keypoint representation addresses a gap in existing methods that often treat historical context and future prediction separately or use representations that are either too dense (e.g., full scene flow) or too abstract (e.g., object poses for deformable objects). By focusing on task-relevant object-gripper keypoints, ChronoFlow provides a more compact and expressive representation of manipulation dynamics.

Second, the proposed **ChronoFlow-Policy**, a diffusion-based visuomotor policy, demonstrates consistent performance gains over established baselines across a range of complex simulated and real-world tasks. The co-training objective, which couples ChronoFlow prediction with action generation, encourages the model to learn temporally grounded manipulation dynamics, contributing to more robust and efficient policy learning.

Third, the evaluation highlights the practical value of unified past-current-future interaction modeling in challenging scenarios:
- **Non-Markovian tasks**: The policy's ability to leverage historical ChronoFlow traces enables it to make stage-dependent decisions, reducing failures in tasks requiring memory of past interactions.
- **Deformable object manipulation**: The keypoint flow representation proves effective in handling the complex and variable geometries of deformable objects, outperforming methods reliant on object poses.
- **Long-horizon tasks**: The integration of temporal reasoning enhances multi-step planning and task progression over extended sequences of actions.

The ablation studies further underscore the importance of specific design components, such as object flows, stochastic keypoint sampling, and spatial data augmentation, for maintaining robustness in real-world deployments. The asynchronous operation of the 3D tracker contributes to the system's practical inference speed.

The potential impact of this work includes advancing the capabilities of robots in complex manipulation environments, particularly those involving sequential decision-making, object deformation, or tight interaction. The unified representation and co-training approach could inspire further research into more comprehensive world models for robot control. While the work acknowledges limitations related to noise and potential failures in 3D tracking, it provides a foundation for developing more robust and uncertainty-aware interaction tracking mechanisms in future research.
