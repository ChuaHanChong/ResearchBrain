# Thinking-while-Generating: Interleaving Textual Reasoning throughout Visual Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Thinking-while-Generating (TWIG)" framework introduces a method to interleave textual reasoning directly throughout the visual generation process, enabling dynamic guidance and reflection. This approach from CUHK and Meituan significantly enhances compositional fidelity and prompt adherence in image synthesis, demonstrating competitive performance on T2I-CompBench++.

## Problem

- Current visual generative models struggle with complex compositional tasks, multi-entity relationships, and precise adherence to nuanced textual instructions.
- Existing reasoning integration methods, such as 'think-before-generation' and 'think-after-generation,' lack on-the-fly multimodal interaction, leading to inflexible or inefficient guidance.
- Generative processes often lack timely, fine-grained adjustments during image synthesis, relying on fixed plans or costly post-hoc revisions.

## Method

- The Thinking-while-Generating (**TWIG**) framework ==interleaves textual reasoning== (thoughts and reflections) with visual synthesis throughout the generative process.
- It defines three core components: 'When to Think' (scheduling reasoning points), 'What to Say' (generating localized sub-prompts for regions), and 'How to Refine' (performing immediate, region-level critique and re-generation).
- The approach is implemented and evaluated using three strategies: zero-shot prompting (TWIG-ZS), supervised fine-tuning (TWIG-SFT) with a curated dataset (TWIG-50K), and reinforcement learning (TWIG-RL) using a multi-component reward model.

## Results

- TWIG-ZS, using zero-shot prompting, achieved substantial improvements on T2I-CompBench, with gains of +**15.41** in Complex categories and +**12.57** in Non-Spatial compared to a baseline.
- TWIG-RL, building upon TWIG-SFT, delivered further gains, showing improvements of +**7.91** in Attribute Binding and +**8.86** in Object Relationship categories over TWIG-SFT.
- On T2I-CompBench++, TWIG-RL achieved competitive scores, matching or outperforming several state-of-the-art generative models like T2I-R1, FLUX.1, and Emu3 in compositional generation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving textual reasoning directly within visual generation provides superior guidance and reflection compared to sequential 'think-before' or 'think-after' paradigms.
> - The framework enables co-evolving reasoning where textual thoughts dynamically guide upcoming visual regions and reflect on previously synthesized ones, enhancing context-awareness.
> - Progressive strategies (zero-shot, SFT, RL) each contribute to unlocking greater control, stability, and performance, with reinforcement learning proving most effective.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual generation has seen rapid advancements, primarily driven by diffusion models and autoregressive models, which now produce high-fidelity synthetic images across various domains. Despite these impressive visual qualities, current generative models frequently struggle with complex challenges such as long-horizon composition (e.g., generating intricate scenes), multi-entity relationships (e.g., accurately placing and interacting multiple objects), and precisely adhering to nuanced textual instructions.

To address these limitations, a growing body of research has explored integrating "reasoning" capabilities, often in the form of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in the language modality. Existing CoT-based approaches can be broadly categorized by *when* the textual reasoning is applied:

- **Think-before-Generation (Pre-planning):** Methods in this category generate a structured plan (e.g., detailed captions, scene layouts, object attributes) *before* the visual generation process begins. While this improves global coherence and entity placement, the plan remains fixed, limiting dynamic guidance or mid-course corrections. Examples include GoT and ImageGen-CoT.
- **Think-after-Generation (Post-refinement):** These approaches synthesize the entire image first and then use textual feedback (e.g., self-critique or external verifiers) to iteratively revise visual errors. This helps with local fixes, but reasoning is loosely coupled to the synthesis, lacks timely adjustments, and often incurs additional, costly inference rounds. Examples include Generation with CoT and Reflect-DiT.

This paper critically observes that both existing paradigms lack "on-the-fly multimodal interaction during the generation itself." Drawing inspiration from the complementary trend in visual *understanding*, where large multimodal models (LMMs) perform image-text interleaved reasoning (e.g., embedding detected objects or zoomed regions into textual CoTs), the authors pose a fundamental question: Can this flow be inverted, allowing text to be interleaved *into* the intermediate visual generation process to provide dynamic, co-evolving guidance?

"Thinking-while-Generating (TWIG)" directly addresses this gap. It introduces the first framework that truly interleaves textual reasoning *throughout* the visual generation process, rather than just before or after. By doing so, it aims to overcome the inflexibility of pre-planning and the inefficiency of post-refinement, setting a new direction for more controllable and context-aware visual synthesis. The work is positioned as a preliminary study, recognizing the novelty and foundational nature of this interleaved paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate "Thinking-while-Generating (TWIG)," a novel interleaved framework that enables co-evolving textual reasoning throughout the visual generation process. The core motivation stems from the limitations of current visual generators and existing reasoning integration strategies:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Generators:** Despite advances in visual quality, existing generative models struggle with complex compositional tasks, accurate multi-entity relationships, and precise adherence to nuanced textual instructions. These models often lack a deep "understanding" of the prompt and the ability to dynamically course-correct during generation.
- **Overcoming Constraints of "Think-before" and "Think-after" Paradigms:**
 - "Think-before" methods are inflexible, as the initial plan cannot adapt once generation begins, making nuanced guidance and mid-course corrections impossible.
 - "Think-after" methods are inefficient and loosely coupled, requiring a full image generation before critique and often leading to costly iterative revisions. The motivation is to provide timely, fine-grained adjustments within a single generative trajectory.
- **Embracing On-the-Fly Multimodal Interaction:** Inspired by the success of interleaved reasoning in LMMs for visual *understanding*, the authors are motivated to reverse this flow. They believe that weaving textual thoughts into the *unfolding canvas* can provide dynamic guidance and reflection, leading to more context-aware and semantically rich visual outputs.
- **Enabling Co-evolving Reasoning:** The goal is for textual reasoning to evolve concurrently with the visual generation. As visual content progressively generates, textual reasoning should guide upcoming local regions and reflect on previously synthesized ones, allowing for precise steering and dynamic revision within a single, continuous generative process.
- **Unveiling the Potential of Interleaved Reasoning:** This preliminary study aims to explore the feasibility and effectiveness of this paradigm through different implementation strategies (zero-shot, SFT, RL), hoping to inspire further research in this promising direction.

In essence, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between "thinking" (textual reasoning) and "generating" (visual synthesis) by making them deeply intertwined and dynamically interactive, moving beyond sequential or post-hoc integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Thinking-while-Generating (TWIG) framework is designed to interleave textual reasoning and visual generation, ensuring on-the-fly guidance and reflection. For clarity and extensibility, the preliminary study adopts a Unified Understanding-Generation LMM (ULM), such as Janus-Pro, with an autoregressive generation paradigm and focuses on text-to-image (T2I) scenarios. The framework is conceptually compatible with other architectures (pipeline of T2I + LMM), generation paradigms (diffusion, discrete diffusion), and tasks (image-to-image, text-to-video, text-to-3D).

The TWIG framework comprises three core components: "When to Think," "What to Say," and "How to Refine."

- **When to Think (Scheduling):**
 - Given an input text prompt (T), the understanding component of the ULM (ULM_u) first determines an interleaved reasoning schedule, S = {V_k}_(K=1)^K. Each V_k represents a target visual region where reasoning will be applied.
 - This effectively decouples the generation process into smaller, more manageable sub-tasks.
 - For this preliminary study, a static schedule with K=3 is found to be optimal. This aligns with the heuristic that many images can be decomposed into three semantic components: upper background, central content, and lower background. Adaptive scheduling is noted as future work due to current ULM limitations.

- **What to Say (Reasoning Content):**
 - At each scheduled reasoning point (V_k), ULM_u generates a textual thought (τ_k) that serves as a localized sub-prompt specifically for V_k. This thought provides fine-grained guidance, conditioned on the input prompt (T), all previous thoughts ({τ_j}_(j<k)), and all previously generated visual content ({V_j}_(j<k)).
 - Subsequently, the generation component of the ULM (ULM_g) synthesizes the target region V_k. Crucially, ULM_g does not require image-to-image capabilities. Instead, the visual context ({V_j}_(j<k)) is preserved at the end of the token sequence, and the textual pre-context is extended from ({τ_j}_(j<k)) to ({τ_j}_(j≤k)) at the beginning. This maintains a single autoregressive generation trajectory without discontinuities.

- **How to Refine (Reflection):**
 - After generating each visual region V_k, ULM_u performs an immediate, region-level revision. This involves generating a reflection tuple c_k = (r_k, ˆτ_k).
 - r_k is a critic score (0-100) evaluating the semantic alignment and visual coherence of V_k with respect to its guiding prompt τ_k.
 - ˆτ_k is a revised sub-caption for potential correction.
 - If r_k exceeds a predefined threshold (θ), the model proceeds to the next thought. Otherwise, a local refinement is triggered: V_k is re-generated as ˆV_k, guided by the revised sub-caption ˆτ_k.
 - Similar to "What to Say," this localized correction is performed by updating the textual pre-context (τ_k to ˆτ_k) and re-generating only the local part ˆV_k at the end of the token sequence, preserving a single generative trajectory without full re-generation.

**Implementation Exploration:** The paper investigates three strategies for implementing TWIG:

1. **Zero-shot Prompting (TWIG-ZS):**
 - Carefully designed, interleave-aware prompts are crafted for the ULM to elicit global plans, reasoning thoughts, and critique scores directly, without parameter updates.
 - Prompts guide "when to think" (sketching high-level semantics), "what to say" (focusing on local regions without spatial anchors), and "how to refine" (providing critique scores on five criteria and local revisions).

2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) (TWIG-SFT):**
 - To enhance zero-shot capabilities, SFT is performed by decomposing the TWIG process into nine supervised tasks, corresponding to three thinking, three reflection, and three generation targets for a fixed three-step reasoning schedule.
 - A high-quality dataset, **TWIG-50K**, is curated using advanced commercial models like GPT-4o (for sub-captioning, critique, and revision) and GPT-4o-Image (for synthesizing images aligned with segmented descriptions). The dataset focuses on learning structured reasoning, localized reflection, and region-wise generation.

3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) (TWIG-RL):**
 - To push performance further, RL is applied using a customized **TWIG-GRPO** (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm.
 - The strategy is to reinforce all components (thinking, generation, reflection) simultaneously within a single rollout using a shared reward based on the final generated image and input prompt. This allows global information to flow and enhances synergy.
 - A sophisticated **Reward Model Design** combines four complementary reward models to mitigate reward hacking: (i) Human Preference Scores (HPS v2), (ii) Object Grounding Scores (GroundingDINO), (iii) VQA Consistency Scores (GIT), and (iv) LMM Alignment Scores (fine-tuned ORM). An unweighted average of these four models is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the Thinking-while-Generating (TWIG) paradigm across three progressive implementation strategies:

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Prompting (TWIG-ZS)

- **Strong Baseline Improvement:** TWIG-ZS, solely through meticulously designed prompts, achieves surprisingly strong improvements over the baseline Janus-Pro-7B on T2I-CompBench. It shows substantial gains in Attribute Binding (+9.52), Object Relationship (+6.27), and significantly in Complex categories (+15.41), Non-Spatial (+12.57).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Interleaving vs. Other Paradigms:** Interleaving (TWIG-ZS) consistently outperforms "Think-before-Generation" and "Think-after-Generation" in the zero-shot setting, validating the core hypothesis that on-the-fly guidance is superior.
 - **Optimal Reasoning Steps:** K=3 steps with uniform spacing proves optimal, aligning with the "upper-central-lower" semantic decomposition of images. K=2 and K=4 show slightly lower performance.
 - **Adaptive Scheduling Challenges:** Current ULMs struggle to reliably follow instructions for adaptive spacing, leading to unstable schedules.
 - **Reflection Efficacy:** A single round of reflection significantly improves performance across most metrics (e.g., Attribute Binding +0.79, Object Relationship +4.47). However, two rounds yield no further gains, suggesting limits to zero-shot ULM critique-and-revision capacity.

#### 4.2. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) (TWIG-SFT)

- **Consistent Gains and Stability:** SFT with the curated TWIG-50K dataset provides modest but consistent gains over TWIG-ZS, particularly in Object Relationship (+10.87) and Spatial (+5.04) categories.
- **Improved Stability:** TWIG-SFT notably reduces the standard deviation across multiple random seeds compared to TWIG-ZS, indicating more predictable and stable behavior.
- **Data Composition Impact:**
 - A balanced mixture of thinking (T) and generation (G) tasks in TWIG-50K yields the best results, strengthening both aspects.
 - Surprisingly, adding reflection (R) data degrades performance, suggesting that TWIG-ZS might already leverage most of the model's inherent reflection proficiency, and oversupplying R data diverts capacity.

#### 4.3. Reinforcement Learning (RL) (TWIG-RL)

- **Substantial Performance Leap:** TWIG-RL, built upon TWIG-SFT, delivers substantial further gains, with significant improvements across Attribute Binding (+7.91) and Object Relationship (+8.86) categories compared to TWIG-SFT.
- **TWIG-GRPO Strategy Validation:** The joint TWIG-GRPO strategy, which simultaneously reinforces all thinking, generation, and reflection passes with a shared reward, significantly outperforms separate optimization of understanding-related (ULM_u-GRPO) or generation-related (ULM_g-GRPO) tasks. This highlights the complementary nature and mutual reinforcement of the components.
- **Reward Model Ensemble Effectiveness:** Progressively incorporating a diverse ensemble of reward models (Human Preference, Object Grounding, VQA Consistency, LMM Alignment) steadily improves performance, with the full ensemble achieving the best overall balance and addressing different aspects of image quality and prompt alignment.

#### 4.4. Overall Comparison and Qualitative Results

- **Competitive Performance:** On T2I-CompBench++, TWIG-RL achieves highly competitive scores across various compositional generation benchmarks, often outperforming or matching several current state-of-the-art generative models like T2I-R1, FLUX.1, and Emu3, demonstrating its effectiveness.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Visualizations (Figures 5, 6, 7) illustrate the progressive qualitative improvements from TWIG-ZS to TWIG-SFT to TWIG-RL, showcasing enhanced compositional fidelity, object counting, visual realism, and the effective capacity for reflection to refine semantic and visual consistency (e.g., spatial alignment, shadow coherence). The detailed interleaved process clearly shows how textual thoughts guide the unfolding image generation.

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate the feasibility and significant advantages of interleaving textual reasoning during visual generation, with each progressive strategy (ZS, SFT, RL) building upon the last to unlock greater control, stability, and performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Thinking-while-Generating (TWIG)" framework represents a significant advancement in the field of visual generation, offering a novel paradigm that promises to enhance the capabilities and controllability of generative AI. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Interleaved Reasoning in Generation:** TWIG is the first framework to truly integrate textual reasoning *during* the visual generation process, moving beyond the limitations of "think-before" pre-planning and "think-after" post-refinement. This fundamental shift allows for dynamic, co-evolving multimodal interaction, making it a groundbreaking conceptual contribution.
- **Enhanced Generative Control and Fidelity:** By interleaving reasoning, TWIG enables fine-grained guidance and real-time corrections. This leads to substantial improvements in complex compositional tasks, accurate attribute binding, correct object relationships, and overall semantic alignment with nuanced prompts. This directly addresses major pain points in current generative models.
- **Increased Context-Awareness and Semantic Richness:** The ability for textual thoughts to reflect on previously generated content and guide upcoming regions ensures that the visual output is more context-aware and semantically rich, maintaining coherence throughout the generation trajectory.
- **Demonstrated Feasibility Across Paradigms:** The success of TWIG through zero-shot prompting, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning highlights its robustness and adaptability. This multi-pronged validation strategy confirms that the interleaved approach is not only conceptually sound but also practically implementable and effective across different training methodologies.
- **Flexible and Generalizable Framework:** The proposed conceptual compatibility with various system architectures (pipeline or ULM), generation paradigms (diffusion, autoregressive), and task scenarios (T2I, image-to-image, video, 3D) suggests that TWIG can serve as a foundational approach for a wide range of future generative AI applications.
- **New Avenues for Human-AI Interaction:** This dynamic, interactive generation process opens up possibilities for more intuitive and powerful human-AI co-creation. Users could potentially guide image generation step-by-step with natural language, leading to unprecedented levels of creative control and precision.
- **Future Research Inspiration:** The paper explicitly positions itself as a preliminary study, aiming to inspire further research. It clearly outlines limitations as promising future directions, such as learning fully adaptive reasoning schedules, exploring more advanced RL algorithms (e.g., recent GRPO variants), and extending TWIG to video, 3D, and image-to-image tasks. This forward-looking perspective sets a research agenda for the community.
- **Bridging Understanding and Generation:** By reversing the flow of interleaved reasoning from LMMs (which typically use images for better understanding), TWIG further blurs the lines between visual understanding and generation, pushing towards more truly unified multimodal AI systems.

In conclusion, Thinking-while-Generating represents a significant step towards more intelligent, controllable, and context-aware visual synthesis. By deeply integrating dynamic textual reasoning into the generative process, it paves the way for a new generation of creative AI tools with enhanced capabilities and a more nuanced understanding of complex human instructions.
