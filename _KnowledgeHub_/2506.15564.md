# Show-o2: Improved Native Unified Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Show Lab and ByteDance researchers developed Show-o2, a native unified multimodal model capable of both understanding and generating text, images, and videos within a single architecture. The model achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across various multimodal benchmarks, supported by a novel dual-path visual representation and an efficient two-stage training strategy.

## Problem

- Existing large multimodal models (LMMs) primarily focus on text and images, often lacking comprehensive and native handling of dynamic video content.
- A challenge in achieving true unification of multimodal understanding and generation within a single, cohesive architecture, rather than assembling disparate specialized models.
- The difficulty of efficiently training powerful generative LMMs to cultivate visual generation abilities while preserving extensive pre-trained language model (LLM) knowledge without relying on prohibitively expensive, web-scale text corpora.

## Method

- Introduces a native unified multimodal architecture that leverages a ==3D causal VAE== for efficient visual encoding/decoding of both images and videos.
- Employs a ==dual-path Unified Visual Representation (UVR)== with ==Spatial (-Temporal) Fusion== to integrate both high-level semantic and low-level structural features for visual inputs.
- Utilizes a ==two-stage training recipe==: an initial visual generation pre-training phase, followed by full model fine-tuning, designed to efficiently impart visual generation skills while preserving the LLM's language knowledge.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance on image understanding benchmarks (e.g., MME-p, GQA, MMMU-val) and image generation benchmarks (GenEval, DPG-Bench, OneIG-Bench).
- Outperforms models with more parameters (e.g., TokenFlow-XL **14B**) on several image understanding tasks and demonstrates strong text-to-video and image-to-video generation on VBench with a **2B** parameter model.
- The two-stage training successfully preserves LLM language knowledge, maintaining performance comparable to original Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B Instruct models on text-only tasks like MMLU and GSM8K.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The dual-path Spatial (-Temporal) Fusion mechanism is crucial for effectively integrating both high-level semantic and low-level structural features, significantly improving both multimodal understanding and generation.
> - A two-stage training strategy effectively cultivates strong visual generation capabilities and adapts visual components to the LLM's embedding space, critically maintaining the pre-trained LLM's language proficiency without requiring massive text-only datasets.
> - Unified visual representations, designed to be scalable for both images and videos, enable robust multimodal understanding and generation within a single, coherent framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "Show-o2" is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence, specifically within the domain of **Large Multimodal Models (LMMs)** and **Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs)**.

The foundation of this landscape lies in the remarkable success of **Large Language Models (LLMs)**, which have demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in understanding and generating text. Building upon this, LMMs extended these capabilities to the visual domain, enabling tasks like visual question answering. Simultaneously, dedicated **visual generative models**—employing paradigms like diffusion and autoregressive modeling—have excelled in creating realistic images and videos from text prompts.

The natural progression of this research is towards **Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs)**, which aim to integrate both understanding and generation capabilities across different modalities within a single model or system. The paper categorizes existing UMMs into two primary approaches:
1. **"Assembling tailored models"**: This involves tuning adapters or learnable tokens to connect separate, specialized LMMs and visual generative models (e.g., NExT-GPT, SEED-X, MetaMorph). While effective, this approach can lead to a less cohesive system.
2. **"Native unified multimodal models"**: These models inherently combine multimodal understanding and generation objectives within a single architecture, directly decoding output sequences into texts, images, and videos (e.g., Chameleon, Transfusion, Show-o).

Show-o2 firmly positions itself within the "native unified multimodal models" paradigm. The critical niche it addresses is the **scalability and seamless unification of text, image, *and video* modalities**. Many existing native UMMs primarily focus on text and image, with video often being an afterthought or handled less comprehensively. Show-o2 aims to overcome this limitation by designing a model that inherently supports dynamic video content from its core visual representation.

The work leverages cutting-edge techniques:
- **Autoregressive modeling**: For sequential text generation and overall sequence flow.
- **Flow matching**: A generative modeling technique that provides a deterministic path between a noise distribution and a data distribution, often offering faster and more stable generation compared to traditional diffusion models.
- **3D causal Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**: For efficient and scalable representation of both images and videos.

By improving upon its predecessor Show-o and integrating these advanced techniques to natively handle video, Show-o2 pushes the boundaries of unified multimodal AI, demonstrating a significant step towards truly general-purpose multimodal agents that can perceive and create across diverse media types.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **improved native unified multimodal models, named Show-o2**, that are highly versatile and scalable across text, image, and video modalities for both understanding and generation tasks.

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific objectives:

- **Bridging the Modality Gap:** While LLMs excel in text and LMMs combine text and images, there's a significant desire for models that can natively and comprehensively handle video as well. Existing UMMs often struggle with video or integrate it in a less native fashion. The motivation is to create a truly unified model that treats text, images, and videos as first-class citizens.
- **Achieving True Unification:** The paper explicitly aims for a "native unified multimodal model," distinguishing it from approaches that assemble disparate specialized models. This motivation stems from the belief that a single, cohesive architecture can lead to more synergistic learning and better performance across modalities, enabling richer multimodal understanding and generation.
- **Scalable and Robust Visual Representations:** A key objective is to design a unified visual representation that can effectively capture features necessary for both multimodal understanding (rich semantics, contextual information) and generation (low-level details, intrinsic structures). This representation must also be scalable to both images and videos without loss of fidelity or information.
- **Efficient and Effective Training:** Training large multimodal models, especially those with generative capabilities, is computationally intensive and often requires massive, high-quality text corpora. A significant motivation is to develop a training recipe that can effectively cultivate visual generation abilities and scale to larger models while **retaining crucial language knowledge without relying on prohibitively expensive web-scale text resources**. This addresses a practical challenge in developing such powerful models.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Ultimately, the researchers aim for Show-o2 to achieve or surpass state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of multimodal benchmarks, thereby validating its architectural and training innovations. This includes image and video understanding, image and video generation, and mixed-modality generation.
- **Enabling Versatile Applications:** By unifying diverse capabilities, the motivation is to enable a single model to tackle a broad spectrum of real-world tasks, such as general-purpose visual question answering, text-to-image/video generation, image-to-video generation, and visual storytelling, thereby simplifying complex multimodal pipelines.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond fragmented multimodal solutions towards a truly unified, scalable, and high-performing model that can seamlessly interact with and generate content across text, images, and videos, addressing both architectural and training challenges in the process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Show-o2 is characterized by a novel integration of several advanced techniques within a unified framework and a strategic two-stage training recipe.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture (Refer to Fig. 1)

The Show-o2 model operates on a sequence of interleaved text, images, and videos. Its core components are:

- **Input Encoders:**
 - **Text:** A standard text tokenizer and embedding layer convert input text into continuous text embeddings.
 - **Image/Video:** A **3D causal Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encoder** processes visual inputs into compact visual latent representations. The 3D nature of the VAE is crucial for accommodating both static images and dynamic video sequences.
- **Unified Visual Representation (UVR) Construction:** This is a cornerstone of Show-o2's scalability and multimodal capability. Recognizing that understanding and generation require different feature dependencies, a **dual-path architecture** is proposed:
 - **Semantic Layers S(·):** These layers extract high-level, rich semantic contextual information. They share the vision transformer blocks of a pre-trained SigLIP model and are pre-distilled to mimic SigLIP's ability to extract semantic features from both clean and noised visual latents.
 - **Projector P(·):** A simpler 2D patch embedding layer that retains complete low-level, intrinsic structural, and textual details from the visual latents.
 - **Spatial (-Temporal) Fusion (STF):** The high-level semantic features from S(·) and low-level features from P(·) are fused. For images, this is spatial fusion; for videos, it's spatial-temporal fusion, ensuring temporal alignment. This fusion is achieved by concatenating features along the dimension, followed by RMSNorm and two MLP layers. A time step embedding is prepended to the UVR for generative modeling.
- **Sequence Modeling:** Text embeddings and the unified visual representations (UVRs) are structured into a single, interleaved sequence (e.g., `[BOS] {Text} [BOI/BOV] {Image/Video} [EOI/EOV] {Text}... [EOS]`). This sequence is then fed into a **pre-trained language model (LLM)**.
 - **Omni-Attention Mechanism:** This mechanism ensures causal attention along the overall sequence (like a standard LLM) while allowing full attention *within* the unified visual representations, preserving contextual integrity for visual tasks.
- **Output Heads:** The LLM is augmented with two specialized heads:
 - **Language Head:** Handles text token prediction using autoregressive modeling.
 - **Flow Head:** Dedicated to image/video generation. It consists of several transformer layers with time step modulation (similar to DiT via adaLN-Zero blocks). This head is trained to predict the velocity field (v_t) for **flow matching**, a generative process that deterministically transforms noise into data.
- **Output Decoders:** A text de-tokenizer and the 3D causal VAE decoder convert the generated tokens and visual latents back into human-readable text and high-resolution images/videos.

#### 3.2. Training Recipe (Two-Stage Approach)

A key innovation is the **two-stage training recipe** designed to effectively learn visual generation capabilities while efficiently preserving the LLM's language knowledge, crucially without requiring a massive, expensive text-only corpus like RefinedWeb.

- **Stage-1: Visual Generation Pre-training**
 - **Trainable Components:** Only the Projector P(·), Spatial (-Temporal) Fusion (STF) mechanism, and Flow Head are trained. The pre-distilled Semantic Layers S(·) and the base LLM remain frozen initially.
 - **Datasets:** Focuses on approximately 66M image-text pairs, progressively adding interleaved data and video-text pairs (e.g., WebVid, Pandas, OmniCorpus).
 - **Objectives:** Autoregressive modeling (L_NTP) for language head (though it's mostly frozen, its loss contributes) and flow matching (L_FM) for the flow head, combined as `L = αL_NTP + L_FM`. Classifier-free guidance is enabled by occasionally dropping captions.
 - **Goal:** The primary goal is to endow the model with strong visual generative capabilities and adapt the visual components to the LLM's embedding space.
- **Stage-2: Full Model Fine-tuning**
 - **Trainable Components:** The full model (excluding the VAE, which is typically fixed) is fine-tuned.
 - **Datasets:** Utilizes higher-quality, curated multimodal understanding instruction data (9M, e.g., Densefusion-1M, LLaVA-OneVision), high-quality visual generation data (16M, filtered from Stage-1 data), and video understanding data (1.6M).
 - **Objectives:** The loss function remains the same, but the emphasis shifts to refining both understanding and generation across all modalities with high-quality data.
 - **Goal:** To enhance multimodal understanding and generation, while critically preserving and refining the LLM's inherent language knowledge.

#### 3.3. Scaling Up Strategy

To scale from smaller (e.g., 1.5B LLM parameters) to larger models (e.g., 7B LLM parameters), the pre-trained flow head from the smaller model is resumed. A lightweight MLP transformation is introduced to align the hidden size of the components with the larger LLM, enabling rapid adaptation and convergence without retraining from scratch.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a flexible architectural design with a strategically phased training approach, allows Show-o2 to natively unify diverse multimodal tasks across text, images, and videos efficiently and effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Show-o2 demonstrates state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across a wide array of multimodal understanding and generation benchmarks, underscoring the efficacy of its architectural design and training methodology.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Understanding on Images and Videos

- **Image Understanding (Table 3):**
 - Both 1.5B and 7B variants of Show-o2 consistently outperform numerous state-of-the-art models across various metrics.
 - The 1.5B model achieves the best scores on MME-p and MMMU-val, and competitive performance on GQA and SEED-Bench, compared to models of similar size.
 - The 7B model surpasses even larger models like TokenFlow-XL (14B parameters) on MME-p, GQA, MMMU-val, MMStar, and AI2D, while remaining competitive on SEED-Bench and MM-Bench. This highlights the robust perception capabilities of its unified visual representations.
- **Video Understanding (Table 4):**
 - The fine-tuned Show-o2 models (Show-o2†) demonstrate strong performance on video understanding benchmarks, achieving competitive results against proprietary models like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro, and open-source models like LLaVA-OV. This confirms its ability to process and comprehend dynamic visual information.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 2):** Show-o2 excels at general-purpose questions, detailed image descriptions, object counting, and text recognition. It can provide step-by-step instructions leveraging world knowledge and supports bilingual (English and Chinese) question-answering, showcasing its versatility.
- **Impact of Image Token Count (Table 14):** Ablation studies reveal that increasing the number of image tokens significantly boosts performance across ChartQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, and TextVQA, highlighting the importance of capturing fine-grained details, especially with the "AnyRes" inference strategy.

#### 4.2. Visual Generation

- **Image Generation (Tables 5, 6, 7):**
 - **GenEval:** Show-o2 surpasses most approaches, including TokenFlow-XL, Show-o, Emu3, and Transfusion. The 7B model achieves 0.76 overall, demonstrating strong image quality.
 - **DPG-Bench:** Show-o2 achieves the best overall scores among generation-only models (like SD3-Medium) and other unified models (Emu3-DPO, Janus-Pro), with scores of 85.02 (1.5B) and 86.14 (7B).
 - **OneIG-Bench:** Show-o2 models also achieve competitive performance, especially for alignment and text generation.
 - **Qualitative Results (Fig. 2):** The model can generate high-quality and realistic images from text prompts.
- **Video Generation (Tables 8, 9, Fig. 2, 3):**
 - **Text-to-Video (Table 8):** With only 2B parameters (total, including LLM and flow head), Show-o2 outperforms models like Show-1 (6B), Emu3 (8B), and VILA-U (7B) on VBench. It shows competitive performance against larger models like CogVideoX (5B) and Step-Video-T2V (30B).
 - **Image-to-Video (Table 9):** Show-o2 achieves strong performance on VBench metrics for image-to-video generation, particularly in Subject Consistency and Background Consistency, outperforming many specialized I2V models.
 - **Qualitative Results (Fig. 2, 3):** Show-o2 generates consistent video frames with reasonable motions from both text prompts and input images.

#### 4.3. Mixed-Modality Generation (Fig. 2)

- Show-o2 effectively handles mixed-modality generation, specifically demonstrated with visual storytelling. It can interleavingly generate coherent text and images, building a narrative sequence based on provided context, thanks to its flexible interleaved sequence format and the ability to dynamically append noise for image generation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Spatial (-Temporal) Fusion (Table 10):** The dual-path spatial (-temporal) fusion mechanism significantly improves both multimodal understanding (MME-p, GQA) and generation (FID-5K), validating the importance of integrating both semantic and low-level features.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and Inference Steps (Table 11):** Increasing CFG guidance scale and inference steps generally improves generation performance on GenEval and DPG-Bench, though improvements level off after certain thresholds (e.g., CFG > 5.0).
- **Two-Stage Training Recipe (Tables 12, 13):**
 - Stage-2 training consistently and significantly improves generation performance on GenEval and DPG-Bench, highlighting its importance.
 - Crucially, the two-stage training recipe effectively preserves the LLM's language knowledge, achieving performance comparable to the original Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B Instruct models on text-only benchmarks (MMLU, GPQA, GSM8K, HumanEval). In contrast, a one-stage full-parameter co-training with external textual data (like RefinedWeb) leads to substantial performance degradation, validating the necessity and effectiveness of the proposed two-stage approach for efficient and balanced learning.

In summary, Show-o2 sets new benchmarks in unified multimodal AI, demonstrating powerful capabilities across understanding and generation, for both images and videos, while addressing critical training efficiency challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Show-o2 represents a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the pursuit of general-purpose multimodal intelligence. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Native Unified Multimodal Intelligence (Text, Image, Video):** Show-o2 pushes the frontier of truly unified multimodal models by seamlessly integrating understanding and generation capabilities across text, images, *and videos* within a single, native architecture. This is a crucial step beyond models primarily focused on text and images, opening avenues for more natural and comprehensive interactions with the digital world. The native integration of video, a highly complex and dynamic modality, is a key differentiator.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Versatility and Cohesion:** By avoiding the "assembling tailored models" approach, Show-o2 offers a more cohesive and potentially synergistic learning experience. A single model can now tackle a diverse range of tasks—from nuanced visual question answering and detailed image/video descriptions to realistic image/video generation and complex mixed-modality storytelling. This versatility simplifies the development of multimodal applications and allows for a deeper, integrated understanding across different media types.
- **Innovative and Efficient Training Paradigm:** The proposed two-stage training recipe is a major practical contribution. It addresses the critical challenge of simultaneously cultivating visual generation capabilities and preserving the extensive language knowledge of pre-trained LLMs. By doing so without requiring a prohibitively expensive, web-scale text corpus, it makes the development of such powerful unified models more accessible and resource-efficient for research and development.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance Across Benchmarks:** Achieving top performance on a broad range of established multimodal understanding and generation benchmarks (MME, GQA, GenEval, DPG-Bench, VBench, etc.) validates the robustness and effectiveness of Show-o2's underlying architectural designs and training strategies. This sets a new benchmark for what unified models can accomplish.
- **Foundational for Future Multimodal AI Systems:** Show-o2 provides a strong foundation for the next generation of multimodal AI. Its architectural choices (e.g., 3D causal VAE, dual-path visual representation, omni-attention) and training innovations can inspire future research into even more capable and efficient unified models that can perceive and interact with the world in increasingly human-like ways.
- **Potential for Wide-Ranging Applications:**
 - **Content Creation:** Tools for generating complex multimedia stories, advertisements, or educational content from simple text prompts, or expanding existing images into dynamic videos.
 - **Intelligent Assistants:** Multimodal assistants that can not only understand spoken or written queries but also comprehend visual context (e.g., analyzing a video clip) and generate appropriate visual or textual responses.
 - **Accessibility:** Generating descriptions of videos or images for visually impaired users, or converting complex visual information into easily digestible text.
 - **Education and Training:** Creating interactive educational content that combines text, images, and videos to explain concepts more effectively.
- **Broader Impact and Ethical Considerations:** The authors responsibly acknowledge potential limitations and broader impacts. The ability to generate realistic images and videos, and coherent text, carries the risk of misuse, such as creating misinformation, deepfakes, or fake profiles. The use of large datasets with copyrighted material and celebrity images also highlights intellectual property and privacy concerns. These considerations underscore the need for ongoing ethical scrutiny and the development of safeguards as such powerful generative AI technologies become more widespread.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of code and models contributes to transparency, reproducibility, and fosters further collaborative research in the AI community.

In conclusion, Show-o2 marks a significant step towards a more holistic and capable AI that can natively understand and generate across text, images, and videos. Its innovations in architecture and training efficiency pave the way for more integrated and powerful multimodal applications, while also drawing attention to the important ethical responsibilities that accompany such advanced capabilities.
