# Kimodo: Scaling Controllable Human Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed Kimodo, a generative model that synthesizes high-quality, controllable human motions by leveraging a 700-hour optical motion capture dataset and a two-stage transformer denoiser. The system provides flexible control via text prompts and kinematic constraints, enabling the creation of diverse human and robot behaviors.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for acquiring 3D human motion (e.g., hand animation, studio mocap) are costly, time-consuming, and require specialized expertise.
- Prior generative models for motion synthesis are constrained by limited public datasets, leading to compromises in motion quality, control accuracy, and generalization.
- Reconstructing 3D motion from monocular videos, while scalable, often sacrifices motion quality due to inherent inaccuracies in the reconstruction process.

## Method

- **Kimodo** leverages the large-scale, high-quality 700-hour ==Bones Rigplay optical motion capture dataset== to train a ==motion diffusion model==.
- The model employs a ==two-stage transformer denoiser== architecture that explicitly decomposes the prediction of global root motion and local body motion.
- It utilizes a comprehensive ==motion representation== (smoothed root, global joint positions/velocities/angles, foot contacts) designed for high quality and direct conditioning via text prompts and various kinematic constraints.

## Results

- **Kimodo** generates realistic human motions for diverse activities and produces high-quality kinematic demonstrations for the Unitree G1 robot by retargeting the training dataset.
- The model achieves precise kinematic control, demonstrating average joint errors of **3.21 cm** for full-body keyframes and **3.63 cm** for end-effector positions in quantitative evaluations.
- Ablation studies show the two-stage denoiser reduces foot skating by **49%** (from 7.59 cm/s to 3.87 cm/s) and improves full-body keyframe position error by **68%** (from 8.37 cm to 2.67 cm) compared to a one-stage baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling training data with high-fidelity optical motion capture is crucial for achieving superior motion quality, precise control, and robust generalization in generative motion models.
> - A two-stage denoiser architecture, which separates root and body motion prediction and interleaves their corrections, significantly improves motion quality and minimizes artifacts like foot skating.
> - A carefully designed motion representation combined with a dual-phase training curriculum enables a single model to effectively handle both semantic text prompts and a wide range of sparse and dense kinematic constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of high-quality 3D human motion data is a foundational requirement across various fields, including robotics, industrial simulation, and entertainment. Traditional methods for acquiring such data, such as hand animation and studio motion capture (mocap), present challenges related to cost, time, and required expertise. Hand animation is often tedious, while studio mocap involves expensive instrumentation and environments. Teleoperation, while popular for robot demonstrations, can be slow and may result in unnatural movements. Recovering high-quality 3D motions from monocular videos, despite videos being a rich source, remains a complex research problem due to inherent inaccuracies in reconstruction.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion models, masked models, and tokenized transformers, have offered a potential alternative by enabling human motion synthesis from intuitive inputs like text prompts. Some of these approaches also incorporate kinematic constraints, such as keyframe poses or 2D waypoints, to enhance control. However, the efficacy of these models has been constrained by the limited scale of publicly available mocap datasets, which often leads to compromises in motion quality, control accuracy, and generalization capabilities. Furthermore, saturated benchmarks on these smaller datasets can obscure the impact of specific architectural and methodological design choices. While some efforts have attempted to scale up motion generation by training on large volumes of motion data recovered from videos, these often sacrifice motion quality due to the inaccuracies inherent in video-based 3D reconstruction.

Kimodo addresses these limitations by leveraging a substantially larger, high-quality optical motion capture dataset and proposing a carefully designed model architecture. This work seeks to advance the field by demonstrating that scaling up training data with high-fidelity mocap, combined with an optimized generative model design, can lead to superior motion quality, precise and versatile control, and improved generalization, thus offering a more practical paradigm for human motion data acquisition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to establish generative models as a viable and accessible paradigm for synthesizing high-quality human motion data with precise control, overcoming the limitations of both traditional methods and prior generative models. The authors contend that for a generative motion model to be practically useful, it should maintain the advantages of traditional motion acquisition while increasing accessibility for a broader range of users, including novice animators and roboticists.

Specifically, the key objectives for Kimodo are outlined as follows:

1. **Produce High-Quality Motions**: The model should generate motions that are on par with the quality typically associated with optical motion capture, avoiding common artifacts often seen in synthesized motions.
2. **Provide a Versatile and Directable Interface**: The system should offer an interface that is akin to hand animation but with more intuitive control mechanisms. This includes supporting text prompts for semantic control and a comprehensive suite of kinematic constraints for precise spatial and temporal direction.
3. **Generate a Diverse Corpus of Motions**: The model should be capable of producing a wide variety of motions to support a broad spectrum of applications in fields such as game development, digital simulation, and robotics.

The motivation for the specific design choices within Kimodo stems from addressing the identified shortcomings in the broader research landscape:

- **Scaling Data**: The primary motivation for using the 700-hour Bones Rigplay dataset is to overcome the limitations imposed by smaller public datasets, which restrict the motion quality, control accuracy, and generalization of existing models. This large-scale, high-quality optical mocap data is intended to provide a robust foundation for learning diverse and realistic human movements.
- **Controllability and Motion Quality**: The model's architecture is motivated by the need for both high motion quality and precise control. Prior works struggled with simultaneously achieving both, especially with sparse, flexible constraints. The authors aim to enable direct and intuitive control without requiring complex post-processing or computationally expensive test-time optimizations.
- **Minimizing Motion Artifacts**: Common issues in synthetic motion, such as floating or foot skating, necessitate a motion representation and model architecture that inherently minimize these artifacts. The decomposition of root and body prediction and the careful design of the motion features are driven by this motivation.
- **Generalization**: Designing a robust system capable of handling various motion styles, actions, and character skeletons (e.g., SOMA, Unitree G1 robot) is crucial for practical applications, motivating the use of augmentations and a flexible representation.
- **Practical Workflow**: The design considers practical animation and robotics workflows, such as providing a smoothed root representation that aligns with how animators typically specify paths, and enabling world-space joint rotation control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Kimodo operates as an explicit kinematic motion diffusion model, building upon principles from image diffusion models but adapted for body pose features. The framework's core design decisions revolve around its motion representation and a two-stage denoiser architecture.

**Motion Representation**:
A motion sequence is standardized such that the root at the first frame is positioned above the origin. Each pose within the motion is represented by a vector of features, designed for both motion quality and amenability to conditioning via direct imputation. These features include:
- `r_p` (R^3): A smoothed global root position, derived from the pelvis position by heavily smoothing its 2D horizontal components (x, z) while maintaining the y-height. This smoothed trajectory provides a stable reference frame and emulates paths used in animation tools.
- `r_a` (R^2): The global root heading direction, represented as `[cos(ψ), sin(ψ)]`.
- `j_p` (R^3J): Joint positions, where J is the number of joints. The 2D horizontal components are relative to the smoothed root position, while the y-height is global. These are not rotated relative to the root heading.
- `j_v` (R^3J): Global joint velocities, computed from global joint positions.
- `j_a` (R^6J): Global joint angles, encoded using a 6D representation.
- `f` ({0,1}^4): Boolean flags indicating foot contacts for the left heel, left toe, right heel, and right toe.

This representation is conceptually divided into a global root component (`r_glob` = [`r_p`, `r_a`]) and a body component (`b` = [`j_p`, `j_v`, `j_a`, `f`]). The global nature of joint positions and rotations facilitates sparse constraint application, unlike purely local or velocity-based representations. The non-canonicalized joint positions avoid discontinuities caused by sudden heading flips, improving training stability. Global joint rotations enable direct world-space rotation constraints, a common requirement in animation.

**Two-Stage Transformer Denoiser**:
The denoiser predicts a clean motion `^x_0` from a noisy motion `x_t`, optionally conditioned on text and/or kinematic constraints.
- **Inputs**: Kinematic constraints are applied by directly imputing (overwriting) specified target pose features (`x_tgt`) into the noisy motion `x_t` using a binary control mask `m`. The final input tokens `x_in` are the imputed motion concatenated with the control mask. Additional conditioning tokens include an LLM2Vec embedding (`c_text`) of the text prompt, the desired initial heading direction (`c_dir`), and extra all-zero tokens (`c_extra`) to enhance representational capacity.
- **Architecture**: The denoiser is composed of two transformer encoders, each with 16 layers, 8 heads, and a latent size of 1024, totaling 282 million parameters in the full model.
 1. **Root Denoiser (Stage 1)**: This transformer first predicts the global root motion (`^r_glob_0`). It is conditioned on the full noisy motion `x_in` to ensure coordination with body motion and constraints.
 2. **Body Denoiser (Stage 2)**: The global root prediction from Stage 1 is transformed into a local representation (`^r_local_0`), which includes angular velocity of heading, planar translation velocity, and absolute height. This local root representation is then concatenated with the body features from `x_in` and fed into the second transformer to predict the body motion (`^b_0`).
- **Output**: The final clean motion prediction `^x_0` is the concatenation of `^r_glob_0` and `^b_0`.
- **Interleaved Denoising**: Unlike prior two-stage models that complete root denoising before body generation, Kimodo's two stages are interleaved, allowing root and body corrections throughout the denoising process for better alignment.

**Training**:
The denoiser is trained using the DDPM framework with a modified smooth L1 loss function that combines losses on root position, root heading, joint positions, joint velocities, joint angles, foot contacts, and an FK loss to align predicted joint angles with positions. Weights are applied to different loss components. Training employs the Adam-atan2 optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-5. Ground truth motions are cropped to a maximum of 10 seconds, translated to start at the origin, and have randomized first-frame heading to improve robustness. The largest model is trained with a batch size of 2048 across 16 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, generating motions at 30 fps.

- **Training Curriculum (Two Phases)**:
 1. **Phase 1 (500k steps)**: Model is trained solely on the text-to-motion task without kinematic constraints, using a dropout rate of 0.1.
 2. **Phase 2 (500k steps)**: The model is trained on a mix of text and kinematic constraints. Constraint patterns are randomly sampled and include full-body keyframes, sparse end-effector positions/rotations, 2D root waypoints/paths, and foot contact patterns. Constraints are mixed 25% of the time, and 10% of the time, only text input is used. Dropout is removed in this phase.
- Text input is dropped 10% of the time in both phases to enable classifier-free guidance during inference. Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is applied to model parameters.

**Motion Generation**:
Inference utilizes the DDIM process, typically with 100 denoising steps. Classifier-free guidance is employed, combining unconditional, text-conditioned, and constraint-conditioned model outputs with adjustable weights (default `w_text` = 2, `w_constr` = 2). For multi-prompt sequencing, motions are generated sequentially with overlaps, and full-body keyframe constraints are applied to ensure smooth transitions between prompts, followed by blending. Optional post-processing (foot locking, IK, optimization) can be applied for exact constraint adherence, though this is omitted during quantitative evaluation for fair comparison.

**Large-Scale Motion Capture Dataset**:
The core of Kimodo's training is the Bones Rigplay dataset, comprising 700 hours of optical mocap data from 170 human subjects. This dataset features a wide range of actions and styles, each labeled with high-level overview descriptions and fine-grained atomic action descriptions. The data is standardized to a 27-joint skeleton but also retargeted to other models like SOMA, Unitree G1, and SMPL-X.
- **Augmentations**: Text descriptions are paraphrased by an LLM (Qwen3-32B) into consistent prompt structures. Motion clips are augmented by stitching random pairs, with smooth transitions generated by a diffusion model, to improve handling of complex, multi-action prompts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of Kimodo's capabilities in text-to-motion generation and constraint-conditioned synthesis, along with detailed ablation studies and scaling analyses.

**Motion Generation with Text and Kinematic Control**:
- **Text-to-Motion Quality and Diversity**: Kimodo, trained on the SOMA body skeleton at 30 fps, demonstrates the generation of realistic human motions for diverse behaviors, including locomotion, everyday activities, dancing, actions, and combat, with various styles. The model can process complex text prompts describing sequential or simultaneous actions.
- **Robot Demonstrations**: The model can directly generate kinematic demonstrations for the Unitree G1 robot by retargeting the training dataset, enabling the acquisition of plausible robot skills like recovering from falls or object interactions. The model also exhibits diversity in outputs for a given prompt, producing plausible variations (e.g., one-handed vs. two-handed pick-up motions).
- **Kinematic Control Accuracy**: Kimodo supports an extensive range of kinematic constraints, including full-body keyframes, sparse joint positions/rotations (end-effectors), 2D waypoints, dense 2D paths, and foot contact patterns. Quantitative evaluation on a diverse test suite (for the SOMA skeleton) yielded:
 - 3.21 cm average joint errors for full-body keyframes.
 - 3.63 cm average joint position errors for end-effector constraints.
 - 6.88 degrees average joint rotation errors for end-effectors.
 - 3.63 cm average root errors across waypoints and paths.
 - The 95th percentile of 2D position error between the smoothed root constraint and projected pelvis was 9.7 cm for the full model, indicating controlled deviation for natural motion.

**Ablation Study (Table 1)**:
Key design decisions were evaluated against baselines:
- **Two-Stage Denoiser**: The full two-stage architecture demonstrated superior performance compared to a one-stage baseline with a similar parameter count. It significantly reduced foot skating (3.87 cm/s vs. 7.59 cm/s for Overview Prompt test set) and constraint errors (e.g., full-body position error of 2.67 cm vs. 8.37 cm), supporting the efficacy of root-body motion decomposition.
- **Local vs. Global Root in Second Stage**: Using a local root representation in the body denoiser (second stage) was found to be more effective than a global representation, resulting in lower foot skating (3.87 cm/s vs. 4.17 cm/s) and improved joint rotation accuracy (4.18 deg vs. 5.67 deg).
- **Smoothed Root Representation**: The smoothed root representation, as opposed to directly using the pelvis projected to the ground, generally reduced foot skating (3.87 cm/s vs. 4.39 cm/s) while maintaining similar constraint accuracy. It also allows for more natural body motion around path constraints.
- **Extra "Register" Tokens**: The inclusion of extra all-zero tokens for conditioning improved text-following (R@3 71.9 vs. 70.9) and motion quality (FID 1.85 vs. 1.95), suggesting they enhance the model's representational capacity.
- **Dual-Phase Training Curriculum**: The two-phase training (initial text-only pre-training followed by text+constraint training) was effective in reducing constraint errors (e.g., full-body position error 2.67 cm vs. 5.80 cm for no curriculum) while maintaining comparable text-following and motion quality.

**Scaling Analysis (Table 2)**:
The impact of scaling along data size, model size, and batch size was investigated:
- **Data Size**: Increasing the amount of training data (from 10% to 50% to 100% of the 700-hour dataset) monotonically improved foot skate metrics and constraint accuracy across all types (e.g., full-body position error decreased from 4.60 cm at 10% data to 2.77 cm at full data). Text-following (R-precision) and motion quality (FID) were less affected in this specific experiment setup, which sampled all unique behaviors even at reduced data sizes.
- **Model Size**: Larger model variants (Large: 282M, Medium: 148M, Small: 56M parameters) consistently improved performance across all metrics. The Large model achieved higher R-precision (e.g., 71.9 vs. 64.0 for Small model on Overview Prompt) and lower FID (1.85 vs. 3.10), indicating better text-following and motion quality. Constraint accuracy also improved with larger models.
- **Batch Size**: Increasing the batch size during training (using 4, 8, or 16 GPUs, corresponding to batch sizes of 512, 1024, and 2048) generally led to performance improvements across the board, particularly in text-following and motion quality. For instance, the Large Batch (16 GPUs) achieved an R@3 of 73.6 for the Overview Prompt compared to 69.4 for the Small Batch (4 GPUs).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Kimodo represents an advancement in the field of human motion generation, establishing a scalable and highly controllable generative model for producing high-quality kinematic motions. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

- **New Motion Data Acquisition Paradigm**: Kimodo offers a robust alternative to traditional, resource-intensive methods (hand animation, studio mocap) and quality-compromised techniques (video-based reconstruction). By synthesizing high-quality motions from intuitive inputs, it streamlines the creation of diverse motion assets.
- **Enhanced Accessibility and Workflow Efficiency**: The model's intuitive interface, combining text prompting with a comprehensive suite of kinematic constraints, lowers the barrier to entry for motion authoring. This enables a wider range of users, including novice animators, game developers, and roboticists, to generate complex and natural human movements without extensive expertise or specialized equipment.
- **High-Fidelity and Precise Control**: By training on an unprecedented scale of high-quality optical mocap data (700 hours), Kimodo overcomes the limitations of previous generative models regarding motion quality, control accuracy, and generalization. The detailed quantitative results demonstrate its ability to adhere to precise kinematic specifications while maintaining natural movement.
- **Broad Application in Robotics**: The ability to generate kinematic demonstrations directly for humanoid robots, such as the Unitree G1, offers a significant advantage for robot learning. It enables rapid, efficient creation of large demonstration datasets for complex skills, which can then be used to train physics-based control policies. This accelerates the development of more agile and human-like robotic behaviors.
- **Advancements in Simulation and Entertainment**: For digital twins and industrial simulations, Kimodo can populate virtual environments with dynamic and plausible human characters interacting realistically. In game development, it facilitates the scalable generation of diverse character animations, fluid transitions between motion clips, and nuanced object interactions, enhancing the realism and interactivity of virtual experiences.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work provides a robust benchmark and methodology for investigating the scaling properties of motion generation models. The detailed ablation studies elucidate the importance of specific architectural choices and training curricula, informing future model designs. The release of model checkpoints, generation code, and an interactive interface further promotes research and development in the community.
- **Addressing Challenges in Data Integration**: Kimodo's success based on clean mocap data sets a strong foundation for future efforts to integrate noisy data sources, such as internet videos, without compromising the learned motion quality. This is a critical step toward even larger-scale and more diverse motion generation capabilities.
- **Inspiration for Real-time and Interactive Systems**: While designed for offline authoring, the research provides insights that could pave the way for real-time, dynamic control models that react to changing environments, potentially by reformulating motion generation into autoregressive problems within learned latent spaces.
- **Enabling Complex Interactions**: The current work primarily focuses on human body motion. However, its robust framework and scalability set the stage for incorporating more complex scene and object interactions, which are crucial for making motion generation models truly practical for a wider array of applications.
