# SOLO: Stable Omni-terrain Long-Horizon Perceptive Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> SOLO is a teacher-student framework for long-horizon perceptive humanoid locomotion. Its Query Reconstructor gives each height-map cell a Fourier-encoded query that cross-attends to a depth-proprioception token memory, preserving sharp terrain edges, while Trajectory-Aware MSE distillation adds next-state teacher-student disagreement to the PPO reward so GAE propagates it backward. Reconstruction error drops 3.3-4.0x, stress-test success reaches 97.5%, and the policy walks 1.5 km outdoors zero-shot.

## Problem

- Prior teacher-student locomotion policies are validated on short isolated-obstacle trials that reset local errors, hiding the drift that compounds during continuous, uninterrupted real-world deployment.
- Standard dense terrain reconstructors compress depth history through a shared recurrent bottleneck acting as a low-pass filter, smoothing stepping-stone boundaries and stair edges that determine footholds.
- Action-MSE distillation matches student to teacher only at the currently visited state, so it cannot separate actions with equal present error but different future consequences.

## Method

- **Query Reconstructor**: every height-map cell carries a ==Fourier-encoded positional query== that ==cross-attends== a Transformer sensor memory of ResNet depth tokens plus proprioception, decoding per-cell height and base velocity without a dense bottleneck.
- **Trajectory-Aware MSE Distillation**: stop-gradient next-state teacher-student action disagreement is subtracted from the ==PPO== task reward, letting ==GAE== propagate future divergence penalties to earlier actions with no extra critic, dynamics model, or teacher rollout.
- Three-stage training: ==AMP-regularized PPO== privileged teacher, TA-MSE student distillation under privileged maps, then joint fine-tuning where the reconstructor is supervised on the student's own on-policy visitation buffer.

## Results

- Query Reconstructor cuts on-policy height-map L1 from **7.59/9.26 cm** for START/DPL to **2.29 cm**; on unseen 10 m scenes those baselines incur **6.1-6.2x** its error.
- With distillation fixed, it lifts mean highest-difficulty success from **75.0-75.6%** to **97.5%** and stepping stones from **0-3%** to **96%**; TA-MSE hits curriculum level **5.5** versus **5.1** for MSE+PPO.
- Zero-shot on the Omni humanoid with one chest depth camera: continuous **1.5 km** outdoor route, uninterrupted indoor mixed-terrain course, and **69/70** isolated-terrain hardware trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Output parameterization can matter more than encoder capacity: per-cell queries retain high-frequency geometry any shared dense decoder averages away, and transfer better to unseen scene extents.
> - Distillation error can be routed through the RL return rather than the loss, buying trajectory-level credit assignment without a second critic or teacher rollout.
> - Isolated-obstacle success overstates reliability; kilometre-scale uninterrupted runs are the honest test, and residual failures concentrate on sparse footholds and sensor blind spots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous humanoid robots capable of navigating complex and unstructured environments over extended periods represents a significant challenge in robotics. Current research in perceptive humanoid locomotion often focuses on achieving dynamic and agile movements over challenging terrains, such as parkour or stair climbing. A common approach involves teacher-student reinforcement learning, where a privileged teacher policy trained with full state information in simulation is distilled into a student policy that operates using onboard sensors like depth cameras and proprioception.

While existing methods have demonstrated success in traversing isolated obstacles or short, discontinuous trials, a persistent limitation is the fragility of these policies during long-horizon, continuous deployment in real-world settings. This fragility arises from the accumulation of perception and control errors over time, which can lead to instability and failure. This paper identifies two compounding causes for this long-horizon fragility:
1. **Lossy dense terrain reconstruction:** Standard methods often compress depth history into a shared representation (e.g., using CNN-RNN encoders), which can act as a low-pass filter. This process smooths out high-frequency, action-critical details such as sharp edges of stairs or boundaries of stepping stones, leading to inaccurate foothold planning.
2. **Myopic pointwise imitation:** Controller distillation typically uses a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss on actions, matching the student's output to the teacher's only at the current state. This objective lacks temporal credit assignment, meaning it does not evaluate how a current action might affect future states or the student's ability to maintain teacher-like behavior over a trajectory. Plausible actions in the current state might lead to future states where the student deviates significantly from the teacher.

SOLO aims to advance the field by directly addressing these identified weaknesses. It proposes a unified framework designed to enhance stability and robustness for uninterrupted, long-distance humanoid locomotion by improving both terrain perception fidelity and the temporal awareness of the policy distillation process. The work seeks to enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with only onboard sensing, a key requirement for practical robot deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve stable, omni-terrain, long-horizon perceptive humanoid locomotion in dynamic and unstructured environments. This overarching goal is driven by the observation that while humanoids can perform complex maneuvers, their performance degrades over extended deployments due to error accumulation. To mitigate this, the paper identifies and addresses two specific, compounding issues:

**Motivation for addressing Long-Horizon Fragility:**
Human performance in traversing diverse and complex terrains over long distances provides a benchmark for robotic capabilities. Humans adapt their perception and control continuously, with each foot placement influencing subsequent actions and errors being corrected dynamically. In contrast, current perceptive humanoid policies, despite their successes in structured or short-duration tasks, exhibit fragility when errors accumulate over continuous, extended rollouts in the real world. This limits their practical applicability in scenarios requiring sustained, uninterrupted operation, such as navigating a kilometer-long outdoor path with varying terrain. The motivation is to bridge this gap by developing policies that can maintain stability and performance without requiring frequent resets or external assistance.

**Specific Objectives and their Motivations:**

1. **Improve Terrain Reconstruction Fidelity for Action-Critical Details:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing dense terrain reconstructors, which compress sensor data into a single, shared latent representation, tend to smooth out crucial high-frequency details in the terrain map. For example, the precise location of a stair edge or the distinct boundary of a stepping stone is critical for accurate foot placement and maintaining balance. When these details are blurred or lost, the student policy receives an inaccurate terrain representation, leading to increased foothold errors and subsequent instability. The goal is to ensure that the reconstructed terrain map accurately reflects these action-critical features.
 - **Objective:** Develop a terrain reconstruction module that can retrieve spatially specific evidence from raw sensor data, thereby preserving sharp terrain boundaries and fine-grained features essential for precise locomotion.

2. **Introduce Trajectory-Aware Credit Assignment in Policy Distillation:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard policy distillation methods often rely on pointwise action matching (e.g., minimizing MSE between student and teacher actions at the current state). While simple, this approach is myopic; it does not explicitly consider the long-term consequences of a student's action. An action that appears similar to the teacher's in the current state might lead the robot to a future state where its behavior significantly diverges from the teacher, accumulating errors over a trajectory. This lack of trajectory-level credit assignment makes policies susceptible to future divergence and instability.
 - **Objective:** Design a distillation mechanism that can incorporate future teacher-student disagreement into the policy optimization process, enabling the student to learn actions that not only match the teacher in the present but also lead to states where future matching remains robust. This aims to provide a more holistic, trajectory-aware learning signal.

By addressing these two intertwined issues through specialized perception and policy learning components, SOLO aims to achieve unprecedented robustness and stability for humanoid locomotion over long horizons in diverse real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SOLO is a unified framework integrating a novel perceptive state estimator with a trajectory-aware policy distillation mechanism. The system is designed to distill a privileged terrain-conditioned teacher policy into a deployable student policy that relies solely on onboard depth sensing and proprioception.

#### 3.1. Teacher-Student Setting and System Overview

- **Teacher Policy (πT):** In the simulator, the teacher observes privileged information, including a clean local height map (m_t), the realized base velocity (v_t), and a comprehensive proprioceptive state (p_t) which includes base angular velocity, gravity vector, commanded planar velocity, joint positions, velocities, and the previous action.
- **Student Policy (πS):** The deployable student receives a history of egocentric depth images (D_t) and proprioceptive states (p_t).
- **Information Flow:** During runtime, depth and proprioceptive histories are processed by the Query Reconstructor (QR) to estimate the local terrain map (m̂_t) and base velocity (v̂_t). These estimates, along with current proprioception, feed into the recurrent student policy, which then outputs 25-dimensional joint position offsets for a low-level controller (50 Hz).
- **Architecture:** Both teacher and student share an Attention-based Map Encoder (AME) backbone. The student adds an LSTM bypass to process proprioceptive history and maintain rollout context.
- **Training Stages:**
 1. **Teacher Pretraining:** The privileged teacher is trained using AMP-regularized PPO.
 2. **Privileged Distillation:** The student policy's AME parameters are initialized from the teacher. The student is then distilled using the Trajectory-Aware MSE (TA-MSE) method, still receiving privileged terrain input. QR is not yet active.
 3. **QR Fine-tuning:** The privileged terrain input to the student is replaced by QR's estimates. Both the student and QR are jointly fine-tuned. QR is updated based on its supervised reconstruction objective, using labels from states visited by the QR-conditioned student policy.

#### 3.2. Query Reconstructor (QR)

QR is designed to provide accurate, high-fidelity terrain and base velocity estimates from deployable observations, specifically preserving sharp boundaries.
- **Sensor Token Memory:**
 - Depth history (e.g., 4 frames) is processed by a lightweight ResNet to produce visual tokens for each frame.
 - Proprioceptive history (e.g., 10 frames) is embedded per-frame by an MLP, and learned temporal position embeddings are added.
 - These visual and proprioceptive token sets are concatenated and then processed by a Transformer encoder, yielding a shared sensor memory (M_t). This memory integrates visual terrain evidence with the robot's motion and contact context.
- **Cell-Query Decoding:**
 - Instead of a dense decoder, QR assigns a unique, Fourier-encoded planar coordinate query (q_i) to each cell in the target robot-centric local height map (e.g., 16x32 cells).
 - These N cell queries then cross-attend to the shared sensor memory (M_t) using stacked multi-head cross-attention layers. This allows each cell query to retrieve spatially specific evidence relevant to its location.
 - A dedicated height head maps the decoded cell query (q’_i) to the corresponding height (ĥ_i).
 - A separate learned velocity query attends to the same sensor memory (M_t) and is decoded into a 3-DoF base-velocity estimate (v̂_t).
 - **Distinctive Feature:** This architecture avoids forcing all terrain information through a single shared bottleneck, thereby preventing the smoothing of localized, action-critical terrain features like sharp foothold boundaries.
- **Reconstruction Objective:** QR is trained with a supervised objective: L_rec = λ_h * L1(ĥ, h_GT) + λ_v * L1(v̂, v_GT), where h_GT and v_GT are privileged ground truth labels from the simulator. During Stage III, QR is updated using data collected by the student's on-policy rollouts.

#### 3.3. Trajectory-Aware MSE (TA-MSE) Distillation

TA-MSE enhances policy distillation by incorporating future teacher-student disagreement into the PPO reward structure, promoting trajectory-level credit assignment.
- **Current-State Teacher Matching (L_aux):** This component retains the standard auxiliary objective of matching the student's action mean (μ_θ(hS_t)) to the teacher's (μ_T(oT_t)) at each visited state: L_aux(θ) = E_t [||μ_θ(hS_t) - μ_T(oT_t)||^2_2]. This term provides direct supervision for the current state.
- **Next-State Disagreement Reward (r_TA):**
 - After the student executes an action (a_t) and transitions from state s_t to s_t+1, the disagreement (ℓ̄_t+1) between the student and teacher policies is evaluated at this *next* state: ℓ̄_t+1 = sg(||μ_θ(hS_t+1) - μ_T(oT_t+1)||^2_2), where `sg` indicates a stop-gradient operation.
 - This disagreement is then incorporated as a penalty into the PPO reward: r_TA_t = r_task_t - β * ℓ̄_t+1. The reward for action `a_t` now explicitly penalizes inducing a state `s_t+1` where the student struggles to match the teacher.
 - **Key Mechanism:** Through Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), this `next-state disagreement penalty` propagates backward along the sampled rollout. This means that actions that lead to future states of high teacher-student divergence are penalized, effectively providing trajectory-aware credit assignment without requiring a separate dynamics model, additional critic, or teacher rollouts.
- **Unified Policy Objective:** The student actor's objective combines the PPO loss with the current-state auxiliary loss: max_θ J_PPO(θ; r_TA) - λL_aux(θ). The weights λ and β control the influence of the current-state MSE and the next-state disagreement reward, respectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SOLO spanned comprehensive simulation tests and zero-shot real-robot deployment, demonstrating the efficacy of both the Query Reconstructor (QR) and Trajectory-Aware MSE (TA-MSE) Distillation.

#### 4.1. Real-Robot Deployment (Zero-Shot)

SOLO was deployed zero-shot on an Omni humanoid robot using only a chest-mounted depth camera and proprioception, without external state or mapping.
- **Outdoor Long-Horizon Locomotion:** SOLO successfully completed a continuous 1.5-kilometer outdoor route, traversing natural stairs, slopes, grass transitions, and uneven ground in a single run. This deployment required no policy resets or physical assistance, indicating robust long-horizon stability.
- **Indoor Mixed-Terrain Course:** An uninterrupted traversal of an indoor course featuring ascending stairs, stepping stones, a gap, descending stairs, and a movable obstacle was demonstrated, confirming continuous operation across varied challenging terrains.
- **Isolated-Terrain Reliability:** Across ten trials each on seven isolated real-world terrains, SOLO achieved a 99% success rate (69 out of 70 trials). Six out of seven terrains (ascending/descending stairs, slope, high platform, gap, movable obstacle) achieved 10/10 success. Stepping stones, a notoriously difficult terrain, yielded 9/10 successes.
- **Comparative Context:** Compared to other reported systems, SOLO demonstrated long-horizon capabilities (1.5 km outdoor, >100 stairs) and sparse-terrain traversal (stepping stones) using a minimal sensing setup (one depth camera), which surpasses the reported capabilities and hardware configurations of systems like AME, CReF, and RPL in terms of continuous, unassisted real-world deployment on complex terrains.

#### 4.2. Reconstruction Accuracy (Simulation)

The Query Reconstructor (QR) was evaluated for its ability to accurately reconstruct terrain height maps compared to established baselines (START-style and DPL-style reconstructors) under a shared highest-difficulty protocol.
- **Quantitative Results:** QR achieved a significant reduction in average height-map L1 error, from 7.59 cm (START) and 9.26 cm (DPL) to 2.29 cm. This represents a 3.3 to 4.0-fold reduction in error. The largest improvements were observed on terrains with sharp discontinuities, such as stepping stones, where local height fidelity is critical.
- **Robustness to Terrain Extent:** When evaluated on unseen 10-meter scenes (after training on 8-meter scenes), QR demonstrated greater robustness. Its L1 error on descending stairs increased from 0.020 m to 0.043 m, whereas DPL and START showed much larger increases (0.117 m to 0.271 m and 0.084 m to 0.264 m, respectively), indicating that QR generalizes better to variations in terrain geometry.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visualizations of reconstructed height maps showed that QR preserved discrete footholds and sharp height transitions more faithfully than baseline methods, particularly on stepping stones, where the precise separation and alignment of footholds with ground truth are crucial.

#### 4.3. Distillation Curriculum Progress (Simulation)

The effectiveness of Trajectory-Aware MSE (TA-MSE) Distillation was assessed by tracking curriculum progression with privileged map input, comparing it against PPO-only and MSE+PPO baselines.
- **Results:** TA-MSE consistently reached higher curriculum levels and continued to improve after both PPO and MSE+PPO plateaued. TA-MSE reached the curriculum ceiling of approximately 5.5, while MSE+PPO plateaued around 5.1, and PPO remained below 5.0 with larger performance oscillations. This indicates that the return-based future disagreement signal provided by TA-MSE contributed to more effective and sustained learning, particularly as terrain difficulties increased.

#### 4.4. Locomotion on Highest-Difficulty Terrain (Simulation)

The integrated system's locomotion performance was evaluated on highest-difficulty terrains, fixing TA-MSE and comparing QR against START and DPL reconstructors.
- **Success Rate:** QR significantly improved mean stress-test success rates from 75.0–75.6% (START/DPL) to 97.5%. Critically, on challenging sparse terrains like stepping stones, QR achieved 96% success, a substantial increase from 3% for START and 0% for DPL. The worst-case terrain success rate also rose from 0–3% to 92%.
- **Foothold Quality Metrics:** QR resulted in lower Foot Stumble and Foothold-Grad penalty magnitudes across all four stair settings. START and DPL incurred 1.78–7.40 times and 1.94–10.83 times QR's penalties, respectively, particularly on 35 cm stairs. These metrics indicate that QR-enabled perception leads to more precise and stable foot placements.

In summary, the findings demonstrate that SOLO's Query Reconstructor significantly improves terrain perception by preserving critical details, and its Trajectory-Aware MSE Distillation enhances policy learning by incorporating temporal awareness. These component-level improvements translate to robust, stable, and long-horizon locomotion capabilities in both high-fidelity simulations and real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SOLO represents a notable contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, specifically in the domain of perceptive locomotion. By systematically addressing two fundamental challenges—lossy terrain reconstruction and myopic policy distillation—the research enhances the robustness and stability of humanoid robots in complex, unstructured environments over extended periods.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

1. **Enhanced Real-World Reliability:** The most significant impact is the demonstration of stable, long-horizon locomotion on a physical humanoid robot. The successful completion of a continuous 1.5-kilometer outdoor route and an indoor mixed-terrain course, both zero-shot and using only onboard sensing, indicates a substantial leap towards practical deployment of humanoids outside controlled laboratory settings. This level of sustained performance on varied natural terrains addresses a long-standing limitation in the field.
2. **Improved Terrain Perception:** The Query Reconstructor (QR) offers a novel approach to terrain mapping. By utilizing Fourier-encoded cell queries and cross-attention to sensor memory, QR effectively preserves action-critical high-frequency details (e.g., stair edges, stepping-stone boundaries) that are often smoothed by traditional dense reconstruction methods. This higher fidelity perception directly translates to more accurate foothold planning and improved locomotion stability, especially on sparse or discontinuous terrains. This method could be influential for other robotic tasks requiring precise environmental understanding from egocentric sensors.
3. **Advanced Policy Learning:** Trajectory-Aware MSE (TA-MSE) Distillation introduces a more sophisticated temporal credit assignment mechanism into policy learning. By penalizing future teacher-student disagreement, TA-MSE encourages the student policy to select actions that not only match the teacher's current behavior but also maintain alignment over future states. This approach moves beyond single-step imitation, leading to more robust and less fragile policies over long rollouts. The demonstrated improvement in curriculum progression underscores its effectiveness in enabling learning for increasingly difficult tasks.
4. **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ability to deploy the learned policy directly from simulation to a physical robot without further real-world fine-tuning, using only onboard depth and proprioception, highlights the effectiveness of the training methodology, including domain randomization and the robust design of QR and TA-MSE. This significantly reduces the time and resources typically required for robot learning in the real world.
5. **Benchmarking for Future Research:** The detailed evaluation protocols, including stress-test terrains, quantitative reconstruction metrics, and real-world deployment scenarios, provide valuable benchmarks for future research in perceptive humanoid locomotion. The reported limitations also open avenues for continued development.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions (as identified by the authors):**

- **Command-Conditioned:** SOLO is currently command-conditioned, meaning a human operator provides planar velocity commands. Future work could explore autonomous navigation or higher-level task execution.
- **Sensor Blind Spots:** The front-facing depth camera creates a rear blind spot, limiting backward locomotion on changing terrain. Integrating longer-lived memory or multi-camera setups could address this.
- **Terrain Representation:** The 2.5D height map assumes a single ground surface per cell and can be affected by transparent or reflective surfaces. Future advancements might involve complementary sensing modalities, 3D volumetric terrain representations, or multi-view perception to overcome these limitations.
- **Generalizability to Extreme Conditions:** While robust, the system's performance in truly extreme weather conditions (e.g., heavy rain, snow, dense fog) or highly deformable terrains remains an area for further investigation.

Overall, SOLO contributes a comprehensive framework that pushes the boundaries of stable, long-horizon humanoid locomotion. Its advancements in perception and policy learning lay a foundation for more autonomous and capable humanoid robots in diverse real-world applications.
