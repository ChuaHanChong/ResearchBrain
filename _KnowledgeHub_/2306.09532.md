# Hierarchical Planning and Control for Box Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical control framework enables physically-simulated characters to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks, such as rearranging boxes, by integrating an A* planner, a diffusion model for kinematic motion generation, and deep reinforcement learning policies. This system demonstrates generalization to varied object properties and robust execution in dynamic environments.

## Problem

- Integrating locomotion and object manipulation for physics-based characters remains a complex challenge in computer graphics and embodied AI.
- Developing generalizable and robust motion synthesis for diverse tasks and varying object properties (size, weight, height) typically requires vast amounts of specific motion capture data.
- Controlling physically-simulated characters for long-horizon, complex tasks involving interaction with dynamic environments is difficult to manage effectively.

## Method

- A three-level hierarchical control architecture is employed, consisting of a high-level A* planner for spatial pathfinding, a mid-level diffusion model for whole-body kinematic trajectory generation, and low-level deep reinforcement learning (DRL) policies for physics-based execution.
- Four motion primitives (walk-only, walk-and-carry, pick-up, put-down) are defined and sequenced to accomplish object rearrangement tasks.
- A diffusion model with novel bidirectional root control generates precise kinematic reference motions, which are then tracked by PPO-trained DRL policies that incorporate object interaction and physical stability.

## Results

- The system successfully generated long-horizon animations of characters rearranging multiple boxes of varying sizes, weights, and heights in cluttered 3D scenes, and also performed tasks like Tower of Hanoi.
- The manipulation policy generalized to pick up and put down single boxes weighing up to **8.0** kg and at heights between **0.05**-1.00m, and multiple boxes up to **3.7** kg/box, all from a single manipulation reference.
- Bidirectional root control reduced trajectory tracking error in the diffusion model by an average of **70%** compared to an egocentric-only model, leading to more stable and precise character navigation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models can effectively serve as mid-level planners for physics-based controllers, particularly when enhanced with bidirectional root control, which significantly improves trajectory tracking precision.
> - Object-aware reinforcement learning policies can generalize complex manipulation skills, such as lifting and placing boxes, to a wide range of object properties (weight, size, height) from a single, sparse reference motion clip.
> - A hierarchical approach efficiently decouples high-level task planning from low-level motor control, simplifying problem complexity and enhancing the scalability and generalization of loco-manipulation behaviors for virtual agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work "Hierarchical Planning and Control for Box Loco-Manipulation" addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of computer graphics, robotics, and artificial intelligence: enabling physically-simulated characters to perform complex everyday tasks involving both locomotion (movement through an environment) and manipulation (interacting with objects). This capability, often referred to as "loco-manipulation," is fundamental for creating truly autonomous and realistic virtual humans or robots.

Historically, the fields of locomotion and manipulation have often been tackled in isolation due to their inherent complexity. In character animation, generating realistic motion can be achieved through kinematic methods (ee.g., motion graphs, motion matching), which prioritize visual fidelity but often lack physical realism and robust interaction with dynamic environments. Physics-based simulations offer a principled way to achieve realism but are notoriously difficult to control, especially for diverse and long-horizon tasks.

Recent advancements, particularly in deep reinforcement learning (RL), have allowed for the generation of diverse and dynamic physics-based character skills, such as walking, running, and even complex acrobatic maneuvers. However, integrating these locomotion skills with object manipulation—where the character needs to physically interact with and change the state of objects in the environment—remains a significant hurdle. Prior attempts at loco-manipulation have often involved kinematic approaches, simplified physics, or heavily engineered model-based methods, especially in robotics where safety and stability are paramount but often result in slow or quasi-static motions.

This paper positions itself as an advancement in overcoming these limitations by:
- **Bridging Kinematics and Physics:** It leverages data-driven kinematic motion generation (specifically diffusion models) to provide high-quality reference trajectories, which are then tracked by physics-based RL controllers. This combines the expressiveness of kinematic data with the robustness of physical simulation.
- **Addressing Generalization:** Unlike methods that learn specific, pre-defined skills, this work aims for generalization to varying object properties (size, weight, height) and diverse environments, a key challenge for real-world application.
- **Adopting a Hierarchical Framework:** By decoupling high-level planning (what to do, where to go) from low-level execution (how to move muscles to achieve it), the system aims to manage complexity and improve scalability, a common strategy in complex control problems. It builds upon previous hierarchical RL efforts (e.g., Merel et al. 2020, Peng et al. 2022) but introduces diffusion models as a novel and effective component for mid-level motion generation.
- **Utilizing Diffusion Models:** This work is at the forefront of applying generative diffusion models, a relatively new and powerful class of models, to the domain of human motion synthesis and control, exploring their potential for generating plausible motion plans for physics-based characters.

In summary, this research contributes to the growing body of work that seeks to create more intelligent and physically capable virtual agents by synergistically combining state-of-the-art techniques from motion synthesis, reinforcement learning, and physics simulation, addressing the critical challenge of integrated locomotion and manipulation in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a physically-simulated human character capable of autonomously performing complex object rearrangement tasks in cluttered environments. This overarching goal is driven by the motivation to create virtual humans that can interact with their surroundings in meaningful and robust ways, mimicking the effortless combination of locomotion and manipulation skills observed in everyday human behavior.

The authors highlight several specific motivations and key objectives:

1. **Synthesize Loco-Manipulation Skills for Virtual Humans:** The core motivation is to equip simulated characters with the ability to both navigate (locomotion) and interact with objects (manipulation), which is essential for realistic virtual agents and embodied AI. This goes beyond mere walking or grasping to include dynamic tasks like lifting, carrying, and placing objects.
2. **Solve Box Rearrangement Tasks:** The specific task chosen for demonstration is rearranging boxes of varying sizes, weights, shapes, and placement heights in a cluttered environment. This task requires a complex interplay of navigation, precise object interaction, and physical stability.
3. **Propose a Hierarchical Control Architecture:** A key objective is to introduce and validate a hierarchical control framework. This architecture aims to decouple high-level planning from low-level motor control, thereby simplifying the problem and promoting generalization. Each level solves the task at a different level of abstraction.
4. **Integrate Planner, Diffusion Models, and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL):** The system aims to synergistically combine these powerful techniques:
 - A high-level planner for spatial pathfinding.
 - A mid-level diffusion model for generating realistic kinematic locomotion trajectories from abstract path constraints.
 - A low-level DRL policy for robust, physics-based motion imitation and object interaction.
5. **Achieve Generalization to Diverse Scenarios:** The authors aim for the system to generalize beyond specific training data to handle variations in box properties (size, weight, height) and environmental conditions (obstacles, pick-up/place-down locations).
6. **Introduce Diffusion Models with Bidirectional Root Control for Physics-Based Control:** A specific technical objective is to demonstrate the efficacy of diffusion models, enhanced with a novel bidirectional root representation, as a suitable planning method for guiding physics-based controllers. This is a novel application of diffusion models in this context.
7. **Generalize Manipulation from Sparse Data:** The work aims to show that even a single reference motion clip for box lifting and placing can be generalized by an object-aware RL policy to encompass a wide range of box weights and heights, addressing the scarcity of diverse manipulation motion capture data.

In essence, the research seeks to develop a robust, generalized, and physically-plausible system for complex loco-manipulation tasks by employing a novel hierarchical integration of state-of-the-art planning, generative modeling, and reinforcement learning techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in a hierarchical control architecture designed to break down the complex problem of loco-manipulation into manageable sub-problems, each handled by a specialized component. The system comprises a high-level planner, a mid-level motion generator based on diffusion models, and low-level physics-based reinforcement learning (RL) policies.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Control Architecture

The system operates on three levels of abstraction:
1. **High-Level Planner (A\*):** Given a 3D scene and a desired sequence of rearrangement tasks (pick-up, carry, put-down), a kinodynamic A\* planner generates a collision-free path for the character. This planner provides waypoints (root positions and heading directions) for the character's locomotion, considering navigation around obstacles and desired approach orientations for object interaction. The planned paths are smoothed with cubic splines to ensure realistic velocities and accelerations.
2. **Mid-Level Motion Generator (Diffusion Model):** This component takes the waypoints from the high-level planner and synthesizes realistic kinematic whole-body locomotion trajectories.
3. **Low-Level Control Policies (Reinforcement Learning):** These policies receive the kinematic reference motions from the diffusion model and translate them into physics-based motor commands (torques) to control the simulated character, ensuring physical plausibility and interaction with the environment.

#### 3.2. Motion Primitives

The system defines four fundamental motion primitives that can be sequenced to accomplish rearrangement tasks:
- **Walk-only:** Character locomotes without carrying an object.
- **Walk-and-carry:** Character locomotes while carrying one or more objects.
- **Pick-up:** Character bends down to lift an object from a surface.
- **Put-down:** Character bends down to place an object onto a surface.

These primitives are linked in a simple graph, defining allowed transitions (e.g., walk-only -> pick-up -> walk-and-carry).

#### 3.3. Locomotion (Walk-only and Walk-and-carry)

Both locomotion primitives utilize the same diffusion model for kinematic trajectory generation but employ separate low-level RL policies for control.

- **Diffusion-based Whole-body Motion Generator:**
 - **Model Type:** A diffusion model, following the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) framework (Ho et al. 2020), is trained to generate full-body pose sequences from noise, conditioned on waypoint vectors.
 - **Training Data:** Trained on approximately 25 minutes of motion capture data from Starke et al. (2019), focusing on walking motions.
 - **Conditioning:** The model is conditioned by two goal waypoints (target root position and direction).
 - **Kinematic Motion Representation:** Motions are represented as sequences of poses including egocentric root trajectory, goal-centric root trajectory, and predicted joint angles.
 - **Bidirectional Root Control:** To improve precision and eliminate drift, a key innovation is the bidirectional root representation. The model predicts root trajectory relative to both the start pose and the next waypoint. During inference, these two predictions are linearly averaged, with the weighting shifting as the character approaches the waypoint. This is crucial for precise waypoint tracking.
 - **Parallel Long-form Generation:** The model generates short, temporally consistent sequences that can be chained together to form arbitrarily long trajectories, ensuring global consistency.
 - **Architecture:** A transformer encoder with feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) units is used for the diffusion model backbone.

- **Locomotion Control Policy:**
 - **Physics Simulator:** RAISIM (Hwangbo et al. 2018) is used for rigid body dynamics simulation (120 Hz simulation, 60 Hz policy query).
 - **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al. 2017) optimizes the policies.
 - **Policy Architecture:** A layer-wise mixture-of-experts neural network (Xie et al. 2022).
 - **Input:** Character's state (root height, velocities, joint orientations/velocities) and deviation from the next frame in the reference motion (root position/orientation, joint orientations).
 - **Reward Function:** An imitation-based reward (multiplicative) encourages tracking of joint poses, root translation, and root orientation from the diffusion-generated reference. An additional term penalizes high-frequency fluctuations in policy output (smoothness).
 - **Action Space:** A combination of Proportional-Derivative (PD) control and a learned torque correction term.
 - **Walk-and-carry Augmentation:** For carrying behavior, the locomotion dataset is augmented with fixed arm poses for carrying. The policy input also includes box position and orientation. Additional rewards are given for maintaining hand-box contact and keeping the box upright, and for stacking multiple boxes correctly.

#### 3.4. Manipulation (Pick-up and Put-down)

These primitives share a single reference trajectory and a unified low-level policy.

- **Manipulation Motion:** A single, simple reference trajectory consisting of crouching down and standing up, with modified arm poses for pick-up (arms transition to carrying pose) and put-down (arms transition from carrying pose to rest pose).
- **Manipulation Policy:**
 - **Generalization:** Trained to generalize to varying box numbers, sizes, weights, and pick-up/put-down heights from this sparse reference data.
 - **Input:** Includes the platform position for the box and a one-hot vector indicating pick-up or put-down task, in addition to locomotion policy inputs.
 - **Reward Function:** Similar to walk-and-carry, with imitation terms for joint, translation, orientation, and specific rewards for box-hand contact, box orientation, and box stacking. A crucial `r_box-height` reward guides the box to a desired interpolated height during the manipulation period. Unlike locomotion, `r_base` (box-hand and box-orientation) is added rather than multiplied to allow more flexibility in trade-offs.

#### 3.5. Transitions Between Primitives

- **Locomotion to Manipulation:** The character comes to a full stop (tracking 30-60 frames of standing pose) at the end of locomotion to ensure a smooth transition.
- **Manipulation to Locomotion:** A linear interpolation of actions from both manipulation and locomotion policies is used during a 30-60 frame standing pose period to bridge the large differences in their outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors present a comprehensive evaluation of their hierarchical loco-manipulation system, demonstrating its capabilities in diverse scenarios, its generalizability, and robustness through qualitative examples, quantitative metrics, and ablation studies.

1. **Diversity of Rearranging Tasks:**
 - The system successfully generated long-horizon animations of characters rearranging assorted boxes in two different 3D scenes, showcasing its end-to-end pipeline.
 - It demonstrated the ability to manipulate multiple boxes and navigate complex environments (e.g., moving three boxes, stacking them, and navigating around obstacles).
 - Beyond boxes, the system was shown to handle rearrangement of cylindrical objects and solve the classic Tower of Hanoi game, indicating flexibility in object types and task structures.

2. **Generalizability of Manipulation Policy:**
 - The manipulation policy, despite being trained with a single reference trajectory, proved highly robust and generalized to various object properties and interaction contexts.
 - It could successfully pick up and put down boxes varying significantly in position (sagittal: 0.20-0.68m, frontal: -0.30-0.32m, transverse: 0.05-1.00m away from the character's front), height (0.05-1.00m), and width (0.12-0.79m) for a single box.
 - For multiple boxes (two or three), the policy also showed good generalization, albeit with slightly reduced ranges for weight and height, confirming its ability to adapt to stacking tasks.
 - The policy successfully handled varied box weights: up to 8.0 kg for a single box, 3.7 kg per box for two boxes, and 2.5 kg per box for three boxes. This is a significant improvement over typical character animation systems that don't account for object weight.

3. **Robustness of Locomotion:**
 - The combination of the diffusion model and RL locomotion policies demonstrated strong robustness. The RL policy successfully tracked 100% of 10-second random trajectories generated by the independently trained diffusion model, indicating good compatibility between the mid-level planner and low-level controller.
 - The walk-and-carry policy exhibited robustness against external perturbations, with characters maintaining hold of carried boxes even when hit by heavy projectiles (e.g., a single box could withstand a 3.7 kg projectile hitting every 0.5 seconds for 10 seconds).
 - The carrying policy was also robust to variations in carried box width (0.21-0.56m for one box) and weight (up to 5.4 kg for one box), similar to the manipulation policy.

4. **Ablation Study on Bidirectional Root Control:**
 - A critical component, the bidirectional root control in the diffusion model, was quantitatively and qualitatively validated.
 - Compared to an egocentric-only model, the bidirectional model consistently reduced trajectory tracking error by 70% on average.
 - Qualitatively, it prevented visible deviations from the path in challenging turning motions and exhibited more stable behavior, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios, highlighting its importance for precise navigation.

5. **Comparison to DReCon (Motion Matching):**
 - When comparing the low-level controller's tracking performance using reference motions generated by their diffusion model versus motion matching (as used in DReCon), the diffusion model yielded superior tracking rewards for joint, translation, and orientation. This suggests that their diffusion model generates motions more "in-distribution" with the data used to train the RL controller.
 - The authors also highlighted the practical advantage of diffusion models condensing large motion datasets into a neural network, offering better scalability for diverse motions compared to motion matching which requires storing a motion database.

Despite these successes, the authors also acknowledged limitations, such as tracking errors that could lead to collisions in cluttered environments, the need for predefined primitive paths, and less natural transitions between primitives due to the discrete nature of the controllers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Hierarchical Planning and Control for Box Loco-Manipulation" presents a significant advancement in the field of physically-simulated character animation and embodied AI, with considerable potential impact across several domains.

**Significance:**

1. **Enabling Complex Loco-Manipulation for Virtual Humans:** This paper demonstrates a robust and generalized solution for a long-standing challenge: seamlessly integrating locomotion and diverse object manipulation for physics-based characters. This moves beyond simple walking or static grasping to dynamic tasks like fetching, carrying, placing, and stacking objects, which are fundamental for realistic virtual agents in interactive environments.
2. **Novel Application of Diffusion Models for Motion Planning:** The successful integration of diffusion models as a mid-level planner for physics-based control is a key methodological innovation. It shows that these generative models can produce kinematic reference trajectories of sufficient quality and consistency to be directly tracked by RL policies. The introduction of bidirectional root control further enhances the precision of diffusion-generated motions, making them viable for robust control. This opens new avenues for using powerful generative models in control hierarchies.
3. **Generalization and Robustness:** The system's ability to generalize manipulation skills to varying box sizes, weights, and placement heights from a single reference motion is highly significant. This addresses a critical limitation of many motion synthesis techniques that often require vast amounts of specific motion capture data. The demonstrated robustness to external perturbations also highlights the practical utility of the physics-based RL controllers.
4. **Efficient Hierarchical Framework:** The proposed hierarchical architecture effectively decouples different aspects of the problem, leading to more efficient training of individual components compared to end-to-end approaches (e.g., comparisons to ASE indicate faster low-level policy training). This modularity simplifies development and allows for easier integration of future improvements.
5. **Foundation for Human-Environment Interaction:** By enabling characters to physically interact with and modify their environment (e.g., rearranging objects), this work lays a crucial foundation for more intelligent and interactive virtual worlds, character behaviors, and ultimately, more immersive experiences.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Computer Graphics and Animation:** The system can revolutionize character animation workflows by allowing animators to specify high-level tasks rather than painstakingly authoring complex loco-manipulation sequences. This could lead to more dynamic, responsive, and realistic virtual characters in games, films, and simulations.
2. **Robotics and Embodied AI:** While demonstrated in simulation, the principles of hierarchical control, robust physics-based policies, and generalized object interaction are highly relevant to humanoid robotics. The data-driven, model-free nature of the low-level controllers, combined with high-level planning, could inspire new approaches for robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments.
3. **Virtual Reality (VR) and Metaverse:** As virtual environments become more interactive, autonomous agents capable of complex physical interactions will be essential. This work directly contributes to building such agents, enhancing the realism and utility of VR applications and future metaverse platforms.
4. **Training and Simulation:** For training scenarios that require realistic human-like agents interacting with objects (e.g., emergency response simulations, ergonomic studies), this system could provide highly functional and adaptable characters.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations (lack of feedback between levels, discrete transitions, simplified object interaction) provide clear roadmaps for future research, such as developing unified diffusion models for all tasks, incorporating dynamic replanning, and learning richer manipulation skills with tactile feedback or articulated hands.

In conclusion, this paper successfully tackles a complex problem by effectively combining hierarchical planning, state-of-the-art generative models, and reinforcement learning. Its contributions not only advance the current capabilities of physically-simulated characters but also offer a promising blueprint for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and realistic agents for a wide array of applications in computer science and engineering.
