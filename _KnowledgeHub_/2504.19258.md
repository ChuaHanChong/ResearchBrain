# OPAL: Visibility-aware LiDAR-to-OpenStreetMap Place Recognition via Adaptive Radial Fusion

> [!summary] Summary
> OPAL, a novel LiDAR-to-OpenStreetMap (P2O) place recognition framework, achieves real-time, meter-level localization accuracy from a single LiDAR scan by employing a cross-modal visibility mask and an adaptive radial fusion module. It demonstrated up to 24.53% improvement in recall at 5m on KITTI sequence 07 and processed descriptors at over 140 FPS.

## Problem

- Traditional LiDAR place recognition relies on costly, storage-intensive, and difficult-to-maintain 3D dense maps or aerial imagery.
- Significant cross-modal disparity and occlusion patterns exist when matching sparse LiDAR scans to abstract, overhead OpenStreetMap (OSM) data.
- Existing single-frame LiDAR-to-OSM methods suffer from limitations in accuracy, robustness to viewpoint changes, and computational efficiency.

## Method

- A cross-modal deterministic ==visibility mask== is generated to identify mutually observable regions, explicitly addressing occlusion and domain disparity between LiDAR and OSM data.
- A ==Siamese CNN== architecture extracts local features from both LiDAR point clouds and rasterized OSM maps, processing visibility-aware inputs.
- An ==Adaptive Radial Fusion (ARF)== module aggregates local features into a global descriptor, using angular average pooling and attention-based refinement for orientation robustness.

## Results

- OPAL improved recall metrics on KITTI, achieving 17.55% and 24.53% higher R@5m on sequences 00 and 07 respectively, compared to prior state-of-the-art.
- The framework demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to diverse environments on KITTI-360, with gains up to 23.56% at R@5m on sequence 05.
- It achieved real-time performance with a total descriptor generation runtime of 7.05 ms (over 140 FPS), making it approximately 12 times faster than previous methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling visibility differences between LiDAR scans (viewpoint-dependent) and overhead map data (OSM) is crucial for accurate cross-modal feature alignment.
> - Aggregating radial features with an adaptive, attention-based mechanism enhances robustness to orientation changes while preserving geometric structure.
> - Leveraging readily available, lightweight OpenStreetMap data significantly reduces the storage and maintenance overhead compared to dense 3D maps, enabling broader application.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Accurate and robust place recognition is a foundational capability for autonomous navigation and robotics, particularly in complex outdoor environments where Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) signals can be unreliable. LiDAR-based place recognition methods offer robustness to varying lighting and weather conditions compared to vision-based approaches, due to their direct depth measurements and geometric detail.

Historically, LiDAR place recognition has largely relied on pre-built 3D dense maps or aerial imagery as reference databases. Uni-modal approaches, such as PointNetVLAD, MinkLoc3D, and CASSPR, use point clouds for both query and reference. While these methods can achieve high performance, they necessitate the time-consuming and costly creation and maintenance of large-scale 3D point cloud maps, leading to significant storage overhead. Cross-modal approaches, like those matching point clouds to aerial images (e.g., by Tang et al.), offer some reduction in reference map complexity but still face challenges related to acquisition cost, storage, and sensitivity to environmental variations.

OpenStreetMap (OSM) has emerged as an alternative, lightweight, and continuously updated geospatial database. OSM-based approaches integrate map priors with sensor data for localization, with examples including OpenStreetSLAM, methods aligning road or building structures, and those using semantic features for navigation. For single-frame LiDAR-to-OSM (P2O) place recognition, initial efforts introduced hand-crafted descriptors that measure distances to building structures. More recent learning-based methods have been proposed, but often require orientation priors or still exhibit limitations in accuracy, robustness, and efficiency. This paper positions itself within the P2O research area, aiming to address the limitations of existing methods, specifically concerning accuracy, robustness to viewpoint changes, and computational efficiency for single-frame LiDAR scans, by leveraging the lightweight and semantic richness of OSM data.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel LiDAR-to-OpenStreetMap (P2O) place recognition framework, named OPAL, that achieves meter-level localization accuracy using a single LiDAR scan while maintaining real-time computational performance. The authors aim to overcome several limitations inherent in existing place recognition methods:

1. **Addressing Storage and Maintenance Overhead:** Traditional LiDAR place recognition often depends on pre-built 3D dense maps or high-resolution aerial imagery. These resources demand substantial storage capacity and are expensive and time-consuming to construct and keep updated. The paper motivates the use of OpenStreetMap (OSM) as a compact, globally accessible, and frequently updated geospatial database to mitigate these issues. OSM's storage efficiency (e.g., 186 KiB for an OSM tile versus 19.61 MiB for a Bird's-Eye View (BEV) point cloud image) and its continuous update by volunteers are highlighted as key advantages.

2. **Bridging Cross-Modal Domain Disparity:** Matching sparse LiDAR scans to structured OSM data presents a significant challenge due to fundamental differences in their data representations and inherent occlusion patterns. LiDAR data captures real-world geometry with occlusions based on sensor viewpoint, while OSM provides an overhead, often abstract, representation of static urban features. The motivation here is to develop mechanisms that effectively align features and account for visibility differences between these disparate modalities.

3. **Improving Accuracy, Robustness, and Efficiency of P2O:** Existing single-frame P2O methods are stated to be limited in terms of accuracy, robustness (especially to orientation changes), and computational efficiency. Hand-crafted descriptors may depend heavily on specific building structures, limiting generalizability. Learning-based approaches sometimes require accurate orientation priors, adding complexity. The authors are motivated to create a learning-based framework that unifies geometric and topological cues from both modalities to achieve high localization accuracy, maintain robustness across diverse environments and viewpoint changes, and operate in real-time.

To achieve these objectives, the authors introduce two main innovations:
- A **cross-modal visibility mask** designed to identify mutually observable regions from both LiDAR and OSM, thereby guiding feature alignment by accounting for viewpoint disparity and occlusions.
- An **adaptive radial fusion (ARF) module** engineered to dynamically aggregate radial features into a discriminative global descriptor, enhancing robustness to orientation variations and preserving geometric structure efficiently.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OPAL (Visibility-aware LiDAR-to-OpenStreetMap Place Recognition via Adaptive Radial Fusion) is a novel P2O place recognition framework designed for single-frame LiDAR scans. The pipeline involves pre-processing, visibility mask generation, feature extraction, and adaptive radial fusion for global descriptor generation.

#### 3.1. Pre-processing

- **LiDAR Point Cloud (P):** The query LiDAR point cloud (P ∈ R^N×3) is first augmented with per-point semantic labels, creating P' ∈ R^N×4. For semantic labeling, the authors used Cylinder3D, a pre-trained model on KITTI, to predict 19-class labels.
- **OpenStreetMap (OSM) Data (O):** Raw OSM data, composed of areas, ways, and nodes, is converted from its structured format in geographic coordinates into a 3-channel grid map. This rasterization occurs in a local 2D East-North coordinate system with a fixed sampling distance (Δo = 50 cm/pixel). From this projected map, 'm' map tiles (O = {Oi}^m_{i=1}) are densely sampled along the ego-vehicle trajectory to form the OSM database. Each tile Oi corresponds to an H × W meter region (100m x 100m) centered at specific geographic coordinates.

#### 3.2. Visibility Mask Generation

To address the domain disparity and occlusion patterns between LiDAR scans and OSM data, OPAL computes a deterministic visibility mask (M) for both modalities.
- **LiDAR Visibility Mask (MP):** The semantic point cloud P' is projected onto a polar Bird's-Eye View (BEV) grid with U radial rings and V angular sectors. For each sector, cells are classified as visible (MP(u, v) = 1) if their radial distance is less than or equal to the maximum valid range measured in that sector. Cells beyond the last valid return are marked as occluded (MP(u, v) = 0). This process directly leverages LiDAR's range measurements.
- **OSM Visibility Mask (MO):** The OSM tile O, initially in Cartesian coordinates, is transformed into a polar BEV grid. As OSM lacks explicit range, visibility is approximated using semantic cues. "Building" elements from the area channel are designated as primary occluders. Through ray-casting in each sector, cells are classified as occluded (MO(u, v) = 0) if they are further than the nearest "building" element in that sector; otherwise, they are visible (MO(u, v) = 1).
The authors emphasize that this deterministic approach avoids approximation errors and training overhead associated with learned visibility estimation, aiming to preserve geometric consistency and computational efficiency.

#### 3.3. Feature Extraction

OPAL employs a Siamese convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture with parallel, symmetric branches for each modality.
- **Point Cloud Branch:** The augmented point cloud P' is first passed through a lightweight multilayer perceptron (MLP) to generate Cpem-dimensional per-point features. These features are then splatted onto the polar BEV grid using max pooling to form a dense feature map (FP ∈ R^(U×V×Cpem)). This feature map is concatenated with the LiDAR visibility mask (MP) and processed by an encoder based on PolarNet, yielding a local feature map (F'P ∈ R^(Z×T×C)).
- **OSM Branch:** Each channel of the rasterized OSM map tile O is embedded into a Coem-channel feature, creating a dense semantic feature map (FO ∈ R^(H×W×(3×Coem))) in Cartesian coordinates. FO is then transformed into a polar BEV feature map via bilinear sampling and concatenated with the OSM visibility mask (MO). This visibility-aware input is passed through a separate PolarNet encoder (with independent weights from the point cloud branch) to produce its local feature map (F'O ∈ R^(Z×T×C)).

#### 3.4. Adaptive Radial Fusion (ARF) Module

The ARF module aggregates the extracted local features (F ∈ R^(Z×T×C)) from both branches into a compact global descriptor, designed to be representative and robust to orientation variations.
- **Angular Average Pooling (AAP):** Radial features (Fr ∈ R^(Z×C)) are first extracted by performing average pooling along the angular dimension, effectively compressing angular information while preserving radial structure. Position encoding (Ere ∈ R^(Z×C)) is added to encode ring-order information.
- **Attention-based Refinement:** Trainable radial proposals (Q ∈ R^(Z×C)) are introduced. These proposals undergo a two-stage attention refinement:
 1. **Self-Attention:** Inter-proposal communication is enhanced using a self-attention module, allowing proposals to capture global contextual awareness and reduce redundancy (Q' = softmax(QQ^T/√C)Q).
 2. **Cross-Attention:** The refined proposals (Q') then selectively aggregate information from the radial features (Fr) via a cross-attention mechanism (F'r = softmax(Q'F_r^T/√C)F_r). This dynamically weights radial features based on their geometric salience for place recognition.
- **Global Descriptor Generation:** The refined radial features (F'r) are combined with the original Fr via a residual connection, flattened into a one-dimensional vector, and then projected to the final global descriptor (d) using a fully connected layer (d = W(Flatten(Fr + F'r))).
The authors state that this combination of LiDAR's native radial geometry with learned attention, through AAP and adaptive radial proposals, allows for dynamic feature weighting without compromising computational efficiency or geometric completeness.

#### 3.5. Loss Function

The model is optimized using the circle loss function. For each query point cloud, its geometrically matching OSM tile is considered a positive anchor, and all other tiles in the mini-batch are negative samples. The objective maximizes the cosine similarity between the query descriptor and its positive anchor, while minimizing similarity with negative anchors, using dynamic weights and margins.

### 4. Main findings and results

The proposed OPAL framework was evaluated on the KITTI and KITTI-360 datasets, with a zero-shot generalization test on KITTI-360, using Recall@Km as the primary metric.

#### 4.1. Place Recognition Results on KITTI

- **Performance:** OPAL demonstrated higher performance on KITTI sequences 00 and 07 compared to baseline methods. On sequence 00, it achieved improvements of 4.73%, 17.55%, and 17.47% in R@1m/5m/10m metrics. On sequence 07, these improvements were 15.98%, 24.53%, and 24.52% compared to Building_2, which was identified as the previous state-of-the-art.
- **Rotational Robustness:** The method maintained robust performance when query point clouds were subjected to random z-axis rotations (uniform samples from [0, 2π]), indicating its resilience to viewpoint changes.

#### 4.2. Zero-shot Generalization on KITTI-360

- **Generalization Capability:** While OPAL showed slightly reduced performance in a building-dominated urban environment (sequence 00 of KITTI-360) compared to Building_2, it achieved notable improvements over baselines in other sequences (05, 06, 09). For instance, it showed performance gains of 14.55%, 23.56%, and 19.04% at R@5m on sequences 05, 06, and 09, respectively. This suggests strong generalization across diverse environments.
- **Recall Curves:** Recall curves for top-N candidates within a 5-meter threshold on both datasets indicated OPAL consistently outperformed baseline methods across various scenes.

#### 4.3. Runtime Performance

- **Efficiency:** OPAL demonstrated high computational efficiency. Descriptor generation for point clouds took 1.91 ms, and for OSM tiles, 5.14 ms, resulting in a total runtime of 7.05 ms. This translates to a throughput exceeding 140 FPS for descriptor generation, indicating suitability for real-time applications. This is approximately 12 times faster than the Building method (84.73ms) and significantly faster than C2L-PR (535.79ms).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies on KITTI sequence 00 investigated the impact of key components:
- **Visibility Mask and ARF Module:**
 - A simple baseline using global average pooling (GAP) without visibility mask or ARF yielded 3.79% R@1.
 - Introducing ARF alone increased R@1 to 21.03%.
 - Incorporating the visibility mask with various feature aggregation methods (GAP, GeM, NetVLAD, MixVPR, AAP) generally improved performance over the baseline, with AAP achieving 17.68% R@1.
 - The combination of both the visibility mask and ARF module yielded the highest performance (21.82% R@1), indicating their joint contribution to effectiveness.
 - Extended ablation showed that incorporating the visibility mask consistently improved performance across all KITTI and KITTI-360 test sequences.
- **Effect of Point Cloud Semantic Label Accuracy:**
 - Using ground-truth semantic labels resulted in the best performance (25.37% R@1, 74.68% R@10m).
 - Labels predicted by Cylinder3D achieved 21.82% R@1, 66.40% R@10m.
 - Labels from Rangenet++ yielded 18.92% R@1, 60.30% R@10m.
 - This demonstrated a reliance of the framework's accuracy on the precision of semantic labels in the point cloud, with a 14.38% performance gap in R@10m between Rangenet++ and ground-truth annotations.
- **Influence of Dynamic Objects:** OPAL showed comparable performance with and without dynamic objects in the query point cloud, suggesting robustness to such distractions.
- **Influence of Noise in OSM Data:** Introducing random offsets (0m, 0.5m) to OSM data to simulate localization noise demonstrated that the method maintained robustness and comparable performance despite the degraded OSM quality.
- **Descriptor Dimension:** With a 2048-dimensional descriptor, OPAL achieved higher performance (65.78% R@5m on KITTI-00) compared to C2L-PR using the same descriptor dimension (28.52% R@5m).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OPAL framework contributes to the field of autonomous navigation and robotics by offering a novel approach to single-frame LiDAR-to-OpenStreetMap (P2O) place recognition. Its significance lies in addressing several critical challenges of current localization systems:

1. **Reduced Reliance on Costly Maps:** By leveraging OpenStreetMap (OSM) as a lightweight, globally accessible, and continuously updated prior, OPAL mitigates the significant storage overhead and maintenance costs associated with traditional 3D dense maps or aerial imagery. This shift toward a more compact and dynamic reference database could enable wider deployment of autonomous systems in diverse environments.

2. **Enhanced Accuracy and Robustness:** The introduction of a deterministic, cross-modal visibility mask effectively resolves occlusion discrepancies between LiDAR scans and OSM data, improving feature alignment. Concurrently, the Adaptive Radial Fusion (ARF) module dynamically consolidates radial features, preserving geometric structure and enhancing robustness to viewpoint and orientation changes. The experimental results, showing improved recall metrics and resilience to rotation, indicate a step forward in robust localization.

3. **Real-time Performance:** Achieving a descriptor generation throughput exceeding 140 FPS (7.05 ms total runtime) positions OPAL as a viable solution for time-sensitive autonomous applications where real-time localization is critical. This efficiency is a significant improvement over prior methods.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Autonomous Navigation:** OPAL could enable more reliable and globally consistent localization for autonomous vehicles and robots, particularly in urban areas where GNSS signals are often degraded.
- **Cross-Modal Localization:** The framework's ability to effectively bridge the domain gap between sparse LiDAR data and structured OSM data can inform future developments in cross-modal perception and sensor fusion.
- **Map-less Navigation:** While still relying on a map (OSM), the method moves towards a less resource-intensive form of localization, potentially facilitating navigation in environments without proprietary high-definition maps.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that OPAL's localization accuracy is influenced by the quality and distinctiveness of surrounding objects in the point cloud and the precision of semantic labels. Failure cases were observed in ambiguous road crossings with limited distinctive features and due to cross-modal discrepancies where roadside vegetation or buildings in LiDAR scans were missing from OSM. Future work aims to:
1. Extend OPAL to sequential point cloud-based place recognition to leverage temporal and geometric consistency for improved reliability.
2. Incorporate additional modalities such as orientation priors, text, or images to reduce the dependence on point cloud semantic label accuracy.

The public availability of code and data supports reproducibility and further research in this domain.
