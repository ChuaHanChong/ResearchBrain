# Benchmarking Action Spaces in Reinforcement Learning for Vision-based Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This study benchmarks four action spaces in reinforcement learning for vision-based robotic manipulation, revealing that joint velocity actions enable robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving 100% success in object picking and the largest displacement in object pushing, alongside smoother motions on a physical robot. Cartesian action spaces, in contrast, often failed to transfer effectively from simulation to reality.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) for robotic manipulation faces challenges in real-world deployment concerning safety, motion smoothness, and reliable sim-to-real transfer.
- Prior research on action-space design in RL for robotics often overlooked vision-based observations and extensive real-world evaluation.
- Vision-based control introduces complexities like partial observability and perceptual noise, which can significantly alter learning dynamics and real-world transferability.

## Method

- Policies were trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in MuJoCo simulation with vision-based observations for a Franka Emika Panda robot.
- Four common ==action-space representations== (pose increment, pose velocity, joint position increment, and joint velocity) were systematically benchmarked.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities were evaluated on a physical robot for two distinct vision-based manipulation tasks: object picking and object pushing.

## Results

- For the object picking task, joint velocity achieved a **100%** real-world success rate with low jerk, whereas Cartesian pose velocity failed in all real-world trials.
- In the object pushing task, joint velocity yielded the largest median aggregate block displacement (**0.405** m) on the physical robot.
- Simulation performance did not reliably predict real-world success for all action spaces, particularly for Cartesian actions which showed significant performance degradation during sim-to-real transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint velocity actions consistently produced smoother motions (lowest jerk) and demonstrated high robustness for real-world vision-based sim-to-real transfer.
> - Cartesian action spaces (pose increment, pose velocity) suffered from degraded real-world performance due to sensitivities to inverse kinematics (IK) discrepancies and real-world actuator limitations.
> - Position-actuated action spaces, while sometimes faster in simulation, tended to induce larger torque differences and higher jerk, potentially due to direct amplification of policy output fluctuations.
