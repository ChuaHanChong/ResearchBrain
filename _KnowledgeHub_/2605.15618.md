# Latent Video Prediction Learns Better World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Melbourne and Monash University conducted a systematic evaluation of self-supervised video models, revealing that models employing latent video prediction consistently learn more robust and causally-aware "world models" than those relying on pixel-reconstruction or contrastive methods. These latent prediction models exhibited superior performance across various robustness axes, including corruption, occlusion, and temporal understanding, even outperforming task-adapted baselines.

## Problem

- Existing evaluations of self-supervised video models are limited to single top-1 accuracy on clean classification benchmarks, offering restricted insights into real-world robustness.
- There was an absence of systematic comparative evaluation across different self-supervised video pretraining paradigms, particularly concerning their response to various realistic perturbations.
- Current assessment methods fail to comprehensively characterize critical "world model" properties such as stability under sensor noise, resilience to environmental changes, tolerance to missing input, and an intrinsic understanding of temporal order.

## Method

- A controlled study compared four matched-capacity video foundation models (**V-JEPA 2.1**, **V-JEPA 2**, **VideoPrism**, **VideoMAEv2**), representing distinct self-supervised learning paradigms, all utilizing a ==ViT-Large== backbone architecture.
- Models were systematically evaluated across five complementary ==robustness axes==: feature discriminability, corruption robustness, fine-grained action discrimination, occlusion robustness, and ==temporal robustness==, primarily using the Something-Something v2 dataset.
- The impact of the pretraining objective was isolated by using ==frozen encoders== with a lightweight ==attentive probe==, and comparisons were made against task-adapted baselines in separate experiments to assess inherent advantages.

## Results

- Latent-prediction models (V-JEPA variants) consistently demonstrated superior robustness across corruption, occlusion, fine-grained action discrimination (e.g., distinguishing "pretending" actions), and temporal understanding axes.
- V-JEPA models achieved a **Directional Semantic Coherence Score (DSCS)** several times higher than VideoMAEv2 and VideoPrism under video reversal, indicating a stronger internalization of temporal causality.
- Frozen V-JEPA 2 backbones maintained their advantages, outperforming fine-tuned VideoMAE and TimeSformer in corruption and occlusion robustness, and remaining competitive in fine-grained pretend action recognition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting in a learned latent space more effectively captures the causal structure and dynamics of the physical world, leading to more robust "world models," rather than objectives focused on raw pixel reconstruction.
> - The robustness advantages conferred by latent prediction objectives are inherent to the learned representations and can persist, or even exceed, those achieved by models optimized through task-specific fine-tuning or full supervision.
> - A critical dissociation exists between high representational stability (e.g., high cosine similarity under perturbation) and actual utility for downstream classification tasks, indicating that geometrically stable features are not always functionally useful.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of self-supervised video models, which are increasingly being conceptualized as "world models" capable of learning physical world dynamics from observation. A central architectural paradigm in this shift is the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which postulates that prediction in a learned latent space, rather than raw pixel reconstruction, may more effectively capture the causal structure of the world.

Existing evaluations of these self-supervised video models are primarily limited to single top-1 accuracy scores on clean classification benchmarks. This approach offers restricted insights into the robustness and capabilities that a deployed world model would require, such as stability under sensor noise, resilience to environmental changes, tolerance to missing input, discrimination of fine-grained actions, and an intrinsic understanding of temporal order. While prior work in self-supervised learning for images and video has explored properties like robustness to occlusion and emergent segmentation capabilities, a systematic comparative evaluation across different self-supervised video pretraining paradigms, particularly concerning their response to various realistic perturbations, has been largely absent. Specifically, earlier studies by Garrido et al. indicated that V-JEPA could acquire intuitive physics understanding through representation-space masked prediction, and Joseph et al. found qualitative differences in physics representations between V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAEv2. However, a comprehensive multi-axis assessment to characterize the structural signatures imparted by different pretraining objectives was missing. This paper aims to address this gap by systematically evaluating various robustness axes relevant to the deployment of video world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic and controlled study to analyze the properties of frontier self-supervised video foundation models, moving beyond traditional single-metric, clean-benchmark evaluations. The authors aim to comprehend these models' potential as "world models" by assessing their robustness across several dimensions crucial for real-world deployment.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations of current evaluation practices:
- **Incomplete Assessment of World Model Capabilities:** Evaluating models solely on top-1 accuracy on clean benchmarks does not provide insights into critical properties required for a world model, such as its stability when confronted with sensor noise, environmental shifts, or missing data. It also fails to assess their ability to perform fine-grained discrimination or to grasp temporal order.
- **Lack of Pretraining Objective Comparison:** There is an absence of comparative evaluations at the level of pretraining objectives, which are hypothesized to shape the fundamental structural properties of the learned representations. Understanding these differences is crucial for developing more robust and capable models.
- **Need for Robustness Characterization:** To function effectively as world models, these systems must remain reliable under conditions that deviate from pristine training environments. This necessitates a detailed characterization of how different pretraining paradigms degrade under various real-world-relevant perturbations.

To achieve this, the study aims to:
- Systematically evaluate four matched-capacity video foundation models (V-JEPA 2.1, V-JEPA 2, VideoPrism, and VideoMAEv2) that represent distinct self-supervised learning paradigms.
- Assess these models across five specific robustness axes: feature discriminability, corruption robustness, fine-grained discrimination (e.g., distinguishing "pretend" actions), occlusion robustness, and sensitivity to temporal direction.
- Isolate the impact of the pretraining objective by controlling for factors such as backbone capacity, evaluation data, and classifier probe protocols.
- Determine if the robustness advantages observed in frozen representations of latent-prediction models persist when compared against models optimized through task-specific fine-tuning or full supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a controlled study design to compare four distinct self-supervised video models, aiming to isolate the effect of their pretraining objectives.

**Model Selection and Configuration:**
The study focused on four publicly available video foundation models, all utilizing a ViT-Large backbone architecture with approximately 300 million parameters to maintain matched capacity. These models represent three dominant self-supervised video paradigms:
- **Latent Prediction:** V-JEPA 2.1 and V-JEPA 2 (Meta)
- **Contrastive + Masked Prediction:** VideoPrism (Google)
- **Masked Prediction (Pixel Reconstruction):** VideoMAEv2 (Open-source)

For most evaluations, these encoders were kept frozen, and their features were read out using a lightweight attentive probe. To prevent probe mismatch artifacts, approximately 100 A100 GPU-hours were invested per encoder to tune the best-performing probe configuration based on clean validation accuracy on Something-Something v2. While pretraining data composition, scale, and schedule were not fully controlled (as models were used as publicly released), the consistent behavioral clustering observed across paradigms suggested the pretraining objective was a primary differentiator.

**Comparison with Task-Adapted Baselines:**
In a separate set of experiments (Section 9), the frozen V-JEPA 2 backbone with its attentive probe was compared against two task-optimized baselines fine-tuned on Something-Something v2:
- **VideoMAE v1:** An end-to-end fine-tuned ViT-B/16 backbone.
- **TimeSformer:** A fully supervised model (trained from ImageNet-21k initialization) with a larger input frame count.
This comparison aimed to assess if the advantages of V-JEPA’s frozen features held against models specifically optimized for the downstream task.

**Dataset:**
All evaluations were conducted using the Something-Something v2 (SSv2) dataset. This dataset comprises over 220,000 videos across 174 fine-grained human-object interaction categories. Its characteristics, including visually similar but temporally distinct action pairs and antonym structures (e.g., "pushing something left" vs. "pushing something right"), made it suitable for probing fine-grained and temporal discrimination capabilities. The extensive evaluation across all experiments required over 1,000 A100 GPU-hours.

**Evaluation Axes and Metrics:**
The models were evaluated across five complementary axes:

1. **Feature Discriminability:**
 - **Data:** 600 videos from 30 SSv2 classes, stratified into three semantic difficulty tiers (different-verb, same-verb, pretend-vs-real).
 - **Metrics:** Linear probe accuracy, kNN accuracy (k=5), and Mean Pairwise Fisher Ratio on global average pooled (GAP) features.

2. **Corruption Robustness:**
 - **Data:** 500 class-balanced videos subjected to six ImageNet-C corruptions (motion blur, snow, pixelation, impulse noise, brightness, elastic transform) at three severity levels.
 - **Metrics:** Top-1 classification accuracy, mean cosine similarity between clean and corrupted features, accuracy retention relative to clean baseline, and degradation slopes.

3. **Fine-Grained Action Discrimination (Pretend vs. Real):**
 - **Data:** 1,992 test videos from 22 "pretending" action classes, where distinguishing real from simulated interactions relies on subtle spatiotemporal contact cues. Classes were categorized by object size and detail sensitivity (high/low).
 - **Metrics:** Top-1 accuracy.

4. **Occlusion Robustness:**
 - **Data:** 1,740-video balanced set across 174 classes, subjected to three spatiotemporal occlusion paradigms at three severity levels.
 - **Paradigms:** Moving Block (spatial occlusion), Temporal Dropout (contiguous frame freezing/replacement), and Spatiotemporal Patch Dropout (random 3D cuboid zeroing).
 - **Metrics:** Top-1 classification accuracy, Representational Similarity Index (RSI - cosine similarity between clean and occluded embeddings), and Classification Consistency Rate (CCR - consistency of kNN predictions).

5. **Temporal Robustness:**
 - **Data:** 1,740-video balanced set across 174 classes, subjected to four temporal disruption paradigms.
 - **Paradigms:** Frame Permutation (random, segment, interleaved), Static Video (single frame repeated), Pure Noise (Gaussian, uniform, static Gaussian), and Video Reversal.
 - **Metrics:** Top-1 accuracy, cosine similarity, Semantic Flip Rate (r_sem - fraction of antonymous prediction changes under reversal), and Directional Semantic Coherence Score (DSCS = r_sem * (1 - cos_rev)), Temporal Dependency Index (TDI).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study identified a distinct and consistent profile for latent-prediction models (V-JEPA variants) across all robustness axes.

- **Feature Discriminability:** V-JEPA models generated more discriminable representations, exhibiting higher linear probe accuracy than VideoMAEv2 and VideoPrism. Their separability profile remained consistent across varying semantic difficulties (different-verb, same-verb, pretend-vs-real actions), unlike VideoMAEv2, which showed a sharp drop on same-verb pairs. The larger gap between linear probe and kNN accuracy for V-JEPA suggested their representations encode exploitable, direction-dependent semantic structure.

- **Corruption Robustness:** Latent-prediction models demonstrated higher robustness to pixel corruption. V-JEPA 2.1 achieved the highest classification accuracy and exhibited the most gradual degradation across severity levels for five of six corruption types. This indicated that the joint-embedding predictive objective prioritizes higher-order temporal and semantic structure over surface-level visual variations. A notable dissociation was observed where VideoPrism maintained high cosine similarity between clean and corrupted features but suffered substantial accuracy loss, suggesting representational stability does not guarantee useful features for classification. V-JEPA models, conversely, showed lower cosine similarity but higher classification accuracy.

- **Fine-Grained Action Discrimination:** Despite not reconstructing pixels, V-JEPA variants consistently outperformed the pixel-reconstruction baseline (VideoMAEv2) and VideoPrism on "pretending" actions. This advantage was more pronounced for "high sensitivity" classes where discrimination relied on subtle absence-of-contact cues. This finding suggests that the latent predictive objective effectively distills spatiotemporal signatures crucial for differentiating real from simulated physical contact, contradicting the intuition that pixel-level reconstruction would be superior for fine details. Some classes, such as "Pretending or trying and failing to twist something," remained universally challenging.

- **Occlusion Robustness:** Latent-prediction models generalized more effectively to missing input. Both V-JEPA variants yielded more stable features and maintained higher classification accuracy under all three occlusion paradigms (Moving Block, Temporal Dropout, Spatiotemporal Patch Dropout). V-JEPA 2.1 was the most robust, showing the most graceful degradation. Similar to corruption robustness, VideoPrism maintained high representational similarity under severe spatiotemporal patch dropout but experienced a collapse in classification accuracy, reinforcing the observation that geometric stability does not equate to decisional utility.

- **Temporal Robustness:** Predictive models, particularly V-JEPA variants, demonstrated a stronger internalization of the "arrow of time." Under video reversal, V-JEPA models coherently flipped predictions to semantically antonymous classes (e.g., "pushing" to "pulling"), achieving a Directional Semantic Coherence Score (DSCS) several times higher than VideoMAEv2 and VideoPrism. This suggests that V-JEPA's pretraining objective, which involves predicting future latent patches, instills a directional understanding of temporal causality into the latent space. VideoPrism detected changes but lacked the temporal axis for interpretation.

- **Comparison with Task-Adapted Baselines:** The advantages of V-JEPA’s frozen representations were largely maintained when compared against fully fine-tuned VideoMAE and TimeSformer. Frozen V-JEPA 2 surpassed these fine-tuned baselines in corruption and occlusion robustness, and remained competitive in fine-grained pretend action recognition. This indicates that the pretraining objective confers inherent robustness properties that can survive or even exceed task-specific optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides empirical evidence supporting the efficacy of latent prediction as a pretraining paradigm for self-supervised video models, particularly in the context of developing robust "world models."

The key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Re-evaluation of Model Assessment:** The study highlights the limitations of relying on single top-1 accuracy scores for evaluating video foundation models. It demonstrates that a multi-axis robustness evaluation is essential to uncover structural differences imparted by pretraining objectives that are critical for real-world deployment, extending beyond curated test sets. This could encourage the adoption of more comprehensive benchmarking practices in the field.
- **Support for Latent Prediction in World Modeling:** The consistent superior performance of latent-prediction models across various robustness axes (corruption, occlusion, fine-grained discrimination, temporal understanding) suggests that this objective inherently promotes the learning of more abstract, causal, and semantically rich representations. This provides strong evidence for the hypothesis that predicting in latent space is more effective for capturing the dynamics and causal structure of the physical world.
- **Challenging Assumptions about Pixel Reconstruction:** The findings challenge the intuitive assumption that pixel-reconstruction objectives are superior for learning fine-grained visual details. Latent-prediction models were shown to capture subtle contact cues more effectively, implying that pixel-level reconstruction might allocate capacity to superficial details, thereby diluting crucial semantic signals.
- **Disentangling Representational Stability and Utility:** The observed dissociation between high representational stability (e.g., high cosine similarity under perturbation) and low task performance (classification accuracy) underscores that simply maintaining feature similarity under various conditions does not equate to retaining useful information for decision-making. This finding emphasizes the need for evaluating models based on downstream task performance rather than solely on representational invariance.
- **Implications for Model Design:** The demonstration that frozen latent-prediction backbones can outperform or compete with task-adapted, fine-tuned, and fully supervised models in robustness suggests that the pretraining objective is a more significant determinant of robust world modeling capabilities than mere scale or direct label supervision. This implies that future research and development should prioritize pretraining objectives that encourage robust, generalizable feature learning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a groundwork for further investigations into world models, particularly by motivating exploration into other robustness dimensions such as adversarial robustness and intentional model manipulation. The insights gained from how latent prediction confers robustness could inform the development of AI systems that are more reliable and dependable in complex, dynamic environments.
