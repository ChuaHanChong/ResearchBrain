# EgoPush: Learning End-to-End Egocentric Multi-Object Rearrangement for Mobile Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> New York University researchers developed EgoPush, an end-to-end framework that enables mobile robots to perform complex multi-object rearrangement tasks using only egocentric visual observations. This method, which employs a constrained teacher-student distillation approach, achieved an 80% success rate in real-world deployment with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Traditional mobile manipulation often relies on brittle global state estimation or explicit mapping, which fails in dynamic, cluttered, and texture-sparse environments.
- End-to-end visual reinforcement learning (RL) from egocentric pixels suffers from poor sample efficiency and brittleness under partial observability and long task horizons.
- Cross-modal distillation for visual RL often creates an 'observability gap' when the privileged teacher exploits information not available to the egocentric student, leading to inconsistent supervision.

## Method

- A two-phase learning framework combines online ==reinforcement learning== for a privileged teacher with supervised learning (distillation) for an egocentric visual student.
- An object-centric latent representation encodes relative spatial relations between the robot and task-relevant objects (active, anchor, obstacles) using sparse keypoints for the teacher and masked depth layers for the student.
- The teacher's observations are constrained by a virtual egocentric field of view masking and 'Center-Gated Visibility' for target references, ensuring learnable active perception behaviors are distilled into the student.
- Long-horizon credit assignment is addressed through stage-aligned, temporally decayed completion rewards, and a novel relational distillation loss transfers the teacher's spatial reasoning to the student.

## Results

- EgoPush significantly outperformed classical mapping-based and end-to-end RL baselines in simulation, achieving **100%** success in a two-object pushing task where baselines largely failed (e.g., SIM at **19.26%** SR, E2E-RGBD at <**1%** SR).
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of constrained teacher observations (full EgoPush teacher yielded **70.70%** student SR vs. **0%** for a global teacher) and the efficacy of the structured reward system for long-horizon tasks.
- The framework demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a TurtleBot3 robot, achieving an **80%** success rate in rearranging five boxes into a cross-shape formation using only an egocentric RGB-D camera.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining the privileged teacher's observations to what is visually recoverable by the egocentric student is critical for effective distillation and learning active perception behaviors.
> - Decomposing long-horizon tasks into stages with temporally decaying, stage-local completion rewards significantly improves teacher convergence speed and credit assignment.
> - Learning object-centric latent representations of relative spatial relations provides robustness against ego-motion and partial observability, moving beyond brittle global state estimation.
