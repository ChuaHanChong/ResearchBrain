# EgoPush: Learning End-to-End Egocentric Multi-Object Rearrangement for Mobile Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> New York University researchers developed EgoPush, an end-to-end framework that enables mobile robots to perform complex multi-object rearrangement tasks using only egocentric visual observations. This method, which employs a constrained teacher-student distillation approach, achieved an 80% success rate in real-world deployment with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Traditional mobile manipulation often relies on brittle global state estimation or explicit mapping, which fails in dynamic, cluttered, and texture-sparse environments.
- End-to-end visual reinforcement learning (RL) from egocentric pixels suffers from poor sample efficiency and brittleness under partial observability and long task horizons.
- Cross-modal distillation for visual RL often creates an 'observability gap' when the privileged teacher exploits information not available to the egocentric student, leading to inconsistent supervision.

## Method

- A two-phase learning framework combines online ==reinforcement learning== for a privileged teacher, whose observations are constrained by a virtual egocentric field of view masking and 'Center-Gated Visibility', with supervised distillation into an egocentric visual student to ensure learnable active perception behaviors.
- An object-centric latent representation encodes relative spatial relations between the robot and task-relevant objects (active, anchor, obstacles) using sparse keypoints for the teacher and masked depth layers for the student.
- Long-horizon credit assignment is addressed through stage-aligned, temporally decayed completion rewards, and a novel relational distillation loss transfers the teacher's spatial reasoning to the student.

## Results

- EgoPush significantly outperformed classical mapping-based and end-to-end RL baselines in simulation, achieving **100%** success in a two-object pushing task where baselines largely failed (e.g., SIM at **19.26%** SR, E2E-RGBD at <**1%** SR).
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of constrained teacher observations (full EgoPush teacher yielded **70.70%** student SR vs. **0%** for a global teacher) and the efficacy of the structured reward system for long-horizon tasks.
- The framework demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a TurtleBot3 robot, achieving an **80%** success rate in rearranging five boxes into a cross-shape formation using only an egocentric RGB-D camera.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining the privileged teacher's observations to what is visually recoverable by the egocentric student is critical for effective distillation and learning active perception behaviors.
> - Decomposing long-horizon tasks into stages with temporally decaying, stage-local completion rewards significantly improves teacher convergence speed and credit assignment.
> - Learning object-centric latent representations of relative spatial relations provides robustness against ego-motion and partial observability, moving beyond brittle global state estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and challenging problem in robotics: enabling mobile robots to perform complex, long-horizon multi-object rearrangement tasks using only egocentric (first-person) visual observations. This area lies at the intersection of mobile robotics, non-prehensile manipulation, reinforcement learning (RL), and computer vision.

**Current Limitations in the Field:**
The broader research landscape for mobile manipulation traditionally relies on either explicit global state estimation or end-to-end learning methods, both of which face significant limitations in dynamic, cluttered, and texture-sparse environments:
- **Reliance on Global State:** Many non-prehensile manipulation systems and model-based approaches (e.g., those using SLAM or visual odometry for mapping and planning) assume access to accurate global robot and object states, or at least a static world for consistent localization. In real-world scenarios, especially during contact-rich interactions where objects are continuously displaced and frequently occlude one another, these assumptions often break down. Global state estimation becomes brittle, leading to inconsistent reference frames and degraded planning performance.
- **Brittleness of End-to-End RL:** Direct end-to-end reinforcement learning from egocentric pixels (visual RL) offers an alternative by avoiding explicit mapping. However, it typically suffers from poor sample efficiency and severe brittleness under partial observability. When task-relevant visual cues repeatedly disappear from view due to occlusions (a common occurrence in egocentric mobile manipulation), purely reactive pixel-based policies struggle to maintain coherent strategies and recover. This is further exacerbated by the long-horizon nature of rearrangement tasks, leading to sparse and delayed rewards, making credit assignment difficult.
- **Observability Gap in Teacher-Student Distillation:** A common technique to improve sample efficiency in visual RL is cross-modal distillation, where a privileged teacher policy (with access to low-dimensional ground truth states) is trained and its behavior is then distilled into a visual student. However, conventional distillation pipelines often use an "omniscient" teacher that exploits information (e.g., off-screen objects, global ground truth) that is not available to a partially observed, egocentric student. This creates an "observability gap," leading to inconsistent supervision and brittle student policies that cannot ground their actions in their limited visual evidence.

**EgoPush's Contribution to the Landscape:**
EgoPush directly confronts these limitations by introducing a novel policy learning framework designed specifically for egocentric, perception-driven rearrangement without relying on explicit global state estimation. Its key contributions address the identified bottlenecks:
- It moves away from explicit global localization by focusing on **object-centric latent representations** that encode *relative spatial relations* among objects, which are more robust to dynamic changes and partial observability.
- It tackles the observability gap in distillation through **constrained teacher observations**, which forces the privileged teacher to behave in ways recoverable from an egocentric viewpoint, thereby inducing *learnable active perception* behaviors in the student.
- It resolves the challenge of **long-horizon credit assignment** by decomposing complex tasks into stage-level subproblems and using temporally decayed, stage-local completion rewards, providing denser and more effective learning signals.

By demonstrating robust performance in simulation and successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, EgoPush pushes the frontier of making mobile robots more capable in unstructured, dynamic, and visually challenging environments, moving beyond the constraints of traditional methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EgoPush stems from observing human capabilities in object rearrangement. Humans can intuitively reconfigure objects in cluttered spaces, navigating occlusions and maintaining a sense of relative spatial relationships without needing precise global coordinates. This ability, where perception and action are tightly coupled and adapt to intermittent occlusions, inspired the authors to equip mobile robots with similar capabilities.

The core problem EgoPush aims to solve is **long-horizon multi-object non-prehensile rearrangement for mobile robots using only a single egocentric camera**. This task involves a mobile robot physically nudging multiple objects into predefined arrangements relative to an anchor object within a texture-sparse environment, where no external landmarks or global visual references exist beyond the objects themselves.

The key objectives and specific challenges addressed by EgoPush are:

- **Overcoming the Need for Global State Estimation:** The main objective is to enable perception-driven rearrangement purely from an egocentric perspective, eliminating the reliance on explicit global state estimation or external tracking, which often fails in dynamic scenes with continuous object movement and occlusions.
- **Learning Object-Centric Relative Spatial Relations:** To achieve the above, the framework aims to learn an object-centric latent space that encodes the relative spatial relationships between the robot and task-relevant objects (active, anchor, obstacles), rather than absolute poses.
- **Bridging the Observability Gap in Distillation:** A critical objective is to make teacher-student distillation viable under partial observability. This involves designing a teacher training regimen that restricts the privileged teacher's observations to what is visually accessible from the student's egocentric viewpoint, ensuring that the teacher's learned behaviors are reproducible by the student and inducing active perception.
- **Improving Long-Horizon Credit Assignment:** For complex, sequential rearrangement tasks, the goal is to address the challenge of delayed and sparse rewards by decomposing the task into stage-level subproblems and employing a structured reward function, specifically using temporally decayed, stage-local completion rewards.
- **Achieving Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A practical objective is to demonstrate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of the learned policies onto a real mobile robot platform, validating the robustness and applicability of the framework in the physical world.
- **Handling Diverse Object Geometries:** The framework aims to generalize to objects with varied geometries (cubes, prisms, cylinders), showcasing versatility beyond simple shapes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoPush is a two-phase learning framework combining reinforcement learning (RL) for a privileged teacher with supervised learning (distillation) for an egocentric visual student.

**A. Object-Centric Latent Representation:**
Central to EgoPush is its object-centric latent space. Objects in the scene are semantically categorized into "active object" (currently manipulated), "anchor" (target reference), and "obstacles." Each category is encoded using a shared-weight state estimator (PointNet for teacher, CNN for student) into group-wise latent embeddings. This design prioritizes relative spatial relations over absolute poses, enabling robustness to ego-motion and partial observability.

**B. Phase 1 (Teacher): Online Reinforcement Learning:**
1. **Action Space:** The robot, a differential-drive base, outputs continuous linear and angular velocities.
2. **Privileged Information (Sparse Keypoints):** The teacher policy receives sparse 3D keypoints representing the geometry and relative poses of the active object, anchor, and obstacles. A set of target reference keypoints ($P^{ref}_t$) defining the goal configuration relative to the anchor is also provided. PointNet, being permutation-invariant, processes these variable-sized point sets.
3. **Constrained Observations (Key Innovation):** To ensure the teacher's behaviors are learnable by the student, two constraints are imposed:
 - **Virtual Egocentric FOV Masking:** Keypoints outside a simulated camera frustum or beyond a maximum range are masked, approximating the student's limited field of view.
 - **Center-Gated Visibility (C-GV) for $P^{ref}_t$:** The reference keypoints are only visible if the anchor object is both within the virtual FOV *and* lies within a central gated region of the robot's view. This forces the teacher to actively orient the robot to bring the anchor into focus, a behavior reproducible by the student.
4. **Rewards (Key Innovation):** To tackle long-horizon credit assignment, a structured reward system is used:
 - **Stage-aligned Completion Reward:** Long-horizon tasks are decomposed into stages (e.g., "reach active object," "place active object near anchor"). A completion reward is given for each stage, weighted by a temporally decaying factor ($\eta_t = (T_s - \tau_t) / (T_s + \epsilon_0)$ where $T_s$ is stage budget and $\tau_t$ is elapsed steps). This encourages timely completion within each stage.
 - **Progress Shaping:** Dense rewards are given for reducing distance (robot-to-active object in "reach" phase, active object-to-reference in "place" phase).
 - **Smoothness and Slowdown Near Target:** Penalties for sudden action changes and rewards for low object speed near the target encourage stable, precise manipulation.
5. **Termination:** Episodes terminate upon task success, robot moving out-of-bounds, collision with obstacles, or reaching a stage-specific time limit.
6. **Training:** The teacher policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with domain randomization on physical parameters to enhance robustness.

**C. Phase 2 (Student): Supervised Learning (Distillation):**
1. **Observation:** The student uses an egocentric RGB-D camera. RGB images are processed for instance-level segmentation to assign visible objects to semantic groups (active, anchor, obstacle). The policy then takes depth-only inputs, where depth pixels are aggregated into three fixed-dimensional depth layers based on their semantic groups.
2. **Image Processing for Real-World:** For real-world deployment, HSV-threshold segmentation is used for color-coded objects. Depth images are handled with simulated noise during training and Navier-Stokes inpainting for denoising in the real world to bridge the sim-to-real gap. A physical pusher is added to the robot to mitigate the depth camera's sensing dead zone.
3. **EgoPush-Student Architecture:** A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encodes the masked depth inputs for each semantic group into latent embeddings. The policy head (MLP) is identical to the teacher's, taking these student-generated latents and the previous action to output robot velocities.
4. **Distillation Process:** An online DAgger-style distillation procedure is used, where the teacher provides action labels for states visited by the student, allowing for continuous, interactive learning.
5. **Relational Distillation Loss (Key Innovation):** Beyond action cloning, a novel relational distillation loss ($L_{rel}$) is introduced. It minimizes the Frobenius norm difference between the pairwise cosine similarity matrices of the teacher's and student's latent embeddings for shared semantic groups (active, anchor, obstacle). This loss implicitly teaches the student the teacher's understanding of relative spatial configurations, crucial for tasks where explicit goal indicators (like $P^{ref}_t$) are not available to the student.
6. **Initialization and Randomization:** The student's MLP policy network is warm-started with the teacher's learned weights. Domain randomization on camera-pose-related observations is applied during distillation to further improve sim-to-real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EgoPush demonstrates superior performance in multi-object rearrangement, validated through extensive simulation experiments and successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Simulation Performance:**
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** EgoPush significantly outperforms both classical mapping-based (Spatial Intention Maps - SIM) and various end-to-end (E2E) deep reinforcement learning baselines (E2E-RGB, E2E-RGBD, RGBD-Sem, E2E-Curriculum, RGBD-RNN). In a simplified two-object pushing task, EgoPush achieved 100% success rate, whereas E2E baselines consistently failed (SR < 1%), and SIM achieved only 19.26% success, mainly due to odometry drift and inability to handle dynamic scenes. This highlights the brittleness of existing methods in long-horizon, contact-rich egocentric tasks.
- **Ablation Studies Validating Design Choices:**
 - **Restricted Observations for RL Teacher:** This was a critical component. The full EgoPush teacher (with FOV masking and Center-Gated Visibility) enabled a student success rate of 70.70%. In contrast, removing Center-Gated Visibility (w/o C-GV) dropped student success to 21.09%, and removing FOV masking (global teacher) resulted in 0% student success. This stark difference, despite similar high teacher success rates across variants, empirically confirms that constraining the teacher to visually recoverable behaviors is essential for effective distillation and learning active perception.
 - **Credit Assignment Ablation:** The progressive addition of reward shaping components demonstrated their effectiveness for long-horizon tasks. Starting from a sparse episode-terminal reward baseline (16.02% SR), adding stage-wise rewards (87.50% SR), then temporal decay (97.95% SR), and finally the stage timer (98.63% SR) significantly improved teacher convergence speed and success rates. This validates the importance of stage-aligned, temporally decayed rewards for long-horizon credit assignment.
 - **Relational Distillation Ablation:** While less critical for simpler, symmetric tasks (e.g., cross-shape), relational distillation proved crucial for more challenging tasks like line-shape formation. Without this loss, the student exhibited a larger action loss during training and completely failed to achieve the desired performance, underscoring its role in bridging the representation gap and inheriting the teacher's spatial reasoning.
- **Generalization to Diverse Geometries:** The student policy demonstrated a high "reach" rate (approaching 100%) for both cylindrical and triangular prism objects, indicating good transfer of the approach sub-skill. However, the overall success rate for placement decreased to 67.48% for cylinders and 54.30% for prisms, suggesting that maintaining stable contact and precise alignment during the interaction phase remains challenging for non-cuboid geometries due to more complex contact dynamics.

**B. Real-World Experiments (Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer):**
- **Successful Deployment:** EgoPush demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a TurtleBot3 Burger in a 3m × 3m arena. The robot, using only an Intel RealSense D435i camera, successfully rearranged five boxes into a cross-shape formation.
- **High Real-World Success Rate:** The real robot achieved an 80% success rate under a loose evaluation metric (allowing minor orientation/centering deviations) within 2 minutes per task. This impressive transfer capability is attributed to domain randomization, careful depth noise modeling, and robust image processing techniques (Navier-Stokes inpainting).
- **Qualitative Behavior Consistency:** Policy behavior in the real world was qualitatively similar to simulation, though action scale restrictions due to servo torque limits slightly impacted robustness and efficiency compared to optimized simulation performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EgoPush framework presents a significant advancement in mobile robotics, particularly in the domain of autonomous manipulation under challenging real-world conditions. Its contributions have several important implications:

**A. Addressing Foundational Robotic Challenges:**
- **Robustness to Partial Observability and Dynamics:** EgoPush directly tackles the long-standing challenge of performing complex tasks in partially observable, dynamic, and texture-sparse environments. By sidestepping the need for brittle global state estimation and focusing on relative, object-centric spatial reasoning from egocentric views, it offers a more robust paradigm for robot autonomy. This is crucial for real-world deployments where perfect localization and static environments are rare.
- **Advancing Egocentric Perception-Action Coupling:** The framework demonstrates how to effectively learn tight coupling between egocentric perception and action. The constrained teacher design and relational distillation loss are key innovations that enable the student policy to learn active perception behaviors that are inherently grounded in its visual input, leading to more intelligent and adaptable robotic control.
- **Improving Long-Horizon Task Learning:** The stage-aligned and temporally decayed reward structure provides a powerful solution for credit assignment in long-horizon tasks, a notoriously difficult problem in reinforcement learning. This approach could be generalized to other complex, multi-stage robotic operations.

**B. Methodological Contributions:**
- **Object-Centric Latent Representations:** The successful use of object-centric latent representations for relative spatial relations offers a promising direction for creating more generalizable and robust perception systems for dynamic scenes, moving beyond pixel-level processing or brittle pose estimation.
- **Constrained Teacher RL:** This paradigm significantly improves the efficiency and stability of teacher-student distillation for tasks with perceptual limitations, making it a viable strategy for bridging the sim-to-real gap and creating learnable active perception behaviors.
- **Relational Distillation Loss:** The introduction of this loss provides a powerful mechanism to transfer abstract spatial reasoning and relationships from a privileged teacher to a visual student, even when their observation spaces are structurally different.

**C. Practical Impact and Future Directions:**
- **Enhanced Mobile Manipulation Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capability underscores the practical applicability of EgoPush. This work paves the way for mobile robots to perform more diverse and complex non-prehensile manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, such as factories, warehouses, or even homes, where rearranging objects is a common requirement.
- **Foundation for More Autonomous Robots:** By enabling robots to operate effectively from egocentric observations, EgoPush contributes to the development of truly autonomous agents that can adapt to unforeseen changes and cluttered scenes without human intervention or extensive pre-mapping.
- **Future Research Avenues:** The identified limitation regarding the student's reactive nature and lack of explicit spatial memory for occluded objects opens clear avenues for future research. Integrating recurrent sequence models (e.g., GRU/LSTM) with the object-centric latent space could enable the robot to maintain a persistent belief over its environment, thereby overcoming oscillatory behaviors and deadlocks in highly cluttered or occluded scenarios. This would further enhance robustness and allow for more sophisticated long-term planning.

In summary, EgoPush offers a compelling solution to a challenging problem in robotics, demonstrating a robust framework for learning complex manipulation skills from egocentric vision. Its innovative methodological contributions and proven real-world transfer capability mark a significant step towards more intelligent and versatile mobile robotic systems.
