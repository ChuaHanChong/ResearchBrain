# MAGE: Multi-scale Autoregressive Generation for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MAGE introduces a multi-scale autoregressive generation framework for offline reinforcement learning that models trajectories in a coarse-to-fine manner. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse long-horizon, sparse-reward benchmarks like Adroit and Franka Kitchen, while also maintaining competitive results in dense-reward tasks.

## Problem

- Existing generation-based offline RL methods struggle with long-horizon tasks, particularly those with sparse rewards, due to inadequate modeling of multi-scale temporal dependencies.
- Traditional transformers often lack global context in long trajectories, while diffusion models can suffer from local generation bias, leading to globally incoherent plans.
- Current hierarchical generation methods typically use rigid two-layer structures, limiting flexibility in capturing diverse temporal abstractions and complicating joint optimization.

## Method

- **MAGE** develops a Multi-scale ==Trajectory Autoencoder== (MTAE) to learn hierarchical, discrete representations of trajectories across multiple temporal scales.
- A Multi-scale Conditional Guide Autoregressive Generator predicts these token maps from coarse to fine, conditioned on the initial state and desired return-to-go (RTG).
- It employs an inverse dynamics model in the latent space for action determination and incorporates a condition-guided refinement loss with an adapter module to ensure adherence to initial conditions.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across numerous offline RL benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in challenging long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks like Adroit dexterous manipulation and Franka Kitchen.
- Qualitatively, MAGE successfully navigated complex maze environments, collecting all objectives and reaching goals without violating constraints, unlike baseline methods.
- Exhibited favorable inference speeds (27.30 	**0.69 ms** per step), significantly faster than hierarchical diffusion methods, making it suitable for real-time robotic control applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A coarse-to-fine, multi-scale autoregressive generation process effectively captures both global coherence and local precision in complex trajectories.
> - Learning hierarchical discrete representations via an autoencoder provides a structured way to encode and leverage temporal dependencies at different resolutions.
> - Explicit RTG-based conditioning and an adapter-based refinement mechanism are critical for generating controllable trajectories aligned with specified initial states and desired outcomes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) involves training agents exclusively from pre-collected datasets without further interaction with the environment. This paradigm offers potential for real-world applications such as robotics and clinical medicine, where online data collection can be costly or unsafe. However, relying on fixed datasets presents challenges, including distributional shift and overestimation bias, which can lead to suboptimal or unstable policies.

Existing offline RL methodologies can be broadly categorized into four groups:
1. **Generation-based approaches**: These methods frame policy learning as conditional trajectory generation, leveraging generative models to capture complex, multi-modal trajectory distributions. Examples include Decision Transformer (DT) and Diffusion-based methods like Decision Diffuser (DD) and Diffusion-QL.
2. **Regularization-based approaches**: These aim to restrict policy deviation from the behavior policy by adding constraints.
3. **Constraint-based approaches**: These assign pessimistic values to out-of-distribution actions to mitigate their selection.
4. **Model-based approaches**: These utilize a learned environment model for planning.

The present work, MAGE, operates within the generation-based paradigm. While generation-based methods have demonstrated competitive performance and trajectory diversity, they often encounter difficulties in long-horizon tasks, particularly those characterized by sparse rewards. This limitation is attributed to inadequate modeling of multi-scale temporal dependencies, especially long-range information. Traditional transformers, due to their unidirectional autoregressive nature, may struggle with global context, while diffusion models can exhibit a local generation bias, producing trajectories that are locally plausible but globally incoherent over extended horizons.

Hierarchical generation methods (HGMs) have been explored as a promising direction to address long-horizon tasks by decomposing them into shorter subproblems, typically using a fixed two-layer hierarchy with distinct policies for each level. However, this rigid structure may limit the ability to capture comprehensive multi-scale temporal abstractions and can introduce optimization complexities when jointly training interdependent policies. MAGE aims to address these identified limitations by proposing a more flexible multi-scale autoregressive generation approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of MAGE are:

1. **Address Challenges in Long-Horizon Sparse-Reward Tasks**: The core motivation is to overcome the performance limitations of existing offline RL methods, particularly generation-based ones, when applied to tasks with long horizons and sparse reward signals. Such tasks are prevalent in real-world scenarios (e.g., robotic manipulation, strategic planning) where delayed feedback and complex temporal dependencies make reliable policy learning difficult.
2. **Improve Multi-scale Temporal Dependency Modeling**: Existing generation-based methods often struggle with adequately capturing both short-term details and long-term structural coherence within trajectories. Decision Transformers' unidirectional nature can limit global context understanding, while diffusion models can suffer from a "local generation bias" leading to trajectories that lack global consistency over extended periods. MAGE seeks to integrate a mechanism that inherently captures temporal dependencies across multiple resolutions.
3. **Develop a Coarse-to-Fine Trajectory Generation Mechanism**: Current hierarchical generation methods typically employ a fixed two-layer structure, which may not be sufficiently flexible to model the diverse temporal abstractions present in complex tasks. MAGE aims to introduce a more granular, top-down generative process that first outlines a macroscopic plan and then progressively refines it with microscopic details, ensuring both global coherence and local precision.
4. **Enhance Controllability and Conditional Guidance**: The ability to generate trajectories that precisely adhere to specified conditions, such as the initial state and desired future return-to-go (RTG), is crucial for effective decision-making. MAGE seeks to ensure that the generated trajectories are not only coherent but also accurately conditioned on these inputs, mitigating potential deviations caused by quantization or training loss limitations.
5. **Achieve High Performance and Efficiency**: Beyond improving theoretical modeling, a practical objective is to develop an algorithm that demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks while maintaining efficient inference speeds suitable for real-time applications.

In essence, MAGE is motivated by the need for offline RL agents that can effectively plan and execute complex actions over extended time horizons, especially in environments where rewards are infrequent, by explicitly modeling and leveraging the multi-scale temporal structure of trajectories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MAGE (Multi-scale Autoregressive GEneration) proposes a novel approach to offline reinforcement learning by generating trajectories in a top-down, coarse-to-fine manner. The method comprises two main components: a multi-scale trajectory autoencoder and a multi-scale condition-guided autoregressive generator.

#### 3.1. Multi-scale Trajectory Autoencoder (MTAE)

The MTAE is designed to learn hierarchical representations of trajectories. It takes a trajectory, defined as a sequence of state and return-to-go (RTG) pairs, $\tau = \{(R_0, s_0), \dots, (R_T, s_T)\}$, and tokenizes it into discrete representations across multiple temporal scales.
- **Encoding Process**: A top-down encoding maps the input trajectory $\tau$ into a multi-scale token map $M = (m_1, m_2, \dots, m_K)$. Each token map $m_k$ is a sequence of $l_k$ tokens, where $m_1$ represents the coarsest, global-level structure, and $m_K$ captures the finest-grained details. The encoder (E) initially processes the trajectory with $R_0$.
- **Quantization**: Similar to a VQ-VAE, the MTAE quantizes continuous latent vectors into discrete tokens by selecting the nearest entry from a shared codebook (C). This ensures a consistent vocabulary across all scales.
- **Residual Connection**: After quantization, the selected codebook vector for scale $k$ ($z_k$) is scaled up to the finest resolution ($l_K$) and subtracted from the current feature representation ($f$). This residual mechanism ensures that finer scales incrementally refine the information from coarser scales.
- **Decoding Process**: The decoder (D) reconstructs the trajectory ($\hat{\tau}$) from the aggregated sequence of quantized and scaled-up latent vectors ($Z = (z_1, \dots, z_K)$) and the initial RTG ($R_0$).

#### 3.2. Multi-scale Conditional Guide Autoregressive Generation

This component is responsible for autoregressively predicting the multi-scale token maps ($m_1, \dots, m_K$) during inference, conditioned on the initial state ($s_0$) and desired RTG ($R_0$).

- **Generative Process**: A multi-scale transformer predicts each token map $m_k$ based on the initial conditions ($s_0, R_0$) and all previously generated coarser-scale token maps ($m_{<k}$). This hierarchical conditioning aims to ensure that finer-scale predictions are consistent with the broader trajectory outline.
- **Training**: The transformer is trained using a cross-entropy loss ($L_{CE}$) to match the predicted categorical distribution of tokens with the ground-truth token maps obtained from the MTAE.
- **Determining Action**: After generating the multi-scale latent representations ($Z$), an inverse dynamics model (I) is used to determine the action ($a$) to be executed. This model operates on the sum of the latent vectors from all scales ($\sum_{k=1}^K z_k$), encouraging the latent representation to preserve dynamics-consistent information for the most recent timestep. An $L_{inv}$ loss (mean squared error between predicted and ground truth initial action) is used for this.
- **Condition-Guided Refinement**: To address potential discrepancies between the decoded initial state of the generated trajectory ($\hat{\tau}_0$) and the true initial condition ($s_0, R_0$), MAGE incorporates an additional condition-guided refinement loss ($L_{cond}$). This is a mean squared error between the decoded initial state-return pair and the true initial condition.
 - **Mechanism**: A parameter-efficient adapter module is integrated into the decoder (D') to modulate its internal representations, making it more sensitive to the specified conditions. The Gumbel-Softmax relaxation is used during training to enable gradient propagation through the discrete codebook lookup, facilitating end-to-end optimization.
- **Overall Loss**: The training of MAGE combines the cross-entropy loss for token prediction ($L_{CE}$) with the condition-guided refinement loss ($L_{cond}$). The inverse dynamics loss ($L_{inv}$) is also part of the training objective, although its explicit equation is not combined into a total loss in the abstract, it guides the latent representation.

This methodology aims to capture long-term planning and short-term control simultaneously, generating trajectories that are both globally coherent and locally precise, while ensuring adherence to initial conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MAGE underwent extensive evaluation against 15 baseline algorithms across five offline RL benchmarks. The results indicate that MAGE integrates multi-scale trajectory modeling with conditional guidance, generating coherent and controllable trajectories, particularly in long-horizon sparse-reward settings.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

MAGE achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks, especially excelling in long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards, while remaining competitive in dense-reward scenarios.

#### 4.2. Illustrative Example (Maze Game)

In a qualitative maze navigation task, MAGE demonstrated superior ability compared to Decision Transformer (DT), Decision Diffuser (DD), and Hierarchical Diffuser (HD). MAGE successfully navigated to collect all required coins (silver, then gold) and reached the final goal, whereas baselines either failed to reach the goal, missed objectives, or produced trajectories that illegally crossed walls.

#### 4.3. Benchmark Comparisons

- **Adroit (Dexterous Manipulation)**: On the Adroit benchmarks (Pen, Door, Hammer, Relocate), which are characterized by sparse rewards, high-dimensional control, and long horizons, MAGE achieved significant improvements. It substantially outperformed baselines, particularly on Pen, Door, and Hammer tasks, demonstrating its capability in fine-grained control despite sparse feedback. Compared to methods like IQL, DD, CARP, ADT, and HD, MAGE showed consistent advantages.
- **Franka Kitchen (Compositional Environments)**: In the Franka Kitchen environment, which requires sequential planning and combinatorial sub-goal achievement, MAGE surpassed all competing algorithms by a notable margin. This performance is attributed to MAGE's ability to capture both global task structure and local sub-goal details through multi-scale generation.
- **Maze Navigation (AntMaze, Maze2D, Multi2D)**: MAGE demonstrated strong performance in maze navigation tasks (AntMaze, Maze2D, Multi2D), where agents must reach distant goals in complex layouts with sparse rewards. MAGE outperformed baselines on 5 out of 6 AntMaze datasets and all datasets in Maze2D and Multi2D scenarios, confirming its effectiveness in long-horizon navigation.
- **Gym Locomotion (Dense-Reward Tasks)**: Although primarily designed for sparse-reward settings, MAGE also showed competitive performance in dense-reward Gym locomotion tasks (HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Ant), achieving top performance in 7 out of 9 tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Multiple Temporal Scales (K)**: Performance generally improved as the number of temporal scales (K) increased up to 8, then slightly declined, suggesting an optimal granularity level that is task-dependent. This validates the benefit of multi-scale modeling.
- **Trajectory Sequence Generation Schemes**: Modeling returns and states (R, S) jointly achieved the best performance compared to modeling only states (S), actions (A), actions with CQL (A+CQL), or returns, states, and actions (R, S, A). This indicates that (R, S) provides an optimal balance for capturing intent and dynamics.
- **Role of RTG-based Conditioning**: The systematic removal of RTG guidance from the autoencoder, multi-scale transformer, or conditional loss ($L_{cond}$) consistently led to performance drops, highlighting its critical role in aligning generated trajectories with desired returns. Reweighting based on RTG provided some improvement but was less effective than direct conditioning.
- **Inference Speed**: MAGE demonstrated favorable inference speeds (27.30 ± 0.69 ms per step), significantly faster than hierarchical diffusion methods like HD (1480.21 ms) and DD (2339.16 ms), and meeting typical real-time robotic control requirements (20 Hz).
- **Inverse Dynamics Model**: Modeling inverse dynamics directly in the latent space was found to be more effective than using an explicit inverse dynamics model on decoded trajectories.
- **Codebook Size and Transformer Layers**: Experiments showed that an optimal codebook size and number of transformer layers exist; too small or too large values could degrade performance due to insufficient representation capacity or overfitting, respectively.
- **Adapter Module for Conditional Guidance**: The dedicated adapter module for applying $L_{cond}$ was critical; directly applying $L_{cond}$ to the main decoder without the adapter resulted in performance degradation.
- **OGBench Tasks**: MAGE performed competitively on extremely long-horizon, sparse-reward OGBench maze environments, securing the best results in three out of four tasks.
- **Default Configuration**: Even with a fixed, untuned default configuration, MAGE consistently outperformed baselines like ADT and HD, indicating the robustness of its core design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MAGE represents a notable advancement in offline reinforcement learning, particularly for tasks characterized by long horizons and sparse rewards. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Enhanced Performance in Challenging Domains**: By successfully integrating multi-scale trajectory modeling with conditional guidance, MAGE addresses a known limitation of existing generation-based offline RL methods. Its superior performance on complex dexterous manipulation (Adroit), compositional (Franka Kitchen), and navigation (AntMaze, Maze2D, Multi2D) tasks, especially under sparse reward conditions, suggests a broader applicability for real-world problems where such conditions are common.
2. **Robust Trajectory Generation**: The coarse-to-fine autoregressive generation process enables MAGE to produce trajectories that are both globally coherent and locally controllable. This is critical for tasks requiring long-term planning coupled with precise immediate actions, offering a more reliable approach than methods susceptible to local generation biases or rigid hierarchical structures.
3. **Efficient Inference**: The demonstrated fast inference speed of MAGE, significantly outperforming diffusion-based hierarchical methods and meeting real-time requirements for robotics, enhances its practical utility. This efficiency can facilitate deployment in real-world control systems where low latency is essential.
4. **Principled Multi-scale Modeling**: MAGE's multi-scale autoencoder and transformer provide a structured way to learn and leverage temporal dependencies at different resolutions. This approach offers insights into how complex sequential decision-making can be decomposed and synthesized across varying timescales, an area where previous hierarchical methods showed limitations.
5. **Improved Conditional Control**: The condition-guided refinement mechanism, particularly with the use of adapter modules, ensures that generated trajectories strictly adhere to specified initial conditions and desired outcomes (return-to-go). This precise control is vital for reliable deployment of learned policies.
6. **Future Research Directions**: MAGE's framework opens avenues for further research, including addressing distribution shifts in out-of-distribution scenarios and extending the multi-scale mechanism to multi-agent reinforcement learning. The paper suggests MAGE could provide a flexible and effective way to capture intricate multi-agent coordination patterns.

In conclusion, MAGE offers a robust and efficient solution for generating high-quality, conditionally controlled trajectories in challenging offline RL settings, positioning it as a significant contribution to the field with potential for real-world applications in robotics and other complex autonomous systems.
