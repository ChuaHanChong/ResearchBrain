# Turning Internal Gap into Self-Improvement: Promoting the Generation-Understanding Unification in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong, Carnegie Mellon University, RIKEN AIP, Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, and Alibaba Group introduce a self-improvement framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that leverages their stronger internal understanding capabilities to enhance their weaker generation performance. This internal, self-contained approach substantially improves generation quality by up to 20% and reduces the internal non-unification gap by 16% across various tasks, while simultaneously boosting understanding abilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit a pervasive 'internal gap' or 'non-unification,' where their understanding capabilities consistently outperform their generation capabilities.
- Existing methods to mitigate this gap often rely on external reward models, additional supervised datasets, or human feedback, introducing complexity and computational overhead.
- A systematic quantification and root cause analysis of this internal imbalance across a diverse range of MLLMs and tasks was lacking.

## Method

- The authors introduce a =='non-unification score,'== an internal consistency metric, to systematically quantify the discrepancy between an MLLM's generated content and its understanding of the original prompt.
- A ==self-improvement framework== is developed where the MLLM's own stronger understanding branch evaluates its generated images, classifying them as 'aligned' or 'misaligned' to create data for ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== or ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==.
- A ==curriculum learning strategy== is incorporated, enabling the MLLM to dynamically expand its post-training data by revisiting and utilizing samples that were initially difficult to generate or understand as its capabilities improve.

## Results

- The internal self-improvement framework achieved up to **+20%** generation performance gains and a **16%** reduction in the non-unification score on benchmarks like T2I-CompBench++.
- Understanding capabilities improved concurrently, with up to **80%** of gains from 'false positive correction' and up to **3%** accuracy increases on standard understanding benchmarks (POPE, MMB, SEED, GQA, MMMU).
- The curriculum learning strategy dynamically expanded post-training data by up to **50%** and delivered further performance gains across generation, understanding, and unification, often outperforming baselines that use external reward models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The internal gap in MLLMs is widespread, with non-unification scores up to 60%, and is predominantly caused by 'weak generation' rather than misunderstanding.
> - A 'co-improvement effect' is empirically discovered, where generation-targeted self-improvement not only enhances generation but also significantly boosts the MLLM's understanding capabilities, particularly in correcting false positives.
> - Theoretical analysis, extending learning dynamic theory to MLLMs, reveals that generation and understanding learning dynamics are coupled through shared empirical neural tangent kernels (eNTK), explaining the co-improvement effect.
