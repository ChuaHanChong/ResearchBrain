# Turning Internal Gap into Self-Improvement: Promoting the Generation-Understanding Unification in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong, Carnegie Mellon University, RIKEN AIP, Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, and Alibaba Group introduce a self-improvement framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that leverages their stronger internal understanding capabilities to enhance their weaker generation performance. This internal, self-contained approach substantially improves generation quality by up to 20% and reduces the internal non-unification gap by 16% across various tasks, while simultaneously boosting understanding abilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit a pervasive 'internal gap' or 'non-unification,' where their understanding capabilities consistently outperform their generation capabilities.
- Existing methods to mitigate this gap often rely on external reward models, additional supervised datasets, or human feedback, introducing complexity and computational overhead.
- A systematic quantification and root cause analysis of this internal imbalance across a diverse range of MLLMs and tasks was lacking.

## Method

- The authors introduce a =='non-unification score,'== an internal consistency metric, to systematically quantify the discrepancy between an MLLM's generated content and its understanding of the original prompt.
- A ==self-improvement framework== is developed where the MLLM's own stronger understanding branch evaluates its generated images, classifying them as 'aligned' or 'misaligned' to create data for ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== or ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==.
- A ==curriculum learning strategy== is incorporated, enabling the MLLM to dynamically expand its post-training data by revisiting and utilizing samples that were initially difficult to generate or understand as its capabilities improve.

## Results

- The internal self-improvement framework achieved up to **+20%** generation performance gains and a **16%** reduction in the non-unification score on benchmarks like T2I-CompBench++.
- Understanding capabilities improved concurrently, with up to **80%** of gains from 'false positive correction' and up to **3%** accuracy increases on standard understanding benchmarks (POPE, MMB, SEED, GQA, MMMU).
- The curriculum learning strategy dynamically expanded post-training data by up to **50%** and delivered further performance gains across generation, understanding, and unification, often outperforming baselines that use external reward models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The internal gap in MLLMs is widespread, with non-unification scores up to 60%, and is predominantly caused by 'weak generation' rather than misunderstanding.
> - A 'co-improvement effect' is empirically discovered, where generation-targeted self-improvement not only enhances generation but also significantly boosts the MLLM's understanding capabilities, particularly in correcting false positives.
> - Theoretical analysis, extending learning dynamic theory to MLLMs, reveals that generation and understanding learning dynamics are coupled through shared empirical neural tangent kernels (eNTK), explaining the co-improvement effect.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is rapidly advancing, with a primary objective to develop unified models capable of both generating (e.g., text-to-image synthesis) and understanding (e.g., visual question answering) multimodal content. While significant progress has been made in developing such unified architectures, an emerging consensus in the research community indicates a persistent "internal gap" or "non-unification" within these models, where their understanding capabilities often outperform their generation abilities. This paper directly addresses this critical issue, aiming to bridge this performance disparity.

Prior works have acknowledged the existence of this internal gap and proposed various mitigation strategies. These often involve:
- **Reliance on External Reward Models**: Approaches like Hermesflow (Yang et al., 2025) and T2I-R1 (Jiang et al., 2025) leverage external, separately trained models (e.g., BERT, VQA models, BLIP, HPMs) to provide feedback or rewards, guiding the MLLM's improvement. This can be effective but introduces dependencies on external systems and their potential biases, adding complexity and computational overhead.
- **Augmentation with Additional Supervised Datasets**: Some methods, such as those discussed by Mao et al. (2025), seek to improve MLLM performance by incorporating more external supervised datasets, which can be labor-intensive and costly to acquire and label.
- **Focus on Single-Task Improvement**: Many studies, including those by Jiang et al. (2025) and Yan et al. (2025), concentrate on enhancing a specific task, primarily generation, without explicitly emphasizing the alignment or unification between generation and understanding branches within the MLLM.

This work distinguishes itself by departing from these established paradigms. Its primary contribution to the broader research landscape is the exploration of an **internal, self-improving mechanism** that leverages the MLLM's own stronger understanding branch to guide its weaker generation branch, *without relying on any external signals or additional supervised data*. This "free bonus" approach offers a more autonomous and efficient path towards MLLM unification.

Furthermore, the paper provides a **systematic and large-scale empirical verification** of the internal gap across a diverse set of MLLMs and tasks, employing a novel *internal consistency metric* (non-unification score). This addresses a gap in previous literature, where conclusions about the internal imbalance were often confined to single models or tasks. The authors also contribute a **mechanistic understanding** of a "co-improvement effect"—where generation-focused self-improvement simultaneously enhances understanding—a phenomenon extensively observed in pre-training but largely underexplored in post-training MLLM contexts. By extending learning dynamic theory to MLLMs, they offer theoretical underpinnings for this interplay. Finally, the integration of a **curriculum learning strategy** based on this co-improvement effect represents a novel method for dynamically expanding post-training data, pushing the boundaries of self-supervision in complex multimodal models.

In essence, this research shifts the focus from external augmentation and single-task optimization to an internal, self-guided unification process, contributing both empirical evidence and theoretical insights to the evolving field of MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to **promote the "Generation-Understanding Unification" within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. The authors identify a significant impediment to this goal: the pervasive "internal gap" where, despite being designed for unified functionality, MLLMs' understanding capabilities consistently outperform their generation capabilities. This imbalance leads to a "non-unification" phenomenon, as illustrated by instances where an MLLM's own understanding branch deems its generated content misaligned with the original prompt (e.g., Fig. 1).

The specific motivations driving this study can be broken down into several key points:

1. **Validate and Quantify the Internal Gap**: The primary motivation was to move beyond anecdotal observations and systematically verify the widespread existence and magnitude of this internal gap across a diverse range of MLLMs and tasks. This required developing a robust, internal metric (the "non-unification score") to quantify the discrepancy without relying on potentially biased external evaluators. A crucial sub-motivation was to pinpoint the root cause: whether the non-unification stems from weak generation, misunderstanding, or both. The authors confirm it is predominantly due to weak generation.

2. **Develop a Self-Improvement Framework without External Signals**: Motivated by the confirmed "stronger understanding, weaker generation" imbalance, a key objective was to investigate whether this internal gap could be leveraged as a "free bonus." The goal was to design a simple yet effective self-improvement framework where the MLLM's inherent stronger understanding capability could guide and enhance its weaker generation capability, without the need for external reward models, human feedback, or additional supervised datasets. This aimed to offer an efficient and autonomous path to model improvement.

3. **Analyze the Interplay between Generation and Understanding**: Beyond just improving performance, the researchers were motivated to gain a deeper mechanistic understanding of how improvements in one branch might affect the other. Specifically, they sought to explore the existence of a "co-improvement effect" during post-training—a phenomenon where generation-targeted improvements also lead to enhancements in understanding. Understanding this dynamic interplay is crucial for designing more effective and holistic training strategies.

4. **Inspire and Design Advanced Self-Improvement Strategies**: Recognizing the potential of the co-improvement effect, a further objective was to leverage this insight to develop more sophisticated self-improvement techniques. This led to the motivation for incorporating curriculum learning, enabling the MLLM to dynamically expand its post-training data by progressively re-utilizing samples that were initially difficult to generate or understand, thus leading to stronger and more comprehensive unification.

In summary, the research is motivated by a desire to systematically characterize and then effectively mitigate a fundamental internal imbalance in MLLMs, doing so efficiently through self-contained mechanisms, while simultaneously deepening the theoretical understanding of their learning dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach, combining empirical verification, framework development, theoretical analysis, and advanced training strategies.

#### 3.1. Phenomenon Verification: Quantifying the Internal Gap (Section 3)

- **Metric Definition**: The authors introduce the "non-unification score," an internal consistency metric. For a given prompt `y`, the MLLM generates an image `x`. The same MLLM's understanding branch then evaluates the pair `(x, q(y))`, where `q(y)` asks, "Does this image describe `y`?". The non-unification score is the proportion of cases where the understanding branch judges `x` as misaligned with `y`. This metric inherently avoids biases from external evaluators.
- **Large-Scale Evaluation**: Six diverse unified MLLMs (EMU3, Show-o, VILA-U, Janus-Pro, BAGEL, BLIP3-o) were evaluated across nine subtasks from GenEval, T2I-CompBench++, and Science-T2I. These tasks were carefully categorized into three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard) to account for task complexity as a confounder.
- **Root Cause Analysis**: To discern whether non-unification stems from weak generation or misunderstanding, an external, stronger MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) was used as an oracle. The "Weak Generation" metric quantifies the agreement between the MLLM's understanding branch (when it rejects an output) and Qwen's judgment. Human evaluation was also conducted to validate Qwen's assessment.

#### 3.2. Internal Gap-Based Self-Improvement Framework (Section 4)

- **Core Principle**: Leverage the observed stronger understanding capabilities to guide the weaker generation branch.
- **Data Construction**: For each text prompt `y` in the post-training candidate set, the MLLM generates `N` (`e.g., 10`) candidate images `{x_i}`. Each `x_i` is then evaluated by the MLLM's *own* understanding branch. Images deemed "aligned" are labeled `x_chosen`, and those "misaligned" are `x_rejected`.
- **Post-Training Strategies**: Standard post-training techniques are applied:
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: The model is fine-tuned using `(y, x_chosen)` pairs.
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**: The model is fine-tuned using `(y, x_chosen, x_rejected)` triplets, aiming to increase the likelihood of `x_chosen` and decrease the likelihood of `x_rejected` relative to `y`.
- **Model Optimization**: Ablation studies (Section F.1) confirmed that updating only the shared LLM component of the MLLM (while freezing vision aligners, generation heads, and vision towers) yielded substantial gains, simplifying the optimization process.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Self-improved MLLMs were evaluated on:
 - **Generation**: Standard text-to-image metrics from T2I-CompBench++, GenEval, and Science-T2I.
 - **Unification**: The newly defined "non-unification score" (or `1 - non-unification score`).
 - **Understanding**: A "win rate" metric (agreement with Qwen when self-improved and pre-trained models disagree) and performance on standard understanding benchmarks (POPE, MMB, SEED, GQA, MMMU).

#### 3.3. Understanding Co-improvement through Learning Dynamics (Section 5)

- **Empirical Observation**: A significant finding was the "co-improvement effect," where generation-targeted self-improvement *also* enhanced the MLLM's understanding capabilities. This was observed through increased win rates for understanding, particularly in "false positive correction" (correctly identifying previously misclassified prompt-misaligned images).
- **Theoretical Framework**: To explain this, the authors extended learning dynamic theory (Ren & Sutherland, 2025) to the multimodal setting. They formalized the one-step learning dynamics for both generation (ΔG_t) and understanding (ΔU_t) under SFT (Proposition 1) and DPO (Proposition 2).
- **Key Insight**: The theory revealed that the learning dynamics of generation and understanding are coupled through shared "empirical neural tangent kernels (eNTK)" terms. The hypothesis is that high similarity between evaluation samples `(y_0, x_0)` and post-training data `(y_u, x_u)` makes these shared eNTK terms dominant, leading to aligned updates for both branches.
- **Empirical Validation of Theory**: The hypothesis was tested by analyzing samples from the "false positive correction" group. They showed:
 - These samples have higher similarity to their corresponding post-training pairs.
 - The shared eNTK terms are indeed dominant in the learning dynamics of understanding.
 - For these samples, generation updates (ΔG_t < 0) are aligned with understanding updates (ΔU_t < 0), reducing both mis-generation and misunderstanding probabilities.

#### 3.4. Curriculum Learning for Stronger Self-Improvement (Section 6)

- **Motivation**: The co-improvement effect suggested that as the MLLM improves, it becomes capable of handling more challenging samples.
- **Curriculum Replay (Algorithm 2)**: This strategy leverages the improved MLLM to revisit a "discard pool" of prompts that were initially unutilized (due to weak generation or inaccurate understanding). Samples that can now be successfully generated and understood by the improved MLLM are added to the post-training dataset, dynamically expanding it and providing further learning signals. This progressively introduces harder samples as the model's capabilities grow.

This comprehensive methodology, spanning empirical validation, practical framework development, theoretical analysis, and an innovative curriculum learning strategy, allowed the researchers to thoroughly investigate and address the generation-understanding gap in MLLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, systematically addressing the internal gap in MLLMs and providing both practical solutions and theoretical insights.

1. **Pervasive Non-unification and its Root Cause**:
 - **Widespread Gap**: Through large-scale evaluation of six unified MLLMs across nine tasks categorized by difficulty, the study confirmed that non-unification is a pervasive phenomenon. Non-unification scores reached as high as 60% on some tasks for certain models, indicating a substantial internal discrepancy.
 - **Weak Generation is the Primary Culprit**: Quantitative analysis, verified by a stronger external model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) and human evaluation, revealed that the internal gap predominantly stems from "weak generation." In most cases, 60% to 100% of the observed misalignments were attributed to the generation branch producing prompt-misaligned images, rather than the understanding branch misinterpreting them.

2. **Effectiveness of Internal Gap-Based Self-Improvement**:
 - **Significant Performance Boosts**: The proposed internal gap-based self-improvement framework, utilizing standard post-training methods like SFT and DPO, significantly improved MLLM performance. For instance, on T2I-CompBench++, generation performance saw gains of up to +20%.
 - **Reduced Internal Gap**: Concurrently with generation improvement, the non-unification score was reduced by as much as 16%, demonstrating progress towards true unification.
 - **Competitive with External Methods**: Notably, this self-improvement approach, which relies solely on internal signals, achieved results comparable to or even surpassing baselines that leverage multiple *external* reward models (e.g., T2I-R1, Hermesflow).
 - **Correlation with Initial Gap**: The improvements in generation and unification were found to be positively correlated with the initial size of the internal gap; models and subtasks with larger initial gaps benefited more from the self-improvement.

3. **Discovery of the Co-improvement Effect**:
 - **Generation Boosts Understanding**: A key empirical discovery was the "co-improvement effect": generation-targeted self-improvement not only enhanced generation but also significantly improved the MLLM's understanding capabilities. This was evidenced by improved win rates (often above 50%) on prompt-image alignment judgments compared to pre-trained models, meaning the self-improved MLLMs were more accurate in detecting misalignments.
 - **False Positive Correction**: The majority (approximately 80%) of the understanding gains stemmed from "false positive correction," where the self-improved MLLM correctly identified images as prompt-misaligned that the pre-trained MLLM had incorrectly labeled as aligned.
 - **Improved Understanding Benchmarks**: Self-improved MLLMs consistently showed performance gains (up to 3% accuracy) on standard understanding benchmarks like POPE, MMB, SEED, GQA, and MMMU.

4. **Theoretical Explanation for Co-improvement**:
 - **Shared Empirical Neural Tangent Kernel (eNTK)**: The theoretical analysis, extending learning dynamic theory to multimodal settings, revealed that the generation and understanding branches share common components in their learning dynamics, specifically related to the empirical neural tangent kernel (eNTK).
 - **Aligned Learning Dynamics**: The theory suggests that when an evaluation sample (y_0, x_0) is highly similar to a post-training sample (y_u, x_u), the shared eNTK term becomes dominant. This leads to aligned learning dynamics (e.g., ΔG_t < 0 implies ΔU_t < 0), causing a simultaneous reduction in the probability of misaligned generation and the probability of misunderstanding (false positives). Empirical evidence corroborated these theoretical predictions regarding sample similarity and the dominance of shared eNTK terms.

5. **Curriculum Learning for Stronger Self-Improvement**:
 - **Dynamic Data Expansion**: Motivated by the co-improvement effect, a curriculum learning strategy was introduced. As MLLM capabilities improved, it could revisit and successfully utilize prompts from a "discard pool" (samples previously unhandled), dynamically expanding the post-training data by up to 50%.
 - **Further Performance Gains**: This curriculum-based self-improvement (C-SFT, C-DPO) consistently delivered additional gains in generation, understanding, and unification across most tasks, even outperforming baseline models that rely on external reward models.

In summary, the research provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of internal gap-based self-improvement, elucidates the underlying co-improvement mechanisms, and demonstrates how these insights can be leveraged to build even more robust and unified MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with profound implications for both fundamental AI research and practical applications. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Paradigm Shift Towards Autonomous Self-Improvement**: The most significant impact is demonstrating that MLLMs can achieve substantial self-improvement and unification without external signals (reward models, human feedback, or additional datasets). This "free bonus" approach offers an efficient and autonomous training paradigm, dramatically reducing the cost and complexity associated with improving multimodal models. This could democratize access to high-performing MLLMs by lowering the resource barrier for fine-tuning and adaptation.

2. **Enhanced MLLM Coherence and Reliability**: By actively promoting "generation-understanding unification," the work directly addresses a core challenge in MLLM development: creating models that are internally consistent and coherent across their diverse functionalities. MLLMs that are truly unified—where generated content aligns with internal understanding—will be more reliable, trustworthy, and less prone to generating "hallucinations" or producing outputs that they themselves cannot logically validate. This is crucial for safety and robustness in AI systems.

3. **Deeper Mechanistic Understanding of MLLM Dynamics**: The empirical discovery and theoretical explanation of the "co-improvement effect" are major scientific contributions. By extending learning dynamic theory and attributing this effect to shared empirical neural tangent kernels (eNTK) between generation and understanding, the authors provide critical insights into the internal workings and learning mechanisms of unified MLLMs. This mechanistic understanding can inform future architectural designs, training objectives, and regularization techniques to inherently foster better alignment between different modalities and tasks.

4. **Novel Training Strategies and Data Utilization**: The integration of curriculum learning, dynamically expanding the post-training data by revisiting previously unutilized samples, offers a powerful new strategy for model training. This approach leverages the model's improving capabilities to progressively tackle harder examples, optimizing data efficiency and enabling continuous learning. This concept could be generalized to other large foundation models, improving how they learn from vast, and often noisy, datasets.

5. **Broadened Applicability and Performance in Multimodal Tasks**: More unified and capable MLLMs will have a wide-ranging impact across various applications:
 - **Creative Content Generation**: Generating images, videos, or other media that are not only aesthetically pleasing but also semantically accurate and internally consistent with complex prompts.
 - **Intelligent Assistants**: Developing more sophisticated AI assistants that can better understand user queries involving visual information and generate relevant, coherent multimodal responses.
 - **Scientific and Medical Imaging**: Improving the interpretation and generation of complex visual data in scientific research and clinical diagnostics, where accuracy and consistency are paramount.
 - **Education and Accessibility**: Creating more effective multimodal learning materials and tools for individuals with diverse learning needs.

**Limitations and Future Directions**:
The authors acknowledge certain limitations, such as the restriction of their current validation to a limited number of MLLMs (Janus-Pro and Show-o). Expanding this validation to more diverse architectures (e.g., Bagel) is a clear future direction. Furthermore, while the shared eNTK explains co-improvement, a deeper investigation into the underlying reasons *why* such NTK sharing arises in unified MLLMs is an intriguing open question that could lead to even more fundamental insights into multimodal learning.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a significant step forward in MLLM development, offering a powerful, efficient, and internally driven path toward achieving true generation-understanding unification, while simultaneously enriching our theoretical understanding of these complex models.
