# Practice Makes Policies: Bootstrapping and Consolidating Robotic Capabilities from Zero Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> HERO, a hierarchical agentic framework, enables robots to autonomously acquire and refine manipulation skills without initial human demonstrations by progressively evolving capabilities from VLM-guided heuristic reasoning to efficient, closed-loop visuomotor policies. The system successfully collected 664 forward and reverse subtask episodes, achieving an 86.0% average task success rate on diverse real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems typically face a trade-off between zero-shot generalization offered by modular agentic frameworks and efficient, closed-loop execution of end-to-end policies.
- Current robot learning approaches struggle with autonomous capability acquisition and continuous self-improvement, often requiring extensive human demonstrations for initial training or focusing on static, task-specific optimization.
- There is a lack of unified mechanisms for robots to progressively transform general-purpose reasoning into efficient, embodied behaviors, hindering the development of 'muscle memory' and long-term skill evolution.

## Method

- **HERO** integrates three distinct capability layers: L1 (==Heuristic Bootstrapper== using a VLM for zero-shot reasoning), L2 (==Exemplar Accelerator== for one-shot adaptation via motion transfer), and L3 (==Reflexive Policy== for closed-loop visuomotor control).
- A VLM-based agentic orchestrator manages subtask planning, dynamically selects the most appropriate capability layer (L1, L2, or L3), monitors execution outcomes, and refines plans based on real-time feedback.
- The framework employs an ==autonomous capability evolution process (H2E2R)== to bootstrap skills from L1, accumulate experiences as L2 exemplars, and consolidate them into L3 policies using autonomously generated and curated data, including scene resetting capabilities.

## Results

- HERO autonomously collected **664** successful forward and reverse subtask episodes across multiple tasks, demonstrating up to **46** consecutive autonomous execution cycles with an average human intervention of **1.03 seconds** per subtask for scene recovery.
- The full HERO framework achieved an **86.0%** average task success rate across four real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming individual capability variants and partial combinations.
- L3 (reflexive policy) demonstrated robustness against real-time execution perturbations, maintaining a **67.2%** subtask success rate with random object displacements, and was the most utilized capability for successful subtasks (**86.7%**) during deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical orchestration of heuristic reasoning, exemplar reuse, and reflexive control enables robust and efficient robotic manipulation capabilities to be bootstrapped and evolved without initial human demonstrations.
> - Autonomous data collection, including self-reseting scenes for continuous experience acquisition, is critical and feasible for transforming general-purpose reasoning into progressively more efficient and embodied robotic skills.
> - The progressive consolidation of accumulated experiences into closed-loop reflexive policies significantly enhances task performance and robustness, effectively developing 'robot muscle memory' from high-level reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation aims to enable robots to perform complex tasks in diverse, open-world environments with minimal human intervention. A significant challenge within this domain is the acquisition and continuous improvement of robotic capabilities. Existing research approaches typically present a trade-off between zero-shot generalization and efficient, closed-loop execution.

**Existing Approaches and their Limitations:**

- **Modular Agentic Frameworks:** These frameworks often leverage large language models (LLMs) or vision-language models (VLMs) for high-level reasoning and task decomposition. While they offer strong generalization capabilities, allowing robots to address novel tasks, they typically suffer from high inference latency. Furthermore, their capabilities often remain static, with limited mechanisms for autonomously accumulating and reusing experiences over time, hindering continuous skill improvement.
- **End-to-End Visuomotor Policies:** These policies directly map visual observations and task instructions to robot actions, providing efficient and reactive control. However, they heavily rely on extensive human demonstrations for training. This reliance makes autonomous capability acquisition difficult and limits their applicability in scenarios where human demonstrations are scarce or costly to obtain.
- **Self-Improving Embodied Agents:** Recent efforts have focused on automating data collection and enabling lifelong learning for robots. While these systems can reduce human supervision and refine policies over time, they often concentrate on task-specific optimization rather than providing a unified framework for transforming general reasoning into progressively more efficient and embodied behaviors. Many still require initial human demonstrations or frequent early-stage human interventions.

**HERO's Contribution to the Landscape:**

This work, through the proposed HERO framework, aims to bridge the gap between these approaches by introducing a unified mechanism for capability acquisition, evolution, and execution. HERO seeks to address the critical challenge of enabling a robot to bootstrap manipulation skills from general-purpose reasoning, autonomously accumulate physical experiences, and progressively compile these experiences into efficient, robust manipulation capabilities. By eliminating the reliance on initial human demonstrations and fostering continuous self-improvement, HERO positions itself as a step towards truly autonomous, general-purpose robotic systems that can adapt and evolve their skills over long durations in varied environments. It directly tackles the need for robots to develop "muscle memory" by transforming deliberate reasoning into increasingly effective, internalized manipulation abilities through repeated physical interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-improving robotic system capable of acquiring and continuously refining manipulation skills autonomously, starting from zero human demonstrations. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

**Motivation:**

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Systems:** Existing robotic systems often acquire skills in a static manner, learning capabilities for specific tasks or settings without a mechanism for adaptive evolution through physical interaction. This contrasts with human learning, where repeated practice leads to the development of "muscle memory" and increasingly proficient skills. The research is motivated by the desire to enable robots to progressively transform interaction experiences into more effective manipulation abilities, akin to human learning.
- **Bridging the Generalization-Efficiency Gap:** Current approaches face a fundamental trade-off: modular agentic frameworks offer zero-shot generalization but suffer from high latency and static capabilities, while end-to-end visuomotor policies provide efficient closed-loop execution but depend heavily on human demonstrations and lack autonomous skill acquisition. The motivation is to overcome this trade-off by unifying these aspects within a single framework.
- **Minimizing Human Intervention:** A significant bottleneck in developing general-purpose robots is the extensive human supervision required for data collection and skill training. This work aims to substantially reduce human involvement by enabling autonomous bootstrapping and continuous capability evolution from zero human demonstrations.

**Key Objectives of the HERO Framework:**

- **Autonomous Capability Bootstrapping:** To enable robots to initiate manipulation tasks without any prior human demonstrations. This involves leveraging general-purpose reasoning capabilities to synthesize initial behaviors for novel tasks.
- **Rapid Experience Accumulation and Reuse:** To facilitate the rapid accumulation of manipulation experiences and enable their efficient reuse. This objective focuses on adapting previously successful behaviors to new scenarios through mechanisms like spatial motion transfer, thereby accelerating learning.
- **Progressive Capability Consolidation:** To transform frequently encountered and successful manipulation experiences into efficient, closed-loop visuomotor policies. This objective aims to convert general reasoning into robust, reactive "robot muscle memory" over time.
- **Unified Hierarchical Orchestration:** To organize heterogeneous capabilities (heuristic reasoning, exemplar reuse, and reflexive execution) into a unified framework. This involves an agentic orchestrator that manages subtask planning, capability selection, execution monitoring, and plan refinement.
- **Autonomous Data Evolution:** To establish a closed-loop system where data collection is tightly coupled with task execution, allowing the robot to autonomously generate, evaluate, and curate its own training data. This includes mechanisms for autonomous scene resetting to enable continuous experience acquisition.
- **Adaptive Task Execution:** To develop a dynamic capability scheduling mechanism that allows the robot to adaptively select and switch between different manipulation capabilities based on the current stage of experience accumulation, task requirements, and real-time execution feedback, balancing efficiency and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers around HERO, a hierarchical agentic framework designed for autonomous capability evolution and adaptive task execution without initial human demonstrations. The framework conceptualizes robot learning as a progression across a spectrum of capabilities, unified by a central orchestrator.

**HERO Capability Hierarchy:**

HERO integrates three distinct execution layers, each representing a level of robotic proficiency:

1. **L1: Heuristic Bootstrapper (Zero-shot Reasoning):**
 - **Functionality:** This layer enables zero-shot task execution without prior human demonstrations. It leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to understand high-level instructions and decompose them into primitive action sequences.
 - **Geometric Grounding:** A 3D workspace is reconstructed from multi-view RGB observations using Depth-Anything-3. Primitive skills are then instantiated on this 3D workspace.
 - **Primitive Skills:**
 - **Goal-directed Skills (Grasp, Place):**
 - *Grasp:* VLM predicts three 2D keypoints (gripper jaw boundaries, x-axis alignment hint) on the target object. These are lifted to 3D to compute the target end-effector (EE) translation and orientation.
 - *Place:* VLM predicts a single 2D keypoint for the desired placement location, which is lifted to 3D for target translation. EE orientation is inherited from the preceding state.
 - **Path-following Skills (Push, Pull):**
 - *Push:* VLM predicts 2D keypoints for push start and end. These are lifted to 3D to define the motion segment. EE orientation is determined by aligning the tool center point (TCP) x-axis with the pushing direction to maximize contact.
 - *Pull:* Similar keypoint prediction for pull start and end. EE orientation is inherited from the preceding pose.
 - **Post-processing & Constraints:** Geometric post-processing steps are applied, including end-effector offset compensation, depth ambiguity correction for path-following skills (using ray-plane intersection), and push direction disambiguation to ensure consistent orientation. Predefined skill-local pre-conditions (approach poses) and cross-skill transition rules (retract steps) are applied for motion continuity and safety.

2. **L2: Exemplar Accelerator (One-shot Adaptation):**
 - **Functionality:** This layer facilitates rapid task execution by adapting previously acquired successful experiences. It avoids repeated VLM calls by transforming existing trajectories.
 - **Demonstration Retrieval:** Given a language instruction, L2 queries an aggregated exemplar library containing historical successful trajectories and associated object point clouds. Word-level Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) is used for semantic similarity matching (with a perfect match threshold of 1.0 after stop-word removal). Exemplars with insufficient point cloud data (fewer than 1000 points) are filtered out. Each demonstration maintains a quality score, which is updated based on successful reuse or failure, prioritizing higher-quality exemplars.
 - **Motion Transfer:** A 3D rigid transformation is estimated between the current object's point cloud (lifted from SAM3 masks) and the retrieved exemplar's object point cloud.
 - *Coarse Initialization:* Translation is derived from centroid offset, and initial rotation is predicted by a lightweight, pretrained PointNet++ regressor.
 - *Fine Registration:* The transformation is refined using Generalized ICP (GICP) with translation perturbations. Results are rejected if unreliable (e.g., too few correspondences, large yaw deviation).
 - *4-DOF Inductive Bias:* The final transformation is constrained to 4-DoF (translation and yaw rotation only) to mitigate noise from 6-DoF registration and ensure stable trajectory transfer. The translation is compensated to preserve the original EE reference position.

3. **L3: Reflexive Policy (Closed-loop Visuomotor Control):**
 - **Functionality:** This layer provides reactive, closed-loop motor control by directly mapping language instructions and visual observations to robot actions. It continuously adapts motions based on real-time feedback.
 - **Instantiation:** The policy is instantiated using $\pi_{0.5}$ (Black et al. 2025).
 - **Training:** Trajectories successfully collected by L1 and L2 are filtered (removing idle frames, debouncing gripper actions) and converted into training data for L3. This consolidates accumulated manipulation experiences into a direct visuomotor control policy.
 - **Language Injection:** A lightweight linear projection layer directly maps mean-pooled VLM text embeddings into the Action Expert's token space, broadcast-added to action tokens. This provides a direct language signal bypasses potentially dominant visual features, enhancing sensitivity to language specifications (e.g., colors).

**HERO Agentic Orchestration:**

A central orchestrator, driven by VLM-based reasoning, unifies these three capabilities and manages two operational processes:

- **Subtask Planning:** Decomposes a user-specified task into a dynamic sequence of subtasks, expressed in free-form natural language.
- **Capability Selection:** Dispatches each subtask to the most appropriate capability layer (L1, L2, or L3).
- **Execution Monitoring:** After each subtask step, the orchestrator evaluates the outcome (successful, failed, call\_human) by comparing observations against the intended objective.
- **Plan Refinement:** Updates the subtask plan based on execution feedback, allowing for advancing, revising future subtasks, or requesting human intervention when necessary.

**Operational Processes:**

1. **H2E2R Autonomous Capability Evolution:** This process describes the autonomous acquisition and transformation of robotic capabilities:
 - **Bootstrapping with L1:** For unseen subtasks, L1 is invoked for VLM-guided reasoning and execution.
 - **Experience Accumulation with L2:** Successful L1 executions are stored as reusable exemplars. As transferable experiences become available, L2 is used to accelerate execution by adapting these trajectories.
 - **Autonomous Scene Reset:** After each completed task, the orchestrator synthesizes and executes a reverse task to reset the scene, enabling continuous data collection cycles with minimal human intervention.
 - **Consolidation into L3:** Once sufficient experiences are accumulated, the collected and curated trajectories are used to train the L3 reflexive policy, completing the evolution from heuristic reasoning to closed-loop control.

2. **R2E2H Adaptive Task Execution:** This process describes how capabilities are dynamically utilized during task deployment:
 - **Prioritization of L3:** The orchestrator first dispatches subtasks to L3 if a trained policy is available, executing until convergence or a maximum inference limit.
 - **Fallback to L2:** If L3 is unavailable or repeatedly fails, the orchestrator falls back to L2 to attempt execution through exemplar reuse.
 - **Fallback to L1:** If no transferable exemplar is available or L2 repeatedly fails, the subtask is finally dispatched to L1 for heuristic reasoning.
 - This adaptive scheduling balances efficiency (L3, L2) with flexibility and generalization (L1).

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Hardware:** Franka Emika Panda robot arm, four RealSense D435i cameras (three for L1/L2 scene reconstruction, one front-facing, one wrist-mounted for L3).
- **VLM:** Gemini-3-Flash is used for all VLM queries.
- **Tasks:** Four real-world tasks: Package Picking, Block Stacking, Drawer Search, and Cuboid Discovery, each with a corresponding reverse task for autonomous scene reset.
- **Data Collection:** HERO autonomously collects 30 successful trajectories per task, totaling 664 forward and reverse subtask episodes. L3 policies are trained on these datasets for 10,000 steps (batch size 32) using 2 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Execution:** Each layer allows a maximum of 3 consecutive retries before fallback. L3 execution terminates upon action convergence (joint difference < 0.02 for 8 consecutive chunks) or after 25 inference rounds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated HERO's ability to autonomously collect experiences, adaptively execute tasks, and analyzed its runtime characteristics and failure modes.

**Q1: Autonomous Capability Evolution**

- **Autonomous Data Collection:** HERO successfully collected 664 successful forward and reverse subtask episodes across all tasks, demonstrating its ability to acquire experiences with an average human intervention time of 1.03 seconds per subtask for scene recovery. The system achieved up to 46 consecutive autonomous subtask execution cycles without human intervention. Reverse tasks, crucial for scene resetting, exhibited an average success rate of 84.3%.
- **Experience Curation:** The orchestrator demonstrated reliability in evaluating and curating collected experiences. It correctly classified 94.5% of trajectories as successful or failed, indicating its effectiveness in autonomously collecting and maintaining a high-fidelity dataset.
- **Experience Acquisition Analysis:** L1 (heuristic bootstrapper) contributed 41.6% of the trajectory data, while L2 (exemplar accelerator) contributed 58.4%. L2 proved to be more efficient in data collection, acquiring 1.32 successful subtask episodes per minute compared to L1's 0.85, highlighting the benefit of experience reuse for accelerating data accumulation.
- **Capability Evolution Progression:** As data collection advanced, the invocation ratio for L2 (exemplar reuse) increased, signifying an expansion of subtask coverage through accumulated experience. Concurrently, the L3 (reflexive policy) task success rate improved, demonstrating the progressive consolidation of experiences into more performant visuomotor policies.

**Q2: Adaptive Task Execution**

- **Capability Composition:** HERO, utilizing all three capabilities, achieved the highest average task success rate of 86.0% across the four diverse tasks, with an average successful task completion time of 3.9 minutes. This outcome exceeded the performance of individual capability variants or partial combinations:
 - L1 alone achieved a 76.0% success rate, showcasing its generalization capabilities through online reasoning.
 - Combining L1 with L3 improved performance to 82.7% success, indicating that validated, internalized experiences complement general reasoning.
 - L2 alone demonstrated efficiency with a 2.8-minute completion time but a 66.7% success rate. The integration of L1 with L2 increased the success rate to 73.3%, suggesting L1 provides necessary reasoning when direct transfer is insufficient.
 - L2 combined with L3 improved over L3-only execution (72.5% vs. 70.0%), implying that explicit exemplar reuse can cover behaviors not fully captured by the learned policy. The overall results indicate that the hierarchical orchestration provides a balance between robustness and efficiency by flexibly exploiting experiences.
- **Reflexive Execution Analysis:** L3 demonstrated robustness in handling execution perturbations. When introduced to random object displacements during tasks like package picking and block stacking, L3 maintained an average subtask success rate of 67.2%. In contrast, L1 and L2 executions were unable to reliably manage such real-time perturbations, underscoring the necessity of closed-loop reflexive execution for robust manipulation.
- **Effect of Replanning:** Execution-time replanning was found to be critical for robust long-horizon manipulation. Removing the replanning mechanism consistently reduced task success rates across all capability variants. For instance, L1-only execution dropped from 76.0% to 66.0% success rate without replanning, demonstrating the importance of continuous monitoring and adaptive plan updates for handling unexpected failures.

**Q3: System Analysis (Runtime and Failure Modes)**

- **Capability Utilization:** During task deployment, L3 (reflexive policy) was the most frequently used capability, completing 86.7% of successful subtasks. L2 (exemplar accelerator) was invoked for 6.5% of successful subtasks, and L1 (heuristic bootstrapper) for the remaining 6.8%. This distribution aligns with the design principle of progressively consolidating experiences into efficient reflexive policies while retaining fallback options.
- **Efficiency Analysis:** The computational cost varied significantly across layers. L1 exhibited the highest latency (46.57 seconds per subtask), primarily due to VLM-based reasoning and keypoint annotation. L2 reduced this cost to 20.96 seconds by reusing experiences. L3 provided stable closed-loop execution with a per-subtask latency of 37.90 seconds. These figures illustrate the trade-offs between generalization (L1), efficiency through reuse (L2), and reliable real-time control (L3).
- **Failure Analysis:** Out of 120 task executions, 73 were completed without any failures. Of the 41 intermediate failures, the closed-loop recovery mechanisms successfully recovered 30 cases, leading to a total of 103 successful task completions.
 - **Execution-level failures (33 cases):** The largest portion (17/33) was attributed to L3, mainly due to wrong policy execution. L1 failures were primarily from wrong VLM annotation (3 cases), and L2 failures from wrong object segmentation (4 cases) and wrong motion transfer (4 cases). Wrong 3D grounding (4 cases) affected both L1 and L2.
 - **Orchestrator-level failures (14 cases):** Most were due to wrong orchestrator planning (12/14), while monitoring failures were minimal (2/14), indicating that execution assessment was generally reliable, but generating optimal high-level plans remains a challenge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HERO framework represents a notable advancement in the pursuit of general-purpose robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to enable autonomous skill acquisition and evolution from foundational reasoning, without relying on human demonstrations for initial learning.

**Key Contributions and Novelty:**

- **Hierarchical Capability Orchestration:** HERO introduces a unified agentic framework that seamlessly integrates diverse capabilities: heuristic reasoning for zero-shot generalization, exemplar reuse for efficient adaptation, and reflexive execution for robust closed-loop control. This hierarchical structure allows the system to balance flexibility and efficiency.
- **Autonomous Capability Evolution:** The work establishes a novel paradigm for capability evolution, starting from zero human demonstrations. It demonstrates a progressive transformation of general reasoning into reusable physical experiences and, eventually, into consolidated visuomotor policies, mirroring the development of "muscle memory" in biological systems.
- **Adaptive Task Execution:** HERO's dynamic capability scheduling mechanism is significant. By prioritizing learned reflexive policies and intelligently falling back to exemplar reuse or heuristic reasoning when needed, the system can robustly handle a wide range of task conditions and failures, optimizing for both efficiency and success.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Reduced Human Intervention:** By enabling autonomous data collection and skill evolution, HERO substantially mitigates the need for extensive and costly human demonstrations in robotic training. This has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of robotic systems in various applications.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The framework contributes to making robots more autonomous and self-sufficient. Robots equipped with HERO-like capabilities could continuously improve their skills by interacting with their environment, leading to greater adaptability and resilience in unstructured, open-world settings.
- **Foundation for Self-Improving Systems:** HERO provides a promising architectural path towards truly self-improving robotic systems. Its ability to bootstrap from scratch, accumulate experiences, and consolidate them into efficient policies lays groundwork for future robots that can learn and adapt over their entire operational lifetime.
- **Bridging Research Gaps:** The research directly addresses limitations identified in existing approaches, such as the static nature of modular agentic frameworks and the reliance on human data for end-to-end policies. By offering a unified solution, it advances the state of the art in embodied intelligence.
- **Broader Applications:** The principles demonstrated by HERO could be applicable to a wide range of robotic tasks beyond the specific manipulation examples in the paper, potentially extending to mobile manipulation, assistive robotics, and industrial automation where adaptability and continuous learning are critical.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors identify several areas for future improvement:
- **VLM Latency and Grounding Errors:** The heuristic reasoning layer (L1) currently incurs noticeable inference latency and can suffer from visual grounding inaccuracies, which could affect zero-shot experience acquisition.
- **Perception Robustness:** The reliance on accurate depth estimation and point cloud reconstruction makes performance sensitive to perception noise and occlusions.
- **Skill Abstraction:** The current framework bootstraps capability evolution from a predefined primitive skill space, implying a human-designed constraint on the types of skills that can be learned. Future work will explore more efficient reasoning, robust perception techniques, and methods for open-ended skill discovery to enable autonomous generation of novel skill abstractions.

Despite these limitations, HERO represents a substantial step toward the realization of autonomous, continuously learning robots.
