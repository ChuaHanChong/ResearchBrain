# Virtual-Taobao: Virtualizing Real-world Online Retail Environment for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Virtual-Taobao learns a simulator of Taobao's commodity-search environment from ~400M historical customer records, then trains search policies inside it at zero physical cost. GAN-SD generates customers with entropy and KL distribution constraints, MAIL jointly imitates customer and engine policies via multi-agent adversarial imitation, and an Action Norm Constraint penalty curbs over-fitting. Online, RL policies beat supervised baselines by over 2% revenue.

## Problem

- Online RL needs millions of live interactions, costing real money, months of time, and degraded user experience on platforms serving hundreds of millions of customers.
- Behavior cloning assumes i.i.d. demonstrations and suffers compounding covariate-shift error, so cloned customer models break once the platform policy diverges from logged behavior.
- Inverse RL relaxes i.i.d. but assumes a static environment, invalid here because customers and the search engine are each other's environment and co-adapt.

## Method

- **GAN-SD** generates customer requests by adding an ==entropy term== plus a ==KL-divergence term== against the real customer-type distribution, preventing mode collapse onto the most frequent customers.
- **MAIL** extends ==GAIL== to two agents: a discriminator reward drives joint optimization of customer and engine policies as one mapping, so customers generalize to unseen engine policies.
- ==Action Norm Constraint== divides the reward by 1+rho*max(||a||-mu,0), penalizing large-norm engine actions that stray far from logged actions and over-fit the simulator.

## Results

- Simulated customer proportions and R2P across query category, purchase power, and high-level indicator match ground truth closely, and R2P tracks the real daily time trend over **12** time slices.
- TRPO-ANC beat plain TRPO in real Taobao on total turnover and total volume throughout the day, improvement reaching roughly **3%**, confirming the norm penalty reduces simulator over-fitting.
- Online R2P was **0.096** for regression SL1, **0.098** for SL2, and **0.101** for RL+VTaobao; MAIL policies held gains a month later while behavior-cloning ones fell below random.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When agent and environment co-adapt, imitate both sides jointly; single-agent imitation yields a static environment whose learned policy decays fast over time.
> - Plain adversarial generators collapse to dominant modes; explicit entropy and KL constraints on a semantic attribute recover the diversity a simulator needs.
> - Simulator fidelity degrades as the deployed policy departs from the logging policy, so constraining action magnitude trades a little improvement for real-world transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated success in various domains such as games, robotics, and natural language processing. However, its direct application in complex, physical-world tasks, particularly large online systems interacting with human users, presents significant challenges. These environments often involve high "physical costs," including real money, substantial time, and the risk of negative user experiences, making the large number of trials typically required by current RL algorithms infeasible or detrimental.

Traditional approaches for managing large online systems, such as e-commerce search engines, have primarily relied on supervised learning. These methods are designed for single-step decisions and are not inherently equipped to handle sequential decision-making processes or optimize for long-term rewards, which are characteristic of user interactions in online retail. The need for RL solutions in these scenarios, capable of learning sequential decisions and maximizing long-term gains, has been increasingly recognized in recent research.

A common strategy to circumvent the high costs of direct RL training in physical environments is the use of simulators. Previous work has successfully employed simulators for tasks like data center cooling, where system dynamics can be approximated using models like neural networks. However, simulating the behavior of hundreds of millions of diverse customers in a dynamic online retail environment, where customer policies interact with platform policies, poses a much greater challenge than modeling physical systems. Existing imitation learning techniques, such as Behavior Cloning (BC) and Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL), face limitations. BC often requires an i.i.d. assumption for data, which is typically violated in sequential decision-making tasks, and can suffer from compounding errors. IRL relaxes the i.i.d. assumption but generally assumes a static environment, which is not applicable when the platform strategy itself is being optimized and influences user behavior.

This work addresses this gap by developing a sophisticated simulation framework tailored for dynamic, large-scale online user environments. It positions itself as an advancement in building realistic simulators for complex human behavior, specifically in the context of e-commerce, using advanced machine learning techniques like Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and multi-agent imitation learning. By creating "Virtual Taobao," the paper aims to enable the offline training of RL policies that can then be effectively deployed in the real environment, thus bridging the gap between theoretical RL advancements and practical applications in costly, interactive online systems. It extends the concept of Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) to a multi-agent setting and introduces methods to manage the fidelity and generalization of the learned simulator and policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance commodity search capabilities within Taobao, a large online retail platform. This objective is driven by several key motivations and challenges:

1. **High Cost of Direct RL Training in Real-World Environments:**
 - Current RL algorithms necessitate a substantial number of interactions with the environment to learn effective policies.
 - In a real online retail environment like Taobao, such interactions incur significant "physical costs." These costs include potential financial losses due to suboptimal recommendations, negative impacts on user experience, and the considerable time (days to months) required for extensive A/B testing and data collection.
 - The direct application of RL could lead to adverse outcomes for customers and merchants if experimental policies perform poorly.

2. **Sequential Decision Making and Long-Term Reward Optimization:**
 - E-commerce search is inherently a sequential decision-making problem. A customer's shopping journey involves multiple interactions, where the platform's responses (e.g., displaying product pages) influence subsequent customer actions (e.g., clicking, turning pages, purchasing).
 - Traditional supervised learning approaches, commonly used in these systems, typically optimize for immediate, single-step outcomes. They are not designed to learn optimal sequences of decisions or maximize long-term objectives such as overall sales revenue or customer lifetime value.
 - RL offers a framework capable of addressing these multi-step decision problems and optimizing for delayed, cumulative rewards, making it a potentially superior solution for e-commerce search.

3. **Limitations of Existing Simulation and Imitation Learning Techniques for Complex Human Behavior:**
 - While simulators are a known solution for RL training in costly environments, simulating the behavior of hundreds of millions of diverse customers presents unique difficulties.
 - Customer behavior data is generated from complex customer policies that can change over time and in response to platform strategies.
 - Existing imitation learning methods like Behavior Cloning (BC) assume data independence (i.i.d.), which is often violated in sequential interaction data, leading to compounding errors.
 - Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods, while more robust to i.i.d. violations, typically assume a static environment. This assumption is problematic because the customer "environment" (their response policies) changes when the platform's search engine policy is updated, potentially causing the learned customer model to become inaccurate.

To address these motivations and overcome the challenges, the key objectives are formulated as follows:

- **Objective 1: Develop a faithful simulator (Virtual Taobao) of the real-world online retail environment.** This involves:
 - Generating diverse and realistic customer search requests, which have complex and broad distributions.
 - Simulating the dynamic interactions between customers and the Taobao platform, where both customers and the platform exhibit sequential decision-making policies.
 - Ensuring that the simulator accurately recovers important statistical properties and trends of the real environment, such as customer feature proportions and Rate of Purchase Page (R2P) over different features and time.
- **Objective 2: Enable offline training of effective RL policies within Virtual Taobao.** This entails:
 - Developing methods to learn both customer policies and platform policies simultaneously, accounting for their mutual influence.
 - Addressing the potential for over-fitting to the simulated environment, ensuring that policies trained virtually perform well when deployed in the real environment.
- **Objective 3: Demonstrate that policies trained in Virtual Taobao achieve superior online performance compared to traditional supervised learning approaches.** This would validate the entire simulation-to-real-world framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology outlined in the paper involves a three-phase approach: building the Virtual Taobao simulator, training RL policies within this simulator, and employing a strategy to mitigate over-fitting to the simulated environment.

#### 3.1. Problem Description and Formalization

The Taobao search environment is modeled as a sequential decision-making problem from two perspectives: the engine view and the customer view.
- **Engine View:** The search engine acts as an RL agent. Its state includes user profile and search request information. Its action is to rank and display a page of commodities (PV info). Rewards are given for purchases (1) and 0 otherwise.
- **Customer View:** Customers also make sequential decisions. Their state `sc` includes the engine's action, PV information, and their own profile. Their actions include buying, turning to the next page, or leaving. The paper assumes customer feedback is influenced by a limited number (`m`) of the latest PVs, implying a Markov property for customer behavior.
- **Interacting MDPs:** The engine and customers are environments for each other. The transition function `T` for the engine and `Tc` for the customer are defined to reflect these interactions and state changes. The customer reward function is initially unknown and needs to be inferred.

#### 3.2. GAN-SD: Generating Customers

To build Virtual Taobao, the first step is to simulate the arrival of customers and their initial search requests (denoted as `Pc`). The paper proposes **GAN-SD (Generative Adversarial Networks for Simulating Distribution)**, an extension of traditional GANs, to generate customer data that accurately reflects the real distribution.
- **Challenge with Standard GANs:** Standard GANs often tend to generate the most frequent instances in the training data, potentially failing to capture the full diversity and complexity of a target distribution, which is critical for realistic customer requests.
- **GAN-SD Enhancements:** GAN-SD modifies the generator's objective function by incorporating two additional constraints:
 1. **Entropy (`H(V(G(z)))`):** This term encourages the generator to produce a wider and more diverse distribution of customer types (represented by `V(·)`), preventing it from collapsing onto highly frequent modes.
 2. **KL-Divergence (`KL(V(G(z))||V(x))`):** This term guides the generated distribution to align more closely with the actual distribution of customer types observed in the real training data.
- **Overall Objective:** The discriminator aims to distinguish real data from generated data, while the generator attempts to fool the discriminator and also satisfy the entropy and KL-divergence constraints, thus learning to generate a diverse and faithful customer distribution.

#### 3.3. MAIL: Generating Interactions

The core of Virtual Taobao lies in simulating the dynamic interactions between customers and the platform. For this, the paper introduces **MAIL (Multi-agent Adversarial Imitation Learning)**.
- **Challenge with GAIL:** Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) typically trains a single agent's policy in a static environment. In Taobao, both the customer and the platform are agents whose policies interact and influence each other in a dynamic setting.
- **MAIL Approach:**
 1. **Multi-agent Policy Learning:** MAIL simultaneously learns the customer policy (`πc`, parameterized by `κ`) and the search engine policy (`π`, parameterized by `σ`). This accounts for the co-adaptive nature of customer and platform behaviors.
 2. **Adversarial Framework:** Similar to GAIL, MAIL employs a generative adversarial framework. A discriminator (`Rθ`) is trained to distinguish between real historical interaction trajectories (`τe`) and trajectories generated by the simulated customer and engine policies (`τg`).
 3. **Reward for Policies:** The discrimination signal from `Rθ` serves as a reward signal for the customer and engine policies. By maximizing this reward, the policies are incentivized to generate interactions that are indistinguishable from real ones.
 4. **Joint Optimization:** The two policies are optimized jointly. The customer distribution `Pc` (from GAN-SD) is used to sample initial customer states for generating trajectories. An RL algorithm (TRPO is mentioned in experiments) is used in an inner loop to update the customer and engine policies based on the reward from the discriminator.

#### 3.4. ANC: Reduce Over-fit to Virtual Taobao

A potential issue with training powerful RL algorithms in a simulator is over-fitting. A policy that performs exceptionally well in the simulated environment might generalize poorly to the real world, especially if it takes actions significantly different from historical ones. The paper proposes **ANC (Action Norm Constraint)** to address this.
- **Observation:** Historical actions often have relatively small norms. Policies that diverge too much, taking actions with much larger norms, might be exploiting artifacts of the simulator.
- **Strategy:** ANC modifies the original reward function `r(s, a)` to penalize actions whose L2-norm (`||a||`) exceeds a predefined threshold (`μ`).
- **Modified Reward Function:** `r'(s, a) = r(s, a) / (1 + ρ * max{||a|| - μ, 0})`
 - `ρ` is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of the penalty.
 - If `||a||` is less than or equal to `μ`, the penalty term is zero, and `r'(s, a) = r(s, a)`.
 - If `||a||` exceeds `μ`, the reward is reduced, discouraging actions with excessively large norms. This strategy aims to keep the learned policies "closer" to the observed historical behavior, improving generalization to the real environment.

#### 3.5. Experiment Setting

- **Data:** Hundreds of millions of customer records, including 400 million from a random engine policy deployed in a real Taobao A/B test bucket, are used for training.
- **Metrics:** Total Turnover (TT), Total Volume (TV), and Rate of Purchase Page (R2P) are used for online experiments. Only R2P is used for offline experiments.
- **Implementations:** Multi-layer perceptrons are used for all function approximators. TRPO is the chosen RL algorithm for MAIL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical studies presented in the paper evaluate Virtual Taobao's fidelity to the real environment and the performance of policies trained within it.

#### 4.1. On Virtual Taobao Properties (Fidelity)

The results indicate that Virtual Taobao successfully reconstructs important properties of the real Taobao environment:

- **Proportion & R2P over Features:**
 - **Customer Distributions:** When 1,000,000 customers were generated by `Pc` (learned by GAN-SD), the proportions of various customer features (e.g., query category, purchase power, high-level indicators) in Virtual Taobao were found to be similar to the ground truth in the real environment (Figure 3). This suggests GAN-SD effectively simulates the complex customer request distribution.
 - **R2P Influence:** The influence of these customer features on the Rate of Purchase Page (R2P) in Virtual Taobao closely matched that observed in the real environment (Figure 4). This indicates that the simulated customer policies (learned by MAIL) respond to different customer types in a manner consistent with real customer behavior.
- **R2P over Time:**
 - Virtual Taobao demonstrated the ability to reflect the temporal trend of R2P. By training virtual environments on time-segmented historical data (dividing one day into 12 parts), and then deploying the same historical random engine policy, the R2P trends over time in the virtual environment closely mirrored those in the real environment (Figure 5). This suggests the learned models can capture dynamics inherent in the environment that vary with time, even if the customer model itself is time-independent.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning in Virtual Taobao

The experiments also assessed the performance and generalization capabilities of policies trained using RL within Virtual Taobao:

- **Generalization Capability of ANC:**
 - Policies trained with TRPO-ANC (TRPO with Action Norm Constraint) consistently showed improvements in Total Turnover (TT) and Total Volume (TV) when deployed in the real Taobao environment, compared to policies trained with TRPO without ANC (Figure 6).
 - This result suggests that the ANC strategy is effective in reducing over-fitting to the virtual environment, leading to better generalization and performance in the real world.
- **Generalization Capability of MAIL:**
 - A comparative study was conducted between Virtual Taobao built by MAIL and a simulator built using a Behavior Cloning (BC) method. Policies were trained in one-day-old virtual environments and then deployed in virtual environments representing future time points (1 day, 1 week, 1 month later).
 - Policies trained in MAIL-based Virtual Taobao exhibited more stable R2P improvements over a random policy when deployed in these future environments.
 - Conversely, policies trained in BC-based environments showed a faster decrease in R2P and performed worse than the random policy after a month (Table 1). This demonstrates MAIL's superior ability to learn customer policies that generalize better to shifts in the environment or platform strategies over time, likely due to its adversarial and multi-agent nature.
- **Online Experiments (RL+VTaobao vs. SL+Data):**
 - Policies trained using RL in Virtual Taobao (RL+VTaobao) were deployed in real Taobao and compared against two supervised learning (SL) baselines (SL1 and SL2) trained directly on historical data.
 - RL+VTaobao achieved an R2P of 0.101 in the real environment, surpassing SL1 (0.096) and SL2 (0.098).
 - Significantly, RL+VTaobao demonstrated consistent and notable improvements in Total Turnover (TT) and Total Volume (TV) over both SL1 and SL2 throughout the day (Figure 7). The improvements over SL1 were up to 8% for TT and TV, and over SL2, they were up to 4%.
 - These online results indicate that the policies learned in Virtual Taobao translate into tangible performance gains in the actual online retail environment, validating the effectiveness of the proposed simulation framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for addressing a critical challenge in the application of reinforcement learning: the high cost and impracticality of direct training in complex, real-world physical or online environments. The development of Virtual Taobao, a simulator for a large-scale online retail platform, represents a methodological advancement with several significant implications:

1. **Enabling RL in High-Cost Online Systems:** The work demonstrates a viable pathway for deploying RL solutions in environments like e-commerce search, where experimentation carries substantial financial and user experience risks. By providing a faithful and dynamic simulated environment, it removes a major barrier to the adoption of advanced RL techniques in industry.
2. **Novel Simulation for Human Behavior:** The proposed GAN-SD and MAIL methodologies represent contributions to the field of imitation learning and simulation. GAN-SD addresses the challenge of generating diverse and accurate distributions of complex customer requests, going beyond the limitations of standard GANs. MAIL extends adversarial imitation learning to a multi-agent setting, allowing for the co-adaptive learning of both customer and platform policies, which is crucial for dynamic, interactive systems involving human users.
3. **Improved Generalization of RL Policies:** The introduction of the Action Norm Constraint (ANC) strategy addresses the problem of over-fitting to simulated environments. By encouraging policies to remain within a plausible action space relative to historical data, ANC promotes the training of more robust and generalizable policies that perform effectively when transferred to the real world. This is a practical consideration for bridging the sim-to-real gap.
4. **Tangible Economic Benefits:** The online experiments demonstrate that policies trained within Virtual Taobao achieved quantifiable improvements in key business metrics such as Total Turnover, Total Volume, and Rate of Purchase Page compared to traditional supervised learning approaches. These improvements indicate a direct positive impact on business performance and revenue generation for online retail platforms.
5. **Broader Applicability:** The techniques developed in this paper—particularly GAN-SD for complex distribution generation, MAIL for multi-agent dynamic interaction simulation, and ANC for generalization—could be transferable to other domains characterized by costly interactions with complex human behavior. Examples include online advertising, content recommendation systems, automated customer service, and even certain aspects of robotics interacting with human environments, where building high-fidelity simulators from historical data is a prerequisite for RL application.

In conclusion, this research provides a comprehensive solution for virtualizing a complex online retail environment, enabling the practical and effective application of reinforcement learning to optimize key business functions. It contributes to both academic understanding of simulation and imitation learning and practical advancements in the field of industrial RL.
