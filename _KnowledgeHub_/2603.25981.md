# Policy-Guided World Model Planning for Language-Conditioned Visual Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Drexel University developed PiJEPA, a framework for language-conditioned visual navigation that integrates a reactive policy with a latent world model, achieving improved long-horizon goal-reaching accuracy by leveraging the policy to provide an informed prior for world model planning. The framework demonstrated its best positional accuracy with a V-JEPA-2 encoder, recording 1.65 m RMSE and 2.88 m Final Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) for XY position.

## Problem

- Reactive policies in embodied AI excel at short-horizon control but lack the capacity for multi-step spatial planning and long-term consequence reasoning.
- Latent world models, while capable of long-horizon planning, often suffer from slow convergence and inefficiency due to uninformed prior distributions when searching in high-dimensional action spaces.
- A systematic method to effectively combine the strengths of reactive policies (efficient action proposal) and world models (deliberative planning) for language-conditioned navigation was needed.

## Method

- The PiJEPA framework integrates a finetuned Octo policy with Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) planning over a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world model.
- The Octo policy generates an informed prior (mean and standard deviation) for action sequences, which warm-starts the MPPI planner, guiding its search more efficiently.
- The JEPA world model, utilizing frozen pretrained vision encoders (DINOv2 or V-JEPA-2), predicts future latent states, allowing MPPI to iteratively refine action sequences by minimizing the distance to an encoded goal.

## Results

- PiJEPA, particularly with the V-JEPA-2 encoder, achieved the best positional accuracy, with 1.65 m RMSE and 2.88 m Final ATE XY, outperforming standalone reactive policies and uninformed world model planning.
- The Octo-WM baseline (world model scoring policy proposals) reduced Final ATE XY from 3.02 m (raw Octo) to 2.87 m with V-JEPA-2, demonstrating the immediate benefits of world model evaluation.
- MPPI planning added a modest 0.35 seconds overhead to the 2.13 seconds of policy sampling, making the integrated PiJEPA framework practical with a total inference time of approximately 2.48 seconds for an 8-step trajectory.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy-guided initialization of the MPPI planner significantly enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of world model-based planning, overcoming limitations of uninformed search.
> - Even simple world model-based scoring of policy proposals (Octo-WM) yields substantial improvements over purely reactive policies, highlighting the value of forward simulation.
> - Video-based vision encoders (V-JEPA-2) generally lead to superior overall positional accuracy compared to image-based encoders (DINOv2) when integrated within a policy-guided planning framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous agents capable of navigating complex environments based on visual input and natural language instructions represents a significant challenge in embodied AI and robotics. The current research landscape in this domain can be broadly categorized into two primary approaches: reactive policies and latent world models.

**Reactive Policies:** Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as Octo, NoMaD, and frameworks like CAST, have enabled agents to interpret visual observations and language instructions to produce immediate motor commands. These models often utilize generalist architectures that are finetuned on diverse robot data, sometimes augmented with techniques like counterfactual language-action pairs to enhance instruction following. While these reactive policies demonstrate proficient short-horizon control and rapid action generation, they typically lack the inherent capacity to reason about the long-term consequences of their actions. This limitation restricts their effectiveness in tasks requiring multi-step spatial planning or complex decision-making over extended horizons, as they operate on a greedy, single-forward-pass basis.

**Latent World Models:** A complementary research direction focuses on learning world models that predict future states based on candidate actions. These models facilitate explicit planning by allowing agents to simulate potential outcomes before committing to an action. Specifically, Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture World Models (JEPA-WMs) have gained traction due to their ability to learn dynamics within the latent space of a frozen, pretrained encoder, which avoids the computational burden of pixel-level reconstruction. At inference time, sampling-based optimizers like Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) or Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) search for optimal action sequences by unrolling the learned predictor and minimizing the distance to an encoded goal in the embedding space. However, a common challenge for these planning approaches is the necessity to initialize the search from an often uninformed prior distribution over a high-dimensional action space. This can lead to slow convergence, computational inefficiency, and susceptibility to local minima during the planning process.

The work presented in this paper, PiJEPA, positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms. It addresses the limitations of both reactive policies and standalone world model planning by integrating a learned policy to provide an informed prior for world model planning. This aims to leverage the language understanding and efficient action proposal capabilities of reactive policies to enhance the planning efficiency and effectiveness of latent world models, particularly for long-horizon, language-conditioned visual navigation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for language-conditioned visual navigation that effectively addresses the challenges of long-horizon planning. The motivation stems from the observed limitations of existing approaches:

- **Addressing Reactive Policies' Shortcomings:** While state-of-the-art reactive policies (e.g., Octo) excel at generating immediate actions based on visual and linguistic cues, their inherent inability to explicitly reason about future states makes them prone to errors on tasks requiring foresight and multi-step planning. The research aims to augment these policies with a deliberative planning component to overcome this "short-sightedness."
- **Mitigating World Models' Initialization Problem:** Latent world models offer a principled method for long-horizon reasoning. However, their practical utility, especially with sampling-based planners like MPPI, is often hampered by the need to search for optimal action sequences from an uninformed prior distribution. This unfocused search in high-dimensional action spaces leads to inefficient exploration, slow convergence, and a higher risk of getting trapped in suboptimal local minima. The motivation here is to provide a "warm start" to the planner, guiding it towards more promising regions of the action space from the outset.
- **Combining Strengths of Complementary Approaches:** The core motivation is to integrate the strengths of both reactive policies and world models. A finetuned VLA policy, while not optimal for long-term reasoning, can provide a highly informative initial action distribution. This policy-derived prior can then significantly accelerate and improve the efficiency of a world model planner, which can subsequently refine these initial proposals to identify optimal long-horizon trajectories.
- **Systematic Evaluation of Vision Encoders:** The study is motivated to systematically investigate the impact of different modern pretrained vision encoders (specifically DINOv2, an image-based encoder, and V-JEPA-2, a video-based encoder) on both the policy learning and world modeling components. This comparison aims to elucidate which types of visual representations are most beneficial for extracting informed action priors and for learning accurate latent dynamics in navigation contexts.

Based on these motivations, the specific contributions of the paper are outlined as follows:

1. **Proposal of PiJEPA Framework:** To introduce a unified framework, PiJEPA, for language-conditioned visual navigation. This framework integrates a finetuned Octo policy with MPPI-based planning over a JEPA world model, crucially utilizing the policy's output to warm-start the planner.
2. **Comparative Analysis of Vision Encoders:** To train both the policy and world model components using modern pretrained vision encoders (DINOv2 and V-JEPA-2) and provide a systematic comparison of how image-based versus video-based representations influence policy learning and world modeling performance in navigation tasks.
3. **Demonstration of Performance Gains:** To empirically demonstrate through experiments on real-world navigation tasks that policy-guided planning (PiJEPA) significantly outperforms both standalone reactive policy execution and uninformed world model planning, leading to improvements in goal-reaching accuracy and instruction-following fidelity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PiJEPA framework operates as a two-stage process for instruction-conditioned visual goal navigation, integrating a learned policy with a latent world model.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

At each timestep *t*, the agent receives an egocentric RGB image observation *o<sub>t</sub>*, a goal image *o<sub>g</sub>*, and a natural language instruction *ℓ*. The agent's task is to generate an action sequence *a<sub>1:H</sub>* over a planning horizon *H* to navigate towards *o<sub>g</sub>* while adhering to *ℓ*.

#### 3.2. Shared Vision Encoder

Both the policy and the world model leverage a frozen, pretrained vision encoder *E<sub>ϕ</sub>*. This encoder maps observations *o<sub>t</sub>* to latent tokens *z<sub>t</sub>* = *E<sub>ϕ</sub>*(o<sub>t</sub>) ∈ R<sup>n×d</sup>, where *n* is the number of spatial tokens and *d* is the embedding dimension. The frozen weights *ϕ* ensure representational consistency across both components. The study explores two encoder families: DINOv2 ViT-S (image-based) and V-JEPA-2 ViT-L (video-based, with frame-duplication for single-frame encoding).

#### 3.3. Stage 1: Policy Prior from Octo

The first stage involves a finetuned Octo policy, which is a transformer-based generalist policy with a diffusion action head.
- **Policy Function:** Given the current observation *o<sub>t</sub>* and instruction *ℓ*, the policy *π<sub>θ</sub>*(a|o<sub>t</sub>, *ℓ*) generates *N<sub>π</sub>* action chunk samples.
- **Finetuning:** An Octo-Small model is finetuned on the CAST dataset. Its original visual encoder is replaced with the frozen *E<sub>ϕ</sub>* followed by a learnable projection layer. Language instructions are encoded using a pretrained T5 model. The diffusion action head, a 3-layer MLP, is trained with the DDPM objective.
- **Coordinate Transformation:** Octo outputs global-frame displacements, while the world model expects local body-frame actions. A mapping *T* transforms these global actions to the local frame by accumulating headings and rotating displacements at each timestep. Actions are normalized to [−1, 1].
- **Prior Generation:** From the *N<sub>π</sub>* transformed action samples, the empirical mean *μ<sub>π</sub>* and standard deviation *σ<sub>π</sub>* are computed. These statistics define the policy-informed initialization for the MPPI planner.

#### 3.4. Stage 2: JEPA World Model and Policy-Guided MPPI Planning

**JEPA World Model:**
The second component is a Joint-Embedding Predictive World Model (JEPA-WM).
- **Architecture:** It consists of the same frozen encoder *E<sub>ϕ</sub>*, a learnable predictor *P<sub>ψ</sub>*, and an action encoder *A<sub>ψ</sub>*.
- **Prediction:** The predictor forecasts the next latent state *ˆz<sub>t+1</sub>* given a context of encoded observations and actions: *ˆz<sub>t+1</sub>* = *P<sub>ψ</sub>*(*z<sub>t−w:t</sub>*, *A<sub>ψ</sub>*(*a<sub>t−w:t</sub>*)). A causal attention mask is used, and actions are injected via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) conditioning at every transformer block.
- **Training:** The world model is trained on CAST trajectory segments. The objective is to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and target embeddings using a multi-step rollout loss, which involves autoregressive unrolling of the predictor with truncated backpropagation through time. Only *P<sub>ψ</sub>* and *A<sub>ψ</sub>* parameters are updated.

**Policy-Guided MPPI Planning:**
At test time, the policy prior is combined with the JEPA world model using Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) planning.
- **Initialization:** MPPI is initialized with the policy-derived statistics (*μ<sub>0</sub>* = *μ<sub>π</sub>*, *σ<sub>0</sub>* = *σ<sub>π</sub>*). For baselines, MPPI starts with *μ<sub>0</sub>* = 0 and *σ<sub>0</sub>* = *σ<sub>max</sub>*.
- **Planning Objective:** A candidate action sequence *a<sub>1:H</sub>* is evaluated by rolling out the world model autoregressively for *H* steps to obtain *ˆz<sub>t+H</sub>*. The planning cost *L<sub>p</sub>* is the squared Euclidean distance between the predicted final latent state *ˆz<sub>t+H</sub>* and the encoded goal *z<sub>g</sub>*.
- **MPPI Refinement:** MPPI iteratively samples *N* candidate action sequences from a Gaussian distribution. Each sequence is evaluated using *L<sub>p</sub>*. Elite trajectories (top *K* lowest costs) are selected, and their weights are computed using a temperature-weighted exponential function. The Gaussian parameters (*μ<sub>j+1</sub>*, *σ<sub>j+1</sub>*) are then updated based on these elite trajectories. This process is repeated for *J* iterations.
- **Execution:** After *J* iterations, one elite trajectory is sampled proportionally to its weight, and its first action is executed. The process repeats at the next replanning step.
- **Language Conditioning:** The language instruction *ℓ* influences the planning process exclusively through the Octo policy, which shapes the initial MPPI distribution. The world model itself remains language-agnostic.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** CAST, which augments navigation data with counterfactual instruction-action pairs to address posterior collapse. Actions are 4D vectors (∆x, ∆y, sin ∆ϕ, cos ∆ϕ).
- **Encoder Variants:** Four configurations were tested: (i) DINOv2-Policy + DINOv2-WM, (ii) V-JEPA-2-Policy + V-JEPA-2-WM, (iii) DINOv2-Policy + V-JEPA-2-WM, and (iv) V-JEPA-2-Policy + DINOv2-WM, to assess cross-encoder transferability and the impact of representation types.
- **MPPI Hyperparameters:** *J* = 4 iterations, *N* = 32 samples, *K* = 4 elites, λ = 0.8, *N<sub>π</sub>* = 4 policy samples, *σ<sub>min</sub>* = 0.01, *σ<sub>max</sub>* = 0.05.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Default MPPI:** Uninformed MPPI (μ=0, σ=σ<sub>max</sub>).
 - **Octo-WM:** Policy samples are scored by world model rollouts; the best policy sample is chosen without MPPI's iterative refinement.
 - **Octo:** Pure reactive policy execution.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) for XY position (m) and heading (degrees), and Relative Pose Error (RPE) for XY position (m) and heading (degrees), reported as RMSE, Mean, and Final.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation on the CAST validation set provides a comparative analysis of PiJEPA against various baselines, considering both DINOv2 and V-JEPA-2 vision encoders.

- **Overall Positional Accuracy (ATE XY):**
 - PiJEPA consistently achieved the best positional accuracy across both encoder configurations. With DINOv2, PiJEPA recorded 1.78 m RMSE and 3.12 m Final ATE XY. With V-JEPA-2, it achieved 1.65 m RMSE and 2.88 m Final ATE XY, with the lowest overall mean (1.32 m). This indicates that the policy-guided warm start significantly improves the world model's ability to navigate accurately towards the goal.
 - The Octo-WM baseline showed a slightly better Final ATE XY (2.87 m) than PiJEPA (2.88 m) with V-JEPA-2, but PiJEPA demonstrated stronger performance across other trajectory-level metrics.

- **Uninformed MPPI Performance:**
 - Default MPPI, initialized without a policy prior, exhibited weaker performance in positional accuracy, particularly with the V-JEPA-2 encoder (1.87 m RMSE, 3.29 m Final ATE XY), confirming the difficulty of uninformed search in complex latent spaces.
 - However, uninformed MPPI demonstrated competitive, and in some cases, superior, performance in heading accuracy across both encoders (e.g., DINOv2: ATE Hdg RMSE 18.79°, RPE Hdg Mean 5.11°). This suggests that the latent-space objective might implicitly regularize heading alignment even when translational planning is less effective.

- **Contribution of World Model Scoring (Octo-WM):**
 - The Octo-WM baseline, which uses the world model solely for scoring policy proposals without iterative optimization, yielded substantial positional accuracy improvements over the raw Octo policy. For instance, with V-JEPA-2, ATE XY Final dropped from 3.02 m (Octo) to 2.87 m (Octo-WM). This indicates that even a simple filtering mechanism based on world model predictions can significantly enhance navigation performance, capturing a considerable portion of the benefit from forward simulation. The remaining gap between Octo-WM and PiJEPA highlights the additional value of iterative MPPI refinement.

- **Reactive Policy (Octo) Strengths:**
 - Despite lacking look-ahead, the standalone Octo policy remained competitive on step-level metrics. With V-JEPA-2, it achieved the best RPE XY Mean (0.48 m) and strong ATE Heading performance (RMSE 18.72°, Final 27.72°). This suggests that the reactive policy provides effective local control.
 - However, PiJEPA demonstrated a clear advantage in trajectory-level metrics (e.g., ATE XY RMSE 1.65 m vs. 1.72 m for Octo with V-JEPA-2), illustrating that the planning stage successfully mitigates the accumulation of per-step errors over longer horizons.

- **Impact of Vision Encoder Choice:**
 - The V-JEPA-2 encoder generally led to stronger overall positional accuracy, with PiJEPA achieving its best performance using this backbone. Even the reactive Octo policy with V-JEPA-2 surpassed the best DINOv2-based method on some metrics.
 - However, the uninformed MPPI performed worse with V-JEPA-2 compared to DINOv2, suggesting that V-JEPA-2's potentially richer temporal dynamics might create a more complex optimization landscape that is harder for uninformed search. Once anchored by the policy prior, this richer latent space appears to become an asset for PiJEPA, as the performance gap between PiJEPA and uninformed MPPI on ATE XY Final was wider with V-JEPA-2 (0.41 m) than with DINOv2 (0.11 m).

- **Planning Latency:**
 - The Octo policy's diffusion sampling for action proposals consumed approximately 2.13 seconds. The MPPI planning stage added a modest 0.35 seconds to this, resulting in a total PiJEPA inference time of roughly 2.48 seconds for an 8-step trajectory. This demonstrates that the MPPI planning overhead is relatively small, making the integrated framework practical.

- **Qualitative Analysis and Failure Cases:**
 - Qualitative results showed PiJEPA trajectories more closely tracked ground truth paths compared to baselines.
 - Failure analysis highlighted challenges with ambiguous language instructions (e.g., "Follow the building") which could lead the Octo policy to misinterpret the referent. Furthermore, in such cases, the world model itself could exhibit stagnation in its rollouts, predicting nearly identical latent states regardless of sampled actions. This world model stagnation limited the planner's ability to make progress. PiJEPA partially mitigated these issues by providing a policy-derived prior, even though it sometimes undershot the ground truth, indicating persistent challenges in these specific scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PiJEPA framework represents a methodological advancement in the field of embodied AI, particularly for language-conditioned visual navigation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Long-Horizon Navigation Capabilities:** By effectively integrating the strengths of reactive policies and latent world models, PiJEPA provides a method for robotic agents to perform long-horizon visual navigation tasks with improved goal-reaching accuracy and fidelity to natural language instructions. This directly addresses a fundamental limitation of purely reactive systems.
- **Improved Efficiency of World Model Planning:** The core contribution of using a policy-derived prior to warm-start MPPI planning significantly enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of world model-based planning. This approach overcomes the common challenge of slow convergence and susceptibility to local minima associated with uninformed priors in high-dimensional action spaces, making world model planning more practical for real-world applications.
- **Insights into Policy-World Model Interaction:** The research provides empirical evidence for a natural division of labor: the reactive policy is effective for local control and initial action proposals, while the world model excels at evaluating and refining these proposals for long-term consistency. This understanding can guide future architectural designs in embodied AI.
- **Comparative Analysis of Vision Encoders:** The systematic comparison of DINOv2 and V-JEPA-2 encoders offers valuable insights into the properties of different self-supervised visual representations for both policy learning and world modeling. The findings suggest that richer temporal features (from V-JEPA-2) can yield superior overall performance when properly leveraged by an informed planning mechanism.
- **Practical Applicability:** The relatively low computational overhead introduced by the MPPI planning stage, particularly in comparison to the diffusion-based policy sampling, indicates the practicality of deploying such a hybrid system in real-time robotic applications where latency is a critical factor.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified failure modes, specifically world model stagnation and challenges with ambiguous instructions, highlight important avenues for future research. This includes developing more robust dynamics architectures, diversity-promoting rollout mechanisms, integrating intermediate waypoint costs, exploring richer action spaces, and enhancing closed-loop replanning strategies.

Overall, PiJEPA contributes to bridging the gap between efficient reactive control and deliberative long-horizon planning, offering a more robust and effective solution for complex language-conditioned visual navigation tasks.
