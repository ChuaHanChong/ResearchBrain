# PixGS: Pixel-Space Diffusion for Direct 3D Gaussian Splat Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PixGS, developed by Qualcomm AI Research, introduces a single-stage pixel-space diffusion model that directly generates 3D Gaussian Splat attributes. The method leverages 2D generative priors to produce high-fidelity 3D objects in approximately 1 second, outperforming multi-stage and 3D-native baselines with up to 21.21 PSNR and 0.842 SSIM in image-conditioned generation and higher CLIP scores for text-conditioned tasks.

## Problem

- Existing multi-stage 3D content generation pipelines are computationally intensive and suffer from quality degradation due to lossy latent compression and decoding artifacts.
- Optimization-based methods are slow due to per-scene optimization and can produce over-smoothed geometry or multi-view inconsistencies (the "Janus" problem).
- 3D-native generative models are constrained by the limited availability of high-quality 3D datasets, hindering their generalization and ability to capture detailed textures.

## Method

- Introduces **PixGS**, a single-stage ==pixel-space diffusion model== that directly operates on ==3D Gaussian Splat attributes==, representing 3D scenes as ==pixel-aligned Gaussians== across multiple views.
- Adapts a 2D pixel-space diffusion architecture (==PixNerd==) to the 3D domain, leveraging pre-trained 2D generative priors and augmenting noisy tensors with ==Plücker coordinates== for geometric context.
- Employs a comprehensive multi-task training objective including ==Flow Matching==, appearance, geometry (depth, normals), ==multi-scale Laplacian of Gaussian== for high-frequency details, and a ==rotation regularizer==, guided by a ==three-phase training curriculum==.

## Results

- PixGS demonstrates superior performance in text-conditioned generation, achieving higher **CLIP Similarity** and **CLIP R-Precision** on T3Bench and producing more detailed 3D objects qualitatively.
- For image-conditioned generation on the GSO dataset, PixGS achieved the highest **PSNR (21.21)**, **SSIM (0.842)**, and lowest **LPIPS (0.123)** among baselines, indicating better fidelity and geometric alignment.
- The model generates high-quality 3D content with an inference speed of approximately **1 second** on a single A100 GPU, and a user study confirmed its outputs were superior in prompt alignment, geometric, and textural quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly denoising 3D Gaussian attributes in pixel space, without latent compression, effectively preserves fine-grained details and avoids decoding artifacts.
> - The successful adaptation of 2D pixel-space diffusion models, combined with multi-view attention and explicit 3D supervision, enables effective transfer of rich 2D generative priors to high-quality 3D synthesis.
> - A progressive three-phase training strategy, transitioning from pseudo-label guidance to ground-truth rendering supervision, ensures stable convergence and decouples final quality from initial pseudo-label limitations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of 3D content from various inputs, such as text or 2D images, has demonstrated significant advancements. However, the field faces challenges including view inconsistency in outputs from 2D generative models and a scarcity of high-quality 3D datasets.

Current approaches to 3D content generation from text or images can be broadly categorized. One common strategy involves adapting large-scale pre-trained 2D text-to-image latent diffusion models to generate 3D Gaussian Splats (3DGS). Examples include methods that first generate multi-view-consistent 2D images using diffusion models and then reconstruct a 3D representation from these views. While these methods leverage powerful 2D priors, their reliance on cascaded pipelines introduces several limitations. These pipelines, such as DiffSplat, often require the training of multiple interdependent models (e.g., a 3DGS reconstructor, a VAE for attribute compression, and a latent diffusion model). This sequential training is computationally intensive, introduces considerable engineering overhead, and the overall system can be susceptible to errors, as the quality of the final 3D asset is constrained by the capacity of each component and the information loss associated with compressed latent spaces. This can lead to decoding artifacts and accumulated errors throughout the pipeline.

Another direction involves optimization-based methods, which iteratively refine 3D representations using 2D diffusion models, often through Score Distillation Sampling. These methods can produce high-quality results without direct 3D data but are slow due to per-scene optimization and may suffer from over-smoothed geometry or multi-view inconsistencies (e.g., the "Janus" problem).

In contrast, 3D-native generative models aim to learn 3D shape distributions directly from geometric data, offering faster inference and better geometric consistency. However, their development is fundamentally constrained by the limited availability and scale of high-quality 3D datasets compared to vast 2D image corpora. This often necessitates large architectural capacities to capture textures and complex topologies, and these models may struggle to generalize beyond their specific training distributions.

Structured 3DGS generation, building on generalizable reconstruction frameworks, attempts to directly synthesize 3DGS as structured 2D attribute tensors. While this format facilitates multi-view consistency and leverages 2D priors, it typically falls back into complex, multi-stage pipelines with similar training fragility issues. Single-stage approaches, like DiffusionGS, exist but often rely on specialized architectures trained from scratch on synthetic 3D datasets, sacrificing the benefits of pre-trained 2D priors, leading to slow convergence and limited generalization.

PixGS positions itself within this landscape by proposing a single-stage pipeline that directly generates 3DGS attributes. It aims to circumvent the drawbacks of multi-stage pipelines, such as latent compression and the associated decoding artifacts, while simultaneously benefiting from the rich generative priors of large-scale 2D pixel-space diffusion models. By operating directly in pixel space on 3D Gaussian attributes, PixGS intends to enable precise, splat-level regularization of both appearance and geometry, offering an alternative to existing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing PixGS stems from addressing several limitations observed in existing 3D content generation methodologies, particularly those focused on 3D Gaussian Splats (3DGS). The authors identified specific bottlenecks and challenges that their proposed single-stage pipeline aims to resolve.

**Challenges with Existing Multi-Stage Pipelines:**
- **Computational Expense and Engineering Overhead:** Current state-of-the-art methods, such as DiffSplat, typically employ complex cascaded pipelines. These pipelines require the training of multiple interdependent models (e.g., a VAE for latent compression, a 3DGS reconstructor, and a latent diffusion model). This sequential training regime is computationally demanding and involves substantial engineering effort, as each component must achieve satisfactory convergence before the next stage can proceed. This sequential dependency renders the overall system fragile and difficult to scale.
- **Quality Constraints from Latent Compression:** A significant limitation of latent-space diffusion models, when adapted for 3DGS, is the inherent compromise in quality. The reliance on a compressed latent space for representing 3D Gaussian attributes can lead to information loss, resulting in decoding artifacts and an accumulation of errors. This bottleneck inherently limits the representational capacity and fine-grained detail of the generated 3DGS.
- **View Inconsistency in 2D-to-3D Approaches:** Methods that first generate multi-view-consistent 2D images using 2D diffusion models, followed by a separate 3D reconstruction stage, often struggle with geometric integrity. Even minor inconsistencies in the generated 2D views can lead to noticeable artifacts, such as "floaters," in the final 3D reconstruction.
- **Limitations of 3D-Native Models and Training from Scratch:** While 3D-native generative models offer advantages in geometric consistency and inference speed, they are severely constrained by the scarcity of large, high-quality 3D datasets. This often prevents them from capturing the detailed textures and complex topologies seen in 2D-prior-leveraging methods. Additionally, single-stage approaches trained from scratch without exploiting pre-trained 2D priors, such as DiffusionGS, suffer from slow convergence and limited generalization capabilities.

**Key Objectives of PixGS:**
To address these limitations, PixGS was developed with the following objectives:
1. **Develop a Single-Stage, Direct 3DGS Generation Pipeline:** The core objective is to create a unified pipeline that bypasses the need for multiple, interconnected stages, thereby reducing computational costs, engineering complexity, and increasing scalability.
2. **Eliminate Lossy Latent Compression:** By leveraging advances in pixel-space diffusion, PixGS aims to directly operate on 3D Gaussian attributes, thereby avoiding the information loss and decoding artifacts associated with compressed latent representations. This is intended to preserve fine-grained details and enhance the fidelity of generated 3D assets.
3. **Harness 2D Generative Priors Effectively:** The motivation is to capitalize on the vast knowledge embedded in large-scale pre-trained 2D pixel-space diffusion models. This is expected to provide a robust foundation for capturing complex visual details, ensuring rapid convergence, and improving generalization without necessitating training from scratch on limited 3D data.
4. **Enable Precise, Splat-Level Regularization:** By directly denoising 3D Gaussian attributes at each timestep, the method aims to allow for precise control and regularization of both appearance and geometry at the fundamental primitive (splat) level.
5. **Incorporate Comprehensive Geometric and High-Frequency Supervision:** Recognizing that prior works often overlook detailed geometric and structural information, PixGS aims to integrate a comprehensive supervision strategy. This includes explicit supervision for surface normals, depth, and high-frequency structural information to ensure geometric plausibility and intricate detail recovery.
6. **Achieve Fast Inference Speed:** A practical objective is to deliver high-quality 3D content generation with a fast inference time, making the method suitable for real-time or near real-time applications.

In summary, PixGS is motivated by the goal of providing a robust, efficient, and high-quality solution for 3DGS generation by streamlining the generative process and directly operating on the primitive attributes within a pixel-space diffusion framework, informed by extensive 2D priors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PixGS operates as a single-stage, pixel-space diffusion model designed for the direct generation of 3D Gaussian Splat (3DGS) parameters. Its methodology is structured to leverage existing 2D image priors while bypassing the limitations of latent space compression.

#### 3.1. 3D Representation

The core of PixGS's approach is its 3D representation, which is an adaptation of the Splatter Image concept. A 3D scene is represented as a grid of pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians. Each pixel `u` in a 2D grid of size HxW stores the parameters of a single 3D Gaussian primitive `g_i`. These parameters include color (c), scale (s), rotation (r), opacity (o), and depth (d). The 3D position `x` of each Gaussian's center is unprojected from its 2D pixel coordinates `u` and predicted depth `d` using the camera's intrinsic `K` and extrinsic `{R, t}` matrices.

For a multi-view perspective, an object is represented by a tensor `G ∈ R^(V_in x g x H x W)`, where `V_in` is the number of camera viewpoints, `g` is the number of Gaussian parameters per primitive, and `H x W` is the spatial resolution of each 2D grid. This multi-view, image-like structure enables the seamless adaptation of 2D pixel-space diffusion models to 3D generation. To initiate the training process, pseudo-labels of these Gaussian attribute tensors are generated using an off-the-shelf 3DGS reconstructor (specifically, GSRecon), which is fine-tuned to map multi-view images into the target attribute space.

#### 3.2. Generative Model

PixGS adapts the PixNerd architecture, a pixel-space image diffusion model, to the 3D domain. This adaptation allows the model to inherit 2D knowledge from a large corpus of images.

- **Model Architecture**: The PixGS pipeline denoises 3D Gaussian attribute tensors `G_t` conditioned on image and text prompts. The forward diffusion process, following the Flow Matching formulation, interpolates between a data tensor `G_1` (target 3DGS attributes) and a Gaussian noise tensor `G_0` to produce `G_t = tG_1 + (1-t)G_0`. The model `v_θ` is trained to predict the constant velocity `G_1 - G_0`.
- **Geometric Guidance**: To provide geometric context, the noisy tensor `G_t` is augmented with Plücker coordinates, which represent the camera rays for each viewpoint.
- **Tokenization and Multi-view Processing**: The spatial dimensions of each viewpoint's attribute grid are partitioned into non-overlapping patches (e.g., 16x16 pixels). These patches are flattened and concatenated into a sequence of tokens. To ensure cross-view geometric consistency, the standard Attention block is extended to a Multi-view Attention block. This allows any patch to attend to all other patches across all viewpoints of the same 3D object. Positional encoding is handled by adapting RoPE2d into a Multi-view RoPE2d, which scales the coordinate manifold to a virtual resolution that accommodates the collective resolution of all views, assigning unique positional embeddings.
- **Local Neural Field for Denoising**: After passing through transformer blocks, the hidden states corresponding to each patch are used to parameterize a local neural field. The weights `W_n^v` for a patch-specific MLP are predicted from these hidden states. This MLP then decodes the velocity `v` at a specific multi-view pixel coordinate, conditioned on its positional encoding and the noisy input `G_t`. This allows for direct denoising of Gaussian splats in pixel space.

#### 3.3. Image-Conditioned Paradigms

PixGS supports image-conditioned generation, for which two approaches were explored:
- **Viewpoint Concatenation**: The reference image `I_cond` is treated as an additional guided viewpoint. Its RGB channels are projected to match the Gaussian attribute dimension and then concatenated along the view dimension with the noisy attribute tensor `G_t`, along with its Plücker coordinates.
- **Image Prompt Adapter**: This method utilizes DINO-v2 to extract features from `I_cond`. An AdaLN module injects viewpoint information, scaling and shifting embeddings based on reference Plücker coordinates. The resulting image embedding is then projected to match the text embedding dimensionality and prepended to the textual prompt tokens.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

The model is trained with a multi-task objective combining generative modeling with pixel-aligned rendering supervision and structural regularization:
- **Flow Matching Loss (L_FM)**: The primary generative loss, driving the model to predict the velocity from noise to data.
- **Appearance Loss (L_app)**: Supervised against ground-truth rendered RGB images (`I_GT^v`) and silhouette masks (`M_GT^v`) at twice the spatial resolution. This encourages dense Gaussian primitive utilization and high-frequency detail recovery. It consists of `L_MSE` and `L_LPIPS`.
- **Geometry Loss (L_geo)**: Regularizes the underlying geometry using `L_MSE` for rendered depth maps (`D_v`) and a cosine similarity term for rendered surface normal maps (`N_v`) against their ground-truth counterparts.
- **High-frequency Loss (L_LoG)**: A Multi-Scale Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) loss is applied to rendered images to emphasize alignment of intricate details and mitigate over-smoothing.
- **Rotation Regularizer (L_rot)**: An explicit term `E[(||r||^2 - 1)^2]` is introduced to ensure that the predicted rotation quaternions maintain unit norm, which is critical for physical validity and numerical stability in 3DGS.
The total objective `L_total` is a weighted sum of these individual loss components.

#### 3.5. Training Scheme (Three-Phase)

A three-phase training schedule is employed to ensure stability and gradually enhance generation quality:
1. **Phase 1 (Flow Matching on Pseudo-Labels)**: In early iterations, the model is guided by the `L_FM` loss using pseudo-labels generated by GSRecon. This phase helps bootstrap convergence and maps the model output to a plausible 3DGS attribute distribution, stabilizing training by avoiding numerical instabilities that would arise from rendering losses on highly noisy, invalid Gaussian parameters.
2. **Phase 2 (Flow Matching + Ground-Truth Rendering Supervision)**: This phase introduces ground-truth (GT) rendering supervision, combining `L_FM` with `L_app`, `L_geo`, `L_LoG`, and `L_rot`. This serves as a smooth transition, allowing the model to refine its output based on physically plausible rendering.
3. **Phase 3 (Ground-Truth Only Supervision)**: The final phase relies entirely on GT supervision, completely decoupling the generative model's final quality from the initial pseudo-label constraints. At this stage, background Gaussians have been suppressed, leading to faster rendering and a focus on high-fidelity details. This phase is typically the longest.

This comprehensive methodology, from representation to training, is designed to enable PixGS to efficiently generate high-quality 3DGS objects directly in pixel space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PixGS involved extensive experiments on 3D content generation, including text-conditioned and image-conditioned tasks, complemented by ablation studies and a user study.

**Datasets:**
The model was primarily trained on G-Objaverse and G-Objaverse-XL Alignment, curated subsets of Objaverse and Objaverse-XL, totaling over 500K high-quality 3D objects. Each object included renders from 38 viewpoints and corresponding textual descriptions from Cap3D. For image-conditioned generation and reconstruction, the model was benchmarked on 300 unseen objects from the GSO dataset.

#### 4.1. Text-conditioned Generation

- **Baselines**: PixGS was compared against five state-of-the-art baselines: DiffSplat (latent diffusion for structured splats), TRELLIS and GaussianCube (3D-native models), DreamGaussian (optimization-based), and LGM (reconstruction-based adapted for text-to-3D).
- **Quantitative Results (T3Bench)**: PixGS demonstrated strong performance across T3Bench categories (Single Object, Single w/ Surr, Multiple Objects). It achieved higher CLIP Similarity and CLIP R-Precision, indicating better prompt alignment. ImageReward scores, assessing human aesthetic preferences, also positioned PixGS favorably among the baselines. For instance, in "Single Object" category, PixGS achieved 31.98% CLIP Sim and 88.75% CLIP R-Pre, outperforming DiffSplat SD-3.5 (30.99% and 84.75%) and all other baselines.
- **Qualitative Results**: Visual comparisons indicated that PixGS successfully handled complex prompts, generating 3DGS with details that previous methods often failed to recover, such as intricate textures and thin structures (e.g., text). This was attributed to bypassing latent compression and leveraging 2D generative priors.
- **Inference Speed**: PixGS achieved an inference speed of approximately 1 second on a single A100 GPU, making it competitive with or faster than most baselines.

#### 4.2. Image-conditioned Generation

- **Baselines**: The model was benchmarked against DiffSplat, DiffusionGS (single-stage pipeline), TRELLIS, and LGM.
- **Quantitative Results (GSO dataset)**: PixGS achieved favorable results in alignment and geometric fidelity. It recorded the highest PSNR (21.21), SSIM (0.842), and the lowest LPIPS (0.123), outperforming all baselines. For example, DiffSplat SD-3.5, the next best, achieved 19.59 PSNR, 0.811 SSIM, and 0.158 LPIPS.
- **Qualitative Results**: PixGS demonstrated superior generalization to unseen images compared to DiffusionGS, which struggled due to being trained from scratch on a limited synthetic 3D corpus. It also exhibited better reference-view alignment than 3D-native models like TRELLIS, which operate in a canonical latent space without explicit viewpoint-conditional supervision.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Strategy (Supervision Losses and Regularization)**:
 - Relying solely on Flow Matching (`L_FM`) resulted in poor 3D consistency and significant "floater" artifacts (16.04 PSNR, 0.708 SSIM, 0.496 LPIPS).
 - Adding `L_app` alone led to numerical instability (NaN gradients) without proper regularization.
 - The `L_rot` regularizer was crucial for stabilizing Gaussian parameterization, preventing numerical issues.
 - `L_geo` provided auxiliary refinement for depth and normal quality.
 - The Multi-Scale Laplacian of Gaussian (`L_LoG`) loss actively promoted the recovery of high-frequency details, mitigating over-smoothing and resulting in sharper geometric boundaries and textures (e.g., final results with `L_LoG` were 30.36 PSNR, 0.969 SSIM, 0.022 LPIPS).
- **Three-Phase Training Curriculum**: The ablation showed the progressive effectiveness of the training scheme. Phase 1 (pseudo-labels only) yielded 16.04 PSNR. Phase 2 (pseudo-labels + GT supervision) improved significantly to 30.36 PSNR. Phase 3 (GT-only supervision) achieved the highest quality (31.15 PSNR, 0.987 SSIM, 0.019 LPIPS), confirming that pseudo-labels primarily serve as early guidance, with final quality determined by ground-truth supervision.
- **Image-conditioned Paradigms**: Both Viewpoint Concatenation and Image Prompt Adapter paradigms yielded comparable performance. Viewpoint Concatenation was found to be more parameter-efficient (1.2B vs. 1.4B parameters) and achieved slightly higher PSNR (30.36 vs. 30.31), suggesting better pixel-level fidelity due to preserved spatial structure.
- **Pseudo-label Dependence**: PixGS's final quality was found not to be strongly tied to the quality of the pseudo-label reconstructor. When pseudo-labels from a lower-quality source (LGM) were used instead of GSRecon, PixGS achieved similar GSO performance (21.15 PSNR vs. 21.21 PSNR), albeit with a slightly longer training time (approximately 10% more iterations). This indicated that pseudo-labels primarily aid early guidance and stable convergence, rather than setting an upper bound on the final generative quality.

#### 4.4. Supplementary Findings (User Study, Data Scalability, Sampling Diversity)

- **User Study**: A user study involving 103 participants rated PixGS outputs as superior in prompt alignment, geometric quality, and textural quality for both text-to-3D and image-to-3D tasks, relative to competitive baselines.
- **Data Scalability**: PixGS demonstrated robust scalability. When trained on a larger dataset (∼1M assets including G-Objaverse-XL), it continued to outperform DiffSplat and DiffusionGS, reinforcing the advantage of its single-stage, 2D-prior leveraging architecture.
- **Sampling Diversity**: PixGS exhibited the ability to generate diverse geometric and textural interpretations while maintaining alignment with the same text prompt when given different random seeds.

Overall, the findings indicate that PixGS establishes a robust and efficient approach for high-quality 3DGS generation, effectively mitigating limitations of prior methods by combining pixel-space diffusion with targeted supervision and a phased training strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PixGS introduces a single-stage pipeline for the direct generation of 3D Gaussian Splats (3DGS), addressing several efficiency and quality challenges prevalent in contemporary 3D content generation. This work presents a notable contribution to the field of generative AI for 3D assets.

**Key Contributions and Achievements:**
- **Bypassing Latent Compression Bottlenecks:** The most significant contribution is the elimination of the lossy latent compression bottleneck inherent in many multi-stage, latent-space diffusion models. By operating directly in pixel space on 3D Gaussian attributes, PixGS avoids decoding artifacts and preserves fine-grained details, leading to higher fidelity in generated 3D objects.
- **Efficient and Scalable Architecture:** The single-stage design streamlines the generation process, reducing the computational expense and engineering overhead associated with training complex, cascaded pipelines. This architecture, coupled with the leveraging of pre-trained 2D pixel-space diffusion priors, enables rapid convergence and improved scalability, as demonstrated by its performance with larger datasets.
- **Enhanced 3D Quality and Consistency:** Through direct splat-level denoising and a comprehensive suite of loss functions—including appearance, geometric (depth, normals), and high-frequency (Multi-Scale Laplacian of Gaussian) supervision—PixGS achieves strong appearance and geometric fidelity. The rotation regularization further ensures the physical validity of Gaussian parameters.
- **Fast Inference Speed:** The ability to generate high-quality 3D objects in approximately one second on a single A100 GPU represents a practical advantage, significantly accelerating the 3D content creation workflow.
- **Robust Training Methodology:** The three-phase training schedule, which strategically transitions from pseudo-label-guided pretraining to ground-truth-only supervision, ensures training stability and decouples the final generation quality from the limitations of initial pseudo-labels.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements introduced by PixGS have several potential impacts across various applications:
- **Accelerated 3D Content Creation:** The fast inference speed and high-quality output can significantly accelerate the prototyping and production of 3D assets for industries such as virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), gaming, film production, and digital design. This could enable creators to rapidly iterate on designs and generate diverse 3D content.
- **Improved Accessibility for 3D Generation:** By simplifying the pipeline and delivering robust performance, PixGS could lower the technical barrier for generating high-quality 3D models from text or images, making advanced 3D content creation more accessible to a broader range of users.
- **Foundational Research for Direct 3D Synthesis:** This work highlights the potential of direct splat-level denoising within a pixel-space diffusion framework as an effective paradigm for 3D generative modeling. It could inspire further research into direct 3D attribute generation, potentially paving the way for new representations or generative architectures that are optimized for 3D structures from the outset.
- **Applications in Digital Prototyping and Simulation:** The detailed and geometrically consistent 3D outputs could be valuable for digital prototyping, product visualization, and creating realistic environments for simulations and synthetic data generation.

In conclusion, PixGS presents a significant step forward in efficient and high-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splat generation. By innovatively adapting pixel-space diffusion to direct 3D attribute generation, it offers a robust, scalable, and fast alternative to existing multi-stage approaches, with broad implications for various industries reliant on 3D content.
