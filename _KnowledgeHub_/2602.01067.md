# A Systematic Study of Data Modalities and Strategies for Co-training Large Behavior Models for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Toyota Research Institute conducted a large-scale empirical study to evaluate data modalities and co-training strategies for Large Behavior Models (LBMs) in robot manipulation. The study found that carefully selected vision-language and cross-embodiment data sources robustly enhance generalization and fine-grained manipulation without degrading in-distribution performance, with a combined 'Final Model' improving real-world language following by 45.3% and achieving 90.2% average task completion on long-horizon tasks after rapid fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Robot learning struggles with a 'data gap,' as robot data collected through costly teleoperation is much smaller than internet-scale datasets, limiting LBM generalization.
- The empirical effectiveness and optimal strategies for co-training Large Behavior Models with diverse, heterogeneous data modalities are not well understood due to inconsistent experimental setups in prior research.
- Developing generalist robot policies that can perceive, understand, and act robustly in the physical world remains challenging, often constrained by data availability and the ability to generalize.

## Method

- The study employed a consistent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architecture, initialized with PaliGemma2-PT and an **Action Flow Transformer (ActionFT)**, across all experiments to ensure comparability.
- It systematically investigated five major categories of ==co-training data modalities==, including standard vision-language data, dense language annotations for robot and human videos, and cross-embodiment robot data.
- Three distinct ==co-training strategies== (single-phase, two-phase 1st-phase-only, two-phase full) and ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) conditioning== were evaluated across 89 VLA policies using approximately 4,000 hours of robot/human data and 50 million vision-language samples.

## Results

- The 'Final Model,' which combined all identified effective co-training modalities, achieved a **36.4%** improvement on simulation unseen tasks and a **45.3%** improvement on real-world language following over the no-co-training baseline.
- Co-training significantly enhanced representation quality, enabling rapid adaptation to new, long-horizon dexterous tasks, with the fine-tuned model achieving **90.2%** average task completion with only **200** demonstrations.
- Discrete action tokens (latent actions, FAST, VQ-VAE) showed no statistically significant performance improvements at full data scale, and explicit Chain-of-Thought conditioning did not improve performance for the evaluated manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse vision-language data (e.g., standard VL data, dense language annotations, VLM-generated human video annotations) and cross-embodiment robot data consistently and substantially improve generalization across various dimensions without degrading in-distribution performance.
> - Combining multiple effective co-training modalities leads to consistent cumulative performance gains across all evaluation dimensions.
> - Training exclusively on robot data degrades the VLM backbone's general visiolinguistic understanding, while effective co-training restores and preserves these capabilities, indicating a strong correlation between VLM strength and robot policy generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself squarely within the rapidly evolving field of **generalist robot learning**, specifically focusing on **Large Behavior Models (LBMs)** and their subclass, **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. The introduction explicitly states that robot learning is moving towards generalist models capable of perceiving, understanding, and acting in the physical world.

The core problem identified is the "data gap": robot data, primarily collected through costly teleoperation, is orders of magnitude smaller than the internet-scale text and image corpora used to train successful non-embodied foundation models like Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This disparity severely limits the generalization capabilities of current LBMs.

To address this data gap, recent research has increasingly turned to **co-training**, a strategy where models learn jointly from target robot data and diverse, heterogeneous data modalities. These modalities include standard vision-language (VL) data, dense language annotations for robot trajectories, cross-embodiment robot data, human videos, and discrete robot action tokens.

However, the authors highlight a critical gap in the existing literature: while many works explore subsets of these co-training modalities, they often do so with inconsistent experimental setups, leading to a fragmented and poorly understood empirical landscape. This paper directly addresses this void by providing a **systematic, large-scale empirical study** across a wide range of co-training data types and training strategies. It aims to offer comprehensive insights and practical guidance, thus serving as a foundational reference for researchers building scalable generalist robot policies. By rigorously evaluating the effectiveness of different data sources and integration methods, this work helps consolidate disparate findings and provides a clearer path forward for the development of robust, generalizable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged **"data gap"** in robot learning. Despite advancements in Large Behavior Models (LBMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, their generalization capabilities remain constrained by the limited availability and high cost of collecting diverse robot data. Co-training, by jointly learning from target robot data and readily available heterogeneous data modalities, offers a promising avenue to expand data coverage and enhance model understanding without incurring prohibitive data collection costs. However, the empirical effectiveness and optimal strategies for such co-training efforts are not well understood.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives, structured as specific research questions:

1. **Evaluate Modality and Strategy Influence:** To systematically investigate how different co-training data modalities (e.g., standard vision-language data, dense language annotations, cross-embodiment robot data, human videos, discrete robot action tokens) and training strategies (single-phase, two-phase) impact policy performance across various dimensions (in-distribution performance, robustness to distribution shifts, generalization to unseen tasks, and language following).
2. **Assess Cumulative Gains:** To determine if combining multiple effective co-training modalities yields cumulative performance improvements.
3. **Analyze Representation Quality:** To understand whether co-training enhances the quality of learned representations, thereby facilitating rapid adaptation (via fine-tuning) to novel, long-horizon, dexterous manipulation tasks.
4. **Characterize VLM Backbone Shaping:** To examine how effective co-training modalities influence and shape the visiolinguistic understanding of the pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone, using standard VLM benchmarks.
5. **Investigate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Conditioning:** To assess the impact of explicitly conditioning action generation on CoT traces learned from co-training data, comparing it against implicit co-training approaches.

Through these objectives, the authors aim to provide a systematic understanding of co-training and actionable insights for developing scalable, generalist robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a large-scale empirical study design, leveraging extensive datasets and a consistent VLA model architecture to ensure comparability across numerous experiments.

**Model Architecture:**
The core of the system is a **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architecture**. It consists of a pre-trained VLM backbone, specifically initialized from **PaliGemma2-PT (google/paligemma2-3b-pt-224)**, and an **Action Flow Transformer (ActionFT)** as the action head. The VLM processes both visual observations (sequences of RGB images) and natural language prompts. A notable architectural choice is using a *single compact observation encoding token* from the VLM's last four layers to condition the ActionFT, which the authors show improves generalization compared to using all VLM layers' attention keys and values. The ActionFT, based on a diffusion transformer design, predicts continuous robot actions through a flow matching (FM) objective. For discrete tokens (text or discrete actions), a cross-entropy (CE) loss is applied. When jointly optimizing, these objectives are combined via a weighted sum.

**Co-training Data Modalities:** The study systematically investigates five major categories of co-training data:
1. **Standard Vision-Language (VL) Data:** Datasets like RoboPoint and RefSpatial, providing diverse tasks such as visual question answering, object localization, and spatial reasoning, to impart commonsense physical world knowledge.
2. **Dense Language Annotations for Robot Trajectories:**
 - **Scripted Annotation:** Heuristic rules generate low-level action primitives (e.g., end-effector movements, gripper states) for TRI-Ramen robot trajectories.
 - **VLM-based Annotation:** A large VLM (GPT-5) is prompted with frames, task instructions, heuristic action hints, and a world-frame reference image to generate rich, contextually grounded, frame-level action descriptions for robot episodes, offering greater linguistic diversity.
3. **Cross-embodiment Robot Data:** A subset of the Open X-Embodiment dataset (OXE-Ramen), encompassing 1,150 hours of manipulation data from 12 different robot setups, to learn generalizable visual and behavioral representations.
4. **Human Videos:**
 - **Latent Actions:** A Latent Action Model (LAM) is trained on publicly available egocentric human video datasets (Ego4D, EgoDex, Something-Something V2, Epic Kitchen, HoloAssist), along with robot data, to learn a unified discrete action representation (8 tokens from a 32-entry codebook per timestep).
 - **VLM-generated Annotations:** GPT-5 is prompted with human video frames, episode instructions, and world-frame references to generate concise motion descriptions for human hand actions, capturing semantic information similar to language annotations for robot data.
5. **Discrete Robot Action Tokens:**
 - **FAST Tokens:** Near-lossless compression of continuous action chunks into sequences of discrete tokens using the FAST tokenizer.
 - **VQ-VAE Discrete Action Tokens:** Compression of action chunks into 8 discrete tokens with a codebook size of 32 using a VQ-VAE, offering a more compact representation.

**Co-training Strategies:** Three distinct phase-based strategies are explored:
1. **Single-phase co-training:** Jointly training on target robot data and co-training data in one phase.
2. **Two-phase 1st-phase-only co-training:** Training only on co-training modalities in the first phase, then only on target robot continuous actions in the second phase.
3. **Two-phase full co-training:** Similar to (2) for the first phase, but training on *both* co-training and target robot data in the second phase.

**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Conditioning:** For selected modalities (language annotations, latent actions), explicit CoT conditioning is tested. The VLM backbone first generates intermediate CoT traces, which then condition the ActionFT for continuous action prediction during inference. Probabilistic CoT conditioning is used during training.

**Evaluation:** A comprehensive evaluation framework is adopted:
- **Simulation Benchmark:** 58,000 rollouts across 13 seen tasks and 8 unseen tasks, evaluated under both nominal and distribution shift (DS) conditions (appearance changes).
- **Real-world Evaluation:** 2,835 rollouts on a dual-arm Franka robot platform. This includes:
 - **Language Following:** With seen objects, instruction generalization (paraphrasing, semantic categories, attributes), and unseen objects.
 - **Long-horizon Dexterous Manipulation:** Three challenging tasks (PackItemsIntoStringBag, PourIngredientsIntoSoup, StoreCleanDishes) requiring fine-tuning to assess learned representation quality.
- **VLM Backbone Benchmarking:** VLMs extracted from trained policies are evaluated on standard vision-language benchmarks (MMBench, MME, SeedBench for semantic/reasoning; RealWorldQA, GQA, SpatialEval for spatial reasoning; LEGO for planning/long-term reasoning) using VLMEvalKit.
- **Statistical Analysis:** Rigorous statistical analysis using pairwise hypothesis tests (Welch's t-test, STEP test) and Compact Letter Display (CLD) to ensure the significance of observed differences, along with Bayesian uncertainty estimates.

The study trains and compares 89 VLA policies using approximately 4,000 hours of robot and human manipulation data and 50 million vision-language samples, making it an exceptionally large-scale and thorough investigation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic study yielded several critical findings, offering clear guidance for co-training LBMs:

1. **Effectiveness of Co-training Modalities:**
 - **Positive Impact:** Diverse vision-language data (standard VL data, dense language annotations for robot trajectories, and VLM-generated annotations for human videos) and cross-embodiment robot data consistently and substantially *improve generalization* to distribution shifts, unseen tasks, and language following. These improvements occur *without degrading in-distribution performance*.
 - **Phase-Specific Benefits:** Standard VL data and VLM-generated human video annotations are beneficial in both the 1st-phase and 2nd-phase of two-phase co-training. In contrast, dense language annotations for robot data and cross-embodiment robot data are primarily effective during the 1st-phase of two-phase co-training, suggesting their main role is in bootstrapping generalizable representations.
 - **No Significant Benefit for Discrete Action Tokens:** Discrete action token variants (latent actions from videos, FAST tokens, and VQ-VAE tokens) yielded *no statistically significant performance improvements* at the full data scale. FAST token co-training even *degraded generalization* on unseen tasks. Latent actions from videos showed benefits *only in low target-robot-data regimes*, with diminishing returns as the target robot data quantity increased.

2. **Cumulative Gains from Combining Modalities:**
 - Combining effective co-training modalities resulted in *consistent cumulative performance gains* across all evaluation dimensions. The "Final Model," which incorporated all identified effective modalities, achieved substantial improvements (e.g., 36.4% improvement on simulation unseen tasks and 45.3% on real-world language following over the no-co-training baseline).

3. **Enhanced Representation Quality and Rapid Adaptation:**
 - Co-training significantly *enhanced the quality of learned representations*. The "FT Final Model" (fine-tuned from the combined co-trained model) rapidly acquired new skills for unseen long-horizon, dexterous tasks, achieving 90.2% average task completion with only 200 demonstrations. This was a 22.8% improvement over a baseline fine-tuned from a model trained only on robot data and a 42.9% improvement over a single-task policy trained from scratch, demonstrating superior fine-grained manipulation precision.

4. **Shaping of the VLM Backbone:**
 - Training exclusively on robot data *eroded the visiolinguistic understanding* of the VLM backbone, leading to degraded performance on standard vision-language, spatial reasoning, and multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
 - Effective co-training, especially with standard vision-language data and the combined modalities, *restored and preserved* these VLM backbone capabilities, improving performance across various benchmarks. This correlation suggests that strengthening the VLM backbone's multimodal understanding is crucial for better robot policy generalization.

5. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Conditioning:**
 - Explicitly conditioning action generation on CoT traces learned from co-training data *did not improve performance* in the simulation benchmark for manipulation tasks with clear, immediate objectives. In some cases, it led to discernible performance degradation, particularly with VLM-based annotations and latent actions, possibly due to inherent inaccuracies in the generated CoT or the direct propagation of these inaccuracies to action prediction. This suggests that implicit reasoning learned during co-training is often sufficient for such tasks.

In summary, the study provides strong evidence that carefully selected and strategically applied vision-language and cross-embodiment data sources are powerful tools for building generalist robot policies, while discrete action tokens, despite prior interest, showed limited utility in this large-scale context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This systematic study offers profound significance and potential impact on the field of robot learning and the development of generalist robot policies:

1. **Actionable Empirical Guidance:** The most immediate impact is providing *clear, evidence-based guidance* on which co-training data modalities and strategies are most effective. Researchers and practitioners can now make informed decisions, prioritizing diverse vision-language data (including standard VL datasets and VLM-generated annotations for both robot and human videos) and cross-embodiment robot data. This helps in allocating resources efficiently and designing more effective training pipelines, preventing wasted effort on less fruitful approaches like discrete action tokens for high-data regimes.

2. **Bridging the Data Gap Cost-Effectively:** The work demonstrates that significant improvements in generalization can be achieved *without collecting additional, expensive target robot data*. By leveraging readily available internet-scale vision-language data and large pools of human videos, the cost-effectiveness of developing capable robot policies is greatly enhanced. This democratizes access to advanced robot learning by reducing reliance on massive proprietary robot datasets.

3. **Emphasis on Visiolinguistic Understanding:** The finding that training exclusively on robot data degrades the VLM backbone's general visiolinguistic understanding, and that effective co-training restores it, is a crucial insight. It reinforces the idea that **progress in generalist robot policies is intrinsically linked to advances in foundational vision-language models**. This shifts focus beyond mere action prediction to holistic multimodal comprehension, suggesting future research should further explore how to best align and transfer VLM capabilities to embodied control.

4. **Accelerated Skill Acquisition:** The ability of co-trained models to *rapidly adapt* to unseen, long-horizon, and dexterous tasks via fine-tuning is highly impactful. This indicates that effective co-training produces high-quality, transferable representations, drastically reducing the data requirements for learning new, complex skills. This is a crucial step towards truly versatile robots that can quickly learn and perform new tasks in dynamic environments.

5. **Demystifying CoT in Manipulation:** The finding that explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) conditioning offers no benefit for straightforward manipulation tasks challenges a prevailing trend in the field. This suggests that for tasks with immediate visual feedback and clear objectives, the implicit reasoning gained through co-training is sufficient, and adding an explicit CoT step might introduce unnecessary complexity or even propagate errors. This guides future CoT research towards more complex, long-horizon, or abstract reasoning tasks where its benefits might be more pronounced.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By systematically mapping the co-training landscape, the paper opens new avenues for research. It highlights areas for deeper investigation, such as the granular impact of specific vision-language task taxonomies, the potential for fine-grained dexterous motion extraction from human videos, and the exploration of richer CoT formulations beyond low-level action abstractions. It also encourages integrating co-training into other learning paradigms like world modeling and reinforcement learning.

In essence, this study serves as a robust empirical benchmark and a practical guide, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with large behavior models and bringing the robotics community closer to realizing general-purpose, adaptable robots for the physical world.
