# Dynamical Low-Rank Compression of Neural Networks with Robustness under Adversarial Attacks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Oak Ridge National Laboratory developed RobustDLRT, a rank-adaptive low-rank training scheme that compresses neural networks up to 94% while simultaneously enhancing adversarial robustness against various attacks (e.g., FGSM, PGD, Mixup) and maintaining clean data accuracy. The method, integrating a novel spectral regularizer, achieves superior performance over non-regularized low-rank and competitive results with full-rank baselines on datasets like UCM, CIFAR10, and ImageNet1k.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks (DNNs) require substantial computational and memory resources, limiting their deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.
- Existing neural network compression techniques often lead to a reduction in model accuracy or increased sensitivity to adversarial attacks.
- Methods designed to improve adversarial robustness frequently incur higher computational costs or degrade accuracy on clean, unperturbed data, creating inherent conflicts with compression objectives.

## Method

- Introduces **RobustDLRT**, a rank-adaptive and orthogonality-preserving ==dynamical low-rank training== scheme for neural networks that factorizes weight matrices into USVopU S V^	opUSVop.
- Integrates a novel spectral regularizer R(S)=∥S⊤S−αS2I∥R(S) = \|S^\top S - \alpha_S^2 I\|R(S)=∥S⊤S−αS2I∥ to control the condition number of each layer's low-rank core matrix SSS during training.
- Employs a three-step process for each layer: basis augmentation, latent space training with the regularized loss, and truncation via SVD for dynamic rank adjustment, ensuring efficient computation and orthonormal bases.

## Results

- Achieved neural network compression rates up to **94%** (e.g., **95.84%** for VGG16 on UCM) while maintaining or improving clean data accuracy compared to full-rank baselines.
- Demonstrated significantly enhanced adversarial accuracy across various attack types (ℓ2-FGSM, Jitter, Mixup, ℓ1-FGSM, PGD) on UCM, CIFAR10, and ImageNet1k, outperforming non-regularized low-rank methods and competitive with or superior to full-rank baselines in certain scenarios (e.g., doubling Mixup accuracy on CIFAR10 for VGG16).
- Exhibited scalability with minimal training overhead (approximately **3%** on ImageNet1k) and proved effective in black-box attack scenarios and when combined with adversarial training, further extending its practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial robustness in low-rank networks can be effectively enhanced by directly controlling the condition number of the low-rank core matrix SSS within each layer, due to the orthonormality of the basis matrices.
> - The proposed spectral regularizer efficiently promotes better conditioning of the core matrix SSS, providing theoretical guarantees on improving the condition number without degrading training performance or clean validation accuracy.
> - By integrating this regularizer into a rank-adaptive dynamical low-rank training framework, it is possible to achieve high compression rates, maintain high clean accuracy, and significantly improve adversarial robustness simultaneously, overcoming previous trade-offs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of deep neural networks (DNNs) has advanced state-of-the-art performance across various domains, including computer vision and data processing. However, this success is often associated with substantial computational and memory requirements, which present challenges for deploying DNNs on resource-constrained devices such as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) or surveillance sensors. These platforms typically operate with limited power and computational budgets and require sustained autonomous functionality.

This context introduces a complex set of interdependent challenges: model compression, maintenance of high accuracy, and robustness to adversarial inputs. Prior research has identified inherent trade-offs among these objectives. Compression techniques, such as low-rank approximations or sparsity methods, can lead to reduced model accuracy. Conversely, methods designed to enhance adversarial robustness, including data augmentation or regularization-based defenses, frequently result in degraded accuracy on clean, unperturbed data. Furthermore, compressed networks, particularly those employing low-rank factorizations, have been observed to exhibit increased sensitivity to adversarial attacks. The computational burden associated with many adversarial robustness methods during both training and inference further complicates their application on constrained hardware.

Existing low-rank compression methods include post-hoc matrix and tensor decompositions, as well as techniques that enforce low-rank constraints during training, such as Dynamical Low-Rank Training (DLRT), Pufferfish, and various Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) extensions. While some LoRA variants introduce rank adaptivity, they typically do not reduce the cost of full training and inference. Pruning is another approach for reducing parameter count, primarily benefiting inference efficiency after a full-rank network has been trained.

In the domain of adversarial robustness, methods often involve enforcing orthogonality in network layers, either softly through regularization or strongly through Riemannian optimization on specific manifolds. Soft approaches are more flexible but provide weaker guarantees on the spectral properties, while hard approaches can enforce explicit bounds but may require careful hyperparameter tuning and potentially affect validation metrics.

This work contributes to the intersection of these fields by addressing the conflict between compression and adversarial robustness. It aims to develop a method that achieves high compression while simultaneously improving robustness, particularly by controlling the spectral properties of network layers during a low-rank training process. This positions the research as an effort to integrate the benefits of efficient low-rank training with enhanced model resilience, without sacrificing accuracy or imposing prohibitive computational overhead.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for compressing neural networks that simultaneously enhances their robustness against adversarial attacks without compromising accuracy on clean data. This objective is motivated by the increasing demand for deploying sophisticated neural network models on resource-constrained edge devices where memory, computational power, and energy are limited, and where models must operate reliably in potentially hostile environments.

Specifically, the core challenges addressed by this work are:
1. **Compression:** To achieve substantial reductions in memory footprint and computational cost, making models suitable for deployment on low-power platforms.
2. **Accuracy Preservation:** To ensure that compressed models maintain high performance on clean, unperturbed data, supporting critical decision-making processes.
3. **Adversarial Robustness:** To make compressed models resilient to adversarial perturbations and noise, mitigating the degradation in performance commonly observed in compressed networks when subjected to attacks.

The authors highlight that these three objectives—compression, accuracy, and robustness—are frequently in conflict. Previous work has demonstrated that low-rank compression can reduce accuracy and increase sensitivity to adversarial attacks. Similarly, many techniques for improving adversarial robustness often incur a computational cost during training or inference and can degrade clean data accuracy. The motivation for this work arises from the necessity to overcome these inherent trade-offs.

The specific contributions outlined by the authors, which reflect their detailed objectives, include:
- **A Low-Rank Compression Framework:** To introduce a novel regularization and integration method within a class of low-rank training schemes. This framework aims to produce low-rank compressed neural networks capable of achieving over a 10-fold reduction in both memory and compute cost while maintaining or improving clean accuracy and adversarial robustness compared to full-rank baselines.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** To analyze the proposed spectral regularizer and derive an explicit mathematical bound on the condition number of the low-rank core matrix within each layer. This theoretical foundation is intended to provide confidence that the regularizer effectively improves adversarial performance.
- **Performance Preservation:** To analytically and empirically demonstrate that the proposed regularizer does not degrade training performance or reduce clean validation accuracy across various network architectures.
- **Extensive Empirical Validation:** To conduct comprehensive experiments across multiple architectures (VGG16, VGG11, Vision Transformers), datasets (UCM, Cifar10, ImageNet1k), and adversarial attack types (FGSM, Jitter, Mixup, PGD). This objective is to demonstrate the effectiveness, robustness, and broad applicability of the proposed method under various conditions.

Additionally, the approach is designed to be model- and data-agnostic, seamlessly integrable with existing adversarial defenses like adversarial training, and capable of operating without assembling full-rank weight matrices, thus ensuring a low memory footprint throughout training and inference. The method also aims to offer new theoretical and algorithmic insights by connecting to dynamical low-rank integration schemes and gradient flow analysis, enhancing model trustworthiness and analyzability through interpretable spectral metrics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology addresses the identified conflicts between neural network compression, accuracy, and adversarial robustness by introducing a dynamical low-rank training scheme enhanced with a spectral regularizer.

**Neural Network Model and Low-Rank Compression:**
The work considers a neural network $f$ as a series of $L$ layers, where each layer computes $z^{\ell+1} = \sigma^{\ell}(W^{\ell}z^{\ell})$. The weight matrices $W^{\ell} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ are approximated by a low-rank factorization $W^{\ell} = U^{\ell}S^{\ell}(V^{\ell})^\top$, where $U^{\ell}, V^{\ell} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ are orthonormal matrices and $S^{\ell} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ is a full-rank coefficient matrix, with $r \ll n$. This factorization reduces storage and computation costs from $O(n^2)$ to $O(2nr + r^2)$. The assumption of orthonormal $U$ and $V$ throughout training is a key differentiator from standard low-rank approaches and is supported by recent literature.

**Adversarial Robustness and Condition Number:**
Adversarial robustness is quantified by a network's sensitivity $S(f, X, \delta)$ to input perturbations $\delta$. For networks with Lipschitz continuous activation functions, this sensitivity can be bounded by the product of the condition numbers $\kappa(W^{\ell})$ of each layer's weight matrix. In a low-rank factorization $U S V^\top$, the condition number $\kappa(U S V^\top)$ simplifies to $\kappa(S)$ due to the orthonormality of $U$ and $V$. Therefore, controlling the adversarial robustness of a low-rank network reduces to controlling the condition number $\kappa(S)$ of the $r \times r$ coefficient matrix $S$.

**Robustness Regularizer R:**
A computationally efficient regularizer, $R(S)$, is introduced to control the condition number of each layer's coefficient matrix $S$ during training.
The regularizer is defined as:
$R(S) = \|S^\top S - \alpha_S^2 I\|$, where $\alpha_S^2 = \frac{1}{r} \|S\|_F^2$ and $I$ is the $r \times r$ identity matrix.
This regularizer penalizes the distance of $S^\top S$ from a scaled identity matrix, which promotes better conditioning. It can also be interpreted as a scaled standard deviation of the squared singular values of $S$.
The gradient of $R(S)$ is provided in closed form: $\nabla R(S) = 2S(S^\top S - \alpha_S^2 I) / R(S)$, enabling efficient integration into optimizers. The computation of this gradient involves only $r \times r$ matrix operations, maintaining computational efficiency for $r \ll n$.
A theoretical bound is derived (Proposition 2), establishing a relationship between $R(S)$ and the condition number $\kappa(S)$:
$\kappa(S) \leq \exp\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\varsigma_r(S)^2} R(S)\right)$, where $\varsigma_r(S)$ is the smallest singular value of $S$. This indicates that reducing $R(S)$ contributes to improving $\kappa(S)$, assuming $\varsigma_r(S)$ is not excessively small. The use of rank-adaptive methods helps ensure $\varsigma_r(S)$ remains sufficiently large. The authors note that this regularizer is effective when $U, V$ are orthonormal, which is not guaranteed by methods like LoRA.

**RobustDLRT: A Rank-Adaptive and Adversarial Robustness Increasing Dynamical Low-Rank Training Scheme:**
The regularizer $R$ is integrated into an existing rank-adaptive, orthogonality-preserving low-rank training scheme (derived from), which the authors term RobustDLRT (Algorithm 1). This scheme ensures orthogonality of $U$ and $V$ and allows for dynamic rank adjustment. The method consists of three main steps for each layer:

1. **Basis Augmentation:** The current orthonormal bases $U_t, V_t$ are augmented by their respective gradient dynamics $\nabla_U \mathcal{L}$ and $\nabla_V \mathcal{L}$ to form expanded, orthonormal bases $\hat{U}, \hat{V}$ (of size $n \times 2r$). Since $R(U S V^\top) = R(S)$, the gradients of $R$ with respect to $U$ and $V$ are zero, so only the loss $\mathcal{L}$ contributes to basis augmentation. This step expands the representational capacity for the next iteration.
2. **Latent Space Training:** The coefficient matrix $\hat{S}$ (of size $2r \times 2r$) in the augmented latent space is updated using a projected gradient flow. The regularized loss $\hat{\mathcal{L}} = \mathcal{L} + \beta R$ is minimized, where $\beta > 0$ is the regularization parameter. The update rule for $\hat{S}$ incorporates the gradient of the regularizer: $\hat{S}_{s+1} = \hat{S}_s - \lambda \nabla_{\hat{S}} \mathcal{L} - \beta \nabla_{\hat{S}} R(\hat{S}_s)$ for $s^*$ local steps. This step directly steers the conditioning of the $S$ matrix.
3. **Truncation:** The updated latent solution $\hat{U} \tilde{S} \hat{V}^\top$ is projected back onto the manifold of rank-$r_1$ matrices. This is achieved by a truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of $\tilde{S}$, discarding singular values below a threshold $\vartheta$. This step allows for rank adaptivity, where the new rank $r_1$ can be determined dynamically.

**Computational Cost:**
RobustDLRT's computational cost is asymptotically similar to LoRA, as it avoids reconstructing the full weight matrix. The orthonormalization, regularizer computation, and SVD steps have complexities of $O(nr^2)$, $O(r^3)$, and $O(r^3)$ respectively. For multiple coefficient update steps ($s^* > 1$), the amortized cost per update is further reduced, as gradients are only required for $\hat{S}$. The $O(n^3)$ scaling of applying $R$ to full-rank matrices makes it inefficient for baselines, justifying its application to low-rank factors.

**Extension to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs):**
For CNNs, where the convolutional kernel $C$ is a tensor, the DLRT method uses a Tucker factorization, compressing $C$ in the feature modes. The regularization is applied to the matricized core tensor $S$, i.e., $R(\text{Mat}(S)^\top)$, where $\text{Mat}(S)$ is an $r_O \times r_I S_W S_H$ matrix. This ensures the regularization is applied to an efficiently computable $r_O \times r_O$ matrix ($S S^\top$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates RobustDLRT against non-regularized DLRT and baseline full-rank training across various architectures, datasets, and adversarial attacks. The reported compression rate (c.r.) is the percentage of pruned parameters relative to the baseline network.

**UCM Dataset Benchmarks (Table 2):**
- **Compression:** RobustDLRT achieves high compression rates (e.g., 95.84% for VGG16, 94.59% for VGG11, 87.9% for ViT-16b), comparable to or slightly higher than non-regularized DLRT.
- **Clean Accuracy:** The method maintains clean accuracy close to or, in some cases, slightly exceeding the uncompressed baseline. For VGG16, RobustDLRT achieves 94.61% clean accuracy (baseline: 94.40%).
- **Adversarial Accuracy:**
 - **ℓ2-FGSM:** RobustDLRT consistently outperforms non-regularized DLRT across all architectures and attack strengths. For VGG16, it recovers or approaches baseline adversarial accuracy (e.g., 78.68% at $\epsilon=0.1$ for RobustDLRT vs. 76.40% for baseline).
 - **Jitter:** RobustDLRT shows significant improvement over non-regularized DLRT, recovering a notable portion of the lost accuracy compared to the baseline.
 - **Mixup:** RobustDLRT demonstrates substantial gains, nearly doubling the baseline accuracy for VGG16 at $\epsilon=0.75$ (47.31% vs. 23.05%). This suggests improved robustness against scale-invariance attacks potentially included in Mixup.

**Cifar10 Dataset Benchmarks (Table 2):**
- Similar trends are observed on the Cifar10 dataset. RobustDLRT achieves high compression rates (e.g., 94.18% for VGG16, 94.67% for VGG11, 75.21% for ViT-16b) and maintains clean accuracy near baseline levels.
- **Adversarial Accuracy:** RobustDLRT consistently outperforms non-regularized DLRT across all tested attacks (ℓ2-FGSM, Jitter, Mixup) and architectures. Particularly for Mixup, RobustDLRT dramatically increases adversarial accuracy, for instance, from 16.12% (baseline) or 38.98% (DLRT) to 81.26% for VGG16 at $\epsilon=0.75$.

**Comparison to Literature on CIFAR10 (Table 4 and 7):**
- **ℓ1-FGSM (Table 4):** RobustDLRT (94.35% c.r.) achieves higher adversarial accuracy than all listed methods (including Cayley SGD, Projected SGD, CondLR, LoRA, SVD prune) for VGG16 under ℓ1-FGSM attacks across all $\epsilon$ values, even surpassing the baseline accuracy. It also achieves a significantly higher compression ratio (at least 15% higher than the next best compression method, CondLR).
- **ℓ2-PGD (Table 7):** RobustDLRT (94.18% c.r.) demonstrates competitive adversarial accuracy to other robustness-improving methods, especially considering its high compression rate. While some non-compressed methods achieve higher adversarial accuracy, RobustDLRT outperforms other compressed methods (CondLR, LoRA, SVD prune) at similar or higher compression rates.

**ImageNet1k Dataset Benchmark (ViT-32l) (Table 3):**
- RobustDLRT, with a 57.98% compression rate, consistently yields higher Top-1/Top-5 accuracy under ℓ2-FGSM and Jitter attacks compared to non-regularized DLRT.
- Notable gains (e.g., +9 points in Top-1 accuracy for ℓ2-FGSM at $\epsilon=0.3$) are observed at larger perturbation strengths, indicating enhanced robustness.
- The clean accuracy remains close to the baseline.
- **Computational Overhead:** On an A100 GPU, RobustDLRT training requires 27m 51s per epoch compared to 26m 07s for DLRT, representing an overhead of approximately 3%, which is considered computationally scalable.

**Black-Box Attacks (Table 5):**
- When adversarial examples are generated using a baseline full-rank network and then evaluated on low-rank networks (where the attacker has no knowledge of the low-rank compression), the low-rank training itself provides some defense.
- RobustDLRT significantly improves adversarial robustness compared to unregularized DLRT in this black-box scenario. For VGG16, RobustDLRT achieves 84.76% accuracy under ℓ2-FGSM with $\epsilon=1.0$, while the baseline drops to 33.23% and DLRT to 76.00%.

**Adversarial Training (Table 6):**
- RobustDLRT is compatible with adversarial training (using ℓ2-FGSM attacked images).
- It benefits from adversarial training, outperforming non-regularized DLRT and approximately matching the non-compressed baseline in adversarial accuracy under adversarial training conditions.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the proposed RobustDLRT method can achieve significant neural network compression (up to 94% parameter reduction) while maintaining or improving clean data accuracy and substantially enhancing adversarial robustness across diverse architectures, datasets, and attack types. The method demonstrates competitive or superior performance compared to existing literature, particularly when considering both compression and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several significant contributions with potential broad impacts across various applications of neural networks.

Firstly, the development of **RobustDLRT** addresses a critical challenge in artificial intelligence: the simultaneous achievement of model compactness and adversarial robustness without compromising accuracy. The demonstrated ability to achieve up to 94% parameter reduction while maintaining or improving adversarial accuracy relative to uncompressed baselines is significant. This directly facilitates the deployment of advanced deep learning models on **resource-constrained edge devices**, such as UAVs, surveillance sensors, or mobile platforms, where memory, computational power, and energy are severely limited. By enabling complex AI capabilities in such environments, the work expands the practical applicability of neural networks beyond data centers.

Secondly, the method's effectiveness against various adversarial attacks, including **ℓ2-FGSM, Jitter, Mixup, ℓ1-FGSM, and PGD**, and its performance in **black-box attack scenarios** and alongside **adversarial training**, suggests a more resilient and trustworthy class of compressed models. This enhanced robustness is crucial for applications where reliability and security are paramount, such as autonomous systems, critical infrastructure monitoring, and medical imaging, where adversarial perturbations could lead to critical failures or misinterpretations. The observed significant improvement in robustness, particularly against attacks like Mixup which involve scale invariance, indicates a more generalizable defense mechanism.

Thirdly, the **theoretical guarantees** provided by the spectral regularizer and the explicit bound on the condition number of each layer's low-rank core contribute to a deeper understanding of adversarial robustness in compressed networks. This interpretability of spectral metrics enhances the **trustworthiness and analyzability** of the compressed models, which is essential for ensuring transparency and accountability, especially in critical decision-making systems. The connection to established dynamical low-rank integration schemes also opens avenues for further theoretical and algorithmic advancements in the field.

Fourthly, the **computational efficiency** of RobustDLRT, with only an approximate 3% overhead during training on ImageNet, indicates that the gains in robustness and compression do not come at a prohibitive training cost. This makes the method practical for adoption, even for large-scale models and datasets. The fact that the approach is **model- and data-agnostic** further increases its potential applicability across a wide range of neural network architectures and tasks.

In summary, the significance of this work lies in its comprehensive solution to the long-standing trade-off between model efficiency and robustness. By enabling compact, accurate, and adversarially robust neural networks, RobustDLRT has the potential to:
- **Advance computer vision models** by making them more accessible for deployment in diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Enhance energy efficiency** of AI systems, contributing to more sustainable computing.
- **Improve the trustworthiness** and reliability of AI applications in sensitive domains.
- **Open new research directions** at the intersection of numerical linear algebra, machine learning theory, and practical adversarial defenses.
