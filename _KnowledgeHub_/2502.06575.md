# Predictive Red Teaming: Breaking Policies Without Breaking Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google DeepMind and Princeton University developed RoboART, a framework for "predictive red teaming" that assesses visuomotor robot policy vulnerabilities against environmental changes using generative image editing and anomaly detection without requiring hardware evaluations. The approach accurately predicted performance degradation across 12 factors, enabling targeted data collection that improved policy success rates 2-7x in specific vulnerable conditions and 2-5x in other untargeted conditions.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policies exhibit brittleness and unpredictable vulnerabilities to environmental variations (e.g., lighting, distractors, object placement).
- Discovering these vulnerabilities through hardware evaluations is costly, time-consuming, and impractical for comprehensively testing diverse off-nominal scenarios.
- Scalable methods are needed to predict policy generalization capabilities in complex and varied environments prior to physical deployment.

## Method

- The **RoboART** (==Robotics Automated Red Teaming==) framework introduces a ==two-step pipeline: edit and predict==, specifically for visuomotor policies relying on RGB images.
- The "edit" step uses ==language-conditioned diffusion models== (e.g., Imagen 3) to synthetically modify nominal RGB observations, simulating various environmental changes, with a ==Vision-Language Model (VLM) critic== filtering for realism and relevance.
- The "predict" step utilizes a ==policy-specific anomaly detector== in the policy's latent embedding space to quantify how 'far' edited observations are from nominal, using anomaly rates to approximate expected performance degradation for each environmental factor.

## Results

- **RoboART** accurately identified policy vulnerabilities with high Spearman rank correlations (**0.8** for the hybrid policy, **0.7** for the vanilla diffusion policy) and low average prediction errors (**0.10** and **0.19**, respectively) compared to hardware evaluations.
- Targeted data collection, guided by **RoboART**'s predictions, improved the fine-tuned policy's performance by **2-7x** in the three off-nominal conditions where additional data was acquired.
- The fine-tuned policy also demonstrated cross-domain generalization, showing **2-5x** performance improvements in other background and lighting conditions for which no additional data had been collected.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy-specific anomaly detection, applied to synthetically altered observations within the policy's latent space, can effectively predict performance degradation due to hypothetical environmental changes before physical robot execution.
> - Generative image editing, when guided by precise language prompts and validated by VLM critics, provides a scalable method to create diverse and realistic off-nominal visual scenarios for proactive vulnerability assessment.
> - Identifying specific environmental conditions that cause performance degradation offline allows for targeted data collection, which can then be used to efficiently fine-tune and significantly improve policy robustness and generalization.
