# RoboCousin: Build Your Own Simulation Playground for Robust Bimanual Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboCousin is an extensible simulation data-generation platform for bimanual manipulation, built on RoboTwin 2.0. It converts object images into simulator-ready URDF assets with collision geometry, physical metadata and automatic grasp-contact candidates, then builds digital-cousin tabletop and room scenes plus navigation segments. It releases 3,000+ annotated objects, 50 backgrounds and over one million expert trajectories across 50 tasks, with validated real-robot transfer.

## Problem

- Existing simulation pipelines generate rollouts inside closed asset libraries and predefined scenes, so more trajectories expand volume without expanding object or environment support.
- Broad 3D collections and generative models give plausible geometry but lack accurate scale, collision meshes, physical attributes and grasp annotations needed by planners and expert policies.
- Blind domain randomization perturbs appearance arbitrarily while exact digital twins are costly and narrow; most synthetic demos also start at a fixed tabletop pose.

## Method

- **Digitalize Anything**: SAM/SAM 3 segmentation plus ==SAM 3D reconstruction== into URDF, with a ==chain-of-thought converter== using 8-level ranked friction and cube-reference scale refinement.
- ==Automated contact-point synthesis== normalizes geometry, extracts AABB/OBB, and a ==symmetry-aware selector== picks a ring-of-side-grasps mixed strategy or opposing-face OBB pairs, then filters infeasible grasps.
- **Digital-cousin generation** separates a JSON semantic layout with ontop support graph from asset instantiation, plus ==A* navigation== on inflated occupancy maps for staged table docking.

## Results

- Automatic contact points reach **76%** pick-up success over **100** sampled trajectories versus **72%** for curated RoboTwin annotations, matching hand-made grasp labels.
- Generated assets match or beat RoboTwin 2.0 assets on all four real tasks with **250** demos each: bottle pick-up **80%** versus **40%**, marker box **65%** versus **50%**.
- Tabletop cousins beat single-twin training on three of four tasks: eyeglass case **55%** versus **0%**, adjust bottle **70%** versus **50%**, mouse pad **25%** versus **20%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling synthetic robot data means making the simulated world extensible, not just running more rollouts inside a fixed asset and scene distribution.
> - Geometric grasp synthesis with symmetry-aware strategy selection and feasibility filtering can replace manual contact annotation without losing executable-grasp quality.
> - Semantically constrained cousin variation beats exact twins for transfer, but gains shrink on longer placement tasks where contact precision dominates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual manipulation is critical for numerous complex real-world tasks. Developing robust policies for such tasks, especially for general-purpose vision-language-action models, necessitates extensive and diverse training datasets. However, collecting high-quality data on physical robots is typically a costly, slow, and unsafe process that is difficult to scale. Simulation environments offer a compelling alternative by enabling efficient data generation with automated supervision and controlled variability.

Current simulation-based data generation pipelines, while capable of producing large volumes of data, frequently operate within closed ecosystems. These systems rely on predefined asset libraries, a fixed set of scenes, and task templates. This closed nature means that integrating new objects or environments, observed in the real world, demands significant manual effort for 3D reconstruction, collision model creation, physical property estimation, interaction annotation, and task integration. Consequently, the diversity of objects and environments supported by these simulators remains largely static, even as the volume of generated trajectories increases.

RoboCousin addresses this limitation by aiming to make the simulated world itself extensible rather than merely accelerating data generation within existing, fixed worlds. It positions itself as a platform that bridges the gap between real-world observations and simulation-ready assets and scenes. The paper acknowledges existing simulation platforms like SAPIEN, RLBench, ManiSkill2/3, Habitat 2.0, BEHAVIOR-1K, RoboCasa, and BiGym as foundational infrastructure. While these platforms facilitate data generation and standardized evaluation, they often depend on curated asset collections, making the integration of novel user-provided objects a separate, labor-intensive engineering process.

Furthermore, RoboCousin builds upon and distinguishes itself from generative methods for simulation construction. While systems like GenSim, GenSim2, and RoboGen focus on task-level generation and automated learning curricula, and asset generation tools like Objaverse and EmbodiedGen provide broad visual coverage or physically grounded assets, RoboCousin emphasizes a complete pipeline from visual observation to reusable, manipulation-ready data. It integrates the generation of visual and collision representations, semantic and physical attributes, and grasp-contact candidates for both manipulable objects and background components, directly supporting expert trajectory collection.

The work also contributes to the concept of "digital cousins," which occupy a middle ground between unconstrained domain randomization and exact "digital twins." Previous work like ACDC and "From Seeing to Simulating" explored generating varied scenes while preserving semantic affordances. RoboCousin extends this by enabling the *generation* of new interaction-ready objects and the reconstruction of tabletop relations from observations, decoupling the "cousin" distribution from a fixed asset collection. By integrating this extensible cousin representation with automated expert trajectory generation, RoboCousin directly translates semantic scene variation into training data for real-world bimanual manipulation, thereby offering a practical path for expanding both the scale and coverage of synthetic data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind RoboCousin is to enhance the scalability of robot data generation by making the *simulated world itself extensible*, rather than merely increasing the rate of data production within a predefined and static environment. The authors identify three interconnected challenges that prevent current simulation pipelines from achieving this extensibility, and RoboCousin aims to address each of these:

1. **Challenge 1: Interaction-Ready Assets**: Existing 3D asset collections and generative models can produce visually plausible geometry, but robot learning requires assets to be "interaction-ready." This involves precise scale, accurate collision geometry, estimated physical attributes (e.g., mass, friction), and detailed manipulation annotations (e.g., grasp points, interaction regions). Without these properties, newly generated objects cannot be reliably used by motion planners or expert policies in simulation.
 - **Objective**: To develop an extensible real-to-sim asset pipeline capable of converting user-provided visual and textual observations into interaction-ready object and background assets. These assets are to be equipped with visual and collision geometry, semantic and physical metadata, and automatically generated grasp-contact candidates.

2. **Challenge 2: Task-Semantic Diversity Preservation**: While conventional domain randomization techniques introduce perturbations to improve robustness, arbitrary variations can lack functional relevance to a task's structure. Conversely, exact "digital twins" are expensive to construct and offer a narrow training distribution. There is a need for a method that provides diversity while preserving task-relevant semantic affordances and spatial relationships, a concept termed "digital cousins." Furthermore, most synthetic manipulation trajectories are limited to a fixed pose on a predefined tabletop, overlooking the broader environment.
 - **Objective**: To introduce a structured digital-cousin generation framework that systematically expands object, scene, and language diversity. This framework aims to preserve task-relevant affordances, spatial relations, and support structures, allowing for semantically related but varied object and scene instances. It also seeks to extend data generation beyond fixed-workspace tabletop tasks to include approach trajectories varying across room layouts, initial poses, and paths, thereby adding spatial and temporal diversity.

3. **Challenge 3: Unified Workflow for Asset-to-Scene-to-Data Generation**: The process of generating assets, constructing scenes, and then collecting data often involves disconnected tools and pipelines. A unified system that seamlessly integrates these stages is necessary to fully leverage the extensibility of the simulated world.
 - **Objective**: To provide a unified asset-to-scene-to-data workflow that allows users to expand the simulator from visual and textual inputs and then generate manipulation data with the resulting assets and scenes. This workflow includes support for both object-centric generation and tabletop/room-level scene construction, culminating in automated bimanual expert trajectory collection.

By addressing these objectives, RoboCousin aims to provide a practical pathway for significantly expanding both the scale and coverage of synthetic bimanual manipulation data, thereby facilitating the development of more robust and generalizable robotic policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboCousin is structured around a unified observation-to-data pipeline, built upon RoboTwin 2.0, that allows users to expand the simulator using visual and textual inputs and subsequently generate manipulation data. The pipeline consists of three primary interconnected modules: "Digitalize Anything" for asset generation, "Generate Digital Cousins" for scene diversity, and "Robots Should Move" for navigation-augmented data.

#### 3.1. Digitalize Anything: Automated Real-to-Sim 3D Asset Production

This module focuses on automatically converting visual and textual inputs into simulation-ready digital assets, covering objects for manipulation, interaction annotations, and scene-level assets for background construction.

- **Image to 3D Asset Generation:**
 - **Object Extraction:** The pipeline supports both point-based interactive segmentation using SAM and text-guided segmentation with SAM 3, providing semantic constraints for accurate object isolation from cluttered backgrounds.
 - **3D Reconstruction and Conversion:** Extracted objects are reconstructed into textured 3D meshes using SAM 3D and then converted into URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) assets.
 - **Semantic and Physical Metadata Inference:** A vision-language model estimates crucial attributes like category, scale, mass, friction coefficients, and color from multi-view renderings. A Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-style converter is employed to enhance physical parameter consistency, including a ranked 8-level friction estimation.
 - **Scale Refinement:** A vision-in-the-loop step corrects scale-level inaccuracies by comparing the generated asset against a virtual 10cm reference cube.
 - **Output:** The final asset includes both visual and collision geometry and can be integrated into the simulator as various types of actors or static objects.

- **Contact Point Inference:** This module automates the synthesis of manipulation-ready contact points from generated assets.
 - **Geometry Normalization:** Asset geometry is normalized, and Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) and Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) representations are extracted to estimate spatial occupancy, principal axes, and grasp-relevant dimensions. Additional alignment and scaling are applied for thin or unusually scaled objects to ensure compatibility with simulator grippers.
 - **Grasp Candidate Generation:** Two strategies are used:
 - **Mixed Strategy:** For objects with approximate rotational symmetry (e.g., bottles), a ring of side grasps is sampled alongside vertical approach poses.
 - **OBB Strategy:** For asymmetric or box-like objects, graspable OBB faces are identified, and opposing contact pairs are generated, aligned with face normals. Faces are pre-filtered based on gripper-width constraints.
 - **Symmetry-aware Selection:** A selector automatically chooses between the two strategies based on the variation of the object's projected extent under rotation.
 - **Candidate Filtering:** Generated contact poses are filtered to remove those violating gripper-width, simulator frame conventions, reachability, or motion-planning feasibility. Episodes lacking valid grasps after filtering are discarded.

- **Scene Asset Generation:** This prepares scene-level assets for household background construction.
 - **Structured JSON Layout:** Given a room prompt, the system generates a JSON layout specifying furniture categories, anchor objects, relative placement, spacing, rotations, and static attributes.
 - **Simulator Instantiation:** Each category-level entry in the JSON layout is matched to assets from a room-asset directory and loaded into the simulator with specified poses and physical states, allowing for consistent instantiation while retaining compatibility with later randomization.

#### 3.2. Generate Digital Cousins: Semantically Meaningful Diversity

This module introduces digital-cousin-style randomization to enhance policy robustness by expanding the training distribution in a semantically meaningful manner.

- **Tabletop Digital Cousin:** From a single RGB image of a real tabletop scene:
 - **Information Extraction:** Object-level information (segmentation, depth, recaptioning) and relative tabletop layout are extracted.
 - **Asset Matching:** Extracted objects are matched to appropriate assets (actor and non-actor) in the RoboTwin asset library.
 - **Orientation Estimation:** Per-instance object orientation is estimated via camera-pose-aware snapshot rendering.
 - **Structured Layout:** The generated tabletop cousin is represented as a structured layout containing object labels, relative positions, matched asset classes, candidate instances, and an explicit `ontop` support graph to preserve stacking relations. This layout enables training on semantically grounded real observations with varied object identities and geometries.

- **Background Asset Digital Cousin:** Builds on the scene-level asset representation:
 - **Instance Randomization:** Concrete mesh instances assigned to each category-level layout entry are randomized (e.g., different types of lamps on tables).
 - **Preservation of Affordances:** This maintains the functional and spatial affordances of the room while varying its visual appearance and geometric details, providing diverse but semantically coherent contexts.

#### 3.3. Robots Should Move: Navigation-Augmented Data Collection

This module integrates room-scale environments with mobile navigation to extend data generation beyond fixed-tabletop tasks.

- **Scene-Consistent Navigation Map:** The JSON layout from scene generation is used to resolve and project furniture/room object meshes onto the ground plane, forming conservative obstacle polygons. Robot base collision meshes are parsed and projected to derive a navigation footprint. Obstacles are inflated by this footprint and a safety margin to create a 2D occupancy map consistent with the rendered scene.
- **Planning and Table Docking:** An A\* algorithm plans a path from a user-specified initial collision-free pose to a target pose (typically the nominal tabletop data-collection pose). A staged docking procedure is used for final approach near the table.
- **Simulation Replay:** The planned route (sequence of planar poses) is replayed, moving the Aloha-AgileX embodiment through alternating rotations and translations. This allows navigation to terminate directly at the pose expected by the manipulation-data pipeline, extending fixed-workspace arm trajectories into longer navigation-and-manipulation episodes.

#### 3.4. RoboCousin Data Generator, User Interface, and Large-Scale Dataset

- **RoboCousin-OBD:** A released object-background dataset comprising over 300 object categories, 3000 instances, and 50 digital-cousin-style background scenes, all reconstructed from internet images and annotated with physical attributes and interaction points.
- **Interactive Multi-Mode Pipeline:** An interactive user interface supports three operational modes: Interactive Asset-Centric Generation, Tabletop Digital-Cousin Randomization, and Scene-Scale Mobile Manipulation. This interface allows for configuration, asset inspection, and monitoring, with human-in-the-loop verification at key stages.
- **Data Generation:** Using this pipeline, over one million dual-arm manipulation trajectories across 50 bimanual tasks were pre-collected.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RoboCousin addressed three primary questions through controlled simulation and real-robot experiments, with policies typically post-trained from a π0.5 base checkpoint on Aloha-AgileX simulation data and deployed on an ARX AC-One real-robot setup.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Automatic Contact-Point Generation

- **Objective**: To determine if automatically generated contact points can support executable grasps in simulation.
- **Method**: A simplified pick-up task (grasp and lift) was used, comparing RoboCousin's automatically generated contact annotations with manually curated ones from the original RoboTwin library for a diverse set of manipulable objects.
- **Results**: The automatically generated contact points achieved a 76% success rate in the pick-up task, slightly exceeding the 72% success rate of the original curated annotations across 100 randomly sampled trajectories.
- **Finding**: The automated pipeline produces reliable and executable contact points, demonstrating its capability to convert reconstructed meshes into interaction-ready assets without manual annotation, thereby removing a significant bottleneck.

#### 4.2. Sim-to-Real Evaluation of Generated 3D Assets

- **Objective**: To assess whether policies trained with RoboCousin's automatically reconstructed assets transfer effectively to real-world manipulation.
- **Method**: Policies were trained with 250 successful simulated trajectories using either RoboCousin-generated assets or their corresponding RoboTwin 2.0 assets. Four tasks of increasing complexity were evaluated on the ARX AC-One real robot: Pick_Up_Bottle (grasp and lift), Place_Microphone_in_Basket (grasp and place), Place_Mouse_Pad (transport and placement), and Place_Marker_Box (orientation-constrained placement).
- **Results**:
 - Pick Up Bottle: RoboCousin 80%, RoboTwin 40%.
 - Place Microphone in Basket: RoboCousin 75%, RoboTwin 75%.
 - Place Mouse Pad: RoboCousin 40%, RoboTwin 30%.
 - Place Marker Box: RoboCousin 65%, RoboTwin 50%.
- **Finding**: Policies trained with RoboCousin assets matched or outperformed those trained with original RoboTwin 2.0 assets across all four tasks, with a notable improvement in the Pick_Up_Bottle task. This indicates that automatically generated assets provide effective sim-to-real supervision for various manipulation tasks.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Evaluation of Tabletop Digital Cousins

- **Objective**: To investigate whether training on a distribution of tabletop digital cousins improves real-world robustness compared to training on a single digital twin.
- **Method**: Policies were trained with 400 successful simulated trajectories. The "Cousin" condition involved training on multiple scenes that varied object instances and appearances while preserving task-relevant semantic affordances. The "Twin" condition involved training on a single simulation scene closely matching an observed real tabletop. Four tasks were evaluated on the ARX AC-One: Pick_Up_Eyeglass_Case, Place_Spray_Bottle_in_Basket, Adjust_Bottle, and Place_Mouse_Pad.
- **Results**:
 - Pick Up Eyeglass Case: Cousin 55%, Twin 0%.
 - Place Spray Bottle in Basket: Cousin 35%, Twin 40%.
 - Adjust Bottle: Cousin 70%, Twin 50%.
 - Place Mouse Pad: Cousin 25%, Twin 20%.
- **Finding**: Training with tabletop cousins improved transfer performance on three of the four evaluated tasks (Pick_Up_Eyeglass_Case, Adjust_Bottle, Place_Mouse_Pad), showing a substantial gain for Pick_Up_Eyeglass_Case. Performance remained competitive with single-scene twin training on the remaining task (Place_Spray_Bottle_in_Basket). This suggests that structured variation through digital cousins can enhance policy robustness and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboCousin presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by directly addressing the scalability and diversity limitations inherent in current simulation-based data generation for bimanual tasks. Its integrated approach, moving from observation to interaction-ready assets, diverse scenes, and expert trajectories, offers a practical and automated pathway that was previously bottlenecked by manual engineering efforts.

The **extensibility of the simulated world** itself is a key impact. By enabling users to convert arbitrary visual observations into simulation-ready assets, RoboCousin shifts the paradigm from relying on fixed, curated asset libraries to a more dynamic and expandable simulation environment. This lowers the barrier for incorporating novel objects and environments into training pipelines, fostering broader applicability to diverse real-world scenarios.

The demonstrated **effectiveness of automatically generated interaction annotations and assets** in sim-to-real transfer is a crucial finding. The fact that policies trained with RoboCousin's automatically derived contact points and assets perform comparably to or better than those relying on manually curated data underscores the system's ability to generate high-fidelity, functionally relevant simulation content. This outcome can accelerate research and development in robotics by significantly reducing the labor and expertise required for asset preparation.

Furthermore, the validation of **digital cousins in improving real-world robustness** suggests a viable strategy for enhancing policy generalization. By systematically varying compatible object instances and backgrounds while preserving task-relevant semantics, RoboCousin's approach to digital cousins provides a powerful method for exposing policies to a richer yet functionally consistent training distribution. This can lead to policies that are more resilient to the inherent variability of real-world environments.

The integration of **room-level scene generation and navigation-augmented data collection** extends the utility of simulation beyond static tabletop setups. This capability is particularly relevant for mobile bimanual platforms, enabling the generation of more complex, long-horizon tasks that combine navigation with manipulation, thereby increasing the ecological validity of simulated training data.

The release of **RoboCousin-OBD and over a million expert trajectories** provides a valuable public resource for the robotics community. This dataset, coupled with the unified asset-to-scene-to-data workflow, equips researchers with tools and data to advance robust bimanual robotic manipulation.

In summary, RoboCousin offers a practical, scalable, and validated solution for addressing core challenges in generating diverse and effective training data for bimanual manipulation. Its potential impact lies in fostering more generalizable robot learning, reducing development costs, and expanding the scope of tasks that can be robustly tackled by robotic systems in varied real-world settings. Future work, as noted by the authors, will focus on broadening evaluation, improving physical calibration, and refining task-aware cousin generation to further enhance its capabilities for complex, contact-sensitive, and long-horizon manipulation tasks.
