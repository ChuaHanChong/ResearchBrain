# LpWM: A Case for Sparse Representations in World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LpWM is a JEPA world model whose encoder produces non-negative sparse latents via RDMReg (Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization), matching features to a Rectified Generalized Gaussian instead of dense isotropic Gaussian targets. Sparse codes lower the predictor capacity needed for planning, beating dense LeWM by up to 57% success rate on PushT at intermediate predictor capacity.

## Problem

- Prior JEPA anti-collapse regularizers like VCReg and SIGReg enforce dense representations where nearly all latent coordinates are nonzero, with no examination of alternate geometries.
- It is unclear whether dense representations are the most favorable latent geometry for modeling action-conditioned dynamics rather than just preventing collapse.
- Existing anti-collapse targets such as VICReg's second-order moment constraints leave the target distribution underspecified, weakening their role as dynamics-friendly priors.

## Method

- ==RDMReg== (Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization) matches per-timestep encoder feature marginals under random projections to a Rectified Generalized Gaussian distribution via 2-Wasserstein discrepancy, inducing sparsity.
- ==RepReLU== (reparameterized ReLU) terminal activation on encoder/predictor MLP projectors yields exact zeros in the forward pass while routing GeLU gradients backward to avoid dying-ReLU pathology.
- A **predictor-complexity ladder** (Deep/Shallow-AdaLN, MLP-LTV, MLP-LTI, LTI variants) built on a ViT encoder plus AdaLN-zero action-conditioned Transformer predictor is swept to test sparse versus dense codes across latent dimensions.

## Results

- On PushT, sparse **LpWM** outperforms dense LeWM by **24-57%** with MLP-LTI(k), **36-45%** with MLP-LTV(k), and **11-23%** with LTI(k) predictors at intermediate capacity, while parity holds at high-capacity Deep-AdaLN.
- On Piecewise navigation, LpWM reaches **84.7%** success on random goals versus **65.3%** for LeWM, and the binary support decodes ground-truth zone identity at **94-99%** accuracy, matching full-embedding decoding.
- LpWM consistently beats a VICReg-based dense JEPA variant across Deep-AdaLN, MLP-LTV, and MLP-LTI predictors, including at high capacity, unlike the SIGReg-based LeWM comparison where the gap closes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparse latent geometry can reduce the predictor capacity required to model action-conditioned dynamics, so simpler, cheaper predictors suffice where dense codes need larger models.
> - Sparsity benefits scale with the gap between predictor capacity and true dynamics complexity, vanishing once predictors are already expressive enough to saturate performance.
> - Sparse codes can become mode-factored, with binary support tracking discrete regimes and magnitudes tracking continuous state, but a temporal prior is needed to make the support track meaningful physical events rather than raw motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of model-based reinforcement learning and representation learning, specifically within the domain of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). JEPAs aim to learn compressed, latent representations of observations and model their dynamics, enabling planning and control.

Current JEPA methods, such as PLDM and LeWM, commonly employ anti-collapse regularization techniques (e.g., VCReg, SIGReg) that often result in dense latent representations, where most or all latent coordinates are non-zero. While effective for preventing representational collapse, the paper notes that it is not definitively established whether dense representations constitute the most advantageous geometry for modeling controlled dynamics.

This work diverges from the prevalent dense representation paradigm by exploring the utility of sparse representations. It investigates whether a different latent geometry—specifically, distributed sparse codes where a significant fraction of coordinates are exactly zero—can simplify the learning of action-conditioned latent dynamics and yield more interpretable structures. This exploration connects to prior work in discrete latent representations (e.g., DreamerV2, Variational Sparse Gating) but focuses on continuous sparse codes rather than strictly categorical variables. The paper also draws theoretical inspiration from the concept of linearizing nonlinear dynamics through high-dimensional (one-hot) encodings, relating to Koopman operator theory and symbolic abstractions.

By proposing LpWorldModel (LpWM) which specifically encourages non-negative sparse codes, this research aims to provide an alternative perspective on feature regularization for JEPAs, potentially leading to more efficient and interpretable world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Investigate the efficacy of sparse representations for action-conditioned latent dynamics modeling:** The central question is whether sparse latent representations can make action-conditioned latent dynamics simpler to model compared to dense representations. This stems from an observation that while dense representations are common in JEPAs, their optimality for dynamics modeling remains underexplored.
- **Understand the dynamical structure emerging from sparse representations:** Beyond performance, the authors aim to characterize the nature of the learned sparse codes, specifically whether they exhibit interpretable properties such as disentangling discrete dynamical regimes from continuous state information.

The motivation for exploring sparse representations is rooted in a theoretical insight and practical considerations:

- **Theoretical Motivation (Latent Linearization):** The paper formally posits that nonlinear Lipschitz dynamics can be approximated arbitrarily well by action-conditioned linear dynamics within a sufficiently high-dimensional one-hot latent space. This approximation’s rollout error diminishes as the latent dimension increases. While exact one-hot codes are impractical due to dimensionality, this theoretical result suggests that distributed sparse representations could serve as a practical, learnable relaxation that simplifies the underlying dynamics. The hypothesis is that such simplification could reduce the complexity required for the predictor model.
- **Hypothesis on Predictor Complexity:** Based on the linearization argument, the authors hypothesize that sparse codes might require less expressive predictors for successful planning compared to dense codes. This implies that simpler, lower-capacity predictor architectures could achieve comparable or superior performance when operating on sparse latent spaces.
- **Hypothesis on Interpretability:** The authors further hypothesize that sparse representations might naturally lead to more interpretable latent dynamics. Specifically, they propose that the *support* (i.e., the set of active, non-zero features) of a sparse code could encode discrete dynamical regimes or modes, while the *magnitudes* of these active features could capture continuous within-regime state variables. This mode-factored representation could enhance the transparency and understanding of the learned world model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the introduction of the LpWorldModel (LpWM) and its evaluation across various environments and predictor complexities.

#### 3.1. LpWorldModel (LpWM) Architecture

LpWM is a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) designed for model-based control from pixels. It comprises an encoder ($f_\theta$) and a predictor ($g_\phi$).

- **Encoder ($f_\theta$):** Maps observations ($o_t$) to a latent space, producing a latent representation ($z_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$). Following prior work, the encoder is a Vision Transformer (ViT) with its CLS token passed through a three-layer MLP projector.
- **Predictor ($g_\phi$):** Takes the current latent representation ($z_t$) and an action ($a_t$) to predict the next latent representation ($\hat{z}_{t+1}$). The predictor is a Transformer with AdaLN-zero action conditioning, ending with a three-layer MLP projector.
- **Loss Function:** The model is trained using a mean squared error (MSE) between the predicted latent $\hat{z}_{t+1}$ and the encoded target $z_{t+1} = f_\theta(o_{t+1})$, augmented with an anti-collapse regularization term:
 $\min_{\theta,\phi} \lVert \hat{z}_{t+1} - z_{t+1} \rVert^2 + \lambda_{\text{RDMReg}} \cdot R(z_{t+1})$.

#### 3.2. Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization (RDMReg)

The core component for inducing sparsity is the Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization (RDMReg) [Kuang et al., 2026b].

- **Mechanism:** RDMReg matches the distributions of rectified encoder features to a Rectified Generalized Gaussian (RGG) distribution.
- **Target Distribution:** The RGG distribution is derived from a Generalized Gaussian distribution, which is maximum-entropy under expected $\ell_p$-norm constraints. When $p \le 1$, these distributions inherently yield sparse targets. By default, the paper uses a Rectified Laplace distribution (where $\mu = 0$, $\sigma = \sqrt{p}^{1/2}$, and $p=1$), which is known to produce non-negative sparse codes. In contrast, prior methods like LeWM use isotropic Gaussian targets (equivalent to $p=2$ without rectification), resulting in dense representations.
- **Wasserstein Distance:** The discrepancy between the feature and target distributions is measured using the 2-Wasserstein distance.

#### 3.3. Architecture and Rectification for Sparsity

To achieve exact zeros in the latent codes, both the encoder and predictor MLP projector outputs are passed through a reparameterized ReLU (RepReLU) function. In the forward pass, RepReLU acts as a standard ReLU, enforcing exact zeros. In the backward pass, gradients flow through a GeLU function to mitigate the "dying ReLU" problem. The authors clarify that RDMReg itself can induce sparsity with standard ReLU, and RepReLU is primarily an optimization safeguard.

#### 3.4. Planning with the Learned World Model

During testing, the trained LpWM serves as the dynamics model for Model-Predictive Control (MPC).

- **Procedure:** Current and goal observations ($o_0, o_g$) are encoded to latents ($z_0, z_g$). An action sequence is then optimized using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to minimize the goal cost $C = \lVert \hat{z}_T - z_g \rVert^2$, where $\hat{z}_T$ is the predicted latent rollout at horizon $T$.

#### 3.5. Predictor Complexity Ladder

To test the hypothesis that sparsity simplifies latent dynamics, the authors evaluate LpWM and dense LeWM across a "predictor complexity ladder" (Table 1 of the paper). This ladder includes:

- **High-capacity models:** Deep-AdaLN(k) (6-block Transformer) and Shallow-AdaLN(k) (1-block Transformer) with AdaLN-zero action conditioning.
- **Intermediate-capacity models:** MLP$\circ$LTV(k) (Linear Time-Varying with a nonlinear readout), and MLP$\circ$LTI(k) (Linear Time-Invariant with a nonlinear readout).
- **Low-capacity models:** LTI(k) and LTI(1) (linear time-invariant state-space models).
- **Output Link:** For LpWM, the output link function $\sigma$ is RepReLU, while for LeWM, it's identity.
- **Context Length:** The history length `k` varies (1 for Wall, 3 for PushT).

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup and Environments

Experiments are conducted on several environments:

- **Wall:** A 2D navigation task through a doorway (simple dynamics).
- **PushT:** A harder environment involving pushing a T-shaped block to a target pose (contact-rich, more complex dynamics).
- **Piecewise:** A synthetic 2D navigation environment with zones of different force fields, providing known piecewise-affine dynamics (for interpretability studies).
- **OGBench-Cube:** A robotic arm manipulating a cube (contact-rich manipulation, for interpretability studies).

Maximal-update parameterization ($\mu$P) is used to stabilize training across varying latent dimensions for fair comparison.

#### 3.7. Temporal Jaccard (TJ) Regularization

For interpretability studies, an optional Temporal Jaccard (TJ) loss is introduced. This loss encourages stability of the binary support vector across adjacent frames, aiming to align support transitions with semantically meaningful dynamical events, rather than just rapid motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the role of sparse representations in latent world models:

#### 4.1. Sparsity and Predictor Complexity

- **Simple Environment (Wall):** For the Wall environment, both LpWM (sparse) and LeWM (dense) achieved nearly 100% closed-loop planning success even with the simplest linear LTI(1) predictor. This indicates that the dynamics of this environment are sufficiently simple to be modeled linearly in the latent space, regardless of representation density, leaving little room for a sparsity advantage to manifest.
- **Complex Environment (PushT):** On the more challenging PushT environment, where simple linear predictors failed for both models:
 - **Intermediate Capacity Predictors:** LpWM exhibited a substantial performance advantage. It outperformed dense LeWM by 24%–57% with MLP$\circ$LTI(k) predictors, 36%–45% with MLP$\circ$LTV(k), and 11%–23% with LTI(k). This suggests that sparse representations indeed simplify the latent dynamics, enabling less expressive predictors to achieve successful planning.
 - **High Capacity Predictors:** With the highest capacity predictors (Deep-AdaLN(k) and Shallow-AdaLN(k)), LpWM and LeWM performed similarly. This indicates a saturation of predictor complexity where highly expressive models can compensate for the less favorable geometry of dense representations.
 - **Comparison with VICReg:** LpWM also consistently outperformed a JEPA model regularized with VICReg (another dense representation method) across Deep-AdaLN(k), MLP$\circ$LTV(k), and MLP$\circ$LTI(k) predictors, including high-capacity settings. This suggests that the benefits of sparsity extend beyond specific dense isotropic Gaussian targets used in LeWM.
- **Sparsity Levels:** The $\ell_0$ norms of LpWM embeddings, as reported in Table 3, indicated that the learned codes were genuinely sparse, with approximately 30–65% of coordinates being active, confirming the intended effect of the regularization.

#### 4.2. Interpretable Latent Dynamics (Mode-Factored Representations)

- **Piecewise Environment:**
 - LpWM achieved an 84.7% success rate with random goals on Piecewise, outperforming LeWM (65.3%).
 - **Support-Encodes-Mode:** Analysis using the Jaccard index for support overlap showed that the binary support of LpWM's sparse representations strongly correlated with the discrete dynamical regimes (zones) in the Piecewise environment. The support changed sharply across zone boundaries, even in the absence of visual cues for the zones, implying it learned the dynamics structure rather than just visual features.
 - **Magnitudes-Encode-Continuous-State:** Linear probes confirmed a mode-factored code: the binary support decoded the ground-truth zone identity with near-perfect accuracy (94–99%), while continuous state information (agent position) was better decoded from the magnitudes of the active features.
 - **Long-Horizon Planning:** LpWM outperformed LeWM in planning success at all tested horizons, with the performance gap widening for longer horizons, suggesting that the organized structure aids long-term prediction.

- **OGBench-Cube Environment:**
 - **Sparsity Alone as Motion Detector:** Without additional temporal regularization, the support instability (1 - Jaccard index between consecutive frames) in LpWM correlated strongly with effector motion (r ≈ 0.87) but weakly with contact (r ≈ 0.05). This suggests that vanilla sparse representations might default to tracking dominant fast-varying signals like motion.
 - **Temporal Jaccard (TJ) for Regime Tracking:** Introducing the optional Temporal Jaccard (TJ) loss, which encourages support stability, significantly altered this behavior. As TJ weight increased, support instability became less correlated with effector motion and more aligned with cube motion and gripper contact (e.g., r for cube motion increased from 0.21 to 0.80). This indicates that with appropriate temporal priors, sparse support can track physical regime changes like object interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work demonstrates that sparse latent representations offer a viable and potentially advantageous alternative to dense representations in Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) for world modeling and control.

The key significance lies in two areas:

- **Reduced Predictor Complexity:** The empirical finding that sparse representations can enable successful planning with simpler, lower-capacity predictors is notable. This suggests that sparse latent spaces induce dynamics that are inherently easier for a model to learn. This could lead to more computationally efficient world models, which is particularly relevant as environments and tasks become more complex, requiring potentially larger latent spaces.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** The discovery of mode-factored representations, where the binary support encodes discrete dynamical regimes and feature magnitudes capture continuous within-regime state, significantly contributes to the interpretability of learned world models. This disentanglement of discrete and continuous aspects of dynamics could lead to a deeper understanding of how the model comprehends and predicts environmental transitions. Furthermore, the demonstration that explicit temporal priors can guide the sparse support to track semantically meaningful events, such as object contact, opens avenues for designing more transparent and controllable learning systems.

The potential impact of this research includes:

- **More Efficient Reinforcement Learning:** By reducing the required complexity of dynamics predictors, sparse representations could enable faster training and deployment of model-based reinforcement learning agents, especially in data-scarce or resource-constrained settings.
- **Improved Debugging and Trustworthiness:** Interpretable latent spaces can make it easier for researchers and practitioners to understand model failures, debug behaviors, and build more trustworthy AI systems by providing insight into the learned dynamics.
- **Foundation for Hybrid Discrete-Continuous Models:** The mode-factored nature of these representations could serve as a foundation for developing hybrid dynamics models that explicitly leverage both discrete (mode-switching) and continuous (within-mode evolution) components, potentially leading to more robust and generalized world models.

While the study acknowledges limitations, such as the curse of dimensionality in the idealized one-hot case and the need for temporal priors to guide semantic grouping in complex environments, it provides empirical evidence and theoretical motivation for continuing the exploration of sparse latent representations in the context of world models.
