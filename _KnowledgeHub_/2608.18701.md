# SoftVTBench: A Deformation-Aware Visuo-Tactile Dataset and Benchmark for Deformable-Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SoftVTBench is a simulated visuo-tactile dataset and closed-loop benchmark for gentle deformable-object manipulation. It pairs 4,000 expert demonstrations of multi-view RGB, dual-finger tactile RGB and marker motion with evaluator-only finite-element states, and scores policies by a Deformation-aware Success Rate that credits a rollout only when it completes the task within a pre-calibrated deformation tolerance. Across three policy families, task success hid deformation violations in every configuration.

## Problem

- General manipulation suites score only task completion on mostly rigid objects, so they cannot distinguish a gentle grasp from one that succeeds by crushing the object.
- Deformable-object benchmarks make shape change the objective, evaluate isolated grasps, or omit tactile input, never testing gentle handling over a complete multi-stage task.
- Visuo-tactile benchmarks expose touch to the policy but log no independent physical ground truth, so tactile signals would have to serve as their own evaluation target.

## Method

- A dual-stream recording: policy-visible multi-view RGB, dual-finger tactile RGB and marker fields, proprioception, language, and actions at 20 Hz, plus hidden finite-element nodal, contact, and drop states.
- Scripted per-object calibration sweeps gripper closure before training to fix a gripper envelope and a deformation tolerance, defining Deformation-aware Success Rate from rigid-motion-removed, size-normalized peak nodal displacement.
- A four-suite two-by-two design over object versus spatial variation and deformable versus matched rigid twins, with dual binary and continuous gripper action encodings and nine single-factor out-of-distribution conditions.

## Results

- All twelve in-distribution deformable configurations contained successes exceeding the tolerance, spanning 0.7-6.5% of successes for FastWAM, 8-20% for the VLA baseline, and 10-24% for Diffusion Policy.
- Deformation-aware scoring reverses rankings: Diffusion Policy visuo-tactile beats vision-only on task success (40.0 versus 37.4) yet loses on deformation-aware success (30.4 versus 33.6).
- Under shift, visuo-tactile variants won task success in six of six comparisons (sign test p=0.016) and deformation-aware success in five; matched ablation showed continuous gripper control alone reproduced the apparent tactile gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Terminal-success metrics conceal how a policy physically interacts; an evaluator-only physical channel, calibrated before training, turns interaction quality into an auditable, policy-independent criterion.
> - Attributing gains to touch requires matching control granularity; finer gripper actuation can reproduce the entire apparent tactile benefit, making unmatched action spaces a confound.
> - Exposing tactile observations does not guarantee useful fusion: touch helps most under distribution shift, and completion gains can widen rather than close the safety gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Robotic manipulation research commonly employs benchmarks to measure progress, focusing on task diversity, language grounding, and generalization capabilities. While existing benchmarks have advanced the evaluation of a policy's ability to complete a task, they predominantly assess success based on the final outcome, often with rigid objects. A significant gap exists in evaluating the *quality of physical interaction*, particularly in the context of deformable object manipulation. Policies might successfully complete a task, but in doing so, could induce excessive slip or compression, which is undesirable for delicate materials such as food, medical supplies, or soft packaging.

Prior work in robotic manipulation can be categorized into several areas:
- **General-purpose manipulation benchmarks** (e.g., Meta-World, RLBench, LIBERO, CALVIN, RoboCasa) standardize multi-task and generalization settings but primarily focus on task success with rigid objects, omitting the physical consequences of contact. Some safety-oriented benchmarks address constraint violations but also concentrate on rigid scenarios.
- **Deformable-object manipulation benchmarks** (e.g., SoftGym, PlasticineLab, ManiSkill2, MoDeSuite) model soft-body dynamics. However, in many of these, deformation itself is the task objective, or evaluation is limited to isolated grasps rather than complete manipulation sequences. Critically, many of these benchmarks do not incorporate tactile observations.
- **Visuo-tactile sensing and policy learning** has seen growth with simulators like Taxim and FOTS, and the integration of touch into vision-language-action models (e.g., VTLA, OmniVTLA). While these works explore how tactile information can be exposed to a policy, they typically evaluate its benefit through task success, often in contexts involving rigid or only incidentally deformable objects, and generally without independent ground truth for object deformation over complete tasks.

SoftVTBench directly addresses the combined absence of complete manipulation tasks, volumetric deformable objects, policy-visible tactile streams, and evaluator-only physical states that allow for explicit deformation scoring. No existing benchmark simultaneously offers all these elements, creating a need for a resource that can differentiate between a policy that achieves task completion through stable, gentle interaction and one that completes the task while mishandling the object.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for introducing SoftVTBench stems from the recognition that for deformable-object manipulation, the quality of physical interaction is as critical as task completion. Current evaluation methodologies, which predominantly focus on whether a task is completed, overlook potentially detrimental interactions such as excessive compression or slippage. This oversight is particularly relevant for applications involving delicate materials (e.g., food, medical instruments, soft consumer goods) where maintaining object integrity is paramount.

The research identifies a specific bottleneck: the lack of comprehensive visuo-tactile datasets that concurrently provide policy-visible contact observations (like vision and touch) and independent physical ground truth of object deformation throughout entire manipulation tasks. Vision provides crucial scene context for approaching and reaching objects, but its utility diminishes at the point of contact where occlusion often occurs. Tactile sensing, conversely, can directly capture critical contact parameters such as geometry, shear, slip, and local compression at the gripper-object interface, offering complementary information. However, tactile observations cannot serve as their own evaluation target; an independent measure of the object's physical state is required to assess the consequences of contact.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the SoftVTBench project are:

1. **Develop a Visuo-Tactile Dataset**: Create a rich dataset, SoftVTBench, that comprises expert demonstrations of deformable-object manipulation. This dataset is designed to synchronize multi-view RGB, dual-finger tactile RGB, marker motion, proprioception, language instructions, and robot actions, specifically for learning contact-aware behavior.
2. **Integrate Evaluator-Only Physical Ground Truth**: Alongside policy-visible data, the dataset must independently record the object's physical state using Finite-Element Method (FEM) states. This evaluator-only stream allows for objective measurement of deformation, decoupled from the policy's sensory inputs.
3. **Establish a Closed-Loop Benchmark**: Build a benchmark framework upon this dataset that facilitates the evaluation of policies in a closed-loop manner. This framework aims to assess not only task success but also the quality of physical interaction.
4. **Define a Deformation-aware Success Rate (DSR)**: Introduce a new metric, DSR, which rigorously credits a policy only if it both completes the instructed task and maintains object deformation within a pre-calibrated, object-specific tolerance. This metric is intended to explicitly quantify the gap between task completion and safe interaction.
5. **Enable Diagnostic Analysis**: Through matched rigid twins, controlled in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) protocols, and dual gripper-action encodings, the benchmark aims to support a controlled analysis of factors influencing manipulation performance, including deformability, tactile sensing, control granularity, and generalization capabilities under varying conditions.

Ultimately, the motivation is to advance the field by providing a comprehensive resource that allows researchers to study "not only whether a policy succeeds, but how it physically interacts with deformable objects and when touch improves that interaction."

### 3. Methodology and approach

The development of SoftVTBench involved a multi-stage process focusing on dataset construction, benchmark establishment, and policy evaluation.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction (SoftVTBench Dataset)

- **Simulation Environment**: All data generation and policy evaluations were conducted within Isaac Sim 4.5.0, utilizing Isaac Lab 0.41.3 and the GPU-accelerated PhysX 5 physics engine. Physics simulations ran at 60 Hz, with a control and logging rate of 20 Hz, ensuring synchronized recording of all data streams.
- **Assets**: The dataset includes over 50 assets, notably 10 distinct volumetric deformable objects (e.g., bakery-style items, geometric primitives) exhibiting variations in geometry, compliance, contact area, and appearance. Each deformable object has a corresponding visually and physically matched rigid twin (same mesh, texture, mass, but negligible deformation capacity).
- **Task Suites**: Four diagnostic suites were designed, each comprising 10 pick-and-place tasks with 1,000 expert demonstrations (4,000 total). These suites form a 2x2 design based on object type (deformable or rigid twin) and variation axis (object identity or spatial layout).
 - **Object-Soft/Rigid**: Fixed scene layout, varied manipulated object, testing adaptation to geometry/compliance.
 - **Spatial-Soft/Rigid**: Two identical instances in a scene, target identified by language instruction, testing target grounding.
- **Data Streams (Synchronized at 20 Hz)**:
 - **Policy-Visible Observations**:
 - Multi-view RGB images (third-person 1024x1024, wrist 512x512, resized to 224x224).
 - Dual-finger tactile RGB images (320x240) and 11x9 marker-motion fields, simulating GelSight Mini sensors using TacEx, Taxim for optics, and FOTS for marker motion. These capture contact appearance and local shear.
 - Proprioceptive states (end-effector pose, arm joint states, current gripper width).
 - Natural language instructions.
 - Robot actions: Arm (absolute end-effector pose targets: 3D position, 3D axis-angle orientation) and gripper (binary open-close command AND continuous closure target). This dual encoding supports ablation studies on control granularity.
 - **Evaluator-Only Physical States**: Hidden from policies, these streams include FEM nodal positions, object poses, contact events, and drop events. They provide ground truth for deformation measurement.
- **Demonstration Collection and Quality Control**: A scripted expert executed sequences (approach, grasp, lift, transport, place), drawing grasp closures from a calibrated gripper envelope (see below). Each trajectory was executed under physics and then rendered from replay to ensure synchronization. All 4,000 demonstrations underwent automatic integrity screening and human verification.
- **Dataset Splits**: A total of 4,000 expert demonstrations were collected.
 - **Training Set**: Used for policy learning.
 - **In-Distribution (ID) Evaluation Set**: 500 held-out initial states per suite (50 per task), with variation factors (lighting, spatial layout, physical properties like mass and stiffness) within the training support range.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Evaluation Set**: Nine single-factor conditions applied to the two deformable suites. These conditions involved changes outside the training range for dome-light intensity (67.5, 180, 270 vs. nominal 135), object mass (1.25x, 1.75x, 2.5x vs. nominal 1.0x), and Young’s modulus (0.5x, 0.8x, 2.0x vs. nominal 1.0x). Each OOD condition isolated a single shifted factor.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Establishment

- **Physical Ground Truth and Per-Object Calibration**:
 - **Deformation Measurement**: Each deformable asset is represented as a volumetric FEM mesh. Deformation is quantified as the peak rigid-motion-removed Root Mean Square (RMS) displacement of the FEM nodes, normalized by the initial bounding-box diagonal. This metric differentiates shape change from rigid-body movement and enables comparison across objects of different sizes.
 - **Per-Object Calibration**: Prior to any policy training, a scripted protocol (grasp, lift, hold) systematically swept gripper closure to establish object-specific interaction limits.
 - `g_min_o`: The minimum gripper closure ratio for a stable, slip-free grasp.
 - `τ_o`: The deformation tolerance, defined as the 90th percentile of peak normalized displacements observed during stable grasps.
 - `g_max_o`: The maximum gripper closure ratio that keeps deformation within `τ_o`.
 - This calibrated gripper envelope `G_o = [g_min_o, g_max_o]` guided expert data collection, and `τ_o` served as the fixed deformation criterion for evaluation.
- **Closed-Loop Evaluation Interface**: Policies interact with the simulated environment in a closed loop, receiving policy-visible observations and predicting action chunks (absolute end-effector pose targets and gripper commands). The simulator executes these actions, updates states, and returns the next observation. Episodes terminate on task success or drop, or after 300 control steps.
- **Metrics**:
 - **Task Success Rate (TSR)**: The fraction of episodes where the instructed object reaches its target region. This is a purely kinematic measure, independent of deformation.
 - **Deformation-aware Success Rate (DSR)**: The fraction of episodes that satisfy the TSR criterion AND maintain the peak normalized deformation (`R_max`) within the calibrated object-specific tolerance (`R_max ≤ 1`). The difference (TSR - DSR) quantifies "physically unsafe" successes.

#### 3.3. Policy Evaluation

- **Baseline Policies**: Three distinct policy families were evaluated: Diffusion Policy, π0.5, and FastWAM. For each, both vision-only (VO-C) and visuo-tactile (VT-C) variants were trained with continuous gripper control. Additionally, binary-control ablations (VO-B, VT-B) were performed for π0.5 to isolate the impact of control granularity.
- **Training and Inference**: Policies largely followed their official architectures and optimization procedures, with benchmark-specific adaptations for the observation/action interfaces (including tactile encoders and marker-motion adapters). All results represent single training runs from fixed schedules, without validation-based checkpoint selection or extensive hyperparameter sweeps across families.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted using SoftVTBench yielded several findings regarding policy performance, the diagnostic value of the benchmark, and the role of tactile sensing in deformable object manipulation.

#### 4.1. Task Success vs. Physical Interaction Quality

The benchmark revealed a consistent discrepancy between task completion and physical interaction quality. In all 12 in-distribution deformable-object configurations evaluated, the Deformation-aware Success Rate (DSR) was lower than the Task Success Rate (TSR). The difference, representing rollouts that completed the task but exceeded the calibrated deformation tolerance, ranged from 0.7% to 24% of each configuration's successful episodes. For instance, Diffusion Policy VT-C showed a 9.6 percentage point gap on Object-Soft and an 8.0 percentage point gap on Spatial-Soft, indicating that approximately 24% of its successful rollouts involved physically unsafe interactions. This finding demonstrates that success-only evaluation can conceal significant interaction quality issues, and that the DSR metric provides crucial additional information for policy assessment.

#### 4.2. Policy Family Differences in Interaction Quality

The rate of "hidden violations" (task completion with excessive deformation) varied considerably across policy families. Diffusion Policy exhibited a violation rate between 10% and 24% of its successful rollouts, while π0.5 's rate ranged from 8% to 20%. In contrast, FastWAM demonstrated a substantially lower violation rate, between 0.7% and 6.5%. FastWAM also achieved the highest spatial manipulation results while maintaining a minimal TSR–DSR gap (≤ 0.4 percentage points on spatial configurations). These results indicate that policies differ in their ability to regulate contact and prevent excessive deformation, highlighting that high task success does not inherently equate to gentle interaction.

#### 4.3. Impact of Deformability

The evaluation using matched rigid twins showed that the "cost" of handling deformable objects is not uniform but depends on the specific policy and task structure. For the vision-only continuous control (VO-C) variants, π0.5 experienced a significant drop of 18.4 percentage points in TSR when moving from rigid to soft objects on object variation tasks. However, Diffusion Policy and FastWAM showed minimal drops (2.6 and 2.0 points, respectively), suggesting negligible impact. Conversely, on spatial variation tasks, FastWAM performed 12.0 points higher on deformable assets, and Diffusion Policy 1.6 points higher. This suggests that factors other than contact physics, such as target grounding or spatial generalization, can sometimes dominate task difficulty, and that a soft-only benchmark might not fully disentangle these effects.

#### 4.4. Disentangling Tactile Gains from Control Granularity

A crucial ablation study with π0.5 demonstrated that apparent tactile gains can be confounded by the granularity of gripper control. On the Object-Soft suite, transitioning from binary vision-only control (VO-B) to continuous vision-only control (VO-C) increased TSR by 11.4 percentage points. Similarly, using binary visuo-tactile control (VT-B) increased TSR by 10.8 points compared to VO-B. However, combining both (VT-C) yielded a TSR of 41.4%, which was not an improvement over continuous control alone (VO-C). This indicates that simply adding tactile input might not be the sole driver of performance improvements if a finer action space is also introduced. The study also revealed that continuous control generally improved DSR across modalities, suggesting that finer actuation can contribute to better interaction quality.

#### 4.5. Tactile Sensing and Distribution Shift

Tactile sensing was more consistently associated with robustness under distribution shifts than with peak in-distribution performance. Under the nine held-out OOD conditions (pooled results), visuo-tactile variants (VT-C) achieved higher TSR than vision-only variants (VO-C) in all six policy–suite comparisons, and higher DSR in five out of six comparisons. This consistent directional improvement under shift, statistically significant, contrasts with the mixed results observed in-distribution. Specific factors like mass changes showed policy-dependent benefits from tactile cues, possibly due to their relevance to contact dynamics. However, adding touch under shift also widened the TSR–DSR gap for Diffusion Policy and π0.5 (policies with larger initial gaps), indicating that completion gains did not always align with improved deformation compliance. FastWAM, however, maintained a small TSR–DSR gap even with tactile improvements under OOD conditions, demonstrating that alignment between completion and interaction quality is achievable.

#### 4.6. Information Content of the Deformation Criterion

The deformation criterion (DSR) proved discriminative and attainable. It provided unique information, sometimes reversing rankings that TSR alone would suggest. For instance, on Object-Soft, Diffusion Policy VT-C had a higher TSR than VO-C, but a lower DSR. The criterion is also attainable, as expert demonstrations consistently remained within the calibrated deformation tolerance, with median peak normalized deformation well below the threshold. The observed violations by learned policies were therefore not inherited from the supervision. FastWAM's performance demonstrated that achieving high task success with minimal deformation violations is a tractable problem.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

SoftVTBench represents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation research by specifically addressing the critical, yet often overlooked, aspect of physical interaction quality in deformable-object manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering a New Evaluation Paradigm**: The introduction of the Deformation-aware Success Rate (DSR) shifts the evaluation paradigm beyond mere task completion. By integrating an objective, FEM-grounded measure of object deformation, SoftVTBench enables a more holistic assessment of robotic policies. This is particularly crucial for applications handling delicate or deformation-sensitive materials (e.g., food processing, medical robotics, soft goods manufacturing), where a successful task outcome must not compromise object integrity.
- **Comprehensive Diagnostic Resource**: The meticulously designed dataset and benchmark provide a rich diagnostic tool.
 - **Matched Rigid Twins**: Allow researchers to isolate the "cost" or benefit of deformability itself, disentangling it from other task complexities, thereby offering clearer insights into policy performance.
 - **Controlled ID/OOD Protocols**: Facilitate robust analysis of policy generalization and robustness under various environmental and physical shifts (e.g., lighting, mass, stiffness), which is essential for deploying robots in diverse real-world scenarios.
 - **Dual Gripper Action Encodings**: Enable precise ablation studies to differentiate the contributions of sensory modality (e.g., tactile input) from control granularity (e.g., continuous gripper control), clarifying the true sources of performance improvements.
 - **Policy-Visible vs. Evaluator-Only Streams**: This clear separation ensures an auditable and objective evaluation of physical interaction, preventing policies from overfitting to or manipulating their own evaluation metrics.
- **Guiding Policy Development**: The benchmark highlights that simply providing tactile observations does not guarantee effective multimodal fusion or improved physical interaction quality. This finding offers a clear challenge and direction for future research in developing more sophisticated visuo-tactile policies that can intelligently fuse sensory data to achieve both high task success and compliant interaction. The observed variability in "hidden violations" across policy families underscores the need for policies specifically designed for gentle handling.
- **Enabling Reproducible Research**: By providing a standardized dataset, simulation environment (Isaac Sim, Isaac Lab), and evaluation protocols, SoftVTBench promotes reproducibility and comparability of research findings in deformable manipulation. This common resource can accelerate progress by allowing researchers to build upon and rigorously compare different approaches.
- **Laying Groundwork for Real-World Applications**: While currently simulated, the detailed modeling of deformable objects and tactile sensing lays a strong foundation for future sim-to-real transfer studies. The insights gained from SoftVTBench about effective tactile sensing and gentle manipulation strategies in simulation are directly relevant to developing robust and safe robotic systems for real-world scenarios involving soft and deformable objects.
- **Revealing Fundamental Challenges**: The consistent observation that task success often conceals excessive deformation indicates a fundamental challenge in current robot learning paradigms. SoftVTBench makes this challenge explicit and measurable, encouraging the community to develop solutions that prioritize both operational efficiency and physical safety.
