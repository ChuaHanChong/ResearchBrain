# TWIST: Teacher-Student World Model Distillation for Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed TWIST, a framework for efficient sim-to-real transfer in vision-based model-based reinforcement learning by distilling a state-based teacher world model into a vision-based student model. TWIST achieved higher success rates in real-world robotic manipulation tasks and consistently outperformed conventional domain randomization and model-free baselines in simulated control environments.

## Problem

- High data requirements and safety concerns for training accurate vision-based world models directly in real-world robotics environments.
- Computational burden and data inefficiency associated with applying Domain Randomization (DR) directly to vision-based model-based RL for sim-to-real transfer.
- Lack of robust and efficient methods for sim-to-real transfer in vision-based model-based RL, often relying on privileged state information unavailable in real deployments.

## Method

- Trained a 'teacher' ==world model== using privileged ground-truth state information in a simulator to learn accurate environmental dynamics efficiently.
- Collected a dataset of corresponding domain-randomized image observations, states, actions, and rewards during teacher training.
- Distilled the learned dynamics of the frozen teacher world model into a vision-based 'student' world model using a combined loss that includes supervision from both real and imagined trajectories.

## Results

- TWIST consistently outperformed all baseline approaches across six continuous control tasks in the Distracting Control Suite, showing statistically significant improvements.
- Achieved significantly higher success rates in real-world manipulation tasks (Block Push and Block Lift) using a Franka Panda robot compared to naive Dreamer with DR, model-free RL, and other baselines.
- An ablation study confirmed that imagined distillation, particularly with added action noise, is a critical component for achieving high performance in world model distillation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging privileged state information in simulation through a teacher model allows for efficient learning of complex environment dynamics, which can then be transferred to a vision-based student model.
> - Distilling learned dynamics via 'imagined trajectories' (where the teacher rolls out and the student imitates) is crucial for robust knowledge transfer and improved long-horizon prediction.
> - Teacher-student world model distillation effectively addresses the data inefficiency and computational cost of directly applying domain randomization to vision-based model-based RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in solving complex control problems across various domains. Among RL paradigms, model-based RL (MBRL) has garnered attention due to its potential for improved sample efficiency, enhanced generalization, and faster adaptation compared to model-free approaches. MBRL operates by learning a world model of the environment, which is then utilized for planning or policy optimization. While MBRL has achieved considerable success in simulated environments, its application to real-world robotics, particularly with vision-based inputs, remains limited. This limitation stems from the significant data requirements to train accurate world models in physical environments, often necessitating unsafe or costly data collection.

A common strategy to mitigate the challenges of real-world data collection in robotics is sim-to-real transfer. This approach involves training an RL agent in a simulator, where data is readily available, and subsequently deploying the learned policy in a real environment. For vision-based tasks, where agents rely on image observations, a crucial step in sim-to-real transfer is bridging the "sim-to-real gap," which refers to the discrepancies between simulated and real-world sensor inputs and dynamics. Domain Randomization (DR) is a widely adopted technique to address this gap. DR exposes the policy to a wide range of randomized visual and physical parameters within the simulator during training, aiming to improve its robustness and generalization to the varied conditions of the real world.

However, DR introduces its own set of challenges. Training policies on highly randomized environments typically demands significantly more data, leading to increased computational costs and extended training times. The majority of existing work on sim-to-real transfer with DR has focused on model-free RL algorithms. Applying DR naively to model-based RL, especially for vision-based inputs, also results in a substantial increase in the data required to train an effective world model, making it computationally demanding. Prior attempts at transferring model-based methods like Dreamer from simulation to real robots have often relied on privileged state information, which is generally unavailable in real-world scenarios, or have been demonstrated only for environments with minimal discrepancies.

Within this context, the presented work positions itself by addressing an under-investigated area: efficient sim-to-real transfer for *vision-based model-based RL*. It aims to harness the sample efficiency benefits of MBRL while overcoming the computational burden and data inefficiency associated with applying DR for world model training. The approach draws inspiration from knowledge distillation and the use of privileged information, concepts that have been explored in model-free RL for policy transfer but are here applied to the world model itself. By focusing on distilling a state-based teacher world model into a vision-based student world model, the paper seeks to provide a method that enables effective sim-to-real transfer for complex, vision-based robotic tasks without requiring real-world state access or extensive real-world data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve efficient sim-to-real transfer for vision-based model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) by addressing the computational and data inefficiency issues associated with standard domain randomization (DR) when applied to world model training.

The motivation for this work arises from several interconnected challenges in real-world robotics:
1. **Limitations of Model-Free RL in Real-World Robotics**: While model-free RL has achieved notable successes, its application to real-world tasks is often hampered by its high sample complexity. Training model-free agents in physical environments typically requires a large volume of interactions, which can be time-consuming, costly, and potentially unsafe for robotic systems.
2. **Unrealized Potential of Model-Based RL**: Model-based RL (MBRL) offers advantages such as improved sample efficiency, broader generalization, and faster adaptation, making it theoretically appealing for real-world robotics. However, its practical application to vision-based real-world tasks is restricted because training a world model from scratch in the real world necessitates extensive and often unsafe data collection.
3. **Challenges of Sim-to-Real Transfer for Vision-Based MBRL**: Sim-to-real transfer is a common paradigm to circumvent real-world data collection. For vision-based tasks, domain randomization (DR) is employed to bridge the visual discrepancy between simulation and reality. However, DR significantly increases the data requirements for policy training, a problem that is exacerbated when applied to training a world model in MBRL. Naively training a vision-based world model with DR becomes computationally intensive and can require substantial resources and time to converge.
4. **Availability and Underutilization of Privileged Information in Simulators**: Simulators inherently provide access to privileged information, such as ground-truth state data, which is unavailable in real-world deployments. This information is valuable for efficiently learning environment dynamics but is typically not directly exploitable by vision-based agents designed for real-world deployment.
5. **Gap in Existing Sim-to-Real Approaches**: The majority of sim-to-real transfer research has concentrated on model-free RL. For model-based RL, existing transfer methods are limited, often relying on state information that is not available in real-world scenarios or demonstrating transfer only to highly similar real environments. There is a need for a robust and efficient method to transfer vision-based model-based RL policies trained in simulation with DR to diverse real-world conditions without requiring privileged information in the real world.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this research, through the proposed TWIST framework, include:
- To leverage privileged state information from a simulator to efficiently train an accurate "teacher" world model.
- To use this state-based teacher world model to supervise the learning of a "student" world model that operates on domain-randomized image observations.
- To achieve efficient and effective knowledge transfer from the teacher to the student world model, enabling the student to accurately represent environment dynamics from vision-based inputs despite domain variations.
- To demonstrate that this distillation process results in a vision-based world model suitable for sim-to-real transfer, capable of achieving high task performance and sample efficiency in both simulated and real-world robotics manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Teacher-Student World model Distillation for Sim-to-Real Transfer (TWIST), addresses the challenge of efficient sim-to-real transfer for vision-based model-based RL by leveraging privileged information from the simulator. The core idea involves training a teacher world model on readily available state information and then distilling its learned dynamics into a student world model that operates on domain-randomized image observations. The DreamerV2 model architecture serves as the foundation for learning the world models and associated policies.

**Problem Formulation**: The real environment is framed as a partially observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) where agents receive image observations ($o \in O$) and continuous actions ($a \in A$). In the simulator, in addition to randomized image observations ($o \in O$), direct access to privileged states ($s \in S$) is available. The objective is to maximize the expected discounted reward.

**Dreamer World Model**: TWIST utilizes Dreamer's Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM) to learn environment dynamics. The RSSM comprises several modules:
- **Sequence Model**: Computes a recurrent deterministic component $h_t$ based on the previous recurrent state, stochastic state, and action.
- **Representation Model**: Infers a stochastic posterior state $z_t$ from the current recurrent state and observation $x_t$.
- **Dynamics Predictor**: Predicts a prior stochastic state $\hat{z}_t$ from the current recurrent state, representing the environment's dynamics.
- **Reward Predictor**: Estimates the reward $r_t$ from the recurrent and stochastic states.
- **Decoder**: Reconstructs the input observation $x_t$ from the recurrent and stochastic states.
These modules are jointly trained to minimize a variational lower bound, encouraging accurate prediction of dynamics, rewards, and observation reconstruction. After training, the world model can generate imagined trajectories by rolling out without observations, using the prior $\hat{z}$ for state prediction. An actor-critic agent is then trained using these imagined trajectories.

**TWIST Approach Overview**:
1. **Teacher World Model Training**: A teacher world model (W$_{\text{teacher}}$) and its associated policy ($\pi$) are trained in the simulator using privileged *state information* ($s_t$) as input. Training from state information allows for efficient learning of accurate environmental dynamics within a compact latent space, as it is unaffected by visual appearance changes introduced by domain randomization.
2. **Domain-Randomized Data Collection**: During the teacher's training, a parallel dataset $D = \{(s_t, o_t, a_t, r_t)...\}$ is collected. This dataset contains tuples of privileged state information, corresponding *domain-randomized image observations*, actions, and rewards. The domain randomization applied to images includes varying background textures and object colors to ensure diversity and prepare for real-world generalization.
3. **Student World Model Distillation**: A student world model (W$_{\text{student}}$), which is vision-based, is trained to imitate the learned latent dynamics of the *frozen* teacher world model. The student takes the domain-randomized image observations ($o_t$) from dataset $D$ as input. This process aligns the student's latent representations with the teacher's, transferring knowledge of environment dynamics without requiring privileged state information for the student in deployment.

**World Model Distillation Loss**: The distillation process is governed by a combined loss function that aligns various components of the teacher and student RSSMs.

- **L$_{\text{distill}}$ (Equation 3)**: This component supervises the student based on trajectories sampled directly from the collected dataset $D$. It comprises three parts:
 - **Deterministic Representation Distillation**: An L2 loss penalizes differences between the recurrent deterministic components ($h_{teacher}$ and $h_{student}$) of the teacher and student models.
 - **Prior Distillation**: A KL divergence term aligns the prior distributions over stochastic states ($p(\hat{z}_{teacher}|h_{teacher})$ and $p(\hat{z}_{student}|h_{student})$) between the teacher and student.
 - **Posterior Distillation**: A KL divergence term aligns the posterior distributions over stochastic states ($q(z_{teacher}|h_{teacher}, s_t)$ and $q(z_{student}|h_{student}, o_t)$). Notably, the teacher's posterior uses privileged state $s_t$, while the student's uses domain-randomized image $o_t$.

- **L$_{\text{imagined}}$ (Equation 4)**: To enhance robustness and diversity, this component extends distillation to *imagined trajectories*:
 - Initial latent states are computed for both teacher and student from a trajectory sampled from $D$.
 - The teacher model generates an *imagined rollout* of length $H$ using its learned policy ($\pi$) and by adding random noise to actions for increased trajectory diversity.
 - The student model then generates its own imagined trajectory by replaying the *same sequence of actions* from the teacher's rollout.
 - L$_{\text{imagined}}$ then aligns the deterministic representations and prior distributions between these teacher-generated and student-replayed imagined trajectories. This bootstraps the dataset $D$ and helps the student to mimic the teacher's predictive dynamics more accurately over longer horizons.

The total loss function for world model distillation is $L = L_{\text{distill}} + L_{\text{imagined}}$. After this distillation, the vision-based student world model, combined with the policy learned in the teacher's world, is deployed in real-world environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TWIST through a series of experiments in both simulated and real-world robotics tasks, comparing its performance against several baselines. The findings address the efficacy of TWIST for efficient sim-to-real transfer using domain randomization (DR) and the importance of imagined trajectory distillation.

**Simulated Results (Distracting Control Suite)**
Experiments were conducted on six continuous control tasks from the Distracting Control Suite, which extends DMControl with visual distractions. The teacher world model was trained on ground-truth state information, while domain-randomized images (varying background textures and object colors) were collected for student distillation. Evaluation was performed on held-out environments with unseen background textures.

- **Performance Comparison**: TWIST consistently outperformed all baseline approaches, often by significant margins, across all six tasks (Cup Catch, Cartpole Balance, Cheetah Run, Hopper Stand, Walker Walk, Finger Easy Turn).
 - **Oracle Dreamer**: Represents an upper bound, trained on privileged information. TWIST approaches its performance more closely than baselines.
 - **Dreamer w/ DR**: A naive vision-based Dreamer trained directly with DR, showed lower performance, highlighting the computational burden and sample inefficiency of this approach.
 - **Dreamer w/o DR**: Performed poorly due to its inability to generalize to unseen scenes without DR.
 - **Dreamer State Recon.**: A vision-based Dreamer attempting to reconstruct state information, showed better performance than other baselines but still struggled compared to TWIST, indicating the difficulty of jointly learning task-relevant information and an actor-critic from limited samples.
 - **Asymmetric SAC**: A model-free RL baseline suitable for DR, performed comparably on simpler tasks like Cup Catch but struggled on more complex tasks, attributed to difficulties in efficiently learning task-relevant visual information.
- **Statistical Significance**: Aggregated Interquartile Mean (IQM) of normalized episodic rewards across 5 tasks, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, indicated that TWIST's performance difference from the baselines was statistically significant, as their confidence intervals did not overlap.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer for Manipulation Tasks**
The effectiveness of TWIST in real-world scenarios was assessed using a Franka Panda robot for two manipulation tasks: Block Push and Block Lift. In simulation, DR was applied to various visual and physical parameters (light brightness, robot/background/table/object textures, object friction).

- **Real-World Success Rates**: TWIST demonstrated significantly higher success rates in both the Block Push and Block Lift tasks compared to all baselines (naive Dreamer with DR, model-free RL, Dreamer State Recon., and Dreamer w/ DR trained with 1M samples).
 - For the Block Push task, which requires precise dynamics modeling, TWIST's high success rate indicated successful distillation and transfer of an accurate world model from simulation to reality.
 - Baseline methods often failed to solve the tasks, primarily due to the sample inefficiency inherent in training agents with DR.
 - Even with a higher number of samples (1M) for naive Dreamer with DR, its success rate remained low, further underscoring the sample inefficiency of the direct DR approach.
 - Dreamer State Recon. showed better performance than other baselines but still struggled to effectively learn from image observations during exploration for manipulation tasks.

**Ablation Study**
An ablation study investigated the contribution of key components of the distillation method, specifically imagined distillation and the addition of random noise to actions during imagined distillation, within the Distracting Control Suite.

- **Importance of Imagined Distillation**: The study revealed that imagined distillation (L$_{\text{imagined}}$) is a critical component for successful world model distillation. The performance difference between the full TWIST method and a variant without imagined distillation was statistically significant, based on non-overlapping 95% bootstrap confidence intervals of normalized IQM.
- **Role of Random Noise**: While less pronounced than imagined distillation itself, the addition of random noise to actions during imagined rollouts also contributed to performance, showing a noticeable, though smaller, improvement.
These results collectively highlight that aligning the latent dynamics during imagined rollouts is important for robust and accurate knowledge transfer.

In summary, the main findings indicate that TWIST effectively leverages privileged information through teacher-student world model distillation to achieve efficient and robust sim-to-real transfer for vision-based model-based RL. It significantly outperforms conventional DR and model-free methods in terms of sample efficiency and task performance in both simulated and real-world robotic environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TWIST framework represents a notable advancement in the field of real-world robotics by addressing a critical challenge: the efficient and robust sim-to-real transfer of vision-based model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Model-Based RL for Real-World Robotics**: Model-based RL, despite its theoretical advantages in sample efficiency, generalization, and adaptation, has seen limited application in real-world robotics, particularly for vision-based tasks. This work provides a practical methodology to overcome the prohibitive costs and risks associated with training world models directly in physical environments. By facilitating the transfer of MBRL, TWIST contributes to unlocking the full potential of these powerful algorithms for a wider range of real-world applications.

2. **Addressing the Sim-to-Real Gap More Efficiently**: Standard Domain Randomization (DR) is a widely used technique for sim-to-real transfer but is notoriously data-intensive and computationally expensive, especially when applied to vision-based inputs for complex tasks. TWIST offers a more sample-efficient alternative by leveraging privileged simulator information through teacher-student distillation. This efficiency reduces the time and computational resources required for training, making complex robotic learning more accessible and scalable. The empirical results demonstrate that TWIST significantly outperforms naive DR approaches, even those with substantially more training samples, indicating a substantial improvement in efficiency.

3. **Effective Utilization of Privileged Information**: Simulators are rich sources of privileged information (e.g., ground-truth states) that are typically unavailable in real-world deployments. TWIST provides a structured and effective way to harness this information by using it to train a robust teacher model. This knowledge is then strategically distilled into a student model that learns from more realistic, domain-randomized visual inputs, ensuring that the benefits of precise state information are transferred without requiring it at deployment time.

4. **Robustness and Generalization for Real-World Tasks**: The successful transfer to challenging real-world manipulation tasks (Block Push and Block Lift) demonstrates the robustness of the distilled world model. The ability to perform well in real environments, where sensory inputs and dynamics can differ from simulation, underscores TWIST's effectiveness in bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex behaviors requiring accurate dynamics modeling.

5. **Broad Applicability of the Distillation Paradigm**: The general approach of combining world model distillation with DR is presented as compatible with any model-based RL algorithm. This suggests that the principles of TWIST could be adapted and extended to other MBRL frameworks, potentially amplifying its impact across the broader research community and various robotic platforms.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work lays a foundation for future advancements in areas such as fine-tuning and reusing distilled world models. The mention of exploring the acquisition of new skills from few real-world observations suggests a pathway towards even greater autonomy and adaptability for robots, where pre-trained foundational models (like the distilled world model) could be rapidly specialized for novel tasks with minimal real-world interaction.

In summary, TWIST offers a practical and efficient solution for bringing the benefits of model-based reinforcement learning to real-world vision-based robotic applications. By mitigating the data inefficiency of traditional sim-to-real methods, it opens avenues for developing more capable, autonomous, and safely deployable robotic systems.
