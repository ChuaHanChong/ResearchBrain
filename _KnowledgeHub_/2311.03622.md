# TWIST: Teacher-Student World Model Distillation for Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed TWIST, a framework for efficient sim-to-real transfer in vision-based model-based reinforcement learning by distilling a state-based teacher world model into a vision-based student model. TWIST achieved higher success rates in real-world robotic manipulation tasks and consistently outperformed conventional domain randomization and model-free baselines in simulated control environments.

## Problem

- High data requirements and safety concerns for training accurate vision-based world models directly in real-world robotics environments.
- Computational burden and data inefficiency associated with applying Domain Randomization (DR) directly to vision-based model-based RL for sim-to-real transfer.
- Lack of robust and efficient methods for sim-to-real transfer in vision-based model-based RL, often relying on privileged state information unavailable in real deployments.

## Method

- Trained a 'teacher' ==world model== using privileged ground-truth state information in a simulator to learn accurate environmental dynamics efficiently.
- Collected a dataset of corresponding domain-randomized image observations, states, actions, and rewards during teacher training.
- Distilled the learned dynamics of the frozen teacher world model into a vision-based 'student' world model using a combined loss that includes supervision from both real and imagined trajectories.

## Results

- TWIST consistently outperformed all baseline approaches across six continuous control tasks in the Distracting Control Suite, showing statistically significant improvements.
- Achieved significantly higher success rates in real-world manipulation tasks (Block Push and Block Lift) using a Franka Panda robot compared to naive Dreamer with DR, model-free RL, and other baselines.
- An ablation study confirmed that imagined distillation, particularly with added action noise, is a critical component for achieving high performance in world model distillation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging privileged state information in simulation through a teacher model allows for efficient learning of complex environment dynamics, which can then be transferred to a vision-based student model.
> - Distilling learned dynamics via 'imagined trajectories' (where the teacher rolls out and the student imitates) is crucial for robust knowledge transfer and improved long-horizon prediction.
> - Teacher-student world model distillation effectively addresses the data inefficiency and computational cost of directly applying domain randomization to vision-based model-based RL.
