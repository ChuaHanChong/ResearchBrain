# Boolean Satisfiability via Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ImitSAT method leverages imitation learning to guide Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) solvers, reducing unit propagations, the main computational cost in Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL). By learning from 'KeyTrace' expert demonstrations, the approach consistently lowers propagation counts, leading to improved wall-clock performance on complex random 3-SAT instances and strong generalization across diverse structured SAT problems.

## Problem

- Hand-crafted branching heuristics in CDCL SAT solvers (e.g., VSIDS) lack adaptability to diverse instance structures.
- Unit propagation constitutes a substantial majority (80-90%) of a CDCL solver's wall-clock runtime, making its reduction key for efficiency.
- Existing learning-based branching methods face challenges like high exploration costs and instability in reinforcement learning (Graph-Q-SAT), or indirect influence on dynamic search decisions from initialization-based approaches (SATformer).

## Method

- Developed **KeyTrace**, an ==expert demonstration== extraction method that identifies 'surviving' decision and propagation events after backtracking, effectively eliminating unproductive search paths.
- Framed the branching problem as an autoregressive sequence modeling task, serializing the CNF formula and KeyTrace prefix into a single input for a Perceiver AR model.
- Trained the model via behavior cloning, providing dense, step-level supervision to predict the next expert decision, and integrated it into CDCL with a limited query budget and fallback to native heuristics.

## Results

- ImitSAT consistently achieved lower Median Relative Propagation Percentage (MRPP) across random 3-SAT test sets, demonstrating a substantial reduction in unit propagations (e.g., **0.73**-**0.83** for 3/5 calls vs. 1.00 for MiniSAT baseline).
- It exhibited strong generalization, outperforming prior learned methods on diverse structured SAT families (JNH, AIM, PARITY, PHOLE, PRET) without retraining, often achieving the lowest MRPP and highest win rates.
- On challenging random 3-SAT instances (61-100 variables) and some industrial benchmarks, ImitSAT achieved the lowest wall-clock time, delivering a net speedup compared to MiniSAT and other learned methods.
- Integration with advanced solvers like CaDiCaL and Kissat (with probing disabled) showed consistent MRPP reduction and strong performance gains, particularly on structured datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expert KeyTraces, representing nearly conflict-free solver runs, provide dense and effective supervision for imitation learning, significantly reducing propagations compared to standard solver behavior.
> - An autoregressive model can effectively learn complex, context-dependent branching policies by observing serialized solver states and expert decision sequences.
> - Combining variable permutation augmentation and staged curriculum learning is crucial for mitigating overfitting, accelerating convergence, and improving generalization capabilities across diverse SAT problem types.
