# ReMix: Reinforcement routing for mixtures of LoRAs in LLM finetuning

> [!summary] Summary
> ReMix, a method for fine-tuning large language models using Mixture-of-LoRAs, addresses the issue of routing weight collapse by utilizing non-learnable, constant routing weights alongside a reinforcement learning-based training procedure. It achieves an average accuracy of 60.77% across three benchmarks, outperforming existing PEFT methods by 2.82% while maintaining competitive parameter efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Mixture-of-LoRAs (MoLoRA) models, which use learned continuous routing weights, suffer from "routing weight collapse."
- Routing weight collapse leads to highly imbalanced weights, causing only a very small number of LoRAs to be effectively utilized, thereby wasting computational resources and limiting expressive capacity.
- The effective support size (ESS) of routing weights often rapidly decreases to 1 during fine-tuning, negating the benefits of configuring multiple specialized LoRAs.

## Method

- ==ReMix== introduces a novel router design that assigns ==non-learnable, constant routing weights== to all 'k' activated LoRAs within a layer, ensuring equal contribution and preventing any single LoRA from dominating.
- Router training is reformulated as a ==reinforcement learning (RL) problem==, where the supervised fine-tuning loss acts as a negative reward, and the ==Reinforce Leave-One-Out (RLOO)== technique is adapted for unbiased gradient estimation.
- For inference, ReMix employs a theoretically grounded ==deterministic top-k selection strategy==, choosing the 'k' LoRAs with the highest probabilities from the trained router distribution.

## Results

- ReMix achieved an average accuracy of **60.77%** across GSM8K, HumanEval, and ARC-c benchmarks on Llama 3 8B, surpassing the strongest competing PEFT methods (DoRA, rsLoRA) by **2.82%**.
- It operates with high parameter efficiency, using an average of **0.070B** trainable parameters, which is a **31%** reduction compared to MixLoRA while delivering superior accuracy.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of both the RLOO-based gradient estimator and the top-k inference selection strategy for ReMix's performance, and its RL-based approach allows for performance scaling with increased training compute.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) demonstrates that, under standard initialization, existing MoLoRA routers tend to concentrate routing weights, limiting the effective support size of activated LoRAs.
> - Enforcing non-learnable, constant routing weights is crucial to prevent routing weight collapse and ensure all activated LoRAs contribute equally to the layer output.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly the RLOO estimator, offers an effective method for training routers with non-differentiable LoRA selection, allowing for principled optimization of the routing policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for large language models (LLMs). As LLMs continue to grow in size, fine-tuning them for specific downstream tasks becomes computationally expensive and resource-intensive. PEFT methods aim to address this by significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters while retaining or improving performance compared to full fine-tuning.

Low-Rank Adapters (LoRAs) have emerged as a prominent PEFT technique. LoRAs inject small, trainable low-rank matrices into the pre-trained weight matrices of an LLM, adapting it to new tasks with a fraction of the original model's parameters. Building on this, Mixture-of-LoRAs (MoLoRA) models have been proposed to further enhance parameter efficiency and expressive power. These models typically route each layer input to a subset of specialized LoRAs within that layer, allowing for conditional computation and specialization.

Existing MoLoRA approaches often employ routers that learn continuous routing weights, trained end-to-end with the task objective via gradient descent. This paper identifies a specific limitation in these existing learned-weight MoLoRA routers: the tendency for routing weights to become highly imbalanced, leading to a "routing weight collapse" where only a very small number of LoRAs are effectively utilized. This phenomenon negates the potential benefits of having multiple specialized LoRAs, effectively wasting computational resources and limiting the model's expressive capacity.

The presented work, ReMix, aims to overcome this limitation by fundamentally redesigning the router mechanism. Instead of learned continuous routing weights, ReMix proposes non-learnable, constant weights for activated LoRAs, combined with a reinforcement learning-based approach to train the router. This places ReMix as an advancement in the design and training of MoLoRA systems, seeking to unlock their full potential by ensuring a more balanced and effective utilization of their components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an improved router design for Mixture-of-LoRAs models that addresses the issue of routing weight collapse, thereby enhancing the realized expressive power and performance of parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

The motivation for this objective stems from a critical observation regarding existing Mixture-of-LoRAs (MoLoRA) architectures. Current MoLoRA routers assign a learned routing weight to each LoRA, enabling end-to-end training via gradient descent. While conceptually designed to allow flexible allocation and capacity balancing, the authors discovered a significant weakness: routing weights often become extremely imbalanced. This phenomenon, termed "routing weight collapse," manifests when one LoRA acquires a dominantly large weight, effectively marginalizing the contributions of other LoRAs even when multiple (k > 1) LoRAs are configured to be active during fine-tuning.

The paper provides both theoretical and empirical evidence for this problem:
- **Theoretical Insight:** Theorem 1 demonstrates that, under standard Gaussian initialization for router parameters, with high probability, only an extremely small number of LoRAs will have relatively large routing weights. This implies a systemic tendency for the router to concentrate its allocation, limiting the effective number of LoRAs.
- **Empirical Observation:** Case studies on existing MoLoRA methods like MixLoRA showed that routing weights in deeper layers often collapse, with one LoRA taking a weight close to one, while others become negligibly small (Figure 2). This imbalance was observed to worsen as fine-tuning progressed, with the "effective support size" (ESS) of the routing weights frequently dropping to 1, indicating that only one LoRA was genuinely contributing (Figure 3).

The consequence of this collapse is that the computational effort expended on the k-1 dominated LoRAs is largely wasted, as using k > 1 LoRAs achieves similar accuracy to k=1. This severely restricts the realized expressive power and performance gains expected from a mixture architecture.

To address this, the authors identified the nature of learnable continuous routing weights as the root cause. Their motivation was to rethink the fundamental design of the router by enforcing a constant routing weight across all activated LoRAs, ensuring equal contribution and preventing dominance. This design, however, introduced a new challenge: such non-learnable weights prevent direct training of the router via conventional gradient descent. Therefore, a secondary objective was to develop an unbiased gradient estimation technique suitable for this new router design, specifically leveraging reinforcement learning principles. The overall goal was to create a robust and efficient MoLoRA system that consistently utilizes its specialized components effectively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, ReMix (Reinforcement Routing for Mixture-of-LoRAs), introduces a novel router design and training procedure to overcome the routing weight collapse observed in existing Mixture-of-LoRAs models.

#### 3.1. Adapter Architecture: Non-Learnable Routing Weights

The core of ReMix's adapter architecture is its approach to routing weights. Instead of learnable continuous weights, ReMix assigns a constant routing weight ($\omega$) to all $k$ activated LoRAs within a layer and zero weight to non-activated LoRAs. This design explicitly enforces that all active LoRAs contribute equally to the layer output, preventing any single LoRA from dominating.
- For each layer $l$, an input $x^{(l)}$ is first processed by a small neural network router, parameterized by $P^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times D}$, to produce an $n$-way categorical routing distribution $q^{(l)} := \text{softmax}(P^{(l)}x^{(l)})$.
- From this distribution $q^{(l)}$, $k$ LoRAs are selected to be activated. The selection mechanism differs between fine-tuning and inference.
- The routing weights $\pi^{(l)}_i$ are then defined as: $\pi^{(l)}_i := \omega \mathbb{1}_{[i \in I^{(l)}]}$, where $I^{(l)}$ is the set of $k$ activated LoRAs and $\omega$ is a constant (e.g., $2/kr$ or $2/\sqrt{kr}$). This ensures that the effective support size ESS($\pi^{(l)}$) equals $k$.
- The layer output $y^{(l)}$ is computed as: $y^{(l)} := W^{(l)}x^{(l)} + \omega \sum_{j=1}^k B^{(l)}_{i^{(l)}_j} A^{(l)}_{i^{(l)}_j} x^{(l)}$, where $W^{(l)}$ is the frozen original weight and $B^{(l)}_{i^{(l)}_j} A^{(l)}_{i^{(l)}_j}$ are the low-rank matrices of the $j$-th selected LoRA.

#### 3.2. Finetuning Procedure: Reinforcement Learning with RLOO

The non-learnable, constant routing weights introduce a non-differentiability challenge for training the router parameters $P^{(l)}$. To address this, ReMix reformulates the router training as a reinforcement learning (RL) problem:
- **RL Formulation:** The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss $L(I)$ (where $I$ is the collection of activated LoRAs across all layers) is treated as the negative reward. The router, which samples LoRA subsets based on $q^{(l)}$, acts as the policy model. The objective becomes minimizing $E_{I^{(l)} \sim q^{(l)}}[L(I)]$.
- **LoRA Sampling:** During fine-tuning, $k$ LoRAs $(i^{(l)}_1, \ldots, i^{(l)}_k)$ are sampled from $q^{(l)}$ without replacement to form the activated subset $I^{(l)}$. This ensures distinct LoRAs are selected.
- **Gradient Estimation:** Due to the intractability of computing the exact gradient of $E_{I^{(l)} \sim q^{(l)}}[L(I)]$, an unbiased gradient estimator is employed. Specifically, the Reinforce Leave-One-Out (RLOO) technique (Kool et al., 2019) is adapted.
- **RLOO Estimator:** For $M$ independently sampled selections $J_1, \ldots, J_M$, the gradient for router parameters $P^{(l)}$ is estimated as:
 $\hat{G}_{P^{(l)}} := \frac{1}{M-1} \sum_{m=1}^M (L(I_m) - \bar{L}) \nabla_{P^{(l)}} \log Q(J_m)$, where $\bar{L} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M L(I_m)$ is the average SFT loss, and $Q(J_m)$ is the probability of sampling the ordered selection $J_m$. This estimator is proven to be unbiased. The RLOO technique is used to reduce the variance of this estimator.
- **Scaling Compute:** This RL formulation allows for scaling up training compute by increasing $M$, the number of sampled selections, to potentially enhance predictive performance.

#### 3.3. Inference Procedure: Top-k Selection

For inference, ReMix adopts a deterministic top-$k$ selection strategy for activated LoRAs:
- **Optimality of Top-k:** Theorem 2 formally states that if the router $q^{(l)}$ is trained sufficiently well such that sampling yields the optimal subset with a probability greater than 0.5, then the $k$ LoRAs with the highest $q^{(l)}_i$ values are guaranteed to constitute the optimal subset.
- **Selection Process:** Based on Theorem 2, ReMix selects the $k$ LoRAs $I^{(l)} := \{\text{argtop}_k_{i=1}^n q^{(l)}_i\}$ (i.e., those with the highest $k$ probabilities in $q^{(l)}$) for activation during inference. This provides a theoretically grounded, deterministic approach for LoRA selection at inference time, avoiding the computational overhead of sampling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that ReMix addresses the identified routing weight collapse and achieves performance improvements over existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods.

#### 4.1. Routing Weight Collapse Analysis

- **Theoretical Validation:** Theorem 1 provides a theoretical basis, showing that the effective support size (ESS) of routing weights in traditional MoLoRAs is severely limited, often collapsing to a very small number of LoRAs.
- **Empirical Evidence:** Figures 2 and 3 illustrate this collapse in MixLoRA. Figure 2 shows histograms where one LoRA dominates with a weight near one, while others have negligible weights. Figure 3 shows that the ESS often rapidly decreases to 1 during fine-tuning, confirming that only one LoRA is effectively utilized. This motivated ReMix's design to enforce ESS = k.

#### 4.2. Overall Performance Comparison (Table 1)

ReMix was evaluated against various PEFT categories on Llama 3 8B across three benchmarks: GSM8K (mathematical reasoning), HumanEval (code generation), and ARC-c (knowledge recall).
- **Superior Accuracy:** ReMix consistently outperformed all baseline methods. It achieved an average accuracy of 60.77% across the three tasks, an improvement of 2.82% over the strongest competing approach (DoRA, rsLoRA).
 - On GSM8K, ReMix achieved 65.66%, surpassing rsLoRA and HydraLoRA by 3.19%.
 - On HumanEval, ReMix achieved a Pass@1 of 32.93%, outperforming (IA)$^3$ by 1.83%.
 - On ARC-c, ReMix achieved 83.73%, exceeding DoRA by 0.34%.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** ReMix achieved these results with a competitive budget of 0.070B trainable parameters on average. This represents a substantial reduction compared to parameter-heavy baselines like VB-LoRA (90% reduction from 0.675B) and a 31% reduction compared to MixLoRA (from 0.101B), while delivering higher accuracy.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Figure 4)

Ablation studies on GSM8K confirmed the contributions of ReMix's key components:
- **RLOO Importance:** Removing the RLOO-based gradient estimator from the fine-tuning procedure resulted in a significant drop in accuracy, indicating its crucial role.
- **Top-k Selection Importance:** Disabling the top-k LoRA selection during inference also led to reduced accuracy, demonstrating the value of this component in optimizing performance.

#### 4.4. Diversity of Activated LoRA Subsets (Table 2)

To confirm that ReMix effectively activates diverse LoRA subsets rather than collapsing to a single set, its performance (using k rank-r LoRAs) was compared to a single rank-kr LoRA (with the same total parameter count).
- ReMix significantly outperformed rank-kr LoRA (e.g., 64.22% for k=4 ReMix vs. 59.21% for rank-32 LoRA on GSM8K). This indicates that ReMix successfully leverages the diversity of its LoRA subsets by selecting them appropriately for different inputs, which is not achievable by a single, larger LoRA.

#### 4.5. Training Efficiency (Table 4)

A comparison with MixLoRA, an existing MoLoRA method, showed ReMix's training efficiency.
- ReMix achieved 58.38% accuracy with a total training time of 1:28:21, while MixLoRA achieved 50.34% in 1:12:56. Despite a 10% increase in training time, ReMix yielded a 15.97% relative improvement in accuracy, demonstrating effective performance even under moderate training compute budgets.

#### 4.6. Scaling the Number of Activated LoRAs (Table 5)

Experiments on GSM8K with $n=8$ total LoRAs showed how increasing $k$ (number of activated LoRAs) impacts performance.
- ReMix consistently improved in predictive accuracy as $k$ increased (e.g., from 56.18% for k=1 to 64.22% for k=4), confirming the intuitive benefit of activating more diverse LoRAs up to $n/2$.

#### 4.7. Scaling the Training Compute (Figure 5)

Leveraging its RL-based gradient estimator, ReMix's performance was evaluated under varying training compute budgets ($M$, number of sampled selections).
- Increasing $M$ from 2 to 32 led to a steady improvement in accuracy (from 56.03% to 58.83%). This indicates that ReMix can effectively utilize additional computational resources for enhanced performance, a unique advantage over deterministic training methods like HydraLoRA and MixLoRA, which have fixed compute budgets.

#### 4.8. LoRA vs. rsLoRA Routing Weight (Table 3)

ReMix's sensitivity to the choice of the constant routing weight $\omega$ was assessed.
- The performance difference between using LoRA-type $\omega$ ($2/kr$) and rsLoRA-type $\omega$ ($2/\sqrt{kr}$) was minimal (53.30% vs 55.72% on GSM8K for k=3), suggesting ReMix is not highly sensitive to this specific hyperparameter.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents ReMix as a significant advancement in the field of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for large language models. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Addressing a Fundamental Limitation:** ReMix systematically addresses the previously unacknowledged "routing weight collapse" in existing Mixture-of-LoRAs (MoLoRA) models. By providing both theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) and empirical evidence of this issue, the paper highlights a critical flaw that limits the expressive power and efficiency of current MoLoRA architectures. Solving this problem allows MoLoRAs to fulfill their promise of leveraging multiple specialized adapters.

- **Novel Router Design and Training Paradigm:** The introduction of non-learnable, constant routing weights is a departure from conventional approaches. This design, combined with a reinforcement learning-based training framework utilizing an RLOO gradient estimator, offers a novel paradigm for optimizing conditional computation in neural networks. This approach could inspire future research in non-differentiable routing mechanisms within other sparse models.

- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** ReMix consistently outperforms various state-of-the-art PEFT methods across diverse benchmarks (mathematical reasoning, code generation, knowledge recall) while maintaining or improving parameter efficiency. This signifies a practical improvement in adapting LLMs to specific tasks with reduced computational and memory overhead. The ability to activate diverse LoRA subsets, confirmed by ablation studies, contributes to this performance gain.

- **Scalability of Training Compute:** A notable advantage of ReMix's RL-based approach is its capacity to scale performance with increased training compute (by varying $M$, the number of sampled selections). This offers a clear pathway for further performance optimization, which is not readily available to deterministic PEFT methods.

- **Impact on LLM Deployment and Resource Management:** For large-scale LLM deployments, multi-task learning scenarios, and environments with limited computational resources, ReMix offers a more effective and efficient fine-tuning solution. By ensuring better utilization of specialized LoRAs, it can lead to more capable and adaptable models without proportional increases in trainable parameters or inference costs.

- **Theoretical Contributions:** The theoretical analyses regarding routing weight collapse (Theorem 1) and the optimality of top-k selection for inference (Theorem 2) provide a deeper understanding of MoLoRA dynamics and offer principled guidance for router design in sparse expert systems.

Overall, ReMix advances the state-of-the-art in PEFT by resolving a critical architectural limitation, introducing an effective training methodology, and demonstrating superior empirical performance. Its implications extend to more efficient and powerful LLM adaptation in a wide range of applications.
