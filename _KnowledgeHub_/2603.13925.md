# SmoothVLA: Aligning Vision-Language-Action Models with Physical Constraints via Intrinsic Smoothness Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A reinforcement learning fine-tuning framework, SmoothVLA, aligns Vision-Language-Action models with physical constraints by integrating an intrinsic smoothness objective into its reward function. This approach leads to VLA policies that exhibit improved task success rates, such as a 6.6 percentage point increase on the LIBERO benchmark, and produce significantly smoother and more robust robotic trajectories.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with a trade-off between task generalization and motion quality in physical robotics.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) yields smooth motions but has limited generalization, while reinforcement learning (RL) improves generalization but often produces jittery, physically non-compliant trajectories.
- The "Exploration-Stability Paradox" describes how RL's exploratory power conflicts with the stability and smoothness required for robotic execution, leading to physical misalignment.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning fine-tuning framework, **SmoothVLA**, builds upon ==SFT-tuned VLA models==, refining them through online interaction.
- It introduces a ==physics-informed hybrid reward function== that combines a ==sparse task success indicator== with a continuous, dense ==trajectory jerk penalty==.
- ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== is employed to optimize the policy by estimating advantage from groups of trajectories, encouraging both task completion and smoothness without needing a critic network.

## Results

- **SmoothVLA-GRPO** demonstrated an average success rate of **80.5%** on the **LIBERO** benchmark, representing a **+6.6 pp** increase over the Octo baseline (**73.9%**).
- Motion smoothness improved by **+4.5%** compared to supervised fine-tuning and **+13.8%** compared to standard RL fine-tuning methods, yielding more stable trajectories.
- The framework enhanced robustness on **LIBERO-Plus**, outperforming supervised fine-tuning methods by **+24.2%** in average success rate across various environmental perturbations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "Exploration-Stability Paradox" reveals a fundamental tension where reinforcement learning's exploratory power for generalization often compromises the critical motion smoothness needed for physical robots.
> - Trajectory smoothness is a functional prerequisite for robotic manipulation, directly affecting task success, mechanical wear, and overall operational safety.
> - Trajectory jerk, derived intrinsically from proprioceptive feedback, effectively quantifies motion quality and can be integrated into a differentiable reward objective for autonomous physical alignment without external sensors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotic manipulation, enabling agents to interpret multimodal instructions and execute a range of tasks. These models aim to bridge high-level linguistic understanding with low-level robotic control. Historically, robot learning systems have often adopted hierarchical planning, where large language or vision-language models generate high-level plans, and separate low-level controllers handle local motion trajectories. While effective in structured environments, these hierarchical approaches can exhibit limitations in motor competence and scalability to diverse real-world manipulation scenarios.

In contrast, end-to-end VLA models endeavor to directly generate actions by scaling low-level control using VLM backbones. Current VLA systems broadly fall into two categories: those that discretize continuous action space into symbolic tokens (e.g., OpenVLA, RT-1, RT-2), which can introduce approximation errors, and those that leverage diffusion models for continuous action prediction (e.g., Diffusion Policy). Regardless of the architectural specifics, most existing VLA models are trained using supervised learning, typically behavior cloning, based on successful demonstration trajectories. This supervised approach inherently restricts their capacity to generalize to novel tasks and environments.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been employed for post-training foundation models, particularly for aligning pre-trained models with desired behaviors. In robotics, RL offers advantages for policy learning. However, applying RL to fine-tune VLA models presents specific challenges: complex and long-horizon manipulation tasks complicate reward specification, collecting human preferences for robotic trajectories is typically resource-intensive, and unstable reward gradients can lead to policy divergence. Approaches like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) have shown that policies can be aligned via RL-style optimization without explicit reward modeling, by directly contrasting trajectories.

This paper identifies a specific gap in the current VLA fine-tuning landscape: the trade-off between task generalization and motion quality. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often produces smooth trajectories but exhibits limited generalization due to its reliance on demonstration data. Online Reinforcement Learning (RL), while improving generalization through exploration, frequently results in erratic, jittery, and physically non-compliant trajectories. This constitutes a misalignment between the high-dimensional outputs of neural networks and the kinematic constraints of physical hardware, a phenomenon the authors term the "Exploration-Stability Paradox." SmoothVLA addresses this paradox by proposing an RL fine-tuning framework that explicitly integrates physical constraints, specifically motion smoothness, into the optimization objective, moving beyond purely supervised or heuristic RL alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed misalignment between VLA model outputs and the physical requirements of robotic hardware. While VLA models enable interpretation of complex instructions, their transition from simulation to physical environments reveals a gap: the high-dimensional actions generated by neural networks often lack the kinematic smoothness essential for reliable physical operation. Trajectory smoothness is described as a functional prerequisite for robotic manipulation, contributing to precise control, reduced mechanical wear, and operational safety.

The paper highlights limitations in current VLA fine-tuning methodologies:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Constrained by the "demonstration bottleneck," where performance is tied to the quality and diversity of human demonstration data. SFT-tuned models often fail to generalize effectively when faced with out-of-distribution environmental perturbations. While SFT can yield visually coherent and smooth trajectories, its generalization capacity is limited.
- **Online Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While enabling autonomous exploration and enhancing generalization beyond demonstration data, RL fine-tuning frequently leads to "high-frequency oscillations" and "jittery motions." This behavior degrades success rates and violates physical priors for smooth motion, creating a physical misalignment where learned behavior is not compatible with hardware.

This analysis identifies an "Exploration-Stability Paradox" where generalization from RL-driven exploration conflicts with the stability and smoothness required for physical execution. The authors support this empirically, showing that RL-tuned models exhibit a higher average jerk (a measure of unsmoothness) compared to SFT-tuned models. Furthermore, the paper argues that trajectory smoothness is not merely an aesthetic preference but a critical functional factor directly affecting task success. Non-smooth motions can exceed actuator bandwidth, cause system oscillations, degrade dynamic stability, and introduce noise into sensory systems, all of which contribute to task failures. Therefore, optimizing for smoothness is presented as a condition for both hardware longevity and the successful translation of control commands into intended physical behavior.

To address this "smoothness–generalization" tension, the paper outlines three non-trivial questions that form the core objectives for the proposed SmoothVLA framework:
1. **Formalizing Motion Quality:** How to define motion quality into a differentiable objective that accurately captures robotic stability without oversimplifying the underlying physics?
2. **Autonomous Physical Feedback:** How can the model autonomously acquire physical feedback from high-dimensional rollouts, thereby eliminating the need for extrinsic sensors or manual reward engineering?
3. **Smoothness-Exploration Balance:** How to optimize for smoothness without simultaneously suppressing exploration, which could lead to policies that are safe but sub-optimal?

SmoothVLA aims to provide a reinforcement learning fine-tuning framework that jointly optimizes both task performance and kinematic smoothness to address these questions and align VLA models with physical-world constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SmoothVLA operates as a reinforcement learning fine-tuning framework designed to optimize both task performance and motion smoothness. The approach initiates with an existing SFT-tuned VLA model, which then undergoes further refinement through online interaction.

The methodology involves the following key components:

1. **Problem Formulation:** Robotic manipulation tasks are formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The state space (S) is multimodal, comprising visual observations, proprioceptive states (e.g., joint positions), and language instructions. The VLA policy (π_θ) maps these states to actions (a_t), typically representing target poses or velocities. Actions are generated in chunks to maintain temporal coherence.

2. **Physics-Informed Reward Function:** The central technical element of SmoothVLA is a hybrid reward function that integrates sparse task rewards with a continuous, dense term derived from trajectory jerk.
 - **Jerk Derivation:** Jerk, defined as the time derivative of acceleration (and the third-order derivative of position), quantifies the rate of change of applied forces. Minimizing jerk aims to reduce mechanical vibration, promote energy efficiency, and produce human-like motion. The paper presents the kinematic relationships for end-effector velocity, acceleration, and jerk:
 - Velocity: `V = J(q) ˙q`
 - Acceleration: `A = ˙J(q, ˙q) ˙q + J(q)¨q`
 - Jerk: `Jerk = ¨J(q, ˙q, ¨q) ˙q + 2 ˙J(q, ˙q)¨q + J(q) ...q`
 Where `q` represents joint positions, `˙q` joint velocities, `¨q` joint accelerations, `...q` joint jerk, `J(q)` the Jacobian matrix, and `˙J`, `¨J` its time derivatives.
 - **Hybrid Reward Structure:** The reward function `R(τ)` for a trajectory `τ` is defined as:
 `R(τ) = I_success ⋅ (1 - λ ⋅ (1/T ⋅ Σ |Jerk(t)|^2))`
 - `I_success`: A binary indicator that is 1 for task success and 0 for failure. This term ensures that the agent primarily prioritizes task completion.
 - `λ`: A scaling factor that balances the importance of the smoothness penalty. Empirically, `λ` was set to 0.2 after grid search.
 - `Jerk(t)`: The magnitude of the jerk at time step `t`. The squared sum of jerk values across the trajectory `T` serves as an intrinsic penalty for unsmooth motion.
 - **Intrinsic Nature:** This reward term is computed directly from policy rollouts and proprioceptive feedback (joint positions, velocities, etc.) through inverse kinematics, removing the requirement for extrinsic sensors or manual reward engineering. This design aims for feedback autonomy.

3. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** To optimize the VLA model without the computational demands of a critic network, SmoothVLA employs GRPO.
 - **Advantage Estimation:** Unlike standard actor-critic methods, GRPO estimates the advantage function by comparing a group of `G` trajectories sampled under the same language instruction.
 - **Optimization Objective:** The GRPO objective aims to maximize an expectation over the clipped importance sampling ratio and an advantage term, with a KL divergence penalty to maintain proximity to the reference policy. The advantage `ˆA_i` for a trajectory `i` is calculated by normalizing its total reward `R_i` within the group: `ˆA_i = (R_i - mean(R))/std(R)`.
 - **Smoothness Preference:** This intra-group competition encourages the policy to favor successful trajectories that also exhibit higher smoothness, effectively guiding the model toward a physically stable trajectory manifold once task completion is achieved.

**Implementation Details:**
The backbone VLA model used is OpenVLA. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) fine-tuning is applied with the AdamW optimizer. Separate learning rates are used for the supervised fine-tuning stage (4 × 10⁻⁵) and the preference fine-tuning stage (2 × 10⁻⁵), with a batch size of 16 for both. The parameter `λ` for the smoothness weight coefficient was set to 0.2. Experiments are conducted on the LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SmoothVLA influences VLA models with respect to motion quality, task success, and generalization.

**Empirical Study on Trajectory Smoothness (Section 2):**
An initial empirical study evaluated the impact of SFT and RL fine-tuning on trajectory smoothness using OpenVLA on a LIBERO pick-and-place task.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** SFT-tuned models produced trajectories characterized by global coherence and smooth curvature, without abrupt changes. In contrast, RL-tuned models exhibited pronounced unsmoothness, including unnecessary detours, backtracking, and local oscillations/jittering, particularly near the grasping point.
- **Quantitative Analysis:** Using jerk as a metric (lower values indicating smoother motion), SFT-tuned OpenVLA models showed an average jerk of 0.374 m/s³, while RL-tuned OpenVLA models displayed a higher average jerk of 0.402 m/s³. This quantitative evidence supports the observation that existing online RL fine-tuning methods, despite improving generalization, introduce trajectory jitter and degrade motion smoothness.

**Main Results on Robotics Benchmarks (Section 4.2):**
Evaluations were conducted on the LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus benchmarks.
- **Task Success Rate (Figure 4):** In in-distribution evaluations on LIBERO, SmoothVLA-GRPO achieved an average success rate of 80.5%, which is a 6.6 percentage point improvement compared to the baseline method, Octo (73.9%). When SmoothVLA was applied as a plugin to different reinforcement fine-tuning methods (DPO and GRPO), the average success rate increased by 2.6%, suggesting compatibility and performance gains.
- **Motion Smoothness (Figure 5):** SmoothVLA demonstrated optimization of motion trajectory quality. Compared to traditional SFT, the smoothness metric improved by 4.5%. Against standard RL fine-tuning methods, the improvement reached 13.8%. These results indicate that the hybrid reward function contributes to balancing task success and motion smoothness, leading to more stable and physically feasible trajectories.
- **Robustness (Table 2):** On the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, designed for robustness analysis under perturbations (language, light, background, object layout), SmoothVLA methods showed improved performance and reduced degradation. SmoothVLA surpassed supervised fine-tuning methods by 24.2% in average success rate across all perturbation types. Variants based on GRPO and DPO exhibited improvements of 7% and 5.6% respectively over their non-SmoothVLA counterparts. SmoothVLA models also demonstrated smaller performance degradation when encountering out-of-distribution perturbations, indicating enhanced generalization and stability.

**Ablation Study of Reward Model (Table 3):**
An ablation study compared three reward function configurations: basic binary sparse reward (R_binary), binary reward with random noise (R_random), and the proposed smoothness reward (R_smooth), which includes the jerk-based term.
- The R_smooth configuration yielded higher overall performance (average 54.1%) compared to R_binary (48.2%) and R_random (46.5%).
- The smoothness reward component contributed to model robustness across different perturbation types, with noticeable improvements in layout variation and language perturbation tasks. This suggests that structured physical priors are more effective than arbitrary reward perturbations.

**Case Study (Figures 6 & 7):**
A case study on a pick-and-place task further illustrated the advantages. While SFT-tuned trajectories were generally smooth with some limitations, RL-tuned trajectories displayed back-and-forth movements and high-frequency jitter. SmoothVLA-generated trajectories were observed to be smoother than the RL baseline, exhibiting minimal backtracking or jitter, maintaining stable posture, and continuous motion, which contributed to successful task completion and obstacle avoidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work addresses an identified challenge in the fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the trade-off between enhancing generalization through reinforcement learning and maintaining the physical smoothness required for robotic operation. The introduction of SmoothVLA provides a framework that explicitly incorporates motion quality into the learning objective, moving towards VLA models that are both versatile and physically compliant.

The significance of SmoothVLA can be considered in several aspects:
- **Bridging the Generalization-Stability Gap:** By defining and addressing the "Exploration-Stability Paradox," the research contributes to the development of VLA models that can leverage the exploratory power of RL without compromising on the critical physical constraint of motion smoothness. This is relevant for deploying VLA models in dynamic and open-ended physical environments.
- **Autonomous Physical Alignment:** The methodology's reliance on an intrinsic, physics-informed reward mechanism, derived from trajectory jerk, offers a scalable approach. It enables autonomous self-correction of motion quality during exploration, circumventing the need for extrinsic sensors, additional supervision, or laborious manual reward engineering, which are common bottlenecks in robot learning.
- **Enhanced Robotic Performance:** The empirical results indicate that SmoothVLA leads to improvements in both task success rates and motion quality. By generating smoother and more physically feasible trajectories, the approach has the potential to contribute to more reliable and robust robotic operation, potentially reducing mechanical wear, energy consumption, and failure rates in precision manipulation tasks.
- **Architecture Agnostic Framework:** The proposed RL post-training protocol is presented as architecture-agnostic, suggesting its applicability to a variety of existing and future embodied models, thereby expanding the utility of current VLA research.
- **Theoretical Formalization:** The work formally defines the "Exploration-Stability Paradox" and establishes trajectory jerk as a mathematically rigorous "high-order differential bridge" to quantify and mitigate the misalignment between neural actions and physical motions.

The potential impact of SmoothVLA extends to the practical deployment of intelligent robots. Robots employing such aligned VLA models could exhibit more natural, efficient, and safe behaviors in complex environments. This could facilitate advances in areas such as industrial automation, service robotics, and assistive technologies, where precise, stable, and adaptive manipulation is crucial.

The authors also outline future research directions, including extending SmoothVLA to real-world robotic platforms and long-horizon tasks, exploring adaptive and learnable smoothness objectives, and integrating smoothness optimization with model-based control or world-model learning. These directions suggest continued development towards VLA systems that more tightly couple high-level reasoning with low-level physical execution capabilities.
