# Embodied-R1.5: Evolving Physical Intelligence via Embodied Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Embodied-R1.5 is an 8B-parameter Embodied Foundation Model unifying cognition/spatial reasoning, task planning/correction, and pointing/location in one architecture, trained via SFT then multi-task-balanced RL on 15B+ tokens; it hits SOTA on 16/24 embodied VLM benchmarks and, via a Planner-Grounder-Corrector loop, autonomously executes and self-corrects long-horizon real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing embodied models cover only fragments of the capability spectrum, or stitch cognition, planning, and grounding together via separate models of different scales rather than one unified model.
- Joint multi-task training across heterogeneous output formats (text, points, trajectories) suffers severe convergence conflict, where capabilities erode one another during optimization.
- Prior Embodied Foundation Models stop at embodied QA benchmarks, never validating whether reasoning is truly grounded in physics under autonomous long-horizon closed-loop decision-making.

## Method

- Three automated data pipelines (3D scene-graph annotation, failure-aware perturbation labeling, affordance/trajectory extraction) build a **15B-token** corpus spanning spatial, planning-correction, and pointing capabilities.
- A ==multi-task balanced RL== recipe combines difficulty-aware data filtering, dynamic degenerate-group masking, and global batch reward normalization (group-mean, batch-std advantage) to resolve intra- and inter-task GRPO imbalance.
- The ==Planner-Grounder-Corrector (PGC)== framework lets one **Embodied-R1.5** instance asynchronously serve all three roles with a lightweight FIFO memory buffer, enabling closed-loop autonomous execution and error recovery.

## Results

- Embodied-R1.5 scores **70.4%** average across 21 main embodied VLM benchmarks, beating Gemini-Robotics-ER-1.5 by **17.0pp** and GPT-5.4 by **21.7pp**, with SOTA on 16 of 24 benchmarks.
- Fine-tuned into Embodied-R1.5-VLA with no large-scale action pretraining, it reaches **92.4%** on SimplerEnv Google Robot Visual Matching (versus pi-0.5's **72.7%**) and **97.3%** on LIBERO without pretraining.
- Ablations show RFT adds **+3.8pp** on pointing over SFT-only, and the full RL recipe beats vanilla GRPO and EMA-GRPO across all three capability dimensions (**70.4** vs **64.8** average).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internalizing rich embodied reasoning upstream can substitute for large-scale action-data pretraining, since the action head then only needs to map understood intent to continuous motion.
> - Batch-level reward normalization generalizes better than per-task moving averages when task data volumes are highly imbalanced, a caution for anyone designing multi-task RL recipes.
> - A single unified model serving as planner, grounder, and corrector avoids cascade information loss, suggesting closed-loop robot autonomy benefits more from capability unification than multi-agent orchestration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The work addresses the challenge of extending the success of large language models (LLMs) from the digital domain to general-purpose physical intelligence in the physical world. A central concept in this endeavor is "embodied reasoning," which involves enabling models to perceive, reason about spatial geometry and task arrangement, and act within the physical environment. The authors propose the development of an Embodied Foundation Model (EFM) as a unified architecture that integrates perception, reasoning, and execution, with all capabilities grounded in physical principles.

Existing research in embodied artificial intelligence (AI) is characterized by several limitations, which Embodied-R1.5 aims to address. First, current embodied models often exhibit fragmented capabilities, specializing in specific aspects such as cognition, planning and correction, or grounding, rather than integrating these into a single, cohesive architecture. Some models attempt to span multiple dimensions but typically rely on separate, distinct models for different tasks (e.g., RynnBrain), which precludes true unification within a single architecture.

Second, a significant challenge in multi-task learning for embodied AI is the presence of multi-task conflicts. Tasks such as long text reasoning, precise trajectory prediction, and diverse grounding tasks often require drastically different output formats and learning objectives, leading to convergence difficulties and potential degradation of individual capabilities when trained jointly.

Third, much of the existing work in EFMs remains at the level of Embodied Question Answering (QA), where reasoning capabilities are demonstrated but not fully validated in the context of closed-loop autonomy for long-horizon, complex physical tasks. This gap means that the grounding of theoretical reasoning in practical physical execution is often not thoroughly verified.

Building upon prior work, specifically Embodied-R1, which specialized in embodied pointing, Embodied-R1.5 expands its scope to a comprehensive EFM. It seeks to unify all three core capability dimensions (cognition, planning, and pointing) within a single model architecture, thereby offering a systematic approach to overcome the fragmented capability, multi-task conflict, and lack of closed-loop validation bottlenecks observed in the current research landscape.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the development of general physical intelligence through the creation of a unified Embodied Foundation Model (EFM), named Embodied-R1.5. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed objectives and motivations:

- **Unified Embodied Capability System:** A core motivation is to integrate a comprehensive set of embodied reasoning capabilities—including embodied cognition, task planning, correction, and pointing—within a single architectural framework. This aims to overcome the limitation of fragmented models that address only parts of the embodied intelligence spectrum. The authors propose that unifying these capabilities within one model allows for seamless information flow and synergistic reasoning, which is essential for complex physical interaction. The capabilities are specifically structured into three dimensions:
 - **Embodied Cognition & Spatial Reasoning:** To enable the model to understand the physical world's semantic and spatial structure. This includes spatial relation understanding (e.g., object orientation, support), metric spatial reasoning (e.g., distance estimation, size judgment), 3D scene perception (e.g., monocular depth, cross-view reasoning), and object and scene cognition from a robot-centric perspective.
 - **Embodied Planning & Correction:** To manage the lifecycle of task execution, from initial planning to error recovery. This encompasses long-horizon task decomposition, next-step planning, process detection (monitoring task completion), error localization (identifying failure causes), and error correction (generating recovery suggestions).
 - **Embodied Pointing & Location:** To ground high-level reasoning into actionable physical interactions. This includes referring expression grounding (localizing objects from language), region referring grounding (identifying suitable regions for placement), object functional grounding (localizing functional parts of objects), and visual trace generation (generating 2D/3D motion paths for end-effectors or objects).

- **Addressing Data and Training Bottlenecks:** The development of a unified EFM necessitates a large and balanced training corpus, as well as robust training strategies to manage the heterogeneity of embodied tasks. The motivation here is to overcome the limitations of existing datasets, which often lack the scale or specific annotations required for comprehensive embodied capabilities, and to mitigate the challenges of multi-task conflicts during joint training. This is achieved by:
 - Constructing a large-scale data system (over 15 billion tokens) using three automated data construction pipelines to expand data coverage for critical, often underrepresented, capabilities.
 - Designing a multi-task balanced Reinforcement Learning (RL) recipe to address the interference and differing output formats inherent in training heterogeneous embodied tasks.

- **Validation of Closed-Loop Autonomy for Long-Horizon Tasks:** A key objective is to demonstrate that the internalized reasoning capabilities can translate into autonomous execution and self-correction for complex, long-horizon tasks in the real world. Many existing EFMs are evaluated primarily on static question-answering, lacking verification of their practical grounding in dynamic physical decision-making. To address this, the authors propose the Planner-Grounder-Corrector (PGC) closed-loop framework, which allows a single model instance to manage planning, execution grounding, and error correction continuously.

- **Hypothesis on VLA Training Efficiency:** The work is motivated by the hypothesis that a robust embodied VLM backbone, with rich internalized reasoning capabilities, can reduce the data requirements for training downstream Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This suggests that investment in a strong reasoning foundation can partially substitute for extensive action pretraining, potentially making VLA development more efficient.

- **Open-Source Contribution:** A final motivation is to foster community research by open-sourcing the model weights, datasets, training code, and a dedicated evaluation framework (EmbodiedEvalKit), aiming to provide a reproducible infrastructure for future advancements in EFMs.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology involves developing Embodied-R1.5 as an 8-billion-parameter Embodied Foundation Model (EFM) through a systematic approach that includes a unified architecture, automated data construction, a two-stage training strategy, and a closed-loop autonomy framework.

### 4. Unified Architecture

Embodied-R1.5 is designed as an 8B-parameter Vision-Language Model (VLM). Its architecture processes all outputs, including natural language reasoning, point coordinates, and trajectory sequences, as plain-text token sequences. Coordinates are normalized to, and trajectories are represented as ordered coordinate sequences. This plain-text representation facilitates the model's ability to interleave reasoning chains and planning steps and maintain visual anchoring across multi-step inferences. The model can be extended into Embodied-R1.5-VLA by attaching a lightweight flow-matching action expert (based on DiT) to the VLM backbone. This forms a dual-system architecture where the VLM provides hidden representations (2048 dimensions) as cross-attention context for the action head to generate 7-dimensional continuous action trajectories via action chunking.

### 5. Training Data Construction

The training corpus, exceeding 15 billion tokens from 34 datasets, integrates existing open-source resources with proprietary data generated via three automated pipelines:
1. **3D Scene Annotation for Spatial Reasoning (ER1.5-Spatial, ~20K samples):** This pipeline addresses the need for fine-grained tabletop manipulation spatial reasoning. It reconstructs 3D semantic scene graphs from RGB images of real-robot scenes (Fractal, BridgeData V2, DROID). The process involves dual-backend semantic understanding (Qwen3-VL, RAM++), metric-scale monocular geometry estimation (MoGe-2) for absolute depth and normals, open-vocabulary instance segmentation (Grounded-SAM-2), and RANSAC-based horizontal plane alignment to a gravity-aligned world frame. Spatial reasoning QA pairs are then programmatically generated.
2. **Failure-Aware Annotation for Planning and Correction (ER1.5-Correction, ~800K samples):** This pipeline generates data for failure detection, localization, and correction. It is organized by two dimensions: failure stage (planning or execution) and cognitive level (detection, localization, correction), yielding six QA types. Planning failures are created by applying five structured perturbation operators (omission, redundancy, swap, object error, action replacement) to correct sub-task plans. Execution failures are simulated through video truncation, description replacement, and physics-engine perturbation injection. Data sources include real-world datasets (BridgeData V2, RoboFail, RoboFAC) and simulated environments (ManiSkill, GEMBench).
3. **Affordance and Trajectory Data for Pointing (ER1.5-Pointing, ~400K samples):** This pipeline enhances open-vocabulary pointing and physical affordance understanding.
 - **Object Functional Grounding (OFG):** Data is generated from simulation environments (ManiSkill-PartNet, PRISM), by reorganizing existing affordance annotations, and by extracting human grasping patterns from interaction datasets. It also includes "Regular Rearrangement" tasks combining spatial reasoning with pointing.
 - **Visual Trace Generation (VTG):** Supports both 2D and 3D end-effector and object flow traces. Automated extraction involves projecting 3D end-effector poses, fine-tuning Detectron2 for gripper tracking, and using Co-Tracker3 for object motion tracking. RoboCasa assets are used for 3D articulated object manipulation.
General visual cognition, logical reasoning, and instruction-following data are also included to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

### 6. Training Strategy

The model is trained using a two-stage paradigm:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Initializes from the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone. Full-parameter fine-tuning is performed for one epoch on the entire data system using a standard causal language modeling objective. An AdamW optimizer with bfloat16 precision, a peak learning rate of 2x10^-6 (cosine decay, 10% warmup), and an effective global batch size of 512 are used. The vision encoder is jointly trained with the LLM.
2. **Reinforced Fine-Tuning (RFT):** The model from the SFT stage is further refined using RL. This stage focuses on boosting performance, particularly for pointing tasks, by leveraging verifiable rewards.
 - **Multi-Task Balanced RL Recipe:** Addresses intra-task (low-variance samples bias) and inter-task (reward scale differences) imbalances. It includes difficulty-aware data filtering (retaining medium-difficulty samples), dynamic filtering (masking degenerate groups with identical rewards), and global batch reward normalization to unify gradient magnitudes across tasks.
 - **Multi-Task Reward Design:** Five families of reward functions are designed: exact-match, IoU (for bounding boxes), point-distance (piecewise-linear decay), trajectory-RMSE (piecewise-linear decay for 2D/3D traces), and semantic-similarity (using Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-4B for open-ended text). A binary format-checking reward is also combined with accuracy rewards.
 - Training uses an improved EasyR1 framework, KL regularization, 8 parallel rollouts per prompt, AdamW (LR 3x10^-6), and gradient clipping. The vision encoder is again jointly trained for 2 epochs.

### 7. Closed-Loop PGC Autonomy Framework

The Planner-Grounder-Corrector (PGC) framework validates the model's long-horizon physical grounding. A single Embodied-R1.5 instance serves all three roles asynchronously:
- **Planner:** Decomposes user instructions into sub-tasks and performs next-step planning.
- **Grounder:** Orchestrates pointing capabilities (e.g., OFG for functional parts, VTG for trajectories) to produce precise spatial grounding commands for a low-level skill executor.
- **Corrector:** Continuously monitors execution state (SUCCESS, PROCESS, FAIL) using current observations and a FIFO memory buffer, triggering replanning or retries upon detecting failures.
The framework itself is a lightweight, stateless harness; all intelligent decisions are driven by the Embodied-R1.5 model's internalized capabilities.

### 8. EmbodiedEvalKit

A unified evaluation framework, EmbodiedEvalKit, was developed and open-sourced to address the unique requirements of embodied evaluation (parsing points, boxes, trajectories; unifying coordinate formats; computing embodied-specific metrics). It features a four-layer modular design: a data layer for standardized benchmarks, an inference layer for model-agnostic interaction, a parsing layer for normalized output extraction, and an evaluation layer for metric computation and aggregation. It supports over 25 benchmarks and 20 models.

### 9. Main findings and results

The evaluation of Embodied-R1.5 was conducted across five main categories: Embodied VLM benchmarks, robotic manipulation in simulation, zero-shot manipulation transfer to real-world tasks, long-horizon closed-loop demonstrations, and ablation analyses.

### 10. Embodied VLM Benchmarks

Embodied-R1.5 was evaluated on 24 embodied VLM benchmarks using the EmbodiedEvalKit, comparing it against 13 general-purpose and embodied VLM baselines.
- **Overall Performance:** Embodied-R1.5 achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on 16 out of 24 benchmarks, with an average score of 70.4% across the 21 main accuracy-based benchmarks. This performance surpassed Gemini-Robotics-ER-1.5 by 17.0% and GPT-5.4 by 21.7%.
- **Planning & Correction:** Achieved the highest average score of 65.3%. Notable scores include 77.2% on RoboFAC and 53.8% on EgoPlan-2, demonstrating a 24.0 percentage point (pp) margin over Gemini-Robotics-ER-1.5.
- **Pointing & Location:** Exhibited the most significant advantage, achieving SOTA on all 9 pointing benchmarks with an average of 72.8%. Performance on Part-Afford (82.9%) and RoboAfford (80.0%) highlighted improvements in object functional grounding (OFG). It also showed strong performance on visual trace tasks, with RMSE scores of 7.00 on VABench-V (object-centric flow) and 15.79 on ShareRobot-V (robot-centric flow).
- **Cognition & Spatial Reasoning:** Achieved the highest average score in this dimension (70.2%), including SOTA on RoboSpatial (69.7%). The model maintained competitive performance across all 8 benchmarks, indicating that embodied training did not compromise general spatial understanding.
- **General Capability Retention:** Evaluation on 7 standard vision benchmarks confirmed minimal degradation from its base model (Qwen3-VL-8B), with improvements observed on MMMU and ScienceQA, demonstrating preservation of general visual understanding.

### 11. Robotic Manipulation in Simulation

Embodied-R1.5-VLA, the VLM extended with a lightweight flow-matching action expert, was evaluated without large-scale action pretraining.
- **SimplerEnv:** Achieved dominant performance across all three settings. On Google Robot Visual Matching, it reached 92.4% overall success, exceeding the second-best 𝜋0.5 by over 20pp. For Google Robot Variant Aggregation, it scored 71.5% overall, and on WidowX, it achieved 74.0%, surpassing GR00T-N1.5 by 12pp.
- **LIBERO:** Achieved 97.3% overall success without action pretraining. This performance matched or exceeded that of pretrained models (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%), supporting the hypothesis that internalized embodied reasoning can reduce the need for extensive action data.
- **LIBERO-Plus:** Demonstrated robustness under distribution shifts, achieving 76.0% overall success, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT by 6.4pp. It achieved the best performance on 6 out of 7 shift types, with significant advantages in Robot (+22.2pp) and Language (+8.5pp) shifts.

### 12. Zero-Shot Manipulation Transfer

- **ManiSkill-Affordance:** Demonstrated a 0.76 average success rate on seen categories and 0.66 on unseen categories, surpassing ManipLLM by 17pp and 12pp, respectively. This indicated strong generalization of physical manipulation reasoning to novel articulated structures due to the OFG capability.
- **RoboTwin:** In a zero-shot setting (no RoboTwin training data), Embodied-R1.5 achieved a 65.0% average success rate, approaching the fine-tuned 𝜋0.5 (72.9%) and substantially outperforming other fine-tuned baselines. This suggests that robust affordance understanding and accurate pointing can serve as effective zero-shot manipulation primitives.
- **Zero-Shot Real-Robot Experiments:** The PGC closed-loop framework was deployed on Xarm6 and ARX Lift2s arms without fine-tuning. Embodied-R1.5 demonstrated strong performance across tasks like Pick&Place (100.0%), Tool Affordance (100.0%), Spatial Reasoning (83.3%), Cup Disassembly (66.6%), and Door Open (16.6%), notably outperforming baselines in multi-step and articulated object tasks.

### 13. Long-Horizon Closed-Loop Demonstrations

The PGC framework enabled fully autonomous, zero-shot execution of complex, long-horizon tasks on bimanual ARX Lift2s and Realman RM75 platforms, including "Make milk tea" (10 steps), "Stack cups into three layers" (6 steps), "Sweep garbage" (cyclic), and "Goods picking from shelves" (open-vocabulary). The system also demonstrated robust correction capabilities under active human perturbations during a pick-and-place task, autonomously re-localizing objects and replanning to complete the task.

### 14. Analysis

- **RFT vs. SFT:** Reinforced Fine-Tuning (RFT) consistently improved performance over Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) alone, yielding gains of +3.8pp on Pointing, +2.7pp on Planning, and +1.3pp on Cognition.
- **VLM Backbone for VLA:** Embodied-R1.5 as a VLM backbone led to faster convergence and higher final performance in VLA adaptation on LIBERO compared to Qwen3-VL-8B, validating the hypothesis that a stronger embodied backbone reduces VLA data and compute requirements.
- **RL Recipe:** The full multi-task balanced RL recipe (including difficulty-aware filtering, dynamic filtering, and global batch reward normalization) achieved the best balance across capability dimensions.
- **Contact-Rich Manipulation:** When combined with a force-aware flow matching policy, Embodied-R1.5 enabled contact-rich manipulation through a vision-to-force handover mechanism, demonstrated in tasks like "Clean the Vase" and "Plug the Charger."

### 15. Significance and potential impact

The research on Embodied-R1.5 presents several significant contributions to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, with notable potential impacts:

- **Advancement Towards General Physical Intelligence:** By systematically integrating and unifying comprehensive embodied reasoning capabilities—cognition, planning, correction, and pointing—within a single architecture, Embodied-R1.5 represents a step towards achieving general-purpose physical intelligence. This unified approach could address fundamental limitations of fragmented systems and pave the way for more holistic and adaptable robot behaviors.

- **Paradigm Shift in Embodied Foundation Model (EFM) Design:** The work challenges the conventional approach of deploying separate, specialized models for different embodied tasks. By demonstrating that a single, 8-billion-parameter model can orchestrate a wide spectrum of capabilities, it suggests a shift towards more integrated and efficient EFM designs. This could simplify development, reduce computational overhead, and enhance the coherence of robotic decision-making by enabling seamless information flow across reasoning stages.

- **Reduced Data Dependency for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** A key finding is that a strong embodied reasoning backbone can significantly reduce the amount of action-specific data required for training downstream VLA models. This has substantial implications for the scalability and feasibility of developing robotic manipulation systems, as large-scale action data collection remains a costly and time-consuming bottleneck. By demonstrating that internalized reasoning can partially substitute for extensive action pretraining, Embodied-R1.5-VLA offers a more data-efficient pathway to deploy capable robotic agents.

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy and Robustness:** The Planner-Grounder-Corrector (PGC) closed-loop framework, driven solely by the Embodied-R1.5 model, represents an advance in autonomous execution for long-horizon tasks. The ability to autonomously plan, execute, monitor progress, detect errors, and self-correct—even under human perturbation—significantly enhances robot robustness and reliability in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in practical applications where unforeseen events are common.

- **Broad Generalization and Transferability:** The model demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to novel objects, articulated structures, and diverse real-world scenarios. This is evidenced by its performance on unseen categories in ManiSkill-Affordance, its competitive zero-shot performance on RoboTwin manipulation tasks compared to fine-tuned baselines, and its successful real-robot deployments. Such broad generalization is essential for reducing the need for extensive task-specific training and enabling robots to adapt to new environments and tasks with minimal prior experience.

- **Community Infrastructure and Acceleration of Research:** The open-sourcing of model weights, comprehensive datasets, training code, and the EmbodiedEvalKit evaluation framework is a significant contribution to the research community. This provides a standardized, reproducible, and extensible infrastructure, which can lower barriers to entry for new researchers and accelerate progress in the development and evaluation of EFMs. It fosters collaborative innovation by providing a common baseline and tools.

- **Future Research Directions:** While achieving strong results, the work also opens new avenues for future research, such as integrating native 3D perception (beyond 2D image inputs), exploring tighter coupling between reasoning tokens and action generation in VLA models, and extending the PGC framework to more complex environments like mobile manipulation and navigation. These directions indicate a clear path for continued advancements leveraging the foundation laid by Embodied-R1.5.
