# CHIP: Adaptive Compliance for Humanoid Control through Hindsight Perturbation

> [!summary] Summary
> CHIP introduces an adaptive compliance method for humanoid control using hindsight perturbation, integrating controllable end-effector stiffness directly into existing motion-tracking policies. The system enables humanoids to perform forceful, contact-rich manipulation tasks while preserving agile locomotion, achieving up to 80% grasp success rates in multi-robot collaboration and enabling autonomous wiping tasks with 60-80% success.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with complex, forceful manipulation tasks requiring precise control over interaction forces, despite advancements in agile locomotion.
- Existing compliance methods for humanoids are often difficult to scale, require extensive synthetic data, or lead to optimization conflicts that degrade motion quality and agility.
- There is a need for a scalable, generalizable, and plug-and-play solution to reconcile high-gain stiffness for agility with variable compliance for safe contact-rich interaction.

## Method

- **CHIP** (==Adaptive Compliance Humanoid== control through Hindsight Perturbation) is a lightweight module that modifies sparse tracking goals as input observations to the policy during training.
- During training, random forces perturb end-effectors, and the policy observes a 'hindsight' goal (original goal minus perturbation effect, scaled by a compliance coefficient), learning to respond compliantly.
- Rewards are calculated based on the original, unmodified reference motion, ensuring that the robot is incentivized to produce natural, agile movements without compromising tracking accuracy.

## Results

- CHIP maintains position tracking accuracy (0.02m local error) comparable to non-compliant baselines while achieving adaptive compliance, unlike methods that degrade tracking.
- The compliant controller (1/k=**0.04**) achieved an **80%** grasp success rate in multi-robot object transportation, a significant improvement over 'always-stiff' (**5%**) and non-perturbed (**40%**) controllers.
- Teleoperated data collected with CHIP allowed VLA models to perform autonomous single-arm wiping (**60%** success) and bimanual whiteboard wiping (**80%** success) tasks requiring compliant contact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modifying input goals with 'hindsight perturbation' is an effective way to train adaptive compliance without altering dense reference motions or causing reward conflicts.
> - Implicitly learning external forces through proprioception and providing continuous compliance coefficients enables fine-grained stiffness modulation for diverse tasks.
> - Integrating adaptive compliance into keypoint-based tracking policies enhances their versatility for teleoperation, multi-robot collaboration, and data collection for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
