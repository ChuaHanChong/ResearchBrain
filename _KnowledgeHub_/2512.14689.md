# CHIP: Adaptive Compliance for Humanoid Control through Hindsight Perturbation

> [!summary] Summary
> CHIP introduces an adaptive compliance method for humanoid control using hindsight perturbation, integrating controllable end-effector stiffness directly into existing motion-tracking policies. The system enables humanoids to perform forceful, contact-rich manipulation tasks while preserving agile locomotion, achieving up to 80% grasp success rates in multi-robot collaboration and enabling autonomous wiping tasks with 60-80% success.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with complex, forceful manipulation tasks requiring precise control over interaction forces, despite advancements in agile locomotion.
- Existing compliance methods for humanoids are often difficult to scale, require extensive synthetic data, or lead to optimization conflicts that degrade motion quality and agility.
- There is a need for a scalable, generalizable, and plug-and-play solution to reconcile high-gain stiffness for agility with variable compliance for safe contact-rich interaction.

## Method

- **CHIP** (==Adaptive Compliance Humanoid== control through Hindsight Perturbation) is a lightweight module that modifies sparse tracking goals as input observations to the policy during training.
- During training, random forces perturb end-effectors, and the policy observes a 'hindsight' goal (original goal minus perturbation effect, scaled by a compliance coefficient), learning to respond compliantly.
- Rewards are calculated based on the original, unmodified reference motion, ensuring that the robot is incentivized to produce natural, agile movements without compromising tracking accuracy.

## Results

- CHIP maintains position tracking accuracy (0.02m local error) comparable to non-compliant baselines while achieving adaptive compliance, unlike methods that degrade tracking.
- The compliant controller (1/k=**0.04**) achieved an **80%** grasp success rate in multi-robot object transportation, a significant improvement over 'always-stiff' (**5%**) and non-perturbed (**40%**) controllers.
- Teleoperated data collected with CHIP allowed VLA models to perform autonomous single-arm wiping (**60%** success) and bimanual whiteboard wiping (**80%** success) tasks requiring compliant contact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modifying input goals with 'hindsight perturbation' is an effective way to train adaptive compliance without altering dense reference motions or causing reward conflicts.
> - Implicitly learning external forces through proprioception and providing continuous compliance coefficients enables fine-grained stiffness modulation for diverse tasks.
> - Integrating adaptive compliance into keypoint-based tracking policies enhances their versatility for teleoperation, multi-robot collaboration, and data collection for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen remarkable progress in recent years, particularly in achieving agile locomotion skills like running, backflipping, and dynamic balancing, largely driven by advancements in reinforcement learning (RL). However, a persistent challenge remains in enabling humanoids to perform complex, forceful manipulation tasks that require precise control over interaction forces, such as pushing, wiping, or lifting heavy objects. Traditionally, such tasks in industrial or tabletop manipulators are handled by model-based compliance or impedance control. The high-gain, stiff control policies often derived from RL for agile locomotion are fundamentally at odds with the need for variable compliance during contact-rich interactions.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It positions itself within the evolving landscape of compliant control for legged and humanoid robots, distinguishing itself from several existing paradigms:

- **Model-based Compliance Control:** While effective for traditional manipulators, these methods are often difficult to generalize to the complex, high-degree-of-freedom dynamics of humanoids and do not seamlessly integrate with the agility of modern RL-based locomotion controllers.
- **RL-based Force Resistance:** Approaches like FALCON train policies to robustly *resist* external forces, maintaining reference trajectories despite disturbances. While good for tasks like pulling heavy carts, this stiffness-centric approach is unsuitable for delicate or collaborative tasks requiring the robot to *yield* in a controlled manner.
- **RL-based Compliance "From Scratch":** Methods like UniFP and FACET learn impedance behaviors for quadruped robots by mimicking spring-damper dynamics. However, scaling these to humanoids is challenging due to the difficulty of generating large volumes of *natural-looking* synthetic compliance data across diverse human motions.
- **Compliance via Data Augmentation or Reward Tuning:** Concurrent efforts like SoftMimic and GentleHumanoid try to build compliance into motion-tracking frameworks by either offline data augmentation (modifying reference trajectories) or reward shaping (modifying reward calculations). The paper critiques these methods for their limitations: SoftMimic's offline IK-based augmentation is computationally intensive and may not maintain dynamic feasibility for agile motions. GentleHumanoid's reward-based approach creates an optimization conflict between tracking accuracy and compliance, often leading to degraded performance or reduced agility.

CHIP differentiates itself by proposing a **scalable and generalizable plug-and-play module** that integrates adaptive compliance *directly into the online RL loop* of existing motion-tracking frameworks. Its core innovation—**hindsight perturbation**—circumvents the limitations of prior work by modifying *observed tracking goals* as input to the policy, rather than altering the dense reference motion for reward calculation or relying on extensive synthetic data generation. This allows the robot to learn compliant responses while still receiving dense, high-quality rewards for tracking the original, natural human motion, thereby preserving agility and naturalness.

By focusing on generalist keypoint-based control interfaces (local and global 3-point tracking), CHIP also contributes to the broader trend of simplifying humanoid control, moving beyond complex joint- and link-level state tracking towards more intuitive goal-based or teleoperation-driven paradigms (e.g., SONIC, OmniH2O). Ultimately, CHIP aims to bridge the long-standing divide between agile humanoid locomotion and fine-grained, forceful manipulation, pushing humanoids closer to truly versatile and human-like physical interaction capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to perform **forceful, contact-rich manipulation tasks** while simultaneously **preserving their agility and natural motion capabilities**. The authors explicitly pose the guiding question: "Can we develop a scalable and generalizable approach for reconciling the high-gain stiffness required for humanoid agile motions with the variable compliance needed for safe contact-rich manipulation?"

The motivation for CHIP stems from several key observations and challenges in current humanoid robotics:

- **Gap between Locomotion and Manipulation:** Modern humanoid robots excel at agile locomotion thanks to RL, but struggle with manipulation tasks requiring consistent and controllable interaction forces.
- **Limitations of Existing Compliance Methods:**
 - Traditional model-based compliance is not scalable or generalizable for complex humanoids.
 - RL-based compliance for quadrupeds requires extensive, difficult-to-generate synthetic data for humanoids.
 - Prior RL-based attempts for humanoid compliance often involve complex data augmentation or reward tuning, leading to either unscalable pipelines, optimization conflicts, or degraded motion quality/agility.
- **Need for a Scalable and Generalizable Solution:** There is a strong demand for a method that can easily integrate adaptive compliance into existing, highly effective RL motion-tracking frameworks without major architectural changes, extensive data preprocessing, or laborious reward engineering.
- **Enhancing Robot Versatility:** A compliant control capability would unlock a wider range of real-world applications for humanoids, including human-robot collaboration, handling diverse objects (from fragile to heavy), and interacting safely with unstructured environments.
- **Simplifying Control Interfaces:** By integrating compliance into keypoint-based tracking, the goal is also to provide a more intuitive and powerful interface for teleoperation and the training of higher-level Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies.

In summary, the key objective is to develop a lightweight, plug-and-play solution that allows humanoid robots to exhibit **controllable end-effector stiffness** for manipulation, adapt to external forces, and engage in tasks requiring force control, all while maintaining the dynamic agility and naturalness learned from motion tracking, overcoming the trade-offs inherent in previous approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CHIP (Adaptive Compliance Humanoid control through Hindsight Perturbation) is designed as a **lightweight, plug-and-play module** that seamlessly integrates into existing keypoint-based motion-tracking frameworks. Its core innovation lies in a novel training procedure that enables adaptive whole-body control with end-effector-level compliance without compromising agile motion.

**Core Principle: Hindsight Perturbation**

Instead of modifying the dense reference motion used for reward calculation (which is complex and can break dynamic feasibility), CHIP modifies the **sparse tracking goals provided as input observations to the policy**. The policy learns to interpret these modified goals in "hindsight" as the compliant response to an applied perturbation.

1. **During Training:**
 - **Perturbation Application:** Random forces ($f_i$) are applied to selected end-effectors (e.g., hands, head) for random durations and directions. The force magnitude follows a trapezoidal profile, mimicking realistic contact dynamics.
 - **Compliance Coefficients:** The policy receives continuous compliance coefficients ($1/k$) as an input, allowing it to learn to modulate stiffness levels.
 - **Hindsight Goal Modification:** The tracking goal observed by the policy ($g_{hind\_ptb}$) is computed by subtracting the effect of the perturbation from the original reference motion's goal ($g$). Specifically, $g_{hind\_ptb} = g - \frac{1}{k} f$. This means the policy observes a goal that "anticipates" the perturbation's effect, implicitly learning to respond compliantly.
 - **Reward Calculation:** Crucially, the reward function still calculates tracking rewards based on the **original, unmodified reference motion's goal** ($g$), i.e., $r(s, g)$. This ensures that the robot is always incentivized to produce natural, agile motions from the reference distribution, preventing the optimization conflicts faced by reward-shaping methods.
 - **Implicit Force Awareness:** The policy implicitly learns to estimate external forces through its proprioceptive observations (joint positions, velocities) and past actions. To aid this, the critic receives the ground-truth external force as a privileged observation, and both actor and critic receive a history of proprioception and actions.
 - **Damper Model:** To enable smooth compliant following, a damper model is implemented. The target end-effector pose ($g_t$) is updated based on a blend of the current end-effector pose ($x_{eef}$) and the previous target ($g_{t-1}$), allowing the robot to adjust its target in response to force-induced deviations.

2. **During Deployment:**
 - The policy receives only the standard tracking goal ($g$) and proprioception.
 - It implicitly estimates any external perturbation forces and generates actions that produce the desired compliant behavior based on the commanded compliance coefficient.

**Advantages of CHIP's Design:**
- **Ease of Implementation:** Modifying sparse input goals is simpler than altering dense reference trajectories or complex reward functions.
- **Motion Naturalness:** By rewarding tracking of the original reference motion, CHIP encourages behaviors within the distribution of natural human movement.
- **Robustness:** Exposing the robot to perturbed observations during training improves its robustness to unexpected interactions at test time.
- **Scalability:** Eliminates the need for extensive synthetic interaction curricula or offline motion augmentation pipelines.

**Applications Demonstrated:**

The paper showcases CHIP's versatility through two main applications:

1. **Compliant Multi-Robot Grasping and Object Transportation:**
 - A **global 3-point tracking policy** is trained to track the 6D pose of the head and 3D positions of both wrists in the *world frame*.
 - This enables multiple humanoids to collaboratively grasp and transport large objects.
 - It extends the SpringGrasp algorithm for multi-humanoid compliant grasp generation.
 - Requires high-frequency, infrastructure-free localization (foot-odometer, Fast-LIO2, IMU fusion) and consistent global frame alignment.

2. **Teleoperation and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model Training:**
 - A **local 3-point tracking policy** tracks head and wrist poses relative to the robot's *root frame*, alongside lower-body joint targets from a kinematic planner.
 - This supports intuitive VR-based teleoperation, where operators command upper-body movements and adjust end-effector compliance online.
 - Teleoperated data, enriched with compliance coefficients and egocentric vision, is then used to fine-tune generalist VLA models (e.g., GR00T N1.5) for autonomous execution of contact-rich tasks.

**Experimental Setup:**
The Unitree G1 humanoid robot with an OAK-D W camera is used as the test platform. Training is performed on a large cluster of NVIDIA L40S GPUs (64 GPUs, 4096 environments/GPU), demonstrating the scale of the RL training. Deployment leverages onboard Jetson NX for local policies and a powerful desktop for global policies and VLA inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research effectively addresses its guiding questions, demonstrating CHIP's capabilities through rigorous experiments in simulation and on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1).

**Q1: How well does CHIP track position and force targets?**
- **Tracking Accuracy:** CHIP achieves position tracking performance comparable to or better than baselines without compliance (FALCON) or without any force perturbation.
 - In the MuJoCo simulator, CHIP's global position error (ϵgp) ranges from 0.08m (for 1/k=0.00, 0.02, 0.05) to 0.11m (no perturb force baseline), and local position error (ϵlp) is consistently 0.02m.
 - Crucially, unlike Gentlehumanoid which compromises tracking accuracy (ϵlp 0.04-0.05m) to achieve compliance, CHIP maintains high tracking fidelity across different compliance settings. This validates the hindsight perturbation design, which avoids the reward conflict seen in other methods.
- **Force Responsiveness:** The policy can differentiate between internal driving forces for agile motion and external perturbation forces, adapting its stiffness based on the continuous $1/k$ input without degrading general tracking performance.

**Q2: What task(s) do adaptive compliance unlock through teleoperation?**
- **Diverse Contact-Rich Manipulation:** CHIP enables a single compliant local 3-point tracking controller to perform a wide variety of tasks:
 - **Compliance-demanding:** Lifting and transporting boxes, wiping surfaces (requiring consistent contact force).
 - **Force-demanding:** Opening a spring-loaded door, actuating a gantry, flipping heavy boxes.
 - **Bimanual Hybrid Compliance:** Demonstrated holding a whiteboard rigidly with one hand ($1/k=0.001$) while compliantly writing with the other ($1/k=0.05$).
- **Online Compliance Adjustment:** Operators can adjust compliance levels in real-time during long-horizon tasks, e.g., stiff for opening a marker, then compliant for writing. This flexibility is critical for complex, multi-stage tasks.

**Q3: How does CHIP facilitate multi-robot collaboration?**
- **Stable Collaborative Grasping and Transportation:** CHIP significantly improves multi-robot collaboration.
 - An adaptive compliant controller (tuned to $1/k=0.04$) achieved an average grasp success rate of **80%** for various objects (boxes, spheres of different heights).
 - This is a substantial improvement over an "always-stiff" controller (5% success) and a controller trained without force perturbation (40% success). The stiff controller often destabilized objects due to excessive opposing forces.
 - After grasping, robots could stably transport the objects to new locations.

**Q4: Can CHIP enable VLA models to perform autonomous forceful manipulation?**
- **Autonomous Contact-Rich Tasks:** By collecting teleoperated data with appropriate compliance settings, CHIP allows VLA policies (e.g., fine-tuned GR00T N1.5) to perform contact-rich tasks autonomously.
 - **Single-Arm Wiping:** 60% success rate for wiping a large whiteboard while maintaining compliant contact. Failures were primarily due to vision limitations (occlusion, text leaving FoV).
 - **Bimanual Small Whiteboard Wiping:** 80% success rate for holding a small whiteboard stiffly with one hand and wiping compliantly with the other, demonstrating end-effector-specific stiffness.
 - **Bimanual Box Lifting:** 90% success rate for lifting and placing a box, benefiting from bimanual compliance for grasp stability. Main failure mode was initial unsuccessful grasp.

**Q5: What is the advantage of the continuous compliance command space used in CHIP?**
- **Optimized Force-Stability Trade-off:** The continuous nature of $1/k$ allows for fine-grained tuning of stiffness. This enables the robot to be "just stiff enough" to lift a heavy object without being so stiff that it becomes brittle or unstable under minor perturbations. This surpasses discrete stiffness settings (e.g., max-cutoff-force in Gentlehumanoid) by allowing adjustment even within smaller force ranges.
- **Semantic Stiffness Adaptation:** The continuous compliance command is physically grounded in Hooke's law, providing an intuitive interface for teleoperators to adapt stiffness based on visual cues (e.g., estimated object mass) or task requirements. This makes the controller highly adaptable and user-friendly.

In summary, CHIP successfully demonstrates that hindsight perturbation is a robust, scalable, and effective method for integrating adaptive compliance into humanoid motion-tracking policies, unlocking a new level of versatility in manipulation and collaboration for these complex robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CHIP represents a significant step forward in humanoid robotics, particularly in bridging the long-standing gap between agile locomotion and compliant, forceful manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Versatile Humanoid Manipulation:** The most direct impact is equipping humanoids with the ability to perform a broad spectrum of contact-rich tasks that were previously challenging or impossible for purely RL-based controllers. This includes everyday tasks like wiping, opening doors, pushing carts, and handling objects of varying weights and fragility, moving humanoids closer to real-world deployment in service or industrial settings.
- **Scalable and Generalizable Solution:** CHIP's plug-and-play design and hindsight perturbation mechanism offer a highly scalable approach. By avoiding the need for extensive synthetic data augmentation or complex reward engineering, it can be easily integrated into existing and future motion-tracking frameworks. This means that advancements in humanoid locomotion can more readily translate into advancements in compliant manipulation without a complete re-architecture of the control stack.
- **Improved Robustness and Naturalness:** The method trains robots to respond robustly to external forces while maintaining motions within the distribution of natural human movements. This enhances safety during human-robot interaction and makes robot behaviors more predictable and intuitive.
- **Advancing Multi-Robot Collaboration:** The demonstration of compliant multi-robot grasping and object transportation highlights CHIP's potential to enable complex collaborative tasks. This is crucial for scenarios involving heavy loads or large objects that exceed the capabilities of a single robot, opening doors for humanoids in logistics, construction, or disaster response.
- **Facilitating Higher-Level AI (VLA Models):** CHIP significantly simplifies the data collection process for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous execution. By allowing human teleoperators to easily imbue compliance into demonstrations, it provides VLA policies with the necessary skills for contact-rich tasks, accelerating the development of truly intelligent and capable humanoid agents. The continuous compliance command space further enriches the semantic information available for learning.
- **Simplified Human-Robot Interaction:** The integration of adaptive compliance into intuitive keypoint-based teleoperation interfaces makes it easier for human operators to control complex humanoid actions, reducing the cognitive load and increasing efficiency.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** CHIP provides a robust foundation for further research into fine-grained force control, tactile sensing integration, and more sophisticated adaptive behaviors in humanoids. Its focus on end-effector compliance opens avenues for exploring dexterity and precision in contact-rich tasks.

Despite some limitations acknowledged by the authors (e.g., reliance on proprioceptive accuracy, manual tuning of compliance coefficients for specific tasks), CHIP's core methodological contribution of hindsight perturbation for adaptive compliance is a powerful and elegant solution that promises to unlock greater utility and versatility for humanoid robots in dynamic and interactive environments.
