# Recursive Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> Recursive Flow Matching (RecFM) introduces a generative framework for forecasting complex spatiotemporal dynamics, achieving high physical accuracy and computational efficiency by enforcing self-consistency across discretization scales. This approach demonstrates competitive or superior performance across scientific benchmarks with significantly fewer inference steps, such as a 20x speedup over diffusion-based emulators.

## Problem

- Traditional numerical solvers are computationally expensive for forecasting complex spatiotemporal dynamics, while existing data-driven generative models often face a speed-fidelity trade-off.
- Generative models, especially diffusion models and vanilla Flow Matching, typically require many iterative steps (tens to hundreds) for accurate predictions, leading to substantial computational overhead and instability in long-term rollouts.
- Acceleration techniques for generative models frequently struggle to preserve high-frequency structures and spatiotemporal fidelity, which are crucial for accurate scientific simulations, as aggressive step reduction can smooth out essential details.

## Method

- **RecFM** introduces a generative framework that learns a single velocity network conditioned on time and a scale parameter, representing an entire family of recursive trajectories.
- It enforces cross-scale consistency during training by aligning velocities of recursively attenuated trajectories at the same spatial point but corresponding to different scales and aligned times.
- The training objective combines trajectory supervision for each scaled trajectory with a consistency loss, which constrains the temporal derivative of the velocity field to reduce truncation errors and 'straighten' the ODE trajectory.

## Results

- RecFM consistently achieves competitive or superior performance across scientific benchmarks (Sea Surface Temperatures, Navier-Stokes Flow, Helmholtz Staircase) with substantial efficiency gains, demonstrating up to a **20x** speedup over diffusion-based baselines like VideoPDE.
- The framework exhibits improved predictive accuracy (e.g., over **15%** lower MSE than Vanilla FM) and better adherence to underlying physical dynamics (e.g., KE Accuracy of 0.9791 for Navier-Stokes, lower PDE Residual for Helmholtz).
- Ablation studies show optimal performance with a moderate consistency loss weight (lambda=1.0) and a recursion depth of D=2, and RecFM generally converges faster during training while maintaining lower validation error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enforcing self-consistency across different discretization scales enables generative models to achieve high fidelity and stability with significantly fewer inference steps (typically 1-4 steps).
> - The recursive modeling of trajectories, inspired by physical phenomena, provides a rich gradient signal that accelerates training convergence and improves performance compared to single-scale flow matching.
> - The cross-scale consistency loss theoretically reduces the K-step Euler generation error bound by constraining the temporal derivative of the velocity field, allowing for more accurate few-step integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of scientific machine learning, specifically focusing on generative models for forecasting complex spatiotemporal dynamics in physical systems. Traditional numerical solvers provide high-fidelity solutions for physical systems, such as in fluid dynamics or climate modeling, but are often computationally intensive and impractical for real-time or large-scale applications. Data-driven approaches, including neural operators and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), offer alternatives, but can face limitations when governing equations are incomplete, computationally prohibitive, or challenging to formulate for complex and stochastic systems like climate dynamics.

Recent advancements in generative modeling have shown promise in learning high-frequency data distributions, addressing challenges in molecular design, material generation, and climate modeling by providing uncertainty quantification and handling sparse or irregular measurements. Within this domain, diffusion-based models have been demonstrated to reconstruct spatiotemporal dynamics. However, a significant limitation of these models is their iterative inference procedure, often requiring tens to hundreds of sequential denoising steps, leading to considerable computational overhead for time-dependent simulations.

Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) and Flow Matching (FM) have emerged as more efficient alternatives by learning continuous vector fields that define probability paths. While these methods reduce the number of function evaluations, a trade-off typically persists: reducing inference steps often results in degraded accuracy and instability, particularly in long-term rollouts. Efforts to accelerate these systems further include consistency models and distillation-based methods, which introduce self-consistency constraints or compress multi-step generation into efficient models. A challenge for these acceleration techniques is the preservation of spectral richness and spatiotemporal fidelity, as aggressive step reduction can smooth out high-frequency structures essential for accurate scientific simulations.

Recursive Flow Matching (RecFM) addresses these identified limitations by introducing a generative framework designed to achieve efficient few-step (typically one to four steps) generation while maintaining trajectory fidelity and stability in physics-based systems. It attempts to overcome the speed-fidelity trade-off by enforcing self-consistency across discretization scales, aiming to reduce discretization errors and improve performance. This approach is positioned as a method to provide high-fidelity dynamic generation comparable to state-of-the-art multi-step solvers, but with substantially reduced computational cost.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a generative framework that can forecast complex spatiotemporal dynamics with high physical accuracy and computational efficiency, thereby overcoming the inherent speed-fidelity trade-off observed in existing generative models for scientific applications.

The key motivations driving the development of Recursive Flow Matching (RecFM) are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing the Speed-Fidelity Trade-off:** Traditional numerical solvers, while accurate, are computationally expensive, making them unsuitable for real-time or large-scale scientific applications. Data-driven approaches, including neural operators and PINNs, have emerged but can struggle with systems where governing equations are unknown, incomplete, or highly complex. Generative models, particularly diffusion models, show promise for learning complex data distributions in scientific domains and quantifying uncertainty. However, these models typically rely on iterative inference, requiring numerous steps (tens to hundreds) to produce high-quality predictions, leading to substantial computational overhead for time-dependent simulations. This iterative nature inherently creates a trade-off between the speed of inference and the fidelity of the generated output.

2. **Improving upon Flow Matching Limitations:** Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) and Flow Matching (FM) are more efficient than diffusion models as they learn continuous vector fields directly. However, these methods still exhibit a trade-off where reducing the number of inference steps often leads to degraded accuracy and instability, especially in long-term dynamic predictions. This makes them less suitable for scenarios requiring rapid, yet accurate, simulations.

3. **Enhancing Consistency and Fidelity in Accelerated Models:** Recent efforts to accelerate generative models, such as consistency models and distillation techniques, aim to reduce inference steps. A critical challenge for these methods is preserving the high-frequency structures and spectral richness of physical fields. Aggressive step reduction can inadvertently smooth out these crucial details, compromising the physical fidelity of the simulations. The motivation is to achieve efficient few-step generation (e.g., one to four steps) without sacrificing the spatiotemporal fidelity and stability required for accurate scientific emulation.

4. **Enabling Real-time Scientific Emulation:** The computational cost of high-fidelity simulations often precludes their use in applications requiring real-time responses or rapid exploration of parameters. By developing a method that can deliver accurate predictions with significantly fewer computational steps, the researchers aim to provide a scalable and efficient solution that facilitates real-time scientific emulation across various domains.

In response to these motivations, RecFM introduces a framework that enforces self-consistency by aligning trajectories across different discretization scales. This mechanism is designed to reduce discretization errors and enhance performance across metrics relevant to physics-based tasks, with the specific goal of achieving high-fidelity one- and few-step dynamic generation that is comparable to state-of-the-art multi-step solvers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Recursive Flow Matching (RecFM) builds upon the principles of Flow Matching (FM) and self-consistency to forecast complex spatiotemporal dynamics efficiently.

#### 3.1. Flow Matching (FM) Background

Flow Matching (FM) is a method for training Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) by learning a time-dependent vector field `v_t(x; theta)`. This vector field defines a probability path `p_t` that connects a target data distribution `p_0` (e.g., real data) to a tractable source distribution `p_1` (e.g., standard Gaussian noise). The transformation of a data sample `x_0` to a noise sample `x_1` is governed by an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE). Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) typically uses a per-sample regression objective, where the target velocity field `u_t(x_t|x_0, x_1)` is often derived from the Optimal Transport (OT) path, commonly represented by linear interpolation `x_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1` and a constant target velocity `u_t = x_1 - x_0`. The effectiveness of FM in terms of sampling efficiency and stability is influenced by the choice of trajectory.

#### 3.2. Self-Consistency and Flow Map

To address the iterative nature of traditional FM, the concept of self-consistency is employed. For a flow map `X_{s,t}` that transports a state from time `s` to `t`, self-consistency implies a semigroup condition: `X_{u,t}(X_{s,u}(x)) = X_{s,t}(x)` for `0 <= s <= u <= t <= 1`. In "one-step" models, a consistency function `f_theta(x_t, t)` is trained to satisfy `f_theta(x_t, t) = x_1` for all `t`. This ensures that the generated path remains consistent regardless of whether it is traversed in a single large step or multiple smaller increments, acting as a regularization to "straighten" the ODE trajectory and minimize truncation errors.

#### 3.3. Physics Intuition: Wall-Bouncing Pendulum

RecFM draws inspiration from the recursive motion of a 1D wall-bouncing pendulum. In this idealized model, a pendulum's velocity magnitude is reduced by a factor `alpha` upon each collision with a wall. This results in a family of trajectories with progressively attenuated velocities, where `v^(i+1) = alpha * v^(i)`. If the half-cycle duration is constant across scales, the amplitude shrinks proportionally with velocity. This physical phenomenon motivates the idea of learning a family of scaled trajectories that are self-consistent.

#### 3.4. RecFM Algorithm Formulation

RecFM defines a velocity network `v_theta(x, t, alpha)` which is conditioned on both time `t` and a scale parameter `alpha`. This single network represents an entire family of trajectories.
1. **Shared Spatial Point:** For a given data sample `x_0` and noise sample `x_1`, a standard linear interpolant `x_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1` is used, with `v* = x_1 - x_0` as the ground-truth primary velocity. This `x_t` serves as a shared spatial point through which multiple trajectories, operating at different scales, pass at correspondingly aligned times.
2. **Recursive Trajectories:** The framework considers a recursion depth `D`, defining `D` trajectories.
 - The **primary trajectory** (`i=1`) corresponds to `alpha^(1) = 1` and `tau^(1) = t`. Its predicted velocity is `v_hat^(1) = v_theta(x_t, t, 1)`.
 - **Secondary trajectories** (`i=2 to D`) are defined by recursively attenuated scales `alpha^(i) = alpha^(i-1)`. The rescaled time for the `i`-th trajectory is `tau^(i) = t / alpha^(i)`. The predicted velocity for the `i`-th trajectory is `v_hat^(i) = v_theta(x_t, tau^(i), alpha^(i))`.
3. **Cross-Scale Consistency:** A key aspect is enforcing the consistency relation `v_hat^(i+1) = alpha * v_hat^(i)` for velocities predicted by the model at the same spatial point `x_t` but corresponding to different scales and aligned times.

#### 3.5. Training Objective

The total training objective `L_total` combines trajectory supervision and cross-scale consistency:
`L_total = sum_{i=1 to D} L_traj^(i) + lambda * sum_{i=2 to D} L_cons^(i)`
- `L_traj^(i) = ||v_hat^(i) - alpha^(i) * v*||^2`: This term supervises each predicted trajectory `v_hat^(i)` towards its scaled ground-truth velocity `alpha^(i) * v*`.
- `L_cons^(i) = ||v_hat^(i) - alpha^(i) * v_hat^(1)||^2`: This term enforces consistency by aligning the predicted velocity of the `i`-th trajectory with the scaled predicted velocity of the primary trajectory `v_hat^(1)`. The parameter `lambda` controls the weight of this consistency loss.

#### 3.6. Inference Sampling

During inference, RecFM solves the ODE defined by the learned velocity field `v_theta(x_t, t, 1)` (using `alpha=1` for the primary trajectory).
- **One-step generation:** A noise sample `x_1` is mapped to the data manifold `x_0` via a single Euler step: `x_0 ≈ x_1 - h * v_theta(x_1, 1, 1)`, with `h=1`.
- **Multi-step generation:** For `K` steps, `x_t_k = x_t_(k-1) - h_k * v_theta(x_t_(k-1), t_(k-1), 1)`.

By integrating cross-scale velocity consistency during training, RecFM aims to learn trajectories that remain stable even with larger integration steps, enabling accurate few-step generation.

#### 3.7. Theoretical Results

Theorem 3.1 states that adding recursive trajectories and the cross-scale consistency loss accelerates the convergence of RecFM by reducing truncation error. The K-step Euler generation error is bounded by a term related to the trajectory acceleration `a(x,t)`. The consistency loss `L_cons` is shown to constrain the temporal derivative of the velocity field (`||partial_t v_theta||`), which is a component of the acceleration `a`. By reducing `||partial_t v_theta||`, RecFM effectively reduces `||a||`, thereby tightening the error bound and promoting "trajectory straightening." This explains why few-step Euler integration becomes more accurate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluates Recursive Flow Matching (RecFM) across various scientific benchmarks and a general image generation task, assessing both fidelity and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Datasets and Experimental Setup

- **Scientific Benchmarks:**
 - **Sea Surface Temperatures (SST):** Real-world climate dataset from NOAA OISSTv2, focused on a 60x60 grid in the eastern tropical Pacific. Predicts 7 days ahead from 1-day input.
 - **Navier-Stokes Flow:** Simulated incompressible channel flow past four circular obstacles on a 221x42 grid (resized to 220x40 for models), comprising velocity components (u, v) and pressure (p). Predicts 16 frames per step for a 64-step reconstruction.
 - **Helmholtz Staircase Equation:** Higher-order analytical solution for acoustic scattering on a 1024x256 grid, comprising real and imaginary pressure components. Generates 7 frames per step for a 49-step reconstruction.
- **Forecasting Configuration:** Autoregressive rollouts are employed. Probabilistic metrics (CRPS, SSR) use M=50 ensemble members. The model architecture is a pixel-level temporal DiT backbone, similar to VideoPDE, operating on a single NVIDIA L40S GPU for inference time measurements. RecFM is primarily evaluated in 1-step and 2-step inference regimes.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive suite of generative and stochastic benchmarks including:
 - Stochastic Methods: Perturbation, Dropout.
 - Iterative Diffusion Models: DDPM, MCVD.
 - Dynamics-Informed Diffusion: DYffusion.
 - State-of-the-art Generative Backbones: VideoPDE (unified solver), Vanilla FM (identical architecture to RecFM without recursive features).
 - Physics-Informed Flow Matching (PBFM) and Shortcut Models are included in appendices for specific comparisons.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS):** Measures the accuracy of the cumulative distribution function, a strictly proper scoring rule. Lower is better.
 - **Mean Squared Error (MSE):** Measures the deterministic accuracy of the ensemble mean prediction. Lower is better.
 - **Spread-Skill Ratio (SSR):** Compares ensemble spread to RMSE, with an ideal value of 1.0 indicating a perfectly calibrated ensemble.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Overall Performance:** RecFM consistently demonstrates competitive or superior performance across all scientific benchmarks compared to baselines.
 - On Sea Surface Temperatures, RecFM (1-step) achieves a CRPS of 0.217 and MSE of 0.162, comparable to or slightly better than DYffusion (CRPS 0.224, MSE 0.173) and VideoPDE (CRPS 0.216, MSE 0.162), but with significantly faster inference.
 - For Navier-Stokes Flow, RecFM (1-step) yields CRPS 0.031 and MSE 0.0064, outperforming DYffusion (CRPS 0.067, MSE 0.022) and VideoPDE (CRPS 0.033, MSE 0.0068).
 - On the Helmholtz Staircase Equation, RecFM (1-step) achieves CRPS 0.0034 and MSE 4.2e-5, representing a notable improvement over VideoPDE (CRPS 0.026, MSE 5.6e-4).
- **Efficiency:** RecFM achieves up to a 20x speedup over the diffusion-based VideoPDE while also improving predictive accuracy and calibration. Compared to Vanilla FM, which typically requires around 5 inference steps, RecFM yields high-quality results with only 1-2 steps, achieving over 15% lower MSE and improved SSR scores.
- **Step-size Performance:** RecFM's best performance is often observed with one- or two-step generation. Increasing the number of steps does not consistently lead to better results, suggesting that errors can accumulate in multi-step scenarios for deterministic physics-governed systems.
- **Physics-informed Metrics (Appendix F):** On Navier-Stokes, RecFM (1-step) shows a KE Accuracy of 0.9791 (closer to ideal 1.0) compared to VideoPDE's 0.9670 and PBFM's 0.9592. For Helmholtz, RecFM (1-step) achieves a PDE Residual of 0.00476, which is lower (better adherence to dynamics) than VideoPDE's 0.00841 and PBFM's 0.00519. This indicates better preservation of underlying physical dynamics.
- **Statistical Significance (Appendix G):** Reported standard deviations show that RecFM's performance advantages are statistically robust across multiple runs.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- **Helmholtz Staircase Visualization (Figure 3, Figure 7):** Visual comparisons show that RecFM produces predictions that closely match the ground truth, effectively capturing circular wave propagation patterns. VideoPDE, the best-performing baseline, appears to struggle more with these details, exhibiting larger absolute errors.
- **Navier-Stokes Rollout (Figure 8):** Visualizations indicate RecFM's ability to maintain stable and accurate dynamics over time, without noticeable accumulation of errors, in contrast to some baselines where errors accumulate.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics and Stability

- **Convergence Speed (Figure 4, Figure 6):** RecFM demonstrates faster convergence during training, achieving lower validation error earlier and maintaining it throughout training, compared to diffusion-based models like VideoPDE and Vanilla FM.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Consistency Loss Weight (λ):** A moderate consistency loss weight (e.g., λ=1.0) provides the best performance. Very low λ (insufficient emphasis on consistency) or very high λ (consistency overwhelming primary objective) degrades performance. Interestingly, even without the explicit consistency term (λ=0), RecFM still outperforms Vanilla FM, attributed to the secondary trajectory loss providing additional supervision.
- **Recursion Depth (D):** A depth of D=2 (primary and one secondary trajectory) is identified as the optimal configuration, balancing performance and efficiency. Increasing the depth to D=3 yields slightly inferior performance and higher memory requirements, suggesting diminishing returns.

#### 4.6. Generalization to Image Generation (Appendix I)

- RecFM-XL achieves competitive Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores (<3 within 16 sampling steps) on the ImageNet-1k dataset, demonstrating its applicability beyond scientific emulation. It requires fewer training epochs and inference steps than some established diffusion transformers (DiT, SiT) and latent diffusion models (LDM). Performance is better at 16 steps than 128, reinforcing the emphasis on few-step generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Recursive Flow Matching (RecFM), a novel generative framework for forecasting complex spatiotemporal dynamics, which demonstrates several significant contributions and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Challenging the Speed-Fidelity Trade-off:** RecFM provides evidence that it is possible to achieve high physical accuracy in generative models without incurring high computational costs, particularly in physics-based applications. The findings suggest that enforcing self-consistency across discretization scales can actually enhance both stability and accuracy, even with fewer inference steps. This outcome questions the conventional assumption of a direct trade-off between sampling efficiency and fidelity in generative modeling.
- **Novel Consistency Mechanism:** The core innovation of RecFM lies in its recursive modeling of a family of trajectories at different inference-time traversal scales. By aligning states that correspond to the same underlying point along the probability path, it ensures coherence across discretizations. This multi-scale coupling provides additional supervision during training, improving stability and performance in one- or few-step inference regimes.
- **High Efficiency and Accuracy:** RecFM achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art multi-step solvers while operating in significantly fewer sampling steps (typically 1-4 steps). Quantitatively, it demonstrates substantial speedups (up to 20x over leading diffusion-based emulators) and improved predictive accuracy (over 15% reduction in MSE compared to vanilla flow matching) across challenging scientific benchmarks. The approach also shows better calibration of probabilistic predictions (SSR closer to 1.0) and better adherence to underlying physical dynamics (lower PDE residuals and higher KE accuracy).
- **Training Stability and Convergence:** The framework contributes to more stable and efficient training, exhibiting faster convergence and consistently lower validation errors compared to existing flow matching and diffusion-based methods. This is attributed to the enriched gradient signal provided by the multi-scale trajectory alignment.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Real-time Scientific Emulation:** The achieved efficiency makes RecFM a candidate for real-time scientific emulation in domains where rapid predictions are critical. This could include applications in weather forecasting, climate modeling, fluid dynamics simulations for engineering design, and other areas requiring quick turnaround for complex physical phenomena.
- **Bridging Data-driven and Physics-based Modeling:** RecFM's ability to model systems governed by explicit PDE formulations or implicitly learned data-driven dynamics offers a unified approach. This flexibility could accelerate discovery and application in interdisciplinary scientific research where governing equations might be partially known or entirely unknown.
- **Foundation Models for Physical Systems:** The promising results, coupled with its generalizability beyond physics-based systems (as indicated by competitive image generation performance), suggest RecFM could serve as a foundational component for developing general-purpose models for multi-physics and real-world dynamical systems. Such foundation models could be fine-tuned for a wide array of scientific and engineering tasks.
- **Advancement in Generative Model Research:** The findings contribute to the broader understanding of generative models, particularly in the context of continuous normalizing flows and consistency-based approaches. By highlighting the importance of trajectory structure and cross-scale coherence, RecFM opens new avenues for research into designing more efficient and accurate generative processes.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its advantages, the paper identifies limitations, particularly concerning the extension of RecFM to high-complexity real-world video. Natural videos involve rich semantic and temporal variations that may necessitate modeling capabilities beyond standard flow matching trajectories. While preliminary image-generation results are competitive, scaling the framework to realistic video domains remains an open research problem. Future work will focus on exploring these complex settings and further investigating RecFM's potential as a general-purpose foundation model for multi-physics and real-world dynamical systems.
