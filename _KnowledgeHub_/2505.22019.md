# VRAG-RL: Empower Vision-Perception-Based RAG for Visually Rich Information Understanding via Iterative Reasoning with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VRAG-RL, a framework developed by Alibaba Group's Tongyi Lab, empowers Vision-Language Models to understand visually rich information by integrating a novel visual perception action space, a fine-grained reward function, and reinforcement learning for iterative reasoning. The approach achieved an over 20% accuracy improvement on a 7B parameter model and over 30% on a 3B parameter model compared to strong baselines in understanding complex visual documents.

## Problem

- Existing visual RAGs struggle to process non-textual visual cues and underutilize VLM reasoning capabilities.
- VLMs often articulate imprecise requirements to search engines, leading to inefficient and irrelevant information retrieval.
- Maintaining stability and consistency during multi-turn reasoning with reinforcement learning for VLMs remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- Introduces a ==visual perception action space== (select, crop, zoom) for VLMs to dynamically focus on information-dense regions using a =="crop and re-input" strategy== to increase visual token density.
- Proposes a ==fine-grained reward function== tailored for RAG, combining retrieval efficiency (NDCG-inspired), pattern consistency, and a flexible model-based outcome reward.
- Employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== within an iterative Thought-Action-Observation loop to achieve stable and consistent multi-turn reasoning with VLMs.

## Results

- VRAG-RL improved accuracy by **22.4 percentage points** (to **57.1%**) for the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model and **32.2 percentage points** (to **53.5%**) for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model over Search-R1-VL baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the RAG-specific reward and the visual perception action space are critical for performance gains, particularly in understanding high-density visual information.
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated enhanced retrieval efficiency (higher Recall@1), reduced invalid action rates, and improved multi-step reasoning capabilities compared to SFT-only methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enabling VLMs to dynamically perceive and re-attend to specific visual regions via dedicated actions significantly activates their fine-grained reasoning capabilities.
> - Integrating retrieval performance directly into the reward function (e.g., through NDCG-inspired efficiency) is crucial for aligning VLM actions with effective RAG outcomes.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), can robustly guide VLMs through complex multi-turn interactions, improving instruction following and strategic adaptability for visually rich documents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of AI has seen significant advancements in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), a paradigm that enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to leverage external information, thereby addressing knowledge-intensive tasks and reducing hallucinations. Traditionally, RAG primarily focused on textual data. However, with the proliferation of digital documents containing complex visual elements like charts, tables, and layouts, there's a growing need for RAG systems to handle "visually rich information." This has led to the emergence of **Multimodal RAG** or **Visual RAG**, where Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are integrated with search engines to understand and reason over both text and images.

Despite these advancements, current visual RAG methods face several critical challenges:
- **Inability to Handle Visual-Related Information:** Traditional text-based RAG is inherently limited in processing non-textual visual cues such as spatial relationships, graphical representations, and overall layout.
- **Insufficient Reasoning Activation:** Existing vision-based RAG approaches often operate with fixed pipelines and tend to merely embed images into context. This "shallow" integration underutilizes the fundamental reasoning capabilities of VLMs, leading to insufficient allocation of reasoning tokens for visual-specific perception.
- **Inefficient and Disjointed Retrieval:** VLMs frequently struggle to articulate precise requirements to search engines, resulting in the retrieval of irrelevant information or repetitive, meaningless interactions. This hampers the overall effectiveness of the RAG process.
- **Inconsistent Multi-Turn Reasoning and Unstable Training:** Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to LMs and VLMs for complex multi-step reasoning tasks is a promising but challenging area. Maintaining stability and consistency during multi-turn interactions with external environments, especially for VLMs with their unique complexities, remains a significant hurdle.

VRAG-RL directly positions itself at the intersection of multimodal RAG and Reinforcement Learning, aiming to overcome these identified limitations. It builds upon the recognized efficacy of RL in enhancing reasoning capabilities in LLMs and extends this to VLMs within a RAG framework. By addressing the deep integration of visual perception, fine-grained retrieval, and stable multi-turn reasoning through RL, VRAG-RL seeks to push the boundaries of how VLMs interact with and understand visually rich external knowledge. This work aims to move beyond simple "image in context" towards a more dynamic, perception-aware, and iteratively refined reasoning process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **VRAG-RL**, a novel reinforcement learning framework specifically designed to train Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for effective retrieval, reasoning, and understanding of visually rich information. The framework aims to empower VLMs to interact dynamically with external search engines, autonomously sampling and optimizing single-turn or multi-turn reasoning trajectories.

The motivation behind VRAG-RL stems directly from the critical observations and limitations identified in the current landscape of visual RAG methods:

1. **To activate and leverage VLMs' full reasoning potential with visual information:** Current methods often underutilize VLMs' capabilities by simply embedding images without proper visual-specific perception. VRAG-RL seeks to enable VLMs to pay higher attention to information-dense areas and extract details, preventing overlooking crucial visual data within limited context lengths.
2. **To improve the efficiency and relevance of retrieval:** Traditional models struggle to articulate complex visual search requirements, leading to inefficient and irrelevant retrievals. VRAG-RL aims to bridge this gap between user inquiries and the retriever by integrating retrieval performance into its optimization process, ensuring that pertinent information is retrieved promptly.
3. **To achieve stable and consistent multi-turn reasoning with VLMs:** The inherent challenges of maintaining stability in multi-turn RL frameworks, particularly for VLMs, are addressed. The goal is to enable VLMs to flexibly adjust strategies and maintain effective reasoning across multiple interactions with the environment.
4. **To align VLMs with real-world RAG applications:** By designing specialized RL strategies and reward mechanisms, the framework aims to make VLMs more practical and effective for complex, real-world tasks involving visually rich documents.

In essence, VRAG-RL is motivated by the need for a more intelligent, adaptive, and perception-aware VLM-driven RAG system that can genuinely understand and reason over the complexities of visual information, moving beyond mere textual interpretations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VRAG-RL proposes a comprehensive reinforcement learning framework that redefines how VLMs interact with visually rich information. Its methodology is built upon three core improvements: a novel visual perception action space, a fine-grained reward function tailored for RAG, and an iterative RL training strategy.

#### 3.1. Visual Perception Action Integration for Understanding Information-Dense Regions

This module addresses the limitation of insufficient reasoning activation in prior multi-modal RAGs. Instead of merely embedding full images, VRAG-RL introduces a dynamic visual perception paradigm:
- **Definition of Visual Perception Actions:** A custom action space is defined for VLMs, incorporating actions like `select`, `crop`, and `zoom`. When a VLM identifies a region of interest in an already retrieved image, it outputs perception tokens `<region>[x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max]</region>`.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Perception:** A role-based function extracts these bounding box coordinates. The specified region is then cropped from the *original raw image* and scaled/re-encoded. This "crop and re-input" strategy effectively increases the visual token density for the region of interest, allowing the VLM to achieve higher perceptual resolution and activate more detailed reasoning within its limited context length.
- **Trajectory Data Scaling-Up:** To provide high-quality data for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) before RL, a multi-expert sampling strategy is employed. Large-scale models (π_LM, e.g., Qwen-VL-max-latest) guide the overall reasoning pathway and tool selections, while smaller, grounding-specific expert models (π_EM, e.g., Qwen2.5VL-72B) annotate precise coordinates for visual perception tokens under the guidance of the larger model's thoughts. This ensures diverse and high-quality training trajectories.

#### 3.2. Fine-Grained Reward Function Tailored for Enhancing RAG Framework

VRAG-RL's reward mechanism addresses the issue of inefficient and disjointed retrieval, moving beyond simple outcome-based rewards. It comprises three weighted components:
- **Retrieval Efficiency Reward (r_Ret):** Inspired by Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG), this reward evaluates how promptly and comprehensively relevant images are retrieved within a multi-turn trajectory. It penalizes irrelevant retrievals and rewards early, accurate information gathering, recognizing that retrieval quality directly impacts overall RAG performance.
- **Pattern Consistency Reward (r_Pat):** This rule-based reward encourages the model to adhere to predefined reasoning patterns and correctly use action tokens (e.g., `<search>`, `<region>`). While often set to 0 after initial SFT, it can be activated (e.g., γ=0.1) during cold-start RL to guide pattern learning.
- **Model-Based Outcome Reward (r_Ans):** Unlike rigid rule-based Exact Match (EM) rewards, this component utilizes an evaluation model (π_RM, specifically Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) to assess the correctness of the final generated answer. This provides a more flexible, comprehensive, and stable training signal, aligning better with real-world answer quality.
- **Integrated Reward Function:** The final reward `r_phi` is a weighted sum: `α · r_Ret + β · r_Ans + γ · r_Pat`.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Framework with Iterative Reasoning

The framework employs a multi-round interaction process and a specific RL algorithm:
- **Multi-Round Generation (Rollout Module):** The VLM (policy model π_θ) interacts with an external environment (which includes a search engine and visual perception functions) in a Thought-Action-Observation (T, A, O) loop. The model generates thoughts and actions (search queries, visual perception tokens, or final answers). Retrieved images or cropped visual regions are then inserted into the context as observations, presented in the role of the user to align with the VLM's pre-training domain.
- **Reinforcement Learning Training Strategy:** The optimization objective aims to maximize the expected cumulative reward. VRAG-RL implements **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an algorithm that leverages the average reward of multiple sampled outputs as a baseline for advantage computation. GRPO optimizes the policy model by maximizing rewards while incorporating a KL divergence penalty to ensure stability and prevent deviation from a reference model (π_ref). The training process involves group-sampled role-play trajectories.
- **Data and Infrastructure:** The authors re-annotated existing datasets (SlideVQA, ViDoSeek, MMLongBench) and developed a data construction pipeline for efficient scaling. A search engine was built from approximately 70,000 visual documents, utilizing ColPali for vision-based retrieval and PP-OCR for OCR-based text extraction.

By combining these innovative components, VRAG-RL enables VLMs to perform more sophisticated, adaptive, and accurate reasoning on visually rich documents, simulating a human-like iterative process of information gathering and perception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on diverse and challenging benchmarks demonstrate the significant effectiveness of VRAG-RL, particularly its ability to outperform existing methods by a substantial margin.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvement

- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct:** VRAG-RL achieved an overall accuracy of 57.1%, marking a remarkable **22.4 percentage point improvement** over the strong baseline Search-R1-VL (34.7%). This translates to an improvement "over 20%" as stated in the abstract.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct:** For the smaller model, VRAG-RL reached 53.5% accuracy, an impressive **32.2 percentage point increase** compared to Search-R1-VL (21.3%), confirming the "over 30%" claim.
These results highlight VRAG-RL's superior capability in handling complex reasoning across visually rich information.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

- **OCR-based vs. Visual-based RAG:** The results clearly show that OCR-based RAG methods (e.g., Vanilla RAG, ReAct, Search-R1 using OCR) are significantly limited on visually intensive benchmarks compared to purely visual methods. This is attributed to the inherent loss of visual information and the inferior perceptual capabilities of OCR models compared to advanced VLMs.
- **Prompt-based vs. RL-based:** Traditional prompt-based baselines like Vanilla RAG and ReAct consistently exhibit poor performance, lagging far behind RL-based approaches. This underscores the necessity of iterative optimization and dynamic interaction facilitated by RL for these complex tasks.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness on Diverse Benchmarks

- **Reasoning-focused Datasets (SlideVQA, ViDoSeek):** VRAG-RL showed substantial improvements in various reasoning tasks (single-hop, multi-hop, extraction, logic), demonstrating its enhanced reasoning capabilities.
- **Visual-focused Dataset (MMLongBench):** Performance gains on MMLongBench, which includes various visual elements (Text, Table, Chart, Figure, Layout), indicate significant improvements in the model's visual perception capabilities. This directly supports the effectiveness of the proposed visual perception action space.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies systematically validated the contribution of each key component:
- **RAG-Specific Reward:** Removing this component resulted in a clear drop in accuracy, confirming that integrating retrieval performance into the reward structure is crucial. It reinforces the principle that high-quality retrieval is a prerequisite for high-quality generation.
- **Visual-Perception Action Space:** Its removal also led to a noticeable decline in performance. This module proved particularly effective in improving tasks requiring understanding of high-density visual information (e.g., Layout, Chart, Figure categories in MMLongBench), demonstrating its ability to provide a fine-grained perspective and activate vision-specific reasoning.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

- **Improved Retrieval and Context:** VRAG-RL significantly enhances retrieval efficiency (higher Recall@1), providing VLMs with a more relevant and coherent context, which directly facilitates higher-quality answer generation.
- **Dynamic Visual Perception:** The visual perception action space allows for human-like coarse-to-fine information extraction, enabling more detailed perception within computational constraints and optimizing resource utilization.
- **Enhanced Multi-step Reasoning via RL:** RL training effectively reduced the "Invalid Action Rate" and increased the "Finish Rate" compared to SFT alone. This indicates that RL guides the model to make more optimal decisions, follow instructions better, and flexibly adjust strategies in multi-turn interactions.
- **Stable Training with Model-Based Reward:** The model-based reward function provided a more flexible and stable training signal compared to strict rule-based rewards like EM, leading to more robust performance.
- **Latency Analysis:** While multi-turn interaction increases latency compared to single-shot methods, VRAG-RL's ability to quickly extract answers after relevant retrieval avoids repetitive ineffective searches, making the trade-off between latency and significantly improved accuracy highly beneficial for complex visual RAG tasks.
- **Case Studies:** Illustrate the model's reflective capability, its ability to identify subtle visual clues, and its selective, human-like engagement in visual perception actions only when needed.

In summary, the findings consistently demonstrate that VRAG-RL's innovative integration of visual perception actions, a comprehensive RAG-specific reward function, and a robust RL training strategy effectively addresses the core challenges in visual RAG, leading to significant performance gains and more intelligent interaction with visually rich information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VRAG-RL framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation, particularly for understanding visually rich documents. Its contributions and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Novel Reinforcement Learning Framework:** VRAG-RL introduces a pioneering RL framework specifically designed for training VLMs to perform complex, iterative reasoning over visually rich information. This fills a crucial gap in existing RL frameworks that often lack specific tailoring for multimodal interactions.
- **Enhanced Visual Understanding and Reasoning:** By defining a unique visual perception action space (selection, cropping, scaling), the framework enables VLMs to dynamically focus on information-dense areas and extract details from a coarse-to-fine perspective. This significantly activates and enhances the VLMs' inherent vision-specific reasoning capabilities, moving beyond static image embeddings. The human-like "crop and re-input" strategy for increasing perceptual resolution within limited context length is a particularly impactful innovation.
- **Improved Retrieval Efficiency and Relevance:** The comprehensive reward structure, which integrates retrieval performance (via a modified NDCG) with pattern consistency and a flexible model-based outcome reward, provides a fine-grained guidance signal. This alignment between the model's actions and real-world application goals helps bridge the gap between user inquiries and effective retrieval, ensuring that the model seeks and uses truly pertinent information.
- **Stable Multi-Turn Interaction:** The application of the GRPO algorithm, combined with a carefully designed sampling strategy, addresses the long-standing challenge of stability and consistency in multi-turn reasoning for VLMs. This enables more robust and reliable interactions with external environments, crucial for complex, multi-step tasks.
- **Practical Applicability:** The demonstrated significant performance improvements (20-30% over strong baselines) across diverse and challenging benchmarks (SlideVQA, ViDoSeek, MMLongBench) highlight VRAG-RL's practical utility. This makes it a highly promising solution for real-world applications involving the automated understanding and querying of visually rich documents like presentations, reports, and scientific papers.
- **Foundation for Future AI Development:** The approach of mimicking human-like perception (e.g., zooming in on details) and iterative reasoning provides a valuable blueprint for developing more intelligent and adaptive AI agents. The authors' statement, "Perhaps this human-like way of thinking and acting is the key to AGI," underscores their belief in the foundational nature of this work for broader AI advancements.

For future work, the authors plan to expand the action space to incorporate more human-like cognitive actions for deeper thinking and to further reduce hallucinations by leveraging more advanced models. These directions suggest a continuous pursuit of more sophisticated and reliable multimodal AI systems, solidifying VRAG-RL's role as a significant step towards more robust and intelligent visual information understanding.
