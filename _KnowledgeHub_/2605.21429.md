# roto 2.0: The Robot Tactile Olympiad

> [!summary] Summary
> Introducing roto 2.0, a benchmark for tactile-based reinforcement learning, standardizes evaluation across diverse robot hands and complex "blind" manipulation tasks. It demonstrates that tactile-only policies can achieve advanced performance, including 13 Baoding rotations in 10 seconds, setting a new performance benchmark for tactile intelligence.

## Problem

- The field of tactile-based reinforcement learning (RL) suffers from fragmentation due to disparate sensor, robot, and experimental setups, hindering systematic comparison and knowledge transfer.
- Existing research in tactile manipulation has largely focused on a limited range of tasks, particularly in-hand object orientation, without fully exploring broader capabilities of tactile feedback.
- High barriers to entry exist for tactile RL, stemming from the complexity of setting up robotic simulations, tuning RL algorithms, and handling sparse, discontinuous tactile observations.

## Method

- The authors introduce **roto 2.0**, a GPU-parallelized simulation benchmark suite within Isaac Lab, featuring four distinct dexterous robot morphologies and two challenging "blind" manipulation tasks: Bounce and Baoding.
- A customized Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, incorporating observation stacking and various training enhancements, is used to train agents in up to 8,092 parallel environments.
- The framework provides a standardized Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation, including detailed observation spaces (proprioceptive and binary tactile history), joint position action spaces, and task-specific reward functions, along with pre-tuned baselines from extensive hyperparameter optimization.

## Results

- In the Bounce task, state-based agents reached approximately 100 bounces in 10 seconds, while blind agents achieved up to 80 bounces with high sample efficiency, demonstrating robust performance across full-hand morphologies.
- For the more complex Baoding task, state-based agents achieved up to 35 rotations in 10 seconds, whereas blind agents reached a new state-of-the-art of 13 rotations in 10 seconds, surpassing previous tactile-only methods.
- The policies developed within roto 2.0 establish a new performance ceiling for tactile intelligence in simulation, with the high-speed manipulation capabilities exceeding current real-world robotic hardware limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile-only policies, relying solely on proprioception and binary tactile feedback without explicit state information, can achieve competent performance in "blind" manipulation tasks, approaching that of state-based agents in simpler scenarios.
> - Hardware morphology influences manipulation strategy and convergence, but full-hand dexterous robots can achieve similar high success rates in tasks like Bounce, even while employing distinct physical interaction methods.
> - Complex multi-object manipulation tasks (e.g., Baoding) present a greater challenge for tactile-only agents, highlighting the difficulty in efficiently extracting relevant features from raw tactile data for intricate interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of tactile-based reinforcement learning (RL) for robotic manipulation is characterized by several challenges that this work aims to address. Historically, research in this domain has been fragmented, with individual laboratories often developing solutions using distinct combinations of sensors, robotic platforms, and experimental setups. This lack of standardization impedes systematic cross-validation, comparison of methods, and the accumulation of transferable knowledge.

Furthermore, a significant portion of existing tactile-based manipulation research has concentrated on a limited range of tasks, particularly in-hand object orientation. While achievements in this specific area are notable, such a narrow focus may not fully explore the broader capabilities and utility of tactile feedback in complex manipulation scenarios. The authors contend that this over-saturation of a single task leaves the comprehensive value of tactile information open to debate.

The inherent difficulty of tactile-based RL stems from a confluence of factors: the complexity of manipulation tasks themselves, the tuning challenges associated with on-policy RL algorithms, and the additional layer of sparse and often discontinuous tactile observations. Existing benchmarks, such as Tactile-Gym 2.0, have been limited in scope, focusing on simpler robot morphologies (e.g., 4-DOF arms) or single-point contact tasks. Other approaches, like VTDexManip, focus on pre-training policies, which differs from the end-to-end learning paradigm.

This work introduces roto 2.0 as a response to these limitations. It seeks to standardize tactile-based RL by providing a unified benchmark that spans multiple dexterous robotic morphologies and focuses on "blind" manipulation tasks. By offering a GPU-parallelized environment and pre-tuned baselines, the project intends to lower the entry barrier for researchers and redirect community efforts towards fundamental algorithmic innovations rather than repetitive environment setup and hyperparameter tuning. The authors position this work as a foundational step, arguing that robust haptic capabilities are a prerequisite for integrating visual modalities effectively, rather than a secondary addition to vision-centric systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of roto 2.0 are multifaceted, aiming to advance the field of tactile-based reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation:

- **Standardize Tactile-Based RL Research:** A core objective is to overcome the fragmentation currently observed in tactile RL. By introducing a standardized benchmark suite, the authors aim to facilitate consistent evaluation, enable direct comparison of different algorithms and approaches, and foster collaborative research efforts across diverse research groups.
- **Broaden the Scope of Tactile Manipulation Tasks:** The work is motivated by the observation that prior research has largely over-saturated simple in-hand orientation tasks. roto 2.0 seeks to introduce more complex and "blind" manipulation challenges that require advanced tactile reasoning, moving beyond tasks that might be solvable with simpler feedback mechanisms.
- **Enable End-to-End "Blind" Manipulation:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the potential of policies that rely solely on proprioception and tactile sensing, without access to direct state information (e.g., object pose) or the need for teacher-student distillation or explicit pose estimators commonly used in other works. This "blind" approach aims to test the intrinsic capabilities of tactile feedback for robust interaction in environments where visual information is occluded or unavailable.
- **Achieve Performance Breakthroughs in Tactile Manipulation:** The researchers are motivated to push the performance boundaries of tactile-based agents in complex tasks. By developing a robust RL training pipeline and leveraging GPU-parallelization, they aim to achieve significantly higher manipulation speeds and success rates than current state-of-the-art methods, establishing a new "performance ceiling" for tactile intelligence.
- **Reduce the Barrier to Entry for Tactile RL:** Recognizing the difficulty associated with setting up complex robotic simulations and tuning RL algorithms, the project aims to reduce this barrier. By open-sourcing the environments, integrating hyperparameter optimization, and providing robustly tuned baselines, the authors intend to allow researchers to focus on fundamental algorithmic challenges rather than the logistical complexities of environment setup and parameter optimization.
- **Investigate Morphological Diversity:** A specific objective is to evaluate how hardware complexity, specifically across four distinct dexterous robot morphologies ranging from 16-DOF to 24-DOF, influences the convergence and performance of tactile policies. This comparative study aims to provide insights into the generalizability and hardware requirements for tactile-based manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a structured approach to develop and evaluate the roto 2.0 benchmark suite.

**A. Reinforcement Learning Framework:**
The agents were trained using a customized implementation of the Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) algorithm. This implementation, derived from SKRL, incorporated several features:
- **Observation Stacking:** Historical observations were stacked to provide temporal context to the policy.
- **Self-supervision:** While not the primary focus of the presented results, the framework supported self-supervision to aid representation learning, as explored in prior work by the authors.
- **Separated Environments:** Dedicated environments were used for continuous evaluation, distinct from the training environments.
- **Training Tricks:** Various established RL training techniques were integrated to enhance stability and performance.

**B. Simulation Environment and Parallelization:**
The benchmark suite is built upon Isaac Lab, a GPU-parallelized simulation framework. This allowed for significant computational efficiency:
- **Parallel Environments:** Training was conducted across 8,092 parallelized environments, while evaluation utilized 100 environments. This high degree of parallelism enabled faster data collection and more efficient policy learning.
- **Physics and Control Frequencies:** The physics simulation operated at 240 Hz, while the control policy updated at 60 Hz.

**C. Robot Morphologies:**
roto 2.0 expands on previous work by including four distinct dexterous robotic hands, providing morphological diversity for evaluation:
- **Shadow Dexterous Hand:** An anthropomorphic hand with 24 Degrees of Freedom (DOF).
- **Shadow Dexterous Hand Lite:** A simplified version with 16 DOF.
- **Allegro Hand:** A 16-DOF hand.
- **ORCA Hand:** A 17-DOF hand.
This selection allowed for an investigation into how varying hardware complexities affect tactile policy convergence.

**D. Tasks:**
The benchmark suite features two distinct "blind" manipulation tasks designed to challenge tactile-based policies:
- **Bounce:** The agent's objective is to bounce a ball as many times as possible within a 10-second (600 timesteps) episode. A bounce is defined as a contact event occurring after a minimum of 5 timesteps (approximately 83ms) without prior contact.
- **Baoding:** This task requires the agent to rotate two balls around each other in-hand as many times as possible within a 10-second (600 timesteps) episode. The ball diameters were adjusted for different hands (1.5 inches for Shadow Hand and ORCA, 2 inches for Allegro, and 1.2 inches for Shadow Lite).

**E. Markov Decision Process (MDP) Formulation:**
- **Observation Space:**
 - **Blind Agents:** Received a history of *k* = 4 timesteps of proprioceptive and binary tactile observations. Tactile sensors were defined for each task-relevant robot link, providing binary contact information. Proprioceptive observations included joint positions, joint velocities, joint command errors, and the last action. For a single timestep, the total observation size varied by hand (e.g., 97 for Shadow Hand, 72 for Shadow Lite, 84 for Allegro, 85 for ORCA). With *k*=4 timesteps, the total observation space ranged from 288 to 388 elements.
 - **State-Based Agents:** In addition to the observations received by blind agents, state-based agents were provided with privileged information, specifically the object's position(s) and linear velocity. This allowed for a comparison of tactile-only vs. full state access performance.
- **Action Space:** Agents were controlled by directly commanding joint positions. The number of actions corresponded to the DOF of each hand (e.g., 20 for Shadow Hand, 13 for Shadow Lite, 10 for Allegro, 17 for ORCA).
- **Reward Function:**
 - **Bounce:** A simple reward of *r_bounce* = 10 was granted for each successful bounce.
 - **Baoding:** The reward function was composed of three parts: *r_dist1* + *r_dist2* + *r_rotation*. Dense distance rewards (*r_dist1*, *r_dist2*) encouraged the balls to move towards two predefined static target positions. When the centers of both balls were within 1.0 cm of their respective targets, the targets would switch, and the agent received a bonus reward of *r_rotation* = 10.
- **Episode Termination:** Episodes concluded if any object went out of reach or upon reaching the maximum length of 600 timesteps.

**F. Hyperparameter Tuning:**
To ensure robust baselines, a hyperparameter sweep was conducted across seven PPO parameters. This process involved 40 trials (including 8 warm-up runs) for each combination of robot (3 hands evaluated), task (2 tasks), and observation setting (blind vs. state-based).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the learning efficiency and asymptotic performance of four robotic morphologies under two observation settings (state-based and blind) across the Baoding and Bounce tasks. Mean evaluation returns were assessed over 5 random seeds for each configuration.

**A. Bounce Task:**
- **State-Based Agents:** In the Bounce environment, state-based agents consistently approached the theoretical maximum return, achieving approximately 1,000 reward units. This corresponds to around 100 successful bounces within the 10-second episode. Despite differences in hardware, all full-hand morphologies converged to similar high success rates, although they exhibited distinct, hardware-specific manipulation strategies (e.g., the ORCA hand adopting an "outstretched" pose).
- **Blind Agents:** Blind agents demonstrated high sample efficiency in the Bounce task, with performance approaching 80 bounces by 200 million simulation steps. This indicates that even without direct object state information, policies relying solely on tactile and proprioceptive feedback can achieve competent performance in this task. Performance trends generally remained consistent across full-hand morphologies, with the Shadow Lite being an exception.

**B. Baoding Task:**
- **Performance Gap:** The Baoding task, involving complex multi-object manipulation, revealed a more significant performance disparity between state-based and blind agents.
- **State-Based Agents:** State-based agents achieved a high throughput, reaching up to 35 rotations within 10 seconds.
- **Blind Agents:** Blind agents exhibited considerably lower performance with higher variance. While a top-performing instance of the Shadow Hand achieved 13 rotations, other seeds failed to converge or showed minimal success. This stochasticity highlights the challenges inherent in efficiently extracting relevant features from raw tactile data for complex multi-object manipulation.
- **Comparison to Prior Work:** The achieved 13 rotations by a blind agent in 10 seconds represents a significant increase compared to the previously reported state-of-the-art of 3 rotations in 10 seconds for Baoding ball rotation using tactile-only methods. The paper also references that integrating self-supervised forward dynamics, as explored in prior work by the authors, could further push this boundary to 25 rotations per 10 seconds, narrowing the gap with state-based agents.

Overall, the results indicate that while blind policies can achieve near-privileged performance in simpler single-object tasks like bouncing, complex multi-object manipulation such as Baoding remains a more substantial challenge for tactile-only approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of roto 2.0 carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and reinforcement learning:

- **Standardization of Tactile RL Research:** By providing a unified, open-source benchmark suite across multiple dexterous robot morphologies, roto 2.0 addresses the issue of fragmented research. This standardization is expected to facilitate more direct and comparable evaluations of different tactile-based RL algorithms, accelerating progress and promoting consensus within the community.
- **Lowered Barrier to Entry:** The provision of GPU-parallelized environments, integrated hyperparameter optimization, and robustly tuned baselines significantly reduces the practical challenges faced by researchers entering the field. This allows them to allocate resources and attention to fundamental algorithmic research rather than time-consuming environment setup and tuning.
- **Establishing a Performance Ceiling:** The high-speed simulated policies developed within roto 2.0, particularly the "blind" agents achieving 13 Baoding ball rotations in 10 seconds (an order of magnitude faster than previous state-of-the-art), establish a new "performance ceiling" for tactile intelligence. These results provide ambitious targets for future algorithmic and hardware development, even exceeding current real-world robotic hardware limitations.
- **Redefining the Role of Tactile Sensing:** By demonstrating that "blind" policies, relying solely on proprioception and tactile data, can achieve advanced manipulation without explicit state information or distillation, the work reinforces the intrinsic value and potential of tactile feedback. The authors argue for establishing robust haptic foundations as a prerequisite for integrating visual modalities, rather than a secondary component, which could reshape developmental priorities in robotic perception.
- **Identification of Key Research Directions:** The performance gap observed between state-based and blind agents in complex tasks like Baoding highlights specific areas requiring further investigation. The paper explicitly identifies the need for research into richer forms of tactile information beyond sparse binary contacts, the development of ML methodologies with inductive biases tailored for tactile data, and the expansion of tasks to include broader applications like whole-body humanoid manipulation.
- **Acceleration of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While the current results are in simulation, the authors are actively pursuing the sim-to-real transferability of these policies. The robust benchmark and high-performance baselines are anticipated to serve as a valuable platform for developing and testing algorithms designed for seamless transition from simulation to physical robots, ultimately contributing to more capable and adaptable real-world robotic systems.
- **Promotion of Community Engagement:** Open-sourcing the roto suite is intended to encourage community contributions, fostering a collaborative environment to collectively advance the field of robotic touch, potentially leading to a "locomotion moment" where tactile capabilities achieve widespread adoption and impact in robotics.
