# roto 2.0: The Robot Tactile Olympiad

> [!summary] Summary
> Introducing roto 2.0, a benchmark for tactile-based reinforcement learning, standardizes evaluation across diverse robot hands and complex "blind" manipulation tasks. It demonstrates that tactile-only policies can achieve advanced performance, including 13 Baoding rotations in 10 seconds, setting a new performance benchmark for tactile intelligence.

## Problem

- The field of tactile-based reinforcement learning (RL) suffers from fragmentation due to disparate sensor, robot, and experimental setups, hindering systematic comparison and knowledge transfer.
- Existing research in tactile manipulation has largely focused on a limited range of tasks, particularly in-hand object orientation, without fully exploring broader capabilities of tactile feedback.
- High barriers to entry exist for tactile RL, stemming from the complexity of setting up robotic simulations, tuning RL algorithms, and handling sparse, discontinuous tactile observations.

## Method

- The authors introduce **roto 2.0**, a GPU-parallelized simulation benchmark suite within Isaac Lab, featuring four distinct dexterous robot morphologies and two challenging "blind" manipulation tasks: Bounce and Baoding.
- A customized Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, incorporating observation stacking and various training enhancements, is used to train agents in up to 8,092 parallel environments.
- The framework provides a standardized Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation, including detailed observation spaces (proprioceptive and binary tactile history), joint position action spaces, and task-specific reward functions, along with pre-tuned baselines from extensive hyperparameter optimization.

## Results

- In the Bounce task, state-based agents reached approximately 100 bounces in 10 seconds, while blind agents achieved up to 80 bounces with high sample efficiency, demonstrating robust performance across full-hand morphologies.
- For the more complex Baoding task, state-based agents achieved up to 35 rotations in 10 seconds, whereas blind agents reached a new state-of-the-art of 13 rotations in 10 seconds, surpassing previous tactile-only methods.
- The policies developed within roto 2.0 establish a new performance ceiling for tactile intelligence in simulation, with the high-speed manipulation capabilities exceeding current real-world robotic hardware limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile-only policies, relying solely on proprioception and binary tactile feedback without explicit state information, can achieve competent performance in "blind" manipulation tasks, approaching that of state-based agents in simpler scenarios.
> - Hardware morphology influences manipulation strategy and convergence, but full-hand dexterous robots can achieve similar high success rates in tasks like Bounce, even while employing distinct physical interaction methods.
> - Complex multi-object manipulation tasks (e.g., Baoding) present a greater challenge for tactile-only agents, highlighting the difficulty in efficiently extracting relevant features from raw tactile data for intricate interactions.
