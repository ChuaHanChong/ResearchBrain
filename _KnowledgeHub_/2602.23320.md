# ParamMem: Augmenting Language Agents with Parametric Reflective Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MBZUAI and Carnegie Mellon developed ParamMem, a parametric memory module that enhances language agents by encoding cross-sample reflection patterns directly into model parameters. This system consistently achieves substantial performance improvements across programming, mathematical reasoning, and multi-hop QA tasks while enabling self-improvement without reliance on stronger external models.

## Problem

- Existing self-reflection mechanisms in language agents often produce repetitive, inaccurate, or unhelpful outputs, hindering their ability to explore diverse solutions and diagnose errors.
- Previous retrieval-based approaches to improve reflection diversity (e.g., DoT-bank) have limitations in capturing compositional patterns and are susceptible to embedding collapse, reducing retrieval effectiveness.
- Empirical evidence shows a strong positive correlation (average Pearson coefficient of 0.76) between reflective diversity and task success in language agents.

## Method

- ParamMem builds a ==parametric memory module (M_g)== by fine-tuning a lightweight LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B with LoRA) on an auxiliary dataset of input samples paired with expert-generated 'global reflections'.
- The fine-tuned ParamMem module generates diverse reflections via ==temperature-controlled sampling==, encoding cross-sample patterns directly into its parameters.
- ParamMem is integrated into agent frameworks (ParamAgent and ==ParamAgent-plus==), unifying this parametric memory with existing episodic memory and, in the case of ParamAgent-plus, cross-sample memory (like DoT-bank) to condition the actor LLM.

## Results

- ParamAgent and ParamAgent-plus achieve consistent and substantial performance gains over state-of-the-art baselines across programming (HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench), mathematical reasoning (MATH), and multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA) tasks.
- ParamMem significantly increases reflective diversity, evidenced by higher average pairwise cosine distances between reflections (**0.351** vs. **0.200** for frozen LLM) and a larger optimal number of reflection clusters (K*=**39** for ParamAgent vs. K*=**11-33** for baselines).
- The method demonstrates high sample efficiency, maintaining strong performance even when trained on a small dataset (e.g., **500** samples), and iterative self-teaching further improves performance without external supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Introducing a parametric memory module for reflection generation provides an orthogonal and complementary source of diversity to episodic and cross-sample memories, enabling generalization of reflection patterns.
> - ParamMem allows for effective self-improvement of language agents using only the base model itself for data generation, without requiring stronger external models or explicit reward signals.
> - The approach facilitates weak-to-strong transfer, where a ParamMem module built on a weaker LLM can still effectively augment the reasoning capabilities of a stronger agent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "ParamMem" is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), agentic reasoning, memory systems, and self-improvement techniques. It directly addresses a critical challenge in enhancing the performance and reliability of LLM-based agents.

**Evolution of LLMs and Agentic Reasoning:** The paper begins by acknowledging the "remarkable progress" of LLMs (e.g., GPT series, LLaMA, PaLM) in complex reasoning tasks. A key insight driving these advances is "test-time scaling," where additional computational resources are allocated during inference to improve reasoning. Within this paradigm, **reflection-based frameworks** have emerged as particularly effective. These frameworks allow language agents to iteratively refine solutions by generating verbal reflections on task feedback and storing these reflections in an episodic memory to guide subsequent attempts. This approach has found success across various domains, including programming, mathematical reasoning, decision-making, and multi-agent systems.

**The Problem of Repetitive Reflections:** Despite their effectiveness, existing self-reflection mechanisms suffer from a significant limitation: they often produce **repetitive and inaccurate outputs**. This lack of diversity can hinder the agent's ability to explore alternative solutions or diagnose errors comprehensively, leading to stagnation in the reasoning process. Previous attempts to address this, such as "Diversity of Thoughts" (DoT) and "DoT-bank" (Lingam et al., 2025), have explored prompt-level modifications and the incorporation of cross-sample reasoning trajectories. While showing promise, these **retrieval-based approaches** have inherent limitations, including a restricted capacity for capturing compositional patterns and a susceptibility to embedding collapse, which reduces retrieval diversity.

**ParamMem's Position:** This paper builds upon these foundational works by proposing a novel mechanism to overcome the limitations of repetitive reflections. The authors first empirically validate a strong positive correlation between reflective diversity and task success, providing a clear motivation. ParamMem then introduces a **parametric memory module** as a fundamentally different approach to generating diverse reflection signals. Unlike prompt-based or retrieval-based methods, ParamMem encodes cross-sample reflection patterns directly into model parameters through fine-tuning. This allows it to generate novel reflections by generalizing from learned patterns, rather than merely retrieving existing ones, thus providing an orthogonal and complementary source of diversity.

**Relation to Parametric Memory and Self-Improvement:** The work also connects to a growing body of research on **parametric approaches to learning from experience**. Works like Retroformer (Yao et al., 2023c) and Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2024) have explored encoding reflection capabilities or self-critique into model parameters. However, ParamMem distinguishes itself by specifically targeting *reflective diversity* via efficient supervised fine-tuning, rather than optimizing for accuracy or requiring complex online reinforcement learning. Furthermore, ParamMem contributes to the field of **self-improving language agents**. Many self-improvement methods (e.g., STaR, ReST, SPIN) rely on filtering for correct samples, reward signals, or external evaluators. ParamMem's self-improvement capabilities, operating without such external dependencies and focusing on diversifying internal thought processes, offer a distinct and potentially more scalable avenue for continual agent enhancement.

In essence, ParamMem addresses a recognized bottleneck in LLM agent performance by innovating on the mechanism of reflection generation, offering a new path for robust and versatile agent development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core of this research is driven by a critical observation and a consequential question.

**Motivation:** The paper's primary motivation stems from an empirical finding: there is a **strong positive correlation between reflective diversity and task success** in language agents. Through experiments on five diverse datasets using LLaMA-3.1-8B, the authors found an average Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.76 between average pairwise cosine distance of reflections (a measure of diversity) and task performance. This robust statistical relationship provides compelling evidence that increasing the variety of an agent's self-reflections can directly lead to improved problem-solving capabilities. This observation underscores the limitations of current reflection mechanisms, which often produce monotonous or unhelpful self-feedback, thereby impeding further progress.

**Problem Statement:** Given the identified correlation and the limitations of existing methods (namely, prompt-based and retrieval-based approaches struggling to consistently generate diverse and compositional reflection patterns or falling prey to embedding collapse), the authors pose a clear research question: **"How can we further expand reflective diversity to achieve stronger reasoning performance?"** This question highlights the gap in current agentic LLM capabilities and sets the stage for ParamMem's proposed solution.

**Key Objectives:** To address this question, the paper sets out with several key objectives:

1. **Introduce ParamMem:** To develop a novel parametric memory module that encodes cross-sample reflection patterns directly into its parameters. This module should enable the generation of diverse reflections, offering a new mechanism beyond prompt engineering or explicit retrieval.
2. **Facilitate Diverse Reflection Generation:** To allow for temperature-controlled sampling from ParamMem, providing a controllable way to produce varied reflective signals.
3. **Integrate into Agent Frameworks:** To propose ParamAgent and ParamAgent-plus, frameworks that seamlessly unify this parametric reflective memory with existing episodic and cross-sample memory components within reflection-based agents.
4. **Demonstrate Performance Superiority:** To empirically show that the proposed ParamAgent and ParamAgent-plus achieve consistent and substantial performance improvements over state-of-the-art baselines across a variety of complex reasoning tasks (programming, math, multi-hop QA).
5. **Analyze Key Properties:** To thoroughly investigate and demonstrate ParamMem's advantageous characteristics, including its sample efficiency, ability to enable weak-to-strong transfer across model scales, and its capacity for self-improvement without reliance on stronger external models or explicit reward signals.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to unlock a higher level of reasoning performance in LLM agents by addressing the fundamental challenge of limited reflective diversity through a novel, efficient, and self-improvable parametric memory mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ParamMem introduces a novel approach to enhancing reflective diversity in language agents by embedding reflection patterns directly into a parametric module. This section details the construction of ParamMem and its integration into the ParamAgent framework.

#### 3.1. Building ParamMem: Parametric Reflective Memory Module

The core innovation is **ParamMem**, a parametric memory module (`M_g`) designed to generate diverse reflections. Unlike methods that rely on explicit retrieval or prompt variations, ParamMem learns to generalize reflection patterns by fine-tuning.

- **Dataset Construction:** The process begins with curating an auxiliary dataset, `D = {(x_i, r^g_i)}_{i=1}^n`. Each entry consists of an input sample (`x_i`, e.g., a programming task) and a corresponding "global reflection" (`r^g_i`).
 - `r^g_i` is generated by prompting a powerful LLM (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) with a task-specific prompt (`P`). This effectively distills expert-level reflections.
 - **For programming and math tasks:** `r^g_i` typically includes reflective feedback, enumerating potential mistakes and outlining buggy implementations or common pitfalls.
 - **For multi-hop QA:** Inspired by cognitive chunking and least-to-most prompting, `r^g_i` involves decomposing the query into compact semantic units, identifying key entities, relationships, constraints, and potential reasoning sub-tasks.
 - A single carefully designed demonstration example is provided in the prompt to ensure the generated `r^g_i` adheres to the desired format.
- **Module Fine-tuning:** A lightweight pretrained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) is then fine-tuned on this dataset `D` using **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**. LoRA is chosen for its efficiency, allowing the module to be fine-tuned with minimal computational cost while still encoding cross-sample patterns into its parameters. The fine-tuned model becomes the parametric memory module `M_g`.

#### 3.2. Incorporating ParamMem into Reflexion-based Frameworks

Once `M_g` is trained, its integration into existing reflection-based frameworks is straightforward.

- **ParamAgent:** This framework extends the standard Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) by adding the parametric memory. At each iteration `k`, the actor LLM (`p_θ`) generates a candidate solution conditioned on:
 1. **Episodic Memory (`r_{1:k-1}`):** Self-reflections accumulated from previous iterations of the *current* task.
 2. **Parametric Memory (`r^g_k`):** A reflection sampled from `M_g` (`p_ψ(· | x)`) for the current input `x`.
 The full conditioning becomes: `y_k ∼ p_θ(· | x, r_{1:k-1}, r^g_k)`.
 - A key aspect of ParamMem is its ability to generate diverse reflections through **temperature-controlled sampling**. A lower temperature (T=0.2) is used for the first iteration to get a more deterministic, foundational reflection, while a higher temperature (T=1.0) is used in subsequent iterations to explicitly promote diverse outputs.
- **ParamAgent-plus:** This variant further enhances ParamAgent by incorporating **cross-sample memory**, similar to DoT-bank. In addition to episodic and parametric memory, the actor also conditions on retrieved reasoning trajectories (`τ_{1:j}`) from a memory bank (`B`) of previously solved tasks.
 - The full conditioning for ParamAgent-plus is: `y_k ∼ p_θ(· | x, r_{1:k-1}, RETRIEVE(B, x), r^g_k)`.
 - The retrieval mechanism for cross-sample trajectories follows that of DoT-bank for fair comparison.

#### 3.3. Inference and Feedback Loop

It is crucial to note that ParamMem (`M_g`) does not directly interact with the environment during inference to generate *new* feedback on solutions. Instead, by conditioning the actor on the model-based feedback (`r^g_k`), ParamMem influences the actor's *initial* solution generation and subsequent self-reflection process. This indirect shaping of the actor's output distribution, which then leads to new self-reflections that are stored in episodic memory, creates a powerful, dynamic feedback loop where the parametric knowledge implicitly guides the iterative refinement.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Domains:** Evaluated across programming (HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench), mathematical reasoning (MATH), and multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Pass@1 for programming; 0-1 accuracy for math and QA.
- **Baselines:** Base LLM, Reflexion, Retroformer, DoT, DoT-bank.
- **Backbone LLMs:** Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.2, Qwen2-1.5B-instruct (with additional 70B models in appendix).
- **Training Details:** 5 iterations for all methods. LoRA fine-tuning for ParamMem with specific hyperparameters (r=128, α=32, lr=2e-5, 3 epochs).

This systematic approach ensures that ParamMem is not only a theoretically sound concept for enhancing diversity but also a practically implementable and empirically validated component for robust language agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation and analyses reveal several key findings demonstrating the effectiveness and unique advantages of ParamMem.

#### 4.1. Substantial Performance Gains (Observation ①)

ParamAgent and ParamAgent-plus consistently achieve significant performance improvements over state-of-the-art baselines across all three tested domains:
- **Programming (HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench):** ParamAgent, even without cross-sample memory, shows remarkable gains over Reflexion and DoT. ParamAgent-plus further enhances performance, often setting new state-of-the-art.
- **Multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA):** ParamAgent substantially outperforms most prior methods, underscoring the standalone effectiveness of the parametric module. ParamAgent-plus again leads to the best results.
- **Mathematical Reasoning (MATH):** While ParamAgent improves upon Reflexion and DoT, the addition of cross-sample memory in ParamAgent-plus proves more critical, outperforming DoT-bank. This suggests that for math problems, analogous solution patterns (from cross-sample memory) are highly beneficial, and ParamMem complements this by providing diverse reflective cues.
- **Comparison with Retroformer:** Retroformer, an accuracy-focused parametric approach, underperforms on programming and QA tasks despite its success on MATH. This suggests that ParamMem's diversity-driven objective allows for better generalization across different task distributions compared to models optimized solely for reflection accuracy.

#### 4.2. Induced Reflective Diversity (Observation ②)

ParamMem effectively introduces a new layer of reflective diversity.
- **Static Analysis:** The parametric module, especially when trained on data generated by a stronger LLM (GPT-4o-mini), yields significantly higher average pairwise cosine distances between sampled reflections (0.351 vs. 0.200 for frozen LLM), indicating greater diversity. Even when trained on data from the base LLM itself, ParamMem still generates more diverse reflections than an un-finetuned model.
- **Dynamic Analysis (Clustering):** When integrated into the Reflexion framework, ParamAgent achieves a substantially larger optimal number of clusters (K*=39) for its reflection history compared to baselines (K*=11-33). This, coupled with consistently higher silhouette scores across various cluster numbers, confirms that ParamMem generates a richer, more varied, and semantically coherent set of reflective signals throughout the iterative refinement process.

#### 4.3. Enlarged Hypothesis Space for Error Diagnosis (Observation ③)

A case study on MBPP demonstrates *why* diversity-driven gains occur. While self-reflections in Reflexion and DoT can often be repetitive, unhelpful, or even misleading, ParamAgent's diverse feedback provides a broader range of diagnostic hypotheses. This increased breadth of suggestions enhances the agent's likelihood of encountering the correct cue for successful problem refinement. This also explains the occasionally higher token consumption, as exploring more diverse paths naturally requires more communication.

#### 4.4. Self-Improvement without External Models (Observation ④)

ParamMem empowers language agents to self-improve without relying on stronger external models for data generation. Even when ParamMem is fine-tuned on synthetic data generated by the *base model itself*, both ParamAgent and ParamAgent-plus achieve significant improvements over their respective baselines (DoT, DoT-bank). This highlights ParamMem's potential as a self-contained, annotation-free module for continuous agent improvement.

#### 4.5. Iterative Self-Teaching (Observation ⑤)

The research shows that ParamAgent (without cross-sample memory) can be further enhanced through iterative self-teaching. By repeatedly using the ParamMem module to generate new reflection targets for the same inputs and then fine-tuning on these updated datasets, ParamAgent's performance steadily improves across iterations. This suggests a progressive diversification of reflective outputs over time. ParamAgent-plus, however, shows only marginal gains, indicating it might be approaching a diversity ceiling with its combination of memories.

#### 4.6. Weak-to-Strong Reasoning Augmentation (Observation ⑥)

ParamMem facilitates weak-to-strong transfer. Even when a weaker model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B or Qwen3-Next-30B) serves as the ParamMem module, it can still effectively enhance a stronger agent (e.g., Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct). Both smaller ParamMem configurations consistently outperform all baselines, demonstrating that smaller models can provide valuable, diverse reflective signals that benefit larger, more capable agents.

#### 4.7. Sample Efficiency (Observation ⑦)

ParamMem proves to be remarkably sample-efficient. Strong performance is retained on HumanEval and MBPP even when ParamMem is trained on a reduced dataset of only 500 diverse samples (selected via K-means clustering). Notably, ParamAgent-plus with just 500 training samples outperforms ParamAgent trained on over 8000 samples, showcasing the powerful synergy of combining episodic, cross-sample, and parametric memories.

#### 4.8. Cost Analysis (Appendix B.4)

While ParamAgent incurs higher token costs compared to simpler methods, it delivers superior results, highlighting the value of its integrated memory approach.

These findings collectively underscore ParamMem's efficacy, flexibility, and practical utility as a significant advancement in building more robust and intelligent language agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ParamMem represents a significant advancement in the development of robust and intelligent language agents, offering a novel solution to a critical limitation in current reflection-based reasoning frameworks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Novel Mechanism for Diversity

The most significant contribution of this work is the introduction of **ParamMem** as a fundamentally new mechanism for generating diverse reflection signals. By encoding cross-sample reflection patterns into model parameters through efficient fine-tuning, ParamMem moves beyond the limitations of prompt-based modifications and retrieval-based memory systems. This parametric approach allows for the generalization and generation of novel reflections, rather than merely re-stating or retrieving existing ones, thereby providing an orthogonal and complementary source of diversity to episodic and cross-sample memories.

#### 5.2. Substantial Performance Enhancement

The consistent and substantial performance gains achieved by ParamAgent and ParamAgent-plus across diverse and challenging reasoning tasks (programming, mathematical reasoning, multi-hop QA) demonstrate the practical efficacy of this approach. Outperforming state-of-the-art baselines across multiple LLM scales (from 1.5B to 80B parameters) signifies ParamMem's broad applicability and effectiveness.

#### 5.3. Practical and Resource-Efficient

- **Lightweight and Integrable:** ParamMem is designed as a lightweight, plug-in module that seamlessly integrates into existing reflection-based agent frameworks. This ease of integration makes it highly practical for researchers and developers to adopt.
- **Sample Efficiency:** The demonstration that ParamMem can achieve strong performance with a relatively small training dataset (e.g., 500 samples) significantly reduces the data annotation and collection burden. This makes the approach more accessible and scalable, especially in domains where large, high-quality reflection datasets are scarce.
- **Cost-Effectiveness (LoRA):** The use of LoRA for fine-tuning ensures that the parametric module can be updated efficiently without requiring full fine-tuning of large models, further contributing to its practical viability.

#### 5.4. Enabling Self-Improvement and Weak-to-Strong Transfer

- **Self-Contained Improvement:** ParamMem’s ability to foster self-improvement using data generated by the base LLM itself, without relying on stronger external models or explicit reward signals, is a critical step towards truly autonomous and continually learning agents. This capability can lead to agents that adapt and refine their reasoning processes over time in an annotation-free manner.
- **Weak-to-Strong Transfer:** The finding that a weaker LLM acting as ParamMem can enhance a stronger LLM agent is particularly impactful. This suggests that the knowledge of diverse reflective patterns is transferable and valuable even when generated by less powerful models. This democratizes access to advanced agent capabilities, as smaller, more efficient models can be utilized to augment the reasoning of larger, more expensive ones.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact on LLM Agent Research

ParamMem contributes to a deeper understanding of how internal thought processes (reflections) can be optimized for better external performance. It shifts focus from merely generating correct answers to generating *diverse and insightful* intermediate reasoning steps. This could inspire new research into:
- More sophisticated parametric encoding strategies for various types of agentic knowledge.
- Adaptive temperature scheduling for reflection generation.
- Hybrid memory architectures that optimally balance episodic, parametric, and cross-sample knowledge.
- The fundamental interplay between diversity in thought and task success in complex AI systems.

#### 5.6. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge a notable limitation: the increased token consumption in certain scenarios, which is an inherent trade-off for the additional reflective diversity. Future work will aim to address this by exploring more token-efficient integration strategies, potentially involving more compact reflection representations or adaptive reflection verbosity.

In conclusion, ParamMem offers a powerful, flexible, and efficient component for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of language agents. Its ability to generate diverse reflections, support self-improvement, and enable weak-to-strong transfer positions it as a significant contribution with the potential to accelerate the development of more intelligent, robust, and autonomous AI systems. The authors explicitly state they do not foresee any potential negative societal impact, focusing on the positive advancements in AI capabilities.
