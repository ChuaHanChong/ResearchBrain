# Model Predictive Adversarial Imitation Learning for Planning from Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Model Predictive Adversarial Imitation Learning (MPAIL) framework unifies Inverse Reinforcement Learning and Model Predictive Control, enabling end-to-end planning from observation to learn reward functions and control policies from sparse, observation-only demonstrations. It achieves improved out-of-distribution generalization and robust real-world performance with better interaction efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning methods often employ a two-stage process (offline IRL, online MPC), which is inefficient and lacks online adaptability for real-world scenarios.
- Policy-based Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) struggles with out-of-distribution generalization, robustness, and interpretability, particularly with ambiguous, observation-only expert data.
- Direct integration of reward learning with planning for real-world, observation-only imitation learning, especially on robotic platforms, remains underexplored and presents practical deployment challenges.

## Method

- Introduces Model Predictive Adversarial Imitation Learning (**MPAIL**), a framework that unifies ==Inverse Reinforcement== Learning (IRL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) into an interactive, end-to-end learning paradigm.
- Utilizes Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) as an embedded planner to optimize trajectories based on a learned reward function and a bootstrapped value function, directly incorporating planning into the adversarial training loop.
- Employs an Adversarial Inverse Reinforcement Learning (AIRL) formulation to learn reward functions directly from observation-only state transitions, without requiring expert actions, and integrates online dynamics model learning when a prior model is unavailable.

## Results

- Demonstrated improved out-of-distribution generalization in simulated navigation tasks, with MPAIL agents successfully reaching goals from states where policy-based GAIL agents consistently failed.
- Successfully reproduced real-world expert trajectories with an average Relative Cross-Track-Error (CTE) of **0.17 m** from a single, partially observable demonstration, outperforming GAIL and a two-stage IRL-MPC approach.
- Achieved optimality with less than half the interaction steps compared to GAIL on a navigation task when an approximate prior model was available, indicating favorable interaction efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online deployment of a learned reward function through continuous planning is essential for achieving robust out-of-distribution generalization, going beyond the mere learning of the reward itself.
> - Integrating the planner directly into the learning process creates a more effective adversarial dynamic, enabling more complete and stable training of reward and value functions from ambiguous, real-world observation-only data.
> - The combination of learned single-step costs and a generalized value function is critical for robust planning behavior, allowing the agent to both follow expert demonstrations and generalize intent beyond immediate observed states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of imitation learning (IL), specifically addressing the challenge of enabling robots to perform new tasks by inferring underlying intent from observed demonstrations. Traditionally, Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) provides a principled method for learning a reward function from expert behavior, offering advantages in reward generalizability and policy discovery. However, deploying these learned rewards in real-time systems often involves a two-stage process: offline IRL to learn the reward, followed by online Model Predictive Control (MPC) for deployment.

Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) has advanced IRL by improving algorithmic complexity and sample efficiency. Nevertheless, conventional AIL methods, which rely on learning a policy, encounter limitations in applications requiring safety constraints, operating under partial observability, or demanding trustworthiness and interpretability. In such scenarios, planning-based approaches using a predictive model are often preferred. While existing research has explored model-based AIL for training stabilization and policy optimization, or planning-based reinforcement learning (RL) when rewards are known, the direct integration of reward learning with planning for real-world, observation-only imitation learning remains less explored. Few studies evaluate planning-based AIL or RL on actual robotic platforms. This paper introduces a unified framework to address these limitations by integrating IRL, AIL, and MPC into a single learning paradigm for planning from observation (PfO).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to unify the procedures of Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) and to introduce an end-to-end planning-from-observation (PfO) framework. The work aims to overcome several limitations observed in existing imitation learning paradigms, particularly in their application to real-world robotics.

The key motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Enabling Planning-from-Observation (PfO) for interpretable and scalable imitation learning:** The authors observe that current policy-based AIL methods often struggle with generalization, robustness, and sample efficiency, especially when learning from ambiguous, observation-only expert data. A predictive model, which allows access to an agent's optimization landscape, is hypothesized to provide crucial insight and steerability into decision-making. By developing a planning-based AIL, the research aims to improve out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, robustness, and sample efficiency. A fundamental limitation identified in policy-based AIL is the absence of effective reward deployment, meaning the learned intent is not fully leveraged during execution.

2. **Unification of IRL and MPC for end-to-end interactive learning:** The traditional approach of separate IRL training and MPC deployment procedures is considered inefficient and rigid. The motivation is to integrate these two processes into a single, interactive learning framework. This unification would allow critical online settings—such as dynamic environmental changes, evolving preferences, or real-time control constraints—to be incorporated directly into the training loop. Furthermore, an end-to-end planner is expected to facilitate experience-based reasoning beyond the immediate planning horizon and to induce a more effective adversarial dynamic compared to policy-based generators, particularly when learning from partial observations in real-world scenarios.

3. **Extending PfO to continuous spaces and interactive learning:** The paper aims to develop a framework that is applicable to continuous action and state spaces, which are common in robotics, and supports interactive learning where the agent continuously refines its understanding through interaction with the environment.

4. **Establishing theoretical foundations:** By selecting Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control as the embedded planner, the research aims to provide a theoretical perspective on planning-based AIL and its relationship to established AIL objectives, such as GAIL. This theoretical grounding underpins the proposed Model Predictive Adversarial Imitation Learning (MPAIL) algorithm.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived gap between theoretical advances in imitation learning and their practical, robust deployment in real-world robotic systems, where interpretability, safety, and efficient learning from limited, ambiguous demonstrations are paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce Model Predictive Adversarial Imitation Learning (MPAIL), a framework designed to learn reward functions and control policies simultaneously from observation-only demonstrations within a Model Predictive Control (MPC) paradigm.

#### 3.1. POMDP Setting and Model Predictive Agent

The work adopts a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) setting, where an agent observes `o` from an unknown world state `s_w` and perceives its state `s_t` from a history of observations `o_0:t`. The agent utilizes a predictive model `f: S × A → S` for self-prediction. Demonstrations consist solely of state transitions `(s, s')`, as expert actions may be unobservable. The core of the agent is a model predictive planner, capable of performing `H`-step model rollouts and evaluating these trajectories using a cost function `C(τ)`.

#### 3.2. Adversarial Imitation Learning from Observation

The research frames the problem using the maximum entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) objective, which seeks to learn a cost function that minimizes expert trajectory costs while maximizing others. This objective is dual to the Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) objective:
`min_π max_D E_π [log(D(s, s'))] + E_πE [log(1 - D(s, s'))] - λH(π)`,
where `D` is the discriminator, `π_E` is the expert policy, and `H(π)` is the entropy.

#### 3.3. Choosing a Policy and Reward

To adapt the AIL objective for planning, the authors modify the RL formulation by replacing the entropy regularization term `−λH(π)` with a Kullback-Leibeler (KL) divergence constraint `β KL(π || π)` on the previous policy `π`. This formulation aims to incorporate prior policy information.

**Planner as Policy:** Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) is chosen as the embedded planner. The paper demonstrates that MPPI, which minimizes a KL-constrained cost over trajectories, solves an equivalent problem to the modified RL objective, provided the MDP is uniformly ergodic. To extend MPPI to infinite horizons and address myopic planning, a value function `V_ϕ : S → R` is introduced to bootstrap the terminal states in the rollout, providing an estimate of the expected future return. The MPPI policy, `π_MPPI`, optimizes a new policy at each timestep.

**Reward Function:** The reward function `r(s, s')` is defined using the Adversarial Inverse Reinforcement Learning (AIRL) formulation, where `r(s, s') = log(D(s, s')) - log(1 - D(s, s'))`, which simplifies to the logit of the discriminator output, `d_θ(s, s')`.

#### 3.4. MPAIL Algorithm Overview

The MPAIL procedure (Algorithm 1) closely mirrors the original GAIL algorithm but integrates the MPPI planner and value function updates:
1. **Data Collection:** The `π_MPPI` agent interacts with the environment to collect transitions `(s, s', r_θ(·))`.
2. **Discriminator Update:** The discriminator parameters `θ` are updated using a Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss to distinguish between expert and agent state-transitions.
3. **Value Function Update:** The value function parameters `ϕ` are updated by minimizing the squared error between estimated returns `G_t` and `V_ϕ(s)`.
Unlike policy-based AIL, MPAIL does not require a separate policy update, as policies are effectively "solved online" by the MPPI planner.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Justification

The paper provides theoretical proofs (Section B of the appendix) validating MPAIL:
- It demonstrates that the KL-constrained RL objective (Eq. 3) has a closed-form solution.
- It proves the equivalence between the MPPI objective and the entropy-regularized RL objective in the observation-only setting, given uniform ergodicity of the MDP.
- It shows that at optimality, the learned reward function `f*θ(s, s')` corresponds to the log-ratio of the expert's and policy's state-transition occupancy measures: `log(ρ_E(s,s') / ρ_π(s,s'))`.
- It establishes that MPAIL minimizes a regularized KL divergence between the policy's state-transition occupancy measure and the expert's.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

Several engineering choices contribute to MPAIL's stability and performance:
- **Regularization:** Spectral normalization is applied to the discriminator for training stability. L2 weight regularization is used in real-world experiments.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key hyperparameters for both AIL and MPPI components are defined, including a temperature decay schedule for MPPI to prevent early collapse and a balanced value-to-discriminator update ratio. A "markup" factor (`η > 1`) is applied to costs in MPPI, conceptually inverse to a reward discount.
- **Computation:** MPAIL is implemented with parallelized model rollouts and costing on the GPU, minimizing impact on training times despite the computational complexity of MPPI.

#### 3.7. Dynamics Model Learning

For tasks without a predefined model (e.g., Cartpole and Ant), a deterministic dynamics model `f_ψ(s, a)` is learned online by minimizing the mean squared error between predicted and observed next states. A transition replay buffer and a step-based learning rate decay are employed to enhance model accuracy and training stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results addressing three core questions related to the advantages of planning-based AIL in generalization, real-world applicability, and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Out-of-Distribution Robustness

**Finding:** MPAIL exhibits improved generalization capabilities in out-of-distribution (OOD) states compared to policy-based AIL (GAIL).
**Experiment:** A simulated navigation task with a 10-DoF vehicle trained on only four expert trajectories, concentrated in a small region, while agents are initialized across a much larger 40x40m area.
**Results:** MPAIL agents were observed to navigate effectively to the goal from widely OOD starting positions, leveraging the generalizability of the learned reward and value functions. In contrast, GAIL policies frequently failed to generalize, even when initialized relatively close to the expert data distribution. This indicates that the online deployment of a learned reward via planning is crucial for OOD generalization, going beyond mere reward learning. Furthermore, when the dynamics model itself was learned online (MPAIL (OM)), the OOD robustness persisted, suggesting that the generalizability benefits extend even when the agent starts with no prior model. Behavior Cloning policies, even with access to expert actions, demonstrated brittle representations and poor OOD performance.

#### 4.2. Real-World Planning from Single Observation

**Finding:** MPAIL successfully enables real-world planning from a single, partially observable, observation-only demonstration, outperforming both policy-based AIL (GAIL) and a two-stage IRL-MPC approach.
**Experiment:** A "Real-Sim-Real" setup using a small-scale RC car. A single partially observable trajectory (position and body-centric velocity) was collected in the real world, the methods were trained in simulation, and then deployed zero-shot back to the real world.
**Results:** MPAIL qualitatively reproduced the expert trajectory with an average Relative Cross-Track-Error (CTE) of 0.17 m and an average speed of 0.7 m/s. It was the only method that reliably converged and performed the task. GAIL consistently failed, often veering off-path or circling, suggesting instability in real-world conditions and with multimodal expert data. IRL-MPC, which learned its reward via GAIL and then deployed it with MPPI, showed improvements over GAIL but still underperformed MPAIL.
**Analysis:** The authors attribute MPAIL's success to several factors:
- **Interpretability:** Access to the agent's optimization landscape (visualized through model rollouts and costing) provided by MPAIL enhances understanding of decision-making.
- **Online Optimization:** The MPC-based agent's ability to perform online trajectory optimization provides inherent disturbance rejection, allowing it to recover and return to the expert distribution even when deviating.
- **End-to-End Learning:** The inclusion of the planner directly in the learning process (instead of offline policy optimization) induces a more competitive adversarial dynamic, allowing the reward and value functions to train more effectively and completely, especially critical for ambiguous real-world data.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Benchmarking

**Finding:** MPAIL exhibits favorable interaction efficiency, particularly when equipped with an approximate prior model.
**Experiment:** Benchmarking on the simulated navigation and Cartpole tasks, comparing MPAIL against GAIL and AIRL across varying quantities of expert demonstrations.
**Results:** On the navigation task (with an approximate kinematic bicycle model as a prior), MPAIL reached optimality in less than half the number of interactions compared to GAIL and demonstrated more stable training. AIRL struggled with the multimodal expert data. On the Cartpole task, where the dynamics model was learned entirely online (MPAIL (OM)), MPAIL's performance was comparable to AIRL. While MPAIL (OM) might require more exploratory interactions due to online model learning and sparse reward signals, it achieved this while retaining the benefits of model-based planning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and High-Dimensional Tasks

**Ablation Study:** An ablation study on the contributions of the single-step costs `c_θ` and the value function `V_ϕ` revealed that both components are necessary for robust behavior across different planning horizons. Value-only planning was found to be unstable, while cost-only planning lacked generalization when the agent moved off the expert distribution. The combination of both allowed the costs to define the expert distribution and the value function to generalize the reward beyond its immediate support.

**High-Dimensional Tasks:** Preliminary experiments on the Isaac Lab Ant-v2 environment (a 60-dimensional observation and 8-dimensional action space) demonstrated that MPAIL (OM) could guide MPPI to achieve walking behaviors, despite the inherent challenges of sample-based planning in such high-dimensional spaces. This indicates the potential for MPAIL to scale to more complex robotic tasks, especially with future advancements in sampling and latent dynamics for MPC.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes contributions to the fields of imitation learning, model predictive control, and robot learning, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Theory-Practice Gap in Robot Learning:** MPAIL directly addresses critical challenges in deploying imitation learning to real-world robots, specifically the need to learn from few, observation-only, potentially ambiguous demonstrations while inferring underlying intent through interaction. This is a common scenario in human-robot interaction and autonomous systems.

2. **Unification of IRL and MPC:** The core contribution of unifying IRL and MPC into an end-to-end framework marks a departure from traditional sequential approaches. This integrated learning paradigm allows for online adaptation to critical real-world factors such as dynamic changes in environment, evolving preferences, and complex control constraints, enhancing the relevance and effectiveness of the learned policies.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The findings highlight that the *deployment* of learned rewards, rather than just their learning, is critical for achieving robust out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. By continuously re-solving for policies online through planning, MPAIL reduces the burden on policies to generalize themselves, instead relying on the generalizability of the learned reward (intent) and value functions. This online optimization also provides inherent control-theoretic disturbance rejection, making the agent more resilient to deviations from the expert's observed behavior, particularly important in partially observable or noisy real-world environments.

4. **Enhanced Interpretability for Trustworthy Robotics:** MPAIL's explicit use of learned models, rewards, and value functions provides direct access to the agent's optimization landscape. This transparency offers crucial insights into the agent's decision-making process, a powerful prerequisite for developing safe, verifiable, and interpretable robot learning systems, especially when dealing with complex human demonstrations.

5. **Practical Efficiency:** Despite the computational demands of MPC, MPAIL demonstrates favorable interaction efficiency, especially when an approximate prior model is available. This addresses concerns about the scalability of zeroth-order policy optimization and supports its characterization as a model-based algorithm that can benefit from sample efficiency.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** MPAIL's design, derived from MPC, model-based RL, and imitation learning, creates a versatile framework. It is explicitly presented as a common ground for integrating future advancements from these adjacent fields, such as off-policy value estimation, improved sampling distributions, latent space dynamics modeling, model-free/model-based reward blending, and diffusion-inspired optimization techniques. This positions MPAIL as a foundational contribution for future research at the intersection of these domains.

7. **Reproducibility and Open-Source Contribution:** The provision of open-source code and detailed implementation instructions, including integration with established robotics platforms (Isaac Lab, MuSHR), enhances reproducibility and facilitates broader adoption and expansion of this research by the community.

Overall, MPAIL represents a step towards creating more capable, adaptable, and understandable robotic systems that can learn effectively from human demonstrations in challenging real-world scenarios.
