# Data-Driven MPC with Data Selection for Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arms

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers developed a data-driven Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach, augmented with a data selection algorithm, for Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arms, achieving an average positioning accuracy of 2.070 mm and a tracking error of 0.541%uFFFD%uFFFD%uFFFDa substantial reduction compared to PID methods%uFFFD%uFFFD%uFFFDwhile decreasing computational time by approximately 80%. This method allows FCRAs to achieve high-precision tasks without requiring an explicit physical model.

## Problem

- Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arms (FCRAs) exhibit complex, nonlinear dynamic behaviors due to cable properties like resilience, hysteresis, and friction.
- Traditional model-based control methods face limitations in accurately capturing these dynamics, leading to suboptimal control precision, slow response, and stability issues.
- Explicit physical models for FCRAs are difficult and inaccurate to construct and maintain, hindering high-precision control.

## Method

- A data-driven ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) strategy was developed, leveraging behavioral systems theory to infer system dynamics from input-output data without an explicit physical model.
- A Data Search Algorithm (DSA) was introduced to dynamically select the most relevant data subgroup for MPC optimization, significantly reducing computational load.
- Pre-trained Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) are used as equivalent forward and inverse models, aiding in both validation and providing target motor velocities for the MPC cost function.

## Results

- The FCRA achieved an average positioning accuracy of **2.070** mm and a repeatability (3-sigma) of **7.750** mm across multiple target points.
- The data-driven MPC demonstrated an average tracking error of **0.541**%uFFFD, significantly outperforming a PID control method which yielded **1.418**%uFFFD error for single-point tasks.
- The Data Search Algorithm reduced the average MPC solution time from approximately 19 ms to 4 ms per step (an **80%** reduction), enabling effective real-time control for complex trajectory tracking, such as drawing letters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex, nonlinear systems like FCRAs can be effectively controlled using implicit models derived directly from input-output data, bypassing the need for explicit physical modeling.
> - Introducing a data selection mechanism significantly enhances the real-time computational efficiency of data-driven MPC, reducing processing time by nearly 80%.
> - The effectiveness of data-driven MPC for nonlinear systems is sensitive to hyperparameters like data length (N) and estimation horizon (n_ini), requiring careful tuning for optimal tracking performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arms (FCRAs) represent a class of robotic systems offering compliant and dexterous motion, making them suitable for applications such as medical robotics, space exploration, and industrial automation. Their design utilizes the flexibility and extensibility of cables for joint actuation, which provides advantages in navigating complex environments compared to rigid-bodied robots.

However, the inherent material properties of these cables, including resilience, hysteresis, and friction, introduce significant challenges for accurate modeling and control. Traditional model-based control methods often struggle to capture these complex nonlinear dynamics precisely, leading to limitations in control precision, response speed, and stability, particularly for tasks requiring high accuracy. Existing studies have made progress in structural design and control, but most rely on complex physical models that frequently fall short in accurately representing the true system dynamics due to these nonlinearities.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a recognized framework for planning and control. However, applying MPC to FCRAs traditionally requires an accurate physical model, which is difficult to construct and maintain. This has led to the exploration of data-driven control methods that bypass explicit physical modeling. Data-driven approaches, based on behavioral systems theory, leverage real-world input-output data to learn system dynamics directly. Such model-free or data-enabled MPC methods have demonstrated effectiveness in various systems, including quadruped robots, aerial robots, and industrial processes, by using experimental data for predictive control.

This research contributes to the field by applying and enhancing data-driven MPC specifically for FCRAs. It addresses the challenge of complex nonlinear dynamics by developing a control strategy that operates solely on input-output data, thereby avoiding the difficulties of physical modeling. Furthermore, it introduces a data selection mechanism to improve the computational efficiency of the data-driven MPC, a practical consideration for real-time robotic control. The work positions itself as an advancement in robust and adaptive control for complex robotic systems, contrasting with both traditional model-based methods and other model-free techniques like reinforcement learning, which often require extensive training or exhibit limited practical performance on physical systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the significant control challenges posed by Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arms (FCRAs) due to the complex, nonlinear dynamic behaviors arising from cable properties such as resilience, hysteresis, and friction. Traditional control methods, relying on explicit physical models, have demonstrated limitations in accurately capturing these characteristics, resulting in suboptimal control precision, slow response, and inadequate stability for high-precision tasks.

Specifically, the motivations and objectives are as follows:

- **Overcome Modeling Challenges of FCRAs**: A central motivation is to bypass the complexities and inaccuracies associated with constructing and maintaining precise physical models for FCRAs. The inherent material properties of cables make their dynamic behavior difficult to predict and model mathematically. The objective is to develop a control strategy that does not require an explicit physical model, relying instead on real-world operational data.
- **Improve Control Accuracy and Performance**: The research aims to enhance the control accuracy and overall performance of FCRAs, particularly in trajectory tracking and positioning. By leveraging observed input-output data, the goal is to develop a system that can effectively predict and control the arm's motion, thereby achieving higher precision than traditional methods.
- **Develop a Data-Driven Implicit Model for MPC**: The study seeks to develop an implicit model based exclusively on input-output data, derived from behavioral systems theory. This model is intended to uncover motion patterns and dynamic characteristics directly from extensive trajectory samples, enabling precise behavioral predictions without requiring explicit knowledge of physical parameters. This implicit model is then to be integrated into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) optimization framework.
- **Enhance Computational Efficiency of Data-Driven MPC**: A practical objective is to address the computational burden associated with using large datasets in data-driven MPC. The motivation is to reduce the real-time solution time per control step. This is pursued by introducing a Data Selection Algorithm (DSA) designed to filter and extract the most representative data points that accurately characterize the current system state, thus optimizing the data used for MPC optimization.
- **Validate the Proposed Method on a Real FCRA Platform**: The ultimate objective is to experimentally validate the effectiveness, accuracy, and efficiency of the proposed data-driven MPC with data selection on a physical FCRA platform. This includes evaluating positioning accuracy, single-point response tracking, and complex trajectory tracking performance, with comparative analysis against conventional control methods like PID.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates an implicit data-driven model within an MPC framework, complemented by a data selection algorithm, to control Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arms (FCRAs).

**A. Modeling of the Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arm**
The FCRA consists of three main parts:
1. **Drive part**: Comprises 3n brushless DC servo motors, each with an encoder for real-time angle measurement. Motors are controlled via CANOPEN protocol.
2. **Transmission part**: Includes linear leadscrews, couplings, and guide rails. The rotation of leadscrews drives sliding blocks that pull cables, actuating joint movements.
3. **Actuation part**: Consists of multiple universal joints, each with two perpendicular degrees of freedom, driven by three cables. Two encoders per joint measure its angles.

Due to the coupling phenomenon where movement in one joint affects driving cables for other joints, a direct kinematic mapping is complex. The system is therefore treated as a black-box model, where inputs are the 3n motor angles ($\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{3n}$) and outputs are the 2n joint angles ($\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{2n}$). For experimental validation, a 3-segment FCRA (n=3) is used, meaning 9 motor inputs and 6 joint outputs.

**B. Construction of Data-Driven MPC**
The approach leverages behavioral systems theory, specifically for linear time-invariant (LTI) systems, and extends it to nonlinear systems.
1. **Hankel Matrix and Trajectory Representation**: For collected input-output data sequences (u_d, y_d), a Hankel matrix H_L(u_d) is constructed. A signal is considered "persistently exciting of order L" if its Hankel matrix has full row rank, ensuring system observability. Theorem 1 establishes that if a collected sequence is a trajectory of an LTI system, then any L-length sequence is a trajectory if a specific vector K exists relating the Hankel matrices of input and output data.
2. **Extension to Nonlinear Systems and Least Squares Approximation**: Recognizing that FCRAs are nonlinear and data contains noise, the explicit identification of an LTI system is avoided. Instead, the method extends by assuming that for sufficiently large data volume N, a least squares approximation of K can be used. This approximation allows the problem to be transformed into a constant linear mapping (Equation 8) where future outputs (y) are predicted from past inputs (u_ini), past outputs (y_ini), and future inputs (u) via a G matrix. The G matrix is defined as G:= Y_f * [U_p; Y_p; U_f]^†, where U_p, Y_p represent past data and U_f, Y_f represent future data in the Hankel matrix decomposition.
3. **General Data-Driven MPC Formulation**: The data-driven MPC problem is then formulated as an optimization (Equation 9) to minimize a cost function involving input and output tracking errors, subject to the data-driven model constraint (Equation 8) and operational bounds.

**C. Data-Driven Motion Planner**
The specific MPC problem for trajectory planning aims to track a target trajectory (y_tar) in joint space and terminal position (y_ter). It is formulated as a strictly convex quadratic programming problem (Equation 10a-10d):
- **Cost Function (10a)**: Minimizes a weighted sum of:
 - Terminal error: (y_l - y_ter)^2_S
 - Tracking errors: (u_k - u_tar_k)^2_R and (y_k - y_tar_k)^2_Q
 - Smoothness terms: (u_k+1 - u_k)^2_F and (y_k+1 - y_k)^2_P, penalizing changes in motor velocities and joint angles.
- **Constraints (10b-10d)**:
 - Data-driven model: y = G * [u_ini; y_ini; u] (linking predictions to collected data)
 - Input/Output bounds: u_k ∈ U, y_k ∈ Y
 - Rate limits: Δy_min ≤ y_k+1 - y_k ≤ Δy_max
The OSQP solver is used for real-time solution.

**D. Data Search Algorithm (DSA)**
To enhance efficiency, the DSA is introduced. Instead of using all N_s collected data points, which is computationally intensive, the data is divided into M groups, each of N points. At runtime, based on the current system state, the algorithm selects the most representative data group {u_d_1:N,be, y_d_1:N,be} from these M groups. This selection is made by minimizing ||K_j|| in Equation 11, where K_j relates the Hankel matrix of a data group j to the current system's sampled trajectory, including slack variables (σ_L,th) for noise. The selected group is then used to construct the G matrix for the MPC, and also to choose the corresponding inverse MLP reference model. This process reduces computation time by approximately 80%.

**E. Equivalent Model Reference**
Forward and inverse models of the system are constructed using Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Forward Model**: Maps motor angles to joint angles, used for algorithm validation.
- **Inverse Model**: Maps joint angles to motor angles, crucial for determining target motor velocities (u_tar) in the MPC cost function.
M distinct MLP models (forward and inverse) are pre-trained, corresponding to the M data groups. When the DSA selects a data group, its corresponding inverse MLP model is also chosen as the input reference.
MLPs are three-layer perceptrons with 256 neurons per hidden layer, trained for 100 epochs using Adam optimizer, RMSE loss, a learning rate of 0.001, and a batch size of 128.

**F. Quintic Polynomial Interpolation**
For single-point target tasks, a quintic polynomial q(t) is used to generate a smooth, continuous reference trajectory. This ensures continuous position, velocity, and acceleration by setting zero initial/final velocities and accelerations, providing a stable path for high-precision tasks like drawing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed data-driven MPC method with data selection was validated through simulation and real-world experiments on a three-segment FCRA (n=3), comprising 9 motor inputs and 6 joint outputs. The control system operated at 50 Hz, coordinating MPC computation and feedback via ROS.

**A. DSA Verification Experiment**
- **Data Collection**: N_s data points were collected with varied loads (0 to 2.5 kg in 0.5 kg increments) applied to the end-effector. These were divided into M=6 groups, each with N=120000 data points.
- **Performance**: The Data Search Algorithm (DSA) identified the data group (indexed j) whose corresponding ||K_j|| value was minimal. This minimum was observed to correlate with the data collected under a payload closest to the actual load.
- **Computational Efficiency**: Directly using all N_s data points for G matrix computation resulted in an average processing time of approximately 19 ms per step. In contrast, using a data group selected by DSA reduced the solving time to approximately 4 ms per step, representing a reduction of nearly 80%. This confirms the DSA's ability to identify relevant data, thus improving real-time performance.

**B. Simulation-based Hyperparameter Tuning Analysis**
Using the MLP models, the influence of hyperparameters (N: total data length, n_ini: estimation horizon) on tracking error was investigated, with l (prediction horizon) also considered.
- **Effect of N**: When the Hankel matrix became at least square, particularly with l=4, the tracking error significantly improved. The study identified a condition N ≥ max{(m+1)(n_ini+l+h), (m+c+1)(n_ini+l)} (where h=15 is number of states) to ensure the Hankel matrix is square.
- **Effect of n_ini**: Increasing n_ini generally led to a reduction in tracking error. This improvement was more pronounced when l=4. The increased n_ini contributed to better initial condition estimation under noisy conditions.

**C. Position Repeatability**
- **Experiment**: Five predefined target positions (P1-P5) were set, and the FCRA was commanded to move between them, repeating the sequence thirty times.
- **Accuracy**: The average positioning accuracy across the five points was measured at approximately 2.070 mm. The average standard deviation was 1.894 mm, and the average 3-sigma distance was 7.750 mm.
- **Comparison**: This accuracy (2.070 mm) was reported to be lower than the 4.9 mm of the Twist Snake, indicating improved positioning performance for the FCRA with the proposed method.
- **Error Source**: The primary source of error was attributed to slight cable elongation over numerous test cycles and extended operation time, leading to minor cable slackening and increased system error, especially given the cable length exceeding one meter.

**D. Tracking Performance**
- **Single-Point Tracking Comparison**: Comparative experiments were conducted between the data-driven MPC and a PID control method for single-point tracking.
 - **PID Method**: Achieved an average tracking error of 1.418°.
 - **Data-Driven MPC**: Achieved an average tracking error of 0.541°, demonstrating a substantial reduction compared to PID. The data-driven MPC exhibited rapid convergence to the target position, even with sudden changes, while the PID method showed significant lag, potentially due to cable hysteresis.
- **Trajectory Tracking (Letter Drawing)**: The FCRA successfully tracked the trajectory for drawing the letters "SMC" in a plane, generating clear and accurate results without relying on a physical model. This demonstrated the method's capability to follow complex trajectories on a physical platform.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the control of Flexible Cable-Driven Robotic Arms (FCRAs) by addressing fundamental challenges associated with their complex dynamics. The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

Firstly, the development of a **data-driven MPC approach that operates exclusively on input-output data** offers a pragmatic solution to the persistent problem of accurately modeling FCRAs. By bypassing the need for explicit physical models, which are often complicated by the inherent nonlinearities, hysteresis, and friction of cable materials, the method provides a robust alternative. This approach can potentially simplify the development and deployment of control systems for such complex robots, as it reduces reliance on detailed mechanical and material characterization.

Secondly, the introduction of a **Data Selection Algorithm (DSA)** represents a practical enhancement for real-time control applications. The DSA effectively reduces the computational burden by identifying and utilizing only the most relevant data points for MPC optimization. The observed reduction in solution time from approximately 19 ms to 4 ms (an improvement of nearly 80%) is a significant factor for systems requiring high-frequency feedback and rapid decision-making, such as robotic arm control. This computational efficiency enhances the feasibility of deploying data-driven control methods in real-world scenarios.

Thirdly, the **demonstrated control performance** on a physical FCRA platform indicates the efficacy of the proposed method. The achievement of an average positioning accuracy of approximately 2.070 mm and a substantially lower average tracking error of 0.541° compared to a PID method (1.418°) highlights the method's capability to achieve high precision. The successful trajectory tracking for complex patterns, such as drawing letters, further validates its robustness and adaptability for various tasks. These results suggest that the proposed data-driven MPC can enable FCRAs to perform high-precision tasks previously challenging for these systems.

The potential impact of this research extends to various fields where FCRAs are applicable. In **medical robotics**, improved precision and control could lead to more dexterous surgical instruments. In **space exploration** or **industrial automation**, enhanced control capabilities could enable FCRAs to operate more reliably and accurately in unstructured or confined environments. The model-free nature also simplifies the adoption of such robots across different configurations or tasks, requiring only new data collection rather than extensive model recalibration.

Future work outlined by the authors includes applying the method to more complex system control tasks, such as robotic arm grasping, and further optimizing the algorithm for enhanced adaptability and robustness in dynamic environments. These future directions suggest the potential for broadening the applicability and refining the performance of this data-driven control paradigm for advanced robotic manipulation.
