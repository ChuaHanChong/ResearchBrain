# PRISM: Personalized Robotic Dataset Generation via Image-based Scene and Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> PRISM introduces an end-to-end pipeline to autonomously generate personalized robotic datasets for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, starting from a single RGB-D image and a natural-language task instruction. The system creates diverse 'digital cousin' scenes and synthesizes executable demonstrations, leading to VLA policies that achieve up to 100% success rates on real-world manipulation tasks and autonomously generates 400 demonstrations per task within 2 to 7 hours.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle to generalize robustly to user-specific, real-world environments due to training data distribution mismatch.
- Existing data collection methods (teleoperation, generic simulation, digital twins) present trade-offs between scalability, instance-level diversity, and alignment with the target environment.
- A lack of automated and scalable methods to generate high-quality, personalized robot demonstrations tailored to specific user scenarios.

## Method

- **Scene Generation**: From a single RGB-D image, it detects objects, retrieves corresponding 3D assets from a library, and constructs '==digital cousin==' simulation scenes that maintain structural similarity to the real environment while introducing instance-level variations.
- **Demonstration Generation**: Translates natural-language task instructions into grounded action sequences and synthesizes executable robot trajectories using a ==TAMP== framework, incorporating a ==motion-aware grasp selection== strategy for smoother, more imitable motions.
- **Dataset Construction**: Replays fixed, successful robot trajectories in simulation while applying ==trajectory-preserving visual randomization== (e.g., lighting, textures, camera poses) to efficiently generate visually diverse training samples.

## Results

- Policies trained on PRISM datasets achieved superior task success and generalization across out-of-domain benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus), demonstrating improved robustness to visual domain shifts.
- Demonstrated strong real-world applicability, with policies fine-tuned on PRISM data consistently outperforming baselines and achieving up to **100%** success rates on specific manipulation tasks.
- Enables autonomous generation of **400** successful demonstrations per task within approximately **2 to 7 hours** of computation, significantly reducing human labor and scaling data acquisition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Utilizing 'digital cousin' scenes, which balance fidelity to the target environment with instance-level diversity, is crucial for improving policy generalization compared to exact digital twins that can lead to overfitting.
> - Motion-aware grasp selection, by prioritizing grasps aligned with canonical end-effector orientations, significantly improves the imitatability of generated demonstrations and enhances policy learning.
> - Trajectory-preserving visual randomization allows for efficient expansion of dataset diversity by replaying the same underlying action sequence with varied visual appearances, reducing computational cost and fostering appearance-invariant policy learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "PRISM" is situated within the evolving landscape of robotic manipulation, particularly in the context of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models, which adapt large vision-language foundation models to predict robot actions from images and natural-language instructions, have demonstrated broad zero-shot competence across various tasks and environments. This competence, however, often diminishes when these models are deployed in user-specific environments, a phenomenon attributed to the mismatch between their training data distribution and the specific deployment conditions. Fine-tuning on data tailored to the target environment is often necessary to bridge this gap, with policy quality being directly correlated to the alignment between training and deployment data.

Existing approaches to acquiring such target-aligned data generally fall into three categories, each with distinct advantages and limitations:
1. **Teleoperation:** This method involves a human directly controlling a robot in the target environment to gather demonstrations. While teleoperation yields data that is structurally and semantically aligned with the deployment environment, it is inherently costly, labor-intensive, and challenging to scale.
2. **Simulation-based scene synthesis:** This approach leverages large language models (LLMs) or vision-language models (VLMs) to compose diverse tasks and scenes from extensive 3D asset libraries. It offers high scalability and diversity but typically generates scenes from a generic, target-agnostic distribution, thereby losing specific alignment with a particular user environment.
3. **Digital Twin reconstruction:** This involves rebuilding a specific target environment from sensor data (e.g., RGB-D video) to create a high-fidelity simulator. While digital twins ensure high alignment with the actual deployment scene, they represent a single instance, lacking the instance-level variation necessary for a policy to generalize beyond that exact configuration.

The existing simulation-based methods thus present a trade-off between target alignment and instance-level diversity. A personalized dataset, as identified by the authors, necessitates both: preserving the target environment's structure (object categories, spatial relations) while introducing variation at the instance level, all paired with task-completing demonstrations at scale. PRISM addresses this gap by proposing an end-to-end pipeline that aims to provide both target alignment and instance-level diversity, coupled with scalable, automated demonstration generation. This positions PRISM as a contribution to enhancing the generalization and practical deployment of VLA models in varied real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop PRISM, an end-to-end pipeline capable of generating personalized robotic datasets. This generation process is initiated from a single RGB-D image of a target environment and a natural-language task instruction.

The motivation behind PRISM stems from several observed limitations in current robotic policy learning and data generation:
1. **Limited VLA Generalization to User-Specific Environments:** Despite advancements in large-scale pretrained VLA models, their performance often degrades significantly when deployed in novel, user-specific environments. This degradation occurs because their broad training data distribution rarely covers any specific user's environment densely enough for reliable task execution. Fine-tuning with data tailored to the target environment is crucial, but obtaining such data without prohibitive human effort is a challenge.
2. **Trade-offs in Existing Data Collection Methods:**
 - **Teleoperation:** While yielding highly aligned data, it is not scalable due to high cost and labor intensity.
 - **Generic Simulation:** Methods that synthesize scenes using LLMs/VLMs are scalable but lack specific alignment to a user's environment, as they draw from a general distribution.
 - **Digital Twins:** These provide excellent alignment to a specific target environment but offer only a single instance, failing to provide the instance-level diversity needed for robust policy generalization.
 The core motivation for PRISM is to overcome this dilemma by providing both target alignment and instance-level diversity simultaneously, allowing for the generation of datasets that are both realistic and varied.
3. **Need for Automated Demonstration Synthesis:** Human teleoperation for demonstration collection is a bottleneck. PRISM seeks to eliminate this by synthetically generating executable demonstrations, thereby improving scalability and reducing reliance on human input.
4. **Enhancing Dataset Quality and Efficiency:** The project aims to incorporate specific design choices to improve the utility of the generated datasets:
 - **Motion-Aware Grasp Selection:** To produce demonstrations that are not just kinematically valid but also easy for a robot policy to imitate, PRISM selects grasps that induce low-complexity, smooth motions.
 - **Trajectory-Preserving Visual Randomization:** To make data generation more efficient and encourage policies to learn task motions invariant to visual appearance, PRISM applies visual randomization while keeping the underlying robot trajectory fixed. This increases visual diversity without requiring the regeneration of complex action sequences.

In essence, PRISM is motivated by the need for a scalable, automated, and effective method to generate high-quality, personalized datasets that enable VLA models to adapt robustly to diverse real-world deployment scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRISM operates as an end-to-end pipeline, processing a single RGB-D image of a target environment and a natural-language task instruction to generate robotic datasets. The pipeline is structured into three main stages: Scene Generation, Demonstration Generation, and Dataset Construction.

#### 3.1. Scene Generation

This stage focuses on constructing a robot-interactable simulation scene that is semantically and geometrically aligned with the user's real-world environment.
- **Object Detection and Segmentation:** Given an input RGB-D image, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) first infers object names present in the scene. Grounded-SAM is then applied to obtain instance-level segmentation masks for each detected object. If RGB-D observations and camera intrinsics are not directly provided, Depth Anything v2 and Perspective Fields are used to estimate depth maps and camera intrinsics, respectively.
- **Object Retrieval:** For each detected object, candidate 3D assets are retrieved from a large-scale 3D asset library (e.g., BEHAVIOR-1k). This is a two-stage process: first, top-n categories with CLIP embeddings closest to the object are identified; second, within these categories, top-m nearest-neighbor assets are retrieved by comparing the segmented object image with rendered 3D assets in DINOv2 embedding space. A VLM then selects the top-k assets most visually similar to the original object.
- **Scene Construction:** The extracted scene information and retrieved assets are integrated. For each detected object, its point cloud is reconstructed from the depth map and segmentation mask to estimate its 3D location. A randomly sampled asset from its top-k candidates is then placed at this estimated location. Optionally, a VLM can infer and place additional task-relevant objects missing from the initial scene. The environment can also be augmented with distractors, walls, and floor surfaces to increase diversity while maintaining structural and visual resemblance to the user's environment. This process creates "digital cousin" scenes that preserve structural similarity but vary at the instance level.

#### 3.2. Demonstration Generation

This stage converts the natural-language task instruction into a sequence of robot actions and then synthesizes executable trajectories.
- **Randomized Scene Initialization:** To ensure diversity in demonstrations, geometric randomization is applied to the newly generated scene. Object positions are perturbed, the robot's initial joint configuration is shifted, and object scales can be varied. This allows demonstrations to be generated across varied object layouts and robot starting states.
- **Instruction Parsing:** A two-phase VLM querying scheme translates the natural-language instruction into an action sequence. The VLM first produces a sequence of actions in natural language, using an object-annotated scene image and the detected object list. In the second phase, this natural-language sequence is translated into a partially grounded action sequence using a predefined set of primitive actions (e.g., `pick(·)`, `place(·, ·)`).
- **Trajectory Generation:** A Task-and-Motion Planning (TAMP) framework is utilized to synthesize an executable robot trajectory from the grounded action sequence. The planner searches for continuous parameters, such as grasp poses, robot configurations, and collision-free motion paths, considering the robot's state and object arrangement.
- **Motion-Aware Grasp Selection:** To improve demonstration quality, PRISM ranks candidate grasp poses. Instead of random sampling, grasps are prioritized based on their rotational alignment with a set of canonical end-effector orientations, which are defined relative to the robot base to correspond to natural approach directions. This guides the TAMP planner towards smoother and more consistent arm motions, making demonstrations easier to imitate.

#### 3.3. Dataset Construction

This final stage builds a diverse dataset for policy learning by replaying the generated executable trajectories in simulation.
- **Trajectory-Preserving Visual Randomization:** Unlike conventional methods that randomize per trajectory, PRISM fixes the action trajectory and physical states, replaying each demonstration multiple times while varying only visual factors (e.g., lighting, background textures, camera poses, robot initial state, object poses). This scheme exposes the policy to visually diverse observations with identical task-relevant content, encouraging learning of appearance-invariant task motions.
- **Data Collection:** During replay in these visually randomized environments, the robot's observations, executed actions, and task success signals are recorded at each timestep. The flexibility of simulation allows for rich sensory information. Only successful demonstrations are retained for the final training dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluated PRISM across several research questions, focusing on task success, generalization, real-world applicability, and pipeline efficiency.

#### 4.1. Sim-to-Sim Experiment (Q1: Task Success Rate & Q2: Generalization)

Policies trained on PRISM-generated datasets were compared against those trained using RoboTwin 2.0 and X-Sim.
- **Task Success and Generalization:** PRISM-generated datasets led to policies with strong performance across out-of-domain settings (LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus benchmarks), often outperforming baselines. Specifically, policies trained on PRISM data exhibited the smallest performance drop when evaluated out-of-domain from In-Domain to LIBERO. This was attributed to PRISM's use of structurally and semantically analogous "digital cousin" environments, which introduced more diversity during data generation compared to exact digital twins. This trend was consistent for both π0.5 fine-tuning and Diffusion Policy training.
- **LIBERO-Plus Generalization:** Detailed analysis on LIBERO-Plus, which introduces perturbations across light, background, noise, and layout, showed that PRISM-trained policies generally maintained higher success rates, particularly under light and background variations, indicating improved robustness to visual domain shifts.

#### 4.2. Real-to-Sim-to-Real Experiment (Q3: Real-World Applicability)

Three real-world manipulation tasks were used to evaluate PRISM's effectiveness in generating data for real-world deployment.
- **Quantitative Results:** Policies fine-tuned on PRISM-generated datasets consistently outperformed those trained with baseline methods, achieving up to a 100% success rate on some tasks. This demonstrated PRISM's ability to generate effective training data while preserving the structural properties of the target environments.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations indicated that PRISM-generated datasets maintained semantic alignment with real scenes without requiring human intervention for determining object categories, sizes, or spatial relationships, unlike some baselines that necessitated manual configuration.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Digital Cousin

An ablation study compared a modified PRISM version using a digital twin scene (PRISM-Twin) against the standard PRISM version using digital cousin scenes (PRISM-Cousin).
- **Instance-Level Diversity:** PRISM-Twin achieved a higher success rate in the exact target environment but showed a sharp performance degradation in variant environments with different objects and background appearances. Conversely, PRISM-Cousin maintained a consistent success rate across both target and variant environments. This result indicated that digital cousin scenes provide sufficient fidelity while introducing enough instance-level diversity to prevent overfitting to a single scene instance, promoting better generalization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study of Motion-Aware Grasp Selection

This study investigated the contribution of the motion-aware grasp selection strategy.
- **Improved Task Success:** Applying motion-aware grasp selection consistently improved task success rates for both π0.5 and Diffusion Policy compared to random grasp sampling. By selecting grasps aligned with canonical approach directions, the system generated arm motions that were more amenable to imitation, benefiting different learning paradigms.

#### 4.5. Effect of Trajectory-Preserving Visual Randomization

An experiment explored the impact of varying the number of visual randomizations applied per fixed demonstration trajectory.
- **Performance Improvement and Efficiency:** Applying multiple randomizations (e.g., 5 or 10) to a single trajectory led to improved policy performance. Additionally, this approach proved more data-efficient, as it reused the same underlying trajectory, reducing the need to generate new demonstrations and lowering the per-sample generation cost.

#### 4.6. Pipeline Efficiency (Q4: Data Generation Efficiency)

The efficiency of PRISM was evaluated in terms of the time and success rate of generating successful task demonstrations.
- **Autonomous Generation:** PRISM can generate 400 successful demonstrations per task within approximately 2 to 7 hours of fully autonomous computation, without requiring any human teleoperation. The process involves scene generation, demonstration generation (VLM-TAMP), and dataset construction (visual randomization).
- **Scalability:** The pipeline's cost is dominated by dataset construction, which involves replaying already-solved trajectories and is highly parallelizable. This allows for practical scaling of large-scale dataset generation. The overall success rate for demonstration generation (VLM-TAMP) across tasks averaged 93.5%.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PRISM introduces a novel methodology that addresses a critical need in the field of robotic manipulation: the scalable and automated generation of personalized datasets for training and fine-tuning robot policies. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Generalization for VLA Models:** By creating "digital cousin" scenes that balance target environment fidelity with instance-level diversity, PRISM enables VLA models to generalize more effectively to user-specific deployment environments. This directly contributes to bridging the sim-to-real gap, a long-standing challenge in robotics.
- **Automated and Scalable Data Generation:** PRISM's end-to-end pipeline significantly reduces the reliance on costly and labor-intensive human teleoperation. The ability to generate hundreds of high-quality demonstrations from a single image and a natural-language instruction, within hours of autonomous computation, provides a scalable solution for data acquisition, accelerating research and development in robotic learning.
- **Improved Data Quality and Efficiency:** The integration of motion-aware grasp selection leads to demonstrations with smoother, more imitable robot motions, which is beneficial for downstream policy learning. The trajectory-preserving visual randomization scheme enhances data efficiency by leveraging existing demonstrations to create visually diverse training samples at a lower computational cost.
- **Broader Applicability:** While currently demonstrated with specific robot arms and rigid objects, the underlying principles of scene synthesis, VLM-guided planning, and intelligent randomization could be extended to a wider range of robotic embodiments, object types (e.g., deformable objects), and more complex manipulation tasks. This could foster the development of more robust, adaptable, and general-purpose robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies limitations, such as constraints to rigid objects and potential inaccuracies from heavy occlusion in single-image reconstruction. These limitations represent clear avenues for future research, including multi-view scene reconstruction and handling deformable objects, further extending the utility and robustness of personalized dataset generation.

In summary, PRISM offers a practical and efficient framework for generating high-quality robotic datasets that are crucial for the deployment of advanced VLA models in diverse real-world settings. Its contributions represent a step towards more autonomous, adaptable, and generalizable robotic intelligence.
