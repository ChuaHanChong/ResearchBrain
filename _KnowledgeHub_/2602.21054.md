# VAUQ: Vision-Aware Uncertainty Quantification for LVLM Self-Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> VAUQ introduces a training-free self-evaluation framework for Large Vision-Language Models that quantifies reliance on visual evidence to mitigate hallucinations. It achieved a +13.3% improvement in self-evaluation AUROC on counterfactual images while reducing inference time by 94.6% compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate 'hallucinations' or outputs ungrounded in visual evidence, limiting their safe deployment in real-world applications.
- Existing LLM self-evaluation methods, primarily language-centric, fail in multimodal contexts due to LVLM's 'language-prior dominance,' leading to confident but visually incorrect answers.
- Current external evaluation (human annotation, LLM-as-a-judge) and some LVLM-specific hallucination detection methods are often costly, time-consuming, or computationally expensive.

## Method

- VAUQ develops an ==Image-Information Score (IS)== that quantifies the reduction in predictive uncertainty when an image is present versus absent, indicating the model's utilization of visual information.
- It employs an ==unsupervised core-region masking strategy==, leveraging the LVLM's internal visual attention weights (from middle-to-later transformer layers) to identify and effectively remove semantically relevant visual evidence.
- The final ==VAUQ score== combines the model's predictive entropy with a 'core-masked' IS, explicitly discounting uncertainty by the model's reliance on identified core visual evidence.

## Results

- VAUQ consistently achieved state-of-the-art self-evaluation performance (AUROC) across various benchmarks (ViLP, MMVet, VisualCoT, CVBench) and three major LVLM architectures.
- On the challenging ViLP dataset with counterfactual images, VAUQ demonstrated a **+13.3%** improvement in self-evaluation AUROC compared to existing methods, effectively mitigating language-prior dominance.
- The framework reduced per-sample inference time by **94.6%** compared to multi-sampling-based baselines while delivering superior accuracy, making it highly efficient for practical deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Informative visual evidence, when properly utilized by an LVLM, should lead to a measurable reduction in its predictive uncertainty, which can be quantified as an Image-Information Score.
> - An LVLM's internal visual attention weights, particularly from mid to later layers, can effectively pinpoint task-relevant visual regions for unsupervised masking without requiring ground-truth annotations.
> - Combining a standard predictive entropy with a vision-aware uncertainty signal (IS_core) provides complementary insights, improving self-evaluation accuracy, especially in scenarios where visual grounding is critical over language priors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated advancements in multimodal tasks, exhibiting visual understanding capabilities. However, a significant limitation is their susceptibility to hallucinations, where models generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect responses. This issue poses risks in real-world applications and necessitates robust evaluation mechanisms.

Existing approaches to evaluate LVLM outputs often involve external evaluators or judges. This method can be resource-intensive and is susceptible to hallucinations from the evaluator itself. An alternative direction is self-evaluation, where models internally estimate the correctness of their own outputs without external supervision. In the realm of Large Language Models (LLMs), various self-evaluation techniques have been explored, including uncertainty quantification (e.g., logit-based, consistency-based), prompting-based confidence reporting, and latent-state analysis. These methods primarily rely on linguistic signals.

Extending these LLM-centric self-evaluation techniques to LVLMs presents challenges. LVLMs process heterogeneous modalities, meaning their uncertainty can originate from both linguistic and visual sources. A notable problem in LVLMs is "language-prior dominance," where models over-rely on linguistic regularities learned during pretraining, potentially under-utilizing visual evidence. Consequently, existing LLM-based self-evaluation methods may assign high confidence to hallucinated responses when the image contradicts common linguistic expectations, as confidence may reflect fluency rather than visual grounding. This discrepancy highlights the need for self-evaluation approaches that explicitly consider the contribution of visual information.

While some prior work has addressed hallucination detection in LVLMs, these efforts often focus on object-level hallucinations (e.g., token-level probabilities, visual attention weights, latent representations related to objects). These methods are limited in their ability to assess response-level hallucination, which is a more general problem across various vision-language tasks. More recent attempts at response-level hallucination detection often require large labeled datasets, computationally intensive multiple sampling, or external natural language inference modules.

This paper positions its work, VAUQ, as an efficient, training-free framework for LVLM self-evaluation that aims to quantify an LVLM's reliance on visual evidence, thereby addressing the limitations of language-prior dominance and the computational expense of some existing LVLM-specific hallucination detection methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenge of hallucination in LVLMs and the inadequacy of current self-evaluation methods. LVLMs frequently generate confident yet incorrect responses, particularly when visual information contradicts linguistic priors. Standard LLM-based self-evaluation metrics often fail in such counterfactual scenarios, exhibiting significant performance degradation because they are heavily influenced by language priors and do not reliably incorporate visual evidence to measure uncertainty. This leads to situations where low predictive uncertainty does not correspond to correct visual grounding, as the model may be confident despite its response being weakly supported or contradicted by the image.

The core research question guiding this work is: "How can we effectively quantify an LVLM’s reliance on visual evidence for self-evaluation?"

To address this, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:
- **Propose a novel framework for vision-aware uncertainty quantification:** Develop VAUQ, a training-free framework designed to enable LVLMs to perform self-evaluation by explicitly measuring the degree to which a model's confidence is justified by visual grounding.
- **Introduce an information-theoretic score with a masking strategy:** Define the Image-Information Score (IS) to quantify the reduction in predictive uncertainty attributable to visual input. Complement this with an unsupervised core-region masking strategy to ensure that the IS reflects semantically meaningful visual information rather than spurious correlations. This approach aims to be label-free and training-free.
- **Conduct comprehensive experimental validation:** Evaluate VAUQ extensively across diverse benchmark datasets and widely used LVLMs. This includes comparing its performance against existing self-evaluation methods and performing detailed ablation studies to analyze the necessity and robustness of each proposed component. The goal is to establish VAUQ's effectiveness, particularly in scenarios requiring strong visual grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VAUQ framework is designed as a training-free self-evaluation method that quantifies an LVLM's reliance on visual evidence. The approach is based on the premise that informative and correctly utilized visual evidence should reduce predictive uncertainty in the LVLM's output.

**Problem Setup:**
The self-evaluation problem is formalized as designing a scoring function `s(x, y)` that measures the likelihood of a generated response `y` being a hallucination for a given multimodal input `x = (v, t)`, where `v` represents visual features and `t` is the textual prompt. A binary self-evaluator `G(x, y)` then classifies the response as hallucinated or correct based on this score against a threshold `τ`.

**Image-Information Score (IS):**
VAUQ introduces the Image-Information Score (`IS_blank`) to quantify the impact of visual input on predictive uncertainty. It is defined as:
`IS_blank = H(y | ∅, t) − H(y | v, t)`
Here, `H(y | v, t)` denotes the length-normalized conditional entropy of the model's predictive distribution given both visual features `v` and text `t`. `H(y | ∅, t)` represents the entropy when visual tokens are entirely removed (i.e., only text input `t` is provided). A higher `IS_blank` indicates that the visual information substantially reduces predictive uncertainty, implying stronger visual grounding for the model's answer generation.

**Unsupervised Core Region Masking:**
To refine `IS_blank`, which can be sensitive to spurious visual correlations, VAUQ incorporates an unsupervised core-region masking strategy. The objective is to focus on task-relevant visual regions.
1. **Visual Attention Aggregation:** Ground-truth evidence annotations are not available at inference time. Therefore, the method estimates salient regions using the model's internal visual attention weights. It aggregates attention from a contiguous range of transformer layers (typically middle to later layers, which empirical analysis suggests are more informative for visual-semantic alignment). The attention weight `Attn(v_i)` for an image token `v_i` is computed by summing attention weights from all generated output tokens `y_j` to `v_i`, across specified layers (`l_s` to `l_e`) and attention heads (`h`).
 `Attn(v_i) = Σ_{l=l_s}^{l_e} Σ_{h=1}^{H} Σ_{j=1}^{M} A^{(l,h)}(y_j, v_i)`
2. **Core Region Identification:** The top K% of image patches with the highest `Attn(v_i)` scores are identified as `v_top`. These are considered the "core regions" that the model is most likely relying on.
3. **Core-Masked Image-Information Score (`IS_core`):** A masked visual input `v_masked` is created by excluding the `v_top` patches. The `IS_core` is then computed using this masked input:
 `IS_core = H(y | v_masked, t) − H(y | v, t)`
 By masking the most attended regions, the method assesses how much predictive uncertainty increases when the primary visual evidence is intentionally removed. This penalizes predictions that remain confident even when their core visual support is absent.

**Vision-Aware Uncertainty Quantification (VAUQ Score):**
The final VAUQ score `s_VAUQ(x, y)` combines the predictive entropy with the `IS_core`:
`s_VAUQ(x, y) = H(y | v, t) − α · IS_core`
where `α` is a weighting hyperparameter. This formulation interprets the score as measuring predictive uncertainty while explicitly penalizing confidence not supported by core visual evidence.
- If a model genuinely relies on visual information, masking core regions will lead to a significant increase in `H(y | v_masked, t)`, resulting in a larger `IS_core`. This leads to a lower `s_VAUQ` score, indicating a more reliable and grounded prediction.
- If a model's prediction is dominated by language priors, `IS_core` will be low (masking visual information does not significantly change uncertainty), leading to a relatively higher `s_VAUQ` value, signaling an increased risk of hallucination.

**Implementation Details:**
The method uses greedy decoding for response generation. For efficiency, instead of modifying raw image inputs, masking is applied to the attention weights associated with visual tokens in `v_top` (attention knockout strategy). The hyperparameters `α` and `K` are tuned on a held-out validation set. The range of transformer layers `(l_s, l_e)` for attention aggregation is heuristically selected based on empirical observations. Ground-truth correctness labels for model responses are obtained via GPT-5 as an LLM-as-a-judge for free-form VQA, and exact matching for multiple-choice tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate VAUQ's performance across various LVLMs and datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness and robustness.

**Overall Performance:**
VAUQ consistently achieves high performance, outperforming eight competitive self-evaluation baselines, including both LLM-based and LVLM-specific approaches. This holds true across LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL3.5 models, and across free-form visual question answering datasets (ViLP, MMVet, VisualCoT) and a multiple-choice benchmark (CVBench). The method demonstrates more consistent performance across different architectures and data distributions compared to baselines.

**Quantitative Improvements:**
- **Against LLM-based methods:** VAUQ shows substantial improvements. For example, on the ViLP dataset with LLaVA-1.5-7B, VAUQ improves AUROC by +13.4% over Semantic Entropy. This improvement is attributed to VAUQ's explicit modeling of visual evidence, which LLM-based methods lack, especially in challenging counterfactual scenarios.
- **Against LVLM-based methods:** VAUQ surpasses the previous state-of-the-art LVLM self-evaluation method, VL-Uncertainty, by +21.4% AUROC on ViLP and +12.6% on VisualCoT. This is achieved without the need for multiple sampling or external modules, which VL-Uncertainty requires.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effectiveness of Core-Region Masking:** Experiments on VisualCoT, which provides ground-truth bounding boxes, reveal that masking the ground-truth evidence region (`IS_GT`) yields higher AUROC than masking the entire image (`IS_blank`) or random masking (`IS_rand`). VAUQ's unsupervised core-region masking (`IS_core`) achieves performance comparable to the oracle `IS_GT` setting, indicating its effectiveness in identifying semantically relevant visual content. Random masking, in contrast, degrades performance.
- **Visual Attention for Region Capture:** Analysis of normalized attention weights on VisualCoT (with LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B) shows that middle to later transformer layers (e.g., layers 10-25) are more reliable in capturing evidence regions compared to early layers (0-10). This supports the design choice of aggregating attention from intermediate layers. Qualitative visualizations further confirm that the core-region masking strategy effectively identifies and masks semantically important regions for reasoning (e.g., a "cereal" in a bowl).
- **Complementary Roles of Components:** A component analysis on the ViLP dataset reveals that predictive entropy performs well on factual samples but degrades significantly on counterfactual samples due to language prior reliance. Conversely, `IS_core` shows strong performance on counterfactual samples, where visual evidence is crucial, while having moderate performance on factual samples. The combination of these two signals in VAUQ leads to robust and balanced performance across both factual and counterfactual settings, indicating their complementary nature.

**Inference Efficiency:**
VAUQ demonstrates a favorable efficiency-performance trade-off. It requires only a constant number of additional forward passes for uncertainty estimation (to compute scores with masked visual inputs), making its overall inference complexity linear in the output length (`O(M)`). This contrasts with multi-sampling-based approaches that incur `O(A * M)` complexity (where `A` is the number of samples). VAUQ achieves a 94.6% reduction in per-sample inference time compared to VL-Uncertainty, while simultaneously delivering superior self-evaluation accuracy.

**Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Generalization:**
- **Weighting Parameter (α):** Moderate values of `α` (between 0.5 and 1.5) generally yield optimal performance, suggesting a balanced contribution from both predictive entropy and `IS_core`. Tasks with higher reliance on visual evidence tend to benefit from larger `α`.
- **Proportion of Masked Patches (K):** Moderate values of `K` (e.g., 30-40%) result in stable performance, with degradation observed at very small or very large `K` values.
- **Generalization across Data Distributions:** VAUQ demonstrates robust generalization. Hyperparameters tuned on one dataset (in-distribution) transfer effectively to different target datasets (out-of-distribution) with minimal performance drop, indicating its practical utility in varied domains.

**Additional Validation:**
- **HallusionBench:** VAUQ consistently outperforms baselines on the HallusionBench dataset, further validating its effectiveness.
- **AUPRC:** Under class-imbalanced conditions (e.g., ViLP dataset), VAUQ shows improved AUPRC (+8.0% over Semantic Entropy), demonstrating its robustness beyond AUROC.
- **Comparison to other masking strategies:** VAUQ's attention-based masking achieves stronger localization quality (Pixel Accuracy, mIoU, mAP) compared to an embedding-based baseline (Contextual Lens) and outperforms a Grad-CAM based masking variant in uncertainty quantification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VAUQ framework contributes to the field of large vision-language models by addressing the critical problem of hallucination during self-evaluation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Safety:** By explicitly accounting for visual grounding, VAUQ provides a more reliable signal for an LVLM to assess the correctness of its own outputs. This can enable safer deployment of LVLMs in real-world, high-stakes applications where hallucinations pose significant risks. The framework facilitates selective prediction and can highlight instances requiring human oversight.
- **Overcoming Language Prior Dominance:** VAUQ directly tackles the "language prior dominance" problem inherent in many LVLMs and LLM-based self-evaluation methods. By introducing a vision-aware uncertainty metric, it ensures that model confidence is justified by actual visual evidence, rather than mere linguistic fluency or statistical regularities.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The training-free and label-free nature of VAUQ, coupled with its efficient inference (linear in output length with minimal overhead), makes it a practical and scalable solution for real-world LVLM applications. It avoids the computational costs associated with fine-tuning, large labeled datasets, or multiple sampling strategies required by some existing methods.
- **Interpretability:** The Image-Information Score provides an interpretable measure of how much visual information contributes to a prediction's uncertainty reduction. This offers insights into the model's reasoning process and its reliance on different modalities.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The consistent outperformance across diverse LVLM architectures and benchmark datasets, along with demonstrated generalization of hyperparameters across different data distributions, underscores VAUQ's robust design and broad applicability. Its strong performance in challenging counterfactual scenarios is particularly impactful.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** VAUQ establishes a strong baseline and encourages further research into vision-aware self-evaluation. This could include developing adaptive or sample-specific strategies for hyperparameter tuning, extending the framework to more complex reasoning tasks (e.g., long chain-of-thought, multi-step visual inference, video understanding), and exploring how visual information contributes across various reasoning steps.
- **Ethical Considerations:** By providing a lightweight tool for identifying unreliable outputs, VAUQ contributes to the responsible development and deployment of multimodal AI systems. While not a definitive safety mechanism on its own, it serves as a valuable complementary tool for existing safeguards and human oversight.
