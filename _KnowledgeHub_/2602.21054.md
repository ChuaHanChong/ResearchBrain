# VAUQ: Vision-Aware Uncertainty Quantification for LVLM Self-Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> VAUQ introduces a training-free self-evaluation framework for Large Vision-Language Models that quantifies reliance on visual evidence to mitigate hallucinations. It achieved a +13.3% improvement in self-evaluation AUROC on counterfactual images while reducing inference time by 94.6% compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate 'hallucinations' or outputs ungrounded in visual evidence, limiting their safe deployment in real-world applications.
- Existing LLM self-evaluation methods, primarily language-centric, fail in multimodal contexts due to LVLM's 'language-prior dominance,' leading to confident but visually incorrect answers.
- Current external evaluation (human annotation, LLM-as-a-judge) and some LVLM-specific hallucination detection methods are often costly, time-consuming, or computationally expensive.

## Method

- VAUQ develops an ==Image-Information Score (IS)== that quantifies the reduction in predictive uncertainty when an image is present versus absent, indicating the model's utilization of visual information.
- It employs an ==unsupervised core-region masking strategy==, leveraging the LVLM's internal visual attention weights (from middle-to-later transformer layers) to identify and effectively remove semantically relevant visual evidence.
- The final ==VAUQ score== combines the model's predictive entropy with a 'core-masked' IS, explicitly discounting uncertainty by the model's reliance on identified core visual evidence.

## Results

- VAUQ consistently achieved state-of-the-art self-evaluation performance (AUROC) across various benchmarks (ViLP, MMVet, VisualCoT, CVBench) and three major LVLM architectures.
- On the challenging ViLP dataset with counterfactual images, VAUQ demonstrated a **+13.3%** improvement in self-evaluation AUROC compared to existing methods, effectively mitigating language-prior dominance.
- The framework reduced per-sample inference time by **94.6%** compared to multi-sampling-based baselines while delivering superior accuracy, making it highly efficient for practical deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Informative visual evidence, when properly utilized by an LVLM, should lead to a measurable reduction in its predictive uncertainty, which can be quantified as an Image-Information Score.
> - An LVLM's internal visual attention weights, particularly from mid to later layers, can effectively pinpoint task-relevant visual regions for unsupervised masking without requiring ground-truth annotations.
> - Combining a standard predictive entropy with a vision-aware uncertainty signal (IS_core) provides complementary insights, improving self-evaluation accuracy, especially in scenarios where visual grounding is critical over language priors.
