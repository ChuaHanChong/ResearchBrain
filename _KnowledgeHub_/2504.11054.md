# Zero-Shot Whole-Body Humanoid Control via Behavioral Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Meta AI, McGill University, and UCL present META MOTIVO, a Behavioral Foundation Model enabling zero-shot whole-body control for humanoid agents across diverse tasks. This model uses an online unsupervised reinforcement learning algorithm, FB-CPR, which learns from unlabeled motion capture data to generate natural, human-like movements. Human evaluations showed a preference for FB-CPR-generated behaviors in terms of naturalness, even over those from reward-optimized agents.

## Problem

- Applying foundation models to control and decision-making tasks for humanoid agents is challenging due to high-dimensional action spaces, unstable dynamics, and complex biomechanical constraints.
- Traditional reinforcement learning struggles with complex environments, while imitation learning typically lacks generalization capabilities for diverse, unseen tasks.
- Creating versatile humanoid agents that can perform a wide range of whole-body tasks without requiring extensive task-specific training or labeled demonstration data.

## Method

- Introduces **META MOTIVO**, a ==Behavioral Foundation Model (BFM)== for zero-shot control of humanoid agents, pre-trained on large datasets of behavior.
- Develops the **FB-CPR** (==Forward-Backward representations with Conditional Policy Regularization==) algorithm, an online ==unsupervised RL== method.
- FB-CPR simultaneously learns FB representations from environment interaction and regularizes the policy to match the distribution of states in unlabeled motion-capture data, conditioned on a latent variable to preserve behavioral diversity.

## Results

- META MOTIVO solved a variety of whole-body humanoid control tasks in a **zero-shot** manner, matching or exceeding task-specific methods like **TD3** and outperforming other unsupervised RL and model-based baselines.
- Demonstrated strong generalization, handling tasks not explicitly present in training data while producing natural, human-like motions.
- Human evaluators often preferred the naturalness and human-likeness of FB-CPR-generated behaviors over reward-optimized agents, even when the latter achieved higher reward scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forward-Backward (FB) representations allow for zero-shot policy evaluation and optimization by decomposing the successor measure into current state (forward) and target state/reward (backward) embeddings.
> - Conditional Policy Regularization, guided by unlabeled motion capture data and conditioned on a latent variable, ensures learned policies produce diverse, human-like states, preventing mode collapse.
> - Combining online unsupervised reinforcement learning with policy regularization from unlabeled motion data enables learning generalizable control policies without explicit task-specific rewards or demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Foundation models have revolutionized AI across language and vision domains, but their application to control and decision-making tasks remains limited. This paper introduces META MOTIVO, a Behavioral Foundation Model (BFM) that enables zero-shot control of humanoid agents across various whole-body tasks.

The researchers from Meta AI, McGill University, and UCL address a fundamental challenge in humanoid control: creating versatile agents that can perform a wide range of tasks without task-specific training. This is particularly difficult because humanoid control involves high-dimensional action spaces, unstable dynamics, and complex biomechanical constraints.

The paper presents a novel approach that bridges the gap between unsupervised reinforcement learning (which often struggles with complex environments) and imitation learning (which typically lacks generalization capabilities). By leveraging unlabeled motion capture data to guide the learning process, the researchers create a foundation model capable of generalizing to unseen tasks while maintaining human-like movements.

### 2. Behavioral Foundation Models

Behavioral Foundation Models represent an emerging paradigm that extends the concept of foundation models from language and vision to the domain of agent behavior. Just as Large Language Models (LLMs) are pre-trained on vast text corpora to generate coherent language, BFMs aim to be pre-trained on behavior data to generate coherent actions in dynamic environments.

The key characteristics of BFMs include:

1. Pre-training on large datasets of behavior
2. The ability to solve multiple downstream tasks without task-specific training
3. A common representation space for states, rewards, and policies

The META MOTIVO model exemplifies these principles by learning from motion capture data to develop general-purpose control capabilities for humanoid agents. Unlike traditional reinforcement learning approaches that require extensive training for each new task, BFMs aim to acquire a broad behavioral repertoire that can be leveraged for diverse applications.

### 3. The FB-CPR Algorithm

The core contribution of this paper is the FB-CPR (Forward-Backward representations with Conditional Policy Regularization) algorithm. This online unsupervised reinforcement learning algorithm combines two key components:

1. Forward-Backward (FB) representations to embed states, rewards, and policies in a common latent space
2. Conditional Policy Regularization to guide the learning process using unlabeled motion capture data

The FB-CPR algorithm operates by simultaneously:

- Learning FB representations through unsupervised interaction with the environment
- Regularizing the policy to match the distribution of states in the unlabeled dataset
- Conditioning this regularization on the latent variable to preserve the diversity of behaviors

This approach allows the algorithm to benefit from both the exploratory nature of reinforcement learning and the structure provided by human demonstrations, without requiring explicit task labels or rewards associated with the demonstration data.

### 4. Forward-Backward Representations

Forward-Backward representations form the backbone of the META MOTIVO model. These representations approximate the successor measure (the expected distribution of future states) using a low-rank decomposition:

The FB representation decomposes the successor measure into:
- Forward embeddings φ(s) that represent the current state
- Backward embeddings ψ(s',r) that represent target states and rewards

This decomposition enables zero-shot policy evaluation and optimization. For any given reward function, the algorithm can compute the value of a state by taking the inner product between the forward embedding of the current state and the backward embedding of the reward.

The FB representations are learned through a contrastive learning objective that encourages the inner product to be high for state-next state pairs that occur in the environment and low for random pairs.

### 5. Conditional Policy Regularization

The second key component of FB-CPR is Conditional Policy Regularization. This technique ensures that the policies learned by the agent produce states that match the distribution of states in the unlabeled dataset.

The regularization works by:

1. Training a discriminator network D(s,z) to distinguish between states from the agent's policy and states from the dataset, conditioned on a latent variable z
2. Optimizing the policy to fool the discriminator, thus encouraging the agent to visit states similar to those in the dataset
3. Conditioning the discrimination on the latent variable to preserve the diversity of behaviors

The conditioning on the latent variable is crucial, as it prevents the policy from collapsing to a single mode of behavior. Instead, different latent variables induce different behaviors, all of which are plausible given the dataset.

The full FB-CPR objective combines the unsupervised RL term with the conditional policy regularization term:

```
L = L_FB + λ L_CPR
```

where L_FB is the FB representation learning objective, L_CPR is the conditional policy regularization objective, and λ is a hyperparameter that controls the strength of the regularization.

### 6. Experimental Setup

The researchers evaluated META MOTIVO on a suite of whole-body humanoid control tasks using the SMPL humanoid model, a widely used parametric model of the human body with 23 joints and 72 degrees of freedom.

The tasks included:
- Tracking: Following reference motions from motion capture data
- Pose-reaching: Moving to target poses
- Reward optimization: Maximizing task-specific reward functions

The state space consisted of proprioceptive observations (joint positions, velocities, etc.), and the action space specified normalized PD targets for the joints. The motion dataset used for regularization was a subset of the AMASS dataset, containing approximately 11 hours of diverse human motions.

The FB-CPR algorithm was compared against several baselines:
- TD3: A state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithm trained specifically for each task
- APV: An unsupervised RL algorithm based on Forward-Backward representations without policy regularization
- DADS: A state-of-the-art unsupervised skill discovery algorithm
- Model-based baselines: Planning algorithms using learned dynamics models

### 7. Key Results

The experiments demonstrated several important findings:

1. META MOTIVO successfully solved a variety of whole-body control tasks in a zero-shot manner, without task-specific training.

2. FB-CPR achieved competitive performance with task-specific methods (TD3) while outperforming state-of-the-art unsupervised RL and model-based baselines.

3. The model demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, effectively handling tasks that were not explicitly represented in the training data.

4. Ablation studies validated the importance of:
 - Online policy regularization vs. offline imitation
 - The unsupervised RL term in the loss function
 - The policy conditioning of the discriminator
 - Network capacity and dataset size

5. The representations learned by FB-CPR captured meaningful features of human motion, allowing for efficient zero-shot adaptation to new tasks.

Unlike some existing approaches, FB-CPR does not perform as well on classic control problems like Bipedal Walker or AntMaze, suggesting that the solution is specialized to human motions and the structure of the humanoid control problem.

### 8. Human Evaluation

One of the most interesting aspects of the research was the human evaluation of the generated behaviors. The researchers conducted a study where human evaluators were asked to rate the naturalness and human-likeness of motions generated by different algorithms.

Remarkably, human evaluators often preferred the FB-CPR-generated behaviors over those generated by a reward-optimized TD3 agent, even when the TD3 agent achieved higher reward scores. This suggests that FB-CPR captures human biases and produces more natural-looking motions than algorithms focused purely on reward optimization.

The human evaluation highlights an important distinction between numeric performance metrics and perceived quality of motion. While reward functions can capture certain aspects of task performance, they often fail to fully encode the naturalness and human-likeness of movements that humans intuitively recognize.

### 9. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive results, META MOTIVO has several limitations that point to directions for future research:

1. Perception: The current model operates on proprioceptive state observations rather than visual inputs. Extending the approach to incorporate visual perception would be a significant advancement.

2. Dataset Coverage: The model's performance depends on the coverage of the motion dataset. Tasks that require behaviors not represented in the dataset remain challenging.

3. Compositional Reasoning: While the model can perform individual tasks well, composing multiple behaviors or following complex instructions remains an open challenge.

4. Language Alignment: Connecting natural language instructions to control policies would enhance the usability of the system.

The authors suggest several promising directions for future work, including:
- Integrating perception modules to enable visual control
- Leveraging video data to expand the range of observable behaviors
- Aligning language with policies to enable instruction-following
- Scaling the approach to even larger and more diverse datasets

### 10. Conclusion

The META MOTIVO model represents a significant step toward Behavioral Foundation Models for control and decision-making. By combining unsupervised reinforcement learning with regularization from unlabeled motion data, FB-CPR enables zero-shot control of humanoid agents across a variety of tasks.

The research demonstrates that foundation model principles can be effectively applied to the domain of control, potentially simplifying the creation of versatile and human-like agents for robotics, virtual avatars, and non-player characters in games and simulations.

As foundation models continue to transform AI research and applications, META MOTIVO points to a future where agents can be pre-trained on diverse behavioral data to develop general-purpose capabilities, much as language models are pre-trained on text to develop general-purpose language abilities. This approach could dramatically reduce the engineering effort required to create capable agents for specific applications, while also improving the naturalness and human-likeness of the resulting behaviors.
