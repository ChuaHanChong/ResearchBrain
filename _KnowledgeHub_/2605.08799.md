# ElasticFlow: One-Step Physics-Consistent Policy with Elastic Time Horizons for Language-Guided Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the University of Oulu introduced ElasticFlow, a one-step, physics-consistent policy that reformulates action generation as an average velocity field learning problem for language-guided robotic manipulation. This framework achieves an inference latency of 14ms (71Hz) and secured a 98.5% average success rate across LIBERO benchmarks, including a 19.0% improvement over MemoryVLA on complex long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- High inference latency in generative policies like diffusion models (dozens of NFEs) limits real-time responsiveness for robotic tasks, especially in dynamic environments.
- Existing acceleration methods for diffusion policies often compromise physical consistency and trajectory smoothness, leading to unrealistic or jittery robot actions.
- Traditional fixed-horizon policies suffer from "Spectral Bias," struggling to adapt to diverse temporal scales in robotic tasks, from fine-grained reactive control to long-range planning.

## Method

![[2605.08799-method.png]]
*The ElasticFlow framework utilizes a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone and an Elastic Time Horizon module to achieve one-step, physics-consistent action generation from multi-modal inputs.*

- **ElasticFlow** redefines action generation as an ==Average Velocity Field== learning problem, enabling direct, one-step mapping from a noise distribution to the target action without iterative denoising.
- The framework utilizes an ==ElasticFlow Identity== derived from the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, which implicitly includes a ==manifold curvature correction== term to ensure physics-consistent and smooth trajectories.
- It incorporates an ==Elastic Time Abstraction== mechanism with ==dual-parameter time encoding== (absolute flow time and time span) into the network architecture, allowing a single policy to dynamically adapt to varying task horizons and control granularities.

## Results

- **ElasticFlow** achieved an average success rate of **98.5%** on LIBERO benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art methods like HiF-VLA (**98.0%**) and Octo (**75.1%**), with significant gains on LIBERO-Long (**97.6%** vs. MemoryVLA **93.4%**).
- It demonstrated an inference latency of **14ms (71Hz)** on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, representing a **5x** improvement over Diffusion Policy and **14x** over OpenVLA, while also achieving superior trajectory smoothness (Jerk: **1.1×10⁻³** vs. CFM's **3.2×10⁻³**).
- In real-world experiments on a UFACTORY xArm6, the policy attained **91.4%** success in seen scenarios and **76.4%** in unseen, successfully performing dynamic interception of targets moving at approximately **10 cm/s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling action generation via an average velocity field, rather than an instantaneous one, allows for a single-step inference process, drastically reducing computational overhead.
> - The ElasticFlow Identity and its curvature correction term provide an inherent differential constraint that promotes the generation of physically compliant and smooth action sequences.
> - Dual-parameter time encoding effectively mitigates the "Spectral Bias" problem, allowing the policy to perceive and adapt to different control horizons (from local high-frequency to global low-frequency) within a unified model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI aims to develop generalist policies capable of mapping multi-modal observations and natural language instructions to continuous action spaces for robotic manipulation. Diffusion policies have emerged as a prominent paradigm in this area, demonstrating capabilities in multi-modal modeling and generating robust action sequences. However, a significant limitation of these methods is their high inference latency, primarily due to the iterative denoising process, which requires multiple network function evaluations (NFEs). This latency can hinder a robot's real-time responsiveness in dynamic environments, such as those involving moving objects.

Existing approaches to mitigate this speed bottleneck include acceleration techniques like Consistency Models and progressive distillation. While these methods can reduce inference time, they often involve complex training pipelines, may suffer from instability, or can compromise the physical consistency and smoothness of the generated trajectories. Similarly, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which often employ auto-regressive token prediction, face issues with quantization errors and latency, and may struggle with long-horizon tasks due to forgetting or increased architectural complexity when incorporating memory or chain-of-thought reasoning.

Flow Matching methods offer a more direct generation path compared to diffusion models by modeling continuous trajectories. However, many still rely on iterative Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) integration for inference. This work positions itself by addressing these limitations through a one-step, distillation-free, and physics-consistent policy framework that aims to provide both high efficiency and robust physical consistency, particularly for tasks with varying temporal horizons. It seeks to improve upon the speed of diffusion and VLA models while maintaining or enhancing the physical realism of generated actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the ElasticFlow framework are:

- **Mitigate High Inference Latency in Generative Policies**: Diffusion policies, despite their effectiveness in multi-modal action distribution modeling, necessitate dozens of network function evaluations (NFEs) during inference. This iterative denoising process results in high latency, typically restricting policy inference frequency to below 20Hz. Such low frequencies impede a robot's ability to react in real-time to dynamic changes in its environment, like tracking moving objects. The motivation is to develop a policy capable of single-step inference to achieve significantly higher control frequencies.

- **Ensure Physics-Consistency and Trajectory Smoothness**: Existing acceleration methods for diffusion policies often compromise physical consistency. Techniques like consistency models or progressive distillation may not adequately preserve geometric trajectory characteristics, leading to actions that lack physical realism or exhibit high-frequency jitter. Auto-regressive VLA models also face challenges in balancing high-frequency control with physical continuity due to discretized token prediction or low-frequency diffusion heads. A key objective is to embed physical consistency directly into the policy generation process, ensuring smoother and more stable robot movements.

- **Address Temporal Heterogeneity and Spectral Bias in Robotic Tasks**: Robotic tasks often involve varying temporal scales, from fine-grained, millisecond-level reactive adjustments to long-range, multi-second macroscopic planning. Traditional fixed-horizon methods in policy learning often suffer from "Spectral Bias," where a single network struggles to learn and represent signals of widely different frequencies simultaneously. This can lead to cumulative errors in long-horizon tasks or sluggish responses in short-horizon reactive tasks. The motivation is to design a mechanism that allows a single policy to adaptively perceive and manage these diverse control granularities, aligning semantic instructions with appropriate physical execution horizons.

- **Develop a Distillation-Free and Simplified Training Pipeline**: Many acceleration techniques, such as progressive distillation, involve complex teacher-student training setups that can be unstable or difficult to manage. Another motivation is to achieve efficient, one-step policy generation without requiring such multi-stage or complex distillation procedures, aiming for a more straightforward and stable training process.

In summary, ElasticFlow seeks to overcome the trade-off between inference speed and physical fidelity, and between local reactivity and global planning, in language-guided robotic manipulation by introducing a novel average velocity field formulation and an adaptive temporal encoding mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ElasticFlow proposes a physics-consistent one-step policy framework by reconstructing action generation as an average velocity field learning problem and incorporating an elastic time horizon mechanism.

**Problem Definition: From Local Tangent to Global Average Field**
The problem is defined as learning a policy $\pi_\theta(x|o, \ell)$ that transforms a prior noise distribution $p_0$ to a target action manifold $p_1$, conditioned on robot observations $o$ and natural language instructions $\ell$. The action $x \in R^{T_h \times D}$ represents an action chunk of length $T_h$ with dimension $D$.
Traditional diffusion models treat this as iterative denoising, while flow matching methods regress an instantaneous velocity field $v(z_\tau, \tau)$. The instantaneous velocity is a local tangential derivative, which necessitates multiple NFEs for numerical integration during inference to describe global trajectory geometry.
ElasticFlow introduces the **Average Velocity Field** $u(z_t, r, t)$, defined as the normalized displacement of the flow within an elastic time interval $[r, t]$:
$u(z_t, r, t) \triangleq \frac{1}{t-r} \int_{r}^{t} v(z_\tau, \tau) d\tau$
This formulation implies that if the average velocity from the noise domain ($r=0$) to the data domain ($t=1$) can be predicted, the target action $\hat{x}$ can be directly recovered via a single-step mapping $\hat{x} = z_1 - u(z_1, 0, 1)$, eliminating iterative solving.

**Policy Learning based on ElasticFlow Identity**
To learn the average velocity field, the **ElasticFlow Identity** is derived from the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:
$u(z_t, r, t) = v(z_t, t) - (t - r) \frac{d}{dt} u(z_t, r, t)$
This identity establishes an intrinsic differential constraint between average velocity $u$ and instantaneous velocity $v$. In the context of Conditional Flow Matching, the ground truth instantaneous velocity is $v(z_t, t) = x_{target} - x_{noise}$.
The term $(t - r) \frac{d}{dt} u$ is interpreted as a **Manifold Curvature Correction Term**. It is proposed that as the time span $t-r$ increases, this term corrects trajectory deviations on the non-linear manifold, thereby suppressing high-frequency jitter and improving control smoothness by forcing the network to learn global geometric features.

**Elastic Time Abstraction and Network Architecture**
To manage the temporal heterogeneity of robotic tasks, a policy network $u_\theta(z_t, r, t, o, \ell)$ supporting **Elastic Time Abstraction** is designed.
- **Dual-Parameter Time Encoding**: The network receives both absolute flow time $t$ and the time span $\Delta t = t-r$. These are encoded using Fourier Feature Mapping and fused via an MLP: $Emb(r, t) = MLP([FF(t), FF(t - r)])$. This dual-parameter encoding is intended to mitigate Spectral Bias and provide explicit perception of the "Control Horizon." A small $\Delta t$ enables focus on local, high-frequency adjustments, while a large $\Delta t$ facilitates long-range trajectory planning, allowing a single model to adapt to different control requirements under unified weights.
- **Vision-Language Condition Injection**: Visual observations $o$ are processed by a SigLIP encoder, and language instructions $\ell$ by a T5 text encoder. These features are fused with the action flow generation process through Cross-Attention layers within a DiT (Diffusion Transformer) based backbone architecture. Time embeddings are injected via AdaLN modulation.

**Classifier-Free Guided One-Step Inference and Training**
- **Training Objective**: The network is trained to minimize the difference between its prediction and a target derived from the ElasticFlow Identity. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is used by occasionally dropping the language condition during training. The loss function $L(\theta)$ involves comparing $u_\theta$ with a target $T_{target}$ which includes $v(z_t, t)$ and the curvature correction term.
 $T_{target} = v(z_t, t) - (t - r) (\nabla_z u_\theta \cdot v(z_t, t) + \partial_t u_\theta)$
 The Jacobian-Vector Product (JVP) for $\nabla_z u_\theta \cdot v(z_t, t)$ is computed efficiently using Forward-mode Automatic Differentiation, avoiding the computational cost of direct Jacobian calculation.
- **Efficient One-Step Inference (1-NFE Inference)**: During inference, given initial Gaussian noise $z_1 \sim N(0, I)$, the parameters $r=0$ and $t=1$ are set. A single forward pass through the network directly yields the denoised action trajectory $\hat{x}$:
 $\hat{x} = z_1 - (u_\theta(\cdot, \emptyset) + w \cdot (u_\theta(\cdot, \ell) - u_\theta(\cdot, \emptyset)))$
 Here, $w$ is the guidance scale for CFG. This one-step process significantly reduces inference latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ElasticFlow was evaluated on three robot manipulation benchmarks: LIBERO, CALVIN, and RoboTwin, along with real-world robot experiments.

**Overall Performance on LIBERO Benchmark:**
- ElasticFlow achieved an average success rate of 98.5% across the LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long suites. This result surpassed previous state-of-the-art methods such as HiF-VLA (98.0%), $\pi_0$ (94.2%), and Octo (75.1%).
- On LIBERO-Long, which involves long-horizon sequential task chains, ElasticFlow attained a success rate of 97.6% (multi-view) and 95.6% (third-person), outperforming baselines like OpenVLA (54.0%) and MemoryVLA (93.4%). The method demonstrated robust performance in complex tasks, such as "Put both pots on stove," with a 19.0% improvement over MemoryVLA.

**Continuous Instruction Following on CALVIN:**
- ElasticFlow set a new benchmark for continuous instruction following, achieving an average chain length of 4.37 in multi-view settings and 4.15 in single-view settings on the CALVIN ABC-D split.
- The policy maintained high success rates even for the 4th and 5th instructions (83.6% and 72.7%, respectively, in multi-view), indicating smooth transitions between semantic goals without significant loss of physical control stability.

**Horizon Dependency Analysis on RoboTwin:**
- Categorized by task horizon length (Short, Medium, Long/Extra Long), ElasticFlow demonstrated stability across varying horizons. In the Long & Extra Long category, ElasticFlow achieved 71.1%, outperforming SimpleVLA-RL (69.0%), $\pi_0$ (43.3%), and RDT (27.8%).
- This suggests that the explicit encoding of $\Delta t$ enables the network to balance reactive control in short tasks with structural coherence in long-duration operations. Qualitative analysis on RoboCasa further supported the generation of smooth, stable, and temporally consistent action sequences for complex kitchen tasks.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- ElasticFlow was deployed on a UFACTORY xArm6 robotic arm. Across seven diverse tasks, the policy achieved an average success rate of 91.4% in seen scenarios and 76.4% in unseen (out-of-distribution) scenarios, which included spatial generalization, instance generalization, and dynamic disturbances.
- For the "Dynamic Interception" task, the system effectively intercepted rolling targets at speeds up to approximately 10 cm/s, demonstrating robust Sim-to-Real transfer.

**Ablation Studies and Mechanism Analysis:**
- **Effectiveness of Elastic Time Horizon:** Removing $\Delta t$ from the network input ("w/o Horizon Input") resulted in an 18.4% drop in success rate for long-range tasks. Fixed Horizon variants (e.g., $\Delta t = 10$ or $\Delta t = 50$) also showed reduced performance when mismatching the task horizon. Forcing a small $\Delta t$ on long-horizon tasks yielded 45.3% success, while forcing a large $\Delta t$ on short-horizon tasks resulted in 55.7% success, confirming the utility of dynamic horizon adaptation.
- **Comparison of Training Objectives (MeanFlow vs. Flow Matching):** A baseline model trained with standard Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) loss achieved only 12.4% success rate with 1-NFE inference. Even with 10-step iteration, CFM's trajectory smoothness (Jerk: $3.2 \times 10^{-3}$) was inferior to ElasticFlow's single-step result (Jerk: $1.1 \times 10^{-3}$). This indicates that the curvature correction term implicit in the MeanFlow Identity contributes to smoother, physically compliant trajectories.
- **CFG Guidance Weight Sensitivity:** Performance was robust within the range of $w \in [1.5, 2.5]$, peaking at 71.1% success rate with $w=2.0$ on RoboTwin long-horizon tasks. Performance degraded with insufficient guidance ($w=1.0$, 32.5%) or excessive guidance ($w>3.0$).

**Inference Latency and Real-Time Analysis:**
- ElasticFlow achieved an inference latency of 14ms per step (approximately 71Hz) on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with a Batch Size of 1.
- This represents a 5x improvement over Diffusion Policy (120ms, $\sim$8Hz) and a 14x improvement over auto-regressive OpenVLA (200ms, $\sim$5Hz), and outperformed Consistency Policy (28ms, $\sim$35Hz) and $\pi_0$ (85ms, $\sim$12Hz). This efficiency positions ElasticFlow for real-time deployment in dynamic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ElasticFlow framework introduces a methodology for language-guided manipulation that addresses critical limitations in existing generative policies, particularly concerning real-time performance and physical consistency. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Enhanced Real-Time Responsiveness**: By enabling 1-NFE (one-network function evaluation) inference, ElasticFlow significantly reduces policy inference latency to approximately 14ms, achieving a control frequency of around 71Hz. This high frequency allows robots to respond more rapidly and effectively to dynamic changes in their environment, which is crucial for tasks like intercepting moving objects or performing high-speed manipulation. This capability is a step towards broader deployment of generative policies in applications requiring low-latency control.

- **Improved Physical Consistency and Trajectory Smoothness**: The reconstruction of Mean Field Theory to directly model the average velocity field, coupled with the ElasticFlow Identity and its implicit curvature correction term, promotes the generation of physically consistent and smooth action trajectories. This helps to mitigate issues such as high-frequency jitter, which can lead to instability, reduced precision, or even damage in physical robotic systems. The quantitative evaluation using the Trajectory Jerk metric supports this claim, indicating smoother outputs than alternative flow matching methods.

- **Adaptive Temporal Control for Diverse Task Horizons**: The Elastic Time Horizons mechanism, through its dual-parameter time encoding, enables a single policy network to adaptively handle tasks requiring different control granularities. This addresses the "Spectral Bias" challenge, allowing the model to perform fine-grained reactive control for short-horizon tasks and structured, long-range planning for complex, multi-stage operations within a unified weight space. This adaptability could simplify the development of generalist robot policies capable of addressing a wider range of manipulation complexities.

- **Simplified and Stable Training**: The distillation-free nature of ElasticFlow's one-step generation process avoids the complexities and potential instabilities associated with multi-stage training pipelines common in distillation-based acceleration methods. This could lead to more robust and easier-to-implement policy learning systems.

- **Competitive Performance on Benchmarks**: The consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art methods on challenging benchmarks such as LIBERO-Long, CALVIN, and RoboTwin suggests ElasticFlow's robustness and generalization capabilities across various manipulation skills and task complexities. Its strong performance on long-horizon tasks, in particular, highlights the effectiveness of its temporal modeling.

**Potential Impact**:
The work offers a paradigm for real-time embodied generative modeling. Its efficiency and robustness could facilitate the development of more capable and reliable robot systems for industrial automation, domestic assistance, and hazardous environment operations. Future directions suggest scaling ElasticFlow with large-scale pre-training to contribute to general-purpose Robot Foundation Models, integrating it with Model Predictive Control (MPC) for enhanced closed-loop control and online correction, and deepening its fusion with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for more sophisticated semantic alignment and execution of complex instructions. The reduced computational energy consumption due to 1-NFE inference also aligns with principles of Green AI, contributing to more sustainable robotic solutions.
