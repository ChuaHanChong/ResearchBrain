# ElasticFlow: One-Step Physics-Consistent Policy with Elastic Time Horizons for Language-Guided Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the University of Oulu introduced ElasticFlow, a one-step, physics-consistent policy that reformulates action generation as an average velocity field learning problem for language-guided robotic manipulation. This framework achieves an inference latency of 14ms (71Hz) and secured a 98.5% average success rate across LIBERO benchmarks, including a 19.0% improvement over MemoryVLA on complex long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- High inference latency in generative policies like diffusion models (dozens of NFEs) limits real-time responsiveness for robotic tasks, especially in dynamic environments.
- Existing acceleration methods for diffusion policies often compromise physical consistency and trajectory smoothness, leading to unrealistic or jittery robot actions.
- Traditional fixed-horizon policies suffer from "Spectral Bias," struggling to adapt to diverse temporal scales in robotic tasks, from fine-grained reactive control to long-range planning.

## Method

![[2605.08799-method.png]]
*The ElasticFlow framework utilizes a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone and an Elastic Time Horizon module to achieve one-step, physics-consistent action generation from multi-modal inputs.*

- **ElasticFlow** redefines action generation as an ==Average Velocity Field== learning problem, enabling direct, one-step mapping from a noise distribution to the target action without iterative denoising.
- The framework utilizes an ==ElasticFlow Identity== derived from the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, which implicitly includes a ==manifold curvature correction== term to ensure physics-consistent and smooth trajectories.
- It incorporates an ==Elastic Time Abstraction== mechanism with ==dual-parameter time encoding== (absolute flow time and time span) into the network architecture, allowing a single policy to dynamically adapt to varying task horizons and control granularities.

## Results

- **ElasticFlow** achieved an average success rate of **98.5%** on LIBERO benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art methods like HiF-VLA (**98.0%**) and Octo (**75.1%**), with significant gains on LIBERO-Long (**97.6%** vs. MemoryVLA **93.4%**).
- It demonstrated an inference latency of **14ms (71Hz)** on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, representing a **5x** improvement over Diffusion Policy and **14x** over OpenVLA, while also achieving superior trajectory smoothness (Jerk: **1.1×10⁻³** vs. CFM's **3.2×10⁻³**).
- In real-world experiments on a UFACTORY xArm6, the policy attained **91.4%** success in seen scenarios and **76.4%** in unseen, successfully performing dynamic interception of targets moving at approximately **10 cm/s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling action generation via an average velocity field, rather than an instantaneous one, allows for a single-step inference process, drastically reducing computational overhead.
> - The ElasticFlow Identity and its curvature correction term provide an inherent differential constraint that promotes the generation of physically compliant and smooth action sequences.
> - Dual-parameter time encoding effectively mitigates the "Spectral Bias" problem, allowing the policy to perceive and adapt to different control horizons (from local high-frequency to global low-frequency) within a unified model.
