# Z3D: Zero-Shot 3D Visual Grounding from Images

> [!summary] Summary
> Z3D introduces a universal zero-shot 3D visual grounding pipeline that localizes objects in 3D scenes from natural language queries using multi-view images. It establishes new state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot 3DVG, including image-only scenarios, by integrating high-quality 3D instance segmentation and advanced Vision-Language Model (VLM) reasoning, achieving significant improvements on ScanRefer and Nr3D benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing 3D visual grounding (3DVG) methods often rely on extensive 3D annotations or textual supervision, limiting generalization and increasing data labeling burden.
- Prior zero-shot 3DVG methods suffered from insufficient quality of 3D object proposals and did not fully leverage advanced VLM reasoning capabilities.
- 3DVG from images, a more practical scenario, was largely limited to single-view inputs or required explicit 3D geometry/camera pose supervision, hindering comprehensive scene-level understanding.

## Method

- Z3D implements a ==modular pipeline for zero-shot 3DVG== from multi-view images, optionally integrating camera poses and depth maps, without requiring geometric supervision or object priors.
- The pipeline combines a ==two-stage view selection== (CLIP and VLM), SAM3-Agent for VLM-guided zero-shot 2D object segmentation, and ==MaskClustering== for class-agnostic 3D proposals.
- For image-only scenarios, Z3D integrates ==DUSt3R for zero-shot 3D reconstruction==, generating dense depth maps and point clouds from multi-view images to enable subsequent grounding.

## Results

- Z3D achieved an absolute improvement of **+38.7** Acc@0.5 over OpenScene on ScanRefer and **56.8%** top-1 accuracy on Nr3D, outperforming prior zero-shot methods like SPAZER (**56.0%**) and VLM-Grounder (**48.0%**) with depths.
- In the images-only scenario, Z3D with DUSt3R achieved **12.9** Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, surpassing a DUSt3R+SeeGround baseline (**8.2** Acc@0.5) and approaching fully supervised VG LLM (**14.9** Acc@0.5).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of VLM view selection and multi-view aggregation, and showed that reconstruction quality (DUSt3R vs. DROID-SLAM) significantly impacts image-only 3DVG performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining advanced VLM reasoning for view selection and prompt-guided 2D segmentation with high-quality zero-shot 3D instance segmentation significantly improves zero-shot 3DVG performance.
> - Robust 3D reconstruction from multi-view images, even without explicit depth or pose supervision, can effectively enable zero-shot 3DVG in challenging image-only settings.
> - A two-stage view selection approach (initial lightweight filtering followed by VLM refinement) efficiently reduces computational load while maintaining accuracy in VLM-based pipelines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

3D visual grounding (3DVG) is a field focused on localizing objects in a 3D environment based on natural language descriptions. This capability is considered fundamental for applications in embodied artificial intelligence, robotics, and human-scene interaction, requiring joint reasoning over language, visual appearance, and spatial structure.

Historically, 3DVG from point clouds has been a prevalent area of study. Early methods, such as Vil3DRel and MiKASA, and subsequent developments like SceneVerse and LIBA, typically relied on full supervision, meaning both 3D bounding boxes and corresponding texts were used during model training. A subsequent category of methods, including ZSVG3D and SeeGround, aimed to reduce the training burden by eliminating the need for textual supervision, though they still exploited annotated 3D bounding boxes. The most challenging scenario, the training-free or "zero-shot" approach, has been explored by LLM-Grounder and VLM-Grounder. However, these zero-shot methods have faced limitations, including reliance on proprietary Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that incorporate non-generative language models like BERT and CLIP in critical pipeline stages, which can constrain performance. Another identified limitation in these approaches is the quality of candidate object proposals.

Concurrently, 3DVG from images represents a more challenging but practically relevant scenario, as it removes the requirement for explicit 3D representations such as point clouds or pre-built scene reconstructions. Most existing image-based 3DVG methods are fully supervised, and many still necessitate ground truth camera poses, either during training (e.g., VG LLM) or testing (e.g., SPAR). Zero-shot 3DVG from images has been explored by modern VLMs like Qwen3-VL and Seed1.5-VL, but these are typically limited to single-view images, hindering scene-level understanding.

The present work, Z3D, addresses the identified shortcomings in the zero-shot 3DVG landscape. It aims to overcome the performance limitations of prior zero-shot methods by integrating advanced VLM reasoning and high-quality 3D instance segmentation. Furthermore, it extends the applicability of zero-shot 3DVG to multi-view images alone, without requiring pre-existing 3D geometry or camera pose supervision, which represents a significant gap in current research. The work specifically contributes by improving existing VLM-based 3DVG pipelines for point cloud inputs and by being among the first to tackle zero-shot 3DVG comprehensively from images only.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a universal pipeline for zero-shot 3D visual grounding (3DVG) that operates effectively from multi-view images, without requiring geometric supervision or object priors during training or inference.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and limitations observed in the existing 3DVG landscape:

1. **Reducing Supervision Requirements:** Traditional 3DVG methods often rely on extensive 3D annotations (e.g., 3D bounding boxes) or textual supervision for training. The authors aim to eliminate these dependencies entirely, moving towards a truly training-free, "zero-shot" paradigm. This reduces the burden of data annotation and enhances model generalization to unseen scenes and objects.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Prior Zero-Shot Methods:** Previous zero-shot approaches, such as LLM-Grounder and VLM-Grounder, exhibited performance limitations. The authors identify two key bottlenecks:
 - **Insufficient Quality of 3D Object Proposals:** The initial candidates for 3D objects generated by these methods were often of inadequate quality, forming a weak foundation for subsequent reasoning.
 - **Underutilization of Modern VLM Capabilities:** Prior methods, despite employing VLMs, reportedly did not fully leverage their advanced reasoning capabilities, sometimes relying on less powerful, non-generative language models in critical parts of their pipelines.

3. **Expanding Input Modality Flexibility:** Most advanced 3DVG methods, including many zero-shot ones, assume access to explicit 3D representations like point clouds or depth maps. This restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios where only multi-view images might be available. A core motivation is to enable 3DVG directly from multi-view images, while also allowing for the optional incorporation of camera poses and depth maps when available, creating a more versatile and practical system.

4. **Enabling Scene-Level Understanding from Images:** Existing zero-shot 3DVG from images is largely limited to single-view inputs, which is insufficient for comprehensive scene-level understanding. The authors aim to address this by developing a method capable of processing multi-view images to achieve a more complete spatial understanding.

In summary, the motivation is to advance the state of zero-shot 3DVG by developing a robust, adaptable pipeline (Z3D) that improves upon prior methods' performance by generating higher-quality 3D proposals and leveraging full VLM reasoning, while critically extending its operational scope to multi-view images alone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Z3D introduces a universal grounding pipeline designed to localize target objects in a 3D scene from natural language queries. The pipeline is structured to flexibly operate on multi-view images, with optional integration of camera poses and depth maps. The methodology addresses challenges in both scenarios: 3DVG with explicit depth/point cloud information and 3DVG from images only.

#### 3.1. 3DVG With Depth (or Point Clouds)

This scenario assumes the availability of depth maps or pre-existing point clouds. The Z3D workflow in this setting decomposes into three main stages:

1. **View Selection:**
 - **Problem:** Processing all images in a multi-view scene with a computationally intensive VLM is inefficient.
 - **Approach:** Z3D employs a two-stage strategy. First, an initial pre-selection of views is performed using CLIP, identifying the six frames most similar to the given language query based on their embeddings. Subsequently, a more powerful VLM (Qwen-VL in the provided figure) is used to select the best three views from these six pre-selected candidates. This approach reduces the VLM computational load while maintaining accuracy by leveraging a lightweight model for initial filtering.

2. **2D Object Segmentation:**
 - **Problem:** Prior methods relying on combinations like Grounding DINO and SAM are tailored for open-vocabulary segmentation with explicit object descriptions, which may perform suboptimally with the less specific prompts often found in 3DVG.
 - **Approach:** Z3D utilizes SAM3-Agent for zero-shot, high-quality object segmentation. SAM3-Agent is guided by VLM reasoning to iteratively generate and refine segmentation prompts. This allows for precise instance extraction from the selected 2D views without requiring geometric supervision or object priors.

3. **2D-to-3D Lifting and Proposal Selection:**
 - **Problem:** Naively lifting 2D object masks directly to 3D and enclosing them in a bounding box can be prone to noise and outliers, impacting the estimated 3D object size and shape.
 - **Approach:** Z3D first generates class-agnostic 3D object proposals independently. This is achieved by leveraging MaskClustering, a zero-shot 3D instance segmentation method that processes input point clouds to produce 3D object masks, which are then converted into 3D bounding boxes.
 - The 2D segmentation masks obtained from the top-3 selected views are then lifted to 3D space. These lifted 3D masks are matched against the MaskClustering 3D proposals. A voting scheme is applied: each lifted 3D mask "votes" for the MaskClustering proposal with which it has the highest 3D Intersection over Union (IoU). The final 3D bounding box prediction is the most voted candidate. In cases of ambiguity (a tie in votes), the proposal associated with the mask from the frame having the highest CLIP and VLM relevance scores is selected.

#### 3.2. 3DVG From Images (Without Depth or Point Clouds)

When explicit depth maps or point clouds are not available, Z3D incorporates a 3D reconstruction step to bridge the gap between 2D visual inputs and 3D geometry.

1. **3D Reconstruction:**
 - **Approach:** The method employs DUSt3R (Dense Universal Stereo 3D Reconstruction), a 3D reconstruction method capable of handling omnimodal inputs (images, or images + camera poses).
 - **Process:** Given multi-view images, DUSt3R estimates dense depth maps and infers camera poses if they are not provided. These estimated depths and poses are then used to fuse the depth information into a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) volume. A point cloud is subsequently extracted from this TSDF volume using the marching cubes algorithm.
 - **Zero-Shot Nature:** The use of DUSt3R is highlighted as maintaining the purely zero-shot nature of the pipeline, as DUSt3R was not trained on datasets like ScanNet, unlike some other recent reconstruction methods.

Once a point cloud is reconstructed, the pipeline reverts to the "3DVG With Depth" methodology described above, using the generated point cloud as input for MaskClustering and subsequent steps.

This modular and flexible design allows Z3D to adapt to varying input modalities while systematically addressing the identified bottlenecks in zero-shot 3DVG.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Z3D approach was evaluated on two standard 3D visual grounding benchmarks: ScanRefer and Nr3D. ScanRefer assesses the ability to localize a target object via its 3D bounding box from scene point clouds and language queries, using Acc@0.25 and Acc@0.5 (3D IoU thresholds) as metrics. Nr3D focuses on selecting the most relevant candidate object from ground-truth 3D bounding boxes using top-1 accuracy.

#### 4.1. 3DVG with Depths (or Point Clouds)

- **Zero-Shot Setting (No Bounding Box or Text Supervision):** Z3D demonstrated substantial performance improvements over prior purely zero-shot methods. On the ScanRefer benchmark, Z3D achieved an absolute improvement of +38.7 Acc@0.5 compared to OpenScene. In an evaluation on a 250-scene subset of ScanRefer, Z3D achieved 51.6 Acc@0.5, outperforming VLM-Grounder (32.8 Acc@0.5) by 18.8 percentage points.
- **Zero-Shot (Inference-Time 3D Bounding Boxes available, no text supervision):** On the Nr3D benchmark, Z3D achieved state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy among zero-shot methods in this setting. For instance, with Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Z3D achieved an overall accuracy of 56.8%, surpassing SPAZER (56.0%) which used the same VLM, and also outperforming VLM-Grounder (48.0%) which utilized GPT-4o. This indicates the effectiveness of Z3D's pipeline components beyond just proposal quality.
- **With Pre-trained 3D Bounding Box Proposals (e.g., Mask3D):** Even when relying on Mask3D, a method trained with ground-truth 3D bounding boxes, Z3D showed improvements. On ScanRefer, Z3D achieved 52.7 Acc@0.5, outperforming SeeGround (39.4 Acc@0.5) and CSVG (39.8 Acc@0.5). On the 250-scene subset of ScanRefer, Z3D with Mask3D achieved 54.4 Acc@0.5, outperforming SeqVLM (49.6%) and SPAZER (48.8%).

#### 4.2. 3DVG from Images

- **Images + Camera Poses:** Z3D established a new state of the art in the posed-images setting. On ScanRefer, Z3D with DUSt3R reconstruction achieved 24.8 Acc@0.5, surpassing the previous fully supervised state-of-the-art method, SPAR (12.4 Acc@0.5). A baseline combining DUSt3R with SeeGround (a competitive depth-aware method) yielded 16.2 Acc@0.5, which Z3D also significantly outperformed.
- **Images Only (no camera poses):** In the most challenging images-only scenario, Z3D with DUSt3R reconstruction achieved 12.9 Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer. This performance exceeded a DUSt3R → SeeGround baseline (8.2 Acc@0.5) and was comparable to the fully supervised VG LLM (14.9 Acc@0.5), despite Z3D operating in a zero-shot manner.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study quantified the contribution of Z3D's components on ScanRefer (with images, camera poses, and depths):
- MaskClustering alone achieved 27.6 Acc@0.5.
- Adding CLIP-based view selection (top-1 view) and SAM3-Agent increased performance to 42.8 Acc@0.5.
- Incorporating VLM view selection (from 6 CLIP-selected views to 1 VLM-selected view) further improved accuracy to 44.8 Acc@0.5.
- Aggregating predictions across the top-3 VLM-selected views yielded the highest performance, reaching 46.0 Acc@0.5.

Additional ablations indicated:
- **VLM Size:** Larger VLM models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking) led to higher overall accuracy on Nr3D compared to smaller models (e.g., 30B or 8B).
- **Number of Images:** Performance on image-based scenarios generally improved with an increased number of input images, correlating with reconstruction quality.
- **Reconstruction Method:** Replacing DUSt3R with DROID-SLAM for 3D reconstruction resulted in a significant drop in scores (e.g., from 12.7 to 4.8 Acc@0.5 in image-only ScanRefer), highlighting the importance of high-quality underlying 3D reconstruction.

#### 4.4. Inference Time

The inference time analysis indicated that MaskClustering constituted the most time-consuming part of the Z3D pipeline (56.3 s out of 61.0 s total), while other components like CLIP view selection, VLM view selection, SAM3-Agent, and multi-view aggregation were comparatively faster. The overall inference time for Z3D was reported to be on par with the zero-shot baseline VLM-Grounder (50.3 s).

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples on ScanRefer demonstrated how the incorporation of additional inputs (from images-only to images+poses+depths) improved prediction quality. On Nr3D, predictions showed selection of the correct ground-truth bounding box. Failure cases were primarily observed in scenarios with multiple similar objects, where the model sometimes struggled to differentiate the target object (e.g., middle monitor in a row, specific pillow on a bed).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Z3D, a zero-shot 3D visual grounding (3DVG) pipeline that operates from multi-view images. Its contributions have several implications for the field:

1. **Advancement in Zero-Shot 3DVG:** Z3D addresses key limitations of prior zero-shot methods by integrating a state-of-the-art zero-shot 3D instance segmentation method for generating high-quality 3D bounding box proposals and by employing advanced reasoning capabilities of modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) via prompt-based segmentation. This approach yields substantial performance improvements on established benchmarks, establishing new state-of-the-art results among zero-shot methods.

2. **Reduced Supervision Requirements:** By operating in a zero-shot setting, Z3D eliminates the need for explicit 3D geometric supervision or object priors. This reduces the significant data labeling burden associated with traditional 3DVG methods, making the development and deployment of 3DVG systems more efficient.

3. **Enhanced Real-World Applicability:** A notable aspect of Z3D is its ability to perform 3DVG from multi-view images alone, without requiring pre-existing 3D representations like point clouds or depth maps. This broadens the applicability of 3DVG to real-world scenarios where only visual input from cameras is readily available, such as in mobile robotics, autonomous navigation, and embodied AI systems. The flexibility to optionally incorporate camera poses and depth maps further enhances its versatility.

4. **Modular and Flexible Pipeline Design:** The Z3D pipeline is designed to be universal and modular, accommodating various input modalities. The integration of 3D reconstruction methods like DUSt3R for image-only scenarios allows the pipeline to generate necessary 3D information on demand, maintaining its zero-shot characteristic.

5. **Benchmarking for Image-Based 3DVG:** By establishing strong baselines and achieving state-of-the-art performance in the images-only and posed-images settings, Z3D provides a new reference point for future research in image-based and supervision-free 3D visual grounding. The work also highlights the critical role of robust 3D reconstruction quality for image-only 3DVG performance.

The development of Z3D contributes to the progression towards more practical and scalable 3D scene understanding systems, particularly by lowering the barrier for entry through reduced supervision requirements and increased flexibility in input modalities.
