# FutureNav: Unified World-Action Modeling for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> FutureNav introduces a unified vision-language model framework that explicitly integrates world and action modeling for continuous vision-and-language navigation. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks, improving key metrics while demonstrating robust zero-shot generalization to real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based navigation methods primarily map observations to actions, often lacking explicit modeling of world-state representation, action-induced state transitions, or future-state prediction, which can hinder long-horizon spatial reasoning.
- Dedicated 'world model' navigation systems frequently treat policy learning and world modeling as separate components, preventing deep coupling between action selection and the understanding of environmental changes.
- Many current world-modeling modules remain active during inference, introducing additional computational overhead for future-state imagination or planning, which can limit practical application.

## Method

- **FutureNav** is a unified ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== framework that simultaneously models both the world state and agent actions for continuous navigation, augmented by a ==frozen spatial encoder (VGGT)==.
- The framework is trained using four complementary objectives: ==action policy==, ==forward dynamics==, ==inverse dynamics==, and ==future generation==, all operating on the VLM's hidden states to provide comprehensive agent-environment understanding.
- During inference, only the ==action policy branch== is utilized for direct action decoding, ensuring that the auxiliary world-modeling components enrich representations during training without incurring additional computational cost at deployment.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE, improving Navigation Error by **11.3%**, Success Rate (SR) by **8.1%**, and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) by **7.9%** over JanusVLN.
- Demonstrated superior performance on RxR-CE, reducing Navigation Error by **29.7%** and improving SR, SPL, and normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW) by **13.7%**, **15.4%**, and **11.3%** respectively, compared to JanusVLN.
- Exhibited robust zero-shot generalization by successfully navigating real indoor and outdoor environments based on natural language instructions, indicating strong transferability of learned world-action representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit world-action modeling through complementary training objectives (forward/inverse dynamics, future generation) significantly enhances a VLM's ability to learn robust spatial reasoning and action prediction beyond direct observation-to-action mapping.
> - Fusing spatial-aware features from a frozen encoder (VGGT) into the VLM's visual representation provides superior world-state modeling compared to other latent representations, improving prediction accuracy for future environmental states.
> - The proposed unified approach allows auxiliary world-modeling components to be deactivated post-training, demonstrating that the benefits of explicit world understanding can be transferred to the action policy without any inference-time overhead, improving efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-language navigation (VLN) requires an embodied agent to interpret natural language instructions and navigate to specified locations within 3D environments, often continuously. Early VLN systems for continuous environments (VLN-CE) relied on explicit navigation structures such as waypoint prediction, topological planning, semantic maps, or learned spatial memories. These methods highlighted the necessity for an agent to comprehend its surrounding world state and anticipate how that state changes in response to its actions.

More recently, the field has seen a shift towards large vision-language models (VLMs) and vision-language-action foundation models for VLN. Approaches like NaVid, Uni-NaVid, NaVILA, StreamVLN, and JanusVLN adapt pretrained multimodal backbones to directly predict navigation actions from language instructions and visual histories. While these VLM-based methods offer a scalable action-decoding interface, they primarily frame navigation as a direct observation-to-action mapping. Consequently, they often lack explicit supervision for world-state representation, modeling action-induced state transitions, or predicting future states prior to action selection. This limitation can potentially hinder long-horizon spatial reasoning and the robustness of navigational decisions.

In parallel, research on "world models" for embodied navigation has explored predicting future observations, latent visual foresight, or world-action modeling. Examples include Navigation World Models (NWM), WorldVLN, WAM-Nav, NavForesee, AstraNav-World, and NavMorph. However, these world-action modeling systems often treat policy learning, action-conditioned dynamics, and future-state generation as separate components or model only a subset of these interactions. This fragmentation means they do not fully integrate the decision of "what action to take" with the understanding of "how the world state changes" and "what future state that action might induce." Furthermore, many existing world-modeling modules remain active during inference, introducing additional computational overhead for future-state imagination or planning.

FutureNav aims to bridge the gap between these two research lines. It proposes a unified VLM-based framework that integrates comprehensive world-action modeling into a single navigation policy. By doing so, it intends to enhance explicit world understanding and action prediction within the efficient VLM paradigm, addressing the limitations of both direct action generation models and fragmented world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for FutureNav stems from observed limitations in current approaches to Vision-and-Language Navigation in continuous environments (VLN-CE). The task requires an agent to ground language instructions in egocentric visual observations, maintain spatial understanding over extended action sequences, and execute precise low-level actions without accumulating errors.

Existing VLM-based VLN methods, despite their advancements in scaling multimodal models, often primarily learn navigation as a direct mapping from observations to actions. This direct action generation approach frequently overlooks the explicit modeling of:
1. **World-state representation**: The internal understanding of the agent's surroundings and its current state within that environment.
2. **Action-induced state transition modeling**: How specific actions alter the world state and the agent's perception of it.
3. **Future-state prediction**: Anticipating what the environment will look like after an action is executed.
This deficiency in explicit world modeling can lead to reduced robustness in long-horizon spatial reasoning and make the agent more susceptible to errors in complex or dynamic environments.

Concurrently, dedicated world-model navigation systems, while focusing on predicting environmental evolution, often implement world modeling and policy learning as distinct or only partially integrated components. This separation means that the decision-making process ("what action to take") is not deeply coupled with the understanding of "how the world state changes" and "what future state the action may induce." Moreover, a significant practical challenge with many world models is their computational cost during inference, as their predictive modules typically remain active, requiring resources for future imagination or planning.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of the FutureNav research are:
- To develop a unified VLM-based framework that simultaneously models both the world and actions for continuous VLN.
- To explicitly supervise the learning of world-state representations, action-induced state transitions, and future spatial states within the VLM architecture.
- To integrate four complementary training objectives—action policy, inverse dynamics, forward dynamics, and future generation—to provide a comprehensive understanding of the agent-environment interaction.
- To strengthen action prediction and overall world understanding through this unified approach, thereby improving navigation performance on challenging benchmarks.
- To achieve these improvements without incurring additional computational cost during inference by allowing the auxiliary world-modeling components to be inactive post-training.
- To demonstrate that this unified modeling can enable a smaller-scale VLM backbone to outperform larger, less explicitly world-aware models, promoting efficiency.
- To validate the framework's ability to generalize to real-world environments in a zero-shot manner, indicating robust spatial and temporal learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FutureNav formulates continuous vision-and-language navigation as a conditional action generation task, augmented with auxiliary world modeling. At each time step `t`, the agent receives a natural language instruction `x` and a history of egocentric RGB observations `O_t = {I_1, ..., I_t}`. The objective is to predict the next low-level navigation action `a_t` from a discrete set of actions, typically including `STOP`, `MOVE_FORWARD`, `TURN_LEFT`, and `TURN_RIGHT`.

The core of FutureNav is a VLM backbone, which serves as the base vision-language-action model. This backbone is enhanced with a frozen spatial encoder, specifically VGGT, designed to provide explicit spatial-aware features.

**Input Encoding:**
1. **Text and Visual Tokenization**: Training samples are formatted as chat-style sequences, comprising the instruction, image placeholders, and the target action response. A tokenizer converts text into language tokens, while image placeholders reserve slots for visual embeddings. During training, losses are applied only to assistant-side action tokens.
2. **Visual Encoder**: For each RGB observation frame, the VLM's internal image processor and vision encoder generate pixel-level inputs, spatial grid metadata, and visual token embeddings (`E_q_t`). When an observation history includes multiple frames, their visual tokens are integrated into the multimodal sequence.
3. **Spatial Encoder**: In parallel, the same observation frames are processed by the frozen VGGT spatial encoder. This encoder, which is not updated during training, outputs spatial tokens (`G_t`) from an intermediate transformer layer. These tokens, representing spatial-aware features, are then projected into the VLM's language model hidden space via a lightweight, trainable MLP merger (`Merger_θ`), yielding `E_g_t`.
4. **Feature Fusion**: The final visual embedding `E_v_t` integrated into the language model's input sequence is derived by a residual addition: `E_v_t = E_q_t + αE_g_t`, where `α` is a scalar residual weight (set to 0.2). This fusion mechanism allows the pretrained VLM's visual representation to remain largely intact while explicitly injecting geometry-aware spatial information.

**World-Action Modeling Objectives:**
FutureNav trains this architecture using four complementary objectives, all operating on the hidden states generated by the VLM's language model:

1. **Action Policy Objective (`L_policy`)**: This is the primary navigation objective. The language model, conditioned on the spatial-enhanced multimodal sequence, autoregressively decodes the next navigation action. Actions are represented as text tokens. The loss is a standard next-token cross-entropy over the unmasked assistant action tokens, teaching the model to map instructions and observations to executable actions.

2. **Forward Dynamics Objective (`L_forward`)**: This head models action-conditioned state transitions. During training, with teacher forcing, the ground-truth action tokens are available. The hidden state corresponding to the last image token (`h_obs_t`) and the hidden state of the ground-truth action token (`h_act_t`) are concatenated. This combined state is passed through a lightweight MLP to predict `ˆz_t+1`, which is the pooled spatial-encoder feature of the *next* observation. The loss is an MSE loss between `ˆz_t+1` and the actual `z_t+1`. This forces the model to predict the visual consequence of its actions.

3. **Inverse Dynamics Objective (`L_inverse`)**: This head encourages the visual representations to capture action-relevant changes between consecutive observations. It takes the mean-pooled hidden states of the previous and current observations (`¯h_v_t-1`, `¯h_v_t`), a context hidden state (`h_ctx_t`), and an explicit visual-change feature (`∆h_v_t = ¯h_v_t - ¯h_v_t-1`). These inputs are combined and fed into an MLP classifier to predict the action that transformed the previous observation into the current one. The loss is a cross-entropy loss against the ground-truth previous action. This objective provides a complementary constraint on action-state consistency by inferring actions from observed state changes.

4. **Future Generation Objective (`L_gen`)**: This head predicts the future spatial state without explicitly conditioning on the action token. It combines the context hidden state (`h_ctx_t`) with the mean-pooled current image-token hidden state (`¯h_v_t`) and uses an MLP to predict `ˆz_gen_t+1`, the next spatial feature. An MSE loss is applied, similar to the forward dynamics. By operating without the action token, this objective enables the model to learn an action-agnostic prior regarding short-horizon visual evolution and scene continuity, complementing the action-conditioned forward dynamics.

**Training and Inference:**
The final training objective is a weighted sum of all four losses: `L = L_policy + λ_f L_forward + λ_i L_inverse + λ_g L_gen`. Only the VLM language components, the multimodal merger, and the world-action modeling heads are optimized, while the VLM vision tower and spatial encoder remain frozen. Crucially, during inference, only the action policy branch is used for direct action decoding. This design ensures that the auxiliary world-modeling components enrich the shared representations during training but do not impose any additional computational cost at inference time, maintaining efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE continuous VLN benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of FutureNav.

**Quantitative Performance:**
- **Performance with Expert Trajectories (0K setting)**: When trained solely on VLN-CE expert trajectories (without external data), FutureNav-4B† (a 4-billion parameter model) achieved substantial improvements over JanusVLN† (a 7-billion parameter model). Specifically, on R2R-CE, it improved Success Rate (SR) by 4.4% and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) by 1.8%. On RxR-CE, it improved SR by 6.0% and SPL by 3.8%. These 0K models notably outperformed several prior state-of-the-art methods (e.g., Uni-NaVid, StreamVLN\*, NaVILA\*) that utilized external training data on R2R SR/SPL, indicating strong inherent capabilities from the unified world-action modeling.

- **Performance with Full Training Data**: With a full training recipe, utilizing external navigation data comparable to existing methods, FutureNav achieved state-of-the-art performance across benchmarks.
 - **R2R-CE**: FutureNav-4B achieved the best overall results, improving over JanusVLN by 11.3% in Navigation Error (NE), 8.1% in SR, and 7.9% in SPL, despite using slightly less external data.
 - **RxR-CE**: FutureNav-8B demonstrated superior performance, reducing NE by 29.7% over JanusVLN and improving SR, SPL, and normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW) by 13.7%, 15.4%, and 11.3%, respectively. It also outperformed other strong models like NavFoM and InternVLA-N1 on RxR-CE.
These results indicate that the unified world-action modeling significantly enhances both data efficiency and final navigation accuracy.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Contribution of World-Action Objectives (Table 2)**:
 - A policy-only baseline achieved 50.1 SR and 45.1 SPL on R2R-CE (0K setting).
 - Adding any single world-action objective consistently improved performance. Forward dynamics provided the largest individual gain, increasing SR to 53.0 and SPL to 48.3. Future generation improved SR/SPL to 51.6/46.9, and inverse dynamics to 50.8/45.8.
 - Combining all three auxiliary objectives yielded the highest performance, reaching 55.1 SR and 50.1 SPL (5.0 point increase in both SR and SPL) and reducing NE from 6.00 to 5.13. This supports the hypothesis that the world-action objectives provide complementary supervision beyond direct action imitation.
- **Effect of Spatial Latent Representation (Table 3)**:
 - Comparing different latent representations for world modeling, the proposed spatial latent representation (derived from VGGT) achieved the best performance (55.1 SR, 50.1 SPL, 5.13 NE) in the 0K setting. This outperformed VAE latents (51.0 SR), VLM visual latents (51.4 SR), and DINO semantic latents (51.8 SR), highlighting the advantage of explicitly modeling spatial-aware states for future world prediction.
- **Training Efficiency (Figure 4)**: FutureNav demonstrated faster learning and consistently achieved higher SR and SPL throughout training compared to a policy-only baseline. For instance, at approximately 42% of the training schedule, FutureNav reached 42.3 SR and 37.8 SPL, significantly higher than the policy-only baseline's 33.2 SR and 27.9 SPL. This suggests that the auxiliary world-action objectives improve both the speed of convergence and the final accuracy.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- **Simulated Environments (Figure 5)**: In simulated VLN-CE environments, FutureNav exhibited more consistent trajectory-level planning, better alignment of actions with visual landmarks, and more accurate stopping compared to JanusVLN. This implies improved spatial context maintenance over multiple decision steps.
- **Real-World Zero-Shot Navigation (Figure 6)**: FutureNav demonstrated the ability to follow natural language instructions in real indoor and outdoor environments without any real-world fine-tuning. The model successfully grounded instructions to salient landmarks, maintained intended routes, and made appropriate stopping decisions, indicating good transferability of its learned world-action representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of FutureNav signifies a notable advancement in the field of vision-and-language navigation (VLN), particularly for continuous environments. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that a unified framework combining explicit world and action modeling within a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone can significantly enhance navigation performance while maintaining inference efficiency.

The key significance and potential impact of this work are:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance with Enhanced Efficiency**: FutureNav achieves leading performance on continuous VLN benchmarks, often surpassing prior methods that utilize larger VLM backbones. This highlights that a more comprehensive understanding of world dynamics, rather than just model scale, can be crucial for performance. The fact that a 4-billion parameter model (FutureNav-4B) can outperform larger 7B/8B models is particularly impactful, suggesting a more efficient paradigm for developing high-performing embodied AI agents.

2. **Unified World-Action Modeling Paradigm**: The paper successfully integrates four distinct yet complementary objectives—action policy, inverse dynamics, forward dynamics, and future generation—into a single, coherent training framework. This unified approach enables the model to learn not only *what* action to execute but also *how* that action transforms the world state and *what* the future state might entail. This holistic understanding of agent-environment interaction provides a richer internal representation for navigation.

3. **Improved Spatial Reasoning and Robustness**: By explicitly supervising world-state representations, action-induced state transitions, and future-state predictions, FutureNav enhances the agent's long-horizon spatial reasoning capabilities and decision robustness. The qualitative results illustrate that the model maintains a more consistent trajectory-level plan and aligns actions better with visual landmarks, suggesting a deeper understanding of the environment and its dynamics.

4. **Inference-Time Efficiency**: A critical practical advantage of FutureNav is that its auxiliary world-modeling heads are only active during training. During inference, only the action policy branch is engaged, meaning the comprehensive world modeling comes at no additional computational cost at deployment time. This makes FutureNav a more practical solution for real-world robotic applications where low-latency decision-making is essential.

5. **Strong Zero-Shot Generalization**: The demonstrated ability of FutureNav to perform complex navigation tasks in real-world indoor and outdoor environments without any real-world fine-tuning is a substantial finding. This indicates that the learned world-action representations are robust and generalize well across sim-to-real appearance, lighting, and layout shifts, a persistent challenge in embodied AI.

6. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI**: By proposing and validating this unified world-action modeling framework, FutureNav "paves the way toward future world-action models for embodied navigation." It provides a robust architectural and training methodology that can serve as a foundation for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous embodied agents capable of complex tasks in diverse and unseen environments. This could lead to advancements in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and other applications requiring sophisticated spatial and linguistic intelligence.
