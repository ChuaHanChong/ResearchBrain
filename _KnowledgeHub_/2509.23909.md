# EditScore: Unlocking Online RL for Image Editing via High-Fidelity Reward Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces EDITSCORE, a specialized high-fidelity reward model series and a comprehensive benchmark, EditReward-Bench, to unlock stable and effective online Reinforcement Learning for instruction-guided image editing. EDITSCORE-72B (Avg@4) achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on the new benchmark, enabling significant performance improvements in both Best-of-N selection and direct RL fine-tuning of generative editing models.

## Problem

- High-resolution image editing lacks a reliable, efficient, and scalable reward function crucial for applying online Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enhance model performance.
- Existing Visual Language Models (VLMs), both proprietary and open-source, are either too expensive/slow or possess insufficient task-specific accuracy to provide a stable reward signal for fine-grained image editing, leading to unstable RL training.
- A comprehensive and rigorously annotated benchmark for evaluating reward models in instruction-guided image editing was absent.

## Method

- A new multi-dimensional benchmark, ==EditReward-Bench==, was established, covering 13 subtasks across diverse editing models and featuring rigorous human annotation via a Two-Annotator Discussion Protocol.
- The **EditScore** series of specialized reward models (7B-72B) was developed by fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL models using a refined data construction pipeline and an inference-time self-ensembling strategy to predict reasoning and scalar scores.
- EditScore was validated by demonstrating its effectiveness in Best-of-N selection for existing generative models and by enabling stable online RL fine-tuning of models like OmniGen2 using the GRPO algorithm.

## Results

- EditScore-**72B** (Avg@4) achieved the highest pairwise preference prediction accuracy on EditReward-Bench, surpassing proprietary models like GPT-5 and significantly outperforming open-source VLMs.
- EditScore successfully enabled stable online RL fine-tuning, leading to substantial performance gains for generative image editing models; for example, policies fine-tuned with EditScore-**7B** outperformed a GPT-4.1-guided policy on the out-of-distribution EmuEdit benchmark.
- The reward model proved versatile, improving performance across various base editing models (e.g., OmniGen2, Flux-Kontext-dev) and demonstrating compatibility with advanced RL algorithms like TempFlow-GRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General-purpose VLMs, even large open-source ones, are inadequate for accurate image editing reward modeling, particularly in consistency judgments, highlighting the need for domain-specialized models.
> - Inference-time self-ensembling significantly boosts reward model accuracy and stability more efficiently than simply scaling model parameters, especially due to shared KV-cache prefilling.
> - For online RL, a higher variance in the reward signal, rather than just absolute annotator accuracy, can be a more critical factor for successful policy optimization and convergence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of instruction-guided image editing has seen significant advancements, enabling users to modify images based on natural language commands. However, existing models often struggle with complex instructions, requiring multiple attempts to achieve a desired outcome. This paper identifies a critical opportunity for Reinforcement Learning (RL) to address these limitations.

RL has demonstrated remarkable power in enhancing large language models (LLMs) and has recently shown success in the text-to-image (T2I) domain, with works like FlowGRPO (Liu et al., 2025a) and DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025) significantly improving T2I generation. Despite these successes, the application of online RL to high-resolution image editing remains largely unexplored and presents a formidable challenge. The primary bottleneck is the absence of a reliable, efficient, and scalable reward function—an oracle that can accurately score the quality of an edit given an instruction.

Current approaches to reward modeling for image generation primarily focus on T2I tasks, often leveraging CLIP-based models fine-tuned with human preferences or employing general-purpose Visual Language Models (VLMs). For image editing, prior work is limited. Some studies focus on controlled edits with predefined masks, while others fine-tune models like BLIP (Li et al., 2022) with human annotations or use CLIP scores to construct training data for VLMs like LLaVA-Next-8B (Liu et al., 2024). However, these existing efforts are often constrained by their reliance on outdated editing models, narrow task coverage, and—critically—a lack of open-source availability and insufficient accuracy/efficiency to support the demanding needs of online RL.

The paper highlights two key issues with existing reward mechanisms:
1. **Proprietary VLMs (e.g., GPT-5):** While powerful, these models are prohibitively expensive and slow for the scale required by online RL, making their use impractical.
2. **Open-source VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B):** Even the largest open-source VLMs, despite their scale, demonstrably fail to provide a sufficiently accurate and consistent reward signal for the fine-grained nuances of image editing. They often lead to unstable training or policy collapse when used as direct reward functions. This "performance gap" underscores that scale alone cannot replace domain-specialized accuracy.

This work addresses this critical void by arguing that the key to unlocking online RL for image editing lies in developing a *specialized, high-fidelity, and efficient reward model*. By systematically building, validating, and deploying such a model, this research aims to provide a foundational toolkit and a systematic path from benchmarking to reward modeling to RL training in image editing, thereby bridging a significant gap in the current research landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind this research is to unlock the full potential of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for instruction-guided image editing. The authors posit that RL, by treating editing as a dynamic trial-and-error process, can enable policy models to discover novel and more effective editing strategies, aligning models more deeply with user intent.

However, the primary obstacle hindering RL's adoption in this domain is the conspicuous absence of a suitable reward function—an oracle that is simultaneously reliable, efficient, and scalable enough to accurately score the quality of an image edit given an instruction. Existing general-purpose Visual Language Models (VLMs), whether proprietary (like GPT-5) or open-source (like Qwen2.5-VL-72B), prove inadequate due to cost, efficiency, or insufficient task-specific accuracy.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Develop a State-of-the-Art, Specialized Reward Model:** The central objective is to create a high-fidelity, domain-specialized reward model tailored specifically for evaluating instruction-guided image editing. This model must be accurate enough to guide policy optimization effectively.
2. **Establish a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** To enable rigorous and reproducible research, a new benchmark, **EditReward-Bench**, is introduced. This benchmark aims to systematically evaluate reward models on editing quality across diverse tasks and editing models, providing a reliable standard for measurement.
3. **Create and Release EditScore Models:** Guided by the benchmark, the authors aim to develop **EditScore**, a series of powerful, open-source reward models (ranging from 7B to 72B parameters) that can accurately assess the quality of instruction-guided edits, ultimately matching or surpassing leading proprietary VLMs.
4. **Validate Practical Utility in Downstream Tasks:** The developed EditScore model must demonstrate its utility in practical applications. This includes:
 - **Best-of-N Selection:** Showing that EditScore can effectively select high-quality edits from multiple generative outputs, thereby improving the performance of existing editing models.
 - **Online RL Training:** Demonstrating that EditScore can serve as a robust and effective reward signal for fine-tuning image editing policies via online RL, leading to substantial and consistent performance improvements over base models.
5. **Provide a Systematic Path for RL-based Image Editing:** Beyond the models and benchmark, the work aims to offer the first systematic and end-to-end pathway from benchmarking and reward modeling to practical RL training in image editing, fostering future research in this area.
6. **Promote Open Science:** A commitment to publicly releasing the code, models, data, and the benchmark to empower the wider research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is structured around three core components: creating a robust evaluation benchmark, developing a specialized reward model, and demonstrating its utility in online reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. EditReward-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark

To establish a reliable foundation for evaluating reward models, the authors introduce **EditReward-Bench**, characterized by:

- **Multi-dimensional Evaluation:** Each edit is assessed across three critical dimensions: **Prompt Following (PF)** (adherence to instructions), **Consistency (C)** (preservation of unedited regions), and **Overall Quality (O)** (holistic assessment).
- **Extensive Task Coverage:** The benchmark spans 13 distinct subtasks grouped into four categories: **Subject** (addition, removal, replace), **Appearance** (color, material, style, tone alteration), **Scene** (background change, extraction), and **Advanced** (portrait beautification, text modification, motion change, hybrid edits). Tasks are curated from established datasets like GEdit-Bench-EN and ImgEdit-Bench.
- **Diverse Model Outputs:** To challenge reward models effectively, candidates were generated by 11 diverse editing models, including both open-source and state-of-the-art proprietary solutions (e.g., OmniGen2, FLUX-Kontext, Gemini-2.5-image-preview).
- **Rigorous Annotation Protocol:** Expert human raters perform a **Two-Annotator Discussion Protocol** to minimize subjectivity and ensure high-quality ground truth. This involves two experts discussing and reaching consensus on tiered rankings (allowing ties) for each of the three dimensions. These tiered rankings are then decomposed into pairwise preference tuples, forming a large-scale (3,072 pairs) and high-fidelity dataset for reward model evaluation using preference prediction accuracy.

#### 3.2. EditScore: The Specialized Reward Model

The core of the methodology is the development of **EditScore**, a series of specialized reward models:

- **Reward Modeling Formulation:** EditScore frames reward modeling as a conditional textual generation task. It fine-tunes powerful **Qwen2.5-VL** series models (7B, 32B, 72B) using a standard autoregressive objective.
- **Input and Output:** The model takes an (Instruction, Input Image, Output Image) triplet as input and generates a (Reasoning, Scalar Score) pair as output. The inclusion of "Reasoning" encourages a Chain-of-Thought process, enhancing interpretability and accuracy.
- **VIEScore Adaptation:** EditScore adopts the VIEScore framework (Ku et al., 2023a), prompting the model to assess two orthogonal aspects in parallel: **Semantic Consistency (SC)** (instruction following and consistency) and **Perceptual Quality (PQ)** (photorealism and artifact absence). The final score is the geometric mean of SC and PQ scores.
- **Data Construction Pipeline:**
 1. **Image Selection & Instruction Creation:** High-quality input images are selected, and diverse editing instructions are generated using Qwen-2.5-VL-72B, guided by reference instructions. A K-center greedy algorithm selects semantically diverse samples.
 2. **Candidate Output Generation:** 5 distinct editing models generate candidate outputs for each image-instruction pair.
 3. **Annotation & Filtering:** GPT-4.1 annotates SC and PQ scores along with reasons. A filtering step removes unachievable editing tasks (based on group-wise maximum scores) and cases with low discriminability (based on group standard deviation). This process yielded 70,000 samples for reward model training and 60,000 for RL training.
- **Inference-Time Ensembling Strategy (Self-Ensemble):** To further boost accuracy and stability, EditScore employs a self-ensemble strategy. For a given input, K independent stochastic forward passes are performed, each yielding a scalar score. The final ensembled score is the arithmetic mean of these K scores. This strategy is shown to be more computationally efficient than simply scaling model parameters, as KV-cache prefilling can be shared across ensemble members.

#### 3.3. Online Reinforcement Learning with EditScore

To validate EditScore's practical utility, it is applied in two settings:

- **Best-of-N Selection:** EditScore is used as a selector, evaluating N candidate outputs from a base editing model (OmniGen2, Flux.1-Kontext-dev, Qwen-Image-Edit) and choosing the best one. This experiment directly measures the reward model's ability to identify high-quality edits.
- **Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning:** EditScore acts as the direct reward signal to fine-tune a strong base model, OmniGen2 (and also Flux-Kontext-dev for generality), via **GRPO (Generative Reinforcement Policy Optimization)**.
 - **RL Training Details:** The policy's stochastic transitions are defined by an SDE derived from Flow Matching, considering a path from noise at t=0 to data at t=1. This SDE's drift term is expressed using the learned vector field. The GRPO objective maximizes a clipped surrogate function over trajectories, using EditScore's terminal rewards to compute advantages. The importance ratio is based on single-step state transition probabilities defined by the SDE.
 - **Hyperparameter Ablations:** The paper also studies the impact of RL hyperparameters like inference steps and group size on training performance.

This systematic methodology, from rigorous benchmarking to sophisticated reward modeling and practical RL integration, provides a comprehensive solution for advancing image editing capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across the development and application of EditScore:

#### 4.1. EditReward-Bench Reveals Performance Gaps

Evaluation on EditReward-Bench highlights a clear hierarchy:
- **Proprietary VLMs (GPT-4.1, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro)** achieve robust pairwise accuracies (0.7-0.75 range), particularly strong in Prompt Following (PF) but somewhat less so in Consistency (C), which requires finer visual comparisons.
- **Open-source VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL series)**, even the 72B variant, fall short, struggling significantly with Consistency judgment (performing worse than random chance) and achieving overall accuracies below 0.612. This underscores their inadequacy as reliable, off-the-shelf reward signals for fine-grained editing tasks.

#### 4.2. EditScore Sets New State of the Art for Reward Modeling

- **Superior Accuracy:** EditScore models achieve substantial improvements. The 7B variant surpasses the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and the 72B variant matches GPT-4.1's accuracy.
- **Effective Self-Ensembling:** The inference-time ensembling strategy (Avg@4) significantly boosts performance across all model sizes. EditScore-72B (Avg@4) establishes a new state of the art on EditReward-Bench, even surpassing GPT-5.
- **Efficiency:** Analysis reveals that self-ensembling offers a superior efficiency-performance trade-off compared to simply scaling model parameters. Wall-clock latency grows sublinearly with K (ensemble size) due to shared KV-cache prefilling, making it a cost-effective way to improve quality. For instance, EditScore-7B (K=4) achieves higher accuracy than single-pass EditScore-32B (K=1) with less latency and hardware.
- **Ablation Studies:** The research finds that an appropriate score range (optimally) and a "reasoning + score" output format are crucial for maximizing accuracy in reward models.

#### 4.3. EditScore Utility in Image Editing Applications

- **Best-of-N Selection:** EditScore proves highly effective in improving the performance of existing generative models. By selecting the best output among N candidates, it boosts the VIEScore of models like OmniGen2, Flux.1-Kontext-dev, and Qwen-Image-Edit, with OmniGen2 showing the most significant gains. This demonstrates its ability to identify high-quality edits.
- **Unlocking Stable Online RL:** This is a core finding. While raw instruction-tuned VLMs (even Qwen2.5-VL-72B) fail to provide an effective, stable signal for online RL fine-tuning, EditScore-7B successfully stabilizes training and leads to significant performance improvements. It competes favorably with proprietary GPT-4.1 and even outperforms it on the out-of-distribution EmuEdit benchmark (e.g., 0.900 vs. 0.884 CLIP-I). Using stronger backbone models for EditScore (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B) further enhances performance.
- **Ensembling Benefits RL:** The ensembled reward signal (Avg@4) not only improves evaluation accuracy but also accelerates convergence and leads to superior final performance in RL training, providing a more consistent gradient.
- **Generality:** EditScore demonstrates robustness and model-agnosticism. It consistently improves performance when applied to a different generative backbone (Flux-Kontext-dev) and is compatible with advanced RL algorithms like TempFlow-GRPO, achieving further gains without introducing instability or reward hacking.
- **Impact of Reward Variance:** A counter-intuitive but crucial finding is that higher reward signal variance, rather than absolute annotator accuracy, can be a dominant factor for successful RL optimization. EditScore trained on GPT-4.1's annotations (which had higher variance) led to a demonstrably better policy than one trained on GPT-5's annotations (which had higher accuracy but lower variance), corroborating similar findings in other RL domains.

#### 4.4. Limitations

The paper acknowledges limitations, particularly in subjective tasks like "Portrait Beautification" and complex "Hybrid Edits," where EditScore's baseline accuracy is lower. While self-ensembling mitigates these weaknesses, challenges remain due to the inherent difficulty of fine-grained pixel-level discernment for subtle aesthetic changes and the limited presence of complex compositional samples in current datasets. Failure cases highlight issues with overly conservative scoring for subtle improvements and over-rewarding partial success in multi-instruction scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work marks a significant advancement in the field of instruction-guided image editing and the broader application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to generative models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking the Reward Barrier for RL in Image Editing:** The paper successfully addresses the critical bottleneck of a reliable reward signal, which has largely hindered the adoption of online RL for complex image editing tasks. By developing EditScore, the authors provide the missing link that enables stable and effective policy optimization in this domain.
2. **Establishing Foundational Benchmarking and Resources:** The introduction of **EditReward-Bench** provides a much-needed, comprehensive, and rigorously annotated benchmark for systematically evaluating reward models in image editing. Coupled with the release of **EditScore** models, code, and data, this work offers a foundational toolkit that will accelerate future research and development in RL-based image editing.
3. **Unlocking New Research Directions:** By demonstrating a systematic path from benchmarking to reward modeling to RL training, this research opens up significant avenues for further exploration. It allows researchers to move beyond static dataset training and leverage the dynamic, exploratory nature of RL to enable models to discover more novel, robust, and user-aligned editing strategies.
4. **Practical Utility for State-of-the-Art Editing Models:** EditScore's demonstrated ability to improve existing state-of-the-art editing models, both through Best-of-N selection and direct RL fine-tuning, has immediate practical implications. It offers a pathway to enhance the performance, consistency, and alignment of current generative image editors with complex user instructions.
5. **Insights into Reward Model Design:** The findings on the effectiveness of inference-time scaling (self-ensembling) and the crucial role of reward signal variance in RL optimization (rather than just absolute accuracy) provide valuable insights that extend beyond image editing. These principles could inform the design of reward models and RL training strategies in other generative AI domains, such as text-to-video or 3D content generation.
6. **Promotion of Open Science:** The commitment to public release fosters a collaborative research environment, allowing the broader community to build upon this work, replicate findings, and accelerate innovation. This aligns with the increasing trend towards open-source AI and democratizing access to powerful research tools.
7. **Advancing Human-AI Interaction:** Ultimately, by enabling image editing models to better understand and execute complex instructions, this work contributes to more intuitive and powerful human-AI interaction, making creative image manipulation more accessible and effective for a wider range of users.

In conclusion, "EDITSCORE" presents a landmark contribution by effectively tackling a major challenge in generative AI. Its systematic approach, powerful models, and open-source commitment are poised to significantly impact the trajectory of RL-based image editing and reward modeling research.
