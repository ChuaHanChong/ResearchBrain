# Improving Pre-Trained Vision-Language-Action Policies with Model-Based Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mila – Quebec AI Institute developed VLAPS, a framework that integrates model-based search with pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies at inference time. This approach consistently enhanced task success rates in simulated robotic manipulation, boosting performance by up to 42 percentage points on various tasks and enabling smaller VLA models to match the performance of significantly larger state-of-the-art models.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, relying on imitation learning, lack explicit reasoning about action consequences, leading to brittle behaviors and failures in novel or out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Traditional model-based planning struggles with the combinatorial explosion of high-dimensional state-action spaces in robotics and the difficulty of designing generalizable heuristics for sparse-reward, language-specified tasks.
- Current VLA models often exhibit sub-optimal performance or unsafe failures in long-horizon tasks because they cannot look ahead or correct errors, impeding their practical adoption.

## Method

- **VLAPS** (Vision-Language-Action Planning & Search) integrates a ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)==-inspired algorithm with a pre-trained VLA policy and a ==world model== at inference time to enable explicit future reasoning.
- The framework refines the search space by operating on temporally-abstract ==action chunks== derived from a finite library, and biases action chunk sampling at each node using the VLA policy to propose contextually relevant candidates.
- **VLAPS** guides MCTS tree traversal with a VLA-based ==prior distribution== during action selection, focusing the search on promising trajectories without requiring explicit learned value functions.

## Results

- VLAPS consistently improved task success rates over VLA-only baselines across all Libero task suites and VLA checkpoints, with a 50k-step VLA policy showing an overall task success rate increase of **42 percentage points**.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed that VLAPS enabled the robot to avoid common VLA errors like mis-grasping or dropping objects, demonstrating its ability to correct brittle behaviors.
- VLAPS boosted the performance of a smaller VLA model (Octo, **93M** parameters) to achieve success rates (e.g., **99%** on Libero-Spatial) comparable to or exceeding much larger, state-of-the-art models (e.g., π0-FAST, **3.3B** parameters).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based search, when integrated with VLA policies, significantly improves robot policy robustness and success rates by enabling explicit lookahead and error correction during inference, overcoming limitations of pure imitation learning.
> - VLA models can effectively serve as intelligent priors and search space refiners, making model-based planning tractable in complex robotic environments by abstracting actions and biasing exploration towards relevant behaviors.
> - Adaptive allocation of test-time computational resources allows VLAPS to achieve larger performance gains for underperforming VLA policies while efficiently confirming actions for competent ones, offering a flexible performance-latency trade-off.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies capable of solving complex, open-ended tasks is a prominent objective in contemporary robotics research. Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage internet-scale vision and language data alongside diverse robot demonstration datasets, represent a promising direction for creating such policies. These models offer natural-language interfaces for task specification and multimodal reasoning, supporting adaptation to new tasks and environments. However, a limitation of current VLA models is their inability to explicitly reason about the potential consequences of their actions, instead relying on imitation of observed behaviors. This can lead to brittle actions and failures, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios when deployed zero-shot.

In parallel, model-based planning algorithms are designed to explicitly reason over future outcomes. Yet, their application in robotics is often hindered by the combinatorial explosion of large state-action spaces and the need for carefully handcrafted heuristics to navigate sparse-reward environments. Designing such heuristics that generalize across diverse, language-specified robotic tasks remains a challenge.

This work, Vision-Language-Action Planning & Search (VLAPS), positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms. It addresses the limitations of VLA models by integrating them with model-based search, while simultaneously leveraging VLA capabilities to mitigate the challenges faced by traditional planning methods.

The paper contextualizes VLAPS within several related areas:
- **VLA Models as Generalist Robot Policies:** VLAPS builds upon recent advancements in VLA architectures and training strategies (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Octo) which focus on behavior cloning from large-scale datasets. Instead of proposing a new VLA architecture or training method, VLAPS aims to improve the *inference-time* performance of existing pre-trained VLA policies.
- **Additional Test-Time Compute for Language Models:** The concept of dynamically allocating more computation at inference time to improve performance has been explored in large language models (LLMs) through techniques like chain-of-thought prompting and MCTS during inference. VLAPS extends this paradigm to robotics and embodied reasoning, injecting prior modeling knowledge into the VLA-driven decision-making loop. This knowledge can come from high-fidelity simulators, learned world models, or video prediction models.
- **Model-Based Search for Learned Policies:** VLAPS draws inspiration from algorithms like AlphaZero and MuZero, which combine deep reinforcement learning with online MCTS to achieve high performance in complex games. It applies the methodology of using model-based search at test time to correct errors in learned policies, similar to how model predictive control (MPC) can correct for errors in learned value functions.
- **Planning with Language-Conditioned Models for Robot Decision Making:** While some prior works use LLMs/VLMs for high-level plan generation (executed by motion planners or skill libraries) or single-path rollouts for action revision, VLAPS distinguishes itself by performing a branching search over action abstractions *derived directly from a VLA*. This approach generates robot-executable action sequences without requiring additional low-level motion planners or pre-defined skill primitives.

By integrating the strengths of VLA models (multimodal reasoning, natural language interface, learned priors) with model-based search (explicit lookahead, error correction), VLAPS aims to develop more flexible, robust, and long-horizon generalist robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models when deployed in real-world or novel scenarios. While VLA models offer a foundation for generalist robot policies, their reliance on behavior cloning means they often fail to reason about the long-term consequences of their actions. This can lead to brittle behaviors, sub-optimal performance, or unsafe failures, particularly in situations not well-represented in their training data. These shortcomings impede their adoption in practical robotics applications.

Conversely, traditional model-based planning, while capable of explicit future reasoning, faces its own set of challenges in complex, high-dimensional robotics environments. The vast, continuous state-action spaces common in robotics, coupled with sparse rewards, can lead to a combinatorial explosion in the search space. Designing effective and generalizable heuristics to guide such search algorithms across diverse, language-specified tasks remains a significant hurdle.

Against this background, the key objectives of the Vision-Language-Action Planning & Search (VLAPS) framework are:

1. **Enhance Performance and Robustness of Pre-Trained VLA Policies:** The core objective is to improve the task success rates and robustness of existing pre-trained VLA policies without requiring additional model training. This is achieved by embedding a model-based search procedure directly into the VLA policy's inference process.
2. **Integrate Model-Based Search with VLA Priors:** To address the intractability of direct search in large robotics environments, VLAPS aims to leverage the VLA policy's learned knowledge. Specifically, it seeks to use VLA-derived abstractions and priors to efficiently guide the model-based search, focusing computational effort on promising regions of the action space relevant to the given language-conditioned task.
3. **Control Test-Time Compute:** The framework is designed to offer a principled mechanism for controlling the computational resources expended during inference. This allows for a configurable trade-off between policy performance and the latency introduced by the search process.
4. **Leverage Environmental Knowledge:** VLAPS aims to incorporate *a priori* knowledge of the robotic environment, typically provided by a world model (e.g., a simulator or learned dynamics model). This enables explicit simulation of future action outcomes, which is crucial for long-horizon planning and error correction.
5. **Bridge Planning/RL and VLA Models:** The research intends to integrate established techniques from planning and reinforcement learning, such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), into the inference process of modern VLA models. This fusion aims to combine the multimodal reasoning capabilities of VLAs with the explicit lookahead abilities of model-based planning.

By achieving these objectives, VLAPS seeks to develop generalist robot policies that are more flexible, robust, and capable of long-horizon reasoning than VLA-only baselines, thereby moving closer to practical deployment in complex robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLAPS integrates a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)-inspired algorithm with a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy and a world model. The framework is designed to operate at inference time, improving policy performance without requiring further VLA training.

**A. Overview of the VLAPS Search Procedure**
At each decision point in a task, VLAPS initiates a search tree with the current environment state $s_t$ as the root node $v_0$. The search proceeds through iterative phases:

1. **Selection:** Starting from the root, child nodes are recursively selected using a VLA-guided variant of the PUCT (Polynomial Upper Confidence Trees) criterion until a leaf node $v$ (representing a potential future state not yet expanded) is reached.
2. **Expansion:** Upon reaching a leaf node, the tree is expanded by proposing a collection of action sequences. These are sampled from a prior distribution defined by the VLA policy. Each sampled action sequence leads to a new child node $v'$.
3. **Simulation:** From each newly expanded child node $v'$, the world model ($ \hat{M} $) is used to simulate rollouts of the VLA policy. These rollouts continue until the task is completed or a predefined maximum horizon length is reached.
4. **Backpropagation:** The value estimates along the path from the expanded node back to the root are updated based on the simulation results.

This iterative process continues until a goal state is reached in the simulation, or a maximum number of MCTS steps or a computational budget (e.g., wall-clock time) is exhausted. If a goal state is found, the sequence of actions leading to it is returned. Otherwise, the most frequently selected action sequence from the root node is returned.

**B. Key Mechanisms for VLA-Integration and Search Space Refinement**
VLAPS incorporates the VLA policy in two principal ways to manage the challenges of large, continuous robotics action spaces and guide the search:

1. **Automatically Defining Task-Oriented, Tractable Search Spaces (Action Chunk Sampling):**
 - **Challenge:** Directly searching over the continuous and high-dimensional raw robot actions ($A$) is computationally infeasible for MCTS, especially for long-horizon tasks.
 - **Solution:** VLAPS performs search over *temporally-abstract action chunks* $u_t = (a_t..., a_{t+H})$. An action chunk represents a short sequence of primitive actions, effectively raising the level of abstraction for planning. The horizon $H$ controls the temporal granularity and frequency of VLA queries.
 - **Finite Library ($\Phi$):** To address the continuity of action chunks, a finite library $\Phi$ of candidate action chunks is constructed. In the experiments, this library is generated by aggregating action sequences from successful VLA runs, normalizing them, and applying K-Medoids clustering to extract 2000 representative prototypes. This restricts the search to behaviorally-relevant actions.
 - **Contextually-Relevant Subset Sampling ($\Phi_v$):** At each leaf node $v$, instead of searching over the entire $\Phi$, a smaller subset $\Phi_v$ of $k$ candidate action chunks is sampled. This sampling is biased by the VLA policy to propose chunks relevant to the current state ($I_t$) and task instruction ($L_T$). The sampling distribution $\beta_{\Phi}(\cdot|I_t, L_T)$ is defined as a softmax-style distribution centered around the VLA's output $u_{vla}$, using Euclidean distance ($\rho$) between flattened action chunks and an inverse temperature parameter ($\alpha$), with $\epsilon$-uniform exploration. This significantly reduces the branching factor from $|\Phi|$ to $k$.

2. **Guiding Tree Traversal with a VLA-Based Prior Policy (Action Selection Biasing):**
 - **Challenge:** Even with a reduced branching factor, uniform exploration within the MCTS can be computationally expensive.
 - **Solution:** During the selection phase of MCTS, VLAPS introduces a VLA-derived prior distribution $\psi_{\Phi_v}$ to bias the tree traversal towards action chunks favored by the VLA policy. This effectively focuses the search on promising subtrees.
 - **Selection Criterion:** VLAPS uses a modified PUCT-like score function (Equation 2 in the paper) that prioritizes actions based on this VLA-based prior $\psi_{\Phi_v}(u_i|I_t, L_T)$ and a node-action visitation count $N(v, u_i)$ term for exploration. The prior $\psi_{\Phi_v}$ is also a softmax-style distribution centered on the VLA model's output, similar to $\beta_{\Phi}$. Notably, this variant of tree traversal omits explicit learned value function estimates (Q=0), relying primarily on the VLA's prior and rollout success to guide the search.

**C. Experimental Implementation**
The experimental evaluation utilized the LIBERO simulated suite of language-specified robotic manipulation tasks. The underlying VLA model was Octo-base-1.5, finetuned on the Libero dataset. Different VLA checkpoints (10k, 50k, 100k, 150k, 200k gradient steps) were used to assess the impact of base model quality. VLAPS was configured with 300 Monte Carlo samples, $k=10$ child nodes per expansion, action chunks of horizon $H=4$, a maximum tree depth of 100, and a 600-second wall-clock execution time limit per task. The action chunk library consisted of 2000 prototypes. Specific inverse temperature and exploration parameters were set for the sampling and prior distributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the LIBERO simulated robotics tasks demonstrate several key findings regarding the performance of VLAPS:

1. **Consistent Performance Improvement over VLA-Only Baselines:** VLAPS consistently outperformed the VLA-only baseline policy across all tested VLA model checkpoints and all five Libero task suites (Libero-Spatial, Libero-Goal, Libero-Object, Libero-90, and Libero-10). These improvements were observed for every evaluated checkpoint of the base VLA model.

2. **Substantial Increases in Task Success Rates:** The gains in task success rate achieved by VLAPS were notable. For instance, with a VLA finetuned for 50k steps, VLAPS boosted the overall task success rate by 42 percentage points. For the 100k-, 150k-, and 200k-step checkpoints, improvements of at least 20 percentage points were observed. In specific tasks like Libero-Object, the 50k-step VLA's success rate increased from 6% to 73% with VLAPS, representing a 67 percentage point increase.

3. **Correction of Common VLA Errors:** Qualitative analysis indicated that VLAPS enabled the robot to avoid common errors frequently encountered with VLA-only policies. These errors included mis-grasping objects, dropping objects, or moving into uncommon states not adequately covered in the training data, which could otherwise derail an entire task attempt.

4. **Largest Relative Gains for Lower-Performing Baselines:** VLAPS yielded the most significant *relative* performance improvements when augmenting VLA-only policies that initially had lower success rates. For example, the substantial gains on Libero-Object (from 6% to 73%) for the 50k-step checkpoint illustrate VLAPS's ability to significantly enhance less specialized models without additional finetuning. However, it was noted that when the base VLA policy exhibited extremely low performance (e.g., 0% success), VLAPS did not necessarily succeed within the allotted search time, indicating limits to its effectiveness when the base policy offers no useful guidance.

5. **Adaptive Allocation of Search Compute:** The average time VLAPS spent on search decreased as the performance of the underlying VLA policy improved. This indicates that VLAPS adaptively allocates computational resources: when the VLA policy is already capable of solving a task, VLAPS quickly confirms the VLA's suggested solution. Conversely, when the base policy struggles, VLAPS expands its search tree more extensively, dedicating additional compute to find a successful trajectory. The largest relative performance gains were observed in scenarios where VLAPS spent more time searching.

6. **Boosting Smaller Model Performance to Match Larger Models:** VLAPS demonstrated the capability to elevate the performance of a relatively smaller VLA model (Octo, 93 million parameters) to levels comparable with much larger, state-of-the-art models (e.g., π0-FAST, 3.3 billion parameters). For instance, Octo+VLAPS achieved 99% success on Libero-Spatial compared to π0-FAST's 96%, and 74% on Libero-10 compared to π0-FAST's 71%. This suggests VLAPS can be an effective method for achieving high performance from more compact VLA architectures.

7. **Tractability through VLA-Driven Search Space Refinement:** The VLA-driven action chunk sampling and prior biasing mechanisms were crucial for making the search tractable. Without these methods, directly searching over the high-dimensional continuous action space or even a large discrete library (e.g., $2000^{100}$ nodes for typical depths) would be computationally infeasible. VLAPS consistently completed search within finite compute budgets by focusing exploration on contextually relevant and VLA-favored trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLAPS framework presents several significant contributions and holds potential impact for the field of robotic learning and generalist robot policies:

1. **Increased Robustness and Reliability for Generalist Robot Policies:** By integrating model-based search with VLA models, VLAPS enables explicit reasoning over future action outcomes. This capability is crucial for moving beyond brittle behaviors learned purely through imitation, leading to more robust and reliable robot performance, particularly in novel or out-of-distribution scenarios. This can foster greater trust and accelerate the deployment of VLA-based robots in various applications.

2. **Enhanced Task Success Rates without Retraining:** VLAPS consistently yields increased task success rates across a range of language-specified manipulation tasks. Importantly, these improvements are achieved *at inference time* without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning of the underlying VLA model. This allows researchers and practitioners to immediately leverage existing pre-trained VLA models and benefit from performance enhancements, making it a cost-effective method for policy improvement.

3. **Adaptive and Controlled Test-Time Compute Allocation:** The framework provides a principled mechanism to dynamically allocate computational resources during inference. This allows for a configurable trade-off between policy performance and planning latency. For tasks where the base VLA is highly competent, VLAPS can quickly confirm actions, maintaining low latency. For more challenging instances where the VLA struggles, it can dedicate more compute to explore alternative trajectories, enhancing the likelihood of success. This adaptive behavior is valuable for practical system design.

4. **Bridging Model-Based Planning and Vision-Language-Action Models:** VLAPS represents a step in integrating established, theoretically grounded model-based planning techniques (like MCTS) with the expressive power of modern large-scale VLA models. It demonstrates how VLA models can effectively serve as intelligent priors and search space refiners, overcoming the traditional challenges of combinatorial complexity and heuristic design in robotic planning. This integration opens avenues for developing hybrid intelligent systems that combine learned perception and language understanding with deliberative reasoning.

5. **Democratization of High-Performance Robot Control:** The finding that VLAPS can boost the performance of relatively smaller VLA models to levels comparable with much larger, state-of-the-art models is noteworthy. This suggests that high-performance robot control might not solely depend on the scale of the base VLA model, but also on efficient inference-time reasoning. This could make advanced robot capabilities more accessible by reducing the computational and data requirements associated with training extremely large models.

6. **Direction for Future Research:** VLAPS highlights key areas for future investigation, including the development of learned world models that can be integrated into the planning loop (similar to MuZero), addressing sim-to-real gaps, and further optimizing VLA inference times through techniques like quantization and distillation. The framework's agnostic nature to the specific VLA model implies that its benefits will likely compound with future advancements in VLA architectures and training methodologies.

Overall, VLAPS offers a pragmatic and effective approach to enhance the capabilities of generalist robot policies, providing a method to achieve more robust, reliable, and intelligent robotic behaviors by integrating explicit future reasoning into the inference process of pre-trained VLA models.
