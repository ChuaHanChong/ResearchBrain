# FRMD: Fast Robot Motion Diffusion with Consistency-Distilled Movement Primitives for Smooth Action Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The FRMD framework accelerates robot motion generation by integrating consistency-distilled models with movement primitives, allowing for single-step, real-time action generation. This approach yields motions 10 times faster than prior diffusion methods while achieving an average 64.8% task success rate and significantly smoother trajectories on manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion-based robot motion generation often produces non-smooth, jerky motions lacking temporal consistency.
- The iterative denoising process in conventional diffusion models leads to high inference latency, making them impractical for real-time robotic control.

## Method

- **FRMD** integrates ==Consistency Models (CMs)== with Probabilistic Dynamic Movement Primitives (ProDMPs) to ensure both fast and smooth action generation.
- A teacher Movement Primitive Diffusion (MPD) model, which generates ProDMP parameters over multiple steps, is distilled into a student FRMD model using consistency distillation.
- The student FRMD model learns to directly predict ProDMP weight vectors from noisy inputs in a single inference step, leveraging skip connections and an EMA target network for stable training.

## Results

- FRMD achieved a substantially reduced average inference time of 17.2ms, making it 10 times faster than MPD (168.6ms) and 7 times faster than Diffusion Policy (119.7ms).
- It demonstrated the highest average task success rate of **64.8%** across 12 Meta-World and ManiSkill manipulation tasks, slightly outperforming its MPD teacher (**64.1%**) and significantly surpassing Diffusion Policy (**50.1%**).
- The framework generated significantly smoother trajectories with fewer non-smooth transitions, for instance, reducing them from 82 for Diffusion Policy to 21 for FRMD in the `PlugCharger-v1` task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining classical movement primitives for structured motion with modern generative diffusion models can address both temporal consistency and expressiveness in robot control.
> - Consistency distillation effectively accelerates diffusion models, enabling single-step inference for real-time applications without compromising performance.
> - Generating low-dimensional movement primitive parameters instead of raw action sequences inherently ensures smoother and more temporally consistent robot trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in FRMD sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving research areas: robot learning, generative models (specifically diffusion models), and classical robot motion planning.

**Diffusion Models in Robotics:**
In recent years, diffusion models, initially popularized in image and video generation, have gained significant traction in robot learning. They are increasingly being adopted as "action decoders" due to their impressive task scalability and multi-modal flexibility, capable of handling complex observations and generating diverse action sequences. Approaches like "Diffusion Policy" (Chi et al., 2023) and "$\pi^0$" (Black et al., 2024) have demonstrated their potential for general-purpose robotic control, showing that these models can effectively map high-dimensional latent representations to precise robot actions across various manipulation tasks. FRMD acknowledges this success but points out two critical limitations: the generation of non-smooth, jerky motions due to a lack of temporal consistency, and the high inference latency caused by their iterative denoising process. FRMD aims to directly address these fundamental shortcomings, positioning itself as an advancement in making diffusion models practically viable for real-time robotic applications.

**Movement Primitives in Robotics:**
Classical robot motion generation methods, such as Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) and Probabilistic Movement Primitives (ProMPs), have long been a cornerstone in robotics for encoding and generating smooth, temporally consistent trajectories. These methods represent complex motions using low-dimensional, parameterized models that inherently ensure smoothness and generalize well to new situations. FRMD is deeply inspired by these concepts, particularly Probabilistic Dynamic Movement Primitives (ProDMPs), which combine the strengths of DMPs and ProMPs by using precomputed basis functions to simplify trajectory generation and guarantee boundary conditions. The paper's novelty lies in integrating these robust, structured motion representations with the powerful generative capabilities of diffusion models. This is not the first work to combine diffusion models with movement primitives; prior efforts (e.g., Scheikl et al., 2024; Carvalho et al., 2024) have shown benefits in generating gentler or smoother motions. However, these earlier integrations still suffer from the high inference latency inherent to multi-step diffusion.

**Consistency Models for Fast Inference:**
A crucial component of FRMD is its adoption of Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) and Consistency Distillation (Song & Dhariwal, 2023). These techniques originated in image generation to drastically accelerate the sampling speed of diffusion models. By enforcing self-consistency along Probability Flow Ordinary Differential Equation (PF-ODE) trajectories, consistency models allow for single-step inference, mapping a noisy input directly to a clean output. While these models have revolutionized vision and language synthesis, their application as fast action decoders in robotics has been largely underexplored. FRMD bridges this gap by extending consistency distillation to robotic motion planning, specifically by applying it to the generation of movement primitive parameters rather than raw actions.

By combining these three elements—diffusion models for their generative power, movement primitives for structured smoothness, and consistency models for accelerated inference—FRMD proposes a unified framework that seeks to overcome the key limitations of existing diffusion-based robot learning approaches. It represents a significant step towards enabling real-time, high-quality action generation, moving beyond merely improving success rates to tackling the practical constraints of robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind FRMD stems from two fundamental limitations observed in current diffusion-based robot motion generation methods:

1. **Generation of Non-Smooth, Jerky Motions:** Existing diffusion models often produce trajectories that lack temporal consistency, leading to physically implausible or "jerky" movements. This is because they typically generate raw action sequences (waypoints) without explicitly accounting for the structured dynamic constraints and smoothness requirements imposed by robot kinematics and dynamics. For many robotic tasks, especially those requiring delicate manipulation or interaction with the environment, smooth and continuous motion is paramount for safety, efficiency, and task success.

2. **Prohibitive Inference Latency:** The iterative sampling process inherent to conventional diffusion models (e.g., DDPM, DDIM) involves multiple denoising steps. This multi-step inference introduces significant latency, making them unsuitable for real-time robotic control, where decisions often need to be made in milliseconds. This bottleneck limits the applicability of diffusion models to tasks that can tolerate delayed action generation or offline planning.

To address these challenges, the paper sets out the following key objectives:

- **Generate Fast Robot Motions:** Develop a method that significantly reduces the inference time of diffusion-based robot action generation, enabling real-time control.
- **Produce Smooth and Temporally Consistent Actions:** Ensure that the generated robot motions are inherently smooth, structured, and temporally consistent, avoiding the jerky movements often seen in raw action sequence generation.
- **Achieve One-Step Inference:** Enable the model to generate high-quality action sequences in a single inference step, eliminating the slow, iterative denoising process.
- **Maintain or Improve Task Success Rates:** Achieve these speed and smoothness improvements without sacrificing, and ideally enhancing, the task success rates compared to existing state-of-the-art diffusion policies.
- **Bridge Structured Motion Representation with Fast Generative Modeling:** Create a unified framework that effectively combines the benefits of classical movement primitives with advanced generative AI models for practical robotic applications.

By achieving these objectives, FRMD aims to unlock new possibilities for real-time, high-quality robot action generation, broadening the applicability of powerful generative models in complex, time-sensitive robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach: Fast Robot Motion Diffusion (FRMD)

FRMD proposes a novel framework that integrates Consistency Models with Probabilistic Dynamic Movement Primitives (ProDMPs) to achieve efficient, single-step, and smooth robot motion generation. The methodology is structured around a teacher-student model approach, leveraging consistency distillation.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The objective is to learn a policy $\pi: O \rightarrow A$ that maps robot observations `o` (RGB images, proprioceptive states) to an action sequence $\tau = \{a_i\}_{i=0}^n$. Instead of directly generating raw actions $a_i$, FRMD focuses on generating parameters for structured movement primitives.

**B. Preliminaries:**

1. **Movement Primitives (ProDMPs):**
 FRMD utilizes Probabilistic Dynamic Movement Primitives (ProDMPs) as the structured motion representation. ProDMPs are chosen because they overcome limitations of traditional DMPs by using precomputed position and velocity basis functions ($\Phi$, $\dot{\Phi}$), thereby eliminating costly numerical integration. Trajectories are formulated as a weighted combination of these basis functions and complementary functions:
 $y = c_1 y_1 + c_2 y_2 + \Phi^\top w$
 $\dot{y} = c_1 \dot{y}_1 + c_2 \dot{y}_2 + \dot{\Phi}^\top w$
 Here, $w$ is a low-dimensional weight vector that the diffusion model will learn to predict. The constants $c_1, c_2$ are determined by boundary conditions (current position and velocity), ensuring smooth transitions. This formulation guarantees smooth, temporally consistent motions by design.

2. **Consistency Models and Consistency Distillation:**
 Consistency Models (CMs) are designed to learn the solution function $f(x_t, t) \rightarrow x_0$ of a Probability Flow Ordinary Differential Equation (PF-ODE), directly mapping any noisy input $x_t$ at time $t$ to the original clean data $x_0$. This "self-consistency" property allows for single-step inference.
 Consistency Distillation is the training method used to achieve this, where a "student" model $f_\theta$ is trained to match the output of a "teacher" model $f_{\theta^-}$ (an EMA of the student or a pre-trained diffusion model) at different noise levels but corresponding to the same clean data point. The consistency loss $L_{CD}$ minimizes the distance between the student's prediction from a highly noisy sample and the target network's prediction from a less noisy sample along the same PF-ODE trajectory.

**C. FRMD: Fast Robot Motion Diffusion (Teacher-Student Architecture):**

1. **Teacher Model Set-up:**
 The teacher model is essentially a Movement Primitive Diffusion (MPD) model, similar to approaches proposed in recent works (e.g., Scheikl et al., 2024). It consists of a trainable model $E_\theta$ that outputs the ProDMP weight vector $w$. This $w$, combined with initial conditions $(y_0, \dot{y}_0)$, is decoded into an action sequence $\tau$ using the ProDMP decoder $P_\Phi$.
 The teacher model's denoiser $F_\theta(\tilde{\tau}, o, t)$ approximates the score function of the conditional distribution $p(\tau | o, \sigma(t))$, where $\tilde{\tau}$ is a noisy action sequence. It is trained using a score matching objective, where the model learns to predict the original clean action sequence $\tau$ from a noisy version $\tilde{\tau}$. During inference, the teacher model uses an ODE solver (e.g., DPM-Solver) to iteratively denoise a unit Gaussian sample over ~10 steps to generate new actions.

2. **Student Model Consistency Distillation (FRMD):**
 FRMD is the student model trained via consistency distillation from the pre-trained teacher MPD model. The process (illustrated in Algorithm 1) involves:
 - **Noise Introduction:** A ground-truth expert action sequence $\tau_0$ is subjected to $n+k$ steps of forward diffusion to obtain a noisy sample $\tau_{n+k}$.
 - **Teacher Prediction:** The noisy sample $\tau_{n+k}$ is fed to the teacher model, which performs a $k$-step denoising (Equation 10) to obtain an intermediate denoised sample $\hat{\tau}_n$.
 - **Student and Target Predictions:** The student model (FRMD) directly predicts the clean action sequence $\hat{\tau}_0$ from $\tau_{n+k}$ using a consistency function $f_\theta(\tau, o, t)$. A separate "target model" $f_{\theta^-}$ (an EMA of the student) predicts $\hat{\tau}_{0^-}$ from $\hat{\tau}_n$.
 - **Consistency Loss:** The consistency distillation loss $L_{CD}$ (Equation 12) minimizes the distance between the student's output $f_\theta(\tau_{n+k}, o, t_{n+k})$ and the target model's output $f_{\theta^-}(\hat{\tau}_n, o, t_n)$. This loss enforces the self-consistency property, ensuring that different noisy points on the same PF-ODE trajectory map to the same clean data.
 - **Model Parameterization:** The consistency function $f_\theta$ is parameterized with skip connections (Equation 11) to stabilize training, using the same underlying structure $F_\theta$ as the teacher model for predicting ProDMP weights.
 - **EMA Update:** The target model parameters $\theta^-$ are updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student model parameters $\theta$.

**D. Network Architectures and Training Details:**
- **Observation Encoder:** A modified ResNet18 is used to encode RGB images into latent embeddings, trained end-to-end.
- **Denoiser Network:** For both the teacher and student, a Transformer-based architecture with 6 layers, 4 heads, and 256 embedding size is used, consistent with Diffusion Policy's recommendations for temporal dependency capture.
- **Training:** AdamW optimizer, cosine decay learning rate scheduler with warm-up, and EMA for the target network.

By distilling the knowledge from a multi-step diffusion teacher into a consistency-aware student, FRMD enables high-quality, structured motion generation in a single inference step, directly addressing the key challenges of latency and smoothness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FRMD was conducted on recognized robotic manipulation benchmarks, Meta-World and ManiSkill, across 12 diverse tasks categorized by difficulty (easy, medium, hard). The performance was assessed using three key metrics: Task Success Rate, Inference Time, and Motion Smoothness. FRMD was compared against state-of-the-art baselines: Diffusion Policy (DP) and Movement Primitive Diffusion (MPD), with MPD serving as FRMD's teacher model.

**A. Success Rate:**
- **Overall Performance:** FRMD achieved the highest average success rate of **64.8%**, slightly outperforming its teacher MPD (64.1%) and significantly surpassing DP (50.1%). This demonstrates that FRMD not only preserves the structured motion quality learned by MPD but also enhances policy expressiveness and robustness, even with single-step inference.
- **Task Difficulty:**
 - **Easy Tasks:** All methods performed well, with FRMD achieving 99.2%, on par with DP (99.3%) and slightly better than MPD (98.9%).
 - **Medium Tasks:** FRMD maintained its advantage with 66.3%, compared to MPD's 64.8% and DP's 41.0%. This confirms the effective distillation of MPD's structured motion representation.
 - **Hard Tasks:** FRMD continued to outperform with 29.0%, showing better generalization and robustness than MPD (28.6%) and significantly better than DP (10.1%).

**B. Inference Time:**
- **Real-time Efficiency:** This is one of FRMD's most significant contributions. FRMD achieved an average inference time of **17.2ms**, making it dramatically faster than both baselines.
- **Speed Comparison:** FRMD was **10 times faster** than its teacher model MPD (168.6ms) and **7 times faster** than vanilla Diffusion Policy (119.7ms).
- **Consistency Across Tasks:** FRMD maintained consistently low latency across all task difficulties: 15.2ms (easy), 18.6ms (medium), and 17.9ms (hard). This highlights its capability for efficient, high-quality action generation in real-time scenarios.

**C. Motion Quality:**
- **Reduced Non-Smooth Transitions:** To quantify smoothness, the curvature of the end-effector trajectory was used, with transitions having curvature $k_i > 1$ classified as non-smooth. In a qualitative visualization for the `PlugCharger-v1` task, FRMD produced significantly smoother trajectories. The number of non-smooth transitions was $N_{FRMD} = 21$, a substantial improvement over $N_{DP} = 82$ for Diffusion Policy. This visually confirms that FRMD generates motions with improved stability and fewer oscillations, particularly near start and goal points, due to its ProDMP foundation and consistency distillation.

**D. Ablation Study:**
- **Backbone Architecture:** An ablation study investigated the impact of different backbone network architectures (Transformer, lightweight Transformer, MLP) for the denoiser.
 - The original Transformer-based architecture (used in FRMD) yielded the highest success rates, albeit with slightly higher inference time.
 - A lightweight Transformer (`Transformer*`) provided a good trade-off, achieving comparable success rates with slightly lower inference times.
 - A simple MLP network was computationally efficient but significantly underperformed on complex tasks, demonstrating the importance of the Transformer's ability to capture temporal dependencies.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that FRMD successfully addresses the primary limitations of existing diffusion-based methods. It generates significantly faster and smoother trajectories while achieving superior or comparable task success rates across a diverse set of manipulation tasks. The one-step inference capability makes it suitable for real-time robotic control, marking a crucial advancement in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Fast Robot Motion Diffusion (FRMD) represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for applications requiring real-time, high-quality motion generation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**A. Bridging the Gap for Real-time Robotic Control:**
The most immediate and impactful contribution of FRMD is its ability to achieve real-time inference for diffusion-based policies. By drastically reducing inference latency (10x faster than MPD and 7x faster than DP, down to an average of 17.2ms), FRMD transforms diffusion models from powerful but slow generative tools into viable candidates for online, real-time robot control. This is critical for tasks requiring immediate reactive behavior, such as human-robot interaction, fast manipulation, or dynamic environments where delays can lead to failures or unsafe conditions.

**B. Enhanced Motion Quality and Safety:**
By integrating Probabilistic Dynamic Movement Primitives (ProDMPs), FRMD inherently generates structured, smooth, and temporally consistent trajectories. This addresses the prevalent issue of jerky or non-smooth motions often produced by raw action sequence generation in other diffusion policies. Smoother motions are not only more aesthetically pleasing but also crucial for tasks involving delicate objects, human safety, energy efficiency, and reducing wear and tear on robot hardware. The significant reduction in "non-smooth transitions" observed in FRMD's outputs directly contributes to more robust and reliable robotic operation.

**C. Unified Framework for Generative and Structured Control:**
FRMD successfully unifies the strengths of modern generative models (diffusion models) with classical, well-understood structured motion representations (movement primitives). This hybrid approach capitalizes on the scalability and multi-modal flexibility of diffusion models for learning complex action distributions from demonstrations, while leveraging the inherent smoothness and temporal consistency guarantees of ProDMPs. This paves the way for future robot learning systems that can learn complex skills from large datasets while still producing physically plausible and controllable actions.

**D. Scalability and Applicability to Complex Tasks:**
The results on Meta-World and ManiSkill benchmarks, spanning easy to hard manipulation tasks, demonstrate FRMD's robustness and generalization capabilities. Its consistent performance across varying complexities suggests that the framework is well-suited for more challenging real-world scenarios. The authors also explicitly highlight the modularity of their action decoder design, suggesting that FRMD can be seamlessly integrated into large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (like $\pi^0$). This implies a significant potential for scaling up to even more complex, high-dimensional tasks, allowing these powerful VLA models to output actions that are both intelligent and practically executable in real-time.

**E. Catalyst for Future Research:**
FRMD opens several avenues for future research. The authors plan to extend FRMD to higher-dimensional tasks and integrate task-specific cost functions into the consistency training framework. This could lead to more nuanced and optimized robot behaviors. Furthermore, the success of consistency distillation in this robotics context encourages its broader adoption for accelerating other slow, iterative processes in robot learning, beyond just action generation.

**F. Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its significant contributions, FRMD acknowledges a limitation: it has not yet been tested on large-scale task pre-training with large datasets and extensively parameterized networks due to time constraints. However, the modular nature of the action decoder design is presented as a strong argument for its compatibility and potential synergistic benefits when paired with powerful pre-trained encoders from VLA models. This suggests that the current work is a stepping stone towards even more capable and scalable robot intelligence systems.

In conclusion, FRMD stands as a pivotal development, addressing core practical limitations that have hindered the widespread deployment of diffusion-based robot policies. By enabling fast, smooth, and high-quality action generation, it moves the field closer to realizing robust, real-time, and general-purpose robotic autonomy.
