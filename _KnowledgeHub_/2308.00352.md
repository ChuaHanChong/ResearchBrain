# MetaGPT: Meta Programming for A Multi-Agent Collaborative Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> MetaGPT is a multi-agent framework that encodes human Standardized Operating Procedures into prompt sequences, assigning LLM agents software-company roles that pass structured documents through a shared message pool. An executable feedback loop lets the Engineer run and debug its own code. It reaches 85.9% Pass@1 on HumanEval and 87.7% on MBPP, and beats ChatDev on SoftwareDev.

## Problem

- Chat-based LLM multi-agent systems rely on unconstrained natural language, so information distorts across turns like a telephone game and produces cascading hallucinations and logic inconsistencies on complex tasks.
- Prior role-play frameworks such as CAMEL and ChatDev lack effective workflows with structured output formats, so they struggle with realistic software engineering tasks beyond simple dialogue.
- Earlier self-correction relied on non-executable code review or self-reflection, which cannot guarantee code executability or runtime correctness because LLM hallucinations let errors slip through review.

## Method

- ==SOP-encoded assembly line==: five roles (Product Manager, Architect, Project Manager, Engineer, QA Engineer) work sequentially, each with a profile, goal and constraints, producing PRDs, system designs and task lists.
- ==Structured communication== with a ==shared message pool== and ==publish-subscribe==: agents exchange documents and diagrams rather than dialogue, and subscribe only to role-relevant messages, which avoids information overload.
- Iterative programming with ==executable feedback==: the Engineer writes code and unit tests, runs them, and debugs using its execution history and the PRD and design, retrying up to 3 times.

## Results

- With GPT-4, MetaGPT reaches **85.9%** Pass@1 on HumanEval and **87.7%** on MBPP, exceeding GPT-4 at **67.0%** on HumanEval and Codex plus CodeT at **65.8%** and **67.7%**.
- On SoftwareDev versus ChatDev, executability is **3.75** versus **2.25** on a 4-point scale, time is **541s** versus **762s**, and tokens per code line are **124.3** versus **248.9**.
- Executable feedback adds **4.2%** Pass@1 on HumanEval and **5.4%** on MBPP and cuts human revisions from **2.25** to **0.83**. Adding roles raises executability from **1.0** with Engineer only to **4.0** with four roles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured intermediate artifacts (PRDs, designs, interface specs) between agents beat free-form chat, reducing hallucination propagation. Constraining handoff format is a cheap, general lever for multi-agent reliability.
> - Decomposing work by role adds cost only modestly: ablations show more specialized roles steadily improve executability and cut human revisions, while total expense rises just slightly.
> - Grounding self-correction in real execution feedback beats LLM self-review alone, since tests give objective error signals. This pattern transfers to any agent task with a runnable verifier.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

MetaGPT operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Model (LLM)-based autonomous agents and multi-agent systems. The advent of powerful LLMs has spurred remarkable progress in automated problem-solving, with early systems demonstrating capabilities in simple dialogue tasks and collaborative scenarios. However, the paper identifies several critical limitations in existing LLM-based multi-agent frameworks, particularly when tackling more complex problems:

- **Logic Inconsistencies and Cascading Hallucinations:** Naively chaining LLMs often leads to accumulated errors and incoherent solutions, as each LLM interaction can introduce inaccuracies that propagate through the system.
- **Oversimplification of Complex Tasks:** Current systems tend to struggle with the inherent complexities of real-world collaborative workflows, failing to achieve effective, coherent, and accurate problem-solving.
- **Inefficient Communication:** Unconstrained natural language communication among agents can lead to ambiguities, information overload, "assistant repeated instruction," or "infinite loops of messages," hindering efficient collaboration.
- **Lack of Self-Correction:** Many existing frameworks lack robust mechanisms for self-correction and debugging, particularly in practical applications like code generation where executability and runtime correctness are paramount.

The research landscape includes several notable precedents and contemporary works that MetaGPT builds upon or distinguishes itself from:
- **LLM-based Agents:** Projects like AutoGPT, LangChain, AgentVerse, ChatDev, ReAct (reasoning and acting), and Reflexion (dynamic memory and self-reflection) have explored various facets of autonomous agents. MetaGPT acknowledges their general problem-solving capabilities but points out their limitations in systematically deconstructing complex requirements.
- **Automatic Programming:** The paper traces the roots of automatic programming back to the 1960s (e.g., PROW) and highlights recent LLM-driven advancements like CodeBERT, CodeLlama, WizardCoder, and commercial tools like Microsoft Copilot. MetaGPT distinguishes itself by integrating these code generation capabilities within a well-organized, specialized multi-agent framework rather than relying on a single LLM or simple role-play.
- **Multi-Agent Collaboration:** Works like Generative Agents (simulating a town), NL-SOM (Natural Language-Based Society of Mind), and others focusing on discussions and debates among agents have explored social phenomena and emergent behaviors. MetaGPT, however, focuses on introducing *human practice* and *structured workflows* into these multi-agent frameworks, specifically addressing task-oriented collaborations that demand consistency and mutual benefit.

MetaGPT's unique contribution lies in its **meta-programming framework** that explicitly incorporates **Standardized Operating Procedures (SOPs)** and an **assembly line paradigm** inspired by human professional workflows, especially in software engineering. By encoding these SOPs into structured prompt sequences, requiring structured outputs, and implementing an iterative executable feedback loop, MetaGPT aims to mitigate the issues of hallucinations, inconsistencies, and inefficient communication that plague less structured multi-agent systems. This approach allows MetaGPT to generate more coherent, functional, and higher-quality solutions for complex software development tasks, pushing the boundaries of what LLM-based agents can achieve in practical, structured problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind MetaGPT stems from the observed shortcomings of existing LLM-based multi-agent systems in handling complex, real-world problems. While these systems show promise in simpler tasks, their performance degrades significantly when faced with intricate, multi-step challenges due to issues such as logical inconsistencies, "cascading hallucinations" from loosely chained LLMs, and an oversimplified understanding of complex workflows. The authors recognize that human collaborative practices, particularly the adoption of Standardized Operating Procedures (SOPs), effectively address these very challenges in real-world professional environments.

Given this, the key objectives of the MetaGPT project are:

1. **Introduce an Innovative Meta-Programming Framework:** Develop a novel framework that can effectively "program to program" by leveraging LLMs within a multi-agent setup, going beyond simple code generation to encompass the entire software development lifecycle.
2. **Incorporate Efficient Human Workflows (SOPs):** Design the framework to explicitly encode and follow human-like Standardized Operating Procedures (SOPs) into prompt sequences. This aims to streamline workflows, ensure structured intermediate outputs, and reduce errors by enabling agents with domain expertise to verify results.
3. **Implement an Assembly Line Paradigm:** Break down complex tasks into subtasks by assigning diverse, specialized roles to various agents. This mirrors an assembly line, where each agent contributes specific expertise and follows defined responsibilities, improving task decomposition and coordination.
4. **Enhance Coherence and Accuracy:** Overcome the logic inconsistencies and cascading hallucinations prevalent in naive LLM chaining by enforcing structured communication and sequential, role-based processing. The goal is to generate more coherent and accurate solutions, particularly for complex software engineering benchmarks.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Demonstrate the practical efficacy of the MetaGPT framework by achieving superior performance on established code generation benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP) and a new, more challenging software development benchmark (SoftwareDev).
6. **Integrate an Executable Feedback Mechanism:** Introduce a robust self-correction mechanism that allows agents, specifically the Engineer, to debug and execute generated code during runtime. This iterative process aims to significantly elevate the quality and executability of the final code.
7. **Provide a Flexible and Convenient Platform:** Create a multi-agent system that is not only robust but also highly convenient and flexible for developing LLM-based multi-agent systems, featuring well-defined functions like role definition and message sharing.

In essence, MetaGPT aims to bridge the gap between the raw generative power of LLMs and the structured, reliable collaboration seen in human teams, thereby enabling LLM-based agents to tackle complex, real-world problems with unprecedented effectiveness and accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MetaGPT's methodology is founded on the principle of mirroring human organizational structures and established workflows, particularly within the context of software development. It essentially simulates a software company with specialized roles, standardized procedures, and structured communication.

The core components of MetaGPT's approach are:

- **Agents in Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs):**
 - **Specialization of Roles:** MetaGPT defines distinct, unambiguous roles for its agents, akin to departments or specialists in a human company. For software development, these roles include Product Manager, Architect, Project Manager, Engineer, and QA Engineer. Each role is given a specific "profile" including its name, goal, constraints, and initial context/skills (e.g., Product Manager can use web search, Engineer can execute code). Agents adopt a ReAct-style behavior, continuously monitoring their environment (message pool) for relevant observations to trigger actions or inform their work.
 - **Workflow across Agents:** The framework strictly adheres to a sequential workflow inspired by real-world software development SOPs. This creates an "assembly line" for complex tasks:
 1. **Product Manager:** Receives user requirements, performs analysis, and generates a detailed Product Requirements Document (PRD) with user stories and requirement pools.
 2. **Architect:** Translates the PRD into technical specifications, including system architecture diagrams, file lists, data structures, and interface definitions.
 3. **Project Manager:** Breaks down the system design into a task list, assigning individual code files or functionalities as separate tasks to Engineers.
 4. **Engineer:** Implements the designated classes and functions based on the system design and task list.
 5. **QA Engineer:** Formulates and runs test cases to ensure stringent code quality, identifying and fixing bugs.
 6. **Final Output:** MetaGPT produces a meticulously crafted software solution.

- **Communication Protocol:**
 - **Structured Communication Interfaces:** A key innovation is moving away from unconstrained natural language dialogue, which can lead to "telephone game" effects and distortions. Instead, agents are required to generate *structured outputs* (e.g., PRDs, system design documents, sequence flow diagrams, API specifications). These structured documents serve as clear, unambiguous handovers between roles, minimizing ambiguities and errors during collaboration.
 - **Publish-Subscribe Mechanism:** To prevent information overload and facilitate efficient sharing, MetaGPT introduces a shared global "message pool." Agents publish their structured messages (outputs) into this pool. Critically, agents don't indiscriminately consume all information; they "subscribe" to relevant messages based on their role profiles, ensuring they receive only task-related information. An agent only activates its action after receiving all its prerequisite dependencies, ensuring logical flow and reducing unnecessary "chatter."

- **Iterative Programming with Executable Feedback:**
 - This mechanism addresses the challenge of ensuring code executability and correctness, which non-executable code reviews often miss. After initial code generation by the Engineer, the code is executed and tested (e.g., with unit tests).
 - If errors occur, the Engineer agent self-corrects by reviewing its own historical execution and debugging memory, cross-referencing past messages (PRD, system design) and code files. This iterative testing and debugging loop continues until tests pass or a maximum retry limit is reached, mimicking a human developer's debugging process.

By combining role specialization, a strict SOP-driven workflow, structured communication, and an active debugging loop, MetaGPT creates a highly organized and robust multi-agent system designed for generating complex, functional software.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MetaGPT's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated across standard code generation benchmarks and a more challenging, comprehensive software development dataset. The results demonstrate significant improvements over existing methods.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance on Code Generation Benchmarks:**
 - **HumanEval:** MetaGPT achieves an impressive 85.9% Pass@1 rate, outperforming all previous approaches, including standalone GPT-4 (67.0%), AlphaCode (17.1%), Codex (47.0%), and CodeGeeX (32.9%).
 - **MBPP:** Similarly, MetaGPT secures a Pass@1 rate of 87.7%, again surpassing other models like GPT-4 (no specific number given for MBPP, but generally MetaGPT is higher), PaLM Coder (47.0%), and Codex+CodeT (67.7%).
 - These results highlight MetaGPT's superior ability to generate correct and functional code on single-file programming tasks.

- **Superior Performance on SoftwareDev Benchmark (Complex Software Projects):**
 - The self-generated SoftwareDev dataset (70 tasks, with 7 representative tasks used for comparison) is designed to evaluate the ability to create complete software projects, not just snippets.
 - **Executability:** MetaGPT achieves an average executability score of 3.75 (on a 1-4 scale, with 4 being flawless), indicating highly functional code. This dramatically surpasses ChatDev (2.25) and open-source baselines like AutoGPT, LangChain, and AgentVerse (all scoring 1.0, signifying complete failure to generate executable code). This is a critical practical result, showing MetaGPT's ability to produce usable software.
 - **Task Completion Rate:** MetaGPT demonstrates a 100% task completion rate on the SoftwareDev benchmark, highlighting its robustness and efficiency in tackling complex development tasks end-to-end.
 - **Efficiency and Productivity:**
 - **Running Times:** MetaGPT completes tasks faster (503-541 seconds) compared to ChatDev (762 seconds).
 - **Token Usage vs. Code Lines (Productivity):** While MetaGPT uses more tokens overall (24,613-31,255 vs. ChatDev's 19,292), it generates significantly more lines of code (194.6-251.4 vs. ChatDev's 77.5). This translates to much higher "productivity" (tokens per line of code): MetaGPT uses 124.3-126.5 tokens per line, whereas ChatDev uses 248.9 tokens, indicating more efficient conversion of LLM cost into functional code.
 - **Human Revision Cost:** MetaGPT significantly reduces the need for manual corrections, with a human revision cost of 0.83, compared to ChatDev's 2.5, suggesting the generated code is closer to a production-ready state.
 - **Code Statistics:** MetaGPT generates more code files and total lines of code, implying it handles higher levels of software complexity and offers more extensive functionality compared to ChatDev.

- **Capabilities Analysis:**
 - MetaGPT comprehensively supports various software engineering capabilities, including PRD generation, technical design generation, API interface generation, precompilation execution, role-based task management, and code review. Many of these features are absent or less developed in other prominent agent frameworks like AutoGPT, LangChain, and AgentVerse.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effectiveness of Roles:** Progressively adding more specialized roles (Product Manager, Architect, Project Manager) to the basic Engineer agent consistently improves executability and reduces human revision costs, validating the importance of role specialization and collaborative workflows.
 - **Effectiveness of Executable Feedback:** The iterative executable feedback mechanism proves crucial, leading to absolute improvements of 4.2% and 5.4% in Pass@1 on HumanEval and MBPP respectively. It also boosts executability (from 3.67 to 3.75) and dramatically lowers human revision costs (from 2.25 to 0.83) on SoftwareDev.
 - **Impact of LLMs and Instruction Levels:** MetaGPT performs best with GPT-4 as the backend LLM, although it can function with GPT-3.5 and Deepseek Coder. Additionally, while detailed user prompts lead to slightly better results, MetaGPT demonstrates robustness in generating good software even from high-level, concise instructions.

In summary, MetaGPT not only achieves new state-of-the-art results in code generation but also showcases a robust and highly effective framework for automating complex software development tasks by systematically integrating human-like collaborative practices and iterative refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MetaGPT represents a significant leap forward in the field of LLM-based autonomous agents and automated software engineering. Its significance and potential impact can be understood across several dimensions:

- **Paradigm Shift in Multi-Agent System Design:** By successfully integrating human-like Standardized Operating Procedures (SOPs) into multi-agent workflows, MetaGPT offers a robust blueprint for overcoming common challenges like hallucinations, logical inconsistencies, and inefficient communication. This "human-inspired" approach, moving beyond naive LLM chaining or unconstrained dialogue, demonstrates that structure and specialization are critical for scaling LLM capabilities to complex, collaborative tasks. This could inspire similar structured frameworks for multi-agent systems in diverse domains beyond software development.

- **Advancing Automated Software Engineering:** MetaGPT pushes the boundaries of automated programming from generating code snippets to creating entire functional software projects. Its ability to produce high-quality, executable code with minimal human intervention (evidenced by 100% task completion and low human revision costs on complex benchmarks) has profound implications for software development productivity. It effectively automates requirement analysis, system design, code generation, debugging, and testing, potentially accelerating development cycles and reducing the manual effort required for iterative refinement.

- **Empowering "Natural Language Programming":** MetaGPT makes programming more accessible by allowing users to define complex software requirements in natural language. This democratizes software creation, potentially enabling individuals with limited coding expertise to develop sophisticated applications. As the authors note, just as programming evolved from punched cards to high-level languages, natural language could be the next frontier, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for software creation.

- **Foundation for Future AI Systems and Research:**
 - **Self-Improvement and Meta-Learning:** The paper explicitly discusses future work on self-improvement mechanisms, where agents learn from past projects and recursively refine their operational constraints, drawing connections to Jürgen Schmidhuber's pioneering work on G¨odel machines and meta-learning. This opens avenues for truly autonomous and continuously improving AI systems that adapt and evolve their own "programming."
 - **Multi-Agent Economies:** The concept of "Multi-Agent Economies" (EOMs), where agents provide services and are compensated based on contributions to collective success, is highlighted as a future direction. DeepWisdom's "AgentStore" platform is presented as compatible with this vision, suggesting a marketplace for AI services and collaborative development environments driven by economic principles. This could lead to highly dynamic and self-organizing AI ecosystems.
 - **Regulating LLM-based Multi-Agent Frameworks:** The structured nature of MetaGPT, with its clear roles and protocols, can be seen as an early attempt to "regulate" the behavior of LLM-based multi-agent systems, ensuring more predictable, controllable, and reliable outcomes.

- **Addressing Deep-Seated AI Challenges:** MetaGPT's design actively mitigates persistent problems in LLM applications, such as efficient context utilization (through structured messages and subscription mechanisms) and reduction of hallucinations (by breaking tasks into granular, guided steps and incorporating executable feedback). This contributes to building more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

- **Ethical Considerations and Societal Impact:** The paper proactively addresses ethical concerns, particularly regarding potential unemployment and skill obsolescence. It argues that natural language programming may increase the demand for programming-related roles by making it more accessible. Furthermore, MetaGPT's open-source nature, human oversight capabilities (initiate, observe, stop), and local operation for privacy demonstrate a commitment to transparency, accountability, and user control.

In conclusion, MetaGPT is not just an incremental improvement but a conceptual breakthrough that offers a blueprint for building more intelligent, robust, and collaborative AI agents by integrating established human organizational intelligence. Its potential impact extends across research, software development practices, and the broader accessibility of AI technologies.
