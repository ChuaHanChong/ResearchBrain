# No More Blind Spots: Learning Vision-Based Omnidirectional Bipedal Locomotion for Challenging Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Oregon State University developed a learning framework for robust, vision-based omnidirectional bipedal locomotion, enabling robots to navigate challenging terrains using direct depth camera inputs. The approach achieved comparable performance to a privileged teacher policy, outperforming blind controllers in success rate and energy efficiency on complex terrains, and demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Cassie robot, while also accelerating student training by up to 10 times.

## Problem

- Existing "blind" bipedal locomotion controllers lack proactive terrain adaptation, failing on complex or unforeseen obstacles due to an absence of environmental sensing.
- Training vision-based bipedal locomotion policies, particularly for omnidirectional movement with multiple cameras, incurs prohibitively high computational costs due to intensive depth rendering in reinforcement learning.
- Robustly transferring complex vision-based policies trained in simulation to physical bipedal robots in diverse real-world conditions remains a challenge.

## Method

- A student-teacher distillation framework where a "privileged" teacher learns from low-cost height maps via PPO, and a "student" policy learns to mimic the teacher using rendered depth images, reducing rendering overhead.
- A hierarchical policy architecture that combines a pre-trained, frozen blind controller with a trainable differential action network, leveraging stable low-level skills for terrain adaptation.
- Novel data augmentation techniques, including velocity command variation and vision augmentations, to enhance student training efficiency, generalization, and robustness to real-world sensor noise.

## Results

- The vision-based student policy achieved success rates over **85%** on challenging terrains, closely matching the privileged teacher and significantly outperforming blind controllers, with fewer foot collisions and lower energy consumption.
- The proposed training methodology, including the pre-trained blind policy and data augmentation, reduced student policy training time to 20 hours (110 million samples), representing a 10-fold speedup compared to conventional methods.
- Successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated on a Cassie bipedal robot, achieving robust omnidirectional traversal over varied structured terrains (e.g., stepping onto 0.5m blocks forward) with end-to-end latency under 20 milliseconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling expensive vision rendering from the intensive reinforcement learning phase through a student-teacher framework significantly reduces computational overhead, making vision-based policy training feasible for omnidirectional locomotion.
> - The integration of a pre-trained blind controller provides a stable baseline for basic locomotion, allowing the vision-based policy to primarily focus its learning on proactive terrain adaptation using perceptual inputs.
> - An innovative data augmentation strategy, varying velocity commands for existing trajectories, dramatically accelerates student training convergence and improves sample efficiency without requiring additional rendering time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Legged locomotion research has extensively focused on enabling robots to traverse natural and unstructured terrains. Recent advancements in learning-based approaches, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), have led to robust "blind" locomotion controllers. These controllers primarily rely on proprioceptive feedback to maintain balance and momentum, demonstrating effectiveness on well-modeled or homogeneous surfaces. However, their performance diminishes when confronted with unforeseen obstacles, complex, or uneven terrains, as they lack the ability to sense or anticipate environmental changes.

To address these limitations, vision-based locomotion controllers have emerged, utilizing exteroceptive sensors such as cameras or depth sensors to provide environmental information. These controllers aim to enable robots to adapt to terrain variations and proactively adjust foot placements, thereby improving stability and efficiency. While vision-based methods offer enhanced agility and adaptability, they introduce challenges related to perception, sim-to-real transfer, and high-dimensional policy learning.

A significant hurdle in training end-to-end vision-based locomotion controllers, especially with RL, is the high computational cost associated with rendering realistic visual inputs in simulation. This cost is amplified in omnidirectional locomotion, which requires processing a larger volume of visual data from multiple sensors. Prior research on vision-based bipedal locomotion has often focused on forward or constrained directional movement to simplify perception and control, or has relied on simplified perceptive inputs like height maps. While height maps reduce rendering costs, they require robust estimation and sensor calibration, which can be complex, particularly in an omnidirectional setup involving multiple cameras.

This research contributes to the landscape by proposing a framework that specifically addresses the computational burden of rendering omnidirectional depth images during RL training and the complexities of sim-to-real transfer for bipedal robots. It extends existing student-teacher distillation approaches, which have seen success in quadrupedal locomotion, to the more computationally intensive domain of omnidirectional bipedal locomotion using direct depth camera inputs, without requiring explicit height map estimation at test time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a learning framework that enables robust, vision-based omnidirectional bipedal locomotion across challenging terrains. This involves allowing a bipedal robot to move seamlessly in all directions (forward, backward, sideways, and turning) using direct depth images from multiple cameras to perceive its environment.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations and challenges identified in the current state of bipedal locomotion robotics:

- **Limitations of Blind Controllers:** While blind locomotion controllers offer a foundation for stable movement, they are inherently reactive and unable to anticipate or effectively navigate complex, unstructured, or obstacle-rich environments. For practical applications in dynamic environments like cluttered indoor spaces or natural terrains, robots require proactive adaptation based on environmental sensing.
- **Need for Omnidirectional Agility:** Many real-world applications demand robots capable of moving and adapting in all directions, not just forward. Prior vision-based bipedal locomotion efforts have largely focused on constrained or forward movement, simplifying the perception and control problems. Achieving full omnidirectional agility with visual input on challenging terrain remains a significant and unresolved challenge.
- **High Computational Cost of Vision-Based RL:** Training vision-based controllers using reinforcement learning (RL) is computationally demanding. Rendering high-fidelity depth or RGB images in simulation is resource-intensive and slows down the training process. This cost is significantly exacerbated when considering omnidirectional perception, which necessitates multiple cameras and thus an even greater volume of visual data to render and process within the RL loop. This renders traditional end-to-end RL impractical for such scenarios.
- **Challenges of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Policies trained in simulation must be robust to real-world discrepancies, including sensor noise, actuator delays, and the absence of privileged information often available during simulation-based training. Ensuring that a vision-based policy trained in simulation can effectively transfer to and operate on a physical robot is a crucial practical consideration.
- **Desire for Direct Vision Input:** While approaches using height maps or elevation maps simplify training by avoiding expensive visual rendering, they often rely on careful sensor calibration and robust height map estimation, which can introduce additional complexity and potential points of failure, especially with multiple cameras in an omnidirectional setup. The authors seek a solution that directly utilizes camera input, reducing reliance on such intermediate representations and complex calibrations at test time.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a framework designed to balance computational efficiency with robust real-world performance by minimizing reliance on expensive depth rendering during training. This is achieved through the integration of a pre-trained blind controller, a student-teacher distillation approach with DAgger, and a novel data augmentation technique.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors formulate the omnidirectional terrain-aware locomotion problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The state space encompasses proprioceptive observations (joint positions/velocities, base orientation/velocities), exteroceptive observations (height maps for teacher, depth images for student), and user commands. The action space consists of low-level PD position setpoints for the robot's 10 actuators. The objective is to learn a policy that maximizes an expected cumulative reward for stable and efficient locomotion.

The core of the methodology is a novel learning framework designed to minimize the computational cost of visual rendering during training while ensuring robust sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Approach Overview (Student-Teacher Framework with Enhancements)**

The framework builds upon the student-teacher distillation approach, extended with DAgger, but specifically tailored to reduce rendering overhead.

1. **Teacher Policy Training:** A "privileged" teacher policy is trained using simulation-intensive Reinforcement Learning (Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO). Crucially, the teacher is provided with easily accessible height maps and environmental state information, which have minimal computational overhead to generate, thereby avoiding expensive depth rendering during the intensive RL phase.
2. **Student Policy Training:** A "student" policy, designed for real-world deployment, is trained via supervised imitation learning (policy distillation) to mimic the teacher's actions. The student's input consists of rendered depth images, which are only required during this supervised learning phase, not the more computationally intensive RL phase.
3. **Hierarchical Policy Architecture:** Both student and teacher policies utilize a hybrid neural network architecture. It combines a pre-trained, frozen blind locomotion controller (adapted from) that provides basic balance and movement with a trainable differential action network. This differential network, conditioned on perceptual input, modulates the blind controller's output, allowing the policy to leverage stable low-level skills while adapting to complex terrains.
4. **Data Augmentation for Student Training:** A novel data augmentation technique is introduced during student training. This technique increases the effective training set size without requiring additional computationally expensive depth rendering or simulation steps, thereby accelerating training and improving generalization.

**B. Policy Architectures**

Both policies share a common structure, differing primarily in their vision encoders.

- **Observation Space:** Includes robot proprioceptive state (joint positions/velocities, base orientation/angular velocity), perceptive input (privileged height map for teacher, egocentric depth images from four cameras for student), and user control commands (desired linear X/Y velocities, angular yaw velocity, base height).
- **Action Space:** PD position setpoints for 10 actuators, output at 50Hz for low-level PD controllers running at 2 kHz.
- **Network Components:**
 - **Pre-Trained Blind Locomotion Controller:** A 2-layered LSTM network (64 hidden units) trained on flat terrain with perturbations, provides a robust baseline. This component is frozen during omnidirectional training.
 - **Proprioceptive and Command Encoder:** A 64-dimensional linear layer encodes proprioceptive states and user commands.
 - **Teacher Vision Encoder:** Processes four concatenated privileged height maps using a two-layer MLP (256, 128 units) into a 128-dimensional perceptual embedding.
 - **Student Vision Encoder:** Utilizes a shared-parameter ResNet-18 convolutional network to encode each of the four depth images independently. Individual MLPs project these encodings to 128 dimensions, which are then concatenated and passed through a final MLP to produce a single 128-dimensional perceptual embedding.
 - **Differential Action Network:** Concatenates the proprioceptive-command vector, the perceptual embedding, and the blind controller's action. This combined representation feeds into a two-layer LSTM network (128 hidden units per layer) to compute a differential action vector.
 - **Action Output:** The final action is the sum of the differential action and the blind controller's baseline action.

**C. Privileged Teacher Training**

- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) actor-critic algorithm.
- **Simulation Environment:** MuJoCo, using 120 parallel cores.
- **Training Episode Generation:**
 - Randomly generated 30m x 30m terrain maps drawn from five types: Flat, Hills, Blocks, Ridges, and Stairs, with specific sampling probabilities to balance stability and complex terrain exposure.
 - Randomization parameters for terrain features (height, length, width, steps) are detailed to avoid feature overlap.
 - The height map provided to the teacher is a 3m x 2m area centered on the robot, rendered at 5cm resolution, relative to the robot's base.
 - Robot starts in a random pose and receives random velocity commands (X velocity: [-0.6, 1.0] m/s, Y velocity: [-0.45, 0.45] m/s, yaw rate: [-22.5, 22.5] deg/s, base height: [0.4, 0.95] m). Commands are modified once per episode.
 - Episodes run for up to 450 timesteps (9 seconds), with early termination conditions for exceeding base roll/pitch, excessive linear velocity, low base height, or body collisions.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function promoting smooth, stable, and transferable locomotion:
 - `R_0` (Base Locomotion Reward): Adapted from, encourages target command following, balance, and regular stepping.
 - `R_feet` (Foot Collision Penalty): Penalizes unwanted foot-terrain collisions during swing phase (-5 per contact).
 - `R_con` (Conrod Contact Penalty): Penalizes contact between the robot's conrods and the terrain to maintain safe leg posture.
- **PPO Modifications:**
 - **Mirror Loss:** Included in the PPO loss to encourage symmetric actions for symmetric terrain inputs.
 - **Privileged Critic Inputs:** The critic network receives additional privileged information (robot height, global foot positions, height map around each foot, multiple rangefinder sensors on legs) to improve reward prediction for complex terrain interactions.

**D. Student Training**

- **Strategy:** Policy distillation using the DAGGER training strategy.
- **Process:** Student (initially random) generates actions, but the privileged teacher provides supervisory data at each step. The student is trained via random mini-batches to mimic the teacher's actions and perceptual encodings.
- **Loss Function:** Combines two Mean Squared Error (MSE) terms:
 1. MSE between teacher and student actions.
 2. MSE between teacher's perceptual encoding and student's perceptual encoding, accelerating learning by directly supervising the vision encoder.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Depth rendering is performed only for data collection during student training, not in the RL loop.
- **Data Augmentation:**
 - **Velocity Command Variation:** For each collected trajectory, new velocity commands (X velocity: [-0.6, 1.0] m/s, Y velocity: [-0.6, 0.6] m/s, Turn rate: [-0.4, 0.4] rad/s) are sampled and injected into student/teacher input states. The student then minimizes the KL divergence between resulting actions under these perturbed commands. This multiplies training examples without additional rendering.
 - **Vision Augmentations:** Standard augmentations (Gaussian noise, dropout, random jittering) are applied to student depth images to improve robustness to real-world sensor noise and imperfections.

**E. Domain Randomization**

Extensive domain randomization is applied during training to enhance sim-to-real transfer and diversify training data. Parameters randomized include:
- **Sim Model:** Mass, center of mass (COM) location, spring stiffness, torque efficiency, and torque delay.
- **Height Map:** XY shift (per episode/step), Z shift (per episode/step), and perception delay.
These perturbations simulate real-world variability, sensor noise, and latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed framework through simulation experiments and real-world hardware deployment.

**A. Simulation Experimental Results: Policy Performance**

Performance was assessed by comparing four policy variants: a state-of-the-art blind controller (SaW), a blind policy trained on the diverse terrain distribution used for this work, the proposed Privileged Teacher policy, and the proposed vision-based Student policy. Evaluation was conducted on "easy" (aggregated across terrain types) and "hard" (broken down by ridge, stair, block) terrains using 100 episodes per variant.

- **Success Rate:** The Privileged Teacher consistently achieved a high success rate (over 85%) even on difficult terrains. The non-privileged Student policy closely matched the teacher's performance, notably outperforming both blind variants, particularly on stair-hard and block-hard terrains. Blind policies exhibited lower success rates on complex terrains.
- **Episodes with Foot Collisions & Terminations due to Foot Collisions:** Both the Teacher and Student policies demonstrated significantly fewer foot collisions and fewer terminations due to collisions compared to the blind policies. Blind policies experienced frequent collisions and high termination rates (up to 60% on block-hard terrain), indicating a lack of terrain anticipation. Teacher and Student policies maintained termination rates below 20% on all terrains.
- **Energy Consumption:** The blind policy, particularly when trained to generalize across diverse terrains without explicit terrain input, exhibited the highest energy consumption (over 35 kJ). This was attributed to a conservative gait strategy with high step heights and exaggerated motions. Both the Student and Privileged policies achieved significantly lower energy usage, indicating more efficient and smoother locomotion enabled by visual information.

**B. Simulation Experimental Results: Training Time Ablations**

The impact of the proposed training strategies on training duration was evaluated:

- **Teacher Training:** Initializing the teacher policy with the pre-trained blind policy reduced convergence time to 152 hours (1.3 billion samples) compared to 216 hours (2.0 billion samples) without it. This indicates that the blind policy provides an effective prior for exploration.
- **Student Training:** When utilizing both the pre-trained blind policy and the proposed data augmentation strategy, the student policy converged in 20 hours (110 million samples). Removing both components extended training to 144 hours (900 million samples).
- **Impact of Data Augmentation:** Training the student with the blind policy but *without* perceptual augmentation resulted in a convergence time of 96 hours (600 million samples). This finding highlights the substantial acceleration and improved sample efficiency attributed to the data augmentation technique.

**C. Hardware Deployment**

The proposed system was deployed on a Cassie bipedal robot equipped with four Intel RealSense D455 depth cameras for 360-degree terrain coverage.

- **Onboard Perception:** An NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano module processed depth images, running the ResNet-based vision encoder to generate a compact vision embedding at 90 Hz.
- **Policy Inference:** The vision embedding was transmitted to an Intel NUC, which executed the remainder of the policy network, combining the vision embedding, proprioceptive input, and control commands to compute motor actions.
- **Latency:** The end-to-end latency from vision pipeline to policy inference was measured to be under 20 milliseconds.
- **Real-World Performance:** The system successfully enabled Cassie to traverse structured terrains, including single high blocks, stairs, and randomized block configurations, in forward, sideways, and reverse directions. The robot demonstrated the ability to step up onto blocks as high as 0.5 meters in the forward direction. Due to morphological constraints (limited joint range of motion), maximum traversable heights were 0.35 meters for sideways motion and 0.2 meters in the reverse direction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a framework for vision-guided omnidirectional bipedal locomotion, addressing several challenges in robotics. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Enabling Vision-Based Omnidirectional Bipedal Locomotion:** To the authors' knowledge, this is the first demonstration of a framework specifically designed for robust, vision-guided omnidirectional bipedal locomotion using direct depth camera inputs. This moves beyond limitations of forward-only or constrained movement and dependency on global localization or precise height map estimation, paving the way for more agile and adaptable bipedal robots.
- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks in RL:** The proposed student-teacher training methodology, where the teacher learns from low-cost height maps and the student from rendered depth images, directly tackles the impractical computational overhead of including depth rendering within the reinforcement learning loop. This approach makes training vision-based bipedal policies more feasible within practical time and resource budgets.
- **Enhancing Training Efficiency and Generalization:** The innovative data augmentation strategy, which varies velocity commands for existing trajectories, significantly accelerates student training (up to 10 times faster compared to conventional methods without augmentation). Coupled with domain randomization, this technique improves sample efficiency, reduces rendering costs, and enhances the policy’s generalization capabilities to diverse real-world conditions and varying command inputs.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The combination of a hierarchical policy architecture (leveraging a pre-trained blind controller), extensive domain randomization, and targeted vision augmentations for the student policy contributes to robust sim-to-real transfer. This enables the complex vision-based behaviors learned in simulation to be effectively deployed on physical hardware.
- **Improved Locomotion Performance:** The simulation results demonstrate that the vision-aware student policy achieves comparable performance to a privileged teacher policy, significantly outperforming blind controllers in terms of success rate, reduction in foot collisions, and energy efficiency on challenging terrains. This indicates that integrating visual perception leads to more proactive, stable, and energetically optimized locomotion.
- **Practical Deployment on Bipedal Robots:** The successful hardware deployment on the Cassie bipedal robot, showcasing omnidirectional traversal capabilities over various structured terrains, validates the practicality and effectiveness of the proposed framework in real-world scenarios.

The implications of this research are substantial for the future development of bipedal robots. By providing a scalable and efficient method for training complex vision-based controllers, it contributes to making bipedal robots more autonomous, versatile, and capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic human-centric environments, such as disaster relief, domestic assistance, or exploration, where navigating challenging and unforeseen terrain in all directions is critical.
