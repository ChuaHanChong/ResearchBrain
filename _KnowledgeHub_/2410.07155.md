# Trans4D: Realistic Geometry-Aware Transition for Compositional Text-to-4D Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> TRANS4D synthesizes complex 4D scenes with realistic geometry-aware transitions from textual prompts, employing multimodal large language models for physics-aware scene planning and a dedicated network for dynamic object appearance and disappearance. The framework achieves higher visual quality, semantic alignment, and user preference compared to prior 4D generation methods.

## Problem

- Existing 4D scene generation methods struggle with complex object deformations and interactions, such as objects appearing, disappearing, or transforming significantly within a scene.
- Current LLM-guided 4D synthesis approaches provide only basic trajectory functions, lacking the detailed, physics-aware spatiotemporal planning needed for coherent and realistic complex scenes.
- Generating expressive geometrical object deformations (e.g., shape changes, coalescence, dissipation) is difficult for existing methods, which often treat objects as rigid or undergoing minor deformations.

## Method

- A "Physics-aware 4D Transition Planning" method leverages MLLMs to decompose prompts, derive spatiotemporal information, and generate detailed scene descriptions including initial positions, speeds, rotations, and transition times for multiple objects.
- A "Geometry-aware 4D Transition Network" (TransNet) controls the appearance and disappearance of individual ==3D Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) points over time, enabling expressive object deformation and transformation.
- An efficient two-phase training strategy first trains dynamics on sparse 3DGS models and then refines visual appearance on denser point clouds, balancing computational cost with high-quality 4D scene generation.

## Results

- TRANS4D quantitatively outperformed state-of-the-art baselines across metrics like QAlign-vid-quality (**3.226**), QAlign-vid-aesthetic (**2.148**), CLIP-score (**0.2941**), and Vid-MLLM-metrics (**0.6483**), indicating superior visual quality and semantic alignment.
- Qualitative results demonstrate the framework's ability to generate realistic and coherent 4D scenes with complex geometry-aware transitions, such as a missile exploding into smoke or a pigeon appearing from a hat.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of MLLM-based planning for rational 4D trajectories and the Transition Network for geometric expressiveness, with the two-phase training strategy significantly improving efficiency and visual fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can be guided to produce detailed, physically plausible spatiotemporal plans for 4D scenes, moving beyond basic prompt interpretation to generate complex initial conditions and transition timings.
> - Explicitly modeling the appearance and disappearance of individual points via a dedicated neural network allows for natural, geometry-aware object transitions, overcoming limitations of simple deformation fields.
> - A staged optimization approach, separating dynamic training from appearance refinement, provides an efficient pathway to generating high-quality 4D content with complex interactions while managing computational resources.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI has witnessed significant advancements, particularly with diffusion models demonstrating capabilities in image, video, and 3D content generation. Building upon these, 4D (3D over time) generation methods have emerged, aiming to create dynamic 3D objects or scenes.

Existing 4D generation techniques can be broadly categorized into:
- **Text-to-4D:** These methods generate 4D content from textual prompts. Early approaches often extend 3D objects into 4D using Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) guided by pre-trained video diffusion models. However, they frequently produce 4D objects with limited and sometimes unrealistic motion, constrained by the underlying video generation models.
- **Single-image-to-4D:** Similar to text-to-4D, these methods use a single image as a condition for 4D synthesis, often facing similar limitations in motion complexity.
- **Monocular-video-to-4D:** These methods leverage dynamics from video inputs to achieve higher quality and more natural motion for 4D object synthesis. A common limitation is the requirement for videos with clear foreground subjects, which can be challenging to acquire, and their focus remains primarily on individual objects rather than complex scenes.

While some text-to-4D and monocular-video-to-4D approaches, such as Comp4D and Dreamscene4D, have extended to 4D scene generation, they typically rely on deformation networks to simulate local object movements. This approach is limited in its capacity to handle significant object deformations or complex interactions within a scene, such as an object transforming into another or appearing/disappearing. For instance, creating a missile transforming into an explosion cloud or a magician conjuring a dancer presents challenges for these methods.

The broader research landscape also includes the increasing integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) into generative tasks. These models are being used for prompt enhancement, planning, and achieving more coherent and realistic outputs in image, video, and 3D generation. However, existing LLM/MLLM applications in 4D generation, such as Comp4D's use of LLMs for basic trajectory functions, have not fully addressed the intricacies of planning both temporal progression and complex spatial layout changes simultaneously, especially for geometric transitions.

The current work, TRANS4D, positions itself to address the gap in generating complex 4D scenes that involve significant geometry-aware transitions and interactions between multiple objects. It aims to overcome the limitations of existing methods by enabling the creation of dynamic scenes where objects can undergo substantial deformations, appear, or disappear in a physically plausible and visually coherent manner. It distinguishes itself by employing MLLMs for detailed, physics-aware scene planning beyond simple trajectories and introducing a dedicated network for geometry-aware transitions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a text-to-4D synthesis framework capable of generating realistic complex scene transitions, especially those involving significant object deformation and interaction, which current methods struggle to produce.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding existing 4D generation capabilities:
1. **Limitations of Existing 4D Scene Generation:** While methods like Comp4D have advanced 4D scene generation, they often rely on deformation networks that primarily adjust local coordinates to simulate movements. This approach is insufficient for scenarios requiring substantial changes in object geometry, such as an object completely transforming (e.g., a missile exploding into smoke) or an object appearing/disappearing within a scene (e.g., a pigeon emerging from a hat). Such complex, geometry-aware transitions are crucial for creating dynamic and engaging 4D narratives.
2. **Lack of Physics-aware Planning:** Existing LLM-guided 4D synthesis methods often provide basic trajectory functions, which may not capture the nuanced physical interactions, initial states, and precise timing required for complex 4D scenes. Without a detailed, physics-aware plan, generating coherent and realistic scene dynamics, especially during transitions, becomes challenging. There is a need for MLLMs to move beyond mere input description or recognition to actively generate detailed physical 4D information.
3. **Demand for Expressive Geometric Deformation:** Current methods often treat objects as rigid or undergoing minor deformations. Real-world dynamic scenes frequently involve objects that change shape significantly, coalesce, dissipate, or appear and vanish. Enabling such expressive geometrical object deformation within a 4D scene is essential for producing more natural and compelling results that can benefit industries like gaming and video production.
4. **Computational Efficiency in 4D Training:** The optimization of 4D scenes, especially with high-fidelity representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and reliance on video diffusion models (DM), can be computationally intensive and memory-demanding. A motivation is to develop an efficient training strategy that can yield high-quality results without prohibitive resource requirements.

To address these motivations, TRANS4D sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To leverage multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to produce a "physics-aware scene description" for 4D scene initialization and effective transition timing planning. This includes generating detailed physical information such as initial positions, movement/rotation speeds, and transition times for multiple objects.
- To propose a "geometry-aware 4D transition network" that can realize complex scene-level 4D transitions by controlling the appearance and disappearance of individual points within 3DGS models, thereby enabling significant object deformation.
- To integrate these components into a novel text-to-4D synthesis framework that can stably generate high-quality 4D scenes even when complex interactions or significant deformations among multiple objects are present.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TRANS4D is designed as a text-to-4D synthesis framework focusing on geometry-aware transitions and complex scene interactions. Its methodology comprises two primary components: Physics-aware 4D Transition Planning and a Geometry-aware 4D Transition Network, supported by an efficient training and refinement strategy.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework builds upon 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the representation for 3D objects. A 3DGS model consists of N Gaussian points, each defined by a center position, covariance, opacity, and color. For text-to-4D generation, the input is a text prompt, and the output is a set of M 3DGS models, each associated with a deformation network (a Multi-Layer Perceptron or MLP) that modifies point positions and orientations over time.

#### 3.2. Physics-aware 4D Transition Planning

This component addresses the challenge of providing MLLMs with the capability to generate detailed, physically plausible spatiotemporal information for 4D scene initialization and transition management.
- **Physics-aware Prompt Expansion and Transition Planning:** Rather than directly interpreting a simple text prompt, the method guides an MLLM through a multi-step process.
 1. The MLLM first analyzes the original prompt using physical principles to derive spatiotemporal information.
 2. It then decomposes the prompt into individual scene prompts for each object {y_i}.
 3. Leveraging its vision-language priors and these decomposed prompts, the MLLM expands the original textual input into a comprehensive, physics-aware scene description. This description includes specific details for each object, such as initial positions (η), movement speeds (v), initial orientation angles (ϕ), rotational speeds (ϖ), and critical scene transition times (T_trans).
 4. This descriptive text is then converted into a structured data format to be used for scene initialization.
- **Initialization of 4D Scene:** Based on the decomposed scene prompts {y_i}, basic 3DGS models {G_i} are first synthesized using Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) guided by a text-to-image generation model. Subsequently, the planned 4D scene data (η, v, ϕ, ϖ) is used to define a transformation function for each point within these 3DGS models over time. This function (x = R(ϕ + ϖ · t)x_ξ + η + v · t) calculates the time-dependent position and orientation of each point, where R is the rotation matrix, x is the arbitrary point position, and x_ξ is its coordinate at the origin. This integration forms the initial 4D scene.

#### 3.3. Geometry-aware 4D Transition

This component is designed to handle significant object deformation, such as appearance or disappearance, which standard deformation networks struggle with.
- **Transition Network (TransNet):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with a Sigmoid activation function at its output layer is proposed.
 1. **Input:** The TransNet takes the 3D position of a point cloud and the current time (t) as input.
 2. **Output (p_trans):** After processing through linear layers and a Sigmoid function (scaled by a coefficient w_trans, typically ≥10), TransNet outputs a scalar p_trans between 0 and 1. This value serves as a reference for the 4D transition.
 3. **Training Stage:** During training, differentiability is maintained by multiplying the opacity (α) of each 3DGS point directly with p_trans.
 4. **Inference Stage:** For a noticeable transition effect, p_trans is used probabilistically to determine whether each Gaussian point appears (B=1) or does not appear (B=0) in the 4D scene (with probability p_trans). This allows for flexible and rational control over point variations during transitions, enabling smooth object appearance/disappearance.

#### 3.4. Efficient 4D Training and Refinement

To manage the computational costs associated with 4D content generation, TRANS4D employs a two-phase optimization strategy:
1. **Phase 1 (Training Dynamics):** The deformation network and TransNet are trained using 3DGS models with a relatively small, fixed number of point clouds (e.g., 20,000 points per model). This phase uses SDS loss based on a video diffusion model (ϵ_vid) to supervise the dynamics across 16 consecutive time steps. During this stage, 3DGS points are neither cloned nor split, ensuring efficient training of the dynamic networks.
2. **Phase 2 (Refinement of Appearance):** After the dynamic networks are trained, the 3DGS models themselves are further refined to enhance visual quality. This involves optimizing the 3DGS parameters using SDS loss based on a text-to-image diffusion model (ϵ_img). In this phase, 3DGS points can be cloned and split to increase detail and fidelity. Critically, the inputs to the deformation and Transition Networks are solely point positions, ensuring that the learned dynamics remain consistent even as the 3DGS models become more detailed. This stepwise approach balances memory efficiency with the generation of high-quality 4D scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate TRANS4D's effectiveness in generating complex 4D scenes with geometry-aware transitions, comparing it against several state-of-the-art 4D generation methods.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

TRANS4D was evaluated on 30 complex textual prompts, many of which involved geometry-aware transitions. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate that TRANS4D consistently outperformed baseline methods across several metrics:
- **QAlign-vid-quality and QAlign-vid-aesthetic:** These metrics, based on the Q-Align model, assess the quality and aesthetic appeal of the generated 4D scenes. TRANS4D achieved the highest scores (3.226 for quality, 2.148 for aesthetic), suggesting superior visual coherence and appeal compared to Consistent4D, 4D-fy, Dream-in-4D, and Comp4D.
- **CLIP-score and Vid-MLLM-metrics:** These metrics evaluate the semantic alignment between the generated 4D results and the input textual prompts. TRANS4D demonstrated the highest CLIP-score (0.2941) and Vid-MLLM-metrics (0.6483). This indicates a stronger and more accurate semantic correspondence with the textual instructions compared to other methods, where some (e.g., 4D-fy and Dream-in-4D) showed high visual quality but lower semantic alignment.
- **User Study:** A user study involving 80 volunteers reinforced these findings, with TRANS4D achieving the highest average score (0.78), further validating its perceived effectiveness and realism by human evaluators.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (e.g., Fig. 1 and Fig. 3) visually demonstrated TRANS4D's capability in generating realistic and coherent 4D scenes with complex interactions and significant object deformations. Examples included a missile colliding with a plane and exploding, a magician conjuring a dancer, and a pigeon appearing from a top hat.
- TRANS4D's rendered videos exhibited natural and coherent transitions, effectively portraying object transformations and interactions.
- Baseline text-to-4D object generation methods (4D-fy, Dream-in-4D), while producing high-quality visuals, often struggled with coherent dynamics and accurately aligning with textual requirements for complex scene-level interactions.
- Monocular-video-to-4D methods (Consistent4D) were noted to perform better for simpler 4D object generation but faced difficulties with complex scene dynamics or when acquiring suitable monocular videos was challenging.

#### 4.3. Model Analysis (Ablation Studies)

Ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of TRANS4D's key components:
- **Rationality of MLLM-planned Trajectory:** A user study (Fig. 4a and Fig. 5) showed that a complete "Physics-aware 4D Transition Planning" method, which includes physics-aware prompt expansion and leverages MLLM's vision-language priors, was crucial. Without physics-aware prompt expansion, the MLLM struggled to generate plausible 4D data. Using an LLM alone (without vision-language priors) also resulted in less precise 4D data, underscoring the benefits of the full MLLM-based planning for reasonable scene initialization.
- **Geometrical Expressiveness:** An ablation study on the "Transition Network" (Fig. 4b and Fig. 6) demonstrated its effectiveness. Volunteers found transitions incorporating the network more natural. Specifically, the method where `p_trans` determines point appearance (probabilistically) received the highest scores, indicating clearer and more distinct geometric transitions compared to simply adjusting opacity.
- **Efficiency and Quality of Refinement:** The two-phase training strategy was shown to balance efficiency and quality effectively (Fig. 7). For complex objects (e.g., "volcano erupting"), sparse initial point clouds (used in Phase 1) were insufficient for high-quality representation. The refinement phase, which allowed for increased point cloud counts, significantly improved visual quality while reducing GPU memory usage (approximately 50GB compared to over 80GB for direct video SDS supervision on dense point clouds).

In summary, TRANS4D consistently demonstrated quantitative and qualitative superiority in generating complex 4D scenes with accurate, high-quality, and geometry-aware transitions, addressing a key limitation in previous 4D generation methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TRANS4D framework presents several significant contributions and holds potential impact across various domains:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing a Core Limitation:** TRANS4D addresses a notable gap in existing 4D generation methods, specifically their struggle with complex scene-level transitions involving significant object deformation and multi-object interactions. Previous methods were often limited to local deformations or simple trajectories, making it difficult to generate scenes where objects appear, disappear, or transform substantially.
- **Novel Planning Paradigm:** The introduction of a "Physics-aware 4D Transition Planning" method, leveraging MLLMs for detailed scene description and planning (including initial positions, speeds, rotations, and transition timings), represents a novel approach to guide 4D synthesis. This goes beyond simple prompt interpretation, enabling more physically plausible and complex scene initializations.
- **Geometry-aware Transition Mechanism:** The proposed "Transition Network" provides a mechanism for dynamic, geometry-aware transitions. By controlling the appearance or disappearance of individual 3D Gaussian Splatting points based on a learned probability, the method enables natural and smooth transformations that were previously challenging to achieve, such as a missile transforming into an explosion cloud.
- **Improved Quality and Coherence:** The experimental results indicate that TRANS4D generates 4D scenes with higher visual quality, greater semantic alignment with textual prompts, and more coherent dynamics compared to baseline methods. The user studies further confirm the perceived realism and effectiveness of its generated outputs.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The two-phase training and refinement strategy offers a practical solution to the high computational costs associated with high-fidelity 4D generation. By initially training dynamics on sparse representations and then refining visual detail, it achieves high quality within more manageable memory constraints.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Generative AI:** TRANS4D contributes to the ongoing advancement of generative artificial intelligence by extending the capabilities of diffusion models and MLLMs into the complex domain of dynamic 3D scene creation. It demonstrates how intelligent planning and explicit transition mechanisms can significantly enhance the realism and expressiveness of generated content.
- **Gaming Industry:** The ability to generate complex 4D scenes with realistic object interactions and transformations can directly benefit game development. This could lead to more dynamic game environments, realistic in-game cinematics, and automated content creation for evolving game worlds.
- **Video Multimedia Production:** For film, animation, and advertising, TRANS4D could enable the rapid prototyping and generation of intricate visual effects and dynamic story segments. Creating transitions where objects morph, appear, or explode seamlessly in 3D space could streamline production workflows and enhance creative possibilities.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Generating highly realistic and dynamic 4D content is crucial for immersive VR/AR experiences. TRANS4D's capabilities could facilitate the creation of rich, interactive virtual worlds and augmented environments where objects behave with physical consistency and undergo complex changes.
- **Simulation and Robotics:** Realistic 4D scene generation can be valuable for training autonomous systems in simulated environments. By creating diverse and dynamic scenarios with complex interactions, TRANS4D could help develop more robust robotic and AI agents.
- **Long-form Content and Storytelling:** By enabling geometry-aware transitions, TRANS4D moves towards facilitating the creation of story-level, long-form content by allowing for more elaborate and physically consistent narrative progression within generated 4D scenes.

In conclusion, TRANS4D represents a step towards more advanced and versatile text-to-4D synthesis, addressing critical limitations in handling complex, geometry-aware scene dynamics and interactions. Its methodologies, particularly the MLLM-driven planning and the dedicated transition network, offer a foundation for future research in creating highly realistic and immersive dynamic content.
