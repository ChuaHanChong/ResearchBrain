# LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper empirically investigates LoRA's performance against full finetuning, demonstrating that LoRA underperforms in continued pretraining but can match full finetuning in instruction finetuning with high ranks, while consistently mitigating catastrophic forgetting. The study reveals that full finetuning induces high-rank perturbations, explaining LoRA's limitations at low ranks.

## Problem

- Clarifying whether Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) can achieve performance parity with full finetuning on challenging, domain-specific tasks like code and mathematics, given conflicting reports in prior literature.
- Empirically assessing the extent to which LoRA mitigates catastrophic forgetting of base model knowledge when models are continually finetuned on new domains.
- Prior studies validating LoRA's parity with full finetuning largely used smaller models or easier benchmarks like GLUE, leaving its behavior on complex, specialized LLM tasks untested.

## Method

- Conducted extensive empirical comparisons of ==LoRA== and ==full finetuning== for **Llama-2-7B** on code and mathematics domains.
- Evaluated across both ==Continued Pretraining (CPT)== and ==Instruction Finetuning (IFT)== regimes using target-domain benchmarks (HumanEval, GSM8K) and source-domain forgetting benchmarks (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-Challenge).
- Performed ==Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)== on finetuned weight matrices to investigate the intrinsic rank of changes induced by full finetuning.

## Results

- LoRA consistently underperforms full finetuning in CPT on code and mathematics; the gap **widens** with increased training data.
- For IFT, LoRA can largely match full FT only with sufficiently high rank (**r=256**); common low ranks (**r=16, 64**) still underperform.
- LoRA consistently forgets **less** of the base model's general language understanding across all domains and regimes — the **canonical forgetting-retention trade-off** for PEFT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Full finetuning induces high-rank perturbations to model weights, challenging the low-rank assumption underlying LoRA and explaining why low-rank LoRA struggles on complex domain adaptation tasks.
> - LoRA effectively constrains the finetuning process, consistently leading to less catastrophic forgetting of base model capabilities and better preservation of generative diversity compared to full finetuning.
> - Optimal LoRA performance is highly sensitive to hyperparameters, with careful tuning of learning rates, using a scaling factor (α = 2r), and targeting all transformer modules (especially MLPs) being crucial.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid growth in size and computational demands of Large Language Models (LLMs) has necessitated the development of parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) methods. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) stands out as one of the most widely adopted PEFT techniques, primarily due to its ability to significantly reduce memory footprint and computational cost during training by injecting small, low-rank matrices into the model's layers.

However, despite its popularity, the performance parity of LoRA with full finetuning has been a subject of ongoing debate in the research community. Initial seminal papers introducing LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) and its quantized variant, QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2024), reported that LoRA could achieve performance comparable or even superior to full finetuning. This positive sentiment was echoed in several empirical studies and industry blog posts, solidifying LoRA's reputation as a "no-compromise" solution for resource-constrained environments.

Conversely, a growing body of evidence has begun to suggest that LoRA might, in fact, underperform full finetuning in certain scenarios (Ivison et al., 2023; Zhuo et al., 2024). This divergence has spurred the development of numerous enhanced LoRA variants and alternative low-rank approximation methods, all aiming to bridge the perceived performance gap.

This paper directly addresses this critical ambiguity. By focusing on challenging, domain-specific tasks (code and mathematics) and conducting extensive empirical comparisons using a state-of-the-art LLM (Llama-2-7B), the authors aim to provide a more nuanced and comprehensive understanding of LoRA's true performance characteristics. Furthermore, the study delves into the aspect of "catastrophic forgetting"—a common phenomenon in continual learning where models lose previously acquired knowledge when trained on new data. LoRA is hypothesized to mitigate such forgetting due to its constrained parameter updates, and this paper rigorously investigates this claim, adding to the relatively sparse literature on forgetting in modern LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives of this research are driven by two fundamental questions regarding LoRA's efficacy in the context of LLM finetuning:

1. **Performance Parity on Challenging Target Domains:** Under what conditions does LoRA approximate the accuracy of full finetuning on demanding target domains, specifically code and mathematics? The motivation here is to clarify the conflicting reports in prior literature regarding LoRA's performance. Many previous studies demonstrating LoRA's parity with full finetuning used smaller models or less challenging tasks (e.g., GLUE benchmarks), which may not reflect the complexities of specialized LLM applications like code generation or complex mathematical reasoning. The authors hypothesize that for these harder domains, LoRA's low-rank constraint might become a bottleneck.

2. **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** To what extent does LoRA mitigate the forgetting of base model capabilities when performing continual learning on a new domain? LLM finetuning is a form of continual learning, where specializing in new domains can lead to a degradation of original general-purpose capabilities. LoRA's design, by perturbing only a small fraction of the model's weights, is intuitively expected to act as a form of regularization, preserving more of the base model's knowledge. This objective aims to empirically validate this intuition and quantify LoRA's effectiveness as a forgetting mitigation strategy compared to full finetuning and other regularization techniques.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide a comprehensive, empirically-backed assessment of LoRA's capabilities, exploring its learning capacity, memory retention, and underlying mechanics, especially when applied to real-world, complex LLM specialization tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic and extensive empirical methodology to compare LoRA against full finetuning for Llama-2-7B models.

- **Base Model:** The study exclusively used **Llama-2-7B** as the base LLM, a widely used and representative open-source model, allowing for generalizable conclusions within the 7B parameter class.

- **Target Domains:** Two challenging and distinct domains were chosen for specialization:
 - **Programming (Code):** Requiring logical reasoning, syntax generation, and problem-solving.
 - **Mathematics:** Demanding complex arithmetic, symbolic manipulation, and multi-step reasoning.

- **Training Regimes:** For each domain, two distinct finetuning regimes were investigated:
 - **Continued Pretraining (CPT):** Training on large volumes of unstructured, domain-specific tokens (billions of tokens).
 - Code CPT: StarCoder-Python (up to 20B tokens).
 - Math CPT: OpenWebMath (14.7B tokens, repeated to 20B).
 - **Instruction Finetuning (IFT):** Training on structured question-answer pairs (tens to hundreds of millions of tokens).
 - Code IFT: Magicoder-Evol-Instruct-110K (72.97M tokens).
 - Math IFT: MetaMathQA (103M tokens).

- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Learning (Target Domain Performance):**
 - Code: **HumanEval** (Chen et al., 2021) for Python program generation (pass@1).
 - Math: **GSM8K** (Cobbe et al., 2021) for grade-school math word problems (strict match pass@1).
 - **Forgetting (Source Domain Performance/Base Model Capabilities):** A composite score averaged across three benchmarks assessing general language understanding and common-sense reasoning:
 - **HellaSwag** (Zellers et al., 2019): Plausible sentence continuation.
 - **WinoGrande** (Sakaguchi et al., 2019): Ambiguous pronoun resolution.
 - **ARC-Challenge** (Clark et al., 2018): Multiple-choice science questions.

- **LoRA Configurations:**
 - **Ranks (r):** Standard low ranks (16, 64) and a higher rank (256) were tested.
 - **Scaling Factor (α):** A critical finding of the study was the importance of setting α = 2r, leading to a constant scaling factor across ranks, which they found crucial for high-rank performance.
 - **Target Modules:** LoRA was applied to *all* transformer modules (attention: W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o; feedforward: W_gate, W_up, W_down), based on best practices.

- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** Extensive learning rate sweeps were performed for each method and setting to ensure optimal performance, a crucial step given LoRA's known sensitivity.

- **Additional Analyses:**
 - **Learning-Forgetting Tradeoff:** Pareto curves were generated to visualize the relationship between target domain performance and source domain forgetting for different training durations and LoRA ranks.
 - **Comparison to Regularization Techniques:** LoRA's forgetting mitigation was compared against classical regularization methods like dropout and weight decay.
 - **Diversity of Generations:** The uniqueness of generated solutions on HumanEval was analyzed to assess the impact of finetuning methods on output diversity.
 - **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) Analysis:** A key theoretical investigation involved performing SVD on the finetuned weight matrices, the pretrained weights, and their difference (the perturbation matrix, Δ). This aimed to understand the intrinsic rank of changes induced by full finetuning, providing insight into why LoRA might underperform.

The experiments were conducted using Databricks MosaicML's composer, streaming, and llm-foundry libraries, along with HuggingFace's peft library, on 32 H100-80GB GPUs, demonstrating a high-resource computational environment suitable for large-scale LLM experimentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several critical insights into LoRA's behavior compared to full finetuning:

1. **Target-Domain Performance (Learning): LoRA Underperforms in CPT, Can Match in IFT with High Ranks.**
 - **Continued Pretraining (CPT):** LoRA consistently and substantially underperforms full finetuning across both code (StarCoder-Python) and math (OpenWebMath) domains, even with higher ranks (r=256). The performance gap widens with more training data. For instance, the best LoRA model (r=256) at 20B tokens roughly matched full finetuning's performance at only 4B tokens for code.
 - **Instruction Finetuning (IFT):** LoRA's performance is highly rank-dependent. While common low ranks (r=16, 64) still underperform full finetuning, a sufficiently high rank (r=256) can close most, if not all, of the performance gap with full finetuning on both code (Magicoder-Evol-Instruct-110K) and math (MetaMathQA) tasks. This suggests that IFT, involving more structured and often less extensive data, might require less "expressive" adaptations compared to CPT.

2. **Source-Domain Performance (Forgetting): LoRA Forgets Less than Full Finetuning.**
 - Across all domains and training regimes, LoRA consistently maintains better base model performance (less forgetting) on general language understanding, world knowledge, and common-sense reasoning tasks (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-Challenge) compared to full finetuning.
 - The extent of forgetting in LoRA is controlled by its rank: lower ranks lead to less forgetting.
 - IFT generally induces more forgetting than CPT, and programming tasks induce more forgetting than math tasks for both methods.

3. **The Learning-Forgetting Tradeoff:**
 - The study presents Pareto curves illustrating the tradeoff. For code tasks, especially in IFT, LoRA with high ranks (r=256) can achieve comparable learning while strictly forgetting less. For math tasks, full finetuning often offers preferable tradeoffs, achieving higher learning with only slightly increased forgetting. This indicates that the optimal choice between LoRA and full finetuning, and the appropriate LoRA rank, depends on the specific domain and the desired balance between specialization and generalization.

4. **LoRA's Constraint and Diversity of Generations:**
 - LoRA effectively constrains the finetuning process. It mitigates forgetting more aggressively than traditional regularization techniques like attention dropout and weight decay. For instance, LoRA r=256 learns as much as full finetuning but forgets significantly less.
 - Analyzing HumanEval solutions, the authors found that full finetuning tends to produce a more limited set of unique generations (indicating "distribution collapse"), while LoRA maintains a wider range of solutions, more akin to the base model. This suggests LoRA helps preserve the base model's generative diversity.

5. **Full Finetuning Learns High-Rank Perturbations:**
 - Contrary to the hypothesis that finetuning results in low-rank perturbations, the SVD analysis revealed that the difference matrix (Δ = W_finetuned - W_pretrained) for full finetuning is high-rank. The rank needed to explain 90% of the variance in Δ is 10-100 times greater than typical LoRA ranks (e.g., r=16 or 64). This finding provides a mechanistic explanation for why LoRA, especially at low ranks, struggles to match full finetuning's performance in learning new complex domains—it simply cannot express the high-rank changes that full finetuning discovers. The rank of these perturbations also increases as training progresses.

6. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Best Practices for LoRA:**
 - LoRA is highly sensitive to hyperparameters. The study found that:
 - **Scaling factor (α = 2r):** Crucial for high ranks; not scaling α with rank leads to inferior performance.
 - **Target Modules:** Targeting all transformer modules (Attention and MLP) or just MLP modules significantly outperforms targeting only Attention modules. MLP blocks appear to be primary loci for continual learning in LoRA.
 - **Learning Rates:** LoRA is more sensitive to learning rates than full finetuning and often benefits from rates an order of magnitude higher (e.g., 5e-5 to 5e-4 vs. 1e-5 for full finetuning), picking the highest value that ensures stable training.
 - Based on these findings, the authors propose practical recommendations for using LoRA:
 - Prefer LoRA for instruction finetuning over continued pretraining.
 - If GPU memory allows, target "All" transformer modules with a rank of 256.
 - Always use α = 2r.
 - Perform careful sweeps over learning rates (e.g., 1e-5 to 5e-4).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a much-needed, empirically robust, and nuanced perspective on LoRA's capabilities, moving beyond the often-simplified narratives in the LLM community. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Challenging the "No-Compromise" Narrative:** The most immediate impact is the direct challenge to the widely held belief that LoRA offers performance equivalent to full finetuning. The study conclusively shows that for challenging domain adaptation tasks, especially in continued pretraining, LoRA often underperforms with commonly used low-rank settings. This finding is crucial for researchers and practitioners who might be making resource allocation and design choices based on an overly optimistic view of LoRA's capacity.

2. **Informing Practical Finetuning Strategies:** The detailed hyperparameter sensitivity analysis and the proposed best practices are invaluable for practitioners. Understanding that LoRA requires careful tuning of learning rates, α scaling, and higher ranks (for certain tasks) can save significant computational resources and time, leading to more effective and efficient LLM specialization. The insight that MLP modules are critical for LoRA's performance provides a new direction for module selection.

3. **Deeper Understanding of Finetuning Dynamics:** The singular value decomposition analysis is a key theoretical contribution. By demonstrating that full finetuning induces *high-rank* perturbations, the paper sheds light on the intrinsic expressiveness required for successful domain adaptation. This finding fundamentally questions the underlying assumption of LoRA (that finetuning can be approximated by low-rank updates) for complex tasks, thereby opening avenues for future PEFT research that might explore higher-rank approximations or different adaptation mechanisms.

4. **Advancing Continual Learning in LLMs:** The comprehensive analysis of forgetting, and the finding that LoRA consistently mitigates it more effectively than full finetuning and traditional regularization, positions LoRA as a valuable tool for continual learning scenarios. This is particularly relevant as LLMs are increasingly deployed in dynamic environments requiring continuous updates without complete re-training, where preserving base model knowledge is critical. The observation that LoRA also maintains greater generative diversity is an important qualitative benefit for downstream applications.

5. **Guiding Future PEFT Research:** By identifying the limitations of classic LoRA and providing insights into the nature of finetuning perturbations, this work sets the stage for the next generation of parameter-efficient methods. Future research can now focus on developing PEFT techniques that are capable of learning higher-rank changes, are less hyperparameter sensitive, or offer more flexible tradeoffs between learning new knowledge and retaining old.

In conclusion, "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less" is a timely and impactful contribution that deepens our understanding of a cornerstone technique in LLM development. It provides empirical clarity on LoRA's true performance envelope, offering both cautious caveats and practical guidelines, thereby helping the community to more effectively and judiciously apply this important finetuning method.
