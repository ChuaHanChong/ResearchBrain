# Learning Visual Quadrupedal Loco-Manipulation from Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou), University of California, Berkeley, and Tsinghua University developed a hierarchical learning framework that enables quadruped robots to perform diverse, real-world manipulation tasks using only their legs while maintaining stable locomotion. It successfully combines a high-level visual planner and a low-level real-time controller, demonstrating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and achieving significantly higher success rates on nine loco-manipulation tasks compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional mobile manipulation systems relying on wheeled platforms face limitations when navigating uneven or challenging terrains.
- Legged robots, while capable of robust locomotion, often lack sophisticated, general-purpose manipulation skills using their own limbs, with existing methods being task-specific, imprecise, or limited to static interactions.
- Integrating locomotion and precise manipulation for quadruped robots presents challenges due to highly nonlinear dynamics and the complexity of using high-dimensional visual data for simulation-to-real deployment.

## Method

- A **hierarchical learning framework** is introduced, comprising a high-level visual planner (==Behavior Cloning==) that predicts end-effector trajectories and a low-level real-time controller (==Reinforcement Learning==) for stable joint position control.
- Manipulation trajectories are parameterized using 6-order rational ==Bézier curves== for position and Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) for orientation, facilitating seamless cooperation between the high-level planner and low-level controller.
- Expert demonstrations for the visual planner are efficiently collected in parallel simulations with randomized object poses, while the low-level controller is trained using ==PPO== with extensive domain and command randomization to enable robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved consistently higher success rates across nine loco-manipulation tasks, outperforming a Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) baseline by large margins (e.g., **92.33%** vs. **21.67%** for 'Press Button', and **82.33%** vs. **0.00%** for 'Pull Handle').
- It demonstrated superior data efficiency, requiring only **20,000** timesteps of visual data for the high-level planner, which is significantly less than end-to-end Behavior Cloning (**500,000** timesteps) or Visual RL (**5 million** timesteps) baselines that often failed.
- The trained policies transferred directly to a real Unitree Aliengo quadruped robot without fine-tuning, exhibiting robustness to unexpected situations like displaced objects and successfully performing tasks such as lifting a basket and pushing a door.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the complex loco-manipulation problem into hierarchical visual planning and low-level dynamic control allows for more efficient learning and robust execution than end-to-end or purely RL-based approaches.
> - Parameterizing manipulation trajectories with adaptable Bézier curves and SLERP provides a flexible and generalizable way for the high-level planner to specify diverse actions for a single agent across multiple tasks.
> - Extensive domain randomization in low-level control training combined with precise real-time visual perception and alignment enables effective zero-shot transfer of policies from simulation to a physical quadruped robot.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The integration of quadrupedal robots into human environments is an ongoing area of research. While significant advancements have been made in quadrupedal locomotion, their ability to interact with objects in realistic, unstructured scenes remains a challenge.

Traditional approaches to mobile manipulation often involve mounting high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) mechanical arms onto wheeled mobile platforms. These systems exhibit robust mobility on flat terrains, which has enabled the application of end-to-end Behavior Cloning (BC) for acquiring complex mobile manipulation skills. However, a primary limitation of wheeled platforms is their restricted mobility on uneven or challenging terrains.

Legged robots, in contrast, offer enhanced mobility over diverse terrains. Recent developments in legged locomotion have seen both model-based and model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches enable quadrupedal robots to achieve robust locomotion on complex surfaces, including fine-tuning policies for novel environments. However, the development of sophisticated manipulation skills for legged robots, particularly using their own limbs, has lagged behind their locomotion capabilities.

Some research efforts have augmented legged robots with additional robotic arms. While this can enable complex manipulation, it introduces increased costs and additional weight, which can compromise the robot's inherent locomotion abilities. Other work has explored using the robot's legs for manipulation tasks such as button pressing, dribbling balls, or shooting. However, these prior approaches often exhibit several limitations:
1. **Task-specificity:** Designs are frequently tailored to specific tasks, resulting in limited adaptability to different manipulation requirements.
2. **Lack of precision:** The manipulation capabilities are often coarse, lacking the fine-grained control necessary for many real-world interactions.
3. **Static manipulation:** Methods primarily focus on tasks performed from a static base, failing to leverage the robot's inherent mobility and dynamic capabilities for loco-manipulation.
4. **Limited DoF tracking:** Some systems are restricted to 3-DoF point tracking for the end-effector, and may require human teleoperation for task execution, rather than autonomous planning.

This current work positions itself within this landscape by addressing these limitations. It proposes a general framework that aims to empower a quadruped robot to execute versatile, precise, and mobile manipulation tasks using only its legs. The approach leverages the inherent dexterity and workspace provided by a quadruped robot's four 3-DoF limbs and its 6-DoF floating base torso, integrating locomotion and manipulation without additional hardware. The work also attempts to mitigate challenges associated with the highly nonlinear dynamics of loco-manipulators and the complexities of incorporating high-dimensional visual data in simulation-to-real deployments.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a quadruped robot to autonomously perform diverse, real-world manipulation tasks using only its legs, while simultaneously maintaining stable locomotion over extended distances.

This objective is motivated by several considerations:
- **Leveraging inherent capabilities:** Quadruped robots possess four 3-DoF limbs and a 6-DoF floating base torso, which collectively provide a significant workspace and flexibility for manipulation. The researchers are motivated to exploit these inherent capabilities rather than resorting to additional, often redundant, robotic arms that increase cost and weight.
- **Addressing limitations of existing leg-based manipulation:** Prior work using quadruped legs for manipulation typically suffered from limitations such as task-specific designs, coarse precision, and reliance on static manipulation. The motivation is to overcome these by developing a general framework capable of versatile tasks, precise control, and dynamic mobile manipulation.
- **Integrating locomotion and manipulation:** The goal is to move beyond mere locomotion or static manipulation to achieve seamless "loco-manipulation," where the robot can interact with objects while actively moving. This capability is crucial for robots operating in dynamic and unstructured human environments.
- **Overcoming complexity challenges:** Loco-manipulation with quadruped legs presents unique challenges. As highly nonlinear and underactuated systems, maintaining stability during manipulation is difficult. Furthermore, effectively utilizing high-dimensional visual data for versatile manipulation, especially when transferring policies from simulation to the real world, poses significant technical hurdles. The research is motivated to develop a framework that can robustly address these complexities.
- **Eliminating teleoperation:** Many existing methods for leg-based manipulation require human teleoperation for complex tasks. A key motivation is to enable autonomous execution of tasks through a visual planner, reducing human intervention.

To achieve these objectives, the researchers propose a hierarchical learning framework. This framework aims to combine the strengths of Behavior Cloning (BC) for high-level manipulation planning with Reinforcement Learning (RL) for low-level real-time control. A central part of their approach is the parameterization of the manipulation trajectory, designed to synchronize the efforts of these two layers and facilitate efficient data collection in simulation.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research introduces a hierarchical learning framework designed to enable quadruped robots to perform loco-manipulation tasks. This framework decomposes the loco-manipulation process into a high-level planner, implemented using Behavior Cloning (BC), and a low-level controller, trained with Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**A. Overview of the Hierarchical Framework**

The system operates with two main components:
1. **High-level Planner ($\pi_p$):** This component processes visual (point clouds) and proprioceptive robot state data to predict the desired trajectory parameters for the end-effector. It operates at a frequency of 10 Hz.
2. **Low-level Controller ($\pi_c$):** This component takes the trajectory parameters from the planner and the current robot state to output desired joint positions, enabling the robot to track the end-effector pose while maintaining stable locomotion. It operates at a frequency of 50 Hz.

**B. Trajectory Parameterization**

To facilitate cooperation between BC and RL, the manipulation trajectory of the end-effector is parameterized. These parameters serve as the output of the high-level planner and an input to the low-level controller.
- **Manipulator Flag ($f$):** A binary flag (0 for Front Left, 1 for Front Right) specifies which forelimb acts as the manipulator.
- **Desired Position Trajectory:** Represented by 6-order rational Bézier curves. Unlike traditional Bézier curves, these include adjustable weights ($w_i$) for control points ($p_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1}$), allowing for flexible and smooth representation of diverse trajectories, including curves and polylines.
- **Desired Orientation:** Calculated using Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) between a target start orientation ($q_0$) and a target end orientation ($q_1$), enabling specification of orientation throughout the trajectory.
- **Reference Frame:** For expert data collection, trajectory parameters ($\textbf{o}p$) are represented in the object frame and then converted to the world frame, accommodating randomized object poses. The high-level planner's input point clouds ($\textbf{w}P$) and output trajectory parameters ($\textbf{w}p$) are in the world frame. The low-level controller utilizes trajectory parameters ($\textbf{b}p$) in the robot's body frame to address potential motion asymmetry.

**C. Learning Visual Manipulation Planning by BC (High-Level Planner)**

1. **Framework:** The planner ($\pi_p$) uses DP3 (Diffusion-based 3D Visuomotor Policy) as its backbone, which is designed for processing 3D visual data and learning manipulation trajectories from demonstrations.
 - **Input:** Robot proprioceptive state ($s_t$) and visual point cloud data ($\textbf{w}P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 3}$, with $n=768$ randomly sampled points). Visual data is captured by depth cameras mounted on the robot and transformed into the world frame.
 - **Output:** Manipulation trajectory parameters ($\textbf{w}p$) in the world frame, including the manipulator flag, target position, and target orientation, which are then fed to the low-level controller.
2. **Expert Demonstration Collection:**
 - Expert trajectory parameters ($\textbf{o}p$) are designed for various objects and tasks. Object positions and yaw angles are randomized to enhance learning across different object poses.
 - Data collection is performed in large-scale parallel simulations using IsaacGym. The trained low-level controller ($\pi_c$) tracks these expert trajectories while robot states, point clouds, and trajectory parameters are recorded.
 - This method allows for rapid collection of a substantial volume of data (e.g., 100 trajectories per task, each 200 timesteps, in approximately 3 minutes).
 - The collected data ($\mathcal{D} = \{\xi_0, \xi_1, \ldots \xi_n\}$) consists of sequences of point cloud observations ($\textbf{w}P$), robot proprioceptive states ($s$), and trajectory parameters ($\textbf{w}p$).

**D. Learning Joint Position Control by RL (Low-Level Controller)**

The low-level controller ($\pi_c$) is trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. Its function is to enable the robot to track any spatial trajectory with a selected end-effector while maintaining stable movement with the remaining three legs, and to handle external forces.

1. **Environment:** The robot is trained to perform locomotion with three legs while one leg tracks a randomly generated target trajectory within a 4m x 4m area around the robot. This setup encourages omnidirectional movement and end-effector tracking. Episode length is 20 seconds.
2. **Policy Input ($o_t$):**
 - **Proprioception states ($s_t$):** History of 15 timesteps, including gravity vector in body frame, joint positions ($q_t \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$), joint velocities ($\dot{q}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$), and previous action ($a_{t-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$).
 - **Manipulation command ($c_t$):** Calculated from the high-level planner's output, including the manipulator flag ($f$), desired point ($\textbf{x}_d^t \in \mathbb{R}^3$), next three desired points ($\textbf{x}_d^{t+1, t+2, t+3}$), and desired orientation ($\theta_d^t$).
 - **Privileged information:** Robot velocity and end-effector positions predicted by a state estimator, which also takes the 15-timestep history as input.
3. **Action Space ($a_t$):** The desired joint positions ($q_d^m \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$) for the robot. These are passed through a low-pass filter and then a joint-level PD controller to generate motor torques ($\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$).
4. **Reward Function:** Comprises three types of rewards:
 - **Tracking Reward:** Encourages precise tracking of both position and orientation. Position tracking is determined by comparing current and target end-effector positions (z-axis component amplified fivefold). Orientation tracking uses the angle difference between target and current end-effector orientation.
 - **Stability Term:** Promotes stable robot movement.
 - **Smoothness Term:** Penalizes high end-effector and body accelerations to ensure fluid movements.
5. **Domain and Command Randomization:** To improve sim-to-real transfer and handle real-world uncertainties (varying loads, dynamics parameters), the following were randomized during training:
 - **Dynamics parameters:** Of the robot and environment.
 - **Manipulation trajectory parameters:** Bézier parameters (control points x,y: [-2.0, 2.0]m; z: [0.01, 1.2]m; weights:) and target orientation (yaw: [0, 2π]; pitch: [0.0, 1.0]). Bézier control points are represented in the body frame, allowing for learning within an effectively infinite range of motion.

**E. Sim-to-Real Transfer**

The trained controller and planner are directly implemented on a real-world Unitree Aliengo quadruped robot without fine-tuning. This process involves:
- Using AprilTag markers (with an external camera) to determine the robot's pose for coordinate system transformations of trajectory parameters and point clouds.
- Applying a post-processing step to the point clouds to ensure alignment between simulated and real-world visual observations.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research evaluated the proposed hierarchical learning framework through simulations and real-world experiments, focusing on performance comparison, robustness to unexpected situations, sim-to-real transfer, and the individual performance of the planner and controller components.

**A. Performance Comparison**
The proposed method utilized 3 billion timesteps of robot state data for low-level controller training and 20,000 timesteps of visual data for high-level planner training. Performance was assessed across nine distinct loco-manipulation tasks against several baselines.

- **Success Rates (Table III):** The proposed method consistently achieved higher success rates across all nine tasks compared to the Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) baseline. For instance, in the "Press Button" task, the proposed method achieved a 92.33% success rate, significantly outperforming HRL's 21.67%. In the "Pull Handle" task, the proposed method achieved 82.33% while HRL recorded 0.00%. This indicates the effectiveness of the combined BC and RL framework.
- **Data Efficiency (Table IV):** The proposed method demonstrated notable data efficiency, requiring only 20,000 timesteps of visual data to successfully complete tasks such as "Press Button" and "Close Dishwasher." In contrast, end-to-end Behavior Cloning (BC) with 500,000 timesteps and HRL with 1 million timesteps either failed to complete the tasks or achieved significantly lower success rates. End-to-end Visual RL (VRL) with 5 million timesteps was unable to accomplish these tasks at all. This efficiency is attributed to the hierarchical design, which allows for the low-level controller to be trained on readily available robot state data.

**B. Robust Manipulation in Unexpected Situations**
Despite the expert trajectories being generated from fixed simulation parameters, the system exhibited robustness to unforeseen environmental changes. As demonstrated in the "Lift Basket" task (Fig. 5), when the basket was displaced by an external force (rolling approximately 1.5 meters away) during the robot's approach, the robot rapidly re-predicted the trajectory parameters based on the updated point clouds of the object. This re-planning allowed the robot to successfully lift the displaced basket, indicating effective real-time visual adaptation.

**C. Sim-to-Real Transfer**
The trained controller and planner were directly deployed on a real-world Unitree Aliengo quadruped robot without the need for further fine-tuning. This "zero-shot" transfer demonstrated the robustness of the approach. The system used AprilTag markers and an external camera for robot pose localization and performed post-processing on point clouds to align real-world visual observations with simulation data. Real-world demonstrations included tasks such as lifting a basket with a forelimb and pushing a door while locomoting (Fig. 1).

**D. Planner Performance**
Analysis of the planner's performance in simulation, exemplified by the "Twist Valve" task (Fig. 6a), showed that while the initial predicted trajectory parameters could be chaotic, they tended to converge as the manipulation task progressed. This indicates that the planner refines its predictions, leading to a more stable and accurate desired trajectory.

**E. Control Policy Performance**
- **Robust Tracking Against Disturbances (Fig. 6b):** The low-level control policy's tracking performance was evaluated across the nine tasks. Both position and orientation errors of the end-effector peaked at the beginning of manipulation but then largely converged. Position error consistently reached a low value after approximately 3 seconds. While contact with objects in the latter half of episodes sometimes caused a slight increase in position errors, they generally remained low. The system prioritized position tracking, resulting in a minimum orientation error of 0.4 radians, acknowledging that precise tracking of both 6-DoF end-effector pose can be challenging for a highly underactuated system.
- **Teleoperation with Control Policy (Fig. 7):** The trained low-level controller could be teleoperated using specific trajectory parameters via a joystick in real-world scenarios. This demonstrated its capability to perform various daily tasks, such as twisting a valve, shooting a ball, and pressing a button, indicating its versatility as a fundamental control skill.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robotics, particularly in the domain of quadrupedal loco-manipulation, with potential impacts on the capabilities and deployment of legged robots.

- **Advancement in Quadrupedal Loco-Manipulation:** The work presents a significant step towards equipping quadruped robots with general, precise, and mobile manipulation abilities using only their legs. It moves beyond previous limitations of task-specific designs, coarse manipulation, or static interactions, allowing robots to integrate locomotion and manipulation seamlessly. This advancement expands the range of practical applications for quadruped robots in complex and dynamic human environments.
- **Generalization and Versatility:** By employing a hierarchical learning framework and parameterizing manipulation trajectories, the approach enables a single agent to learn and execute a diverse set of manipulation skills, including pushing, pulling, lifting, and interacting with various objects. This generalization reduces the need for developing specialized solutions for each task.
- **Data Efficiency:** The demonstrated data efficiency, particularly concerning visual data for the high-level planner, makes the training process more practical and scalable. This is crucial for real-world robotic applications where collecting vast amounts of annotated visual data can be resource-intensive and time-consuming. The separation of low-level control (trained with abundant proprioceptive data) from high-level planning (trained with less visual data) contributes to this efficiency.
- **Robustness to Environmental Uncertainty:** The observed ability of the robot to adapt to unexpected situations, such as a displaced object, through rapid visual re-planning, enhances its robustness. This adaptability is critical for robots operating in unstructured and unpredictable environments where conditions can change dynamically.
- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The direct deployment of policies trained in simulation onto a real-world quadruped robot without further fine-tuning (zero-shot transfer) is a notable achievement. This highlights the effectiveness of the domain randomization strategies and point cloud post-processing, significantly reducing the development time and effort required for real-world implementation.
- **Development of a New Benchmark:** The introduction of nine challenging loco-manipulation tasks serves as a valuable benchmark for the research community. This addresses a gap in existing robotic manipulation benchmarks, which largely focus on fixed-base manipulators, providing a standardized way to evaluate future algorithms in this emerging field.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The study lays groundwork for future developments. Identified limitations, such as accumulated errors in the hierarchical system, reliance on point cloud post-processing, and inference speed of diffusion-based BC, offer clear directions for subsequent work. Potential future advancements include enhancing planner inference speed for dynamic scenarios and exploring methods for scaling up or mixing real-world data for expert demonstrations, further pushing the boundaries of autonomous legged robot capabilities.
