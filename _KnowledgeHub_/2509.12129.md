# Embodied Navigation Foundation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> NavFoM, an embodied navigation foundation model, achieves cross-task and cross-embodiment generalization by leveraging a dual-branch architecture that integrates multimodal inputs with an LLM. This model demonstrates state-of-the-art or competitive performance across seven diverse benchmarks, including VLN, object searching, tracking, and autonomous driving, achieving a 5.6% increase in success rate on single-view VLN-CE RxR and a 78% success rate in real-world VLN tasks.

## Problem

- Existing embodied navigation methods based on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are limited to narrow task settings, embodiment-specific architectures, and restricted instruction formats, hindering broad generalization.
- Current cross-task navigation approaches often assume consistent camera configurations and are typically confined to indoor environments, lacking applicability to diverse scenarios like autonomous driving or UAV navigation.
- Cross-embodiment navigation models often process multimodal inputs without explicit spatial and temporal cues, leading to potential ambiguities and limited generalization to out-of-distribution embodiments.

## Method

- NavFoM integrates a dual-branch architecture, extending video-based VLMs with pre-trained visual encoders (DINOv2, SigLIP) and an LLM (Qwen2-7B) to handle both navigation and open-world question-answering tasks.
- It introduces ==Temporal-Viewpoint Indicator (TVI)== tokens that explicitly encode spatial (viewpoint) and temporal information, enabling the LLM to interpret diverse camera setups and navigation horizons.
- A Budget-Aware Temporal Sampling (BATS) strategy dynamically samples historical tokens with an exponential growth-based probability to manage computational load and GPU memory, prioritizing recent observations while maintaining historical context.

## Results

- NavFoM achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on VLN-CE R2R/RxR and OpenUAV benchmarks, improving the success rate (SR) on single-view VLN-CE RxR from **51.8%** to **57.4%** and multi-camera RxR from **56.3%** to **64.4%**.
- The model outperformed previous fine-tuned SOTA methods in zero-shot object goal navigation (HM3D-OVON) with a **45.2%** SR and achieved SOTA in single-view active visual tracking (EVT-Bench) at **61.4%** SR.
- Real-world experiments on multiple robotic platforms (quadrupeds, humanoids, drones, wheeled robots) demonstrated strong practical applicability and significant performance gains over baselines (e.g., **78%** SR vs. **56%** for VLN), producing 8-waypoint trajectories within **0.5** seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-tuning the model with a diverse mixture of navigation and open-world QA samples significantly improves performance across various tasks, facilitating the learning of generalized navigation skills and broader environmental understanding.
> - Explicitly embedding spatial and temporal context through TVI tokens in visual features is critical for an LLM to effectively generalize across varying camera configurations and temporal horizons in multimodal embodied navigation.
> - An efficient temporal sampling strategy like BATS, which prioritizes recent observations while retaining historical context within a token budget, is essential for handling long video sequences and multiple camera inputs in real-time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied AI, which endows agents with the ability to perceive and interact within physical environments, has seen significant advancements, particularly with the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). VLMs have demonstrated remarkable zero-shot generalization capabilities in tasks like retrieval and captioning from large-scale open-world data. However, as the authors highlight, their application in embodied navigation has remained largely confined to narrow task settings, embodiment-specific architectures, and restricted instruction formats. This limits their generalizability and practical utility for diverse robotic platforms and complex real-world scenarios.

The existing research landscape for embodied navigation can be broadly categorized into two main areas, both facing limitations that NavFoM aims to address:

1. **Cross-Task Navigation:** Previous methods, such as Uni-NaVid, have attempted to unify various tasks like vision-and-language navigation, object searching, and target tracking. However, these approaches often assume a consistent camera configuration for the robot and are typically confined to indoor environments, lacking generalizability to broader scenarios like autonomous driving or UAV navigation.
2. **Cross-Embodiment Navigation:** Efforts to create navigation models that generalize across robots with varying shapes, sizes, and sensor configurations (e.g., GNM, ViNT) have shown potential using transformer-based policies on large datasets. Yet, these models often process multimodal inputs without explicit spatial and temporal cues, leading to potential ambiguities and limited generalization to out-of-distribution embodiments. They implicitly learn priors about physical shape but are frequently restricted to specific navigation tasks.

The "EMBODIED NAVIGATION FOUNDATION MODEL" (NavFoM) directly addresses this critical gap by proposing a truly *cross-embodiment and cross-task Navigation Foundation Model*. It builds upon the success of large language models (LLMs) and VLMs but specifically tackles their shortcomings in handling the diversity of navigation tasks and robotic platforms. By aiming to process multimodal navigation inputs from varying camera configurations and temporal horizons within a unified architecture, NavFoM pushes the boundaries towards a more "generalist navigation" paradigm. This aligns with the broader trend in AI towards developing versatile foundation models capable of solving a wide range of problems with minimal fine-tuning, thus significantly advancing the field of embodied AI beyond specialized, siloed solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a **cross-task and cross-embodiment Embodied Navigation Foundation Model (NavFoM)**. This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Generalization Gap:** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown impressive zero-shot generalization in many domains, their application to embodied navigation remains constrained by narrow task definitions and embodiment-specific architectures. The authors are motivated to close this gap, enabling navigation agents to perform robustly across a wide spectrum of tasks and physical robots, akin to human adaptability.
2. **Unifying Diverse Navigation Challenges:** Navigation is a fundamental capability, but it manifests differently across various robots (quadrupeds, drones, wheeled robots, vehicles) and tasks (VLN, object searching, target tracking, autonomous driving). The paper aims to unify these disparate challenges under a single, coherent framework, moving away from fragmented, specialized solutions.
3. **Enhancing Real-World Applicability:** Existing approaches often struggle with the practical demands of real-world deployment, such as hardware memory constraints and inference speed limitations. A key motivation is to design a model that is not only generalist but also efficient enough for real-time operation on diverse robotic platforms.
4. **Leveraging Multimodal Data Effectively:** The authors recognize the rich information embedded in egocentric videos and natural language instructions. Their objective is to develop an architecture that can robustly process and interpret multimodal inputs, including varying camera configurations and temporal contexts, which are crucial for comprehensive environmental understanding and task execution.

In essence, NavFoM seeks to be the foundational model for embodied navigation, capable of learning generalized navigation skills from a vast and diverse dataset, and applying them without task-specific fine-tuning, thereby fostering truly intelligent and adaptable embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NavFoM formulates generalist navigation as the task of processing egocentric videos (from multiple cameras) and natural language instructions to predict subsequent trajectories. The proposed solution is built upon a dual-branch architecture that extends vanilla video-based Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to handle both navigation and question-answering (QA) tasks.

#### 3.1. Basic Architecture and Observation Encoding

- **Vision Encoders:** Pre-trained DINOv2 and SigLIP are used to extract visual features from raw RGB images. For efficiency, these features are then pooled into two resolution scales: fine-grained (`V_fine` - 64 tokens) for the latest observation and image QA, and coarse-grained (`V_coarse` - 4 tokens) for navigation history and video data.
- **Cross-modality Projector:** A 2-layer MLP projects these visual features into the latent space of a Large Language Model (LLM).
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** Qwen2-7B is employed as the core reasoning engine, processing concatenated visual and language tokens.
- **Planning Head:** A 3-layer MLP decodes the LLM's action token into a waypoint-based trajectory.
- **Language Modeling Head:** For QA tasks, the model predicts the next token autoregressively.
- **Co-tuning:** NavFoM is trained end-to-end on a mixture of navigation and QA samples, allowing the LLM to acquire both navigation skills and open-world knowledge.

#### 3.2. Key Innovations for Generalization and Efficiency

- **Temporal-Viewpoint Indicator (TVI) Tokens:** A novel mechanism to enable the LLM to discern spatial (viewpoint) and temporal information from diverse camera setups and navigation horizons. TVI tokens comprise three types of embeddings:
 - **Angle Embedding (`AnglePE(ϕ)`):** Uses sinusoidal position encodings of cosine and sine values to preserve circular continuity for azimuthal angles.
 - **Time Embedding (`TimePE(t)`):** Sinusoidal position encoding for temporal order, robust to irregular sampling.
 - **Learnable Base Embedding (`E_Base`):** A foundational token for visual input.
 - These components are combined flexibly depending on the task: `E_Base` for Image QA, `E_Base + TimePE(t)` for Video QA, and `E_Base + TimePE(t) + AnglePE(ϕ)` for navigation. This strategy provides explicit cues to the LLM without disrupting the existing visual token space, crucial for multi-view and multi-temporal learning.

- **Budget-Aware Temporal Sampling (BATS) Strategy:** To address the computational burden of extensive visual tokens from long video sequences and multiple cameras, BATS dynamically samples historical tokens.
 - It employs an *exponential growth-based sampling probability* (inspired by the "forgetting curve"), prioritizing more recent observations while retaining sufficient historical context.
 - This strategy is constrained by a token budget (`B_token`), ensuring stable inference speed and GPU memory usage. It adapts directly to varying numbers of cameras.

#### 3.3. Token Organization and Trajectory Prediction

- **Token Organization:** Visual tokens, augmented with TVI tokens, are concatenated with language tokens for LLM processing. Image QA uses fine-grained visual tokens with only base TVI. Video QA uses coarse-grained visual tokens with base and time TVI. Navigation uses fine-grained for latest observations and coarse-grained for history, both with full temporal and viewpoint TVI.
- **Trajectory Prediction:** The planning head outputs M waypoints. These waypoints are normalized to a `[-1, 1]` range and then rescaled using task-specific factors (`α_task`) to accommodate the varied spatial scales of different embodiments (e.g., meters for indoor robots vs. tens of meters for cars). Loss functions include Mean Squared Error (MSE) for navigation trajectories and cross-entropy for QA.

#### 3.4. Extensive Data Collection and Training

- **Dataset:** A comprehensive and diverse dataset of 12.7 million samples was collected, significantly larger than previous methods (e.g., Uni-NaVid: 5.9M).
 - **Navigation Samples (8.02M):** Sourced from public datasets (VLN-CE R2R/RxR, OpenUAV, HM3D-OVON, EVT-Bench, nuScenes, OpenScene) and pseudo web-video data (Sekai). This covers diverse embodiments (quadrupeds, drones, wheeled robots, cars) and tasks (VLN, object searching, target tracking, autonomous driving). Data includes multi-view RGB videos, instructions, and ground-truth trajectories, often with camera randomization.
 - **Open-World QA Samples (4.76M):** Comprising image-based and video-based QA data from off-the-shelf datasets, enriching NavFoM's open-world understanding.
- **Training:** Conducted on a cluster with 56 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for 72 hours (4,032 GPU hours). Visual encoders and the LLM are initialized with pre-trained weights, with only specific parameters fine-tuned.
- **Training Acceleration:** A visual feature caching mechanism is implemented for coarse-grained tokens, pre-computing and storing these features offline. This speeds up training by 2.9x and reduces GPU memory usage by 1.8x.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NavFoM demonstrates remarkable performance and generalization capabilities across a wide array of embodied navigation tasks and robotic platforms, often achieving state-of-the-art (SOTA) or highly competitive results without task-specific fine-tuning.

- **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN):**
 - **VLN-CE (R2R, RxR):** NavFoM achieved SOTA performance on both R2R and RxR benchmarks. On the challenging single-view VLN-CE RxR, the success rate (SR) improved from 51.8% to 57.4%. In multi-camera settings (four views), it achieved an SR of 64.4% on RxR, significantly outperforming previous SOTA methods (56.3% SR) that required RGB-D and odometry information with only RGB inputs. This highlights the effectiveness of multi-view observations and the model's ability to generalize across camera configurations.
 - **OpenUAV:** NavFoM achieved SOTA performance in UAV navigation scenarios, despite using a simpler trajectory collection method and not relying on downward-facing cameras like baselines. This reinforces the benefits of learning from diverse navigation tasks.

- **Object Goal Navigation (HM3D-OVON):**
 - NavFoM outperformed the previous fine-tuned SOTA method (43.6% SR) on the VAL UNSEEN split, achieving 45.2% SR in a zero-shot setting. Consistent improvements were observed when transitioning from single-camera to four-camera setups, even though trained on single-camera search samples, showcasing robust generalization to varied camera configurations.

- **Active Visual Tracking (EVT-Bench):**
 - The model achieved SOTA performance in single-view tracking, outperforming TrackVLA (a specifically fine-tuned method) in the "Distracted Target" split (SR 61.4% vs. 57.6%). Performance further improved with a four-view setup (zero-shot), demonstrating strong adaptability to different sensor configurations.

- **Autonomous Driving (NAVSIM, nuScenes):**
 - NavFoM achieved performance comparable to SOTA specialized autonomous driving methods on both NAVSIM and nuScenes, notably without explicitly modeling driving-specific information such as lane markings or surrounding vehicles. This underscores the model's general perception and planning capabilities.

- **Real-World Experiments:**
 - Evaluations on 110 reproducible real-world test cases demonstrated strong practical applicability. NavFoM exhibited significant performance gains over a strong baseline (Uni-NaVid) across VLN (78% SR vs. 56%), searching (86% SR vs. 53%), and tracking (93% SR vs. 83%).
 - Qualitative results across quadrupeds, humanoids, drones, and wheeled robots in challenging scenarios further confirmed its robust generalizability and ability to handle long-horizon instructions, producing an 8-waypoint trajectory within 0.5 seconds.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Multi-task Synergy:** Co-tuning with diverse navigation tasks (VLN, searching, tracking, driving) consistently improved performance across all tasks, with particularly significant gains for searching (10.3% to 45.2% SR) and tracking (12.6% to 62.0% SR). This highlights the model's ability to learn generalized skills that transfer across different problem domains.
 - **Camera Count:** Increasing cameras from one to four consistently improved performance in VLN-CE RxR, validating the benefit of richer environmental observations. A slight degradation with six cameras was attributed to token budget limitations impacting historical context.
 - **TVI Tokens and BATS:** Both innovations proved crucial. BATS significantly outperformed other temporal sampling strategies (uniform, linear probability), maintaining better navigation-to-ground-truth alignment (nDTW) and efficiency. TVI tokens achieved markedly better performance than alternative positional encoding schemes, confirming their effectiveness in embedding temporal and viewpoint information without disrupting visual token representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "EMBODIED NAVIGATION FOUNDATION MODEL" (NavFoM) represents a groundbreaking contribution to the field of embodied AI, significantly advancing the quest for generalist robotic agents. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Pioneering a Unified Generalist Navigation Paradigm:** NavFoM is a pioneering step towards a truly universal navigation foundation model. By demonstrating cross-embodiment and cross-task generalization within a single architecture, it challenges the traditional approach of developing specialized models for each robot or task. This unification promises to streamline the development of embodied AI, making it more efficient and scalable.

2. **Robustness and Adaptability through Novel Architectural Designs:** The introduction of Temporal-Viewpoint Indicator (TVI) tokens and the Budget-Aware Temporal Sampling (BATS) strategy are critical innovations. TVI tokens provide an elegant solution for handling diverse camera configurations and temporal contexts, allowing the LLM to intrinsically understand varying sensor inputs. BATS addresses the practical constraints of real-world deployment by balancing performance with computational efficiency, ensuring that the model can operate effectively under limited resources. These designs enable NavFoM to generalize effectively without requiring task-specific fine-tuning, a major leap in practical applicability.

3. **Empirical Validation Across Broad Domains:** The extensive experimental validation, spanning seven public benchmarks across diverse tasks (VLN, object searching, tracking, autonomous driving) and embodiments (quadrupeds, drones, wheeled robots, vehicles), coupled with compelling real-world demonstrations, provides strong evidence of NavFoM's capabilities. Its ability to achieve SOTA or competitive performance consistently underscores the power of its generalist approach. The real-world experiments on multiple robotic platforms further validate its practical readiness and strong generalizability.

4. **Inspiring Future Research in Intelligence-Centric Navigation:** As acknowledged by the authors, NavFoM serves as a starting point. Its success is likely to inspire a new generation of research into more sophisticated techniques for multimodal integration, adaptive multi-view token encoding, more comprehensive datasets, and new benchmarks tailored for evaluating foundation models in embodied AI. This shift towards "intelligence-centric navigation" will push the boundaries of how robots perceive, reason, and act in complex, dynamic environments.

In conclusion, NavFoM fundamentally redefines the scope of embodied navigation research, moving from niche, specialized solutions to a unified, adaptable, and robust generalist framework. Its practical innovations and strong empirical performance pave the way for more autonomous, versatile, and intelligent robotic systems capable of navigating and interacting with the real world in unprecedented ways.
