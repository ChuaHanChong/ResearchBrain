# $\mathcal{D(R,O)}$ Grasp: A Unified Representation of Robot and Object Interaction for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the National University of Singapore (NUS) presents D(R, O) Grasp, a framework that employs an "interaction-centric" representation to achieve efficient and generalizable dexterous grasping. The system demonstrates an 87.53% success rate in simulation and 89% in real-world tasks on novel objects, while generating grasps in under one second.

## Problem

- Existing robot-centric grasping methods are fast but lack generalization across different robotic hand embodiments, requiring extensive re-training for each new hand.
- Current object-centric grasping methods generalize well across hands and objects but are computationally slow due to the need for post-prediction optimization steps like inverse kinematics.
- Developing a unified representation that can achieve both broad generalization (across hands and objects) and high efficiency for dexterous grasping remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- Introduces D(R, O) (Distance between Robot and Object), a unified representation as a point-to-point distance matrix between sampled robot hand and object points, encoding their interaction geometry.
- Employs a configuration-invariant pretraining method for the robot encoder, using point-level contrastive learning to teach the network to align features across different robot hand configurations.
- Utilizes a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) to predict the D(R, O) matrix, and then efficiently derives final robot joint configurations through multilateration, 6D link pose estimation via SVD, and iterative joint optimization.

## Results

- D(R, O) Grasp achieved an average success rate of 87.53% in simulation across three diverse dexterous hands and 89% in real-world experiments on novel objects with the LEAP Hand.
- The method generates grasps in less than one second in simulation, significantly outperforming optimization-based baselines (e.g., DFC >1800 seconds) and learning-based methods with post-optimization (e.g., GenDexGrasp 14-25 seconds).
- It demonstrated high robustness to partial object point cloud inputs (maintaining high success rates with 50% occlusion) and successfully generated diverse and firm grasps for various objects and desired palm orientations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An 'interaction-centric' D(R, O) representation, encoding the distance between robot hand and object points, unifies robot-specific details with object geometry for improved generalization.
> - Configuration-invariant pretraining using contrastive learning allows a robot encoder to understand hand articulation consistently, regardless of joint configuration, which is crucial for cross-embodiment transfer.
> - Deriving robot joint configurations from the D(R, O) matrix via multilateration, SVD-based link pose estimation, and efficient optimization provides a fast and robust method for grasp generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous grasping, the ability of robotic hands to precisely and stably interact with objects, is a cornerstone of advanced robotic manipulation. However, its complexity, stemming from the high degrees of freedom (DoF) in multi-fingered hands and the geometric intricacies of objects, makes it a profoundly challenging problem. The current research landscape in dexterous grasping is broadly characterized by two main paradigms:

- **Robot-centric representations**: Methods like UniDexGrasp++ directly learn to map observations to robot-specific control commands (e.g., joint values, wrist poses). While these approaches can enable fast inference once trained, they suffer from low sample efficiency and, critically, struggle with generalization across different robotic hand embodiments. A model trained for a ShadowHand, for instance, cannot be directly applied to an Allegro hand without extensive re-training.
- **Object-centric representations**: Approaches such as UniGrasp and GenDexGrasp focus on object properties like contact points or contact maps. These methods excel at generalizing across various object shapes and different robot hands by abstracting away robot-specific joint values. However, they typically require an additional, computationally intensive optimization step (e.g., inverse kinematics) to translate the object-centric grasp representation into actionable robot commands, leading to slow inference speeds.

D(R, O) Grasp fundamentally redefines this landscape by proposing an **"interaction-centric"** representation. It addresses the limitations of both prior paradigms by developing a unified model that directly encodes the relationship between the robot hand's grasping pose and the object. This novel approach seeks to combine the generalizability of object-centric methods with the efficiency of robot-centric approaches. By directly predicting a representation that allows for rapid calculation of valid joint configurations, D(R, O) Grasp aims to push the boundaries of cross-embodiment generalization and real-time dexterous manipulation, thus offering a more versatile and practical solution for complex robotic tasks. This work builds upon and contrasts with previous efforts like DFC (optimization-based), GenDexGrasp (contact map, optimization), and ManiFM (contact map, point-contact), clearly demonstrating its superior performance in terms of success rate, diversity, and computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of D(R, O) Grasp is to enable **broad generalization for dexterous grasping across various robotic hands and object geometries** while maintaining **high efficiency and robustness**. The authors identify the core limitations of existing robot-centric and object-centric paradigms as their motivation:

- **Bridging the gap between generalizability and efficiency**: Robot-centric methods are fast but lack generalization, while object-centric methods generalize well but are computationally slow due to the need for post-prediction optimization. The motivation is to create a unified representation that overcomes this trade-off.
- **Developing a unified, interaction-centric representation**: The paper proposes the D(R, O) (Distance between Robot and Object) representation, which directly captures the spatial relationship between the robotic hand and the object in the desired grasp pose. This is envisioned to encapsulate both the articulated structure of the hand and the object's geometry in a single, learnable format.
- **Ensuring kinematically valid and stable grasps**: The model aims to predict grasps that are not only geometrically feasible but also physically stable, satisfying force closure conditions.
- **Promoting cross-embodiment generalization**: A significant goal is to allow the trained model to adapt to different robot hand designs without extensive re-training. This is partly addressed through a novel "configuration-invariant pretraining" method.
- **Achieving fast inference speed**: The framework is designed to quickly convert the predicted D(R, O) representation into executable joint configurations, aiming for real-time performance.
- **Demonstrating robustness to real-world challenges**: This includes handling partial object point cloud inputs and achieving high success rates in actual physical robot deployments.

In essence, the authors are motivated to develop a robust, versatile, and efficient dexterous grasping solution that can seamlessly transfer across different robotic platforms and object variations, moving beyond the current limitations of specialized or computationally expensive approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The D(R, O) Grasp framework introduces a novel, unified approach to dexterous grasping by modeling the interaction between the robot hand and the object through a distance-based representation. The methodology is structured into three main components: configuration-invariant pretraining, D(R, O) prediction using a CVAE, and efficient grasp configuration generation.

#### 3.1. D(R, O) Representation

At the core of the method is the **D(R, O) representation**, a point-to-point distance matrix (R^(N_R x N_O)) that encodes the relative distances between a set of sampled points on the robot hand (in its desired grasping pose) and a set of sampled points on the object. Both point clouds are implicitly brought to a common origin, allowing the matrix to represent their interaction geometry directly. This representation is unique because it combines aspects of both robot and object in an "interaction-centric" manner, unlike purely robot-centric (joint values) or object-centric (contact points) methods.

#### 3.2. Configuration-Invariant Pretraining

To enable robust cross-embodiment generalization and to facilitate the network's understanding of hand articulation, the authors propose a **configuration-invariant pretraining method**. This method addresses the challenge that the local geometric features of a robot hand point cloud vary significantly between open-hand and closed-hand (grasp) configurations.
- **Objective**: Train a robot encoder network to learn an inherent alignment between various hand configurations. This means that features corresponding to the same physical point on the robot hand should be consistent regardless of the hand's joint configuration.
- **Approach**: It uses point-level contrastive learning. For a given successful grasp configuration (q_A) and a canonical open-hand configuration (q_B) with similar wrist pose, corresponding point clouds P_A and P_B are generated. The encoder produces point-wise features for both. A contrastive loss function (L_p) is applied, pulling embeddings of positive pairs (points at the same index/location on the hand across different configurations) closer together while pushing negative pairs (different points) apart. This effectively teaches the network to "understand" the self-articulation of the robot hand. The pre-trained robot encoder is then frozen during the main D(R, O) prediction phase.

#### 3.3. CVAE-based D(R, O) Prediction

After pretraining the robot encoder, the main network predicts the D(R, O) matrix.
- **Inputs**: Point clouds of the open robot hand (P_R) and the object (P_O), along with a specified wrist pose (e.g., user-defined palm orientation or randomly sampled).
- **Feature Extraction**: Two identical encoder networks (modified DGCNNs) extract point-wise features (ϕ_R, ϕ_O) from P_R and P_O. The robot encoder uses the pre-trained and frozen weights.
- **Correspondence Learning**: Two multi-head cross-attention transformers integrate these features, creating correlated feature sets (ψ_R, ψ_O) that embed the relationship between robot and object points.
- **CVAE Prediction**: A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) takes the combined point clouds (P_G from a ground-truth grasp) and the learned features (ψ_R, ψ_O) to predict the D(R, O) matrix. The CVAE's latent variable (z) enables the generation of diverse grasps. A kernel function, applied to the concatenated latent variable and point features, computes the pairwise distances that form the D(R, O) matrix.

#### 3.4. Grasp Configuration Generation from D(R, O)

Once the D(R, O) matrix is predicted, the final robot joint values are derived in a series of efficient steps:
- **Robotic Grasp Pose Point Cloud Generation**: For each point on the robot hand, its desired position in the grasp pose (p'_R_i) is determined by a multilateration method. This technique uses the predicted distances in D(R, O) to all object points to solve a least-squares optimization problem, effectively "positioning" the robot point cloud relative to the object. The use of 512 reference points (N_O) ensures robustness.
- **6D Pose Estimation of Links**: Directly solving inverse kinematics from a point cloud is complex. Instead, the predicted robot point cloud (P_P) is segmented into individual link point clouds. For each link, a rigid body registration technique (using Singular Value Decomposition, SVD) is applied to estimate its 6D pose (translation and rotation) in the world frame.
- **Joint Configuration Optimization**: Finally, the system optimizes the robot's joint values to align the actual link translations with the predicted 6D poses. This is formulated as an iterative optimization problem using CVXPY, which efficiently computes delta joint values (δq) while respecting joint limits. This step is designed to be fast and stable, even for high-DoF hands.

#### 3.5. Loss Function

The overall loss function for training the network is a composite of four terms:
- **L_L1**: An L1 loss for the direct prediction of the D(R, O) matrix against ground truth.
- **L_T**: A loss (L_ℓ) that penalizes the difference between the predicted 6D poses of individual links and their ground truth. This includes both translational (L2 norm) and rotational (arccosine of trace) components.
- **L_P**: A penetration penalty (L_P) that sums the negative values of the Signed Distance Function (SDF) between the robot hand and the object point clouds, ensuring the generated grasp is penetration-free.
- **L_KL**: A KL divergence term (D_KL) for the CVAE's latent variable, encouraging it to follow a standard Gaussian distribution for diverse grasp generation.

This holistic approach, integrating novel representation, specialized pretraining, and efficient post-prediction processing, constitutes a significant advancement in dexterous grasping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted in both simulation and real-world environments validate the effectiveness and robustness of the D(R, O) Grasp framework.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison

- **High Success Rates**: D(R, O) Grasp achieved an impressive average success rate of **87.53% in simulation** across three diverse dexterous hands (Barrett, Allegro, and ShadowHand), significantly outperforming baselines like DFC (73.77%), GenDexGrasp (57.40%), and ManiFM (42.60%). In real-world experiments with the LeapHand, it demonstrated an average success rate of **89%** on novel objects.
- **Exceptional Efficiency**: A key strength of D(R, O) Grasp is its speed, generating a grasp in **less than one second** in simulation. This is a dramatic improvement over optimization-based methods like DFC (which takes over 1800 seconds) and learning-based methods with complex post-optimization steps like GenDexGrasp (14-25 seconds) and ManiFM (9 seconds). This efficiency is crucial for practical robotic applications.
- **Firm Grasps**: Successful grasps generated by D(R, O) Grasp resulted in minimal object displacement (under 2mm average) and rotation (below 1 degree average) when subjected to disturbance forces, indicating high stability and firmness.

#### 4.2. Diverse Grasp Synthesis

- The CVAE-based architecture allows for the generation of **diverse grasps** by sampling from the latent variable `z`. D(R, O) Grasp's diversity metric (standard deviation of joint values) was competitive, showcasing its ability to produce multiple valid grasp configurations for the same object and wrist orientation.
- The model also demonstrated **controllability over grasp pose**, consistently generating feasible grasps based on specified input palm orientations (e.g., top-down, side grasps), which is a valuable feature for task planning.

#### 4.3. Configuration Correspondence Learning

- The **configuration-invariant pretraining** proved highly effective. Visualization showed excellent point matching between open-hand and closed-hand configurations, confirming that the pretrained encoder successfully captures inherent alignment across varying hand poses.
- The ablation study (training with and without pretraining) further confirmed its importance: removing the pretraining significantly degraded performance across all robotic hands, validating its contribution to grasp generation performance and cross-embodiment feature alignment.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Partial Object Point Cloud Input

- D(R, O) Grasp demonstrated remarkable **robustness to incomplete sensor data**. When trained and tested with 50% of the object point cloud removed (simulating real-world occlusion), the method maintained high success rates (Barrett: 84.70%, Allegro: 87.60%, ShadowHand: 81.80%). This indicates that its interaction-centric representation does not rely on full object visibility, a critical advantage for real-world deployment.

#### 4.5. Real-World Validation

- Real-robot experiments using a uFactory xArm6 with a LEAP Hand and Realsense D435 camera yielded an impressive **89% average success rate across 10 novel objects**. This practical validation confirms the sim-to-real transferability and real-world applicability of the method on unseen objects, showcasing its strong generalization capabilities beyond simulation.

#### 4.6. Zero-shot Generalization to Novel Hands

- Exploratory experiments on zero-shot generalization to entirely novel hands revealed an interesting asymmetry: the model showed a certain level of performance when transferring **from high-DoF hands to low-DoF hands**, but largely failed in the opposite direction. This suggests that training on complex articulation spaces (high-DoF) enables learning broader articulation-invariant features that can generalize to simpler hand designs, but the reverse is not true.

In summary, D(R, O) Grasp consistently demonstrates superior performance across key metrics – success rate, speed, diversity, and robustness – positioning it as a leading method in dexterous grasping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The D(R, O) Grasp framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation, with profound implications for both research and real-world applications.

- **Paradigm Shift in Representation**: The introduction of the "D(R, O) interaction-centric" representation is a key conceptual breakthrough. By transcending the limitations of conventional robot-centric and object-centric paradigms, it offers a unified and more comprehensive way to model grasping interactions. This could inspire new research directions in robotics, encouraging the development of other interaction-centric models for various manipulation tasks.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability**: The ability of D(R, O) Grasp to generalize robustly across diverse robotic hands (different DoF, designs) and novel object geometries is paramount. This cross-embodiment capability drastically reduces the need for extensive retraining when deploying robots in new environments or with different end-effectors, accelerating development and deployment cycles. This is particularly impactful for service robotics, manufacturing, and logistics where varied objects and robotic systems are common.
- **Real-time Dexterous Grasping**: The remarkable inference speed (under one second) achieved by D(R, O) Grasp makes dexterous manipulation feasible in real-time applications. This opens doors for dynamic, responsive manipulation in unstructured environments, such as grasping objects on a moving conveyor belt or responding to human-robot interaction in collaborative tasks. Current, slower methods are often limited to static pick-and-place scenarios.
- **Robustness to Real-world Imperfections**: The demonstrated resilience to partial object point cloud inputs directly addresses a major challenge in real-world perception. Depth cameras often provide noisy or incomplete data due to occlusions or sensor limitations. D(R, O) Grasp's ability to operate effectively under such conditions makes it more reliable for practical deployment outside controlled laboratory settings.
- **Foundation for Broader Manipulation Tasks**: By solving dexterous grasping efficiently and generally, D(R, O) Grasp lays a crucial foundation for more complex manipulation tasks that involve not just grasping but also re-grasping, in-hand manipulation, and tool use. A robust initial grasp is often the prerequisite for any subsequent complex action.
- **Advancement in Learning Methodologies**: The configuration-invariant pretraining with contrastive learning is a notable methodological contribution. This approach for learning inherent structural properties of robotic hands can be extended to other areas of robot learning, such as pose estimation, imitation learning, or even robot design, where understanding body kinematics and morphology is essential.

In conclusion, D(R, O) Grasp offers a practical, high-performance solution that significantly pushes the boundaries of dexterous grasping. Its unified representation, combined with efficient learning and execution, holds the potential to accelerate the adoption of advanced robotic manipulation capabilities in a wide array of industries and research domains.
