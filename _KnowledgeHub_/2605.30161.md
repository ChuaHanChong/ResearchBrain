# Why Far Looks Up: Probing Spatial Representation in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University and NVIDIA investigated whether Vision-Language Models (VLMs) achieve spatial reasoning via genuine 3D understanding or by exploiting statistical shortcuts, discovering a pervasive “vertical-distance entanglement” where 2D vertical position is conflated with 3D depth. They introduced a representation-level analysis framework and a synthetic benchmark to diagnose this bias, demonstrating that disentangled internal representations correlate with robust spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- It is unclear if high VLM performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks reflects genuine 3D understanding or relies on statistical shortcuts inherent in 2D image data.
- VLMs may exploit common perspective biases in natural images, like objects higher in an image being farther away, leading to a conflation of 2D vertical position and 3D depth.
- Existing research lacks systematic methods to analyze how global geometric spatial relations are organized and disentangled within VLM representation space, particularly concerning the entanglement of 2D image cues with 3D spatial properties.

## Method

- A representation-level analysis framework was developed to probe VLM internal embeddings using minimal contrastive VQA pairs, generating "delta vectors" to isolate spatial relation representations.
- Metrics such as "Axis Coherence" and a "VD-Entanglement Index" were introduced to quantify the structuredness and degree of vertical-distance entanglement for horizontal, vertical, and depth axes within VLM representations.
- A novel synthetic benchmark called "SpatialTunnel" was created using Blender to systematically decouple an object's vertical image-plane position from its true 3D depth, alongside an object size variant, to rigorously test for shortcut biases in a controlled environment.

## Results

- Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks were found to be heavily skewed, with **80.9%** of EmbSpatial-Bench and **60.5%** of CV-Bench-3D examples being "consistent" (farther objects appear higher).
- All tested VLM families (Molmo, NVILA, Qwen) and scales exhibited a substantial accuracy gap, performing significantly better on perspective-consistent examples than on "counter" examples (e.g., a 36.9 percentage-point difference for Qwen2.5-VL on EmbSpatial-Bench).
- The SpatialTunnel benchmark confirmed universal "vertical-distance entanglement" (positive accuracy gap for all models) and revealed reliance on apparent object size as a depth cue, though larger models or those with extensive spatial fine-tuning showed reduced bias (e.g., RoboRefer-2B-SFT had a relatively small gap of +0.046).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs generally exhibit a widespread and model-intrinsic "vertical-distance entanglement," conflating 2D vertical image position with 3D depth due to statistical correlations in natural training data.
> - High performance on existing spatial reasoning benchmarks can be misleading, as these benchmarks are often skewed towards perspective-consistent examples, allowing models to succeed without robust 3D understanding.
> - The coherence of the "distance" axis (Coh_D) within VLM representations is a critical predictor; models with higher Coh_D and lower VD-Entanglement Index demonstrate more robust and generalizable spatial reasoning across diverse tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are increasingly integrated into applications requiring spatial reasoning, such as robotics, embodied agents, and multimodal assistants. Despite being predominantly trained on 2D image-text pairs, these models often achieve high performance on existing spatial reasoning benchmarks. This success has led to ongoing efforts to enhance VLM spatial capabilities through model scaling and the incorporation of more spatial training data.

However, a fundamental question remains regarding the nature of this observed spatial understanding: does it reflect genuine, structured 3D comprehension, or does it stem from the exploitation of statistical regularities and shortcuts prevalent in natural photographic data? For instance, the phenomenon of perspective projection in everyday images often correlates an object's vertical position with its depth—objects appearing higher in an image are frequently farther from the camera. Models leveraging such correlations might succeed on benchmarks without developing a robust, separable understanding of distinct spatial dimensions.

Previous research has identified limitations in VLM spatial reasoning through controlled benchmarks, exposing failures in basic positional distinctions and frame-of-reference consistency. Other studies have begun to probe internal model behaviors, such as attention dynamics. Nevertheless, these efforts primarily assess individual task performance or localized mechanisms. The broader investigation into how global geometric spatial relations are organized and disentangled within VLM representation space, particularly concerning the entanglement of 2D image cues with 3D spatial properties like depth, has remained less explored. This paper addresses this gap by introducing a framework for representation-level analysis and a novel synthetic benchmark designed to isolate and expose these potential shortcuts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether the strong spatial reasoning performance of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on benchmarks genuinely reflects structured 3D understanding or if it is partially attributable to reliance on statistical shortcuts inherent in natural images. The authors aim to move beyond purely behavioral evaluations to diagnose how spatial information is encoded within VLM internal representations.

The motivation for this work stems from several detailed considerations:

- **Ambiguity of Benchmark Accuracy:** While VLMs achieve high scores on spatial reasoning benchmarks, the interpretability of these scores is limited. Two models might yield similar output accuracy but achieve it through fundamentally different internal mechanisms. One model might employ a structured, separable encoding of spatial relations, while another might rely on brittle correlations present in natural imagery. Current benchmarks do not sufficiently differentiate these scenarios, potentially overestimating true spatial capabilities.
- **The "Vertical-Distance Entanglement" Hypothesis:** A key hypothesis is that VLMs exploit a common perspective bias in natural photographs: objects located higher in an image are often farther away in 3D space. This leads to a shortcut where models conflate 2D vertical image position with 3D depth, termed "vertical-distance entanglement." This reliance could lead to systematic failures on "counter-heuristic" examples where this correlation is reversed or absent.
- **Need for Representation-Level Analysis:** To understand *how* models perform spatial reasoning, it is necessary to examine their internal representations. This involves analyzing how different spatial axes (horizontal, vertical, depth) are organized and disentangled within VLM embeddings, rather than solely focusing on the correctness of output predictions.
- **Need for Bias-Controlled Evaluation:** Natural image datasets intrinsically contain correlations that can enable shortcuts. To isolate and rigorously test the "vertical-distance entanglement" bias, a controlled environment is required where these correlations can be systematically removed or manipulated. Existing benchmarks do not adequately provide this control.

Based on these motivations, the paper outlines three specific contributions that serve as key objectives:

1. **Develop a Representation-Level Analysis Framework:** Introduce a method to analyze how spatial relations are organized within VLM embeddings. This framework aims to diagnose whether models possess structured spatial reasoning or rely on shortcut cues by constructing minimal contrastive pairs.
2. **Investigate the Predictive Power of Spatial Representations:** Demonstrate that models with similar benchmark performance can exhibit distinct internal spatial representations. Furthermore, show that models with more structured (disentangled and coherent) spatial representations are more robust and generalize better across diverse spatial reasoning tasks.
3. **Construct a Bias-Controlled Synthetic Benchmark:** Create a novel synthetic dataset, "SpatialTunnel," designed to decouple vertical image position from 3D depth. This benchmark aims to expose shortcut biases that might remain hidden in standard evaluation settings due to correlations present in natural images.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged approach: a representation-level analysis framework and a novel synthetic benchmark.

#### 3.1. Representation-Level Analysis Framework

The framework analyzes how spatial axes (horizontal, vertical, depth) are organized within VLM internal embeddings using controlled contrastive examples.

- **Contrastive Pair Construction:** Given a spatial-relation Visual Question Answering (VQA) task, a minimal contrastive pair is generated by swapping the order of the queried objects. For example, "Is A to the left or right of B?" is swapped with "Is B to the left or right of A?" This action flips the ground-truth spatial relation (e.g., left becomes right) while keeping other visual context consistent. For depth questions on EmbSpatial-Bench, which are multiple-choice, the target and reference objects are swapped to achieve a similar label-flip.
- **Delta Vector Computation:** For each question in a pair, the final-token hidden state (h_q) at a selected intermediate layer (L*) of the VLM is extracted. A "delta vector" (δ) is computed as the difference between the hidden states of the swapped questions (δ = h_q2 - h_q1). This vector is intended to isolate the representation-space displacement induced by the spatial relation swap.
- **Metrics for Representation Analysis:**
 - **Axis Coherence (Coh_axis):** For each spatial axis (horizontal, vertical, distance), delta vectors from opposing categories (e.g., "far" and "close") are pooled. Vectors from the "opposite" category are negated to align their direction with a canonical direction (e.g., all pointing towards "far"). Axis coherence is then calculated as the mean pairwise cosine similarity over this set of sign-corrected delta vectors. High coherence indicates a stable, consistent direction for that axis in the embedding space.
 - **VD-Entanglement Index (VD-EI):** This metric quantifies the degree of vertical-distance entanglement. It is calculated based on the mean delta vectors (μ_c) for "above," "below," "far," and "close" categories. Specifically, VD-EI averages the cosine similarities between perspective-aligned pairs (above↔far, below↔close) and subtracts the average cosine similarities between perspective-opposing pairs (above↔close, below↔far). A positive VD-EI indicates directional coupling between vertical and distance representations, consistent with perspective projection bias.
- **Layer Selection:** A single representative intermediate layer (L*) is chosen for each model family. Criteria for selection prioritize layers where axis coherence across all three axes is near its peak and VD-EI is stable, while avoiding the model's final layers which are often specialized for output generation.

#### 3.2. SpatialTunnel Synthetic Benchmark

- **Design Philosophy:** To decouple an object's vertical image-plane position from its 3D depth, SpatialTunnel utilizes a custom Blender-generated scene. The environment features a tunnel-shaped corridor with symmetric walls, ceiling, and floor, observed from a single-point perspective.
- **Scene Generation:** Two objects are placed at predetermined, distinct depths within the tunnel, with one always farther from the camera than the other. Objects are parameterized by their depth (z) and an angular position (θ) on the tunnel's cross-section. By holding z fixed and varying θ, the objects' 2D image-plane positions (vertical and horizontal) can be changed independently of their 3D depth ordering.
- **Controlled Stimuli:** The environment discretizes object angular positions into a 16x16 grid of configurations (θ1, θ2). For visual diversity and robustness, object appearance (shape, color, size) and scene lighting are randomized across renders.
- **VQA Protocol:** For each rendered image, the model is asked binary depth-comparison questions (e.g., "Is {obj1} closer to / farther from the camera than {obj2}?"). Four question templates (varying object order and comparison direction) are used to control for wording and answer-polarity biases.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Mean accuracy (v):** Average correctness score across all images and questions.
 - **Consistent accuracy (v_cons):** Mean correctness on examples where the farther object appears higher in the image (perspective-consistent).
 - **Counter accuracy (v_ctr):** Mean correctness on examples where the farther object appears lower in the image (counter-heuristic).
 - **Accuracy gap (∆):** The difference (v_cons - v_ctr), quantifying the vertical-distance entanglement; a positive gap indicates reliance on the shortcut.
- **Object Size Variant:** An additional experiment using SpatialTunnel systematically varies object sizes while keeping true depth fixed to test reliance on apparent size as a depth cue. The size of the farther object (s1) and nearer object (s2) are anti-correlated (s1 + s2 = constant), allowing for "size-consistent" (farther object smaller) and "size-conflicting" (farther object larger) scenarios. A "size-bias gap" (∆s) is calculated similarly to the accuracy gap.

#### 3.3. Models and Data

- **Models Evaluated:** A range of open-source VLM families were assessed: Molmo-7B-O-0924, NVILA-Lite-2B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct. Variants of these models were fine-tuned with increasing scales of spatial data (80k, 400k, 800k, 2M samples). RoboRefer-2B-SFT and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct were included as large-scale reference models. Proprietary models (GPT-5.2, Gemini-2.5-Pro) were also evaluated on SpatialTunnel using exact-match accuracy.
- **Training Data:** Spatial fine-tuning data mixes were composed from five existing spatial understanding datasets: SAT, RoboSpatial, SPAR-7M, RefSpatial, and PRISM.
- **External Benchmarks:** Performance was also evaluated on established spatial reasoning benchmarks: EmbSpatial-Bench, the 2D-spatial and 3D-spatial splits of CV-Bench, and the spatial relationship and relative depth splits of BLINK.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding spatial representation and reasoning in VLMs:

#### 4.1. Vertical-Distance Entanglement on Existing Benchmarks

- **Data Skew:** Analysis of existing real-world spatial reasoning benchmarks (EmbSpatial-Bench, CV-Bench-3D) revealed a significant skew towards "consistent" examples (80.9% in EmbSpatial-Bench, 60.5% in CV-Bench-3D). In these examples, the object farther from the camera consistently appears higher in the image, aligning with the natural perspective bias. Conversely, "counter" examples, where this correlation is reversed, constitute only about 10% of the datasets.
- **Systematic Failure on Counter Examples:** Across all tested VLM families (Molmo, NVILA, Qwen), model sizes, and spatial fine-tuning scales, there was a substantial accuracy gap. Models consistently performed significantly better on "consistent" examples than on "counter" examples. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL fine-tuned on 2M samples achieved 60.9% accuracy on consistent EmbSpatial-Bench examples but only 24% on counter examples, a 36.9 percentage-point difference. This pattern indicates a widespread "vertical-distance entanglement" across diverse architectures and training regimes.

#### 4.2. Behavioral Analysis with SpatialTunnel

- **Universal Entanglement:** Experiments on the SpatialTunnel benchmark confirmed that "vertical-distance entanglement" is a universal phenomenon. All base and fine-tuned models exhibited a positive accuracy gap (∆ = v_cons - v_ctr), meaning they performed better on perspective-consistent configurations within the controlled environment.
- **Examples:** The base Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, for example, achieved a v_cons of 0.776 but a v_ctr of only 0.360, demonstrating a strong reliance on the vertical-position shortcut.
- **Impact of Data Scaling:** While incremental spatial fine-tuning did not eliminate the bias, models trained on very large-scale or diverse spatial datasets showed reduced entanglement. RoboRefer-2B-SFT (trained on >20M QA pairs) achieved a relatively small gap (∆ = +0.046) with strong overall accuracy. Qwen3-VL-235B, a very large model, also attained high accuracy (v = 0.908) with a small gap (∆ = +0.068), suggesting that extensive pretraining can mitigate this bias.
- **Object Size Entanglement:** An additional SpatialTunnel variant showed that models also rely on apparent object size as a shortcut for depth. Performance degraded significantly when the farther object was rendered larger than the nearer one, contradicting the typical size-depth correlation. RoboRefer demonstrated greater robustness to this size-based bias as well.

#### 4.3. Representation Analysis via Contrastive Probing

- **Weakest Distance Axis Coherence:** Across all models and training scales, the "distance" axis coherence (Coh_D) was consistently the lowest among the three spatial axes (horizontal, vertical, distance). This suggests that models struggle to form a stable, consistent directional representation for depth compared to horizontal or vertical relationships.
- **Distance Coherence Predicts Counter Accuracy:** A positive correlation was observed between Coh_D and counter accuracy on EmbSpatial-Bench. As models, particularly NVILA and Molmo variants, increased their Coh_D through spatial fine-tuning, their ability to correctly answer counter-heuristic questions also improved. Conversely, models like Qwen, which showed stagnant Coh_D, did not resolve the entanglement and saw their counter accuracy decline or remain low. This suggests that a more coherent internal representation of depth is critical for robustness against shortcuts.
- **Cross-Domain Validity:** Coh_D measured on SpatialTunnel correlated significantly with counter accuracy on both EmbSpatial-Bench and CV-Bench-3D, indicating that Coh_D captures a robust, generalizable aspect of spatial representation quality beyond specific evaluation datasets.
- **Characteristics of Strong Spatial Representations:** Models exhibiting robust spatial reasoning (e.g., RoboRefer-2B-SFT and Qwen3-VL-235B) were characterized by:
 - High Coh_D (RoboRefer had the highest Coh_D (0.182) in its family).
 - Low VD-Entanglement Index (RoboRefer had the lowest VD-EI (0.362)).
 - PCA visualizations of delta vectors for these models showed three clearly separated clusters, each aligned with a distinct principal component representing the horizontal, vertical, and distance axes. In contrast, other models often showed horizontal and vertical separation but significant overlap between vertical and distance delta vectors, reflecting their entanglement.
- **Heatmaps of Cross-Category Similarity:** Heatmaps confirmed that opposing categories on the same axis (e.g., left vs. right) were consistently anti-aligned (cosine similarity near -1), and horizontal was largely orthogonal to vertical and distance. However, perspective-aligned pairs (above↔far, below↔close) between vertical and distance axes showed meaningful positive similarity (0.1–0.65), directly demonstrating the directional coupling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has several significant implications for the understanding and development of Vision-Language Models:

- **Identifies a Pervasive Intrinsic Bias:** The discovery of "vertical-distance entanglement" as a widespread, model-intrinsic bias across various VLM families and scales reveals a fundamental limitation in their ability to robustly reason about 3D space from 2D images. This bias highlights that current VLMs may not fully disentangle 2D visual cues from underlying 3D spatial properties.
- **Re-evaluates Benchmark Efficacy:** The findings demonstrate that high performance on existing spatial reasoning benchmarks does not necessarily equate to robust 3D spatial understanding. Models can achieve high scores by exploiting statistical shortcuts, leading to an overestimation of their true capabilities. This calls for more nuanced evaluation strategies that specifically probe for shortcut reliance.
- **Introduces Novel Diagnostic Tools:** The development of a representation-level analysis framework and the SpatialTunnel synthetic benchmark provides researchers with concrete tools to:
 - Diagnose the presence and extent of spatial shortcut biases within VLM embeddings.
 - Quantitatively measure the structuredness and disentanglement of internal spatial representations.
 - Conduct controlled experiments to isolate the influence of specific visual cues on spatial judgments.
- **Provides Guidance for Model Development:** The strong correlation between structured spatial representations (high distance coherence, low vertical-distance entanglement) and robust performance across diverse benchmarks offers a clear target for future VLM development. This suggests that training strategies should not merely aim for higher behavioral accuracy but also for the explicit formation of disentangled and coherent internal spatial axes. This might involve new architectural designs, diverse training data that reduces shortcuts, or regularization techniques that encourage axis disentanglement.
- **Impacts Embodied AI and Robotics:** For applications in robotics, embodied agents, and multimodal assistants, robust 3D spatial understanding from 2D vision is critical for safe and effective interaction with the physical world. Models susceptible to vertical-distance entanglement could make unreliable spatial judgments, leading to errors in navigation, manipulation, and interaction planning. This work provides a foundation for building more reliable and trustworthy VLMs for these crucial domains.
