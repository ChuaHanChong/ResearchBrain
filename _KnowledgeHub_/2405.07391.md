# AnyRotate: Gravity-Invariant In-Hand Object Rotation with Sim-to-Real Touch

> [!summary] Summary
> The "AnyRotate" framework enables a robot hand to perform gravity-invariant, multi-axis in-hand object rotation using a unified policy and dense featured sim-to-real touch. It demonstrates zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world, allowing manipulation across arbitrary hand orientations and during continuous arm movement, and exhibiting emergent reactive behaviors for grasp stabilization.

## Problem

- Existing robotic in-hand manipulation policies often focus on single-axis rotation or require fixed hand orientations, struggling with arbitrary object reorientation against varying gravity.
- Achieving a unified manipulation policy that works across continuously changing hand orientations and gravity vectors remained a significant challenge.
- The sim-to-real gap limited the practical use of high-resolution tactile data, often requiring simplification of valuable contact information for robotic control.

## Method

- A teacher-student policy distillation framework, where a teacher policy is trained with privileged information in simulation using PPO, and a student policy learns to imitate it using only real-world observations.
- A novel sim-to-real approach for dense tactile features, extracting both contact pose (local position/orientation) and contact force magnitude from tactile images via a CNN for integration into the policy.
- An auxiliary goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation, defining object reorientation towards a moving target, along with an adaptive curriculum to guide learning of stable and effective finger-gaiting motions.

## Results

- The dense touch policy achieved successful zero-shot transfer to the real world, consistently outperforming policies based on proprioception alone or binary touch for gravity-invariant, multi-axis object rotation.
- The policy demonstrated emergent reactive behaviors, detecting unstable grasps through boundary contact and dynamically adjusting finger-gaiting to re-stabilize objects, a capability absent in policies with simpler tactile inputs.
- Manipulation was successfully performed with a continuously moving and rotating hand, allowing 6D reorientation of objects while the arm's orientation relative to gravity was constantly changing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense tactile representations (contact pose and force magnitude) are crucial for robust dexterity, especially for handling objects with out-of-distribution (OOD) mass and shape properties, outperforming simpler tactile inputs.
> - The auxiliary goal formulation, which frames the task as object reorientation to a moving target, is significantly more effective for training multi-axis in-hand rotation than angular velocity-based objectives.
> - Successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of complex, dense tactile features is achievable through careful data collection, perception model training, and simulation parameter tuning combined with extensive domain randomization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The quest for robotic hands capable of human-like dexterity in manipulating objects has been a long-standing goal in robotics. Historically, this complex problem has been approached through classical control methods relying on simplified models, which, despite offering performance guarantees, are often constrained by their underlying assumptions. The advent of machine learning has significantly propelled advancements in dexterous manipulation, with prominent works like those by OpenAI demonstrating remarkable feats (e.g., solving the Rubik's cube). However, a common characteristic of many advanced manipulation systems, particularly in deep reinforcement learning, is their reliance on vision as the primary sensing modality. While powerful, vision-based systems face inherent limitations in contact-rich tasks due to self-occlusion, the need for multiple cameras, and their inability to capture fine-grained local contact information crucial for stable grasps and intricate movements.

In response to these limitations, the research landscape has seen a growing interest in utilizing proprioception (joint positions, velocities) combined with touch sensing for in-hand manipulation. Prior work in this area, however, often grapples with several constraints:
- **Limited scope of manipulation:** Many studies focus on object rotation about primary axes (e.g., single-axis rotation) or require separate policies for different rotation axes.
- **Fixed hand orientations:** A significant challenge is performing manipulation when the hand is not in an "easy" orientation (e.g., palm up), as gravity constantly works to destabilize the object. While some progress has been made with downward-facing hands using gravity curricula or precision grasp techniques, achieving a *unified policy* that works across *arbitrary hand orientations* and *continuously changing gravity vectors* remains an open problem.
- **Simplified tactile data:** Due to the large sim-to-real gap inherent in transferring high-resolution tactile data from simulation to the real world, researchers often resort to low-dimensional representations such as binary contact detection or discretized contact locations. This simplification, while practical for transfer, might sacrifice valuable information necessary for truly dexterous tasks.

"AnyRotate" directly addresses these critical gaps. It aims to achieve **gravity-invariant multi-axis in-hand object rotation** using a **unified policy** by fully leveraging **dense featured sim-to-real touch**. This work represents a significant advancement by demonstrating manipulation with a *continuously moving and rotating hand*, which is a novel capability that pushes the boundaries beyond previous fixed-orientation or single-axis manipulation paradigms. By focusing on a rich tactile representation and developing a robust sim-to-real transfer method for this data, the paper challenges the notion that high-resolution tactile information must be simplified for practical robotic deployment, thus broadening the potential for tactile sensing in dexterous manipulation. It fits into the broader trend of enhancing robot autonomy and adaptability by integrating multimodal sensing and advanced learning techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "AnyRotate" project is to enable a robot hand to perform **gravity-invariant, multi-axis in-hand object rotation** using a system that combines **dense featured sim-to-real touch**. This ambitious goal is broken down into several specific aims, each driven by clear motivations stemming from the limitations of prior research and the desire to achieve more human-like dexterity in robotic systems.

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Develop a unified policy for arbitrary rotation axes and hand orientations:** The primary goal is to train a single, generalizable policy capable of rotating unseen objects around any chosen axis (e.g., x, y, or z) while the robot hand is in any orientation relative to gravity (e.g., palm up, palm down, thumb up/down, base up/down). This seeks to overcome the limitations of policies typically trained for specific axes or fixed hand poses.
2. **Leverage rich tactile sensing through a dense representation:** The authors aim to utilize a "dense featured" tactile representation, specifically comprising both **contact pose** (local contact position and orientation) and **contact force magnitude**. The objective is to demonstrate that this detailed contact information is crucial for enhancing dexterity, particularly when handling objects with varying physical properties like mass and shape, and for detecting and reacting to unstable grasp conditions.
3. **Achieve zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer for rich tactile sensing:** A major technical objective is to bridge the significant sim-to-real gap for this rich tactile data. This involves developing a robust method to accurately predict dense tactile features from real tactile sensor images and integrate them into a policy trained entirely in simulation, enabling the policy to work in the real world without further fine-tuning.
4. **Demonstrate robustness and emergent behaviors:** The system is intended to exhibit high robustness across diverse objects, hand directions, and rotation axes. A specific objective is to observe if the rich tactile sensing enables emergent reactive behaviors, such as the detection and recovery from unstable grasps.
5. **Enable manipulation with a continuously moving/rotating hand:** An advanced objective is to validate the policy's capability to perform in-hand object rotation even when the robot arm itself is continuously moving and rotating, constantly changing the gravity vector's influence on the hand.

**Motivation:**

The motivations underpinning these objectives are multifaceted:

- **Mimicking human dexterity:** Human hands are incredibly versatile, capable of effortlessly manipulating objects in various orientations and motions, often relying heavily on touch. Replicating this "effortless" dexterity in robots is a fundamental challenge and a key step towards more capable and autonomous robots in real-world environments.
- **Overcoming limitations of vision-only systems:** While vision has driven significant progress, it struggles with self-occlusion in close-quarters manipulation and lacks the local contact information vital for precise, stable grasps, especially during continuous object reorientation. Tactile sensing directly addresses these shortcomings.
- **Addressing the gravity challenge:** Manipulating objects in free space, without support surfaces, is intrinsically unstable. Gravity constantly acts on the object, making it harder to maintain a stable grasp, especially when the hand is not facing upwards. Developing gravity-invariant policies is crucial for truly general-purpose manipulation.
- **Unlocking the potential of high-resolution tactile sensors:** Existing high-resolution vision-based tactile sensors provide a wealth of detailed contact information. However, this richness is often downsampled or simplified for robotic control due to the sim-to-real transfer challenge. The authors are motivated to prove that by effectively transferring dense tactile features, more sophisticated and robust manipulation behaviors can be achieved, justifying the investment in these advanced sensors.
- **Efficiency and scalability of learning:** Training complex manipulation policies in the real world is data-intensive, time-consuming, and prone to wear and tear. Sim-to-real transfer offers a scalable solution, allowing policies to be trained efficiently in high-fidelity simulations before deployment.

In essence, "AnyRotate" is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of robotic in-hand manipulation by integrating advanced learning techniques with rich, directly-transferred tactile information to achieve unprecedented levels of dexterity and adaptability in the face of varying gravity conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "AnyRotate" system employs a sophisticated methodology that combines reinforcement learning (RL), policy distillation, and a novel sim-to-real approach for dense tactile sensing. The core idea is to train a highly capable policy in simulation using rich, privileged information, and then distill this knowledge into a student policy that operates solely on real-world observations, including novel dense tactile feedback.

#### 3.1. Robot Hardware and System Setup

- **Robot Hand:** A 16-Degrees of Freedom (DoF) Allegro Hand is used, a fully actuated multi-fingered robot hand capable of complex movements.
- **Tactile Sensors:** Custom-designed, finger-like, front-facing vision-based tactile sensors (a redesigned DigiTac version based on TacTip technology) are attached to each of the four fingertips. These sensors provide high-resolution tactile images.
- **Robot Arm:** The Allegro Hand is mounted on a 6-DoF UR5 robot arm. This setup allows the entire hand to be positioned and oriented arbitrarily in space, crucial for testing gravity-invariant manipulation.
- **Control Rate:** The hand is controlled at 20 Hz, with tactile and proprioceptive readings streamed asynchronously at the same rate. Joint commands are converted to torque by a PD controller at 300 Hz.

#### 3.2. Task Formulation: Multi-axis In-hand Object Rotation

- **Problem as Object Reorientation:** Instead of directly optimizing for angular velocity (which can be noisy and lead to local optima), the task is formulated as object reorientation towards a **moving target goal**. These targets are generated by rotating the current object orientation about the desired axis at regular intervals.
- **Markov Decision Process (MDP):** The problem is framed as a goal-conditioned MDP, where the agent learns a policy $\pi(a_t | s_t, g)$ to maximize expected return.
- **Observations ($O_t$):** Includes current and target joint positions, previous action, fingertip positions and orientations, binary contact, **contact pose** (local position on fingertip) and **contact force magnitude** (dense tactile features), and the desired rotation axis.
- **Privileged Information (for Teacher):** In simulation, the teacher policy additionally receives object position/orientation/angular velocity, gravity vector, and current goal orientation.
- **Action Space:** Relative joint positions ($\Delta\theta$) for smooth finger motion, applied with an exponential moving average.
- **Reward Design:** A composite reward function is used:
 - `r_rotation`: Encourages progress towards the goal using a keypoint-based formulation, with a sparse bonus for reaching a goal and a delta rotation reward for continuous movement.
 - `r_contact`: Rewards tip contacts and penalizes contacts with other parts of the hand, promoting stable, precise grasps.
 - `r_stable`: Includes penalties for high object angular velocity, deviation from a canonical hand pose, controller work, and torque, fostering stable and efficient manipulation.
 - `r_terminate`: A penalty for early termination if the object is dropped or the rotation axis deviates too far.
- **Adaptive Curriculum:** A linear curriculum coefficient is applied to `r_contact` and `r_stable` terms. These terms initially have less weight and increase as the agent achieves more rotations per episode, helping the agent first learn to rotate and then refine its contact and stability for better sim-to-real transfer, avoiding local optima.

#### 3.3. Learning Process: Teacher-Student Policy Distillation

- **Teacher Training:**
 - Trained with privileged information using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in IsaacGym, a high-performance GPU-based physics simulator.
 - This teacher policy serves as an oracle, learning the optimal manipulation strategy.
- **Student Training:**
 - Aims to imitate the teacher's actions.
 - Relies *only* on real-world observations (proprioception and tactile feedback).
 - Uses a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) encoder to process a sequence of proprioceptive and tactile observations into a low-dimensional latent vector.
 - Trained via supervised learning, minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) of latent vectors and Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) of action distributions.
 - Goal update tolerance ($d_{tol}$) is increased during student training to mitigate the goal-reaching accuracy gap due to the absence of explicit object/goal information.

#### 3.4. Sim-to-Real Dense Featured Touch

- **Dense Tactile Representation:** The key tactile features are **contact pose** (represented by polar angle R_x and azimuthal angle R_y) and **contact force magnitude** (||F||).
- **Data Collection for Perception Model:** A UR5 robot arm moves a tactile sensor on a flat stimulus, simultaneously recording tactile images and ground-truth contact depth, shear, and force components from a force/torque sensor. This data captures various contact interactions within realistic sensing ranges.
- **Tactile Perception Model Training:** A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is trained to extract these explicit contact features (pose and force) from raw tactile images. Separate models are trained for each fingertip.
- **Simulated Touch Processing:** In simulation, raw contact information from the rigid-body simulator is post-processed:
 - Binary contact is derived from a force threshold.
 - An exponential moving average simulates sensing delay due to elastic deformation.
 - Saturation and re-scaling are applied to align simulated contact values with real-world sensor ranges.
- **Real-world Deployment:** For real-world deployment, tactile images are preprocessed (grayscale, resized, blurred). The Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) is used for binary contact detection, which then masks the contact pose and force predictions from the CNN perception model.

#### 3.5. Sim-to-Real Gap Reduction Strategies

- **System Identification:** CMA-ES optimization is used to tune simulation parameters (stiffness, damping, mass, friction, armature) of the Allegro Hand to match its real-world counterpart.
- **Domain Randomization:** During both teacher and student training, various parameters are randomized, including object properties (capsule/box dimensions, mass, friction, center of mass), hand PD controller parameters, observation noise (joint, fingertip position/orientation, tactile pose/force), and disturbances (scale, probability, decay).

This comprehensive methodology allows "AnyRotate" to tackle the complex challenges of dexterous, gravity-invariant manipulation by leveraging high-fidelity simulation and sophisticated tactile sensing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for "AnyRotate" yielded several significant findings across training performance, simulation evaluation, and real-world deployment, highlighting the benefits of the proposed methodology.

#### 4.1. Training Performance

- **Auxiliary Goal Formulation is Crucial:** The paper demonstrates that the proposed auxiliary goal formulation, which defines object reorientation to a moving target, is vastly superior to angular velocity-based objectives. In single-axis settings, angular velocity led to lower accuracy, and in multi-axis settings, it often resulted in training failure, with the agent getting stuck in stable grasp configurations without effective rotation. The smooth, goal-driven reward, especially when combined with privileged information, effectively guided the agent through the intrinsically unstable manipulation task.
- **Adaptive Curriculum Positively Contributes:** The adaptive curriculum, which gradually increases the influence of contact and stability rewards, was found to be beneficial. It helps prevent the agent from getting stuck in local optima where it can stably grasp but not rotate the object, promoting the learning of more realistic finger-gaiting motions.

#### 4.2. Simulation Results

- **Gravity Invariance Complexity:** A policy trained in a fixed hand orientation performed poorly when generalized to arbitrary hand orientations, confirming that gravity invariance significantly increases task complexity.
- **Rich Tactile Sensing Improves Performance:** A consistent trend was observed: performance (rotation count and episode length) improved with more detailed tactile sensing. The dense touch policy (contact pose + contact force) outperformed simpler representations:
 - Proprioception (lowest)
 - Binary Touch (better than proprioception, contrary to some previous findings, which the authors attribute to its inclusion in teacher training)
 - Discrete Touch (slight improvement over binary, but less suited to their sensor morphology)
 - Dense Force (w/o Pose) and Dense Pose (w/o Force) (each individually contributed)
 - Dense Touch (Ours) (best performance across OOD mass and OOD shape objects).
- **Ablation Studies Validate Dense Features:**
 - Contact force proved beneficial for handling objects with different **mass properties**, aiding in understanding interaction physics.
 - Contact pose was advantageous for handling **unseen shapes**, providing precise geometric contact information.
 - Excluding either contact pose or contact force resulted in suboptimal performance, confirming the synergistic value of the full dense representation.

#### 4.3. Real-world Results (Zero-Shot Policy Transfer)

- **Successful Zero-Shot Transfer:** The dense touch policy successfully transferred from simulation to the real world without further fine-tuning, consistently outperforming policies based on proprioception alone or binary touch across diverse real-world objects. Proprioception and binary touch policies often struggled with stability, leading to object drops or getting stuck.
- **Impact of Hand Orientation:** Performance decreased systematically based on hand orientation difficulty, from palm up/down (easiest) to base up/down, and then thumb up/down (most challenging). This was attributed to larger sim-to-real gaps in horizontal finger positions and gravity loading hindering actuation. Despite this, the dense touch policy maintained the most stable performance across all orientations.
- **Impact of Rotation Axis:** Rotation about the z-axis was easiest, followed by x- and y-axes. The latter require more sophisticated finger-gaiting, where the dense touch policy demonstrated its critical advantage over simpler tactile inputs.
- **Emergent Reactive Behavior:** A key finding was the emergence of reactive behaviors with dense tactile sensing. When perturbed (e.g., via a grasp offset), the policy could detect unstable grasps through boundary contact and adjust finger-gaiting motions to re-stabilize the object, preventing slipping. This sophisticated recovery was absent in policies using only proprioception or binary touch.
- **Gravity Invariance with Rotating Hand:** The policy successfully adapted to a continuously rotating hand, where the gravity vector was constantly changing relative to the hand's frame of reference. This capability allows for 6D reorientation of an object while simultaneously repositioning the grasp, marking a significant step towards dynamic in-hand manipulation.
- **Object Property Challenges:** While generally robust, the policy had difficulties with larger objects due to hand joint limits and objects with sharp geometric features (e.g., cube, gum box corners), which could cause fingers to get stuck. The gum box, with sharp corners and shifting mass, was the most challenging.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that the "AnyRotate" system, powered by its auxiliary goal formulation, adaptive curriculum, and especially its dense sim-to-real tactile sensing approach, achieves unprecedented gravity-invariant multi-axis in-hand object rotation, including dynamic manipulation with a moving hand, and exhibits sophisticated emergent reactive behaviors for grasp stabilization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "AnyRotate" research paper presents a significant stride forward in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation, particularly through its innovative integration of rich tactile sensing with robust sim-to-real learning. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

#### 5.1. Advancing General Tactile Dexterity

The most immediate significance lies in demonstrating **gravity-invariant multi-axis in-hand object rotation** with a **unified policy** on a fully-actuated multi-fingered robot hand. This achievement directly addresses long-standing challenges in robotic dexterity, moving beyond fixed hand orientations or limited rotation axes. The ability to manipulate objects continuously against gravity and even during arm movements brings robots closer to human-like versatility in handling objects in free space. This is a crucial step towards equipping robots with the general-purpose manipulation skills needed for unstructured environments.

#### 5.2. Underscoring the Importance of Rich Tactile Sensing

The paper robustly validates the hypothesis that **dense tactile representations**, specifically encompassing both contact pose and contact force, are not merely incremental improvements but are qualitatively superior for complex dexterous tasks. The finding that rich tactile data enables **emergent reactive behaviors** for unstable grasp detection and recovery is particularly impactful. This suggests that providing robots with detailed, continuous contact feedback is essential for truly adaptive and robust manipulation, moving beyond simple binary contact detection. This work will likely spur further research into developing and effectively utilizing high-resolution tactile sensors.

#### 5.3. Breakthrough in Sim-to-Real for Tactile Robotics

Achieving zero-shot policy transfer for *dense featured tactile data* is a major technical accomplishment. The sim-to-real gap has historically been a bottleneck for tactile robotics, often forcing simplification of tactile information. "AnyRotate" demonstrates a viable pipeline—from data collection and perception model training to robust simulation processing and domain randomization—that allows complex tactile policies to be developed efficiently in simulation and deployed directly in the real world. This advancement accelerates research and development, as it reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection and iterative hardware testing, making complex tactile skills more accessible.

#### 5.4. Enabling New Levels of Dynamic Manipulation

The demonstration of successful in-hand rotation while the robot arm itself is continuously moving and changing its orientation relative to gravity is a critical leap. This capability enables **6D reorientation of objects** during transport or other dynamic tasks, expanding the operational envelope of robotic systems. It provides fundamental primitives for tasks such as reorienting a tool while moving towards a workspace, or smoothly transferring objects between robots.

#### 5.5. Practical Implications and Future Directions

- **Industrial Automation:** More dexterous robot hands could revolutionize tasks requiring fine manipulation, such as assembly of complex products, handling delicate components, or working with diverse object geometries in manufacturing.
- **Service and Assistive Robotics:** Robots in home or care settings could perform a wider range of tasks, from picking up dropped items to preparing food, requiring robust object manipulation skills under varying conditions.
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** The ability to manipulate objects of varying shapes and sizes, independent of gravity, could improve efficiency in sorting, packing, and handling diverse inventory.

The paper also candidly highlights limitations, particularly concerning objects with sharp geometric features and the mechanical limitations of current hardware (e.g., Allegro Hand's actuation weakening in certain orientations). These insights provide clear future research directions: developing richer tactile representations capable of discerning precise geometric features, integrating visual information for object shape perception, and designing more capable, low-cost multi-fingered robot hands.

In conclusion, "AnyRotate" not only pushes the technical boundaries of robot manipulation by achieving a highly challenging task but also fundamentally shifts the perception of how tactile sensing can and should be integrated into advanced robotic systems. By showcasing the power of dense tactile information effectively transferred from simulation, this work lays a strong foundation for the next generation of truly dexterous and adaptive robots.
