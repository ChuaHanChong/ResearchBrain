# Wake up for Touch! Mask-isolated Tactile Alignment Learning in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Splash, a framework from Ewha Womans University, integrates tactile signals into small-scale Multimodal Large Language Models (sMLLMs) by selectively updating 'dormant' parameters, preventing catastrophic forgetting of vision-language capabilities. The approach achieves superior tactile-semantic performance on VTL benchmarks while maintaining VL understanding and incurring zero additional inference latency.

## Problem

- Integrating touch into compact Multimodal Large Language Models (sMLLMs) often leads to a 'zero-sum trade-off,' where new tactile capabilities degrade existing vision-language (VL) reasoning.
- Existing MLLMs with large backbones are computationally intensive, limiting their deployment for real-time inference on resource-constrained edge robots.
- Naive fine-tuning of sMLLMs with tactile data causes catastrophic forgetting, corrupting pretrained visual and language representations.

## Method

- **Splash** identifies and isolates a ==dormant parameter subspace== within the sMLLM's LLM backbone, deemed minimally critical for existing vision-language tasks, using a ==visual-relative importance metric==.
- A ==binary mask== is generated based on this importance metric, allowing only the dormant parameters and the tactile front-end to be updated during a unified, single-stage training process.
- The first and last transformer blocks of the LLM are explicitly frozen to maintain input grounding and high-level semantic representations, further preventing ==catastrophic forgetting==.

## Results

- Splash-3B achieved an average GPT-4o score of **4.91** on VTL benchmarks (SSVTP, TVL, TacQuad), outperforming previous 7B-scale methods (e.g., UniTouch **3.74**, TVL-LLaMA **4.31**).
- The framework preserved general-purpose vision-language capabilities, with Splash-3B improving MMMUval from **50.0** (TVL baseline) to **55.3** and MathVista from **52.8** to **65.3**, comparable to the zero-shot baseline.
- Splash incurred **zero** additional inference latency compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, achieving a P99 latency of **134.55 ms** and a control frequency of **7.50 Hz** for real-time applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Selectively updating mask-isolated 'dormant' parameters enables MLLMs to acquire new modalities like touch without degrading pretrained vision-language capabilities, resolving the 'zero-sum trade-off'.
> - Analysis revealed that the dormant pathways become highly sensitized and responsive to new tactile inputs, empirically validating the selective plasticity design.
> - Freezing the LLM's boundary layers (first and last transformer blocks) is crucial for stable adaptation, preserving input grounding and high-level semantic representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of multiple sensory modalities, particularly vision and touch, is a long-standing challenge in robotics and artificial intelligence. Humans inherently use touch to perceive intrinsic material properties such as friction, compliance, and rigidity, which are often difficult or impossible to infer from vision alone. Early research in visuo-tactile integration focused on discriminative tasks like force estimation, cross-modal synthesis, and robotic manipulation, typically learning shared representations for specific tasks.

With the advent of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), research attention has shifted towards Visuo-Tactile-Language (VTL) alignment. MLLMs, leveraging large language model (LLM) backbones, offer the capacity for open-vocabulary reasoning, complex semantic understanding, and instruction following, which is critical for enabling robots to not only perceive but also reason about the physical consequences of their actions. Existing MLLMs, however, often rely on computationally intensive backbones (e.g., LLaMA-7B), which limit their deployment on resource-constrained edge robots requiring real-time inference.

A significant challenge in adapting compact MLLMs (sMLLMs, e.g., ≤ 3B parameters) to new sensory modalities like touch is catastrophic forgetting. When parameters of an sMLLM are updated to incorporate tactile information, there is a risk of distorting or overwriting the existing vision-language representations, leading to degraded performance on established vision-language (VL) reasoning tasks. This creates a fundamental trade-off: expanding sensory capabilities versus preserving pretrained reasoning abilities. Previous VTL alignment methods often employ multi-stage training with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques like LoRA, but these can still suffer from this trade-off or introduce additional inference latency.

This work draws parallels with pruning-based multi-task learning and continual learning, where specific parameter subsets are allocated to different tasks to mitigate interference and catastrophic forgetting. While these prior efforts primarily focused on intra-modality adaptation (e.g., packing multiple language tasks), this paper extends the principle to modality-level parameter isolation, addressing the severe representation gap encountered when aligning a new sensory modality such as touch. The research aims to overcome the identified trade-off by developing a method that allows sMLLMs to acquire tactile reasoning capabilities without compromising their general-purpose vision-language understanding, thereby making them more suitable for real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named Splash, that effectively aligns tactile signals into small-scale Multimodal Large Language Models (sMLLMs) while simultaneously mitigating the problem of catastrophic forgetting in their vision-language (VL) capabilities. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Zero-Sum Trade-off:** The paper identifies a critical challenge in equipping compact MLLMs with new sensory modalities like touch. Due to their limited parameter budgets, these models often face a "zero-sum trade-off" where acquiring a new modality comes at the cost of degrading established vision-language reasoning abilities. The motivation is to overcome this trade-off, enabling compact MLLMs to gain tactile perception without sacrificing their foundational VL understanding.

2. **Enabling Tactile Reasoning for Resource-Constrained Robots:** Many robotic applications, particularly those deployed at the edge, operate under strict computational constraints. Existing MLLMs capable of complex reasoning often use large, computationally intensive backbones (e.g., 7B parameters), which are impractical for real-time inference on such platforms. The motivation is to develop a method that allows sMLLMs (e.g., 1B or 3B parameters) to perform sophisticated tactile reasoning efficiently, making them suitable for real-world robotic manipulation and interaction.

3. **Preventing Catastrophic Forgetting:** A central motivation is to prevent the phenomenon of catastrophic forgetting. As illustrated in preliminary experiments (Figure 1), naively fine-tuning sMLLMs with tactile data can corrupt their pretrained visual features, leading to a significant degradation in their ability to perform general VL tasks. The paper is motivated to devise a strategy that protects these pretrained visual and language representations while integrating tactile information.

4. **Achieving Non-Destructive Modality Expansion:** The research aims for a "non-destructive" expansion of MLLM capabilities. This means adding tactile sensing without perturbing the core knowledge embedded in the pretrained MLLM. The motivation is to allow sMLLMs to internalize tactile alignment without requiring substantial architectural changes, external additive modules, or incurring additional inference overhead in the LLM component.

5. **Simplifying Training Workflow:** Existing VTL alignment methods often involve complex multi-stage training procedures (e.g., separate alignment and fine-tuning steps). A motivation for Splash is to streamline this process into a unified, single-stage training pipeline, thereby reducing overall training cost and complexity.

In summary, the key objectives are to introduce a parameter-isolated tactile adaptation framework (Splash) that enables sMLLMs to achieve state-of-the-art tactile reasoning, maintain their general-purpose vision-language capabilities without catastrophic forgetting, and do so with zero additional inference overhead and a simplified, single-stage training process, ultimately making advanced multimodal intelligence accessible for resource-limited robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Splash (Mask-isolated Tactile Alignment Learning), addresses the challenge of integrating tactile sensing into sMLLMs while mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework Design

MLLMs typically comprise a vision encoder, a modality adapter, and a large language model (LLM) that processes fused multimodal input. Splash uses a pretrained sMLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL or InternVL) to initialize the LLM parameters ($\theta$) and the visual front-end ($F_\phi$). For the tactile front-end ($F_\psi$), an ImageNet-pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT-Tiny) is employed to extract features from contact images. During training, the visual parameters ($\phi$) are frozen. Only the tactile parameters ($\psi$) and a selectively determined subset of the LLM parameters ($\theta$) are updated. The goal is to generate a natural language tactile description $Y$ given an RGB image $X_R$, a tactile contact image $X_C$, and a text prompt $X_T$:
$Y = LLM_{\theta}(F_{\phi}(X_R), F_{\psi}(X_C), X_T)$

#### 3.2. Locating the Dormant Subspace

The core of Splash involves identifying and isolating a "dormant" parameter subspace within the pretrained LLM that contributes minimally to its existing vision-language (VL) tasks. This is based on the premise that only a subset of parameters is critical for maintaining model function, and the remaining redundant parameters can be repurposed.

- **Importance Metric:** A visual-relative importance metric, inspired by Wanda, quantifies each parameter's contribution. For each weight $W_{i,j}$ in the LLM's linear layers (connecting the $i$-th input feature to the $j$-th output neuron), the importance score $S_{i,j}$ is defined as:
 $S_{i,j} = |W_{i,j}| \cdot \parallel x_i \parallel_2$
 where $|W_{i,j}|$ is the weight magnitude and $\parallel x_i \parallel_2$ is the $\ell_2$-norm of the corresponding input activation.
- **Calibration:** These scores are computed using a small calibration set (e.g., 128 samples) drawn from general VL data (e.g., CC12M).
- **Mask Generation:** Given a specified sparsity ratio $s\%$, a threshold $\tau_j(s)$ is determined for each layer such that $s\%$ of the scores $\{S_{i,j}\}$ within that layer fall below it. A binary mask $M_{i,j}$ is then created:
 $M_{i,j} = \mathbb{1}(S_{i,j} < \tau_j(s))$
 where $\mathbb{1}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function. A value of 1 denotes a dormant (trainable) parameter, while 0 denotes a critical (frozen) parameter.
- **Boundary Layer Freezing:** To enhance training stability and prevent representation collapse, the parameters in the first and last transformer blocks of the LLM are explicitly set to critical ($M_{i,j}=0$) and remain frozen. This preserves the input grounding and high-level semantic representations. This process effectively partitions the LLM into frozen critical weights and trainable dormant weights.

#### 3.3. Mask-isolated Tactile Alignment

Unlike typical two-stage VTL alignment methods that first align a tactile encoder and then fine-tune the LLM, Splash employs a unified, single-stage training process.

- **Unified Training Objective:** The training objective $L$ maximizes the likelihood of the autoregressively generated tactile description:
 $L = -\sum_{n=1}^{N} \log P(y_n | Y_{<n}, X_R, X_C, X_T)$
 where $N$ is the number of tokens in the output sequence $Y$.
- **Parameter Updates:**
 - **LLM Parameters:** The binary mask $M$ is applied to the LLM backbone parameters during gradient updates. Let $\eta$ be the learning rate:
 $\theta^* \leftarrow \theta - \eta (M \odot \nabla_{\theta} L)$
 The Hadamard product $\odot$ ensures that critical parameters (where $M_{i,j}=0$) receive zero gradient, effectively freezing them, while dormant parameters (where $M_{i,j}=1$) are updated.
 - **Tactile Front-end Parameters:** The tactile front-end parameters $\psi$ are fully trainable:
 $\psi \leftarrow \psi - \eta \nabla_{\psi} L$
- **Mechanism for Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation:** The frozen critical vision-language weights serve as stable anchors, maintaining the integrity of the multimodal manifold during adaptation. By confining updates strictly to the dormant subspace, Splash ensures that pretrained VL reasoning pathways remain largely undisturbed, preventing catastrophic forgetting without introducing additional inference latency or complex architectural modifications (unlike additive PEFT methods like LoRA). This design simplifies the training pipeline and integrates tactile reasoning cohesively into the MLLM.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Base sMLLMs:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL2.5-1B.
- **Tactile Encoder:** ViT-Tiny (5.8M parameters), initialized with ImageNet-pretrained weights.
- **Feature Projection:** A two-layer MLP projector with LayerNorm and GELU activation maps tactile features to the language embedding space.
- **Input Processing:** Natural and tactile images are resized to 224x224. Tactile, visual, and textual tokens are concatenated into a single autoregressive sequence.
- **Training Configuration:** AdamW optimizer with weight decay 0.0, cosine decay scheduling, and 0.05 warmup ratio. Splash-3B trained for 1 epoch, Splash-1B for 2 epochs.
- **Sparsity Ratio:** Default sparsity ratio $s=60\%$ for mask-guided fine-tuning. For LoRA baselines, rank $r=8$ with $\alpha=16$.
- **Evaluation:** GPT-4o is used as an LLM-based judge for semantic evaluation of tactile descriptions, alongside keyword-based F1 score and Top-5 Accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across visuo-tactile-language (VTL) tasks, vision-language (VL) capability preservation, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison on VTL Tasks

(Referencing Table 1 and Figure 3)
- **Superior Tactile-Semantic Performance:** Splash-1B and Splash-3B models demonstrate superior performance on VTL benchmarks (SSVTP, TVL, TacQuad) when evaluated by an LLM-based judge (GPT-4o score) and objective metrics (F1 score, Top-5 Accuracy). Splash-3B achieves an average score of 4.91, outperforming previous 7B-scale methods like UniTouch (3.74 average) and TVL-LLaMA (4.31 average).
- **Effectiveness with Smaller Backbones:** Splash-1B, despite its significantly smaller parameter size, achieves a competitive average score of 5.01, demonstrating the approach's effectiveness even for compact VLM backbones.
- **Robustness to Distribution Shift:** Splash-3B notably outperforms TVL (LoRA-based) on SSVTP (5.48 vs. 4.98) and TacQuad (4.86 vs. 4.22), indicating stronger visuo-tactile alignment and robustness to tactile distribution shifts.
- **Limitations of Other PEFT Methods:** TVL-(IA)3, while competitive on smaller VTL datasets, shows a significant performance drop on TacQuad (3.84), suggesting it lacks the representational capacity for real-world tactile dynamics generalization.

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison for Preservation of VL Capability

(Referencing Table 2)
- **Preservation of General-Purpose Capabilities:** Splash-3B consistently outperforms TVL baselines across diverse VL benchmarks including MMMUval, MathVista, MMEsum, MMBench-EN, and MMBench-CN. For instance, Splash improves MMMUval from 50.0 (TVL-Qwen2.5-VL-3B) to 55.3 and MathVista from 52.8 to 65.3.
- **Comparable to Zero-Shot:** The performance of Splash remains comparable to the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline, confirming that tactile adaptation with Splash does not degrade the pretrained general-purpose VL capabilities of the MLLM.
- **Balanced Performance:** While TVL-(IA)3 achieves high scores on some VL benchmarks by minimally perturbing pretrained weights, it does so at the expense of tactile learning (under-fitting tactile modality). Splash demonstrates a unique balance, achieving strong tactile performance while preserving VL abilities.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

(Referencing Table 3, Table 4, Table 5, Figure A2, Table A2, Figure A3)
- **Sparsity Ratio:** A moderate sparsity ratio of 60% for Splash-3B yielded the optimal trade-off between tactile-semantic performance and VL capability retention. Excessive sparsity (100%) caused severe catastrophic forgetting, while too little sparsity (30%) slightly limited tactile adaptation capacity. Smaller backbones like Splash-1B showed higher sensitivity to sparsity levels.
- **Calibration Data Robustness:** Splash's performance was robust across varying calibration sample sizes (64, 128, 256) and data choices (CC3M, TVL). Even with unpaired random noise images as calibration data, Splash maintained competitive performance, suggesting it captures intrinsic architectural redundancies rather than overfitting to specific semantic alignments.
- **Tactile Frontend Initialization:** Comparisons using identical tactile-pretrained encoder initialization revealed that Splash's performance gains primarily stem from its mask-guided adaptation strategy rather than the frontend initialization itself.
- **Freezing Boundary Layers:** Freezing the first and last transformer blocks was found to be important for stable adaptation and preventing a reduction in average VTL scores.
- **Dormant Subspace Analysis:** Analysis of parameter shifts within the dormant subspace indicated that 48.8% of dormant weights increased in magnitude, and 67.1% of corresponding activations showed a strong positive shift in their $\ell_2$-norm. This suggests that the dormant pathways become highly sensitized and responsive to new tactile inputs, empirically validating the selective plasticity design.
- **Adaptive vs. Uniform Sparsity:** Uniform sparsity allocation consistently outperformed an adaptive sparsity strategy, indicating that it ensures a more balanced tactile flow in dormant spaces.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

(Referencing Figure 4, Figure 5, Figure A5, Figure A6, Figure A7)
- Splash models consistently produced more accurate, diverse, and detailed tactile attribute descriptions that closely matched ground truth across VTL benchmarks. Baselines often generated visually plausible but tactually inconsistent or generic attributes, relying more on visual cues when tactile grounding was weak. Splash more effectively integrated tactile observations to infer physically consistent surface properties.

#### 4.5. Inference Cost

(Referencing Table A3)
- Splash incurred zero additional inference latency compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, achieving a P99 latency of 134.55 ms and a control frequency of 7.50 Hz. This demonstrates its suitability for real-time robotic perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Splash framework represents an advancement in integrating novel sensory modalities into compact Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with significant implications for the development of embodied AI and robotics.

**Overcoming the Modality Expansion Trade-off:** The most immediate impact of Splash is its ability to resolve the "zero-sum trade-off" identified in compact MLLMs. By selectively updating only a "dormant" subspace of parameters while freezing critical ones, the framework allows for the acquisition of new sensory capabilities (like touch) without sacrificing the model's pretrained vision-language reasoning abilities. This addresses a fundamental challenge in expanding MLLMs to richer perceptual understanding.

**Enabling Practical Embodied AI for Resource-Constrained Systems:** For applications in robotics, particularly those requiring on-device inference, computational efficiency is paramount. Splash's design, which introduces zero additional inference overhead in the LLM component and enables unified, single-stage training, makes it highly suitable for deployment on resource-constrained edge robots. This can accelerate the development of dexterous manipulation, intelligent physical interaction, and more grounded robotic agents that can actively perceive and reason about material properties.

**Methodological Innovation in Parameter Adaptation:** Splash introduces a novel parameter-isolated adaptation framework that goes beyond additive PEFT methods. By using a mask-guided approach to repurpose underutilized parameters, it offers a more structurally isolated and non-destructive way to expand model capabilities. This methodology could be generalizable to integrating other sensor modalities or continually learning new tasks in large pretrained models, potentially extending the lifespan and utility of foundation models.

**Enhanced Multimodal Perception and Reasoning:** By effectively integrating tactile sensing, Splash enables MLLMs to infer intrinsic material properties (e.g., friction, compliance, rigidity) that are critical for realistic physical interaction but often ambiguous from vision alone. This leads to a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of the physical world, moving MLLMs closer to human-like perception. The improved performance on visuo-tactile-language benchmarks demonstrates a more robust and nuanced ability to describe tactile attributes.

**Foundation for Future Research:** While demonstrating strong capabilities, the paper also outlines future research directions, such as dynamically adaptive masking strategies for time-varying parameter utilization and cross-sensor generalization. Splash provides a solid foundation upon which these more advanced adaptation techniques can be built, further extending the robustness and applicability of multimodal models in complex, dynamic environments. The exploration of intrinsic architectural redundancies, even with "noise" calibration data, suggests deeper insights into model learning and plasticity are possible.
