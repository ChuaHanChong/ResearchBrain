# UNeMo: Collaborative Visual-Language Reasoning and Navigation via a Multimodal World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> UNeMo, a framework developed by researchers from Shenzhen University and collaborators, enables embodied agents to perform collaborative visual-language reasoning and navigation by integrating a multimodal world model with a hierarchical prediction-feedback navigator. It improves navigation accuracy and generalization in unseen environments, showing a 2.1% increase in Success Rate (SR) on the R2R val unseen split and enhanced robustness in long-horizon navigation.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods often limit reasoning to the linguistic modality, lacking robust visual state reasoning capabilities crucial for dynamic environmental changes.
- Reasoning modules (e.g., for instruction parsing or future state prediction) are frequently optimized separately from navigation policies, leading to objective conflicts and suboptimal integration in VLN systems.
- Current world models for navigation often separate state prediction from policy learning, preventing dynamic adaptation and refinement of reasoning based on policy progress.

## Method

- Introduces a Multimodal World Model (MWM) built on a Conditional Variational Autoencoder that predicts future visual states by leveraging current visual observations, natural language instructions, and proposed navigational actions.
- Designs a Hierarchical Prediction-Feedback Navigator (HPFN) that integrates the MWM's predicted future visual states into the navigation policy through a two-layer decision-making architecture.
- Establishes a bidirectional promotion mechanism within the HPFN, where initial policy decisions feed into the MWM for state prediction, and the MWM's reasoning continuously refines the policy's fine-grained decisions.

## Results

- Achieved a 2.1% improvement in Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) on the R2R val unseen split (72.1% SR, 61.1% SPL) compared to baseline LLM-based methods.
- Demonstrated enhanced robustness in long-horizon navigation on the R2R val-seen split, showing a 5.6% increase in SR for paths with length ">=" 7 (from 64.2% to 69.8%).
- Outperformed state-of-the-art methods on the REVERIE dataset, achieving a navigation SR of 49.36% on Val Unseen and a Remote Grounding Success (RGS) of 34.28% on Val.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Collaborative optimization between visual state reasoning and navigation policies, facilitated by a hierarchical feedback mechanism, is critical for improving overall VLN performance and generalization in unseen environments.
> - A multimodal world model that fuses visual features, language instructions, and predicted actions provides more effective and accurate future state prediction than purely visual or topological map-based approaches.
> - Integrating prospective exploration and visual state inference allows agents to make more informed and robust decisions, particularly enhancing performance in complex, long-horizon navigation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), a domain that requires embodied agents to navigate environments using visual observations and natural language instructions. Traditionally, VLN research has focused on end-to-end deep learning frameworks, utilizing techniques such as sequence-to-sequence models, attention-based cross-modal fusion, and graph-based representation learning to align vision-language inputs with action policies. While effective on task-specific datasets, these methods often exhibit limited generalization to out-of-distribution environments or novel instructions.

The recent emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has introduced new avenues for enhancing VLN capabilities due to their strong knowledge generalization and complex reasoning abilities. Initial attempts integrated LLMs by parsing instructions, enriching scene descriptions, or extracting task knowledge, often in a decoupled manner. More recent approaches, such as NavGPT and NavGPT2, leverage LLMs for direct navigation action prediction or fine-tune them on navigation data. However, these LLM-based methods typically focus primarily on linguistic reasoning, potentially lacking robust visual reasoning capabilities. Furthermore, their reasoning modules are often optimized separately or frozen during policy training, leading to potential incompatibilities and suboptimal integration with navigation policies.

Another related research area involves the use of World Models in embodied decision-making. World models aim to compress high-dimensional sensory data into dynamic state representations, supporting tasks like self-supervised policy learning and planning. Prior efforts in this area for navigation, such as Pathdreamer and Dreamwalker, have focused on predicting future observations to extend planning horizons, but they have generally maintained a separation between state prediction and policy learning.

UNeMo aims to address these identified limitations by integrating a multimodal world model for visual state reasoning directly into the navigation policy optimization process. It positions itself as an advancement in LLM-based VLN by enabling collaborative optimization between visual state reasoning and navigational decision-making, thereby bridging the gap between linguistic and visual reasoning capabilities and fostering a dynamic interaction between future state prediction and action selection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two challenges observed in current Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) systems, particularly those leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs):
1. **Limited Visual Reasoning in LLM-based Methods:** While LLMs have demonstrated strong linguistic reasoning and generalization abilities, their application in VLN often confines reasoning to the linguistic modality. This limits their capacity to perform visual state reasoning, which is crucial for understanding dynamic environmental changes and making informed navigation decisions based on visual cues.
2. **Disjoint Optimization of Reasoning Modules and Navigation Policies:** Existing VLN methods, including those incorporating LLMs or world models, frequently optimize their reasoning modules (e.g., for instruction parsing, scene understanding, or future state prediction) separately from the navigation policies that dictate actions. This separation can lead to optimization objective conflicts and an inability for reasoning capabilities to dynamically adapt and refine in response to policy training progress, thus imposing inherent limitations on overall navigation performance and generalization.

To address these challenges, the authors established the following key objectives for the UNeMo framework:
- **Enable Collaborative Optimization of Visual State Reasoning and Navigational Decision-Making:** The overarching objective is to develop a unified framework where the processes of understanding and predicting visual environmental states are tightly integrated and co-optimized with the agent's action selection policy. This aims to create a cohesive system where reasoning directly informs decision-making and vice-versa.
- **Introduce a Multimodal World Model (MWM) for Cross-Modal Reasoning:** A specific objective is to design an MWM capable of taking diverse inputs—visual features, natural language instructions, and proposed navigational actions—to jointly predict subsequent visual states. This MWM is intended to endow the agent with dynamic visual state reasoning capabilities beyond mere linguistic interpretation.
- **Design a Hierarchical Prediction-Feedback Navigator (HPFN) for Synergistic Interaction:** A further objective is to implement a hierarchical mechanism that facilitates a bidirectional promotion between the MWM and the navigation policy. This mechanism should enable the MWM's reasoning outputs to guide the navigation policy's fine-grained decisions, while simultaneously allowing the policy's decisions to provide feedback that improves the MWM's reasoning accuracy. This aims to establish a closed-loop interaction for continuous refinement.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enhance the generalization capabilities and navigation accuracy of VLN agents in complex, unseen environments, moving towards more robust and adaptive embodied intelligent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UNeMo framework is designed for the collaborative optimization of visual state reasoning and navigational decision-making in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN). It introduces two core components: a Multimodal World Model (MWM) and a Hierarchical Prediction-Feedback Navigator (HPFN). The base navigation policy is adapted from NavGPT2, one of the advanced LLM-based navigation approaches.

**Preliminary (Base Navigator Adaptation):**
UNeMo builds upon NavGPT2, which comprises three main components:
- **Node Embedding:** A Topology Node Embedding (TNE) encoder processes visual (E_o) and textual (E_x) features, which are first jointly encoded by a pre-trained LLM into F_o and F_x. TNE then projects these into a shared topological space to form node embeddings V_t for navigation.
- **Decision Making:** A two-layer feedforward network predicts scores for each navigation action (node), and the highest-scoring node is selected.
- **Loss Function:** Combines behavioral cloning (L_BC) with DAgger (L_DAG) objectives for training, using expert demonstrations and pseudo-labels derived from shortest-path analysis.

**Multimodal World Model (MWM):**
The MWM is introduced to predict future visual states, addressing the limitation of topological maps that encode only historical states. It is constructed using a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE).
- **Purpose:** To predict the visual state (S_o_t+1) of a future node, leveraging current visual observations, language instructions, and predicted actions.
- **Encoder:**
 - Takes a partially observed view embedding (E_o_j) of a candidate node (j, typically the highest-scoring action node) and the instruction feature (F_x) as input.
 - These features are fused through a series of L=3 cross-attention layers, where E_o_j serves as the query and F_x as key/value, producing a cross-modal embedding (c_j).
 - This fused feature (c_j) is then passed through two MLPs to estimate the mean (μ_t) and variance (σ_t^2) of a future-state distribution.
- **Decoder:**
 - The mean (μ_t) and variance (σ_t^2) are reparameterized to sample a latent variable (z_t).
 - Node j's intrinsic embedding (v_t_j) is concatenated with z_t as a conditional feature.
 - An MLP uses this combined vector to predict the future visual-state embedding (S_o_t+1).
- **Training:** An autoencoder is pre-trained to compress high-dimensional visual features (from a frozen Vision Transformer) into a 768-dimensional latent code. The MWM then learns to predict these compressed representations.

**Hierarchical Prediction-Feedback Navigator (HPFN):**
This component integrates the MWM's future visual state predictions into the navigation policy through a hierarchical decision-making architecture and enables bidirectional interaction.
- **Coarse-Grained Action Prediction (First Layer):**
 - Topological-map node embeddings (V_t) are initially fed through a two-layer feedforward network to predict a coarse "lookahead" action (a'_t). This identifies a potential next node.
- **MWM Feedback (Visual State Prediction):**
 - The MWM then uses the predicted lookahead action (corresponding to node j) to infer the expected post-execution visual-state embedding (S_o_t+1) as if the agent had arrived at node j.
- **Fine-Grained Decision Refinement (Second Layer):**
 - The MWM's predicted future visual state (S_o_t+1) is integrated with the original topological-map node embeddings (V_t) using a cross-attention module. V_t acts as the query, and S_o_t+1 as key/value. Residual connections preserve original node information. This process enriches node embeddings with forward-looking information, yielding updated representations (V'_t).
 - These updated node embeddings (V'_t) are then fed into the policy head to compute the final, fine-grained action probability distribution (a''_t), from which the action is selected.
- **Bidirectional Promotion:** This hierarchical structure creates a dynamic loop where navigation policy decisions (a'_t) feed into the MWM for state prediction, and the MWM's reasoning (S_o_t+1) continuously refines the navigation policy's decisions (a''_t).

**Training Strategy:**
To ensure stability and prevent overfitting, a phased training strategy is employed. State reasoning tasks (MWM optimization) are only activated during the initial 10% of training batches in each phase. This allows the CVAE module to converge without premature influence from insufficient reasoning data and periodically retrains the MWM to align with the evolving feature space of the navigation policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The UNeMo framework was evaluated on two benchmark datasets, R2R (Room-to-Room) and REVERIE (Remote Embodied Visual Referring Expression in Real Indoor Environments), using standard VLN evaluation metrics such as Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Trajectory Length (TL), Navigation Error (NE), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), Remote Grounding Success (RGS), and RGSPL.

**R2R Dataset Results:**
- **Overall Performance:** UNeMo demonstrated superior performance compared to several state-of-the-art LLM-based VLN methods, including NavCoT, LangNav, NaviLLM, and NavGPT2.
- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - On the `val unseen` split, UNeMo achieved a 2.1% improvement in both SR and SPL compared to baseline methods. Specifically, it reached 72.1% SR and 61.1% SPL.
 - On the more challenging `test unseen` split, UNeMo showed gains of 1.5% in SR (72.5%) and 1.3% in SPL (61.3%).
- **Efficiency:** UNeMo, utilizing a FlanT5-1.5B backbone, demonstrated better navigation performance while requiring less than half the GPU memory (12GB vs 27GB) compared to NavGPT2, which uses a FlanT5-5B backbone.
- **Long-Horizon Navigation:** UNeMo exhibited enhanced robustness in long-horizon navigation scenarios. On the R2R `val-seen` split, it achieved a 5.6% increase in SR for paths with a length of ≥7 (from 64.2% to 69.8%), whereas for short paths (<7), the SR only increased by 1.2% (from 71.1% to 72.3%). This suggests improved capability in handling complex, extended trajectories.

**REVERIE Dataset Results:**
- **Overall Performance:** UNeMo outperformed both the original DUET method and its reproduced results (DUET*) on goal-oriented navigation and remote object grounding.
- **Navigation and Object Grounding:**
 - It achieved a navigation SR of 49.36% on `Val Unseen` and 53.21% on `Test Unseen`.
 - Its Remote Grounding Success (RGS) reached 34.28% on `Val` and 32.15% on `Test`.
- **Efficiency Trade-off:** UNeMo exhibited marginal drops in path efficiency metrics (SPL and RGSPL). This was attributed to REVERIE's coarse-grained instructions, which necessitate more agent exploration and path correction, where UNeMo's hierarchical prediction-feedback mechanism allows for course correction at the expense of potentially longer paths, prioritizing task success.

**Ablation Study (Comparison of Different State Reasoning Methods):**
- **TopoState (Topology-Map-Based):** Aggregating node features in a topological map to predict the next state yielded 2.2% and 1.7% improvements in SR and SPL, respectively. However, this method faces challenges in label acquisition.
- **Cond2Vis (Vision-Based Decoding):** Directly predicting candidate view features of expert-action-targeted nodes as future state representations showed 2.4% SR and 2.7% SPL gains, implicitly optimizing node encoding.
- **Vis-WM (Vision-Based World Model):** Predicting future states solely from partial observations resulted in slightly inferior performance (71.8% SR) compared to Cond2Vis.
- **UNeMo (Multimodal World Model):** By incorporating natural language instructions and cross-attention fusion with local observations, UNeMo achieved the highest performance: 2.9% SR (72.9%) and 2.7% SPL (61.7%) improvements over the baseline. This validated the efficacy of multimodal state reasoning.

**Ablation Study (Hierarchical Prediction-Feedback Navigator - HPFN):**
- **Importance of HPFN:** When the MWM was used as an auxiliary training branch without HPFN integration, navigation metrics on `val unseen` showed a substantial decline, indicating HPFN's critical role in enhancing performance by bridging visual state reasoning and actionable policies.
- **HPFN Learning Policy:** Experiments on dual-node scoring demonstrated that the learning policy focusing solely on the second scoring output (a'' only), which integrates MWM's state inference results, provided the optimal balance between navigation accuracy and path efficiency.

**Visualization Analysis of State Prediction:**
- Qualitative and quantitative analyses confirmed the MWM's ability to infer future states accurately. Randomly sampled predictions closely approximated ground-truth labels.
- Statistical analysis across `val unseen` scenarios showed an average cosine similarity of approximately 90.15% and an average Mean Squared Error (MSE) of about 0.09 between predicted next-state representations and ground-truth labels. This robustly supports the inference module's capability for accurate prospective exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UNeMo framework presents a significant contribution to the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by addressing several key limitations of existing approaches, particularly those leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) and world models.

**Key areas of significance include:**
- **Integration of Visual Reasoning with LLM-based Navigation:** UNeMo directly tackles the problem of LLM-based VLN methods lacking robust visual reasoning capabilities. By introducing a Multimodal World Model (MWM) that performs cross-modal reasoning to predict future visual states, the framework enriches the agent's understanding of environmental dynamics beyond linguistic interpretation. This represents a step towards more visually intelligent embodied agents.
- **Collaborative Optimization of Reasoning and Policy:** The Hierarchical Prediction-Feedback Navigator (HPFN) establishes a dynamic, bidirectional promotion mechanism between the MWM and the navigation policy. This tight integration ensures that reasoning directly informs decision-making, and conversely, policy decisions feed back to refine the MWM's reasoning accuracy. This joint optimization approach mitigates the conflicts and suboptimal performance often seen when reasoning and policy modules are trained separately.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Accuracy in Unseen Environments:** Experimental results on both R2R and REVERIE datasets demonstrate UNeMo's ability to outperform state-of-the-art methods in unseen scenes, indicating improved generalization capabilities. The reported increases in success rate (e.g., 2.1% on R2R val unseen) suggest a more reliable navigation performance in novel environments.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** UNeMo, even with a smaller backbone model (FlanT5-1.5B) compared to some baselines, achieved superior or comparable performance while requiring significantly less computational resources (e.g., less GPU memory). This indicates a more efficient and potentially scalable approach for developing advanced VLN systems.
- **Robustness in Complex Scenarios:** The demonstrated improvements in long-horizon navigation on the R2R dataset highlight UNeMo's ability to handle extended and intricate trajectories, a common challenge in real-world navigation tasks. The framework's capacity for prospective exploration and course correction, as implied by the REVERIE results, further contributes to its robustness.

**Potential impact of this work:**
The advancements proposed by UNeMo could influence the development of more intelligent and autonomous embodied agents across various applications.
- **Robotics:** The framework's ability to improve navigation accuracy and generalization in complex indoor environments could directly benefit robotic systems used for tasks such as autonomous delivery, elderly care, or industrial inspection, enabling them to operate more effectively in dynamic and unfamiliar settings.
- **Virtual Assistants and Simulation:** Enhanced VLN capabilities can lead to more sophisticated virtual agents in gaming, virtual reality, or simulation environments, allowing them to follow complex instructions and interact with virtual worlds in a more human-like manner.
- **Foundation Models for Embodied AI:** By integrating multimodal world modeling with hierarchical decision-making, UNeMo offers a new direction for building foundation models that are adept at understanding and navigating the physical world based on diverse sensory inputs and linguistic commands.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** The efficiency gains demonstrated by UNeMo could potentially lower the computational requirements for training and deploying advanced VLN systems, making such technologies more accessible for researchers and developers.

Future work outlined by the authors, including extending UNeMo to more complex settings and validating its real-world navigation performance via physical robot deployment, indicates a trajectory towards practical, real-world application of this research.
