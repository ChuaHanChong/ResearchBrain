# INTENTION: Inferring Tendencies of Humanoid Robot Motion Through Interactive Intuition and Grounded VLM

> [!summary] Summary
> The INTENTION framework allows humanoid robots to perform autonomous manipulation by acquiring and utilizing interactive intuition, integrating Vision-Language Model-based scene reasoning with an interaction-driven memory system. This approach demonstrated enhanced adaptability and robustness, particularly in tasks requiring intuitive physical understanding without explicit instructions, outperforming traditional and LLM-based baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic control methods often fail in unstructured, dynamic real-world environments due to reliance on precise physical models, generalization limits, and task-specificity.
- Existing Vision-Language Action (VLA) methods require extensive, high-quality datasets for complex humanoid robots and struggle to model object dynamics or effectively bridge high-level semantic understanding to low-level robot control.
- Robots lack a human-like "interactive intuition" for physical properties and object motion tendencies, hindering their ability to handle novel tasks and unpredictable environmental interactions without explicit guidance.

## Method

- The **INTENTION** framework combines a pre-trained VLM-based ==Intuitive Perceptor== for extracting explicit spatial and semantic information from raw sensory input with an interaction-driven memory system called MemoGraph.
- A learning phase involves the robot engaging in interaction episodes to collect data (scene observations, VLM-processed states, actions, task evaluations) which are then encoded as graph representations and stored in the MemoGraph.
- During inference, a novel scene is processed by the Intuitive Perceptor to generate a current scene graph, which is then compared against stored task graphs in MemoGraph using a scoring-based graph matching algorithm to retrieve relevant experiences and select appropriate actions.

## Results

- INTENTION achieved **84%** planning success and **72%** execution success in interactive intuition tasks (without explicit instructions), significantly outperforming LLM-BT (**20%** planning, **15%** execution) and entirely surpassing WB-MPC and BT-Planner which failed these tasks.
- For standard manipulation tasks, INTENTION performed comparably to state-of-the-art LLM-BT (**96%** planning, **92%** execution vs. **98%** planning, **94%** execution) while maintaining its capability for intuitive reasoning.
- The humanoid robot successfully inferred appropriate motions based purely on interactive intuition for tasks such as handling objects (**72%** success), lifting desks (**68%**), pushing chairs (**72%**), and refilling tea (**64%**), demonstrating robust autonomous behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating VLMs with structured prompt engineering can effectively extract physically grounded spatial and semantic information from visual inputs, transforming raw sensory data into actionable scene representations for robots.
> - An interaction-driven memory system (MemoGraph) enables robots to continually accumulate and leverage experiential knowledge, facilitating generalization to novel tasks and dynamic environments based on successful prior interactions.
> - By bridging high-level VLM scene understanding with low-level robot control through graph matching and a motion library, robots can infer context-appropriate, physically grounded actions even without explicit semantic instructions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional approaches to robotic manipulation and control often rely on explicit physical models and predefined action sequences. These methods, typically employed in Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), demonstrate efficacy in structured environments such as industrial assembly lines. However, they frequently encounter limitations in unstructured or dynamic real-world settings due to modeling inaccuracies, perceptual uncertainties, and insufficient generalization capabilities. Model-free learning techniques, including reinforcement learning, offer robustness in specific scenarios but are often task-specific, requiring extensive and costly retraining for novel tasks or environmental variations, thus exhibiting limited flexibility.

In parallel, human interaction with the environment is characterized by an intuitive understanding of physical properties, such as stability, reachability, and object motion tendencies, derived from accumulated experience rather than precise physical models. Endowing robots with such "interactive intuition" is an ongoing research goal, spanning cognitive science, machine learning, and robotics. Prior model-based research struggled with generalization, while learning-based methods, despite progress, often remain task-specific.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have introduced new capabilities in robotic cognition and decision-making. These multimodal models facilitate semantic understanding, spatial reasoning, and contextual awareness, enabling robots to interpret visual and linguistic cues for autonomous navigation and manipulation in unstructured environments. This integration represents a step toward embodied intelligence and robotic autonomy.

However, existing Vision-Language Action (VLA) methods, which aim to directly generate robot actions from pretrained models, face challenges. They typically require large-scale, high-quality datasets for training, which are particularly difficult to obtain for complex, high-degree-of-freedom humanoid robots, limiting control accuracy and adaptive capabilities. Furthermore, current VLMs in robotics primarily focus on static image understanding, often lacking the ability to model object properties, interactive dynamics, and interactive relationships, which impedes their capacity to handle novel tasks and predict motion in unstructured environments. A significant gap also persists between the high-level semantic outputs of language models and the low-level motion control required for robot execution, especially in tasks involving whole-body coordination or fine-grained control across multiple articulated components.

This work, INTENTION, positions itself by addressing these identified challenges. It integrates VLM-based scene reasoning with an interaction-driven memory system specifically for humanoid manipulation. The framework aims to leverage foundation models for spatial and object-level reasoning to reconstruct a form of physically grounded intuition, thereby enabling robots to infer appropriate interaction behaviors in new scenes without explicit instructions or extensive retraining, and to bridge the gap between high-level understanding and low-level control for complex platforms like humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a novel framework, INTENTION, that enables humanoid robots to acquire and utilize learned interactive intuition for autonomous manipulation in diverse, unstructured scenarios. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and aspirations in contemporary robotics:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Traditional Robotic Control:** Traditional model-based and model-free robotic control systems often fail in real-world, unstructured environments due to reliance on precise physical models, predefined action sequences, and task-specific learning. The research aims to overcome these limitations by enabling robots to adapt flexibly to novel tasks and dynamic environments.
2. **Mimicking Human Intuition for Physical Interaction:** Humans possess an "interactive intuition" derived from accumulated experience, allowing them to understand physical properties and dynamics (e.g., stability, reachability, motion trends) without explicit modeling. The motivation is to imbue robots with a similar approximate yet efficient reasoning ability, enabling them to handle a wide variety of complex manipulation tasks more effectively.
3. **Enhancing VLM Capabilities for Robotic Interaction:** While VLMs show promise in semantic understanding and spatial reasoning, their application in robotics is limited by challenges such as reliance on large datasets, insufficient modeling of object dynamics and interactive relationships, and a disconnect between high-level VLM outputs and low-level robot control. A key objective is to extend VLM capabilities to extract task-relevant physical information and contextual awareness from dynamic interactive environments, particularly for humanoid robots.
4. **Enabling Autonomous Behavior Generation with Minimal Instructions:** Existing VLA methods typically require detailed instructions or large datasets for robust instruction-action mapping. This work seeks to enable robots to infer appropriate interaction behaviors and generate physically grounded actions in new scenes with minimal or even zero explicit instructions, relying instead on learned intuition and accumulated experience.
5. **Building a Continual Learning System for Intuitive Physics:** The motivation includes developing a mechanism for robots to continuously accumulate knowledge and experience from human-robot and environment interactions. This "experiential prior" would allow robots to incrementally build a human-level intuitive physics, supporting future task and motion planning in novel scenarios and enhancing their autonomy and adaptability.
6. **Bridging Semantic Understanding and Low-Level Control:** For complex platforms like humanoid robots, there is a substantial gap between the high-level semantic outputs of language models and the fine-grained, whole-body coordination required for robot execution. The framework aims to bridge this gap by grounding language model reasoning in physically relevant observations and robot affordances, facilitating the generation of appropriate low-level motions.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond rigid, instruction-dependent robotic systems towards autonomous agents that can adaptively reason and act in complex, dynamic, and unstructured real-world settings by leveraging VLM-based scene understanding and a structured memory of interactive experiences, akin to human intuition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The INTENTION framework is designed to endow robots with learned interactive intuition and autonomous manipulation capabilities through a combination of VLM-based scene reasoning and an interaction-driven memory system. The methodology is structured into two main phases: a learning phase for memory construction and an inference phase for deployment in new scenarios. The framework consists of four main components: an Intuitive Perceptor, a MemoGraph, a Motion Library, and a graph matching and evaluation mechanism.

**A. Learning Phase: Data Collection and Memory Construction**

1. **Skill Library (Π):** The robot is equipped with a predefined skill library, Π, comprising a set of parameterized action or perception primitives executable on the robot hardware. Each primitive includes a code interface for direct robot control and a semantic description.
2. **Interaction Episodes:** The robot engages in a sequence of interaction episodes to build a memory-based representation of experiences.
 - **Scene Observation (S_n):** In each interaction `n`, the robot observes a scene state `S_n`, consisting of a human-issued instruction `I_n` and raw sensory input from the environment (e.g., RGB images from a camera).
 - **Intuitive Perceptor (P):** The raw sensory input is processed by the Intuitive Perceptor, which is built upon a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically the `o4-mini` model from OpenAI API.
 - The Perceptor extracts distilled spatial and geometric observations, identifying 3D features within the task scene.
 - It uses prompt words and function calling to guide the VLM, ensuring the extraction of explicit spatial information and the generation of output in a specific structured format, such as JSON. This ensures the VLM outputs a graph-structured representation encoding semantic state attributes and geometric relationships among objects and agents.
 - This output is referred to as the object/environment state `T_n`.
 - **VLM-based Planner (V_p):** A VLM-based planner then selects the most appropriate action `a_n` from the skill library Π, conditioned on `I_n`, `S_n`, and `T_n`.
 - **Action Execution and Evaluation:** The selected action `a_n` is executed by the robot. Subsequently, a task success evaluation `C_n` is computed to assess the outcome.
 - **MemoGraph (M) Construction:** All collected interaction information (`S_n`, `T_n`, `a_n`, `C_n`) is encoded into a graph representation `g_n`. This `g_n` is a structured task graph `G = (N, L, I)`, where `N` represents nodes (objects or agents), `L` represents links (spatial or semantic relationships between nodes), and `I` is the human instruction. The content of nodes, links, and instructions is encoded in text format. These task graphs are stored in the MemoGraph `M`, a topological graph structure designed to accumulate reusable experience across diverse task contexts.

**B. Inference Phase: Generalization to New Scenarios**

1. **New Scene Observation and Extraction:** When presented with a novel task scenario, the robot agent observes the current environment state `S_t` and extracts the relevant object configuration `T_t` using the Intuitive Perceptor `P`. A current scene graph `g*_t` is then encoded from `S_t` and `T_t`.
2. **MemoGraph Query and Retrieval:** The robot queries the MemoGraph `M` to retrieve all stored task graph instances `G = {g_m1, g_m2..., g_mn}`.
3. **Graph Matching Algorithm:** To identify the most relevant prior experience, a scoring-based graph matching method compares the current scene graph `g*_t` with each stored task graph `g_m` in `M`.
 - **Instruction Similarity (S_I):** Cosine similarity is computed between the embeddings of the current instruction `I_t` and the stored instruction `I_m`, using CLIP as the text encoder.
 `S_I(I_t, I_m) = cosine-sim(E(I_t), E(I_m))`
 - **Graph Structural Similarity (S_N, S_L):** Pairwise similarities are computed between the node sets (`N_t`, `N_m`) and link sets (`L_t`, `L_m`) of the current and stored graphs. Embeddings for nodes and links are extracted, similarity matrices are computed, and then thresholding and bipartite matching functions are applied to determine the best-aligned pairs and average similarity scores (`S_N`, `S_L`).
 - **Overall Matching Score (S_W):** The final matching score `S_W` is a weighted combination of the individual similarity scores:
 `S_W = α · S_N + β · S_L + γ · S_I`, where `α + β + γ = 1` are weighting coefficients.
4. **Action Selection and Execution:** The language-model-based evaluator analyzes the plausibility of each match based on `S_W`. It filters the most appropriate actions from the task graph of the selected match that best fit the current scenario. This involves selecting a corresponding action from the Motion Library, generating the control code, and executing the task on the humanoid robot. The robot's status `C_t` is updated, and the process continues until the task is completed.

This approach allows the robot to accumulate interaction experience into a structured memory and leverage this memory to generate context-aware, physically grounded actions in new, previously unseen scenarios, even with minimal or no explicit instructions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted real-world experiments to evaluate the INTENTION framework on a centaur-like humanoid robot equipped with 37 degrees of freedom, two arms, a claw gripper, a RealSense Depth camera, and joint torque sensors. The robot utilized the `o4-mini` VLM from OpenAI API for the Intuitive Perceptor and evaluator, and CLIP for text encoding.

**A. Comparison with Other Methods**

A comparative study was performed against three baseline methods: Whole-Body Model Predictive Control (WB-MPC), Behavior Tree Planner (BT-Planner), and LLM-BT (LLM-based Behavior Tree planner). The evaluation included two types of tasks: a standard manipulation task (Pick and Place) and an interactive intuition task, where the robot operated without explicit task instructions, relying on its embodied physical reasoning.

- **Standard Manipulation Task:** INTENTION achieved 96% planning success and 92% execution success, performing comparably to LLM-BT (98% planning, 94% execution) and significantly better than WB-MPC (84% planning, 72% execution) and BT-Planner (92% planning, 80% execution). The average planning and execution time for INTENTION was 23.75s, which was efficient.
- **Interactive Intuition Task:** INTENTION demonstrated notable adaptability and robustness, achieving 84% planning success and 72% execution success with an average time of 24.86s. In contrast, LLM-BT showed significantly lower performance (20% planning, 15% execution), while WB-MPC and BT-Planner were unable to complete these tasks (Time Limit Exceeded - TLE), indicating their failure in scenarios requiring intuitive physical reasoning without explicit guidance. These results suggest that INTENTION significantly outperforms baselines in tasks demanding an understanding of environmental dynamics and affordance extraction.

**B. Learning Scene Extraction and Task Planning**

Experiments were conducted on four distinct manipulation tasks: handling an object, lifting a desk, pushing a chair, and refilling tea, to evaluate the VLM-based Intuitive Perceptor (IP) and the contribution of MemoGraph.

- **MemoGraph Construction:** Extensive offline experiments were performed to construct a MemoGraph dataset, where the IP extracted physical information and generated scene graphs for each task. A VLM-based task planner inferred actions, and evaluated task graphs were stored.
- **Impact of MemoGraph Size:** The relationship between INTENTION’s success rate and the number of stored task graphs in MemoGraph was investigated in two modes:
 - **Instruction Mode:** The robot received explicit semantic instructions.
 - **Intuitive Mode:** No instruction was provided, requiring the robot to rely solely on interactive intuition and prior memory.
 The results showed that the success probability of INTENTION, in both modes, improved with an increasing number of stored task graphs in MemoGraph, particularly for tasks relying on human-level scene understanding and intuition.

**C. Motion Planning with Intuitive Physics**

Real-world experiments assessed INTENTION's ability to infer appropriate robot motions purely based on interactive intuition and prior experience, without semantic instruction input.

- **Handling Object Task:** The robot correctly generated a receiving motion when an operator handed over different objects, with a 72% success rate.
- **Lift Desk Task:** The robot inferred a collaborative lifting motion based on the operator’s intention, achieving a 68% success rate.
- **Push Chair Task:** The robot assisted an operator in sitting down by pushing a chair when the operator approached and turned their back toward it, demonstrating a 72% success rate.
- **Refill Tea Task:** The robot autonomously refilled a cup when an empty cup was presented and a teapot was detected, achieving a 64% success rate.
These tasks posed significant challenges to the robot’s scene understanding and interactive reasoning capabilities, and INTENTION demonstrated consistent performance.

**D. Results Analysis**

The experimental findings collectively indicate that the INTENTION framework is effective across a range of real-world humanoid manipulation tasks. Its ability to leverage MemoGraph-based experiential memory and VLM-driven scene understanding contributes to its high adaptability and robustness, particularly in intuitive scenarios where other methods struggle. The performance improvement with increasing MemoGraph size highlights the importance of accumulated experience. Even without explicit instructions, INTENTION maintained high success rates (≥ 65%) across various tasks, validating its capacity to infer context-appropriate robot motions from interactive intuition and prior knowledge. This suggests that INTENTION significantly enhances the adaptability, robustness, and generalization capabilities of humanoid robots in novel task scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The INTENTION framework represents a notable contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by introducing a novel approach for learning interactive intuition and enabling autonomous task planning in complex, real-world environments.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enhanced Autonomy in Unstructured Environments:** By moving beyond traditional reliance on precise physical models and predefined action sequences, INTENTION allows humanoid robots to operate effectively in unstructured and dynamic settings. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in domestic, healthcare, and service industries where environments are constantly changing and tasks are often ambiguous.
2. **Robustness to Ambiguous and Novel Tasks:** The framework enables robots to infer appropriate behaviors even with minimal or no explicit instructions, solely based on situational context and prior experience. This addresses a major limitation of current robotic systems that often struggle with generalization to novel tasks or require extensive retraining. The ability to handle "interactive intuition tasks" sets INTENTION apart from methods that fail without clear guidance.
3. **Grounding Language Models in Physical Interaction:** INTENTION effectively bridges the gap between high-level semantic outputs of vision-language models and the low-level motion control required for complex robot platforms. By integrating VLM-based scene reasoning with physically grounded interactive memory (MemoGraph), the framework provides a pathway for language models to inform and generate nuanced, context-aware physical actions.
4. **Development of Experiential Learning for Robots:** The MemoGraph component facilitates continuous learning by storing semantic and spatial information from past human-robot and environment interactions. This incremental accumulation of "intuitive physics" knowledge allows robots to build a richer understanding of their surroundings over time, mimicking a human-like learning process. This could lead to more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents that learn from continuous engagement rather than being pre-programmed for every eventuality.
5. **Applicability to Complex Humanoid Platforms:** The validation on a high-degree-of-freedom humanoid robot demonstrates the framework's capability for complex manipulation and whole-body coordination tasks. This is significant because applying advanced AI methods to humanoid robots often presents unique challenges due to their intricate kinematics and dynamics.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for future research in scaling the MemoGraph to broader task domains, enhancing motion generalization across different robotic platforms, and improving the efficiency of memory retrieval and action inference in highly dynamic conditions. It also provides a testbed for exploring how robots can acquire and refine intuitive physical understanding through embodied interaction.

In summary, INTENTION contributes to the development of more intelligent, autonomous, and adaptable humanoid robots by providing a method for learning and utilizing interactive intuition, thereby extending their operational capabilities in complex, human-centric environments.
