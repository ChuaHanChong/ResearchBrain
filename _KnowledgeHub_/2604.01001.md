# EgoSim: Egocentric World Simulator for Embodied Interaction Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoSim introduces a closed-loop egocentric world simulator that generates spatially consistent interaction videos and persistently updates the underlying 3D scene state. The system leverages a scalable data pipeline and achieves superior visual quality, spatial consistency, and adaptable interaction generation for both human hands and robotic manipulators.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric simulators often lack explicit 3D grounding, leading to structural drift and misalignment during viewpoint changes in interaction videos.
- Prior world simulators struggle to consistently update the state of the virtual world, causing manipulated objects to revert to initial configurations in continuous, long-sequence interactions.
- A significant data bottleneck exists in acquiring large-scale datasets with densely aligned static 3D scenes, camera trajectories, and embodiment action pairs for robust world model development.

## Method

- A ==closed-loop system== iteratively alternates between generating visual observations and updating the 3D scene state to ensure temporal and physical consistency.
- Utilizes a ==Geometry-action-aware Observation Simulation== model that synthesizes dynamic ==interaction residuals== while adhering to explicit 3D environment conditions.
- Features an ==Interaction-aware State Updating== module for training-free reconstruction and ==incremental fusion== of the 3D scene state, enabling continuous simulation with persistent object modifications.

## Results

- **EgoSim** achieved superior visual quality (PSNR **25.056** on EgoDex) and spatial consistency (Depth-ERR **8.888** on EgoDex) compared to all baseline methods.
- Successfully generated continuous, long-horizon simulations by accurately updating 3D states of interacted objects across multiple stages, maintaining contextual coherence.
- Demonstrated effective cross-embodiment transfer, with pretraining on human interaction data boosting robotic manipulation performance (e.g., PSNR from 15.180 to **18.670** on AgiBot-World).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D scene grounding and a closed-loop state update mechanism are critical for maintaining spatial consistency and propagating interaction effects across long-horizon egocentric simulations.
> - A scalable, automated data pipeline for extracting aligned 3D scene, camera trajectory, and embodiment action data from large-scale monocular videos effectively addresses data acquisition bottlenecks for world model training.
> - Pretraining on extensive egocentric human interaction data provides generalizable dynamics priors that significantly enhance performance in diverse cross-embodiment tasks like robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of interactive world simulators has seen significant advancements, largely driven by progress in video diffusion models. These simulators hold promise for applications in spatial intelligence, game engines, and embodied AI. Existing world simulators typically generate realistic videos and construct immersive scenes but often exhibit limitations. These include supporting only restricted forms of action control, such as navigation, and primarily rendering scenes from a third-person perspective, which positions users as passive observers rather than active participants.

A subset of research has begun to explore egocentric world simulators that aim to support vivid interactions driven by human motions. These methods, given an egocentric scene image or video as context, condition on full-body or hand-only actions to synthesize interactive videos. However, current egocentric simulators encounter several critical limitations that EgoSim aims to address:

- **Lack of Explicit 3D Grounding and Spatial Consistency:** Some approaches leverage implicit camera motion mechanisms in video diffusion models but struggle to ensure the 3D consistency of generated interaction videos and strict alignment between camera trajectories and synthesized frames. This can lead to "structural drift" under viewpoint changes. While some works have attempted to decouple static 3D scenes from dynamic changes, they often still lack sufficient 3D consistency.
- **Absence of Persistent World State Updating:** A fundamental capability for any continuous world simulator is the ability to remember and update the environment state based on generated observations. Existing methods frequently treat the scene as static, failing to update world states across multi-stage interactions. This limitation restricts their ability to simulate long-horizon tasks where object states dynamically change (e.g., an object being moved or a door being opened).
- **Data Bottleneck and Limited Generalization:** Current world simulators are often trained on limited datasets. These datasets may consist of aligned multi-view videos or data collected from synthetic environments, which are considerably smaller in scale compared to the vast amount of web-scale monocular egocentric videos available. This data scarcity severely constrains the scalability and generalization ability of models to complex, in-the-wild scenes and diverse, dexterous human or robotic interactions.

EgoSim positions itself within this landscape by introducing a closed-loop egocentric world simulator that explicitly models 3D scenes as updatable world states. It aims to generate spatially consistent interaction videos and persistently update the underlying 3D scene, thereby overcoming the structural drift and static scene limitations of prior work. Furthermore, it addresses the data bottleneck through a scalable pipeline that leverages large-scale monocular egocentric videos, enhancing generalization and enabling cross-embodiment transfer to robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop EgoSim, an egocentric world simulator designed to achieve spatially consistent interaction video generation and persistent updating of the underlying 3D scene state for continuous simulation. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific limitations identified in existing world simulators:

1. **Overcoming Structural Drift and Ensuring Spatial Consistency:** Current egocentric simulators often generate videos that lack explicit 3D grounding. This deficiency can lead to structural drift, where the 3D integrity of the scene is compromised under viewpoint changes, and inconsistencies arise between camera trajectories and the generated visual content. The motivation for EgoSim is to provide robust spatial consistency by modeling 3D scenes as explicit, updatable world states. This involves leveraging point clouds to anchor generated interactions in 3D space, ensuring that scene elements maintain their relative positions and appearances even with significant camera motion.

2. **Enabling Continuous Simulation through Persistent World State Updating:** A significant limitation of prior simulators is their inability to maintain and update a consistent world state over multiple interaction stages. If an object is manipulated, its changed state (e.g., location or configuration) is typically not remembered for subsequent interactions, leading to a static and non-persistent virtual world. EgoSim is motivated to address this by designing an "Interaction-aware State Updating" module. This module is intended to track and update the 3D geometry of manipulated objects, allowing the simulator to continuously evolve the scene state and support long-horizon, multi-stage interactions where physical changes persist.

3. **Enhancing Scalability and Generalization with Diverse Data:** Existing world models are often trained on limited datasets, such as meticulously aligned multi-view videos or synthetic environments. Such data scarcity restricts their ability to generalize to the complexity and diversity of real-world, "in-the-wild" scenarios and a wide range of dexterous interactions. A key motivation for EgoSim is to overcome this data bottleneck. The researchers aim to develop a scalable data construction pipeline that can process massive amounts of readily available monocular egocentric videos. This pipeline is designed to extract the necessary aligned static 3D scenes, camera trajectories, and embodiment actions, thereby enabling EgoSim to learn from a more extensive and diverse set of interactions and improve its generalization capabilities.

4. **Supporting Embodied Interaction and Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** Human and robotic agents engage with environments through diverse and fine-grained behaviors, such as dexterous hand movements, which go beyond simple navigation. Current simulators often offer only coarse control, reducing agents to passive observers. EgoSim is motivated to facilitate more active and nuanced embodied interaction. Furthermore, a specific objective is to demonstrate that the learned interaction capabilities can transfer across different embodiments, specifically from human hands to robotic manipulators, thus extending the applicability of the simulator to embodied AI and robotics research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoSim is conceptualized as a closed-loop egocentric world simulator that cyclically alternates between generating visual observations and updating the 3D scene state. The simulation process is formalized by three core components: the environment State (S), the interaction Action (A), and the visual Observation (O). At any stage *k*, the simulator maintains a 3D world state *S<sub>k-1</sub>*. The input action *A<sub>k</sub>* is decoupled into a camera trajectory *C<sub>k</sub>* and a hand interaction sequence *H<sub>k</sub>*. The simulation loop involves two sequential operations: generating the visual observation *O<sub>k</sub>* by rendering the static background and synthesizing dynamic residuals from hand actions, followed by updating the 3D state *S<sub>k</sub>* from *S<sub>k-1</sub>* and *O<sub>k</sub>*.

The methodology comprises two core modules and a scalable data pipeline:

#### 3.1. Geometry-action-aware Observation Simulation Model

This module is responsible for synthesizing action-conditioned visual dynamics and ensuring strict adherence to the 3D environment.
- **Input Conditions:** The model takes an initial egocentric frame (with hands removed via inpainting), a reconstructed point cloud of the static scene (as a spatial condition), a hand keypoint video (*O<sub>action</sub>*) representing the action condition, and a binary mask video (*M*) indicating unobserved regions.
- **Action Representation:** 3D hand keypoints are extracted and projected onto the 2D observation plane using camera intrinsics and extrinsics. This approach provides a universal, cross-embodiment action representation, facilitating adaptation from human hands to robotic end-effectors.
- **Training:** A denoising network, implemented as a Diffusion Transformer (DiT), is employed. It is initialized with pre-trained inpainting weights to hallucinate unmodeled regions indicated by *M*. The DiT processes concatenated latents (noisy input, background, hand, mask) to predict Gaussian noise, thereby generating the observation. This setup encourages the model to preserve the known background while generating dynamic interactions in regions conditioned by actions.

#### 3.2. Interaction-aware State Updating Module

This training-free module reconstructs and updates the 3D scene state to maintain consistency across continuous simulations.
- **State Reconstruction:**
 - **Initial Geometry:** Camera intrinsics are estimated using DepthAnything3, and instance masks are extracted using SAM3 guided by text phrases related to interactive objects.
 - **Depth and Pose Estimation:** Per-frame depths and camera poses are predicted and aligned using a dual-pass DROID-SLAM system with multi-view depth alignment, which resolves scale ambiguity.
 - **Object Decoupling:** Interactive objects are detected (via Grounding DINO and SAM3) and decoupled from static objects during the reconstruction process.
- **Interaction-aware Object State Update:**
 - **Interactive Object Identification:** A vision-language model is used to identify object phrases involved in embodiment interactions.
 - **Tracking and Masking:** An open-vocabulary tracking pipeline based on SAM3 estimates a 3D point mask for each interactive object. A hierarchical filtering process tags embodiment-centric instances, selects mask candidates by IoU overlap, and refines them with depth consistency.
 - **Fusion:** Point clouds excluding the interactive object masks are fused into a static background (*P<sub>bg</sub>*). The latest-frame geometry of interactive objects is then composited into *P<sub>bg</sub>* to form the updated scene candidate (Ŝ<sub>k</sub>).
- **Incremental State Fusion:**
 - **Alignment:** The coordinate systems of the accumulated previous state (*S<sub>k-1</sub>*) and the new state candidate (Ŝ<sub>k</sub>) are aligned using the Sim3 Umeyama algorithm.
 - **Merging:** Their point clouds are merged via TSDF (Truncated Signed Distance Function) fusion, with overlapping regions updated from Ŝ<sub>k</sub>. Interactive objects retain their geometry from their last observed frame, ensuring an up-to-date scene representation.

#### 3.3. Scalable Data Pipeline for Interactive World Simulators

To address the data bottleneck, a fully automated pipeline is designed to extract aligned quadruplets (static 3D scene, camera trajectory, embodiment-action sequence, and interaction video) from large-scale monocular egocentric and robotic videos.
- **3D Static Scene Initialization:** The first frame of each video clip is used. Hand regions are masked (SAM3) and removed (Qwen-Image-Editing model). DepthAnything3 estimates monocular depth, unprojecting the 2D image into 3D to form the initial point cloud.
- **Camera Trajectory Estimation and Static Rendering:** DepthAnything3 extracts per-frame camera parameters (rotation, translation, intrinsics). The initial point cloud is rendered from this estimated trajectory to produce a geometry-consistent reference video.
- **Interaction Action Extraction and Rendering:**
 - **Human Hands:** HaMeR is used to estimate 3D hand pose sequences, with filters for duplication and outliers.
 - **Robotic End-Effectors:** Precise 3D action representations are computed from joint trajectories and URDF files.
 - **Projection:** These 3D keypoints are projected onto the 2D camera plane to define the spatial action condition. The use of keypoints facilitates cross-embodiment generalization.
- **Dataset Construction:** This pipeline is used to process 240K clips from EgoDex and 160K clips from EgoVid datasets for training.

#### 3.4. EgoCap: Real-World Data Collection Pipeline

EgoCap is introduced as a low-cost, intrinsics-free pipeline for collecting real-world data with uncalibrated smartphones.
- **Uncalibrated Scene Scanning:** A user scans the environment with a smartphone, and the ARTDECO framework builds a global 3D Gaussian Splatting map with dynamic intrinsic self-calibration, yielding a scale-consistent scene point cloud.
- **Dynamic Interaction Recording and Relocalization:** The same device records egocentric interaction. A dense matching localizer continuously tracks visual features against the pre-built map to recover the 6-DoF camera trajectory, even with unknown focal lengths, occlusions, or motion blur.
- **Continuous Trajectory Optimization and Aligned Rendering:** Localization noise is suppressed via cubic spline interpolation for translations, SLERP for rotations, and a Savitzky-Golay filter for global smoothing. The refined trajectory is then used to re-render the 3D scene, producing a geometry-consistent reference video aligned with the interaction viewpoint.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate EgoSim's capabilities in visual quality, spatial consistency, generalization, and adaptability to robotic manipulation and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Normal Settings (Single-Clip):** EgoSim generated realistic interactive videos with notable spatial consistency across various unseen test environments. For instance, in a "scooping ice" task from EgoDex, EgoSim accurately simulated physical state changes and complex depth relationships, whereas baseline methods like InterDyn exhibited depth ambiguity and spatial misalignment. Mask2IV showed issues with hand and object identity preservation, leading to distorted interactions, and CosHand lacked temporal coherence. In an "in-the-wild watercolor painting" task from EgoVid, EgoSim synthesized realistic interaction semantics and coherently hallucinated unobserved background regions, outperforming Wan2.1-14B-InP, which primarily reconstructed incomplete inputs without capturing dynamics. EgoSim consistently maintained viewpoint transitions, unlike baselines that struggled with egocentric head motion.
- **Continuous Generation:** Visualizations demonstrated that EgoSim successfully updated the 3D states of interacted objects, registering their physical displacement within the global scene after each generation stage. This allowed for contextually coherent long-horizon simulations, such as cup lids remaining closed or breads remaining on plates across consecutive generated clips.
- **Real-World Adaptation (EgoCap):** After minimal fine-tuning (50 steps on 30 clips) on data collected using the EgoCap pipeline in a supermarket environment, EgoSim synthesized physically plausible hand-object interactions and consistent backgrounds on unseen test scenes, indicating efficient adaptation to novel real-world environments.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison

- **Normal Settings (Single-Clip):** Table 1 presented quantitative results on EgoDex and EgoVid test sets.
 - **Video Quality:** EgoSim demonstrated superior performance across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics. For EgoDex, it achieved a PSNR of 25.056 and SSIM of 0.896, significantly outperforming InterDyn (PSNR 22.250, SSIM 0.830). Similar trends were observed on the EgoVid dataset.
 - **Spatial Consistency:** EgoSim showed substantial improvements in spatial controllability, as measured by Depth-ERR and Cam-ERR. On EgoDex, its Depth-ERR was 8.888 compared to InterDyn's 44.345, and Cam-ERR was reduced by over an order of magnitude. These results support the effectiveness of EgoSim's explicit spatial conditioning.
- **Continuous Generation:** Table 2 indicated that EgoSim's performance remained robust in the continuous generation setting (121-frame sequences generated as two consecutive clips). While a marginal drop in metrics was noted (e.g., PSNR 19.165, Depth-ERR 10.943), this was attributed to cumulative error and noise from updated states.
- **Fine-grained Interaction Metrics (Supplementary):** On the TRUMANS dataset, EgoSim achieved superior quality and spatial consistency compared to baselines. Additionally, fine-grained metrics on EgoDex and EgoVid showed EgoSim achieved better 3D PA-MPJPE for hand pose and higher 2DAP/3DAP for 6DoF object pose accuracy compared to InterDyn.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Table 3 presented quantitative ablation results on EgoDex, validating key architectural choices.
- **Effect of Camera Trajectory Rendering:** The "w/o trajectory" variant, which ignored camera ego-motion, showed a significant drop in PSNR (23.380), demonstrating the necessity of explicitly rendering point clouds along the true camera trajectory for accurate background parallax and scene geometry.
- **Effect of Mask Constraints:** While the "w/o mask" variant still yielded a high PSNR (23.988) due to the generative prior, the full EgoSim model with accurate visibility masks achieved the best PSNR (25.056) by optimally blending dynamic interactions with hallucinated regions.
- **Effect of Updatable Interaction-aware 3D States:** Qualitative visualizations (Figure 9) confirmed the importance of this module. Without interactive object filtering, the static point cloud was corrupted by dynamic elements. Removing TSDF fusion resulted in fragmented reconstructions, and omitting spatial mask filtering introduced artifacts. The full pipeline maintained a clean, coherent 3D representation, ensuring permanent registration of object displacement.

#### 4.4. Robotic Simulation with Egocentric Pretraining

Table 4 showed that pretraining on large-scale egocentric human hand data (1,200 steps) before finetuning on AgiBot data (200 steps) significantly outperformed training solely on robot data (1,400 steps). PSNR increased from 16.36 to 18.67, SSIM from 0.69 to 0.72, and LPIPS decreased from 0.31 to 0.28, indicating improved spatial and object consistency in generated robot videos. Furthermore, in an application experiment (Supplementary Section 2.1), EgoSim's generated data, used in a model-based planning scheme for an AgiBot G1 robot, improved the task success rate from 53.3% to 66.7%, confirming its utility for downstream policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoSim addresses several fundamental limitations in existing egocentric world simulators, contributing significantly to the fields of computer vision, embodied AI, and robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancing Simulation Fidelity and Consistency:** By anchoring visual generation to an explicit, updatable 3D point cloud, EgoSim achieves a higher level of spatial consistency and fidelity in egocentric interaction videos compared to prior methods. This capability eliminates structural drift under viewpoint changes and ensures that manipulated objects maintain their physical state across continuous interactions, which is crucial for realistic long-horizon simulations.

- **Enabling Continuous and Persistent World Modeling:** The development of the Interaction-aware State Updating module represents a step toward truly persistent world models. This module allows the simulator to "remember" and incorporate the dynamic changes caused by interactions, making the simulated environment reactive and consistent over time. This is vital for complex tasks requiring memory of past actions and their effects on the environment.

- **Scalable Data Utilization for Enhanced Generalization:** The introduction of a scalable data construction pipeline is a key innovation. By automating the extraction of aligned 3D scenes, camera trajectories, and embodiment actions from vast amounts of in-the-wild monocular egocentric videos, EgoSim overcomes the data bottleneck that has hindered the generalization of previous simulators. This approach allows models to learn from a much wider and more diverse range of real-world interactions, leading to more robust and generalizable simulation capabilities.

- **Facilitating Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** EgoSim demonstrates the ability to transfer learned manipulation priors from human demonstrations to robotic manipulation. This "cross-embodiment" capability, enabled by a universal keypoint-based action representation, is important for bridging the gap between human expertise and robotic learning, potentially accelerating the development of dexterous robotic systems through human-guided simulation.

- **Supporting Embodied AI and Robotics Research:** The simulator's ability to generate physically plausible interactions, coupled with its robust spatial and temporal consistency, makes it a valuable tool for embodied AI and robotics. It can serve as a training ground for robot policies, enable model-based planning, and facilitate the evaluation of inverse dynamics models. The demonstrated success in improving robot task success rates through EgoSim-generated data highlights its direct applicability.

- **Democratizing Data Collection:** The EgoCap pipeline offers a low-cost, intrinsics-free method for collecting high-quality, viewpoint-aligned real-world data using uncalibrated smartphones. This could democratize access to the type of complex, paired data needed for advanced world models, reducing reliance on expensive and specialized multi-camera setups.

- **Potential for Diverse Applications:** Beyond core research, EgoSim's capabilities could impact various applications, including creating more immersive and interactive experiences in virtual reality and gaming, developing advanced spatial intelligence systems, and generating diverse training data for real-world robotic deployments in complex, unstructured environments. The capacity to simulate both correct and failure interactions is also valuable for training more robust and adaptable agents.

In summary, EgoSim contributes a comprehensive framework that pushes the boundaries of egocentric world simulation, offering a more spatially consistent, persistent, generalizable, and versatile platform for studying and developing embodied intelligence.
