# Unleashing Large-Scale Video Generative Pre-training for Visual Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance Research introduced GR-1, a GPT-style transformer that leverages large-scale video generative pre-training on egocentric videos to achieve enhanced generalization and data efficiency in multi-task visual robot manipulation. The model outperforms baselines on benchmarks and real-world tasks, demonstrating robust performance in unseen scene and language generalization, as well as with limited robot data.

## Problem

- Robot learning traditionally faces data scarcity, as robot data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and often requires costly human demonstrations.
- Existing robot policies often struggle with generalization to challenging, out-of-distribution scenarios, including unseen scenes, novel object instances, or synonymous language instructions.
- Many current robot manipulation approaches are not unified, relying on separate modules for visual processing, high-level planning, and low-level control, often requiring sparse keypoint predictions rather than end-to-end action generation.

## Method

- ByteDance Research introduced **GR-1**, a unified ==GPT-style (decoder-only) transformer== for multi-task language-conditioned visual robot manipulation.
- The approach uses a ==two-phase training strategy==: large-scale ==language-conditioned video generative pre-training== on 800,000 clips from the ==Ego4D dataset==, followed by finetuning on robot data from the ==CALVIN benchmark== and custom real robot data.
- **GR-1** leverages frozen ==CLIP text== and ==MAE ViT image encoders== for input, and jointly predicts continuous robot actions and future video frames through learnable ==[ACT]== and ==[OBS] tokens==, with a ==combined loss== for finetuning.

## Results

- GR-1 achieved a **94.9%** success rate on the CALVIN benchmark for multi-task learning, outperforming the best baseline (HULC) at **88.9%**, and demonstrated substantial improvement in zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes (**85.4%** vs. **53.3%**).
- The model showed strong data efficiency, maintaining a **77.8%** success rate when trained on only **10%** of the robot data, compared to the best baseline at **66.8%**.
- In real robot experiments, GR-1 achieved a **0.79** success rate for seen object transportation (vs. **0.27** for RT-1) and **0.75** for articulated object manipulation, demonstrating robust performance and generalization to unseen object instances (**0.73**) and categories (**0.30**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generative pre-training on large-scale non-robotics data, such as Ego4D, effectively transfers visual-textual understanding to robot manipulation tasks.
> - A unified GPT-style transformer architecture can seamlessly bridge the gap between large-scale video prediction and multi-modal robot control, providing visual foresight that guides action generation.
> - The joint objective of predicting future video frames alongside robot actions significantly improves policy learning, enhancing generalization and data efficiency for robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: large-scale generative models, computer vision, natural language processing, and robot learning.

**a. Generative Pre-training for Foundation Models:** The paper directly builds upon the success of generative pre-trained models (GPT-style) that have revolutionized natural language processing (e.g., GPT-3, Llama) and made significant inroads into computer vision (e.g., MAE, ViT). These models demonstrate remarkable effectiveness by learning general, useful representations from massive datasets, enabling them to generalize to diverse downstream tasks with minimal finetuning. The core idea of this paper is to extend this powerful paradigm to the domain of visual robot manipulation.

**b. Challenges in Robot Learning and Data Scarcity:** Robot learning traditionally faces unique challenges compared to other AI domains. Robot data is inherently sparse, expensive, and time-consuming to collect, often requiring costly human demonstrations. Moreover, robot data is multi-modal, combining images, robot states, actions, and language instructions. These factors limit the scalability of traditional robot learning approaches. Previous research has explored various pre-training methods to address this, including learning useful visual representations (e.g., R3M, masked image modeling), learning world models, or using in-domain videos. This paper differentiates itself by leveraging *large-scale, out-of-domain, non-robotics video data* (Ego4D dataset) for generative pre-training, aiming to circumvent the robotics data bottleneck.

**c. Language-Conditioned Visual Robot Manipulation:** This is a crucial area of robotics research, aiming to enable more intuitive and flexible robot control through natural language instructions. Existing methods in this space vary widely: some exploit Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level task planning (e.g., PaLM-E, Inner Monologue), while others co-finetune robot trajectory data with vision-language data (e.g., RT-2) or leverage pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP (e.g., CLIPort, PerAct). Many of these approaches rely on predicting sparse end-effector keypoints, necessitating additional motion planners. GR-1 distinguishes itself by adopting a fully end-to-end approach that directly predicts continuous robot actions (and future images), offering potentially greater flexibility and adaptability. It also differs from methods that learn from unstructured play data by focusing solely on demonstrations with language labels.

**d. Transformers in Sequential Decision Making:** The use of transformer architectures (Vaswani et al., 2017) for sequential decision-making has gained significant traction, exemplified by models like Decision Transformer (DT), VIMA, GATO, and RoboCat. These models process sequences of observations, actions, and goals to generate behaviors. GR-1 aligns with this trend by using a GPT-style decoder-only transformer. A key distinction from previous works like RoboCat is GR-1's incorporation of large-scale *video generative pre-training* and its focus on *language-conditioned* multi-tasking, rather than goal-image conditioning or solely relying on heterogeneous robot data.

In summary, this work represents a significant step towards developing generalist robot agents by synergistically combining the power of large-scale generative pre-training (proven in NLP/CV) with the unique challenges of robot learning. By demonstrating the efficacy of pre-training on generic video data for robotic tasks, it offers a promising path towards more data-efficient, robust, and generalizable robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to provide **inaugural evidence that a unified GPT-style transformer, augmented with large-scale video generative pre-training, exhibits remarkable generalization to multi-task visual robot manipulation.**

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Leveraging Generative Pre-training Success:** The authors observe the "remarkable effectiveness" of generative pre-trained models in language and vision. They are motivated to extend this success to the visual robot manipulation domain, believing that similar benefits (learning useful representations, generalizability, robustness) can be achieved.
- **Addressing Robot Data Scarcity:** A significant bottleneck in robot learning is the high cost and time-consuming nature of collecting diverse and large-scale robot data. The paper is motivated to overcome this by using readily available, massive-scale non-robotics video datasets for pre-training, thereby improving data efficiency during robot-specific finetuning.
- **Exploiting Video-Action Relationship:** The core hypothesis is that "video generative pre-training is a closely related task to robot action learning." A robot trajectory is inherently a video sequence. The ability to predict future frames based on past observations and language instructions should empower the robot to anticipate future events, which is crucial for generating relevant and effective actions. This foresight, learned from extensive video data, can serve as a powerful "signpost" for policy learning.
- **Developing a Unified, Flexible Model:** The motivation is to create a single, straightforward GPT-style model (GR-1) that can seamlessly transition from large-scale video prediction pre-training to robot action finetuning. This unified architecture simplifies the learning process and allows for direct transfer of learned representations.
- **Enhancing Generalization Capabilities:** A major goal is to enable robots to generalize effectively to challenging, out-of-distribution scenarios, including unseen scenes, novel object instances, entirely new object categories, and synonymous language instructions. The authors hypothesize that pre-training on diverse human-object interaction videos will imbue the model with robust visual-textual understanding necessary for such generalization.
- **Improving Data Efficiency:** Given the expense of robot data, a crucial motivation is to achieve strong performance even with limited robot demonstration data, demonstrating that large-scale pre-training can significantly reduce the need for vast amounts of task-specific robot trajectories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named **GR-1**, adopts a two-phase training strategy: large-scale video generative pre-training followed by finetuning on robot data. At its core, GR-1 is a GPT-style (decoder-only) transformer model designed for multi-task language-conditioned visual robot manipulation.

**a. Problem Formulation:**
- **Video Generative Pre-Training:** The model `π` is trained to predict future video frames (`o_t+Δt`) given a language description (`l`) and a sequence of past video frames (`o_t-h:t`). This is formulated as `π(l, o_t-h:t) → o_t+Δt`.
- **Multi-Task Visual Robot Manipulation:** After pre-training, `π` is finetuned to map a language instruction (`l`), a sequence of past observation images (`o_t-h:t`), and robot states (`s_t-h:t`) to a robot action (`a_t`), *in addition to* predicting a future image (`o_t+Δt`). This joint prediction is formulated as `π(l, o_t-h:t, s_t-h:t) → o_t+Δt, a_t`.

**b. Architecture (GR-1):**
GR-1 is a simple GPT-style transformer capable of handling various modalities.
- **Input Encoders:**
 - **Language Input:** Language instructions (`l`) are encoded using a frozen **CLIP Text Encoder** (Radford et al., 2021), leveraging its powerful general-purpose vision-language embeddings.
 - **Visual Input:** Visual observations (`o`) are encoded via a frozen **Vision Transformer (ViT)** pre-trained with Masked Autoencoders (MAE) (He et al., 2022). The global `CLS` token provides a high-level image representation, while patch tokens are processed by a Perceiver Resampler (Jaegle et al., 2021) for local features, reducing token count.
 - **Robot State Input:** Robot states (`s`), comprising 6D end-effector pose and binary gripper status, are encoded using linear layers.
- **Tokenization and Sequencing:** Embeddings from all modalities are passed through linear layers for dimension alignment.
 - Learnable `[ACT]` and `[OBS]` tokens are introduced for action and future image prediction, respectively.
 - The language token is repeated at each timestep within a sequence to ensure its prominence.
 - Learned relative timestep embeddings are added to inject temporal information, shared across modalities at the same timestep.
- **Network:** A causal transformer (Radford et al., 2018) is used. Critically, during both pre-training and finetuning, `[ACT]` and `[OBS]` tokens are masked, meaning they cannot attend to subsequent tokens, preserving the autoregressive nature and preventing information leakage.
- **Outputs and Decoders:**
 - **Video Prediction:** A separate transformer decoder (consisting of self-attention blocks and MLPs) operates on the outputs corresponding to the `[OBS]` tokens and additional mask tokens (for image patches). The loss function (`L_video`) is Mean Squared Error (MSE) between reconstructed and original image pixels, similar to MAE.
 - **Action Prediction:** Outputs from the `[ACT]` tokens are fed through linear layers to predict continuous arm actions (delta XYZ positions and delta Euler angles) and binary gripper actions. Arm actions are optimized with Smooth-L1 loss (`L_arm`), and gripper actions with Binary Cross Entropy (`L_gripper`).

**c. Training Procedure:**
- **Pre-Training:**
 - **Data Source:** Ego4D dataset (Grauman et al., 2022), an extensive collection of egocentric human-object interaction videos (over 3,500 hours). The authors curated 800,000 short video clips (8M frames) with natural language annotations.
 - **Task:** Language-conditioned video prediction. GR-1 predicts `o_t+Δt` from `l` and `o_t-h:t`. `Δt` is set to 1/3 second, representing sufficient visual difference.
 - **Loss:** `L_video`.
- **Robot Data Finetuning:**
 - **Data Source:** CALVIN benchmark (Mees et al., 2022c) for simulation, and custom-collected real robot data.
 - **Task:** Multi-task language-conditioned visual robot manipulation, jointly predicting actions and future frames.
 - **Loss:** A combined loss `L_finetune = L_arm + L_gripper + L_video`.
 - **Encoder Freezing:** The CLIP text encoder and MAE image encoder are kept frozen throughout both pre-training and finetuning, preserving their powerful pre-learned representations. The input sequence length is set to 10 timesteps.

This methodology represents a systematic adaptation of the successful generative pre-training paradigm to the complex, multi-modal domain of robotics, leveraging existing large-scale datasets and powerful pre-trained encoders to enhance learning efficiency and generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments on both the challenging CALVIN benchmark (simulation) and a real robot to evaluate GR-1's performance across various settings.

**a. CALVIN Benchmark Experiments:**
The CALVIN benchmark involves 34 diverse language-conditioned manipulation tasks. GR-1 was compared against state-of-the-art baselines including MCIL, RT-1, HULC, and MT-R3M (a multi-task version of R3M). Performance was measured by the success rate of sequentially completing multiple tasks in a row and the average length (number of completed tasks in a row of 5).

- **Multi-Task Learning (ABCD→D Split):** GR-1 significantly outperformed all baselines. Its success rate improved from the best baseline's 88.9% (HULC) to **94.9%**. The average length of completed tasks improved from 3.06 (HULC) to **4.21**, demonstrating superior long-horizon capabilities.

- **Zero-Shot Unseen Scene Generalization (ABC→D Split):** This setting evaluated generalization to an environment (`D`) not seen during training. GR-1 showed a substantial improvement, raising the success rate from 53.3% (RT-1) to **85.4%**. This highlights GR-1's strong ability to generalize to novel visual contexts.

- **Data Efficiency (10% Data):** When trained on only 10% of the available robot data, GR-1 maintained a strong performance, achieving a success rate of **77.8%** compared to the best baseline (HULC) at 66.8%. This finding is critical for real-world robotics, where data collection is expensive.

- **Zero-Shot Unseen Language Generalization:** GR-1 was tested with synonymous language instructions generated by GPT-4, which were not present in the training data. GR-1 again outperformed baselines, achieving a success rate of **76.4%** (compared to HULC's 71.5%). This suggests robust language understanding and generalization.

**b. Real Robot Experiments:**
GR-1 was evaluated on real-world object transportation and articulated object manipulation tasks.

- **Object Transportation:**
 - **Seen Objects:** GR-1 achieved a high success rate of **0.79**, significantly outperforming RT-1 (0.27) and MT-R3M (0.15). It also showed robustness to distracting objects and background changes.
 - **Unseen Instances:** GR-1 maintained strong performance at **0.73** (vs. RT-1 0.13), demonstrating generalization to novel instances of known objects.
 - **Unseen Categories:** In the most challenging setting, GR-1 achieved **0.30** (vs. MT-R3M 0.10), indicating promising, albeit still challenging, generalization to entirely new object categories.

- **Articulated Object Manipulation (Drawer):** For opening and closing a drawer, GR-1 achieved a success rate of **0.75**, substantially outperforming RT-1 (0.35) and MT-R3M (0.30).

**c. Qualitative Analysis on Video Prediction:**
The paper shows qualitative results where GR-1 successfully reconstructs future frames from both CALVIN and real robot data, even if minor details are missed. This indicates that the video prediction objective provides a meaningful signal that guides action predictions.

**d. Ablation Studies:**
- **Impact of Video Prediction & Pre-training:** Ablation studies confirmed that both video prediction as a training objective and large-scale video generative pre-training significantly contribute to GR-1's performance across various settings (multi-task learning, generalization, data efficiency). Models without these components performed noticeably worse.
- **Future Prediction Steps:** The authors found that predicting frames slightly into the future (e.g., 3 steps) was more beneficial than predicting only the immediate next frame, possibly because it provides more meaningful temporal context. However, predicting too far into the future (e.g., 5 steps) did not provide further benefits for local action prediction.
- **Task Success Rates:** Analysis revealed that video generative pre-training particularly improved performance on complex block manipulation tasks and tasks where data was scarce.

In essence, the findings consistently support the core hypothesis: large-scale video generative pre-training, combined with a unified GPT-style transformer, yields superior performance, generalization, and data efficiency for visual robot manipulation compared to existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, with the introduction of GR-1, holds significant implications for the field of robot learning and embodied AI:

- **Pioneering a New Paradigm:** GR-1 provides strong, inaugural evidence that the highly successful generative pre-training paradigm from NLP and CV can be effectively transferred and applied to complex multi-task visual robot manipulation. This could mark a shift in how foundational models for robotics are conceptualized and trained, moving towards leveraging vast, readily available, non-robotics visual data.

- **Addressing the Robot Data Bottleneck:** One of the most persistent challenges in robot learning is the high cost and scarcity of diverse, labeled robot demonstration data. By demonstrating that pre-training on large-scale egocentric video datasets (like Ego4D) significantly enhances data efficiency during finetuning, GR-1 offers a practical pathway to develop more capable robots with less robot-specific data collection. This reduces the barriers to entry for new robot applications and accelerates skill acquisition.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The robust performance of GR-1 in zero-shot generalization settings (unseen scenes, unseen objects, unseen language instructions) is particularly impactful. Real-world robot deployment demands high generalization beyond the training distribution. The ability of GR-1 to adapt to novel visual environments and new task descriptions without explicit retraining is a crucial step towards truly intelligent and adaptable robotic agents. This generalization is attributed to the rich visual-textual alignment learned during large-scale pre-training.

- **Unified and Versatile Architecture:** GR-1's straightforward GPT-style transformer architecture, capable of simultaneously predicting both robot actions and future visual states, simplifies the overall learning framework. This unified approach provides visual foresight that can implicitly guide action generation, potentially leading to more robust and context-aware behaviors.

- **Advancing Multi-Task and Long-Horizon Learning:** The significant improvements on the challenging CALVIN benchmark, especially in completing multiple tasks in a sequence, underscore GR-1's capability in learning complex, long-horizon manipulation skills. This is vital for real-world applications that involve sequences of interacting sub-tasks.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens up several promising avenues for future research, including exploring the benefits of combining video data with and without language annotations, investigating the optimal relevance of pre-training video data to manipulation tasks, and scaling up robot data diversity for even broader skill acquisition.

In conclusion, GR-1 represents a compelling step forward in robotic manipulation. By demonstrating the power of large-scale video generative pre-training, it offers a scalable and efficient approach to training robust, generalizable, and data-efficient robot policies, paving the way for more capable and intelligent robots in diverse real-world scenarios.
