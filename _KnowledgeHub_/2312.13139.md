# Unleashing Large-Scale Video Generative Pre-training for Visual Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance Research introduced GR-1, a GPT-style transformer that leverages large-scale video generative pre-training on egocentric videos to achieve enhanced generalization and data efficiency in multi-task visual robot manipulation. The model outperforms baselines on benchmarks and real-world tasks, demonstrating robust performance in unseen scene and language generalization, as well as with limited robot data.

## Problem

- Robot learning traditionally faces data scarcity, as robot data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and often requires costly human demonstrations.
- Existing robot policies often struggle with generalization to challenging, out-of-distribution scenarios, including unseen scenes, novel object instances, or synonymous language instructions.
- Many current robot manipulation approaches are not unified, relying on separate modules for visual processing, high-level planning, and low-level control, often requiring sparse keypoint predictions rather than end-to-end action generation.

## Method

- ByteDance Research introduced **GR-1**, a unified ==GPT-style (decoder-only) transformer== for multi-task language-conditioned visual robot manipulation.
- The approach uses a ==two-phase training strategy==: large-scale ==language-conditioned video generative pre-training== on 800,000 clips from the ==Ego4D dataset==, followed by finetuning on robot data from the ==CALVIN benchmark== and custom real robot data.
- **GR-1** leverages frozen ==CLIP text== and ==MAE ViT image encoders== for input, and jointly predicts continuous robot actions and future video frames through learnable ==[ACT]== and ==[OBS] tokens==, with a ==combined loss== for finetuning.

## Results

- GR-1 achieved a **94.9%** success rate on the CALVIN benchmark for multi-task learning, outperforming the best baseline (HULC) at **88.9%**, and demonstrated substantial improvement in zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes (**85.4%** vs. **53.3%**).
- The model showed strong data efficiency, maintaining a **77.8%** success rate when trained on only **10%** of the robot data, compared to the best baseline at **66.8%**.
- In real robot experiments, GR-1 achieved a **0.79** success rate for seen object transportation (vs. **0.27** for RT-1) and **0.75** for articulated object manipulation, demonstrating robust performance and generalization to unseen object instances (**0.73**) and categories (**0.30**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generative pre-training on large-scale non-robotics data, such as Ego4D, effectively transfers visual-textual understanding to robot manipulation tasks.
> - A unified GPT-style transformer architecture can seamlessly bridge the gap between large-scale video prediction and multi-modal robot control, providing visual foresight that guides action generation.
> - The joint objective of predicting future video frames alongside robot actions significantly improves policy learning, enhancing generalization and data efficiency for robotic tasks.
