# Toward Skill-Native LLMs: Skill Entropy for Benchmarking and Training Long-Horizon Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Skill-Entropy RL trains LLMs on cross-skill long-horizon tasks by having the model predict a skill label alongside each step's answer, rewarded jointly for answer correctness and for matching a skill-entropy-derived difficulty rank against the gold skill sequence. Paired with the new Skill2-Bench benchmark (558 skills, 9 domains), it raises Qwen3-4B-Instruct's score from 34.4% to 68.4% and Qwen3-1.7B's from 14.6% to 40.1%, outperforming skill-aware baselines.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks evaluate single skills in isolation and provide no principled way to measure how well a model switches between distinct reasoning skills mid-chain.
- Frontier models perform well on single-skill benchmarks yet remain visibly brittle on long-horizon, compositional tasks, even when each component skill is individually mastered.
- No prior measure quantifies task-level difficulty of skill switching, so it is unclear which skill transitions (e.g., planning to extraction) are structurally harder than others.

## Method

- **Skill Entropy**: a directed pairwise metric computing the smoothed ratio of single-skill accuracy to two-step cross-skill accuracy under a fixed reference model, aggregated along a task's skill sequence into a task-level difficulty score.
- **Skill2-Bench**: a benchmark pipeline that builds per-domain skill banks from seed datasets, derives pairwise skill entropies via a reference model, then uses an LLM proposer plus verifier to fuse seed questions into coherent scenario-based tasks at controlled entropy levels.
- **Skill-Entropy RL**: a ==GRPO== framework where the model emits a skill label before each step's answer, trained with a combined reward of per-step answer correctness and a skill-entropy reward comparing predicted versus gold skill-sequence entropy ranks.

## Results

- Skill-Entropy RL improves Skill2-Bench score from **34.4%** to **68.4%** on Qwen3-4B-Instruct and from **14.6%** to **40.1%** on Qwen3-1.7B, beating GRPO by **+9.6%** and **+7.9%** respectively.
- Across **12** evaluated models, cross-skill accuracy drops **4-13%** relative to single-skill accuracy on the same skill, with accuracy falling nearly monotonically from low to high skill-entropy task levels.
- Skill-Entropy RL beats the strongest skill-aware baseline STAT by **+7.0%** and **+7.1%** on the two model sizes and wins on **seven of nine** domains, including open-ended domains excluded from RL training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Per-skill competence and cross-skill switching ability are largely orthogonal capabilities; strong isolated performance on a skill does not predict how well a model chains it with others.
> - The dominant cross-skill failure mode is inertia: models default to reusing the prior step's skill and answer modality rather than switching to what the current step requires.
> - Explicitly rewarding a model's predicted reasoning-plan structure, not just final-answer correctness, transfers gains to held-out domains and external benchmarks beyond the training distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated increasing capabilities in long-horizon reasoning, facilitating advancements in areas such as deep research, agentic coding, and multi-step planning. However, existing literature indicates that while LLMs may perform proficiently on benchmarks evaluating individual skills, they exhibit brittleness when confronted with long-horizon and compositional tasks that necessitate switching between distinct reasoning skills within a single chain of thought. Previous works have explored skill-aware analyses for composing, diagnosing, or augmenting training data, as well as reasoning benchmarks that either focus on single skills or general agentic environments. Reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards has also been applied to enhance reasoning, often by densifying within-step supervision.

This research addresses a recognized gap in the evaluation and training of LLMs for cross-skill long-horizon reasoning. It introduces a quantitative measure for the difficulty of skill transitions, a benchmark specifically designed to assess these transitions, and a training framework that leverages this measure. This work distinguishes itself by providing a directed, pairwise metric for skill-switching difficulty, which is then integrated into both benchmarking and a reinforcement learning reward signal, rather than solely focusing on individual skill performance or within-step reasoning. It positions itself as a complement to existing approaches, offering an orthogonal signal for improving LLM performance on tasks requiring dynamic skill orchestration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed performance disparities of LLMs on single-skill versus multi-skill, long-horizon reasoning tasks. While modern LLMs have demonstrated proficiency in various individual reasoning skills, their ability to fluidly transition between different skills within a coherent reasoning chain, particularly in cross-domain problems, remains an area requiring improvement. The authors formalize this challenge as "cross-skill long-horizon tasks," characterized by multi-step sequences where each step demands a different reasoning skill and depends on prior outputs.

The key objectives of this work are twofold:

1. **To develop a principled method for measuring the difficulty of skill switching:** Existing benchmarks typically evaluate individual skills or aggregate performance on compositional tasks without a granular metric for the cognitive load associated with transitioning between distinct skills. The authors aim to introduce "Skill Entropy" as a directional, pairwise quantity that quantifies this specific difficulty, thereby providing a more precise diagnostic tool for LLM capabilities in long-horizon reasoning.
2. **To establish a benchmark for evaluating cross-skill long-horizon reasoning:** Building upon the concept of Skill Entropy, a further objective is to construct "Skill$^2$-Bench." This benchmark is designed to systematically assess LLMs' performance on tasks requiring sequential application of diverse skills, with tasks calibrated by their skill-entropy scores to represent varying levels of switching difficulty. This aims to expose potential skill-switching gaps that are not apparent in single-skill evaluations.
3. **To utilize skill entropy as a training signal for enhancing LLM long-horizon reasoning:** Beyond evaluation, the research seeks to transform skill entropy into a practical training mechanism. The objective is to propose "Skill-Entropy RL," a reinforcement learning framework where models are encouraged to predict not only the answer but also the skill employed at each step. The reward mechanism is designed to incentivize alignment between the model's predicted skill sequence and the gold standard, thereby training LLMs to become more "skill-native" in handling complex, multi-skill reasoning.

These objectives are motivated by the need to better understand, diagnose, and ultimately improve LLMs' ability to perform complex, real-world tasks that inherently demand dynamic skill composition and switching.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the defined objectives through a multi-faceted approach involving the introduction of a novel metric, the construction of a new benchmark, and the development of a training framework.

#### 3.1. Skill Entropy Framework

The core conceptual contribution is "Skill Entropy," SkE($s_a, s_b$), a directional pairwise measure quantifying the difficulty of switching from skill $s_a$ to $s_b$ within a cross-skill task.
- **Definition:** SkE($s_a, s_b$) is defined as a smoothed ratio: $0.5 \times (\text{Accuracy}(s_a) + \text{Accuracy}(s_b) + \alpha) / (\text{Accuracy}(s_a, s_b) + \alpha)$, where $\text{Accuracy}(s)$ is the accuracy of a reference model on single-skill questions involving skill $s$, and $\text{Accuracy}(s_a, s_b)$ is the average per-step accuracy on two-step cross-skill tasks transitioning from $s_a$ to $s_b$. A value greater than 1 indicates increased difficulty in switching. Laplace smoothing constant $\alpha=0.1$ is applied.
- **Task-Level Skill Entropy:** For a task $\tau$ with skill sequence $\mu(\tau) = (s_1, \dots, s_L)$, the task-level skill entropy SkE($\tau$) is calculated as the average pairwise skill entropy along the task's directed skill switches: $\frac{1}{L-1} \sum_{i=1}^{L-1} \text{SkE}(s_i, s_{i+1})$.
- **Approximation:** To manage the computational cost of evaluating all $O(|S|^2)$ skill pairs, the pairwise skill entropy is approximated by factorizing it into skill-to-domain and domain-to-skill transitions: SkE($s_a, s_b$) $\approx$ SkE($s_a, d_{s_b}$) $\cdot$ SkE($d_{s_a}, s_b$). Claude-opus-4.7 serves as the fixed reference model for skill entropy derivation.
- **Difficulty Levels:** Tasks are categorized into low, medium, and high skill-entropy levels based on empirical thresholds.

#### 3.2. Skill$^2$-Bench Benchmark Construction

Skill$^2$-Bench is a benchmark of cross-skill long-horizon tasks, built through a three-stage pipeline:
1. **Skill Bank Creation:** A bank of 558 fine-grained skills across 9 domains (Math, Science, Coding, Logic, Information Extraction, Planning, Creative Writing, Context Retrieval, Instruction Following) is established. For verifiable domains, skills are labeled on seed datasets via LLM prompting and clustering; for open-ended domains, LLMs directly generate skill lists.
2. **Skill Entropy Derivation:** The reference model (Claude-opus-4.7) is evaluated on single-skill and two-step cross-skill questions to estimate Accuracy($s$), Accuracy($d$), Accuracy($s,d$), and Accuracy($d,s$), which are then used to compute pairwise skill entropies (Section 4.1).
3. **Task Scenario Proposal:** Tasks of length 2 to 10 steps are generated. For each task, a skill sequence is sampled at a target skill-entropy level. An LLM proposer rewrites existing seed questions (for verifiable domains) or generates new questions/rubrics (for open-ended domains) to fit a unifying scenario, ensuring inter-step dependency and answer preservation. A verifier filters out tasks failing consistency, dependency, and answer leakage checks.

#### 3.3. Model Evaluation on Skill$^2$-Bench

- **Models:** 8 frontier models and 4 open-source models are evaluated.
- **Tasks:** A held-out test set of 300 cross-skill tasks, balanced across skill-entropy levels and domains.
- **Settings:** Models are queried in two modes: "single-skill" (each step isolated) and "cross-skill" (full long-horizon task).
- **Metrics:** Verifiable steps are graded by correctness; open-ended steps use an LLM judge (Claude-opus-4.7) against rubrics. Performance is the average per-step score.

#### 3.4. Skill-Entropy RL Training Framework

Skill Entropy is leveraged as a training signal within an RL framework.
- **Response Format:** Models are trained to produce responses that include a predicted skill label for each step, e.g., `<skill>Domain_1, Skill_1</skill><answer>Step_1 Answer</answer>`.
- **Reward Function:** A combined reward $r = \lambda_{\text{ans}} r_{\text{ans}} + \lambda_{\text{ent}} r_{\text{ent}}$ is used.
 - $r_{\text{ans}}$ (answer reward) is the mean per-step accuracy using domain-specific scorers.
 - $r_{\text{ent}}$ (skill-entropy reward) grades the alignment between the model's predicted skill sequence and the gold sequence. It is defined as $1 - |\hat{\rho} - \rho^\star|$, where $\hat{\rho}$ and $\rho^\star$ are the CDF ranks of the predicted and gold task-level skill entropies, respectively.
- **Skill Matching:** Predicted skill labels are mapped to the canonical skill bank using embedding similarity to compute entropies.
- **Training Protocol:** A two-stage pipeline is used: SFT warm-up with skill-annotated traces from a teacher model, followed by GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement with Policy Optimization) using the combined reward. Training data includes 9K cross-skill tasks from verifiable domains.
- **Off-the-Shelf Data Application:** The framework is also applied to existing datasets like OpenR1-Math by prompting an LLM annotator to segment reasoning traces into steps and assign skill labels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding LLMs' cross-skill reasoning capabilities and the effectiveness of Skill-Entropy RL.

#### 4.1. Skill$^2$-Bench Evaluation Results

- **Skill-Switching Gap:** Evaluation of 8 frontier and 4 open-source models on Skill$^2$-Bench revealed a consistent "skill-switching gap." Accuracy generally decreased as task-level skill entropy increased for nearly all models, confirming that skill entropy correlates with cross-skill task difficulty (Table 2).
- **Cross-Skill Performance Degradation:** Models consistently experienced accuracy drops (ranging from -4% to -13%) when the same skills were exercised within a cross-skill task compared to single-skill evaluation. This degradation was observed even for skills where models performed well in isolation, highlighting a limitation not captured by single-skill benchmarks.
- **Dominant Failure Mode:** Analysis indicated that a primary failure mode in cross-skill tasks was the model's tendency to reuse the previous step's skill and answer modality rather than adapting to the current step's requirements. This often led to incorrect skill choices, significantly reducing per-step accuracy (Figure 3, Figure 5).
- **Domain Difficulty vs. Skill Entropy:** The research demonstrated that per-domain skill entropy is largely decoupled from intrinsic domain difficulty. For example, science tasks, while showing high individual skill accuracy, exhibited high skill entropies, suggesting that science skills are easier but more domain-specific, making transitions challenging (Figure 2).

#### 4.2. Skill-Entropy RL Performance

- **Improved Skill$^2$-Bench Performance:** Skill-Entropy RL significantly improved the Skill$^2$-Bench scores for the target models. On Qwen3-4B-Instruct, the score increased from 34.4% (base model) to 68.4%, and on Qwen3-1.7B, it improved from 14.6% to 40.1%. These results represent substantial gains over the base models and also outperformed competitive baselines such as GRPO (which uses only answer reward), Skill-Distill, SkillRL, and STAT (Table 3).
- **Generalization Across Domains:** The gains from Skill-Entropy RL spanned both verifiable and open-ended domains, with the method achieving the best per-domain accuracy on seven of nine domains for both model sizes. Notably, performance on open-ended domains improved even though RL training was restricted to verifiable domains, suggesting transferability of the learned skill-switching ability.
- **Enhanced Skill-Switching Behavior:** Case studies illustrated that Skill-Entropy RL enabled models to more accurately switch between skills and answer modalities across steps, aligning with the gold skill sequence, unlike base models that often perseverated on previous skills (Figure 3).
- **Application to Off-the-Shelf Data:** When applied to the OpenR1-Math dataset, Skill-Entropy RL continued to show benefits. Adding the skill-entropy reward on top of vanilla GRPO led to sustained improvement in final-answer reward, even after GRPO performance plateaued (Figure 4). It achieved the best scores across six external math benchmarks, outperforming GRPO by an average of +1.9% and the base model by +7.7% (Table 11).
- **Generalization to External Benchmarks:** Skill-Entropy RL also showed transferability to five external benchmarks, including long-horizon reasoning benchmarks (MuSR, LongBench-MuSiQue) and general reasoning benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU, IFEval), achieving the highest average score at both model sizes without compromising general reasoning performance (Table 16).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that quantitatively measures and addresses a specific challenge in LLM reasoning: the difficulty of switching between distinct skills in long-horizon tasks. The contributions have several implications:

- **Refined LLM Evaluation:** The concept of Skill Entropy provides a granular, directed metric for assessing LLM capabilities beyond individual skill proficiency. Skill$^2$-Bench, being calibrated by skill entropy, offers a benchmark that can diagnose specific failure modes related to skill transitions, exposing performance gaps not visible in traditional evaluations. This could lead to more targeted and comprehensive assessments of LLM reasoning.
- **Enhanced Training Paradigms:** The Skill-Entropy RL framework demonstrates that skill entropy can serve as an effective training signal. By encouraging models to explicitly commit to a skill plan and rewarding alignment with gold skill sequences, the method improves LLMs' ability to orchestrate diverse skills and dynamically adapt their reasoning style. This suggests a pathway toward developing LLMs that are more "skill-native," capable of fluidly navigating complex, multi-domain problems.
- **Broader Applicability:** The demonstrated ability to apply skill-entropy-based training to off-the-shelf datasets, such as OpenR1-Math, indicates the reusability and practical utility of this signal. This suggests that existing training data, even without explicit skill structure, can be augmented to improve LLM reasoning capabilities without necessitating entirely new data pipelines.
- **Addressing a Fundamental LLM Limitation:** By pinpointing the tendency of LLMs to reuse prior skills and modalities as a dominant failure mode in cross-skill tasks, the research highlights a fundamental limitation. The proposed training method offers a mechanism to mitigate this, potentially leading to more robust and adaptable LLMs for real-world applications that inherently involve compositional reasoning across varied domains.
- **Contributions to the RL Landscape:** The integration of a task-structural reward signal based on skill entropy into RL extends the existing work on verifiable and dense rewards. This orthogonal signal, which targets between-step difficulties, complements within-step supervision methods, opening new avenues for designing more effective reward functions for complex LLM behaviors.

Overall, this work contributes to a deeper understanding of LLM reasoning, provides tools for more precise diagnosis of their limitations, and offers an effective method for enhancing their performance on complex, long-horizon tasks requiring dynamic skill management.
