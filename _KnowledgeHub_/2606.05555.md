# Representation Learning Enables Scalable Multitask Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MR.Q, a model-free actor-critic algorithm enhanced with predictive representation learning, achieved performance comparable to or exceeding world-model-based methods like Newt in multitask deep reinforcement learning, particularly within low-data interaction budgets. The work demonstrates that high-quality latent representations, shaped by auxiliary predictive objectives, are a core factor for scalable multitask agents, leading to improved sample efficiency and robust generalization without explicit planning.

## Problem

- Scaling Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents across diverse multitask environments faces challenges such as optimization instabilities, representation collapse, and underutilization of model capacity.
- The precise sources of scalability benefits in model-based RL (MBRL) are unclear, as current systems often bundle predictive world models, shared architectures, and planning mechanisms, making it difficult to isolate the impact of planning.
- Many MTRL benchmarks evaluate performance at very high interaction budgets (e.g., 100M+ steps), which can obscure genuine sample efficiency in more realistic, low-data regimes.

## Method

- Introduces **MR.Q**, a ==model-free actor-critic== algorithm built upon ==TD3==, which incorporates a ==learned encoder== to map observations and task information into a ==latent representation== (using a frozen ==DINOv2== encoder for visual inputs).
- Augments the core algorithm with ==auxiliary predictive objectives== that train latent models to forecast future latent representations, rewards, and termination signals from current states and actions.
- These ==predictive representation-learning== models serve solely to shape the latent representations by providing ==dense auxiliary supervision==, explicitly avoiding ==planning==, latent rollouts, or trajectory imagination for policy improvement.

## Results

- MR.Q consistently matched or surpassed the world-model-based Newt algorithm in both sample efficiency and final performance across diverse multitask continuous control benchmarks, particularly in the **10M environment steps** low-data regime.
- Ablation studies revealed that MR.Q significantly outperformed an encoder-free TD3 baseline, yielding higher **SRank** and effective dimensionality in latent representations (e.g., 95%-d of **89** vs. **21** on DMControl-Ext), alongside a lower fraction of dormant neurons and more stable value learning.
- MR.Q demonstrated superior scalability across task diversity (achieving a **+37%** relative normalized score over Newt at **2M steps** on **200 tasks**), model and data capacity, UTD ratios, and provided a **50%** stronger zero-shot initialization for few-shot finetuning on unseen tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predictive representation learning, providing dense auxiliary supervision, is a primary driver for scalable multitask RL, enabling sample efficiency and robust generalization, rather than explicit planning.
> - High-quality latent representations, characterized by higher SRank and effective dimensionality, are crucial for mitigating optimization instabilities, promoting network plasticity, and achieving more stable value learning in multitask settings.
> - A well-tuned model-free architecture leveraging predictive representations can achieve competitive performance with significantly less computational overhead and higher wall-clock efficiency compared to complex world-model-based planning approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in various domains, often in single-task environments requiring extensive interactions. The broader machine learning landscape, particularly in language and vision, has demonstrated that scaling models across diverse data distributions, through shared representations, enables generalization, transfer, and robustness. Extending these principles to online deep RL presents ongoing challenges due to factors such as non-stationary data, bootstrapped targets, and long-horizon credit assignment, which can lead to optimization instabilities like representation collapse and unstable value estimation.

Multitask RL (MTRL) aims to address these challenges by training a single agent across a distribution of tasks. However, increasing task diversity in MTRL often exacerbates issues such as instability, task interference, and underutilization of model capacity. Prior work has shown that increasing value function capacity can yield multitask gains, but simply scaling model size is often insufficient without appropriate training objectives and representation learning mechanisms. This highlights representation quality as a critical factor, as effective representations can reduce temporal difference (TD) variance, accelerate learning, and stabilize training.

Model-based RL (MBRL) approaches pursue improved representations by using predictive objectives, specifically by learning latent dynamics models, which provide a denser supervisory signal compared to traditional TD learning alone. This richer signal has contributed to advances in MBRL, with large-scale systems combining predictive representation learning, shared architectures, and planning to achieve strong multitask performance. However, these bundled approaches make it challenging to isolate the specific sources of performance gains. Additionally, planning introduces computational overhead, hyperparameter sensitivity, and compounding model errors, potentially undermining the efficiency benefits.

This work critically examines the premise that model-based control is the primary driver of scalability in multitask RL. It hypothesizes that a significant portion of the benefits attributed to MBRL, particularly in terms of sample efficiency and generalization, stems primarily from the quality of representations learned through predictive objectives, rather than the explicit planning components. The research positions itself by investigating whether a simpler model-free approach, augmented with these representation-shaping predictive objectives, can achieve competitive scalability and performance without the complexities of planning, thereby offering a more computationally efficient alternative. Furthermore, it addresses the common practice in MTRL benchmarks of evaluating at very high interaction budgets (e.g., 100M+ steps), which can obscure genuine sample efficiency. This study focuses on a more challenging low-data regime of 10M environment steps to better evaluate sample efficiency.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether model-free reinforcement learning, when augmented with predictive representation learning, can achieve scalable multitask performance comparable to, or surpassing, state-of-the-art world-model-based approaches, without relying on explicit planning.

The motivation for this objective is multifaceted:

- **Addressing Multitask RL Scalability Challenges:** Deep RL has shown considerable success in single-task environments, but scaling agents to diverse multitask settings remains a central challenge. This difficulty arises from optimization instabilities inherent in RL, such as non-stationary data, bootstrapped targets, and long-horizon credit assignment, which can lead to representation collapse, loss of plasticity, and unstable value estimation.
- **Deconstructing Model-Based RL Success:** Recent advancements in model-based RL have demonstrated strong performance in multitask settings. These approaches typically combine predictive world models, large shared architectures, and planning mechanisms. However, the intertwined nature of these components makes it difficult to pinpoint which elements are most crucial for scalability. The researchers hypothesize that the substantial benefits often attributed to model-based control (i.e., planning) might instead originate from the quality of representations learned through predictive objectives.
- **Mitigating Computational Overhead and Complexity:** Explicit planning in model-based RL introduces significant computational overhead, memory usage, implementation complexity, and hyperparameter sensitivity. It can also lead to instability due to compounding model errors over imagined trajectories. If representation learning alone, via predictive objectives, is sufficient for scalability, then a simpler model-free approach could significantly reduce these burdens.
- **Improving Sample Efficiency in Realistic Regimes:** Many existing large-scale MTRL benchmarks evaluate performance at very high environment interaction budgets (e.g., 100 million steps or more). This can mask true sample efficiency, which is critical in real-world applications where data collection is costly. The research is motivated to evaluate performance in a more challenging low-data regime (10 million environment steps) to highlight genuine sample efficiency gains.
- **Exploring the Role of Representation Quality:** Previous work suggests that representation quality is a bottleneck in scaling deep RL systems, as better representations can reduce variance, accelerate learning, and stabilize training. This motivates a focused investigation into how predictive objectives can enhance latent representations to enable effective scaling in multitask settings.

By addressing these motivations, the study aims to offer insights into the fundamental drivers of scalable multitask RL, potentially redirecting research efforts towards more efficient and less complex algorithmic designs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs MR.Q, a model-free actor-critic algorithm, and evaluates its performance against a prominent model-based baseline, Newt, within a comprehensive multitask reinforcement learning framework.

**Algorithm Design (MR.Q):**
MR.Q is presented as an extension of TD3 (Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient), a model-free off-policy actor-critic algorithm for continuous control. While TD3 typically operates directly on environment observations, MR.Q incorporates a learned encoder, $\phi_{\xi}$, to map observations ($s_t$) and task information ($\tau$) into a latent representation ($z_t = \phi_{\xi}(s_t, \tau)$). Both the actor and twin critics then operate within this latent space.
The core augmentation in MR.Q involves auxiliary predictive objectives. These objectives train latent models to predict future latent representations ($\hat{z}_{t+1}$), rewards ($\hat{r}_t$), and termination signals ($\hat{d}_t$) from the current latent state ($z_t$) and action ($a_t$). These auxiliary objectives are optimized using supervised losses, and their gradients are backpropagated through the shared encoder $\phi_{\xi}$. A critical distinction is that MR.Q explicitly avoids planning, latent rollouts, or trajectory imagination for policy improvement; the predictive models serve solely to shape the latent representations by providing dense auxiliary supervision, thereby isolating the benefits of predictive learning from the computational overhead and instabilities associated with explicit model-based control.

**Baselines and Evaluation Protocol:**
The primary comparison is against **Newt** [Hansen et al., 2026], a world-model-based method that builds on TD-MPC2. Newt learns a latent dynamics model to predict future states, rewards, and values, and uses model predictive control (MPC) to optimize action sequences via planning in latent space.
The evaluation focuses on a **low-data regime of 10 million environment steps**, emphasizing sample efficiency. Selected longer runs (up to 50 million steps) are also conducted to assess asymptotic performance. Performance is measured using aggregate learning curves and final scores, averaged over five random seeds, with 95% confidence intervals.
The training protocol adheres to the **multitask language-conditioned setup** introduced in MMBench [Hansen et al., 2026]. A single agent is trained across a diverse set of environments, sharing observation and action spaces but varying in dynamics and reward functions. Task identity is provided via language instruction embeddings, which are concatenated with state or latent features as conditioning signals. Training is off-policy, utilizing shared replay buffers from which minibatches are uniformly sampled to optimize the actor, critics, and auxiliary objectives.
For **visual observations**, a frozen DINOv2 encoder [Oquab et al., 2024] is used to extract features from pixels, which are then processed by the policy and value networks. This setup aims to isolate the impact of downstream representation adaptation rather than visual feature learning from scratch.

**Analyses:**
1. **Ablation Study:** To isolate the impact of predictive representation learning, MR.Q is compared against an encoder-free baseline, **TD3**. In this setup, the encoder is removed, and the actor receives a direct concatenation of the raw low-dimensional state and a 512-dimensional language instruction embedding. The critic additionally receives the raw action.
2. **Representation Geometry:**
 - **SRank** [Kumar et al., 2021] is measured on state representations to quantify feature capacity.
 - **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** is performed on latent features to visualize their structure and quantify effective dimensionality (95%-d, the number of principal components explaining 95% of the variance).
3. **Training Dynamics:**
 - The **fraction of dormant neurons** [Sokar et al., 2023] in the actor and critic networks is monitored as an indicator of underutilized capacity and reduced plasticity.
 - **Value loss** is tracked to assess optimization stability.

**Evaluation at Scale:**
The scalability of MR.Q is investigated across several dimensions:
- **Task Diversity:** Training on a large combined benchmark of 200 tasks spanning multiple domains (locomotion, manipulation, navigation, arcade).
- **Model and Data Capacity:** Evaluating performance with varying network sizes and amounts of available training data.
- **Update-to-Data (UTD) Ratio:** Assessing how efficiently the algorithm reuses stored experience by varying the number of gradient updates per environment interaction.
- **Few-shot Finetuning:** Evaluating transferability to 28 unseen tasks by finetuning from a pre-trained multitask checkpoint, assessing both zero-shot performance and adaptation speed.
- **Computational Impact:** Measuring wall-clock efficiency to compare the real-world training time required to achieve performance, accounting for the overhead of model-based methods.

All experiments are conducted on NVIDIA A100 GPUs within a distributed Slurm-based compute cluster, with typical training times ranging from 12 to 60 hours per run.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy and scalability of representation learning in multitask deep reinforcement learning, using the MR.Q algorithm in comparison to the world-model-based Newt algorithm.

**Overall Performance and Sample Efficiency:**
- **Multitask Benchmarks (10M steps):** MR.Q consistently matched or surpassed Newt in terms of both sample efficiency and final performance across a diverse suite of multitask continuous control tasks (MMBench domains including DMControl Ext, ManiSkill, Atari, DMControl, OGBench, Box2D, MetaWorld, PyGame, MuJoCo, and RoboDesk).
- **Extended Training (50M steps):** The performance gains of MR.Q persisted in extended training regimes, maintaining or exceeding Newt's performance, indicating that the benefits of structured representations are not limited to the low-data setting.
- **Visual Observations:** When evaluated with high-dimensional visual inputs (using a frozen DINOv2 encoder), MR.Q maintained its advantage, outperforming Newt in sample efficiency and final performance across all tested domains. This suggests that predictive objectives aid in learning task-relevant representations even when strong pre-trained visual features are available.

**Ablation Study and Role of Predictive Representations:**
- **Encoder-Free Baseline (TD3):** An encoder-free TD3 baseline (operating on raw states and language embeddings) demonstrated competitive performance, matching or outperforming Newt across four tested domains. This observation indicates that a well-tuned model-free architecture, even without an explicit representation learning module, can be a strong baseline and challenges the notion that explicit world-modeling is strictly necessary for multitask RL.
- **MR.Q vs. Encoder-Free TD3:** MR.Q exhibited superior performance and sample efficiency compared to the encoder-free TD3 baseline in three out of four domains, with comparable results in the remaining domain. This highlights the contribution of predictive representation learning within the MR.Q framework.

**Analyses of Representation Quality and Training Dynamics:**
- **Representation Geometry:** MR.Q demonstrated significantly higher SRank and effective dimensionality (95%-d) in its latent representations compared to the encoder-free TD3 baseline. For instance, in DMControl-Ext, the 95%-d dropped from 89 (MR.Q) to 21 (TD3), and in MuJoCo, from 66 to 15. This indicates that predictive representation learning in MR.Q promotes structured, high-rank, and well-separated task representations, while its absence leads to collapsed and lower-rank embeddings.
- **Training Dynamics:** MR.Q exhibited a substantially lower fraction of dormant neurons in both its actor and critic networks compared to the encoder-free baseline. This lower dormancy was particularly pronounced in the critic and was associated with lower value losses, suggesting that MR.Q maintains better utilization of network capacity, greater plasticity, and more stable value learning in multitask environments.

**Scalability Across Multiple Axes:**
- **Task Diversity (200 tasks):** On a large combined benchmark of 200 diverse tasks, MR.Q showed substantially higher sample efficiency during training, achieving a +37% relative normalized score at 2 million environment steps compared to Newt, while converging to similar final performance.
- **Model and Data Scaling:** MR.Q exhibited stable and consistent performance gains with increased model capacity and data availability. In contrast, Newt showed greater sensitivity to reduced data and weaker, less predictable scaling with model size.
- **Update-to-Data (UTD) Ratio:** MR.Q consistently benefited from higher UTD ratios, demonstrating improved performance with more gradient updates per environment interaction, indicating efficient data reuse. Newt exhibited slower improvements and diminishing returns at higher UTDs.
- **Few-shot Finetuning and Transfer:** MR.Q provided a stronger zero-shot initialization for 28 held-out tasks, achieving a 50% relative advantage in normalized score over Newt. This advantage was maintained throughout finetuning, with MR.Q showing approximately 13% higher performance at 100k and 200k finetuning steps. MR.Q outperformed Newt on 17 out of 28 held-out tasks, indicating more transferable representations.
- **Computational Impact (Wall-Clock Efficiency):** MR.Q demonstrated superior wall-clock efficiency, reaching higher returns in significantly less training time compared to Newt. This is attributed to MR.Q avoiding the additional computational overhead associated with learning dynamics models and performing latent rollouts inherent in model-based approaches.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research contributes to the field of deep reinforcement learning by re-evaluating the essential components for achieving scalability in multitask settings. Its findings suggest several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Re-focus on Representation Learning:** The study posits that predictive representation learning, rather than explicit planning, is a primary driver for scalable multitask RL. This perspective challenges the prevailing emphasis on complex model-based control components and suggests that future research might benefit from a concentrated effort on designing robust and efficient representation learning mechanisms within model-free frameworks.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** By demonstrating that a model-free approach (MR.Q) can match or surpass complex world-model-based methods (Newt) while significantly reducing computational overhead and improving wall-clock efficiency, the work points towards more resource-friendly solutions for large-scale RL. This can lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners with limited computational budgets, making advanced multitask RL more accessible.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The strong performance of MR.Q in a challenging low-data regime (10 million environment steps) indicates that effective representation learning can enable agents to extract more information from less interaction data. This is critical for real-world applications where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, or unsafe.
- **Robustness and Generalization through Structured Representations:** The detailed analyses showing that MR.Q yields improved representation geometry (higher SRank, effective dimensionality, and well-separated task representations), better optimization stability (reduced dormant neurons, lower value loss), and stronger transfer to unseen tasks, highlight the role of high-quality representations in fostering more robust and generalizable multitask agents.
- **Potential for Hybrid Approaches:** While MR.Q demonstrates strong performance without planning, the paper acknowledges that combining scalable model-free representation learning with latent planning or imagination-based rollouts could offer complementary benefits. This suggests a future direction for designing hybrid architectures that leverage the strengths of both paradigms.
- **Guidance for Algorithm Design:** The insights into how predictive objectives improve network plasticity and enable better utilization of model capacity provide concrete guidance for designing new RL algorithms. Understanding the mechanisms underlying these improvements could lead to more principled theoretical foundations for sample-efficient multitask agents.

The findings advocate for a representation-centric view of deep RL scaling, opening opportunities for developing more efficient, stable, and general-purpose multitask agents. However, the study's primary focus on continuous-control benchmarks implies a limitation, as the extension of these findings to other diverse domains, such as long-horizon environments, remains an open question for future investigation.
