# GeoAlign: Beyond Semantics with State-Guided Spatial Alignment in VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoAlign, developed by researchers from institutions including Tongji University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that uses state-guided spatial alignment to enhance robot manipulation with geometry-aware features derived from RGB images. It achieves an average success rate of 78.8% on real-world ALOHA tasks, outperforming an RGB-only baseline by 13.8 percentage points on geometry-critical manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often prioritize high-level semantic understanding, leading to failures in fine-grained manipulation tasks that require precise geometric detail.
- Challenges arise in tasks demanding tight clearances, exact alignments, contact-sensitive motions, or handling objects like transparent or annular structures where standard depth sensing can be unreliable.
- Existing methods struggle to dynamically select locally relevant geometry from visual inputs while maintaining a compact and efficient conditioning interface for continuous action generation.

## Method

- **GeoAlign** employs offline post-training of a pre-trained depth model's encoder on robot-domain RGB-D data to create Geometry-Enhanced Post-Trained (GEP) features from RGB images, without using predicted depth during policy execution.
- The robot's proprioceptive state dynamically generates query slots, which cross-attend to these GEP features, extracting compact, phase-dependent geometry tokens.
- These geometry tokens are then integrated with standard RGB-language semantic tokens to condition a flow-matching action decoder, enabling precise action generation with critical spatial awareness.

## Results

- GeoAlign achieved an average success rate of **99.0%** on the LIBERO benchmark, improving upon the RGB-only Isaac-GR00T N1.6-3B baseline's **97.0%**.
- On eight geometry-critical real-world ALOHA tasks, GeoAlign demonstrated an average success rate of **78.8%**, surpassing the RGB-only baseline (**65.0%**) and π0.5 (**67.5%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both robot-domain geometry post-training and proprioceptive state-guided querying were crucial for performance, with variants lacking these components showing up to an 7.4 percentage point decrease in LIBERO success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adapting a depth-aware visual encoder on robot-specific RGB-D data allows for the distillation of robust geometry features from RGB images, making the system less susceptible to depth sensing failures with challenging objects.
> - Leveraging the robot's proprioceptive state to dynamically query and align with image-space geometry features enables the VLA model to focus on execution-relevant spatial cues as the task progresses.
> - Combining these compact, state-guided geometry tokens with high-level semantic context effectively bridges the gap between semantic understanding and the precise geometric intelligence needed for successful physical robot manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressing towards developing generalist robot policies capable of executing complex language-conditioned manipulation tasks across diverse environments and objects. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant approach in this endeavor, leveraging pre-trained vision-language representations and robot demonstration data to map observations and instructions to robot actions. Recent advancements in VLA models have expanded to incorporate heterogeneous robot data, various action interfaces, and predictive or generative world-model representations.

Despite these developments, fine-grained manipulation often necessitates a level of spatial detail that can be obscured or lost in high-level semantic representations. Current VLA models frequently prioritize semantic grounding, which, while useful for understanding instructions and identifying objects, may not adequately address the geometric precision required for physical execution. Challenges arise in scenarios demanding tight clearances, precise alignments, contact-sensitive motions, stable object releases, or geometry reasoning for objects such as transparent or annular structures. These issues highlight a "local geometry problem" where the action decoder requires guidance from spatial features that determine the physical executability of an action.

Existing research has pursued two main strategies to bridge the gap between semantic grounding and executable manipulation. One approach focuses on enhancing the perception component of VLA models by integrating stronger spatial understanding through depth-aware modules, 3D region context, spatial grounding, or auxiliary depth supervision. While these methods augment the spatial information available prior to action decoding, those relying on measured depth or explicit 3D inputs can be susceptible to sensing limitations, particularly with transparent or thin objects. A complementary strategy involves directly incorporating spatial structure into policy execution and action generation. This includes methods that utilize explicit spatial action maps, geometry-aware action representations, spatial affordance prediction, or structured spatial reasoning. These methods aim to increase the spatial expressiveness of the action interface itself. However, for continuous VLA action heads, a challenge remains in dynamically selecting locally relevant geometry while maintaining a compact conditioning interface for action generation.

GeoAlign contributes to this landscape by introducing a state-guided spatial alignment architecture. It addresses the limitation of current VLA models that may neglect critical geometric details by generating RGB-derived geometry features and employing proprioceptive state to dynamically select phase-relevant local geometry for action generation. This approach seeks to provide the necessary spatial grounding for fine-grained manipulation without directly relying on potentially problematic measured depth during policy rollout.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for executable robot manipulation by integrating geometry-aware spatial alignment. The authors identify a significant limitation in current VLA models: their tendency to prioritize semantic understanding over the precise geometric details essential for successful physical interaction with the environment.

The motivation stems from the observation that while VLA models can often correctly interpret instructions and identify target objects (semantic grounding), they frequently fail in tasks requiring fine-grained motor skills. Such tasks include:
- **Tight Clearance Operations:** Manipulating objects in confined spaces where precise positional accuracy is crucial to avoid collisions.
- **Precise Alignment:** Aligning components during assembly or placing objects in specific orientations.
- **Contact-Sensitive Motion:** Executing actions that require careful control of contact forces or maintaining stable contact.
- **Stable Release:** Releasing objects in a manner that ensures their stability, rather than dropping them.
- **Geometry Reasoning over Challenging Objects:** Handling transparent objects (e.g., clear bottles, tape) or annular objects (e.g., tape rolls) where standard depth sensors may provide incomplete or erroneous information, and their shape requires specific manipulation strategies.

These challenges collectively define a "local geometry problem," where the success of an action depends on spatial features that determine its physical executability. The current action decoders in VLA models often lack mechanisms to effectively leverage or dynamically attend to these critical local geometric cues.

The paper aims to address two specific, interconnected challenges within this problem:
1. **Extracting Robust Geometry Features from RGB:** Relying solely on measured depth can be problematic for certain objects (e.g., transparent ones). The research seeks to develop a method to derive reliable geometric information primarily from RGB images, which are less susceptible to these sensing failures.
2. **Dynamic Selection of Relevant Geometry:** The geometric features relevant for an action can change significantly throughout a manipulation task (e.g., during reaching, grasping, inserting, or releasing). A static or globally pooled representation of geometry may not provide the precise, phase-dependent cues required. The objective is to enable the model to dynamically select only the execution-relevant local geometry information, while simultaneously keeping the conditioning interface for continuous action generation (like diffusion or flow-matching policies) compact and efficient.

By addressing these objectives, GeoAlign intends to improve the reliability and precision of VLA models in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, moving beyond purely semantic understanding to incorporate the necessary geometric intelligence for physical execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoAlign operates through a two-stage pipeline designed to provide geometry-aware spatial alignment for VLA policy learning. The overall approach combines RGB-derived geometry features with proprioceptive state-guided attention to dynamically extract relevant spatial cues for an action decoder.

#### 3.1. Overview

The pipeline consists of:
1. **Offline Geometry Post-Training:** A pre-trained depth prediction model's encoder is adapted using robot-domain RGB-D supervision. The depth prediction head is subsequently discarded, retaining only the geometry-enhanced encoder features.
2. **Policy Training and Rollout:** During this stage, the frozen, post-trained geometry encoder extracts image-space features from RGB images. The robot's proprioceptive state generates query slots that cross-attend to these geometry features, producing compact, phase-dependent geometry tokens. These tokens are then combined with RGB-language semantic tokens to condition a flow-matching action decoder.

#### 3.2. Geometry-Enhanced Post-Trained Feature Extraction (GEP Features)

- **Initialization:** The geometry branch is initialized from a Depth Anything V2-Small model. This model has a DINOv2 visual backbone.
- **Post-Training Data:** The model is post-trained on a robot-domain RGB-D dataset, specifically the RobbyVLA subset of LingBot-Depth. This dataset comprises 580,960 paired RGB-D frames collected from real-world VLA manipulation tasks involving Franka and UR7e robot arms, equipped with RealSense D405 cameras. The post-training data do not include the specific ALOHA task trajectories used for evaluation.
- **Supervision:** During post-training, the temporary depth head predicts metric depth from RGB images. The training objective uses a Scale-Invariant Logarithmic (SiLog) loss, which is applied only to valid pixels. The post-training fine-tunes the DA-V2 encoder-side feature extractor with a lower learning rate and the depth prediction layers with a higher learning rate.
- **Feature Extraction:** After post-training, the depth prediction head is *discarded*. The retained encoder-side descriptors, specifically from `geometry feature stage=1` (reassembled with factor 2 to a 74x74 feature map), are used as the Geometry-Enhanced Post-Trained (GEP) features. These features are then mapped via a linear projector to a 256-dimensional space, yielding 5,476 spatial tokens per view. These tokens are augmented with 2D positional and view embeddings, forming an image-space geometry feature grid (Φgeot) without explicit 3D reconstruction. This means depth supervision shapes the RGB-derived geometry representation, but the policy never directly uses predicted depth maps as input.

#### 3.3. State-Guided Spatial Alignment

- **Motivation:** The relevance of local geometry cues changes throughout a manipulation task. GeoAlign addresses this by dynamically generating queries based on the robot's proprioceptive state.
- **State Encoding:** The robot's proprioceptive state (st, 14 dimensions: 6 for each arm, 1 for each gripper) is encoded into a single state embedding (ht).
- **Query Generation:** An MLP generates K=8 query slots (Qt) from the state embedding, augmented with a learned positional embedding.
- **Cross-Attention:** These state-guided query slots then perform cross-attention with 8 attention heads over the geometry feature grid (Φgeot). This process extracts phase-dependent spatial cues.
- **Compact Geometry Tokens:** The output of the attention mechanism is processed by a feed-forward block and projected to the VLA decoder dimension, resulting in 8 compact geometry tokens (Zgeot). These tokens represent a focused summary of the relevant geometry selected by the current robot state.

#### 3.4. Action Generation and Training

- **VLA Backbone:** An Isaac-GR00T N1.6-3B backbone encodes multi-view RGB observations and language instructions into semantic conditioning tokens (Zvlmt). The backbone uses Eagle-Block2A-2B VLM features.
- **Decoder Context:** The compact geometry tokens (Zgeot) are concatenated with the semantic tokens (Zvlmt) to form the complete decoder context (Ct).
- **Action Decoder:** A 32-layer GR00T flow-matching DiT (Diffusion Transformer) action head predicts an action chunk (At) over a horizon H=16. The decoder operates on noised action chunks and discretized flow time, additionally conditioning on the encoded robot state and GR00T future tokens.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a flow-matching objective. Given a ground-truth action chunk and Gaussian noise, a flow time is sampled, and a noised action chunk is constructed. The decoder predicts the target velocity, and the loss function is an action-mask normalized L2 loss.
- **Inference:** During inference, the policy initializes from Gaussian noise and integrates the predicted velocity over Node Euler steps to generate the action chunk, which is then executed before replanning.
- **Training Stages:** Training occurs in two stages: first, the geometry branch is post-trained. Then, the post-trained geometry encoder is frozen, and only the downstream projector, alignment module, and action decoder are trained using policy demonstrations.

This methodology aims to provide the VLA model with dynamically selected, geometry-aware spatial context, derived from RGB images and guided by the robot's current state, without relying on measured depth during policy execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GeoAlign spanned across three distinct settings: the LIBERO benchmark, SimplerEnv-Fractal tasks in simulation, and real-world ALOHA tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results on LIBERO

- **Overall Performance:** GeoAlign achieved an average success rate of 99.0% across the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).
- **Comparison to Baseline:** This represents an improvement over the RGB-only Isaac-GR00T N1.6-3B baseline, which achieved 97.0% average success.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** The most notable improvements were observed on suites requiring significant spatial reasoning. For the Spatial suite, GeoAlign reached 100.0% success, up from 97.65% for the RGB-only baseline. In the Long suite, success increased from 94.35% to 96.6%. Object and Goal suites, which are described as being closer to semantic grounding, also showed high performance (99.5% and 100.0% respectively).

#### 4.2. Simulation Results on SimplerEnv-Fractal

- **Overall Performance:** GeoAlign achieved an unweighted average success rate of 85.3% across the three shared SimplerEnv-Fractal task families: Pick Coke Can (100.0%), Move Near (85.5%), and Open/Close Drawer (70.3%).
- **Comparison to Baseline:** This represents a gain of 5.7 percentage points over the RGB-only baseline, which achieved an average of 79.6% (Pick Coke Can: 97.5%, Move Near: 75.5%, Open/Close Drawer: 65.8%).

#### 4.3. Real-World ALOHA Evaluation

- **Overall Performance:** GeoAlign demonstrated an average real-world success rate of 78.8% across eight geometry-critical tabletop tasks.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** This significantly outperformed the controlled RGB-only baseline (65.0%) and also exceeded π0.5 (67.5%) under the same experimental setup.
- **Task Examples:** The tasks included clear tape retrieval from an occluded box (GeoAlign: 35.0% vs. RGB-only: 20.0%), transparent bottle insertion (GeoAlign: 75.0% vs. RGB-only: 35.0%), tape-roll insertion (GeoAlign: 65.0% vs. RGB-only: 40.0%), and various plate-relative placement tasks where GeoAlign consistently performed at or near the top.

#### 4.4. Ablation Analysis

Controlled ablations were conducted on LIBERO to isolate the contributions of key design choices:

- **Robot-Domain Geometry Post-Training:**
 - The "w/o post-training" variant, which used unadapted Depth Anything V2 encoder features instead of robot-domain post-trained GEP features, achieved 95.9% average LIBERO success, a decrease from GeoAlign's 99.0%. This suggests the importance of adapting the geometry representation to the robot's workspace.
 - The "w/ unfrozen encoder" variant, which updated the geometry encoder during policy training, achieved 95.93% average success, indicating that preserving the frozen, pre-adapted GEP representation is beneficial.

- **Proprioceptive State Querying:**
 - The "w/o spatial querying" variant, which replaced spatial cross-attention with global average pooling of post-trained GEP features, achieved 91.6% average success. This highlights the value of retaining spatial detail and using attention.
 - The "w/o state queries" variant, which used learned query slots instead of proprioceptive-state-generated queries for spatial cross-attention, achieved 96.2% average success. GeoAlign, with its state-guided queries, outperformed this, particularly on Spatial and Long suites where dynamic relevance of geometry to robot state is higher. This demonstrates the effectiveness of dynamically generating queries based on the robot's state.

#### 4.5. Runtime

The alignment module added a computational overhead, but the end-to-end inference runtime remained practical for deployment. On an H200 GPU, GeoAlign operated at 92.1 ± 3.8 ms per action chunk (10.9 Hz), compared to 71.9 ± 3.1 ms (13.9 Hz) for the RGB-only baseline.

In summary, the results indicate that GeoAlign's state-guided spatial alignment, leveraging RGB-derived geometry features shaped by robot-domain depth supervision, consistently improved VLA policy performance across diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks, especially those requiring precise geometric execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeoAlign framework offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and VLA model development.

Firstly, its primary significance lies in directly addressing a critical limitation of existing VLA models: the gap between high-level semantic understanding and the precise geometric reasoning required for executable manipulation. By explicitly focusing on "local geometry," GeoAlign enables VLA policies to perform fine-grained tasks that demand tight clearances, accurate alignment, and interaction with challenging objects like transparent or annular structures, where purely semantic representations often fall short.

Secondly, the methodology of deriving Geometry-Enhanced Post-Trained (GEP) features from RGB images, supervised by robot-domain RGB-D data, provides a robust alternative to relying solely on measured depth during policy rollout. Measured depth maps can be unreliable for transparent or highly reflective objects, a common issue in real-world environments. GeoAlign circumvents this vulnerability by distilling geometric cues into RGB-derived features, making the system potentially more robust and broadly applicable in diverse settings.

Thirdly, the introduction of a "state-guided spatial alignment" mechanism is a notable methodological advancement. By allowing the robot's proprioceptive state to dynamically query an image-space geometry feature grid, GeoAlign ensures that the action decoder receives compact, phase-dependent spatial cues. This dynamic attention mechanism enables the policy to focus on the most relevant geometric information as the manipulation task progresses (e.g., distinguishing between reaching, grasping, inserting, or releasing phases), leading to more effective and efficient action generation. This contributes to maintaining a compact conditioning interface for continuous action heads, a challenge in prior work.

The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across diverse benchmarks—LIBERO, SimplerEnv-Fractal, and real-world ALOHA tasks—support the practical utility of GeoAlign. The gains on geometry-critical tasks in simulation and real-world scenarios indicate its potential to enhance the capabilities of generalist robot policies, making them more adept at physically interacting with their environment. The ablation studies further substantiate the individual contributions of geometry post-training and proprioceptive state querying, providing insights into the mechanisms underlying its effectiveness.

The potential impact of GeoAlign extends to making robots more capable in complex real-world applications. By improving geometric precision without requiring direct depth sensing during inference, it could facilitate the deployment of VLA models in industries requiring delicate assembly, intricate object handling, or interaction with visually challenging materials.

However, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as not explicitly modeling collision or contact constraints, and the reliance on the visual coverage and distribution of the RGB-D supervision data. Future work could potentially integrate persistent spatial memory and force feedback to address these, further enhancing the framework's robustness and applicability. Overall, GeoAlign represents a step towards VLA models that are not only semantically intelligent but also spatially competent for executing fine-grained manipulation tasks.
