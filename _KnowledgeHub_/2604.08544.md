# SIM1: Physics-Aligned Simulator as Zero-Shot Data Scaler in Deformable Worlds

> [!summary] Summary
> A physics-aligned simulation pipeline named SIM1, developed by Shanghai AI Lab, enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for deformable object manipulation by generating high-fidelity synthetic data, achieving a 27x cost reduction and 6.8x throughput increase compared to real-world data collection. Policies trained solely on SIM1's synthetic data demonstrate superior generalization and robustness across various domain shifts.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects requires vast amounts of interaction data, which is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive to acquire in the real world.
- Existing sim-to-real (S2R) approaches for rigid objects fail for deformables due to geometric mismatches, inaccurate soft-body dynamics, and inadequate motion primitives in simulations.
- The ungrounded nature of current simulations, lacking fidelity in physics and geometry, renders synthetic data unreliable for zero-shot deployment on real robots.

## Method

- **SIM1** implements a ==real-to-sim-to-real (R2S2R) data engine== that systematically aligns simulation with the physical world across geometric, dynamic, and movement stages.
- ==High-fidelity geometric alignment== is achieved by digitizing real-world assets (garments, robots, environment) using professional 3D scanning and CAD models with submillimeter precision.
- A ==deformation-stable physics solver==, inspired by ==Augmented Vertex Block Descent (AVBD)==, ensures physically consistent rigid-soft coupling and realistic deformable dynamics.

## Results

- Policies trained exclusively on **SIM1**'s synthetic data achieved performance comparable to real-data baselines and enabled zero-shot task acquisition with a **76%** success rate from de novo initialization, where real-data baselines failed (**0%**).
- Synthetic data significantly improved generalization, outperforming real-data baselines by **50%** in spatial shifts, **13%** in texture variation, and **47%** in lighting perturbations, achieving **70%** success on an unseen polo shirt zero-shot.
- **SIM1** demonstrated high data scaling efficiency, achieving a **27x** cost reduction and **6.8x** throughput increase over real-world data acquisition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Metric-accurate geometric alignment is a critical prerequisite for bridging the sim-real gap in deformable manipulation, as minor discrepancies propagate into significant dynamic and contact errors.
> - A specialized deformation-stable physics solver is essential for realistic and stable simulation of soft materials and their interaction with rigid bodies, as generic solvers exhibit unrealistic artifacts.
> - Structured deformable manipulation synthesis, combining trajectory decomposition with diffusion-based motion generation and validity checking, is crucial for producing diverse, high-quality, and physically valid synthetic data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects, such as garments, by robots is an area of robotics that presents significant challenges due to the complex and continuously evolving nature of their geometry, contact dynamics, and topology. This domain is characterized by a high demand for diverse data, which is difficult and expensive to acquire in real-world settings.

The broader research landscape in embodied learning for robotic manipulation has increasingly focused on data scaling as a primary driver for performance improvement, particularly in rigid-object settings. Sim-to-real (S2R) synthetic data generation has emerged as a strategy to address data scarcity. In rigid-object domains, robust asset libraries and automated generation pipelines have allowed for increased synthetic diversity to translate into real-world performance gains.

However, the paper identifies that this established S2R paradigm largely fails in deformable manipulation for several reasons:
1. **Geometric Mismatch**: Simulated scenes often lack precise alignment with real environments, relying on coarse calibration or manually constructed assets that lack metric fidelity. This inconsistency is amplified by soft-body deformation.
2. **Fragile Soft Dynamics**: Physics engines are predominantly optimized for rigid-body dynamics, resulting in unstable or inaccurate responses when simulating cloth and other soft materials under interaction.
3. **Inadequate Motion Primitives**: Behavior generation methods for deformable objects often rely on rigid-object paradigms, such as simple grasp-point or pick-and-place primitives, which are insufficient for complex garment manipulation.

Consequently, synthetic data in deformable manipulation primarily serves for pre-training, with reliable performance still requiring substantial real-world post-training. The paper posits that the issue with simulation is not its synthetic nature but its lack of grounding in physical reality. Prior work in real-to-sim (R2S) alignment and physical twin reconstruction has shown promise in rigid manipulation, but a fundamental degradation persists in deformable tasks due to insufficient alignment of simulated dynamics with real-world physics.

SIM1 addresses this gap by proposing a physics-aligned real-to-sim-to-real (R2S2R) paradigm. This approach aims to establish comprehensive alignment across geometric, dynamic, and movement aspects of simulation with the physical world. This positions SIM1 as a contribution that seeks to transform simulation into a reliable and scalable source of supervision for deformable manipulation, bridging the gap between existing simulation capabilities and the requirements for high-fidelity, zero-shot real-robot transfer. The work extends previous efforts in data scaling, S2R generation, and R2S asset digitization by integrating these elements into a unified, physics-grounded pipeline specifically designed for deformable objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a physics-aligned simulation pipeline, named SIM1, that can generate high-fidelity synthetic data for deformable object manipulation. This data is intended to be a scalable and reliable substitute for real-world data, enabling policies trained purely in simulation to achieve zero-shot transfer and robust generalization to physical robots.

The motivation for SIM1 stems from several critical challenges in the field of robotic manipulation with deformable objects:

1. **Data Intensity of Deformable Manipulation**: Dexterous manipulation of deformable objects, such as clothing, is a fundamental capability for robotics but is exceptionally data-intensive. The continuous evolution of shape, complex contact mechanics, and topological changes in deformable objects demand extensive state and visual coverage in training data, far exceeding the variability encountered with rigid objects.
2. **Prohibitive Cost of Real-World Data Acquisition**: Acquiring large-scale real-world manipulation data for deformable objects is costly. It necessitates skilled human operators, specialized hardware, and significant manual labor, which places practical limits on the size and diversity of datasets. This limitation restricts the generalization capabilities of vision-language-action models trained on such sparse data.
3. **Limitations of Current Sim-to-Real Pipelines**: While sim-to-real (S2R) data generation has shown promise in rigid-object domains, its application to deformable manipulation is hindered by several shortcomings:
 - **Unaligned Geometries**: Existing simulated scenes are often not accurately aligned with real environments, frequently relying on coarse calibration or manually constructed assets that lack metric fidelity. These geometric inconsistencies are particularly problematic for soft-body deformations.
 - **Inaccurate Soft Dynamics**: Physics engines are predominantly optimized for rigid-body dynamics, which often leads to unstable, unrealistic, or inaccurate physical responses for deformable materials like cloth.
 - **Inadequate Behavior Generation**: The methods for generating manipulation behaviors in simulation are often adapted from rigid-object paradigms, focusing on simple grasp points or pick-and-place actions, which are insufficient for the complex and continuous interactions required for garment manipulation.

The authors contend that simulation's failure in this context is not due to its synthetic nature but rather its lack of grounding in the physical world. This motivates a shift from post-hoc adaptation to an "alignment-first simulation" approach, which prioritizes correspondence between simulated and physical dynamics over superficial realism. The central question driving this research is: "how can simulation be grounded so that synthetic data is a reliable substrate for real-robot deployment?"

To address these motivations, SIM1 advocates for a real-to-sim-to-real (R2S2R) paradigm. This approach aims to align each stage of the data generation pipeline with the physical world, thereby enabling direct, zero-shot transfer of policies to real robots. The specific objectives include:
- Developing a system for metric-consistent scene digitization.
- Calibrating deformable dynamics through elastic modeling to ensure physical consistency.
- Expanding behaviors via diffusion-based trajectory generation combined with quality filtering.
- Ultimately, transforming sparse real-world observations into scalable synthetic supervision with high fidelity for real-robot deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SIM1 adopts a real-to-sim-to-real (R2S2R) paradigm to achieve fidelity across geometry, dynamics, and motion, enabling synthetic data to serve as a reliable training substrate for robot learning. The framework is structured into three main components: SIM1-Scene for real scene digitization, SIM1-Sim for deformation-stable physics simulation, and SIM1-DataGen for structured deformable manipulation synthesis.

#### 3.1. SIM1-Scene: Real Scene Digitization

This initial stage focuses on establishing metric-accurate geometric alignment between real and simulated environments.
- **Deformable Assets**: For garments, a professional 3D scanner (EinScan Rigil Pro) is used to capture high-fidelity meshes and textures. The garment is scanned on a mannequin to maintain its natural shape. Multi-view RGB images and LiDAR scans are fused to create a dense point cloud. Manual segmentation removes the mannequin, and the garment point cloud undergoes surface refinement (e.g., Poisson reconstruction) and mesh post-processing (hole filling, smoothing, remeshing) to produce a clean, watertight, textured OBJ asset with sub-millimeter precision.
- **Robot Assets**: The ARX ACONE robot's kinematic structure, joint limits, collision geometries, and visual meshes are imported directly from manufacturer-provided URDF files (CAD models). Dimensional accuracy is verified, and the root transform is adjusted for alignment with the world coordinate frame. Bimanual coordination is calibrated.
- **Environment Assets**: Static objects (e.g., tables) are sourced from 3D model repositories or created manually. These are imported as mesh files and placed with scaled dimensions and orientations to replicate the real-world setup.

#### 3.2. SIM1-Sim: Deformation-Stable Physics Simulation

This component addresses the challenge of achieving physically consistent rigid-soft coupling in real-time.
- **Stabilized Solver via Augmented Vertex Block Descent (AVBD)**: The paper extends the Newton–VBD solver with an AVBD formulation.
 - **Mechanism**: After vertex positions are updated by standard Newton–VBD, the deformation of each mesh edge is examined. If the distance between connected vertices exceeds a predefined stretch threshold (Equation 1), a virtual elastic constraint is activated. This constraint introduces an additional tensile force, injected into the VBD optimization, to accelerate convergence towards physically plausible cloth configurations (Equation 2).
 - **Parameter Update**: The penalty stiffness ($k$) and Lagrange multiplier ($\lambda$) are updated iteratively (Equation 3) to progressively enforce the strain constraint, preventing explosive stretching and improving deformation stability during contact.
 - **Internal Force Computation**: The solver uses a St. Venant–Kirchhoff (StVK) hyperelastic model for stretching and shearing resistance (Equation 5) and a dihedral-angle bending model for out-of-plane deformations (Equation 6). These forces, along with the new strain constraint forces and external forces, are assembled to compute vertex displacement updates (Equations 7 and 8).
 - **Penetration Avoidance**: A geometric safety filter is implemented to prevent interpenetration. A safe displacement bound clips the raw Newton update, ensuring vertices do not overshoot safety margins and preventing tunneling or numerical instability (Equation 9).
- **Simulation Infrastructure for Parameter Calibration**:
 - **Workflow**: An expert operates the physical dual-arm robot to perform manipulation sequences. The robot's joint states are streamed in real-time to the simulator, causing the simulated twin to reproduce identical motions. Renderings from the simulator are visually compared with real executions.
 - **Calibration**: Based on visual feedback of discrepancies (e.g., draping, folding, contact behavior), physical parameters such as density ($\rho$), Young’s modulus ($E$), Poisson’s ratio ($\nu$), friction ($\mu$), restitution ($\eta$), and relaxation ($\zeta$) are iteratively adjusted. This process aims for behavioral consistency rather than exact physical parameter recovery, ensuring simulated interactions closely resemble reality.
 - **Acceleration**: The simulation leverages NVIDIA Warp, compiling Python code into CUDA kernels for GPU acceleration, achieving approximately 15 frames per second (fps).
 - **Data Recording**: Asynchronous logging records per-frame robot states and gripper openness in NPZ format for policy learning, and full session trajectories and contact manifolds in USD for diagnostic analysis.

#### 3.3. SIM1-DataGen: Structured Deformable Manipulation Synthesis

This stage focuses on generating diverse and scalable manipulation trajectories suitable for deformable objects.
- **Trajectory Decomposition**: Expert demonstrations (D) collected via teleoperation are segmented into "interacting" and "moving" regions. Interacting segments (stable grasp configurations and contact states) are preserved and pooled. New demonstrations are generated by randomly selecting segments from this pool, reusing templated grasp poses while varying sequence order and task context. This preserves physically valid interactions while expanding behavioral diversity.
- **Diffusion-based Motion Generation**: For smooth transitions between decomposed interacting segments, trajectory synthesis is treated as a sequence completion task. A conditional diffusion model (a transformer sequence model) predicts intermediate motions, learning human-like transitions from demonstration data. It reconstructs trajectories from partially corrupted tokens, applying stochastic masking and noise recovery (Equation 4).
- **Validity Checking**:
 - **State-based Filtering**: An initial lightweight filter uses garment particle states and "vibe coding" to synthesize threshold rules. These rules define admissible regions, efficiently pruning physically implausible trajectories early.
 - **Video Discriminator**: A binary video discriminator ($D$) is trained on head-view RGB observations (V) to distinguish valid demonstrations from low-quality (e.g., penetration, adhesion) samples. Negative cases are naturally generated during simulation. A ResNet-18 feature extractor and Transformer encoder aggregate temporal information, outputting a validity score. Trajectories exceeding a threshold ($\tau_{disc}$) are retained.
- **Visual Randomization**: Valid trajectories are rendered in Blender with randomized materials, lighting, and camera parameters. Multiple variations are generated per trajectory using cycle path tracing to produce photorealistic RGB images synchronized with trajectory timestamps. The final dataset combines rendered observations with robot states and actions in the LeRobot format for imitation learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the SIM1 pipeline's ability to generate scalable, high-fidelity synthetic data for deformable manipulation, focusing on sim-to-real transfer, cross-domain generalization, and data scaling efficiency.

#### 4.1. Experimental Protocol and Setup

- **Data Collection**: 1,000 real trajectories were collected using kinesthetic teaching on an ARX ACONE dual-arm robot. 200 source simulation demonstrations were teleoperated within SIM1, using ARX X5 arms mapped to the simulated robot.
- **Tasks**: T-shirt folding served as the primary benchmark. Additional tasks (Towel Flipping, Shorts Folding, Long-Sleeve Garment Folding, Crumpled Garment Flattening & Folding) were explored for generalizability.
- **Evaluation**: Policies, trained either exclusively on real data or SIM1 synthetic data, were evaluated over 30 trials per configuration. Success was defined by the garment reaching the target folded configuration without dropping or unfolding. Both in-domain (same layout, garment, etc.) and generalization evaluations (spatial, texture, lighting, viewpoint shifts) were conducted.
- **Synthetic Data Generation**: From the 200 source simulation demonstrations, SIM1 generated 10,000 synthetic samples for in-domain training and 2,000 for out-of-domain evaluation. Diversity was introduced through garment translation/rotation, 17 table and 28 cloth material types, 90 randomized environment combinations, and camera elevation perturbations.

- **Out-of-Domain Robustness (Q2)**:

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Scene Reconstruction and Physics Solver**:
 - SIM1-Scene's sub-millimeter accurate reconstruction was found to be superior to marker-assisted methods (e.g., AR Code), which yielded coarser, artifact-prone meshes.
 - The deformation-stable solver (SIM1-Sim) effectively eliminated unrealistic dynamics (excessive stretching, slippage, spiky deformations) observed in generic deformable solvers, producing stable and physically consistent garment behavior. This validated the necessity of both geometric and dynamic alignment.

- **Module Contributions (Table 1)**:
 - A baseline trajectory strategy (adapted from rigid-body manipulation) failed completely (0% pass rate, 0% success).
 - Adding trajectory decomposition enabled a 65% pass rate for data generation but yielded 0% task success.
 - Incorporating diffusion-based generation improved data realism (47% in-domain success) but showed weak generalization.
 - The full SIM1 pipeline, including the deformation-stable solver, yielded substantial gains, increasing in-domain success to 67% and achieving an average success rate of 76% across all metrics. This underscored the critical role of solver stability and movement alignment in generating physically consistent and generalizable manipulation trajectories.

#### 4.4. Cost-Efficiency Analysis (Table 4)

- The SIM1 simulation framework demonstrated a 27x reduction in unit cost and a 6.8x increase in throughput compared to real-world data collection. A synthetic trajectory cost approximately $0.10, versus $2.71 for a real trajectory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in SIM1 demonstrates a significant advancement in addressing the data bottleneck for robotic manipulation of deformable objects. By introducing a physics-aligned real-to-sim-to-real (R2S2R) pipeline, the work provides a practical pathway for generating high-fidelity synthetic data that is directly applicable to real-world robotic tasks.

The core significance lies in its ability to enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. Policies trained exclusively on SIM1-generated synthetic data achieved performance comparable to, and often surpassing, those trained on limited real-world data, particularly under various domain shifts. This indicates that the proposed physics-aligned simulation paradigm successfully grounds synthetic environments in physical reality, minimizing the sim-to-real gap that has historically hampered progress in deformable object manipulation.

The potential impact of SIM1 is multi-faceted:
- **Reduced Data Acquisition Costs**: By demonstrating that synthetic data can effectively substitute for and scale beyond real-world data, SIM1 offers a cost-efficient alternative to labor-intensive and expensive real-robot data collection. The measured 27x cost reduction and 6.8x throughput increase highlight its economic advantages.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The structured synthesis approach, combined with extensive visual randomization, enables the generation of diverse datasets that lead to policies with superior generalization capabilities. This is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unconstrained environments.
- **Accelerated Policy Learning**: The ability to rapidly generate large volumes of high-quality training data from limited initial demonstrations can significantly accelerate the development and iteration cycles for robotic manipulation policies, moving towards data-efficient policy learning.
- **Broader Task Coverage**: The framework's applicability to various garment types and manipulation tasks (folding, flipping, flattening) beyond a single benchmark suggests its potential for addressing a wide spectrum of deformable manipulation challenges.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: SIM1 establishes a principled foundation for future research in embodied AI and robot learning, particularly in areas involving complex interactions with soft materials. It encourages a shift towards alignment-first simulation methodologies.

A recognized limitation is that the material calibration process currently relies on expert-guided parameter tuning for each specific asset type, which impedes full automation across arbitrary cloth materials. Addressing this could further enhance the pipeline's efficiency and applicability. Nevertheless, by validating that high-fidelity simulation can serve as a scalable and reliable source of supervision, this work provides a practical foundation for advancing data-efficient robotic development in domains such as healthcare (e.g., dressing assistance), logistics (e.g., sorting textiles), and domestic robotics (e.g., laundry).
