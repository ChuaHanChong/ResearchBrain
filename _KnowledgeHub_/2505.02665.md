# A Survey of Slow Thinking-based Reasoning LLMs using Reinforced Learning and Inference-time Scaling Law

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey details recent progress in Large Language Models designed for "slow thinking" (System 2 cognition) by analyzing over 160 studies across test-time scaling, reinforced learning, and dedicated slow thinking frameworks. The work synthesizes how these methods enhance deliberative reasoning, consistently showing improved performance on benchmarks like MATH, MMLU, and GPQA.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often lack robust, deliberate reasoning capabilities for complex problems, failing to fully emulate human "System 2" slow thinking.
- The current research landscape for reasoning LLMs is fragmented, lacking a comprehensive survey that integrates cognitive inspiration (slow thinking) with key technical solutions (RL and inference-time scaling).
- LLMs require enhanced deliberative processes to reliably tackle challenging tasks in fields like mathematics, multi-agent planning, and medical diagnosis.

## Method

- The survey systematically reviews over **160** studies, classifying slow-thinking LLM methodologies into three core categories: ==Test-Time Scaling==, ==Reinforced Learning==, and ==Slow Thinking Frameworks==.
- It details solutions involving dynamic inference-time scaling, such as advanced search, sampling, and verification mechanisms, to adjust computational resources based on task complexity.
- It analyzes methods leveraging Reinforcement Learning for iterative reasoning refinement, including policy network optimization, diverse reward design, and self-evolution strategies.

## Results

- LLMs incorporating slow-thinking principles, RL, and inference-time scaling demonstrate consistent performance improvements across diverse reasoning benchmarks.
- Specific models like DeepSeek-R1 achieved **79.8** on MATH and **90.8** on MMLU, while Kimi k1.5 scored **77.5** on MATH and **87.4** on MMLU.
- Test-time scaling methods, including advanced search algorithms and dynamic verification, enhance output quality and reasoning efficiency without altering core model parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Slow thinking" from cognitive science offers a potent conceptual framework for advancing LLMs beyond heuristic responses to deliberate reasoning.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Inference-time Scaling are identified as crucial, synergistic technical drivers for equipping LLMs with System 2-like reasoning capabilities.
> - Future LLM development should focus on hybrid architectures that balance fast and slow thinking, robust RL reward mechanisms, and self-improving learning paradigms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid proliferation and impressive capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4, LLaMA, and Qwen have revolutionized numerous domains, yet they often exhibit limitations in tasks demanding deep, deliberate reasoning—a cognitive process akin to "System 2" thinking described by Daniel Kahneman in "Thinking, Fast and Slow." This survey is situated precisely within the burgeoning field addressing these limitations by developing LLMs capable of "slow thinking."

The broader research landscape for reasoning LLMs has seen a surge in survey efforts, analyzing various facets of this complex area. Some existing surveys focus on specific dimensions, such as the mechanisms of Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT), or emphasize core technical approaches, positioning Reinforcement Learning (RL) as a primary driver for model advancements. Other works analyze model evolution through training paradigms like self-evolution or provide broad overviews of technical stages like post-training (fine-tuning, RL, test-time scaling). Notably, some conceptualize LLM reasoning through the lens of Kahneman's dual-system theory (System 1/System 2), much like the present work.

However, this survey carves out a unique niche by offering a *synthesized and focused perspective*. Its primary contribution lies in analyzing "Slow Thinking" (System 2 cognition) as a *cognitive goal* and distinctively identifying **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** and the **Inference-time Scaling Law** as the principal and synergistic technical mechanisms for achieving this advanced reasoning capability. While RL and test-time scaling are individually addressed in other surveys, this paper uniquely integrates these two methodologies within the conceptual framework of "Slow Thinking." By synthesizing insights from over 160 studies, it provides a structured roadmap for researchers, charting the evolution of slow-thinking-based reasoning LLMs and highlighting the interplay between cognitive inspiration and technical innovation. This positions the survey as a crucial resource for understanding the current state and future directions of LLMs moving beyond rapid, heuristic responses towards more human-like, deliberate problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this survey is to comprehensively explore recent advancements in reasoning LLMs that are specifically designed to emulate "slow thinking," a deliberative reasoning process inspired by human cognition. The authors aim to synthesize the vast body of research in this domain, providing a structured overview of key technologies and methodologies.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations of traditional LLMs. Despite their remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding, code generation, and multi-modal reasoning, these models often struggle with tasks demanding deep, sustained deliberation—qualities associated with Kahneman’s "System 2" cognition. For instance, complex mathematical problems, nuanced ethical evaluations, or multi-agent planning require more than just "fast thinking" (System 1), which excels in quick, intuitive judgments but can falter in complex scenarios.

To address this gap, researchers have increasingly turned to the "slow thinking" paradigm in AI, focusing on careful, step-by-step reasoning. Recent innovations, exemplified by models like OpenAI’s o1 and Deepseek R1, demonstrate this shift by dynamically scaling computational resources during inference (inference-time scaling) and refining decision-making through iterative improvement via reinforced learning. This integration broadens LLMs' applicability across critical domains such such as mathematics, visual reasoning, medical diagnosis, and multi-agent debates, where robust and reliable reasoning is paramount.

Thus, the key objectives are to:
- Present a comprehensive overview of the evolution of reasoning LLMs, detailing technological advancements like slow thinking mechanisms, reinforcement learning, and knowledge distillation.
- Categorize and thoroughly analyze existing research (over 160 studies) into three primary areas: test-time scaling, reinforced learning, and slow thinking, evaluating their performance and effectiveness.
- Identify and discuss critical challenges and future directions for advancing reasoning in LLMs, including achieving an optimal balance between fast and slow thinking, designing robust reward mechanisms, and incorporating human-in-the-loop refinement.

The ultimate motivation is to synthesize current knowledge and chart a path toward LLMs that combine human-like deep thinking with scalable efficiency, thereby unlocking their full potential in real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This survey adopts a structured and comprehensive approach to analyze the landscape of slow-thinking-based reasoning LLMs. The authors synthesize insights from over 160 studies, systematically organizing current methodologies into three primary, interconnected categories, each with its own sub-themes:

1. **Test-Time Scaling**: This category focuses on mechanisms that dynamically adjust computational resources during inference based on task complexity.
 - **Search and Sampling**: Explores strategies like Beam Search and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for systematic exploration of reasoning paths, as well as sampling techniques (e.g., Majority Vote, Best-of-N) to generate and aggregate multiple output candidates. It also includes "Long to Short CoT" methods aimed at optimizing the efficiency of extended Chain-of-Thought sequences.
 - **Dynamic Verification Mechanism**: Examines approaches where LLMs generate multiple candidates and then employ domain-specific verifiers or self-refinement strategies to enhance output quality without altering base model parameters. This includes Verification Guided Strategies and Self-Refine Strategies (e.g., self-checking, error localization, backtracking).

2. **Reinforced Learning (RL)**: This section delves into how RL refines decision-making and enhances reasoning capabilities through iterative improvement.
 - **Policy Network**: Covers strategies for acquiring training data (Data Synthesis and Augmentation, Transfer Learning) and multi-stage training strategies (Cold Start Fine-Tuning, Rejection Sampling and Supervised Fine-Tuning) to develop and refine policy models for reasoning.
 - **Reward Design**: Analyzes the critical role of reward models in steering policy optimization. This includes supervision-based methods (Process Supervision, Outcome Supervision, Hybrid Models) and reward source-based models (Rule-Based Reward Models, Preference Learning approaches like PPO and DPO).
 - **Self-Evolution**: Focuses on mechanisms where models progressively improve their performance without continuous, intensive human supervision. This involves Self-Evaluation and Feedback, and Reinforcement Learning combined with Self-Training (e.g., "Generate-Filter-Learn" cycles, Self-Play).

3. **Slow Thinking Frameworks**: This category outlines overarching frameworks that structure problem-solving with manageable, deliberate steps, directly mimicking "System 2" cognition.
 - **Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**: Examines techniques crucial for enabling multi-step deliberation, including Data Distillation from larger models, methods for Long-Context Extension & Improvement, fostering Implicit Reasoning without explicit verbalization, and mechanisms for Reflection and Backtracking to detect and correct errors.
 - **Hierarchical Reasoning**: Addresses frameworks that impose modularity (e.g., explicit structures, agentic collaboration, dynamic processes, latent representations) to tackle complex, multi-step problems more controllably.
 - **Hybrid Thinking**: Explores frameworks inspired by dual-process cognitive theories, integrating rapid, intuitive processing (System 1) with deliberate, logical reasoning (System 2) through Guided Search, Adaptive Control, Specialized Architectures, and Tailored Training.

The report integrates qualitative descriptions of these methodologies with quantitative performance comparisons where available (e.g., on benchmarks like MATH, AIME, MMLU, GSM8K, GPQA), leveraging figures and tables from the original paper (e.g., Figure 1 for model timelines, Tables 1-4 for model comparisons and performance). This structured categorization and detailed exposition allow the survey to systematically chart the evolution of reasoning LLMs and identify key trends and challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey synthesizes over 160 studies to present a comprehensive landscape of advancements in slow-thinking-based reasoning LLMs. The main findings are organized around the three core methodological categories: Test-Time Scaling, Reinforced Learning, and Slow Thinking frameworks.

#### 4.1. Test-Time Scaling

The survey highlights the crucial role of dynamically adjusting computational resources during inference.
- **Search and Sampling:** Innovations like LLaVA-O1 demonstrate that tailoring Beam Search to domain-specific cognitive workflows (e.g., stage-level search in multimodal reasoning) can significantly enhance performance, even for smaller models compared to larger proprietary ones. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), foundational in AlphaZero, has been refined in LLM contexts by methods like Marco-O1 (granular token-level exploration) and STILL-1 (triple-optimization for mathematical reasoning), improving search granularity and verification. Sampling techniques, initially shown by Brown et al. to exponentially expand problem-solving coverage, have evolved to include efficiency-focused methods like Speculative Rejection (16x–32x computational gains) and Feynman-Kac Steering (adaptive resampling for high-reward paths). Efforts like Long to Short CoT (e.g., LightThinker, TokenSkip) aim to optimize reasoning length and efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.
- **Dynamic Verification Mechanisms:** These strategies improve output quality at inference time without modifying base model parameters. Verification Guided Strategies (e.g., CoRe, SCoRe, HuatuoGPT-o1) leverage dual-system cognitive frameworks and domain-specific validators (e.g., medical validators) to guide reasoning and re-rank outputs. Self-Refine Strategies (e.g., Self-Verification, Reflexion, SelfCheck) empower models to intrinsically evaluate their outputs, identify errors at intermediate steps (step-wise verification), and initiate corrective actions, often using confidence estimation or guided beam search.

#### 4.2. Reinforced Learning

RL is identified as a pivotal mechanism for incentivizing and refining LLM reasoning.
- **Policy Network:** Research shows effective strategies for policy optimization. **Training Data Acquisition** has moved beyond human-curated data to include advanced Data Synthesis and Augmentation (e.g., T1, ReST, SPIN generating self-correction trajectories) and Transfer Learning (e.g., DeepSeekMath initializing from code-trained models). **Multi-Stage Training Strategies** are common, beginning with a Cold Start Fine-Tuning Stage (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, BOLT) using high-quality data to build foundational reasoning, followed by a Rejection Sampling and Supervised Fine-Tuning Stage to refine models with filtered, high-quality reasoning chains.
- **Reward Design:** Reward models are fundamental for guiding RL. **Supervision-Based Methods** include Process Supervision (PRMs), which evaluate intermediate steps (e.g., Lightman et al. found PRMs outperform ORMs on MATH), Outcome Supervision (ORMs), which assess final output correctness (often simpler but prone to "shortcut solutions"), and Hybrid Models that combine both for robust guidance (e.g., HuatuoGPT-o1 using an outcome-validator to guide search before RL). **Reward Source-Based Models** use Rule-Based Rewards (e.g., Open-Reasoner-Zero, HuatuoGPT-o1 using code execution or medical validators) or Preference Learning (e.g., PPO, DPO, GRPO) to align models with human intent or desired behaviors, often through pairwise comparisons, reducing reliance on explicit reward functions.
- **Self-Evolution:** Models are increasingly capable of improving autonomously. **Self-Evaluation and Feedback** mechanisms (e.g., Self-Rewarding Correction, Selfcheck, CRITIC) allow models to critique their own outputs, provide corrective suggestions, and measure step-wise confidence. **Reinforcement Learning and Self-Training** (e.g., STaR, ReST, SPIN) operate through iterative "Generate-Filter-Learn" cycles, where models generate solutions, select high-quality ones using validators/reward models, and then fine-tune themselves. Self-Play (e.g., SPIN) introduces adversarial learning to improve by discriminating between self-generated and human-annotated data.

#### 4.3. Slow Thinking Frameworks

These frameworks aim to directly emulate deliberate, human-like problem-solving.
- **Long CoT:** Capabilities are enhanced through several strategies. **Data Distillation** via SFT (e.g., Wu et al., DeepSeek-R1, AtomThink) transfers complex reasoning patterns from large teacher models to smaller student models, including implicit reasoning. **Long-Context Extension & Improvement** (e.g., Kimi k1.5 with 128K tokens, Marco-o1, Phi-4) expand the context window and optimize attention mechanisms to handle extensive reasoning. **Implicit Reasoning** (e.g., Kimi k1.5 using <think> tags, Deng et al. removing intermediate steps) encourages internal structured problem-solving. **Reflection and Backtracking** (e.g., DeepSeek-R1's Self-Refinement, RedStar, Gan et al. with MCTS) allow models to monitor reasoning, detect errors, and dynamically adjust trajectories.
- **Hierarchical Reasoning:** These frameworks introduce modularity for controllable and interpretable reasoning. **Explicit Structures** (e.g., ReasonFlux with Hierarchical RL, Search-o1 with bi-layered RAG) counter static reasoning paths. **Agentic Systems** (e.g., MALT, OctoTools, Agentic Reasoning) automate optimization of agent roles and integrate tools. **Dynamic Control Mechanisms** (e.g., MixLLM, AdaptiveStep) offer flexibility by adjusting based on task, cost, or model confidence. **Latent Space Manipulations** (e.g., PEARL, ThoughtProbe) target internal processes and representations for modular control.
- **Hybrid Thinking:** Inspired by dual-process theories, these frameworks integrate fast (System 1) and slow (System 2) processes. **Guided Search** (e.g., HDFlow, HaluSearch with MCTS, Q* with Q-values) orchestrates the interplay between modes. **Adaptive Control** (e.g., DAST, Entro-duction, SIFT) dynamically adjusts reasoning strategies based on task difficulty or model state. **Specialized Architectures** (e.g., Talker-Reasoner agents, SYMBOLIC-MoE) structurally embody dual processes. **Tailored Training** (e.g., RELAY, Mix Distillation, B-STaR) adapts training to balance efficiency and deliberative logic.

The performance metrics presented in Tables 2, 3, and 4 (e.g., MATH, AIME 2024, GPQA, MMLU, GSM8K, LiveCodeBench, HumanEval) consistently show improvements across various reasoning benchmarks when these slow-thinking, RL-enhanced, and inference-scaled methods are applied, indicating their practical efficacy. For example, DeepSeek-R1 achieves 79.8 on MATH and 90.8 on MMLU, while Kimi k1.5 scores 77.5 on MATH and 87.4 on MMLU, highlighting the competitive performance of these "o1-like" models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for both current research and the future trajectory of Large Language Models, serving as a critical resource for understanding and advancing reasoning capabilities in AI. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Comprehensive Roadmap for Research**: By synthesizing over 160 studies and categorizing them into distinct yet interconnected paradigms—test-time scaling, reinforced learning, and slow thinking frameworks—the survey provides a meticulously structured roadmap. This helps researchers navigate the complex landscape of reasoning LLMs, identify leading-edge techniques, and understand the historical progression of ideas. It serves as an invaluable starting point for newcomers and a structured reference for seasoned practitioners.

2. **Bridging Cognitive Science and AI**: The paper’s foundation in Daniel Kahneman’s "Thinking, Fast and Slow" offers a compelling cognitive analogy for advanced LLM reasoning. This framework not only inspires novel architectural designs and training methodologies but also fosters interdisciplinary collaboration between cognitive science and artificial intelligence, potentially leading to more human-aligned and robust AI systems.

3. **Guiding Future Development in "Slow Thinking" LLMs**: The survey explicitly highlights current challenges and outlines crucial future directions. These include:
 - **Balancing Fast and Slow Thinking**: Developing hybrid architectures that dynamically switch between intuitive (System 1) and deliberate (System 2) modes based on task demands, ensuring both efficiency and depth.
 - **Multi-modal Reasoning**: Extending slow-thinking capabilities to integrate and reason across diverse modalities (text, images, audio, video), crucial for real-world applications.
 - **RL Stability and Reward Design**: Addressing instability and "reward hacking" in RL-based fine-tuning by designing more robust reward models that accurately reflect reasoning quality, potentially incorporating intermediate steps or human-in-the-loop feedback.
 - **Generalization vs. Over-Optimization**: Developing techniques to improve cross-domain robustness and prevent overfitting to specific benchmarks, ensuring models perform well on unfamiliar tasks.
 - **Self-Improving RL Frameworks**: Exploring meta-reinforcement learning and iterative self-training paradigms that enable models to continuously refine their reasoning policies, mimicking human learning and adaptation.
 - **Human-in-the-Loop Refinement**: Integrating human expertise through interactive feedback mechanisms to guide models towards better reasoning strategies and enhance reliability.
 - **Application in Other Domains**: Expanding slow-thinking models to high-impact fields such as robotics (complex action planning), recommendation systems (deeper user preference analysis), and healthcare (evidence-based diagnosis), each requiring tailored adaptations.

4. **Informing Model Design and Evaluation**: The detailed breakdown of methodologies, from search algorithms and reward model types to data distillation and self-evolution strategies, offers practical guidance for designing and evaluating next-generation LLMs. By comparing various approaches and their performance on key benchmarks, the survey enables researchers to make informed decisions about which techniques are most suitable for specific reasoning challenges.

In conclusion, this survey is highly significant as it not only consolidates the current state of "slow thinking" in LLMs but also explicitly charts a forward path. By tackling the crucial problem of deliberate reasoning, the work contributes directly to unlocking the full potential of LLMs, enabling them to move beyond mere pattern recognition to become truly intelligent, adaptable, and reliable agents capable of addressing complex, real-world problems.
