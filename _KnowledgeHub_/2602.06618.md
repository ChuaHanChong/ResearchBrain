# Structured Learning for Electromagnetic Field Modeling and Real-Time Inversion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich and the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems developed structured learning models, ActuationNet and PotentialNet, for electromagnetic field modeling in navigation systems. These models preserve current-linearity, enabling real-time, closed-form inversion for high-bandwidth magnetic control while achieving predictive accuracy comparable to analytical methods and maintaining physical consistency.

## Problem

- Existing analytical models like the Multipole Expansion Model (MPEM) are limited by strict physical assumptions (e.g., source symmetry, isolation) and sensitive calibration processes, restricting their use in complex electromagnetic systems.
- Previous data-driven models for electromagnetic navigation systems often sacrifice the algebraic structure crucial for real-time, well-conditioned field inversion, necessitating computationally expensive iterative optimization or non-optimal inverse mapping.
- MPEM-based calibrations frequently suffer from spurious ill-conditioned regions in the workspace, hindering reliable closed-loop control.

## Method

- The researchers introduced **ActuationNet** and **PotentialNet**, structured learning models implemented as MLPs, which learn complex nonlinear spatial mappings while explicitly preserving the ==current-linearity== of the magnetic field on coil currents.
- **PotentialNet** was designed to be physics-informed, predicting scalar potentials whose negative gradients directly yield a curl-free magnetic field, thereby embedding fundamental physical principles into the model's architecture.
- By maintaining current-linearity, these models enable rapid, ==closed-form minimum-norm field inversion== using the ==MoorePenrose pseudoinverse==, allowing for real-time control applications with high bandwidth requirements.

## Results

- The proposed ActuationNet and PotentialNet models achieved predictive accuracy comparable to the established Multipole Expansion Model and other unstructured learning baselines, with all models converging to an RMSE of approximately **0.5 mT** on the OctoMag dataset.
- The structured learning models demonstrated high computational efficiency, with evaluation times for the field actuation matrix ranging from **0.25 to 0.75 ms**, and combined field and gradient evaluation times remaining below **1.5 ms**, suitable for high-frequency real-time magnetic control loops.
- The structured models, particularly PotentialNet, exhibited superior physical consistency by achieving significantly lower divergence and curl magnitude residuals, with PotentialNet demonstrating **zero curl error**, indicating better adherence to Maxwellvs equations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The observed RMSE error floor of approximately 0.5 mT, consistent across all models, is primarily attributed to irreducible geometric uncertainties (sub-millimeter position and degree-scale orientation errors) in the ground-truth data acquisition setup, rather than model capacity limitations.
> - Structured learning models (ActuationNet and PotentialNet) demonstrated greater robustness against training data reduction and showed less overfitting in low-data regimes compared to unstructured learning baselines.
> - Ill-conditioned regions in MPEM actuation maps can be effectively mitigated by training with data sampled closer to the magnetic sources (near-field) and by employing a sufficiently high multipole expansion order for the model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Precise magnetic field modeling is a foundational requirement for the closed-loop control of electromagnetic navigation systems (eMNS), which are employed in applications ranging from telesurgery to targeted drug delivery using micro- and nanorobots. The established standard for this purpose is the analytical Multipole Expansion Model (MPEM). However, the MPEM operates under strict physical assumptions, such as source symmetry and isolation, and its calibration relies on an optimization process that is sensitive to initial conditions. These factors restrict its practical application, particularly in systems with complex or irregular coil geometries.

The broader research landscape for electromagnetic field modeling includes both analytical and data-driven approaches. Analytical models like the MPEM are physics-based but have inherent limitations in complex scenarios. Data-driven methods, such as those utilizing random forests, multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs), or convolutional neural networks, have been applied to eMNS to improve accuracy, especially in non-linear regimes where ferromagnetic cores approach saturation. However, these models frequently sacrifice the algebraic structure that is crucial for real-time, well-conditioned field inversion, often necessitating computationally expensive iterative optimization or the learning of a separate inverse map. Directly learned inverse maps also carry the limitation of not guaranteeing energy-optimal actuation, which is a significant consideration for control strategies.

This work introduces an alternative modeling paradigm that seeks to bridge the gap between analytical rigor and data-driven adaptability. It proposes learning-based models implemented with MLPs designed to preserve the linear dependence of the magnetic field on coil currents. This preservation is key because it enables fast, closed-form minimum-norm inversion, which is vital for high-bandwidth magnetic control. By developing models that maintain this linearity while learning complex, nonlinear spatial mappings, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of both traditional analytical models and previous purely data-driven, black-box approaches. The work positions itself as a benchmark against both the MPEM and other unstructured learning-based regressors (standard MLPs, gradient-boosted linear-tree models) to empirically compare model-based and model-free strategies while specifically addressing and mitigating a known failure mode of the MPEM: spurious ill-conditioned regions in the workspace. The methodology is applied to medical eMNS but is stated to be broadly applicable to other electromagnetic platforms, including MRI field shaping, magnetic particle imaging, magnetic levitation, wireless power transfer, and remote handling in nuclear fusion facilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and evaluate a new paradigm for electromagnetic field modeling in eMNS, leveraging structured learning-based architectures to overcome the limitations of existing methods while enabling real-time control.

The specific key objectives include:

- **Develop Current-Linear Learning Models:** Design and implement multilayer perceptron (MLP) based models (ActuationNet and PotentialNet) that learn nonlinear magnetic field mappings but strictly preserve the linear dependence on coil currents. This preservation is intended to facilitate tractable workspace analysis and rapid, closed-form minimum-norm inversion.
- **Achieve Predictive Fidelity and Data Efficiency:** Demonstrate that these data-driven models can achieve predictive accuracy comparable to the de facto standard Multipole Expansion Model (MPEM) while maintaining similar data efficiency.
- **Enable Real-Time Inversion:** Ensure that the developed models allow for fast, closed-form minimum-norm inversion using the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse, with evaluation times suitable for high-bandwidth magnetic control systems (e.g., approximately 1 ms).
- **Investigate and Mitigate Workspace Ill-Conditioning:** Analyze the phenomenon of spurious ill-conditioned regions in the workspace, frequently reported in MPEM-based calibration, and demonstrate that straightforward design choices (e.g., data distribution, model order) can effectively mitigate this issue.
- **Promote Future Research:** Release the complete codebase, experimental datasets (from OctoMag and Navion systems), and pre-trained models open source to facilitate further research and validation by the scientific community.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges and requirements in remote magnetic manipulation:

- **Limitations of MPEM:** The current standard MPEM is constrained by stringent physical assumptions regarding source symmetry and isolation. Its reliance on optimization-based calibration is also highly sensitive to initialization, which limits its applicability to systems with complex or irregular coil geometries.
- **Demand for Real-time Control:** Many eMNS applications, particularly in medical contexts (e.g., telesurgery), require high-bandwidth control, necessitating field models that are not only accurate but also computationally efficient for both forward prediction and inverse calculation.
- **Preserving Algebraic Structure:** While non-linear learning models can offer global accuracy, they often forfeit the advantageous algebraic structure present in the linear operating regime. This structure (specifically, current-linearity) is crucial for efficient inverse problem solving (via pseudoinverse) and for ensuring energy-optimal actuation in end-to-end control scenarios. Directly learning inverse maps, as some prior works propose, may not guarantee energy optimality.
- **Addressing Physical Consistency:** Unstructured learning models may not inherently respect Maxwell’s equations. The motivation includes exploring structured approaches, like the PotentialNet, that are designed to uphold fundamental physical principles, such as being curl-free.
- **Generalizability:** The need for accurate and efficient electromagnetic field modeling extends beyond medical eMNS to various other engineering and scientific domains, including MRI, magnetic particle imaging, and future applications in nuclear fusion.

In summary, the motivation is to develop robust, efficient, and physically consistent field models that advance the state-of-the-art in eMNS control by leveraging structured learning to overcome the limitations of both traditional analytical models and general black-box learning approaches, particularly in scenarios demanding real-time performance and flexible applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on developing and evaluating structured learning-based models for electromagnetic field mapping, benchmarking them against both an established analytical model (MPEM) and unstructured learning models.

**Data Collection:**
Magnetic field data was collected from two distinct electromagnetic navigation systems:
- **OctoMag:** A research-grade eMNS with eight coils and an approximate 13 cm hemispherical workspace. Data was acquired using a 4x4x4 array of Melexis MLX90395 triaxial high-field magnetometers with 5 mm spacing. The array was mounted in a rigid fixture and tracked by an infrared motion-capture system, yielding a dense dataset with an effective spatial sampling of approximately 1.7 mm. Currents were ramped from -4 A to 4 A in 1 A increments for one coil at a time, while others were held at zero.
- **Navion:** A clinical-grade eMNS with three coils and a human-scale workspace. Data was acquired using a 5x5x5 grid of Allegro A1301 magnetometers with 5 cm spacing. The array was manually positioned and tracked by an infrared motion-capture system. Currents were swept from 0 A to 30 A in 5 A steps, one coil at a time.
For both systems, the collected current ranges were verified to fall within their respective linear operating regimes by fitting affine models to the measured field-current relationships, demonstrating high R² values and a persistent constant field offset.
**Outlier Removal:** The RANSAC algorithm was used for outlier detection. For each position and active coil, an affine model was fitted using RANSAC. Samples with scale-aware relative residuals (defined as `r = ||b - b̂|| / max(||b||, b_T)`) exceeding 20% were classified as outliers. This procedure removed approximately 2.33% of the OctoMag dataset and 3.11% of the Navion dataset.

**Modeling Methods:**
The magnetic fields are governed by quasi-static Maxwell's equations (∇ · b = 0, ∇ × b = 0) in the current-free workspace, which implies the existence of a scalar potential Φ such that b = -∇Φ, satisfying Laplace's equation (∇²Φ = 0).

- **Multipole Expansion Model (MPEM):** This served as the primary physics-based baseline. It models each magnetic source by an azimuthally symmetric scalar potential, allowing for a truncated multipole expansion. The model ensures curl- and divergence-free fields by construction. It considers linearity in currents (b(p) = i ⋅ b̃(p)), and is augmented to include contributions from induced magnetization in ferromagnetic components and a constant field offset. Calibration involves estimating multipole coefficients and source poses through iterative non-linear least squares optimization (Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm).
- **ActuationNet:** A proposed learning-based model implemented as a multilayer perceptron (MLP). It takes a workspace position (p) as input and directly predicts the local field actuation matrix (A_b ∈ R^(3xS)) and the affine offset (b₀ ∈ R^3) as `(A_b, b₀) = f_AN(p)`. The predicted field is then `b = A_b(p)i + b₀(p)`. Tanh activation functions are used in hidden layers.
- **PotentialNet:** Another proposed learning-based model, designed to be physics-informed. This MLP takes a workspace position (p) as input and predicts a set of scalar potentials: one unit-current potential per coil (ϕ ∈ R^S) and a bias potential (ϕ₀ ∈ R). The total predicted scalar potential is `Φ(p, i) = ϕ(p)ᵀi + ϕ₀(p)`. The predicted affine map (A_b(p) and b₀(p)) is derived by taking the negative gradient of these potentials with respect to position (`A_b(p) = -∇ϕ(p)`, `b₀(p) = -∇ϕ₀(p)`). This formulation inherently guarantees a curl-free predicted field, though it does not explicitly enforce divergence-freeness during training. Tanh activation functions are used.
- **Benchmark Mappings (Unstructured):** Two models were used as baselines that directly learn the current-to-field map `b = f(p, i)` without explicitly preserving affinity in currents:
 - **DirectNet:** A standard MLP.
 - **DirectGBT:** A gradient-boosted linear-tree regressor.
 These models were considered benchmarks due to their direct learning approach from single-coil sweep data, which might limit their extrapolation to multi-coil actuation.

**Training:**
All models were trained to minimize the mean-squared error (MSE) between predicted and measured magnetic fields. The full datasets were split into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% test sets based on positional features to ensure that all samples corresponding to a given position remained within one split, preventing data leakage and enabling reliable affine parameter estimation. Neural networks were trained using the ADAM optimizer, with early stopping based on validation RMSE improvement. MPEM training stopped when validation RMSE change was below a threshold. DirectGBT training ceased after a set number of boosting iterations without validation improvement.

**Gradient Modeling:**
Spatial derivatives of the magnetic field (gradients) are crucial for magnetic control (force calculation). The models facilitate gradient estimation:
- MPEM: Gradients are derived analytically.
- Neural Models (ActuationNet, PotentialNet, DirectNet): Gradients are obtained via automatic differentiation due to their continuous and smooth nature.
- DirectGBT: Gradients are piecewise-constant, derived by summing derivatives of active linear leaves.
A reduced 5-element gradient vector `g` (e.g., `[∂b_x/∂x, ∂b_x/∂y, ∂b_x/∂z, ∂b_y/∂y, ∂b_y/∂z]`) is utilized, consistent with Maxwell's equations.

**Evaluation:**
The models were evaluated comprehensively on the OctoMag dataset for:
- **Predictive Accuracy:** Test-set RMSE as a function of model complexity and dataset size.
- **Data Efficiency:** Performance degradation with reduced training data resolution.
- **Generalization Gap:** Difference between test and train RMSE to assess overfitting.
- **RMSE Floor Analysis:** Investigation of the residual error floor, attributing it to geometric uncertainties in the sensor array using a first-order Taylor expansion for field-error covariance.
- **Physical Consistency:** Adherence to Maxwell’s equations (divergence and curl magnitude) for learning-based models.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Average evaluation times for the field actuation matrix (A_b) and combined field and gradient actuation matrices (A_b,g) to assess real-time control suitability.
The Navion dataset was used to specifically investigate and mitigate workspace ill-conditioning in the MPEM, by analyzing the effects of training data distribution (radial segments) and approximation order on the condition number of the field actuation matrix.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the performance, physical consistency, and computational efficiency of the proposed learning-based models, as well as insights into the limitations of the MPEM.

- **Field Prediction Accuracy and Model Complexity:** All evaluated modeling approaches, including the proposed ActuationNet and PotentialNet, the MPEM, and the unstructured DirectNet and DirectGBT baselines, achieved comparable test-set predictive accuracy. They converged to an RMSE of approximately 0.5 mT on the OctoMag dataset. This performance was largely insensitive to increases in model capacity or complexity rank, suggesting that an error floor exists, not primarily driven by model limitations. The generalization gap (RMSE_test - RMSE_train) remained near zero for most models, indicating minimal overfitting with the full dataset.

- **Data Efficiency:** When the models were retrained using progressively smaller subsets of the training data, predictive accuracy and generalization remained stable down to 20% of the full dataset for most models. At lower data regimes (1-5%), the unstructured DirectNet and DirectGBT models exhibited increased signs of overfitting (larger generalization gap). In contrast, the structured learning-based models (ActuationNet and PotentialNet) maintained a smaller generalization gap, demonstrating greater robustness against data reduction. The PotentialNet showed less degradation than the ActuationNet, and the MPEM was the least sensitive to data reduction. This indicates that model structure provides benefits in mitigating overfitting, particularly in low-data scenarios.

- **RMSE Floor and Uncertainty Analysis:** The observed RMSE floor of approximately 0.5 mT, common across all models, was investigated. A geometric sensitivity analysis, modeling isotropic translational and rotational errors in sensor positioning, demonstrated that realistic combinations of sub-millimeter position errors and degree-scale orientation errors in the sensor array could reproduce this 0.5 mT error floor. This finding suggests that the residual predictive error is predominantly attributable to irreducible geometric uncertainties in the ground-truth data acquisition setup, rather than limitations in the models' capacity or fundamental architectural design.

- **Physical Consistency:** The structured learning-based models (ActuationNet and PotentialNet) exhibited substantially lower divergence and curl magnitude residuals when evaluated on the test set, compared to the unstructured DirectNet and DirectGBT baselines. This indicates that the structured models maintain greater physical consistency with Maxwell’s equations. Notably, the PotentialNet, by virtue of its scalar-potential formulation, successfully produced a curl-free magnetic field model, demonstrating zero curl error.

- **Computational Efficiency:** For real-time control applications, evaluation time is a critical factor. The proposed structured learning-based models, ActuationNet and PotentialNet, demonstrated fast evaluation times. Computing the field actuation matrix (A_b) required approximately 0.25 ms to 0.75 ms, depending on model complexity. Joint evaluation of both field and gradient actuation matrices (A_b,g) was also performed rapidly, remaining well below 1.5 ms across all complexity ranks. These times indicate that the models are computationally efficient enough for high-frequency real-time magnetic control loops.

- **Workspace Ill-conditioning in MPEM:** The study addressed the known issue of spurious ill-conditioned regions in the MPEM actuation map. Using the Navion dataset, it was found that:
 - **Training Data Distribution:** MPEMs trained predominantly on near-field samples (closer to the coils) yielded significantly better-conditioned actuation maps compared to those trained on far-field samples. This suggests that near-source sampling is more informative for MPEM calibration.
 - **Approximation Order:** Increasing the MPEM's multipole expansion order (e.g., from dipole to quadrupole to octopole) further reduced the extent of ill-conditioned regions in the workspace. This indicates that limited model expressiveness also contributes to these conditioning artifacts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an advancement in electromagnetic field modeling for navigation systems, offering an alternative to traditional analytical methods with implications for real-time control and broader applications.

- **Enhanced Real-Time Control Capabilities:** The primary significance lies in the development of learning-based field models that preserve current-linearity. This architectural choice is crucial because it enables fast, closed-form minimum-norm inversion via the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. With evaluation times of approximately 1 ms, the proposed ActuationNet and PotentialNet models meet the stringent latency requirements for high-bandwidth feedback loops in real-time magnetic control, which is essential for dynamic manipulation tasks in fields like telesurgery and micro-robotics.

- **Robustness Beyond Idealized Conditions:** Unlike the MPEM, which relies on strict assumptions of source symmetry and magnetic isolation, the data-driven models are agnostic to these constraints. This inherent flexibility makes them suitable for eMNS platforms with complex or irregular coil geometries or in environments where surrounding ferromagnetic materials are present, scenarios where analytical models may perform inadequately. This expands the applicability of such systems to challenging domains like remote maintenance in nuclear fusion facilities.

- **Equivalence to Analytical Models with Added Benefits:** The study demonstrates that the proposed learning-based models achieve predictive fidelity equivalent to the MPEM while maintaining comparable data efficiency. This establishes that data-driven approaches can effectively replace analytical formulations without sacrificing the critical algebraic structure required for control, offering a viable alternative or complement to established methods.

- **Understanding and Mitigating Model Limitations:** The identification of geometric uncertainties in the sensor setup as the dominant factor for the observed RMSE error floor (approximately 0.5 mT) provides a clear understanding of the fundamental limits of predictive accuracy with current measurement technologies. This insight can guide future experimental design, emphasizing the need for improved metrology rather than solely focusing on increasing model complexity. Furthermore, the demonstrated methods to mitigate MPEM-induced ill-conditioning through appropriate data sampling (near-field) and sufficient model order offer practical guidance for improving the robustness of classical calibration approaches.

- **Promotion of Open Science and Future Research:** The open-source release of the complete codebase and high-density datasets from both research-grade (OctoMag) and clinical-grade (Navion) systems provides a valuable resource for the research community. This initiative facilitates reproducibility, encourages comparative studies, and offers a foundation for further development and innovation in magnetic field modeling and control.

- **Broad Applicability:** While focused on medical eMNS, the findings and methodology are relevant to a wider array of electromagnetic platforms. These include applications in MRI field shaping, magnetic particle imaging, magnetic levitation and bearing actuation, and wireless power transfer, where precise and efficient real-time field prediction and inversion are necessary.
