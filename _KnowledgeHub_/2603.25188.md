# AnyID: Ultra-Fidelity Universal Identity-Preserving Video Generation from Any Visual References

> [!summary] Summary
> AnyID introduces a framework for universal identity-preserving video generation that processes multiple free-form visual references, including images and video clips, to achieve high identity fidelity and attribute-level control. The system demonstrates superior identity consistency across diverse facial angles and expressions compared to single-reference methods, alongside enhanced prompt controllability in generated videos.

## Problem

- Relying on a single 2D image for identity-preserving video generation is an ill-posed problem, leading to identity deviation when facial angles or expressions change significantly.
- Existing methods lack precise attribute-level control, making it difficult for users to specify changes to specific elements (e.g., clothing, hairstyle) while preserving others.
- Standard supervised training with pixel-wise loss often yields overly smooth outputs, failing to capture the fine-grained features crucial for human-perceived identity fidelity and prompt controllability.

## Method

- An ==omni-referenced== architecture unifies diverse visual references, including static images and video clips, into a robust identity representation through VAE encoding and latent concatenation.
- A primary-referenced generation paradigm with a differential prompt mechanism allows users to specify only desired attribute changes relative to a canonical anchor reference, maintaining consistency in unmentioned aspects.
- Human-centric reinforcement learning, specifically Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on a preference dataset, refines generation quality to align with human perception of identity fidelity and prompt controllability.

## Results

- AnyID achieved the highest identity fidelity scores, with Holi-Arc at **73.22%** and Holi-Cur at **67.52%**, indicating robust dynamic identity preservation across varying facial expressions and angles.
- The model demonstrated superior prompt controllability (App. **86.56%**, Mot. **60.31%**, Bg. **84.79%**) and visual quality (Sta. **82.03%**, Dyn. **91.12%**) compared to baseline methods.
- User studies confirmed human preference for AnyID, with over **70%** of matchups favoring its identity fidelity and prompt controllability, supported by ablation studies validating each proposed component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using multiple, free-form visual references (images and videos) provides a more comprehensive and robust identity representation than single-reference inputs, mitigating identity deviation.
> - A "primary-referenced generation" paradigm with "differential prompts" effectively allows attribute-level control by specifying only changes from an anchor reference, ensuring consistency for unmentioned elements.
> - Fine-tuning with human-centric reinforcement learning, using preference data for identity fidelity and prompt controllability, aligns generated video quality more closely with human perception than pixel-wise loss alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative modeling has seen substantial progress with the advent of diffusion models, particularly in the creation of visual content like images and videos. Large-scale training leveraging architectures such as the diffusion transformer (DiT) combined with variational autoencoders (VAEs) has enabled the generation of visually coherent media. Despite these advancements, the realism of universal human-centric video generation, which demands consistency in elements such as identity, hairstyle, clothing, and background across synthesized videos, remains a challenge.

Identity-preserving video generation, a specific area within human-centric generation, aims to allow users to produce videos featuring a particular character in various contexts. Earlier methods in this domain, often referred to as tuning-based pipelines, necessitated fine-tuning the model for each new identity introduced by the user. This approach imposed significant usability limitations due to its laborious per-identity requirement.

More recent advancements have shifted towards encoder-based pipelines. These methods typically utilize a single facial image as input, employing a facial encoder (often leveraging visual expert models) to inject facial information into a DiT architecture. The models are then trained on curated datasets to achieve generalized identity modeling, thereby avoiding per-identity tuning.

However, the authors identify a fundamental limitation in this prevalent single-reference paradigm: it constitutes an ill-posed problem for identity modeling. A single 2D image is considered insufficient to capture the multifaceted information defining a person's identity, which includes both their 3D facial structure and characteristic dynamic expressions. This inherent ambiguity can lead to identity deviation or "drift" when the generated facial angles or expressions diverge significantly from the single reference image. Furthermore, this approach restricts creative flexibility by not adequately accommodating diverse real-world input formats (e.g., multiple photos, video clips) that users might possess.

AnyID addresses these limitations by departing from the single-reference paradigm. It proposes a framework that leverages multiple, free-form visual references, including both static images and video clips. This approach aims to provide a more complete and robust identity representation, thereby mitigating the ill-posedness of single-reference methods. By doing so, AnyID seeks to enhance both the fidelity of identity preservation and the precision of attribute-level control in generated videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve ultra-fidelity universal identity-preserving video generation, enabling users to customize videos featuring specific characters with high accuracy and control. The motivation for AnyID stems from several identified limitations in existing identity-preserving video generation methods:

1. **Addressing the Ill-Posed Nature of Single-Reference Inputs:**
 - **Motivation:** Current methods primarily rely on a single identity reference (e.g., a single face image). This single source presents an ill-posed problem because a 2D snapshot cannot fully capture the complex 3D facial structure and dynamic expressive patterns that define an individual's identity. This limitation leads to identity deviation, especially when the generated character exhibits different facial angles or expressions than the reference. Moreover, a single-reference approach restricts creative flexibility by not accommodating diverse real-world visual inputs users might provide (e.g., multiple photos, video clips).
 - **Objective:** To overcome this, AnyID aims to incorporate multiple "free-form" visual references (faces, portraits, videos). The objective is to design a scalable omni-referenced architecture that can effectively unify these heterogeneous identity inputs into a cohesive and comprehensive representation, thereby allowing the model to infer more robust 3D shape and dynamic patterns.

2. **Achieving Precise, Attribute-Level Controllability:**
 - **Motivation:** While identity preservation is crucial, users often desire to modify specific attributes (e.g., clothing, hairstyle, expression) while maintaining others. Conventional prompting strategies, which typically involve absolute descriptions of the target scene, are often laborious for users to craft and struggle to precisely specify incremental changes relative to a reference. This can lead to unintended alterations in unmentioned attributes or difficulty in reconciling contextual inconsistencies (e.g., varying lighting, makeup) across multiple potential references.
 - **Objective:** To enable fine-grained control over attribute variation and invariance, AnyID proposes a "primary-referenced generation paradigm." This paradigm designates one reference as a "canonical anchor" for static attributes. A novel "differential prompt" mechanism is introduced, designed to describe only the changes required from this primary reference, ensuring that unmentioned aspects remain consistent.

3. **Aligning Generation Quality with Human Perception:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard supervised training methods, often relying on pixel-wise Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, tend to produce overly smooth outputs. While effective for low-level reconstruction, MSE loss can degrade the fine-grained features essential for maintaining high identity fidelity and achieving precise prompt controllability as perceived by humans. There is a recognized gap between pixel-level metrics and high-level human perceptual judgment.
 - **Objective:** To bridge this gap and refine the generation quality to better align with human expectations, AnyID aims to incorporate a human-centric reinforcement learning strategy. This involves leveraging a preference dataset constructed from human evaluations, where annotators provide feedback on identity fidelity and prompt controllability, to fine-tune the model.

In essence, AnyID's motivations center on enhancing the robustness, fidelity, and controllability of identity-preserving video generation by adopting a multi-reference input strategy, introducing a differential prompting mechanism, and incorporating human feedback into the training process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AnyID framework integrates two main training stages: supervised training and reinforcement learning (RL) tuning, supported by a multi-reference data curation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The task is defined as synthesizing a video (`x`) that preserves a person’s identity from a set of `N` free-form visual references (`R = {r_i}`) while adhering to a given target prompt (`d`). The references can be of mixed modalities (static images, video clips). The generated video must satisfy both identity preservation and prompt controllability. This formulation directly addresses the perceived ill-posed nature of single-reference video generation.

#### 3.2. Omni-Referenced Identity-Preserving Model

To handle diverse identity references effectively, AnyID introduces a scalable omni-referenced architecture.

- **Unifying Diverse Identity References:**
 - **Input Standardization:** All visual references, including static images treated as single-frame videos, are padded to a uniform resolution (H x W) while preserving their aspect ratios.
 - **VAE Encoding:** Each standardized input `r_i` is independently encoded into latent representations using a single pre-trained VAE encoder `E(·)`. This approach leverages the VAE’s generalization capability, aiming for simplicity and broader compatibility compared to dedicated external encoders.
 - **Latent Concatenation:** These latent codes are concatenated along the temporal dimension to form a unified condition (`y = Concat({E(I(r_i))})`).
 - **Timestep Manipulation:** During training, all reference latents are assigned a static timestep of `t=0` (indicating they are noise-free), while the target latent (`z_t`) receives its corresponding sampled timestep `t > 0`. The composite sequence, along with description embeddings (`c`) and timestep embeddings, is fed into a DiT backbone. The model computes the Rectified Flow (RF) loss (`L_RF`) only for the target output, discarding components corresponding to the zero-timestep references.
- **Aggregating Unconditional Guidance:** For Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) during inference, AnyID establishes a single, unified unconditional state. This is achieved by simultaneously nullifying all modalities: inputting the latent code of pure black pixels (`y_∅`) visually and the embedding of an empty prompt (`c_∅`) semantically. This design aims to avoid extra inference computations compared to performing separate inference passes for multiple conditions.

#### 3.3. Primary-Referenced Generation

To manage attribute variation and invariance, AnyID proposes a primary-referenced generation paradigm with a differential prompt mechanism.

- **Primary Reference (`r_1`):** One reference is designated as the canonical anchor for all static attributes (e.g., hairstyle, clothing). This helps resolve contextual inconsistencies (e.g., lighting, makeup variations) that might exist across multiple references.
- **Differential Prompt:** Unlike conventional prompts, a differential prompt describes only the changes desired from the primary reference to the target content. Attributes not mentioned in the prompt are expected to remain consistent with the primary reference. This strategy aims to minimize descriptive noise and focus the model’s attention on specific changes while maintaining a stable contextual foundation.
- **Differential Prompt Generation Process:** The process involves two stages:
 1. Video content is dissected into seven predefined portrait-video elements (shot type, hairstyle, clothes, accessory, expression, action, background). A Vision Language Model (VLM) generates detailed descriptions for these elements for both the primary reference (`d_ref`) and the target video (`d_tar`).
 2. A Large Language Model (LLM) performs a comparative analysis, quantifying the semantic similarity between corresponding elements in `d_ref` and `d_tar`. Elements with a similarity score exceeding a threshold (`γ`, set to 0.8) are considered unchanged and pruned from `d_tar`. The remaining descriptions form the final differential prompt. This two-stage pipeline aims for robustness, mitigating hallucinations and omissions observed with direct VLM usage.

#### 3.4. Human-Centric Reinforcement Learning

To align generation quality with human perception, AnyID incorporates a reinforcement learning (RL) stage, specifically Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

- **Motivation:** Supervised training with pixel-wise MSE loss can lead to overly smooth outputs and degrade fine-grained features critical for human-perceived identity fidelity and prompt controllability. RL, guided by human feedback, is employed to bridge this gap.
- **Preference Data Collection:** Numerous input sets are extracted from the training dataset, and two videos are sampled for each set using the model trained in the supervised stage. These paired samples are then evaluated by human annotators across two tracks:
 1. **Identity Fidelity:** Annotators, provided with the complete source ID-group clips, select the video that better preserves the person's dynamic identity.
 2. **Prompt Controllability:** Annotators review the primary reference and the differential prompt, selecting the video that better aligns with the prompt's intended transformation while preserving consistency in unmentioned aspects.
- **DPO Fine-tuning:** A pair is considered a valid training sample if one video is unequivocally superior to the other across both evaluation axes (Pareto dominance). A dataset of 1,000 such valid pairs is collected to fine-tune the model using the DPO objective.

#### 3.5. Multi-Reference Data Curation

A meticulously curated dataset is crucial for the model's robustness and high fidelity.

- **Building Meta-Data Pool:** The PortraitGala dataset is curated to construct a large-scale human identity meta-data pool. This involves:
 - Applying face detection to isolate single-subject clips.
 - Filtering out low-quality samples (e.g., blurry faces, extreme head poses).
 - Augmenting annotations with face and person detection outputs (bounding boxes).
 - The resulting meta-set comprises 100k ID-groups and 300k video clips.
- **Constructing Diverse Reference Forms:** To simulate real-world varied inputs, a data augmentation strategy synthesizes diverse reference forms from the curated clips:
 - From one ID-group, `N` reference videos and a target video `x` are sampled.
 - Each of the `N` reference videos is transformed into one of three forms:
 - **Face image reference:** Facial region cropped from a single, randomly selected frame.
 - **Portrait image reference:** A wider or tighter portrait generated by cropping the region around the person from a random frame.
 - **Video reference:** A random video segment is sampled, and its unified person region is cropped.
 This multi-faceted data structure aims to provide the model with versatility in handling heterogeneous reference inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AnyID involved comparisons against several baselines and comprehensive metric assessments, supported by a user study and ablation experiments.

**Implementation Details:**
AnyID is built upon Wan-5B. The generated video resolution is 1280x704 with 121 frames (equivalent to 5 seconds of 720p video). The number of references (N) varied from 1 to 5. Training and inference utilized a null condition probability of 0.1 and a guidance scale of 5.0. Both supervised training and RL tuning were conducted using LoRA, requiring approximately 4,500 NVIDIA A100 GPU hours in total.

**Baselines:**
Comparisons were made against ConsisID, FantasyID (both based on CogvideoX-5B), Phantom, and SkyReels-A2 (both based on Wan-14B). Baselines received only the primary reference, due to their incompatibility with multiple references.

#### 4.1. Benchmark

A benchmark tailored for universal identity-preserving video generation was developed.

- **Evaluation Set:** 50 free-form reference sets of 50 celebrities were collected, each containing 5 visual references showing various facial angles and motions. An LLM generated 50 Identity-Preserving Generation (IPT2V) prompts (preserving only identity) and 50 Identity-Element-Preserving Generation (IEPT2V) prompts (preserving identity along with hairstyle, hairstyle&clothing, or hairstyle&clothing&background).
- **Metrics:** Four dimensions were assessed:
 1. **Identity Fidelity:** Holi-Arc and Holi-Cur (average normalized cosine similarity between generated and *all* reference faces using ArcFace and CurricularFace).
 2. **Element Consistency:** Ele-CLIP and Ele-DINO (normalized cosine similarity between embeddings of Grounded-SAM segmented elements from primary reference and generated video, using CLIP and DINO).
 3. **Prompt Controllability:** VLM scores for alignment between prompt and video in human appearance (App.), camera and human motions (Mot.), and background (Bg.).
 4. **Visual Quality:** VLM scores for static aspects (Sta.) like clarity and color saturation, and dynamic aspects (Dyn.) like smoothness and temporal consistency (following Vbench).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation (Figure 3)

- **IPT2V Task:** Baselines, when limited to a single facial reference, exhibited limitations characteristic of the ill-posed problem. ConsisID, FantasyID, and Phantom showed inferior dynamic fidelity, with identity deviation visible during head rotations or changes in facial expression. SkyReels-A2 demonstrated a significant bias towards the reference image, often disregarding prompt instructions and introducing visual distortion. In contrast, AnyID maintained superior identity fidelity across varying facial angles and motions, attributed to the rich multi-view information provided by free-form references.
- **IEPT2V Task:** Phantom continued to show identity distortion. SkyReels-A2 exhibited persistent bias, with "copy-paste" artifacts for clothing and sometimes backgrounds. AnyID, however, achieved high facial fidelity while balancing element consistency and prompt controllability. For example, it could preserve identity, hairstyle, and clothing, and render natural visual effects aligned with described environments and lighting, indicating effective modeling of identity invariance and alignment with human preferences due to supervised training and RL tuning.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Evaluation (Table 1, Figure 5)

- **Metric Results (Table 1):**
 - **Identity Fidelity:** AnyID achieved the highest scores on Holi-Arc (73.22%) and Holi-Cur (67.52%), indicating robust dynamic identity fidelity.
 - **Element Consistency:** SkyReels-A2 scored highest (78.14 Ele-CLIP, 79.87 Ele-DINO), which the authors attribute to its "copy-paste" behavior rather than genuine consistency modeling. AnyID showed competitive performance (68.78 Ele-CLIP, 69.50 Ele-DINO).
 - **Prompt Controllability:** AnyID outperformed baselines with the highest scores for App. (86.56%), Mot. (60.31%), and Bg. (84.79%).
 - **Visual Quality:** AnyID led in visual quality metrics with 82.03% Sta. and 91.12% Dyn.
- **User Study (Figure 5):** A user study involving 20 participants conducting binary voting across 40 matchups confirmed AnyID's strong performance. AnyID won over 70% of matchups for identity fidelity and prompt controllability, and over 60% for element consistency and visual quality, demonstrating alignment with human preferences. The higher element consistency score in the user study, compared to some objective metrics, was attributed to participants' subjective inclusion of "naturalness" in their judgments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 1, Figures 4, 7, 8, 9)

Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of AnyID’s proposed components:

- **AnyID w/o P&D (Primary-Reference and Differential Prompt):** Without this design, the model struggled with hairstyle and clothing consistency, relying on weaker prompt constraints. The full model, using P&D, successfully anchored to the primary reference for attribute preservation and showed higher prompt controllability metrics.
- **AnyID w/o RL (Reinforcement Learning):** The model without RL tuning exhibited minor flaws, such as inconsistencies in element details or less satisfactory facial fidelity. The full model, benefiting from RL, demonstrated stable identity fidelity, element consistency, and prompt controllability, especially in refining subtle aspects like hair shine, texture, and facial shading. This was reflected in slightly lower scores across most metrics for the w/o RL model.
- **AnyID w/o AR (Auxiliary References):** The absence of auxiliary references during inference resulted in lower identity fidelity (lower Holi-Arc and Holi-Cur scores). Qualitative examples illustrated that a single-angle reference lacked sufficient prior knowledge for faithful reconstruction of facial markers or dynamic features, leading to inferior fidelity compared to the model with multiple references.

These ablation results validate that the omni-referenced architecture, primary-referenced generation with differential prompting, and human-centric reinforcement learning are integral to AnyID's performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnyID represents an advancement in the field of identity-preserving video generation by systematically addressing key limitations of existing methods. Its significance lies in several core contributions:

1. **Overcoming Single-Reference Limitations:** By introducing a scalable omni-referenced architecture, AnyID effectively unifies heterogeneous identity inputs (faces, portraits, videos). This approach mitigates the ill-posed problem inherent in single-reference methods, enabling the model to learn a more comprehensive and robust representation of an individual's 3D structure and dynamic expressions. This capacity leads to higher identity fidelity across varied facial angles and motions.
2. **Enhancing Controllability:** The proposed primary-referenced generation paradigm, coupled with a novel differential prompt, provides a mechanism for precise, attribute-level control. This allows users to specify desired changes while ensuring unmentioned attributes remain consistent with a designated primary reference, which is a notable improvement over conventional prompting strategies.
3. **Aligning with Human Perception:** The integration of human-centric reinforcement learning, based on preferences for identity fidelity and prompt controllability, allows the model's outputs to better align with high-level human judgments, moving beyond limitations of pixel-wise loss functions.

The potential impact of AnyID is considerable across various domains:

- **Creative Industries:** It offers powerful tools for creative expression, enabling users to customize videos featuring specific characters for narrative continuation, short film creation, or long movie generation. This capability could lower barriers to professional-grade video production, potentially fostering inclusivity in digital storytelling.
- **Personalized Media:** Applications include creating virtual avatars, personalized storytelling, and generating adaptive avatars for immersive user experiences in metaverse and AR/VR environments.
- **Efficiency for Developers and Creators:** The framework empowers creators, educators, filmmakers, and developers by simplifying the process of generating highly consistent, controllable, and expressive visual content from diverse visual references.

**Societal Impacts and Safeguards:**
The authors acknowledge the dual nature of such powerful generative AI technologies. While AnyID offers significant benefits for creative empowerment, it also carries substantial societal risks. The potential for misuse includes generating convincing deepfakes, non-consensual impersonations, or deceptive media, posing threats to individual privacy, public trust, and information integrity. Additionally, the automation of high-quality video production might disrupt traditional creative workflows.

To mitigate these concerns, AnyID incorporates robust safeguards inherited from the Wan foundation model suite, including built-in content moderation filters and output watermarking for provenance tracking. The authors also commit to principles of responsible AI, pledging to publicly release code, models, and datasets under licenses that prohibit misuse and require attribution, aiming to balance innovation with ethical stewardship.
