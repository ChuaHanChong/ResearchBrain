# Fail-RAG : A Retrieval Augmented Generation Informed Framework for Robot Failure Identification

> [!summary] Summary
> Fail-RAG detects robot operation failures without fine-tuning any model. Time-sequenced camera frames are compiled into one image, encoded with CLIP, and matched by cosine similarity against a small database of labeled failure scenarios; the retrieved cases plus a structured JSON template condition an off-the-shelf VLM to output failure type, status and cause. It averages 25 percentage points higher detection accuracy than the bare VLM across five warehouse operations.

## Problem

- Rule-based and specialized-hardware anomaly detectors break as tasks and environments change, and scale poorly to general-purpose robots covering many operations and edge cases.
- Most recent failure detectors fine-tune VLMs or VLAs, which requires collecting vision-action failure datasets and heavy compute for billion-parameter models.
- Training-free alternatives detect only categorical failures via outliers or behavior trees, discarding the semantic reasoning needed to explain causes and guide human intervention.

## Method

- ==RAG failure memory==: time-sequenced frame collages paired with ground-truth scenarios are encoded as 512-dimensional ==CLIP ViT-B-32== vectors and retrieved at runtime by ==cosine similarity==, ==L2==, or ==dot product==.
- ==Reinforced instruction structuring==: a per-task JSON template constrains outputs to failure object, status limited to normal/anomalous/unknown, sub-failure, and free-text cause, giving API-integrable responses.
- ==View-planner== frame compilation stacks 5 to 25 video frames into a single query image at sampling rates from 0.05 to 2.5 Hz, keeping inference lightweight on an off-the-shelf **Qwen2.5-VL 32B**.

## Results

- **Fail-RAG** beats the vanilla VLM by roughly **25%** accuracy on average across palletizing, MoMa transport, MoMa traverse, depalletizing and assembly, with some cases gaining up to **40%**.
- Retrieval-only top-1 scores show L2 and cosine similarity perform well across sampling frequencies, while MoMa transport retrieves poorly under every metric and frame rate.
- Mean intra-database cosine distance correlates positively with accuracy: databases near **0.03** sit around **60%** while those near **0.14** approach **100%**, confirming the embedding-distinctness hypothesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Retrieval over a small labeled failure memory can substitute for fine-tuning a VLM, making new edge cases a data-insert rather than a training run.
> - Retrieval quality is bounded by how separable the stored embeddings are, so curate reference sets for mutual distinctness rather than sheer volume.
> - Constraining a VLM's answer to a fixed status vocabulary inside a JSON template raises absolute accuracy and yields a directly measurable, API-consumable output.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of industrial automation is undergoing significant transformation, driven by advancements in robotics, the emergence of generalist robots and embodied AI, and increasing labor shortages in manufacturing. As robots are deployed in increasingly complex and unstructured environments for tasks such as material handling, logistics, and assembly, the need for robust and adaptable failure detection mechanisms has become critical. This work addresses the challenge of identifying unexpected events, or failures, in robot operations, particularly within warehouse settings.

Traditionally, robot failure and anomaly identification have relied on rule-based detection methods or specialized hardware. While effective in controlled environments, these methods face significant limitations in dynamic environments where failure modes can evolve. Rule-based systems are often brittle and challenging to scale for general-purpose robots due to the combinatorial explosion of potential edge cases and the complexity of hardware integration.

Recent advancements in generative models, specifically Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have introduced new avenues for anomaly detection. VLMs, which process both visual and textual information, offer a promising solution for monitoring robot operations primarily through camera vision. This approach provides flexibility and scalability across various robotic tasks without requiring task-specific sensors or extensive algorithm development. Furthermore, VLMs generate human-interprehensible natural language outputs, which can bridge the understanding gap between robots and human operators regarding complex reasoning.

The existing literature on VLM-based failure detection can be broadly categorized:
- **Difference-driven identification:** Some approaches focus on detecting discrepancies between observed visual information and a baseline or expected state. Examples include identifying "Semantic Misalignment Failures" by comparing robot actions to instructions using post-trained VLMs with classification heads, training VLMs on fine-grained failure data, or constructing scene graphs from multi-view images to compare against expected scene graphs generated during planning. These methods often involve detecting deviations from a predefined normal state.
- **Failure identification and recovery:** A significant body of work focuses not only on identifying failures but also on enabling subsequent recovery. These frameworks often involve fine-tuning language models or VLMs on extensive failure datasets to detect failures and, in some cases, generate executable recovery actions. While powerful, these methods typically require considerable computational resources for model fine-tuning (potentially involving billions of parameters) and depend on the availability of large datasets comprising vision-action failure scenarios.
- **Fine-tuning-free approaches:** A smaller subset of research explores failure identification without relying on extensive datasets or model fine-tuning. These methods might classify anomalies into robot-driven and environment-driven categories using a mixture-of-experts framework combining VLMs and Gaussian-mixture models, or directly employ VLMs for monitoring during task execution and generating skills for recovery via behavior trees. However, these works have sometimes been limited in their capacity to identify only categorical failures (e.g., outliers) and may not fully leverage the semantic reasoning capabilities of VLMs to provide detailed insights into the causes of failures.

This work, Fail-RAG, is positioned to address two specific gaps in the current landscape: first, the need for a failure identification framework that does not require extensive fine-tuning of large models, thereby making it easier to adopt and less computationally intensive; and second, to fully exploit the semantic reasoning capabilities of VLMs to not only identify failures but also provide actionable insights into their underlying causes. By utilizing a Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) approach, Fail-RAG aims to provide an adaptable, real-time, and insightful solution for robot failure detection, contrasting with fine-tuning-heavy or purely categorical detection methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the evolving landscape of industrial automation, characterized by the increasing deployment of robots in more complex, dynamic, and often unstructured environments, particularly in applications like warehouse material handling. As robots transition towards generalist capabilities and embodied AI, their ability to autonomously operate and react to unexpected events becomes paramount. Traditional failure detection methods, such as rule-based systems or those relying on specialized hardware, face limitations in these dynamic settings. The form of failures can change unpredictably due to environmental and task variability, rendering static rule sets ineffective and costly to maintain or scale.

The development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) presents an opportunity to overcome these challenges by enabling robot monitoring primarily through camera vision. VLMs offer a flexible solution that can be applied across a wide variety of robotic tasks without requiring extensive task-specific sensor installations or the development of specialized algorithms. A key advantage of VLMs is their ability to generate human-comprehensible output in natural language, which can facilitate human understanding of robot reasoning and enhance human-robot collaboration.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this research were formulated:

- **To develop a novel framework for robot failure identification:** The overarching goal was to create an intelligent system capable of detecting operation-related failures in robots, specifically in warehouse automation contexts. This framework, named Fail-RAG, was conceived as a Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)-based system.
- **To mitigate the need for extensive model fine-tuning:** A significant objective was to address the computational burden and data requirements associated with fine-tuning large VLMs for new failure scenarios. Fail-RAG aimed to provide a fine-tuning-free approach, making it more computationally efficient and easier to integrate into existing robot operation workflows without requiring extensive vision-action failure datasets.
- **To leverage semantic reasoning capabilities of VLMs:** Beyond mere detection, a core objective was to utilize the advanced capabilities of VLMs to provide semantic reasoning and detailed insights into the potential causes of identified failures. This would enable operators to understand "why" a failure occurred, thereby facilitating more effective intervention and correction, particularly in scenarios involving human-robot collaboration.
- **To evaluate the framework's performance in real-world relevant scenarios:** The research sought to rigorously evaluate Fail-RAG's effectiveness using both simulation and physical experiments across multiple robot platforms (fixed robot arms and mobile manipulators) and common warehouse automation tasks. This evaluation included assessing accuracy compared to off-the-shelf VLMs.
- **To investigate the impact of RAG formulation parameters:** The study aimed to conduct a statistical analysis of how different parametric variations within the RAG formulation, such as distance metrics and data collection procedures (e.g., varying frame rates), influence the framework's performance.
- **To gain insights into the role of vector embeddings:** A specific objective was to provide deeper insights into how the nature of vector embeddings (e.g., their distinctness in the embedding space) influences the retrieval performance and the overall VLM performance within the RAG framework.

These objectives collectively aimed to develop a more efficient, adaptable, and insightful approach to robot failure detection that addresses the practical constraints and evolving requirements of modern industrial robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Fail-RAG, integrates Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to identify and analyze robot operational failures. The methodology involves several interconnected components designed to process multi-modal data efficiently and provide detailed failure diagnostics.

**A. Overall Framework Architecture:**
The core idea of Fail-RAG is illustrated in its overview (Figure 2). An inspection camera streams video, which is then processed by an image processing layer. This layer compiles video frames into a single time-sequence image. This compiled image is fed into an off-the-shelf VLM, along with a task-specific prompt and a structured answer template. The answer template is typically in a JSON or YAML format, facilitating seamless integration with Application Programming Interfaces (APIs).

**B. Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) Embeddings:**
To circumvent the need for extensive fine-tuning of VLMs on failure scenarios, Fail-RAG employs a RAG-based approach. Failure scenarios, consisting of pairs of time-sequenced images and their corresponding ground truth scenarios (textual descriptions), are embedded and stored in a memory stack.
- **Encoder:** The Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training (CLIP) encoder (specifically, ViT-B-32) is utilized to generate multi-modal embeddings for both images and text. CLIP is chosen for its efficiency in processing multi-modal data.
- **Retrieval Process:** In real-time operation, an incoming image sequence (query image) is encoded into a CLIP embedding. This query embedding is then used to retrieve relevant information from the pre-compiled memory stack by comparing it with the stored failure scenario embeddings.
- **Distance Metrics for Retrieval:** The performance of RAG can vary significantly based on the metric used to measure the distance or similarity between embeddings. Three common metrics were investigated:
 - **Cosine Similarity:** Calculates the cosine of the angle between two vectors. It focuses on the orientation of vectors, disregarding their magnitude (1 - cos(θ), where 0 indicates identical, 1 indicates orthogonal).
 - **L2 Distance (Euclidean Distance):** Measures the straight-line distance between two vectors in Euclidean space (||u1 - u2||).
 - **Dot Product:** Computes the scalar product of two vectors, which accounts for both their magnitudes and the angle between them (u1 · u2).

**C. Instruction Structuring for VLM Query:**
A reinforced instructional structuring method is adopted to guide the VLM's output and ensure consistency. This involves creating a standardized JSON template for each robotic task. This template specifies four primary attributes:
1. **Types of failures:** Categorical identification of failure objects relevant to the operation (e.g., robot-arm, loading pallet, flipping-station for palletization).
2. **Deterministic status:** A constrained status for each failure object, limited to ‘normal’, ‘anomalous’, or ‘unknown’. This constraint simplifies VLM responses, improving performance and providing a quantifiable metric.
3. **Sub-failure:** Specific attributes or components associated with a failure type that help pinpoint the cause (e.g., 'suction-gripper failure' or 'joint-motion limitations' for a 'robot-arm' failure).
4. **Reason for sub-failure:** An unconstrained natural language explanation that provides detailed debugging information for operators or inspectors.
This structured approach enables the VLM to provide concise, relevant, and machine-readable outputs that can be integrated into existing API tools.

**D. VLM Models:**
The open-source Qwen family of models, specifically `qwen2.5vl:32b`, was selected for VLM inference. This choice was motivated by their availability in different parameter sizes, open-source nature, adaptability to various APIs (e.g., Ollama), and the study's focus on benchmarking the improvements brought by RAG augmentation rather than the standalone performance of different VLMs.

**E. RAG Dataset Composition:**
A comprehensive RAG dataset was curated for five different robot operations, encompassing both simulation and physical experiments:
- **Operations:**
 1. **Palletizing (UR5 fixed arm):** Simulates placing boxes on a pallet, including a flipping station. Failures include irregular box size, inaccurate L-shape station location.
 2. **MoMa - Transport (Mobile Manipulator):** Transports goods in a warehouse. Failures involve unstable goods due to acceleration/deceleration.
 3. **Depalletizing (Fanuc M-20iB):** Removes packages from a pallet to a conveyor. Failures include picking failures or loose grasping due to soft plastic packaging.
 4. **MoMa - Traverse (Mobile Manipulator):** Navigates between points. Failures include collisions due to un-updated minor layout changes.
 5. **Assembly (UR5 fixed arm):** Assembles a mechanical kit. Failures involve assembly errors due to inaccurate workstation setup.
- **Data Capture:** For each operation, several failure scenarios were defined. Data was captured at five distinct sampling frequencies ranging from 0.05 Hz to 2.5 Hz to assess the trade-off between real-time computational load and information content.
- **Dataset Instances:** For every unique combination of {operation, failure scenario, sampling frequency}, five distinct image instances were captured to enhance generalization.
- **Validation Split:** Performance validation was conducted on three images per scenario that were not included in the RAG dataset, resulting in an approximate 60-40 training-validation split.

**F. Experiment Design:**
The experimental evaluation proceeded in two stages:
1. **Retrieval-only performance:** This stage assessed the effectiveness of the chosen RAG distance metrics (cosine similarity, L2, dot product) in purely retrieving the correct failure modes from the RAG database, given a query image. This measures the mathematical accuracy of the embedding and retrieval process.
2. **VLM-RAG vs. Pure VLM performance:** This stage compared the overall accuracy of the Fail-RAG framework (VLM augmented with RAG) against a baseline VLM (without RAG augmentation) in identifying failures and their types. The accuracy was measured by the VLM's ability to correctly characterize sub-failures (e.g., robot arm, payload) as 'normal' or 'anomalous' based on its structured response.

This multi-faceted methodology was designed to systematically evaluate the Fail-RAG framework's ability to enhance robot failure identification through efficient retrieval and semantic reasoning, while also providing insights into the underlying mechanisms driving its performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Fail-RAG encompassed both the performance of its retrieval component and the overall VLM-augmented system, comparing it against a baseline VLM. An analysis of the vector embeddings was also conducted to provide deeper insights into the framework's behavior.

**A. Retrieval-only Performance:**
The first phase of evaluation focused on the ability of the RAG component to retrieve relevant failure information based on image embeddings, without VLM interpretation.
- **Metric Comparison:** The study investigated three distance metrics: cosine similarity, L2 distance, and dot product. The results indicated that the choice of metric did not consistently lead to a singular best performer across all operations. Instead, performance varied depending on the specific combination of the distance metric and the robot operation.
- **Sampling Frequency Impact:** No uniform trend was observed regarding the impact of image sampling frequency on retrieval performance across all operations.
- **General Performance Trends:** L2 distance and cosine similarity generally demonstrated robust performance across different sampling frequencies and operations.
- **Outlier Operation:** The "MoMa - Transport" operation consistently exhibited lower retrieval performance across all tested distance metrics and sampling frequencies compared to other operations.
- **Chosen Metric:** Based on these evaluations, cosine distance was selected as the preferred method for the RAG framework. This choice was justified by its equivalent performance for both image and text vector embeddings, aligning with the multi-modal nature of the framework.

**B. VLM-RAG Performance vs. Pure VLM:**
This segment evaluated the comprehensive Fail-RAG framework, which integrates the RAG component with VLM reasoning, against an off-the-shelf VLM (baseline) that operated without RAG augmentation. The metric used was "Failure Identification Accuracy," defined as the correct characterization of various operational indicators (e.g., robot arm, payload, overall operation) as "normal" or "anomalous" by the VLM.
- **Overall Accuracy Improvement:** Fail-RAG demonstrated an average increase of approximately 25 percentage points in failure detection accuracy across the five tested robot operations when compared to the baseline VLM. Some specific scenarios exhibited an accuracy improvement of up to 40 percentage points.
- **Consistent Outperformance:** Fail-RAG consistently showed superior performance compared to the vanilla VLM for nearly all operations and frame rates, with only a single outlier scenario for "MoMa - Transport" where it did not significantly outperform the baseline.
- **Impact of Physical vs. Simulation Environments:** The performance improvements of Fail-RAG were more pronounced in physical experiments (Depalletizing and Assembly) compared to simulation scenarios. The researchers suggested that real-world factors such as variations in shadows and lighting might make the RAG context more critical and beneficial for physical experiments.
- **No Universal Frame Rate Trend:** Similar to retrieval performance, there was no noticeable universal pattern indicating that increasing or decreasing the frame rate consistently improved Fail-RAG's performance across all operations. While individual operations had optimal frame rates (e.g., 15 frames for Palletizing, 5 frames for MoMa-Transport), a general trend was not identified.

**C. Analysis of Vector Embeddings and Performance:**
To further understand the variability in Fail-RAG's performance, an analysis was conducted on the internal mechanics of the RAG framework, specifically focusing on the properties of the vector embeddings.
- **Hypothesis:** The researchers hypothesized that the RAG performance is influenced by the distinctness of the vector embeddings for each failure scenario within the memory stack. If embeddings for different failure scenarios are highly similar, it could lead to confusion during retrieval.
- **Cosine Distance Analysis:** The study measured the mean cosine distance between all pairs of vector embeddings within each specific RAG database (corresponding to a unique operation-image sequence combination). Higher mean cosine distance indicates that the failure scenario embeddings are more distinct from one another in the embedding space.
- **Correlation with Performance:** A general trend was observed where higher mean cosine distance among the RAG database vectors correlated with improved Fail-RAG performance. This suggests that when failure scenarios are encoded distinctly in the embedding space, the retrieval mechanism can more accurately identify the relevant context, leading to better VLM reasoning and higher failure identification accuracy. While some outliers to this trend were noted, the overall alignment supported the hypothesis.

These findings suggest that Fail-RAG provides a method for enhancing robot failure identification by strategically leveraging external knowledge through RAG, leading to substantial accuracy improvements over direct VLM application, particularly in varied and real-world conditions. The analysis also highlights the importance of the internal structure and distinctness of the vector embeddings within the RAG database for effective performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Fail-RAG framework represents a notable contribution to the field of robot failure identification, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

**A. Addressing Computational and Data Bottlenecks:**
A primary significance of Fail-RAG lies in its design as a fine-tuning-free framework. Unlike many contemporary approaches that require extensive datasets of vision-action failure scenarios and significant computational resources for fine-tuning large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Fail-RAG operates by augmenting an off-the-shelf VLM with a Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) mechanism. This design choice substantially reduces the computational overhead and data dependency, making the framework more accessible and practical for integration into existing robot operation workflows, particularly for companies with limited computational infrastructure or access to vast failure datasets.

**B. Enhanced Semantic Reasoning and Actionable Insights:**
Fail-RAG leverages the powerful language understanding capabilities of VLMs to not only identify the occurrence of a failure but also to provide semantic reasoning regarding its potential causes. By using a structured instruction template, the framework generates human-comprehensible natural language explanations for sub-failures. This capability is critical for human operators and inspectors, as it moves beyond simple binary failure detection to offer diagnostic insights. Such detailed reasoning can significantly streamline the debugging process, enable more targeted interventions, and improve overall operational efficiency and safety in human-robot collaborative environments.

**C. Improved Reliability and Accuracy in Diverse Environments:**
The demonstrated improvement of approximately 25 percentage points in failure detection accuracy over baseline VLMs, with gains up to 40% in some scenarios, indicates the practical effectiveness of Fail-RAG. This enhanced accuracy, particularly in physical experimental setups where real-world complexities like lighting and shadows are prevalent, underscores the framework's robustness. This can lead to more reliable autonomous operations, reducing downtime and preventing potential damage or safety hazards in industrial settings such as warehouses and manufacturing plants.

**D. Generalizability Beyond Robot Failure Identification:**
The core principles of Fail-RAG, which involve using multi-modal embeddings, retrieval mechanisms, and structured VLM prompting, are not exclusive to robot failure detection. The insights gained regarding the importance of vector embedding distinctness and the efficacy of RAG in enhancing VLM performance suggest that this framework can potentially be adopted for a broader range of vision-based monitoring, inspection, and anomaly detection tasks across various domains. This offers a generalizable methodology for intelligent visual analysis where context-specific knowledge needs to be dynamically integrated with powerful generative models.

**E. Foundations for Future Research and Development:**
The work opens several avenues for future research. The observation that embedding distinctness influences performance encourages further investigation into mathematical modeling and formalization of this relationship, which could lead to more efficient and targeted RAG dataset curation strategies. Additionally, the challenge of minimizing failure identification delay, which is inherent in processing time-sequence images, points towards future work in latent space analysis, novel operational views for early prediction, or more advanced real-time processing techniques.

In summary, Fail-RAG offers an innovative, computationally efficient, and highly effective approach to robot failure identification. By combining the strengths of Retrieval Augmented Generation with Vision-Language Models, it not only improves detection accuracy but also provides crucial semantic insights, thereby advancing the state of the art in reliable and intelligent robot automation.
