# Detection and Mitigation of Hallucination in Large Reasoning Models: A Mechanistic Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Renmin University of China investigated reasoning hallucinations in large reasoning models (LRMs) from a mechanistic perspective, proposing a "Reasoning Score" to quantify step-level reasoning depth. They identified three distinct internal patterns of hallucination and developed a detection framework that outperformed baselines, alongside a mitigation strategy that improved reasoning accuracy and generalization.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate 'reasoning hallucinations,' which are logically coherent but factually incorrect multi-step reasoning traces.
- Traditional hallucination detection methods are insufficient for these complex errors, as reasoning hallucinations are embedded within structured processes, making them deceptive and difficult to identify.
- A limited understanding of the underlying internal mechanisms of reasoning hallucinations hinders the development of robust detection and mitigation strategies.

## Method

- A "==Reasoning Score==" was developed to quantify the depth of reasoning at each step of an LRM's output, utilizing Jensen-Shannon divergence of vocabulary distributions from late transformer layers.
- An empirical study using the Reasoning Score on the ReTruthQA dataset identified three patterns of reasoning hallucination: early-stage fluctuations, incorrect backtracking, and spurious verification (overthinking with high perplexity).
- A Reasoning Hallucination Detection (RHD) framework was designed using these patterns, and a mitigation method, GRPO-R, was proposed, incorporating step-level deep reasoning rewards into a reinforcement learning framework.

## Results

- The Reasoning Score was validated to differentiate shallow processing from deep reasoning, with misled reasoning steps consistently showing lower scores.
- The RHD framework consistently outperformed various baseline methods in detecting reasoning hallucinations across diverse tasks, achieving higher AUC and improved multi-trace ranking.
- The GRPO-R mitigation strategy improved LRM reasoning accuracy on both in-domain (MATH500, AIME 2024) and out-of-distribution (GPQA) benchmarks, enhancing model reliability and generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Reasoning Score effectively differentiates between shallow pattern-matching and deeper reasoning engagement by observing stability or significant changes in late-layer internal representations.
> - Reasoning hallucinations are characterized by quantifiable internal model behaviors, including unstable reasoning trajectories, misguided attention to erroneous earlier steps, and 'spurious verification' where perceived deep reasoning steps coincide with internal instability.
> - Integrating step-level reasoning rewards into LRM training paradigms can encourage more meaningful intermediate reasoning, thereby improving factual accuracy and the reliability of the overall reasoning process, suggesting a path to more robust models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has long grappled with the challenge of hallucination, where models produce fluent and coherent outputs that are semantically inaccurate or lack factual grounding. With the emergence of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI’s O-series, AI systems have demonstrated advanced capabilities in multi-step reasoning tasks. These models often generate explicit multi-step reasoning traces prior to producing a final answer.

However, alongside these advancements, a more complex form of error, termed "Reasoning Hallucination," has gained prominence. This phenomenon occurs when LRMs generate logically coherent reasoning traces that appear plausible but are factually incorrect, leading to erroneous conclusions. Unlike traditional hallucinations, reasoning hallucinations are embedded within structured reasoning processes, making them more deceptive and challenging to detect. This deceptive nature can lead users to misplaced trust, analogous to the conjunction fallacy, where detailed but misleading explanations are perceived as more credible.

Previous research primarily focused on assessing the correctness of reasoning paths in standard Chain-of-Thought (CoT) tasks, often for relatively simple problems, with limited investigation into the underlying mechanisms of hallucinations in LRMs. While recent work has extended evaluation to longer CoT generated by LRMs, the focus has largely remained on error identification rather rather than uncovering the causal factors. Furthermore, directly analyzing model-generated traces can be problematic due to the subtle nature of these hallucinations, and the rise of Latent CoT, where reasoning is embedded in hidden states, further complicates detection. These challenges highlight a need for probing the internal mechanisms that contribute to reasoning hallucinations to enable more interpretable and robust detection and mitigation strategies.

The present work draws inspiration from mechanistic interpretability studies and cognitive science, which suggest that incorrect answers often arise when models rely on shallow pattern-matching rather than genuine deep reasoning. Prior research has also indicated a functional division within transformer-based models, where early layers are primarily involved in information transmission, while later layers execute more complex reasoning over aggregated context. This layered functional specialization provides a basis for investigating the internal thinking patterns of LRMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate reasoning hallucinations in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) from a mechanistic perspective. This investigation aims to develop a deeper understanding of these errors, which can then inform the creation of robust detection and mitigation strategies.

The specific objectives and motivations for this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Quantify Reasoning Depth:** A central challenge in understanding reasoning hallucinations is to determine whether a model is engaging in superficial pattern-matching or performing genuine, deep reasoning at each step. The motivation is to develop a quantifiable metric that can differentiate these internal thinking patterns. This metric is intended to serve as a proxy for the "depth" of a model's reasoning at a granular, step-by-step level.

2. **Analyze Reasoning Hallucination Patterns:** Building on the proposed reasoning depth metric, a key objective is to conduct an empirical study to identify specific, recurring patterns associated with reasoning hallucinations. The motivation is to move beyond simply identifying incorrect reasoning traces and instead characterize the internal model behaviors—such as unstable reasoning trajectories or misdirected attention—that contribute to the emergence of hallucinations. This analysis seeks to understand the "why" behind these errors.

3. **Develop a Reasoning Hallucination Detection (RHD) Framework:** Based on the insights gained from the mechanistic analysis and identified hallucination patterns, an objective is to design and implement a framework capable of effectively detecting reasoning hallucinations. The motivation for this is to provide a practical tool that leverages internal model signals rather than relying solely on external critique or uncertainty estimates, which may struggle with the deceptive coherence of reasoning hallucinations.

4. **Mitigate Reasoning Hallucinations:** Recognizing that current outcome-based reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms for training LRMs may inadvertently foster hallucination-prone behaviors (e.g., shallow pattern-matching or overthinking), a further objective is to propose and evaluate a mitigation strategy. The motivation is to enhance LRM training by incorporating step-level supervision that encourages deep and meaningful reasoning while penalizing flawed intermediate steps. This aims to improve the overall quality, reliability, and generalization capabilities of LRMs.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the need to address the safety and trustworthiness implications of reasoning hallucinations in advanced AI systems. By understanding the internal mechanisms, the researchers aim to develop more interpretable, robust, and generalizable solutions for controlling and preventing these pervasive and challenging errors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved a multi-stage approach encompassing the development of a reasoning depth metric, empirical analysis of hallucination patterns, and the subsequent design of detection and mitigation frameworks.

**1. Reasoning Score: Measuring Reasoning Depth**
The researchers proposed a "Reasoning Score" to quantify the depth of reasoning at each step of an LRM's output. This score is inspired by mechanistic interpretability, which suggests that deeper reasoning processes involve significant transformations in the representations of later layers in a transformer model.
- **Calculation:** The Reasoning Score for a step $c_k$ is defined as the mean Jensen–Shannon divergence (JSD) between vocabulary distributions derived from the hidden states of selected late transformer layers and the final layer's anchor distribution.
- **LogitLens:** The LogitLens technique was used to project layer-normalized hidden states into the vocabulary space, enabling a layer-wise interpretation of token prediction.
- **Interpretation:** A higher Reasoning Score indicates substantial changes in output distributions within late layers, suggesting active, deep reasoning. A lower score implies distributional stability, consistent with shallow pattern-matching or heuristic-based processing.
- **Validation:** The Reasoning Score was validated using a re-implemented GSM-NoOp dataset. Reasoning steps misled by semantically irrelevant "No-Op" phrases consistently exhibited lower Reasoning Scores, supporting its correlation with shallow pattern-matching behavior.

**2. Empirical Study of Reasoning Hallucination Patterns**
An empirical study was conducted on the ReTruthQA dataset, spanning Math, Science, and MultiHopQA domains, using the Reasoning Score to analyze LRM R1-7B's thinking patterns in relation to hallucinations.
- **Case Analysis (GSM-NoOp):** Initial analysis of hallucinated vs. truthful traces revealed two preliminary patterns:
 - **Pattern #1 (Early-stage Fluctuations):** Hallucinated traces showed large fluctuations in Reasoning Score during early steps, often involving low scores (shallow pattern matching) followed by excessively high scores (overthinking attempts at self-verification).
 - **Pattern #2 (Incorrect Backtracking):** In hallucinated traces, later steps often misguidedly attended to earlier shallow or overthinking steps, preventing correction and leading to persistent errors.
- **Generalization Analysis (ReTruthQA):** These patterns were quantitatively assessed for generalizability:
 - **Pattern #1 Measurement:** The Coefficient of Variation (CV Score) of Reasoning Scores in the first $\lceil K/r \rceil$ steps was used. Hallucinated traces consistently exhibited significantly higher CV scores.
 - **Pattern #2 Measurement:** An "Attention Score" was introduced. This score quantified the proportion of top-K attended earlier steps (from later reasoning steps) whose Reasoning Scores fell outside a normal range (indicating shallow pattern-matching or overthinking). Hallucinated traces showed significantly higher Attention Scores.
- **Mechanism Analysis of Reasoning Fluctuation:** The researchers further investigated the mechanisms behind Pattern #1.
 - **Trigger for Verification:** By comparing "Stable" reasoning triples with "Rising" (Reasoning Score spike) triples, it was found that verification behavior was more likely triggered when earlier steps showed logical inconsistency.
 - **Overthinking and Accuracy:** In "Rising-2" triples (extreme Reasoning Score spikes), subsequent steps with high Reasoning Scores often introduced errors, indicating that excessively high scores could signal detrimental "overthinking."
 - **Spurious Verification (Pattern #3):** Analysis of perplexity revealed a generally negative correlation with Reasoning Score (higher RS, lower PPL). However, overthinking steps in hallucinated traces (Rising-2) showed higher PPL despite higher RS, indicating internal instability. This was termed "spurious verification," suggesting misguided validation driven by outcome-based reward optimization. This led to **Pattern #3: Overthinking steps exhibit a positive correlation between Reasoning Score and perplexity.**

**3. Reasoning Hallucination Detection (RHD) Framework**
Based on the identified patterns, the RHD framework was developed to detect reasoning hallucinations.
- **Hallucination Score:** An overall Reasoning Hallucination Score ($H_C$) is computed as a weighted linear combination of four components: average Reasoning Score, CV Score (Pattern #1), Attention Score (Pattern #2), and Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) between Reasoning Score and perplexity (Pattern #3).

**4. Reasoning Hallucination Mitigation (GRPO-R)**
To address the root causes of shallow pattern-matching and overthinking (attributed to outcome-based RL), a mitigation method called GRPO-R was introduced.
- **Step-Level Deep Reasoning Reward:** An auxiliary process-level reward was designed based on the Reasoning Score. This reward encourages meaningful reasoning while penalizing shallow or overthinking steps.
- **Potential-Based Reward Shaping:** The clipped Reasoning Score (where scores exceeding a threshold $\tau$ are set to zero) was integrated into the reward function using potential-based reward shaping. This technique preserves the optimal policy while redistributing credit to intermediate steps.
- **Theoretical Analysis:** A uniform convergence bound was derived, suggesting that the reasoning score-based shaping acts as a regularizer, reducing Rademacher complexity and enhancing generalization.
- **Integration with GRPO:** The shaping framework was integrated into Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a common RL algorithm for LRMs, creating GRPO-R.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning reasoning hallucination, its detection, and mitigation, supported by experimental results across multiple reasoning domains.

**1. Validation of Reasoning Score**
The proposed Reasoning Score, designed to quantify reasoning depth, was empirically validated using the GSM-NoOp dataset. Reasoning steps that were observed to be misled by irrelevant "No-Op" phrases consistently exhibited significantly lower Reasoning Scores compared to non-misled steps. This finding supports the hypothesis that the Reasoning Score effectively differentiates between shallow pattern-matching and deeper reasoning engagement (Figure 3a).

**2. Identification of Reasoning Hallucination Patterns**
Through an extensive analysis on the ReTruthQA dataset, the study identified and validated three distinct patterns of reasoning hallucination:
- **Pattern #1 (Early-stage Fluctuation):** Hallucinated reasoning traces consistently demonstrated significantly higher Coefficient of Variation (CV Score) in their Reasoning Scores during early steps compared to truthful traces. This indicates greater instability and fluctuation in reasoning depth at the beginning of a hallucinated process (Figure 3b, Figure 6).
- **Pattern #2 (Incorrect Backtracking):** Hallucinated traces exhibited significantly higher Attention Scores, indicating that later reasoning steps were more prone to attending to earlier steps characterized by shallow pattern-matching or overthinking. This misguided attention contributed to the propagation of errors (Figure 3c, Figure 6).
- **Pattern #3 (Spurious Verification):** An analysis revealed that while higher Reasoning Scores generally correlated with lower perplexity (indicating certainty), overthinking steps within hallucinated traces surprisingly showed high Reasoning Scores concurrently with high perplexity. This phenomenon, termed "spurious verification," suggests that these steps, despite appearing to reflect deep reasoning, were internally unstable and often introduced errors, driven by potentially misguided attempts at self-correction (Figure 4c, 4d). The analysis further demonstrated that verification behaviors were often triggered by logical inconsistencies in earlier steps, and that these "overthinking" verification steps frequently modified correct reasoning into incorrect steps (Figure 4a, 4b).

**3. Performance of Reasoning Hallucination Detection (RHD) Framework**
The RHD framework, which integrates the three identified hallucination patterns, demonstrated robust performance across the ReTruthQA dataset.
- RHD consistently outperformed a range of baseline methods, including ensemble-based, uncertainty-based, self-awareness-based, LLM-as-Critic, Process Reward Models, and length-based approaches, in both binary hallucination detection (AUC, PCC) and multi-trace ranking (MC1, MC2, MC3) tasks (Table 1).
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of the RHD framework (average Reasoning Score, CV Score, Attention Score, and the correlation between Reasoning Score and perplexity) contributed to its overall effectiveness, with varying impact across different domains, suggesting domain-specific manifestations of hallucination patterns (Table 4).
- Sensitivity analyses indicated the robustness of RHD to hyperparameter variations and choices of attention layers, affirming its stability (Figure 7, Table 5).

**4. Efficacy of Reasoning Hallucination Mitigation (GRPO-R)**
The proposed GRPO-R algorithm, which incorporates step-level deep reasoning rewards into a reinforcement learning framework, showed effectiveness in mitigating reasoning hallucinations.
- GRPO-R consistently improved reasoning accuracy compared to the standard GRPO algorithm across various in-domain math benchmarks (MATH500, AIME 2024) and out-of-distribution science benchmarks (GPQA), when fine-tuning models like DeepSeek-R1-1.5B and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Table 2). This indicates that encouraging meaningful intermediate reasoning steps enhances both factual accuracy and the reliability of the reasoning process.
- The improvements observed on GPQA suggest that GRPO-R also leads to better generalization capabilities beyond the training data distribution.
- Sensitivity analysis of the reasoning score reward weight in GRPO-R demonstrated an optimal range, beyond which excessive weighting could lead to performance degradation, suggesting a balanced approach is necessary to complement outcome-based rewards (Figure 8).

**5. RHD-Guided Reasoning Distillation**
The research also explored the application of RHD in data distillation. Using RHD to rank and select high-quality reasoning traces for distilling knowledge to smaller LLMs resulted in improved performance compared to random selection or using 100% of raw, potentially noisy data. This indicates RHD's utility in curating cleaner, more reliable training data, thereby preventing the propagation of undesirable reasoning behaviors to smaller models (Table 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a contribution to the understanding and management of reasoning hallucinations in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), with several potential impacts:

**1. Enhanced Mechanistic Understanding of LRM Failures:** By proposing and validating the "Reasoning Score" and identifying specific hallucination patterns (early-stage fluctuations, incorrect backtracking, and spurious verification), this work offers a fine-grained, mechanistic perspective on how and why LRMs produce logically coherent yet factually incorrect reasoning. This moves beyond surface-level error detection to probe the internal "thinking patterns" of these models. This understanding is critical for developing more targeted and effective interventions.

**2. Improved Hallucination Detection Capabilities:** The Reasoning Hallucination Detection (RHD) framework, derived from the mechanistic insights, demonstrates robust performance across diverse reasoning domains and model sizes. Its ability to leverage internal model activations for accurate detection represents an advancement over existing baselines, which often struggle with the deceptive nature of reasoning hallucinations. An effective detection tool is essential for deploying LRMs responsibly in high-stakes applications where factual accuracy of reasoning is paramount.

**3. Development of More Robust Training Paradigms:** The introduction of GRPO-R addresses a fundamental limitation of outcome-based reinforcement learning (RL) by integrating step-level reasoning rewards. This approach not only improves the factual accuracy of LRM outputs but also encourages the generation of more reliable and deeply reasoned intermediate steps. The theoretical generalization guarantees and empirical performance gains suggest a path toward training LRMs that are inherently less prone to hallucination, thus enhancing their trustworthiness and reliability.

**4. Optimization of Knowledge Distillation:** The utility of RHD in curating high-quality reasoning data for distillation purposes has significant implications. By filtering out hallucinated or low-quality reasoning traces, RHD can help prevent the propagation of undesirable reasoning behaviors from larger teacher models to smaller student models. This could lead to more efficient and effective transfer of complex reasoning capabilities, enabling the development of smaller, more accessible, and reliable reasoning models.

**5. Contribution to AI Safety and Trust:** Reasoning hallucinations, especially due to their persuasive coherence, can undermine user trust and pose safety risks in critical domains. By offering both detection and mitigation strategies grounded in a deeper understanding of internal model mechanisms, this research directly contributes to the broader goals of AI safety, interpretability, and responsible AI development. The findings open avenues for further research into how internal model states can be leveraged for better control and understanding of complex AI behaviors.

While the work is currently limited to open-source LRMs due to its reliance on internal activations and experiments on moderate-scale models, the fundamental patterns and methodologies introduced could inspire future work, including proxy-based extensions for black-box models and large-scale applications across an even wider array of reasoning tasks and model architectures.
