# Polychromic Objectives for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed Set Reinforcement Learning and polychromic objectives to prevent policy diversity loss during reinforcement learning fine-tuning. The resulting Polychromic PPO algorithm improved generalization, creativity, and pass@k performance across robotic control and algorithmic creativity tasks, often surpassing pretrained policies.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RLFT) often leads to 'entropy collapse,' where policies converge on a limited set of high-reward behaviors, losing the diversity of the pretrained model.
- This reduction in policy diversity restricts exploration, limits the discovery of novel strategies, and causes underperformance in pass@k metrics for larger k.
- Existing regularization techniques, such as entropy bonuses, primarily induce local variation and are often insufficient to promote semantic or trajectory-level diversity.

## Method

- Introduced '==Set Reinforcement Learning== (**Set RL**)' which optimizes an objective function over a set of independently sampled trajectories, rather than a single trajectory, using a shared advantage term.
- Proposed a 'polychromic objective' within the Set RL framework that jointly optimizes for cumulative reward and the semantic diversity of the sampled trajectory set.
- Developed Polychromic PPO (Poly-PPO), an adaptation of PPO that employs 'vine sampling' for efficient on-policy data collection and a modified advantage function to optimize the polychromic objective.

## Results

- Poly-PPO consistently matched or outperformed baselines in average episodic reward and success rate in BabyAI/MiniGrid environments, achieving an **80.2%** success rate in 'Goto' compared to PPO's **46.2%**.
- Demonstrated substantially higher pass@k rates, with pass rate continuing to rise until k=80 (vs. k=20 for baselines), and improved 'creativity' and 'diversity' in Algorithmic Creativity tasks.
- Exhibited more reliable generalization under large initial-state perturbations, achieving a **60.6%** pass rate in 'Goto' under perturbation, significantly higher than REINFORCE (**41.3%**) or PPO (**21.1%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing over sets of trajectories with a shared learning signal provides a principled way to encourage global diversity, effectively mitigating entropy collapse.
> - Polychromic objectives actively prevent policies from concentrating probability mass on homogeneous successful behaviors, instead attracting it towards diverse sets of successful actions.
> - Maintaining policy diversity during fine-tuning is crucial for enhancing generalization to new or perturbed tasks and for amplifying the benefits of test-time compute scaling.
