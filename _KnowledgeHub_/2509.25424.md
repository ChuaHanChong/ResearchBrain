# Polychromic Objectives for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed Set Reinforcement Learning and polychromic objectives to prevent policy diversity loss during reinforcement learning fine-tuning. The resulting Polychromic PPO algorithm improved generalization, creativity, and pass@k performance across robotic control and algorithmic creativity tasks, often surpassing pretrained policies.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RLFT) often leads to 'entropy collapse,' where policies converge on a limited set of high-reward behaviors, losing the diversity of the pretrained model.
- This reduction in policy diversity restricts exploration, limits the discovery of novel strategies, and causes underperformance in pass@k metrics for larger k.
- Existing regularization techniques, such as entropy bonuses, primarily induce local variation and are often insufficient to promote semantic or trajectory-level diversity.

## Method

- Introduced '==Set Reinforcement Learning== (**Set RL**)' which optimizes an objective function over a set of independently sampled trajectories, rather than a single trajectory, using a shared advantage term.
- Proposed a 'polychromic objective' within the Set RL framework that jointly optimizes for cumulative reward and the semantic diversity of the sampled trajectory set.
- Developed Polychromic PPO (Poly-PPO), an adaptation of PPO that employs 'vine sampling' for efficient on-policy data collection and a modified advantage function to optimize the polychromic objective.

## Results

- Poly-PPO consistently matched or outperformed baselines in average episodic reward and success rate in BabyAI/MiniGrid environments, achieving an **80.2%** success rate in 'Goto' compared to PPO's **46.2%**.
- Demonstrated substantially higher pass@k rates, with pass rate continuing to rise until k=80 (vs. k=20 for baselines), and improved 'creativity' and 'diversity' in Algorithmic Creativity tasks.
- Exhibited more reliable generalization under large initial-state perturbations, achieving a **60.6%** pass rate in 'Goto' under perturbation, significantly higher than REINFORCE (**41.3%**) or PPO (**21.1%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing over sets of trajectories with a shared learning signal provides a principled way to encourage global diversity, effectively mitigating entropy collapse.
> - Polychromic objectives actively prevent policies from concentrating probability mass on homogeneous successful behaviors, instead attracting it towards diverse sets of successful actions.
> - Maintaining policy diversity during fine-tuning is crucial for enhancing generalization to new or perturbed tasks and for amplifying the benefits of test-time compute scaling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RLFT) is a prevalent paradigm for enhancing pretrained policies, particularly those trained on extensive datasets, to perform specific downstream tasks. These pretrained policies often exhibit a wide spectrum of behaviors, which, while promising, may lack refinement. A recognized challenge in RLFT is the phenomenon of "entropy collapse," where policies tend to converge on a limited set of easily exploitable high-reward behaviors, thereby losing the diversity inherent in the pretrained model. This reduction in diversity hinders exploration, which is considered essential for expanding the capabilities of a policy and for leveraging the benefits of test-time compute scaling. Existing regularization techniques, such as entropy bonuses, primarily induce local or token-level variation and may not adequately promote semantic or trajectory-level exploration. Moreover, these bonuses can be overshadowed by the primary RL objective. The present work addresses this documented issue of entropy collapse by proposing an alternative approach to policy gradient optimization that explicitly encourages the exploration and refinement of diverse generations, rather than solely maximizing cumulative reward. This positions the work within the domain of reinforcement learning exploration strategies, specifically focusing on maintaining and leveraging policy diversity during fine-tuning processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate methods for inducing reinforcement learning policies to explore and refine a diverse repertoire of generations during fine-tuning. This objective is motivated by several identified shortcomings of conventional RLFT:

1. **Entropy Collapse:** Prior research indicates that RLFT often leads to policies concentrating probability mass on a narrow set of high-reward behaviors. This reduces the diversity of strategies a policy can employ, effectively sacrificing a broader range of potentially useful behaviors.
2. **Limited Exploration:** The collapse of diversity restricts a policy's ability to explore the environment effectively. This limits the discovery of novel or alternative strategies that could extend the base model's capabilities beyond its initial learned repertoire.
3. **Underperformance in Pass@k Metrics:** Empirical evidence, particularly from pass@k experiments (which measure the probability of success in at least one out of *k* independent attempts), shows that RL-fine-tuned models can underperform their pretrained counterparts for larger *k*. This suggests that while fine-tuning might improve average performance, it can diminish the breadth of problem-solving approaches, making the policy less robust when multiple attempts are permitted or when facing varied task instances.
4. **Implications for Generalization and Test-Time Compute:** The authors highlight that policy diversity is critical for generalization to new or perturbed tasks and for amplifying the benefits of test-time compute scaling. A diverse policy can adapt better to unexpected scenarios and leverage multiple attempts more effectively.
5. **Inadequacy of Standard Regularization:** Conventional regularization methods, such as entropy bonuses, are noted to typically promote local or token-level variation, which may not translate to semantic or trajectory-level diversity. Furthermore, these bonuses can be insufficiently influential compared to the main reward objective.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes developing an objective for policy gradient methods that explicitly encourages policies to maintain and refine the diverse generations already embedded in the pretrained distribution, leading to a unified formulation that optimizes for a diverse set of successful behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a framework termed "set reinforcement learning" and instantiates a specific "polychromic objective" within this framework, which is then optimized using a modified version of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

#### 3.1. Set Reinforcement Learning

The authors introduce Set Reinforcement Learning (Set RL) as a variant of the standard RL setup. Unlike traditional RL, which maximizes the expected return of a single trajectory, Set RL aims to maximize an objective function defined over a *set* of independently sampled trajectories, $\tau_{1:n} = \{\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_n\}$. The objective function is $f(s_0, \tau_1, \ldots, \tau_n)$, where $s_0$ is the initial state. A key characteristic of Set RL, in this formulation, is that all trajectories within a sampled set receive the same learning signal during policy gradient updates. This is achieved by using a shared advantage term for the entire set, $f(s_0, \tau_{1:n}) - \hat{f}(s_0)$, where $\hat{f}(s_0)$ is a baseline independent of the specific trajectories in the set. This design ensures that the policy is optimized with respect to the collective quality of the set, rather than individual trajectories, allowing the value of one trajectory to depend on others within the same set. The authors extend the performance difference lemma to Set RL, defining set value functions ($V^\#$) and set Q-functions ($Q^\#$) based on a "state-visitation tree" where each state branches into *n* children for *n* sampled actions. The discount factor is constrained to $\gamma \in (0, 1/n)$ to ensure boundedness due to the branching factor.

#### 3.2. Polychromic Objective

Within the Set RL framework, the authors introduce "polychromic objectives," which are designed to train a policy to explore and learn a diverse set of behaviors. The specific instance used in the algorithm and experiments is defined as:
$f_{\text{poly}}(s, \tau_{1:n}) := \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n R(\tau_i) d(s, \tau_{1:n})$
Here, $R(\tau_i)$ is the discounted sum of rewards for trajectory $\tau_i$, and $d(s, \tau_{1:n})$ quantifies the diversity of trajectories within the set. Both $R(\tau_i)$ and $d(s, \tau_{1:n})$ are normalized between 0 and 1. The objective's design, combined with the shared advantage term in Set RL, aims to amplify learning signals for exploratory trajectories that may not yet yield high rewards, thereby encouraging the discovery of diverse strategies. The algorithm is designed to be agnostic to the specific diversity metric used. For experiments, diversity $d(s, \tau_{1:n})$ is defined as the fraction of semantically distinct trajectories in the set. In MiniGrid/BabyAI, distinct trajectories visit different sets of rooms, while in Algorithmic Creativity, they visit different sets of nodes. If all trajectories visit the same rooms or nodes, diversity is 0.

#### 3.3. Polychromic PPO (Poly-PPO) Algorithm

To optimize the polychromic objective, the authors adapt Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), resulting in Polychromic PPO. This adaptation involves two primary modifications:

1. **Vine Sampling for On-Policy Data Collection:** To address the exponential data requirements of directly implementing set advantage functions, Poly-PPO employs vine sampling. After collecting an initial set of rollouts, a subset of visited states, called "rollout states" ($s_t$), are selected. From each rollout state, $N$ additional rollouts are generated independently. This process ensures multiple sets of independently sampled trajectories stemming from specific states, allowing for the application of the polychromic advantage. The specific scheme used is "p equally spaced out states" along a main trajectory. The algorithm requires environment resets.
2. **Modified Advantage Function:** For rollout states $s_t$ from which $N > n$ trajectories are generated, the polychromic advantage is estimated as:
 $A^\# (s_t, a_t; f_{\text{poly}}) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n R(\tau_i) d(s_t, \tau_{1:n}) - \hat{V}^\# (s_t; f_{\text{poly}})$
 where $a_t \in \tau_i$ for some $i$. This means all actions taken from $s_t$ within a given set $\tau_{1:n}$ receive the same update signal. The value baseline $\hat{V}^\#$ is a Monte Carlo estimate using $M$ independently sampled sets of trajectories. For non-rollout states, the standard Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is used. The PPO objective is then optimized with these modified advantages.
 Additionally, for stability, a per-state KL penalty $D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\beta(\cdot|s) || \pi_\theta(\cdot|s))$ is included. A "polychromic window" ($W$) is also introduced, where advantages for states $s_t, \ldots, s_{t+W}$ following a rollout state $s_t$ are also set to the polychromic advantage, aiming to extend the exploratory behavior encouraged by the polychromic advantage across a sequence of states.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

Poly-PPO is evaluated against REINFORCE (with baseline), standard PPO, and variants of both including a UCB-style exploration bonus, across three environments:
- **BabyAI and MiniGrid:** Grid-world environments requiring language-conditioned navigation and manipulation. Policies are CNN-GRU based, pretrained on expert demonstrations, and fine-tuned on 50 fixed configurations. Reward is sparse.
- **Algorithmic Creativity (Triangle Discovery):** An environment for quantifying creativity, where an agent generates sequences of triangles from unobserved graphs. Policies are decoder-only Transformers, pretrained on graph data, and fine-tuned on 3 fixed graphs. Reward is sparse (+1 for a valid triangle).

For all settings, the number of trajectories generated at rollout states $N=8$, and the set size for Poly-PPO $n=4$. The number of sets $M$ used for baseline computation is 4. The trajectory budget $B=136$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess Poly-PPO's impact on performance, ability to encourage diverse trajectories, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Performance Impact

- **BabyAI and MiniGrid:** Poly-PPO consistently matched or outperformed the best baselines in terms of average episodic reward and success rate (Table 1). For instance, in "Goto," Poly-PPO achieved a success rate of 80.2%, compared to PPO's 46.2% and REINFORCE's 73.0%. The UCB bonus was found to be complementary to Poly-PPO, further enhancing performance in some environments like "Pickup" and "Bosslevel."
- **Algorithmic Creativity:** Poly-PPO achieved slightly lower "validity" (number of valid triangles constructed) than standard PPO but substantially higher "creativity" (percentage of unique, valid triangles not seen in pretraining data) and "diversity" (unique number of valid triangles) (Figure 2). This indicates a trade-off where Poly-PPO prioritizes novelty and breadth of solutions.

#### 4.2. Encouraging Diverse Trajectories (Pass@k Analysis)

- **BabyAI:** Pass@k curves (Figure 3) demonstrated Poly-PPO's ability to generate diverse and effective trajectories.
 - REINFORCE and PPO, while improving success rates, showed lower coverage than the pretrained policy at large *k* values, suggesting overfitting and forgetting of useful behaviors. PPO, in particular, started with lower pass rates at small *k*.
 - Poly-PPO consistently achieved substantially higher pass rates than all baselines, often surpassing the pretrained policy across most *k* values. Its pass rate continued to rise until approximately *k*=80, whereas baselines saturated earlier (around *k*=20). This effect was notable in "Bosslevel," where Poly-PPO achieved about 15% higher pass rate at *k*=160.
 - The combination of UCB with Poly-PPO generally maintained or improved coverage, indicating that Poly-PPO effectively preserves and refines pretrained diversity without collapsing into narrow behaviors.
- **Algorithmic Creativity:** Poly-PPO outperformed baselines in both "creativity pass@k" (generating at least one novel triangle) and "diff@k" (number of unique triangles in *k* attempts) (Figure 4). While REINFORCE showed high diversity, it came at a significant cost in "validity@1," falling below the pretrained policy. Poly-PPO achieved higher validity performance than the pretrained policy while demonstrating superior creativity and diversity.

#### 4.3. Generalization to State Perturbations

- **Minigrid and BabyAI:** Under large initial-state perturbations (placing the agent in randomly selected positions within reachable rooms), Poly-PPO exhibited more reliable generalization than the baselines (Table 2). For example, in "Goto," Poly-PPO achieved a 60.6% pass rate, significantly higher than REINFORCE (41.3%) or PPO (21.1%). This suggests that policies trained with polychromic objectives develop a broader understanding of tasks, allowing them to adapt to varied starting conditions that require different strategies.

#### 4.4. Entropy Analysis

The paper includes a theoretical analysis of how policy entropy evolves under the polychromic objective.
- **Proposition 5.1:** Characterizes the first-order change in entropy after one policy update, showing its dependence on the covariance between the log-probability of actions and the influence of those actions on the objective function's value.
- **Definition 5.2 (Scaffold Value):** Introduces the "scaffold value" $\Lambda_f(a_{1:n}; \pi)$, which measures a policy's propensity to concentrate entropy around a given set of actions $a_{1:n}$.
- **Lemma 5.3:** States that the change in log probability of sampling an action set is approximately proportional to its scaffold value.
- **Proposition 5.4:** Demonstrates that for homogeneous sets of successful actions (e.g., all actions are the same and yield reward 1), the polychromic objective creates a negative scaffold value if the action's probability exceeds a certain threshold. This indicates that the objective actively prevents further entropy collapse onto such single, repeatedly successful actions. The upper bound on the scaffold value for homogeneous sets decreases with larger set sizes *n*.
- **Proposition 5.5:** Establishes that heterogeneous sets containing successful actions are "attractors" of probability mass, meaning their scaffold values are positive. This indicates that the polychromic objective encourages policies to find and reinforce diverse sets of successful actions.
- **Definition 5.6 (General Polychromic Objective):** Formalizes a polychromic objective as a function $\phi(s, \tau_{1:n}) = \phi^{(R)}(s, \tau_{1:n})\phi^{(d)}(s, \tau_{1:n})$, where $\phi^{(R)}$ correlates positively with return and $\phi^{(d)}$ correlates negatively with homogeneity, and both share the same range. This ensures a balanced optimization for both success and diversity, guiding entropy collapse towards exploratory policies rather than trivial memorization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel conceptual framework, "Set Reinforcement Learning," which broadens the scope of RL objectives beyond single-trajectory return maximization to explicitly consider the collective properties of multiple sampled trajectories. The proposed "polychromic objective" within this framework offers a principled approach to mitigate entropy collapse, a known challenge in RL fine-tuning. By jointly optimizing for both reward and diversity, the method helps policies retain and refine a broader repertoire of behaviors.

The experimental results demonstrate that Polychromic PPO consistently performs on par with or surpasses baselines in traditional performance metrics while substantially improving policy diversity and generalization capabilities, particularly evident in pass@k evaluations and robustness to state perturbations. This suggests that the approach can yield more robust and adaptable policies, especially in settings where a wide range of problem-solving strategies is beneficial, or where test-time compute can be leveraged. The theoretical analysis further provides insights into how this objective mechanistically prevents collapse onto homogeneous behaviors and attracts probability mass towards diverse, successful strategies.

Potential impact includes:
- **Improved RL Fine-Tuning:** The method offers a solution to a critical failure mode of RLFT, making the fine-tuning of large pretrained models more effective for complex downstream tasks that require nuanced and varied solutions.
- **Enhanced Exploration:** By explicitly incentivizing exploration through diversity-aware objectives, the approach could facilitate the discovery of novel strategies in sparse reward environments or tasks requiring creative solutions.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** Policies trained with polychromic objectives may exhibit greater robustness to variations in initial conditions and better generalization to unseen but related task configurations, which is valuable in real-world applications.
- **Applications in Large Language Models (LLMs):** The context of LLM fine-tuning is directly referenced as a domain where diverse generation is highly desirable for complex reasoning and instruction following. This work could lead to LLMs that generate a broader range of high-quality, relevant responses, supporting more sophisticated human-AI interactions.
- **Theoretical Contributions:** The formalization of Set RL and the entropy analysis provide a theoretical foundation for understanding and developing new objectives that balance exploitation with diverse exploration.

However, the approach has limitations. It requires the ability to reset the environment for vine sampling, which may not be feasible in all real-world or simulation settings. Designing or learning effective diversity functions can be challenging, especially in complex or continuous control domains. Additionally, the reliance on Monte Carlo estimates for advantages could lead to high variance in certain scenarios, indicating a need for more efficient estimators in future work. Despite these limitations, the work presents a significant step towards addressing the critical problem of diversity in reinforcement learning.
