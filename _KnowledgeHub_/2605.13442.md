# Asymptotically Optimal Ergodic Coverage on Generalized Motion Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> This research extends ergodic coverage to time-varying domains influenced by flow fields, providing formal long-term coverage guarantees for information distributions transported by dynamic vector fields. The proposed flow-adaptive Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) based planner achieved a 42% improvement over baseline methods in oceanic exploration simulations and demonstrated effective exploration in various real-world and simulated dynamic environments with resource-constrained robots.

## Problem

- Most adaptive robotic exploration methods assume static environments, lacking robust coverage guarantees in domains that change over time due to flows.
- A challenge exists in balancing long-horizon comprehensive visitation with short-term information gain when the search space and target distribution are continuously evolving.
- Resource-constrained and under-actuated robotic platforms (e.g., UxS) require path planning methods that can generate stable, long-duration trajectories, ideally in open-loop, to manage computational limits.

## Method

- The research reformulates adaptive search as an ==ergodic coverage== problem specifically for time-varying domains influenced by flow fields.
- It expands upon the ==Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)== based ergodic metric to derive a **flow-adaptive formulation** that explicitly incorporates domain evolution into the coverage objective.
- An optimization method is developed that generates ==ergodic trajectories on flows==, enabling effective exploration in under-actuated and open-loop planning settings by leveraging ambient environmental dynamics.

## Results

- The Flow-Adaptive EMMD method achieved an **18.28%** reduction in mean model error in an ocean eddy exploration simulation, demonstrating a **42%** improvement over the best-performing baseline (Flow-Modified Information Maximization).
- Robots using the proposed method visited up to **98.67%** of spatial samples within **10 seconds** in a vortex flow field, showcasing effective coverage even when under-actuated (robot velocity up to **19%** of average flow speed).
- The approach was validated across diverse scenarios, including real-world drone tracking of Spheros, quadruped robot search for humans in an office, simulated cattle behavior inspection, and whale tracking with real ocean currents, consistently demonstrating effective and robust coverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formal ergodic coverage guarantees can be maintained for information distributions transported by time-varying vector fields by consistently mapping trajectory points to a reference domain (either initial or a common future time).
> - Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) serves as an effective kernel-based metric for quantifying ergodic coverage in complex, non-convex, and flow-constrained domains, without requiring explicit density functions.
> - Integrating environment dynamics directly into the path planning objective allows robots to strategically leverage ambient flows, enhancing visitation efficiency and robustness for resource-constrained systems without continuous replanning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses limitations in autonomous robotic exploration, particularly in dynamic and extreme environments where the search domain evolves over time due to flow fields.

**Information-Theoretic Exploration:** Traditional methods focus on maximizing information gain or reducing uncertainty, often using probabilistic models or Gaussian Processes. While effective for static mapping or short-term tasks, these approaches generally lack long-term coverage guarantees. Some advancements have enabled flow-tracking, but they primarily focus on high-value regions and may not ensure comprehensive exploration over extended periods.

**Ergodic Trajectory Optimization:** Ergodic search methods aim to generate trajectories that maximize time-averaged visitation statistics proportional to a predefined information distribution, offering asymptotic coverage guarantees over sufficiently long time horizons. Early spectral methods were confined to known, bounded Euclidean domains, limiting their application in complex, non-convex geometries. Recent work has introduced Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) as a kernel-based ergodic metric, enabling exploration in arbitrarily structured domains. However, current MMD-based formulations largely assume stationary environments.

**Flow Tracking:** In environments with time-variant topography or information measures, autonomous systems require the ability to track movements. Lagrangian Coherent Structures (LCS) provide a framework for analyzing time-dependent flows, identifying material surfaces that guide optimal motion. Approaches involve estimating fluid velocity or tracking discrete points within the flow. Full flow approximation can be computationally expensive, and some methods restrict exploration to easily approximated regions, potentially missing crucial areas.

**Gap Addressed by This Work:** The paper positions itself at the intersection of these fields by addressing the deficiency of existing ergodic search methods in handling time-varying domains induced by flows. While information-theoretic and flow-tracking methods exist, they often lack the formal, long-term coverage guarantees provided by ergodic approaches, especially in the context of evolving search spaces and under-actuated systems common in remote environments (e.g., Uncrewed Underwater Systems - UxS). The work extends the MMD-based ergodic formulation to explicitly account for domain evolution, thereby bridging the gap between robust coverage guarantees and dynamic environment interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges of autonomous robotic exploration in dynamic, remote, and extreme environments. Such environments, frequently encountered in oceanography or ecological monitoring, are characterized by continuously deforming flow fields (e.g., ocean currents, animal migration patterns, human crowds).

The specific issues that motivate this work are:
- **Limitations of existing methods:** Most adaptive exploration methods assume static environments, which renders them inadequate for providing robust coverage or formal guarantees in time-varying domains. Traditional coverage methods do not account for structural changes in the search domain.
- **Balancing competing objectives:** A fundamental difficulty in exploring evolving domains is the need to balance efficient long-horizon visitation proportionality (comprehensive coverage) with short-term information gain (identifying high-value regions).
- **Constraints of robotic platforms:** Energy-constrained and under-actuated systems, such as Uncrewed Underwater Systems (UxS), have restrictive computational and payload requirements. This necessitates path planning methods that can generate stable and robust long-duration trajectories, ideally in open-loop settings, to avoid the high computational cost of iterative replanning.
- **Absence of formal guarantees in dynamic settings:** Traditional ergodic search methods typically rely on *a priori* knowledge of a static search domain and information distribution. Few consider time-varying information, and even fewer account for time-varying search domains themselves. While Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) has extended ergodic exploration to arbitrary spatial domains, its application has been limited to stationary environments.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Formulate adaptive search as an ergodic coverage problem** over domains that evolve according to flow-induced dynamics. This involves defining what constitutes "ergodic coverage" when the target distribution and the domain itself are continuously changing.
2. **Investigate certifying coverage in the ergodic sense** over these evolving domains, ensuring that formal guarantees can be provided despite the dynamic nature of the environment.
3. **Expand upon the MMD-based ergodic metric** to derive a flow-adaptive formulation. This formulation explicitly incorporates domain evolution into the coverage objective, allowing the system to reason about coverage in the presence of ambient flows.
4. **Demonstrate the preservation of ergodic coverage guarantees** even when operating in ambient flows.
5. **Enable effective exploration in under-actuated and open-loop planning settings** by integrating environment dynamics directly into the path planning process.
6. **Provide a theoretical formulation of ergodicity** that maintains coverage guarantees for information distributions transported by time-varying vector fields.
7. **Develop a novel MMD-based optimization method** capable of performing ergodic exploration in non-convex and flow-constrained domains.
8. **Validate the approach experimentally** across diverse spatiotemporal processes, including ocean exploration, and tracking human and cattle movement, using both simulated and physical robotic platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research reformulates adaptive search as an ergodic coverage problem for time-varying domains influenced by flow fields. The methodology builds upon existing concepts of ergodicity and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), extending them to dynamic environments.

**A. Ergodicity Foundations:**
The work begins by defining fundamental concepts:
- **Robot State and Trajectory:** A robot's state $x_t \in \mathcal{X} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ at discrete time $t$, forming a trajectory $x = \{x_0, \dots, x_{T-1}\}$ under control inputs $u_t$ and dynamics $x_{t+1} = f(x_t, u_t)$.
- **Operational Search Space:** A closed and bounded space $\Omega$, where a function $g: \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \Omega$ maps the robot's state to its position in the search space.
- **Time-Averaged Trajectory Distribution ($\rho_{x,T}$):** Defined as the visitation histogram of a trajectory, $\rho_{x,T}(\omega) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \delta[\omega - g(x_t)]$, where $\delta$ is the Dirac-delta function.
- **Ergodicity (Definition 2):** A trajectory $x$ is ergodic with respect to a probability measure $\mu: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^+$ if its time-averaged statistics $\rho_{x,T}(\omega)$ converge weakly to $\mu$ as $T \rightarrow \infty$. This implies that the trajectory visits regions in $\Omega$ with probability proportional to the expected value of $\mu$.

**B. Ergodic Control:**
Ergodic control aims to generate trajectories that are ergodic in a defined operational search space. This is achieved by minimizing an ergodic metric $E_\mu(x) = \lVert F[\rho_{x,T}] - F[\mu] \rVert_F^2$, which quantifies the difference between the robot's time-averaged visitation and a target distribution. The optimization problem is generally formulated as:
$$ \min_{x,u} E_\mu(x) + \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \ell(x_t, u_t) $$
subject to robot dynamics and constraints ($h_1, h_2$). Prior work used Fourier transforms for $F$, while recent approaches have adopted kernel-based techniques.

**C. Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD):**
MMD is employed as the chosen kernel-based metric. It is a two-sample statistical metric that measures the similarity between samples from two probability distributions ($p$ and $q$) using kernel mean embeddings in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). A key advantage of MMD is that it does not require explicit access to the underlying density functions; only samples are needed. For finite samples, an empirical approximation of MMD can be calculated.

**D. Ergodicity Over Flows – The Core Innovation:**
The paper introduces the concept of ergodicity for time-varying domains, which is the central theoretical contribution.
- **Domain Evolution Model:** A compact static initial domain $\Omega_0$ with a probability measure $\mu_0$ is assumed. The time-varying domain $\Omega_t$ evolves according to a discrete flow function $\zeta_t: \mathbb{Z}^+ \rightarrow C(E,E)$, such that $\Omega_t = \zeta_t(\Omega_0)$. The information measure also evolves as a push-forward measure $\mu_t := (\zeta_t)_*\mu_0$.
- **Invertible Flows (Definition 4):** For invertible discrete flows (where $\zeta_t$ has a continuous inverse), time-varying ergodicity is defined by mapping the robot's trajectory $g(x_t)$ back to the original domain $\Omega_0$ using $\zeta_t^{-1}$. The MMD metric is then applied to the back-mapped trajectory points and samples drawn from the initial distribution $\mu_0$.
- **General (Non-Invertible) Flows (Definition 5):** For general (potentially non-invertible) flows, the concept is to map all trajectory samples forward to a common later time $T-1$ using $\zeta_{T-1-t}$. This creates a forward-flow trajectory distribution $\rho_{\zeta,f}^{x,T}$ which is then compared with the target measure $\mu_{T-1}$ using MMD.
- **Equivalence for Lipschitz Invertible Flows (Proposition 1):** The paper proves that these two definitions of time-varying ergodicity are equivalent if the flow $\zeta$ is discrete, invertible, and each $\zeta_t$ is a Lipschitz continuous function with a Lipschitz constant $\leq 1$. This provides a theoretical link between the backward and forward mapping approaches.

**E. Ergodic Trajectory Optimization on Flows:**
The generalized optimal control problem for generating ergodic trajectories on flows is formulated. The ergodic metric $E_k^\mu(x, \zeta)$ (either the backward-mapped or forward-mapped MMD) is minimized, subject to robot state and control constraints. This formulation requires knowledge of the flow map $\zeta$, which dictates how the initial information measure $\mu_0$ transforms into $\mu_t$.

**F. Computational Complexity:**
The computational complexity of the flow-adaptive MMD metric is $O(T^2 + TM + M^2)$ per optimization step, where $T$ is the time horizon (number of trajectory control knots) and $M$ is the number of spatial samples for the target distribution. By pre-computing the target distribution's self-similarity term (which is constant), the complexity reduces to $O(T^2 + TM)$. The summation-based structure of MMD is noted to be amenable to GPU parallelization, potentially reducing computational scaling to approximately linear with respect to the planning horizon.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both simulated and physical experimental results to validate the proposed flow-adaptive ergodic planner across various scenarios, comparing its effectiveness against existing methods and evaluating its performance under different conditions.

**A. Comparison to Existing Methods:**
- **Scenario:** An autonomous underwater system (UxS) is tasked with exploring an eddy in the Gulf of America over one month (6-hour control intervals) to refine a flow model. The utility grid represents discrepancies between true and approximated Gulf flow data.
- **Baselines:** Flow-Modified Information Maximization and Energy-Optimal Planning.
- **Results (Table I, Figure 4):**
 - The proposed Flow-Adaptive EMMD method achieved an 18.28% ± 3.80% reduction in mean model error (flow discrepancy).
 - Flow-Modified Information Maximization achieved 12.87% ± 0.65%.
 - Energy-Optimal Planning achieved 11.47% ± 7.51%.
- **Analysis:** The flow-adaptive ergodic planner demonstrated a 42% improvement over the best-performing baseline (Flow-Modified Information Maximization). The superior performance is attributed to its balanced approach to local information gain and global visitation proportionality, which enables the robot to anticipate the advection of high-utility features rather than merely reacting to them. The baseline methods suffered from suboptimal revisitation or numerical stability issues (Energy-Optimal Planning's reliance on the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy condition).

**B. Energy-Constrained Exploration:**
- **Scenario:** A robot is placed in a vortex flow field (peak speed 3.46 m/s, mean 2.56 m/s) containing 75 randomly placed samples. The robot's velocity control bound was varied (0 to 0.5 m/s, approximately 19% of the average flow speed). The task was to maximize the number of visited points within 10 seconds.
- **Results (Figure 5):** When provided with the maximum velocity control bound (0.5 m/s), the flow-adaptive ergodic planner enabled the robot to visit up to 98.67% of spatial samples within 10 seconds. Coverage quality increased proportionally with the robot's velocity bound.
- **Analysis:** Even in under-actuated conditions, the method facilitated trajectories that leveraged the flow field, enabling the robot to work with ambient dynamics to maximize visitation despite limited actuation capacity.

**C. Experimental Validation:**
- **Real-World Exploration Using Crazyflie Drone:**
 - **Scenario:** A Crazyflie 2.1 drone tracked a fleet of 8 Spheros in a 3m x 3m space. The Spheros followed trajectories imitating movement through a flow field. The drone, dynamically independent of the flow, had to observe each Sphero and revisit them.
 - **Results (Figure 6):** The drone successfully planned a coverage trajectory, visiting each Sphero within 30 seconds while respecting its flight dynamics. The MMD-based metric's use of discrete samples proved effective for sparse information distributions (8 samples).
- **Simulated Use-Case of Cattle Behavior Inspection:**
 - **Scenario:** A drone monitored 30 cows, including two aggressive ones, in a 30m x 30m ranch with three feeding troughs (flow attractors). The goal was to identify conflicts involving aggressive cows in an open-loop setting. Cattle movement was approximated as a behavioral flow.
 - **Results (Table II, Figure 7):** The flow-adaptive ergodic planner achieved a 50.8% success rate in guiding the drone to aggressive interactions, marking a 42.8% improvement over Flow-Modified Information Maximization (35.6%). Energy-Optimal Planning failed due to numerical instability issues related to high-velocity gradients.
 - **Analysis:** This validates the method's ability to generalize beyond fluid currents to predictable behavioral flows.
- **Indoor Human Search on Quadruped Robot:**
 - **Scenario:** A Unitree Go2 quadruped robot searched for a target person (wearing all-black) among four people in an office building hallway. Human motion was modeled as a constrained social flow, with individuals introducing stochastic deviations from their instructed paths.
 - **Results (Figure 8):** The Go2 successfully navigated the non-convex indoor environment, observed each person, and located the target within a 2-minute runtime.
 - **Analysis:** The kernel-based spatial integration of MMD provided resilience to minor flow model deviations. This demonstrated the method's robustness and applicability to architecturally-constrained human motion patterns.
- **Simulated Whale Tracking on Real-World Gulf Data:**
 - **Scenario:** A simulated underwater robot (1.16 knots actuation) was tasked with inspecting 6 whales migrating with ocean currents in the Gulf stream over one month.
 - **Results (Figure 1):** The approach successfully observed each whale, leveraging the ambient flow to ensure visitation before the pod separated.
 - **Analysis:** This highlights the method's capability to consider the optimal timing for visitation, sometimes delaying short-term visits to maximize long-term proportionality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of autonomous robotics by addressing the fundamental challenge of robust coverage in dynamic, flow-affected environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Ergodic Coverage:** The work extends the theoretical framework of ergodic search to time-varying domains, providing formal coverage guarantees for information distributions transported by dynamic vector fields. This addresses a recognized limitation of previous ergodic methods, which were largely restricted to static environments.
- **Enhanced Robotic Exploration in Dynamic Environments:** By integrating flow dynamics directly into the coverage objective, the proposed method enables autonomous systems to effectively explore environments where the search space or information distribution evolves. This is particularly relevant for applications in remote and extreme conditions like oceanography, atmospheric monitoring, or wildlife tracking, where static assumptions are often invalid.
- **Improved Efficiency for Resource-Constrained Systems:** The formulation supports robust open-loop planning, which is crucial for under-actuated and energy-constrained platforms (e.g., UxS, drones) that have limited onboard computational resources. By reducing the need for continuous replanning, it allows for longer mission durations and more stable trajectories.
- **Leveraging Ambient Dynamics:** The approach enables robots to leverage environmental flows to their advantage, improving mobility and strategically intercepting high-value features as they are advected. This contrasts with traditional planning methods that might primarily focus on counteracting flows, thereby potentially reducing energy consumption and increasing operational efficiency.
- **Versatility Across Diverse Applications:** The experimental validation demonstrates the method's broad applicability beyond fluid dynamics. Its success in modeling and planning for behavioral flows (e.g., cattle movement, human crowds) suggests its utility in surveillance, monitoring, and search and rescue operations within complex social or ecological systems. The concept of "pseudo-deterministic flows" induced by architectural constraints or universal attractors expands its practical scope.
- **Robustness to Model Imperfections:** The kernel-based spatial integration inherent in MMD provides a degree of robustness to minor deviations or uncertainties in flow models, as demonstrated in the human tracking experiment. This is important for real-world deployment where perfect flow knowledge is rarely available.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a theoretical and practical foundation for further research into adaptive ergodic control, particularly in addressing open challenges such as real-time recursive adaptation (e.g., MPC with flow model correction) and quantifying coverage guarantees in such dynamic, closed-loop settings.

In conclusion, this research offers a novel and effective solution for enabling autonomous robots to perform comprehensive and formally guaranteed coverage in environments characterized by continuous motion fields, with implications for a wide range of scientific, environmental, and security applications.
