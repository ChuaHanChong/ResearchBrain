# Stop Summation: Min-Form Credit Assignment Is All Process Reward Model Needs for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed PURE, a framework that leverages a novel min-form credit assignment to enable stable and efficient reinforcement fine-tuning of Large Language Models with process reward models. This method prevented training collapse, matched verifiable reward performance with a 3x efficiency gain, and achieved state-of-the-art results on mathematical reasoning benchmarks when combined with a small fraction of verifiable rewards.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) for LLMs with sparse verifiable rewards (VRs) faces credit assignment inefficiency, especially for long, complex reasoning paths.
- Process Reward Models (PRMs) offer dense step-by-step feedback, but their use in RFT leads to severe reward hacking and training instability due to the canonical summation-form credit assignment.
- A fundamental challenge is effectively utilizing PRMs for RFT without succumbing to reward hacking, which limits their potential to enhance LLM reasoning and training efficiency.

## Method

- Introduced "==min-form credit assignment==," redefining the value of a reasoning trajectory based on its lowest-scoring step, aligning with PRM usage in best-of-N selection.
- Developed the **PURE** (Process sUpervised Reinforcement lEarning) framework, which transforms step-level process rewards to implement min-form credit assignment, compatible with PPO-style RL frameworks.
- Employed a token-level baseline for advantage estimation to avoid biases against longer responses and explored a hybrid approach combining min-form process rewards with a small percentage of verifiable rewards.

## Results

- Min-form credit assignment in PURE successfully prevented training collapse observed with summation-form PRM methods, maintaining stable training over hundreds of steps.
- PURE-PRM achieved comparable mathematical reasoning performance to verifiable reward-based RFT but with approximately a **3x** reduction in required training steps.
- The hybrid PURE-PRM+VR approach yielded the highest performance, achieving **82.5%** accuracy on AMC23 and an average of **53.3%** across five mathematical benchmarks on Qwen2.5-Math-**7B**, outperforming PURE-VR by about 5 percentage points.
- Identified three distinct types of PRM-induced reward hacking ("only thinking, not solving", "extremely few steps (1 step)", "extremely few steps (0 step)") and analyzed training collapse caused by repetitive pseudo-positive samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The canonical summation-form credit assignment is identified as the root cause of reward hacking and training collapse when using PRMs for LLM reinforcement fine-tuning.
> - Min-form credit assignment effectively prevents reward hacking and stabilizes training by focusing on the weakest link in a reasoning chain, thereby making PRMs viable for RFT.
> - A small proportion of verifiable rewards (e.g., 10%) can synergistically combine with min-form process rewards to yield superior overall performance and robustness.
