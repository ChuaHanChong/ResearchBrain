# Toward Reliable Sim-to-Real Predictability for MoE-based Robust Quadrupedal Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Xi'an Jiaotong University developed a unified framework combining a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) policy and the RoboGauge predictive assessment suite to enhance quadrupedal locomotion. The MoE policy improved multi-terrain and command representation using only proprioception, while RoboGauge provided a reliable sim-to-real transferability metric using sim-to-sim evaluation, enabling high-speed (up to 4 m/s), robust real-world robot performance.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning policies optimized in simulation often fail to transfer reliably to physical quadrupedal robots due to the 'sim-to-real gap' and overfitting.
- Current simulation-based performance metrics are unreliable, necessitating extensive, risky, and inefficient physical validation for locomotion policies.
- Existing proprioception-only methods struggle with robustly representing complex multi-terrain and command spaces, limiting generalization capabilities.

## Method

- A ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture was integrated into the locomotion policy to enhance multi-terrain and command representation using only ==proprioceptive information==, decomposing latent modeling with ==gated specialist experts==.
- The **RoboGauge** predictive assessment framework was developed to quantitatively measure ==sim-to-real transferability== using ==sim-to-sim metrics== across diverse terrains, difficulties, and domain randomizations in MuJoCo.
- The training framework utilized an adapted reward function with ==hip joint regularization== and extensive ==domain randomization== in IsaacGym to facilitate robust policy learning and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- RoboGauge demonstrated lower average absolute errors (**0.0873**) when compared to real-world ground truth data, indicating higher reliability than IsaacGym metrics (**0.0221** for linear movement).
- The MoE method consistently achieved superior performance under RoboGauge, scoring **0.6713** overall and an average maximum terrain level of **7.85**, outperforming baselines such as CTS (**0.5786** score, **6.83** level).
- On a physical Unitree Go2 robot, the MoE model achieved **4.01 m/s** on flat ground, a **17/20** success rate over a 30 cm obstacle, and maintained a **100%** success rate during diverse outdoor field tests.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RoboGauge's sim-to-sim metrics, particularly when implemented in MuJoCo, provide a more accurate and reliable prediction of real-world robot performance compared to metrics derived from the training simulator.
> - The MoE architecture significantly enhances the discrimination of encoded features across diverse terrains and motion commands in the latent space, contributing to superior robustness and generalization.
> - A higher average training terrain level does not directly correlate with superior performance in predictive assessment, highlighting the need for specialized evaluation frameworks like RoboGauge to assess actual model transferability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Quadrupedal robots are recognized for their mobility and adaptability in complex and dynamic environments. Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a methodology for motion control, facilitating continuous policy optimization through simulation to enhance robot locomotion robustness. However, a significant challenge in this field is the "sim-to-real gap," where policies optimized in simulation often fail to perform reliably on physical hardware. This discrepancy arises because simulation-based performance metrics can be unreliable, and policies may overfit to the specific dynamics of the simulated environment, leading to a degradation in generalization capability in real-world scenarios.

Current RL approaches for quadrupedal locomotion primarily employ frameworks such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or teacher-student training to achieve multi-terrain traversal, often at velocities below 1 m/s. Advances in adaptability have been made through latent parameter estimation, recurrent belief encoders, and contrastive learning in parallelized simulations. Efforts have also pushed agility to higher velocities (e.g., 3.9 m/s) using command curricula, and diverse gaits have emerged from energy optimization rewards and multi-expert gating architectures.

A related challenge is the limited availability of robust evaluation frameworks for locomotion models. In contrast, robotic manipulation research has begun to address this by developing ranking metrics to verify consistency between simulation and reality. High-fidelity digital twins offer closed-loop assessment through environmental reconstruction but are often limited by high costs and scalability issues across diverse real-world scenarios. The absence of reliable quantitative proxies in locomotion research often necessitates direct physical validation, a process that is both risky for hardware and inefficient in terms of development time.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a unified framework that seeks to mitigate the sim-to-real problem directly. It introduces a novel predictive assessment suite to quantify sim-to-real transferability using sim-to-sim metrics, thereby reducing the dependency on extensive physical trials. Concurrently, it enhances the locomotion policy architecture with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model to improve terrain and command representation based solely on proprioception, aiming to achieve more robust and generalized locomotion. The work aims to provide a more reliable and efficient pathway from simulated training to real-world deployment for quadrupedal robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two significant challenges in developing agile and robust quadrupedal locomotion policies using reinforcement learning: the "sim-to-real gap" and the inefficiency of current policy validation methods. Despite the promise of RL for quadrupedal locomotion with proprioception-only sensing, policies trained in simulation frequently fail to transfer effectively to physical robots. This failure is often attributed to the sim-to-real gap and reward overfitting, particularly in complex simulated terrains, where policies may become overly specialized to simulator dynamics.

Current practices necessitate extensive and costly physical validation, which is inherently risky for hardware and inefficient for iterative development. There is a recognized need for reliable quantitative proxies that can accurately predict real-world performance based solely on simulation-based evaluation, thus enabling researchers to select policies with high transferability without direct physical deployment. Additionally, existing methods that rely solely on proprioception often exhibit limitations in representing complex multi-terrain and command spaces, impacting their generalization capabilities.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To propose RoboGauge:** A comprehensive predictive assessment framework designed to quantitatively measure sim-to-real transferability. This framework utilizes a sim-to-sim methodology across multiple metrics, terrains, difficulty levels, and domain randomizations to provide a reliable proxy for real-world performance. The aim is to mitigate hardware damage risks associated with direct physical deployment.
2. **To integrate a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture:** This module is incorporated into the locomotion policy to enhance multi-terrain and command representation. By decomposing latent terrain and command modeling using a gated set of specialist experts, the MoE policy aims to achieve superior deployment robustness and generalization using only proprioceptive information, thereby avoiding the fragility of exteroceptive sensors in extreme conditions.
3. **To develop a unified framework:** This framework integrates the MoE policy with RoboGauge to enable reliable policy selection and facilitate robust deployment of quadrupedal robots for agile locomotion across diverse challenging environments.
4. **To demonstrate high-performance locomotion:** The research aims to show that the proposed framework enables the robot to achieve high-speed locomotion, such as 4 m/s on flat terrain, and exhibit emergent stable gaits, like a narrow-width gait, associated with improved stability at high velocities.
5. **To validate generalization capabilities:** The objective includes demonstrating the robot's ability to operate reliably in diverse, challenging, and previously unseen real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a unified closed-loop training framework comprising a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) locomotion policy and the RoboGauge predictive assessment suite.

**MoE Latent Representation Learning (Training Phase):**
The locomotion control task is formulated as an infinite-horizon Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The objective is to learn an optimal policy that maps proprioceptive observations to joint torque commands. Observations include angular velocity, projected gravity, joint positions and velocities, linear and yaw rate commands, and the previous action. Actions are joint position offsets, applied via a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller.

The MoE architecture is integrated into the student encoder of a Concurrent Teacher-Student (CTS) framework. This network consists of *K* parallel expert subnetworks, each specializing in processing observation data under specific command types or environmental contexts. A gating network dynamically allocates weights to these experts based on the observation sequence, ensuring that the resulting latent state is a weighted sum of expert outputs. An auxiliary load balancing loss encourages uniform task distribution among experts, promoting representational diversity.

The reward function is adapted from established methodologies and features specific additions. A hip joint position reward is introduced to mitigate outward thigh abduction during rapid locomotion. For high-speed flat-ground locomotion, an external hip symmetry reward regularizes joint positions. The vertical linear velocity reward weight dynamically decays to zero, and the base height reward weight is adjusted to mitigate body sagging.

Training is conducted in the IsaacGym simulation environment, utilizing 8192 parallel agents on a Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robot. The environment incorporates a curriculum encompassing seven terrains (flat, wave, slope, rough slope, stairs up/down, obstacle) with varying difficulty levels. To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, extensive domain randomization is applied to parameters such as friction, payload mass, link mass, base center of mass, restitution, PD gains, actuator strength and offset, and control latency. Enhancements to the training process include dynamic velocity tracking precision adjustment, which scales constraints based on terrain difficulty and command magnitude, and a comprehensive command design suite. This suite features a command curriculum (gradually increasing command ranges), extreme command sampling (prioritizing stationary and maximum velocity commands), and dynamic command sampling (ensuring sufficient cumulative command length for terrain level progression).

**RoboGauge Predictive Assessment Framework (Evaluation Module):**
RoboGauge is implemented in the MuJoCo simulation environment and operates through a three-stage hierarchical evaluation process:

1. **BasePipeline:** Performs atomic, single-environment evaluations incorporating specific terrains and domain randomizations, managing exception handling and observation noise.
2. **Multi/Level Pipeline:** Conducts parallelized difficulty assessments and domain randomizations across diverse random seeds. It employs a binary search strategy to identify the maximum difficulty level a model can successfully navigate (defined by an 80% success rate in a target-reaching task).
3. **Stress Pipeline:** Synthesizes a unified robustness score by aggregating results across the entire terrain suite.

The framework employs six quantitative performance metrics, normalized to a range where higher values indicate better performance. These metrics address three critical aspects:
- **Hardware Safety and Efficiency:** Dof power consumption and Dof limits (joint angles exceeding soft limits).
- **Tracking Precision:** Linear velocity error and angular velocity error.
- **Motion Stability:** Torque smoothness and orientation stability (gravity projection on the lateral axis).
Additionally, two formal physical criteria are integrated: the Zero Moment Point (ZMP) margin, which quantifies the horizontal distance error of the ZMP relative to the geometric center of active contacts, and a Coulomb friction margin, which computes the normal-force-weighted average slack to the friction-cone boundary.

The evaluation environment utilizes a systematic matrix of motion goals (e.g., maximum velocity, emergency stops, diagonal steps, target position tasks), five distinct terrain categories (flat, wave, slopes, stairs, obstacles), each with 10 difficulty levels, and a comprehensive set of domain randomizations (environmental factors and robot properties).

A hierarchical scoring methodology aggregates these metrics. For a given terrain, randomization, and difficulty level, an execution quality score is computed using a weighted geometric mean of the normalized metrics, with a worst-case mean aggregation strategy for motion goals. The terrain quality score `S_i,j` balances the maximum passable difficulty level `L*_i,j` with the execution quality score `Q_i,j(L*_i,j)`. The final framework score is obtained by arithmetically averaging the robust scores across all terrains and domain randomizations. Multiprocessing is used to accelerate concurrent environment instances.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings from both simulation-based evaluations using RoboGauge and real-world physical deployment.

**RoboGauge Metric Reliability:**
Experimental comparisons demonstrated that RoboGauge, utilizing the MuJoCo simulation, yielded significantly lower errors when its metrics were compared against real-world ground truth data (captured via a motion capture system) than metrics obtained from the IsaacGym training environment. For instance, the average absolute error for linear, lateral, and angular movements was 0.0873 in RoboGauge versus 0.0221 for linear movement in IsaacGym, with varying trends for other categories. This indicates that RoboGauge provides a more accurate reflection of real-world performance, offering a dependable basis for model selection.

**Comparison of Baselines under RoboGauge:**
The proposed MoE-based method was benchmarked against state-of-the-art proprioception-only RL algorithms: DreamWaQ, HIM, and CTS. Across all evaluated metrics within the RoboGauge framework (overall score, tracking, safety, quality, and maximum terrain level), the MoE method consistently achieved superior performance. For example, the MoE model achieved an overall RoboGauge score of 0.6713 and an average maximum terrain level of 7.85, outperforming CTS (0.5786 score, 6.83 level), HIM (0.5379 score, 6.19 level), and DreamWaQ (0.5054 score, 5.74 level). Visualizations further indicated that the MoE policy consistently surpassed baselines across all seven evaluated terrains. Importantly, the study noted that a higher average training terrain level did not directly correlate with superior performance in the predictive RoboGauge assessment, emphasizing RoboGauge's role in evaluating actual model capabilities. The MoE model also demonstrated consistent proficiency across a range of friction coefficients.

**Ablation and Latent Representation of MoE:**
Ablation studies on the MoE structure, including variants like MoE-NG (excluding command info from MoE input), AC-MoE (MoE on Actor-Critic networks), and MCP (multiplicative composition for actions), showed that the proposed MoE method achieved the best performance. Variants like AC-MoE and MCP were observed to be prone to loss divergence during training, suggesting instability when expert combinations directly influence the action space. PCA visualizations of the student encoder latent space revealed that the MoE architecture achieved superior discrimination of encoded features across diverse terrains and motion commands compared to a vanilla CTS setup.

**Physical Deployment and Generalization (Unitree Go2 Robot):**
Real-world experiments on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot demonstrated the framework's effectiveness:

- **Terrain Challenges:** The MoE model exhibited superior robustness. It achieved an 18/20 survival rate under sudden lateral pulls (80-100 N), significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., Built-in RL: 5/20, CTS: 11/20). On 15.5 cm tile stairs, the MoE model successfully traversed 85/85 steps, compared to baselines achieving 12-24/85. It was the only model to successfully surmount a 30 cm obstacle, with a 17/20 success rate.
- **Velocity Tracking Precision:** The robot demonstrated robust tracking proficiency, traversing stairs at an average speed of 1.31 m/s with a tracking error of 0.15 m/s. On a 30-degree wooden slope, it maintained an average velocity of 1.53 m/s, completing traversal 1.7 seconds faster than a built-in RL baseline. In high-speed tests on flat ground, the robot reached a peak velocity of 4.01 m/s within 2.16 seconds with a 0.20 m/s tracking error, showing effective acceleration and braking. An emergent narrow-base gait was observed, contributing to stability at high velocities.
- **Stability and Generalization:** The model displayed effective emergency recovery capabilities, maintaining stability under continuous lateral pulls (25-40 N) and sudden impulses (85-100 N). It also successfully recovered from a 60 cm drop and an unexpected fall from flat ground onto stairs, rapidly reconfiguring its gait. Field tests in diverse outdoor environments, including sand, ice, slopes, and uneven terrains, resulted in a 100% success rate with no unexpected terminations, indicating robust generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of quadrupedal locomotion by addressing critical challenges related to the sim-to-real gap and the efficiency of policy development. The introduction of the RoboGauge predictive assessment suite represents a methodological advancement, providing a quantitative and multi-dimensional framework for evaluating sim-to-real transferability using only simulation-based metrics. This capability is significant as it offers a more reliable and less risky alternative to extensive physical validation, potentially accelerating the development cycle for robust locomotion policies and reducing the incidence of hardware damage.

The integration of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture into the locomotion policy, relying solely on proprioception, demonstrates an effective strategy for enhancing multi-terrain and command representation. This approach improves policy robustness and generalization, allowing robots to navigate complex and previously unseen environments without relying on external sensors that can fail in challenging conditions. The empirical results, particularly the demonstrated high-speed locomotion (4 m/s), successful traversal of significant obstacles (30 cm), and robust disturbance rejection (100 N impulses), indicate a notable increase in the performance ceiling for proprioception-only quadrupedal control.

The framework’s ability to achieve a 100% success rate in diverse outdoor field tests highlights its practical utility and the generalization capabilities of the learned policies. This suggests that the proposed synergy between predictive assessment and a modular policy architecture provides a pathway toward more dependable and efficient deployment of agile quadrupedal robots in real-world applications.

Potential future impacts of this work include:

- **Accelerated Robotics Development:** By offering a more reliable simulation-to-real performance prediction, the framework could significantly shorten the iteration cycles for designing and validating robot control policies.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The demonstrated robustness and generalization across diverse terrains and challenges could lead to robots that are more capable of operating autonomously in unstructured and dynamic environments.
- **Broader Applicability:** The methodologies, particularly the RoboGauge framework, could be extended to assess other robotic morphologies, such as humanoid robots, and integrated with exteroceptive perception systems to enable navigation through more complex structural obstacles. This expansion could further bridge the gap between simulated learning and real-world robotic capabilities across a wider range of tasks.
