# ZeroMimic: Distilling Robotic Manipulation Skills from Web Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Pennsylvania researchers develop a system that enables robots to learn manipulation skills directly from web videos without requiring robot demonstrations, achieving 71.0% success rate in real-world tasks through a two-phase approach combining human affordance prediction and trajectory learning from tracked wrist movements.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic imitation learning requires expensive, time-consuming robot-specific demonstrations
- Significant domain gap exists between human videos and robot execution due to differences in embodiment, perspective, and environments

## Method

- Two-phase system combining grasping (using affordance prediction and grasp generation) and post-grasp trajectory following
- Action chunking transformer policy trained on human wrist trajectories extracted from web videos
- Zero-shot transfer methodology enabling direct deployment without robot-specific training

## Results

- **71.0%** success rate in real-world tasks and **73.8%** in simulation without robot-specific training
- Successful generalization to unseen objects and different robot embodiments
- Outperforms existing zero-shot robotic systems on several manipulation tasks
- Limited by perception module performance and camera-robot configuration sensitivity

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relative action representations outperform absolute representations for trajectory learning
> - Human affordance-based grasping improves task-relevant grasp selection
> - Camera information and structured motion analysis essential for accurate wrist trajectory extraction

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Imitation Learning and Robotics:** ZeroMimic addresses a significant challenge in imitation learning for robotics. Traditional imitation learning requires robot-specific demonstrations, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. This limits the scalability of the approach for general-purpose robots.
- **Learning from Human Video:** The paper taps into the vast potential of readily available human video datasets on the web. This is a compelling direction because it circumvents the data bottleneck of robot-specific demonstrations. Learning from "in-the-wild" human videos presents significant challenges due to differences in embodiment, action spaces, and the inherent noise and variability in such data.
- **Bridging the Domain Gap:** A key aspect of the research landscape is the "domain gap" between human demonstrations and robot execution. Human videos are collected from a different perspective, with different kinematics, and in different environments than the robot operates. ZeroMimic tackles this gap by abstracting action spaces and using visual understanding techniques to transfer knowledge effectively.
- **Comparison to Related Work:** The paper provides a good overview of related work, categorizing approaches based on the generality of the source human videos and the level of knowledge transfer. It positions ZeroMimic as a novel approach that directly generates policies from in-the-wild videos without requiring any additional robot data. The paper clearly differentiates ZeroMimic from related works such as R3M, VIP, MimicPlay, WHIRL, DITTO, R+X, and H2R, by highlighting its unique contributions in addressing the challenges of learning from diverse and unstructured web data.
- **Vision Language Models (VLMs):** The paper also acknowledges the recent surge in leveraging VLMs for robotics but points out their limitations in physical understanding and inference speed. The paper directly compared ZeroMimic with a VLM method (ReKep) and shows that ZeroMimic can outperform VLMs in certain robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop a system (ZeroMimic) that can distill useful robotic skill policies from web videos without requiring robot-specific demonstrations or exploration.
- **Motivation:** The motivation stems from the desire to overcome the data acquisition bottleneck in robotics and leverage the abundance of human demonstration videos available on the web. The idea is to create robots that can learn complex manipulation skills simply by watching humans perform them, similar to how humans learn new skills.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** A critical objective is to achieve zero-shot transfer, meaning that the learned policies can be deployed on robots in diverse, unseen environments without further training or fine-tuning.
- **Generalization:** Another implicit objective is to enable robots to generalize to new objects and task setups.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **System Overview:** ZeroMimic is a two-phase system consisting of a grasping phase and a post-grasp phase.
- **Grasping Phase:** The grasping phase uses a combination of human affordance prediction (VRB) and robot grasp generation (AnyGrasp). VRB predicts the appropriate contact region based on the human video, and AnyGrasp selects a suitable grasp for the robot's gripper within that region. This allows the robot to select task-relevant grasps based on human demonstrations.
- **Post-Grasp Phase:** The post-grasp phase uses an imitation learning policy trained on human wrist trajectories extracted from web videos. The wrist trajectories are extracted using a hand-tracking model (HaMeR) and a structure-from-motion algorithm (COLMAP). An action chunking transformer (ACT) policy is then trained to predict future wrist poses given the current image, goal image, and current wrist pose.
- **Data:** The system is trained on the EpicKitchens dataset, a large-scale dataset of egocentric human videos of daily activities in the kitchen.
- **Implementation Details:** The paper provides details on the training of the ACT policy, the action representation (relative 6D wrist poses), and the retargeting of the human wrist policy to the robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Zero-Shot Performance:** ZeroMimic achieves impressive zero-shot performance in both real-world and simulated environments. The system achieves an overall success rate of 71.0% in the real world and 73.8% in simulation.
- **Generalization Ability:** The system demonstrates the ability to generalize to new objects unseen in the training data and to different robot embodiments.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies confirm the importance of each component of the system, including the human affordance-based grasping and the camera information for wrist trajectory extraction. The paper also shows the ACT and Diffusion Policy architectures yield similar performance. Relative actions in both translation and orientation significantly outperform absolute representations.
- **Comparison to Other Systems:** ZeroMimic outperforms a state-of-the-art zero-shot robotic system (ReKep) on several tasks.
- **Failure Analysis:** A failure analysis reveals that the system is limited by the performance of the underlying modules, such as AnyGrasp, VRB, and the post-grasp policy. Common failure modes include point cloud sensing failures, incorrect contact location predictions, and sensitivity to camera-robot configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Advancement of Imitation Learning:** ZeroMimic represents a significant advancement in imitation learning by enabling robots to learn from readily available human video data. This can significantly reduce the cost and time required to train robots for complex manipulation tasks.
- **Scalable Robot Learning:** The approach offers a more scalable way to train robots for general-purpose tasks. By leveraging the vast amount of human demonstration videos on the web, robots can potentially learn a wide range of skills without requiring robot-specific data collection.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** The work has implications for human-robot interaction. By learning from human videos, robots can potentially learn to perform tasks in a way that is more natural and intuitive for humans.
- **Real-World Applications:** The system has potential applications in various domains, including household robotics, manufacturing, and healthcare.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper highlights several limitations of the current system, such as the need for improved perception and the inability to handle in-hand manipulation and two-arm tasks. These limitations suggest directions for future research. The success of ZeroMimic shows the viability of distilling robotic skills from web videos and shows that by improving the underlying perception model and increasing the amount of training data, ZeroMimic's performance can be further improved.

In summary, "ZeroMimic: Distilling Robotic Manipulation Skills from Web Videos" presents a novel and significant contribution to the field of robotics. It addresses a key challenge in imitation learning by enabling robots to learn from readily available human video data. The system achieves impressive zero-shot performance and demonstrates the potential for scalable robot learning.
