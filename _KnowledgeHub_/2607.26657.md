# Enfold: Folding World-Generator Computation into Predictive Representations for Efficient Embodied Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Enfold introduces a method to reduce action latency in embodied control by internalizing the complex computations of a world generative model into a predictive representation derived solely from the present. This approach achieved up to a 10.1x speedup in action chunk prediction (from 493 ms to 49 ms using Enfold-Flash) while maintaining or improving control performance on diverse manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Integrating world generative models (WGMs) into robot control loops typically incurs high computational costs and significant action latency due to the need for explicit future video generation at each step.
- Existing methods for leveraging WGMs either involve costly video co-training or use encoded future features, which might not fully capture the intricate multi-level dynamics of how a future is constructed.
- The challenge lies in obtaining rich predictive information from WGMs without requiring their real-time execution during robot inference, balancing prediction quality with efficiency.

## Method

- The **Enfold** framework 'enfolds' future-conditioned generative dynamics into a current-only predictive representation ('z') using a novel ==bidirectional coupling strategy== during training.
- It supervises a predictive encoder to learn 'z' by predicting the generative world model's internal multi-level hidden states (==G2R learning==) as it processes real future video, rather than predicting future pixels or features.
- Task-specific action heads consume a detached version of 'z', and the generative model itself is conditioned by 'z' (==R2G learning==), but critically, task and R2G gradients do not flow back to shape the predictive encoder, ensuring 'z' learns purely from generative dynamics.

## Results

- Enfold achieved a **97.8%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, reducing action latency to **134 ms** (**3.7x** faster than Fast-WAM), with Enfold-Flash further cutting latency to **49 ms** (**10.1x** faster).
- It demonstrated strong performance in real-world bimanual manipulation, outperforming Fast-WAM by **11.9 percentage points** in-distribution and showing qualitative adaptability to manual scene alterations mid-task.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multi-level generator state supervision yielded the highest success (**97.8%**), and conditioning the generator with 'z' improved future video prediction quality (e.g., PSNR increased from **25.92 dB** to **27.27 dB**), validating the bidirectional training approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The complex, multi-level internal computation of a world generator, which organizes appearance and interaction across scales, can be effectively distilled into a representation inferred solely from the current visual context and language instruction.
> - Supervising with multi-level generator states provides more stable and comprehensive information for representation learning compared to single-level states, future pixels, or actions alone, yielding better control performance.
> - The learned representation 'z' filters out nuisance variations (e.g., lighting sensitivity) and focuses on dynamic interaction structures relevant for future changes, allowing for efficient, robust control and adaptability to scene interventions without explicit future generation at inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses challenges in embodied control for robots, particularly concerning the computational cost and latency associated with integrating world generative models (WGMs) into control loops. Traditional approaches to leveraging WGMs for robotics often involve materializing a visual future (imagine-then-act systems) or jointly modeling videos and actions within a single, often costly, decoding process. While these methods allow robots to anticipate scene changes and interactions, they typically incur significant action latency due to the generative model's execution at each control step.

Recent research has attempted to mitigate this dependency on explicit video generation. Some methods retain video co-training but skip explicit future generation at test time (e.g., Fast–WAM), while others consume internal video-model features or reusable latent context (e.g., predictive policies, asynchronous WAMs, ImageWAM). VLA–JEPA, for instance, predicts future target-encoder features from a current-only pathway. These advancements demonstrate that predictive information need not be carried by a rendered future.

Enfold extends this line of inquiry by investigating whether the *internal computation* that constructs a future can itself be transferred into a representation derived solely from the present. Unlike prior work that uses existing visual encoder features or directly processes generative model outputs, Enfold aims to internalize the complex, multi-level generative dynamics of a WGM into a current-only predictive representation. This positions Enfold at the intersection of generative world modeling, predictive representation learning, and efficient vision-language-action (VLA) models, seeking to extract the "how" of future construction into a readily available control signal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable efficient embodied control by integrating the predictive power of world generative models without incurring their typical computational overhead during deployment. The authors argue that while rendered future videos or latent contexts from generative branches are commonly used, the more reusable asset of a world generator is the *computation* it performs to construct a future.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Internalizing Generative Computation:** To determine if the internal, multi-level computation of a world generator—which organizes appearance, spatial layout, and interaction across various levels of abstraction as it transforms a corrupted future into a coherent trajectory—can be transferred into a representation predicted solely from the current visual context and language instruction. This contrasts with existing methods that rely on executing the generator or its costly branches at inference time.
- **Reducing Action Latency:** A significant practical motivation is to reduce the action latency inherent in systems that rely on explicit video generation for control. By moving the generator's contribution to an offline training phase and making the predictive representation available from the present alone, Enfold aims to achieve faster action chunk prediction during real-time robot operation.
- **Developing a Reusable Predictive Representation:** The goal is to learn a representation that is not only efficient but also robust, retaining the generator's transition-relevant structure while suppressing nuisance variations that are difficult to predict from the present. This representation should be capable of supporting both future generation (unfolding a visual future) and direct action readouts without being reshaped by task-specific gradients, thereby serving as a shared, general-purpose interface.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Supervision Targets:** The authors motivate the use of generator states as supervision by highlighting limitations of alternative targets. Action supervision provides only indirect evidence of scene evolution. Future pixels, while dense, require the representation to preserve all visual details, including potentially irrelevant appearance variation. Features from a frozen future encoder are compact but describe an encoded endpoint, not the computational process of constructing the future. Generator states, conversely, expose how the observed transition is progressively organized across different scales and corruption levels, making them a promising, yet challenging, supervision space.
- **Ensuring Adaptability and Counterfactual Consistency:** Beyond efficiency and raw performance, the research aims to demonstrate that the learned representation supports adaptive behavior. The motivation is to achieve a system that can re-imagine continuations and adjust actions when the current scene is altered (e.g., by human intervention), indicating a form of context-sensitive replanning rather than mere fixed trajectory replay.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Enfold proposes a framework that "enfolds" future-conditioned generative dynamics into a representation inferred from the present. This representation serves as a shared interface for both unfolding a visual future and directly reading out actions. The core components include a predictive encoder, a generative world model, and lightweight prediction and task heads, coupled in their forward computation but separated in optimization.

#### 3.1. Components

- **Predictive Encoder (Uϕ):** Takes the current video context (`c`) and language instruction (`e`) as input to produce a compact representation `z = Uϕ(c, e)`.
- **Generative World Model (Gθ):** Instantiated as a conditional latent video generator (specifically, Cosmos-Predict 2.5 2B). This model is trained to synthesize future observations.
- **Prediction Head (Fω):** A timestep-conditioned head that maps the representation `z` to the generator's internal states.
- **Task Heads (Dψ):** Lightweight readouts that consume the representation `z` to predict task-specific outputs, such as robot actions.

#### 3.2. Training Mechanisms

Enfold employs a bidirectional coupling strategy during training, ensuring that `z` captures generative dynamics without being directly influenced by task-specific gradients:

- **Generation-to-Representation (G2R) Learning:**
 - The generative world model `Gθ` processes a *real, observed future video chunk* (`y0`), corrupted at a specific timestep `t` (`yt`), along with the current context (`c`), instruction (`e`), and a *detached* version of the predictive representation (`¯z = sg(z)`).
 - During this teacher-forced pass, multi-level hidden states (`hGℓ,t`) from selected generator blocks are exposed. These states, which capture how the generator organizes the observed transition, are concatenated (`A(·)`) and layer-normalized to form the target `rGt`.
 - The predictive encoder `Uϕ` (via `Fω`) learns to predict this multi-level target `rGt` from the current-only representation `z` and a timestep embedding `γ(t)`. The loss `LG2R` uses SmoothL1 regression.
 - **Crucial Design Choice:** Gradients from `rGt` are stopped (`sg(rGt)`). This ensures that `Uϕ` is trained to predict future-dependent structure from the *present context only*, without direct access to the future `y` or explicit future-generation targets during its own learning. The choice of multi-level states is motivated by an analysis showing that different generator depths offer complementary utility and stability for supervision across various corruption levels.

- **Representation-to-Generation (R2G) Learning:**
 - A *detached copy* of `z` (`¯z = sg(z)`) is fed back to condition `Gθ` during its own future generation process (objective `LR2G` using flow matching).
 - This objective teaches `Gθ` to effectively *use* the learned representation `¯z` to improve its future modeling capabilities. Detaching `z` prevents `LR2G` from directly shaping `Uϕ`. This path serves as a functional test to confirm that `z` contains useful predictive information for the generator itself.

- **Detached Task Readouts:**
 - Task heads `Dψ` (e.g., action heads for embodied control) read a *detached* representation `sg(z)`.
 - The task loss `Ltask` (e.g., conditional flow-matching for actions) updates `Dψ`.
 - **Crucial Design Choice:** Task gradients are prevented from flowing back into `Uϕ`. This ensures that `Uϕ`'s learning is guided solely by the generative dynamics exposed through G2R, rather than being biased by specific task objectives or acting as a policy backbone directly.

#### 3.3. Optimization and Inference

- The overall objective function combines the three losses: `L = LG2R + λR2G LR2G + λtask Ltask`.
- Gradient routes are distinct: `Uϕ` and `Fω` are updated by `LG2R`, `Gθ` by `LR2G`, and `Dψ` by `Ltask`.
- **At inference time**, only `Uϕ` is executed once to obtain `z`. Actions are then predicted directly from `z` by `Dψ`. The generative world model `Gθ` is *not executed* as part of the control loop, only if explicit video imagination is requested. This algorithmic separation is the primary source of latency reduction.
- **Enfold-Flash** is an optimized variant that applies TensorRT operator-level acceleration without altering the model architecture or inference path, providing further speedups.

#### 3.4. Backbones and Configuration

- The predictive visual encoder uses DINOv3 ViT-H+/16.
- The generative world model is Cosmos-Predict 2.5 2B.
- Language features are derived from Cosmos-Reason1-7B.
- Experiments are conducted across LIBERO, RoboTwin2.0, and real-robot tasks, with specific configurations for camera views, input resolutions, and action head depths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several key findings across control performance, adaptability, ablation studies, and representation analysis:

#### 4.1. Efficient Control without Video Rollout

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** Enfold achieved an average success rate of 97.8%, which is 0.2 percentage points higher than Fast–WAM, without requiring embodied pre-training. Crucially, it reduced action latency to 134 ms per action chunk, a 3.7× speedup compared to Fast–WAM (493 ms). Enfold-Flash, utilizing TensorRT, further reduced latency to 49 ms (10.1× faster) with a minimal performance decrease to 97.5% success. This indicates that removing generative rollout does not compromise control quality while significantly improving efficiency.
- **RoboTwin2.0:** Enfold obtained an average success rate of 91.77% across clean and randomized scenes, with Enfold-Flash reaching 92.02%. These results are comparable to or slightly above Fast–WAM and close to top-performing models like LingBot–VA, demonstrating its effectiveness in dual-arm manipulation and robustness to scene randomization.
- **Real-World Bimanual Manipulation:** On four AgileX dual-arm tasks, Enfold achieved an average normalized completion score of 89.7% in-distribution, outperforming Fast–WAM by 11.9 points and π0.5 by 3.6 points. In out-of-distribution (OOD) settings (e.g., altered object placement or appearance), Enfold scored 76.6% (6.6 points above Fast–WAM), although π0.5 showed stronger OOD aggregate performance at 83.3%. This suggests Enfold's representation facilitates robust control beyond the demonstrated data.

#### 4.2. Reimagining and Recovery After Intervention

- Qualitative experiments on Store Plate and Fold Towel tasks demonstrated that when the scene was manually altered (e.g., plate displaced, cloth geometry changed) mid-task, Enfold's predicted future continuation and subsequent executed actions adapted to the modified scene. This behavior suggests that the model recomputes its plan based on the new visual context rather than replaying a fixed, pre-computed trajectory, providing evidence of counterfactual consistency.

#### 4.3. Dissecting the Bidirectional Coupling

- **G2R (Generator States as Supervision):** Ablation studies on LIBERO showed that multi-level generator states as supervision yielded 97.8% success, outperforming single-level states (96.3%), future-pixel prediction (96.0%), and action-only training (94.9%). The gains from multi-level supervision were particularly evident in LIBERO-Goal and LIBERO-Long tasks (2.8 points improvement over single-level), implying that complementary information from different generative depths is valuable for complex, multi-stage interactions.
- **R2G (Representation-to-Generation Learning):** Conditioning the generator on the learned representation `z` improved future video prediction quality. PSNR increased from 25.92 dB to 27.27 dB, SSIM from 0.885 to 0.902, and LPIPS decreased from 0.0550 to 0.0483. This indicates that `z` effectively reorganizes the available context into a more useful form for the generator, validating the bidirectional coupling.

#### 4.4. What Enfold Encodes (Representation Analyses)

- **Stability and Nuisance Suppression:** The Enfold predictive encoder demonstrated a 7.9–10.4× reduction in lighting sensitivity compared to raw Cosmos generator layers, indicating it effectively suppresses nuisance variation. Simultaneously, its effective rank was higher (31.8 vs. max 10.7 for generator features), suggesting it creates a more diverse and stable representation rather than suffering from feature collapse.
- **Predicting Future Change:** Probing the representation's ability to predict future frozen-DINO features showed that Enfold's representation (from the current frame) outperformed using only current-frame DINO features, particularly on patches that undergo significant change and at longer future horizons. For instance, on the most-changing tokens, it reduced residual cosine error by 18.1% and 18.2% at future times t=16 and t=32, respectively. This suggests the representation focuses on dynamics relevant to future transformations rather than merely being a better static image descriptor.
- **Interaction Structure in Token Geometry:** Visualization of token similarities revealed that while frozen DINO primarily associated a gripper query point with visually similar parts of the arm, Enfold's representation extended high similarity to the manipulated object and contact-relevant regions, even before physical contact. This indicates that the generator-mediated supervision encourages the representation to organize around the relational and interaction structure crucial for future manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Enfold represents a shift in how world generative models can be leveraged for embodied AI. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that the complex, future-conditioned computation of a generative model can be effectively *internalized* into a predictive representation derived from the present, rather than requiring the generator's execution at every control step.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Paradigm Shift in World Model Utilization:** Enfold redefines the role of world generators, transforming them from mandatory action-time simulators into powerful training-time supervisors for structured predictive representation learning. This provides a new pathway for extracting and utilizing the rich dynamics knowledge embedded within generative models.
- **Significant Efficiency Gains for Embodied Control:** The substantial reduction in action latency (up to 10.1× faster than previous methods) has direct implications for real-world robot deployment. Faster response times can enable more agile and reactive control, critical for dynamic and safety-sensitive tasks.
- **Development of More Robust and Adaptive Robot Policies:** The learned predictive representation, which filters out nuisance variation and focuses on future-dependent interaction structures, inherently leads to more stable and robust control. The qualitative evidence of adaptability to human intervention suggests a step towards policies capable of counterfactual reasoning and dynamic replanning in uncertain environments.
- **Generalizable Representation Learning:** By learning a representation that is useful for both future generation and direct action prediction, Enfold proposes a general-purpose predictive interface that could serve as a foundation for a broader range of embodied tasks. The separation of optimization paths (G2R shaping the encoder, R2G and task heads reading from a detached representation) contributes to the generality and reusability of the learned `z`.
- **Insights into Predictive Information Encoding:** The detailed representation analyses offer insights into what information generator-mediated supervision encourages. The findings that Enfold suppresses nuisance, predicts changes over longer horizons, and organizes interaction structure within its token geometry advance the understanding of how predictive representations for dynamic environments can be effectively learned.

While the generator is still required during the training phase and the intervention evidence remains qualitative, Enfold establishes a principle for efficiently distilling rich generative knowledge. This work opens avenues for future research into further enhancing representation robustness, extending adaptability to larger distribution shifts, and exploring the broader applicability of internalizing complex computational processes for more efficient and intelligent AI systems.
