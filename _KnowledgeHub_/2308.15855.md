# IIDM: Inter and Intra-domain Mixing for Semi-supervised Domain Adaptation in Semantic Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> IIDM introduces a semi-supervised domain adaptation (SSDA) framework for semantic segmentation that improves model performance by combining inter-domain and intra-domain data mixing strategies. The framework achieves 69.5% mIoU on the GTA5→Cityscapes benchmark with only 100 labeled target images, outperforming existing SSDA methods by significant margins (e.g., +8.3% mIoU over DLDM).

## Problem

- Semantic segmentation models experience performance degradation when applied to real-world target domains due to domain shift from source domains, hindering practical deployment.
- Current Semi-Supervised Domain Adaptation (SSDA) methods for semantic segmentation often under-exploit the intra-domain information within the target domain, specifically the relationship between a small set of labeled target data and a larger set of unlabeled target data.
- Existing approaches primarily focus on the inter-domain gap (source to target) and general unlabeled target data exploitation, neglecting the 'intra-domain gap' inherent in the limited, potentially biased, labeled target subset.

## Method

- Proposed **IIDM (Inter and Intra-domain Mixing)**, a novel SSDA framework based on a ==mean teacher== architecture that integrates supervised learning with two unsupervised mixing strategies.
- Utilized ==ClassMix== for **Inter-domain Mixing**, blending source images (with ground truth) and unlabeled target images (with teacher-generated pseudo-labels) to mitigate the domain shift between source and target.
- Implemented ==ClassMix== for **Intra-domain Mixing**, combining labeled target images (with ground truth) and unlabeled target images (with pseudo-labels) to enrich the target domain's internal representation and address the gap within the target domain itself.

## Results

- IIDM consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance across GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes benchmarks, particularly when labeled target data was scarce.
- On GTA5→Cityscapes, IIDM obtained **69.5% mIoU** with only **100** labeled target images, outperforming DLDM by **+8.3% mIoU** and surpassing some UDA methods (using 0 labeled target images) by **+6.8% mIoU**.
- The framework proved orthogonal and complementary to advanced UDA techniques, with IIDM+HRDA+MIC achieving **78.6% mIoU** with **100** labeled target images, indicating robust and extensible performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effectively addressing both the inter-domain gap (source to target) and the intra-domain gap (labeled target to unlabeled target) concurrently is essential for robust SSDA in semantic segmentation.
> - Leveraging the internal relationships within the target domain, specifically between limited labeled data and abundant unlabeled data, can significantly enhance feature learning and model performance.
> - Applying both inter- and intra-domain mixing on the same unlabeled target image through separate streams improves the learning of consistent and domain-invariant feature representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Semantic segmentation models have demonstrated advancements in performance; however, their deployment in real-world scenarios is often hindered by performance degradation attributed to domain shift. This issue arises when models trained on a source domain (e.g., synthetic datasets like GTA5 or SYNTHIA) are applied to a target domain with different data distributions (e.g., real-world datasets like Cityscapes).

The existing research landscape in domain adaptation for semantic segmentation is largely dominated by Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA). UDA methods aim to bridge the domain gap using only labeled source data and unlabeled target data. Common UDA techniques include adversarial training for distribution alignment and pseudo-labeling for unlabeled target data. While UDA has shown progress, the assumption of having no labeled target data can be restrictive and limit model performance in practical applications.

Semi-Supervised Domain Adaptation (SSDA) emerges as a more practical scenario, addressing the limitations of UDA by leveraging a small amount of labeled target data in addition to labeled source data and a large volume of unlabeled target data. Despite its practical relevance, SSDA has received comparatively less research attention in semantic segmentation than UDA.

Current SSDA methods in semantic segmentation are often influenced by UDA paradigms. They primarily focus on exploiting unlabeled target data and source data, with the limited labeled target data typically used only for direct supervised learning. This approach often under-exploits the rich intra-domain information residing within the target domain itself, specifically the relationship between the small set of labeled target data and the larger set of unlabeled target data. Previous methods, such as those employing cross-domain data mixing or dual-level mixing, tend to overlook or insufficiently utilize this intra-domain information.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying and addressing the under-exploitation of intra-domain information in SSDA for semantic segmentation. It proposes that effectively leveraging the relationship between labeled and unlabeled target data can significantly improve model performance, especially when labeled target data is scarce. The work aims to complement existing inter-domain adaptation strategies with a robust intra-domain approach, thereby offering a more comprehensive solution to the SSDA problem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel Semi-Supervised Domain Adaptation (SSDA) framework, named Inter and Intra-domain Mixing (IIDM), that effectively addresses the performance degradation of semantic segmentation models due to domain shift in real-world applications. Specifically, the framework aims to improve performance when only a limited amount of labeled target data is available.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations within the existing research landscape:

1. **Limitations of Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA):** While UDA is a dominant approach, the assumption of having no labeled target data is often overly restrictive in practical scenarios. Labeling a small portion of target data is frequently feasible and can offer valuable supervision.
2. **Under-exploitation in Existing SSDA Methods:** Current SSDA approaches for semantic segmentation are frequently derived from UDA paradigms. These methods primarily concentrate on mitigating the domain gap between the source and target domains (inter-domain adaptation) and leveraging unlabeled target data. However, the limited labeled target data is often only used for basic supervised training, neglecting its potential for enriching the understanding of the target domain's internal data distribution.
3. **Importance of Intra-domain Information:** The authors highlight that the relationship and distributional gap between the *labeled target data* and the *unlabeled target data* within the target domain itself are significant but often overlooked. When labeled target data is scarce, this small subset may not fully represent the entire target domain's data distribution, creating an "intra-domain gap." Exploiting this internal relationship could provide more discriminative information about the target domain.
4. **Addressing Both Domain Gaps Simultaneously:** The paper posits that learning should address two distinct but related domain gaps:
 - **Inter-domain gap:** The difference in data distribution between the source and target domains.
 - **Intra-domain gap:** The disparity within the target domain, particularly between the limited labeled samples and the abundant unlabeled samples, due to the inherent bias introduced by random sampling of a small labeled subset.
 The authors suggest that previous methods focusing solely on inter-domain mixing or general semi-supervised learning do not fully optimize the feature learning process. By explicitly combining both inter-domain mixing (e.g., between source and unlabeled target data) and intra-domain mixing (e.g., between labeled target and unlabeled target data), the network can form a more accurate representation of the target domain, drawing closer to its true data center. This combined approach is hypothesized to enable the network to capture more domain-invariant features.

In essence, the core motivation is to move beyond simply using labeled target data for supervision and instead to actively leverage it, alongside unlabeled target data, to construct a richer and more representative understanding of the target domain's characteristics. This dual-mixing strategy is expected to yield substantial performance improvements in SSDA semantic segmentation, particularly when labeled target resources are highly constrained.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Inter and Intra-domain Mixing (IIDM) framework builds upon a mean teacher architecture to address semi-supervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation. The methodology integrates supervised learning on source and labeled target data with two novel unsupervised mixing strategies: inter-domain mixing and intra-domain mixing.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Setup

The SSDA setting assumes access to three datasets:
- $D_s = \{(x_s^i, y_s^i)\}_{i=1}^{N_s}$: Labeled source domain images ($x_s^i$) and their ground truth labels ($y_s^i$).
- $D_t = \{(x_t^i, y_t^i)\}_{i=1}^{N_t}$: A small set of labeled target domain images ($x_t^i$) and their ground truth labels ($y_t^i$).
- $D_u = \{(x_u^i)\}_{i=1}^{N_u}$: A large set of unlabeled target domain images ($x_u^i$).
The objective is to train a model $g_\theta$ that performs well on the target domain.

#### 3.2. Holistic Framework Overview

IIDM utilizes a student-teacher model. The student network $g_\theta$ is trained on augmented data, while the teacher network $h_\phi$ provides pseudo-labels for unlabeled data. The teacher's parameters $\phi$ are updated as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's parameters $\theta$: $\phi_{t+1} \leftarrow \alpha\phi_t + (1-\alpha)\theta_{t+1}$. This ensures stable and high-quality pseudo-labels.

#### 3.3. Inter-domain Mixing

This component aims to mitigate the domain shift between the source and target domains by creating intermediate samples.
- **Technique:** ClassMix is employed as the mixing strategy.
- **Input:** A source image $x_s^i$ from $D_s$ and an unlabeled target image $x_u^i$ from $D_u$.
- **Process:**
 1. The unlabeled target image $x_u^i$ is fed into the teacher network $h_\phi$ to generate a pseudo-label $\hat{y}_u^i$.
 2. A binary mask $M_1$ is created by randomly selecting approximately half of the semantic categories present in the source ground truth label $y_s^i$. Pixels belonging to these selected categories are set to 1 in $M_1$, and others to 0.
 3. A mixed image $x_{inter}^i$ is generated by combining $x_s^i$ and $x_u^i$ according to $M_1$: $x_{inter}^i = M_1 \odot x_s^i + (1 - M_1) \odot x_u^i$.
 4. A corresponding mixed label $\hat{y}_{inter}^i$ is generated by combining $y_s^i$ and $\hat{y}_u^i$: $\hat{y}_{inter}^i = M_1 \odot y_s^i + (1 - M_1) \odot \hat{y}_u^i$.
 5. The mixed image $x_{inter}^i$ is then fed into the student network $g_\theta$ to produce a prediction, which is supervised by $\hat{y}_{inter}^i$.

#### 3.4. Intra-domain Mixing

This component addresses the intra-domain gap between the labeled target data and the overall unlabeled target data distribution, enriching the target domain information.
- **Technique:** ClassMix is also used, similar to inter-domain mixing.
- **Input:** A labeled target image $x_t^i$ from $D_t$ and the same unlabeled target image $x_u^i$ used in inter-domain mixing.
- **Process:**
 1. The pseudo-label $\hat{y}_u^i$ for $x_u^i$ is reused from the inter-domain mixing step.
 2. A binary mask $M_2$ is created by randomly selecting approximately half of the semantic categories present in the labeled target ground truth $y_t^i$.
 3. A mixed image $x_{intra}^i$ is generated by combining $x_t^i$ and $x_u^i$ according to $M_2$: $x_{intra}^i = M_2 \odot x_t^i + (1 - M_2) \odot x_u^i$.
 4. A corresponding mixed label $\hat{y}_{intra}^i$ is generated by combining $y_t^i$ and $\hat{y}_u^i$: $\hat{y}_{intra}^i = M_2 \odot y_t^i + (1 - M_2) \odot \hat{y}_u^i$.
 5. The mixed image $x_{intra}^i$ is then fed into the student network $g_\theta$ for prediction.

#### 3.5. Loss Functions

The overall training objective combines four loss terms:
1. **Supervised Source Loss ($L_s$):** Cross-entropy loss on the labeled source data $D_s$.
 $L_s = -\sum_{j,c} (y_s^i)_{(j,c)} \log g_\theta(x_s^i)_{(j,c)}$
2. **Supervised Labeled Target Loss ($L_t$):** Cross-entropy loss on the labeled target data $D_t$.
 $L_t = -\sum_{j,c} (y_t^i)_{(j,c)} \log g_\theta(x_t^i)_{(j,c)}$
3. **Unsupervised Inter-domain Mixing Loss ($L_{inter}$):** Cross-entropy loss between the student's prediction on $x_{inter}^i$ and the mixed pseudo-label $\hat{y}_{inter}^i$. This loss is weighted by a quality estimate $q_i$ for the pseudo-labels, which is the ratio of pixels whose maximum softmax probability exceeds a threshold $\tau$.
 $L_{inter} = -\sum_{j,c} q_i (\hat{y}_{inter}^i)_{(j,c)} \log g_\theta(x_{inter}^i)_{(j,c)}$
4. **Unsupervised Intra-domain Mixing Loss ($L_{intra}$):** Cross-entropy loss between the student's prediction on $x_{intra}^i$ and the mixed pseudo-label $\hat{y}_{intra}^i$. This loss is also weighted by $q_i$.
 $L_{intra} = -\sum_{j,c} q_i (\hat{y}_{intra}^i)_{(j,c)} \log g_\theta(x_{intra}^i)_{(j,c)}$

The final objective function is a weighted sum of these losses:
$L_{final} = L_s + L_t + \lambda L_{inter} + \mu L_{intra}$
where $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are hyperparameters for the mixing loss weights.

#### 3.6. Experimental Details

- **Datasets:** Source domains: GTA5 and SYNTHIA. Target domain: Cityscapes. Different numbers of labeled target images (100, 200, 500, 1000, 1488, 1975, 2475) are used to simulate varying SSDA scenarios.
- **Network Architecture:** DeepLabv2 with a ResNet-101 backbone, pre-trained on ImageNet-1k.
- **Training:** AdamW optimizer, specific learning rates for encoder and decoder, learning rate warmup, rare class sampling, and batch composition (2 source, 2 labeled target, 2 unlabeled target images per mini-batch) over 40k iterations.
- **Hyperparameters:** Default $\alpha = 0.99$ (EMA decay), $\tau = 0.968$ (pseudo-label threshold), $\lambda = 1.0$, $\mu = 2.0$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted comprehensive experiments on GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes benchmarks to evaluate the IIDM framework, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art UDA, SSL, and SSDA methods. The primary evaluation metric was mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU).

#### 4.1. Performance on GTA5→Cityscapes

IIDM consistently demonstrated higher performance across various quantities of labeled target images compared to other methods.
- **Comparison with UDA:** With only 100 labeled target images, IIDM achieved 69.5% mIoU, surpassing recent SOTA UDA methods like DIGA (62.7% mIoU) by a margin of +6.8% mIoU (when UDA uses 0 labeled target images for comparison, the baseline from the table is DIGA 62.7% for 0 labeled target images). The performance of purely supervised DeepLabv2 with 100 labeled images was 41.9%, indicating the challenges of scarce labeled data.
- **Comparison with SSL:** IIDM also outperformed SSL methods, including CowMix and DST-CBC. Even when compared to DusPerb (a component of UniMatch), a recent SOTA SSL method, IIDM showed superior performance (69.5% mIoU vs. 61.8% mIoU with 100 labeled target images).
- **Comparison with SSDA:** IIDM outperformed all other SSDA methods. For instance, with 100 labeled target images, IIDM achieved an 8.3% mIoU improvement over DLDM (69.5% vs. 61.2%). This IIDM performance with 100 labeled images even exceeded DLDM's performance with 1000 labeled images. Similarly, IIDM achieved +13.4% mIoU over ASS+SDSS with 100 labeled target images.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations indicated that IIDM produced smoother and more complete segmentation results, including for challenging classes like 'sidewalk' and 'truck,' even with limited labeled target data.

#### 4.2. Performance on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes

The consistent performance was observed on the Synthia to Cityscapes benchmark (13-class mIoU).
- IIDM achieved the highest mIoU across all ratios of labeled target images.
- With 100 labeled target images, IIDM scored 74.2% mIoU, outperforming ComplexMix by +3.6% mIoU. This performance also surpassed supervised learning even when using 1000 labeled target images (74.2% vs 67.5%).

#### 4.3. Results with SOTA UDA Methods

The framework was further tested by integrating it with advanced UDA training techniques from HRDA and MIC (multi-resolution self-training, increased resolution, robust backbones).
- IIDM+HRDA achieved 77.8% mIoU with only 100 labeled target images, a notable improvement over HRDA alone (73.8% mIoU, 0 labeled target images) and DIGA+HRDA (74.3% mIoU, 0 labeled target images).
- Combining IIDM with both MIC and HRDA resulted in further improvements, achieving 78.6% mIoU with 100 labeled target images, indicating that IIDM is orthogonal and complementary to these advanced UDA techniques.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Component Ablation:**
 - Using only supervised losses ($L_s, L_t$) resulted in poor performance, especially with scarce labeled data.
 - The combination of $L_t$ and $L_{intra}$ (representing an SSL method) performed suboptimally without source domain data (59.1% mIoU with 100 labeled images), highlighting the role of inter-domain transfer.
 - Employing only intra-domain mixing ($L_s + L_t + L_{intra}$) yielded better results than only inter-domain mixing ($L_s + L_t + L_{inter}$), demonstrating the significance of intra-domain information.
 - The full IIDM model (all four loss components) consistently achieved the best performance (69.5% mIoU with 100 labeled images), validating the synergy of both mixing strategies.
- **Intra and Inter-domain Mixing Strategies:** The strategy of applying both inter- and intra-domain mixing on the *same unlabeled target image* and learning via two separate streams ("one $x_u$ two streams") performed optimally. This indicates that simultaneous exposure to both domain gaps on the same sample enhances the learning of consistent feature representations across domains.
- **Loss Weights (λ and μ):** The optimal performance was observed when the inter-domain mixing weight $\lambda$ was 1.0 and the intra-domain mixing weight $\mu$ was 2.0. This suggests that a greater emphasis on intra-domain mixing, which was largely unaddressed in prior SSDA methods, is beneficial. Lower weights for either mixing type led to performance degradation, particularly with limited labeled target data.
- **Impact of More Labeled Target Images:** As the number of labeled target images increased beyond 1000, the performance gain of IIDM over supervised/SSL methods diminished. At 2475 labeled target images, DusPerb (SSL) surpassed IIDM. This suggests that the utility of domain adaptation, particularly inter-domain mixing, decreases when a sufficiently large number of labeled target images naturally reduces the domain shift and intra-domain gap.

In summary, the main findings confirm the effectiveness of IIDM, demonstrating its ability to robustly adapt semantic segmentation models by comprehensively leveraging both inter-domain and intra-domain information, especially under conditions of limited labeled target data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IIDM framework addresses a practical challenge in semantic segmentation by providing a robust solution for Semi-Supervised Domain Adaptation (SSDA). Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Addressing a Practical and Under-investigated Problem:** The paper shifts focus to SSDA, a more practical scenario than purely unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) in many real-world applications where a small amount of target data can be labeled. The significant performance gains achieved in SSDA demonstrate the value of investigating this setting, which has historically received less attention than UDA.

2. **Highlighting the Importance of Intra-Domain Information:** A core contribution is the emphasis on effectively utilizing intra-domain information, specifically the relationship between labeled and unlabeled target data. Prior SSDA methods often under-exploited this aspect, treating labeled target data primarily as simple supervision. IIDM demonstrates that active mixing within the target domain can substantially enrich available information, leading to more discriminative and robust feature learning. This insight could guide future research in SSDA to explore more sophisticated ways of leveraging target-internal data relationships.

3. **Novel and Effective Framework:** IIDM proposes a straightforward yet effective framework that integrates both inter-domain and intra-domain mixing within a mean teacher architecture. By simultaneously addressing the source-to-target domain gap and the internal gap within the target domain, the network learns more consistent and domain-invariant representations. The significant performance improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods across two challenging benchmarks (GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes) validate the efficacy of this dual-mixing approach.

4. **Performance in Data-Scarce Scenarios:** The framework exhibits particularly strong performance when labeled target data is scarce (e.g., 100 images). This is a critical advantage for real-world deployment where extensive manual labeling can be prohibitively expensive or time-consuming. The ability of IIDM to surpass other methods, including those with significantly more labeled target data, underscores its utility in resource-constrained environments.

5. **Orthogonality and Extensibility:** The demonstrated compatibility of IIDM with advanced UDA techniques like HRDA and MIC, leading to further performance gains, suggests that IIDM is a complementary approach rather than a replacement. This orthogonality means IIDM can potentially be integrated with future advancements in both UDA and SSL, serving as a foundational component for next-generation SSDA methods.

6. **Potential for Real-World Impact:** The improved robustness and accuracy of semantic segmentation models under domain shift, especially with limited labeled target data, have direct implications for various applications. These include autonomous driving (where models trained on simulated data need to perform well in diverse real-world conditions), medical imaging (adapting models across different hospital datasets), and remote sensing (applying models across varying geographical regions or sensor types). By making SSDA more effective, IIDM contributes to bridging the sim-to-real gap and accelerating the deployment of deep learning models in critical domains.

In conclusion, the IIDM framework offers a valuable step forward in semi-supervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation. By highlighting and effectively addressing the significance of intra-domain information, it provides a robust, simple, and extensible solution with considerable potential to impact both academic research and practical applications.
