# ViLL-E: Video LLM Embeddings for Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> ViLL-E introduces a unified VideoLLM that integrates text generation with embedding-based retrieval capabilities within a single model. This model significantly surpasses previous VideoLLMs in retrieval tasks, showing over 8% average improvement on moment retrieval, while maintaining competitive performance on video question answering, and enables zero-shot composed video retrieval and retrieval using extensive text descriptions.

## Problem

- Existing VideoLLMs excel at generative tasks (e.g., captioning) but significantly underperform specialized embedding models in retrieval tasks (e.g., text-to-video retrieval).
- Specialized embedding models, while strong in retrieval, often lack generative capabilities and require per-dataset fine-tuning, leading to a need for separate model stacks for different video understanding tasks.
- Current VideoLLMs typically lack effective mechanisms to generate dense, aligned embeddings for efficient similarity-based search, hindering their application in retrieval scenarios.

## Method

- Develops **ViLL-E**, a unified VideoLLM built on a pretrained ==PaliGemma-3B== foundation, featuring a novel ==attentive-pooling "embedding head"== for dense video embedding generation.
- Implements an adaptive ==end-of-sequence triggered mechanism== for video embedding generation, allowing dynamic adjustment of computational steps based on video complexity.
- Employs a ==three-stage training strategy== combining ==generative== (captioning, QA), ==contrastive== (text-video retrieval), and ==matching== (text-video pair classification, temporal localization) objectives across large-scale and high-quality datasets.

## Results

- Achieved an average improvement of over **8%** in moment retrieval benchmarks (ActivityNet, Charades-STA) over specialized VideoLLMs and surpassed fine-tuned dual-encoder models by up to **4%** on text-to-video retrieval (MSR-VTT).
- Maintained competitive performance on video question answering tasks, achieving **top scores** in several categories (e.g., MSR-VTT QA, VCG) among temporally-specialized VideoLLMs.
- Enabled novel zero-shot capabilities, including composed video retrieval with over **5%** higher R@1 accuracy compared to baselines and improved retrieval using long, detailed captions (**11.4%** R@1 T2V improvement).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating generative and contrastive learning objectives within a single LLM architecture effectively bridges the performance gap between generative VideoLLMs and specialized retrieval models.
> - An adaptive embedding generation mechanism, which adjusts processing based on video complexity, enhances representational quality and generalization without introducing substantial inference latency.
> - Pre-training with large-scale, detailed human-like captions significantly improves the model's capacity to encode fine-grained video information, benefiting both generative and retrieval tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen significant advancements, extending the capabilities of LLMs from natural language understanding to processing other modalities such as images and audio. Video Large Language Models (VideoLLMs) specifically integrate video data, enabling tasks like video question answering and captioning by encoding video frames and aligning visual features with LLM feature spaces. These models typically excel when the output is text-based.

However, a distinct category of video understanding tasks, such as Text-to-Video Retrieval and Moment Retrieval, traditionally relies on specialized embedding-based models. These specialized models, often built on architectures like CLIP or BLIP extended for video, generate dense, aligned video and text embeddings for efficient similarity-based search. Current VideoLLMs generally underperform these specialized embedding models in retrieval tasks, and some are inherently incapable of generating embeddings. This creates a dichotomy where one class of models (VideoLLMs) handles generative tasks well, while another (specialized embedding models) excels at retrieval, often requiring per-dataset fine-tuning.

This work addresses this dichotomy by proposing a unified architecture that aims to bridge the performance gap between generative VideoLLMs and specialized retrieval models. It seeks to integrate strong performance on both generative (e.g., Video QA) and embedding-based retrieval tasks within a single model, drawing inspiration from recent NLP research demonstrating that LLMs can be adapted for retrieval through contrastive fine-tuning. The paper positions ViLL-E as a solution to consolidate these capabilities, eliminating the need for separate model stacks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified VideoLLM that can effectively perform both text generation tasks (such as Video Question Answering and Video Captioning) and embedding-based retrieval tasks (like Text-to-Video Retrieval and Moment Retrieval). The current landscape presents a trade-off: VideoLLMs are proficient in generative tasks but struggle with retrieval, while specialized embedding models excel at retrieval but are often task-specific and lack generative capabilities. This necessitates maintaining distinct models for different types of video understanding tasks.

The motivation behind ViLL-E is multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Performance Gap:** Existing VideoLLMs, even those with temporal reasoning enhancements (e.g., LLaVA-ST), lag significantly behind expert retrieval and localization models on standard benchmarks. The authors observe that top-performing models in video retrieval and temporal localization consistently leverage embeddings, a capability largely absent or underperforming in current VideoLLMs. The goal is to narrow this performance disparity.
- **Achieving Unified Capabilities:** The research aims to demonstrate that a single model can robustly handle both generative outputs (text) and discriminative outputs (embeddings). This unification could streamline deployment and reduce computational overhead by avoiding the need for separate, task-specific models.
- **Adaptive Computation for Video Complexity:** Videos exhibit varying levels of complexity. A motivation is to enable the model to adapt its "thinking time" based on video complexity, allowing for longer processing for intricate videos and quicker responses for simpler ones, thereby optimizing computational efficiency and representational quality.
- **Unlocking New Zero-Shot Capabilities:** By combining generative and contrastive learning, the authors hypothesize that the model can acquire novel zero-shot capabilities for tasks like composed video retrieval and retrieval from long, detailed text descriptions, which are challenging for traditional models due to context window limitations.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** The adoption of techniques like Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA) is motivated by the need to efficiently fine-tune the large language model backbone without retraining all parameters, ensuring the approach is practical for large-scale VideoLLMs.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire for a versatile and efficient VideoLLM that consolidates the strengths of both generative and embedding-based approaches to video understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ViLL-E model is built upon a multi-modal LLM architecture, initialized with a pretrained PaliGemma-3B model, which itself uses a SigLIP-SO-400M ViT as its vision encoder. The approach involves a novel embedding generation mechanism and a three-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The core architecture comprises:

- **LLM Backbone:** A PaliGemma-3B model forms the foundation, with its transformer layers shared for both generative and embedding tasks. It processes visual tokens and input prompts using bidirectional attention for prompts and causal attention for autoregressively generated suffixes, following the PrefixLM paradigm.
- **Vision Encoder and Projection:** Individual video frames are first processed by the pretrained SigLIP vision encoder. The extracted visual features are then projected into the LLM's feature space via linear projection layers.
- **Embedding Head:** A novel, learnable attentive-pooling "embedding head" is introduced after the LLM decoder. This head is designed with four goals:
 1. Dedicated modeling capacity separate from generative tasks.
 2. A bottleneck to yield dense embeddings without over-restricting representation power.
 3. Adaptability to variable token lengths, allowing for dynamic inference steps based on video complexity.
 4. Parameter efficiency.
 The embedding head uses a transformer-based self-attention layer. It adaptively weighs tokens from the final LLM transformer layer, using the LLM's tokens as queries and a set of `P` learnable key and value "pooling tokens." After attention, the weighted representations are projected into the embedding space via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) and then mean-pooled. This "KV-Former" design enables variable-length outputs while acting as a bottleneck.
- **End-of-Sequence (<EOS>)-Triggered Embedding Generation:** For video embedding generation, the model leverages an <EOS>-triggered mechanism. This allows the model to generate a variable number of tokens to feed the embedding head, dynamically adjusting computation based on video complexity. For text embedding, standard single-step inference is used.

#### 3.2. Training Objectives

The model employs a three-stage training strategy, integrating four distinct tasks:

- **Text–Video Retrieval (Figure 3a):** Utilizes a CLIP-like contrastive loss to align video and text embeddings, ensuring paired examples are more similar than in-batch negatives.
- **Video Captioning & Question–Answering (Figure 3b):** Uses a next-token prediction loss. For captioning, the model generates video descriptions. For QA, it generates answers to questions about the video, with the question serving as part of the prompt.
- **Text–Video Matching (Figure 3c):** A binary classification task where the model determines if a given video and caption form a matching pair. Supervised by a binary classification loss.
- **Temporal Localization (Figure 3d):** Aims to match specific video segments to text annotations. It employs a contrastive loss with sliding window hard negative mining to differentiate correct segments from similar but incorrect ones (IoU < 0.2).

#### 3.3. Three-Stage Training Strategy

1. **Stage 1: Generative-Contrastive Pre-Training:**
 - **Data:** Large-scale licensed Shutterstock stock videos dataset (10M video-caption pairs), similar in scale to WebVid-10M.
 - **Objective:** Jointly trains the network using both generative (next-token prediction for captioning) and contrastive (CLIP-like loss for text-video retrieval) supervision. This involves two forward passes: one for video input to generate captions and video embeddings, and a second lighter pass for text-only input to generate text embeddings.
 - **Parameter Efficiency:** LoRA adapters (rank 128) are used for fine-tuning LLM transformer layers, while the vision projection module and embedding head are fully trained.

2. **Stage 2: Continued Pre-Training on High-Quality Captions:**
 - **Data:** A smaller, re-captioned subset of 200,000 Shutterstock videos. Captions are generated using Claude-3-Sonnet, prompted to provide detailed narrative descriptions (average length 130+ words, compared to <10 words for original). The dataset is balanced by under-sampling common concepts.
 - **Objective:** Continues the joint generative-contrastive pre-training from Stage 1. This phase aims to improve the model's ability to preserve fine-grained details from video content in its feature space, beneficial for downstream tasks.

3. **Stage 3: Multi-Task Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Data:** A high-quality, multi-task instruction fine-tuning (IFT) dataset comprising 100,000 samples from various public datasets (MSR-VTT, ActivityNet, DiDeMo, and a subset of Shutterstock original captions). This dataset covers captioning, QA, retrieval, and temporal localization.
 - **Objective:** Integrates all four training tasks (captioning/QA, retrieval, matching, and temporal localization) to develop well-rounded capabilities. Gradients are accumulated across all tasks. LoRA adapters (rank 32) are also used in this stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ViLL-E demonstrates a unified capability across diverse video understanding tasks, often outperforming or matching specialized models.

#### 4.1. Retrieval Tasks

- **Moment Retrieval (ActivityNet, Charades-STA, QVHighlights):** ViLL-E significantly outperforms specialized VideoLLMs by an average of over 8% (e.g., 8.2% on ActivityNet, 6.7% on Charades-STA). It achieves performance comparable to or surpassing state-of-the-art non-LLM expert methods (e.g., TR-DETR on Charades-STA R@1, IoU=0.5).
- **Text-to-Video Retrieval (MSR-VTT, DiDeMo, VATEX):** ViLL-E surpasses fine-tuned state-of-the-art dual-encoder models like VidLA and SigLIP on MSR-VTT by up to 4% (e.g., 62.5% R@1 on MSR-VTT vs. VidLA's 58.0%). It also demonstrates competitive performance on DiDeMo and VATEX.

#### 4.2. QA Tasks

ViLL-E remains competitive with state-of-the-art VideoLLMs on various Video QA benchmarks:

- It achieves top performance in several categories (MSR-VTT QA, VCG, MVBench) among temporally-specialized VideoLLMs, with improvements of up to 3.7% over previous methods (e.g., 65.2% on MSR-VTT, 3.7 on VCG).
- While slightly underperforming general VideoLLMs like Qwen-2.5VL and LLaVA-Video on MVBench and VideoMME, ViLL-E maintains strong competitive scores.

#### 4.3. Additional Tasks (Zero-Shot Capabilities)

ViLL-E's unified architecture enables novel tasks:

- **Two-Stage Retrieval using Re-Ranking (MSR-VTT):** By combining embedding-based retrieval (Stage 1) with LLM-based reasoning (Stage 2 matching), ViLL-E shows an improvement of approximately 2% in R@1 accuracy for Text-to-Video and Video-to-Text retrieval, demonstrating the benefit of leveraging both generative and discriminative abilities.
- **Composed Video Retrieval (Zero-Shot):** The model retrieves videos matching a query video modified by a text instruction. In a zero-shot setting, ViLL-E significantly outperforms recent baselines by over 5% (e.g., 53.13% R@1 vs. Thawakar et al.'s 47.52%).
- **Detailed Video Caption Retrieval (Zero-Shot):** Unlike models limited by short context windows (e.g., CLIP's 77 tokens), ViLL-E supports much longer texts. On the AuroraCap-VideoDetailCaption dataset, it benefits substantially from long captions (average 500 words), improving R@1 by 11.4% (Text-to-Video) and 9.6% (Video-to-Text) compared to short captions, outperforming LongCLIP.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Supervision Types:** Combining generative, contrastive, and matching objectives during fine-tuning yielded the best performance across both generative and embedding tasks. Removing contrastive training significantly degraded retrieval and localization performance.
- **Embedding Head Design:** The proposed attentive pooling (KV-Former) embedding head consistently outperformed alternative designs such as attention-free pooling, linear projection, MLP, self-attention, Q-Former, and K-Former across retrieval and localization benchmarks.
- **Pre-Training Stages:** The large-scale Stage 1 pre-training was crucial for retrieval tasks. The Stage 2 continued pre-training with high-quality, detailed captions particularly improved performance on longer video datasets and generative tasks.
- **Adaptive Embedding Mechanism:** The adaptive <EOS>-triggered token generation mechanism allowed the model to use more tokens for complex/longer videos (e.g., 14.8 tokens for 180s+ videos vs. 8.6 tokens for 0-60s videos), resulting in better generalization and significantly outperforming fixed-token approaches across all video duration bins (e.g., 67.9% R@1 overall on ActivityNet compared to 66.5% for fixed 5 tokens). This adaptive mechanism introduced only marginal inference latency overhead compared to fixed-token baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ViLL-E represents a notable advancement in multimodal LLMs by effectively unifying text generation and embedding-based retrieval capabilities within a single model. This unification has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Consolidated Video Understanding:** ViLL-E demonstrates that a single architectural framework can achieve competitive performance across a broad spectrum of video understanding tasks, including video question answering, captioning, temporal localization, and text-to-video retrieval. This challenges the traditional paradigm of specialized models for distinct task types, offering a more generalized and efficient approach.
- **Enhanced Performance on Retrieval Tasks:** By equipping a VideoLLM with an effective embedding generation mechanism and a specialized training strategy, ViLL-E significantly narrows the performance gap between generative LLMs and highly specialized, non-LLM retrieval models. Its ability to outperform prior VideoLLMs on localization and achieve state-of-the-art performance on video retrieval indicates a substantial improvement in handling discriminative tasks.
- **Novel Zero-Shot Capabilities:** The joint generative-contrastive training methodology unlocks new capabilities that are difficult for existing models. These include performing composed video retrieval, which requires combining visual and textual modification cues, and effective retrieval using very long and detailed text descriptions, overcoming the context window limitations of many dual-encoder models. These capabilities broaden the range of applications for video understanding systems.
- **Improved Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** A unified model reduces the need to develop, train, and maintain separate model pipelines for different tasks. This could lead to more efficient resource utilization in terms of computational power, storage, and development effort for practical applications involving diverse video understanding requirements. The adaptive embedding generation mechanism further contributes to efficiency by tailoring computational effort to video complexity.
- **Advancement in Multimodal AI:** This work contributes to the broader goal of developing more versatile and robust multimodal AI systems. By demonstrating that LLMs can serve as a foundation for both generative and discriminative tasks across modalities, it provides a blueprint for future research into general-purpose multimodal intelligence.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that ViLL-E inherits some limitations from its base PaliGemma LLM, such as lacking general multi-turn conversation abilities. Furthermore, the model's training dataset being primarily in English suggests a potential reduction in PaliGemma’s multilingual capabilities. These aspects indicate areas for future development to enhance the model's practical applicability.
