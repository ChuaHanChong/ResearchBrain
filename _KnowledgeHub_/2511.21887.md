# UniArt: Unified 3D Representation for Generating 3D Articulated Objects with Open-Set Articulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A diffusion-based framework, UniArt, unifies 3D geometry, visual appearance, and articulation parameters into a single latent representation for generating articulated objects from a single image. It improves geometric accuracy and visual fidelity, achieving 28.52 PSNR for resting states, and generalizes to unseen object categories by maintaining over 85% of its performance.

## Problem

- Traditional retrieval-based methods for articulated 3D object generation suffer from geometric misalignment, limited diversity, and poor generalization due to reliance on predefined asset libraries.
- Existing generative models often produce parts individually, leading to global structural inconsistencies like misaligned joint interfaces and discontinuities.
- Prior work depends on predefined semantic joint categories, restricting the generation of objects with novel articulation patterns or unseen joint types.

## Method

- **UniArt** introduces an end-to-end, ==diffusion-based== framework that synthesizes entire articulated 3D objects from a single image input.
- It develops a ==unified latent representation== that jointly encodes geometry, visual appearance, part segmentation, and kinematic parameters.
- A reversible ==joint-to-voxel embedding== scheme aligns joint-level kinematics with a voxelized geometric representation, and a ==part-wise UniArt attention== mechanism integrates these features.

## Results

- UniArt achieved quantitative improvements on the PartNet-Mobility benchmark, with a PSNR of **28.52** (resting state) and **23.77** (articulated states), and lower Chamfer Distance (**0.0095** for resting state) compared to baselines.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to unseen object categories, maintaining over **85%** of its closed-set performance, unlike baselines that degraded below **60%**.
- Generated articulated assets and their URDFs were successfully deployed in physics simulators (MuJoCo, PyBullet) and real-world robotic manipulation setups, confirming their functional usability without post-processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatially aligning kinematic parameters with volumetric geometry through a reversible joint-to-voxel embedding enables a unified representation that ensures coherence between object shape and motion.
> - Formulating articulation type prediction as an open-set problem allows the model to generalize to novel joint categories and object types not explicitly seen during training.
> - Leveraging a VAE with part-wise attention and a rectified-flow latent diffusion model enables stable and consistent generation of both geometry and articulation parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of 3D articulated objects is a field of interest for applications in realistic simulation, embodied robotics, and digital content creation. Articulated objects, which possess movable parts and defined motion patterns, are more complex to model than static objects due to their inherent structural and kinematic properties. Manually constructing such assets is resource-intensive and challenging to scale for the diverse requirements of modern applications.

Traditional approaches to articulated object generation often follow a multi-stage, retrieval-based pipeline. These methods typically involve predicting articulation parameters from input images, retrieving corresponding part geometries from a predefined asset library, and then assembling these parts. While providing a shortcut for model generation, these techniques are constrained by several limitations: geometric misalignment between retrieved parts, restricted diversity tied to the asset collection, and limited generalization to objects outside the training distribution. These issues impede their utility in open-world scenarios characterized by wide variations in object form and function.

Recent advancements in 3D generation, particularly those employing diffusion and transformer-based frameworks, have demonstrated capabilities in synthesizing novel 3D objects from various inputs, including text or images. Some works have explored conditional generation of articulated objects to address geometric misalignment. However, many of these approaches still rely on a part-wise generation strategy, where individual sub-parts are generated and then assembled. This compositional process can lead to global structural inconsistencies, such as discontinuities at part boundaries or misaligned joint interfaces. A further common limitation in prior work is the dependence on predefined semantic joint categories (e.g., "door," "drawer," "handle"). This reliance restricts the diversity of generatable objects, as models cannot produce objects with novel joint categories or configurations not explicitly seen during training. This work aims to address these identified limitations by proposing an end-to-end, unified generative framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of UniArt are:

- **To overcome the limitations of retrieval-based methods:** Traditional methods rely on pre-existing asset libraries, leading to geometric inconsistencies, limited diversity, and poor generalization to novel objects. The motivation is to move away from copying components from a database to a fully generative approach that synthesizes geometry, part structure, and articulation parameters continuously.
- **To address structural inconsistency in part-wise generative models:** Even recent generative models often generate sub-parts individually before assembly. This can result in global structural inconsistencies, such as misaligned joint interfaces, discontinuities at part boundaries, and an overall lack of geometric coherence. UniArt seeks to generate the entire articulated object directly within a unified framework, aiming to ensure mutual consistency across all components.
- **To eliminate reliance on predefined semantic joint categories (Open-Set Articulation):** Previous methods are often limited by a fixed set of joint labels (e.g., "door," "knob"). This restricts the types of articulated objects that can be generated and hinders generalization to unseen object categories with novel articulation patterns. A key motivation is to formulate articulation type prediction as an open-set problem, allowing the model to generalize to novel joint categories and object types without needing explicit high-level semantic labels during the generative process.
- **To establish a unified latent representation:** To achieve the above objectives, the authors aim to create a single, compact latent representation that jointly encodes all critical aspects of an articulated 3D object: geometry, visual appearance, part segmentation, and kinematic parameters. This unified encoding is intended to facilitate the generation of coherent and physically plausible objects.
- **To enable scalable and automated generation:** The ultimate motivation is to provide an automated, end-to-end solution for generating "robot-ready" articulated 3D objects directly from a single image input. This aims to reduce the manual labor and time associated with asset creation, enabling the rapid construction of diverse and high-fidelity 3D assets for high-fidelity simulation environments and embodied robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniArt operates as a diffusion-based framework designed to synthesize fully articulated 3D objects from a single image in an end-to-end manner. The core of its methodology centers on a unified latent representation and a novel approach to encoding articulation.

#### 3.1. Articulated Object Parameterization

The method adopts the URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) for parameterizing articulated objects, representing each object as a graph where nodes are links (parts) and edges are joints. It assumes a kinematic graph that is connected, acyclic, and features screw joints with at most one prismatic translation and one revolute rotation, covering a range of real-world objects. A key distinction is the intentional exclusion of predefined categorical annotations (semantic labels like "base," "door") for links, promoting generalization to novel articulation morphologies. The URDF parameters (joint type, axis, motion limits) are serialized into a sparse adjacency tensor.

#### 3.1.1. Reversible Joint-to-Voxel Embedding

A central technical contribution is the joint-to-voxel embedding scheme. This aligns joint-level kinematics with a voxelized geometric representation. For each node in the kinematic tree, its attributes are associated with the edge connecting it to its parent. These attributes are then projected onto the 3D voxel space corresponding to the child part's geometric occupancy. All active voxels belonging to a child part inherit the same parameter assignment, thereby embedding kinematic constraints directly into the part's spatial representation. This creates a unified voxel-level representation where geometric and kinematic information coexist. The design ensures reversibility during decoding, allowing unique recovery of node attributes and kinematic graph structure from the voxel-level encoding.

#### 3.2. UniArt VAE with Geometry-Articulation Interaction

A variational autoencoder (VAE) is employed to learn a compact latent space for this unified representation, named UniArt Latent.
- **Feature Preparation**: The input mesh is converted into a binary occupancy grid, enriched with visual features derived from multiview averaging. Simultaneously, the voxelized articulation representation and part-aware features (from a pretrained PartField model) are combined to form per-voxel articulation attributes. These are defined on the same voxel space.
- **Part-wise UniArt Attention**: Instead of simple concatenation, a novel part-wise UniArt attention mechanism dynamically aligns the geometric features ($V_{geo}$) and articulation features ($V_{art}$). The articulation feature serves as the query, and the geometric feature as key-value pairs in a cross-attention module. This aggregates motion-aware features consistent with the underlying geometric structure. A residual connection is used to preserve geometric detail.
- **Unified Voxel Feature Map**: The fused representation is channel-wise concatenated with the geometric features to form a unified voxel feature map ($V$). This ensures each voxel contains both spatial occupancy and articulation-aware information, encouraging learning physically plausible correlations between part shape and kinematic behavior.
- **VAE Architecture and Loss**: The VAE encoder ($E_{vae}$) processes $V$ through attention layers to learn hierarchical spatial features, preserving alignment. The sampled latent embedding ($z$) is passed to the VAE decoder ($D_{vae}$), which simultaneously reconstructs both geometry and articulation features. The VAE is optimized with a compound loss function: $L_{vae} = L_{geo} + L_{art} + L_{kl}$, measuring reconstruction fidelity of voxel occupancy, recovery of articulation attributes, and Kullback–Leibler regularization, respectively.

#### 3.3. Articulated Latent Generation

For generating novel articulated objects, a latent diffusion model based on a rectified flow model is utilized. The generator models structural layout and articulation parameters simultaneously. Training employs a conditional flow matching objective, where the model learns a continuous and deterministic velocity field to transport noisy samples to the clean latent distribution. This rectified-flow formulation aims to simplify training and generation compared to stochastic diffusion processes, improving stability and coherence between geometry and motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniArt was evaluated on the PartNet-Mobility benchmark, with assessments conducted for both closed-set and open-set conditions, including unseen categories.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons (Closed-Set)

UniArt demonstrated quantitative improvements over existing methods on the PartNet-Mobility test set across appearance-articulation, geometry-articulation, and shape-articulation metrics.
- **Appearance Fidelity (PSNR):** In the resting state (RS-), UniArt achieved a PSNR of 28.52, surpassing SINGAPO by 11.36. In articulated states (AS-), UniArt reached 23.77, an improvement of 9.87 over SINGAPO, indicating robust texture reconstruction even under significant part motions.
- **Geometric Accuracy (Chamfer Distance):** For resting states, UniArt obtained a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.0095, an improvement over DIPO (0.0132). In articulated states, UniArt achieved 0.0376, outperforming DIPO (0.0423).
- **Shape-Image Alignment (OpenShape):** UniArt achieved the highest OpenShape scores for both resting and articulated states (0.1457 and 0.1176, respectively), indicating stronger alignment between generated shapes and conditional input images compared to baselines.
The overall results suggest that the unified voxel–articulation latent representation supports stable geometry and motion consistency throughout the articulation process.

#### 4.2. Open-Set Evaluation

Evaluation on unseen object categories (e.g., Toilet, Laptop, TrashCan), excluded from training, demonstrated UniArt's generalization capability. Despite not observing these specific articulation patterns during training, the model generated realistic and coherent articulations, maintaining plausible motion patterns and detailed appearances. Quantitatively, UniArt maintained over 85% of its closed-set performance across metrics on unseen categories, while adapted versions of methods like SINGAPO experienced performance degradation (falling below 60%). This supports the effectiveness of UniArt's open-set formulation for articulation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Uni-encoding of Geometry and Articulation:** Replacing the sparse structure attention with a vanilla concatenation and convolution approach for feature aggregation resulted in a notable performance drop (e.g., RS-dPSNR decreased from 28.52 to 23.75). This indicates that the sparse structure attention is effective in aligning part-specific geometry and articulation.
- **Effectiveness of 3D Shape Prior:** Removing the pretrained 3D shape prior, derived from a large-scale 3D generative model, and training UniArt purely from scratch led to a significant degradation across all metrics. This highlights the importance of leveraging a 3D prior for stabilizing geometry–articulation interactions and preventing unrealistic geometries.
- **Effectiveness of part-wise UniArt Attention:** Comparisons with alternative aggregation strategies (pooling, residual fusion, vanilla self-attention) showed that the proposed part-wise UniArt Attention achieved the highest scores. For instance, it improved AS-dPSNR from 22.04 (vanilla self-attention) to 23.77 and reduced AS-dCD from 0.0457 to 0.0376. These results confirm the dynamic attention mechanism's role in enhancing local structural coherence and global articulation plausibility.

#### 4.4. Additional Experiments

- **Multi-View Conditioning:** Training UniArt with multi-view image conditioning (two or three views) yielded only marginal improvements (around 2% PSNR gain) compared to single-view input. This suggests that the unified latent representation effectively captures sufficient spatial correlations from a single image.
- **Real-World Deployment:** The generated articulated assets, along with their predicted URDFs, were successfully deployed in physics simulators (MuJoCo, PyBullet) and a real-world robotic manipulation setup. This demonstrated the functional usability of the generated models for tasks such as hinge opening, slider pulling, and compound motions without manual post-processing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniArt framework introduces an end-to-end approach for generating articulated 3D objects, which has several implications for related research and applications.

- **Reduced Manual Effort and Scalability:** By synthesizing fully articulated 3D objects from a single image, UniArt addresses the challenge of costly and labor-intensive manual asset construction. This automation can significantly increase the speed and scale at which diverse 3D assets are generated, which is beneficial for populating large-scale simulation environments and for data generation in robotics.
- **Enhanced Realism and Consistency:** The unified latent representation that jointly encodes geometry, texture, part segmentation, and kinematic parameters aims to produce objects with greater internal consistency. This unified approach can lead to improved visual fidelity, geometric accuracy, and physically plausible articulation compared to multi-stage or retrieval-based methods that often suffer from misalignments and inconsistencies.
- **Generalization to Novel Object Categories and Articulations:** The formulation of articulation type prediction as an open-set problem is a key contribution. This capability allows UniArt to generate objects with joint categories and kinematic structures not explicitly seen during training, moving beyond the limitations of methods that rely on predefined semantic labels. This broadens the applicability of generated models to novel and diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to generate "robot-ready" articulated objects with accurate URDF specifications directly from images supports the development of more realistic and diverse simulation environments for embodied AI research and robot learning. The demonstrated deployment in physics simulators and real-world robotic manipulation scenarios suggests that UniArt can contribute to faster prototyping and testing of robot manipulation policies on a wider range of objects.
- **Foundation for Future 3D Generative Models:** The methodology of spatially aligning kinematic parameters with volumetric geometry via reversible joint-to-voxel embedding, coupled with a unified VAE and diffusion model, provides a framework for integrating complex structural and functional properties into 3D generative processes. This could inform the development of more sophisticated generative models capable of producing functionally rich 3D content.
