# tttLRM: Test-Time Training for Long Context and Autoregressive 3D Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> tttLRM presents a 3D reconstruction model that performs high-resolution, long-context, and autoregressive 3D reconstruction from streaming visual input using Test-Time Training. This approach achieves up to 1 dB PSNR improvement over prior state-of-the-art feedforward methods and maintains competitive performance with optimization-based approaches while being hundreds of times faster.

## Problem

- Existing Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) are limited to a small number of input views (e.g., 4-32) due to the quadratic computational complexity of attention mechanisms, hindering scalability for large scenes.
- Methods for 3D reconstruction from streaming visual input generally lack efficient long-context processing and memory mechanisms for progressive updates.
- A trade-off often exists between the high rendering quality of implicit neural representations (like NeRFs) and the real-time efficiency of explicit representations (like 3D Gaussian Splatting), lacking a unified solution.

## Method

- **tttLRM** leverages ==Test-Time== Training (TTT) and Large Chunk Test-Time Training (LaCT) blocks to update "fast weights" during inference, enabling linear computational complexity with respect to input sequence length.
- The architecture tokenizes input images and their ray embeddings, feeding them into LaCT blocks to encode scene information into fast weights, which are then queried by virtual tokens to decode explicit 3D representations like Gaussian Splats.
- It supports autoregressive reconstruction by incrementally updating fast weights with new input views, allowing for progressive refinement of the 3D representation from streaming data.

## Results

- tttLRM surpasses attention-based baselines like GS-LRM by achieving up to a **1** dB PSNR improvement at 512x512 resolution while being twice as fast, and scales effectively to 1024x1024 resolutions without out-of-memory issues.
- It outperforms Long-LRM by approximately **1** dB PSNR on scene-level reconstruction tasks (e.g., DL3DV-140) and is hundreds of times faster than optimization-based methods like 3DGS, while maintaining competitive quality for up to 64 views.
- The model effectively supports autoregressive reconstruction, progressively improving rendering quality and scene coverage from streaming data, demonstrating robust generalization across datasets and input view counts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-Time Training with Large Chunk (LaCT) blocks provides an effective mechanism for handling long input sequences with linear complexity, overcoming the quadratic bottlenecks of traditional attention.
> - Pretraining on novel view synthesis tasks, such as with TTT-LVSM, establishes strong priors that significantly accelerate convergence and enhance the quality of explicit 3D reconstruction models.
> - An autoregressive strategy that performs full reconstruction at each step is more effective than a "Predict & Merge" approach, preventing error accumulation in sequential processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D reconstruction from visual input aims to generate explicit 3D representations that enable photorealistic rendering. Historically, methods such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) focused on geometric recovery, often requiring extensive, scene-specific optimization. The advent of deep learning introduced feedforward 3D reconstruction techniques, but these generally focused on depth estimation or point cloud generation and did not consistently produce photorealistic novel view syntheses.

More recently, neural representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have advanced the state of the art in novel view synthesis and geometry reconstruction, achieving high-quality rendering. However, these methods typically require scene-specific optimization processes that can take minutes to hours, or, in the case of feedforward models, often have limited scalability in terms of the number of input views they can process.

Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) have emerged as a paradigm to accelerate 3D reconstruction by directly inferring 3D structures from input images using neural networks, thus eliminating per-scene optimization. Examples include models for reconstructing NeRFs, meshes, or 3DGS. A common limitation of many LRMs is their restriction to a small number of input views (e.g., four), largely due to the quadratic computational complexity of traditional attention mechanisms employed in their transformer-based architectures. While models like Long-LRM have extended input view counts to 32, they still rely on attention layers that limit further scalability and hinder efficient processing of streaming or very long contexts. Furthermore, while implicit latent-space 3D representations offer high novel view synthesis quality, their rendering speed is typically slower than explicit representations like 3DGS, and they can lack controllability.

This paper positions tttLRM within this landscape by addressing the challenges of long-context processing, autoregressive reconstruction, and computational scalability while simultaneously maintaining the advantages of explicit 3D representations for real-time rendering. By integrating Test-Time Training (TTT) with a linear-complexity architecture, tttLRM aims to provide a unified framework that overcomes the limitations of prior LRMs in handling extensive and streaming visual data. It specifically seeks to bridge the gap between the quality of neural network rendering and the efficiency of real-time explicit 3D systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a large 3D reconstruction model capable of high-resolution, long-context, and autoregressive 3D reconstruction from streaming visual input. This objective is motivated by several limitations of existing 3D reconstruction methodologies:

- **Mimicking Human Perception:** The authors draw an analogy to human perception, where individuals continuously observe visual streams, construct an abstract internal representation, and decode this into explicit 3D only when detailed information is required. The model aims to replicate this human-like process for explicit 3D reconstruction from streaming input.
- **Scalability Limitations of Existing LRMs:** Current Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) are often limited to a small number of input views (e.g., 4-32 views). This limitation stems from the quadratic computational complexity of attention layers, which become prohibitive for longer input sequences. This restricts their applicability to large-scale scenes or scenarios requiring extensive observations.
- **Inefficiency in Streaming Contexts:** Existing methods are generally not designed for long-context scenarios with memory mechanisms, which are crucial for processing streaming visual inputs efficiently and causally. Solutions like Long-LRM, while extending input views, still face scalability issues that prevent efficient processing of streamed data.
- **Trade-off Between Quality and Efficiency:** While neural radiance fields (NeRFs) and similar implicit representations achieve high-quality novel view synthesis, their rendering speed can be slow due to repetitive network inference. Conversely, explicit representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offer real-time rendering but typically require per-scene optimization, which is time-consuming. There is a need for a model that combines the high quality of neural methods with the real-time performance of explicit representations.
- **Lack of Controllability and Interpretability:** Implicit latent-space 3D representations, despite their synthesis capabilities, can be difficult to control or interpret directly, limiting their utility in applications requiring explicit manipulation or understanding of the 3D scene.

To address these motivations, tttLRM aims to achieve the following specific objectives:
- Enable **high-resolution (1024px) single-image-to-3D reconstruction** through a multi-view generator.
- Support **long-context (up to 64 input views and beyond) and feedforward 3DGS reconstruction** with linear computational complexity, overcoming the quadratic bottlenecks of attention-based models.
- Facilitate **autoregressive streaming reconstruction and refinement** from sequential observations, allowing the model to progressively update its 3D representation.
- Efficiently **compress multiple image observations into fast weights** of a Test-Time Training (TTT) layer, forming an implicit 3D representation that can be decoded into various explicit formats like Gaussian Splats (GS) or NeRFs.
- Demonstrate that **pretraining on novel view synthesis tasks effectively transfers to explicit 3D modeling**, leading to improved reconstruction quality and faster convergence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

tttLRM proposes a novel 3D reconstruction model based on Test-Time Training (TTT) and specifically leverages Large Chunk Test-Time Training (LaCT) blocks to handle long input sequences with linear computational complexity.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: TTT and LaCT Layer

- **Test-Time Training (TTT):** The core idea of TTT is to learn a set of "fast weights" (W) that are updated during inference based on the incoming input tokens. This process encodes the key-value pairs of the input sequence into a fixed-size neural memory, enabling contextual expressivity without the quadratic complexity of standard attention mechanisms. The update rule minimizes a mean-square error between the predicted output from fast weights and the value vectors.
- **Large Chunk Test-Time Training (LaCT):** Original TTT models process small mini-batches, leading to low GPU utilization. LaCT addresses this by updating fast weights with large chunk sizes (up to 1 million tokens). It computes gradients of the summed loss over all keys and values within a chunk, improving efficiency and scalability.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The tttLRM architecture processes a set of posed input images and decodes them into explicit 3D representations, such as 3D Gaussian Splats.
- **Input Processing:** Input images ($I_i$) are concatenated channel-wise with their ray embeddings ($R_i$) to provide positional information. These combined inputs are then divided into non-overlapping patches, which are subsequently tokenized using a lightweight linear layer, forming a sequence of visual tokens ($T_{i,j}$).
- **LaCT Blocks for Fast Weight Update:** The visual tokens are fed iteratively into a series of LaCT blocks. Within each LaCT block, the input tokens first undergo a window attention operation to capture local relationships within each view. Subsequently, these tokens are used to update the model's "fast weights" (W) via a Muon optimizer. Both the update and application (querying) operations of the fast weights maintain linear computational complexity with respect to the sequence length.
- **Querying Fast Weights:** To retrieve 3D information, a distinct set of "virtual tokens" is introduced. For 3DGS reconstruction, these virtual tokens represent virtual view planes. For other representations like triplane-based NeRFs, these are learnable triplane features. These virtual tokens query the updated fast weights but do not contribute to their update.
- **Decoding Explicit 3D Representations:** The updated query tokens are then passed through a linear token decoder. This decoder transforms them into explicit 3D representations, such as per-patch Gaussian parameters (RGB color, scale, rotation, opacity). Gaussian positions are determined by decoding depth per pixel and converting it to real depth using a range function (object-centric or linear), which is then used with known ray locations and directions to establish 3D positions.

#### 3.3. Autoregressive Reconstruction

The model supports an autoregressive, streaming mode. Instead of processing all input views jointly, it incrementally updates its internal state (fast weights) as new mini-batches of views arrive. For each incoming batch, the fast weights are updated, and the model immediately predicts the corresponding 3D Gaussian parameters for new query views. This allows for progressive 3D reconstruction and refinement, similar to an RNN, where the internal state evolves with new observations.

#### 3.4. Distributed Feedforward Reconstruction

To handle a large number of input views and high-resolution images, tttLRM implements sequence parallelism.
- **Token Sharding:** Tokenized input views are partitioned along the sequence dimension and distributed across multiple GPUs.
- **Parallel Gaussian Prediction:** Each GPU predicts pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives for its assigned virtual views after the fast weights have been synchronized.
- **Scene Aggregation:** The predicted Gaussians from all devices are gathered to form a complete scene representation.
- **Parallel Rendering and Loss Computation:** Each GPU renders a subset of novel views and computes photometric reconstruction losses against ground truth.
- **Gradient Synchronization:** Gradients are then all-reduced and backpropagated across all devices, ensuring consistent global optimization due to the linearity of LaCT fast-weight updates.

#### 3.5. Training Objective

The model is trained without explicit 3D supervision, relying on a rendering loss.
- **Rendering Loss ($L_{RGB}$):** A combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and perceptual loss (based on VGG-19 features) between predicted and ground truth rendered images.
- **Training Variants:** For non-autoregressive training, random unordered input-target image pairs are sampled. For autoregressive training, ordered input sequences are sampled to simulate streaming data.
- **Regularization (Scene-level):** For scene-level data, additional regularization terms are used:
 - **Depth Regularization ($L_{depth}$):** A scale-invariant depth loss aligns Gaussian positions with pseudo ground truth depth derived from a monocular depth estimator.
 - **Opacity Regularization ($L_{opacity}$):** Used to reduce the number of redundant Gaussians.
- **Final Loss Function:** $L = L_{RGB} + \lambda_{depth} L_{depth} + \lambda_{opacity} L_{opacity}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that tttLRM achieves strong performance across various 3D reconstruction tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art feedforward methods and often being competitive with or superior to optimization-based approaches, particularly in terms of efficiency and scalability.

#### 4.1. Object-level Reconstruction (Objaverse and GSO datasets)

- **Performance vs. Baselines:** tttLRM consistently surpasses GS-LRM, an attention-based baseline, in both inference speed and reconstruction quality across different resolutions and input view counts (Table 1). For example, at 512x512 resolution with 8 input views, tttLRM achieves a 1 dB PSNR improvement while being twice as fast.
- **Scalability to High Resolution:** The model scales effectively to 1024x1024 resolution, a setting where GS-LRM encountered out-of-memory issues.
- **Generalization:** tttLRM exhibits robust generalization; a model trained with 8 input views can be directly applied to 16 or 24 views, with quality improving as context length increases.
- **Image-to-3D Generation:** When combined with a multi-view diffusion model, the 1024x1024 tttLRM can reconstruct high-fidelity 3D models from a single image, capturing fine-grained details (Figure 5).

#### 4.2. Scene-level Reconstruction (DL3DV-140 and Tanks&Temples datasets)

- **Superiority over Feedforward Baselines:** tttLRM significantly outperforms Long-LRM (the prior state-of-the-art for long-sequence Gaussian reconstruction), achieving approximately a 1 dB PSNR improvement across various input view counts (Table 2). This holds true even when Long-LRM is combined with additional post-optimization steps.
- **Competitiveness with Optimization-Based Methods:** Compared to optimization-based methods like 3DGS, Mip-Splatting, and Scaffold-GS, tttLRM achieves better results on 16 and 32 input views and remains competitive for 64 views, all while being orders of magnitude faster (hundreds of times).
- **Generalization Across Datasets:** A single tttLRM model demonstrates effective generalization to new datasets like Tanks & Temples and different sequence lengths.
- **Visual Quality:** Qualitative comparisons show tttLRM reconstructing scenes with higher fidelity, fewer artifacts, and sharper details compared to both optimization-based and other feedforward methods (Figure 4).
- **Distributed Acceleration:** The method benefits from linear acceleration when input is distributed across multiple GPUs.

#### 4.3. Autoregressive Reconstruction

- The model successfully supports autoregressive reconstruction, progressively improving rendering quality and scene coverage as additional input views are streamed (Figure 1, Table 2). The autoregressive variant of tttLRM consistently outperforms Long-LRM and remains competitive with or superior to optimization-based baselines.

#### 4.4. Decoding into Other 3D Formats

- The architecture demonstrates flexibility by being able to decode the latent 3D representation into formats other than 3DGS, such as triplane-based NeRFs (Figure 6). This is achieved by querying the fast weights with triplane tokens and finetuning with a rendering loss.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Pretraining from TTT-LVSM:** Leveraging pretrained weights from a novel view synthesis model (TTT-LVSM) significantly accelerates convergence and leads to higher final reconstruction quality for both 3DGS and triplane-based models (Figure 7, Table 3). This suggests that learned priors from novel view synthesis act as an effective inductive bias.
- **Autoregressive Strategy:** The proposed full reconstruction at each step in the autoregressive setting yields better results than a "Predict & Merge" strategy, which tends to accumulate errors from previous steps (Table 4).
- **Optimizer and Losses:** The Muon optimizer contributes to better results and stability, particularly in lower-resolution settings. The inclusion of depth and opacity regularization helps in reducing opaque Gaussians and improving reconstruction quality (Table 5).

#### 4.6. Limitations

- The fixed-size memory of fast weights may limit the model's ability to handle extremely complex scenarios with an exceptionally large number of input views, particularly in the autoregressive setting where earlier inputs might be forgotten.
- There is a slight quality degradation compared to the pretrained LVSM model when fine-tuning for explicit 3D representations, indicating a potential trade-off between implicit rendering quality and the benefits of explicit representation (speed, controllability).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

tttLRM introduces several significant advancements that can impact the field of 3D reconstruction:

- **Scalability for Long Contexts:** By leveraging Test-Time Training with linear computational complexity, tttLRM overcomes a major bottleneck of previous Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs). This enables the processing of significantly longer input sequences (up to 64 views and more with distributed training, handling millions of tokens) and higher resolutions (1024x1024), making it suitable for complex scenes and large-scale environments.
- **Efficient Autoregressive Reconstruction:** The framework's ability to perform online, causal updates and progressive refinement from streaming observations is critical for real-world applications requiring continuous perception. This allows for dynamic 3D scene understanding and refinement as new data becomes available.
- **Bridging the Implicit-Explicit Gap:** tttLRM effectively combines the high-quality novel view synthesis capabilities derived from implicit latent-space representations (through pretraining and fast weights) with the real-time rendering speeds and controllability of explicit 3D representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting. This addresses a long-standing trade-off in the field.
- **Unified and Flexible Framework:** The model provides a unified architecture that supports both feedforward and autoregressive modes of operation. Its flexibility in decoding the implicit latent 3D representation into various explicit formats (e.g., 3DGS, triplane-based NeRFs) expands its utility for diverse downstream applications.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving superior reconstruction quality and efficiency compared to existing feedforward methods, and competitive performance with optimization-based approaches, establishes tttLRM as a significant advancement in the field.
- **Practical Applications:** The advancements in long-context, autoregressive, and efficient 3D reconstruction have implications for applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, augmented/virtual reality, and large-scale digital twin creation, where real-time, high-fidelity 3D scene understanding from streaming data is essential.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for further exploration into advanced memory mechanisms for fixed-size fast weights, strategies to further enhance reconstruction quality while maintaining speed, and optimizing for real-time performance in even more complex and dynamic scenarios.
