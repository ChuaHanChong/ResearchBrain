# Discovered Policy Optimisation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at FLAIR Oxford, UC Berkeley, and Google Brain discovered a new reinforcement learning algorithm, Discovered Policy Optimisation (DPO), by meta-learning a key component within the theoretically-sound Mirror Learning framework. DPO consistently outperforms PPO across various continuous control tasks, generalizes to unseen environments, and is a simple, analytically derived algorithm with provable guarantees.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning algorithms are hand-designed, leading to methods that can be brittle to hyperparameters, lack robustness, and struggle to generalize.
- Existing algorithm discovery methods often produce black-box solutions without theoretical guarantees, frequently fail to consistently outperform handcrafted algorithms, and struggle with generalization beyond training distributions.
- A lack of interpretability in meta-learned algorithms hinders understanding and systematic improvement of policy optimization principles.

## Method

- Utilize the ==Mirror Learning framework==, which guarantees monotonic policy improvement and convergence, to define a space of theoretically sound algorithms.
- Parameterize the ==drift function== within Mirror Learning using a neural network, imposing architectural constraints to ensure theoretical validity.
- Meta-train the drift function's parameters using ==Evolution Strategies (ES)== to optimize the final return of the inner-loop RL agent, allowing for learning from scratch (LPO-Zero) or fine-tuning from PPO (LPO).
- Analyze the behavior of the learned LPO drift function to derive a novel, interpretable, ==closed-form analytical expression== for Discovered Policy Optimisation (DPO).

## Results

- The meta-learned LPO algorithm consistently outperformed PPO across most Brax continuous control environments and demonstrated strong generalization to unseen environments and varied hyperparameter settings.
- Analysis of the learned LPO drift function led to the formulation of Discovered Policy Optimisation (DPO), a novel, closed-form algorithm that is theoretically sound and easily implementable.
- DPO matched LPO's state-of-the-art performance, consistently outperforming PPO on both **Brax** and **Minatar** tasks, showcasing its transferability and practical utility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective policy optimization benefits from a 'rollback' mechanism for actions with negative advantages and decreased probabilities, and 'cautious optimism' for actions with positive advantages and increased probabilities.
> - The learned drift function implicitly encourages higher policy entropy throughout training without an explicit entropy bonus, promoting robust exploration.
> - Sophisticated, asymmetric update rules, learned by the meta-algorithm, can lead to superior performance and generalization compared to symmetric clipping methods like PPO.
> - Meta-learning within a principled, theoretically-sound framework enables the discovery of high-performing, interpretable algorithms with provable guarantees.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is strategically positioned at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL), meta-learning, and algorithm design, addressing fundamental challenges in developing robust and generalizable RL methods.

**Challenges in RL Algorithm Design:**
For the past decade, RL has seen remarkable progress, largely driven by the manual design of algorithms like TRPO and PPO. These algorithms, though effective and stable for many tasks, are products of human intuition, mathematical derivation, and extensive experimentation. This human-centric approach has inherent limitations: it's constrained by human understanding and ingenuity, often leading to algorithms that are brittle to hyperparameter settings, lack robustness guarantees, and struggle to generalize to novel environments without significant re-tuning. The field recognizes the need to move beyond these handcrafted solutions.

**Evolution of Algorithm Discovery:**
To overcome these limitations, the concept of "algorithm discovery" has emerged, aiming to automate the design of learning algorithms.
- **Meta-RL (task-specific adaptation):** Approaches like RL² and MAML enable agents to rapidly adapt to tasks *within a pre-specified distribution*. However, they typically fail to generalize to tasks truly outside their training distribution.
- **Black-box algorithm discovery (general algorithms):** More ambitious methods attempt to meta-learn general RL algorithms from scratch. While some show promise in specific scenarios (e.g., Evolved Policy Gradients surpassing PPO on average performance but with higher variance), they often fail to consistently outperform existing handcrafted algorithms, lack theoretical guarantees of convergence or improvement, and their learned mechanisms are often difficult to interpret. This lack of interpretability hinders systematic improvement and the extraction of general principles.

**Role of Mirror Learning:**
"Discovered Policy Optimisation" seeks a crucial middle ground by leveraging **Mirror Learning**, a recently introduced theoretical framework. Mirror Learning defines an infinite family of RL algorithms, all of which come with *provable guarantees* of monotonic policy improvement and convergence to an optimal policy. These algorithms share a common template but differ in specific components, such as the "drift function," which intuitively guides the agent's update by penalizing large changes. Popular algorithms like PPO are instances of this framework.

By operating within the Mirror Learning space, this paper addresses the critical flaw of previous algorithm discovery methods: the lack of theoretical soundness. It shifts the paradigm from purely black-box search to a *principled exploration* within a theoretically sound framework. The work aims to demonstrate that meta-learning components within this framework can yield algorithms that not only perform well but also inherit robust theoretical guarantees and offer interpretable insights, bridging the gap between empirically driven and theoretically grounded RL research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **discover novel, high-performing, and theoretically sound reinforcement learning algorithms through meta-learning**, specifically by optimizing a key component within the principled Mirror Learning framework.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Handcrafted Algorithms:** Traditional RL algorithms, despite their success, are designed manually. This process is inherently limited by human understanding and can lead to algorithms that are sensitive to hyperparameter choices, lack robustness, and struggle to generalize across diverse environments. The authors are motivated to move beyond this manual design paradigm to unlock greater performance and reliability.
- **Overcoming Shortcomings of Existing Algorithm Discovery Methods:** Previous attempts at meta-learning RL algorithms often suffer from critical weaknesses. Many meta-RL approaches struggle with generalization to truly unseen tasks, while more general algorithm discovery methods frequently fail to outperform handcrafted algorithms and, crucially, lack theoretical guarantees. This absence of theoretical grounding makes it difficult to trust their reliability or apply them to safety-critical domains.
- **Leveraging Theoretical Guarantees from Mirror Learning:** The Mirror Learning framework offers a unique opportunity. It provides a vast space of algorithms, all of which are *provably correct*, guaranteeing monotonic policy improvement and convergence to optimality. The motivation is to explore this space using meta-learning to design new algorithms that inherit these strong theoretical foundations by construction, thereby addressing the issue of lacking guarantees in meta-learned algorithms.
- **Pursuit of Interpretability and General Principles:** Beyond merely finding a high-performing algorithm, a significant motivation is to *understand* what makes a discovered algorithm effective. By parameterizing a specific component (the drift function) within a structured framework like Mirror Learning, the authors aim to analyze the learned function, extract general insights into policy optimization, and potentially formulate these insights into a novel, closed-form algorithm. This interpretability allows for a deeper understanding of the learning process, which is invaluable for future algorithm design.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** Ultimately, the authors are motivated to develop algorithms that not only possess theoretical soundness but also demonstrate superior performance, robustness, and transferability across a wide range of tasks and hyperparameter settings compared to existing state-of-the-art methods like PPO.

In essence, the paper aims to combine the power of automated algorithm discovery with the rigor of theoretical guarantees, leading to a new class of RL algorithms that are both effective and principled.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research revolves around using meta-learning to discover an optimal "drift function" within the Mirror Learning framework, which is then analyzed to derive a new, interpretable RL algorithm.

**The Mirror Learning Framework:**
The paper utilizes Mirror Learning, a theoretical framework where an RL algorithm's policy update is defined by maximizing a "mirror objective." This objective balances maximizing the advantage function with minimizing a "drift function" `D_pi_k(pi|s)`. The drift function, intuitively, measures the significance or cost of changing from the current policy `pi_k` to a candidate policy `pi` at a given state `s`. A key aspect of Mirror Learning is that if the drift function satisfies specific mathematical conditions (non-negativity, zero at identity, zero gradient at identity), the resulting algorithm is guaranteed to achieve monotonic policy improvement and converge to an optimal policy.

**Parameterizing the Drift Function:**
The central idea is to learn this drift function using a neural network. The drift function `D_pi_k(pi|s)` is parameterized as `E_a~pi_k [f_phi(x)|s]`, where `f_phi` is a fully-connected neural network with parameters `phi`.
- **Input Features:** To make the learned drift function generalizable and interpretable, `f_phi` takes as input `x`, which is composed of features derived from the probability ratio `r = pi(a|s)/pi_k(a|s)` and the normalized advantage `A = A_pi_k(s,a)`. Specifically, the input `x_r,A` includes terms like `(1-r)`, `(1-r)^2`, `(1-r)A`, `log(r)`, `log(r)^2`, and `log(r)A`. These inputs are chosen for their transferability and direct relevance to policy updates.
- **Ensuring Validity:** To guarantee that `f_phi` constitutes a valid drift function within the Mirror Learning framework, specific architectural constraints are imposed:
 - Bias terms are excluded from the network to ensure `f_phi(x)` is zero when `r=1` (i.e., when `x_r,A = 0`).
 - A shifted ReLU activation (`ReLU(x - ξ)`) is applied to the last layer's output to ensure non-negativity. These measures ensure the learned function satisfies the theoretical requirements for monotonic improvement.

**Meta-Training with Evolution Strategies (ES):**
The neural network parameters `phi` of the drift function are "meta-trained" using Evolution Strategies (ES).
- **Meta-Objective:** The meta-objective `F(phi)` is defined as the expected final return `E[eta(pi_K)|alg_phi]` of an RL agent after `K` inner-loop training iterations using the algorithm `alg_phi` (i.e., the Mirror Learning algorithm with the learned drift function). This optimizes the *performance of the learning algorithm itself*.
- **Why ES?** ES is a gradient-free optimization method that is well-suited for meta-learning because it avoids the computational complexity and potential biases associated with backpropagating gradients through an entire inner-loop RL training episode, a common challenge in meta-gradient approaches.
- **Process:** In each outer-loop iteration (generation), ES samples perturbations of `phi`. For each perturbed `phi`, a full RL training run (inner loop) is performed using the policy optimization algorithm defined by `alg_phi`. The final performance (`eta(pi_K)`) is then used to estimate the gradient of `F(phi)` with respect to `phi`, guiding the update of `phi`.

**Two Meta-Training Setups:**
The authors explored two main setups for training `f_phi`:
1. **LPO-Zero:** Learning the drift function completely from scratch without any prior initialization from existing algorithms.
2. **LPO (Learnt Policy Optimisation):** Initializing the drift function *near the PPO drift function*. This is achieved by adding the PPO drift function's core component to the output of the hidden layer before the final shifted ReLU. This setup directly tests if the neural network can improve upon PPO's design.

**From LPO to DPO (Discovered Policy Optimisation):**
A critical part of the methodology is to move beyond a black-box neural network. After meta-training LPO and identifying a high-performing drift function, the authors perform a detailed **analysis of the learned LPO drift function's behavior**. They visualize its derivatives and identify consistent, interpretable features that contribute to its performance (e.g., how it treats positive vs. negative advantages, how it encourages or discourages policy changes). These insights are then used to **formulate a novel, closed-form, analytical expression** for a new algorithm, DPO (Discovered Policy Optimisation). This DPO drift function is then mathematically verified to satisfy the Mirror Learning conditions.

The meta-training was conducted using the Brax physics simulator, which offers high parallelization on accelerators, making the extensive inner-loop RL training feasible. The policy architecture and core training hyperparameters were kept consistent with Brax's PPO implementation, ensuring a fair comparison and focus on the drift function's impact.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy of meta-learning within the Mirror Learning framework to discover high-performing and interpretable RL algorithms.

**1. LPO-Zero (Learning from Scratch):**
When the drift function was learned entirely from scratch (LPO-Zero), it did exhibit some PPO-like characteristics, such as clipping for negative advantages. However, it generally **underperformed** compared to LPO, which was initialized closer to PPO. This suggests that while algorithm discovery from scratch is possible, it likely requires substantially more meta-training resources to reach competitive performance levels.

**2. LPO (Learnt Policy Optimisation) Performance and Generalization:**
LPO, trained with a PPO-initialized drift function, demonstrated **state-of-the-art performance** in Brax continuous control environments.
- **Superior Performance:** LPO consistently **outperformed PPO** across most evaluated Brax environments (e.g., Ant, Humanoid, HalfCheetah, Walker2d), often achieving higher mean evaluation returns and showing greater consistency across multiple random seeds (Figure 6).
- **Generalization to Unseen Environments:** Crucially, LPO was meta-trained on only a single environment (Ant) but successfully **generalized to unseen Brax environments**. This indicates its robustness and ability to learn general policy optimization principles.
- **Robustness to Hyperparameter Settings:** LPO also showed robustness to varied hyperparameter settings that were originally tuned for PPO across different Brax tasks, further highlighting its generalizability.

**3. Insights from the Learned LPO Drift Function:**
Analysis of LPO's learned drift function provided key insights into effective policy optimization strategies, leading to the identification of four central features (visualized in Figure 3):
- **Rollback for Negative Advantage:** For actions with negative advantages (A < 0) and decreased probability ratios (r < 1), LPO's objective derivative remained positive in a significant region. This "rollback" mechanism encourages the agent not to completely abandon actions that currently appear poor, maintaining some exploration and preventing premature convergence or getting stuck in local optima.
- **Cautious Optimism for Positive Advantage:** For actions with positive advantages (A > 0) and increased probability ratios (r > 1), LPO exhibited a "cautious optimism." Instead of simply clipping updates like PPO, it gradually decreased the pace of updates, preventing over-reliance on potentially noisy or erroneous critic estimates for extremely high advantage values.
- **Implicit Entropy Maximization:** The combination of rollback and cautious optimism effectively encourages the agent to spread its policy probability mass more moderately across actions. This leads to **higher policy entropy** throughout training compared to PPO (Figure 4), promoting richer exploration without requiring an explicit entropy bonus term in the objective function. This is a significant "discovered" mechanism.
- **Update Asymmetry:** LPO learned to apply asymmetric update rules depending on the sign of the advantage, a sophisticated departure from PPO's largely symmetric clipping. This asymmetry respects the natural difference in consequences between increasing (potentially strengthening a good action) and decreasing (potentially abandoning a poor action) action probabilities.

**4. DPO (Discovered Policy Optimisation): A Novel Closed-Form Algorithm:**
Based on the identified key features of LPO (cautious optimism and rollback), the authors formulated **Discovered Policy Optimisation (DPO)**, a novel closed-form RL algorithm (Equation 9).
- **Mathematical Formulation:** DPO employs a drift function that takes different functional forms based on the sign of the advantage, using `tanh` to create smooth, non-linear penalties that mimic LPO's behavior.
- **Theoretical Soundness:** The DPO drift function was mathematically verified to satisfy all the conditions of a valid Mirror Learning algorithm, thus inheriting its theoretical guarantees.
- **Performance:** DPO **matched LPO's performance** and consistently **outperformed PPO** across both Brax and Minatar environments (Figure 6 and Figure 7). This is particularly noteworthy as DPO is a simple, analytical model derived from LPO, yet it retains LPO's high performance and generalization capabilities, including transfer to out-of-distribution Minatar tasks.
- **Practicality:** DPO's closed-form nature makes it extremely easy to implement on top of existing PPO codebases, requiring only a few lines of modification.

In summary, the paper successfully demonstrated that meta-learning within the Mirror Learning framework can lead to superior RL algorithms. Crucially, the approach allowed for the extraction of interpretable insights, enabling the formulation of a theoretically sound, high-performing, and easily deployable analytical algorithm (DPO).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of reinforcement learning, offering a novel paradigm for algorithm discovery that combines theoretical rigor with empirical excellence.

**1. A New Paradigm for RL Algorithm Design:**
The most profound significance of this work lies in establishing a viable and effective methodology for "algorithm discovery." By restricting the meta-learning search space to the theoretically sound Mirror Learning framework, the authors successfully navigate the trade-off between arbitrary black-box optimization and rigid human design. This approach demonstrates that algorithms can be automatically "discovered" while retaining crucial theoretical guarantees, addressing a major limitation of previous meta-learning efforts in RL. This opens the door to a new era where RL algorithms are not solely handcrafted but are systematically learned and improved.

**2. Theoretical Soundness for Learned Algorithms:**
A core contribution is the development of DPO, which is explicitly highlighted as "the first theoretically-sound, scalable deep RL algorithm that was discovered via meta-learning." This is a critical advancement. Traditional algorithm discovery often produces opaque, heuristic-based solutions lacking formal guarantees. By leveraging Mirror Learning, the paper ensures that both LPO (the meta-learned function) and DPO (its analytical approximation) inherently possess properties like monotonic policy improvement and convergence, which are vital for reliability and trustworthiness in complex or sensitive applications.

**3. Enhanced Performance, Generalization, and Robustness:**
LPO and DPO consistently outperform the state-of-the-art PPO algorithm across a variety of Brax environments and exhibit strong generalization capabilities to unseen environments and hyperparameter settings. This addresses common pathologies in RL, such as hyperparameter sensitivity and poor domain transfer. The improved performance and robustness offered by DPO could lead to more efficient and reliable training of agents in various real-world scenarios.

**4. Interpretability and Insights into Policy Optimization:**
The ability to analyze the learned LPO drift function and distill its effective mechanisms into a simple, closed-form algorithm like DPO is highly impactful. The identification of principles like "rollback for negative advantage" and "cautious optimism for positive advantage," alongside the implicit discovery of "entropy maximization," provides valuable, human-understandable insights into *why* these algorithms perform well. This interpretability is crucial not only for understanding and trusting the discovered algorithms but also for informing future human-led design efforts in RL. It transforms a black-box discovery into actionable knowledge.

**5. Practical Impact and Ease of Adoption:**
DPO, as a two-line analytical model, is remarkably easy to implement on top of existing PPO codebases. This low barrier to adoption, coupled with its superior performance and theoretical guarantees, makes DPO a highly attractive alternative for RL practitioners. It offers an immediate, practical benefit to the community by providing a more robust and effective policy optimization algorithm.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge a potential limitation: the learned drift function might overfit to specific code-level details of the Brax PPO implementation. Future work could address this by expanding the inputs to the drift function (e.g., incorporating algorithm hyperparameters, training progress statistics, or even task-specific information) and by meta-learning other attributes of the Mirror Learning framework beyond just the drift function. Such advancements promise to yield even deeper insights and more powerful, universally applicable RL algorithms.

In conclusion, "Discovered Policy Optimisation" represents a landmark study, showcasing how principled theoretical frameworks, combined with advanced meta-learning techniques, can fundamentally transform the way RL algorithms are designed, leading to solutions that are simultaneously high-performing, theoretically sound, and interpretable.
