# Embodiment-Aware Generalist Specialist Distillation for Unified Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces EAGLE, an iterative framework employing generalist-specialist distillation and embodiment-aware observations for unified whole-body control of heterogeneous humanoid robots. The framework enables a single policy to generate complex behaviors like walking, squatting, and leaning, demonstrating robust zero-shot sim2real transfer on four distinct physical humanoid robots.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid whole-body control policies are highly specific to individual robot embodiments, requiring extensive retraining and fine-tuning for each new hardware platform due to variations in dynamics and degrees of freedom.
- Prior generalist approaches for legged robots are often limited to low-dimensional velocity commands, restricting the behavioral repertoire and preventing more complex whole-body movements like torso pitching or squatting.
- Many cross-embodiment control methods are primarily validated in simulation, lacking robust demonstrations of transferability and stability on diverse real-world humanoid hardware.

## Method

- The EAGLE framework employs an iterative generalist-specialist distillation loop, where a generalist policy is refined by distilling knowledge (actions and latent representations) from fine-tuned robot-specific specialist policies.
- A unified 5-dimensional command space and an embodiment-aware observation (rigid body descriptors of mass, CoM, inertia) are designed to enable rich behaviors and allow the policy to distinguish between heterogeneous robot morphologies.
- An embodiment alignment strategy unifies action spaces via zero-padding and fixed index mapping, allowing a single neural network to control robots with varying degrees of freedom and kinematic layouts.

## Results

- The EAGLE framework achieved superior command-tracking accuracy across five heterogeneous humanoid robots in simulation, consistently outperforming baseline generalist PPO and other distillation methods like Kickstarting and COMPASS.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the embodiment-aware observation is necessary for the policy to effectively integrate robot-specific knowledge, leading to improved performance.
- The simulation-trained EAGLE policy demonstrated robust zero-shot sim2real transfer on four physical humanoid robots (Unitree H1, G1, Fourier N1, Booster T1), successfully executing diverse and stable whole-body behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodiment-aware observations are critical for the policy to learn distinct, morphology-specific internal representations for different robots, as evidenced by separated clusters in t-SNE visualizations of latent embeddings.
> - Iterative generalist-specialist distillation, particularly with representation-level alignment loss, effectively transfers refined skills from specialized policies to a generalist, enabling unified control without per-robot reward tuning.
> - A unified high-dimensional command interface allows a single policy to execute a rich repertoire of whole-body behaviors, including precise velocity tracking, base height adjustments (squatting), and body pitch control (leaning).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of policies capable of transferring across diverse robot platforms represents a significant challenge and active area of research in robotics. In the domain of robot manipulation, large-scale visuomotor models and cross-domain datasets have demonstrated success in enabling generalization across different manipulators and tasks. Similar progress has been observed in robotic navigation, where policies trained on diverse data can transfer to various environments.

However, the field of humanoid whole-body control (WBC) presents distinct difficulties that differentiate it from manipulation or navigation tasks. Data-hungry paradigms, such as imitation learning, which rely on extensive teleoperation for data collection in manipulation, are not directly applicable to legged locomotion. A robot without an existing controller cannot be easily teleoperated to generate training data. Consequently, reinforcement learning (RL) has become a primary method for humanoid WBC, achieving advanced behaviors like running, hopping, and dancing. Nevertheless, most of these RL-driven achievements are highly specific to a single robot embodiment. Variations in hardware attributes, including dynamics, degrees of freedom (DoFs), and kinematic topology, typically prevent a single policy from transferring directly to different humanoid robots. This often necessitates retraining and fine-tuning of reward functions for each new robot, which impedes efficient deployment.

Previous attempts to address this embodiment gap have involved methods such as diffusion-based motion priors or extensive randomization of URDF (Universal Robot Description Format) parameters during training. While these approaches have shown some progress, they are often limited to low-dimensional velocity commands, precluding more complex behaviors like torso pitching, squatting, or leaning. Furthermore, many of these methods have primarily been validated in simulation, with limited demonstrations of robust transfer to diverse physical humanoid hardware.

This research by Peng et al. directly addresses these limitations by proposing a framework designed to achieve unified whole-body control across multiple heterogeneous humanoids with a single policy. It aims to support a richer set of behaviors beyond simple walking and to demonstrate robust performance on real-world, diverse humanoid hardware. This work contributes to the broader goal of developing generalist robot controllers that can operate a fleet of varied robots, thereby advancing the scalability and practicality of humanoid robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a single, unified whole-body control policy capable of commanding multiple heterogeneous humanoid robots, while simultaneously enabling a rich repertoire of whole-body behaviors. This objective is motivated by several challenges and limitations observed in the current state of humanoid robotics:

1. **Heterogeneity of Humanoid Embodiments:** Humanoid robots exhibit significant variations in their physical characteristics, including the number of degrees of freedom (DoFs), kinematic topology, and dynamic properties. These discrepancies fundamentally hinder a single control policy from directly transferring between different robot models. The prevailing approach often involves developing and tuning a separate controller for each new robot, which is resource-intensive and time-consuming. The paper seeks to overcome this by creating a generalist policy that can abstract away these embodiment-specific details to a sufficient degree, allowing it to function across a diverse fleet.

2. **Limited Behavioral Repertoire of Existing Generalist Policies:** While some prior work has explored cross-embodiment learning for legged robots, these efforts have largely focused on low-dimensional commands, typically related to base velocities. This restricts the range of movements a robot can perform, limiting them to basic locomotion such as walking. There is a need for policies that can support more complex and nuanced whole-body commands, such as changes in body height (squatting), torso orientation (leaning), or combined movements. The authors aim to enable such rich behaviors through a high-dimensional command interface.

3. **Inefficiency of Per-Robot Reward Tuning:** Training reinforcement learning (RL) policies for new robot embodiments often requires extensive and iterative tuning of reward functions to achieve desired behaviors and stability. This process is a significant bottleneck for deployment. A core motivation for this work is to eliminate the need for such per-robot reward tuning, allowing the generalist policy to operate effectively on new or varied embodiments without additional manual calibration. This contributes to the goal of scalable, fleet-level robot control, where a single policy can be deployed across numerous robots with minimal customization.

4. **Lack of Real-World Validation for Cross-Embodiment Humanoid Control:** Many proposed methods for cross-embodiment control in legged robots have been evaluated predominantly in simulated environments. The transferability and robustness of these policies to real-world hardware, which introduces complexities like unmodeled dynamics, sensor noise, and hardware tolerances, remain largely underexplored. A key objective of this work is to rigorously validate the proposed framework on multiple physical humanoid robots in zero-shot sim2real transfer scenarios, demonstrating its practical applicability and robustness.

In summary, the motivation is to advance the state of unified humanoid control by developing a framework that is both robust to embodiment heterogeneity and capable of executing a broad range of whole-body movements, thereby reducing development overhead and facilitating broader adoption of humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce EAGLE (Embodiment-Aware Generalist-Specialist Distillation), an iterative framework designed for unified whole-body control across heterogeneous humanoids. The methodology involves several key components:

#### 3.1. A. Unified Command and Observation Space

1. **Command Design:** To enable a richer set of behaviors beyond simple walking, a unified 5-dimensional command vector `c_t = [v_x, v_y, ω, h, p]^T` is designed.
 - `v_x, v_y, ω`: Task commands specifying desired linear velocities (forward/sideways) and angular velocity (yaw).
 - `h, p`: Behavior commands modulating base height offset (for squatting) and body-pitch angle (for leaning).
 This separation allows a single policy to generate diverse movements.

2. **Embodiment-aware Observation:**
 - **Proprioception:** The actor receives a window of `K=5` frames of proprioceptive data (`o_prio`), including joint positions, joint velocities, base angular velocity, and projected gravity.
 - **Gait Clock:** A gait clock function `sin(2πφ)` is included in the observation to assist with foot lifting patterns, incorporating gait frequency `f` and phase offset `ϕ` between legs.
 - **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** The critic receives additional privileged information, such as base linear velocity, height error, foot clearance, contact forces, link collision state, terrain friction, and height scan samples.
 - **Embodiment-aware Observation (`o_ea`):** A crucial component for the network to reason about robot morphology. `o_ea` is provided as privileged information to the critic and the actor is tasked to estimate it. It consists of `m` (chosen as 3: torso, both feet) rigid body descriptors, each containing mass, center of mass (CoM) position, and inertia matrix. This input helps the model distinguish between robots and their locomotion patterns.

#### 3.2. B. Embodiment Alignment

To allow a single neural network to share weights across different humanoids with varying DoF counts and kinematic layouts, an embodiment alignment strategy is employed:
- **Unified Action Space:** All robots' native action spaces (`a_i` ∈ R^n_i) are embedded into a unified, larger action space (`˜a_i` ∈ R^D_a) by zero-padding. A maximum action length `D_a = 32` is chosen to cover all potential joints (lower limbs, waist, head, upper limbs).
- **Fixed Index Mapping:** A robot-specific permutation matrix `P_m` maps each joint to a fixed global index within `D_a`. At execution, an inverse mapping `S_i` recovers the native actions.
- **Robot-Specific Parameters:** Parameters like foot swing height, joint stiffness, and damping are preserved and adjusted per robot.
- **Parallel Training:** During training, different humanoid models are spawned together in parallel environments with equal probability.

#### 3.3. C. Reward Function Design

The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm with an asymmetric actor-critic paradigm is used. The per-step reward `r_t` is a weighted sum of three terms:
- `r_task`: Encourages tracking of task commands (`v_x, v_y, ω`).
- `r_beh`: Encourages tracking of behavior commands (`h, p`), including foot-swing position and contact consistency.
- `r_reg`: Regularization term penalizing undesirable actions (e.g., falls, high torques, abrupt changes, foot slip, joint deviation).
Most reward terms are inherited from HugWBC. Reward weights are shared across robots, but term-specific targets or scales (e.g., nominal base height `h_target`) are adjusted to account for individual robot morphology.

#### 3.4. D. Distillation Loop Overview

The core of EAGLE is an iterative generalist-specialist distillation loop, which improves the generalist policy (`π_g`) beyond what direct PPO training on pooled embodiments can achieve. This loop alternates between two phases:

1. **Specialize Phase:**
 - The current generalist policy (`π_g`) is copied to create `N` robot-specific specialist policies (`{π_s_i}`).
 - Each specialist `π_s_i` is then fine-tuned exclusively on its corresponding robot `i` to refine its performance.

2. **Generalize Phase:**
 - Trajectories are collected by running the generalist policy (`π_g`) across the diverse set of robots.
 - For these collected trajectories, the actions proposed by `π_g` are relabeled with the actions dictated by the corresponding specialist policies (`π_s_1:N`).
 - The generalist policy (`π_g`) is then updated using a combined distillation loss `L`, which incorporates three components:
 - **PPO Loss (`L_PPO`):** Maintained to preserve the generalist's exploration capabilities.
 - **Action Alignment Loss (`L_a`):** A DAgger-style `L2` loss `E_τ∼π_g E_o_t∼τ [π_g(o_t) - π_s(o_t)]^2` that aligns the generalist's output action distributions with those of the specialists.
 - **Representation Alignment Loss (`L_e`):** A novel `L2` loss `E_τ∼π_g E_o_t∼τ [e_π_g(s_t) - e_π_s(s_t)]^2` that aligns the hidden feature representations (`e(·)`) of the actor networks for the generalist and specialists, encouraging them to learn similar internal representations of proprioceptive states. Coefficients `α = 0.02` and `β = 1` are used for `L_a` and `L_e` respectively.

This iterative process of specializing and then distilling ensures that the generalist policy continuously improves by incorporating the refined skills learned by the individual specialists, leading to a robust unified controller without per-robot reward tuning or network redesign. The loop continues until the generalist's performance converges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on five humanoid robots in simulation (Unitree H1, Unitree G1, Booster T1, Fourier N1, and PNDbotics Adam) and validated on four in real-world settings (Unitree H1, G1, Fourier N1, Booster T1).

#### 4.1. A. Command Tracking Accuracy Analysis

- **Overall Performance:** EAGLE (after a single distillation round) and EAGLE w/ ID (iterative distillation) consistently demonstrated superior command-tracking accuracy compared to baseline methods. They achieved lower episode-averaged errors for most command dimensions (linear velocities E_vx, E_vy; angular velocity E_ω; base height E_h; body pitch E_p).
- **Comparison to PPO (Generalist without Distillation):** Both EAGLE and EAGLE w/ ID significantly surpassed the baseline PPO policy, which was trained on all robots simultaneously without the distillation mechanism.
- **Comparison to Other Distillation Methods:**
 - **Kickstarting:** While an improvement over PPO, Kickstarting (a KL-based distillation) exhibited instability on certain embodiments (e.g., G1, Adam, T1), leading to significantly higher errors (e.g., E_vx = 0.761 on T1, five times higher than PPO). EAGLE, using a DAgger-style loss, proved more robust across diverse embodiments, winning on 80% of robot-metric pairs and avoiding catastrophic failures.
 - **COMPASS:** This cross-embodiment method showed good results on specific robots (e.g., Fourier N1) but failed to generalize consistently across all embodiments, yielding high errors on others (e.g., E_vx = 0.725 on H1, 0.763 on Adam, 0.831 on T1). EAGLE maintained low E_vx errors across all robots, demonstrating more stable and unified control.
- **Impact of Iterative Distillation (EAGLE w/ ID):** EAGLE w/ ID generally achieved lower task-command errors compared to EAGLE (single distillation round). Tracking-error curves showed that both specialists and generalists progressively improved in tracking accuracy over successive distillation cycles, confirming the effectiveness of the iterative loop.
- **Comparison to Single-Robot Specialists:** The EAGLE-generalist policy achieved performance comparable to single-robot PPO policies trained exclusively on one robot type (e.g., Unitree H1). Furthermore, the EAGLE-specialist policies (fine-tuned versions of the generalist) sometimes surpassed these single-robot baselines, indicating that cross-embodiment training with distillation can lead to highly capable specialized policies.

#### 4.2. B. Ablation of Embodiment-Aware Observation

- **Necessity of `o_ea`:** The ablation study showed that without the embodiment-aware observation (`o_ea`), the PPO policy's overall performance deteriorated. For instance, on Unitree H1, the E_vx tracking error increased significantly, suggesting that the policy struggled to integrate robot-specific knowledge without this explicit morphological information.

#### 4.3. C. Analysis of Learned Representations

- **Latent Space Visualization:** A t-SNE visualization of the actor network's latent embeddings (`e_π(·)`) was performed. For policies trained without embodiment-aware observation, the latent vectors for different robot types collapsed into overlapping clusters. In contrast, the EAGLE policy, which incorporates `o_ea`, produced distinct and well-separated clusters for each of the five humanoid embodiments. This result confirms that the embodiment-aware observation effectively enables the network to learn morphology-specific representations, allowing it to distinguish and reason about the unique dynamics of different robots.

#### 4.4. D. Zero-shot Sim2real Performance

- **Robust Real-World Transfer:** The EAGLE policy, trained purely in simulation, was deployed in a zero-shot manner on four real humanoid robots: Unitree H1, Unitree G1, Fourier N1, and Booster T1.
- **Diverse Behavior Execution:** The robots successfully executed a variety of commands, including walking, leaning, and squatting. Despite the inherent differences in morphology and real-world hardware dynamics, the controller consistently produced stable and controlled motions across all tested embodiments. This demonstrated the robust sim2real transfer capabilities of the framework and its ability to generalize effectively to heterogeneous physical humanoid platforms. The synchronous movement of different robots executing commands like leaning and squatting was also highlighted.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EAGLE framework represents a notable advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Scalable, Fleet-Level Humanoid Control:** The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to enable a single, unified policy to control multiple heterogeneous humanoid robots. This addresses a critical bottleneck in robotics by moving away from the paradigm of developing and fine-tuning a distinct controller for each robot embodiment. This scalability is essential for the practical deployment of humanoid fleets in diverse applications, significantly reducing the development time and resources required for new robot platforms.

2. **Reduction of Development Overhead:** By eliminating the need for per-robot reward tuning and network redesign, EAGLE streamlines the development process. The iterative generalist-specialist distillation framework automates the transfer of refined skills back into a generalist policy, making it more efficient to adapt controllers to new humanoid models. This could accelerate research and development cycles for new humanoid hardware.

3. **Expanded Behavioral Capabilities:** The introduction of a unified, high-dimensional command interface allows the generalist policy to execute a richer repertoire of whole-body behaviors, beyond simple locomotion. Capabilities such as squatting, leaning, and precise base velocity tracking enhance the versatility and utility of humanoid robots, enabling them to perform more complex and human-like tasks in varied environments.

4. **Robust Sim2Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot deployment of the simulation-trained policy on four distinct real-world humanoid robots demonstrates strong sim2real transfer capabilities. This is a crucial validation for any robot learning framework, as it bridges the gap between simulated training environments and the complexities of physical hardware. Such robustness is vital for practical applications where robots must operate reliably in unstructured real-world settings.

5. **Methodological Contributions to Cross-Embodiment Learning:** The iterative generalist-specialist distillation loop, combined with the novel representation-level alignment loss and embodiment-aware observation, provides a robust methodology for knowledge transfer across diverse robot morphologies. The t-SNE visualizations confirm that the embodiment-aware observations allow the policy to learn distinct internal representations for different robots, which is a key factor in its generalization capabilities. This methodological contribution could be extended to other domains of robotics facing similar cross-embodiment challenges.

6. **Foundation for Future Generalist Robot Learning:** This work lays a foundation for future research into more generalist robot controllers. The authors acknowledge limitations, such as not exhaustively evaluating on unseen embodiments and using coarse embodiment-aware observation. These point to promising future directions, like integrating explicit URDF randomization or richer morphology descriptors, which could further enhance the generalization capabilities of such frameworks.

In conclusion, EAGLE represents a significant step towards practical and scalable whole-body control for heterogeneous humanoid robots, offering a more efficient and versatile approach to developing advanced robotic capabilities.
