# USO: Unified Style and Subject-Driven Generation via Disentangled and Reward Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ByteDance's Intelligent Creation Lab developed USO, a unified framework that seamlessly integrates style-driven and subject-driven image generation by treating them as complementary tasks. The model, leveraging a novel cross-task co-disentanglement paradigm and Style Reward Learning, achieves state-of-the-art performance on a newly introduced USO-Bench benchmark across subject consistency, style fidelity, and text controllability.

## Problem

- Existing generative methods treat style-driven and subject-driven image generation as separate, disjoint tasks, leading to sub-optimal feature disentanglement.
- This isolated approach results in challenges such as content leakage from style references and difficulty in maintaining subject identity across varying styles or poses.
- A lack of unified frameworks capable of simultaneously handling diverse conditioning, including text, style references, and subject references, within a single model.

## Method

- A ==Cross-Task Triplet Curation== framework was developed to generate a large, high-quality dataset comprising content, style, and stylized content images, crucially including both layout-preserved and layout-shifted variations.
- The Unified Style-Subject Optimized (USO) model employs a two-stage training process: ==Style Alignment Training (SAT)== using a SigLIP semantic encoder for style, followed by ==Content-Style Disentanglement Training (CSDT)== with a separate VAE encoder for content.
- ==Style Reward Learning (SRL)== is integrated during fine-tuning, using a VLM-based reward to explicitly disentangle style from content and optimize for style similarity.

## Results

- USO consistently surpasses competing open-source models, achieving the highest DINO (**0.793**) and CLIP-I (**0.623**) scores for subject consistency and a leading CSD (**0.557**) score for style similarity on the new USO-Bench.
- Qualitative evaluations and a user study confirm USO's superior performance in maintaining subject identity, preserving intricate style details, and aligning with complex textual prompts across individual and combined tasks.
- Ablation studies validate the critical contributions of Style Reward Learning, Style Alignment Training, and the disentangled encoder design, demonstrating their necessity for USO's enhanced performance and artifact reduction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Style-driven and subject-driven generation are complementary tasks that benefit from a shared "cross-task co-disentanglement paradigm," where improving one task mutually reinforces the model's ability to perform the other.
> - Explicitly disentangling content and style features through separate encoding pathways (SigLIP for style, VAE for content) and a hierarchical style projector is crucial for achieving high-fidelity control and mitigating content leakage.
> - Style Reward Learning not only enhances style fidelity but also has a positive, unexpected impact on subject consistency, reducing common generative artifacts like a "plastic appearance."

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "USO" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence, particularly **generative AI, controllable image synthesis, and multimodal learning**.

The past few years have witnessed extraordinary advancements in **Text-to-Image (T2I) generation**, driven by models like Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, and more recently, Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) such as FLUX. These models have enabled unprecedented levels of image generation quality and realism from abstract text prompts. However, a significant challenge remains in achieving **fine-grained control** over the generated images, especially when conditioning on specific visual references rather than just text.

This paper specifically addresses two prominent challenges in visually-conditioned generation:
- **Style-Driven Generation (Style Transfer):** Applying the artistic style of a reference image to new content while preserving content integrity. Early methods focused on neural style transfer (e.g., AdaIN), while recent approaches leverage T2I models with specialized modules (e.g., IP-Adapter, DEADiff, StyleStudio) to extract and inject style features. A key issue here is **content leakage**, where non-style features from the reference image inadvertently influence the output.
- **Subject-Driven Generation (Personalization):** Generating new images of a specific subject (person, object) from a few reference images, often with varying poses, scenes, or actions, while maintaining the subject's identity and appearance (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, UNO). This task also grapples with **disentanglement**, specifically separating the subject's identity from its background, pose, or expression in the reference images.

Crucially, the authors highlight that existing literature generally treats these two tasks as **disjoint**, developing separate methodologies, datasets, and disentanglement strategies for each. This "isolated, single-task context" for disentanglement is identified as a fundamental limitation.

"USO" proposes a conceptual shift: viewing both style-driven and subject-driven generation as two sides of the same coin, ultimately concerning the **disentanglement and re-composition of content and style**. This aligns with a growing trend in the broader generative AI landscape towards **unified and multimodal frameworks** (e.g., OmniControl, DreamO, OmniGen) that aim to integrate diverse conditional generation capabilities within a single model. The work leverages the power of **Diffusion Transformers (DiT)** and their **in-context learning (ICL)** capabilities, which have shown promise in handling multiple visual and textual inputs flexibly.

Furthermore, the introduction of **Style Reward Learning (SRL)** places this work within the emerging paradigm of **reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)** or similar alignment techniques (like ReFL, ImageReward) applied to generative models. These methods use reward signals, often derived from learned preference models, to guide the model towards outputs that better align with desired attributes (in this case, style similarity).

Finally, the creation of **USO-Bench**, a novel benchmark for jointly evaluating style similarity and subject fidelity, underscores the maturity of this research area and the need for standardized, comprehensive evaluation protocols that reflect the complex interactions between different generative objectives. This contribution helps to define the landscape for future research in unified customization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind USO stems from a critical observation about the current state of style-driven and subject-driven image generation:
- **Problem of Disjoint Approaches:** Existing methods tackle style-driven and subject-driven generation as separate problems. While style-driven methods focus on stylistic similarity, subject-driven methods prioritize subject consistency. This isolated development leads to an "apparent antagonism" and sub-optimal disentanglement of features.
- **Complementarity Argument:** The authors argue that these tasks are not antagonistic but **complementary**. Both tasks fundamentally require disentangling content from style (or specific visual features from irrelevant ones). For instance, style-driven generation aims to *include* style features while *excluding* subject appearance, whereas subject-driven generation aims to *include* subject appearance while *excluding* style. Learning to perform one task effectively can, therefore, inform and enhance the model's ability to perform the other, leading to a "mutually reinforcing disentanglement process."

Based on this motivation, the **key objectives of the USO project** are:
1. **Unification:** To develop a **Unified Style-Subject Optimized (USO) customization model** that can seamlessly handle style-driven, subject-driven, and joint style-subject-driven generation within a single framework.
2. **Cross-Task Co-Disentanglement:** To implement a novel paradigm that explicitly models the coupling and complementarity between tasks, enabling a more precise separation of relevant and irrelevant features.
3. **High-Quality Data Curation:** To construct a large-scale, high-quality **triplet dataset** (content image, style image, stylized content image) specifically designed for cross-task training, including both layout-preserved and layout-shifted scenarios.
4. **Novel Disentangled Learning Scheme:** To introduce a training approach that simultaneously aligns style features, disentangles content from style, and is enhanced by reward signals.
5. **Performance Enhancement:** To achieve state-of-the-art performance in terms of subject consistency, style fidelity, and text controllability across all individual and combined tasks.
6. **Unified Benchmarking:** To establish **USO-Bench**, the first benchmark specifically designed for jointly evaluating style similarity and subject fidelity, to facilitate comprehensive assessment of unified customization models.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of task-specific disentanglement and push the boundaries of controllable generative AI towards more versatile and powerful applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The USO framework introduces a sophisticated, multi-stage approach for unifying style-driven and subject-driven generation. It builds upon latent diffusion models, specifically DiT (Diffusion Transformer) architectures, known for their strong foundational capabilities and in-context learning.

#### 3.1. Cross-Task Triplet Curation Framework

A cornerstone of USO is its novel data curation. Unlike prior work that often restricts content to layout-preserved scenarios, USO creates diverse triplets for deeper content-style disentanglement:
- **Foundation Models:** It starts with a state-of-the-art subject-driven model (UNO) and other advanced T2I models.
- **Specialized Experts:**
 - **Stylization Expert:** Trained to generate a new subject in a target style (I_s_ref from I_tgt).
 - **De-stylization Expert:** Trained to convert a stylized image back into a photorealistic counterpart (I_c_ref from I_tgt), supporting flexible layout shifts or preservation.
- **Triplet Construction:** For each curated stylized target image (I_tgt), its corresponding style reference (I_s_ref) and content reference (I_c_ref) are synthesized.
- **VLM-based Filtering:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM) filter ensures high style similarity between I_tgt and I_s_ref, and strong subject consistency between I_tgt and I_c_ref.
- **Dataset Output:** This process yields 200k stylized image pairs, forming triplets of `<style reference, de-stylized subject reference, stylized subject result>`, crucially including both **layout-preserved and layout-shifted** variations, which is vital for enabling subject re-positioning.

#### 3.2. Unified Customization Framework (USO)

The USO model is trained in two progressive stages:

- **Stage 1: Style Alignment Training (SAT)**
 - **Objective:** To imbue a pre-trained T2I model (FLUX.1 dev) with the ability to accept style reference images.
 - **Style Encoder:** Instead of a simple VAE, the model employs a **SigLIP semantic encoder** to process the reference style image (I_s_ref). The rationale is that style requires richer, more abstract semantic information than a VAE typically captures, especially for complex artistic styles.
 - **Hierarchical Projector (M_Proj):** A lightweight projector extracts multi-scale, fine-grained visual features from SigLIP embeddings and projects them into style tokens (z_s).
 - **Multimodal Input:** These style tokens are concatenated with text tokens (c) and noisy latent (z_t) to form the DiT's input: `z_1 = Concatenate(z_s, c, z_t)`.
 - **Training:** During this stage, all DiT parameters are frozen except for the Hierarchical Projector, allowing the extracted style features to align rapidly with the model's native textual distribution.

- **Stage 2: Content-Style Disentanglement Training (CSDT)**
 - **Objective:** To integrate subject conditioning and explicitly disentangle content and style features within the unified framework.
 - **Content Encoder:** The content image (I_c_ref) is encoded into conditional content tokens (z_c) using a **frozen VAE encoder**. This design ensures separate encoding pathways for content and style, mitigating content leakage from the style reference.
 - **Comprehensive Multimodal Input:** All conditional tokens are now fused: `z_2 = Concatenate(z_s, c, z_t, z_c)`. The content tokens receive positional indices via UnoPE for spatial awareness.
 - **Training:** The Hierarchical Projector remains frozen, while the DiT parameters are unfrozen and fine-tuned on the curated triplet dataset. This stage allows the model to learn to recombine any subject with any style, supporting layout shifts.

#### 3.3. Style Reward Learning (SRL)

To further enhance disentanglement and performance, USO incorporates a Style Reward Learning paradigm:
- **Objective:** To explicitly disentangle style from content during optimization by leveraging a reward signal.
- **Base Objective:** The model is pre-trained using a standard flow-matching objective (L_Pre) to predict velocity.
- **Reward Definition:** A reward score is defined as the style similarity between the generated image (I_θ) and the reference style image (I_s_ref), measured by a VLM-based filter or the CSD model (M_RM).
- **Reward Loss (L_SRL):** This is a negative reward loss, `L_SRL = -M_RM(ˆI_0, I_s_ref)`, where ˆI_0 is the predicted original image.
- **Combined Loss:** The final objective is a weighted sum: `L = L_Pre + λL_SRL`. The weighting coefficient λ is applied after an initial warm-up period to stabilize training.
- **Algorithm:** SRL alternates between gradient-free inference to predict `ˆI_0` and a reward-backward step to update model parameters. This fine-tunes the model, encouraging higher style fidelity.

#### 3.4. USO-Bench

To provide a rigorous evaluation platform for this unified approach, the authors developed **USO-Bench**:
- **Scope:** The first unified benchmark for evaluating subject-driven, style-driven, and joint style-subject-driven generation.
- **Content:** Comprises 50 content images (20 human-centric, 30 object-centric) and 50 style references.
- **Prompts:** Includes 30 subject-driven prompts (spanning pose, descriptive, instructive stylization) and 30 style-driven prompts. These are combined to create style-subject-driven scenarios.
- **Output:** Generates multiple images per prompt for individual tasks and a single image for combined tasks, leading to a substantial dataset for evaluation.
- **Metrics:** Evaluates subject consistency (CLIP-I, DINO), style similarity (CSD score), and text-image alignment (CLIP-T).

This comprehensive methodology, from data curation to a two-stage training process enhanced by reward learning and a dedicated benchmark, forms the backbone of USO's unified generation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on USO-Bench and DreamBench validate the effectiveness of the USO framework, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across all evaluated tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Summary

USO consistently outperforms competing open-source models in subject-driven, style-driven, and joint style-subject-driven generation. It achieves the highest scores in key metrics (CLIP-I, DINO, CSD) and leading performance in text-image alignment (CLIP-T). The qualitative results further corroborate these quantitative advantages, showcasing superior output quality and adherence to complex prompts.

#### 4.2. Subject-Driven Generation

- **Quantitative:** On USO-Bench, USO achieves the highest DINO (0.793) and CLIP-I (0.623) scores for subject consistency, and a competitive CLIP-T (0.288) score, surpassing baselines like UNO, BAGEL, and FLUX.1 Kontext dev (Table 1). On DreamBench, USO also obtains the highest CLIP-I (0.838) and DINO (0.777) scores among compared methods, with a very close CLIP-T score (0.317) to the top performer (Table 5).
- **Qualitative:** Figure 5 illustrates USO's ability to maintain high subject consistency even with complex descriptive and instructive style edits. It accurately preserves human appearance and identity, avoiding "copy-paste" artifacts seen in some competitors and producing more realistic, less "plastic" results (Figure 7).

#### 4.3. Style-Driven Generation

- **Quantitative:** USO achieves the highest CSD (0.557) and CLIP-T (0.282) scores on USO-Bench (Table 1), outperforming specialized style transfer methods like InstantStyle-XL, StyleStudio, and CSGO.
- **Qualitative:** Figure 6 showcases USO's superior style preservation, capturing intricate details like global color palettes, brushwork, and even abstract material textures (e.g., "Pixar-style," "wool felt style"). It handles highly abstract style references flawlessly, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Style-Subject-Driven Generation

- **Quantitative:** For the combined task, USO again leads with the highest CSD (0.495) and CLIP-T (0.283) scores (Table 2), significantly outperforming unified baselines like OmniStyle and StyleID.
- **Qualitative:** Figure 8 demonstrates USO's versatility, supporting both layout-preserved generation (with empty prompts) and layout-shifted scenarios while simultaneously maintaining subject identity, matching reference style, and aligning with textual prompts.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user study involving both domain experts and non-experts further validates USO's performance. Radar charts (Figure 9) indicate that USO consistently ranks highest across metrics such as text fidelity, visual appeal, subject consistency, style similarity, and overall quality for both subject-driven and style-driven tasks.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies provide critical insights into the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Effect of Style Reward Learning (SRL):** Removing SRL leads to a significant drop in CSD score (style similarity) and also negatively impacts CLIP-I (subject consistency) and CLIP-T. This finding is crucial, as it validates the core hypothesis that enhancing one aspect (style) through reward learning can mutually benefit complementary tasks (subject consistency), reducing artifacts like the "plastic appearance" (Figure 10, Table 3).
- **Effect of Style Alignment Training (SAT):** Without SAT, CLIP-T for subject-driven tasks and CSD for style-subject-driven tasks degrade. Qualitatively, style fidelity (e.g., "oil-painting style") becomes noticeably weaker (Figure 11, Table 3). This highlights the importance of the initial style-feature alignment.
- **Effect of Disentangled Encoder (DE):** Replacing the separate SigLIP/Hierarchical Projector for style and VAE for content with a single VAE for both harms almost all metrics (CLIP-I, CLIP-T, CSD) and leads to significant content leakage and identity loss (Figure 11, Table 3). This strongly supports the necessity of explicitly disentangled encoding.
- **Effect of Hierarchical Projector:** The hierarchical projector for style encoding yields the highest CSD and a leading CLIP-T score compared to simpler projectors (resampler, MLP), emphasizing its effectiveness in capturing multi-scale style features (Table 4).

In summary, USO not only achieves quantitative and qualitative superiority but also demonstrates the profound impact of its novel cross-task co-disentanglement paradigm and reward learning strategy through rigorous ablation experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The USO framework represents a significant advance in controllable image generation, offering several impactful contributions to the field:

1. **Paradigm Shift for Disentanglement:** The most profound contribution is the introduction of the **"cross-task co-disentanglement paradigm."** By formally recognizing the complementary nature of style-driven and subject-driven generation, USO moves beyond isolated task-specific solutions. This conceptual shift suggests a more holistic approach to feature disentanglement in generative models, where learning to precisely *include* certain features for one task inherently strengthens the ability to *exclude* them for another. This could influence how future multi-modal generative models are designed and trained.

2. **Truly Unified Generative Model:** USO successfully unifies two complex and traditionally separate generation tasks into a single, cohesive framework. This reduces model complexity and redundancy in development, offering a more versatile tool for creators. A single model capable of handling diverse conditioning (text, style reference, subject reference, or any combination) is a significant step towards general-purpose visual synthesis systems.

3. **Enhanced Controllability and Fidelity:** The superior performance demonstrated by USO across subject consistency, style similarity, and text alignment translates directly into higher-quality and more controllable outputs. Users can now freely recombine subjects with various styles, poses, and backgrounds without compromising identity or stylistic integrity, even supporting layout shifts—a capability often missing or limited in prior work.

4. **Novel Methodological Components:**
 - **Triplet Curation for Cross-Task Learning:** The systematic approach to generate diverse triplet data (including layout-shifted variants) is a valuable blueprint for future research in multi-conditional generation.
 - **SigLIP and Hierarchical Projector for Style:** Leveraging a semantic encoder like SigLIP with a hierarchical projector for style representation is a sophisticated way to capture abstract style features, which could inspire better representation learning for other complex visual attributes.
 - **Style Reward Learning (SRL):** The integration of reward learning for style fidelity, and its surprising positive impact on subject consistency, highlights the potential of reinforcement learning techniques for complex multi-objective optimization in generative AI. It also hints at a path to mitigate common generative artifacts like "plasticity."

5. **Standardized Evaluation with USO-Bench:** The release of USO-Bench is a critical infrastructure contribution. A unified benchmark for evaluating combined style and subject generation capabilities will provide a standardized and comprehensive platform for assessing future models. This will accelerate research and enable fairer comparisons, helping the community converge on robust metrics and best practices.

6. **Broad Practical Applications:** The capabilities of USO have immense potential in various creative and commercial domains:
 - **Art and Design:** Artists can rapidly prototype ideas, apply diverse styles to subjects, and create visually consistent characters in different artistic renditions.
 - **Entertainment:** For game development, animation, and film, USO could aid in generating character assets, stylized environments, or consistent visual themes.
 - **E-commerce and Marketing:** Customizing product images in various styles or placing specific products into diverse scenes becomes more efficient and scalable.
 - **Personalized Content:** Creating personalized digital avatars or stylized user-generated content for social media.

Overall, USO pushes the boundaries of controllable image synthesis by offering a conceptually elegant and technically robust solution to unifying style and subject generation. It sets a new standard for performance and provides a valuable research framework, paving the way for more sophisticated and versatile generative AI applications in the future.
