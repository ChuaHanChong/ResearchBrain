# Label-Free Target-Domain Adaptation for Unconstrained Event-Image Feature Matching via Dual-Stage Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce a two-stage dual-distillation framework that enables label-free target-domain adaptation for unconstrained event-image feature matching. The method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with 32.23 AUC@20° on MVSEC and 28.56 AUC@20° on TUM-VIE, addressing challenges in real-world multi-sensor systems.

## Problem

- Existing cross-modal event-image matching methods rely on costly ground-truth labels or strictly aligned hardware, limiting their use in unconstrained real-world scenarios with physically decoupled sensors.
- Previous distillation-based approaches often learn representations specific to pretraining environments, leading to poor generalization to diverse, unseen target domains.
- Current evaluation benchmarks for event-image matching either use synthetic 2D homographies or idealized sensor setups, failing to reflect the complexities of genuine multi-sensor systems.

## Method

- A two-stage training paradigm is proposed: first, label-agnostic pretraining via ==cross-modal distillation== from a frozen image feature extractor on filtered event-image pairs.
- Second, ==epipolar-guided self-distillation== adapts the pretrained model to unlabeled target domains by using ==geometric consistency== and external ==epipolar geometry== as self-supervision.
- A new, more rigorous benchmark is constructed based on the **TUM-VIE** dataset, featuring physically decoupled event and standard cameras with heterogeneous properties for realistic evaluation.

## Results

- The full two-stage framework achieved state-of-the-art accuracy, reaching **32.23 AUC@20°** on MVSEC and **28.56 AUC@20°** on the newly introduced TUM-VIE benchmark with a lightweight **1.48M** parameter model.
- Epipolar-guided self-distillation significantly boosted performance, improving accuracy by **21.9%** on MVSEC (from **26.44 to 32.23 AUC@20°**) and **16.6%** on TUM-VIE (from **24.49 to 28.56 AUC@20°**) over zero-shot baselines.
- The new TUM-VIE benchmark provides a challenging, realistic platform for evaluating cross-modal pose estimation, demonstrating the ineffectiveness of direct modality translation baselines (e.g., RoMa achieved only **8.01-10.88 AUC@20°**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Native cross-modal representation learning is essential for event-image matching, as direct modality translation into pseudo-images is largely ineffective.
> - Epipolar-guided self-distillation, leveraging external geometric priors, effectively mitigates confirmation bias and bridges significant hardware-induced domain gaps in unlabeled, unconstrained systems.
> - An upgraded pretraining strategy, utilizing localized score distribution and contrastive descriptor losses on quality-filtered data, yields a strong foundational model with robust zero-shot generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the fundamental task of establishing pixel-level correspondences between event camera data and standard image data, which is crucial for multi-sensor systems. This field, known as cross-modal feature matching, has seen increasing interest due to the complementary nature of different sensory modalities. Event cameras, which report brightness changes asynchronously at the pixel level, offer advantages such as high temporal resolution, high dynamic range, and low latency, making them suitable for challenging scenarios like high-speed motion or difficult lighting. However, their sparse, asynchronous output differs significantly from dense, synchronous image frames, posing a challenge for traditional feature matching techniques designed for image-to-image correspondence.

Previous efforts in event-image matching generally fall into two categories. The first involves methods that rely on large-scale end-to-end training using synthetic multimodal data with matching labels. These approaches, exemplified by MINIMA, generate multimodal representations from datasets like MegaDepth, requiring known camera poses and pixel-wise depth to create ground-truth correspondences. Their limitation lies in their dependence on synthetic data and their inability to generalize to real-world scenarios where such labels are unavailable or to adapt to target domains without explicit supervision.

The second category comprises approaches that relax the need for synthetic labels by distilling knowledge from aligned event-image pairs, such as EI-Nexus. These methods leverage specialized hardware (e.g., coaxial DAVIS cameras) that provides pixel-perfect spatial alignment between event streams and images. While effective in their specific training environments, these alignment-dependent techniques are fundamentally restricted by their reliance on strict hardware alignment, rendering them unsuitable for deployment in unconstrained downstream datasets where sensors are physically decoupled and their spatial relationships are unknown.

Parallel to these developments, self-supervised methods have emerged as a significant area in correspondence estimation for single-modal tasks (e.g., image-to-image matching or tracking) due to the difficulty of obtaining pixel-level ground-truth correspondences in real-world settings. Methods like RIPE, Sonata, BootsTAP, and TAPNext utilize various self-supervision signals, often incorporating geometric priors or consistency checks, to learn robust features without explicit labels. This paper extends the self-supervised paradigm to the more challenging domain of cross-modal event-image matching, particularly addressing the unique challenges posed by unconstrained sensor configurations and label scarcity.

This research aims to bridge the gap left by previous methods by proposing a framework that operates in unlabeled and unconstrained real-world scenarios, thereby enabling broader applicability for event-image multi-sensor systems beyond laboratory or tightly controlled environments. It addresses the need for a robust and adaptable feature matching solution that does not require pre-existing matching labels or strict hardware alignment during deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel training paradigm that enables robust event-image feature matching in real-world, unconstrained, and unlabeled multi-sensor systems. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

Firstly, a central motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing cross-modal matching methods that either rely on expensive ground-truth matching labels or necessitate strictly aligned hardware configurations. In practical real-world applications, such as distributed sensor networks or mobile platforms, sensors are frequently physically decoupled, leading to unknown spatial relationships and a lack of prior geometric information. This makes it infeasible to obtain pixel-level matching ground truth or to assume pre-defined sensor relationships. The proposed method seeks to enable model optimization on target data without these restrictive requirements.

Secondly, the authors aim to enhance the foundational robustness and generalization capabilities of event-image matching models. Previous distillation-based methods, while effective, often learned representations specific to their pretraining environments, leading to limited transferability. The motivation is to develop a pretraining strategy that yields highly generalizable cross-modal features, minimizing overfitting to specific spatial configurations and enabling superior zero-shot performance on unseen data.

Thirdly, the research is motivated by the need for a mechanism that allows models to adapt and improve directly on unlabeled and unconstrained downstream data, effectively bridging domain gaps caused by hardware heterogeneity. This involves developing a self-supervision framework that can generate reliable learning signals without external labels, allowing the model to "self-evolve" its performance in diverse target environments. The incorporation of external geometric priors, specifically epipolar geometry, is motivated by the need to ensure that the self-generated supervision adheres to fundamental 3D physical constraints, thereby preventing error accumulation and confirmation bias often associated with purely data-driven self-supervision.

Finally, the authors identify a critical deficiency in existing evaluation benchmarks for event-image matching. Current benchmarks either rely on synthetic 2D homographies that fail to capture 3D scene complexities or utilize idealized sensor setups (e.g., coaxial DAVIS cameras) that do not reflect the spatial offsets, distinct intrinsics, and heterogeneous properties common in genuine multi-sensor systems. This motivates the construction of a new, more rigorous evaluation benchmark based on the TUM-VIE dataset, featuring physically decoupled cameras with varying intrinsic parameters and resolutions. This benchmark aims to provide a more realistic and challenging platform to accurately assess the performance of event-image matching algorithms in authentic multi-sensor applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a two-stage training paradigm for label-free target-domain adaptation in unconstrained event-image feature matching.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Label-Agnostic Pretraining via Distillation

The initial stage focuses on learning a robust and generalizable event representation through cross-modal distillation from a frozen image feature extractor.

- **Data Selection and Filtering**: The COESOT dataset, which provides large-scale, approximately aligned event-image pairs from DAVIS346 cameras, is used for pretraining. To ensure data quality, a Laplacian variance-based filtering strategy is applied to exclude pairs with motion blur or HDR artifacts in images, thereby selecting scenes with sharp edges suitable for distillation. For each sample, events within a 100 ms temporal window preceding the image timestamp are extracted, normalized, and voxelized into a 16-bin grid. A 1-channel binary event mask is concatenated, resulting in a 17-channel input.
- **Distillation Architecture**: A teacher-student paradigm is employed. The teacher is a frozen SuperPoint network (pre-trained image feature extractor), providing a stable image feature space. The student event network, referred to as the event branch, is a lightweight VGG-based architecture (1.48M parameters) with a backbone and two heads: a score head (outputting a 𝐻𝑐 × 𝑊𝑐 × 65 local score distribution) and a descriptor head (outputting a dense descriptor map). This structural alignment facilitates knowledge transfer. Synchronized geometric augmentations (random cropping, flips) are applied to both modalities during this stage.
- **Distillation Objectives**: Unlike pixel-wise regression, the pretraining incorporates three localized distillation losses:
 - **Latent Feature Loss (L𝑓𝑒𝑎𝑡)**: An L2 distance minimizes the discrepancy between intermediate semantic representations from the event and image backbones, promoting alignment of their feature spaces.
 - **Local Score Distribution Loss (L𝐾𝐿)**: Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence is used to match the 8x8 local probability distributions of keypoint scores between the event and image outputs. This encourages the student to learn relative keypoint likelihoods rather than absolute pixel values, enhancing keypoint robustness.
 - **Contrastive Descriptor Loss (Lℎ𝑖𝑛𝑔𝑒)**: A contrastive hinge loss is applied to create a modality-invariant descriptor space. It encourages high similarity between positive cross-modal pairs while pushing negative pairs beyond a specified margin, thereby improving discriminative power.
- **Optimization**: The student network is optimized using AdamW for 20 epochs. Supervision is exclusively applied to spatial pixels where events occurred, focusing learning on relevant regions.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Epipolar-Guided Self-Distillation

This stage adapts the pretrained model (initialized with 𝜃𝑝𝑟𝑒) to unlabeled and unconstrained downstream data, leveraging self-supervision derived from geometric consistency.

- **Framework**: A teacher-student architecture is again used. The student network parameters (𝜃) are optimized via gradient descent, while the teacher network parameters (𝜃𝑡) are updated by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's weights, ensuring a stable teacher. The image branches for both teacher and student remain frozen with SuperPoint weights.
- **Asymmetric Input Augmentation**: For the student network, the event stream (𝐸) is augmented with a random homography (H) to create a warped stream (𝐸𝐻). The image input (𝐼) remains unwarped and is fed to both teacher and student image branches. This creates challenging asymmetric inputs.
- **Feature Extraction and Matching**: Both teacher and student networks process their respective inputs (unwarped 𝐸, 𝐼 for teacher; warped 𝐸𝐻, unwarped 𝐼 for student) to produce keypoint locations and descriptors. Mutual Nearest Neighbor (MNN) matching is then performed to obtain initial cross-modal match sets for both the teacher (M𝑡) and student (M𝑠).
- **Cross-Modal Consistency Verification**: To generate reliable pseudo-labels, a dual-threshold consistency verification process is applied:
 - **Image-side Consistency**: For each matched image keypoint from the teacher (𝐼𝑃𝑡𝑖), its nearest neighbor in the student's image keypoints (𝐼𝑃𝑠𝑖) must be within a threshold (𝑡ℎ𝑖 = 1.5 pixels).
 - **Event-side Consistency**: The teacher's matched event keypoint (𝐸𝑃𝑡𝑖) is projected into the student's warped coordinate system using the known homography (𝐸𝐻𝑃𝑡→𝑠𝑖 = H · 𝐸𝑃𝑡𝑖). This projected point must then align with the student’s matched event point (𝐸𝐻𝑃𝑠𝑖) within a threshold (𝑡ℎ𝑒 = 5.0 pixels). If both conditions are met, 𝐸𝐻𝑃𝑡→𝑠𝑖 is considered a high-quality pseudo-ground-truth match.
- **Epipolar Geometric Confidence**: To mitigate confirmation bias, an external geometric filter is introduced. RANSAC is applied to the teacher’s initial matches to estimate a Fundamental matrix (F). For each matched pair, an epipolar distance (𝑒𝑟𝑟) to F is calculated. This error is converted into a normalized geometric confidence score (𝐶𝑖 = 𝑒𝑥𝑝(−𝑒𝑟𝑟𝑖)), which dynamically weights the self-distillation loss, prioritizing matches that adhere to 3D scene structure.
- **Self-Supervised Distillation Objectives**:
 - **Score Regression Loss (L𝑠𝑐𝑜𝑟𝑒)**: An L2 loss ensures the student’s event score at the pseudo-ground-truth location (𝐸𝐻𝑃𝑡→𝑠𝑖) mimics the corresponding teacher's image score.
 - **Contrastive Descriptor Loss (L𝑑𝑒𝑠𝑐)**: A contrastive hinge loss aligns the student’s event descriptor with the teacher’s image descriptor for positive pseudo-matches, while repelling hard negative descriptors.
- **Total Self-Supervised Loss**: The total loss is a sum of the score and descriptor losses for each pseudo-label, dynamically weighted by the geometric confidence (L = ∑𝑁𝑖=1 𝐶𝑖(L𝑠𝑐𝑜𝑟𝑒𝑖 + L𝑑𝑒𝑠𝑐𝑖)).
- **Dataset-Specific Configurations**: Hyperparameters such as training epochs, EMA momentum, event temporal slice window, and training resolution are adapted for MVSEC and TUM-VIE datasets to account for their distinct characteristics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several key findings across various evaluations:

- **TUM-VIE Event-Image Matching Benchmark**: A new and rigorous benchmark was introduced based on the TUM-VIE dataset, featuring physically decoupled and heterogeneous event and standard cameras. This benchmark facilitates the evaluation of cross-modal relative pose estimation under realistic conditions, addressing limitations of prior benchmarks that relied on synthetic data or idealized sensor setups.
- **Ineffectiveness of Direct Modality Translation**: Attempts to use single-modal image matchers (e.g., SuperPoint, LoFTR, RoMa) by converting event data into pseudo-images (I↔I matching) yielded poor performance on both MVSEC and TUM-VIE datasets. For instance, RoMa (outdoor) achieved only 8.01 AUC@20° on MVSEC and 10.88 on TUM-VIE. This indicates that native cross-modal representation learning (E↔I) is essential, as direct translation discards crucial spatiotemporal event dynamics.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - **Label-Supervised Baselines (MINIMA)**: MINIMA variants (LG, LoFTR, RoMa) showed vulnerability to domain overfitting. For example, MINIMA-RoMa's AUC@20° performance dropped significantly from 26.10 on TUM-VIE to 9.80 on MVSEC, demonstrating a lack of generalization across different sensor setups due to its reliance on precise matching labels.
 - **Alignment-Dependent Baselines (EI-Nexus)**: The proposed label-agnostic pretrained model (Stage 1) demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization, outperforming EI-Nexus (26.44 vs. 24.24 AUC@20° on MVSEC). Crucially, EI-Nexus's inability to fine-tune on unaligned downstream data was a significant bottleneck.
 - **Overall State-of-the-Art Performance**: The full two-stage framework, combining pretraining and self-distillation, achieved state-of-the-art accuracy. It reached 32.23 AUC@20° on MVSEC and 28.56 AUC@20° on TUM-VIE. The model maintained a lightweight architecture (1.48M parameters).
- **Effectiveness of Two-Stage Design and Self-Distillation**:
 - The pretrained model demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization, suggesting that the upgraded distillation objectives (local score distribution and contrastive descriptor losses) foster more generalizable features.
 - The epipolar-guided self-distillation (Stage 2) effectively bridged severe hardware-induced domain gaps in unlabeled and unconstrained systems. Fine-tuning on MVSEC data improved performance by 21.9% (from 26.44 to 32.23 AUC@20°) compared to the zero-shot baseline. Similarly, fine-tuning on TUM-VIE data resulted in a 16.6% improvement (from 24.49 to 28.56 AUC@20°). This confirms the necessity and efficacy of target-domain adaptation for genuinely heterogeneous multi-sensor systems.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - **Pretraining Configurations**: Upgrading from naive regression to KL divergence for score distribution and contrastive hinge loss for descriptors significantly boosted generalization. Furthermore, using filtered, high-quality COESOT data was critical for stable distillation and improved performance, outperforming unfiltered data. Scaling pretraining data (Filtered COESOT + MVSEC) further enhanced generalization.
 - **Self-Distillation Strategies and Confidence Metrics**: The epipolar-guided confidence was crucial. Naive self-distillation without confidence weighting improved performance over the baseline, but relying solely on descriptor similarity for weighting led to performance degradation due to confirmation bias. The "Full paradigm" utilizing objective epipolar geometry achieved the highest accuracy.
 - **Sensitivity of Thresholds**: Optimal performance was observed with an image-domain threshold of 1.5 pixels and an event-domain threshold of 5.0 pixels, indicating a balance between robust pseudo-label generation and the introduction of geometric noise.
- **Synergy Between Pretraining and Self-Distillation**: Experiments showed that a stronger pretrained foundational model (e.g., trained on combined COESOT + MVSEC) provided a higher baseline and, after self-distillation, achieved an even greater final performance (32.87 AUC@20° on MVSEC). This indicates that the self-distillation framework leverages richer geometric priors from a superior pretrained model to reach a higher optimization ceiling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact:

Firstly, the proposed two-stage training paradigm directly addresses a long-standing challenge in event-image feature matching: the reliance on expensive matching labels or strictly aligned hardware. By enabling robust model deployment on unconstrained and unlabeled downstream data, this work expands the applicability of multi-sensor systems to a wider range of real-world scenarios, particularly in distributed sensor networks, mobile robotics, and autonomous systems where precise calibration and ground-truth data are often unavailable or impractical to acquire. This effectively democratizes the use of event cameras in conjunction with standard cameras.

Secondly, the introduction of an epipolar-guided self-distillation framework represents a novel approach to self-supervised learning in cross-modal contexts. Leveraging external geometric priors (epipolar geometry) to supervise network self-evolution is a significant methodological advancement. This mechanism provides a robust way to learn accurate correspondences without explicit supervision, mitigating issues of confirmation bias and error accumulation often associated with purely data-driven self-supervision. This principle could be extended to other cross-modal learning tasks where geometric relationships are inherent but labels are scarce.

Thirdly, the construction of a rigorous cross-modal pose estimation benchmark based on the TUM-VIE dataset is a valuable contribution to the research community. This benchmark, featuring physically decoupled sensors with heterogeneous properties and genuine 3D parallax, offers a more realistic and challenging evaluation platform than previous idealized datasets. It provides a standardized testbed for future research, driving the development of more robust and generalizable event-image matching algorithms for real-world applications.

Finally, the achievement of state-of-the-art performance on both MVSEC and TUM-VIE datasets with a lightweight architecture (1.48M parameters) demonstrates the practical viability of the proposed method. The robust zero-shot generalization capabilities of the pretrained model, coupled with the significant performance boosts from self-distillation, suggest a powerful and efficient solution for handling domain shifts inherent in diverse multi-sensor deployments. This efficiency makes the approach suitable for resource-constrained embedded systems and real-time applications.

The paper also outlines a potential future impact by suggesting an extension of the framework to dense matching architectures. This indicates an avenue for further enhancing matching robustness and coverage, which could be particularly beneficial in scenarios with repetitive patterns or sparse textures where sparse keypoint matching might be insufficient. Overall, this work contributes to advancing the state of the art in robust, adaptable, and label-efficient multi-sensor perception.
