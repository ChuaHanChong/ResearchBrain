# Scaling Large Motion Models with Million-Level Human Motions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University, Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence, and collaborators introduced MotionLib, the first million-level human motion dataset, and MotionBook, a novel motion encoding method. This enabled the training of Being-M0, a large motion model that achieves state-of-the-art text-to-motion generation performance and empirically validates the benefits of scaling data and model size for motion models.

## Problem

- The severe scarcity of large-scale, high-quality human motion data limits the generalizability and performance of text-to-motion (T2M) generation models.
- Existing motion representations, particularly 1D VQ-based tokenizers, suffer from information loss, limited codebook sizes, and inefficient feature formats, hindering expressive motion generation.
- A lack of systematic understanding regarding the impact of scaling data and model size on motion generation performance.

## Method

- ==Developed MotionLib==, a million-level dataset curated from public videos and online platforms, refined through a multi-stage pipeline including 3D motion extraction, physical plausibility refinement, and hierarchical text description via LMMs.
- Proposed MotionBook, a novel motion encoding approach featuring SMPL-D135 (a compact, lossless motion feature) and 2D Lookup-Free Motion Quantization (2D-LFQ) to improve motion representation and enable larger codebooks.
- Trained Being-M0, a large motion model based on a decoder-only LLM backbone (e.g., LLaMA-2), using a two-stage process: motion-text alignment pre-training on MotionLib followed by motion instruction tuning.

## Results

- Introduced MotionLib, the first million-level human motion dataset, containing over **1.21** million sequences (**15x** larger than HumanML3D/Motion-X) with high-quality hierarchical text descriptions.
- Being-M0, trained on MotionLib with LLaMA2-13B and 2D-LFQ, achieves state-of-the-art R-Precision performance on the HumanML3D benchmark and demonstrates superior out-of-distribution generalization on UNSEEN-90K.
- Empirically validated that increasing model parameters (e.g., LLaMA-2 13B vs. GPT-2 355M) and training data scale (1.2M vs. 0.02M clips) leads to consistent performance gains in motion generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling both the data volume and model size consistently improves motion generation performance, validating the existence of scaling laws in this domain.
> - Hierarchical text descriptions (body-level and part-level) provide richer semantic understanding, enhancing the quality of generated motions.
> - The novel 2D Lookup-Free Quantization (2D-LFQ) method effectively utilizes larger codebooks (up to 2^16) for motion tokenization, overcoming limitations of traditional VQ methods like codebook collapse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion understanding and generation is experiencing a paradigm shift, largely inspired by the unprecedented success of Large Language Models (LLMs) in natural language processing and multimodal AI. This paper directly addresses the emerging trend of developing "large motion models" (LMMs), positioning itself at the forefront of this evolving landscape.

Historically, text-to-motion (T2M) generation has relied on models that typically combine motion tokenizers (like Vector Quantization, VQ), text encoders (like CLIP), and generative decoders (like GPT-2). While these models have achieved notable results, they face a critical bottleneck: the severe scarcity of massive, high-quality human motion data. Existing datasets, such as HumanML3D and Motion-X, are orders of magnitude smaller (e.g., 15x smaller) than the vast datasets available for text or image modalities (e.g., ImageNet). This data disparity severely limits the generalizability of current T2M models, restricting them to narrow scenarios and hindering their performance on diverse or unseen motions.

Recent efforts have begun to explore LLM-based generalist models for motion, such as MotionGPT, MotionLLM, and AvatarGPT, aiming for broader instruction and task generalization. However, as the authors note, these models have not yet fully demonstrated the effectiveness of scaling in the motion domain and often struggle with out-of-domain instructions or lack competitiveness compared to specialized models.

This research paper directly aims to bridge this gap. Its core contribution to the landscape is tackling the fundamental challenge of data scarcity by introducing **MotionLib**, the first million-level dataset for motion generation. By providing data on a scale comparable to foundational visual benchmarks, MotionLib enables the training of truly large, generalist motion models. Furthermore, the paper contributes to the representational challenge by proposing **MotionBook**, a novel motion encoding approach that addresses limitations of traditional VQ (information loss, limited codebook size) and inefficient motion features (H3D-format). By systematically investigating the impact of scaling both data and model size, the authors provide critical empirical evidence and insights into "scaling laws" for motion generation, much like those observed in LLMs, thereby laying a crucial foundation for future advancements in this domain. This work is a significant step towards creating versatile and powerful motion generation models that can understand and produce human movements with a level of fidelity and diversity previously unattainable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of human motion generation by developing and evaluating large motion models, drawing inspiration from the success of large language models. The authors frame their central inquiry as: **“Can scaling the large motion model and data benefit motion generation?”**

This overarching question motivates several key objectives:

- **Address Data Scarcity:** The most significant motivation is to overcome the critical bottleneck of insufficient high-quality, large-scale human motion data. Existing datasets are deemed too small and limited in scope, hindering the development of truly generalist motion models. The objective is to construct a **million-level dataset**, MotionLib, to serve as a robust foundation and comprehensive testbed for future research.
- **Investigate Scaling Laws:** A core objective is to systematically investigate the impact of scaling both the data volume and the model size on motion generation performance. For the first time, the authors aim to demonstrate the existence of scaling laws in this domain, providing insights into how increasing data and model capacity contributes to improved performance and generalization.
- **Improve Motion Representation:** The authors identify that current motion representations are inadequate, leading to information loss and limited expressiveness. Specifically, existing VQ-based tokenizers suffer from:
 - **Information Loss:** Compressing complex, multi-dimensional motion states into single 1D embeddings inevitably discards fine-grained details, limiting part-level resolution.
 - **Limited Codebook Size:** Small codebooks restrict the diversity of generated motions and are prone to "codebook collapse."
 - **Inefficient Feature Formats:** Common formats like H3D-Format omit crucial information (e.g., original rotation), leading to computational inefficiencies in downstream applications.
 The objective is to propose **MotionBook**, a novel motion encoding approach comprising a compact, lossless feature representation (SMPL-D135) and an innovative 2D lookup-free motion tokenizer (2D-LFQ) to address these issues.
- **Develop a Generalist Large Motion Model:** Based on the new dataset and encoding approach, the objective is to train **Being-M0**, a large motion model capable of robust performance across a wide range of human activities, including unseen ones, and to demonstrate its state-of-the-art capabilities.
- **Provide Key Insights for Future Design:** Beyond achieving performance gains, the research aims to identify critical factors influencing the effectiveness of large motion models and offer valuable insights for future model design, such as optimal tuning strategies (e.g., LoRA vs. full-parameter tuning).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research revolves around three interconnected pillars: large-scale data curation (MotionLib), advanced motion encoding (MotionBook), and the development and training of a large motion model (Being-M0).

#### 3.1. MotionLib: Million-Level Motion Library

The construction of MotionLib addresses the data scarcity issue through a systematic data collection and refinement pipeline:

- **Million-Level Motion Collection:** The process begins by gathering over 20 million videos from public datasets (e.g., Kinetics-700, NTU-RGBD-120, BEDLAM, GTA-Human) and online platforms like YouTube. This raw video data undergoes several filtering steps:
 - **Human Presence and Bounding Box Filtering:** Videos without human figures or with only partially visible humans are removed.
 - **2D Keypoint Detection:** Pretrained models (e.g., VitPose) are used to detect 2D human keypoints.
 - **Occlusion and Blur Filtering:** Clips with low keypoint confidence scores or large object obstructions (identified via segmentation masks from models like Segment Anything) are filtered out. Motion blur is mitigated by smoothing trajectories using adjacent detection results.
 - **3D Motion Extraction:** The WHAM model is employed to regress 3D SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) parameters from collected videos, ensuring world-coordinate human motion.
 - **Short Boundary Detection:** Videos are segmented to focus on human activity, identifying and extracting relevant temporal clips and discarding segments without human presence.
- **Hierarchical Motion Descriptions:** To overcome the limitations of short, undetailed text descriptions in existing benchmarks, MotionLib offers hierarchical textual annotations:
 - **Part-Level Description:** Detailed captions for individual body parts (e.g., left arm, torso) are generated using large multimodal models (LMMs) like Gemini-1.5-pro, prompting for fine-grained movements.
 - **Body-Level Description:** A comprehensive paragraph summarizing the whole-body movement is also generated.
 - This hierarchical structure provides significantly richer and more granular textual content.
- **Motion and Description Refinement:**
 - **Motion Refinement:** Initial raw motions are noisy. Precise 3D keypoints are estimated, followed by local-global motion optimization. An RL-based policy (π_refine) further refines motions to ensure physical plausibility (balance, realism) and eliminate issues like jittering and foot-sliding, operating in a simulated environment. For challenging movements, a smaller weight is assigned during pretraining to guide the model towards high-quality motion. RoHM is used to reconstruct complete motions for noisy or occluded sequences.
 - **Description Refinement:** GPT-4o is used to assess and refine the textual content by conditioning on one level of description (e.g., body-level) and refining the other (e.g., part-level), ensuring precision and reliability.

#### 3.2. MotionBook: Towards Effective Motion Encoding

MotionBook is a novel motion encoding approach designed to address the limitations of existing motion representations.

- **Lossless Motion Feature (SMPL-D135):** Instead of the conventional H3D-Format, which loses original rotational information, MotionBook adopts SMPL-D135. Each frame is encoded as a 135-dimensional vector, comprising:
 - Root (9D): 6D rotation, 2D XZ-plane velocity, 1D height.
 - Body Joints (126D): 6D rotation vectors for each of the 21 key body joints.
 This feature directly encodes complete joint and root rotation data, preserving critical information and being more compact.
- **2D Lookup-Free Motion Quantization (2D-LFQ):** This component addresses the limitations of 1D VQ-based tokenizers.
 - **2D Representation:** A motion sequence M = {m₁..., m_T} is treated as a single-channel 2D image M ∈ R^(T × D × 1), where D is the feature dimension. This expands the dimensionality from 1D to 2D, allowing the encoder to capture more complex patterns and retain fine-grained details during tokenization.
 - **Lookup-Free Quantization:** To enable larger codebooks without codebook collapse, a finite scalar quantization method is adopted. The traditional codebook C ∈ R^(Kxd) is replaced with an integer set C, where |C|=K. Each dimension of a feature vector is quantized to either -1 or 1, and the token index is computed as a sum of powers of 2 based on these binary values. This eliminates the need for token lookups, boosting representational capacity and efficiency.
 - **Training:** The tokenizer is trained with standard reconstruction, perceptual, and commitment losses, along with an entropy penalty, but notably, without GAN loss, which was found to destabilize training.

#### 3.3. Being-M0: Scaling up Large Motion Model

Being-M0 is the large motion model trained using MotionLib and MotionBook, designed as a generative model leveraging a pre-trained LLM backbone.

- **Architecture:** It adapts a decoder-only LLM (e.g., GPT-2, LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3) to treat motion as a distinct "language." The motion tokenizer encodes raw motion sequences into token embeddings, which are integrated into the LLM's vocabulary as additional tokens, marked by special `<mot>` and `</mot>` tokens.
- **Training Process (Two Stages):**
 1. **Motion-Text Alignment (Pre-training):** The model is pre-trained on the entire MotionLib dataset. This stage allows the model to learn fundamental correlations between motion and text from diverse data, building a robust generalist foundation.
 2. **Motion Instruction Tuning (Fine-tuning):** A subset of high-quality motions from MotionLib is used to create an instructional dataset (900K instructions). Over 250 instruction templates are employed (e.g., "Show me a demonstration of <Caption Placeholder> through movement."), with corresponding text inserted. Gemini-Pro is used to refine these instructions for naturalness and fluency. This stage better aligns the model with real-world human commands, enabling instruction-following capabilities.
- **Optimization:** The model is optimized by minimizing the negative log-likelihood over predicted tokens. Full parameter tuning is generally performed, as LoRA fine-tuning was found to be less effective, likely due to the need for substantial parameter updates to learn new motion tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence supporting the benefits of scaling data and model size in motion generation, alongside the effectiveness of the proposed MotionLib and MotionBook.

- **MotionLib: A Game-Changer in Data Scale:**
 - MotionLib is confirmed as the *first million-level dataset* for motion generation, featuring over 1.21 million motion sequences and 2.48 million motion-text pairs, totaling 1456.4 hours of motion. This is at least 15 times larger than previous counterparts like HumanML3D and Motion-X.
 - The hierarchical text descriptions (body-level and part-level) are shown to be of significantly higher quality compared to existing datasets, validated by GPT-4o evaluation (e.g., MotionLib scores 3.837 for "text-only" and 3.823 for "visual-text align," vastly surpassing HumanML3D and Motion-X).
- **Validation of Scaling Laws for Motion Generation:**
 - **Model Size:** Increasing the model's parameter count (from GPT-2 355M to LLaMA-2 13B and LLaMA-3 8B) consistently leads to performance improvements in motion generation, across both smaller (Motion-X) and larger (MotionLib) evaluation sets. For instance, LLaMA2-13B consistently achieves higher R@1 scores than smaller models. This validates that larger models are better able to capture complex motion patterns.
 - **Data Scale:** When using the same LLM backbone, significantly increasing the training data scale (from 0.02M to 1.2M motion clips) yields substantial performance gains, particularly on the MotionLib test set. For LLaMA2-13B, training with 1.2M data showed an R@1 of 0.185 on MotionLib, a significant increase over models trained on smaller subsets. This confirms the critical role of data scale in enhancing semantic alignment between generated motions and text.
- **Being-M0 Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - Being-M0, especially when incorporating 2D-LFQ and trained on MotionLib with LLaMA2-13B, achieves *state-of-the-art (SoTA)* R-Precision performance on the widely adopted HumanML3D benchmark. It outperforms a range of specialist diffusion-based and autoregressive models, as well as other LLM fine-tuning methods (e.g., MotionGPT, MotionLLM, AvatarGPT) across metrics like R@1, R@3, FID, and MMDist. This demonstrates its ability to generate high-quality, text-aligned motion sequences.
- **Superior Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:**
 - When evaluated on the UNSEEN-90K dataset, which consists entirely of out-of-domain samples, the model trained on MotionLib significantly outperforms those trained on HumanML3D or Motion-X. This is a crucial finding, highlighting that large-scale, diverse pretraining enables Being-M0 to generalize effectively to unseen motion categories and scenarios.
- **Effectiveness of MotionBook and Ablation Insights:**
 - **Motion Instruction Tuning:** Ablation studies confirm that motion instruction tuning significantly improves performance metrics (e.g., R@1 increased by 0.017), indicating enhanced language-motion alignment and user-friendliness.
 - **Richer Text Description:** Training with hierarchical descriptions (body and part levels) improves R@1 and R@3 metrics compared to single-level descriptions, underscoring the benefits of richer semantic understanding.
 - **Motion Feature Design:** While SMPL-D135, SMPL-D130, SMPL-D263, and SMPL-D268 yield similar retrieval performance, SMPL-D135 is chosen for its balance of simplicity, computational cost, and completeness in preserving original motion information.
 - **2D-LFQ Motion Tokenization:** 2D-LFQ outperforms vanilla VQ and Residual Quantization (RQ) on larger and out-of-domain datasets (Motion-X, MotionLib) in motion reconstruction, showing lower FID and MPJPE. Crucially, 2D-LFQ effectively utilizes larger codebooks (up to 2^16), whereas VQ/RQ methods suffer diminishing returns or degradation due to codebook collapse when codebook sizes exceed 2^10, demonstrating 2D-LFQ's superior representational capacity and utilization. The 2D quantization strategy itself significantly outperforms its 1D counterpart.
 - **Training Specifics:** Full parameter fine-tuning is found to be more effective than LoRA, and fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs is superior to training from scratch, emphasizing the importance of initial text comprehension. Training without description masking also proves beneficial for preventing catastrophic forgetting and reducing overfitting. Decoder-only LLM architectures are found to be superior to encoder-decoder ones for text-motion alignment.
 - **Convergence:** While large motion models converge faster with larger sizes and pre-trained weights, they still require more epochs than large multimodal models like LLaVA, partially attributed to the limited representation capacity of current motion tokenizers.
- **Limitations of Current Evaluation Metrics:** The authors also note that current evaluation metrics for motion generation, which often rely on lightweight retrieval models trained on limited data, may not be as robust or generalizable as those in vision (e.g., InceptionNet for FID), potentially leading to inconsistent results and highlighting a critical area for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant milestone in the field of human motion understanding and generation, offering foundational contributions with far-reaching potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Foundational Advancements

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The introduction of **MotionLib**, the first million-level dataset for motion generation, directly tackles the most critical bottleneck hindering the development of truly generalist large motion models. This massive, high-quality, and hierarchically annotated dataset provides an unprecedented resource for researchers, enabling training at scales previously impossible. It lays the essential groundwork for future breakthroughs, much like ImageNet revolutionized computer vision.
- **Establishing Scaling Laws:** For the first time, this work systematically demonstrates the importance and benefits of scaling both data and model size in motion generation. This empirical validation of "scaling laws" provides crucial insights and a clear roadmap for the design and development of future, more powerful motion models, guiding resource allocation and architectural choices.
- **Revolutionizing Motion Encoding:** **MotionBook**, with its compact, lossless SMPL-D135 feature and the innovative 2D Lookup-Free Quantization (2D-LFQ), represents a major step forward in motion representation. By overcoming the limitations of information loss and small codebook sizes in traditional VQ, MotionBook enables a more detailed, expressive, and efficient encoding of human movements. This enhanced representation is key to unlocking finer-grained motion control and higher-fidelity generation.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact and Applications

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The trained large motion model, **Being-M0**, demonstrates state-of-the-art capabilities in text-to-motion generation, particularly in its robust performance across a wide range of human activities and its superior generalization to unseen, out-of-distribution motions. This signifies a tangible step towards generalist motion AI.
- **Enabling Realistic Digital Humans:** The ability to generate highly realistic, diverse, and controllable human motions from natural language has profound implications for various industries:
 - **Video Games:** More natural and dynamic character animations, reducing manual labor and enhancing player immersion.
 - **Filmmaking and Animation:** Automated generation of complex character movements for virtual productions, special effects, and synthetic media, leading to more believable digital actors.
 - **Robotics:** Development of robots capable of performing more human-like movements, improving human-robot interaction and task execution in complex environments.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Creation of highly expressive and interactive avatars.
- **User-Friendly Interaction:** The emphasis on instruction tuning makes Being-M0 more user-friendly, allowing users to generate complex motions through intuitive natural language commands, democratizing access to advanced animation capabilities.

#### 5.3. Future Research Directions

- **Further Scaling:** The success of MotionLib and Being-M0 encourages even further scaling of datasets and models, potentially leading to even more powerful and versatile motion AI.
- **Robust Evaluation Metrics:** The identified limitations of current motion evaluation metrics highlight a critical need for developing more robust, generalizable, and perception-aligned benchmarks, which is essential for fair comparison and accurate progress tracking.
- **Integration with Broader Multimodal AI:** The approach of treating motion as a "language" within an LLM framework opens doors for seamless integration of human motion into broader multimodal large AI models, enabling sophisticated interactions involving text, vision, and motion.
- **Exploring Diverse Data Sources:** While static and synthetic data showed marginal gains, the research encourages further exploration into how such readily available data sources can be effectively leveraged to augment dynamic motion datasets.

#### 5.4. Ethical Considerations

The authors responsibly acknowledge the potential for misuse of such powerful motion generation technology, particularly for creating misleading content. Their commitment to utilizing open-source and online video data with careful review processes to avoid copyright issues and their pledge for "responsible development" and continuous monitoring of societal impact are crucial for navigating the ethical landscape of advanced AI.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a foundational contribution by addressing the data and representation challenges in large motion models. It not only achieves state-of-the-art results but also provides invaluable insights into the scaling dynamics of motion AI, setting a robust trajectory for future research and diverse applications.
