# MolmoAct: Action Reasoning Models that can Reason in Space

> [!summary] Summary
> MolmoAct introduces a class of Action Reasoning Models that integrate depth-aware perception tokens and visual reasoning traces into a three-stage pipeline to enhance robotic manipulation. This approach yields state-of-the-art performance across simulation and real-world benchmarks, improves out-of-distribution generalization, and allows for intuitive visual steering.

## Problem

- Existing robotic foundation models, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often lack adaptability, robustness, and opaque decision-making when transferring across tasks, scenes, or robot embodiments.
- Current VLA models struggle with a lack of 3D spatial understanding, relying purely on RGB images, and suffer from information loss when distilling complex 3D trajectories into high-level linguistic descriptions for planning.
- The brittleness and ambiguity of language-only steering make precise control difficult and are sensitive to out-of-distribution phrasing.

## Method

- A three-stage autoregressive pipeline predicts ==depth-aware perception tokens==, mid-level spatial plans as ==visual reasoning traces==, and then low-level robot actions, grounding reasoning in explicit spatial understanding.
- ==Action tokenization== discretizes continuous action dimensions into byte-level BPE symbols, preserving ordinal structure and reducing training time significantly.
- A new steerability mechanism allows users to draw visual reasoning traces directly on camera images, which are then used to condition the model's action predictions, providing precise and unambiguous control.

## Results

- MolmoAct-7B-D achieved an **86.6%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrated superior adaptation efficiency, with an average of **+10%** task progression over π0-FAST on real-world single-arm tasks and **+22.7%** on bimanual tasks.
- The model showed strong out-of-distribution generalization, achieving **72.1%** success on SimplerEnv variant-aggregation tasks (**7.8%** higher than RT-2-X) and a **23.3%** average improvement in task progression over π0-FAST across real-world OOD conditions.
- Visual trace steering achieved a **75%** success rate, representing a **33%** improvement compared to steering with open-ended natural language instructions, demonstrating enhanced precision and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit spatial reasoning through depth perception tokens and visual reasoning traces overcomes limitations of purely linguistic Chain-of-Thought approaches for precise 3D manipulation.
> - Structured intermediate representations, like visual reasoning traces, enable both greater explainability of robot behavior and intuitive, precise user steerability.
> - A carefully curated MolmoAct Dataset, designed for mid-training, alongside efficient action tokenization, is crucial for improving general manipulation performance and spatial reasoning in real-world household settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

MolmoAct enters a robotics landscape that, despite significant advancements, still lags behind the rapid generalization seen in large language models (LLMs) and vision models (VLMs). Existing robotic foundation models, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models such as GR00T, π0, RT-1, TraceVLA, OpenVLA, and others, often struggle with adaptability, robustness, and opaque decision-making when transferring across tasks, scenes, or robot embodiments. This limitation stems from two main issues: data scarcity in robotics, which demands costly and fine-grained embodied interaction, and a lack of structured reasoning in their architectures, which predominantly map perception and instructions directly to control signals.

The paper argues that robotics must emulate the shift observed in LLMs, moving from brute-force data scaling towards integrating intermediate representations that support reasoning, abstraction, and control—a concept often termed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT). MolmoAct positions itself as an "Action Reasoning Model (ARM)" that aims to fill this gap by introducing a structured reasoning pipeline grounded in *spatial* understanding, rather than relying solely on high-level linguistic reasoning.

Key challenges MolmoAct addresses within the VLA landscape include:
- **Lack of 3D Understanding:** Most VLMs are trained purely on RGB images, lacking intrinsic depth estimation capabilities crucial for precise robotic manipulation in 3D environments.
- **Information Loss in Language Planning:** Attempting to distill complex 3D trajectories into linguistic descriptions often results in significant loss of spatial and temporal information, making precise control difficult.
- **Ambiguity in Language-Only Steering:** Relying solely on language for robot steering can be ambiguous, brittle to out-of-distribution phrasing, and requires vast, high-quality language-action datasets.

By focusing on "reasoning in space" through explicit, structured representations for depth perception and planned trajectories, MolmoAct carves out a distinct niche that enhances explainability, steerability, and generalization in robotic manipulation, offering a novel approach to overcome limitations of current end-to-end VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of MolmoAct is to introduce and validate **Action Reasoning Models (ARMs)** as a new class of robotic foundation models capable of transforming perception into purposeful action through grounded, structured reasoning in space. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific objectives:

**Motivation:**
- **Bridging the Reasoning Gap:** Current robotic models often lack the nuanced reasoning capabilities that allow humans to perform intentional, adaptable, and explainable actions. The authors highlight the need for robots to "reason" with context, goals, and constraints, much like humans do, by grounding their actions in physical experience.
- **Addressing VLA Limitations:** The brittleness, opacity, and poor generalization of existing VLA models—despite massive data collection efforts—underscore the need for a more structured approach beyond direct perception-to-control mapping.
- **Leveraging Structured Learning:** Inspired by the success of structured learning (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) in LLMs, the authors motivate applying similar principles to robotics to improve generalization and semantic grounding.
- **Enhancing Explainability and Steerability:** The opaqueness of many black-box robot policies makes debugging and human-robot collaboration difficult. Explicit intermediate reasoning steps are desired for greater transparency and user control.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Develop a Three-Stage Action Reasoning Pipeline:** Design a model architecture that sequentially predicts (i) depth-aware perception tokens, (ii) mid-level spatial plans as visual reasoning traces, and (iii) precise low-level robot actions.
2. **Achieve Explainable and Steerable Robot Behavior:** Ensure that the intermediate representations generated by the model are interpretable and can be directly manipulated by users (e.g., through visual trajectory editing).
3. **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Performance:** Surpass existing competitive baselines on widely recognized simulation benchmarks (SimplerEnv, LIBERO) and in real-world robotic tasks across various embodiments (single-arm, bimanual).
4. **Improve Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** Show that MolmoAct can effectively adapt to novel tasks, environments, and object perturbations that deviate from its training distribution.
5. **Curate and Release High-Quality Datasets:** Introduce and make public the MolmoAct Dataset, a novel collection of robot trajectories in diverse household settings, specifically designed for mid-training action reasoning models.
6. **Foster Open Science:** Release all model weights, training code, and datasets to provide an "open blueprint" for building ARMs, promoting reproducibility and collaborative research in the robotics community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MolmoAct's methodology is centered on its novel, structured three-stage action reasoning pipeline, built upon an existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone and supported by extensive data curation and a multi-stage training recipe.

#### 3.1. MolmoAct Architecture and Action Tokenization

MolmoAct leverages the **Molmo** VLM as its foundation, comprising a vision encoder (e.g., SigLIP2 or OpenAI CLIP), a vision-language connector, and a large language model (LLM) backbone (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B or OLMo2-7B). The chosen **MolmoAct-7B-D** variant uses SigLIP2 and Qwen2.5-7B.

To enable action prediction, MolmoAct formulates it as a vision-language sequence modeling task. Continuous action dimensions are normalized and discretized into 256 uniform-width bins. Unlike prior work that uses arbitrary language tokens, MolmoAct assigns these bins to specific byte-level BPE symbols from the Qwen2 tokenizer, preserving ordinal structure and local correlation. This "similarity-preserving initialization" substantially reduces training time (e.g., 9,216 GPU hours for MolmoAct pre-training vs. 50,000 for GR00T N1.5).

#### 3.2. Action Reasoning Model (ARM) - "Reasoning in Space"

The core innovation is a three-stage autoregressive reasoning process, where the model generates structured intermediate representations before predicting actions:

1. **Depth Perception Tokens:**
 - **Motivation:** Address the lack of 3D understanding in most RGB-trained VLMs.
 - **Approach:** The model first predicts a sequence of 100 discrete tokens, `⟨DEPTH_k⟩`, representing a quantized depth map of the scene. These tokens are generated by a pre-trained specialist depth estimator (trained on Depth Anything V2) using a VQVAE, which distills dense depth maps into a fixed-length token sequence. This specialist-to-generalist distillation grounds MolmoAct's spatial understanding from the RGB input.

2. **Visual Reasoning Trace:**
 - **Motivation:** Provide precise motion planning that aligns visual inputs with control outputs.
 - **Approach:** Conditioned on the RGB image, language instruction, and generated depth tokens, the model then predicts a 2D polyline, `τ = (p_1..., p_L)`, consisting of 1 to 5 points (end-effector locations) on the image plane. These points are subsampled from the future trajectory of the end-effector. This trace serves as an explicit visual plan, guiding the subsequent action prediction.

3. **Action Tokens:**
 - **Approach:** Finally, conditioned on the RGB image, language instruction, depth tokens, and the visual reasoning trace, MolmoAct autoregressively predicts the sequence of low-level robot action tokens. This sequential conditioning ensures that actions are spatially grounded in both the inferred depth and the planned motion sketch.

This "reasoning in space" contrasts with linguistic Chain-of-Thought approaches, focusing on visual representations to avoid information loss in translating 3D motion to language.

#### 3.3. Action Steerability via Visual Reasoning Trace

MolmoAct introduces a novel steerability mechanism: users can draw a visual reasoning trace (`τ`) directly on the camera image to specify the desired end-effector path. This user-drawn trace is overlaid onto the RGB image, creating an augmented observation that directly conditions the model's action prediction. This visual steering aims to be more precise, unambiguous, and robust than language-only steering.

#### 3.4. Data Curation and Generation

MolmoAct is trained on a diverse data mixture across three stages:
- **Action Reasoning Data:** Conventional robot datasets (a subset of Open X-Embodiment, including BC-Z, BridgeData V2, and RT-1) are converted into MolmoAct's action reasoning format by generating ground-truth depth perception tokens (using VQVAE on DepthAnything-v2) and visual reasoning traces (using Molmo to point to gripper locations).
- **Auxiliary Robot Data:** Additional datasets are created for specialized supervision: Auxiliary Depth Data (RGB to depth tokens), Auxiliary Trace Data (RGB to visual trace), and Trajectory-conditioned Action Data (augmented RGB + trace to action). The latter is crucial for enabling the steerability feature.
- **MolmoAct Dataset:** A significant contribution, this new dataset comprises over 10,000 high-quality trajectories from a single-arm Franka robot in diverse real-world household and tabletop environments. This dataset is specifically designed for **mid-training** to enhance general manipulation performance and spatial reasoning.
- **Multimodal Web Data:** A mixture of academic datasets (e.g., VQA v2.0, Text VQA, OK-VQA) and PixMo data from Molmo's pre-training is included to imbue general visual skills and robustness.

#### 3.5. Training Recipe

MolmoAct's training consists of three stages:
1. **Pre-training:** MolmoAct is first trained on the large-scale mixture of Action Reasoning Data (OXE subset), Auxiliary Robot Data, and Multimodal Web Data (26.3M samples total) using 256 H100 GPUs for 100k steps (approx. 9,728 GPU hours).
2. **Mid-training:** The model undergoes a second stage of training on the MolmoAct Dataset (1M action reasoning and 1M trajectory-conditioned samples) using 128 H100 GPUs for 50k steps (approx. 2,304 GPU hours). This stage is critical for aligning with real household manipulation.
3. **Post-training:** For rapid adaptation to new tasks and embodiments, MolmoAct is fine-tuned using parameter-efficient LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) on small sets (30-50) of tele-operated demonstrations per task. Action chunking is applied here.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MolmoAct-7B-D consistently demonstrates strong performance across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios, often surpassing state-of-the-art baselines.

- **SimplerEnv Evaluation (Google Robot):**
 - **Zero-shot:** MolmoAct-7B-D-Pretrain achieved 70.5% zero-shot accuracy on SimplerEnv Visual Matching tasks, outperforming competitive closed-source models like GR00T N1.5 and π0.
 - **Fine-tuned:** After fine-tuning on the RT-1 subset of OXE, its performance improved to 71.6%, exceeding Magma by 3.2%.

- **LIBERO Benchmark (Franka Panda Simulation):**
 - MolmoAct-7B-D achieved the highest overall average success rate of 86.6%, outperforming all autoregressive baselines.
 - It showed particularly strong gains on challenging long-horizon tasks, surpassing ThinkAct by 6.3%.

- **Real-World Fine-Tuning and Generalization:**
 - **In-Distribution Tasks:** MolmoAct demonstrated superior adaptation efficiency, achieving an average of +10% task progression over π0-FAST on single-arm tasks and an impressive +22.7% gain on bimanual tasks.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:**
 - **Simulation (SimplerEnv variant-aggregation):** The fine-tuned MolmoAct-7B-D-Pretrain reached 72.1% success, outperforming RT-2-X by 7.8% and demonstrating robustness to visual and distributional shifts.
 - **Real-World:** MolmoAct consistently surpassed baselines across language variation, spatial variation, distractors, and novel object conditions, achieving a 23.3% average improvement in task progression over π0-FAST.

- **Effect of the MolmoAct Dataset:**
 - Mid-training with the MolmoAct Dataset yielded a consistent average performance boost of 5.5% on real-world tasks (close_lid, rotate_pot, pour_tea) compared to MolmoAct without this mid-training stage. Even without mid-training, MolmoAct outperformed π0-FAST and OpenVLA.

- **Instruction Following Capabilities:**
 - **Open-ended Instructions (Simulation):** MolmoAct-7B-D-Pretrain achieved the highest Elo rating in human evaluations for instruction following, outperforming SpatialVLA by 109 points and OpenVLA by a larger margin.
 - **Visual Trace Generation:** It also outperformed Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-4o, and HAMSTER in generating visual traces conditioned on language prompts, indicating strong language-grounded generalization.

- **Steerability via Visual Reasoning Trace:**
 - MolmoAct-7B-D showed significantly better steerability via visual trace inputs, achieving a 75% success rate, which was 33% higher than steering with open-ended natural language instructions.
 - MolmoAct's language-based steering also surpassed π0-FAST by 29%, highlighting its improved instruction-following.

- **Open Release:** The project openly released all model weights, training code, the MolmoAct Dataset, and other relevant data, providing a complete blueprint for reproducibility and further research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MolmoAct represents a significant stride in the field of robotics and embodied AI, proposing and validating a novel architectural paradigm: Action Reasoning Models (ARMs). Its core significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Structured Spatial Reasoning for Robotics:** By explicitly integrating depth perception tokens and visual reasoning traces into a three-stage autoregressive pipeline, MolmoAct moves beyond traditional end-to-end learning in VLAs. This "reasoning in space" approach offers a more robust, interpretable, and generalizable method for robotic control, mimicking human-like spatial cognition. It directly addresses the shortcomings of linguistic chain-of-thought methods for precise 3D manipulation by grounding reasoning in visual and spatial cues.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** MolmoAct consistently outperforms strong baselines across a wide array of simulation and real-world tasks. Its superior zero-shot capabilities, fast adaptation through lightweight fine-tuning (LoRA), and exceptional out-of-distribution generalization capabilities demonstrate its potential to handle complex, unstructured environments and novel scenarios, which are critical for deploying robots in real-world settings. The impressive gains on long-horizon tasks and across various generalization axes (language, spatial, distractors, novel objects) highlight its robustness.

- **Enhanced Explainability and Intuitive Steerability:** The structured reasoning process intrinsically provides explainability, allowing researchers and users to visualize intermediate plans (depth maps, trajectory traces) and understand the model's decision-making. More importantly, the ability to directly steer robot actions by drawing visual traces offers a profoundly intuitive and precise human-robot interaction mechanism. This visual steering addresses the inherent ambiguity and brittleness of language-only control, paving the way for more effective human collaboration with robotic agents.

- **Accelerating Open Research and Development:** The comprehensive open-sourcing of MolmoAct's model weights, training code, and critically, the newly curated MolmoAct Dataset, is a monumental contribution to the research community. This "open blueprint" significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, enabling reproducibility, fostering community-driven innovations, and accelerating the development of next-generation robotic foundation models. The MolmoAct Dataset, specifically tailored for household manipulation, will be invaluable for future work in generalist robotics.

- **Efficiency in Training:** MolmoAct's efficient action tokenization scheme significantly reduces training time compared to prior work, demonstrating that advanced capabilities do not necessarily require exponentially increasing computational resources. This efficiency can make complex robotic models more accessible to a wider range of research groups.

In conclusion, MolmoAct establishes a new benchmark for robotic foundation models by integrating explicit spatial reasoning, delivering superior performance, and offering unprecedented levels of explainability and steerability. Its open-source nature and the release of high-quality data are poised to catalyze future breakthroughs in embodied AI, moving us closer to truly intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.
