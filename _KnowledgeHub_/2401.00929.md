# GenH2R: Learning Generalizable Human-to-Robot Handover via Scalable Simulation, Demonstration, and Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> GenH2R learns generalizable vision-based human-to-robot handover by scaling simulation, demonstrations, and imitation together. GenH2R-Sim procedurally builds a million handover scenes from ShapeNet objects, generated dexterous grasps, and Bézier-curve hand trajectories; landmark planning produces distillation-friendly expert demonstrations; forecast-aided 4D imitation learning distills them into a point-cloud visuomotor policy, lifting success rates by at least 10% over baselines in simulation and on a real robot.

## Problem

- Existing handover simulators depend on motion capture, supplying only about 1000 trajectories over 20 objects, too little geometry and motion diversity for generalizable handover policies.
- Collecting real robot demonstrations of receiving objects from moving humans is costly and unsafe, blocking the scale-up recipe that drove progress in other robot-learning domains.
- Prior planners either shortcut to the privileged destination or replan densely each step, yielding unsmooth multi-modal trajectories whose weak vision-action correlation resists distillation into closed-loop policies.

## Method

- **GenH2R-Sim** procedurally composes handover scenes from 3,266 ShapeNet objects, roughly one million generated dexterous grasp poses, and multi-segment Bézier-curve hand trajectories with object rotation, the hand pausing as the gripper nears.
- **Landmark planning** trains a pose-forecasting network, thresholds its error to split trajectories at unforeseeable endpoints, then replans ==OMG-planner== grasp and motion every P frames toward the next landmark, with nearest-grasp sorting and warm-started inverse kinematics.
- **Forecast-aided 4D imitation learning** feeds ICP-derived inter-frame flow into ==PointNet++==, decodes a 6D egocentric action through an MLP, and adds a weighted auxiliary loss predicting future object poses.

## Results

- Landmark planning trained on the million-scene t0 set reaches **86.57%**, **85.65%**, **41.43%**, and **68.33%** success across the four test sets, beating Handover-Sim2real by **11.34%** to **16.90%**.
- Ablations drop success by **9.77** points without flow input and **2.25** points without the forecasting loss; using only **10%** of t0 costs **5.93** points on the unseen t1 set.
- Destination planning collapses to **0.93%** success when trained on t0, while the real-robot user study gives **90.0%** versus **56.7%** (simple) and **70.0%** versus **33.3%** (complex).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Demonstration success alone is insufficient: distillability, meaning the correlation between visual observation and planned action, decides whether generated data can train closed-loop policies.
> - Privileged-information planners should restrict lookahead to horizons the observation can actually foresee; unbounded foresight silently destroys the learning signal on complex trajectories.
> - Large-scale domain-randomized synthetic data outperforms small real mocap datasets even on real-captured test sets, so per-sample realism can be traded for diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI aims to develop robots capable of interacting and collaborating with humans in various environments. A fundamental aspect of this goal is enabling robots to reliably receive objects from humans, a task referred to as human-to-robot (H2R) handover. This capability is essential for robots to assist in diverse real-world applications such as cooking, domestic chores, and assembly tasks.

Historically, progress in scaling H2R handover skills that generalize across different objects and human behaviors has encountered several challenges. Training robots in real-world human interaction scenarios is resource-intensive and poses safety risks, which limits the scalability of data collection. Consequently, there is a demand for simulated environments to pre-train robots before real-world deployment. However, existing H2R simulators, such as HandoverSim, often provide a limited number of object geometries and human motion trajectories (e.g., 20 objects and 1000 trajectories in HandoverSim derived from DexYCB). This lack of diversity restricts the generalization capabilities of learned policies to the complexities of real-world interactions, which involve unseen object geometries and a wide range of dynamic human behaviors.

Furthermore, acquiring a sufficient quantity of high-quality robot demonstrations for visuomotor policies remains a significant hurdle. While large language models have demonstrated the potential of large-scale data for learning, collecting millions of robot demonstrations of receiving objects from real humans is prohibitively costly and unscalable. Even when demonstrations are generated through methods like Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), ensuring their "distillability" into closed-loop visuomotor policies is challenging. Demonstrations must exhibit a strong correlation between visual observations and planned actions; otherwise, observation-irrelevant or unsmooth actions can hinder effective policy learning.

This work, GenH2R, addresses these limitations by introducing a comprehensive framework designed to learn generalizable H2R handover at scale. It tackles the challenges of limited simulation assets and costly demonstrations by proposing methods for scalable synthetic asset creation, automated and distillation-friendly demonstration generation, and an effective imitation learning approach that leverages temporal visual information and future forecasting. The research aims to move beyond small-scale datasets and static or simple handover scenarios to enable robots to adapt to dynamic and varied human behaviors with novel objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GenH2R is to develop a framework for learning generalizable vision-based human-to-robot (H2R) handover skills. This encompasses equipping robots with the ability to reliably receive objects with previously unseen geometries, even when humans present these objects through complex and varied trajectories. The overarching motivation is to achieve this generalizability through learning H2R handover at scale.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Lack of Diversity in Simulations**: Existing H2R simulators offer limited diversity in object geometry and human motion, which curtails the generalization capacity of learned policies. The paper aims to create a simulation environment, GenH2R-Sim, that vastly expands the number of handover scenes and unique objects to better represent the complexities of real-world human-robot interaction. This is motivated by the understanding that a larger, more diverse dataset, even if synthetic, can lead to more robust and generalizable policies compared to smaller, real-world datasets.
- **Enabling Scalable and High-Quality Demonstration Generation**: Collecting real-world robot demonstrations for H2R handover is expensive and impractical for scaling. The research seeks to automatically generate millions of high-quality robot demonstrations within the simulated environment. A key motivation is to ensure these demonstrations are "distillation-friendly," meaning they promote a strong vision-action correlation suitable for training closed-loop visuomotor policies. Traditional planning methods often produce robot actions that are either too foresighted (lacking real-time visual correlation) or too shortsighted (resulting in jerky, unnatural movements), both of which hinder effective policy learning.
- **Developing an Effective Learning Algorithm for Dynamic H2R Handover**: Distilling large-scale, dynamic demonstrations into a robust visuomotor policy requires a learning method capable of processing spatio-temporal information effectively. The paper is motivated to develop a novel imitation learning approach that can infer robot actions from dynamic visual observations, considering both current scene geometry and future object motion. This is in contrast to methods that primarily focus on robot morphology or rely on extensive, unstable reinforcement learning training. The goal is to create a policy that can anticipate human actions and respond in a predictive, human-like manner.
- **Achieving Strong Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Ultimately, the work aims for policies that can generalize to entirely new objects and human behaviors not encountered during training, and that can effectively transfer from the simulation environment to real-world robotic systems without requiring extensive real-world fine-tuning. This motivation stems from the practical need for robust robotic systems that can operate reliably in unstructured, dynamic human environments.

By addressing these objectives, GenH2R intends to significantly advance the field of human-robot collaboration by providing a robust and scalable solution for H2R handover.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GenH2R framework integrates three main components to achieve generalizable H2R handover: a scalable synthetic simulation environment, an automated method for generating distillation-friendly demonstrations, and a forecast-aided 4D imitation learning approach.

#### 3.1. GenH2R-Sim: Scalable Synthetic Handover Simulator

To overcome the limitations of existing simulators in terms of asset diversity and scale, GenH2R-Sim was developed through procedural generation.

- **Diversity in Object Geometry and Grasp Poses**: The simulator leverages large-scale 3D model repositories such as ShapeNet and dexterous grasp generation methods like DexGraspNet. Approximately 1,000,000 grasp poses were generated for 3,266 distinct objects, encompassing a wide range of sizes and shapes relevant to real-life handovers.
- **Complex Hand-Object Moving Trajectories**: Bézier curves are employed to generate smooth yet intricate hand-object motion trajectories. Multiple Bézier curves are linked to represent different stages of motion, allowing for scenarios of varying complexity. Control points for these curves and the human hand's speed are adjusted to create diverse motion profiles. To enhance realism and necessitate appropriate robot grasp selection, consistent object rotations are integrated into the trajectories. The destination of the hand-object within the robot's reach is randomized.
- **Scale and Scope**: GenH2R-Sim generates millions of handover scenes, representing a three-order-of-magnitude increase in scene quantity and a two-order-of-magnitude increase in unique objects compared to prior simulators like HandoverSim. This extensive scale is critical for fostering generalizable policies.
- **Reactive Human Behavior**: Unlike HandoverSim, which often replays pre-recorded motions, GenH2R-Sim introduces a reactive element to the simulated human hand. The hand stops moving and waits for the robot arm to grasp the object once the robot approaches a defined proximity. This modification aims to simulate a more natural and cooperative handover process.
- **System Configuration**: The simulator includes a Panda 7-DoF robotic arm equipped with a gripper and a wrist-mounted RGB-D camera, interacting with a simulated human hand. The handover process transitions from a pre-handover kinematic phase to a dynamic phase upon contact with the gripper.

#### 3.2. Generating Demonstrations for Distillation

The paper addresses the challenge of creating high-quality, distillable robot demonstrations for closed-loop visuomotor control by proposing a "distillation-friendly" generation method, specifically "landmark planning."

- **Limitations of Alternative Planning Strategies**:
 - **Foresighted Planner (Destination Planning)**: Plans a smooth trajectory directly to the final human handover destination. While efficient, it lacks real-time vision-action correlation during dynamic interactions and requires accurate, difficult-to-forecast future states.
 - **Shortsighted Planner (Dense Planning)**: Independently replans grasp and motion at each time step based on privileged human and object states. This can lead to unsmooth or multi-modal robot trajectories despite smooth visual inputs, due to robot morphology constraints and the nature of common robot planners. This degrades distillability.
- **Proposed Landmark Planning Method**: This method balances the advantages of foresighted and shortsighted planning while prioritizing vision-action correlation and trajectory smoothness.
 - **Improved Shortsighted Planner Foundation**: The method builds upon the OMG planner but adapts it for dynamic scenarios. This involves: 1) sorting candidate grasps by their distance to the robot's end-effector and attempting Inverse Kinematics (IK) from the nearest viable grasp; 2) initializing IK with the robot arm's pose from the previous time step to ensure temporal smoothness; and 3) invoking the OMG planner only when IK can be successfully solved. This encourages the object to remain within the wrist camera's field of view and ensures smoother joint trajectories, enhancing vision-action correlation.
 - **Sparse Landmark-Based Replanning**: To address the inefficiency and unnaturalness of dense, step-by-step replanning, the approach sparsely samples "landmark states" along the human handover trajectory. An object pose forecasting network predicts future object poses, and these predictions are used to segment trajectories into portions where future poses are predictable. Replanning occurs periodically (every P frames) but targets a future landmark defined as `min(t + P, l_i+1)`, where `l_i+1` is the next endpoint of a predictable trajectory segment. This ensures planning considers a visually foreseeable future without attempting to predict or react to sharply transitioning human motions that are unpredictable.
 - **Trajectory Resampling (Supplementary)**: An additional strategy resamples the generated robot joint trajectories to ensure a consistent step length regardless of the distance between the initial and target end-effector poses. This standardization makes expert actions more consistent and easier for vision-based models to learn.

#### 3.3. Forecast-Aided 4D Imitation Learning

This component focuses on effectively distilling the large-scale demonstrations into a closed-loop visuomotor policy.

- **Input Representation**: The policy uses 4D point cloud data acquired from an egocentric camera. To capture dynamic information, each point's feature is augmented with its 3D coordinates in previous `L1` time steps, derived from estimated "flow information."
 - **Flow Information Calculation**: The Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm is used to estimate transformation matrices between the current point cloud and point clouds from `L1` previous frames, in the static world frame. These transformations, though potentially imprecise, provide sufficient motion cues. The flow information is then converted back to the current egocentric frame and integrated into each point's feature vector (3D coordinates + 2 for hand/object labels + 3 * `L1` for past coordinates).
- **Network Architecture**: A PointNet++ network processes the augmented point cloud to extract a global spatio-temporal feature representation.
- **Dual-Head Output and Loss Functions**:
 - **Policy Head**: Decodes the global feature into a 6D egocentric action, representing the target gripper pose relative to the current gripper pose. The primary loss function, `L_action`, is an L1 loss that minimizes the distance between 3D points on the robot gripper based on the predicted and ground-truth actions.
 - **Prediction Head (Auxiliary Task)**: Simultaneously, an auxiliary head predicts the future motion of the object, specifically the transformations between the current and `L2` future object poses. The corresponding loss, `L_pred`, is the sum of L1 norms of the differences between the predicted and ground-truth future pose transformations (Equation 1). This auxiliary task helps the policy anticipate object movement.
 - **Combined Loss**: The total loss for policy training is a weighted sum: `L = L_action + λL_pred`, where `λ` is a hyper-parameter balancing the two objectives.
- **Rationale**: The use of 4D point clouds with flow features allows for effective capture of dynamic scene geometry and motion. The future forecasting objective explicitly leverages the vision-action correlation inherent in the landmark-planned demonstrations, enhancing the policy's responsiveness to human motion and extending its anticipatory capabilities. The imitation learning paradigm provides efficient training (hours) compared to reinforcement learning, and aims for high generalizability through large-scale, high-quality demonstrations.
- **Grasping and Retraction Heuristic**: Similar to other approaches, the robot employs a heuristic: if a sufficient number of points are within the gripper's vicinity, it attempts to grasp the object and retract to a designated target location in an open-loop manner, overriding the policy's continuous egocentric actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates GenH2R through extensive experiments in both simulated environments and the real world, utilizing several benchmarks and conducting ablation studies. Performance is measured by success rate (S), execution time (T), and average success (AS), which accounts for both success and completion efficiency.

#### 4.1. Evaluating on Different Benchmarks

Experiments were conducted on two training datasets: "s0" (small-scale real-world from HandoverSim, 20 objects) and "t0" (large-scale synthetic from GenH2R-Sim, 3266 objects). Evaluation occurred on four test sets: "s0 (Sequential)", "s0 (Simultaneous)" (HandoverSim benchmarks), and "t0", "t1" (GenH2R-Sim benchmarks, with "t1" comprising real-world mocap sequences from HOI4D).

- **Impact of Large-Scale Synthetic Data**: Policies trained on GenH2R's large-scale synthetic dataset ("t0") consistently outperformed all methods trained on the smaller, real-world "s0" dataset. GenH2R's landmark planning method trained on "t0" showed significant success rate increases (ranging from +11.34% to +16.90%) compared to the state-of-the-art Handover-Sim2real trained on "s0." When compared to GenH2R trained on "s0," the "t0" trained version achieved success rate increases of +6.48% to +14.13%. This indicates that large-scale synthetic data provides substantial benefits for policy generalizability, even for real-world scenarios.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: GenH2R (landmark planning) demonstrated significant improvements over Handover-Sim2real, particularly in the "true simultaneous" setting where the robot moves from the beginning. When trained on "t0," GenH2R achieved success rate improvements of +8.12% to +46.76% over the reproduced Handover-Sim2real results across the four test sets. The superior performance in dynamic benchmarks ("s0 (Simultaneous)", "t0", and "t1") highlights GenH2R's effectiveness in handling complex, dynamic robot perception.
- **Comparison of Demonstration Strategies**:
 - **Destination Planning**: Performed poorly, especially with complex "t0" trajectories, showing a large decrease in success rate. This strategy struggles to maintain vision-action correlation in dynamic environments.
 - **Dense Planning**: Generally achieved higher success rates than Destination Planning but resulted in slower task completion and less natural movements, as indicated by lower Average Success (AS) scores compared to Landmark Planning.
 - **Landmark Planning (Proposed)**: Consistently outperformed Dense Planning in both success rate and AS across all benchmarks. For example, when trained on "t0," it showed AS improvements of +5.2% to +9.5% over Dense Planning. This suggests that landmark planning provides a more efficient and generalizable approach by balancing future anticipation with maintaining strong vision-action correlation.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Different Dataset Scales

Further experiments demonstrated the importance of dataset scale. Reducing the utilization of the "t0" dataset to 10% resulted in a 5.93% drop in success rate on the unseen "t1" test set. This confirms that the substantial scale of GenH2R-Sim's data is a critical factor for achieving high generalization performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of different modules in the forecast-aided 4D imitation learning method:

- **Flow Information**: Removing the flow information from the input ("w/o Flow") led to a 9.77% decrease in success rate, indicating the importance of spatio-temporal motion cues.
- **Prediction Task**: Eliminating the auxiliary future prediction loss ("w/o Prediction") resulted in a 2.25% decrease in success rate.
- **Combined Impact**: Removing both flow information and the prediction task ("w/o Flow & Prediction") caused a 4.39% decrease. These results confirm that both components contribute positively, especially when integrated, as future prediction benefits from past motion context.
- **Alternative Input (Frame Stacking)**: Replacing the 4D flow-augmented input with traditional frame stacking resulted in a 5.26% decrease in success rate, demonstrating the effectiveness of GenH2R's 4D spatial-temporal learning approach.
- **Landmark Selection**: Excluding trajectory endpoints from consideration during landmark selection caused a 1.70% decrease in success rate, emphasizing the importance of strategic landmark selection for demonstration quality.

#### 4.4. Real World Experiments (Sim-to-Real Transfer)

The models trained in GenH2R-Sim were deployed on a real robotic platform (ROKAE xMate 3 ER robot with two Intel RealSense Depth Camera D435 devices).

- **User Study**: A user study involving 6 participants compared GenH2R's landmark planning-based method against Handover-Sim2real across 5 objects in two settings (simple and complex), and then against GA-DDPG and Handover-Sim2real across 15 diverse and novel objects.
 - **Simple Setting (5 objects)**: GenH2R achieved a 90% success rate, significantly outperforming Handover-Sim2real (56.7%) and GA-DDPG (60%).
 - **Complex Setting (5 objects)**: GenH2R achieved a 70% success rate, substantially better than Handover-Sim2real (33.3%) and GA-DDPG (43%).
 - **Diverse/Novel Objects (15 objects)**: GenH2R achieved a 72.2% success rate, outperforming Handover-Sim2real (28.9%) and GA-DDPG (43.3%).
- **Observations**: The real-world experiments demonstrated strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities and robustness. GenH2R exhibited smooth object tracking and predictive intention, resembling human-like grasping behavior. Baselines frequently struggled with novel objects and complex human trajectories. Challenges for GenH2R primarily involved transparent objects due to corrupted depth sensing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GenH2R framework presents a novel and comprehensive solution for learning generalizable human-to-robot (H2R) handover skills, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Scalable Simulation for HRI**: The development of GenH2R-Sim, with its unprecedented scale of millions of diverse human handover animations and thousands of unique objects, provides a critical resource for robot learning. This addresses a major bottleneck in HRI research by enabling the generation of vast amounts of synthetic data, which is demonstrated to be more effective for generalizable learning than smaller, real-world datasets. This approach can serve as a blueprint for creating scalable and diverse simulation environments for other complex human-robot interaction tasks.
- **Innovative Approach to Demonstration Generation**: The introduction of a "distillation-friendly" demonstration generation method, particularly landmark planning, is a key contribution. This method effectively balances the efficiency of foresighted planning with the necessity of strong vision-action correlation, overcoming limitations of prior methods. By systematically generating high-quality demonstrations, GenH2R provides a scalable pathway for acquiring the large datasets necessary for modern imitation learning paradigms, ensuring the generated data is suitable for closed-loop visuomotor policies.
- **Robust and Efficient Imitation Learning**: The forecast-aided 4D imitation learning method effectively distills large-scale, dynamic demonstrations. By leveraging 4D point cloud input with flow information and an auxiliary future forecasting objective, the policy becomes more responsive and anticipatory. This approach demonstrates a practical balance between effectiveness and computational efficiency, offering a robust alternative to expensive and potentially unstable reinforcement learning methods for dynamic manipulation tasks.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The experimental results confirm that GenH2R achieves substantial improvements in generalizability to novel object geometries and complex human motions. Crucially, the framework facilitates effective sim-to-real transfer, allowing policies trained entirely in simulation to perform robustly on real robotic systems. This reduces the dependency on costly and risky real-world data collection and fine-tuning, accelerating the deployment of HRI capabilities.
- **Practical Implications for Human-Robot Collaboration**: By enabling robots to reliably and robustly receive objects in dynamic and varied scenarios, GenH2R contributes directly to the development of more capable and adaptable robots for collaborative tasks. This can expand the practical applications of robots in various domains, including manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and domestic assistance, where seamless human-robot interaction is essential.

In summary, GenH2R offers a complete framework that tackles fundamental challenges in H2R handover learning through a scalable, data-driven approach. Its contributions pave the way for more generalizable and practical robot manipulation skills in dynamic human-centric environments, providing a foundation for future research in embodied AI and human-robot interaction.
