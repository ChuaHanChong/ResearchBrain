# LumiSculpt: Enabling Consistent Portrait Lighting in Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> LumiSculpt, a new framework, addresses consistent portrait lighting in video generation by integrating a unique synthetic dataset, LumiHuman, and a novel dual-branch training framework with a disentanglement loss. This approach enables precise control over light source position, intensity, and trajectory, demonstrating superior inter-frame consistency and lighting accuracy over existing text-to-video and image relighting methods.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality, lighting-specific datasets, especially for videos, that provide explicit lighting information for generative models.
- Complexity in accurately representing and integrating lighting effects into end-to-end video generation models without extensive annotated data.
- Challenge of decoupling specific lighting control from other visual attributes like appearance and semantics to prevent overfitting and ensure diverse content generation.

## Method

- Constructed LumiHuman, a large-scale portrait lighting dataset with over 220,000 videos, created using Unreal Engine for precise control over lighting parameters and diverse human subjects.
- Introduced a novel lighting representation that projects lighting information onto a blank canvas in pixel space, facilitating effective integration into latent diffusion models.
- Developed **LumiSculpt** as a plug-and-play module within latent diffusion models, employing a dual-branch training framework with a disentanglement loss (L_dis) to isolate lighting control from appearance.

## Results

- LumiSculpt achieved superior quantitative performance across five metrics, including a Lighting Direction RMSE of **0.3500** and Frame-wise CLIP Image Similarity of **0.9951**, outperforming Open-Sora and other baselines.
- Qualitative evaluations confirmed LumiSculpt's ability to maintain video coherence and visual quality while achieving precise control over lighting trajectory and intensity, addressing flickering and static lighting issues seen in comparative methods.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the critical roles of caption augmentation for diverse content generation and the dual-branch framework with disentanglement loss for robust appearance decoupling and accurate lighting control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthetic datasets, generated with precise parameter control (e.g., LumiHuman), can effectively overcome data scarcity challenges for fine-grained attribute control in generative tasks.
> - Representing complex lighting conditions as a simple pixel-space projection onto a blank canvas offers an effective and model-agnostic way to integrate lighting information into latent diffusion models.
> - A dual-branch training framework combined with a disentanglement loss provides an effective mechanism to learn specific attribute control (like lighting) while preserving the diverse generative capabilities and preventing appearance leakage from the foundational model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in generative models, particularly text-to-image (T2I) and text-to-video (T2V) synthesis using latent diffusion models (LDMs). While significant progress has been made in generating realistic and semantically consistent video content from text prompts, challenges remain in achieving granular, consistent control over specific visual attributes.

Current T2V methods primarily focus on generating content aligned with textual descriptions or on broader controls like video editing, customization, and motion control. However, the precise and consistent manipulation of lighting conditions in generated videos remains an underexplored area. Lighting is a fundamental element in visual media, influencing aesthetics, mood, character emphasis, and audience attention. Its deep coupling with other video elements (objects, scenes) makes independent modeling and control challenging. Existing relighting techniques, often relying on light stage data and high dynamic range (HDR) environmental maps, are typically image-to-image or video-to-video approaches that leverage existing image or video priors. These methods are not directly transferable to text-to-video generation, where content is synthesized from scratch without a visual input.

This paper addresses this gap by focusing specifically on enabling precise and consistent lighting control within the text-to-video generation paradigm. It distinguishes itself from prior work by aiming for explicit control over light source position, intensity, and trajectory in a generative context, rather than merely relighting existing content or specifying fixed light types via text.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a method, named LumiSculpt, that enables precise and consistent control over lighting conditions in text-to-video generation models. The authors identify three main challenges that motivate their research:

1. **Dataset Scarcity:** There is a lack of high-quality, lighting-specific datasets, particularly for videos, that provide explicit, well-defined lighting information. Existing relighting datasets, often derived from light stage systems, have data formats that are not readily adaptable for text-controlled content generation scenarios. This scarcity hinders the training of models capable of disentangling and manipulating lighting independently.
2. **Complexity of Lighting Representation:** Accurately representing lighting effects, which depend on factors like light source type, direction of illumination, and object material properties, poses a challenge in an end-to-end video generation context. Directly embedding lighting parameters as input requires extensive annotated data to establish reliable mappings to a 2D image plane, which is often infeasible. A simple yet effective lighting representation is needed that relates primarily to lighting parameters.
3. **Attribute Decoupling:** Similar to other control tasks in generative models, lighting control requires effective disentanglement from other visual attributes, such as semantics, color, and object appearance. If lighting information is deeply coupled with appearance in training data, models risk overfitting to specific appearances, limiting their ability to generate diverse content while maintaining precise lighting control.

To address these motivations, the paper aims to:
- Construct a lightweight and flexible dataset for portrait lighting in images and videos with known lighting parameters, adaptable for text-controlled generation.
- Develop a novel lighting representation that projects lighting information onto a blank canvas, facilitating integration into generative models.
- Propose a learnable, plug-and-play module that can precisely control the intensity, position, and trajectory of an assumed light source within video diffusion models.
- Design a mechanism for injecting lighting conditions into the generative model without negatively influencing other attributes, specifically by decoupling lighting from appearance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of LumiSculpt involves the creation of a specialized dataset, a novel lighting representation, and a plug-and-play architectural module integrated with a dual-branch training framework and a disentanglement loss.

#### 3.1. LumiHuman Dataset Construction

To address data scarcity, the authors introduce **LumiHuman**, a portrait lighting dataset.
- **Scale and Diversity:** LumiHuman comprises over 220,000 continuous lighting videos (approximately 2.3 million images) at 1024x1024 resolution. It features 65 diverse human subjects, over 30,000 distinct lighting positions, and more than 3,000 light source trajectories per individual.
- **Generation Method:** The dataset is created using Unreal Engine for lighting simulation, allowing for precise control and knowledge of lighting parameters.
- **Lighting Design:** A 3D grid (160 cm x 160 cm x 160 cm) with 5 cm spacing defines 33x33x33 potential lighting positions. A point light source moves across these grid points, illuminating subjects from various angles.
- **Lighting Trajectory Design:** Predefined horizontal, vertical, and diagonal trajectories are composed from these grid points to simulate dynamic lighting changes. The dataset also allows for flexible arrangement of individual frames to form user-specified trajectories (e.g., arc-shaped, multi-light-source superpositions).
- **Character Construction:** High-quality 3D models of 65 diverse individuals from the MetaHuman dataset are used to enhance the richness of visual lighting effects across different characters.
- **Flexibility and Storage:** The dataset stores images rendered from individual lighting positions, with each character having 33x33x33 unique samples. This flexible structure allows users to generate videos from predefined or custom trajectories efficiently, optimizing storage by avoiding duplicate frames.
- **Text Annotation and Augmentation:** BLIP is used for automated video captioning. GPT-4 is then employed for caption augmentation to generate diverse contextual narratives, enriching the dataset's textual diversity and preventing the model from relying solely on exact dataset matches.

#### 3.2. Lighting Representation

Instead of directly embedding lighting parameters, LumiSculpt represents lighting by projecting it onto a blank canvas. For each lighting position, this projection is visualized as an image where brighter regions indicate stronger illumination and darker regions weaker lighting. This pixel-space representation is designed to align better with the model's latent space, enabling more effective integration of lighting information.

#### 3.3. LumiSculpt Architecture and Integration

LumiSculpt is designed as a plug-and-play module integrated into latent diffusion models for video generation.
- **Lighting Encoder:** Lighting features, extracted using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) shared with the generative model, are fed into a dedicated light encoder. This encoder employs a transformer architecture with self-attention layers, designed to capture the global spatial and temporal dynamics of lighting across video frames.
- **Conditional Injection:** The light encoder processes the lighting features through transformer blocks, matching the number of layers in the backbone DiT (Diffusion Transformer) model. It outputs a sequence of latent representations. These latents are then integrated into the DiT architecture of the T2V model (specifically Open-Sora-Plan) via element-wise addition with the latent video features, followed by a linear layer. Multi-stage weighting is utilized as the conditional injection mechanism.

#### 3.4. Lighting Learning with Dual-Branch Framework and Disentanglement Loss

To address the challenge of appearance leakage and attribute decoupling during training, LumiSculpt employs a novel training framework:
- **Dual-Branch Framework:** This framework consists of a training branch (which includes LumiSculpt's light control module) and a frozen reference branch. The frozen branch uses a pre-trained foundational denoising model (sharing weights with the generation backbone but with frozen parameters) to serve as an appearance reference. Both branches receive the same textual condition and noisy latent input, predicting noise values (𝜖_pred and 𝜖_reg, respectively). This setup provides diverse appearance priors from the pre-trained model in a cost-efficient manner without requiring additional diverse lighting data.
- **Disentanglement Loss (L_dis):** To evaluate and suppress appearance information leakage, a disentanglement loss is proposed. Inspired by neural style transfer, L_dis uses Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN) to compare the feature statistics (mean and variance) of the latent representations from the two branches. By aligning the appearance distribution of the training branch with the frozen reference branch, the model is encouraged to isolate lighting information while retaining the original model's diverse generative capacity.
- **Overall Training Objective:** The total loss (L_total) combines the standard denoising loss (L_denoise) with the disentanglement loss (L_dis), weighted by a balancing coefficient (𝛽=3.0). This objective optimizes the generative model to match the training video via L_denoise while enforcing appearance disentanglement through L_dis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate LumiSculpt's effectiveness in achieving precise and high-quality lighting control in video generation.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluations

LumiSculpt was evaluated against Open-Sora-Plan, IC-Light (an image relighting method), and ControlNet (an image control method) using five metrics on a test set of 500 novel light paths and captions:
- **Frame-wise CLIP Image Similarity (↑):** Measures semantic coherence between frames. LumiSculpt achieved 0.9951, outperforming Open-Sora (0.9845), IC-Light (0.9703), and ControlNet (0.8081), indicating stronger inter-frame semantic stability.
- **Frame-wise LPIPS Consistency (↓):** Evaluates feature-level coherence. LumiSculpt achieved 1.1312, significantly lower than Open-Sora (1.3503), IC-Light (2.5329), and ControlNet (5.9324), demonstrating higher inter-frame consistency.
- **Lighting Direction RMSE (↓):** Assesses alignment with reference lighting direction. LumiSculpt scored 0.3500, outperforming Open-Sora (0.4542), IC-Light (0.5264), and ControlNet (0.5500), indicating better lighting direction accuracy.
- **Brightness Consistency (↑):** Measures consistency of brightness distribution with reference. LumiSculpt achieved 0.8779, higher than Open-Sora (0.8229), IC-Light (0.8632), and ControlNet (0.8032), reflecting better alignment of brightness patterns.
- **CLIP Text-Image Similarity (↑):** Measures alignment between generated frames and text prompts. LumiSculpt scored 0.3597, higher than Open-Sora (0.3182), IC-Light (0.3145), and ControlNet (0.3440), indicating better overall generation quality and semantic alignment.

Overall, LumiSculpt demonstrated superior performance across all quantitative metrics, particularly in maintaining strong inter-frame consistency and text-image alignment while achieving precise lighting control.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluations

Qualitative comparisons with IC-Light, ControlNet, and Open-Sora involved testing strong/soft light intensity and horizontal/vertical light movement directions.
- **IC-Light:** Produced accurate lighting for single frames but exhibited poor video coherence and noticeable flickering in the background due to its image-centric design and lack of inter-frame awareness.
- **Open-Sora:** Generated coherent and aesthetically pleasing videos, but it lacked the ability to control lighting direction via textual conditions, resulting in largely static lighting throughout the video.
- **LumiSculpt:** Successfully maintained video coherence and visual quality while achieving precise control over both lighting trajectory and intensity, effectively balancing generative quality with specific lighting manipulation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted on key components:
- **Without Caption Augmentation:** Removing caption augmentation led to the model relying solely on exact text matches from the dataset, improving appearance fitting but reducing the diversity of textual guidance and thus limiting diverse content generation.
- **Without Disentanglement Loss (L_dis) / Dual-Branch Structure:** Without L_dis and the dual-branch framework, the generated appearances tended to overfit the training data. This made it challenging to produce diverse backgrounds and showed limitations in decoupling lighting from appearance.
- **Full LumiSculpt:** The complete model successfully balanced diverse appearances with accurate lighting control.
- **Guidance Scale (Hyper-parameter):** Varying the guidance scale (an inference parameter) revealed a trade-off. Increasing the scale intensified lighting guidance, enhancing accuracy of direction and brightness. However, an excessively large scale could reduce the model's capacity for diverse outputs. Decreasing the scale reduced lighting accuracy. A scale of 0.5 was found to provide a suitable balance.

#### 4.4. Generalization

LumiSculpt demonstrated generalization capabilities by successfully applying lighting priors to animals, suggesting its potential applicability beyond human portraits to other real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LumiSculpt represents a significant advancement in controllable video generation, specifically addressing the long-standing challenge of precise and consistent lighting control in text-to-video synthesis.

- **Enabling Fine-Grained Control:** By introducing a learnable plug-and-play module, LumiSculpt empowers users with explicit control over the intensity, position, and dynamic trajectory of light sources within generated videos. This capability moves beyond general textual cues, allowing for artistic expression and practical application in scenarios requiring specific lighting conditions.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The creation of **LumiHuman** is a substantial contribution. As a lightweight, flexible, and comprehensive portrait lighting dataset with known lighting parameters, it directly tackles the issue of insufficient training data for controllable lighting tasks in generative models. Its design, allowing for combinatorial generation of diverse lighting paths, makes it a valuable resource for future research in both image and video generation.
- **Advancing Attribute Disentanglement:** The proposed dual-branch framework combined with the disentanglement loss (L_dis) offers an effective methodology for decoupling lighting information from appearance-related features. This is crucial for maintaining the diversity and generalization capabilities of generative models while enabling targeted control over specific attributes. This approach holds potential for generalization to other generative tasks requiring attribute isolation.
- **Improved Video Quality and Coherence:** Quantitative and qualitative results demonstrate that LumiSculpt not only achieves accurate lighting control but also maintains strong inter-frame consistency and overall video quality, outperforming existing text-to-video and image relighting methods.
- **Broad Applications:** The ability to consistently control lighting in generated videos has implications for various domains, including film production, virtual reality, game design, and advertising, where specific moods, visual aesthetics, or functional lighting scenarios are required. It allows creators to sculpt the emotional tone and visual focus of generated narratives more precisely.

In conclusion, LumiSculpt and LumiHuman offer valuable methodologies and resources that are expected to serve as foundational contributions for future explorations in controllable video generation, meeting increasing aesthetic and practical demands for precise lighting manipulation.
