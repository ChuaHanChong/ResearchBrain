# Robotic Manipulation by Imitating Generated Videos Without Physical Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named RIGVid enables robots to execute manipulation tasks solely by imitating AI-generated videos, bypassing the need for physical demonstrations. The system achieved an average success rate of 85% across four diverse tasks on an xArm7 robot, matching performance observed with real human demonstrations while outperforming VLM-based trajectory prediction by 35 percentage points.

## Problem

- Robots traditionally require extensive physical demonstrations or large-scale real-world video datasets to acquire diverse manipulation skills.
- Physical demonstrations are laborious, costly, and demand precise alignment between the demonstration and the robot's execution setup.
- Large-scale public video datasets often introduce 'domain gaps,' necessitating complex adaptation for specific robot embodiments and task environments.

## Method

- RIGVid leverages AI-generated videos from state-of-the-art diffusion models (e.g., Kling v1.6) as the exclusive source of supervision for robotic manipulation.
- An automatic filtering mechanism, based on GPT-4o, assesses generated videos for command following and physical plausibility, ensuring only suitable videos are used.
- The system extracts 6D object pose trajectories from filtered videos using a 6D object pose tracker (FoundationPose) and retargets them to the robot's end-effector for closed-loop execution with real-time adjustments.

## Results

- RIGVid achieved an average success rate of 85% across four complex manipulation tasks (pouring, lifting, placing, sweeping) on an xArm7 robot, demonstrating performance comparable to using real human demonstrations.
- The framework significantly outperformed a state-of-the-art VLM-based trajectory prediction method (ReKep), which achieved 50% success, and other video-based trajectory extraction baselines.
- The GPT-4o-based video filtering mechanism proved highly effective, achieving a 0.84 Pearson correlation with human judgments and improving success rates (e.g., pouring from 80% to 100% and placing from 50% to 90% for Kling v1.6 videos).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art video generation models, when effectively filtered, can provide sufficient information for real-world robotic manipulation tasks without any physical demonstrations or robot-specific training.
> - Dense, structured 6D object pose trajectories extracted from generated videos are more robust for robot execution than sparser VLM-based abstractions or point/flow-based tracking methods.
> - The accuracy of monocular depth estimation is a primary bottleneck for overall robot performance, even when using high-quality generated videos and robust pose tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a significant challenge in robotic manipulation: the acquisition of diverse skills. Traditionally, robots learn manipulation tasks through two primary methods: leveraging large-scale public datasets of real-world videos or imitating specific physical demonstrations. Both approaches present limitations. Large-scale datasets often introduce "domain gaps," requiring complex adaptation to specific robot embodiments and task environments. Physical demonstrations, while effective, are laborious to collect, demanding precise alignment in viewpoints, morphologies, and interaction modalities between the demonstration and the robot's execution setting.

This work introduces a potentially new paradigm by exploring the use of AI-generated videos as the sole source of supervision for robotic manipulation. Recent advancements in video generation models, such as SORA and Kling, have demonstrated capabilities in producing realistic-seeming videos from language and image inputs. However, these generated videos can exhibit issues like distorted object geometries, physically implausible interactions, and unrealistic scene dynamics, raising questions about their direct utility in robotics. Prior research incorporating video generation into robotics typically mitigated these issues by relying on additional supervision, such as task-specific training or fine-tuning on offline robot trajectory datasets.

The presented research distinguishes itself by posing a more fundamental question: Can a robot perform real-world manipulation tasks *solely* by imitating generated videos, *without any additional supervision or task-specific training*? This positions the work at the intersection of generative AI, computer vision, and robotic control, aiming to bridge the gap between abstract visual information synthesis and concrete physical action in a truly zero-shot, zero-demonstration manner.

The paper contextualizes its approach against existing imitation learning techniques:
- **Direct Imitation from Videos**: These methods aim to match visual states from demonstration videos without needing expert action labels or robot state information. While effective, they necessitate demonstrations collected under conditions closely matching the target task. RIGVid seeks to eliminate this requirement by generating task- and scene-conditioned videos.
- **Imitation from Offline Videos**: This paradigm utilizes large, pre-existing video datasets to learn affordance models, predicting interaction points and short motion trajectories. Such methods often suffer from domain gaps between training videos and specific task environments and require mechanisms to obtain task-conditioned affordances. RIGVid, conversely, uses generated, task- and scene-specific videos for imitation rather than explicitly predicting affordances from general datasets.
- **Video Generation for Robotics**: Previous work in this area often requires robot-specific data to train video generation models or robotic policies. The most closely related work, Liang et al., relies on human-collected robot demonstrations and tracks tools attached to the robot. In contrast, RIGVid requires no such data collection and focuses on tracking objects, thereby enabling a broader range of manipulation tasks without any robot-specific data.
- **6D Pose Estimation and Tracking & Motion Retargeting**: The work leverages advancements in these fields, specifically using instance-level 6D pose tracking that requires a 3D object model, and an object-centric motion retargeting process that assumes a fixed transformation between the end-effector and the object after grasping. The novelty lies in applying these established techniques to trajectories extracted from generated videos, replacing the traditional reliance on human demonstrations.

By proposing a framework that operates entirely from synthetic visual supervision, this work aims to reduce the laborious and costly process of real-world data collection, potentially broadening the applicability of robots to novel, open-world manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a system, named Robots Imitating Generated Videos (RIGVid), that enables robots to execute complex manipulation tasks exclusively by imitating AI-generated videos. This approach aims to circumvent the need for any physical demonstrations, teleoperation, or robot-specific training data.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations inherent in existing robotic learning paradigms:

- **High Cost and Labor of Data Collection**: Traditional imitation learning methods heavily rely on human demonstrations or large datasets of real-world videos. Collecting these demonstrations is a time-consuming, expensive, and often laborious process, requiring human experts to physically interact with robots or carefully record manipulation tasks. This creates a bottleneck for scaling robotic capabilities to a wide array of tasks and environments.
- **Domain Gaps and Generalization Challenges**: Even with large datasets, a significant domain gap often exists between the training environment (e.g., publicly available human videos or controlled demonstration settings) and the robot's target execution environment. This necessitates complex adaptation mechanisms and limits the generalizability of learned skills to novel objects, scenes, or robot embodiments.
- **Limitations of Prior Video Generation in Robotics**: While video generation has been explored in robotics, previous methods typically required supplementary supervision. This often involved using robot-specific data to either train the video generation model itself or fine-tune downstream robotic policies. The current work seeks to remove this dependency, probing whether off-the-shelf generative models, without any robotics-specific conditioning, can provide sufficient information.
- **Need for Open-World Manipulation**: Enabling robots to learn from abstract, easily synthesizable task descriptions could unlock capabilities for open-world manipulation, where tasks are diverse and environments are unstructured.

To address these motivations, the authors established several key research questions:

1. **Can AI-generated videos serve as the *sole* source of supervision?**: The core question is whether a single, synthesized video, tailored to an input environment and task description, can entirely replace physical demonstrations.
2. **How reliable are current video generation models for robotic tasks?**: Given the known issues of physical implausibility and inconsistencies in generated videos, it is critical to assess their utility and identify methods for filtering out inadequate generations.
3. **Are dense visual representations (videos) necessary, or can sparser abstractions suffice?**: Video generation is computationally intensive. The research investigates whether more compact representations, such as keypoint predictions from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), could offer a more efficient alternative while still providing sufficient detail for robust robot execution.
4. **What is the most effective method for extracting executable trajectories from generated videos?**: Given a generated video, the choice of trajectory extraction method (e.g., 6D object pose tracking vs. dense feature point tracking, optical flow, or sparse point tracking) is crucial for translating visual cues into precise robot actions. The work aims to identify the most robust approach.

By answering these questions, the authors aim to develop a framework that is data-efficient, adaptable to various tasks and embodiments, and robust to real-world perturbations, ultimately moving towards a future where robots can learn skills from readily available or synthetically created visual information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RIGVid (Robots Imitating Generated Videos) framework is designed to enable robots to perform manipulation tasks using only AI-generated videos, without physical demonstrations or robot-specific training. The method operates in a pipeline consisting of several key stages:

1. **Video Generation and Depth Prediction (Sec. 3.1)**:
 - **Input**: The system takes an initial scene RGB-D image and a free-form language command (e.g., "pour water on the plant").
 - **Video Generation Model**: A state-of-the-art video diffusion model (specifically, Kling v1.6 was found most effective) generates a candidate video depicting the task.
 - **VLM-based Filtering**: Since generated videos may not always accurately follow the command, an automatic filtering mechanism is employed. GPT-4o is used as a Vision-Language Model (VLM). It receives four evenly spaced frames from the generated video (concatenated vertically as a summary) along with the language command. GPT-4o assesses whether a visible hand performs the described action successfully. If the assessment is negative, the system regenerates the video, up to five attempts.
 - **Monocular Depth Estimation**: For each frame of the generated video, a monocular depth estimator (from Ke et al.) predicts depth. To ground these relative depth values in real-world units, an affine scale-and-shift transformation is computed by aligning the predicted depth in the first frame with the initial real depth map around the active object. This transformation is then applied consistently across all video frames.

2. **Active Object Identification and 6D Object Pose Trajectory Extraction (Sec. 3.2)**:
 - **Active Object Localization**: To identify the object to be manipulated, GPT-4o is prompted with the initial image and task command to determine the most likely manipulated object. Grounding DINO then localizes this object with a bounding box, which is further refined into a precise segmentation mask using SAM-2.
 - **6D Pose Tracking**: The active object's 6D pose (position and orientation) is tracked across the generated video using the segmented mask and the scaled predicted depth. FoundationPose, an instance-level 6D pose tracker, is utilized for its performance and real-time capabilities. This tracker requires a pre-computed 3D object mesh, which is obtained by recording a short RGBD video of the object being rotated (using BundleSDF).
 - **Trajectory Smoothing**: To ensure stable and realistic robot motion, the extracted 6D object pose trajectory undergoes smoothing using an averaging filter, particularly to reduce abrupt changes in rotation and translation.

3. **Object to Robot Motion Retargeting (Sec. 3.3)**:
 - **Grasping**: Before executing the trajectory, the robot grasps the active object. AnyGrasp, an off-the-shelf grasper, is used to identify and execute an optimal grasp within the object's boundary.
 - **Transformation Derivation**: Once the object is grasped, a fixed transformation between the robot's end-effector and the object is assumed. This transformation is derived by composing two rigid-body transforms: the pose of the object relative to the gripper at the grasp moment, and the offset between the gripper and the robot's end-effector.
 - **Trajectory Retargeting**: The robot's end-effector trajectory is generated by applying this fixed end-effector-to-object transformation to each 6D object pose in the extracted trajectory. This formulation makes the approach embodiment-agnostic, requiring only an update to this transformation for deployment on different robots or grippers.

4. **Closed-Loop Execution (Sec. 3.4)**:
 - **Real-time Tracking and Adjustment**: During task execution, RIGVid maintains a closed-loop control system. FoundationPose continuously tracks the object's 6D pose in real time. This feedback allows the robot to dynamically adjust its end-effector trajectory.
 - **Disturbance Recovery**: If the object deviates from the planned trajectory (e.g., due to external perturbations, gripper slip) beyond a predefined threshold (3 cm in position or 20 degrees in orientation), the robot automatically backtracks to the last successfully executed trajectory point and resumes execution from there. This recovery mechanism enhances the system's robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RIGVid involved experiments on an xArm7 robot with an Orbbec Femto Bolt camera, across four diverse everyday manipulation tasks: pouring water, lifting a lid, placing a spatula on a pan, and sweeping trash. Each task involved 10 trials, with success determined by human judgment.

#### 4.1. Video Generation Model Quality and Filtering Effectiveness

- **Video Model Comparison**: Qualitative and quantitative analysis showed significant differences among video generation models. Sora frequently altered scene layouts, object sizes, or camera viewpoints, resulting in 0% filtering pass rates due to poor command following. Kling v1.5 showed better scene preservation but still suffered from physically implausible behaviors and command-following failures (e.g., 52.6% pass rate for pouring, 4% for placing). Kling v1.6 demonstrated substantial improvements in command following and physical plausibility, yielding significantly higher pass rates (e.g., 83% for pouring, 55% for placing, 45% for sweeping).
- **VLM-based Filtering**: The GPT-4o-based filtering mechanism proved highly effective, achieving a strong average Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.84 with human judgments of generation correctness. Most filtering errors were false negatives (discarding usable videos), rarely false positives (passing incorrect videos). Standard video evaluation metrics like VBench++'s video-text consistency and I2V subject consistency showed weak or inconsistent correlation with task success, underscoring the utility of the VLM-based filter.

#### 4.2. Impact of Video Quality on Robot Performance

- **Quality-Success Correlation**: A direct correlation was observed between the quality of generated videos and robotic task success rates. Unfiltered Sora videos resulted in 0% success. Performance improved with Kling v1.5, and further with Kling v1.6.
- **Filtering Benefits**: Filtering substantially enhanced reliability. For filtered Kling v1.6 videos, success rates improved from 80% to 100% on pouring, 60% to 80% on lifting, 50% to 90% on placing, and 20% to 70% on sweeping.
- **Generated vs. Real Videos**: Critically, RIGVid's performance with filtered Kling v1.6 videos was comparable to that achieved with real human demonstration videos. This indicates that, with current generative model quality and filtering, synthetic data can effectively substitute real-world demonstrations.
- **Failure Analysis**: The majority of failures (excluding one gripper slip) with filtered Kling v1.6 videos were attributed to inaccuracies in monocular depth estimation, encompassing both imprecise depth values and temporal flickering. This limitation was also observed when using predicted depth with real videos, where performance dropped from 100% (with real depth) to 85% (with predicted depth), suggesting the depth model as a primary bottleneck.

#### 4.3. Comparison to VLM-Based Trajectory Prediction (ReKep)

- RIGVid achieved an average success rate of 85% across the four tasks, significantly outperforming the state-of-the-art VLM-based trajectory prediction method, ReKep, which managed 50%.
- ReKep's failures were primarily linked to inaccurate keypoint predictions (e.g., suboptimal placement or clustering of keypoints), indicating that sparse, high-level VLM abstractions may lack the rich, dense visual and temporal details necessary for robust robot execution in these complex tasks.

#### 4.4. Comparison to Alternative Trajectory Extraction Methods

- RIGVid, leveraging 6D object pose tracking with FoundationPose, consistently achieved the highest success rate at 85.0%.
- Other trajectory extraction methods yielded lower success rates: Gen2Act (point tracks) achieved 67.5%, AVDC (optical flow) 32.5%, 4D-DPM (feature fields) 35.0%, and Track2Act (point tracks from start/goal images) 7.5%.
- RIGVid's advantage was particularly pronounced on challenging tasks like spatula placement and sweeping, where it outperformed the next best baseline by 20-25%. This suggests that structured 6D pose trajectories are more robust to depth variations, occlusions, and significant object rotations compared to methods relying on sparse point tracks or dense optical flow, which often suffer from track loss or cumulative errors.

#### 4.5. Generalization Capabilities

- **Embodiment-Agnostic Transfer**: RIGVid successfully transferred to a different robotic platform, the ALOHA robot, achieving 80% success on the pouring task. It also demonstrated generalization to a bimanual setup for placing shoes.
- **Extension to Additional Tasks**: Preliminary results on a wider array of challenging manipulation tasks, including wiping, mixing, ironing, uprighting a ketchup bottle, unplugging a charger, and spilling beans (tasks involving extreme rotations), showed promising performance, further highlighting the framework's versatility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation by introducing RIGVid, a framework that enables robots to perform complex tasks purely through imitation of AI-generated videos, without requiring any physical demonstrations or robot-specific training. This work holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Reduction in Data Collection Burden**: The most immediate impact is the substantial reduction, and in many cases, elimination, of the laborious and costly process of collecting real-world robot demonstrations. This addresses a major bottleneck in scaling robotic capabilities and democratizes access to advanced manipulation skills for a broader range of users and applications.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency**: By leveraging the knowledge encapsulated within sophisticated video diffusion models, RIGVid offers a data-efficient approach to robotic skill acquisition. This paradigm shifts the focus from real-world interaction data to readily synthesizable visual information, unlocking new avenues for learning and skill transfer.
- **Validation of Generative AI for Robotics**: The study provides empirical evidence that state-of-the-art, off-the-shelf generative vision models (specifically Kling v1.6) can serve as a viable and effective source of supervision for real-world robotic tasks. This validates the potential of foundational generative models as "world simulators" or "demonstrators" for embodied AI.
- **Performance Parity with Human Demonstrations**: The finding that filtered generated videos can enable robot performance on par with real human demonstration videos is a critical achievement. It suggests that synthetic data is not merely a supplementary resource but can, under the right conditions, be a direct substitute for real demonstrations, opening up possibilities for synthesizing diverse and niche scenarios that are difficult or dangerous to demonstrate physically.
- **Robustness and Generalization**: The closed-loop execution mechanism, coupled with robust 6D object pose tracking, contributes to the system's ability to handle environmental disturbances and adapt to execution-time variations. The demonstrated embodiment-agnostic transfer and successful application to a wide array of diverse and challenging tasks (including those with extreme rotations) underscore RIGVid's potential for broad generalization across different robots and manipulation scenarios.
- **Methodological Insights**: The comprehensive comparative analysis highlights the superiority of rich, dense visual and temporal cues from generated videos over sparser VLM-based abstractions for trajectory prediction. It also empirically establishes the importance of structured 6D object pose trajectory extraction for robust imitation, outperforming alternative point-based or flow-based tracking methods, particularly in scenarios involving occlusion, depth changes, or significant rotations.
- **Direction for Future Research**: The identification of monocular depth estimation as the primary source of remaining errors, even with high-quality generated videos, points to a clear and critical area for future research and improvement. Addressing this bottleneck could further enhance the reliability and precision of such systems. Similarly, developing efficient mesh-free tracking solutions would broaden the applicability by removing the prerequisite for pre-computed object meshes.

In conclusion, this work represents a significant step towards enabling robots to learn autonomously from the vast and increasingly sophisticated visual knowledge available through generative AI. By reducing reliance on laborious physical data collection, RIGVid offers a pathway to accelerating the development and deployment of intelligent robots capable of performing complex, open-world manipulation tasks.
