# VScan: Rethinking Visual Token Reduction for Efficient Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VScan introduces an empirically-guided, two-stage framework for reducing visual tokens in large vision-language models, which combines global/local visual scans with token merging and text-aware pruning at intermediate LLM layers. The method achieves up to 2.91 × prefilling speedup on LLaVA-NeXT-7B while retaining over 95% of original performance across diverse benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) incur significant computational and memory costs due to processing thousands of visual tokens, leading to quadratic complexity in self-attention mechanisms and limiting real-time deployment.
- Prior visual token reduction methods often rely on single-stage pruning strategies, which can overlook critical visual information or prune at suboptimal stages of the LVLM pipeline.
- There was a lack of systematic empirical understanding regarding how visual tokens are processed throughout both the visual encoding and language decoding stages of LVLMs.

## Method

- Conducted an in-depth empirical analysis to understand the evolution of visual information in the visual encoder (local to global attention) and cross-modal interactions in the LLM's decoding layers.
- Proposed a ==two-stage, training-free visual token reduction framework== that first reduces visual redundancy during encoding via ==complementary global and local scans with token merging==, then reduces textual irrelevance by ==pruning at intermediate LLM layers==.
- Designed the framework to be compatible with diverse LVLM architectures and existing hardware-aware optimizations like FlashAttention, without requiring additional fine-tuning.

## Results

- Achieved significant efficiency gains, including up to **2.91×** prefilling speedup and **2.05×** overall inference speedup on LLaVA-NeXT-7B, with only a **1.4%** performance decline at an **11%** visual token retention rate.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art baselines across **16** image and video understanding benchmarks, retaining **96.7%** of LLaVA-1.5's performance at an **88.9%** token reduction (**4.0%** better than VisionZip).
- Maintained robust performance on challenging referring grounding tasks and video understanding benchmarks, demonstrating effective KV cache compression and full compatibility with FlashAttention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shallow layers of the visual encoder retain fine-grained local visual details, while deeper layers capture broader global context, necessitating a multi-faceted approach to visual token selection.
> - Early layers of the LLM exhibit strong positional biases towards visual tokens and weak cross-modal interaction, whereas middle layers show reduced bias and mature cross-modal integration, making them ideal for text-aware pruning.
> - A combination of global token selection from deep visual encoder layers and local token selection from shallow layers, along with similarity-based token merging, effectively preserves comprehensive visual information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in multi-modal understanding, particularly with high-resolution images and multi-image/video inputs. This increased capacity for fine-grained perception, however, necessitates processing a significantly larger number of visual tokens. For instance, models like LLaVA-NeXT can encode thousands of visual tokens, which can be orders of magnitude higher than typical text token sequences. This high visual token count leads to substantial computational and memory overhead due to the quadratic complexity of the self-attention mechanism within Transformer architectures. Such overhead impedes the scalability and real-time deployment of LVLMs in practical applications.

To address this challenge, prior research has explored visual token reduction techniques, which generally fall into two categories:
1. **Text-agnostic pruning:** These methods aim to reduce visually redundant tokens during the visual encoding stage, typically by leveraging self-attention or `[CLS]` token attention from the visual encoder's output layer. Examples include VisionZip and FOLDER.
2. **Text-aware pruning:** These approaches selectively remove visual tokens deemed irrelevant to a given text query during the early layers of the language model (LLM) decoding stage. Examples include FastV, SparseVLM, and PyramidDrop.

While these existing methods contribute to improving efficiency, the current work posits that their performance is often limited by their single-stage design and a lack of systematic understanding of how visual tokens are processed throughout the entire LVLM pipeline. VScan aims to integrate and advance these concepts by conducting a detailed empirical analysis of token processing and proposing a two-stage reduction framework that combines complementary visual encoding-stage reduction with a more strategically placed pruning step within the LLM decoding stage. This approach attempts to offer a more comprehensive and effective solution to the computational challenges of LVLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and robust visual token reduction framework for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) that significantly accelerates inference with minimal performance degradation. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed motivations and specific objectives:

- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** Modern LVLMs, especially those processing high-resolution or multi-modal inputs, generate an excessively large number of visual tokens. This leads to substantial computational and memory costs, primarily due to the quadratic scaling of self-attention mechanisms, thereby hindering real-time deployment and scalability. The research is motivated by the need to mitigate these resource-intensive operations.

- **Reassessing Existing Reduction Paradigms:** Prior visual token reduction methods often adopt a single-stage approach, either pruning visually redundant tokens during encoding or textually irrelevant tokens during early LLM decoding. The authors observe that these methods have limitations, such as potentially discarding semantically important background details or pruning tokens before essential cross-modal interactions have fully occurred. A key objective is to empirically re-evaluate these design choices to identify their strengths and weaknesses.

- **Gaining Deeper Insights into Token Processing:** A core motivation is the lack of a comprehensive understanding of how visual tokens are processed and utilized across the entire LVLM pipeline, from visual encoding through language decoding. The research aims to conduct in-depth empirical analyses to reveal:
 - How visual attention patterns evolve across different layers of the visual encoder (e.g., transition from local details to global context).
 - How position bias and visual information integration unfold across different layers of the LLM decoder.
 - At which LLM layers next-token predictions begin to stabilize.
 These insights are intended to guide the design of a more effective token reduction strategy.

- **Developing a Two-Stage, Training-Free Framework:** Based on the empirical findings, the research aims to propose VScan, a novel two-stage framework that progressively prunes uninformative tokens. This involves:
 - **Visual Encoding Stage:** Designing a method to effectively reduce visual redundancy while preserving comprehensive visual information, including both global context and fine-grained local details. This aims to overcome the limitation of single-focus pruning strategies (e.g., solely relying on `[CLS]` attention from the output layer).
 - **LLM Decoding Stage:** Introducing a pruning strategy that accounts for cross-modal interactions and prediction convergence within the LLM, specifically targeting intermediate layers to avoid early-layer disruption and late-layer minimal impact.

- **Ensuring Broad Applicability and Compatibility:** A practical objective is to ensure that the proposed VScan framework is training-free and can be seamlessly integrated into various existing open-sourced LVLM architectures, while also being compatible with hardware-aware optimizations like FlashAttention. This aims to maximize its utility for real-world applications.

- **Achieving Superior Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** Ultimately, the motivation is to develop a method that achieves a more advantageous balance between accelerating inference speed and maintaining high task performance compared to current state-of-the-art approaches across a diverse set of benchmarks and LVLM scales.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VScan is proposed as a two-stage, training-free visual token reduction framework designed to progressively eliminate uninformative tokens during both the visual encoding and language model (LLM) decoding stages. The design of VScan is guided by empirical analyses of how LVLMs process visual tokens.

#### 3.1. Empirical Analysis Guiding Design

The methodology begins with an empirical study to understand token processing:
- **Visual Encoding Stage Analysis:** Visualizations of `[CLS]` attention and self-attention maps across visual encoder layers reveal a "local-to-global" transition. Shallow layers capture fine-grained local details, while deeper layers concentrate on global semantic relevance, suggesting that relying solely on output layer attention can discard crucial local information.
- **LLM Decoding Stage Analysis:** Three studies are conducted:
 - **Position Bias:** Early LLM layers show a strong positional bias, favoring visual tokens appearing later in the sequence (e.g., from the bottom of an image, positioned closer to instruction tokens). This bias diminishes in deeper layers.
 - **Visual Information Processing:** Middle LLM layers are identified as primarily responsible for cross-modal interactions with visual tokens, while early and deep layers focus more on text.
 - **Prediction Convergence:** Next-token predictions are observed to stabilize in the mid-to-late layers (e.g., around layer 16-20), indicating that early layers are still forming core cross-modal semantics.

These insights inform the two stages of VScan.

#### 3.2. Reducing Visual Redundancy via Complementary Global and Local Scans (Visual Encoding Stage)

To address visual redundancy while preserving comprehensive visual information, VScan employs a two-pronged token selection strategy at the visual encoding stage, followed by token merging:

- **Global Scan:** This strategy leverages the global information captured by the final layers of visual encoders. It selects tokens based on their average attention scores from the `[CLS]` token in the output layer (e.g., penultimate layer). For LVLMs without a `[CLS]` token, average self-attention scores are used. A soft threshold based on a top percentile of attention scores is applied to retain a target number of tokens.
- **Local Scan:** To complement global tokens and capture fine-grained details, the image is divided into non-overlapping windows. Within each window, locally important tokens are selected based on their `[CLS]` attention scores (or average self-attention) from a *shallow* visual encoding layer (e.g., layer 6 for LLaVA-series, layer 8 for Qwen-2.5-VL). Token budgets are uniformly allocated across windows. The final set of selected tokens is the union of global and local tokens, with a default balance of 50% global and 50% local tokens, resulting in a retention rate of R1%.
- **Token Merging:** To minimize information loss from discarded tokens, a similarity-based merging strategy is applied. Each unselected visual token is merged with its most similar selected token (based on cosine similarity). The final representation for each selected token is computed as the average of itself and all unselected tokens assigned to it.

#### 3.3. Reducing Textual Irrelevance via Middle Layer Pruning (LLM Decoding Stage)

Building on the empirical findings regarding position bias, cross-modal interaction, and prediction convergence in the LLM, VScan introduces pruning at intermediate layers of the LLM decoding stage:

- **Middle Layer Pruning:** After the initial visual redundancy reduction, the refined set of visual tokens undergoes further pruning based on their relevance to the text query. This pruning occurs at a strategically chosen *middle layer* of the LLM (e.g., layer 16 for LLaVA-series, layer 14 for Qwen-2.5-VL-7B). Textual relevance is determined by computing the attention scores between all visual tokens and the last instruction token. Tokens with the highest average text attention scores across heads are retained, achieving a retention rate of R2%. This placement aims to avoid early-layer position bias and disruption of critical cross-modal semantics while ensuring tokens are pruned after essential information integration.

#### 3.4. Compatibility and Implementation

VScan is designed to be fully compatible with standard KV caching mechanisms, as pruning occurs before tokens are added to the cache, resulting in fewer entries. It is also compatible with FlashAttention, as attention scores for the last instruction token are recomputed using vanilla attention outside standard LLM layers. The framework is training-free, integrating into existing LVLM architectures without requiring additional fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Empirical Analysis Findings

The empirical studies provided key insights that informed VScan's design:
1. **Visual Encoder Processing:** Shallow layers of the visual encoder primarily capture fine-grained local details, while deeper layers focus on a condensed set of tokens encapsulating broader global context. This suggests that exclusive reliance on output layer `[CLS]` attention overlooks rich local information.
2. **LLM Decoding Processing:**
 - Early LLM layers exhibit a strong positional bias towards visual tokens appearing later in the sequence, often neglecting semantic relevance.
 - Middle LLM layers are identified as the primary stage for gathering and processing visual information through cross-modal interactions.
 - Next-token predictions typically converge in the mid-to-late layers (e.g., layer 16 for simpler tasks, layer 20 for more complex ones), indicating that early layers are still forming core semantics.

These findings collectively motivated the two-stage approach: a complementary global-local scan during visual encoding and pruning at middle LLM layers. An empirical validation on GQA using LLaVA-1.5-7B confirmed that pruning at middle layers (e.g., layers 16 or 20) yields superior performance compared to early-layer pruning, which can lead to up to a 1.9% accuracy drop.

#### 4.2. Performance and Efficiency Results

VScan was extensively evaluated across four LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen-2.5-VL, Video-LLaVA) and sixteen image and video understanding benchmarks, comparing against six state-of-the-art methods: ToMe, FastV, SparseVLM, HiRED, PyramidDrop, and VisionZip.

- **Performance on LLaVA-1.5-7B:** On 9 image understanding benchmarks, VScan demonstrated robust performance retention. With an average retention of 192 tokens (66.7% reduction), it retained 99.0% of the original performance, and with 128 tokens (77.8% reduction), 98.8%. At an aggressive 64 tokens (88.9% reduction), VScan maintained 96.7% of the original performance, outperforming the second-best VisionZip by a margin of 4.0% in average accuracy.
- **Performance on LLaVA-NeXT-7B:** Under an 88.9% reduction rate (320 tokens retained), VScan achieved 95.4% of the original LLaVA-NeXT-7B performance. It secured the best performance on 6 out of 9 benchmarks, indicating its effectiveness on more advanced LVLMs.
- **Performance on Qwen-2.5-VL (3B, 7B, 32B scales):** VScan consistently outperformed FastV, PyramidDrop, and VisionZip across various retention rates (11.1% to 50%) and LLM scales on general visual understanding benchmarks (MMBench, MMBench-CN, MME). It showed robustness even when other methods degraded under low token budgets with larger LLMs. For challenging grounding tasks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg), VScan maintained 80.7% of original performance with a 75% reduction rate, and 96.1% with a 50% reduction rate, substantially outperforming baselines.
- **Performance on Video-LLaVA-7B:** On 4 video question answering benchmarks, VScan preserved nearly the full performance of the original Video-LLaVA-7B even with a 75% token reduction rate, consistently surpassing prior methods in both accuracy and GPT-evaluated scores.
- **Efficiency Analysis:**
 - On LLaVA-1.5-7B, retaining 11% of visual tokens resulted in a 1.37× speedup in overall inference time and a 1.77× speedup in the prefilling stage, with only a 0.9% performance decline.
 - On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, VScan delivered a 2.05× speedup in inference and a 2.91× speedup in the prefill stage with 11% token retention.
 - VScan effectively compressed KV cache storage, reducing it to 19.9% and 13.1% for LLaVA-1.5-7B and LLaVA-NeXT-7B respectively, at 11% retention.
 - The method is compatible with FlashAttention, offering further minor speedups.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments validated VScan's design choices:
- **Retention Rates R1 and R2:** A balanced two-stage pruning strategy (R1=16.7%, R2=33.3%) achieved the best performance, indicating that combining both visual encoding and LLM decoding stage reductions is more effective than relying on a single stage or overly aggressive pruning.
- **Global and Local Token Mixing:** An equal ratio (50% global, 50% local) of selected tokens yielded the best performance, confirming the complementary roles of global and local information capture.
- **Local Scan Encoding Layer:** Performing the local scan at a shallow layer (l=6) of the visual encoder was most effective, aligning with the empirical finding that shallow layers capture fine-grained local details.

#### 4.4. Additional Results

VScan generalized to other challenging real-world benchmarks like DocVQA and InfoVQA, and showed effectiveness in accelerating training, reducing GPU hours by 41.7% while retaining 96.7% of original performance. It also supports multi-turn conversations by reassessing token importance, with negligible overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VScan framework represents an advancement in the field of efficient Large Vision-Language Models by systematically addressing the computational overhead associated with processing extensive visual token sequences. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** VScan significantly reduces the inference cost of LVLMs, offering substantial speedups (e.g., 2.91× in prefilling for LLaVA-NeXT-7B) and lower FLOPs (e.g., 10× reduction for LLaVA-NeXT-7B). This efficiency gain makes LVLMs more viable for real-time applications and deployment in resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices or autonomous systems.
- **Improved Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** The research demonstrates that VScan achieves a superior balance between accelerating inference and maintaining high model performance. By retaining a high percentage of original performance (e.g., 95.4% for LLaVA-NeXT-7B with aggressive token reduction), it mitigates the common trade-off where efficiency gains often come at a significant cost to accuracy.
- **Systematic Understanding of Token Processing:** The empirical studies conducted to understand how visual tokens are processed across visual encoding and language decoding stages provide valuable insights for future research in multi-modal model optimization. This deeper understanding informs the design of more effective and targeted reduction strategies.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** VScan's effectiveness across diverse LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen-2.5-VL, Video-LLaVA) and a wide array of benchmarks (16 image and video tasks, including challenging grounding and real-world scenarios) highlights its robustness and broad applicability. Its training-free nature further simplifies integration into existing models.
- **Practical Implications:** The framework's compatibility with standard KV caching and FlashAttention ensures its practicality for current hardware and software ecosystems. Furthermore, its ability to accelerate training opens avenues for reducing the computational burden of developing and fine-tuning large multi-modal models.
- **Addressing Limitations of Prior Work:** By adopting a two-stage approach informed by empirical analysis, VScan directly addresses the limitations of single-stage token reduction methods, which often struggled with preserving comprehensive visual details or pruning at sub-optimal LLM layers.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge an inherent trade-off between efficiency and accuracy. While VScan significantly reduces inference costs, aggressive token pruning may still distort visual information and lead to degraded performance, particularly on challenging tasks requiring fine-grained understanding or complex compositional reasoning.

In conclusion, VScan contributes a well-analyzed and empirically supported method for making large vision-language models more efficient and accessible, potentially accelerating their adoption and impact across various application domains requiring multi-modal understanding.
