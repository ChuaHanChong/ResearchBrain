# VScan: Rethinking Visual Token Reduction for Efficient Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VScan introduces an empirically-guided, two-stage framework for reducing visual tokens in large vision-language models, which combines global/local visual scans with token merging and text-aware pruning at intermediate LLM layers. The method achieves up to 2.91 × prefilling speedup on LLaVA-NeXT-7B while retaining over 95% of original performance across diverse benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) incur significant computational and memory costs due to processing thousands of visual tokens, leading to quadratic complexity in self-attention mechanisms and limiting real-time deployment.
- Prior visual token reduction methods often rely on single-stage pruning strategies, which can overlook critical visual information or prune at suboptimal stages of the LVLM pipeline.
- There was a lack of systematic empirical understanding regarding how visual tokens are processed throughout both the visual encoding and language decoding stages of LVLMs.

## Method

- Conducted an in-depth empirical analysis to understand the evolution of visual information in the visual encoder (local to global attention) and cross-modal interactions in the LLM's decoding layers.
- Proposed a ==two-stage, training-free visual token reduction framework== that first reduces visual redundancy during encoding via ==complementary global and local scans with token merging==, then reduces textual irrelevance by ==pruning at intermediate LLM layers==.
- Designed the framework to be compatible with diverse LVLM architectures and existing hardware-aware optimizations like FlashAttention, without requiring additional fine-tuning.

## Results

- Achieved significant efficiency gains, including up to **2.91×** prefilling speedup and **2.05×** overall inference speedup on LLaVA-NeXT-7B, with only a **1.4%** performance decline at an **11%** visual token retention rate.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art baselines across **16** image and video understanding benchmarks, retaining **96.7%** of LLaVA-1.5's performance at an **88.9%** token reduction (**4.0%** better than VisionZip).
- Maintained robust performance on challenging referring grounding tasks and video understanding benchmarks, demonstrating effective KV cache compression and full compatibility with FlashAttention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shallow layers of the visual encoder retain fine-grained local visual details, while deeper layers capture broader global context, necessitating a multi-faceted approach to visual token selection.
> - Early layers of the LLM exhibit strong positional biases towards visual tokens and weak cross-modal interaction, whereas middle layers show reduced bias and mature cross-modal integration, making them ideal for text-aware pruning.
> - A combination of global token selection from deep visual encoder layers and local token selection from shallow layers, along with similarity-based token merging, effectively preserves comprehensive visual information.
