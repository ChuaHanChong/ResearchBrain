# Physically Plausible Full-Body Hand-Object Interaction Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> A physics-based reinforcement learning method that synthesizes full-body dexterous hand-object interaction in simulation. Decoupled body and hand skill priors are pretrained adversarially from motion capture, then a high-level hand-object policy acts inside both latent spaces under a combined adversarial style and grasp-plus-trajectory task reward. It approaches, grasps unseen objects and carries them along target trajectories, reaching 0.79 grasp success versus 0.13 for kinematic baselines.

## Problem

- Prior human-object interaction work covers only isolated stages: approaching and grasping, or post-grasp manipulation, never the whole approach-grasp-manipulate sequence physically.
- Purely data-driven kinematic synthesis must learn physical constraints from data alone, producing foot skating, ground penetration and hand-object interpenetration artifacts.
- Physics-based character control either handles coarse whole-body scene interaction like sitting or carrying boxes, or isolated dexterous hand grasping without body motion.

## Method

- Two adversarially trained ==low-level skill priors==, one for body and one for detached hand, each decoding a latent skill vector into joint targets driven by ==PD controllers==.
- A ==high-level hand-object interaction policy== predicting body and hand latents, conditioned on object pose, static grasp reference distances, contact targets, waypoint offsets and a task phase variable.
- A hand-object reward mixing dual body and hand ==discriminator== style terms with position, orientation, contact, trajectory rewards, plus target guidance auxiliary loss and two-stage fixed-then-free object training.

## Results

- In the approaching phase grasp success reaches **0.79** versus **0.13** for the kinematic baseline and **0.29** for motion capture ground truth, with interpenetration volume **0.11** against **1.97** cubic centimetres.
- In manipulation success is **0.64** versus **0.20** for the intent-driven baseline, foot skating **0.9%** versus **8.0%**, and **0.85** of unseen trajectory targets reached on the object test split.
- Ablations show removing prior decoupling collapses performance to zero success; decoupling alone gives **0.55**, adding two-stage training **0.77**, and target guidance **0.79** success with **0.85** targets reached.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating coarse body and fine finger skill spaces before joint training prevents latent mode collapse, where a single unified prior drowns out small finger movements entirely.
> - Simulation in the loop can outperform its own training data on physical metrics, correcting motion capture noise such as floating, penetration and skating.
> - Contact ratio poorly reflects grasp quality, since interpenetrating kinematic outputs score high while legitimate fingertip pinch grasps score low; simulation stability is the better proxy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the challenge of synthesizing realistic and physically plausible human-object interactions, a fundamental area with implications for human-computer interaction, robotics, animation, and augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR). The field broadly comprises two main approaches: data-driven (kinematics-based) and physics-based methods.

Prior data-driven methods have shown progress in modeling specific aspects of human-object interactions. Some focus on the approaching and grasping phases, such as GOAL and SAGA, while others address object manipulation after grasping, like IMoS. FLEX optimizes hand and body pose priors for diverse full-body grasping. However, these data-driven techniques often rely heavily on motion capture (mocap) data, which can lead to physical artifacts like foot-skating, interpenetration between body parts and objects, or unnatural movements due to the inherent limitations and noise in the training data. They learn physical constraints implicitly from data, which may not always generalize well or enforce strict physical plausibility.

Conversely, physics-based human motion synthesis leverages physics simulations and reinforcement learning (RL) to explicitly enforce physical constraints, thereby mitigating the artifacts observed in purely data-driven approaches. Existing physics-based works have explored character control [3, 15, 28, 31–34, 37–39, 51] and coarser object interactions, such as carrying a box or sitting. Other efforts have focused on dexterous hand grasping in isolation. While these physics-based methods produce physically robust motions, they often neglect either the full-body context or fine-grained dexterous hand manipulation. For example, learns coarse object interaction, while focuses on isolated hand-object interactions.

This work integrates these two lines of research. The authors propose the first physics-based method that simultaneously achieves full-body control and dexterous hand-object interactions throughout the entire process of approaching, grasping, and manipulating an object. By combining the strengths of physics simulation and hierarchical reinforcement learning, it aims to overcome the limitations of both purely data-driven methods (lack of physical plausibility) and existing physics-based methods (limited scope, either full-body without dexterity or dexterous without full-body context). Table 1 in the paper positions this method as unique in covering all aspects: full-body, whole interaction, and dexterous grasping, under a physics-based paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a physics-based method for synthesizing dexterous hand-object interactions within a full-body context, covering the complete interaction sequence. This entails generating motions where a human character approaches an object, performs a precise grasp, and subsequently manipulates the object by following a specified trajectory.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several identified challenges and limitations in the existing research landscape:

1. **Lack of Comprehensive Physics-Based Full-Body Grasping:** Current methods either focus on isolated phases of human-object interaction (e.g., approaching or manipulation) or employ data-driven techniques that can yield physically implausible motions. A comprehensive physics-based approach that addresses the entire interaction process, from initial approach to dexterous grasping and subsequent manipulation, was previously absent.
2. **Mitigating Data-Driven Artifacts:** Purely data-driven approaches often suffer from artifacts such as foot-skating, interpenetration between body parts and objects, and unnatural movements because physical constraints are learned implicitly from potentially noisy or limited motion capture data. The authors aim to leverage physics simulation and reinforcement learning to explicitly enforce these constraints, thereby generating more natural and physically plausible behaviors.
3. **Complex Skill Integration:** Synthesizing dexterous grasps with full-body control is inherently challenging. It requires the simultaneous learning and coordination of multiple complex tasks, including maintaining body balance, performing natural body movements towards an object, achieving precise finger control for a stable grasp, and executing object manipulation tasks. Existing unified training setups for such complex, multi-scale movements can lead to issues like mode collapse, where fine-grained finger movements are overshadowed by coarse body movements.
4. **Enabling Flexible and Controllable Synthesis:** The ability to generate motions for unseen objects and follow flexibly definable target trajectories is crucial for practical applications. The method aims to provide a framework where the synthesized interactions are adaptable to various scenarios and user-defined objectives.
5. **Applications in Relevant Fields:** The ability to simulate and understand such human-object interactions has broad applications in human-computer interaction (HCI), robotics (e.g., training robots for dexterous tasks), animation (for realistic character behavior), and AR/VR environments (for immersive and believable virtual interactions).

In pursuit of these objectives, the research proposes a hierarchical framework to decouple and manage the complexity, combined with a novel reward function to guide learning towards natural and stable interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method utilizes a hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) framework within a physics simulation environment (Isaac Gym) to synthesize physically plausible full-body hand-object interactions. The approach is structured in two main stages: pre-training of low-level skill priors and training of a high-level hand-object interaction policy.

#### 3.1. Simulation Environment

The human character model is based on SMPL-X, excluding eyeballs and jaw, with 57 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) for body joints and 48 DoF for right-hand fingers, totaling 105 DoF. Joints are actuated using proportional-derivative (PD) controllers. Collision geometries of rigid body meshes are approximated (e.g., with boxes, cylinders, capsules) to reduce computational complexity, except for ankles and feet. Object meshes are decimated for speed. The state space `s` includes human pose and velocity, object pose and velocity, and net contact forces `f` on body joints, object, and table.

#### 3.2. Pre-Training of Body and Hand Priors

To manage the complexity of full-body and dexterous hand control, the training of body and hand movements is decoupled. This prevents mode collapse and allows for separate learning of coarse body movements and fine-grained finger control. Each prior is a policy `π(a | ϕ(s), z)` conditioned on extracted state features `ϕ(s)` and a latent skill vector `z`. Priors are trained using a reward function that combines a motion imitation objective (via an adversarial discriminator `D`) and an unsupervised skill discovery objective (via an encoder `q` to recover `z`).

- **Hand Prior:** A policy `πh(ah | ϕh(s), zh)` controls wrist and finger joints. Its features `ϕh(s)` include local hand joint rotations `θh`, angular velocities `˙θh`, and wrist-relative 3D finger joint positions `xh`. During training, the hand is detached from the body and its global position is fixed. The discriminator `Dh` uses a pruned set of fingertip positions `xD_h`.
- **Body Prior:** A policy `πb(ab | ϕb(s), zb, tb, th)` controls all body joints except the hands. It is goal-conditioned on 3D target positions for the root (`tb`) and wrist (`th`). Features `ϕb(s)` include root-relative body joint rotations `θb` and velocities `˙θb`, 3D joint positions `xb` and velocities `˙xb` (excluding root), root height `hb`, and root linear velocity `˙tb`. An auxiliary reward based on these targets is used during high-level policy training. The body prior's reward function also includes a trajectory reward (`rb_ξ`) to reach sampled root and wrist targets, and a regularization reward (`rb_reg`) to prevent fast wrist movements.

#### 3.3. Training of Hand-Object Interaction Policy

After pre-training, a high-level policy `πho` is trained to orchestrate full-body object interactions by predicting latent vectors (`zb`, `zh`) for the pre-trained body and hand priors. This policy also predicts auxiliary position targets (`˜tb`, `˜th`) for the root and wrist.

- **Hand-Object Features `ϕho(s, Ψ, ξ)`:** This encompasses task-relevant information:
 - Object pose and velocity (`To`, `˙To`) relative to the root.
 - Grasp metrics (`gx`, `gθ`, `gc`) derived from a static hand pose reference `Ψ` (e.g., distance between current and target hand joint positions/orientations, contact targets).
 - Trajectory guidance (`gξ`): Distance to the next root and wrist waypoints from the target trajectory `ξ`.
 - Contact forces (`fh`) on hand joints, object, and table.
 - Distance between wrist joint and table (`xtab`).
 - Phase variable (`η`): A continuous value indicating the progress of the task (e.g., distance to object, contact, lifted object).
- **Hand-Object Reward Function `rHO`:** This function combines task and style rewards:
 - **Task Reward `rT`:** Incentivizes stable grasping and trajectory following. It includes:
 - Position reward (`rx`): Guides hand and finger joints to target 3D positions from `Ψ`.
 - Orientation reward (`rθ`): Aligns hand and finger joint orientations with `Ψ`.
 - Contact reward (`rc`): Promotes target contacts (based on `Ψ`), stable contact forces (`fc_force`), and avoids unwanted table contact (`rc_tab`).
 - Trajectory reward (`rξ`): Guides the root and wrist towards the targets in `ξ`, and includes a component (`αs Nsuccess`) that rewards reaching successive targets.
 - Regularization reward (`rreg`): Penalizes object linear velocity and hand jerk.
 - **Style Reward `rS`:** Uses discriminator predictions from both the body (`Db`) and hand (`Dh`) priors to encourage natural, motion-capture-like movements.
- **Target Guidance:** To ensure robust trajectory following, during training, the policy's conditioning on the target trajectory (`ξ`) is alternated between ground-truth and its own predicted targets (`˜ξ`). An auxiliary objective `Lξ = ||ξ - ˜ξ||^2_2` is used to regularize this prediction. This mechanism is applied after the object has been grasped.

#### 3.4. Data and Training Details

The GRAB dataset is used for training and evaluation, focusing on right-handed grasps. Subject-specific GRAB data is converted to a neutral SMPL-X model. Hand pose references are refined using ContactOpt. Target trajectories are extracted from mocap data. A two-stage training procedure is employed for the high-level policy: initially, the object is fixed to the surface to learn stable grasping, then it becomes non-stationary to learn lifting and trajectory following. Random noise is applied to hand pose references, trajectories, and initial object/table positions to enhance robustness and prevent overfitting. Gaussian smoothing is applied as a post-processing step during evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both quantitative and qualitative evaluations, comparing the proposed physics-based method against kinematics-based baselines in two phases: approaching and grasping, and manipulation after grasping.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

The method was evaluated against GOAL for the approaching phase and IMoS for the manipulation phase, using metrics from prior works. The results are summarized in Table 2 of the paper.

- **Physical Plausibility:** The proposed method demonstrated superior physical plausibility compared to both baselines.
 - **Ground Distance (GD):** Achieved significantly lower GD (2.1mm approaching, 1.8mm/1.9mm manipulation) compared to GOAL (6.8mm) and IMoS (6.1mm ground truth for reference). This indicates reduced instances of floating above ground or ground penetration. The slight non-zero GD in SMPL-X space is attributed to rigid body approximation in simulation; it equates to 0.0 directly in the physics engine.
 - **Foot Skating (FS):** Exhibited considerably less foot skating (5.7% approaching, 0.9%/1.2% manipulation) than GOAL (14.4%) and IMoS (8.0%).
 - **Interpenetration Volume (IV) and Depth (ID):** Showed substantially lower IV (0.11 cm³ approaching, 0.22/0.18 cm³ manipulation) and ID (1.1mm approaching, 2.7mm/2.9mm manipulation) compared to GOAL (1.97 cm³, 5.3mm) and IMoS (5.07 cm³, 6.8mm). This indicates reduced occurrences of hand-object interpenetration.
 - Notably, the method often displayed better physics-based properties even compared to the ground-truth motion capture data, suggesting it can correct artifacts inherent in mocap and labeling noise.
- **Grasp Success Rate:** The method achieved significantly higher grasp success rates (0.79 approaching, 0.64/0.79 manipulation) in the physics simulation compared to the baselines (0.13 for GOAL, 0.20 for IMoS). This metric reflects the stability of the generated grasps.
- **Trajectory Targets Reached (TTR):** In the manipulation phase, the method successfully reached a high proportion of trajectory targets (0.65 for S10 test set, 0.85 for GOAL test set), demonstrating its ability to follow diverse, unseen trajectories. This metric is exclusive to this method as baselines do not support explicit trajectory following.
- **Contact Ratio (CR):** The method showed a lower CR (0.026) than GOAL (0.128) in the approaching phase but a comparable CR (0.053/0.055) to IMoS (0.057) in manipulation. The authors discuss that CR may not directly correlate with grasp quality, as different grasp types (e.g., pinch) naturally involve fewer contacts, and baselines sometimes show high CR due to interpenetration.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative results (Figure 3 and 4) illustrate the method's ability to generate natural-looking full-body hand-object interaction sequences. The motions demonstrate less hand-object interpenetration and ground penetration compared to kinematics-based baselines, reinforcing the quantitative findings on physical plausibility.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study (Table 3) confirmed the contribution of key components:
- **Decoupling of Body and Hand Priors:** This was identified as a critical component. Training a unified full-body prior directly led to mode collapse in the latent space, preventing the learning of the full-body grasping task.
- **Two-Stage Training Procedure:** This significantly improved performance, allowing the policy to first stabilize the grasp before focusing on trajectory following.
- **Target Guidance:** Explicitly conditioning on target positions and using an auxiliary training objective further enhanced grasping success and trajectory following performance.

#### 4.4. Generalization

The method demonstrated generalization to unseen objects from the GOAL test set (Table 5). However, it encountered difficulties in grasping very large objects that require the fingers to be fully stretched, suggesting a limitation in handling extreme object sizes within its current learned skill space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a notable advancement in the field of human motion synthesis and physically simulated character control, specifically for complex human-object interactions. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **First Physics-Based Full-Body Dexterous Grasping:** The paper introduces the first method to achieve physics-based full-body dexterous grasping that covers the entire interaction sequence—from approaching an object to grasping it and subsequently manipulating it along a specified trajectory. This addresses a long-standing gap in the literature, where previous physics-based methods either focused on coarser full-body movements or isolated dexterous hand interactions.
2. **Enhanced Physical Plausibility and Realism:** By leveraging physics simulation and reinforcement learning, the method generates motions that are demonstrably more physically plausible and natural than those produced by kinematics-based data-driven approaches. It effectively mitigates common artifacts such as foot-skating, hand-object interpenetration, and ground penetration, which are frequently observed in purely data-driven synthesis. This leads to higher quality and more believable synthetic human behaviors.
3. **Robust and Controllable Motion Synthesis:** The hierarchical framework, combined with goal-conditioned body priors and an explicit target guidance mechanism, enables robust control over the synthesized motions. The ability to follow flexibly definable wrist and root trajectories allows for diverse interaction scenarios and provides a higher degree of controllability for users or downstream applications.
4. **Decoupled and Hierarchical Learning:** The two-stage hierarchical training scheme, which decouples coarse body movements from fine-grained finger control during pre-training, is crucial for addressing the multi-scale complexity of full-body dexterous interactions. This approach prevents mode collapse and allows for effective learning of distinct skill sets that can then be coordinated by a high-level policy.
5. **Broad Applicability:** The advancements in generating realistic and physically sound human-object interactions have far-reaching implications across multiple domains:
 - **Robotics:** For training service robots to perform dexterous manipulation tasks in human environments, generating realistic human demonstrations, or for safer human-robot collaboration.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and AR/VR:** To create more immersive, interactive, and believable virtual characters and environments, enhancing user experience.
 - **Animation and Game Development:** For automating the generation of complex character animations with realistic physics, reducing manual effort and improving visual quality.
 - **Biomechanics and Ergonomics:** For simulating and analyzing human interactions with tools and environments under physically accurate conditions.

While the work primarily focuses on single-handed grasping, it lays a foundation for future research directions, including conditioning policies on language prompts for more intuitive control, learning from multiple hand reference poses, exploring varying body shapes, and tackling the more complex challenge of physics-based bi-manual full-body grasping. The provision of code and video demonstrations also contributes to transparency and reproducibility within the research community.
