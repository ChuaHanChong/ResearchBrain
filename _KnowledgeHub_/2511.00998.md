# GauDP: Reinventing Multi-Agent Collaboration through Gaussian-Image Synergy in Diffusion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> GauDP, developed by researchers from Sun Yat-sen University, The University of Hong Kong, and others, introduces a multi-agent diffusion policy that integrates 3D Gaussian scene representations with local 2D image features. This framework enables robust collaborative robotic tasks using only decentralized 2D RGB camera inputs, achieving higher average success rates (19.67%) on the RoboFactory benchmark compared to 3D point cloud methods (14.33%).

## Problem

- Multi-agent embodied systems face a dilemma between achieving fine-grained local control from individual observations and maintaining global scene understanding for coordination.
- Existing 2D vision-based imitation learning struggles with robust 3D spatial reasoning for manipulation, while 3D sensor-based methods are costly and require specialized hardware.
- Current multi-agent policies often lead to miscoordination from local views or imprecise control from overly generalized global views.

## Method

- A self-supervised, feed-forward 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) module reconstructs a globally consistent 3D scene representation from decentralized 2D RGB camera inputs, fine-tuned with depth supervision.
- This global 3D context is selectively allocated and fused at a pixel-aligned level with each agent's local 2D image features, creating a 'Gaussian-image synergistic representation'.
- A global diffusion policy uses these fused features to jointly predict actions for all agents, promoting coordinated behavior by modeling inter-agent dependencies.

## Results

- GauDP achieved a **19.67%** average success rate across RoboFactory benchmark tasks, outperforming 2D image-based policies (e.g., DP's **3.67%**) and 3D point cloud-based policies (e.g., DP3's **14.33%**).
- The 3D Gaussian reconstruction module showed significant quantitative improvement over the Noposplat baseline (e.g., PSNR increased from **17.918** to **23.424**).
- Real-robot experiments confirmed practical efficacy, with GauDP achieving higher success in tasks like "Card Box Stacking" (17/30 vs. DP's 11/30) and demonstrating robustness to visual disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust 3D scene understanding for multi-agent collaboration can be achieved with only 2D RGB inputs by explicitly reconstructing a consistent 3D Gaussian field.
> - Pixel-level fusion of global 3D context with local 2D image features is crucial for balancing fine-grained local control with comprehensive global awareness.
> - A global diffusion policy, utilizing the synergistic representation, effectively models inter-agent dependencies, leading to superior coordination compared to independent local policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multi-agent embodied collaboration is critical for deploying robotic systems in complex real-world environments, ranging from industrial automation and surgical robotics to household assistance. A fundamental challenge in this domain is achieving effective coordination among agents that must simultaneously maintain fine-grained local control and a comprehensive understanding of the global environment.

Existing approaches to multi-agent control typically fall into two categories:
1. **Local Observation Aggregation:** Agents fuse their individual, local observations into a shared policy. While providing detailed views for precise manipulation, this often fails to capture a consistent joint collaborative state, leading to miscoordination (e.g., collisions, out-of-sync actions).
2. **Global Observation:** Agents rely on a centralized, holistic view of the entire environment (e.g., a global point cloud). This ensures consistent decision-making but often lacks the high-resolution, agent-specific information necessary for accurate low-level control, resulting in compromised individual agent performance (e.g., biased localization).

The research landscape for robotic imitation learning has seen significant advancements, particularly with generative approaches like **Diffusion Policies (DP)**, which excel at generating high-fidelity action trajectories. While 2D vision-based DPs (e.g., Diffusion Policy) offer convenience, they often struggle with robust 3D spatial reasoning crucial for manipulation. Conversely, 3D input DPs (e.g., 3D Diffusion Policy using point clouds) provide geometric awareness but might lack the detailed textural and semantic cues from RGB images or impose higher sensing requirements.

The broader field of **3D reconstruction from multi-view images** has seen revolutionary progress with techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**. 3DGS, in particular, has emerged as a powerful tool for rapidly reconstructing high-fidelity 3D scenes from multiple views, offering both photorealistic appearance and accurate geometry. However, traditional 3DGS often requires dense input views and significant optimization time, making it challenging for dynamic, real-time robotic scenarios. Recent works, such as Noposplat, have addressed these limitations by enabling feed-forward reconstruction from sparse and unposed views, paving the way for practical integration into robotic systems.

**GauDP's Contribution to the Landscape:**
GauDP positions itself at the intersection of these fields, addressing the critical gap of integrating robust 3D scene understanding into multi-agent embodied policies while relying *only* on decentralized 2D RGB observations. It leverages the strengths of 3DGS for global scene representation and integrates this with diffusion policies.
- **Bridging the Local-Global Gap:** GauDP directly tackles the core dilemma of balancing local precision with global coherence by explicitly reconstructing a globally consistent 3D representation (Gaussian field) from local 2D views and then intelligently distributing this global context back to individual agents.
- **Image-Only 3D Reasoning:** Unlike 3D point cloud-based methods that require specialized sensors, GauDP achieves comparable (or superior) 3D spatial reasoning using only standard 2D RGB cameras, making it highly practical and cost-effective.
- **Synergistic Representation:** The "Gaussian-image synergy" is a novel aspect, where the 3D Gaussian field acts as a unified, view-independent global context that is dynamically reallocated and fused with local 2D image features at a pixel level. This fine-grained fusion is key to its enhanced performance.
- **Scalability:** By using a flexible Gaussian representation, the framework inherently scales to more agents without requiring significant architectural changes, a crucial advantage in multi-agent systems.

In essence, GauDP represents a significant step towards more capable and scalable multi-agent robotic systems by demonstrating that a self-supervised, explicit 3D reconstruction from standard 2D camera feeds can effectively resolve the inherent trade-offs in multi-agent perception and control, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with image-based imitation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of GauDP are:

1. **Address the Local-Global Trade-off in Multi-Agent Collaboration:** The core motivation is to resolve the fundamental challenge in multi-agent embodied systems where agents struggle to simultaneously achieve fine-grained local control (requiring detailed local observations) and comprehensive global scene understanding (requiring a holistic view). Existing approaches either lead to miscoordination (local-only) or imprecise control (global-only). GauDP aims to offer a solution that unifies both.

2. **Enable Scalable and Perception-Aware Imitation Learning from Decentralized RGB Observations:** The authors aim to develop a method that allows multiple agents to learn complex collaborative tasks through imitation, using only standard 2D RGB camera inputs. This is crucial for practical deployment as it avoids the need for specialized 3D sensors (like LiDAR or depth cameras) and simplifies data collection. The system should also be scalable, meaning its performance should ideally not degrade drastically as the number of agents increases.

3. **Construct a Globally Consistent and Spatially Detailed Scene Representation:** A key objective is to create a robust 3D representation of the environment that is consistent across all agents' viewpoints and provides sufficient spatial detail for accurate manipulation tasks. This representation should serve as a shared "understanding" of the environment, facilitating coordinated actions.

4. **Facilitate Adaptive Querying of Task-Critical Features:** Beyond just constructing a global 3D model, the system needs a mechanism for individual agents to effectively utilize this shared knowledge. The goal is to allow each agent to adaptively query and integrate task-critical features from the global representation into its local decision-making process, ensuring both coordination and individual precision.

5. **Achieve Performance Comparable to 3D Sensor-Based Methods with Only 2D Inputs:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate that an image-based approach, augmented with intelligent 3D reconstruction, can match or even surpass the performance of methods that directly utilize explicit 3D inputs (like point clouds), thereby showcasing the power of synergistic representations.

By achieving these objectives, GauDP seeks to "reinvent multi-agent collaboration" by providing a novel, perception-aware framework that overcomes current limitations in visual grounding and coordination, thereby enabling more robust and generalizable robotic policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GauDP introduces a novel **Gaussian-image synergistic representation** within a diffusion policy framework to facilitate scalable, perception-aware imitation learning for multi-agent collaborative systems. The methodology can be broken down into three main stages: Global Context Reconstruction, Global Context Allocation, and Policy Learning with Pixel-level Synergy.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and Diffusion Policy (DP)

- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** GauDP leverages 3DGS, which represents a 3D scene as a collection of anisotropic Gaussian primitives. Each Gaussian is defined by a mean position, scaling vector, rotation (quaternion), opacity, and color. 3DGS offers efficient, differentiable rasterization, allowing for optimization by minimizing discrepancies between rendered images and ground-truth observations across multiple views. The key insight used is that multi-view consistency ensures a geometrically faithful 3D reconstruction.
- **Diffusion Policy (DP):** DP formulates action generation as a conditional denoising diffusion process. Given past observations, a denoising network learns to iteratively transform a noisy action sequence into a clean, context-aware action trajectory. GauDP integrates its novel contextual features into this DP framework.

#### 3.2. Global Context Reconstruction

The core of GauDP's approach is to build a globally consistent, view-independent 3D representation from decentralized 2D RGB observations.
- **Motivation:** Traditional 3DGS requires dense views and lengthy scene-specific optimization, making it impractical for dynamic embodied scenarios. GauDP needs a rapid, feed-forward reconstruction.
- **Noposplat Adaptation:** The framework adopts **Noposplat**, a feed-forward network designed to reconstruct 3D Gaussian representations directly from sparse and unposed views. This pretrained model is then fine-tuned using multi-view observations collected from the specific robotic manipulation scenarios, ensuring relevance to the downstream task.
- **Architecture (Figure 2b):**
 - **Multi-view Encoding:** Each raw RGB image from an agent's perspective is independently encoded by a shared-weight Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder.
 - **Cross-View Fusion:** The per-view features are then fed into a cross-view ViT decoder, which uses cross-attention layers to fuse information across different perspectives, creating a unified understanding of the scene.
 - **Gaussian Parameter Prediction:** A dedicated head predicts the parameters (mean, scale, rotation, opacity, color) of a set of 3D Gaussians for each pixel based on these fused features.
- **Self-supervised Fine-tuning with Depth Supervision:**
 - The reconstruction process is supervised in a self-supervised manner using a reconstruction loss (`L_rec`).
 - `L_rec` consists of an RGB reconstruction loss (`L_rgb`) between the rendered images (from the predicted Gaussians) and the input ground-truth images across all views.
 - Crucially, an additional **depth supervision loss (`L_depth`)** is introduced during fine-tuning. By rendering a synthetic depth map from the predicted Gaussians and comparing it to ground-truth depth maps, the model is geometrically constrained to recover more accurate 3D structures.
 - **Pose-Free at Inference:** Importantly, while depth maps and camera poses are used during fine-tuning to establish robust 3D reconstruction, the framework *relies solely on multi-view RGB observations* during policy training and inference, making it lightweight and pose-free in deployment.

#### 3.3. Global Context Allocation and Pixel-level Synergy

Once the globally consistent 3D Gaussian field is reconstructed, GauDP focuses on effectively integrating this information with each agent's local perspective for decision-making.
- **Selective Dispatch Mechanism:** Instead of feeding the entire global context to every agent, GauDP selectively dispatches only the subset of 3D Gaussians that are relevant to each agent's viewpoint. These Gaussians have already benefited from multi-view information fusion during their reconstruction, effectively providing a distilled, relevant global summary for that specific agent.
- **Pixel-Aligned Fusion:** The selected 3D Gaussians are transformed back into a 2D grid that matches the spatial dimensions of the original local image observation. This ensures **pixel-level alignment**.
- **Fusion Module:** These global context features are then concatenated with the agent's local image features and passed through a lightweight convolutional fusion module. This module learns to combine the complementary strengths of the high-resolution local perception (from the original image) and the spatially coherent global understanding (from the Gaussians).
- **Policy Input:** The resulting fused features, which now contain both local and globally consistent 3D information, are fed into a standard Diffusion Policy via cross-attention to predict the agent's future action sequence.

#### 3.4. Multi-Agent Policy

GauDP employs a **Global Diffusion Policy** to jointly predict actions for all agents. This design explicitly models inter-agent dependencies through the unified representation, reducing redundant or conflicting actions and promoting coordinated behavior, as opposed to independent local policies.

In summary, GauDP innovatively uses a self-supervised 3DGS-based reconstruction module to generate a global, consistent 3D context from decentralized 2D inputs. This context is then intelligently distributed and fused at a fine-grained, pixel-aligned level with each agent's local observations before being fed into a diffusion policy, thereby enabling robust and coordinated multi-agent collaboration with only RGB cameras.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GauDP was evaluated on the RoboFactory benchmark, a suite of diverse multi-arm manipulation tasks designed to assess multi-agent coordination and collaboration. The evaluation covered tasks with 2, 3, and 4 robotic arms, measuring success rates.

#### 4.1. Reconstruction Results

- **Quantitative Improvement:** The fine-tuned 3D Gaussian reconstruction model (part of GauDP) significantly outperforms the pretrained Noposplat baseline. Metrics show substantial improvements: PSNR increased from 17.918 to 23.424, SSIM from 0.580 to 0.779, and LPIPS decreased from 0.492 to 0.148. These metrics confirm a higher accuracy in 3D reconstruction.
- **Qualitative Fidelity:** Visualizations demonstrate that GauDP produces significantly sharper, more detailed, and geometrically faithful reconstructions. It consistently yields high-fidelity renderings across both reference and novel views, with clearly defined object boundaries and accurate robot arm positions, contrasting with the blurry and distorted results of the pretrained model.

#### 4.2. Multi-Agent Manipulation Performance

- **Superiority over Image-based Baselines:** GauDP consistently and significantly outperforms existing 2D diffusion policies (DP, 2D Dense Policy) across all tasks. For instance, in "Lift Barrier" (2 arms), GauDP achieved a 72% success rate, dramatically higher than DP's 9% or 2D Dense Policy's 3%. In "Align Camera" (3 arms), GauDP reached 26%, while others were 0-3%. This highlights GauDP's superior ability to model geometry-aware visual representations and achieve better spatial understanding.
- **Competitive with 3D Point Cloud-based Baselines:** Despite using only 2D RGB inputs, GauDP achieves performance comparable to, and often surpasses, point-cloud-based diffusion policies (DP3, 3D Dense Policy). For example, in "Lift Barrier," GauDP's 72% success rate far exceeds DP3's 31%. In "Align Camera," GauDP's 26% is better than DP3's 18%. While GauDP lags in some tasks like "Place Food" (15% vs DP3's 25%), its overall average success rate of 19.67% across all tasks is the highest, significantly outperforming DP3's 14.33%. This demonstrates that explicit 3D reconstruction from images can effectively compensate for the lack of direct 3D sensor input.
- **Scalability:** The results show GauDP's strong scalability; it maintains robust performance even as the number of agents increases (2, 3, or 4 arms), a key advantage for complex multi-agent systems.

#### 4.3. Training and Inference Efficiency

- GauDP exhibits slightly longer training times (6.5 GPU hours for "Lift Barrier") compared to DP (4.8h) and DP3 (2.5h) due to its additional geometry-aware modules.
- However, its inference speed (1.28 FPS) remains comparable to DP (1.49 FPS) and DP3 (1.57 FPS), indicating that the complexity of the global context reconstruction does not introduce significant real-time bottlenecks during deployment.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

- Real-robot experiments on tasks like "Card Box Stacking," "Card Box Handover," and "Grab Roller" confirm GauDP's practical efficacy. It consistently outperforms standard DP, particularly in tasks requiring precise spatial coordination (e.g., 17/30 vs. 11/30 for Card Box Stacking success, and 19/30 vs. 14/30 for Card Box Handover success).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Coordinate System:** Using local camera-coordinate parameterization for Gaussians performed better than a unified world-coordinate system, suggesting that preserving agent-centric spatial relationships is crucial and avoids alignment errors.
- **Fusion Strategies:** Fine-grained, pixel-level fusion of global and local context significantly outperformed coarse, feature-level concatenation. This highlights the importance of spatial alignment and granular cross-modal reasoning.
- **Role of Image and Gaussian:** Both image input and Gaussian representation are essential. Removing either (e.g., "w/o Image" or "w/o Gaussian") led to substantial performance drops. Images provide appearance cues, while Gaussians offer global geometric context; their synergy is critical.
- **Global vs. Local Policy:** A Global Diffusion Policy (jointly predicting actions) significantly outperformed a Local Diffusion Policy (independent control), reaffirming the importance of modeling inter-agent dependencies.
- **Model Size Normalization:** When DP was scaled up (LargeDP) to match GauDP's parameter count, GauDP still achieved higher average success rates. This confirms that GauDP's performance gain is due to its Gaussian-based global perception framework, not merely increased model capacity.

#### 4.6. Robustness Evaluation

- GauDP demonstrated strong robustness to visual disturbances, maintaining the highest success rate (50% for "Grab Roller") even under challenging conditions with random lighting variations and distractor objects, surpassing DP (20%) and DP3 (46%). This attributes to the pre-training on diverse datasets and depth-based constraints in Gaussian estimation.

In conclusion, the results strongly validate GauDP's core hypothesis: a Gaussian-image synergistic representation effectively addresses the limitations of purely 2D or 3D inputs in multi-agent collaboration. It achieves superior performance by seamlessly integrating fine-grained local control with comprehensive global scene understanding, using only standard RGB camera feeds, and demonstrating robust scalability and real-world applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GauDP presents a significant advancement in multi-agent embodied collaboration, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts across various domains:

1. **Breaking the 2D vs. 3D Bottleneck for Multi-Agent Systems:** One of the most significant impacts is demonstrating that robust 3D scene understanding, crucial for precise manipulation and coordination, can be effectively achieved using *only* 2D RGB camera inputs. This bypasses the need for specialized and often costly 3D sensors (like depth cameras or LiDAR) that many 3D-based methods require. This makes advanced multi-agent robotics more accessible, economical, and easier to deploy in diverse real-world settings.

2. **Enhanced Multi-Agent Coordination and Scalability:** By constructing a globally consistent 3D Gaussian representation and adaptively distributing this context back to individual agents, GauDP effectively solves the long-standing dilemma between fine-grained local control and comprehensive global awareness. This leads to dramatically improved coordination, reduced collisions, and more reliable task completion in complex multi-agent scenarios. The inherent scalability of the Gaussian representation also means the approach can generalize to systems with varying numbers of agents without fundamental architectural changes.

3. **Foundational Shift in Perception for Embodied AI:** GauDP revisits the foundational perspective that accurate action prediction fundamentally relies on robust 3D scene understanding. It argues that implicit 3D reasoning from 2D images, or coarse aggregation without 3D priors, is insufficient. By explicitly reconstructing a coherent 3D representation and integrating it at a pixel-level, GauDP establishes a stronger visual grounding for decision-making, which is critical for complex physical interactions.

4. **Plug-and-Play Enhancement for Existing Policy Learning Pipelines:** The framework's design allows for seamless integration into existing policy learning pipelines that rely on 2D inputs. This "plug-and-play" capability means that current methods can be enhanced with stronger 3D understanding and localization capabilities without requiring a complete overhaul or additional hardware. This accelerates the adoption of more advanced perception modules in existing robotic systems.

5. **Robustness to Visual Disturbances:** The use of a pre-trained and fine-tuned Gaussian estimation module, augmented with multi-view depth constraints, naturally improves the model's resilience to variations in lighting and the presence of distractor objects. This robustness is vital for real-world deployments where environmental conditions are rarely pristine.

6. **Future Directions and Broader Applicability:**
 - **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** The proposed 3D Gaussian representation, being flexible and capable of encoding semantic features when tokenized, holds significant potential as an input for VLA models. This could bridge the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level physical actions, enabling more intelligent and human-interpretable multi-agent collaboration.
 - **Dynamic Scene Understanding and World Models:** Leveraging Gaussians to improve the representation of dynamic scenes could significantly advance world models for multi-agent environments. This would enable agents to anticipate changes, understand cause-and-effect, and plan actions in dynamic, interactive settings more effectively.
 - **General Embodied Scenarios:** While demonstrated in multi-arm manipulation, the principles of GauDP are broadly applicable to various embodied scenarios, including mobile robotics, human-robot interaction, and complex assembly tasks, where detailed 3D scene reconstruction and consistent spatial awareness are paramount.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges limitations, including the current reliance on a limited number and diversity of available embodied tasks for fine-tuning, which can restrict generalizability. Future work will focus on designing more generalizable Gaussian-based networks, incorporating multi-modal inputs and semantic features for richer scene understanding, and extending the framework to dynamic scenes and world models.

In conclusion, GauDP not only offers a highly effective solution for multi-agent collaboration but also contributes a fresh perspective on how robots can perceive and interact with their 3D environments using readily available 2D visual data. Its impact is poised to accelerate the development of more capable, scalable, and adaptable robotic systems across numerous real-world applications.
