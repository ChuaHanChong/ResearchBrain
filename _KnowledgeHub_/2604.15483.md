# $π_{0.7}$: a Steerable Generalist Robotic Foundation Model with Emergent Capabilities

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Physical Intelligence developed π0.7, a 5-billion-parameter generalist robotic foundation model. This model utilizes a diversified prompting strategy, incorporating language instructions, episode metadata, and generated subgoal images, to achieve strong out-of-the-box performance and compositional generalization across diverse tasks and robot platforms.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack broad generalization, requiring task-specific fine-tuning for new problems and struggling with compositional skill recombination.
- Integrating large, heterogeneous datasets, especially those containing suboptimal or non-robot data, without degrading overall performance remains a challenge for generalist robotic policies.
- Traditional language commands provide "what to do" but often lack the precise "how to do it" information necessary for complex robotic manipulation tasks, leading to ambiguity.

## Method

- The **π0.7** model, building on prior VLA architectures, enhances ==multimodal context conditioning== by integrating ==subtask instructions==, ==multi-view subgoal images== (generated by a learned world model), and ==episode metadata== (e.g., quality, speed, mistakes) during training and inference.
- It is trained on an expansive dataset comprising diverse robot demonstrations, autonomous experience, egocentric human videos, and auxiliary non-robot data, employing a ==Knowledge Insulation (KI)== training recipe and ==prompt dropout== for flexibility.
- At runtime, π0.7 can be prompted with desired control modes and episode metadata, with ==subgoal images asynchronously regenerated== to provide richer, spatially grounded objectives.

## Results

- π0.7 (a **5-billion-parameter** generalist robotic foundation model) achieved out-of-the-box performance competitive with or surpassing task-specific fine-tuned specialists on dexterous, long-horizon tasks like espresso making, box building, and laundry folding.
- The model demonstrated superior instruction following in novel environments and for complex referential instructions, exhibiting high absolute success rates across diverse multi-step commands.
- It exhibited strong ==cross-embodiment transfer==, including for dexterous tasks to morphologically different robots (e.g., folding laundry on a bimanual UR5e with no specific training data), sometimes matching human expert performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diversified context conditioning, especially through episode metadata, enables a robotic foundation model to effectively learn from mixed-quality and suboptimal data without compromising high-performance objectives.
> - The integration of generated subgoal images provides robust, spatially grounded objectives that enhance instruction following, allowing the model to overcome dataset biases and interpret complex referential language.
> - Comprehensive training on diverse data sources, coupled with advanced prompting, facilitates strong cross-embodiment transfer and the emergence of novel manipulation strategies adapted to different robot morphologies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies, often termed robotic foundation models or Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, represents a significant area of research in artificial intelligence and robotics. These models aim to imbue robots with capabilities that generalize across diverse tasks, environments, and robot platforms, drawing parallels to the compositional generalization observed in large language models. Previous VLA models, such as π0, π0.5, and π0.6, along with other generalist policies, have made advancements by leveraging pre-trained vision-language models, video generation models, and incorporating architectural components like memory and hierarchical planning. Training datasets for these models commonly include robot demonstration data, sometimes supplemented with web data, egocentric human videos, and autonomous robot experience.

Despite these advancements, prior VLAs have frequently exhibited limitations in their ability to generalize to entirely new tasks or to recombine learned skills in novel ways. They often require task-specific fine-tuning to achieve fluent performance, even on tasks within their training distribution. The challenge in physical intelligence lies in enabling "compositional generalization," where knowledge from diverse datasets can be applied and composed in unforeseen ways to solve novel problems. This work positions itself as an effort to address these limitations, particularly the struggle of previous VLAs with generalization and robust instruction following without specialized post-training. It builds upon existing VLA architectures, notably the π0.6-MEM architecture, by introducing an expanded context conditioning strategy to facilitate learning from more diverse and heterogeneous data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic foundation model, named π0.7, that exhibits strong compositional generalization capabilities, enabling it to perform a wide array of dexterous tasks in various scenarios without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. The authors aim for the model to achieve strong "out-of-the-box" performance, comparable to or exceeding specialized fine-tuned models.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Enhancing Compositional Generalization:** A core motivation is to enable the robot to compose previously learned skills in new ways to solve novel tasks, a capability that has been challenging for prior robotic foundation models. This includes interacting with new appliances or performing tasks in new sequences.
- **Improving Language Following and Generalization:** The authors seek to develop a model that can robustly follow diverse language instructions, including complex, unseen language references and multi-stage commands, in entirely novel environments. This is crucial for enabling flexible human-robot interaction and teaching.
- **Achieving Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** A significant goal is to demonstrate that the model can transfer dexterous skills from one robot embodiment to another without any prior training data on the target robot for that specific task. This addresses the practical challenge of collecting extensive demonstration data for every robot platform and task.
- **Leveraging Diverse and Suboptimal Data:** Traditional VLA training often relies on carefully curated, high-quality demonstration data. A key motivation for π0.7 is to overcome this limitation by effectively learning from a broader spectrum of data, including suboptimal behaviors, failed episodes, autonomous rollouts from prior models, egocentric human videos, and general multimodal internet data, without compromising performance. The challenge here is to prevent such diverse data from averaging modes and producing suboptimal results.
- **Steerability and Context Conditioning:** The central hypothesis is that detailed context conditioning during training can resolve ambiguities in large, diverse datasets and enable precise steering of the model's behavior. The motivation is to explore how multimodal conditioning signals, beyond simple language commands, can provide richer context about "how to do it," not just "what to do."

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to advance robotic learning from a paradigm of task-specific data collection and fine-tuning to one where a generalist model can be flexibly prompted and coached to perform a wide range of new tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The π0.7 model is a 5-billion-parameter Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that builds upon the existing π0.6 architecture and the MEM (Multi-scale Embodied Memory) system. Its methodology focuses on leveraging diverse and heterogeneous datasets through an expanded and multimodal context conditioning strategy during training.

**Model Architecture:**
- **VLM Backbone:** π0.7 utilizes a 4-billion-parameter VLM backbone initialized from Gemma3, which includes a 400-million-parameter vision encoder.
- **Memory System:** It incorporates a MEM-style video history encoder, which processes up to six history frames from multiple cameras (front, two wrist views, optional rear) and compresses them into a fixed number of tokens, enabling the model to retain temporal context.
- **Action Expert:** An 860-million-parameter transformer serves as the action expert, trained to predict continuous actions using a flow matching objective. This expert attends to the VLM backbone activations.
- **Knowledge Insulation (KI):** The model employs the KI training recipe, where the VLM backbone is supervised with FAST tokens, and gradients from the action expert do not flow back into the VLM backbone. This maintains the VLM's stable discrete cross-entropy loss while the action expert learns from its continuous action prediction.

**Diverse Prompting Strategy:**
The core innovation is the expansion of the context (prompt) given to the model, denoted as C_t, which goes beyond conventional language instructions. This multimodal context includes:

- **Subtask Instructions (ˆℓ_t):** In addition to a high-level task description, intermediate, semantic subtask descriptions (e.g., "open the fridge door" for "clean the kitchen") are provided. These can be generated by a high-level policy or a human supervisor during inference, enabling step-by-step coaching.
- **Subgoal Images (g_t):** Multi-view images depicting the desired near-future state of the scene are included. These provide richer, spatially grounded specifications of the objective. At runtime, these are produced by a lightweight world model.
 - **World Model:** The world model (g_ψ) is initialized from BAGEL, a 14B mixture-of-transformers model with web-scale pre-training for image generation and editing. It takes the current observation, subtask instruction, and episode metadata to generate future subgoal images, bridging semantic and physical concepts from diverse data.
- **Episode Metadata (m):** To enable learning from mixed-quality and suboptimal data, various attributes of the training episode are included in the context:
 - **Overall Speed:** Discretized length of the episode in timesteps.
 - **Overall Quality:** A score (1-5) indicating task execution quality.
 - **Mistake:** A binary label indicating if a mistake occurred in an action segment.
 This metadata allows the model to correlate different execution styles and quality levels with target actions and, at test time, be prompted for high-quality, efficient execution.
- **Control Mode (c):** A text identifier (e.g., "joint", "ee") specifies whether joint-level or end-effector actions are used for low-level execution, allowing the model to adapt its control strategy.

**Training Details:**
- **Data Diversity:** The training dataset D is expansive, including human demonstration data across numerous robot platforms (static, mobile, single/bimanual) and environments, autonomous data from policy evaluations, human interventions, open-source robot datasets, egocentric human video data, and auxiliary non-robot web data (object localization, VQA, text-only prediction, video captioning).
- **Suboptimal Data Integration:** Crucially, the dataset heavily incorporates suboptimal robot data, including lower-quality demonstrations and data from prior model evaluations (e.g., RL training rollouts). Episode metadata is essential here to contextualize these varied behaviors.
- **Prompt Dropout:** During training, each component of the prompt (subgoal images, subtask instructions, individual metadata elements) is randomly dropped out with certain probabilities. This ensures the model's flexibility to operate with partial context at test time. Subgoal images are included in 25% of examples, and subtask instructions are dropped 30% of the time when subgoal images are present. Episode metadata is dropped 15% entirely, with individual components dropped 5% of the time.
- **Subgoal Image Training:** The model is trained with a mix of real future images (sampled from 0-4 seconds ahead or end-of-segment) and generated images from the world model to mitigate train-test mismatch.
- **Real-time Action Chunking (RTC):** Training incorporates a version of RTC to generate smooth action trajectories despite inference delays, simulating delays of 0 to 12 timesteps.

**Runtime Prompting and Inference:**
- **Standard Prompting:** At runtime, π0.7 is always prompted with a control mode and episode metadata (e.g., "Quality: 5", "Mistake: false", and task-specific 15th percentile speed).
- **Subtask Instruction Source:** Subtask instructions (ˆℓ_t) are provided either by a learned high-level language policy or a human supervisor.
- **Subgoal Image Refresh:** Subgoal images are regenerated by the world model when the semantic intent (ˆℓ_t) changes or every 4 seconds.
- **Asynchronous Inference:** Visual subgoal and subtask instruction generation occur in separate threads from VLA inference to use the latest available context.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** CFG is optionally applied on episode metadata to guide actions towards desired behaviors (e.g., higher speed) during the flow matching denoising steps.

**Robot Systems:**
The model is evaluated on a variety of robot platforms, including bimanual mobile manipulators, static bimanual manipulators ("BiPi"), and a bimanual UR5e system, as well as single-arm 6 DoF systems. These platforms vary significantly in morphology, kinematics, and dynamics, posing challenges for cross-embodiment transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that π0.7 exhibits a range of emergent capabilities, including strong out-of-the-box performance, broad generalization across instructions and embodiments, and compositional task generalization.

- **Out-of-the-Box Performance on Challenging Tasks:**
 - π0.7 achieved performance competitive with or superior to task-specific RL-finetuned specialist models (e.g., π\*0.6 models) on dexterous tasks such as espresso making, box building, and laundry folding. It matched or exceeded specialists in throughput for diverse laundry folding and box building.
 - It also performed comparably to SFT specialists (trained on π0.6) on various other dexterous tasks, including making a peanut butter sandwich, turning a shirt inside-out, slicing zucchini, peeling vegetables, and taking out trash.
 - Ablation studies confirmed that both episode metadata and the inclusion of autonomous evaluation data during training were critical for π0.7's strong performance, particularly for throughput.
 - π0.7 showed similar or better performance compared to fine-tuned memory specialists (from the MEM paper) on tasks requiring explicit memory, such as "Swap 3 Mugs" and "Scoop Coffee."

- **Instruction Following:**
 - **Broad Instruction Following:** π0.7 significantly outperformed prior models (π0.5 and π0.6) in following diverse, open-ended language instructions across 4 unseen kitchen and 2 unseen bedroom environments, achieving high overall instruction following success rates.
 - **Complex Referential Instructions:** On object re-arrangement tasks, π0.7 improved over prior models on complex instructions that used unusual language or intricate spatial references (e.g., "pick up the fruit on the largest plate"). The use of subgoal images generated by the world model (π0.7 (GC)) further boosted this performance.
 - **Breaking Dataset Biases:** π0.7 demonstrated the ability to follow instructions that contradicted strong dataset biases (e.g., "Reverse Bussing," "Reverse Fridge to Microwave"), a task where prior models struggled. For "Reverse Fridge to Microwave," conditioning on generated subgoal images was critical for success.

- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:**
 - **Object Rearrangement:** For simpler tasks, all models showed some cross-embodiment transfer. However, as the morphological gap between source and target robots increased (e.g., from bimanual UR5e to a smaller static bimanual robot for "Bag In Backpack"), π0.7 maintained strong performance while π0.5 degraded. For the largest embodiment gap (static bimanual to single-arm UR5e for "Shirt Bagging"), π0.7 significantly outperformed prior models.
 - **Dexterous Tasks (Laundry Folding):** π0.7 successfully folded towels and shirts on a bimanual UR5e system, a platform on which no laundry folding data was collected. It exhibited emergent strategies adapted to the target embodiment (e.g., vertical grasps on the UR5e instead of tilted grasps seen in source data). When using generated subgoal images (π0.7 (GC)), performance improved further.
 - **Human-Policy Comparison:** In a zero-shot cross-embodiment setting for shirt folding on the UR5e, π0.7 (GC) achieved task progress and success rates (85.6% task progress, 80% success rate) comparable to highly experienced human teleoperators (90.9% task progress, 80.6% success rate) who were also performing the task on the UR5e for the first time.

- **Compositional Task Generalization:**
 - **New Short-Horizon Tasks:** π0.7 performed several short-horizon tasks (e.g., pressing a French press plunger, scooping rice, wiping office supplies, spinning articulated objects) directly out-of-the-box, even though no specific robot data was collected for these tasks.
 - **Language-Coached Longer-Horizon Tasks:** For complex, unseen multi-stage kitchen tasks (e.g., "Loading an Air Fryer," "Toasting a Bagel"), π0.7 could be "coached" with step-by-step language instructions, achieving higher task progress than prior models, especially with generated subgoal images.
 - **Autonomous Capability Acquisition:** The coaching data could be used to train a high-level language policy, enabling π0.7 to perform these previously unseen, long-horizon tasks fully autonomously with performance matching live human coaching, without additional low-level action data collection.

- **Scaling with Diverse Context and Data:**
 - **Mixed-Quality Data:** In controlled ablations, π0.7 (with metadata) continuously improved its performance on a "Laundry" task even when trained on progressively larger datasets that included lower-quality data. In contrast, π0.7 (without metadata) showed performance degradation with increased mixed-quality data, indicating the metadata's role in effective learning.
 - **Dataset Diversity:** Removing the 20% of data with the highest task diversity from the training set significantly degraded π0.7's performance on short-horizon unseen tasks, demonstrating that the model effectively leverages diverse data to enhance generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of π0.7 represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic foundation models, primarily by demonstrating the efficacy of a diverse multimodal context conditioning strategy for unlocking emergent capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** π0.7 exhibits strong out-of-the-box performance across a wide range of dexterous and complex tasks, often matching or exceeding the performance of specialist models without task-specific fine-tuning. This suggests a step towards more truly general-purpose robotic policies, reducing the need for extensive, domain-specific engineering.
- **Scalability to Diverse and Suboptimal Data:** The model's ability to effectively learn from a mix of high-quality demonstrations, suboptimal autonomous rollouts, and non-robot data sources (human videos, web data) is critical. This approach makes the training process more data-efficient and scalable, as it leverages readily available, unfiltered data, circumventing the laborious and resource-intensive process of meticulous data curation. The metadata prompting mechanism is central to this, allowing the model to "distill" specialist performance and learn from varied behaviors without compromising target performance.
- **Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The demonstration of zero-shot transfer of complex, dexterous skills like laundry folding to morphologically distinct robots (e.g., UR5e) is a significant scientific finding. This capability can revolutionize how new robotic systems are deployed, potentially enabling the transfer of skills from low-cost, easy-to-teleoperate platforms to high-payload industrial arms, where direct human demonstration data collection is challenging and expensive. The emergence of novel, adapted manipulation strategies for the target embodiment further underscores this impact.
- **Compositional Task Generalization and Coaching:** The ability to perform new short-horizon tasks out-of-the-box and, more profoundly, to be "coached" through new long-horizon tasks purely with language, opens up new paradigms for human-robot interaction. This "teach-by-prompting" approach, where coaching data can then be used to train autonomous high-level policies, could drastically reduce the data and expertise required to deploy robots in new scenarios, making them more adaptable to evolving user needs.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a robust framework and empirical evidence for the power of detailed multimodal prompting in robotics. It lays a foundation for future research into more sophisticated context conditioning, improved world models for subgoal generation, and exploration of how such steerable models can be further fine-tuned or adapted with minimal new data (e.g., via reinforcement learning in the test environment).

**Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge that while π0.7 generalizes broadly, zero-shot generalization success rates (60-80%) are typically lower than for in-distribution tasks (>90%). This suggests that future work could focus on leveraging the model's steerability for efficient learning from small amounts of test-time data (e.g., detailed language coaching or autonomous RL). A practical challenge in such large-scale models is definitively distinguishing "seen" from "unseen" tasks, as related skills might implicitly exist in diverse training data. However, the authors argue that the ability to "remix" existing skills into novel combinations is a fundamental aspect of compositional generalization, leading to similar practical benefits as truly novel skill acquisition.
