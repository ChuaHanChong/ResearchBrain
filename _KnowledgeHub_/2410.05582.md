# Gen-Drive: Enhancing Diffusion Generative Driving Policies with Reward Modeling and Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Gen-Drive, developed by researchers from Nanyang Technological University, NVIDIA Research, and Stanford University, introduces a framework for autonomous driving that generates and evaluates diverse future scenarios using diffusion models and a reward model trained on human preferences. The system, fine-tuned with reinforcement learning, improves performance on a closed-loop planning benchmark across metrics like progress, comfort, and rule compliance compared to existing learning-based approaches.

## Problem

- Traditional prediction-based autonomous driving methods struggle with the inherent uncertainty and multi-modality of real-world, dynamic traffic environments.
- Sequential perception-prediction-planning pipelines suffer from error propagation and difficulty in capturing interactive dynamics between ego and surrounding agents.
- Designing robust handcrafted rules or reward functions for complex and diverse driving scenarios is exceptionally challenging and can lead to brittle systems.

## Method

- A diffusion-based Scene Generator creates diverse, consistent future trajectories jointly for all agents, including the ego vehicle, within a traffic scene.
- A Scene Evaluator, consisting of a ==reward model== trained on human preference data curated by a Vision Language Model (GPT-4o), assesses the quality of generated scenarios.
- Reinforcement Learning fine-tunes the scene generator to steer it towards producing driving behaviors that receive high rewards from the learned reward model.

## Results

- Gen-Drive outperforms existing learning-based approaches and competitive baselines on the nuPlan closed-loop planning benchmark across metrics such as progress, comfort, and rule compliance.
- Reinforcement Learning fine-tuning significantly improves the planning performance of the diffusion policy, with 1,000 RL steps achieving optimal results.
- Using the learned reward model for evaluation and RL fine-tuning leads to better planning performance compared to relying solely on human-designed rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting from prediction-based to generation-based planning better handles uncertainty and interactive dynamics by modeling all agents simultaneously.
> - Learning reward functions from human preference data via Vision Language Models offers a scalable alternative to handcrafted rules for evaluating driving scenarios.
> - Reinforcement learning can effectively fine-tune generative diffusion models to optimize for desired behaviors, ensuring generated trajectories are not only realistic but also high-quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Autonomous driving technology faces significant challenges in navigating complex, unpredictable environments where multiple agents interact dynamically. Traditional approaches to autonomous driving planning have relied heavily on prediction-based methods, which attempt to forecast the behavior of other road users before planning the ego vehicle's actions. However, these methods struggle with the inherent uncertainty and multi-modality of real-world driving scenarios.

The research paper "Gen-Drive: Enhancing Diffusion Generative Driving Policies with Reward Modeling and Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning" introduces a novel framework that shifts the autonomous driving paradigm from prediction-based planning to generation-based planning. This paper, authored by researchers from Nanyang Technological University, NVIDIA Research, and Stanford University, represents an important advancement in how autonomous vehicles make decisions in complex traffic environments.

### 2. Limitations of Traditional Approaches

Traditional autonomous driving systems typically follow a sequential pipeline:
1. Perception: Identify objects and map features in the environment
2. Prediction: Forecast the future trajectories of other road users
3. Planning: Determine the ego vehicle's actions based on these predictions

This approach has several significant limitations:

1. **Error Propagation**: Errors in perception or prediction cascade through the system, potentially leading to poor planning decisions.
2. **Uncertainty Handling**: Predicting a single trajectory for each agent fails to account for the multiple possible futures that could unfold.
3. **Interactive Dynamics**: Traditional prediction models often treat other agents as independent entities, failing to capture how they might react to the ego vehicle's actions.
4. **Decision-making Complexity**: Designing handcrafted rules or reward functions that cover all possible driving scenarios is extremely challenging.

### 3. The Gen-Drive Framework

The Gen-Drive framework addresses these limitations by introducing a fundamentally different approach to autonomous driving. Instead of relying on sequential prediction and planning, Gen-Drive generates multiple possible future scenarios jointly for all agents including the ego vehicle, then evaluates these scenarios to select the most appropriate action.

The framework consists of three main components:

1. **Scene Generator**: A diffusion-based model that creates diverse, consistent future trajectories for all agents in the scene
2. **Scene Evaluator**: A reward model trained on human preference data to assess the quality of generated scenes
3. **Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning**: A process to optimize the generator toward producing high-reward driving behaviors

This approach offers several advantages:
- It handles uncertainty by generating multiple potential futures
- It captures interactive dynamics by modeling all agents simultaneously
- It learns reward functions from human preferences rather than relying on handcrafted rules

### 4. Scene Generation with Diffusion Models

At the core of Gen-Drive is a diffusion-based scene generator that creates realistic future scenarios for all agents in a traffic scene.

The scene generator architecture includes:

1. **Query-centric Encoding Transformer**: This component processes the input data, which includes:
 - Vector map information (lane geometries, traffic rules)
 - Object trajectories (historical paths of surrounding vehicles, pedestrians, etc.)
 - Ego's route polylines (intended path of the autonomous vehicle)

2. **Diffusion Denoising Transformer**: Implements the diffusion model that generates trajectories by gradually denoising random noise into coherent future paths.

The diffusion model works by:
1. Starting with pure noise
2. Gradually refining this noise through a series of denoising steps
3. Producing realistic trajectories that respect physical constraints and traffic rules

The model is trained on a large dataset of real-world driving scenarios from the nuPlan dataset, learning to generate plausible future trajectories for all agents. Importantly, it generates these trajectories jointly, capturing potential interactions between agents.

The mathematical formulation of the diffusion process can be represented as:

$$x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\epsilon$$

where $x_t$ is the noisy trajectory at timestep $t$, $x_0$ is the clean trajectory, $\alpha_t$ is a noise schedule, and $\epsilon$ is Gaussian noise.

### 5. Reward Modeling with Vision Language Models

A key innovation in Gen-Drive is its approach to reward modeling. Rather than relying on handcrafted reward functions, which can be brittle and fail to capture the nuances of human driving preferences, Gen-Drive learns a reward model from human preference data.

The process works as follows:

1. The base diffusion model generates pairs of driving scenarios (A and B)
2. These scenarios undergo basic safety checks (collision detection, off-road detection)
3. If the scenarios are significantly different, they are sent to GPT-4o (a Vision Language Model) for evaluation
4. GPT-4o is prompted to judge which scenario demonstrates better driving behavior
5. The resulting preference judgments (A > B, B > A, or A = B) form a dataset for training the reward model

The prompt to GPT-4o includes:
- A front-facing camera image showing the current driving context
- Bird's-eye view (BEV) representations of the two scenarios
- Detailed instructions for evaluating driving behavior

The reward model is trained to predict human preferences using a Bradley-Terry model, which estimates the probability that one scenario is preferred over another:

$$P(A > B) = \frac{e^{r(A)}}{e^{r(A)} + e^{r(B)}}$$

where $r(A)$ and $r(B)$ are the reward scores for scenarios A and B respectively.

This approach scales much better than collecting human judgments for every scenario pair, while still capturing human values and preferences in driving situations.

### 6. Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning

Once the reward model is trained, Gen-Drive employs reinforcement learning (RL) to fine-tune the diffusion model, steering it toward generating trajectories that receive high rewards.

The RL fine-tuning process involves:

1. Generating a batch of scenarios using the current diffusion model
2. Evaluating these scenarios with the reward model
3. Computing policy gradients to update the diffusion model parameters
4. Iterating this process to progressively improve the quality of generated trajectories

The objective function for RL fine-tuning can be expressed as:

$$J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_\theta(x)}[r(x)]$$

where $p_\theta(x)$ is the probability of generating trajectory $x$ under the current policy parameters $\theta$, and $r(x)$ is the reward assigned by the reward model.

This fine-tuning process helps address one limitation of generative models in planning: they might generate realistic but suboptimal trajectories. By using RL to optimize for reward, Gen-Drive ensures that its generated trajectories not only look realistic but also exhibit high-quality driving behavior.

### 7. Experimental Results

The researchers evaluated Gen-Drive on the nuPlan closed-loop planning benchmark, which tests planning algorithms in real-world driving scenarios. The results demonstrate several key findings:

1. **Improved Performance**: Gen-Drive outperforms existing learning-based approaches and competitive baselines across multiple metrics, including:
 - Progress along the route
 - Comfort (smooth acceleration and deceleration)
 - Rule compliance (following traffic rules)
 - Overall driving score

2. **Effectiveness of RL Fine-tuning**: Fine-tuning with reinforcement learning significantly improves the planning performance of the diffusion policy. The experimental results show that 1,000 RL fine-tuning steps achieved optimal performance, with further fine-tuning potentially leading to performance degradation due to overfitting.

3. **Advantage of Learned Rewards**: Using the learned reward model for evaluation or RL fine-tuning leads to better planning performance compared to relying solely on human-designed rewards.

4. **Importance of Modeling Multiple Agents**: Including a sufficient number of surrounding objects (50 in the experiments) in the generation process enhances performance compared to modeling only the ego vehicle, confirming the value of joint interaction reasoning.

The experiments also revealed interesting insights about the learned reward model:
- It prefers smoother trajectories with appropriate speed adjustments
- It rewards proactive behaviors such as yielding to crossing traffic
- It penalizes hesitant or overly conservative driving in clear situations
- It values proper lane-keeping and appropriate handling of lane changes

### 8. Implications and Future Directions

Gen-Drive represents a significant shift in how autonomous driving systems approach decision-making. By generating and evaluating multiple possible futures rather than relying on a single prediction, it better handles the uncertainty inherent in driving environments.

Several important implications and future directions emerge from this research:

1. **Scalability**: The use of Vision Language Models like GPT-4o for preference data curation significantly enhances the scalability of reward modeling, potentially enabling continuous improvement of driving policies.

2. **End-to-End Learning**: Future work could incorporate a perception module directly into the generative framework, creating a fully end-to-end learnable driving system that processes raw sensor inputs.

3. **Real-world Deployment**: While showing promising results in simulation, additional work is needed to bridge the sim-to-real gap for deployment on physical vehicles.

4. **Value Alignment**: The approach of learning rewards from human preferences aligns with the broader goal of ensuring AI systems reflect human values and preferences, a critical consideration for autonomous systems operating in human environments.

5. **Computational Efficiency**: Further research could focus on improving the inference speed of the diffusion model to meet the real-time requirements of autonomous driving.

### 9. Conclusion

Gen-Drive introduces a novel paradigm for autonomous driving that leverages diffusion models for scene generation, reward modeling based on human preferences, and reinforcement learning for policy optimization. By generating multiple possible futures and evaluating them with a learned reward model, this approach better handles the uncertainty and interactive dynamics of complex driving scenarios.

The research demonstrates that combining generative modeling with reinforcement learning and human preference learning can significantly improve the performance of autonomous driving systems. It also highlights the potential of using large language models like GPT-4o to scale up preference data collection, enabling more robust reward modeling.

As autonomous driving technology continues to evolve, approaches like Gen-Drive that can handle uncertainty, learn from human preferences, and reason about interactive dynamics will be essential for creating safe, reliable, and human-like autonomous vehicles.
