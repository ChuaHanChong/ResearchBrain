# Adaptive Vision-Language Grasping via Composable Foundation Priors and Generalizable Grasp Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaRoboVLG is a vision-language grasping framework that decouples task-dependent understanding from physical grasp synthesis through a structured grasp interface of object geometry, contact grasp representations, and grasp types. Composable spatial, cognitive, and temporal foundation-model priors construct and update that interface, while a hand-agnostic base policy converts it into executable poses, reaching 83.3% real-world functional grasping success over 510 cluttered trials.

## Problem

- End-to-end vision-language-action grasp policies entangle perception, reasoning, and grasp generation in one network, so adaptability is capped by training-data coverage, model scale, and compute.
- Existing grasp methods stay task- and hand-specific: new embodiments or task intents demand fresh data collection and full policy retraining rather than modular updates.
- Object-centric cross-hand methods need costly nonconvex optimization, while scene-centric clutter methods couple both grasp generation and grasp scoring to one hand embodiment.

## Method

- A ==structured grasp interface== pairs object geometry with ==contact grasp representations== (contact point, approach and baseline vectors, width) and taxonomy-consistent grasp types, bridging task understanding and hand-specific synthesis.
- The base policy uses explicit ==kinematic mapping== from a precomputed ==grasp-width-to-closing-synergy lookup table==, then a ==hand-agnostic decision model== scoring ==force-closure contact geometry== via ==PointTransformer== plus ==Contact-Aware Multi-Pooling==.
- Three composable priors: ==DINOv3==-lifted spatial CGR prediction with ==Contact-GraspNet==, an LLM using ==retrieval-augmented chain-of-thought== plus ==SAM3== grounding for functional parts, and SAM3/DINOv3 ==semantic-field optimization== for temporal tracking.

## Results

- On the DexGraspNet 2.0 clutter benchmark it averages **88.4%** success across three hands, exceeding AnyDexGrasp by **3.0%** and **9.2%** on random and loose splits.
- Language-guided target grasping averages **81.3%** and **86.0%** on GraspClutter6D and GraspNet-1Billion; functionality inference reaches **94%** part and **87%** grasp-type accuracy versus **82%** and **63%** for a base LLM.
- Dynamic grasp tracking attains perfect **1.0** association accuracy with **0.22 cm** translation error, while real-world functional grasping succeeds **83.3%** over **510 trials** and **89.7%** under human disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A stable hand-agnostic interface lets foundation-model upgrades translate directly into better grasping, decoupling progress in perception and reasoning from retraining the low-level control policy.
> - Encoding contacts as object-centric hand-object point pairs abstracts force closure, so one decision model trains faster and transfers across two-finger to five-finger end-effectors.
> - Modularity pushes failures upstream: functional-part recognition caused 29.4% of errors, and rotationally symmetric or heavily occluded objects break semantic-feature pose tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping has progressed significantly, yet it faces challenges in achieving robust, adaptive, and generalizable grasping in complex real-world scenarios. Traditional approaches often struggle with the inherent contextual nature of grasping, where factors such as object surroundings, task objectives, and interaction dynamics critically influence the required grasp strategy. Existing Vision-Language-Grasp (VLG) methods, while demonstrating strong generalization through large-scale training, typically integrate perception and grasp execution in an end-to-end framework. This tight coupling can limit adaptability to novel task contexts, new robotic hands, and unseen environmental conditions, often requiring extensive data collection and retraining.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that decouples task-dependent understanding from physical grasp synthesis. This approach is analogous to human cognition, where rich prior knowledge guides perception, reasoning, and motor coordination. The paper leverages advancements in foundation models (e.g., large language models, vision transformers) which have shown utility in providing informative priors for visual recognition, spatial understanding, embodied reasoning, and robot control. By modularizing task understanding and grasp execution, the work aims to create a more scalable and extensible paradigm for robotic grasping, allowing for the integration of future foundation model improvements without requiring redesign or retraining of the core grasp policy. This positions the work as a contribution towards more adaptive and generalizable robot manipulation in diverse, real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a Task-Adaptive Vision-Language-Grasp (VLG) Framework:** The main goal is to create a VLG system capable of adapting its grasping strategies based on diverse contextual conditions and task-dependent factors, such as cluttered scenes, functional requirements, and dynamic object movements.
- **Enable Generalizable Grasp Synthesis Across Robotic Hands:** The framework aims to support various robotic hands (from parallel grippers to multi-finger dexterous hands) without requiring hand-specific retraining of the core grasp policy.
- **Decouple Task-Dependent Understanding from Physical Grasp Synthesis:** A central objective is to separate the high-level understanding of task instructions and environmental contexts from the low-level generation and evaluation of physically feasible grasp poses. This decoupling is intended to enhance adaptability and efficiency.
- **Incorporate Composable Foundation Priors:** The research seeks to leverage specialized foundation models to provide "priors" for spatial understanding, cognitive reasoning, and temporal tracking. These priors are designed to be composable and to integrate into the grasp synthesis process via a structured interface.
- **Demonstrate Efficiency and Robustness:** The authors aim to show that the proposed base policy can learn efficiently and generalize well, and that the integrated foundation priors enable effective, robust grasping in challenging simulated and real-world scenarios.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges in current robotic grasping systems:

- **Contextual Complexity:** Real-world grasping is highly contextual. A robot needs to adapt its grasp based on the object's surroundings (clutter), the intended use (e.g., grasping a mug to pour water vs. hand it over), and whether the object or robot is in motion. Existing end-to-end VLG policies often struggle to scale to this vast diversity of conditions.
- **Limited Generalization of End-to-End Policies:** Many VLG methods tightly couple foundation models with end-to-end grasp policies. While effective for trained scenarios, their performance is often constrained by the training data's coverage and the computational resources required for large-scale multimodal data. Adapting to new tasks or robotic embodiments typically necessitates costly retraining or data collection.
- **Human Inspiration:** Humans adapt grasping effortlessly by utilizing rich priors from commonsense knowledge and past experience. The authors are motivated to emulate this by integrating foundation models as sources of such priors.
- **Scalability and Extensibility:** By decoupling, the framework aims to improve data and training efficiency. This design could establish a stable interface that allows future advancements in perception and reasoning (e.g., new foundation models) to directly enhance grasping capabilities without requiring a complete system overhaul.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, AdaRoboVLG, adopts a decoupled approach to vision-language grasping, separating task-dependent understanding from physical grasp synthesis. This is mediated by a "structured grasp interface."

**A. Structured Grasp Interface**
The interface `I` is defined by three components:
1. **Object Geometry (`O`)**: The 3D representation of the target object.
2. **Contact Grasp Representations (CGRs) (`A`)**: A set of local contact primitives on the object surface, each encoding a potential contact point (`p_c`), approach vector (`v_a`), closing direction (`v_b`), grasp width (`w`), and a stability probability (`p`). These are hand-agnostic.
3. **Grasp Types (`T_H`)**: A set of human-like grasp taxonomies (e.g., power grasp, precision pinch) that are kinematically compatible with the specific robotic hand (`H`).

**B. Base Grasp Policy**
The base policy `f_π(I, H)` synthesizes executable grasp poses from the structured interface and hand embodiment. It comprises two stages:

1. **Hand-Specific Grasp Mapping (`M_H`)**:
 - This function translates the hand-agnostic CGRs and grasp types into hand-specific grasp candidates.
 - It precomputes a lookup table for each grasp type, mapping grasp width to a hand configuration (`q*`) and a Principal Closing Frame (PCF) transformation. The PCF represents the hand's base pose relative to the contact point.
 - During inference, given a CGR (`a_j`) and a grasp type (`τ_k`), the system reconstructs the world-frame PCF and retrieves the corresponding hand configuration and hand-frame PCF from the lookup table.
 - The hand base pose and joint configuration are then computed, generating a candidate grasp pose (`g_j,k`). These candidates are augmented by sampling along the approach direction and filtered for collisions.

2. **Hand-Agnostic Grasp Evaluation (`D`)**:
 - This module evaluates the physical stability of grasp candidates using a learned decision model.
 - **Hand-Object Interaction (HOI) Representation (`R_HOI`)**: For a given grasp candidate, object geometry, and hand specification, this representation is extracted. It consists of the coordinates and normals of contact points at the hand-object interface. Points within a proximity threshold are considered contact pairs. This representation is designed to be unified across different hand embodiments.
 - **Grasp Decision Model**: A lightweight point-based neural network (using a PointTransformer encoder) takes the HOI representation as input. It extracts local geometric embeddings and employs a Contact-Aware Multi-Pooling (CAMP) mechanism (max, mean, standard deviation pooling with dynamic weighting) to aggregate these features into a global contact descriptor. An MLP then maps this descriptor to a scalar stability score (0-1), indicating the probability of a successful grasp. The model is trained using Binary Cross-Entropy loss.

**C. Foundation Priors for Adaptive Grasping**
The framework integrates three composable foundation-model priors to construct and update the structured grasp interface based on task context:

1. **Spatial Prior (`M_s`) for Cluttered Grasping**:
 - **Input**: Multi-view RGB-D observations of a cluttered scene.
 - **Functionality**: Constructs scene semantic representations and predicts scene-level CGRs.
 - **Mechanism**: DINOv3 extracts pixel-level semantic features from RGB images, which are then lifted to 3D space using an implicit field function. Contact-GraspNet is used to predict dense per-point CGRs from the scene point cloud and semantic representations. These CGRs, filtered by contact probability, provide spatially feasible contact constraints.

2. **Cognitive Prior (`M_c`) for Functional Grasping**:
 - **Input**: Multi-view scene observations and a natural language instruction.
 - **Functionality**: Infers the target object, its functional part, and appropriate grasp types, then aligns these with 3D scene representations.
 - **Mechanism**:
 - **Grasp Functionality Inference**: A Large Language Model (LLM), specifically Seed-1.8, augmented with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) over a grasp taxonomy database and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, parses the language instruction. It infers the target object description, functional part description, and a set of compatible grasp types.
 - **Object Semantic Representation Refinement**: Seed-1.8 generates bounding box annotations on RGB images, which are used as prompts for SAM3 to extract multi-view instance masks. These 2D masks are lifted to 3D to localize the target object and refine its semantic representations and geometry.
 - **Functional CGRs Alignment**: The parsed functional part description is used with SAM3 and 3D lifting to identify functional regions. Scene-level CGRs are then filtered to retain only those consistent with both the grounded target object and its functional part, forming functional CGRs.

3. **Temporal Prior (`M_t`) for Dynamic Grasping**:
 - **Input**: An initial grasp interface and a continuous sequence of observations.
 - **Functionality**: Updates the grasp interface over time to maintain temporally consistent grasp tracking for dynamic objects.
 - **Mechanism**: It estimates the real-time SE(3) transformation of the target object. SAM3's mask-based tracking propagates instance masks over time, ensuring consistency. DINOv3's cross-frame semantic consistency is used in an optimization problem to estimate the rigid transformation by minimizing the discrepancy between initial semantic descriptors and features queried from the current scene's implicit field. This estimated transformation is then applied to update the object geometry and CGRs in the interface. For efficiency, the initial stable grasp pose is directly updated by this rigid transformation.

The framework allows these priors to operate independently or jointly, depending on the complexity of the task context.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world environments.

**A. Evaluation of Base Policy for General Grasp Synthesis**
- **Learning Efficiency**: The Hand-Object Interaction (HOI) representations (both object-centric and hand-centric) resulted in faster network convergence and lower final training losses compared to a separate encoding of hand and object point clouds. This suggests that explicit modeling of contact interaction simplifies the learning task.
- **Cross-hand Generalization**: A decision model trained on combined data from three diverse robotic hands (DH3, Allegro, Inspire) achieved higher grasp quality prediction accuracies (DH3: 82.95%, Allegro: 88.02%, Inspire: 91.07%) compared to models trained on single-hand data. t-SNE visualizations showed substantial overlap in HOI feature distributions across different hands, indicating the representation's hand-agnostic property and suitability for cross-hand generalization.

**B. Evaluation of Spatial Prior for Clutter-Aware Grasping**
- **Clutter-aware Grasping**: On the DexGraspNet 2.0 benchmark, AdaRoboVLG demonstrated strong zero-shot and cross-hand grasp synthesis. It achieved higher average success rates (3.0% and 9.2% improvement) on the "random" and "loose" clutter splits for all three robotic hands (DH3, Allegro, Inspire) compared to AnyDexGrasp. While slightly lower on the "dense" split, it showed more stable performance across varying clutter levels. The use of compact CGRs and a human-inspired grasp taxonomy contributed to this robustness.

**C. Evaluation of Cognitive Prior for Functional Grasping**
- **Grasp Functionality Inference**: An ablation study on an open-set instruction dataset showed that combining RAG and CoT mechanisms significantly improved functional part accuracy (0.94) and grasp type accuracy (0.87) in the cognitive reasoning module, reducing reliance on large-scale language-action paired data.
- **Language-Guided Target Grasping**: On simulated benchmarks built from GraspClutter6D and GraspNet-1Billion, AdaRoboVLG achieved the highest average success rates for all three dexterous hands. It outperformed AnyDexGrasp by 2.7% and 2.5% on GraspClutter6D and GraspNet-1Billion, respectively, and substantially improved upon D(R,O)-Grasp (23.7% and 27.1% average improvements). This indicates that the LLM-based grounding and SAM3-based segmentation provide accurate constraints for functional grasp generation.

**D. Evaluation of Temporal Prior for Dynamic Grasping**
- **Dynamic Grasping**: On the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark, AdaRoboVLG achieved perfect Multiple Grasp Tracking Accuracy (MGTA = 1.0), the lowest mean translation error (0.22-0.28 cm), and competitive rotation errors (3.28-3.68 degrees) across seen, similar, and novel object splits, outperforming several baselines. This was attributed to the integration of SAM3's mask tracking and DINOv3's cross-frame semantic consistency.
- **Long-horizon Tracking**: In a long-horizon tracking evaluation, the method robustly tracked most objects. Tracking failures occurred primarily with highly symmetric objects (due to rotational invariance) and severely occluded objects lacking sufficient discriminative semantic cues.

**E. Real-World Experiments**
- **Language-Guided Functional Grasping in Static Cluttered Scenes**: AdaRoboVLG achieved an overall success rate of 83.3% across 510 trials with 102 everyday objects on two robotic platforms equipped with a dexterous hand. The system demonstrated reliable target identification, functional grasp intent inference, and collision-free grasp generation. Failure analysis indicated functional-part recognition errors (29.4%), grasp pose estimation errors (22.3%), and insufficient contact stability (20.0%) as primary failure modes. The framework also successfully transferred to a parallel-jaw gripper.
- **Language-Guided Functional Grasping in Dynamic Cluttered Scenes**: The system performed functional grasping on a moving conveyor belt (2-5 cm/s), updating grasp poses online at 5 Hz. It maintained an average grasping success rate of 89.7% under various human-induced disturbances (out-of-view recovery, fast motion, continuous occlusion, simultaneous translation and rotation), demonstrating robustness in dynamic environments.
- **Extensibility**: The framework was shown to be extensible to new scenarios, such as bimanual sorting (with a task-allocation module) and transparent-object grasping (with a depth-completion module), by integrating additional task-relevant modules without retraining the core policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of AdaRoboVLG lies in its modular and adaptive approach to robotic grasping, offering several potential impacts on the field:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability**: By decoupling task-dependent understanding from physical grasp synthesis and using a structured grasp interface, the framework demonstrates improved generalization across diverse robotic hands and adaptability to various contextual conditions (clutter, functional requirements, dynamics). This addresses a critical limitation of many existing end-to-end VLG policies which often struggle with unseen scenarios or new embodiments.
- **Increased Efficiency and Scalability**: The modular design allows for independent development and integration of task-specific foundation priors. This could lead to more efficient data utilization and training, as advances in perception and reasoning (e.g., new foundation models) can be directly incorporated by updating specific modules rather than retraining an entire monolithic policy. This promotes a scalable paradigm for future robotic grasping systems.
- **Robustness in Complex Environments**: The integration of spatial, cognitive, and temporal priors enables the system to handle challenging real-world scenarios, including cluttered scenes, language-guided functional tasks, and dynamic object manipulation. The demonstrated robustness against human-induced disturbances in dynamic settings is a notable achievement for practical robotic deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI**: The framework establishes a stable interface between high-level understanding and low-level execution. This aligns with the broader trend of leveraging foundation models for embodied AI, offering a blueprint for how sophisticated reasoning and perceptual capabilities from large models can be effectively channeled into physical robot actions. It encourages further research into specialized, composable foundation models for robotics.
- **Cross-Hand Deployment**: The ability of the base grasp policy to generalize across different hand embodiments (from parallel-jaw grippers to multi-finger dexterous hands) with minimal or no retraining is a practical advantage, reducing the cost and effort of deploying robots with varying hardware.
- **Reduced Data Dependency**: The use of foundation models and explicit kinematic mapping, rather than solely relying on large-scale multimodal datasets for end-to-end learning, suggests a pathway to reduce the data dependency for achieving robust grasping performance, particularly in scenarios where extensive paired data is unavailable.

While the paper acknowledges limitations related to perception uncertainty, the lack of closed-loop physical interaction (e.g., tactile feedback), and the focus on single-step grasping, the proposed framework provides a solid foundation for addressing these in future work through the integration of more advanced foundation models and sophisticated control strategies. The work contributes to moving robotic manipulation towards more intelligent, flexible, and human-like capabilities.
