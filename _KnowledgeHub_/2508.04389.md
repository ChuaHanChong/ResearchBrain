# GuirlVG: Incentivize GUI Visual Grounding via Empirical Exploration on Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> GuirlVG presents a reinforcement learning framework for Graphical User Interface Visual Grounding (GUI-VG) that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy using significantly less training data. The approach, which optimizes Group Relative Policy Optimization with a Soft Reward Function and a novel Adversarial KL Factor, outperforms previous supervised fine-tuning baselines by up to 17.2% while utilizing as few as 2K-5.2K training samples.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for GUI Visual Grounding (GUI-VG) is data-intensive and computationally expensive, requiring extensive labeled datasets and frequent retraining for evolving MLLMs.
- The SFT paradigm often leads to models that memorize specific patterns rather than developing robust reasoning capabilities, limiting generalization across diverse GUI layouts.
- Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) had not been systematically explored for GUI-VG, and initial naive applications underperformed SFT, indicating a need for tailored RFT design.

## Method

- Developed **GuirlVG**, a Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) approach based on ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO), specifically adapted for data-efficient GUI Visual Grounding.
- Introduced a Soft Reward Function (SRF) that provides partial credit for output elements and relaxes strict formatting, leading to a smoother and more stable reward signal during training.
- Proposed an Adversarial KL Factor, a novel technique to dynamically scale the KL penalty in GRPO, which stabilizes training and mitigates reward over-optimization.

## Results

- GuirlVG achieved new state-of-the-art average accuracies on key GUI-VG benchmarks: **88.7%** on ScreenSpot (+**7.7%** over OS-Atlas), **91.9%** on ScreenSpot v2 (+**7.8%** over OS-Atlas), and **36.1%** on ScreenSpot-Pro (+**17.2%** over OS-Atlas).
- The method demonstrated high data efficiency, achieving superior performance with only **2K** to **5.2K** training samples, representing up to 6,790 times less data than existing SFT baselines.
- The proposed Adversarial KL Factor alone contributed a +**1.8%** accuracy gain over the best static KL penalty settings, validating its effectiveness in stabilizing RFT training and enhancing performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Carefully designed RFT (with appropriate reward functions and regularization) significantly outperforms SFT in GUI-VG with orders of magnitude less data, particularly using direct point prediction with an "In-Bbox" binary reward over IoU-based bounding-box metrics.
> - Dynamically adjusting the KL penalty in GRPO with the Adversarial KL Factor is crucial for preventing reward over-optimization and ensuring robust RFT performance.
> - Withholding image resolution information during training but providing it at test time encourages models to learn more robust spatial reasoning, leading to improved generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving field of AI agents capable of interacting with digital environments, particularly Graphical User Interfaces (GUIs), through visual observation (screenshots). A cornerstone capability for such agents is GUI Visual Grounding (GUI-VG), which involves localizing specific actionable elements within a screenshot based on textual instructions.

Historically, the dominant paradigm for developing GUI-VG capabilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has been **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**. This approach, exemplified by methods like SeeClick, UGround, AGG, OS-Atlas, and UI-TARs, relies on curating vast amounts of domain-specific, high-quality labeled data and incurs significant training costs. These methods often involve extensive pretraining pipelines and specialized data augmentation techniques to achieve reasonable performance. The paper highlights a critical concern with this SFT paradigm: its inefficiency and escalating costs as MLLMs continue to advance and require re-tuning for each new generation. The necessity of exhaustive SFT becomes increasingly questionable, especially as modern MLLMs (like Qwen2.5-VL used in this study) are already incorporating GUI-related data during their general pretraining phase.

Into this landscape, the authors introduce **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)** as a promising alternative. RFT, particularly rule-based RFT with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has recently demonstrated notable success in other complex domains, such as mathematical reasoning, code generation, and logical inference, with DeepSeek-R1 being a prominent example. Unlike SFT, which enforces token-level supervision, RFT allows models more freedom to explore reasoning processes and provides feedback primarily at the level of the final output. This characteristic is often cited as leading to stronger reasoning capabilities and improved generalization, as opposed to SFT's tendency to "memorize".

The critical gap identified by this paper is the **lack of systematic exploration of RFT specifically for GUI-VG**. The authors explicitly state that naive application of RFT to GUI-VG, under fair experimental settings, *underperforms* the SFT baseline. This unexpected result motivates their deep empirical study, aiming to bridge this gap by meticulously investigating how to optimally formulate RFT objectives and implement GRPO for the unique challenges of GUI-VG, such as diverse layouts and high-resolution visual inputs. By demonstrating RFT's superior data efficiency and effectiveness, GuirlVG challenges the prevailing reliance on large-scale SFT, positioning itself as a potential paradigm shift in post-training strategies for GUI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind GuirlVG stems from the inefficiencies and limitations of the current SFT-centric approach to GUI visual grounding. The authors identify several key drivers:

- **Cost and Data Intensity of SFT:** Traditional SFT methods for GUI-VG require massive, domain-specific datasets and significant computational resources for training. This poses a sustainability challenge as MLLMs rapidly evolve, demanding continuous and expensive re-training.
- **Advancing MLLM Capabilities:** Modern MLLMs are increasingly capable due to large-scale pretraining, some even incorporating GUI-related data. This raises the fundamental question of whether such extensive post-training via SFT remains the most efficient strategy.
- **Untapped Potential of RFT:** Rule-based RFT has shown remarkable success in other complex reasoning domains, offering a more flexible and potentially data-efficient alternative to SFT. However, its applicability and optimal formulation for GUI-VG remained unexplored.
- **Initial RFT Underperformance:** A crucial motivation was the preliminary finding that a "trivial" application of RFT actually performed worse than SFT, indicating that a deeper, systematic investigation was necessary to unlock its potential for GUI-VG.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets forth several clear objectives for GuirlVG:

1. **Systematic Empirical Study of RFT:** To conduct the first comprehensive empirical study of rule-based RFT within the GUI visual grounding domain, specifically focusing on the GRPO algorithm.
2. **Optimal GRPO Formulation:** To deconstruct GRPO into its core components (format reward, accuracy reward, KL penalty) and systematically ablate each to derive an optimal configuration tailored for GUI-VG.
3. **Novel Stabilization Technique:** To introduce a novel method, the "Adversarial KL Factor," which dynamically scales the KL penalty to stabilize the learning process and mitigate reward over-optimization, a known challenge in reinforcement learning.
4. **Best Practices for RFT Training:** To explore a wide range of training setups, including hyperparameter tuning, LoRA enablement, and prompt engineering, to identify best practices for effective RFT on GUI-VG tasks.
5. **Demonstrate Data Efficiency and Effectiveness:** To validate that the optimized RFT approach (GuirlVG) can achieve state-of-the-art results using significantly fewer training examples compared to existing SFT methods, thereby showcasing its data efficiency and strong generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GuirlVG methodology is built upon a systematic empirical exploration of rule-based Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, specifically adapted for GUI visual grounding.

**Base Model and Fine-tuning Strategy:**
The authors fine-tune **Qwen2.5-VL** as their base Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM). To ensure efficiency, they primarily utilize **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)** with a rank of 64 and an alpha of 128, keeping the vision module of the MLLM frozen. They also investigate full fine-tuning (Full-FT) as a comparison.

**Core Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) Algorithm:**
The work is grounded in **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. GRPO generates a group of `N` candidate responses for each input, evaluates them with rule-based reward functions, and then computes a relative reward (advantage) for each. A KL divergence penalty is applied to keep the fine-tuned model close to the original reference model, preventing excessive deviation and over-optimization.

**Systematic Empirical Exploration and Innovations:**

1. **Initial RFT Baseline (Trivial RFT):** The study begins by implementing a "trivial" RFT, following common practices from HuggingFace and Shen et al.. This initial setup uses a strict format reward (exact tag matching) and an accuracy reward based on IoU@0.5 for predicted bounding boxes. This establishes the baseline that RFT, without careful design, underperforms SFT.

2. **Reward Function Design (Soft Reward Function - SRF):**
 - **Problem:** The default rigid reward function penalizes minor stylistic deviations or missing tags severely, leading to training noise.
 - **Solution:** GuirlVG introduces the **Soft Reward Function (SRF)**, which provides partial credit for the presence of each expected tag (`<think>`, `</think>`, `<answer>`, `</answer>`) and relaxes output style requirements, focusing on extracting numeric values rather than strict JSON format. This aims to provide a smoother and more stable reward signal.

3. **Model Prediction Format and Accuracy Reward:**
 - The authors explore different combinations of prediction formats (bounding box vs. point) and accuracy reward functions (IoU@0.5, continuous IoU, Distance@k, In-Bbox).
 - **Finding:** They discover that direct **point prediction with an In-Bbox binary reward** (reward of 1 if the predicted point falls inside the ground-truth bounding box, 0 otherwise) is most effective, aligning directly with the task's functional goal.

4. **KL Penalty Balancing (Adversarial KL Factor):**
 - **Problem:** The `β` hyperparameter in GRPO, which controls the strength of the KL penalty, is highly sensitive and a static `β` may not adequately counteract reward-driven over-optimization, especially when the original model already assigns high probability to high-reward responses.
 - **Solution:** They propose the **Adversarial KL Factor ($\alpha_i$)**, a novel technique that dynamically scales the KL penalty based on the ratio of the current response's reward to the maximum possible reward ($ \alpha_i = r_i / m $). This ensures that regularization strengthens adaptively with increasing rewards, mitigating over-optimization.

5. **Training Configurations Exploration:**
 - **Fine-tuning Type:** Comparison between Full-FT and LoRA-FT for the LLM component. They find LoRA-FT to be significantly more efficient with comparable performance.
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Empirical study of `group size` (number of candidate responses, N) and `batch size` in GRPO. They identify optimal settings (group size 6, batch size 4).
 - **Prompt Engineering for Image Resolution:** Investigation into when to provide image resolution information in the prompt (never, during training and testing, or only at test time).
 - **Finding:** The best results are achieved when image resolution is **withheld during training** but **provided at test time**, suggesting it forces the model to learn better spatial reasoning which is then refined by context at inference.

**Training Data and Evaluation:**
- **Training Data:** GuirlVG uses a small dataset of 2K to 5.2K training samples randomly drawn from ShowUI, which aggregates web and desktop data. This is significantly less data than SFT baselines.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is measured by accuracy (whether the predicted point coordinate falls inside the ground-truth bounding box) on three widely-used GUI-VG benchmarks: ScreenSpot, ScreenSpot v2, and ScreenSpot-Pro.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical exploration conducted by the authors yielded several key findings that cumulatively led to the development of GuirlVG:

1. **Initial RFT Underperformance (Finding 1):** Naive application of RFT (RFT-trivial) was found to underperform the SFT baseline (79.2% vs. 82.6% on ScreenSpot), highlighting that a simple adoption of RFT is insufficient and careful design is essential for GUI-VG.
2. **Benefit of Soft Reward Function (Finding 2):** The proposed Soft Reward Function (SRF), which provides partial credit and relaxes output format constraints, improved accuracy by +0.7% over the default rigid reward function, demonstrating that looser, more stable reward signals are crucial for effective RFT training.
3. **Optimal Prediction Format (Finding 3):** Point prediction with an "In-Bbox" reward function was identified as the most effective strategy, achieving 83.4% accuracy, outperforming bounding box prediction with IoU-based rewards (e.g., +3.5% over Bbox IoU@0.5). This aligns the reward directly with the task's functional goal.
4. **Effectiveness of Adversarial KL Factor (Finding 4):** The KL penalty in GRPO was found to be highly sensitive to the `β` hyperparameter. The novel Adversarial KL Factor, which dynamically scales the KL penalty based on reward magnitude, led to a substantial +1.8% gain over the best static `β` setting (87.4% vs. 85.6%), proving its efficacy in stabilizing training and preventing reward over-optimization.
5. **Efficiency of LoRA Fine-tuning (Finding 5):** LoRA-FT achieved comparable performance to full model fine-tuning (Full-FT) while being dramatically more efficient (over 25 times faster training time per iteration). This makes LoRA-FT a practical and preferred choice for RFT in this domain.
6. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Finding 6):** GRPO's performance was significantly impacted by group size and batch size. The optimal configuration of group size 6 and batch size 4 was empirically determined (87.4%), emphasizing the importance of detailed hyperparameter tuning.
7. **Strategic Prompting of Image Resolution (Finding 7):** The most effective strategy for integrating image resolution information was to withhold it during training but provide it only at test time. This approach yielded 88.0% accuracy, suggesting that it encourages the model to learn robust spatial reasoning during training, which is then refined by explicit context at inference.

**Overall Performance of GuirlVG:**
After integrating all the optimized design choices, GuirlVG achieved **new state-of-the-art results across all three challenging GUI-VG benchmarks**:

- **ScreenSpot:** GuirlVG (7B model, 5.2K samples) achieved **88.7%** average accuracy, significantly outperforming the best SFT baseline, OS-Atlas (7B, 13.58M samples), by **+7.7%** (88.7% vs. 81.0%). Notably, it showed a +11.8% improvement on the Mobile-Icon subset, despite not being trained on mobile-specific data, highlighting its strong generalization.
- **ScreenSpot v2:** With 5.2K training examples, GuirlVG reached **91.9%** average accuracy, surpassing OS-Atlas (7B) by **+7.8%** (91.9% vs. 84.1%). The gains were consistent across all subdomains.
- **ScreenSpot-Pro:** For high-resolution professional GUIs, GuirlVG (5.2K samples) achieved **36.1%** average accuracy, marking an absolute gain of **+17.2%** over the best SFT baseline, OS-Atlas (7B), which only achieved 18.9%. This robustness extended to challenging domains like Creative, CAD, and OS.

**Data Efficiency:** A paramount result is GuirlVG's ability to achieve superior performance with an extremely small training dataset (2K to 5.2K samples) compared to SFT methods that leverage hundreds of thousands to over ten million samples (e.g., 6.79K times less data than OS-Atlas).

**Qualitative Results:** Qualitative examples further illustrated GuirlVG's advanced textual understanding and reasoning capabilities, showcasing interpretable "thinking steps" that lead to correct element grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GuirlVG paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of AI agents and multimodal learning, with substantial potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **Challenging the SFT Paradigm for GUI-VG:** The most profound impact of this work is its direct challenge to the entrenched paradigm of Supervised Fine-Tuning for GUI visual grounding. By demonstrating that RFT, when meticulously designed, can drastically outperform SFT methods using orders of magnitude less data, GuirlVG provides compelling evidence for a shift in post-training strategies. This could reshape future research directions for developing GUI agents.

2. **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Reduced Costs:** The ability of GuirlVG to achieve state-of-the-art performance with as few as 2K-5.2K training samples is revolutionary. This data efficiency directly translates to:
 - **Lower Data Curation Burden:** Reducing the need for extensive, costly, and time-consuming manual annotation or complex synthetic data generation pipelines.
 - **Reduced Computational Costs:** Minimizing the training resources (GPUs, energy) required for fine-tuning MLLMs, making advanced GUI-VG capabilities more accessible to researchers and smaller organizations.
 - **Faster Iteration Cycles:** Enabling quicker adaptation of GUI agents to new MLLM generations or specific domain requirements.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The observed superior performance of GuirlVG on out-of-domain tasks (e.g., Mobile-Icon subset without mobile-specific training) and challenging benchmarks like ScreenSpot-Pro supports the hypothesis that RFT fosters stronger reasoning and generalization, as opposed to SFT's tendency to memorize. This leads to more robust and adaptable GUI agents capable of handling diverse and complex real-world GUI environments.

4. **Methodological Innovation:** The introduction of the **Adversarial KL Factor** is a notable technical contribution. This dynamic scaling mechanism for the KL penalty addresses a fundamental challenge in RFT—preventing reward over-optimization—and could be broadly applicable to other RFT applications, enhancing training stability and performance beyond GUI-VG.

5. **Advancing the Development of GUI Agents:** By providing a highly efficient and effective method for GUI-VG, GuirlVG directly accelerates the development of more capable and intelligent GUI agents. These agents have the potential to automate a wider range of digital tasks, from professional software usage to assistive technologies, ultimately enhancing human productivity and interaction with digital interfaces.

6. **Interpretability of Agent Behavior:** The qualitative results, showcasing explicit "thinking steps" generated by GuirlVG, offer a degree of interpretability that is highly valuable. Understanding *how* an agent arrives at a decision can aid in debugging, building trust, and refining agent behavior in complex scenarios.

In summary, GuirlVG is a landmark study that not only achieves new benchmarks in GUI visual grounding but also critically re-evaluates and redefines the conventional wisdom regarding MLLM post-training for this domain. Its emphasis on data efficiency, generalization, and methodological innovation positions it to significantly influence the future trajectory of GUI agent research and development.
