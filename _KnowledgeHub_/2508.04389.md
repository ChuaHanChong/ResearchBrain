# GuirlVG: Incentivize GUI Visual Grounding via Empirical Exploration on Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> GuirlVG presents a reinforcement learning framework for Graphical User Interface Visual Grounding (GUI-VG) that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy using significantly less training data. The approach, which optimizes Group Relative Policy Optimization with a Soft Reward Function and a novel Adversarial KL Factor, outperforms previous supervised fine-tuning baselines by up to 17.2% while utilizing as few as 2K-5.2K training samples.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for GUI Visual Grounding (GUI-VG) is data-intensive and computationally expensive, requiring extensive labeled datasets and frequent retraining for evolving MLLMs.
- The SFT paradigm often leads to models that memorize specific patterns rather than developing robust reasoning capabilities, limiting generalization across diverse GUI layouts.
- Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) had not been systematically explored for GUI-VG, and initial naive applications underperformed SFT, indicating a need for tailored RFT design.

## Method

- Developed **GuirlVG**, a Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) approach based on ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO), specifically adapted for data-efficient GUI Visual Grounding.
- Introduced a Soft Reward Function (SRF) that provides partial credit for output elements and relaxes strict formatting, leading to a smoother and more stable reward signal during training.
- Proposed an Adversarial KL Factor, a novel technique to dynamically scale the KL penalty in GRPO, which stabilizes training and mitigates reward over-optimization.

## Results

- GuirlVG achieved new state-of-the-art average accuracies on key GUI-VG benchmarks: **88.7%** on ScreenSpot (+**7.7%** over OS-Atlas), **91.9%** on ScreenSpot v2 (+**7.8%** over OS-Atlas), and **36.1%** on ScreenSpot-Pro (+**17.2%** over OS-Atlas).
- The method demonstrated high data efficiency, achieving superior performance with only **2K** to **5.2K** training samples, representing up to 6,790 times less data than existing SFT baselines.
- The proposed Adversarial KL Factor alone contributed a +**1.8%** accuracy gain over the best static KL penalty settings, validating its effectiveness in stabilizing RFT training and enhancing performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Carefully designed RFT, with appropriate reward functions and regularization, can significantly outperform SFT in GUI-VG tasks while requiring orders of magnitude less training data.
> - Using direct point prediction with an "In-Bbox" binary reward is more effective for GUI-VG than bounding box prediction with IoU-based metrics, as it directly aligns with the task's functional goal.
> - Dynamically adjusting the KL penalty in GRPO with the Adversarial KL Factor is crucial for preventing reward over-optimization and ensuring robust RFT performance.
> - Withholding image resolution information during training but providing it at test time encourages models to learn more robust spatial reasoning, leading to improved generalization.
