# Not All History Helps: Velocity-Aware Selective Memory for Long-Horizon End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> StableDrive is an end-to-end autonomous-driving planner that gates cross-cycle historical planning states with a Mamba-based Selective Momentum Memory and trains motion-stage awareness via a removable scaffold, then merges both endpoints at a fixed parameter midpoint. On nuScenes it cuts collision rate 23.3%, TPC 30.9%, L2 11.8% over prior best, with no extra inference cost.

## Problem

- Momentum-based planners like MomAD normalize attention over cached historical modes, only redistributing relative influence rather than deciding if the whole cached state is stale.
- Self-generated historical planning states carry prior estimation errors and motion assumptions that can become invalid after braking, stopping, restarting, or new car-following transitions.
- Generic Mamba-based driving modules do not define the source, causal boundary, or ego-planning semantics of cross-cycle memory, leaving reliability of self-predicted history unaddressed.

## Method

- **Selective Momentum Memory (SMM)** uses a ==causal Mamba selective state-space update== over score-modulated one-cycle cached query and current query, with a learned near-identity residual gate and explicit no-history bypass.
- **Motion-Stage Training Scaffold (MSTS)** builds per-horizon motion tokens from stage probabilities, coarse trajectory, and cached query, processed by a shared ==Horizon Mamba==, supervised by phase, far-horizon, velocity, and acceleration losses, then discarded before inference.
- **Fixed Midpoint Checkpoint Construction** interpolates matched tensors of the SMM-only and MSTS-trained endpoints at alpha=0.5, yielding one deployable checkpoint without ensembling or extra inference compute.

## Results

- On nuScenes 1-6s, StableDrive reduces average L2 to **1.20m**, Col. Rate to **0.66%**, and TPC to **0.85m**, beating best prior reported values by **11.8%**, **23.3%**, and **30.9%** respectively.
- On curated LT-nuScenes longitudinal-transition subset, StableDrive lowers 6-s collision rate by **23.81%**, TPC by **10.90%**, and L2 by **6.37%** versus a matched MomAD reproduction.
- On NAVSIM v2 navhard_two_stage, StableDrive reaches **42.6 EPDMS**, a **5.7-point** gain over the previous best of 36.9, while also topping NAVSIM v1 and v2 navtest aggregate scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reliable long-horizon planning benefits more from selectively gating stale self-generated history than from simply extending or reweighting temporal context.
> - Separating candidate-level score modulation from context-dependent state integration lets a model explicitly reject an entire historical prior rather than just redistribute attention over it.
> - Training-only auxiliary scaffolds can be retired via checkpoint surgery, and averaging architecture-aligned endpoints' weights can combine complementary accuracy and safety gains without ensembling cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

End-to-End Autonomous Driving (E2E-AD) represents a paradigm shift in autonomous vehicle control by integrating perception, prediction, and planning into a single learning framework. This approach aims to reduce information fragmentation and establish a more direct link between scene understanding and decision-making compared to traditional modular pipelines. While E2E-AD has shown promise, a significant challenge lies in achieving reliable long-horizon planning. This involves anticipating future motion evolution, potential consequences, and maintaining safe, consistent driving behavior over extended periods in dynamic traffic environments.

Existing E2E-AD methods often leverage historical and temporal context to enhance planning. Approaches such as MomAD, BridgeAD, and GraphWorld incorporate historical planning states, perception momentum, or latent world states to inform current and future plans. However, a limitation identified in these methods is the uncritical reuse of self-generated historical planning states. These historical states, being predictions from earlier cycles, can accumulate estimation errors and retain outdated motion assumptions, particularly when the ego vehicle undergoes significant motion-stage transitions (e.g., accelerating, braking, stopping, restarting, or changing car-following behavior). Indiscriminate propagation of such "stale" priors can lead to trajectory deviations, delayed reactions, and increased collision risk, especially as the planning horizon lengthens.

The present work, StableDrive, addresses this gap by focusing on the conditional reliability of self-generated planning history. It aims to develop a mechanism that selectively retains or suppresses historical information based on its relevance to the current and evolving motion state. This distinguishes it from prior work that primarily focuses on *how much* history to retain or *how* to integrate it, without explicitly considering *when* that history becomes unreliable. The use of selective state-space models (like Mamba) in autonomous driving is an emerging trend for handling long temporal contexts, but previous applications have not specifically targeted the reliability of self-generated planning states under motion transitions. StableDrive introduces a targeted approach to manage this temporal uncertainty, seeking to improve long-horizon planning by filtering out unreliable historical priors and guiding within-horizon motion-stage learning, all while maintaining a deployable inference path without added complexity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a reliable long-horizon end-to-end planning framework for autonomous driving that explicitly addresses the limitations of existing methods regarding the use of historical planning states. The authors identify two primary challenges:

1. **Cross-cycle historical reliability:** Previous momentum-based planning methods, such as MomAD, continuously propagate historical planning states. While these states can provide useful temporal cues during stable motion, they are self-generated predictions from earlier cycles and therefore encapsulate estimation errors and prior motion assumptions. When the ego vehicle's motion stage changes significantly (e.g., from cruising to braking or from stationary to accelerating), the longitudinal trends encoded in these historical plans may become outdated or "stale." Continuing to trust and propagate such unreliable priors can lead to accumulated errors, diverging trajectories, and increased collision risk over longer planning horizons. The paper highlights a case where MomAD's trajectory diverges and collides at 6 seconds due to a stale prior, whereas the proposed method remains collision-free. This motivates a mechanism to selectively control the influence of preceding self-predicted planning states.
2. **Within-horizon motion-stage evolution:** Long-horizon planning requires the autonomous system to anticipate and accurately transition through various motion phases (acceleration, cruising, braking, stopping, restarting, car following) within the prediction horizon. Existing methods often struggle to maintain coherence and accuracy as these motion states continuously evolve. Small errors in velocity, acceleration, or following distance can compound, leading to substantial deviations. This motivates a training mechanism that explicitly guides the planner to learn and understand these future motion-stage evolutions, ensuring stage-aware trajectory generation throughout the planning horizon.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives are to:
- Design a selective memory mechanism that can dynamically regulate the influence of self-generated historical planning states on the current planning cycle, suppressing unreliable or stale information while retaining useful context.
- Develop a training scaffold that provides explicit supervision for learning within-horizon motion-stage transitions and longitudinal motion characteristics, thereby enhancing the planner's ability to generate coherent long-term trajectories.
- Integrate these mechanisms into a unified framework that improves long-horizon planning accuracy, safety, and temporal consistency without introducing additional inference-time computational overhead or complexity.
- Demonstrate the framework's effectiveness through rigorous evaluation on standard benchmarks and a newly curated dataset designed to test performance under longitudinal motion transitions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

StableDrive is proposed as a long-horizon end-to-end planning framework built upon an inherited sparse perception and planning pipeline, augmented with two main components: Selective Momentum Memory (SMM) and a Motion-Stage Training Scaffold (MSTS). A key aspect of the approach is a "train-and-retire" strategy for MSTS and a fixed midpoint checkpoint construction for deployment.

**A. Overall Framework**
StableDrive extends the MomAD and SparseDrive pipelines. It encodes multi-view images into a sparse scene representation, which is then used by a joint scene interaction module to generate command- and mode-conditioned planning slots, denoted as Q_t. These slots are then refined by SMM. A planning head predicts displacement increments and candidate scores, followed by collision-aware rescoring and selection to obtain the final plan.

**B. Selective Momentum Memory (SMM)**
SMM is designed to regulate the influence of the preceding self-predicted planning state across consecutive planning cycles. Unlike previous momentum planning that uses normalized attention to redistribute influence, SMM separates candidate-level score modulation from context-dependent state integration and employs a planning-specific causal state update.
- **Cache Mechanism:** SMM maintains a one-cycle cache (H1 cache) consisting of SMM-enhanced planning queries (`b_q_t-1,c,k`) and their corresponding score logits (`b_s_t-1,c,k`) from the previous cycle. This cache is rank-local, scene-isolated, and is reset when history is invalid, the scene changes, or temporal ordering is discontinuous. Importantly, no ground-truth information, future observations, or future features are used in forming this cache.
- **Score Modulation:** For each command-trajectory-anchor slot `(c, k)`, a score-modulated history token (`h_t-1,c,k`) is created by applying a sigmoid activation to the previous score logit and element-wise multiplying it with the stop-gradient version of the previous SMM-enhanced query (`σ(b_s_t-1,c,k) * sg(b_q_t-1,c,k)`). This provides candidate-level attenuation.
- **Selective State Update:** The modulated history token is concatenated with the current planning query (`q_t,c,k`) to form a causal sequence `Z_mem_t,c,k = [h_t-1,c,k, q_t,c,k]`. This sequence is then processed by a Mamba selective state-space operator (`Φ_mem`). The Mamba operator performs input-dependent state updates, allowing information to be selectively retained or suppressed.
- **Residual Readout:** SMM applies a near-identity residual readout to the current query. If valid history (`r_t=1`) is available, the current query is enhanced by adding a learned-scaled, layer-normalized terminal state from the Mamba output. If no valid history (`r_t=0`) is available, the original current query is passed through, providing an explicit no-history bypass. The learned scalar `α_mem` is initialized to a small value (10^-3) to keep the initial update close to the original planner.
- **Cache Update:** After the current planning heads produce their outputs, the current SMM-enhanced queries and score logits are detached and stored as the cache for the next cycle.

**C. Motion-Stage Training Scaffold (MSTS)**
MSTS is a training-only component designed to guide the shared planner in learning within-horizon motion-stage evolution without affecting inference complexity.
- **Ground-Truth Motion Stages:** During training, ground-truth ego kinematics (velocity and acceleration) are used to classify each future horizon step into one of four motion stages: stationary, accelerating, decelerating, or cruising. These labels serve as supervision targets only; they are never fed as input to the planner.
- **Token Construction:** For each candidate trajectory and horizon step, motion-stage tokens (`z_t,c,k,h`) are constructed. These tokens incorporate the SMM-enhanced query, the coarse trajectory's displacement, velocity, and acceleration, a one-cycle query cache, a horizon embedding, and a shared stage context derived from the average of all candidate queries and coarse trajectories.
- **Horizon Mamba:** A shared Horizon Mamba block (`Φ_hor`) processes these candidate-specific horizon tokens. It models the horizon evolution for each candidate sequence independently, without cross-candidate state exchange.
- **Refinement and Supervision:** A gated residual refinement (`f_g`, `f_r`) is applied to the Horizon Mamba output, producing refined trajectory increments (`∆y_ref_t`). The MSTS-assisted training objective (`L_MSTS`) combines the base planning objective (`L_base`) (evaluated on `∆y_ref_t`) with auxiliary losses: `L_phase` (masked cross-entropy on predicted motion stages), `L_far` (masked L1 loss on cumulative xy positions for far horizons), and `L_long` (masked L1 losses on longitudinal position, velocity, and acceleration). The residual head `f_r` is initialized to zero, ensuring MSTS starts as an identity refinement.
- **Retirement:** Critically, MSTS-specific parameters (`ϕ_MSTS`) are removed before inference. The benefits of MSTS are implicitly retained in the shared planner parameters (`θ_VT`) that were optimized during MSTS-assisted training.

**D. Train–Retire Endpoint Construction**
The training process involves two parallel branches, both starting from the same SMM initialization (`θ_init`) and optimized for a matched budget:
1. **SMM-only endpoint (`θ_SM`):** Trained using only the base planning objective and SMM.
2. **MSTS-assisted endpoint (`θ_VT`, `ϕ_MSTS`):** Jointly optimizes the shared planner and MSTS.
After training, the MSTS scaffold is "retired" by discarding all `ϕ_MSTS` tensors, resulting in `θ_VT` that retains the effects of MSTS-assisted optimization but has the same architecture as the SMM-only endpoint for deployment.

**E. Fixed Midpoint Checkpoint Construction**
To leverage the complementary strengths of the two trained endpoints (`θ_SM` and `θ_VT`) without ensembling or adding inference complexity, StableDrive constructs a single final checkpoint (`θ_SD`) by taking the fixed arithmetic midpoint (`α = 0.5`) of their shared floating-point parameters. This direct interpolation is possible because both endpoints share the same deployed SMM architecture, start from the same initialization, and are trained under matched budgets. This construction minimizes the maximum Euclidean distance to either endpoint along the interpolation segment, providing a symmetric and data-independent method for consolidation. The final `f_SD(x)` model uses the common deployed SMM computation graph and requires only one checkpoint and one forward pass at inference.

**F. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics**
- **nuScenes:** Standard dataset for camera-only input, evaluated on the validation split over a 6-second horizon (12 future waypoints at 0.5s intervals). Metrics include L2 trajectory error, collision rate (Col. Rate), and trajectory prediction consistency error (TPC). Lower values are better for all.
- **LT-nuScenes (New):** A curated subset of nuScenes validation, specifically designed to evaluate planning robustness during longitudinal motion transitions (stopping, restarting, sustained acceleration/deceleration, and reversals).
- **NAVSIM v1 and v2:** Closed-loop, data-driven simulation environments. Metrics for v1 include No-at-fault Collision (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Time to Collision (TTC), Comfort (Conf.), Ego Progress (EP), and the aggregate Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS). NAVSIM v2 extends this with Driving Direction Compliance (DDC), Traffic-Light Compliance (TL), Lane Keeping (LK), History Comfort (HC), and Extended Comfort (EC), aggregated into EPDMS. For navhard_two_stage, first- and second-stage subscores are reported and combined. Higher values are better for all NAVSIM metrics.

**G. Implementation Details**
StableDrive uses a ResNet-50 image backbone with 704x256 multi-view inputs. Training employs AdamW with specific learning rate schedules and gradient clipping. MomAD† refers to their local reproduction of MomAD for consistent comparison. All reported results use a single checkpoint and forward pass.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across nuScenes, LT-nuScenes, and NAVSIM v1/v2 demonstrated the performance of StableDrive, supported by ablation studies and qualitative analyses.

**A. nuScenes Performance (Table I)**
StableDrive achieved the lowest L2 error, collision rate, and average TPC across all reported planning horizons (1 to 6 seconds).
- **L2 Error:** Achieved the lowest L2 error at every horizon, with an average of 1.20 m, representing an 11.8% reduction over the previously reported best average.
- **Collision Rate:** Demonstrated the lowest collision rate at every horizon, with an average of 0.66%, indicating a 23.3% reduction over the best average.
- **TPC:** Achieved the lowest average TPC of 0.85 m, a 30.9% reduction.
These consistent gains indicate improvements in long-horizon accuracy, safety, and cross-cycle stability without compromising short-horizon performance.

**B. LT-nuScenes Performance (Table II)**
On the curated LT-nuScenes dataset designed for longitudinal motion transitions, StableDrive exhibited robust performance.
- **L2 Error:** Matched or reduced MomAD†'s L2 error at every horizon, with a 6-second L2 of 2.48m (0.17m reduction) and an average L2 of 1.32m (3.65% reduction).
- **Collision Rate:** Consistently achieved a lower collision rate, with a 6-second collision rate of 4.23% (1.33 percentage point reduction) and an average collision rate of 1.49% (51.62% reduction).
- **TPC:** Reduced average TPC from 0.85m to 0.79m (7.06% improvement).
These results confirm StableDrive's effectiveness in scenarios involving dynamic changes in longitudinal motion.

**C. NAVSIM v1 and v2 Performance (Tables III, IV, V)**
StableDrive demonstrated strong performance across three NAVSIM evaluation settings.
- **NAVSIM v1 navtest (Table III):** Achieved the highest aggregate Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 90.4. This was supported by high No-at-fault Collision (NC) at 99.1 and Time to Collision (TTC) at 95.2.
- **NAVSIM v2 navtest (Table IV):** Achieved the highest Extended Predictive Driver Model Score (EPDMS) of 90.0. Key safety and compliance metrics like NC (98.7), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) (99.1), and Driving Direction Compliance (DDC) (99.4) remained high.
- **NAVSIM v2 navhard_two_stage (Table V):** Showed its largest advantage, achieving an EPDMS of 42.6, which is 5.7 points higher than the previous best (36.9). In this challenging setting, designed for perturbed follow-up scenes, StableDrive maintained high first-stage NC (98.3) and DAC (97.5). In the second stage, it achieved high DAC (86.1), DDC (93.1), and the highest Lane Keeping (LK) score (56.9) among compared methods.
These results collectively indicate that StableDrive improves long-horizon planning across multiple criteria including trajectory accuracy, temporal consistency, safety, and rule compliance in diverse and challenging simulation environments.

**D. Ablation Study: Component Contributions (Tables VI, VII)**
The ablation studies on nuScenes and LT-nuScenes elucidated the contributions of SMM, MSTS, and their combination.
- **SMM Endpoint:** Showed improvements in collision rate and TPC but a slight increase in L2 error.
- **MSTS-Trained Endpoint (retired):** Improved long-horizon L2 and TPC but was less effective in collision avoidance.
- **StableDrive (Fixed Midpoint):** Successfully combined the strengths of both endpoints, ranking first in all 18 reported metrics on nuScenes. Compared to MomAD†, it achieved average reductions of 5.0% in L2, 21.5% in Col. Rate, and 10.1% in TPC. On LT-nuScenes, StableDrive maintained strong L2 and Col. Rate improvements while keeping TPC competitive with the MSTS-trained endpoint.

**E. Ablation Study: MSTS Retirement and Endpoint Construction (Table VIII)**
- **MSTS Retirement (T1-on vs. T1-off):** Removing the MSTS scaffold at inference (T1-off) resulted in parameter reduction (0.811 M active parameters eliminated) and slight improvements in L2 and TPC without affecting collision rate. This confirms that MSTS primarily acts as a training scaffold.
- **Fixed Midpoint (StableDrive vs. T0/T1-off):** The fixed midpoint construction further reduced L2 (to 1.83 m), collision rate (to 1.19%), and TPC (to 1.20 m) compared to both individual endpoints. Crucially, this was achieved with no change to the deployed SMM inference graph, maintaining the same active parameter count (87.153 M) and FLOPs (192.728 G), and requiring a single checkpoint and forward pass.

**F. Ablation Study: History-Length Comparison (Table IX)**
Comparing one-, two-, and four-frame planning histories for StableDrive revealed that the one-frame setting achieved the best average L2 (1.20 m), collision rate (0.66%), and TPC (0.85 m). Extending the history window to two or four frames did not yield consistent gains and, in some cases, led to slightly worse performance (e.g., higher average L2 and Col. Rate). This finding supports the core hypothesis that *selective* use of recent history is more beneficial than indiscriminate temporal accumulation, as longer histories can introduce stale or incompatible planning states.

**G. Qualitative Analysis (Figures 6, 7)**
- **Cross-Cycle Planning Consistency (Figure 6):** Comparison with MomAD† showed that StableDrive produced plans that remained more concentrated near the ground-truth future path across consecutive planning cycles (t-1, t, t+1) when aligned to a shared spatiotemporal reference. MomAD†, in contrast, exhibited increasing terminal dispersion, indicating greater cross-cycle planning drift. This visually supports StableDrive's improved TPC.
- **Adaptation to Longitudinal Driving Transitions (Figure 7):** Visual examples demonstrated SMM's ability to selectively reuse one-cycle history across various longitudinal motion stages (restart, sustained acceleration, deceleration, stopping). The weights assigned to the six cached planning modes were non-uniform and varied depending on the motion stage. This indicates that StableDrive dynamically adjusts the influence of cached history based on its compatibility with the current driving context, rather than applying it uniformly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, StableDrive, that advances the field of end-to-end autonomous driving, particularly for long-horizon planning, by addressing critical limitations related to temporal context utilization. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability in Long-Horizon Planning:** The work directly tackles the problem of "stale" self-generated historical planning priors, a significant source of error in long-horizon E2E-AD. By introducing Selective Momentum Memory (SMM), StableDrive offers a mechanism to dynamically assess and regulate the influence of past predictions based on their relevance to the current motion stage. This conditional reliability is crucial for generating accurate and consistent trajectories over extended periods, especially in complex and evolving traffic scenarios.
- **Improved Safety and Consistency:** The quantitative results, particularly the significant reductions in collision rate and trajectory prediction consistency (TPC) across nuScenes and NAVSIM benchmarks, demonstrate a direct impact on safety and temporal coherence. Lower collision rates contribute to safer autonomous driving systems, while improved TPC ensures smoother, more predictable driving behavior over successive planning cycles, which is important for passenger comfort and interaction with other road users.
- **Robustness to Longitudinal Motion Transitions:** The introduction of LT-nuScenes and the strong performance on this dataset, along with qualitative analyses, highlight StableDrive's enhanced ability to handle challenging longitudinal motion changes (e.g., stopping, accelerating, decelerating). This is a critical capability for real-world driving, where abrupt or subtle changes in velocity and acceleration are common.
- **Novel Architectural Components:**
 - **Selective Momentum Memory (SMM):** The integration of a Mamba selective state-space operator for input-dependent state updates provides a novel and effective way to manage planning memory, moving beyond simple momentum propagation or normalized attention.
 - **Motion-Stage Training Scaffold (MSTS):** This innovative "train-and-retire" approach demonstrates how explicit, stage-aware supervision can be leveraged during training to improve complex motion learning without incurring any inference-time computational cost or parameter increase.
 - **Fixed Midpoint Checkpoint Construction:** This method for merging complementary training endpoints into a single, deployable model (without ensemble or extra inference) offers a practical and efficient strategy for model consolidation in complex deep learning systems.
- **Practical Deployability:** A key advantage of StableDrive is that its final deployed model maintains a single checkpoint and a single forward pass, matching the inference complexity of its base planner. This is vital for real-world autonomous driving systems, where computational efficiency and low latency are paramount.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The finding that "not all history helps" and that one-frame selective history outperforms longer, indiscriminate temporal windows provides valuable insights for future research into temporal context management in E2E-AD. This suggests a shift from simply extending memory to intelligently filtering and weighting historical information.

The potential impact of StableDrive is to contribute to the development of more reliable, safer, and performant end-to-end autonomous driving systems, particularly for advanced planning capabilities required in dynamic and challenging urban environments. The introduced methodologies could also inform memory management strategies in other sequence modeling tasks within robotics and AI. The identified limitations, such as the reliance on candidate generation and score-based selection, also lay out clear directions for subsequent advancements in trajectory planning and decision-making for autonomous vehicles.
