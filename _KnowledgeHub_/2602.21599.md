# Iterative Closed-Loop Motion Synthesis for Scaling the Capabilities of Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called CLAIMS from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and University of Oxford autonomously generates high-dynamic motion data and iteratively refines both the dataset and physics-based humanoid control policies. This approach reduces the average failure rate on complex test sets by 45%, enhancing controller generalization for professional-level skills.

## Problem

- Physics-based humanoid controllers are limited by the high cost and constrained scale of expert-driven motion capture (MoCap) datasets, which often lack diversity and high-difficulty actions.
- Current motion datasets exhibit a fixed difficulty distribution, preventing controllers from progressively mastering more complex skills beyond their initial training boundaries.
- Existing training pipelines lack mechanisms for dynamically adapting the difficulty of training data in response to the controller's evolving capabilities, hindering generalization to high-dynamic or diverse skill sets.

## Method

![[2602.21599-method.png]]
*Overview of the CLAIMS pipeline: a closed-loop system that refines prompts from a 5-domain library (martial arts, dance, combat, sports, gymnastics), synthesizes motions with MDM, filters them by physics and VLM checks, trains humanoid trackers with RL, and uses multimodal feedback to generate progressively harder tasks.*

- CLAIMS (==Closed-Loop Automated Iterative Motion== Synthesis) autonomously generates high-quality, high-dynamic motion data across various professional domains using a text-conditioned motion diffusion model guided by difficulty-aware language prompts.
- A competitive iterative curriculum co-evolves the synthetic motion dataset and the physics-based controller, with data difficulty escalating based on a multi-modal feedback loop.
- The feedback loop combines objective physical tracking metrics (e.g., MPJPE, velocity/acceleration discrepancies) with subjective evaluations from Vision and Language Models (VLMs) to guide subsequent prompt generation and training iterations.

## Results

- The iterative framework reduced the average failure rate of a PHC controller on out-of-distribution, high-difficulty test sets by **45%** (from **41.7%** to **23.1%**) compared to an AMASS baseline after six loops.
- The method demonstrated compatibility and consistent performance improvements across various high-difficulty benchmarks when applied to a MaskedMimic controller, outperforming its baseline on tasks like Kungfu (**65.8%** vs. **57.2%**) and AIST++ (**83.9%** vs. **68.9%**).
- Analysis confirmed that later iterative loops generated systematically harder motions, exhibiting progressively higher velocities and broader speed distributions, with VLM-assigned difficulty scores monotonically increasing with designed difficulty tiers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Text-conditioned motion diffusion models, when guided by expert-designed, difficulty-aware prompts, can synthesize out-of-distribution motions that represent professional and high-dynamic behaviors beyond their original training data manifold.
> - A multi-modal feedback system, integrating objective physics-based metrics and subjective VLM assessments, effectively drives a curriculum that allows physics-based controllers to progressively master increasingly challenging motion data.
> - The use of a structured variable template library for prompt generation, coupled with an LLM-guided policy, provides stability and domain fidelity to the iterative data synthesis process, leading to more targeted and diagnostically challenging motions.
