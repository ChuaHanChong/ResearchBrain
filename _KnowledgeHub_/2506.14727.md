# Casper: Inferring Diverse Intents for Assistive Teleoperation with Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A research team from The University of Texas at Austin, UCLA, and Carnegie Mellon University developed CASPER, an assistive teleoperation system that leverages Visual Language Models to infer diverse human intentions and execute complex mobile manipulation tasks. The system achieved 88.9% task success, reduced human workload, and improved user satisfaction compared to baselines, demonstrating generalized intent inference in open-ended environments.

## Problem

- Existing assistive teleoperation methods are limited to simple, predefined tasks or closed sets of objects, struggling to generalize to novel environments or diverse human goals.
- Current systems lack commonsense reasoning, making them unable to interpret nuanced human control inputs within a rich visual context and often leading to misinterpretations.
- Supporting long-horizon, multi-step mobile manipulation tasks that involve a wide range of objects and interactions remains a challenge for shared-control robots.

## Method

- CASPER integrates pre-trained ==Visual Language Models (VLMs)== for open-world understanding and commonsense reasoning to infer diverse human intentions in real time.
- An intent inference module continuously generates open-set intent candidates using VLMs and selects the most likely intent, enhanced by visual prompting and a confidence estimation mechanism.
- A parameterized skill library with 8 behaviors, combined with open-world perception, enables autonomous execution of diverse, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks upon user confirmation.

## Results

- CASPER achieved an average **88.9%** task success rate, outperforming full teleoperation (**76.4%**) and baseline assistive methods (**40.3**-**45.0%**) in real-world mobile manipulation tasks.
- The system significantly reduced perceived cognitive and physical workload (NASA-TLX) and improved user satisfaction across all 10 metrics compared to baselines (p < **0.05**).
- Offline unit tests confirmed CASPER's superior intent inference accuracy, with visual prompting improving success by **5.7%** and confidence estimation reducing false prediction rates across all history lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual Language Models can effectively infer diverse and open-ended human intentions in real-time by leveraging commonsense reasoning from visual context and user inputs.
> - Visual prompting, such as highlighting gripper position and motion history, significantly enhances VLM comprehension and intent inference accuracy in cluttered robotic environments.
> - A self-consistency-based confidence estimation module is crucial for filtering ambiguous VLM predictions, deferring assistance when intent is unclear, and thereby reducing incorrect suggestions and improving user trust.
