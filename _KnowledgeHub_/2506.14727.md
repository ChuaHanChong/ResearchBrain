# Casper: Inferring Diverse Intents for Assistive Teleoperation with Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A research team from The University of Texas at Austin, UCLA, and Carnegie Mellon University developed CASPER, an assistive teleoperation system that leverages Visual Language Models to infer diverse human intentions and execute complex mobile manipulation tasks. The system achieved 88.9% task success, reduced human workload, and improved user satisfaction compared to baselines, demonstrating generalized intent inference in open-ended environments.

## Problem

- Existing assistive teleoperation methods are limited to simple, predefined tasks or closed sets of objects, struggling to generalize to novel environments or diverse human goals.
- Current systems lack commonsense reasoning, making them unable to interpret nuanced human control inputs within a rich visual context and often leading to misinterpretations.
- Supporting long-horizon, multi-step mobile manipulation tasks that involve a wide range of objects and interactions remains a challenge for shared-control robots.

## Method

- CASPER integrates pre-trained ==Visual Language Models (VLMs)== for open-world understanding and commonsense reasoning to infer diverse human intentions in real time.
- An intent inference module continuously generates open-set intent candidates using VLMs and selects the most likely intent, enhanced by visual prompting and a confidence estimation mechanism.
- A parameterized skill library with 8 behaviors, combined with open-world perception, enables autonomous execution of diverse, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks upon user confirmation.

## Results

- CASPER achieved an average **88.9%** task success rate, outperforming full teleoperation (**76.4%**) and baseline assistive methods (**40.3**-**45.0%**) in real-world mobile manipulation tasks.
- The system significantly reduced perceived cognitive and physical workload (NASA-TLX) and improved user satisfaction across all 10 metrics compared to baselines (p < **0.05**).
- Offline unit tests confirmed CASPER's superior intent inference accuracy, with visual prompting improving success by **5.7%** and confidence estimation reducing false prediction rates across all history lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual Language Models can effectively infer diverse and open-ended human intentions in real-time by leveraging commonsense reasoning from visual context and user inputs.
> - Visual prompting, such as highlighting gripper position and motion history, significantly enhances VLM comprehension and intent inference accuracy in cluttered robotic environments.
> - A self-consistency-based confidence estimation module is crucial for filtering ambiguous VLM predictions, deferring assistance when intent is unclear, and thereby reducing incorrect suggestions and improving user trust.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge within the domain of assistive teleoperation, a paradigm where control is shared between a human operator and a robotic system. This approach is positioned as a balance between full teleoperation, which imposes high cognitive load on users, and fully autonomous robots, which may not align with nuanced human intentions. The broader research landscape increasingly emphasizes deploying robots in human-centric, unstructured environments, such as households, which necessitates systems capable of flexible and intuitive human-robot collaboration.

Existing assistive teleoperation methods are identified as having limitations that restrict their applicability in real-world scenarios. These limitations include being confined to simple, predefined tasks, operating within closed sets of objects, or being bound by specific task-specific data distributions encountered during training. Consequently, these systems often struggle to generalize to novel environments or interpret diverse, open-ended human goals. A critical gap in current methods is the lack of commonsense reasoning, which is essential for understanding human control inputs within a rich visual context and for generalizing intent inference to new scenes and behaviors.

CASPER (Commonsense-Aware Shared-control for Perception, Execution, and Reasoning) integrates pre-trained Visual Language Models (VLMs) to introduce commonsense knowledge and open-world understanding into assistive teleoperation. This positions the work as an advancement that aims to bridge the gap between limited, pre-programmed robotic assistance and the dynamic, context-dependent nature of human intentions in complex environments. By leveraging foundation models, CASPER attempts to move assistive teleoperation towards supporting long-horizon, mobile manipulation tasks that involve a wide range of objects and interactions, thereby enhancing both user agency and task performance in human-robot collaborative settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate an assistive teleoperation system, CASPER, that can infer a diverse range of human intentions from user control inputs in real time and subsequently assist users by executing correct and appropriate robotic actions for complex, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current assistive teleoperation systems:
- **Inferring Diverse and Open-ended Intents:** A fundamental challenge in assistive teleoperation is the robot's ability to accurately infer human intentions from user control inputs. In real-world environments, these intentions are often open-ended, involving a wide variety of objects, tasks, and contexts, which goes beyond the capabilities of methods limited to closed-set prediction or predefined scenarios.
- **Lack of Commonsense Reasoning:** Human intentions are frequently nuanced and context-dependent. For instance, a robot moving a jar of pasta near both a laptop and a cooking pot is likely intended to pour pasta into the pot, not onto the laptop, even if the latter is closer. Existing systems often lack the commonsense reasoning required to interpret such contextual cues, leading to misinterpretations and ineffective assistance.
- **Generalization to Novel Scenarios:** Current data-driven or rule-driven intent inference methods are often task-specific or limited by their training data distributions. This inhibits their ability to generalize to novel objects, scenes, or interaction styles, thereby restricting their utility in varied real-world settings.
- **Support for Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation:** Real-world assistive tasks typically involve multiple steps, encompassing both navigation and diverse contact-rich manipulation behaviors. Traditional assistive teleoperation systems often focus on single skill types (e.g., picking), failing to provide comprehensive support for complex, multi-stage tasks.
- **Minimizing User Disruption and Cognitive Load:** Premature or incorrect robot assistance, based on ambiguous or incomplete human inputs, can frustrate users, disrupt their control flow, and increase their cognitive load. A crucial motivation is to design a system that offers assistance only when confident, operating unobtrusively in the background.

To address these motivations, CASPER aims to integrate open-world perception for generalized scene understanding, VLM-powered commonsense reasoning for intent inference, and a flexible, parameterized skill library for executing a broad spectrum of mobile manipulation tasks. The system is motivated by the goal of enhancing task performance, reducing human cognitive load, and increasing user satisfaction in human-robot collaborative settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CASPER is structured around two primary modules: an intent inference module and a skill execution module, which operate in parallel with human teleoperation.

**Problem Formulation:**
Assistive teleoperation is formalized as a sequential decision-making problem. The robot's state (`s`) includes an RGB image, proprioceptive data (gripper status, base/end-effector poses), and a list of detected foreground objects (`O`). Actions (`a`) originate either from the human (`a_h`) during teleoperation or the robot (`a_r`) during autonomous execution. Each episode consists of one-step subtasks, where a human intent (`z_i = (l_i_z, o_i_z)`) for the i-th subtask specifies an intended skill (`l_i_z`) and a target object (`o_i_z`). CASPER's goal is to infer `z_i` from a snippet of human teleoperation trajectory (`ξ_T_h`) and execute a robot trajectory (`ξ_r`) to fulfill that intent.

**Intent Inference Module (Background Operation):**
This module continuously predicts human intent while the user controls the robot.
- **Intent Candidates Generation:** An open set of potential intent options (`{c_1..., c_m}`) is generated by a Visual Language Model (VLM), `f_candidate`. This VLM analyzes the current state (`s_t`) to identify actionable objects and filters feasible object-skill pairs based on object affordances and the robot's current state (e.g., not suggesting "place" if the gripper is empty). This zero-shot reasoning ensures that generated intents are semantically plausible and relevant to the visual scene.
- **Human Intent Selection:** Given the generated candidates, a second VLM, `f_intent`, predicts the most likely user intent (`hat{z}`). This VLM analyzes a history of subsampled robot observations, including downsized images and robot base/end-effector poses. To enhance VLM comprehension in cluttered environments, visual prompting is employed, which includes annotating segmented objects with Set-of-Marks, highlighting gripper position with masks, and indicating gripper motion history with arrows. This provides the VLM with contextual information relevant to the user's focus.
- **VLM Confidence Estimation:** To mitigate user disruption from premature or incorrect suggestions, CASPER uses a confidence-based validation mechanism. This method, inspired by self-consistency in LLMs, involves making multiple parallel VLM calls (K). The system offers assistance only if the number of VLM outputs agreeing on the most frequent prediction (`hat{z}_mode`) exceeds a predefined threshold (`η`). This mechanism allows the system to defer intervention when human intent is ambiguous.
- **Parallel Foreground-Background System Design:** To address the latency of VLM inference, CASPER is designed to operate with the VLM processing inputs in the background while the user teleoperates in the foreground. If the VLM is still processing or its confidence is low, the system remains silent. Assistance is triggered only when a confident prediction is made, minimizing disruption to the user's control flow.

**Fulfilling Intents with Skill Execution:**
Once an intent is confidently inferred and confirmed by the user, CASPER transitions to autonomous execution.
- **Control Switching:** When a confident prediction is available, the robot provides an audible suggestion. The user can confirm or deny the action via keyboard input. Confirmation initiates autonomous execution, while denial allows the user to continue teleoperating.
- **Parameterized Skill Library:** CASPER employs a library of 8 parameterized skills to handle diverse, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks. These skills cover various behaviors, including object manipulation (e.g., `PickUp[Obj.]`, `Place[Obj.]`, `Pour[Obj.]`), environment interaction (e.g., `PushDoor`, `TapCard`, `PressButton`), and navigation (e.g., `GoToLandmark`, `NavigateToPoint`). Each skill is defined by a behavior primitive and a parameter, allowing flexible application. The system utilizes open-world perception (GSAM and GroundingDINO) to identify target objects. Low-level actions for arm control are generated via Inverse Kinematics, and base navigation uses motion planning on a 2D occupancy map.
- **Skill Parameter Selection and Execution:** After user confirmation, a VLM (`f_skill`) identifies the specific parameters required for the confirmed skill (e.g., the pose of the target object `o_hat{z}`). The skill execution module then performs the specified action. Upon subtask completion, the robot prompts the user to resume control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CASPER involved extensive empirical studies, including human studies, offline unit testing of the intent inference module, and ablation studies, using a TIAGo mobile manipulator.

**User Study: Real-World Mobile Manipulation Tasks (RQ1)**
- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a TIAGo robot using a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse for teleoperation, with real-time RGB images and GPT-4o as the VLM backbone.
- **Tasks:** Three multi-step mobile manipulation tasks were designed: "Shelf" (3 steps), "Toy" (5 steps), and "Door" (2 steps with 3 variations), requiring the robot to disambiguate user intent among multiple plausible options.
- **Participants:** N=13 able-bodied participants.
- **Baselines:** Full Teleoperation (manual control), HAT (assistive teleoperation using proximity), and RBII (assistive teleoperation using Bayesian inference from temporal inputs). HAT and RBII were modified to predict only target objects, while CASPER predicted both skill and object for a more conservative comparison.
- **Metrics:** Task success rate, task completion time, NASA-TLX for subjective workload, and a user satisfaction questionnaire.
- **Results:**
 - **Task Performance:** CASPER achieved significantly higher task success rates (average 88.9%) compared to Full Teleoperation (76.4%), HAT (40.3%), and RBII (45.0%) (p < 0.05). It also demonstrated the lowest average task completion time (218.5s) across all tasks. Baselines struggled with tasks requiring context or commonsense.
 - **NASA-TLX:** CASPER significantly reduced perceived cognitive and physical workload compared to Full Teleoperation (all metrics except "performance") and RBII (all metrics) (p < 0.05). It also outperformed HAT in most metrics, indicating lower user burden.
 - **User Satisfaction:** CASPER showed statistically significant improvements across all 10 user satisfaction metrics compared to all baselines (p < 0.05), suggesting an enhanced user experience and simplified assistance process.
 - **Qualitative Feedback:** Participant interviews indicated CASPER was generally preferred for its ease of use and reliability. Full Teleoperation was viewed as precise but demanding. HAT and RBII were described as unreliable and inconsistent, leading to frustration and reduced trust.

**Unit Testing: Intent Inference Accuracy (RQ2)**
- **Methodology:** Offline unit tests were conducted on teleoperation segments collected from the subtasks. Success was defined by correctly predicting both the intended skill and target object. CASPER was compared against HAT and two variants of RBII.
- **Results:** CASPER demonstrated superior intent inference success rates compared to HAT and RBII baselines (Fig. 5 Left). Baselines frequently mispredicted targets by relying on proximity or gripper motion trends without incorporating commonsense reasoning (e.g., attempting to pour pasta into a sweetener box instead of a cooking pan). CASPER's VLM-based inference leveraged commonsense to achieve more accurate predictions.

**Ablation Studies (RQ3)**
- **Impact of Visual Prompting (VP):** Integrating visual prompting (gripper mask, motion arrow) into the VLM input for CASPER yielded an average 5.7% increase in intent inference success rate, with a notable +9.8% improvement in the "Toy" task (Fig. 5 Left). This suggests visual prompting enhances the VLM's attention and reasoning, although the primary gains are attributed to the VLM's inherent commonsense capabilities.
- **Required Human Teleoperation History Length:** The accuracy of intent inference was observed to improve with longer segments of human teleoperation history. Accuracy plateaued beyond approximately 100 timesteps (Fig. 5 Middle), indicating diminishing returns for further increases in history length while potentially increasing user burden. A history length of T=100 was selected for the user study.
- **Role of Confidence Estimation:** Ablating the self-consistency-based confidence estimation module resulted in consistently higher false prediction rates across all history lengths (Fig. 5 Right). This finding indicates that the confidence module effectively filters out ambiguous cases, deferring predictions when intent is unclear and thereby reducing incorrect suggestions and improving overall system reliability (Fig. 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CASPER system represents a notable development in assistive teleoperation by leveraging the capabilities of Visual Language Models (VLMs) for real-time intent inference and flexible skill execution. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Intent Inference for Assistive Teleoperation:** CASPER addresses a long-standing challenge by enabling robots to infer diverse human intentions in open-ended, unstructured environments. Unlike previous methods often confined to closed-set problems or specific task distributions, CASPER's VLM-powered commonsense reasoning allows for a more generalized understanding of novel objects and complex scenarios. This capability is critical for moving assistive teleoperation beyond constrained laboratory settings.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** By accurately predicting and fulfilling diverse human intents, CASPER contributes to more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration. The empirical results demonstrate that the system reduces human cognitive and physical workload while increasing user satisfaction and task success rates. This makes human-robot interaction more efficient and less burdensome, fostering a more natural partnership.
- **Broader Applicability of Mobile Manipulators:** The system's open-world perception and flexible library of parameterized skills enable it to handle a wider range of long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks. This expands the potential applications of assistive robots in human-centric environments such as homes, hospitals, or workplaces, where robots need to perform varied tasks involving both navigation and fine manipulation.
- **Reliable and User-Centric Assistance:** The integration of a confidence estimation module is significant as it allows the robot to offer assistance only when predictions are robust, minimizing disruptive interventions based on ambiguous inputs. This parallel foreground-background design contributes to a more reliable and user-friendly experience, building user trust in the robot's assistance capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Robotics Systems:** CASPER's architecture, which combines advanced VLMs with a modular skill library and a confidence-aware inference mechanism, provides a framework for integrating large foundation models into real-time shared control systems. This approach could serve as a foundation for developing robots capable of continuous learning from human interactions and adapting to evolving user needs and environmental conditions.
- **Insights into VLM Application in Robotics:** The ablation studies highlight the benefits of components like visual prompting and the amount of teleoperation history required for accurate VLM-based intent inference, providing valuable insights for future research in applying VLMs to embodied robotics.

The study acknowledges limitations, including the current focus on able-bodied users and the scope of supported intents. Addressing these in future work, such as involving users with motor or cognitive disabilities and supporting finer-grained expressive behaviors, will further enhance the system's impact and generalizability.
