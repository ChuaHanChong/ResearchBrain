# RoboSafe: Safeguarding Embodied Agents via Executable Safety Logic

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboSafe is a runtime safety guardrail for VLM-driven embodied agents that converts safety reasoning into executable Python predicates. A guardrail VLM queries a hybrid long-short memory: backward reflective reasoning checks recent trajectories for temporal violations and triggers replanning, while forward predictive reasoning retrieves safety knowledge to block contextually hazardous actions. It cuts risky execution by 36.8% relative to leading baselines while keeping benign task success near original.

## Problem

- Training-time safety alignment for embodied agents demands costly data collection and compute, so downstream deployers who cannot touch the backbone model have no practical lever.
- Existing runtime guardrails use static rule filters or safety prompts, missing contextual risks where a benign action turns hazardous only under specific unobserved environment states.
- Prior defenses also ignore temporal risks spanning action sequences, and aggressive model-based interceptors over-block benign steps, collapsing long-horizon task performance.

## Method

- ==Forward Predictive Reasoning== builds a long-term safety memory of decoupled reasoning demonstrations plus ==executable predicates==, retrieved by a ==label-balanced multi-grained scorer== blending coarse context and fine-grained action embeddings.
- ==Backward Reflective Reasoning== infers prerequisite, obligation and adjacency ==temporal predicates== from the instruction, then verifies them against short-term trajectory memory and inserts corrective replanning actions within a step window.
- Both predicate sets assemble into code the ==Python interpreter== executes as a lightweight verifier, emitting block or replan; successfully executed entries write back into long-term memory for lifelong accumulation.

## Results

- On SafeAgentBench contextual unsafe instructions, RoboSafe averaged **89.89%** accurate refusal and **4.78%** hazardous execution across ProgPrompt, ReAct and Reflexion, versus **2.33%** refusal and **84.11%** execution undefended.
- On long-horizon temporal unsafe tasks it reached **36.67%** safe planning and **32.00%** execution, over triple the undefended **10.00%** and **8.00%**, while ThinkSafe dropped safe planning to **6.00%** by over-blocking.
- Benign task success held at **89.00%** average, only **7.22** points below undefended agents; contextual jailbreak execution fell to **5.22%**, and runtime overhead matched the lightest baseline at **0.15** seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling safety into natural-language reasoning plus executable predicates keeps a VLM monitor interpretable and cheap, since verification runs as deterministic code rather than repeated model judgment.
> - Guardrail quality is a two-sided metric: the strongest blocker, ThinkSafe, destroyed benign performance, so report false-positive cost alongside refusal rate.
> - Grounding monitoring in observations and trajectory rather than the agent's prompt makes the defense largely immune to prompt-level jailbreaks that compromise the policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "RoboSafe" is situated at the crucial intersection of embodied AI, large vision-language models (VLMs), and AI safety. In recent years, VLM-driven embodied agents have achieved remarkable progress, demonstrating impressive capabilities in executing complex, long-horizon tasks within interactive and dynamic real-world environments. These agents leverage the powerful reasoning and planning abilities of VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-flash) to understand abstract multimodal inputs and translate them into executable, multi-step plans that affect the physical world. This evolution has shifted the paradigm from text-centric LLM planners to more sophisticated multimodal agents that can process and act upon rich visual information.

However, this increased capability comes with a critical vulnerability: the potential for these agents to execute hazardous instructions, leading to immediate and irreversible real-world safety threats. Unlike traditional LLMs where harmful outputs are confined to textual generation, embodied agents can translate unsafe commands into physical actions, such as throwing objects, causing damage, or creating unsafe environmental conditions. This amplification of risk necessitates robust safety mechanisms.

The broader research landscape has seen various approaches to address this safety challenge. **Training-time strategies** involve costly data collection and extensive computational resources to imbue models with safety alignment during their development. In contrast, **runtime safety guardrails** offer a more flexible and lightweight solution by monitoring and intercepting agent actions during inference, thereby bypassing the need for retraining the core model. Existing runtime defenses, such as ThinkSafe, POEX, AgentSpec, and Pro2Guard, typically rely on pre-defined static rules or prompt-level control mechanisms. While these methods are effective against explicitly stated hazardous instructions, they struggle significantly with *implicit risks* that arise in dynamic, temporally dependent, and context-rich environments. These limitations create a critical gap in ensuring truly safe and reliable embodied agent systems.

RoboSafe distinguishes itself by specifically targeting this gap. It moves beyond static filters and prompt-based adjustments by introducing a hybrid reasoning framework that can infer and verify both contextual and temporal safety logic dynamically. This approach positions RoboSafe as a significant advancement in the field of AI safety, providing a more adaptive and robust defense mechanism crucial for the responsible deployment of increasingly capable embodied agents in diverse real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RoboSafe stems from the recognition that while VLM-driven embodied agents are becoming highly capable, their deployment in real-world settings is severely hampered by their vulnerability to hazardous instructions. Existing safety mechanisms fall short in addressing the nuanced and dynamic nature of real-world risks.

The key objectives of RoboSafe are to:

1. **Mitigate Implicit Contextual Risks:** Address scenarios where an action, seemingly benign in isolation, becomes hazardous due to the immediate, specific environmental context. For example, "turn on the microwave" is safe with food but dangerous with a metal fork inside. Current static rules or prompt-level controls often fail to detect such context-dependent hazards.
2. **Mitigate Implicit Temporal Risks:** Address hazards that emerge not from a single action, but from an unsafe sequence of actions over time. An example is "leaving a hot stove overheating for a long time without turning it off." These multi-step, time-dependent risks are often overlooked by existing defenses that focus on immediate action verification.
3. **Develop an Adaptive and Verifiable Safety Logic:** Create a safety guardrail framework that can dynamically reason about safety in changing environments and generate interpretable, executable safety logic (as code) for robust and reliable verification. This ensures transparency and allows for a clearer understanding of why an action is deemed safe or unsafe.
4. **Maintain High Task Performance:** Ensure that the safety guardrail does not unduly interfere with the agent's ability to complete benign tasks, thereby achieving an optimal balance between safety and utility. Unnecessary intervention or high false-positive rates would render the defense impractical.
5. **Demonstrate Practicality and Robustness:** Validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework through extensive experiments across multiple agent architectures in simulated environments, and crucially, confirm its practicality through real-world evaluations on physical robotic arms and against advanced adversarial attacks like jailbreaks.

By achieving these objectives, RoboSafe aims to bridge the critical gap in embodied agent safety, enabling their more secure and trustworthy operation in complex and dynamic physical environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboSafe proposes a novel hybrid reasoning runtime safeguard that leverages executable predicate-based safety logic, integrating a guardrail VLM with a Hybrid Long-Short Safety Memory. The framework is designed to perform a bidirectional reasoning process to verify and mitigate implicit risks dynamically.

The core components of RoboSafe's methodology are:

#### 3.1. Hybrid Long-Short Safety Memory

The system utilizes two types of memory:
- **Long-Term Knowledge Memory ($\mathcal{M}_L$):** This memory stores lifelong safety experiences, structured as knowledge. It comprises high-level reasoning demonstrations ($\rho_t$) and corresponding low-level, executable logical predicates ($\Phi_t$). This decoupling allows for nuanced reasoning guided by demonstrations while ensuring reliable, code-based verification.
- **Short-Term Working Memory ($\mathcal{M}_S$):** This memory stores the agent's recent trajectory ($\tau$) for the current instruction, enabling reflection on past actions and states.

#### 3.2. Forward Predictive Reasoning (for Contextual Risks)

This module focuses on preventing immediate, context-aware risks by anticipating upcoming actions based on multimodal contextual reasoning and retrieved safety knowledge.

- **Safety Knowledge Generation:** Initial safety knowledge is generated by a powerful language model using few-shot examples from a training set (SafeAgentBench). This knowledge is structured into: 1) human-readable safety reasoning demonstrations, and 2) low-level, executable logical predicates (e.g., checking object properties or states).
- **Multi-grained Safety Knowledge Retrieval:** At each timestep, the guardrail VLM extracts visual information (objects, states, materials) and integrates it with the agent's ego-state to form a multimodal observation ($o_t$). A multi-grained retrieval strategy then queries $\mathcal{M}_L$ using both coarse-grained context (observation, instruction $T$, recent behavior $\mathcal{M}_S$) and fine-grained action-level context ($a_t$). A label-balanced similarity metric is used to retrieve the most relevant safety experiences, mitigating bias towards common "benign" examples.
- **Contextual Logic Verification:** Using the retrieved safety experiences as in-context knowledge, the guardrail VLM performs safety reasoning ($ \rho_t$) over $o_t$. This reasoning guides the generation of verifiable low-level logical predicates ($\Phi_t$), which are then executed by a Python interpreter. If any predicate in $\Phi_t$ detects a risk, a `block` action is triggered, immediately preventing the hazardous action ($a_t$).
- **Memory Update:** The $\mathcal{M}_L$ is iteratively updated with new, verified, and error-free safety experiences, fostering autonomous learning of safety knowledge.

#### 3.3. Backward Reflective Reasoning (for Temporal Risks)

This module is designed to mitigate implicit temporal risks by enforcing multi-step temporal requirements through continuous reflection on the short-term memory ($\mathcal{M}_S$).

- **Temporal Predicate Classification:** Temporal safety requirements are categorized into three verifiable predicate types:
 - **Prerequisite predicate ($\psi_p$):** Ensures a required action has occurred before a dependent, risky action.
 - **Obligation predicate ($\psi_o$):** Verifies that a risky "trigger" action is followed by a "response" action within a specified step window (e.g., turning off a stove after turning it on).
 - **Adjacency predicate ($\psi_a$):** Enforces immediate response actions after a trigger to prevent unsafe intermediate states.
- **Temporal Predicate Inference:** At the start of a task, the guardrail VLM infers safety-aware sequential dependencies from the global instruction ($T$) and decomposes them into initial temporal predicate instances ($\Psi$), each parameterized by type, trigger/response actions, and a step window.
- **Temporal Logic Verification:** Before each action $a_t$, the guardrail identifies active predicates ($\Psi_t$) whose trigger actions match $a_t$. It then continuously reflects on $\mathcal{M}_S$ to verify the backward temporal logic ($L_b(·)$). If a temporal predicate is violated, a `replan` action is triggered. This proactively inserts a corrective action sequence (the "response action") into the agent's plan, guiding it to mitigate the hazard and then resume its original trajectory.

#### 3.4. Executable Safety Logic and Guardrail Scope

Both forward and backward reasoning processes culminate in the generation of interpretable and executable safety logic. A Python interpreter acts as a lightweight verifier, executing this code. The guardrail treats the embodied agent as a black-box system, with its knowledge limited to the global instruction ($T$), current observation ($o_t$), and the agent's proposed action ($a_t$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboSafe's effectiveness was rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments across various embodied agent architectures (ReAct, ProgPrompt, Reflexion) in the AI2-THOR simulator, against leading baselines, and further validated on a physical robotic arm.

#### 4.1. Performance on Unsafe Instructions

- **Contextual Risks (Table 1):**
 - **Vulnerability of Original Agents:** Agents without any guardrail were highly vulnerable to immediate contextual hazards, with a negligible Accurate Refusal Rate (ARR) of 2.33% and a catastrophic Execution Success Rate (ESR) for hazardous actions of 84.11%.
 - **RoboSafe's Superiority:** RoboSafe demonstrated significantly superior defense, achieving the highest ARR of 89.89% and the lowest ESR of 4.78% (average across agents). This highlights its precision in identifying and preventing context-aware risks.
 - **Baseline Limitations:** While ThinkSafe was the strongest baseline (ESR 7.56%), RoboSafe's higher ARR (89.89% vs. 86.67% average for ThinkSafe) indicated more accurate reasoning. Other baselines (Poex, AgentSpec, GuardAgent) were largely ineffective due to static rules or insufficient contextual analysis.

- **Temporal Risks (Table 2):**
 - **Difficulty of Long-Horizon Tasks:** Original agents struggled with long-horizon tasks requiring temporal constraints, exhibiting a low Safe Planning Rate (SPR) of 10% and an ESR of 8%.
 - **RoboSafe's Profound Mitigation:** RoboSafe achieved an average SPR of 36.67% and an ESR of 32%, representing a more than threefold improvement over agents without guardrails. This confirms its ability to identify temporal violations and proactively guide agents to complete complex tasks safely via replanning.
 - **Baseline Failures:** All baseline approaches failed significantly on temporal risks (SPR/ESR around 10%), either by being overly sensitive (ThinkSafe, blocking benign actions) or by lacking the capacity to understand temporal hazards (Poex, AgentSpec).

#### 4.2. Performance on Safe Instructions (Table 3)

An ideal guardrail should prevent hazards without hindering benign task performance.
- **RoboSafe's Performance Preservation:** RoboSafe maintained excellent performance on benign tasks, achieving a high average ESR of 89.00%. This represents only a minimal and consistent degradation (-7.22% on average) compared to original agents, demonstrating precise intervention without unnecessary interference.
- **Baseline Trade-offs:** ThinkSafe, despite its strength in contextual risk mitigation, suffered a severe drop in ESR (24.11%) on safe tasks due to a high false-positive rate. AgentSpec showed high preservation (ESR 91.22%) but failed on unsafe tasks, illustrating the limitations of static rules.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Figure 5)

- **Guardrail VLM Choice:** Gemini-2.5-flash was identified as the most effective guardrail VLM, yielding the highest ARR (92.33%) and best ESR on safe tasks (90.33%), attributed to its strong scene understanding.
- **Hyperparameter $\lambda$ (trade-off factor for retrieval):** A $\lambda$ value of 0.6 provided the optimal balance between ARR on unsafe instructions and ESR on safe instructions.

#### 4.4. Defense Against Jailbreak Attacks (Table 4)

- RoboSafe proved highly robust against contextual jailbreak attacks, suppressing the average ESR to only 5.22%. This significant outperformance (+45.75% compared to other defenses) is attributed to RoboSafe's reasoning being grounded in objective observations and trajectory, making it independent of compromised prompts.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis (Table 5)

- RoboSafe introduced negligible time cost, matching the most lightweight baselines and remaining comparable to the original agent's execution time. This efficiency stems from generating single, lightweight safety logic for each action.

#### 4.6. Physical World Case Study (Figure 6)

- Real-world evaluations on a 6-DoF myCobot 280-Pi manipulator demonstrated RoboSafe's practicality. It successfully prevented hazardous actions like "wielding a knife in the air" and "dropping a wooden cube to smash a lying person" by blocking the risky actions after detection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RoboSafe represents a significant advancement in the field of AI safety, particularly for embodied agents operating in dynamic, open-world environments. Its contributions carry substantial potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Safety for Embodied AI:** RoboSafe directly addresses a critical and often overlooked vulnerability: implicit contextual and temporal risks. By effectively mitigating these complex hazards, it makes VLM-driven embodied agents significantly safer for real-world deployment, reducing the potential for physical damage, injury, or undesirable outcomes. This is crucial for applications ranging from household robots to industrial automation and autonomous vehicles.

2. **Bridging the Gap in Runtime Guardrails:** Existing runtime defenses often fall short in dynamic, context-rich scenarios. RoboSafe's hybrid reasoning framework, combining forward predictive reasoning for contextual risks and backward reflective reasoning for temporal risks, effectively fills this gap. It pushes beyond static rule-based systems to enable adaptive and intelligent safety verification.

3. **Interpretable and Executable Safety Logic:** The generation of predicate-based, executable safety logic as code is a major strength. This approach enhances the transparency and verifiability of the safety mechanism, allowing developers and regulators to understand *why* an action was blocked or replanned. Such interpretability is vital for building trust in safety-critical AI systems.

4. **Proactive Risk Mitigation:** The Backward Reflective Reasoning module's ability to trigger `replan` actions for temporal risks is a powerful feature. Instead of merely reacting by blocking a dangerous action, RoboSafe can proactively guide the agent to perform corrective steps, thereby preventing a hazardous sequence from unfolding. This proactive capability is superior to purely reactive defenses.

5. **Robustness Against Diverse Threats:** The demonstrated effectiveness across multiple agent architectures, against various types of unsafe instructions (contextual, temporal), and even against sophisticated jailbreak attacks, underscores RoboSafe's robustness. This makes it a versatile defense mechanism capable of handling unforeseen adversarial manipulations.

6. **Practicality for Real-World Deployment:** The validation on a physical robotic arm, coupled with its negligible runtime overhead, confirms RoboSafe's practicality. This low computational cost makes it feasible for integration into resource-constrained robotic systems without significantly impeding performance.

7. **Foundation for Trustworthy AI:** By improving the safety and reliability of embodied agents, RoboSafe contributes directly to the broader goal of developing trustworthy AI systems. As AI increasingly interacts with the physical world, robust safety guardrails like RoboSafe are indispensable for fostering public confidence and enabling responsible innovation.

In conclusion, RoboSafe offers a novel and highly effective solution to a pressing challenge in AI. Its hybrid reasoning, interpretable logic, and proven practicality position it as a foundational work that could significantly influence the future design and deployment of safe and reliable embodied artificial intelligence. Future work, as highlighted by the authors, will focus on generalizing to more diverse robotic platforms, scaling to more complex tasks, and enhancing robustness against even more unforeseen adversarial attacks.
