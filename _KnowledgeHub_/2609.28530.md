# Know Your Body: A Harness for Direct and Self-Improving Robot Control with VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> KnowBody is a harness that gives a frozen vision-language model an explicit, queryable, partial body model: supported motion-to-image response, functional-part geometry, and full-arm path feasibility, initialized from a single off-task trajectory and revised by validated interaction evidence, with rules rechecked when their body dependencies change. On four real-robot tasks it completes 75% of fixed-budget trials versus 25% for the native Codex harness, using fewer planner rounds.

## Problem

- General vision-language harnesses carry broad task knowledge but no account of how a specific robot's motions and functional parts produce the intended physical effects.
- Trained visuomotor policies and demonstration-conditioned methods fix embodiment in weights or task-matched demos, so a new tool or robot needs retraining or a matching demonstration.
- Existing memory and reflection methods reuse past interactions unchanged, so a corrected body estimate leaves stored rules resting on stale geometric conditions.

## Method

- ==Versioned partial body model== storing physical configuration, permanent functional geometry, motion-observation response, supported operating domain and validation provenance, initialized from one off-task trajectory; queries outside the domain return unknown.
- ==Body-conditioned queries==: ==pseudoinverse image-Jacobian== direction solve, functional-part offset conversion mapping a desired tool-tip target to a commanded pose, and full-arm path check separating endpoint reachability from continuous feasibility.
- ==Dependency-aware revision lane== where candidate body and rule updates are frozen, validated on independent interactions, and any rule sharing a revised body component is refit and withheld until revalidated.

## Results

- Across 32 fixed-budget trials on four real-robot tasks, **12/16** (**75%**) completion versus **4/16** (**25%**) for the native Codex harness under the same frozen model.
- On the three tasks both completed, mean planner rounds for successes drop **17-44%**: **27.0** to **15.3** for duck placement, **69.0** to **45.3** for writing.
- With persistent updates, rounds fall **29-53%** from first to fifth success (pushing **19** to **9**); a DROID-trained policy hit **19/20** on duck but **0/20** on writing and pouring.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frozen-weight adaptation can live entirely in an external versioned body representation, so capability gains need not come from retraining the policy or the model.
> - A partial model that returns unknown outside its supported domain avoids silent overreach, and turns missing geometry into a cue to gather more observation.
> - Stored experience carries geometric dependencies: correcting a body estimate reinterprets past interactions, so memory needs dependency tracking, not just an outcome verification loop.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

KnowBody targets a gap in robot manipulation. A vision-language model (VLM) may recognize the intended effect, yet the command it can issue refers to a robot frame rather than to the part that touches the world. The paper's motivating example is writing with a held pen: the robot is commanded through an end-effector pose, but the pen tip makes the mark. This example illustrates the problem; it does not show that the robot always writes successfully.

KnowBody gives a frozen model a way to query how this robot's motion and functional parts relate to commanded movement. It does this without changing the VLM's weights. With the same frozen GPT-6 VLM, KnowBody completes 12 of 16 real-robot trials (75%), compared with 4 of 16 (25%) for the native Codex harness.

Author overview video of the KnowBody system.

### 2. A body model between command and effect

The system starts from a single off-task trajectory. During fixed-budget evaluation, that trajectory was grasping bread and placing it in a basket. It seeds supported motion-response relations; it is not a task-specific sequence to imitate. Functional geometry is populated separately, by independently versioned measurements alongside declared hardware information. The VLM reasons about the task, queries relevant relations, proposes commands, and receives execution feedback.

Three query types show what "body knowledge" means here:

- relating achieved robot motion to a change in the wrist-camera image;
- combining permanent end-effector geometry with the current attachment to locate the active tool tip;
- distinguishing a reachable endpoint from a continuous feasible whole-arm path.

These are partial, evidence-supported relations, not a complete dynamics model. Unsupported queries return "unknown." Path queries and the executor's checks do not certify unmeasured scene clearance.

(a) Before/after wrist-camera views with displacement marks relate motion to image shift. (b) A commanded frame and the active tip on a held marker. (c) A sequentially checked path is continuous (green); an endpoint-only alternative has an invalid intermediate branch (red). These are visual explanations of the queries, not experimental results.

KnowBody also separates current episode state from reusable knowledge. Fresh observations update the scene and attachment state. Reusable body estimates and action-effect rules require validation. A correction to body geometry can change the conditions assigned to an unchanged historical interaction. So when a body estimate changes, the harness recomputes affected rule conditions from the original observations. It withholds those rules until independent revalidation passes. Rules that do not depend on the changed component can remain eligible. This is the proposed validity mechanism; the task-success comparison does not establish it.

### 3. What the trials establish

The fixed-budget study used a Franka Research 3 with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and the same frozen GPT-6 VLM at high reasoning effort. Objects were randomly repositioned, and each method ran four trials per task. The tasks were duck placement, apple pushing, writing, and pouring, with reasoning-round limits of 30, 30, 80, and 100. A round is a model decision, not a robot-motion segment. Completion means finishing autonomously within the assigned budget. KnowBody started each trial from the same initialization, with cross-episode learning disabled. Improvement appears on all four tasks.

This comparison tests the combined harness-and-reference design. It cannot attribute the gain specifically to body queries, the reference initialization, or another harness component.

Upper right: reasoning rounds by successful-trial index, where writing rises at its last point; failures between recorded successes are not plotted. Lower right: successes for native Codex versus KnowBody were duck 1/4 vs 4/4, apple 2/4 vs 3/4, writing 1/4 vs 3/4, and pouring 0/4 vs 2/4.

A separate continual protocol enabled persistent updates and ran each task until five successful completions were recorded. Failures between successes could also contribute updates. In apple pushing, rounds fell from 19 at the first recorded success to 9 at the fifth (52.6%). This measures rounds on successful completions, not elapsed time, tokens, or total attempts. Writing does not decline monotonically: its last point rises from 30 to 32 rounds. The curves lack a no-update comparison, and they do not establish which updates caused the change.

### 4. Closing

A robot model needs more than a goal and a movement command; it needs a supported account of how the body turns one into the other. Correcting that account can also change how earlier experience should be interpreted. The established result is that the integrated harness completed more trials within budget. The main boundary is that no ablation isolates the body model itself.
