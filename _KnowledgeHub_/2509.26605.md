# Fine-tuning Behavioral Cloning Policies with Preference-Based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Zurich and ETH AI Center developed BRIDGE, a theoretical framework and algorithm that combines offline expert demonstrations with online human preferences to fine-tune reinforcement learning policies. This work establishes the first theoretical regret bounds for this hybrid approach, quantifying how offline data enhances the sample efficiency and safety of online preference-based learning, outperforming standalone methods.

## Problem

- Reward mis-specification, where translating complex task goals into precise numerical reward functions for RL is difficult and can lead to unintended behaviors.
- Risky and data-hungry exploration, as standard online RL often requires extensive, potentially unsafe interactions with the environment.
- Lack of a rigorous theoretical foundation for the empirically successful paradigm of combining reward-free expert demonstrations with online preference-based feedback.

## Method

- The **BRIDGE** (==Bounded Regret== with Imitation Data and Guided Exploration) algorithm is proposed, a two-stage framework integrating offline behavioral cloning with online preference-based reinforcement learning.
- In the offline stage, an initial policy is learned from expert demonstrations, and a Hellinger-distance-based confidence set is constructed around it, which is theoretically proven to contain the optimal policy with high probability.
- During the online stage, policy refinement through preference queries is strictly constrained to this pre-defined confidence set, guiding exploration efficiently while also leveraging uncertainty in both the preference and transition models.

## Results

- The paper provides the first theoretical framework and regret bounds for offline imitation learning combined with online preference-based fine-tuning, demonstrating an optimal O(\sqrt{T}) dependence on the online horizon and an inverse dependence on the number of offline demonstrations 'n'.
- Empirically, BRIDGE consistently achieves lower cumulative regret and faster policy search space refinement compared to standalone Behavioral Cloning and purely online Preference-Based Reinforcement Learning across discrete and continuous control tasks.
- Ablation studies validate that the size of the offline confidence set and the quantity/quality of offline data are critical factors that directly impact BRIDGE's performance, aligning with theoretical predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Offline expert demonstrations can be theoretically proven to significantly reduce the sample complexity and online regret of subsequent preference-based reinforcement learning.
> - Constructing a Hellinger-distance-based confidence set from offline data provides an effective method for safely bounding the online policy search space, thereby preventing risky exploration.
> - The regret bounds derived establish a precise, quantifiable trade-off between the quantity of offline data and the online query efficiency, indicating that higher quality and quantity of offline data lead to more efficient online learning.
