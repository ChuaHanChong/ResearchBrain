# SkillOS: Learning Skill Curation for Self-Evolving Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillOS, developed by researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Google Cloud AI Research, introduces an experience-driven reinforcement learning framework that enables Large Language Model (LLM) agents to automatically curate reusable skills. This framework facilitates continuous self-evolution, leading to improved task success rates and increased efficiency across diverse agentic and reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- LLM agents often act as "one-off" problem solvers, lacking the ability to accumulate, refine, or reuse knowledge from past interactions in streaming task environments.
- Existing skill curation methods face limitations due to manual effort, rigid heuristics, or short-horizon reinforcement learning (RL) approaches that fail to provide adequate feedback for complex, long-term skill evolution.
- Learning high-quality skill curation policies is challenging because optimal curation decisions have delayed and indirect effects on future task performance, making it difficult to attribute environmental feedback to specific actions.

## Method

- **SkillOS** employs a ==modular, multi-agent design== that decouples a trainable LLM-based ==Skill Curator== (managing an external ==SkillRepo==) from a frozen ==Agent Executor==.
- It introduces an ==experience-driven Reinforcement Learning training recipe== for the Skill Curator, utilizing ==grouped task streams== where skills derived from earlier experiences are evaluated by their utility in solving later, related tasks.
- A ==composite reward function== is designed to attribute downstream executor performance to specific curation decisions, incorporating signals for task outcome, function call validity, skill content quality, and SkillRepo compactness.

## Results

- SkillOS consistently outperforms memory-free and existing memory-based baselines on multi-turn agentic (ALFWorld, WebShop) and single-turn reasoning tasks, improving success rates and efficiency (e.g., **55.7% to 61.2%** success on ALFWorld with Qwen3-8B).
- The learned skill curator generalizes effectively across different executor LLM backbones (e.g., **Qwen3-8B to Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite**) and transfers knowledge across task domains, with reasoning-trained curators showing strong transferability to agentic tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of both the composite reward function and grouped task streams, with the latter leading to the most significant performance degradation when removed.
- Analysis of the SkillRepo's evolution shows a shift from simple skill insertion to frequent refinement (`update_skill`) and the emergence of **"meta-strategy skills"** (e.g., verification, adaptive planning), indicating the curator learns to expand the agent's strategic capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted RL training for skill curation can be more effective than using larger, frontier LLMs as zero-shot curators, as it produces skills better aligned with the executor's specific needs.
> - Structuring training instances into grouped task streams is critical for providing dense, long-horizon feedback, enabling the curator to learn complex, long-term skill management operations like updating and deleting skills.
> - The use of a multi-faceted composite reward function is essential for successfully optimizing skill curation, as it balances various aspects such as task success, operational correctness, skill quality, and repository efficiency.
> - The learned skill curator demonstrates robust generalization capabilities, improving performance for diverse executor models and showing effective knowledge transfer across different task domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a core challenge in the development of Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents: their ability to learn and adapt over time, particularly in streaming, sequential task environments. Current LLM agents are often characterized as "one-off" problem solvers, meaning they tackle each task independently without effectively accumulating or refining knowledge from past interactions. This limits their long-term utility and scalability in dynamic real-world deployments.

The broader research landscape for LLM agents is increasingly focused on self-evolution, which entails agents continually accumulating, refining, and reusing experience. A critical component of self-evolution is procedural memory, often realized through reusable "skills" distilled from past interactions. However, existing approaches to skill curation face significant limitations:
- **Manual Curation:** Requires extensive human expertise and does not scale to the diverse and complex tasks agents encounter.
- **Heuristic-based Curation:** Relies on predefined rules for memory operations, lacking feedback from downstream performance and thus unable to adapt to an agent's specific needs.
- **Short-Horizon Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While some RL approaches exist for skill optimization, they typically focus on short-term adaptation within limited task streams, providing insufficient learning signals for managing complex, long-term skill evolution, such as updating or deleting skills.

SkillOS positions itself by tackling the bottleneck of high-quality skill curation for self-evolving agents. It aims to overcome the limitations of manual, heuristic, and short-horizon RL methods by proposing an experience-driven RL training recipe specifically designed for learning complex, long-term skill curation policies. By doing so, it contributes to the goal of creating LLM agents that can move beyond static task execution towards continuous learning and proficiency improvement in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for LLM-based agents to learn how to effectively curate reusable skills from their own experiences, enabling them to self-evolve and improve performance over a sequence of tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and addresses specific challenges:

1. **Transition from "One-off" to Self-Evolving Agents:** LLM-based agents are increasingly deployed in real-world scenarios where tasks arrive sequentially. The current paradigm of agents solving tasks in isolation ("one-off") is inefficient and prevents them from gaining long-term proficiency. The motivation is to enable agents to continually accumulate, refine, and reuse knowledge, moving towards a paradigm of self-evolution where performance improves over time.

2. **Addressing the Skill Curation Bottleneck:** Reusable skills, accumulated as procedural memory, are identified as a crucial substrate for self-evolution. However, the process of skill curation—extracting high-quality lessons and integrating them into a skill collection—is a significant bottleneck. The objective is to automate and optimize this curation process.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Curation Methods:**
 - **Manual curation** is resource-intensive and unscalable.
 - **Prompting or heuristic-based methods** lack adaptability because they rely on fixed rules and do not incorporate feedback on downstream performance. They cannot learn to meet the executor's actual needs.
 - **Prior RL-based methods** for memory or skill management often focus on short-horizon adaptation, which is inadequate for learning complex operations like revising outdated skills or deleting harmful ones, necessary for robust long-term evolution. The motivation is to create a system that can learn complex, long-term curation policies from indirect and delayed feedback.

4. **Enabling Executor-Grounded Learning:** Curation decisions should be informed by their actual impact on the agent's ability to solve future tasks. The motivation is to provide learning signals that attribute the environmental feedback (task outcomes) to specific curation decisions, which is challenging due to the delayed and indirect nature of such feedback.

To achieve these objectives, SkillOS proposes an experience-driven RL training recipe that:
- Decouples the trainable **Skill Curator** (which manages an external **SkillRepo**) from a frozen **Agent Executor**. This modular design allows the curator to evolve the skill set without retraining the executor.
- Introduces **grouped task streams** during training, where skills derived from earlier experiences within a group are evaluated by their utility in solving later, related tasks. This provides dense, long-horizon feedback for curation.
- Designs **composite rewards** to better attribute downstream executor performance to specific curation decisions, incorporating signals for task outcome, function call validity, skill content quality, and SkillRepo compactness.

In summary, the key objective is to establish a practical, modular, and experience-driven RL framework that empowers LLM-based agents to learn how to curate skills effectively, thereby enabling them to self-evolve and achieve sustained performance improvements in streaming environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkillOS proposes an experience-driven reinforcement learning (RL) training recipe for learning skill curation within self-evolving agents. It employs a modular, multi-agent framework operating in a streaming task setting.

#### 3.1. Streaming Skill Curation with Multi-Agent Modular Design

The system operates in a streaming test-time setting where an LLM-based agent solves a sequence of tasks ($D = \{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_T\}$). The core components are:

- **Skill Repository (SkillRepo, $S_t$):** An external collection of reusable skills. Each skill is represented as a Markdown file, adhering to a format inspired by Anthropic's SKILL.md. This format includes:
 - **YAML frontmatter:** Specifies the skill name and a concise natural-language description (used for retrieval).
 - **Markdown instructions:** Describes executable knowledge, workflows, constraints, and reusable heuristics.
- **Agent Executor ($\pi_L$):** A frozen (untrainable) LLM-based agent responsible for solving tasks. Given a task $x_t$, it retrieves a subset of relevant skills ($\tilde{S}_t \subseteq S_t$) from the SkillRepo using BM25, and then generates actions based on the current environment observation and retrieved skills.
- **Skill Curator ($\pi_S$):** A trainable LLM-based component that manages the SkillRepo. After the executor completes a task $x_t$, the curator observes the execution trajectory ($\xi_t$), the self-judged correctness of the outcome ($1_{\xi_t}$), and the subset of skills used ($\tilde{S}_t$). Based on this information, it generates a sequence of structured curation operations ($c_t = (u_1^t, \ldots, u_M^t)$) to update the SkillRepo. The allowed operations are `insert_skill`, `update_skill`, and `delete_skill`, implemented as function calls that manipulate the Markdown files in the SkillRepo. The updated SkillRepo ($S_{t+1}$) is then used for subsequent tasks, creating a closed-loop self-evolution process.

#### 3.2. Learning Skill Curation with RL

The core of SkillOS is optimizing the skill curator ($\pi_S$) using RL, while the agent executor ($\pi_L$) remains fixed. This addresses the challenge of indirect and delayed feedback for curation decisions.

#### 3.2.1. Training Instance Construction

To provide effective learning signals, training instances are structured as groups of related tasks rather than isolated individual tasks.
- **Attribute Annotation:** Each task $x_i$ in the dataset is first annotated with a set of skill-relevant attributes ($Z_i$), such as topic, skills/capabilities, mathematical concepts, heuristic strategies, and common pitfalls. This is done using Gemini-2.5-Pro to generate short phrases in a structured JSON format. For agentic tasks, default task type annotations are leveraged.
- **Task Grouping:** The dataset is partitioned into $M$ task groups ($D = \{G_1, G_2, \ldots, G_M\}$), where tasks within a group ($G_m = \{x_{m,1}, x_{m,2}, \ldots, x_{m,|G_m|}\}$) exhibit non-trivial dependencies in terms of required skills. This grouping ensures that skills induced from earlier experiences within a group can be evaluated by their utility for later related tasks, providing long-term utility signals. A two-stage pipeline is used for grouping: Stage 1 annotates tasks with latent attributes, and Stage 2 constructs groups by sampling a seed task and iteratively appending related tasks based on phrase similarity, dependency gates (shared concepts/skills, minimal new concepts, curriculum direction), and a scoring function.

#### 3.2.2. Training Loop and Policy Optimization

The training loop for the skill curator employs Grouped Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) for stability and sample efficiency (Algorithm 1).
- **Initialization:** For each training step, a task group $G$ is sampled, and the SkillRepo is initialized as empty.
- **Sequential Execution and Curation:** The system iterates through each task $x_i$ in the group:
 1. Relevant skills ($\tilde{S}$) are retrieved from the current SkillRepo (S) using BM25.
 2. The frozen executor ($\pi_L$) runs the task using these skills, producing a trajectory ($\xi_i$).
 3. The skill curator ($\pi_S$) observes the trajectory and other context, then samples a sequence of curation decisions ($c_i$).
 4. These operations are applied, updating the SkillRepo (S).
- **Composite Reward Function:** After processing all tasks in a group, a composite reward ($r$) is calculated for the entire rollout. This reward function combines four signals:
 - `r_task`: Average success rate over tasks in the group (excluding the first, which starts with an empty SkillRepo). This provides executor-grounded feedback on the long-term utility of curation decisions.
 - `r_fc`: Measures the fraction of valid and successfully executed function calls generated by the curator, rewarding proper use of the skill manipulation interface.
 - `r_comp`: Encourages conciseness by rewarding repository updates that are shorter than the input context, discouraging verbatim copying of trajectories.
 - `r_cnt`: A scalar score from an external judge (Qwen3-32B) that evaluates the semantic meaningfulness and potential usefulness of the curated skills.
- **Policy Update:** For each task group, $N$ independent rollouts are sampled. The GRPO advantage ($A_n$) is computed for each rollout based on its composite reward compared to the average reward of other rollouts. The curator policy ($\pi_S$) is then updated using a clipped surrogate objective to maximize this advantage.

This methodology combines a modular architecture with a sophisticated RL training strategy that leverages task grouping and multi-faceted rewards to address the complexities of learning long-term skill curation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on multi-turn agentic tasks (ALFWorld, WebShop) and single-turn reasoning tasks (AIME24, AIME25, GPQA-Diamond) reveal several key findings:

1. **Superior Performance and Efficiency:**
 - **Effectiveness:** SkillOS consistently outperforms both memory-free baselines and strong memory-based methods (ReasoningBank, MemP) across all benchmarks. For example, on ALFWorld with a Qwen3-8B executor, SkillOS improved the average success rate from 55.7% (ReasoningBank) to 61.2%. Similar trends were observed for WebShop (Score and SR) and reasoning tasks (Accuracy).
 - **Efficiency:** SkillOS achieved these performance gains with increased efficiency, requiring fewer interaction steps in agentic tasks and fewer tokens in reasoning tasks. On ALFWorld, it reduced average interaction steps by 2.2 to 3.1 compared to the "no memory" baseline, and consistently outperformed other memory-based approaches in efficiency. This suggests that learned skills enable the executor to identify procedural shortcuts and reduce redundant exploration.
 - **Value of RL Training:** A smaller 8B model trained with SkillOS as a curator (SkillOS) consistently outperformed a much stronger frontier model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) used directly as a zero-shot curator (SkillOS-gemini). This highlights that targeted RL training for curation can outweigh raw model scale, as it produces skills better aligned with the executor's needs.

2. **Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **Cross-Executor Generalization:** The RL-trained skill curator successfully generalized across different frozen agent executor backbones, including open-source models (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B) and frontier models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite). SkillOS consistently improved the performance of all tested executors, demonstrating its compatibility with varying executor capacities.
 - **Cross-Task Domain Generalization:** The learned skill curator also transferred effectively across different task domains. While training and testing on the same task provided the strongest gains, SkillOS still delivered performance improvements when generalized to different task types. Notably, curators trained on reasoning tasks transferred particularly well to agentic tasks, attributed to their acquisition of more abstract and high-level strategies (e.g., decomposition, verification, adaptive planning) which are broadly useful. Skills learned from agentic tasks, being more environment-specific, showed less transferability.

3. **Impact of Design Choices (Ablation Studies):**
 - **Composite Rewards:** Removing any component of the composite reward function (task outcome, function call validity, skill content quality, compression) led to performance degradation. The `r_cnt` (content quality) reward proved important for guiding skill updates, while `r_comp` (compression) contributed to more concise and usable repositories.
 - **Grouped Task Streams:** The most significant performance drop occurred when training on random task sequences instead of grouped task streams. This confirmed the importance of the grouped formulation in providing a consistent signal for learning long-term skill curation by evaluating early curation decisions against future related tasks.

4. **Evolution of Skill Curator Behavior and SkillRepo Contents:**
 - **Curator Behavior:** Early in training, the curator primarily focused on `insert_skill` operations, populating the SkillRepo with new knowledge. As training progressed, `update_skill` operations became increasingly frequent, indicating a shift from mere expansion to refining and consolidating existing skills. `delete_skill` remained a smaller fraction but showed a slight increasing trend.
 - **SkillRepo Structure:** The internal structure of skills evolved. Early skills included generic Markdown sections (e.g., "tips"). Over time, these additions shifted towards more actionable structures, such as "failure-handling logic" and "conditional branches," indicating a move towards execution-oriented refinement.
 - **Meta-Skill Emergence:** The SkillRepo's global organization also evolved. Initially dominated by narrow, task-specific skills, later repositories included a more diverse set of "meta-strategy skills" covering verification, fallback planning, systematic search, and strategy adjustment. This suggests the curator learned to expand the repository's strategic space, moving from isolated task-local procedures to more compositional, cross-task control knowledge.

5. **Attribution of Skill Usage:**
 - SkillOS showed a higher skill usage rate (skills invoked for all evaluation examples) and a higher successful skill usage rate. It also achieved greater skill coverage within the repository, implying better overall utility of the curated skills. Crucially, SkillOS used *fewer* skills on average per example compared to baselines, suggesting that performance gains came from more precise and targeted skill selection, rather than simply providing more context.

In essence, SkillOS demonstrated that a learned skill curator, trained with appropriate RL signals on grouped task streams, can enable LLM agents to effectively self-evolve by building, refining, and utilizing a high-quality skill repository, leading to improved performance and efficiency across diverse tasks and executor models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SkillOS research presents a significant contribution to the field of LLM-based agents, particularly in the domain of self-evolution and continuous learning. Its potential impacts are multifaceted:

1. **Advancing Self-Evolving Agents:** SkillOS directly addresses a fundamental challenge for LLM agents: transitioning from "one-off" task completion to sustained, adaptive learning in streaming environments. By providing a concrete method for agents to learn skill curation, it moves closer to the vision of truly autonomous agents that improve their capabilities over time without constant human intervention.

2. **Enhancing Agent Robustness and Scalability:** The ability of agents to dynamically update, refine, and even delete skills based on experience can lead to more robust and adaptable systems. Agents can learn from failures, consolidate successful strategies, and discard outdated or unhelpful knowledge, making them more resilient to novel situations and changes in their operating environment. This is crucial for deploying LLM agents in real-world, dynamic scenarios.

3. **Modular and Practical Agent Design:** By explicitly decoupling the trainable skill curator from a frozen agent executor, SkillOS proposes a highly modular architecture. This has significant practical implications:
 - It allows for independent development and iteration of the curation policy.
 - It enables the same learned curator to be paired with various LLM backbones for the executor, demonstrating broad applicability and reducing the need for extensive retraining of the core agent model.
 - The use of Markdown files for skills provides an auditable and interpretable memory format.

4. **Novel RL Paradigm for Long-Term Memory Management:** The introduction of grouped task streams and a composite reward function specifically designed to attribute delayed and indirect feedback to curation decisions is a methodological innovation. This approach overcomes a key challenge in applying RL to long-horizon memory management, where direct feedback is sparse. It offers a blueprint for future research in training meta-level agents that manage the knowledge base of primary task-solving agents.

5. **Efficiency Gains and Cost Reduction:** The demonstrated reduction in interaction steps and tokens per task implies that agents equipped with SkillOS can achieve better performance with lower computational costs. This makes the deployment of complex LLM agents more economically viable and environmentally sustainable.

6. **Insights into Emergent Learning Behaviors:** The analysis revealing the evolution of curator behaviors (e.g., shift from insertion to updating) and the emergence of higher-level meta-skills (e.g., failure recovery, systematic search) within the SkillRepo provides valuable insights into how agents can learn to organize and abstract knowledge. This contributes to a deeper understanding of agentic intelligence and learning dynamics.

7. **Foundational for Future Research:** SkillOS opens up several promising avenues for future work, including:
 - **Agentic search over memory:** Moving beyond static retrieval to active, iterative memory access.
 - **Hierarchical and compositional skills:** Enabling agents to learn and manage skills with nested structures and dependencies.
 - **Multi-agent and shared memory systems:** Extending the concepts of learned curation to collaborative agent environments.

Overall, SkillOS represents a concrete step towards building truly intelligent and adaptive LLM-based agents that can continuously learn from experience, manage their knowledge effectively, and operate with greater autonomy and efficiency in complex, dynamic settings.
