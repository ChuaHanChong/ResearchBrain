# SkillOS: Learning Skill Curation for Self-Evolving Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillOS, developed by researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Google Cloud AI Research, introduces an experience-driven reinforcement learning framework that enables Large Language Model (LLM) agents to automatically curate reusable skills. This framework facilitates continuous self-evolution, leading to improved task success rates and increased efficiency across diverse agentic and reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- LLM agents often act as "one-off" problem solvers, lacking the ability to accumulate, refine, or reuse knowledge from past interactions in streaming task environments.
- Existing skill curation methods face limitations due to manual effort, rigid heuristics, or short-horizon reinforcement learning (RL) approaches that fail to provide adequate feedback for complex, long-term skill evolution.
- Learning high-quality skill curation policies is challenging because optimal curation decisions have delayed and indirect effects on future task performance, making it difficult to attribute environmental feedback to specific actions.

## Method

- **SkillOS** employs a ==modular, multi-agent design== that decouples a trainable LLM-based ==Skill Curator== (managing an external ==SkillRepo==) from a frozen ==Agent Executor==.
- It introduces an ==experience-driven Reinforcement Learning training recipe== for the Skill Curator, utilizing ==grouped task streams== where skills derived from earlier experiences are evaluated by their utility in solving later, related tasks.
- A ==composite reward function== is designed to attribute downstream executor performance to specific curation decisions, incorporating signals for task outcome, function call validity, skill content quality, and SkillRepo compactness.

## Results

- SkillOS consistently outperforms memory-free and existing memory-based baselines on multi-turn agentic (ALFWorld, WebShop) and single-turn reasoning tasks, improving success rates and efficiency (e.g., **55.7% to 61.2%** success on ALFWorld with Qwen3-8B).
- The learned skill curator generalizes effectively across different executor LLM backbones (e.g., **Qwen3-8B to Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite**) and transfers knowledge across task domains, with reasoning-trained curators showing strong transferability to agentic tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of both the composite reward function and grouped task streams, with the latter leading to the most significant performance degradation when removed.
- Analysis of the SkillRepo's evolution shows a shift from simple skill insertion to frequent refinement (`update_skill`) and the emergence of **"meta-strategy skills"** (e.g., verification, adaptive planning), indicating the curator learns to expand the agent's strategic capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted RL training for skill curation can be more effective than using larger, frontier LLMs as zero-shot curators, as it produces skills better aligned with the executor's specific needs.
> - Structuring training instances into grouped task streams is critical for providing dense, long-horizon feedback, enabling the curator to learn complex, long-term skill management operations like updating and deleting skills.
> - The use of a multi-faceted composite reward function is essential for successfully optimizing skill curation, as it balances various aspects such as task success, operational correctness, skill quality, and repository efficiency.
> - The learned skill curator demonstrates robust generalization capabilities, improving performance for diverse executor models and showing effective knowledge transfer across different task domains.
