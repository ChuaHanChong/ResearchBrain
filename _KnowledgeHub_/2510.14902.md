# VLA^2: Empowering Vision-Language-Action Models with an Agentic Framework for Unseen Concept Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA2 is an agentic framework that wraps OpenVLA with external tools to manipulate objects whose appearance or naming is out of distribution. A planner splits instructions into subtasks, a vision module localizes targets with MM-GroundingDINO plus web image retrieval and paints SAM color masks, a language module swaps unknown nouns for known vocabulary, and a fine-tuned verifier gates progress, lifting hard-level success by 44.2%.

## Problem

- VLA models pre-trained on robot data collapse when object textures, appearances, or descriptions fall outside training distribution, despite handling in-domain visual and language variation well.
- Injecting broad concept knowledge by co-training on web-scale multimodal data is compute-heavy and cannot be re-run each time new object concepts appear.
- Prior agentic robot pipelines pass raw task-list prompts to the backbone, so out-of-distribution subgoals diverge from training and stall verification and execution.

## Method

- **VLA2**'s cognition module masks targets with ==SAM2.1-L== color overlays propagated by ==Cutie== video segmentation, so the backbone keys on instance regions rather than surface texture.
- Visual search branch retrieves web image collages for unknown words, has ==GLM-4.1V== generate five attribute keywords, re-prompts ==MM-GroundingDINO==, and caches both in ==vision memory==.
- Language module substitutes tokens absent from an NLTK-built ==KnownList== with GLM-generated in-vocabulary words; a ==Qwen2.5-VL verifier== checks subtask completion and triggers gripper-lift recovery.

## Results

- On the custom hard-level LIBERO variant VLA2 reaches **76.2%** success versus **60.0%** for pi-0 and **47.4%** for OpenVLA-OFT, averaging **81.5%** across all custom environments.
- In-domain LIBERO average success is **80.1%**, above Agentic Robot at **79.6%** and fine-tuned OpenVLA at **76.5%**, with a small dip to **86.2%** on Object tasks.
- Ablations on hard tasks drop success by **25.0** points without lexical replacement, **11.4** without masks, **11.0** without web retrieval, and **50.0** with all modules removed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning novel words to an in-distribution vocabulary matters more than richer visual cues; lexical substitution was the single dominant lever for out-of-distribution grounding.
> - Tool augmentation around a frozen action backbone can buy generalization that would otherwise demand costly re-training on web-scale multimodal corpora.
> - Gains rest on simulated appearance changes only, with detection accuracy limited by low-resolution views and no real-world validation reported.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself squarely within the rapidly evolving landscape of foundation models and their application to robotics, particularly in the domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and robotic action execution, hold immense promise for enabling general-purpose robots in open-world environments. They represent a paradigm shift towards end-to-end learning from large-scale robotic manipulation datasets.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation of current VLA models: their poor generalization to "unseen concepts"—objects or descriptions that lie outside their pre-training data distribution. While existing VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, RT-2) demonstrate robust performance on familiar tasks and variations within their training domain, their success rates plummet when confronted with novel object textures, appearances, or semantic descriptions. This lack of robust out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization is a significant barrier to deploying truly adaptable robots.

Previous attempts to address this challenge have included training VLAs on massive web-scale multimodal datasets (e.g., RT-2, ChatVLA). While these approaches can embed extensive conceptual knowledge, they are computationally demanding, require substantial resources, and struggle with the impracticality of iteratively updating models to incorporate newly emerging concepts.

This paper proposes an alternative and complementary approach rooted in the concept of "embodied agents." Inspired by the success of tool-augmented Large Language Model (LLM) agents, VLA$^2$ integrates an existing VLA model (OpenVLA) as a powerful execution backbone within a broader agentic framework. This framework leverages external "tools" such as web retrieval, object detection, and task planning modules to actively acquire and process information about unseen concepts. By transforming unfamiliar inputs into representations comprehensible by the underlying VLA, VLA$^2$ aims to overcome the generalization barrier without necessitating expensive re-training of the core VLA model. This strategy marks a shift from purely monolithic, end-to-end VLA models to hybrid systems that combine the strengths of foundation models with intelligent, tool-using agentic reasoning, thus pushing the frontier of robot generalization in open-vocabulary and dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for the VLA$^2$ framework stems from a critical weakness observed in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their significant failure rates when encountering "unseen concepts" in manipulation tasks. Despite impressive multi-task capabilities and generalization to variations in visual and language instructions, state-of-the-art VLA models, often pre-trained on large robotic datasets, cannot effectively handle objects whose visual characteristics or linguistic descriptions fall outside their training distribution. This limitation severely constrains their applicability in dynamic, open-world environments where novelty is common.

The authors articulate this problem by highlighting specific failure cases, such as OpenVLA's inability to comprehend instructions involving entirely novel objects or to transfer learned manipulation skills to scenarios with unfamiliar items. While approaches involving joint training with web-scale multimodal data aim to imbue VLAs with broader conceptual knowledge, they are resource-intensive and lack the agility needed for continuous adaptation to new concepts.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the VLA$^2$ framework are:

1. **To enhance the generalization capabilities of VLA models:** Specifically, to enable them to effectively manipulate objects described by unseen concepts (e.g., novel object textures, appearances, or language descriptions) without requiring re-training of the core VLA model.
2. **To develop a novel agentic framework:** This framework would integrate an existing VLA (like OpenVLA) as an execution module and orchestrate the invocation of diverse external tools.
3. **To leverage external knowledge sources:** By actively utilizing modules such as web retrieval and advanced object detection, the framework aims to gather visual and textual knowledge about target objects. This knowledge is then processed to bridge the gap between unseen concepts and the VLA's "known" conceptual space.
4. **To provide a flexible and adaptable solution:** The agentic design seeks to offer a more practical and less resource-intensive method for handling out-of-distribution objects compared to large-scale pre-training or fine-tuning, allowing for iterative updates with emerging concepts.
5. **To rigorously evaluate performance:** The authors aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of VLA$^2$ by creating a challenging new evaluation benchmark with varying levels of OOD difficulty within the LIBERO simulation environment.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond the limitations of purely end-to-end VLA models by augmenting them with intelligent, adaptive "cognitive" capabilities that allow them to dynamically interpret and act upon novel information, thereby making robotic manipulation more robust and generalizable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA$^2$ proposes a novel agentic framework that integrates an existing VLA model with external, specialized modules to enhance generalization to unseen concepts. The system is structured into three main components: Preliminary Information Processing, Cognition and Memory, and Judgment and Execution, all operating within the LIBERO simulation environment.

#### 3.1. A. Preliminary Information Processing

This stage focuses on initial decomposition and processing of high-level instructions and visual inputs.
1. **Planner:** Utilizes a locally deployed GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking model to break down complex natural language instructions into a sequence of structured, executable subtasks. Each subtask adheres to strict format constraints, including a single action verb and explicit specification of relevant objects and locations. A robust three-layer post-processing module handles linguistic extraction, error detection, regeneration, and hard-coded parsing to ensure high-quality output for downstream modules.
2. **Vision Pre-processing:** Employs a fine-tuned MM-GroundingDINO model to generate initial bounding boxes for objects and locations mentioned in the task, based on the first observed image. This step is designed to accurately recognize task-relevant objects within the LIBERO environment, with a custom dataset of 500 annotated images used for fine-tuning.

#### 3.2. B. Cognition & Memory

This is the core of VLA$^2$'s out-of-distribution (OOD) handling, actively transforming novel information into a format comprehensible by the VLA.
1. **Vision Module:** This module actively processes visual information to handle unseen object appearances.
 - **Mask Overlay:** Overlays transparent, color-coded masks on task-related objects and locations in the robot's third-person view. This design reduces reliance on surface textures, mitigating visual overfitting and providing clear visual cues.
 - **Double Judgment & Visual Search:** For each object/location, the system checks for a valid bounding box and auxiliary keywords. If either is missing, a "visual search branch" is triggered. This branch uses `bbid` (Bulk Bing Image Downloader) to retrieve web images for the unknown word, creates an image collage, and sends it to a GLM Understanding (Vision) module.
 - **GLM Understanding (Vision):** Based on the first image, web collage, and the unknown word, this module generates five concise, descriptive keywords (e.g., color, shape, function) that anchor the novel concept to elemental attributes. These keywords and the collage are cached in "vision memory" for future reuse.
 - **Vision Processing:** The enriched prompt (original text + keywords) is resubmitted to the MM-GroundingDINO detector for improved localization.
 - **SAM Segmentation & Color Encoding:** Validated bounding boxes are converted into pixel-accurate masks using SAM2.1-L. These masks are color-encoded (distinct palettes for objects and locations) and packaged with vision memory. This package is routed to the Language module and the VOS (Video Object Segmentation) pipeline, which uses Cutie to generate temporally consistent, color-coded masked image flows for the VLA.
 - **"Instant Learning" Rationale:** This pipeline converts unfamiliar visual inputs into familiar, attribute-based representations, enabling MM-GroundingDINO to generalize by decomposing novel concepts into known elemental properties, akin to how humans use prior knowledge to assimilate new information.
2. **Language Module:** This component aligns object-related tokens in task prompts with a controlled, known vocabulary.
 - **Double Judgment & Substitution:** For tokens not present in the VLA's "KnownList" (a vocabulary of training-set objects), a substitution mechanism is activated. The GLM Understanding (Text) module (shared with the planner) generates a replacement word.
 - **GLM Understanding (Text):** This module receives a rich context including the first image, cropped bounding boxes, web search collage, original prompt, web-derived keywords, the KnownList, and external API information. It generates a semantically appropriate replacement word, which is then stored in "text memory" for subsequent reuse.
 - **Text Processing:** All target terms in the task list are lexically replaced, and the final task list is repaired to ensure consistency.

#### 3.3. C. Judgment & Execution

This final stage monitors task progress and interacts with the environment through the VLA backbone.
1. **Judgment (Verifier):** A fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model acts as the verifier, assessing subtask completion. It's fine-tuned on LIBERO video segments with task-specific prompts to improve accuracy. A dynamic recovery mechanism is implemented to address stuck end-effectors, resetting the current task to "lift gripper" and resuming later.
2. **Execution (OpenVLA Backbone):** OpenVLA is chosen as the VLA backbone and is fine-tuned on a reformulated LIBERO dataset.
 - **Modified Input:** The original third-person RGB videos are replaced with RGB videos augmented by the transparent colored masks generated by the Vision module.
 - **Enhanced Task Prompts:** Task descriptions are reformatted into temporally segmented, plan-based prompts like "now do ‘current subtask’, the whole task is ‘joint of all subtasks’." This provides the VLA with both immediate and overarching task context, improving execution performance.
 - **KnownList:** An NLTK-based extractor builds a vocabulary of known object terms during OpenVLA fine-tuning, used by the Language module for lexical alignment.

#### 3.4. Custom Environment Design

To rigorously test OOD generalization, the authors constructed three new LIBERO variants (Easy, Medium, Hard) by modifying *only object appearances* in the Spatial and Goal environments.
- **Easy:** Simple color variations (e.g., black bowl to orange).
- **Medium:** Object replacements and recoloring with native LIBERO objects (e.g., black bowl to white bowl, wine bottle to blue bottle).
- **Hard:** Complete redesigns with complex patterns and semantic shifts (e.g., wine bottle to Moutai, black bowl to blue-and-white porcelain bowl, cream cheese to butter).

This comprehensive methodology, integrating sophisticated language models, computer vision tools, and a refined VLA backbone within an agentic control flow, forms a powerful system designed to tackle the critical challenge of unseen concept manipulation in robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate VLA$^2$'s zero-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) adaptability, its in-domain performance, and the contributions of its key modules through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. A. In-Domain Performance (Original LIBERO Environments)

On the original LIBERO benchmark (Table I), VLA$^2$ was compared against other VLA baselines (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, $\pi^0$, $\pi^0$-FAST) and the Agentic Robot framework. For a fair comparison, VLA$^2$ (using OpenVLA as its backbone) was primarily benchmarked against other OpenVLA-based systems (Class 2).
- VLA$^2$ achieved the highest average success rate (SR) in Class 2 at **80.1%**, surpassing Agentic Robot (79.6%) and fine-tuned OpenVLA (76.5%).
- A slight degradation on "Object" tasks (86.2% SR) was observed. The authors attributed this to a "perception bottleneck" where low-resolution observations, imprecise object names, and discrepancies between web-retrieved images and simulator views made fine-grained recognition difficult for MMGroundingDINO. Despite this, VLA$^2$ maintained competitive performance in-domain.

#### 4.2. B. Out-of-Distribution Generalization (Custom Environments)

The core evaluation focused on VLA$^2$'s performance on newly designed OOD environments (Easy, Medium, Hard) (Tables II and III).
- **Overall Trend:** All methods exhibited a decline in SR as OOD difficulty increased, but VLA$^2$ consistently demonstrated superior generalization.
- **Average Performance:** VLA$^2$ achieved the best overall average SR of **81.5%** across all custom environments.
- **Hard-Level Benchmark:** The most significant advantage was seen on the Hard environment, where VLA$^2$ achieved **76.2% SR**. This was a substantial improvement over leading baselines like $\pi^0$ (60.0%) and OpenVLA-OFT (47.4%).
- **Task-Level Robustness:** VLA$^2$ showed remarkable robustness to large semantic shifts. For instance, in the "moutai-rack" task (involving a redesigned wine bottle), VLA$^2$ scored 72% SR compared to $\pi^0$'s 44%. In "bowl-saucer" (novel bowl and synonym replacement), VLA$^2$ achieved 88% SR versus $\pi^0$'s 16%. These results strongly support the hypothesis that VLA$^2$'s knowledge-alignment pipeline effectively reforms unfamiliar inputs, making it less susceptible to OOD shifts.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Study (LIBERO-Hard Setup)

Three key modules were ablated to quantify their contribution to VLA$^2$'s generalization performance on the challenging Hard environment (Table III).
- **VLA$^2$ (w/o mask):** Disabling transparent instance/region overlays and color mask injection reduced the average SR from 76.2% to 64.8% (**-11.4%**). The largest drops occurred in interaction-heavy and cluttered scenes (e.g., "open-drawer" -26%, "moutai-rack" -36%), indicating the masks are crucial for object localization and discrimination, especially with occlusions.
- **VLA$^2$ (w/o replace):** Turning off lexical normalization and semantic substitution yielded the largest average degradation, from 76.2% to 51.2% (**-25.0%**). Catastrophic failures were observed when novel or compositional nouns needed grounding (e.g., "bowl-saucer" -72%, "moutai-rack" -56%). This strongly indicates that synonym/alias replacement is the **dominant lever** for bridging textual OOD concepts to the model's in-distribution vocabulary.
- **VLA$^2$ (w/o web):** Removing web image/text search and retrieved memory lowered the average SR to 65.2% (**-11.0%**). This disproportionately harmed tasks involving novel-brand targets (e.g., "moutai-rack" -48%, "moutai-cabinet" -52%), demonstrating the decisive net benefit of external retrieval for unfamiliar concepts.
- **Agentic Robot (RP) (all modules removed and no subtask augmentation):** This ablation, closely replicating the original Agentic Robot framework without VLA$^2$'s enhancements, resulted in a dramatic collapse of average SR to 26.2% (**-50.0%**). Many hard tasks dropped to zero. This highlights the combined importance of VLA$^2$'s perception, cognition, and execution prompting strategies. The original Agentic Robot's task-list prompt format, when combined with OOD elements, led to subgoals diverging from training distributions, causing verifier failures and preventing task progression. VLA$^2$'s "now do current subtask, while conditioning on the full task context" prompt format, combined with its cognitive modules, proved crucial for stabilizing execution.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that VLA$^2$'s agentic framework, particularly its "cognition and memory" modules (masking, lexical replacement, and web retrieval), significantly improves VLA generalization to unseen concepts, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on challenging OOD tasks without degrading in-domain performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA$^2$ framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of generalization to unseen object concepts. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Generalization Gap for VLAs:** The most profound impact of VLA$^2$ is its ability to overcome a fundamental limitation of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) objects and instructions. By demonstrating a substantial improvement in success rates on tasks involving novel object textures, appearances, and semantic descriptions, VLA$^2$ moves the field closer to robust, general-purpose robots capable of operating in diverse, open-world environments where encountering unseen items is inevitable.

2. **Pioneering a Modular, Agentic Approach:** Instead of solely relying on increasingly large and resource-intensive pre-training of monolithic VLA models, VLA$^2$ champions a modular, agentic framework. This approach augments existing VLA models (like OpenVLA) with a suite of external, specialized tools (task planning, web retrieval, object detection, verification). This modularity offers greater flexibility, allowing for easier integration of new capabilities and more agile adaptation to emerging concepts without requiring a full re-training cycle of the foundational VLA. This paradigm shift could make VLA development more scalable and practical.

3. **Introduction of "Instant Learning" for Robotics:** The "Cognition and Memory" component, particularly the dynamic use of web search and large language models (GLM) for keyword generation and lexical substitution, introduces a form of "instant learning" to robotic perception and language understanding. The ability to decompose novel concepts into elemental attributes and anchor them to known ones, on-the-fly, provides a powerful mechanism for rapid assimilation of unfamiliar information, a crucial capability for intelligent agents.

4. **Enhanced Robustness through Multimodal Alignment:** The framework's design, including color-coded masking for visual perception and robust lexical replacement for language understanding, strengthens the alignment between visual and textual modalities. This fine-grained, instance- and token-level alignment contributes directly to improved performance and robustness, especially in cluttered or semantically ambiguous scenes.

5. **Rigorous Benchmarking for OOD Generalization:** The creation of the Easy, Medium, and Hard LIBERO-based OOD benchmarks is itself a valuable contribution to the research community. It provides a standardized and challenging testbed for evaluating future methods tackling generalization in robotic manipulation, facilitating clearer comparisons and driving further innovation.

6. **Future Directions and Broader Impact:** The authors acknowledge current limitations, such as reliance on rigid procedural structures and the lack of real-world experiments. Future work on enhancing system autonomy, enabling more diverse tool invocation, and validating performance in physical open-world scenarios will be essential. This research sets a strong foundation for future embodied AI systems, pushing towards robots that can not only execute complex tasks but also intelligently perceive, reason about, and interact with objects they have never encountered before, ultimately fostering the development of truly intelligent and adaptable robotic assistants.

In conclusion, VLA$^2$ offers a compelling and practical solution to a significant hurdle in robotic intelligence. By intelligently combining the strengths of foundation models with an adaptive agentic framework, it paves the way for a new generation of robots that are more robust, adaptable, and capable of navigating the inherent uncertainties of the real world.
