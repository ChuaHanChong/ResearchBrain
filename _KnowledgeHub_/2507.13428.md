# "PhyWorldBench": A Comprehensive Evaluation of Physical Realism in Text-to-Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of California, Santa Cruz, NVIDIA Research, and Northeastern University introduce PhyWorldBench, a comprehensive benchmark to evaluate text-to-video models' adherence to physical laws. The benchmark evaluates 12 state-of-the-art models across fundamental, composite, and "Anti-Physics" categories and proposes a Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) method using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for efficient, objective assessment of physical realism.

## Problem

- Text-to-video (T2V) models produce visually compelling content but struggle with accurately simulating and adhering to the laws of physics.
- Existing T2V benchmarks often lack comprehensive coverage of diverse physical phenomena, including scenarios specifically designed to test for physical understanding.
- Evaluating physical realism in generated videos is costly and subjective, requiring a more efficient and objective methodology.

## Method

- Developed **PhyWorldBench**, a multi-dimensional benchmark featuring 10 main physics categories, 50 subcategories, and 1,050 curated prompts, including a novel "Anti-Physics" category to probe models' understanding of physical law violations.
- Introduced the Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) method, which leverages Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with explicit context and Chain-of-Thought reasoning for scalable and objective zero-shot physics evaluation.
- Conducted an extensive evaluation of 12 state-of-the-art text-to-video generation models by generating 12,600 videos, assessing their physical realism through both human annotation and the CAP method.

## Results

- Pika 2.0 achieved the highest overall success rate of 0.262 in physical realism, with other models demonstrating varying strengths but generally low performance, revealing significant room for improvement across the board.
- Models struggled particularly with scenarios involving erratic visual changes (22.1% success rate) and complex physical interactions (11.7% success rate), often simplifying or avoiding abrupt transformations.
- Physics-enhanced prompts generally led to better results, indicating models can utilize explicit physical cues in prompts, but detailed narrative prompts primarily enhanced visual aesthetics rather than physics accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art text-to-video models exhibit consistently low success rates (e.g., Pika 2.0 at 26.2%) in generating physically accurate content, indicating substantial limitations in physics understanding.
> - Models frequently 'rationalize' failures in "Anti-Physics" scenarios by reverting to physically plausible or static scenes, suggesting they primarily reproduce patterns from real-world training data rather than demonstrating a deep causal understanding of physics.
> - The Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) method significantly improves MLLM-based evaluation, achieving ROC-AUC scores of 80.3 for Semantic Adherence and 75.1 for Physical Commonsense, validating MLLMs as effective tools for objective and scalable assessment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video (T2V) generation has advanced significantly, producing visually coherent and often photorealistic content. These advancements enable applications in various sectors, including entertainment, education, and scientific visualization. However, a critical challenge remains: the ability of these models to accurately simulate physical phenomena and adhere to the laws of physics. Existing T2V benchmarks, such as VBench and EvalCrafter, primarily focus on metrics like visual quality, temporal coherence, diversity, and compositionality. While some recent works, like Morpheus, VideoPhy, PhyGenBench, DEVIL, VideoPhy-2, and Physics-IQ, have begun to address physical plausibility, they often have limitations. These limitations include narrower coverage of physical laws, smaller datasets, reliance on specific event orders, or evaluators fine-tuned to particular benchmarks, which may not generalize well.

This work introduces PhyWorldBench to address these gaps by providing a substantially expanded and more diverse benchmark. It aims to comprehensively evaluate T2V models' understanding of physics across a wide range of principles and scenarios. A distinguishing feature is the inclusion of "Anti-Physics" scenarios, which are designed to test whether models genuinely understand physics principles or merely replicate patterns from real-world training data. The benchmark also introduces an efficient, scalable, and objective evaluation metric leveraging multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to reduce evaluation costs compared to traditional human evaluations. By identifying specific challenges and proposing prompt guidelines, PhyWorldBench contributes to the development of more robust and physically accurate video generation models, aiming to bridge the gap between visual fidelity and physical realism in synthetic video content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to comprehensively evaluate the capability of text-to-video generation models to simulate real-world physical phenomena. This overarching goal is motivated by the observation that despite significant progress in generating high-quality and photorealistic videos, current models frequently fail to adhere to fundamental laws of physics, leading to outputs that are visually appealing but physically implausible.

The specific objectives and motivations include:

- **To establish a comprehensive and multi-dimensional benchmark:** The researchers aim to create a rigorous evaluation framework, PhyWorldBench, that systematically assesses T2V models across various levels of physical complexity. This includes fundamental principles (e.g., object motion, energy conservation), composite real-world phenomena (e.g., rigid body dynamics, human motion), and deliberately unphysical scenarios. The motivation is to move beyond superficial visual fidelity and probe models' deeper understanding of how the world works.
- **To identify pivotal challenges in physical realism:** By evaluating state-of-the-art T2V models against this benchmark, the research seeks to pinpoint specific areas where models struggle with physics adherence. This involves identifying difficult scenarios, problematic physics categories, and common failure modes (e.g., temporal inconsistency, unrealistic motion, ignoring physical constraints). The motivation is to guide future research and development efforts toward improving the physical reasoning capabilities of T2V models.
- **To assess genuine physics understanding versus pattern replication:** The introduction of an "Anti-Physics" category is a key objective. Prompts in this category intentionally violate physical laws (e.g., defying gravity). The motivation behind this is two-fold:
 1. To determine if models truly comprehend physical laws or merely reproduce patterns observed in their predominantly real-world training data. If a model generates a physically accurate video despite being prompted for an unphysical scenario, it suggests a lack of genuine understanding or an over-reliance on learned real-world priors.
 2. To explore the practical value of generating anti-physics content for creative applications where imaginative or impossible scenarios are desired.
- **To develop an efficient and objective evaluation methodology:** Recognizing the cost and subjectivity of large-scale human evaluation, the researchers aim to design a novel, zero-shot evaluation method utilizing current MLLMs. The motivation is to provide a scalable, unbiased metric for assessing physical realism, making continuous evaluation of new models more feasible.
- **To provide practical recommendations for prompt design:** The study aims to analyze the effect of different prompt variations (general, physics-enhanced, detailed narrative) on model performance. Based on this analysis, the motivation is to offer targeted recommendations for crafting prompts that can potentially enhance the fidelity of generated videos to physical principles, assisting users and developers in creating more physically accurate content.

Overall, the motivation is to elevate the expectations for T2V models from mere photorealism to a deeper, physics-aware simulation capability, thereby unlocking more robust and reliable applications across scientific, educational, and entertainment domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the creation and application of PhyWorldBench, a structured benchmark designed for evaluating the physical realism of text-to-video (T2V) generation models. The approach integrates a meticulous dataset creation pipeline, comprehensive evaluation metrics, and an extensive assessment of various state-of-the-art T2V models.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design (PhyWorldBench)

PhyWorldBench is structured hierarchically to cover a wide spectrum of physical principles:
- **Categories:** Derived from physics literature (e.g., *Fundamentals of Physics*, *Classical Mechanics*) and expert consultations, the benchmark defines 10 main physics categories. These are further organized into three levels:
 - **Fundamental Physics:** Covers basic laws such as object motion, energy transfer, fluid dynamics, lighting, and material deformation.
 - **Composite Physics:** Involves complex real-world phenomena emerging from multiple interacting principles, such as rigid body dynamics, scale effects, and human/animal motion.
 - **Anti-Physics:** Scenarios deliberately designed to violate real-world physical laws, including defying gravity or perpetual motion.
- **Subcategories and Scenarios:** Each of the 10 main categories is divided into 5 subcategories (e.g., "Object Motion and Kinematics" includes "Linear Motion," "Parabolic Motion"). Under each subcategory, 7 distinct scenarios are developed.
- **Prompt Variations:** For each scenario, three types of prompts are crafted to assess models under varying levels of detail and complexity:
 - **Event Prompt:** A concise, straightforward description of an event (e.g., "A rocket launches into the sky.").
 - **Physics-Enhanced Prompt:** Builds on the event prompt by incorporating physics-related phenomena without explicitly stating physical laws (e.g., "A rocket launches vertically into the sky, traveling straight into the air.").
 - **Detailed Narrative Prompt:** Enriches the event prompt with vivid contextual elements and descriptive details, aiming for immersive and visually rich outputs.
This structured design results in a total of 1,050 curated prompts (10 categories * 5 subcategories * 7 scenarios * 3 prompt types).

#### 3.2. Dataset Creation Pipeline

The creation of PhyWorldBench follows a three-step human-in-the-loop pipeline (Figure 3):
1. **Initial Physics Categories Definition:** Physics experts and authors collaborated to define the 10 main categories and their 5 subcategories, ensuring comprehensiveness and relevance to fundamental physics concepts.
2. **Prompt Creation:**
 - State-of-the-art large language models (LLMs) such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro were initially used to generate 20 "Event Prompts" for each subcategory.
 - Human experts then selected 7 diverse and representative prompts from these 20.
 - These "Event Prompts" were meticulously reviewed and validated by human experts for quality and relevance.
 - Human annotators then created "Physics-Enhanced Prompts" by adding natural physical consequences.
 - "Detailed Narrative Prompts" were generated by LLMs based on the "Event Prompts" using specific queries and subsequently verified by human annotators.
 - An ethical review was conducted to ensure all prompts were unbiased and free from harmful content. This rigorous process yielded 1,050 high-quality prompts.
3. **Standard Creation:**
 - A Yes/No evaluation metric was adopted to objectively assess video quality.
 - **Basic Standards:** Define whether essential objects are present and if the main action/event is depicted (Semantic Adherence - SA).
 - **Key Standards:** Describe the specific physical phenomena that should occur if the event takes place in the real world (Physical Commonsense - PC). Annotators were instructed to focus on natural physical consequences.
 - For a video to be deemed "successful" in terms of physical realism, it must satisfy both SA=1 and PC=1.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics and Process

- **Human Evaluation:**
 - Large-scale human evaluation was conducted using Amazon Mechanical Turk.
 - Annotators assessed generated videos against the Basic and Key Standards, providing a "Yes" or "No" for each criterion.
 - Each video was evaluated by three independent annotators, with the final decision determined by a majority vote.
- **Automated Evaluation (MLLM as Zero-Shot Physics Evaluator):**
 - To address the cost and subjectivity of human evaluation, a novel method called **Context-Aware Prompt (CAP)** was developed.
 - The observation was made that MLLMs, primarily trained on real-world videos, tend to rationalize unrealistic content. Their accuracy improved when informed that the video was AI-generated.
 - CAP explicitly instructs the MLLM (e.g., GPT-o1, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Qwen-VL-2.0) that the video is generated and might contain physics issues.
 - CAP employs a two-step "Chain-of-Thought" prompting strategy:
 1. The MLLM first generates a detailed description of the video, including objects, events, and observed physics phenomena.
 2. It then uses this description to provide a final "Yes/No" answer against the defined Basic and Key Standards.
 - Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the "Context" and "Chain-of-Thought" components in improving evaluation accuracy, particularly for Physical Commonsense.
 - CAP's neutrality was validated by measuring minimal false positive rate changes in physics-correct videos.

#### 3.4. Models Evaluated

The study evaluated 12 state-of-the-art text-to-video generation models:
- **Proprietary Models (5):** Sora-Turbo (OpenAI), Gen-3 (Runway Team), Kling 1.6 (KlingAI), Pika 2.0 (Pika Labs Team), Luma (Luma AI Team).
- **Open-Source Models (7):** Hunyuan 720p (Kong et al.), Open-Sora 2.0 (Peng et al.), Open-Sora-Plan 1.3 (Lin et al.), CogVideoX-1.5 (Hong et al.), LTX-Video (HaCohen et al.), Step-video-T2V (Ma et al.), Wanx-2.1 (WanTeam et al.).

A total of 12,600 videos (1,050 prompts * 12 models) were generated for evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 12 state-of-the-art T2V models on PhyWorldBench yielded several key findings regarding their ability to simulate physical realism:

#### 4.1. Overall Model Performance

- **Limited Physical Realism:** Despite advancements in visual quality, all models demonstrate substantial limitations in accurately simulating real-world physics. The overall success rates (satisfying both Semantic Adherence and Physical Commonsense) remain low across the board.
- **Leading Models:** Among proprietary models, Pika 2.0 achieved the highest overall success rate (0.262). For open-source models, Wanx-2.1 showed the strongest overall performance (0.189), closely followed by Hunyuan 720p (0.185).
- **Performance Gap:** A noticeable gap exists between proprietary and open-source models in overall physics adherence, with proprietary models generally performing better, though some open-source models like Hunyuan and Wanx can outperform certain proprietary counterparts in specific metrics.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Physics Categories

- **Declining Performance with Complexity:** Model performance consistently declined from Fundamental Physics to Composite Physics and further to Anti-Physics categories (Table 3). This indicates that models struggle more with intricate interactions, multiple physical principles, and especially with scenarios that intentionally defy physics.
- **Challenges in Anti-Physics:** The Anti-Physics category showed the lowest success rates across all models, highlighting a difficulty in generating content that deliberately violates physical laws. Models often generated a physically plausible scene instead of the requested unphysical one.

#### 4.3. Model-Specific Analysis

- **Realism vs. Stylization:** Models like Wanx, Step-Video-T2V, Gen-3, Hunyuan, Kling, and Sora produced generally realistic videos. In contrast, Pika and Luma frequently exhibited stylized lighting and colors.
- **Object Stability and Motion Quality:** Stability of generated objects varied significantly. CogVideoX-1.5, Open-Sora, and LTX-Video often generated warped or distorted shapes. Kling and Gen-3 produced smoother motion, while Open-Sora-Plan and Luma sometimes introduced slow-motion effects, and CogVideoX-1.5 and Open-Sora showed jittery movements.

#### 4.4. Effect of Prompt Enhancement

- **Physics-Enhanced Prompts Improve Results:** Explicitly integrating physics-related phenomena into prompts (Physics-enhanced Prompts) generally led to better performance compared to concise "Event Prompts" (Table 4). This suggests models possess a strong prompt-following ability.
- **Detailed Prompts Not Always Beneficial:** However, adding extensive narrative details (Detailed Narrative Prompts) did not consistently or significantly improve physics accuracy. While such prompts might enhance clarity or aesthetic quality, they did not resolve deeper physics limitations.

#### 4.5. Anti-Physics Analysis and Static-Scene Rationalization

- **Prioritizing Real-World Patterns:** In Anti-Physics scenarios, models frequently defaulted to generating content consistent with real-world physics rather than following the unphysical instructions (e.g., a glass with "reversed gravity wine" would remain still). This suggests models prioritize mimicking patterns from their training data over a genuine understanding of underlying physical laws.
- **Static-Scene Rationalization:** An emerging failure mode termed "static-scene rationalization" was observed (Table 13). Higher-quality models, when faced with complex or unphysical prompts, often generated static or minimally animated scenes to avoid producing incorrect physical motion, effectively "rationalizing" the failure by minimizing dynamics.

#### 4.6. Failure to Handle Erratic Visual Changes and Higher Complexity

- **Smoothed Animations over Disruptions:** Models struggled to depict events involving erratic visual changes, such as collisions, shattering glass, or deformation (Table 16). Instead of simulating realistic disruptions, they tended to produce continuous, smoothed animations where objects remained intact or interactions were muted.
- **Breakdown at Higher Complexity:** Performance deteriorated significantly in scenarios involving multiple interacting forces, objects, or complex constraints (Table 17, Figure 20). For instance, models failed to correctly simulate differential falling speeds for a feather and a stone or realistic interactions between multiple objects. This indicates a lack of robust understanding of how multiple physical forces interact in complex environments.

#### 4.7. Cinematic Effects vs. Physical Plausibility

- Well-performing models often prioritized cinematic aesthetics, introducing exaggerated motion dynamics (e.g., unnatural acceleration or floating objects) for dramatic effect rather than strict physical realism. This can lead to physically distorted but visually appealing outputs.

#### 4.8. MLLM-based Automated Evaluation (CAP)

- **Effectiveness of CAP:** The proposed Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) method, leveraging MLLMs, significantly outperformed other MLLM-based evaluation strategies, particularly in assessing Physical Commonsense (Table 2, Table 5, Table 6). CAP achieved ROC-AUC scores of 80.3 for Semantic Adherence and 75.1 for Physical Commonsense, demonstrating its strong performance without fine-tuning.
- **Consistency with Human Evaluation:** CAP's results showed high consistency with human evaluation, making it a viable and efficient alternative for large-scale assessment.

#### 4.9. Influence of Model Scale and Sampling

- **Scaling Benefits:** Larger models consistently exhibited better performance on foundational physics tasks compared to their smaller counterparts (Table 8).
- **Compute Impact:** Increased sampling budgets (longer generation times or more steps) also correlated with improved physics accuracy, suggesting that dedicating more computational resources to finer-grained sampling can enhance adherence to physical laws.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhyWorldBench represents a significant contribution to the field of text-to-video generation by establishing a comprehensive and rigorous benchmark for evaluating physical realism. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancing Physical Realism in T2V Models:** By providing a structured and multi-dimensional evaluation framework, PhyWorldBench elevates the standard for T2V model development. It shifts the focus beyond mere photorealism to include adherence to physical laws, pushing the field towards generating more scientifically accurate and plausible video content.
- **Identifying Critical Research Directions:** The benchmark meticulously identifies specific gaps and challenges faced by current T2V models in simulating physics, such as handling complex interactions, erratic visual changes, and anti-physics scenarios. These insights provide clear directions for future research, encouraging the development of models that incorporate deeper physical reasoning, physics-aware architectures, and better training strategies.
- **Guiding Model Development and Training:** The findings regarding the impact of prompt variations, model scale, and sampling budgets offer actionable recommendations for developers. For instance, explicitly integrating physical phenomena into prompts is shown to be beneficial, and continued scaling of models and computational resources for generation are suggested pathways to improvement.
- **Enabling Efficient and Objective Evaluation:** The introduction of Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) as a zero-shot MLLM-based evaluator offers a scalable, cost-effective, and objective alternative to human evaluation. This innovation facilitates continuous and widespread assessment of new T2V models, accelerating research progress by providing rapid feedback on physics capabilities.
- **Broadening Application Scope:** Models that can accurately simulate physics will unlock new applications in various domains:
 - **Scientific Visualization:** Generating physically accurate simulations for research and education in fields like engineering, material science, and astronomy.
 - **Education:** Creating interactive, physically realistic learning materials for demonstrating scientific principles.
 - **Entertainment and Gaming:** Producing more believable and immersive virtual worlds and special effects.
 - **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Simulating complex physical environments for training and testing AI agents in a physically consistent manner.
- **Addressing Fundamental AI Questions:** The "Anti-Physics" category probes whether AI models truly "understand" physics or merely mimic patterns. This contributes to the broader discussion on AI's ability to reason and generalize beyond its training data, moving towards more intelligent and grounded AI systems.
- **Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI:** The accompanying impact statement highlights the ethical implications, emphasizing the need for transparency, fairness, and responsible deployment of physics-driven video synthesis. By promoting physically accurate generation, the benchmark helps mitigate risks associated with misinformation, biased simulations, or unintended real-world consequences arising from unrealistic AI-generated content.

In conclusion, PhyWorldBench establishes a foundational tool for advancing the physical realism of text-to-video generation, providing both a robust evaluation method and critical insights to guide the development of more capable and trustworthy AI models.
